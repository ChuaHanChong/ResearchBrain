# SoftCoT++: Test-Time Scaling with Soft Chain-of-Thought Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> SoftCoT++ introduces the first framework for scalable test-time reasoning in continuous latent space, leveraging specialized initial tokens and a contrastive learning loss to generate diverse soft thoughts. This approach consistently boosts performance across various reasoning tasks and LLM architectures, outperforming prior methods and demonstrating complementarity with discrete-space scaling.

## Problem

- No established methods existed for applying Test-Time Scaling (TTS) to continuous-space Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning, preventing these approaches from fully realizing their potential.
- The deterministic nature of continuous latent representations makes it non-trivial to sample diverse reasoning paths or multiple hypotheses, which is a core mechanism for improving performance in discrete CoT TTS methods.
- The challenge was to enable scalable test-time reasoning in continuous latent space to enhance LLM performance on complex tasks.

## Method

- **SoftCoT++** introduces ==multiple specialized initial tokens== to prompt the assistant model to generate diverse ==continuous latent soft thought representations== for the same input query.
- It incorporates a ==contrastive learning loss== to maximize the distance and variance among these diverse soft thought representations in the ==latent space==, encouraging a broader exploration of the solution space.
- The approach optimizes a ==lightweight projection module== while keeping the main LLM and assistant model frozen, maintaining efficiency benefits during inference.

## Results

- **SoftCoT++** consistently outperforms all established baselines, including its predecessor SoftCoT-SC, across **5** diverse reasoning benchmarks (e.g., GSM8K, StrategyQA) and **2** LLM architectures (LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct and Qwen3-8B).
- Ablation studies confirm that the contrastive learning objective is critical for maximizing diversity among soft thoughts, significantly contributing to the performance gains.
- Combining SoftCoT++ (thinking-stage scaling) with Self-Consistency (reasoning-stage scaling) yields further additive performance improvements, demonstrating their synergistic effect.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Test-time scaling can be effectively applied to continuous latent space, which was previously a challenge for continuous-space reasoning methods.
> - Explicitly promoting diversity among continuous latent thoughts via a contrastive learning objective is crucial for realizing performance gains from test-time scaling.
> - Scaling in the continuous "thinking" stage is complementary and synergistic with scaling in the discrete "reasoning" stage, allowing for additive performance gains.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research on SoftCoT++ is situated at the intersection of several rapidly evolving fields within artificial intelligence: Large Language Models (LLMs), Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning, and Test-Time Scaling (TTS).

The spectacular performance gains in LLMs over recent years (e.g., GPT series, PaLM, LLaMA, Qwen) have primarily been driven by scaling up training-time computation, leading to models with emergent reasoning abilities. A pivotal development in harnessing these abilities has been **Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting**, which guides LLMs to generate explicit intermediate reasoning steps, significantly boosting their accuracy on complex tasks.

Building upon this, a newer paradigm, **Test-Time Scaling (TTS)**, has emerged. TTS aims to further enhance LLM performance by allocating additional computational resources *during inference*, without modifying the model's parameters. This approach is highly appealing as it allows for performance improvements on already deployed, fixed models, offering flexibility and efficiency gains compared to costly retraining. Existing TTS methods broadly fall into parallel scaling (e.g., Self-Consistency, Best-of-N, where multiple reasoning chains are generated independently and aggregated) and sequential scaling (where later computations are guided by earlier intermediate steps). A common characteristic of these conventional TTS methods, however, is their reliance on operating within the *discrete token space* for generating intermediate steps. This token-by-token generation can be inefficient and potentially lead to information loss.

In parallel to the advancements in discrete-space reasoning, a nascent but growing area of research has begun exploring **reasoning in a continuous latent space**. Pioneers like Coconut (Hao et al., 2024) and the authors' own SoftCoT (Xu et al., 2025) have demonstrated that leveraging continuous latent "thoughts" can enhance reasoning quality. The core idea is that these latent representations encode informative thinking more richly, without the limitations and information loss associated with discrete, autoregressive token generation. SoftCoT, in particular, was noted for its superior performance on state-of-the-art LLMs compared to Coconut, achieving strong results while freezing the main LLM and using a small assistant model to generate these soft thoughts.

Despite the promise of continuous-space reasoning, a significant challenge has remained: **how to apply Test-Time Scaling to these continuous representations.** Unlike discrete token spaces, where stochastic sampling (e.g., temperature sampling) naturally allows for generating multiple, diverse reasoning paths, continuous latent representations are typically deterministic for a given input. This determinism fundamentally limits the ability to explore diverse "thinking paths" or generate multiple hypotheses—a core mechanism exploited by parallel TTS methods like self-consistency.

SoftCoT++ directly addresses this critical gap. It aims to be the **first framework to enable scalable test-time reasoning in continuous latent space**. By providing a mechanism to generate diverse latent thoughts, SoftCoT++ attempts to bring the benefits of TTS, previously largely confined to discrete CoT, into the more expressive and information-rich domain of continuous reasoning. This work extends the capabilities of continuous-space reasoning, potentially unlocking new performance ceilings for LLMs at inference time and pushing the boundaries of what is possible with efficient, fixed-parameter models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for SoftCoT++ stems from the inherent limitations of existing approaches to Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning and Test-Time Scaling (TTS), particularly concerning the relatively new paradigm of continuous-space reasoning. The paper identifies two core issues:

1. **Absence of Test-Time Scaling for Continuous-Space CoT:** While TTS has proven highly effective in boosting performance for discrete CoT methods by expanding computational budgets during inference, no established methods existed for applying TTS to continuous-space reasoning. This absence represents a significant bottleneck, preventing continuous-space approaches from fully realizing their potential on complex tasks where additional inference-time computation could be beneficial.
2. **Scalability Challenges Due to Deterministic Continuous Representations:** This is the more fundamental problem. In discrete token space, generating diverse reasoning trajectories is straightforward through sampling (e.g., varying temperature for token generation). This diversity is crucial for ensemble methods like self-consistency, which aggregate multiple paths to achieve robustness and higher accuracy. However, in continuous latent space, the representation generated for a given input is typically deterministic and fixed. This makes it non-trivial, or even impossible, to sample diverse reasoning paths or multiple hypotheses from the latent space itself. Consequently, a key mechanism for improving performance—exploring a variety of reasoning trajectories—is unavailable.

Given these limitations, the paper explicitly poses its central research question: **"How can we enable scalable test-time reasoning in continuous latent space?"**

The key objectives of SoftCoT++ are therefore:

- **To extend SoftCoT to the Test-Time Scaling paradigm:** The primary goal is to adapt SoftCoT, which uses continuous latent thoughts, to support the allocation of extra computation during inference to improve reasoning performance.
- **To enable diverse exploration of thinking paths in continuous space:** This involves finding a method to overcome the deterministic nature of latent representations, allowing the model to "think" in multiple distinct ways even within the continuous domain.
- **To enhance LLM reasoning performance through continuous-space scaling:** By introducing diversity in the thinking stage, the aim is to boost the overall accuracy and robustness of LLMs on various reasoning benchmarks.
- **To ensure compatibility with conventional discrete-space scaling techniques:** The authors also aim for SoftCoT++ to be complementary to existing TTS methods like self-consistency, allowing for a synergistic effect where scaling can occur in both the continuous thinking stage and the discrete reasoning stage.
- **To provide a more accurate estimation of the true latent-thought distribution:** The theoretical motivation suggests that by encouraging greater variance and diversity among generated soft thoughts, SoftCoT++ can better approximate the underlying distribution of effective latent reasoning paths.

In essence, SoftCoT++ seeks to marry the expressiveness and information-preserving qualities of continuous-space reasoning with the powerful performance-boosting capabilities of test-time scaling, thereby pushing the frontier of efficient and effective LLM inference.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SoftCoT++ builds directly upon the SoftCoT framework, aiming to introduce test-time scaling capabilities into the "thinking" stage, which operates in a continuous latent space. The methodology revolves around generating diverse soft thought representations and enhancing this diversity through a novel training objective.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation and SoftCoT Review

The paper first formalizes the LLM problem-solving process into three stages: Thinking (generating intermediate steps `T`), Reasoning (producing rationales `R` based on `T`), and Answer Generation (`A`). Classical Chain-of-Thought (CoT) methods generate `T` and `R` as discrete tokens.
SoftCoT, the foundation of this work, deviates by employing a smaller "assistant model" to produce **latent soft thought tokens (`T_soft`)**. These are continuous representations that serve as implicit cues for a larger, fixed LLM to then generate discrete rationales and answers. Crucially, in SoftCoT, only a lightweight "projection module" (mapping assistant model hidden states to the reasoning model's input space) is trained, while both the assistant and main LLM remain frozen.

#### 3.2. Challenges in Scaling Continuous Space Reasoning

The core challenge identified is that, unlike discrete token sampling which naturally allows for multiple reasoning paths, a continuous latent representation `T_soft` generated by SoftCoT is deterministic for a given input. This makes direct "sampling" of diverse continuous thoughts problematic.

#### 3.3. Pilot Experiments: SoftCoT-P (Perturbed SoftCoT)

To explore the feasibility of continuous-space scaling, the authors conducted pilot experiments using "SoftCoT-P". Based on **Assumption 1** (that `T_soft` is a single sample from a smooth, differentiable density `P_G`) and **Lemma 1** (that small perturbations `δ` keep a sample within a high-probability region of `P_G`), they attempted to simulate diversity by adding small perturbations (`δ_i`) to the deterministic `T_soft`: `T_i_soft = T_soft + δ_i`.
While this approach demonstrated feasibility (comparable performance to SoftCoT-SC, which scales in discrete space), it did not outperform it. The hypothesis was that small perturbations explore only a very narrow neighborhood, limiting the true diversity of reasoning paths.

#### 3.4. SoftCoT++ Innovations

To overcome the limitations of simple perturbation and achieve a better approximation of the true latent thought distribution `P_G`, SoftCoT++ introduces two main mechanisms, guided by **Lemma 2**. Lemma 2 theoretically suggests that a candidate distribution `Q2` is better than `Q1` at describing `P_G` if `Var[Q1] < Var[Q2] ≤ Var[P]`. This implies two strategies: (1) generate multiple distinct soft thought representations, and (2) encourage higher variance among them.

- **Diverse Input Sequence via Specialized Initial Tokens:**
 - Instead of using a single type of placeholder token (e.g., `[UNK]`) for the assistant model to aggregate soft thoughts, SoftCoT++ introduces *multiple specialized initial tokens* (e.g., `[INI]_i`).
 - Each `[INI]_i` is a distinct token in the vocabulary. When presented to the assistant model, each distinct `[INI]_i` prompts the assistant to generate a *different* soft thought representation (`T_i_soft`) for the same input query.
 - This effectively simulates parallel sampling in the continuous space, generating `N` distinct "thinking paths" from the assistant model.

- **Contrastive Learning Loss:**
 - To further promote diversity and enforce a larger variance among these `N` distinct soft thought representations (`T_i_soft`), SoftCoT++ incorporates a contrastive learning objective (`L_cl`).
 - The `L_cl` loss pushes different soft thought representations (`T_k_soft` and `T_j_soft` where `k ≠ j`) away from each other in the latent space while encouraging `T_k_soft` to be similar to itself. This maximizes the distance between the generated diverse soft thoughts, increasing their variance and enabling a broader exploration of the latent solution space.

#### 3.5. Overall Pipeline

During the training phase, the projection module (fθ) is optimized. The process involves:
1. For a given input, generate multiple distinct soft thoughts (`T_i_soft`) by feeding the assistant model with different specialized initial tokens (`[INI]_i`).
2. Pass these `T_i_soft` representations to the main LLM to generate reasoning chains and answers.
3. Apply a contrastive learning loss (`L_cl`) on the `T_i_soft` representations to maximize their diversity.
4. The final answer can be obtained by aggregating the outputs from the multiple reasoning paths, similar to Self-Consistency, but now with diverse initial latent thoughts.

This methodology strategically addresses the deterministic nature of continuous representations by leveraging multiple specialized inputs to create initial diversity, and then explicitly amplifies that diversity through a contrastive learning objective, all while keeping the heavy LLMs fixed for efficiency.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

SoftCoT++ undergoes comprehensive evaluation across five reasoning benchmarks and two distinct LLM architectures, demonstrating consistent and significant performance improvements.

#### 4.1. Superior Performance Across Baselines, Architectures, and Tasks

- **Outperformance:** SoftCoT++ consistently outperforms all established baselines, including Zero-Shot CoT (SC), Zero-Shot Assist-CoT (SC), Coconut-SC, and its direct predecessor, SoftCoT-SC. This holds true for both LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct and Qwen3-8B model families.
- **Task Versatility:** The improvements are observed across diverse reasoning tasks: mathematical reasoning (GSM8K, ASDiv-Aug, AQuA), commonsense reasoning (StrategyQA), and symbolic reasoning (Date Understanding). This indicates the broad applicability and robustness of SoftCoT++.
- **Effectiveness of Continuous-Space TTS:** The results definitively validate the effectiveness of applying test-time scaling in the continuous latent space, a capability previously lacking in continuous-space reasoning methods.
- **Stability:** The reported reduced standard deviations for SoftCoT++ suggest that scaling soft thoughts does not destabilize predictions, which is a crucial attribute for reliable test-time scaling.
- **Backbone-Agnostic Design:** SoftCoT++'s consistent performance across different LLM architectures (LLaMA and Qwen, which differ in pretraining, tokenization, etc.) highlights its generalizability without requiring architecture-specific modifications.
- **Unlocking Latent Potential:** For mathematical reasoning tasks, where inference diversity is a key bottleneck, SoftCoT++ effectively explores the model's internal reasoning capabilities by generating qualitatively distinct inference paths, leading to higher-quality inferences. However, on StrategyQA, diminishing returns were observed with increasing chain numbers, suggesting the model's capacity for that specific task might be saturated.

#### 4.2. Ablation Study: The Critical Role of Contrastive Learning

The ablation study, comparing SoftCoT++ (with contrastive loss) to SoftCoT+ (without contrastive loss), provides crucial insights:
- **Benefits from Diverse Input Tokens (SoftCoT+):** Even without the contrastive learning objective, SoftCoT+ shows improved performance when scaled using multiple soft thought representations (generated by diverse initial tokens). This confirms that simply introducing different starting points for latent thought generation is beneficial for scaling.
- **Contrastive Learning is Indispensable:** Despite SoftCoT+ benefiting from scaling, it only marginally outperforms SoftCoT-SC and performs significantly worse than SoftCoT++. This strongly underscores that the contrastive learning objective is critical. It actively pushes the soft thoughts apart in the latent space, maximizing their diversity and variance, which is essential for fully realizing the potential of test-time scaling in the continuous domain, as theorized by Lemma 2.

#### 4.3. Synergistic Effect of Scaling in Both Thinking and Reasoning Stages

The paper explores the orthogonality of continuous-space thinking-stage scaling (SoftCoT++) and discrete-space reasoning-stage scaling (Self-Consistency).
- **Additive Performance Gains:** When SoftCoT++ (10 thinking chains) is combined with Self-Consistency (10 reasoning chains per thinking chain, totaling 100 chains), the performance is further improved beyond what either method achieves alone. This demonstrates that scaling in the thinking stage is complementary to scaling in the reasoning stage, and they can be used simultaneously to amplify the overall scaling effect.
- **Thinking-Stage Benefits Beyond Reasoning-Stage Scaling:** Interestingly, SoftCoT+ (without contrastive training) combined with self-consistency showed even greater performance gains than SoftCoT-SC. This suggests that the initial diversity injected at the thinking stage, even without explicit contrastive regularization, introduces external benefits not achievable by scaling only the discrete reasoning chains. This supports the idea that diversifying latent thoughts offers a richer foundation for subsequent reasoning.

In summary, the results convincingly demonstrate that SoftCoT++ represents a significant leap forward in scaling LLM reasoning, particularly by successfully extending test-time scaling to the continuous latent space, a previously challenging frontier. The strategic combination of diverse initializations and contrastive learning is key to its effectiveness, and its compatibility with existing methods points to practical implications for future LLM inference optimization.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

SoftCoT++ marks a significant advancement in the field of Large Language Models, particularly in optimizing their reasoning capabilities during inference. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Pioneering Test-Time Scaling in Continuous Latent Space:** SoftCoT++ is presented as the first framework to enable scalable test-time reasoning in the continuous latent space. This is a crucial breakthrough, as it addresses a fundamental limitation of previous continuous-space reasoning methods: their inability to naturally generate diverse reasoning paths for performance enhancement at inference time. This opens up entirely new avenues for research into how LLMs "think" and how those internal processes can be manipulated for better outcomes.

2. **Enhanced LLM Reasoning Performance without Retraining:** By effectively applying test-time scaling to the thinking process, SoftCoT++ offers a powerful method to improve the accuracy and robustness of pre-trained LLMs without altering their parameters or undergoing expensive fine-tuning. This is highly practical for deployed models, where modifying parameters might be infeasible or too costly. The gains observed across various reasoning tasks underscore its utility.

3. **Bridge Between Discrete and Continuous Reasoning Paradigms:** The work successfully integrates the benefits of continuous latent representations (information preservation, expressiveness) with the performance-boosting capabilities of discrete-space test-time scaling. By demonstrating that scaling in the continuous thinking stage is orthogonal and complementary to scaling in the discrete reasoning stage, SoftCoT++ offers a more holistic and powerful approach to LLM inference optimization. This synergistic effect suggests future research might explore increasingly hybrid approaches that leverage the strengths of both paradigms.

4. **Increased Efficiency and Robustness in Inference:** SoftCoT++ maintains the efficiency benefits of continuous latent thinking (as established by SoftCoT), while adding a robust scaling mechanism. The reduced standard deviations in results suggest more reliable predictions, which is critical for real-world applications where consistent performance is paramount.

5. **Generalizability Across LLM Architectures:** The backbone-agnostic nature of SoftCoT++, demonstrated by its consistent performance across LLaMA and Qwen model families, implies broad applicability. This means the proposed techniques are likely transferable to a wide range of current and future LLMs, regardless of their specific architectures or pretraining details.

6. **Insights into LLM Internal Processes:** By successfully perturbing and diversifying latent thoughts, the research provides implicit insights into the nature of LLM reasoning. It suggests that LLMs do possess a "latent potential" that can be unlocked through diverse exploration of internal states, rather than just brute-force scaling of discrete outputs. This might inform future model design and training strategies.

7. **Future Research Directions:** The paper openly acknowledges its limitations, particularly regarding the preliminary exploration of the latent thought distribution and its focus on fixed 8B-scale models. This openness itself points to significant future research avenues:
 - Investigating the evolution and characteristics of the soft thought distribution during training.
 - Extending SoftCoT++ to much larger and potentially trainable LLMs.
 - Exploring more sophisticated methods for sampling and diversifying continuous latent representations.
 - Applying continuous-space scaling to other LLM tasks beyond reasoning.

In essence, SoftCoT++ contributes a novel and effective method for improving LLM reasoning by pushing the boundaries of test-time scaling into the continuous latent space. Its immediate impact lies in offering a practical way to enhance existing LLMs, while its broader significance paves the way for a deeper understanding and more sophisticated control over the internal "thinking" processes of large language models.
