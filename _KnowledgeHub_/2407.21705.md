# Tora: Trajectory-oriented Diffusion Transformer for Video Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> A new framework named Tora introduces the first trajectory-oriented Diffusion Transformer for video generation, enabling precise control over object motion while maintaining high video quality and scalability. The approach achieves 3-5 times higher trajectory accuracy and 30-40% better visual quality (FVD) for 128-frame videos compared to previous UNet-based motion control methods.

## Problem

- UNet-based video diffusion models were limited to generating short videos at fixed resolutions and struggled with maintaining motion consistency and physical dynamics in longer sequences.
- Existing motion control techniques for video generation did not scale effectively to longer, higher-resolution videos, often resulting in motion blur, object deformation, and deviation from specified trajectories.
- Diffusion Transformers (DiT) showed high capabilities for generating long, high-quality videos but lacked explicit mechanisms for controllable motion, particularly through user-defined trajectories.

## Method

- Introduces **Tora**, a Spatial-Temporal ==Diffusion Transformer== (ST-==DiT==) framework built upon OpenSora, incorporating two novel components: the Trajectory Extractor (TE) and the Motion-guidance Fuser (MGF).
- The Trajectory Extractor (TE) converts user-provided (x,y) trajectories into motion patches compatible with DiT's latent space using a customized 3D Variational Autoencoder (VAE) for compression.
- The Motion-guidance Fuser (MGF) integrates multi-level motion conditions from the TE into each ST-DiT block via an adaptive normalization layer, which was found to be the most effective and computationally efficient approach.

## Results

- Tora demonstrated 3-5 times higher trajectory accuracy (e.g., Trajectory Error of **11.72** vs. **38.39** for MotionCtrl on 128-frames) and 30-40% better visual quality (e.g., FVD 494 vs. 731 for MotionCtrl) compared to UNet-based motion control methods.
- The framework drastically reduced Trajectory Error (**11.72** for 128-frames) compared to OpenSora (**373.17**) while achieving competitive or superior visual performance (FVD 494 vs. 533 for OpenSora) and effectively suppressing temporal artifacts.
- Qualitative results and user studies indicated Tora's superior ability to generate smoother, physically accurate movements adhering to specified trajectories, and improved object fidelity in complex multi-object scenes over existing methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The use of a customized 3D VAE for trajectory compression is crucial for effectively extracting relevant motion features and preserving movement details, outperforming simpler compression methods.
> - An adaptive normalization layer within Temporal DiT blocks, initialized as an identity function, serves as the most effective and efficient mechanism for integrating motion conditions.
> - A two-stage hybrid training strategy, starting with dense optical flow and then fine-tuning with sparse trajectories, significantly improves the model's adaptability and overall motion control performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of generative artificial intelligence has advanced significantly, particularly with diffusion models demonstrating proficiency in synthesizing high-quality images and videos. Early video diffusion models, primarily based on UNet architectures, exhibited limitations in generating videos of extended durations (typically around two seconds) and were often restricted to fixed resolutions and aspect ratios. These models also faced challenges in maintaining motion consistency across longer sequences and accurately emulating physical world dynamics, often leading to artifacts like distortion or unnatural movements.

A notable development in this landscape has been the introduction of Diffusion Transformers (DiT), exemplified by models such as Sora and OpenSora. These DiT-based models have surpassed previous state-of-the-art methods by producing longer videos (tens of seconds to a minute) with higher quality, diverse resolutions, various aspect ratios, and improved adherence to physical laws. This architectural shift from UNet to Transformer has enabled greater scalability and versatility in video generation.

Concurrently, research has focused on enhancing motion control in video generation. Previous methods, including VideoComposer and DragNUWA, introduced generalized motion manipulation through motion vectors or trajectories. MotionCtrl further refined this by allowing independent control over camera and object motions. However, these UNet-based motion control techniques were typically limited to generating short video clips (e.g., 16 frames) at lower resolutions. Applying them to longer sequences often resulted in motion blur, object deformation, and deviations from specified trajectories, particularly during significant positional shifts. This indicated a need for models capable of generating longer videos with robust and consistent motion control, integrated within more scalable architectures.

Tora positions itself at the intersection of these two trends by introducing a trajectory-oriented Diffusion Transformer framework. It seeks to combine the scalability and high-quality generation capabilities of DiT-based models with precise, controllable motion, addressing the limitations of prior UNet-based motion control methods when applied to extended video sequences. The work aims to bridge the gap between advanced video synthesis and user-friendly, physically accurate motion manipulation in a scalable manner.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a scalable video generation model that enables effective and precise motion control, specifically through the integration of arbitrary trajectories. The authors aim to establish Tora as the first trajectory-oriented Diffusion Transformer (DiT) framework that can concurrently integrate textual, visual, and trajectory conditions for video generation.

The motivation for this work arises from several observed limitations in the existing video generation landscape:

1. **Limitations of UNet-based Video Generation:** While UNet-based video diffusion models have shown promise, they are constrained in their capacity to generate long videos (e.g., typically limited to 16 frames at lower resolutions) and struggle with varying resolutions and aspect ratios. This inherent architectural limitation hinders the smooth portrayal of motion, especially when significant positional shifts are required by a trajectory, leading to artifacts such as distortion or unnatural "parallel drifting" that diverge from real-world physics.
2. **Scalability Deficiencies in Existing Motion Control:** Previous motion control techniques, while demonstrating a degree of control, have not effectively scaled to longer, higher-resolution videos. The authors identify an urgent need for a model capable of producing extended videos with both robust motion control and detailed physical representations, which existing methods fail to provide consistently.
3. **Untapped Potential of DiT for Controllable Motion:** Diffusion Transformers (DiT) have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in producing high-quality, long-duration videos with diverse parameters (e.g., Sora, OpenSora). However, the potential of these transformer-based diffusion models for effectively generating videos with controllable motion, particularly via explicit trajectory guidance, remained largely unexplored. The authors seek to leverage DiT's inherent scalability and generative knowledge to overcome the shortcomings of previous motion-controlled video generation approaches.
4. **User-Friendly Control and Physical Plausibility:** The motivation also includes providing a more accessible and intuitive method for users to guide video content dynamics. By using trajectories as a direct input, the model aims to ensure that generated movements not only adhere precisely to the specified path but also effectively emulate realistic physical dynamics, enhancing the visual coherence and believability of the output.

In summary, the research is motivated by the desire to combine the scalability and quality of DiT-based video generation with a robust, user-friendly, and physically accurate motion control mechanism, thereby addressing critical limitations of prior models in generating long, controllable, and coherent video content.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Tora is built upon the Spatial-Temporal Diffusion Transformer (ST-DiT) architecture, specifically utilizing OpenSora as its foundational model. To enable motion control that aligns with DiT's scalability, the framework introduces two novel components: the Trajectory Extractor (TE) and the Motion-guidance Fuser (MGF).

#### 3.1. Preliminary: Spatial-Temporal DiT

The foundational ST-DiT model, adopted from OpenSora, processes video data. A video autoencoder first reduces the spatial and temporal dimensions of an input video (X ∈ R^(L×H×W×3)) into a video latent representation (z₀ ∈ R^(l×h×w×4)). This latent is then "patchified" into a sequence of input tokens (I ∈ R^(l×s×d)). The ST-DiT architecture alternates between Spatial DiT Blocks (S-DiT-B) and Temporal DiT Blocks (T-DiT-B). S-DiT-B blocks include Spatial Self-Attention (SSA) and Cross-Attention, followed by a feed-forward layer. T-DiT-B blocks are similar but replace SSA with Temporal Self-Attention (TSA). Textual prompts are embedded using a T5 encoder and integrated via cross-attention. This design allows the denoising ST-DiT to handle variable-duration videos due to its ability to process variable-length sequences.

#### 3.2. Trajectory Extractor (TE)

The TE is designed to convert arbitrary user-provided trajectories (sequences of (x,y) coordinates per frame) into motion patches compatible with the DiT's latent space.
1. **Trajectory Map Conversion:** The input trajectory (traj) is first converted into a trajectory map (g ∈ R^(L×H×W×2)), where each pixel (x,y) at frame `i` contains the displacement to the next frame (u(xᵢ, yᵢ) = xᵢ₊₁ - xᵢ, v(xᵢ, yᵢ) = yᵢ₊₁ - yᵢ). A Gaussian Filter is applied to this map to mitigate scatter. The first frame uses a fully-zero map.
2. **Flow Visualization:** The trajectory map `g` is then translated into the RGB color space (`g_vis ∈ R^(L×H×W×3)`) using a flow visualization technique.
3. **3D Motion VAE Compression:** A 3D Variational Autoencoder (VAE) compresses `g_vis`. This VAE is based on the MAGVIT-v2 architecture, simplified by omitting codebook dependencies, and its spatial compression component is initialized using the VAE of SDXL to accelerate convergence. This VAE achieves an 8x spatial and 4x temporal reduction, aligning the compressed motion latent representation (`g_m ∈ R^(l×h×w×4)`) with the OpenSora framework. The VAE is trained using a reconstruction loss.
4. **Spacetime Motion Patch Generation:** The motion latent `g_m` is then patchified using the same patch size as the video latents and encoded through a series of convolutional layers. Skip connections are employed between these layers to extract multi-level motion features (`f_i ∈ R^(l×s×d')`), which serve as motion conditions for corresponding ST-DiT blocks.

#### 3.3. Motion-guidance Fuser (MGF)

The MGF integrates the multi-level motion conditions (`f_i`) from the TE into each ST-DiT block. Three fusion architectures were explored:
1. **Extra Channel Connections:** Motion patches (`f_i`) are concatenated with the hidden state (`hᵢ₋₁`) along the channel dimension, followed by an MLP to maintain consistent latent size.
2. **Cross-Attention Layer:** An additional Cross-Attention layer is introduced after the SSA or TSA in the DiT block, where motion patches serve as the key and value to integrate with the hidden state.
3. **Adaptive Norm Layer:** This method converts `f_i` into scale (γᵢ) and shift (βᵢ) parameters using two zero-initialized convolutional layers. These parameters are then integrated into the hidden state (`hᵢ₋₁`) through a linear projection: `hᵢ = γᵢ · hᵢ₋₁ + βᵢ + hᵢ₋₁`.

Through experimentation, the adaptive normalization layer was identified as the most effective and computationally efficient method for integrating motion conditions, and it is the default MGF design in Tora. Initializing the normalization layer as an identity function and placing the MGF module within the Temporal DiT block were found to be crucial for optimal performance.

#### 3.4. Data Processing and Training Strategy

1. **Data Processing:** A structured approach is used to obtain high-quality video-text pairs with consistent object motion. Raw videos are segmented, and invalid videos (encoding errors, zero duration, low aesthetic/flow scores) are removed. Videos with a minimum resolution of 720p are retained. Camera motion filtering is applied using a camera motion detector and motion segmentor to exclude videos dominated by camera movement, ensuring focus on object trajectories. Videos with dramatic object motions are probabilistically retained. Captions for eligible videos are generated using the PLLaVA model, and GPT-4o is used for prompt refinement during inference.
2. **Motion Condition Training (Two-stage):**
 - **Stage 1 (Dense Optical Flow):** Tora is initially trained by extracting dense optical flow from training videos as the trajectory. This provides rich motion information for foundational learning.
 - **Stage 2 (Sparse Trajectories):** The model is fine-tuned to work with more user-friendly sparse trajectories. This involves randomly selecting 1 to N object trajectories based on motion segmentation and flow scores from videos, and applying a Gaussian filter for refinement.
3. **Image Condition Training:** A mask strategy is employed where frames are randomly unmasked during training, ensuring their corresponding video patches are not subjected to noise.
4. **Adapter-like Strategy:** Only the temporal blocks of the ST-DiT, along with the TE and MGF, are trained. This strategy aims to integrate DiT's pre-existing generative knowledge with external motion signals efficiently.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of Tora focused on quantitative and qualitative comparisons with existing motion-controllable video generation methods, as well as ablation studies on its key components.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Comparisons

- **Performance against UNet-based Methods:** Tora consistently demonstrated superior performance compared to UNet-based methods such as VideoComposer, DragNUWA, AnimateAnything, TrailBlazer, and MotionCtrl. Across 16-frame, 64-frame, and 128-frame configurations, Tora achieved substantially lower Trajectory Error (indicating higher motion fidelity) and better visual quality as measured by Fréchet Video Distance (FVD) and CLIP Similarity (CLIPSIM). For instance, in 128-frame tests, Tora achieved a Trajectory Error of 11.72 compared to 38.39 for MotionCtrl and 41.25 for DragNUWA, representing a 3-5 times higher trajectory accuracy. Its FVD for 128 frames was 494, approximately 30-40% better than the best UNet-based method (MotionCtrl: 731). This performance gap widened with increasing video duration, indicating Tora's robustness for longer sequences.
- **Comparison with OpenSora (DiT Baseline):** While OpenSora achieved high visual quality, its lack of explicit motion control resulted in significantly higher Trajectory Error (e.g., 373.17 for 128-frames) due to random object trajectories. Tora not only drastically reduced this error (11.72 for 128-frames) but also demonstrated competitive or superior visual performance (FVD 494 vs. 533 for OpenSora at 128 frames). Tora was observed to suppress temporal artifacts and motion blurring more effectively than OpenSora, leading to enhanced temporal coherence.
- **Comparison with OpenSora-based DragNUWA Adaptation:** An adaptation of DragNUWA's motion trajectory design to the OpenSora foundation model showed improved motion controllability over UNet-based methods (TrajError 21.75 for 128-frames) but degraded visual quality compared to the OpenSora baseline (FVD 565 vs. 533). This suggested intrinsic incompatibility between DragNUWA's motion representations and DiT's latent space, a limitation that Tora's purpose-built modules address.
- **Trajectory Error across Resolutions and Durations:** Analysis showed that Tora maintained effective trajectory control over varying resolutions and durations, with a gradual increase in error as duration extended. This behavior aligned with the general decrease in video quality observed in DiT models at longer durations, but Tora's control remained robust.
- **User Study:** A user study comparing Tora against OpenSora-v1.2, CogVideoX-2B, Vidu, and Kling v1.0 indicated Tora's superiority in physics simulation (71% win rate vs. OpenSora), sensory quality (61% win rate vs. OpenSora), and instruction adherence (64% win rate vs. OpenSora) over OpenSora and CogVideoX. Tora achieved competitive results against Vidu and showed a strong baseline against Kling.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Comparisons

Qualitative results illustrated Tora's ability to generate videos with smoother, more physically accurate movements that closely adhered to specified trajectories. Examples showed Tora realistically capturing intricate motions (e.g., pedaling, object oscillation, circular swirling) while other methods produced unnatural poses, object deformations, or failed to depict multiple objects accurately. Tora demonstrated improved preservation of object fidelity and enhanced video fluidity in scenarios involving complex object movements (e.g., multiple jellyfish moving in different directions) compared to OpenSora, which often exhibited flickering and object deformations in such cases.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Trajectory Compression Methods:** The 3D VAE approach for trajectory compression yielded the best performance (TrajError 14.25, FVD 513) compared to simpler methods like sampling keyframes (TrajError 27.61, FVD 581) or average pooling (TrajError 20.97, FVD 558). This highlights the effectiveness of the customized 3D VAE in extracting relevant motion features and preserving movement details for guidance.
- **MGF Block Design:** The Adaptive Norm layer in the MGF achieved the lowest FVD (513) and Trajectory Error (14.25) while also being computationally efficient, outperforming Extra Channel (FVD 542, TrajError 21.07) and Cross-Attention (FVD 526, TrajError 18.36) designs. The study also found that initializing the normalization layer as the identity function and placing the MGF module within the Temporal DiT block were crucial for optimal motion control.
- **Training Strategies:** The two-stage hybrid training strategy (dense optical flow followed by sparse trajectories) demonstrated superior adaptability and overall performance (TrajError 14.25) compared to training solely with dense optical flow (TrajError 39.34) or only sparse trajectories (TrajError 24.73).
- **Scaling Motion-Control Ability:** Experiments scaling Tora to larger CogVideoX architectures (2.5B and 6.3B parameters) and increasing training data volume showed a consistent improvement in motion control ability (decreasing TrajError). This confirmed the seamless compatibility of Tora's modules with DiT's scalability framework.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Tora represents a notable contribution to the field of generative artificial intelligence, particularly in video synthesis with controlled motion. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Advancement in Controllable Video Generation:** Tora is presented as the first trajectory-oriented Diffusion Transformer framework, addressing a key gap in the ability to generate scalable, high-quality videos with precise motion control. By effectively integrating textual, visual, and trajectory conditions, it expands the capabilities of existing DiT models to encompass user-defined movement.
2. **Enhanced Scalability and Fidelity:** The work demonstrates that motion control can be successfully integrated with the scalability properties of Diffusion Transformers. This allows for the generation of longer videos (up to 204 frames) at higher resolutions (720p) that adhere accurately to specified trajectories. This overcomes a major limitation of previous UNet-based methods, which struggled with consistency and quality in extended sequences.
3. **Improved Physical Plausibility:** Tora shows superiority in simulating movements that align with physical world dynamics. This is crucial for creating realistic and believable video content, as it mitigates common artifacts like unnatural object deformation or parallel drifting observed in other models.
4. **Novel Architectural Components:** The introduction of the Trajectory Extractor (TE) and Motion-guidance Fuser (MGF) provides effective mechanisms for converting abstract trajectory data into a format compatible with DiT architectures and seamlessly integrating these motion signals. The ablation studies on these components offer empirical insights into their optimal design (e.g., 3D VAE for compression, Adaptive Norm for fusion), serving as a baseline for future research.
5. **Robust Training Strategy:** The two-stage training approach, initially leveraging dense optical flow and then fine-tuning with sparse, user-friendly trajectories, contributes to the model's adaptability and versatility in handling diverse motion patterns. The data processing pipeline, including meticulous filtering for quality and camera motion, ensures a high-quality training dataset.
6. **Broadened Applications:** The capacity to generate high-quality, motion-manipulable videos has potential implications across various domains. These include:
 - **Content Creation:** Enabling artists, animators, and filmmakers to generate complex scenes with precise character or object movements, reducing manual labor.
 - **Gaming and Virtual Reality:** Creating more dynamic and interactive virtual environments.
 - **Simulation and Robotics:** Generating realistic simulations for training autonomous systems or testing robotic movements.
 - **Advertising and Marketing:** Producing engaging video advertisements with specific product or character movements.

By providing a robust framework for trajectory-oriented video generation within a scalable DiT architecture, Tora lays a foundation for future advancements in controllable and physically consistent video synthesis, potentially democratizing access to sophisticated video production tools.
