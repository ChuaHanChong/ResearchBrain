# Generative Models From and For Sampling-Based MPC: A Bootstrapped Approach For Adaptive Contact-Rich Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> The Generative Predictive Control (GPC) framework enhances Sampling-based Model Predictive Control (SPC) for adaptive contact-rich manipulation by leveraging offline-trained conditional flow-matching models. This approach successfully learns robust control priors directly from noisy simulated SPC data, yielding improved performance and real-world validation on a quadruped robot.

## Problem

- Reactive, contact-rich manipulation in high-dimensional state and action spaces poses significant computational challenges for real-time control using methods like Sampling-based Model Predictive Control (SPC).
- Traditional SPC methods suffer from high computational costs and sample inefficiency due to the need for numerous forward simulations and extensive exploration of complex search spaces.
- Purely offline-trained policies often struggle with out-of-distribution states and lack the real-time adaptability required for dynamic and unstructured environments.

## Method

- Conditional flow-matching models are trained offline to learn high-quality control sequence proposal distributions directly from noisy SPC data generated in simulation.
- The learned generative model is integrated into an online Sampling-based ==MPC== (GPC-CEM) to provide informed samples, augmenting the exploration capabilities of the online planner.
- A hierarchical control structure is employed for contact-rich loco-manipulation, where GPC plans for high-level torso and arm movements, while a low-level policy handles robot locomotion and balance.

## Results

- GPC-CEM achieved up to **99.8%** success rates in simulated contact-rich manipulation tasks (e.g., Push-T), significantly outperforming baselines like standard CEM (**85%**) and MPPI (**62%**).
- The framework demonstrated strong generalization to unseen task variations (**96%** success on a Push-K task without retraining) and robustness to reduced planning horizons, maintaining **96%** success with a 1-second horizon compared to CEM's **78%**.
- On a physical Spot quadruped robot, GPC-CEM achieved **60%** success in a chair-pushing task, notably outperforming baseline CEM (**10%** success) by navigating complex obstacles and managing real-world contact dynamics.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Meaningful generative models for control can be effectively learned directly from noisy control sequences produced by an online SPC algorithm, simplifying the data collection and training pipeline.
> - Bootstrapping online SPC with offline-learned generative priors combines the efficiency and informed sampling of learned models with the real-time adaptability and robustness of online optimization.
> - The proposed GPC-CEM strategy, which mixes samples from the learned generative model and the current MPC distribution, enhances search efficiency and exploitation in complex, multimodal action spaces.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses a fundamental challenge in robotics: enabling reactive, contact-rich manipulation and locomotion in high-dimensional state and action spaces for real-time control. This domain is critical for deploying robots in unstructured and dynamic environments, where robots must interact physically with objects and adapt to unexpected changes.

The existing landscape of solutions can be broadly categorized:

- **Sampling-based Model Predictive Control (SPC)**: SPC is a principled framework for real-time trajectory optimization that handles non-convex problems and nonlinear dynamics, making it suitable for contact-rich tasks. Methods like Cross-Entropy Method (CEM) and Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) fall into this category. However, SPC suffers from high computational cost due to numerous forward simulations, especially in high-dimensional and contact-rich scenarios, limiting its applicability to complex behaviors or high-frequency control. This work directly aims to mitigate this core limitation.

- **Offline Learning and Generative Models**: A parallel line of research focuses on "amortizing" computational costs by shifting policy learning to an offline phase. This involves collecting high-quality data (e.g., expert demonstrations or planner rollouts) and training generative models (like diffusion or flow-matching models) to capture distributions of useful actions or control sequences. These models can then guide or warm-start online planning, drastically improving efficiency. Recent works have shown the power of diffusion and flow-matching models for learning expressive end-to-end policies. Offline Model-Based Reinforcement Learning (MBRL) also leverages precomputed data to approximate optimal solutions.

- **Bootstrapping Online Planners with Offline-Trained Models**: This paper specifically fits into a growing trend where offline-trained generative models are used to *bootstrap* online planners. The motivation here is to combine the strengths of both: the efficiency and learning capabilities of generative models with the adaptability and robustness of online optimization. While offline policies can struggle with out-of-distribution (OOD) states or tasks, online planners can adapt. Recent works like,– explore this hybrid approach.

**Key distinctions and contributions within this landscape:**

- **Learning from Noisy SPC Data Directly**: A significant aspect of this paper is its demonstration that meaningful proposal distributions can be learned *directly from noisy SPC data* without requiring expert refinement or expensive numerical solvers (like those generating near-globally optimal trajectories used in). This challenges the assumption in some prior work that SPC data is too noisy for direct generative model training, necessitating iterative refinement. This paper shows a simpler, single-shot training approach works effectively.
- **Real-World Contact-Rich Loco-Manipulation**: The paper presents the *first* application of this bootstrapping approach to real-world contact-rich loco-manipulation with a quadruped robot. This is a crucial step towards validating the practicality and robustness of such methods in complex, high-stakes environments, moving beyond purely simulated demonstrations.
- **Addressing Myopia and Sample Efficiency**: While other approaches focus on infinite-horizon value approximation to address the myopia of finite-horizon MPC, this work directly improves the search mechanism within MPC by providing better-informed sampling distributions, which is complementary.

In essence, this research pushes the boundaries of real-time control by presenting a practical and robust method that leverages the strengths of generative models to enhance the performance and efficiency of sampling-based MPC, particularly in the challenging domain of dynamic, contact-rich robot interactions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and efficient control framework for complex robotic tasks, specifically focusing on **reactive contact-rich (loco-)manipulation in high-dimensional state and action spaces**. The authors identify the significant computational challenges associated with such tasks when relying solely on traditional online optimization methods like Sampling-based Model Predictive Control (SPC).

The core motivations are:

1. **Amortize Computational Burden**: SPC, while principled and adaptive, is computationally expensive due to the need for numerous forward simulations and extensive exploration of high-dimensional search spaces. This limits its real-time applicability to simpler behaviors or lower control frequencies. The authors aim to shift much of this computational cost to an offline phase.
2. **Improve Sample Efficiency**: The online planning process often requires a large number of samples to find good control sequences, especially in complex, non-convex environments. By providing a more informed starting point or guiding the sampling process, the authors seek to significantly reduce the number of samples needed during online execution.
3. **Enhance Robustness and Generalization**: Purely offline-trained policies often struggle with out-of-distribution (OOD) states or task variations. The goal is to develop a method that retains the adaptability of online optimization while benefiting from the learned priors, thereby improving robustness and generalization to unseen situations or task modifications.
4. **Reduce Planning Horizon Requirements**: Long planning horizons are often necessary for SPC to achieve non-myopic, goal-directed behavior. However, longer horizons exacerbate computational costs. The authors aim to enable non-myopic planning even with tighter computational budgets and shorter online horizons by embedding long-horizon knowledge into the learned models.
5. **Leverage Noisy SPC Data Directly**: A specific technical motivation is to demonstrate that meaningful generative models for control can be learned directly from "noisy" control sequences generated by an SPC algorithm in simulation, without requiring iterative refinement or cleaner data from gradient-based solvers. This simplifies the data collection and model training pipeline.
6. **Real-World Validation**: Ultimately, the work is motivated by the need to demonstrate the practical viability of such advanced control frameworks on physical hardware, specifically for contact-rich loco-manipulation tasks which are notoriously difficult to control in the real world.

In summary, the authors seek to overcome the limitations of online SPC by bootstrapping it with generative models, enabling more efficient, robust, and generalizable real-time control for complex robotic interactions, and validating this approach on a real quadruped robot.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, **Generative Predictive Control (GPC)**, bootstraps Sampling-based Model Predictive Control (SPC) with conditional flow-matching models. The core idea is to train a generative model offline on successful control sequences generated by SPC in simulation, and then use this learned model as a proposal distribution to guide and accelerate online SPC.

**A. Data Collection:**
The foundational step is an offline data collection phase, crucial for training the generative model.
- **Source of Data**: The training data consists of open-loop control sequences collected by running a Cross-Entropy Method (CEM) in simulation. CEM is chosen because it's widely used and can be tuned for different tasks without the real-time constraints of online control, allowing for longer planning horizons and larger sample sizes to generate high-quality, non-myopic sequences.
- **Data Content**: For each time step $\tau$ in successful episodes, the algorithm records the current state ($x_{\tau}$), a fixed-window history of states ($h_{\tau}$), and the mean control sequence ($U^*_{\tau}$) from the CEM distribution. This ensures the generative model learns from behavior that led to task completion.
- **Control Sequence Representation**: To reduce complexity and improve runtime efficiency, each full control sequence ($U_{\tau}$) is represented by $K$ spline interpolation points over the planning horizon $H$.
- **CEM Enhancements during Data Collection**: Progress-based heuristics are used to reset variances, preventing premature mode collapse, and action-level annealing increases exploration for control points further into the horizon.

**B. Learning Control Sequence Proposal Distributions:**
Once the dataset is collected, a generative model is trained.
- **Generative Model Choice**: Conditional flow-matching models are chosen for their ability to capture complex, multi-modal distributions and their superior inference speed compared to diffusion models.
- **Learning Objective**: The flow-matching model learns a time-varying state-conditional vector field $v_{\theta}(U, x_{\tau}, h_{\tau}, t)$ that transports samples from a simple prior distribution (e.g., standard Gaussian noise) to the target distribution $p_{\theta}(U | x_{\tau}, h_{\tau})$. This target distribution represents control sequences likely to lead to successful task completion given the current state and its history.
- **Usage Modes**: The learned generative model $p_{\theta}(U | x_{\tau}, h_{\tau})$ can be used in two ways:
 - **GPC-Shoot**: Directly sample control sequences from $p_{\theta}$ and select the best based on value functions (a random shooting approach).
 - **GPC-CEM**: Augment the sampling distribution of an online SPC algorithm (e.g., CEM) with samples from $p_{\theta}$. This is the primary method proposed.

**C. GPC-CEM: Bootstrapping SPC with Flow-Matching:**
This is the core contribution for online control, combining the strengths of learned priors with online adaptability.
- **Augmented Sampling**: At each control step, GPC-CEM samples $N_{CEM}$ trajectories from the current Gaussian CEM distribution $\pi_{\phi}(U)$ and $N_{Flow}$ trajectories from the learned generative model $p_{\theta}(U | x_{\tau}, h_{\tau})$. The total number of rollouts is $N = N_{CEM} + N_{Flow}$.
- **Cost Evaluation and Elite Selection**: All $N$ sampled trajectories are simulated forward from the current state estimate and evaluated using the task-specific cost function. The $N_{elite}$ proposals with the lowest costs are selected.
- **Distribution Update**: The CEM distribution's parameters ($\phi = (\bar{U}, \Sigma)$) are updated based on these elite trajectories. Crucially, the mean $\bar{U}$ is updated by time-shifting the *single best-performing* candidate control sequence, not the mean of all elites. The variance $\Sigma$ is updated using the variance of the elite set. This approach allows GPC-CEM to better exploit multi-modal proposals from the generative model without collapsing them into a single mode.
- **Action Execution**: The first action of the best-performing candidate sequence is executed.
- **State History Update**: The state history $h_{\tau}$ is updated with the current state $x_{\tau}$.

**D. Application to Loco-Manipulation:**
The framework is applied to a challenging **non-prehensile object pushing task with a Spot quadruped robot**.
- **Hierarchical Control**: To manage the robot's 19 positional Degrees of Freedom (DoF), a hierarchical control structure is employed. The high-level action space, which is sampled by GPC, consists of 9 DoF (3 for torso, 6 for arm). A pre-trained, fixed low-level locomotion policy handles balance and stability while tracking these high-level inputs. This simplification reduces the action space dimensionality for planning and adds robustness.
- **Cost Functions**: Task-specific cost functions are designed, including robot-object proximity, velocity penalties, goal-reaching terms, joint limits, fall penalties, and object tipping penalties.

This comprehensive approach combines the data efficiency and robustness of generative models with the adaptability of online MPC, addressing the limitations of each in isolation for complex robotic control problems.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted across simulated and real-world tasks, focusing on contact-rich, non-prehensile manipulation. The key findings are summarized below:

**A. Push-T Task (2-DoF Circular Robot Pushing a T-shaped Block)**

- **Superior Performance**:
 - **GPC-CEM (10 denoising steps)** achieved a 99.8% success rate, significantly outperforming baselines like standard CEM (85%), MPPI (62%), and Dial-MPC (86%). It also reduced completion time by nearly 50% compared to GPC-CEM with 2 denoising steps.
 - **GPC-Shoot (10 denoising steps)** also achieved high success (99.2%), demonstrating the effectiveness of the learned generative model even in a direct sampling approach.
- **Robustness to Reduced Horizons**: GPC-CEM maintained a 96% success rate even when the planning horizon was reduced from 3 seconds to 1 second, whereas CEM performance dropped to 78%. This indicates GPC's ability to enable non-myopic planning under tighter computational constraints.
- **Generalization to Task Variations (Push-K)**: When tested on a Push-K task (using a K-shaped block instead of a T-shaped block) without retraining the generative model, GPC-CEM achieved 96% success. This vastly outperformed CEM (55%) and MPPI (34%), highlighting the strong transferability and structural prior captured by the learned proposal distribution.
- **Comparison to Iterative Training (Kurtz et al.)**: Unlike which assumed SPC data is too noisy and proposed iterative refinement, this paper found that iterative training *did not improve performance* and even caused success rates to decline. This suggests that the iterative process might diminish the multi-modality of the learned distribution, whereas direct training on noisy SPC data (as done in this work) is effective.
- **Ablation Studies**:
 - **Sample Split (N_CEM/N)**: GPC-CEM benefits from a mix of CEM and generative model samples; increasing CEM samples initially improves performance, but relying solely on CEM leads to a significant drop. This confirms the complementary value of the learned proposal.
 - **Dataset Size**: Performance rapidly improved with dataset size, surpassing 90% success with only 200 MPC rollouts and saturating around 1000, demonstrating sample-efficient training for the proposal model.
 - **Flow vs. CVAE**: Flow-matching models consistently outperformed Conditional Variational Auto-Encoders (CVAE) in both tasks and variations, especially in generalization and adapting to different horizon lengths.

**B. Spot Loco-Manipulation Task (Quadruped Pushing a Chair)**

- **Simulation Results**:
 - **GPC-CEM** achieved up to 83% success (with 2 denoising steps), significantly outperforming CEM (33%) and MPPI (57%). GPC-CEM also completed tasks in fewer steps.
 - **Reduced Planning Horizons**: GPC-CEM maintained 60% success under a reduced 3-second horizon (from 4 seconds), while baselines degraded further (CEM 26%, MPPI 29%).
 - **Added Obstacle Avoidance Cost**: In a task variant with an additional obstacle avoidance cost, GPC-CEM still led with a 56% success rate, outperforming MPPI (30%) and CEM (27%).
 - **Denoising Steps**: Interestingly, fewer denoising steps (2 vs. 10) yielded better performance for this task, suggesting that higher step counts might reduce sample diversity and impair exploration in environments with deceptive local minima.
 - **CEM Sample Ratio**: Higher CEM sample ratios were observed compared to Push-T, indicating that for this more complex task, the generative model alone was less accurate and benefited more from the online CEM augmentation.
- **Real-World Hardware Validation**:
 - **GPC-CEM (60% success)** significantly outperformed CEM (10% success) on the physical Spot robot (20 trials for GPC-CEM, 10 for CEM).
 - **Failure Modes**: CEM frequently failed by getting stuck in the local minimum caused by the C-shaped obstacle. GPC-CEM mostly navigated around this, with failures stemming from pushing the chair out of the workspace or discrepancies between simulated and real chair behavior (friction, contact).
- **Computation Time**: Policy rollout accounted for over 90% of total compute time, limiting control frequency to 5 Hz, primarily due to collision handling and contact dynamics in the physics engine.

In summary, the main findings demonstrate that GPC-CEM consistently achieves higher success rates, improved sample efficiency, and greater robustness compared to baseline SPC methods, both in simulation and on real hardware. It effectively generalizes to task variations and maintains performance under computational constraints, critically, by directly learning meaningful priors from noisy SPC data.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in this paper, "Generative Models from and for Sampling-Based MPC: A Bootstrapped Approach for Adaptive Contact-Rich Manipulation," holds significant implications for the field of robotics and control, particularly in advancing the capabilities of autonomous systems in complex, real-world environments.

**A. Significance of Findings:**

1. **Validation of Direct Learning from Noisy Planner Data**: A central and highly significant contribution is the demonstration that meaningful generative proposal distributions can be learned directly from noisy control sequences generated by Sampling-based MPC (SPC). This challenges the prevailing assumption (e.g., in Kurtz et al.) that such data is too noisy and requires iterative refinement or reliance on expert trajectories from gradient-based solvers. This finding simplifies the data collection and training pipeline, making the approach more accessible and scalable.
2. **Bridging the Gap between Offline Learning and Online Adaptation**: The GPC-CEM framework effectively combines the strengths of offline-trained generative models (for efficient, informed sampling) with the adaptability and robustness of online Model Predictive Control. This hybrid approach overcomes the generalization limitations of purely learned policies and the computational bottlenecks of purely online planners, leading to a powerful synergy.
3. **Enhanced Performance in Complex Tasks**: The impressive success rates, reduced completion times, and improved sample efficiency achieved by GPC-CEM in challenging contact-rich tasks like Push-T and Spot Loco-Manipulation represent a substantial leap forward. These tasks are typically difficult for real-time control due to high dimensionality, non-convexities, and contact dynamics.
4. **Robustness and Generalization**: The demonstrated robustness to reduced planning horizons and strong generalization to significant task variations (e.g., Push-K with a different object, Spot with obstacle avoidance) without retraining the generative model highlights the framework's practical utility. This suggests that the learned priors capture fundamental structural information about the task, rather than just memorizing specific trajectories.
5. **Real-World Hardware Validation**: The successful deployment and superior performance of GPC-CEM on a physical Spot quadruped robot in a contact-rich loco-manipulation task is a crucial validation. Real-world robotics is plagued by sim-to-real gaps and unpredictable dynamics, making hardware demonstrations particularly impactful. This showcases the method's ability to operate reliably in practical settings.

**B. Potential Impact:**

1. **More Capable and Autonomous Robots**: This research paves the way for robots that can perform more complex, adaptive, and dexterous manipulation and locomotion tasks in unstructured environments. This could enable new applications in logistics, manufacturing, service robotics, and exploration, where robots need to interact dynamically with their surroundings.
2. **Reduced Development and Deployment Costs**: By providing a framework that improves sample efficiency and reduces the need for extensive online tuning or complex iterative training, the approach could lower the barriers to entry for developing and deploying advanced robotic systems. The ability to learn from noisy, readily available SPC data further contributes to this.
3. **Faster and More Responsive Control**: The method's ability to maintain high performance under reduced planning horizons signifies a potential for higher-frequency and more reactive control, which is essential for dynamic interaction tasks.
4. **Foundation for Future Research**: The work opens several avenues for future research, including:
 - **Sim-to-Real Transfer**: Investigating domain randomization or combining simulated and real-world data to address current sim-to-real discrepancies.
 - **Variable Goals**: Extending the framework to handle variable goals through goal-centric representations.
 - **Computational Acceleration**: Exploring GPU acceleration for simulation and inference to further boost real-time performance.
 - **Vision-Based Control**: Distilling state-based policies into vision-based ones or integrating fast vision-based dynamics models.
 - **Long-Horizon Planning**: Combining generative priors with learned infinite-horizon value functions to provide even more global guidance to the MPC.

In conclusion, this paper presents a significant advancement in real-time control for complex robotic tasks. By elegantly combining the power of generative models with the adaptability of MPC, and crucially, by demonstrating its effectiveness with real-world hardware, it offers a robust and practical solution that promises to make autonomous robots more capable, efficient, and deployable.
