# Latent Denoising Improves Visual Alignment in Large Multimodal Models

> [!summary] Summary
> A latent denoising framework enhances the internal visual representations of Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) during instruction tuning, leading to improved multimodal understanding and increased robustness to common visual corruptions. This training-time approach consistently boosts performance across various LMM architectures without incurring additional inference overhead.

## Problem

- Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) often receive only indirect supervision for visual tokens via autoregressive language modeling, leading to weak or brittle internal visual representations.
- LMMs exhibit unstable performance under distribution shifts, such as common image corruptions, and are prone to object hallucination and insufficient utilization of visual evidence.
- Internal analyses show suboptimal visual processing, including attention sink effects and sparse visual grounding, indicating visual information is not robustly preserved.

## Method

- A training-time ==latent denoising framework== corrupts projected visual tokens using a ==saliency-guided mixture of masking and Gaussian noising==.
- The LMM is trained to ==recover clean teacher patch features== from an intermediate hidden state of the LLM via a lightweight decoder.
- The training objective combines standard ==LM loss== with auxiliary losses: ==reconstruction==, ==relational alignment==, and ==contrastive learning== — providing direct visual supervision.

## Results

- Consistent improvements across **18 multimodal benchmarks**: LLaVA+SigLIP improved on **all 18**, Qwen-2.5-VL on **17/18**.
- Significant robustness gains under noise, blur, weather, and digital corruption families.
- Internal analyses: higher CKA with DINOv2 (**+0.13**) and improved kNN classification (**+3.8%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Direct visual supervision through latent denoising effectively refines internal visual representations in LMMs, overcoming the limitations of indirect language modeling objectives.
> - Improving the quality of internal visual features leads to enhanced LMM robustness against input corruptions and better performance on fine-grained visual reasoning tasks.
> - The framework's effectiveness generalizes across different LMM architectures and vision encoders (e.g., LLaVA with CLIP/SigLIP, Qwen-2.5-VL) and operates without inference-time overhead.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Large Language Models (LLMs) has led to the emergence of Large Multimodal Models (LMMs), which integrate visual and linguistic reasoning capabilities. A common approach for constructing LMMs involves coupling a pre-trained LLM with a vision encoder via a lightweight projector, followed by multimodal instruction tuning. Examples include LLaVA, Qwen-VL, and InternVL. While these models have demonstrated proficiency across diverse multimodal tasks such as visual question answering, document understanding, and compositional visual reasoning, analyses have identified limitations in their visual understanding capabilities.

These limitations include a tendency to over-rely on textual inputs when in cross-modal conflict, susceptibility to object hallucination, and insufficient utilization of visual evidence, characterized by visual attention sink effects and sparse visual grounding. Such observations suggest that the standard autoregressive language modeling objective, which provides only indirect supervision to visual tokens, is often insufficient for developing robust internal visual representations.

Prior research has attempted to address this challenge through several strategies:
1. **Backbone Refinement:** Enhancing the visual encoder itself, for instance, by improving feature locality or aligning CLIP-like features to stronger vision-centric representations before integration into LMMs.
2. **Feature Alignment:** Injecting explicit supervision into the multimodal stack by guiding projector outputs or internal visual tokens using refined shallow-layer embeddings or alignment to vision foundation models.
3. **Reconstructive Supervision:** Introducing objectives based on latent reconstruction, masked image modeling, or diffusion-based generation.

While these approaches acknowledge the importance of direct visual supervision, they often involve specialized teachers, auxiliary generators, or narrowly scoped objectives. This paper positions itself within this landscape by exploring a unified and efficient mechanism for refining internal visual representations within LMMs, drawing inspiration from the effectiveness of latent denoising in learning high-quality visual tokenizers for generative tasks. The work investigates whether this principle can be adapted to improve internal visual features for multimodal understanding within LMMs, thereby bridging concepts previously applied primarily in visual generation to the domain of visual understanding.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to enhance the quality of internal visual representations in Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) to improve their multimodal understanding and reasoning capabilities, particularly under challenging conditions such as distribution shifts.

The motivation for this objective stems from several identified shortcomings of current LMMs:

1. **Indirect Visual Supervision:** Standard LMM training typically relies on an autoregressive language modeling objective. This means that visual tokens within the model receive only indirect supervision, as their quality is inferred from their contribution to the accuracy of the generated text response. This indirect mechanism can lead to weak or brittle internal visual representations.
2. **Brittle Behavior under Distribution Shift:** A consequence of weak internal visual representations is that LMMs can exhibit unstable performance when faced with images that deviate from their training distribution. This includes scenarios with common corruptions (e.g., noise, blur) or naturally challenging, low-quality imagery.
3. **Cross-Modal Conflict and Hallucination:** Studies indicate that LMMs can prioritize textual cues over visual evidence, leading to phenomena like object hallucination and failures in visual context reasoning. This suggests a deficiency in visual grounding and integration within the LLM pathway.
4. **Suboptimal Internal Visual Processing:** Analyses of LMMs' internal mechanisms reveal issues such as diminishing visual token contribution with increasing network depth (attention sink effects) and sparse visual grounding, indicating that visual information may not be robustly preserved or utilized throughout the model.

To address these issues, the authors propose to introduce a form of direct visual supervision during LMM instruction tuning. They are motivated by recent advances in visual representation learning, where latent denoising—the process of recovering clean latent targets from corrupted latent inputs—has demonstrated success in yielding high-quality visual tokenizers beneficial for image generation. The core hypothesis is that if latent denoising can improve visual token spaces for generation, it might similarly serve as an effective supervisory mechanism for the internal visual features employed in multimodal understanding within LMMs.

Specifically, the key objectives articulated in the paper's contributions are:
- To develop a training-time latent denoising framework for LMM instruction tuning. This framework is designed to refine internal visual representations by corrupting projected visual tokens using a saliency-guided mixture of masking and Gaussian noising, then training the LMM to recover clean teacher patch features from intermediate hidden states using a decoder, complemented by structure-preserving and discriminative objectives.
- To establish a standardized, practical common-corruption evaluation protocol for LMMs, inspired by ImageNet-C, to systematically assess robustness to non-adversarial corruptions applied directly to benchmark images.
- To empirically demonstrate that this latent denoising approach consistently improves multimodal understanding, reasoning, and robustness across various LMM architectures and benchmark settings, without incurring additional inference-time overhead.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method introduces a training-time latent denoising framework designed to improve the quality of internal visual representations within LMMs. The framework modifies the instruction-tuning phase of LMM training without affecting inference-time performance.

#### 3.1. LMM Setup and Standard Training

The work considers a decoder-only LMM architecture comprising a frozen vision encoder ($E_{\phi}$), a trainable vision-language projector ($P_{\psi}$), and a trainable autoregressive language model ($L_{\theta}$). For an input image $\mathbf{X}$, the vision encoder produces a sequence of patch-level visual features $\mathbf{V}$, which are then projected into the LLM's embedding space as $\mathbf{Z}$. These projected visual tokens are concatenated with tokenized user query embeddings ($\mathbf{Q}$) and processed by the LLM to autoregressively generate a target answer sequence ($\mathbf{A}$).

Standard LMM training involves two stages: projector pretraining (optimizing $P_{\psi}$ on image-text pairs with $E_{\phi}$ and $L_{\theta}$ frozen) and multimodal instruction tuning (adapting $P_{\psi}$ and $L_{\theta}$ on multimodal instruction-following data, with $E_{\phi}$ typically frozen). Both stages primarily use a teacher-forced autoregressive language modeling loss ($L_{lang}$). This indirect supervision for visual tokens is the problem addressed by this research.

#### 3.2. Latent Denoising Framework

The core of the method involves corrupting the projected visual tokens ($\mathbf{Z}$) and training the LMM to recover corresponding clean teacher features from an intermediate layer of the LLM.

- **Clean Teacher Targets:** A separate, frozen teacher model ($F_{\zeta}$), typically the same visual backbone as the front-end encoder, provides clean patch features $\mathbf{Y} = F_{\zeta}(\mathbf{X})$ to serve as recovery targets.
- **Latent Corruption Process (Joint Latent Deconstruction):**
 - **Saliency-Guided Selection:** Patches are initially assigned saliency scores ($s_i$) derived from the frozen visual encoder's attention patterns (or feature-space surrogates for certain models). Based on these scores, two disjoint sets of patches are selected for corruption: a noised set ($\mathcal{N}$) and a masked set ($\mathcal{M}$). High-saliency patches are preferentially assigned to Gaussian noising, while lower-saliency patches are preferentially masked. This asymmetric strategy aims to preserve crucial visual information while providing a tractable recovery task.
 - **Gaussian Noising:** For patches $i \in \mathcal{N}$, the projected visual token $z_i$ is perturbed using an interpolation strength $\tau_i \sim \text{Unif}(0, \tau_{max})$ and Gaussian noise $\epsilon_i \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2 \mathbf{I})$: $\tilde{z}_{i}^{gauss} = (1 - \tau_i)z_i + \tau_i \epsilon_i$. A learned noise-level embedding, discretizing $\tau_i$ into bins, is also added to the corrupted token to provide explicit conditioning on corruption magnitude.
 - **Masking:** For patches $i \in \mathcal{M}$, the projected visual token $z_i$ is entirely replaced with a learnable mask embedding $e_{mask}$.
 - **Corrupted Sequence:** The resulting sequence of projected tokens, $\tilde{\mathbf{Z}}$, comprises noised, masked, and uncorrupted tokens.

- **Recovering Aligned Visual Targets:**
 - The LMM processes the corrupted visual sequence $\tilde{\mathbf{Z}}$ along with the text query.
 - Visual hidden states $h_{i}^{(\ell_s)}$ are extracted from a selected intermediate layer $\ell_s$ of the LLM. The choice of an intermediate layer is motivated by findings that visual signal often remains strongest in the middle layers of LMMs.
 - A lightweight decoder $D_{\chi}$ (a two-layer MLP) is attached to the LMM at layer $\ell_s$. It decodes the hidden states corresponding to the corrupted token positions to predict the clean teacher features: $\hat{y}_i = D_{\chi}(h_{i}^{(\ell_s)})$ for $i \in \mathcal{C} = \mathcal{N} \cup \mathcal{M}$.

#### 3.3. Training Objective

The overall training objective combines the standard language modeling loss with three auxiliary visual supervision terms:

1. **Reconstruction Loss ($L_{rec}$):** An L2-normalized reconstruction loss that aligns the decoded predictions $\hat{y}_i$ with their corresponding clean teacher targets $y_i$: $L_{rec} = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{C}|} \sum_{i \in \mathcal{C}} \|\text{norm}(\hat{y}_i) - \text{norm}(y_i)\|_2^2$. This provides direct patch-level supervision, invariant to feature scale.
2. **Relational Alignment Loss ($L_{rel}$):** A KL divergence between the temperature-scaled pairwise similarity distributions of decoded and teacher features on the corrupted-token set. This encourages the decoded features to maintain the global visual structure of the teacher representation.
3. **Contrastive Loss ($L_{con}$):** An intra-image InfoNCE objective where each decoded corrupted token $\hat{y}_i$ is a positive pair with its teacher target $y_i$ and a negative pair against other corrupted-token targets $y_j$ ($j \neq i$) within the same image. This promotes patch-level distinctiveness and mitigates representation collapse.

The final training objective is a weighted sum: $L = L_{lang} + \lambda_{rec} L_{rec} + \lambda_{rel} L_{rel} + \lambda_{con} L_{con}$. A warmup-hold-decay schedule is applied to auxiliary loss weights and corruption rates. Critically, all corruption and auxiliary heads are active only during training; during inference, they are disabled, ensuring no additional computational overhead.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

The method was evaluated on three LMM architectures:
- LLaVA-1.5 with CLIP ViT-L/14@336, paired with Vicuna-7B-v1.5.
- LLaVA-1.5 with SigLIP-SO400M/14@384, paired with Vicuna-7B-v1.5.
- Qwen-2.5-VL-7B-Instruct.

Training followed LLaVA's two-stage recipe (projector pretraining on LLaVA-558K, instruction tuning on LLaVA-mix-665K), with latent denoising enabled during instruction tuning. A comprehensive evaluation suite of 18 multimodal benchmarks was used, covering VQA, hallucination, general reasoning, science/math, text understanding, charts, and robustness tasks.

#### 3.5. Corruption Evaluation Protocol

An adapted ImageNet-C common-corruptions protocol was introduced for LMMs. Corruptions from four families (noise, blur, weather, digital) were applied to evaluation images at severity levels 3 and 5. A deterministic, seeded hash selected corruption subtypes for reproducibility. This allowed for assessing whether improved internal representations translated to robustness under input-level distribution shifts, independent of the training-time latent-space corruption.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several key findings regarding the effectiveness of latent denoising in improving LMMs.

#### 4.1. Consistent Performance Improvement Across Architectures

The proposed latent denoising framework consistently improved performance across diverse LMM architectures. On a suite of 18 multimodal benchmarks under clean evaluation:
- LLaVA+CLIP model showed improvements on 15 out of 18 benchmarks.
- LLaVA+SigLIP model improved on all 18 benchmarks.
- Qwen-2.5-VL model improved on 17 out of 18 benchmarks.

These results indicate that the method generalizes across different vision encoders and language model backbones, including a strong, pre-instruction-tuned Qwen model, suggesting its utility even in post-tuning scenarios.

#### 4.2. Enhanced Visual Reasoning and Grounding

The most notable gains were observed on benchmarks requiring fine-grained visual reasoning and compositional understanding:
- GQA (compositional spatial reasoning) improved by +1.6 to +4.4 across architectures.
- MMStar (holistic multimodal understanding) improved by +1.2 to +3.1.
- NaturalBench group accuracy (compositional consistency) improved by +0.9 to +2.4.
These improvements support the hypothesis that strengthening internal visual representations through latent denoising directly benefits complex visual reasoning tasks.

#### 4.3. Improved Robustness to Challenging Inputs

Even without applying corruptions during evaluation, benchmarks featuring naturally challenging or low-quality imagery demonstrated gains:
- VizWiz (crowd-sourced images from visually impaired users) improved by +0.8 to +3.4.
- Q-Bench (low-level vision) improved by +0.9 to +2.0.
- RealWorldQA (real-world noisy imagery) improved by +1.1 to +4.2 on SigLIP and Qwen.
The MME score, which showed a slight decline on clean evaluation for two architectures, surprisingly improved under all corruption conditions, suggesting that latent denoising helps MME under degraded visual inputs.

#### 4.4. Superior Corruption Robustness

When evaluated using the adapted common-corruptions protocol (noise, blur, weather, digital at severity 5), models trained with latent denoising consistently outperformed baselines:
- For both LLaVA+CLIP and Qwen-2.5-VL, latent denoising improved performance across all four corruption families on all 11 evaluated benchmarks.
- Analysis of performance degradation across severity levels (clean, severity 3, severity 5) revealed that models with latent denoising exhibited consistently smaller drops in accuracy. The performance gap between baseline and the proposed method widened with increasing corruption severity, indicating a reduction in performance degradation. For instance, on GQA under severity-5 noise, the baseline dropped by 4.5 points, while the latent denoising model dropped by 3.1 points.

#### 4.5. Measurably Better Internal Visual Representations

Beyond task-level metrics, an analysis of internal visual features demonstrated concrete improvements:
- **CKA with Reference Models:** Latent denoising maintained higher Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA) with the LMM’s CLIP backbone in early-to-mid layers (+0.02 to +0.04) and a substantial improvement at the final layer (+0.11). Furthermore, CKA with DINOv2 (an external self-supervised model) showed even stronger gains (+0.08 to +0.13), peaking at layer 9. This suggests the denoising objective fosters structural improvements in visual representations that generalize beyond the specific teacher model.
- **kNN Classification:** The characteristic mid-network dip in semantic discriminability observed in baseline models was largely eliminated. Latent denoising increased kNN accuracy by up to +3.8% at the supervised layer (layer 16) and maintained a +2.4% gain at the final layer, indicating improved and propagated discriminative power.
- **Effective Rank:** The singular value spectrum showed that latent denoising led to slower spectral decay at mid-layers, implying higher intrinsic dimensionality and more expressive visual features.
- **Cross-Attention Visualization:** Cross-attention heatmaps from intermediate layers indicated that the proposed method produced more spatially focused attention on question-relevant regions, with higher average entropy, suggesting better visual grounding and less reliance on attention sinks.

#### 4.6. Ablation Study Insights

Ablation studies confirmed the importance of various design choices:
- **Loss Components:** The full objective combining reconstruction, relational alignment, and contrastive losses, coupled with saliency-guided token selection, achieved the best overall performance. Removing any single loss or using random token selection consistently reduced gains.
- **Corruption Rates:** Optimal Gaussian noise and mask rates were identified, with both modalities contributing to performance. Using only noise or only masking resulted in weaker performance, highlighting their complementary roles.
- **Supervision Layer:** Supervising an intermediate layer (layer 16, 50% depth) yielded optimal results, supporting the observation that visual information is often strongest in the middle of the LMM.
- **Noise Conditioning:** Providing an explicit noise-level embedding for Gaussian-corrupted tokens was beneficial, particularly for robustness.
- **Gaussian Parameters:** The interpolation strength ($\tau_{max}$) and noise variance ($\sigma$) parameters exhibited an inverted-U pattern, with the default values being near-optimal and the method showing robustness to a range of settings.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework that offers a practical and effective approach to a fundamental challenge in Large Multimodal Models (LMMs): the insufficient direct supervision of internal visual representations. By introducing latent denoising, the work provides a mechanism to explicitly encourage the LMM to learn and maintain robust visual features throughout its processing pipeline.

The significance and potential impact of this work include:

1. **Improved Core Capabilities of LMMs:** The consistent performance gains across a wide array of multimodal benchmarks, particularly in visual reasoning and compositional tasks, indicate that the method enhances the fundamental visual understanding capabilities of LMMs. This suggests LMMs trained with this approach could more accurately answer complex visual questions and interpret intricate visual information.
2. **Enhanced Robustness to Real-World Conditions:** The demonstrated reduction in performance degradation under various common visual corruptions (noise, blur, weather, digital) is a notable practical benefit. This indicates that LMMs can operate more reliably in real-world scenarios where imagery is often noisy, blurry, or of variable quality, making them more dependable for applications in diverse environments.
3. **Deeper Understanding of LMM Internals:** The internal feature analysis, including CKA, kNN classification, and effective rank, provides valuable insights into how LMMs process visual information. By showing that latent denoising leads to better preserved, more discriminative, and higher-dimensional visual features, the research contributes to the understanding of effective visual representation learning within complex multimodal architectures. This may guide future architectural designs and training strategies.
4. **Efficiency and Practicality:** The framework operates entirely at training time, introducing no additional computational overhead during inference. This makes it a highly practical solution for improving LMMs, as it does not compromise the speed or efficiency of deployed models.
5. **Generalizability:** The observed effectiveness across different LMM architectures (LLaVA with CLIP and SigLIP, Qwen-2.5-VL) and vision encoders suggests broad applicability of the latent denoising principle. This indicates that the method is not narrowly tailored to a specific model configuration but represents a generalizable strategy.
6. **Foundation for Future Research:** This work establishes latent denoising as a viable and impactful paradigm for visual supervision in LMMs. It opens avenues for future research to explore how this framework might compose with other visual supervision techniques, adapt to larger-scale datasets and models, or be extended to other modalities or tasks within the multimodal domain. For example, investigating its synergy with complementary visual supervision paradigms could lead to further breakthroughs in LMM capabilities.

In summary, this research offers a well-supported and empirically validated method for strengthening the visual backbone of LMMs, leading to models that are not only more capable in understanding complex visual data but also more resilient to real-world image degradations, all without impacting inference efficiency.
