# Latent Denoising Improves Visual Alignment in Large Multimodal Models

> [!summary] Summary
> A latent denoising framework enhances the internal visual representations of Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) during instruction tuning, leading to improved multimodal understanding and increased robustness to common visual corruptions. This training-time approach consistently boosts performance across various LMM architectures without incurring additional inference overhead.

## Problem

- Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) often receive only indirect supervision for visual tokens via autoregressive language modeling, leading to weak or brittle internal visual representations.
- LMMs exhibit unstable performance under distribution shifts, such as common image corruptions, and are prone to object hallucination and insufficient utilization of visual evidence.
- Internal analyses show suboptimal visual processing, including attention sink effects and sparse visual grounding, indicating visual information is not robustly preserved.

## Method

- A training-time ==latent denoising framework== corrupts projected visual tokens using a ==saliency-guided mixture of masking and Gaussian noising==.
- The LMM is trained to ==recover clean teacher patch features== from an intermediate hidden state of the LLM via a lightweight decoder.
- The training objective combines standard ==LM loss== with auxiliary losses: ==reconstruction==, ==relational alignment==, and ==contrastive learning== — providing direct visual supervision.

## Results

- Consistent improvements across **18 multimodal benchmarks**: LLaVA+SigLIP improved on **all 18**, Qwen-2.5-VL on **17/18**.
- Significant robustness gains under noise, blur, weather, and digital corruption families.
- Internal analyses: higher CKA with DINOv2 (**+0.13**) and improved kNN classification (**+3.8%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Direct visual supervision through latent denoising effectively refines internal visual representations in LMMs, overcoming the limitations of indirect language modeling objectives.
> - Improving the quality of internal visual features leads to enhanced LMM robustness against input corruptions and better performance on fine-grained visual reasoning tasks.
> - The framework's effectiveness generalizes across different LMM architectures and vision encoders (e.g., LLaVA with CLIP/SigLIP, Qwen-2.5-VL) and operates without inference-time overhead.
