# VHOI: Controllable Video Generation of Human-Object Interactions from Sparse Trajectories via Motion Densification

> [!summary] Summary
> VHOI is a two-stage video diffusion framework for controllable human-object interaction (HOI) generation: a trajectory augmentor densifies sparse user trajectories into part-aware HOI mask sequences, then a dense control model conditions video synthesis on these masks, using visibility and confidence gating to handle noisy signals, achieving state-of-the-art controllable HOI video generation.

## Problem

- Sparse motion controls like keypoint trajectories are easy for users to specify but lack instance-awareness, causing incoherent or physically implausible human-object motion.
- Dense signals such as optical flow, depth, or 3D meshes give strong motion cues but are costly and impractical for users to obtain at inference time.
- Prior HOI video synthesis methods either need external interpolation models for navigation-interaction, or rely on ground-truth dense masks unavailable in practice.

## Method

- **Trajectory Augmentor**: a fine-tuned I2V DiT model with a sparse control branch that converts blurred trajectory and visibility maps into dense HOI mask sequences via ==FiLM-modulated fusion==.
- ==HOI-aware color-coded motion representation==: part-based palette distinguishes human body parts and objects consistently across sparse trajectories and dense masks for instance-aware semantics.
- **Dense Control Model** with ==confidence-gated fusion==: a HOI extractor and fuser inject predicted mask features into the frozen DiT backbone, with a learned confidence head downweighting unreliable mask regions.

## Results

- On HOIGen-1M, VHOI improves FVD from **1359** (TORA) to **915** and Contact Accuracy from **0.775** to **0.827**, beating both TORA and Go-with-the-Flow baselines.
- On BEHAVE dataset, VHOI achieves FVD **434** versus **675** (TORA) and **737** (Go-with-the-Flow), with lowest Trajectory Error at **5.34**.
- Ablations show densifying sparse signals closes most of the gap to dense ground-truth upper bounds, and HOI-aware masks outperform optical flow or coarse instance masks on FVD, TE, and CA.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Bridging sparse and dense control via a learned intermediate representation resolves the usability-versus-fidelity trade-off common to motion-conditioned video generation.
> - Encoding domain semantics (part-level human-object structure) into the color palette itself, not just the network, materially strengthens instance-aware motion understanding.
> - Confidence and visibility gating let a model stay robust to noisy auxiliary-model-derived training signals without being strictly necessary for baseline-beating performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The generation of realistic videos, particularly those involving human-object interactions (HOI), represents a significant challenge within the field of computer vision. HOI scenes are complex due to the intricate, instance-specific dynamics required for both human and object movements, including fine-grained hand articulation, contact, and object manipulation. Generic video generation models typically struggle to capture these specific details.

Recent advancements in controllable video generation have introduced various methods to guide video synthesis using different control signals. These approaches generally fall into categories such as training-based methods, fine-tuning techniques (e.g., ControlNet, LoRA), and training-free methods. The control signals themselves range from sparse inputs like point-wise trajectories to dense signals such as per-frame masks, depth maps, or 3D meshes.

A fundamental trade-off exists between the ease of specifying control signals and their informativeness. Sparse controls, such as 2D trajectories, are intuitive for users but often lack sufficient spatial and geometric structure, which can lead to incoherent or physically implausible interactions. For instance, guiding a grasping motion with only a hand trajectory makes it difficult for models to coherently move the arm, body, and object. Conversely, dense signals offer precise controllability and higher video quality but are often impractical and costly to obtain at inference time.

In the context of HOI video synthesis, prior works like TAVID, GenHSI, and InterDyn have addressed aspects of this problem. TAVID leverages attention-based loss but may require external models for navigation and interaction. GenHSI decomposes the task into script writing and 3D keyframe generation, which involves computationally intensive inpainting and pose optimization. InterDyn uses ground-truth mask sequences as control signals, which are not readily available in practical scenarios. Many existing methods for bridging sparse and dense signals are designed for generic video generation and do not incorporate HOI-specific semantics, which are critical for realistic interactions.

This research aims to address this gap by proposing a general approach that integrates the complementary benefits of sparse and dense control signals, specifically tailored for HOI. It seeks to provide a practical and effective solution for controllable HOI video generation by focusing on instance-aware motion representations and a two-stage process that densifies sparse user inputs into richer, semantically meaningful control signals.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to synthesize realistic human-object interaction videos with fine-grained, controllable motion from user-specified sparse input signals. This overarching goal is driven by several motivations and specific aims:

1. **Addressing the Sparse-Dense Control Trade-off for HOI:** The core motivation stems from the inherent challenge in controllable video generation for HOI. Users desire easy-to-specify controls (sparse trajectories), but these inputs are typically insufficient to produce coherent and physically plausible HOI dynamics. Dense controls provide the necessary detail but are impractical to acquire at inference. The objective is to develop a method that effectively bridges this gap, allowing users to leverage simple sparse controls while achieving the fidelity usually associated with dense signals.

2. **Enhancing Instance-Awareness and Interaction Fidelity:** Human-object interactions require a nuanced understanding of how specific body parts interact with specific objects. Existing generic video generation models, even with motion control, often fail to capture the precise deformations, contacts, and coordinated movements characteristic of HOI. A key objective is to introduce an intermediate motion representation that is "HOI-aware," incorporating semantic information about body parts and object identities, thereby strengthening the model's ability to understand and generate realistic HOI dynamics.

3. **Enabling Coherent Motion Synthesis:** The paper aims to overcome issues of incoherence and physical implausibility that arise when using sparse controls directly. By densifying sparse trajectories into structured mask sequences, the method intends to provide richer spatial and geometric cues to the video generation model, ensuring that the human's body, arms, and hands move coherently with the object during interactions like grasping or pushing.

4. **Supporting End-to-End HOI Scenarios:** Beyond mere interaction, the research is motivated by the need to generate broader HOI scenarios, including human navigation leading up to object interactions. The objective is to provide a framework capable of handling these complex, sequential movements in an end-to-end manner, reducing reliance on external interpolation models or costly keyframe generation processes used in prior work.

5. **Practical Applications:** The development of controllable HOI video generation has potential applications in streamlining animation workflows, allowing animators to guide character-object interactions with intuitive controls. Furthermore, it can be used to generate diverse synthetic training scenarios for robotics, providing valuable data for training robots to perceive and interact with objects.

In summary, the research seeks to achieve robust, controllable, and realistic HOI video generation by addressing the limitations of current control signals through a novel sparse-to-dense motion densification paradigm and HOI-specific semantic representations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, VHOI, is a two-stage framework designed for controllable HOI video generation. It operates by first densifying sparse user-specified trajectories into rich HOI mask sequences and then conditioning a video diffusion model on these dense masks to synthesize the final video.

#### 3.1. Overall Framework

Given an input image (I), a text prompt (τ), a set of user-specified point trajectories (ξ) with optional frame-wise visibilities (o), and initial HOI masks (Mh, Mo) for the human and object, VHOI aims to synthesize a realistic HOI video (V). The framework consists of:
1. **Trajectory Augmentor (A):** A network that converts sparse point trajectories (ξ) into dense HOI mask sequences (Mhoi).
2. **Dense Control Model (D):** A video diffusion model that generates the video (V) conditioned on the predicted dense HOI mask sequences (Mhoi).

#### 3.2. Video Diffusion Model Preliminaries

VHOI builds upon a DiT-based (Diffusion Transformer) image-to-video (I2V) latent diffusion model, specifically adapted from CogVideoX. This model uses a 3D Variational Autoencoder (VAE) to encode input images (I) into image latents (l_img) and target videos (V) into video latents (l_v). The image latent is temporally zero-padded and concatenated with noisy video latents before being fed into a patch embedder (Ep) to create video tokens. These tokens are combined with text tokens, and hybrid attention is performed within DiT blocks. AdaLN (Adaptive Layer Normalization) injects the diffusion timestep. The base I2V DiT model (B) remains frozen throughout the training of VHOI, with only additional feature extractors and fusers being fine-tuned. The training objective follows a velocity-prediction scheme.

#### 3.3. Sparse to Dense Motion Representations

VHOI introduces a novel HOI-aware motion representation at two levels to enable instance-aware interaction reasoning:
- **Sparse Representation:** Instead of conventional 2D path rendering or optical-flow color encoding for trajectories, VHOI uses a part-based color palette to code trajectory tracks. Each human body part (e.g., left hand, torso, face) and the target object receives a consistent, distinct color. This provides fine-grained spatiotemporal cues linking specific body parts to objects. Optional track visibilities (Mv) can also be incorporated as auxiliary reliability cues. Both the trajectory rendering (Mξ) and visibility map (Mv) are blurred to ensure visibility after VAE encoding.
- **Dense Representation (Mhoi):** This intermediate representation is a sequence of HOI masks. It is constructed by combining ground-truth human (Mh) and object (Mo) mask sequences using an element-wise maximum operation. The color encoding of Mhoi is consistent with the sparse trajectory rendering Mξ, maintaining the fine-grained HOI semantics across representations. These dense masks explicitly model how body parts and the object deform and interact over time, providing a rich, structured signal.

#### 3.4. Trajectory Augmentor (A)

The augmentor (A) is responsible for converting the sparse motion cues (blurred trajectory rendering ˜Mξ and blurred visibility map ˜Mv) into structured HOI mask sequences (Mhoi).
- **Architecture:** It consists of the frozen base I2V DiT model (B) and a learnable sparse control branch.
- **Inputs and Adaptations:** The augmentor receives a motion-only text prompt (without appearance cues), the first-frame HOI mask (M0_hoi), and the original input image (I) to provide appearance context. A finetuned patch embedder (Epa) accommodates the increased channel dimensions.
- **Sparse Control Branch Details:**
 - **Trajectory Extractor (Et):** Encodes the VAE-processed ˜Mξ into trajectory features (Fa).
 - **Visibility Embedder (Ev):** Processes the downsampled ˜Mv to produce visibility features, which form a learnable gate (Gv) that acts as a confidence score.
 - **Augmentor Fuser (Ea):** Predicts FiLM (Feature-wise Linear Modulation) scale (γaug) and shift (βaug) parameters from Fa.
 - **Visibility-Aware Gating:** Gv modulates the FiLM parameters (˜γaug, ˜βaug) to adaptively adjust the influence of trajectory features, enhancing robustness to unreliable or noisy sparse cues. These gated FiLM parameters then modulate the DiT tokens before the attention layer in each block.

#### 3.5. Dense Control Model (D)

The dense control model (D) takes the predicted HOI mask sequence (ˆMhoi) from the augmentor (A) and generates the final video. Its purpose is to translate the geometry-based intermediate representation into realistic appearance and interaction dynamics.
- **Architecture:** It utilizes the same diffusion backbone (B) and a control branch design similar to the augmentor, but with key differences.
- **Inputs and Adaptations:** It operates in a standard I2V setting, conditioned only on the text prompt (τ) and the initial image (I). The patch embedder (Ep) remains frozen without channel expansion, as it generates a full HOI video rather than a mask sequence.
- **Dense Control Branch Details:**
 - **HOI Extractor (Ehoi):** Processes the predicted ˆMhoi to obtain HOI features (Fhoi).
 - **Dense Control Fuser (Em):** Fuses Fhoi with the DiT tokens.
 - **Confidence-Based Fusion:** A confidence head (Ec) predicts a scalar confidence score (s) from Fhoi. This score modulates the HOI features (˜Fhoi), downweighting unreliable conditioning signals that might arise from flickering or incomplete masks during training or prediction. ˜Fhoi then modulates the transformer tokens via FiLM layers.

#### 3.6. Implementation Details

Trainable components include Ehoi, Et, Ea, Em, Ev, and Ec, each independently instantiated. Ehoi and Et share architecture, as do Ea and Em, but not weights. ResNet blocks are used for extractors, and LayerNorm replaces BatchNorm for stability. Fusers and embedders use spatial and temporal convolution layers, while the confidence head uses 3D convolutions. The diffusion backbone B is frozen. During training of the dense control model, a dropout strategy (50% both human/object, 20% human only, 20% object only, 10% no conditioning) is applied to improve robustness. For augmentor training, the number of trajectories is randomly sampled. The models are fine-tuned on subsets of HOIGen-1M and BEHAVE datasets. Gaussian smoothing is applied to sparse inputs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of VHOI was evaluated through extensive experiments across diverse HOI scenarios, utilizing both qualitative and quantitative comparisons against baseline methods and conducting ablation studies.

#### 4.1. Evaluation Datasets

Three datasets were used for evaluation:
1. **HOIGen-1M:** A hold-out split of 50 videos from the HOIGen-1M dataset, featuring various objects and interaction behaviors.
2. **BEHAVE:** 50 videos from the BEHAVE dataset, specifically including challenging cases where the human initially navigates towards an object before interaction.
3. **In-the-wild:** 50 curated videos with varied subjects and object categories to assess generalization to real-world scenarios.

#### 4.2. Baselines

The performance of VHOI was compared against:
- **TORA:** A trajectory-conditioned video generation model.
- **TORA-Finetuned (TORA*):** A re-trained variant of TORA, fine-tuned on HOIGen-1M for fair comparison.
- **Go-with-the-Flow:** A diffusion conditioning method based on noise warping, adapted to use first-frame masks and rigid transformations based on input trajectories.
- **MotionI2V:** A two-stage UNet-based framework with optical-flow based densification (detailed in supplementary material).

#### 4.3. Evaluation Metrics

A comprehensive suite of metrics was employed:
- **General Video Quality:** VBench metrics (Subject Consistency (SC), Background Consistency (BC), Dynamic Degree (DD), Motion Smoothness (MS), Aesthetics Quality (AQ), Imaging Quality (IQ)), Frechet Video Distance (FVD), and CLIP Score for Text-Video Alignment (CLIPSIM).
- **Controllable HOI Synthesis:** Contact Accuracy (CA), measuring per-frame hand-object contact correctness relative to ground truth using a contact detector, and Trajectory Error (TE), quantifying the Euclidean distance between ground-truth and generated trajectories using CoTracker3.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Comparisons

Qualitative comparisons (Figure 5) indicate that VHOI produces higher interaction fidelity and visual quality compared to baselines. Go-with-the-Flow exhibited background blur near motion regions. TORA* often suffered from lower interaction fidelity, including blurry hands and implausible object transformations. VHOI consistently generated more coherent and realistic interactions. Supplementary video demonstrations corroborate the accuracy of trajectory control.

#### 4.5. Quantitative Comparisons

Across all three datasets (Tables 1, 2, 3), VHOI demonstrated competitive performance:
- **Video Quality (FVD):** VHOI consistently achieved the lowest FVD scores across all datasets (HOIGen-1M: 915; BEHAVE: 434; in-the-wild: 484), indicating superior overall video quality compared to all baselines.
- **Interaction Fidelity and Trajectory Adherence (TE, CA):** VHOI generally outperformed baselines in Trajectory Error (TE) and Contact Accuracy (CA) on HOIGen-1M (TE: 10.64, CA: 0.827) and in-the-wild (TE: 6.79, CA: 0.721). On BEHAVE, VHOI achieved the lowest TE (5.34), though its CA (0.721) was slightly lower than TORA-Finetuned (0.803), suggesting a different balance between trajectory following and contact realism in this dataset.
- **Perceptual Metrics (VBench, CLIPSIM):** While differences in VBench and CLIPSIM scores were smaller, VHOI often matched or surpassed the top-performing baselines, suggesting comparable or improved subject/background consistency, motion smoothness, and text-video alignment.
- **Comparison with MotionI2V (Table 4, Supplementary):** VHOI significantly outperformed MotionI2V, which is a UNet-based, optical-flow densification approach. VHOI achieved a substantially lower FVD (915 vs 1629) and TE (14.82 vs 19.06), and higher CA (0.830 vs 0.745), indicating more realistic interactions and better control despite resolution differences.
- **User Study (Supplementary):** A perceptual user study involving 46 participants corroborated these findings. VHOI was preferred over TORA-Finetuned and Go-with-the-Flow in terms of both naturalness of human-object interaction (62.2% vs TORA*, 86.1% vs Go-Flow) and accuracy of trajectory adherence (60.9% vs TORA*, 75.2% vs Go-Flow), with all improvements being statistically significant.
- **Additional Improvement (Table 5, Supplementary):** A modification where sparse trajectories are overlaid onto object masks during dense model training (VHOI-Traj) further improved TE (8.74 vs 10.64) and CA (0.833 vs 0.827) on HOIGen-1M, suggesting that explicit trajectory awareness in the dense model can boost performance.

#### 4.6. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies (Table 1) were conducted on HOIGen-1M to understand the contribution of different components:
1. **Sparse vs. Dense Control:** A clear performance gap was observed between direct sparse trajectory conditioning (Sparse HOI) and ground-truth dense HOI masks (Dense HOI upper bound), highlighting the importance of the augmentor for densification.
2. **Motion Representation Choice:**
 - Augmenting any representation improved performance over baselines.
 - Foreground optical flow (Augmented flow) resulted in lower perceptual quality (higher FVD) and less realistic interactions (higher TE, lower CA), indicating difficulties in training and error propagation due to lack of HOI semantics and sensitivity to camera motion.
 - Instance masks (Augmented mask) improved performance but lacked the fine semantic detail of HOI-aware masks.
 - The proposed HOI representation (VHOI) achieved the best FVD, TE, and CA among learned representations, demonstrating its effectiveness for interaction reasoning, despite potential issues with mask smoothness from SAPIEN-derived training masks.
3. **First-Frame Image Context:** Removing the first-frame appearance input from the augmentor (VHOI w/o first image) led to a noticeable drop in CA, emphasizing its importance for grounding the interaction context.
4. **Visibility and Confidence Gating:** Both the visibility gate (VHOI w/o vis) and the confidence head (VHOI w/o conf) contributed to robustness against noisy or imperfect signals. While not strictly critical for the model to outperform baselines, their inclusion provided additional stability (e.g., VHOI w/o conf showed slightly higher TE and lower CA than VHOI).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The VHOI framework introduces a methodological contribution to controllable video generation by effectively bridging the gap between sparse, user-friendly control signals and the dense, semantically rich motion cues required for high-fidelity Human-Object Interaction (HOI) video synthesis.

The primary significance of this work lies in its two-stage augment-and-control paradigm, which allows for the creation of realistic HOI videos from easily specifiable, yet instance-aware, sparse trajectories. By learning to densify sparse inputs into HOI mask sequences with part-level semantics, VHOI provides a structured intermediate representation that disentangles geometry and motion from appearance. This design enables the video generation model to better capture the complex spatiotemporal dynamics inherent in HOI.

Potential impacts include:
- **Animation and Creative Workflows:** VHOI can streamline animation processes by allowing artists to intuitively guide character-object interactions with simple trajectory inputs, significantly reducing the manual effort involved in creating complex and realistic movements.
- **Robotics and Synthetic Data Generation:** The ability to generate diverse and controllable HOI scenarios offers a valuable tool for creating synthetic training data for robots. This can aid in developing more robust robotic perception, manipulation, and interaction capabilities by exposing models to a wide range of human-object dynamics.
- **Advancement in Controllable Video Generation:** The introduction of HOI-aware motion representations and the demonstration of the sparse-to-dense paradigm establish a new benchmark for fine-grained control in video generation, particularly for human-centric tasks.

Despite its effectiveness, the research acknowledges certain limitations. The quality of the generated videos remains influenced by the capabilities of the underlying video diffusion backbone, potentially leading to occasional imperfections in identity preservation, physical implausibility, or 3D awareness. Furthermore, the reliance on auxiliary segmentation and tracking models for deriving control signals during training means that inaccuracies from these upstream components can propagate and affect the augmentor's predictions.

However, the model-agnostic nature of VHOI's sparse-to-dense control design and its HOI-aware motion representation position it for future integration with more powerful video generators and perception models. This suggests a pathway for continued improvements in HOI fidelity and controllability as foundational models evolve.
