# C-Drag: Chain-of-Thought Driven Motion Controller for Video Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> C-Drag presents a training-free framework that employs Vision-Language Models (VLMs) with Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning to generate videos featuring realistic multi-object interactions. The approach accurately infers the dynamic trajectories of all affected objects from a single controlled object's movement, achieving a 35.5% improvement in Moving Object Consistency (MOC) over prior methods.

## Problem

- Existing trajectory-based video generation methods primarily focus on single-object motion control, often failing to account for dynamic interactions between multiple objects and leading to unrealistic video content.
- Previous attempts to incorporate physics, such as PhysGen, struggled with deformable objects, accurate parameter estimation, and were limited to specific motion types, lacking generalization for diverse and complex scenarios.
- There was a lack of suitable benchmark datasets and quantitative metrics specifically designed to evaluate the realism and accuracy of multi-object motion trajectories in generated videos.

## Method

- **C-Drag** introduces a training-free, plug-and-play framework comprising an object perception module, a ==CoT==-based motion reasoning module, and integration with a pre-trained trajectory-based video generation diffusion model.
- The CoT-based motion reasoning module utilizes a ==VLM== and stage-wise text prompts to simulate human-like reasoning, predicting motion trajectories for all interacting objects based on an initial image and the specified trajectory of one controlled object.
- A new Video Object Interaction (VOI) dataset with ground-truth bounding boxes and trajectories for all moving objects, and a novel Moving Object Consistency (MOC) metric, were developed to facilitate robust evaluation.

## Results

- C-Drag achieved superior quantitative performance on the VOI dataset, with an FVD score of **771.83**, FID of **95.87**, and an MOC score of **46.47**, outperforming baseline methods like DragNUWA (MOC **72.09**, a 35.5% improvement).
- The method demonstrated consistent outperformance across all three interaction categories in the VOI dataset: Collision and Chain Reaction, Gravity and Forces, and Levers and Mirrors.
- Qualitative results showed C-Drag generates more temporally consistent and physically plausible videos, accurately depicting multi-object interactions such as seesaw dynamics, coordinated bird motion, and mirror reflections, without the deformation or unrealistic movements observed in other methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Vision-Language Models combined with Chain-of-Thought reasoning can be effectively leveraged in a generative context to infer and predict complex multi-object dynamic interactions from an initial image and a single controlled trajectory.
> - A structured, multi-stage reasoning process (scene understanding, relationship inference, interaction prediction, iterative refinement, validation) significantly enhances the accuracy and plausibility of predicted trajectories for all interacting objects.
> - Accurate and detailed object perception, including class-agnostic segmentation and open-set detection, is fundamental for providing the precise input necessary for robust multi-object interaction reasoning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of controllable video generation has experienced substantial advancements, primarily driven by innovations in diffusion models. Current methodologies are broadly categorized into text-based, image-conditioned, and trajectory-based approaches. Text-based methods generate videos from textual descriptions, while image-conditioned techniques produce videos consistent with a given image's style or content. While these methods offer forms of control, they frequently lack the precision required for explicit manipulation of object motion across frames.

Trajectory-based approaches have emerged to address this limitation by focusing on building object motion sequences. However, existing trajectory-based methods often face challenges in complex scenarios involving multiple interacting objects. They typically concentrate on generating the motion trajectory of a single, explicitly controlled object, largely overlooking the dynamic interactions between this object and its surrounding environment or other objects in the scene. Consequently, these methods may produce videos that lack physical realism and coherence when interactions such as collisions, gravitational effects, or reflections are central to the scene's dynamics.

Previous attempts to incorporate physics, such as PhysGen, leverage Large Language Models (LLMs) to infer physical parameters and employ physics simulators. However, these methods often struggle with deformable objects, accurate estimation of physical parameters solely from visual input, and their generalization capabilities are limited to specific, often planar, motion types, resulting in suboptimal outcomes in diverse and complex scenarios.

More recently, Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting have demonstrated strong visual understanding and reasoning abilities in language understanding and reasoning tasks, including video question answering and grounding. While Video-of-Thought explores CoT for fine-grained spatial-temporal video grounding, its application has been primarily in understanding existing video content rather than proactively generating new video with precise, reasoned multi-object motion. The application of CoT-based reasoning specifically tailored for controllable video generation, particularly to infer future object trajectories from a single initial frame and a controlled object's movement, has remained largely unexplored.

C-Drag positions itself by directly addressing the limitations of existing trajectory-based methods regarding multi-object dynamic interactions. It integrates VLM and CoT reasoning to emulate human cognitive processes for motion prediction, aiming to generate motion trajectories for *all* affected objects based on the input trajectory of a single controlled object. This approach seeks to bridge the gap between precise single-object trajectory control and the generation of videos featuring realistic, common-sense driven multi-object interactions, without requiring a high-precision physics engine or extensive model retraining.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel motion controller, termed C-Drag, for controllable video generation that can accurately reason about and synthesize the dynamic interactions between multiple objects in a scene. This aims to overcome the limitations of existing trajectory-based methods, which predominantly focus on single-object motion control and often neglect the complex interplay between objects and their environment.

The motivation for C-Drag stems from several observations and identified gaps in the current state of controllable video generation:

- **Limited Realism in Multi-Object Scenes:** While trajectory-based video generation offers intuitive control, it frequently struggles with maintaining visual and logical coherence in scenes involving multiple interacting objects. Current methods often only generate the trajectory of a pre-defined controlled object, ignoring how its movement impacts other objects (e.g., a ball hitting another ball, an object influencing a seesaw). This limitation restricts their application in scenarios demanding high precision and realism.
- **Human-like Motion Reasoning:** Human cognitive patterns for understanding and predicting motion, especially in multi-object scenarios, involve two key elements: accurate object perception (identifying and locating all relevant objects) and robust cognitive ability (understanding relationships, applying common sense knowledge of physical dynamics like collisions, gravity, or reflections). Existing computational models often lack this multi-stage, human-like reasoning process. The authors argue that predicting the trajectories of all objects in a scene typically involves stages such as object identification, relationship comprehension, inferring interactions based on a specified object's movement, and ensuring coherence with real-world principles.
- **Underutilization of VLM and CoT Capabilities for Generation:** Vision-Language Models (VLMs) combined with Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in visual understanding and complex reasoning tasks by decomposing problems into manageable sub-problems. However, this powerful reasoning framework has not been extensively applied to the *generative* task of predicting multi-object motion trajectories for video synthesis. Specifically, there is a gap in leveraging CoT to infer the future trajectories of affected objects as a regression problem, based on an initial image and the trajectory of a controlled object.
- **Lack of Suitable Benchmarks and Metrics:** Current evaluation metrics like Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) and Fréchet Video Distance (FVD) primarily assess visual quality and temporal consistency but do not specifically measure the realism or accuracy of object motion trajectories, particularly in multi-object interaction scenarios. While some metrics like ObjMC exist for controlled objects, a comprehensive metric for *all* moving objects in interaction scenes is needed.

To address these motivations, C-Drag sets out to achieve the following specific objectives:
1. **Develop an Object Perception Module:** This module is designed to accurately identify and extract detailed information (position, category, segmentation masks) for all relevant objects within an input image, serving as the foundational perceptual input for reasoning.
2. **Propose a CoT-based Motion Reasoning Module:** This central component is tasked with performing stage-wise reasoning, guided by VLM and CoT principles, to predict the precise motion trajectories of not only the initially controlled object but also all other objects dynamically affected by it. This reasoning is intended to account for various interaction types (e.g., collision, gravity, leverage).
3. **Integrate with Diffusion Models:** The reasoned trajectories are to be seamlessly integrated into existing pre-trained trajectory-based video generation diffusion models to synthesize high-quality videos that exhibit realistic multi-object interactions.
4. **Create a Video Object Interaction (VOI) Dataset:** To facilitate rigorous evaluation, a new benchmark dataset is to be constructed, featuring diverse videos with various object interaction types, accompanied by ground-truth bounding boxes and trajectories for all moving objects.
5. **Introduce Moving Object Consistency (MOC) Metric:** A novel quantitative metric, MOC, is to be proposed to specifically measure the plausibility and realism of predicted object motions by comparing generated trajectories against ground-truth data for all interacting objects.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The C-Drag approach is a training-free, plug-and-play framework designed to generate controllable videos with dynamic multi-object interactions. It takes a single RGB image and one or more user-defined drag motion trajectories as input and processes them through three main components: an object perception module, a Chain-of-Thought (CoT)-based motion reasoning module, and a pre-trained trajectory-based video generation model.

**Overall Architecture:**
1. **Object Perception Module (OPM):** Extracts detailed information about all objects in the input image.
2. **CoT-based Motion Reasoning Module:** Predicts the motion trajectories of all interacting objects based on the perceived scene and a given controlled object's trajectory.
3. **Trajectory-based Video Generation Module:** Utilizes the reasoned trajectories to synthesize the final video using an existing diffusion model.

**1. Object Perception Module (OPM):**
The OPM is responsible for acquiring comprehensive information about all objects within the initial input image. Given an input image I and the beginning point P_b of a motion trajectory for the controlled object, the module performs the following steps:
- **Class-Agnostic Segmentation:** It first employs a class-agnostic segmentation model, specifically the Segment Anything Model (SAM), to generate a precise mask M_c for the controlled object corresponding to P_b.
- **Open-Set Object Detection:** Next, the controlled object's mask M_c and a predefined text prompt T are fed into a Vision-Language Model (VLM) to detect all other objects in the image. This step yields a preliminary set of bounding boxes B_i and associated object categories C_i for N objects.
- **Bounding Box and Mask Refinement:** An open-set detection model, GroundingDINO, is then used to refine the detected bounding boxes and generate fine-grained segmentation masks M_f_i for all identified objects.
- **Output:** The OPM outputs a final set of information for all objects `O_final = {(B_f_i, C_i, M_f_i)}_N_i=1`, where `B_f_i` denotes the refined bounding box coordinates, `C_i` is the object category, and `M_f_i` is the fine-grained segmentation mask for each object `i`.

**2. CoT-based Motion Reasoning Module:**
This module is designed to predict the motion trajectories of all objects (the controlled object and any objects it interacts with) by simulating human-like, stage-wise reasoning. It uses a VLM and carefully crafted text prompts at each stage to guide the reasoning process.
- **Stage 1: Scene and Object Understanding:** The VLM is prompted with the formatted object information from the OPM to identify the scene type (e.g., "playground scene") and relevant real-world rules or forces (e.g., "gravity and lever principle") affecting objects in that scene. This establishes the physical context.
- **Stage 2: Reasoning Object Relationships:** The VLM analyzes the spatial arrangement, sizes, and types of objects, as well as the rules identified in Stage 1, to infer relationships between objects (e.g., "balancing on opposite ends of a seesaw") and potential interaction possibilities. This stage helps define how objects might influence each other.
- **Stage 3: Interaction Trajectories Reasoning:** Based on the input motion trajectory of the controlled object and the contextual understanding from previous stages, the VLM identifies the type of interaction (e.g., collision, gravitational, leverage). It then predicts how other objects, affected by the controlled object, will move, aligning with identified real-world rules.
- **Stage 4: Iterative Reasoning and Ranking:** This stage refines the initial trajectory predictions. The model repeatedly performs reasoning similar to Stages 2 and 3. For each iteration's output, it generates a set of object coordinates for each frame and assigns a rationality score (1-10) based on common sense. The VLM then ranks these outputs and selects the most plausible sequence of motion and interaction responses.
- **Stage 5: Validation and Final Reasoning Outcome:** A two-part validation process is applied. "Forward Check" verifies if the predicted trajectories adhere to the established scene rules and context. "Backward Check" involves reverse-engineering the input trajectory from the generated object motions and comparing it against the actual input. Discrepancies trigger re-iterations and adjustments to the reasoning until a consistent and accurate final set of trajectories is obtained.

**3. Trajectory-based Video Generation Module:**
The output from the CoT-based motion reasoning module, which comprises the predicted motion trajectories for all interacting objects, is then fed into a pre-trained trajectory-based video generation diffusion model. The authors specifically use DragNUWA for their experiments, which synthesizes the final video incorporating the reasoned multi-object interactions. The C-Drag framework operates without requiring any re-training of this underlying video generation model.

**The VOI Dataset:**
To enable robust evaluation of multi-object interaction video generation, the authors constructed a new Video Object Interaction (VOI) dataset.
- **Data Collection:** It comprises 72 videos, primarily collected from existing datasets like LaSOT and CADP.
- **Categories:** The dataset is divided into three interaction types:
 - **Collision and Chain Reaction:** Scenes demonstrating objects colliding and triggering subsequent reactions (e.g., billiard balls, Newton's cradle, traffic).
 - **Gravity and Forces:** Scenes where objects move under gravity and external forces (e.g., basketball, football).
 - **Levers and Mirrors:** Scenes featuring coupled systems like seesaws or mirror reflections.
- **Annotations:** For quantitative evaluation, the dataset includes bounding-box and ground-truth trajectory annotations for *all* relevant objects across frames. Bounding boxes were either sourced from original datasets or generated using GroundingDINO. Object center point trajectories were tracked using CoTracker and then manually verified for accuracy. The dataset contains 711 annotated trajectories across 72 videos.

**Evaluation Metric (Moving Object Consistency - MOC):**
Recognizing the limitations of standard video generation metrics (FID, FVD) in assessing motion plausibility, the authors introduce a new metric, Moving Object Consistency (MOC).
- **Definition:** MOC measures the similarity between predicted and ground-truth trajectories for *all* moving objects within a video, not just the controlled one.
- **Calculation:** It is computed as the Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) between the predicted `(x_p, y_p)` and ground-truth `(x_gt, y_gt)` object positions across all frames and all moving objects in the dataset. A lower MOC score indicates higher consistency and more accurate motion generation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of C-Drag was conducted on the newly introduced Video Object Interaction (VOI) dataset and compared against existing trajectory-based video generation methods.

**Quantitative Comparison with Existing Methods:**
C-Drag demonstrated superior performance across various metrics on the entire VOI dataset (Table 2) and its subsets (Table 3):
- **Video Quality (FVD):** C-Drag achieved an FVD score of 771.83, significantly outperforming PhysGen (1795.87), DragAnything (1295.94), and DragNUWA (1034.89). This indicates that C-Drag generates videos with higher temporal consistency and overall quality.
- **Image Quality (FID):** C-Drag reported an FID score of 95.87, which was lower than PhysGen (129.30), DragAnything (129.07), and DragNUWA (102.80), signifying an improvement in the visual quality of individual frames.
- **Motion Consistency (MOC):** C-Drag achieved an MOC score of 46.47, a substantial improvement compared to DragAnything (71.50) and DragNUWA (72.09). This indicates that C-Drag is more effective at generating accurate and consistent motion trajectories for *all* moving objects in a scene, reflecting a 35.5% improvement over DragNUWA in this specific metric.
- **Subset Performance:** The method consistently outperformed existing approaches across all three VOI subsets: Collision and Chain Reaction, Gravity and Force, and Levers and Mirrors, demonstrating its robustness and generalization across different types of physical interactions (Table 3).

**Qualitative Comparison:**
Figure 5 presents a visual comparison illustrating the qualitative advantages of C-Drag:
- **PhysGen:** Showed limitations in handling deformable objects and non-planar scenarios, often requiring manual parameter tuning and resulting in unrealistic movements, such as a character falling off a seesaw due to incorrect interaction modeling.
- **DragAnything and DragNUWA:** Exhibited challenges in maintaining temporal consistency for uncontrolled objects. Examples included merged birds in crowded scenes and severely deformed mirror-reflected objects, suggesting their primary focus on the trajectory of the directly controlled object without sufficient reasoning about secondary interactions.
- **C-Drag (Ours):** Demonstrated the ability to accurately perceive and infer the movements of all objects involved in interactions while maintaining temporal consistency for all elements. This was evident in scenarios like coordinated bird motion in crowded environments, realistic seesaw dynamics under gravity, and coherent mirror reflections of moving objects.

**Ablation Studies:**
The research included ablation studies to evaluate the contribution of C-Drag's components:
- **Effect of Different Modules (Table 4):**
 - Integrating only the CoT-based motion reasoning module into the DragNUWA baseline improved FVD by 79.28, FID by 2.31, and MOC by 4.96.
 - The synergistic integration of both the Object Perception Module (OPM) and the CoT-based motion reasoning module yielded further significant improvements, achieving FVD 771.83, FID 95.87, and MOC 46.47, reinforcing the value of both components.
- **Components of Object Perception (Table 5):**
 - Using only a VLM for object perception provided initial improvements over the baseline.
 - The addition of an open-set detector further enhanced performance.
 - The integration of a class-agnostic segmentation component alongside the VLM and open-set detection achieved the best results for object perception, confirming that detailed and accurate object information is crucial.
- **Stages of CoT-based Motion Reasoning (Table 6):**
 - Each of the five stages in the CoT-based reasoning module (Scene and Object Understanding, Reasoning Object Relationships, Interaction Trajectories Reasoning, Iterative Reasoning and Ranking, Validation and Final Reasoning Outcome) incrementally contributed to improved results.
 - Employing all five stages achieved the optimal performance, demonstrating that the structured, multi-stage reasoning process is effective in enhancing motion prediction accuracy and realism.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The C-Drag framework represents a development in controllable video generation by directly addressing a core limitation of previous trajectory-based methods: the accurate simulation of dynamic multi-object interactions. Its significance and potential impact span several areas:

- **Enhanced Realism and Plausibility:** C-Drag moves beyond simply controlling a single object's path to reasoning about and generating the resulting motions of *all* affected objects based on common-sense physical principles. This capability can lead to the creation of more physically plausible and visually realistic videos, reducing common artifacts such as objects merging, incorrect collision responses, or unrealistic gravitational effects seen in prior work.
- **Intuitive and Comprehensive Control:** By allowing users to specify a trajectory for a single object and then having the system automatically infer and generate the trajectories for all interacting elements, C-Drag offers a more intuitive and powerful form of video control. This abstracts away the complexity of manually defining multiple object paths, making the video generation process more accessible and efficient for users aiming for complex scene dynamics.
- **Novel Application of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and Chain-of-Thought (CoT):** This research demonstrates a novel and effective application of VLM and CoT reasoning in a generative context. While these techniques have been widely successful in understanding and analytical tasks, C-Drag showcases their potential to proactively predict and generate complex dynamic behaviors, extending their utility in creative AI applications beyond content analysis. The training-free nature of the reasoning module further highlights its adaptability and efficiency.
- **Foundational Benchmark and Metric:** The introduction of the Video Object Interaction (VOI) dataset and the Moving Object Consistency (MOC) metric provides a valuable standardized resource for the research community. VOI addresses the previous lack of datasets specifically designed for evaluating multi-object interaction in video generation, while MOC offers a more pertinent quantitative measure of motion realism and accuracy than general video quality metrics. These contributions can accelerate future research and enable more rigorous comparisons in this specialized domain.
- **Potential for Broader Applications:** The capabilities of C-Drag could have implications for various industries:
 - **Content Creation:** Filmmakers, animators, and game developers could leverage C-Drag for rapid prototyping and generating complex interactive scenes, improving efficiency in production workflows.
 - **Simulation and Robotics:** Generating realistic interaction scenarios could enhance training environments for autonomous systems and robotics, where understanding and predicting physical interactions are critical.
 - **Education and Visualization:** It could be used to create educational content that visually explains complex physical phenomena with accurate object dynamics.

Despite its advancements, the authors acknowledge that C-Drag may occasionally struggle with highly complex trajectories involving numerous direction changes. Future research directions suggested include exploring the integration of more sophisticated temporal modeling capabilities to better capture the intricate temporal dynamics of such complex trajectories, which could further enhance the system's robustness and realism.
