# C-Drag: Chain-of-Thought Driven Motion Controller for Video Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> C-Drag presents a training-free framework that employs Vision-Language Models (VLMs) with Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning to generate videos featuring realistic multi-object interactions. The approach accurately infers the dynamic trajectories of all affected objects from a single controlled object's movement, achieving a 35.5% improvement in Moving Object Consistency (MOC) over prior methods.

## Problem

- Existing trajectory-based video generation methods primarily focus on single-object motion control, often failing to account for dynamic interactions between multiple objects and leading to unrealistic video content.
- Previous attempts to incorporate physics, such as PhysGen, struggled with deformable objects, accurate parameter estimation, and were limited to specific motion types, lacking generalization for diverse and complex scenarios.
- There was a lack of suitable benchmark datasets and quantitative metrics specifically designed to evaluate the realism and accuracy of multi-object motion trajectories in generated videos.

## Method

- **C-Drag** introduces a training-free, plug-and-play framework comprising an object perception module, a ==CoT==-based motion reasoning module, and integration with a pre-trained trajectory-based video generation diffusion model.
- The CoT-based motion reasoning module utilizes a ==VLM== and stage-wise text prompts to simulate human-like reasoning, predicting motion trajectories for all interacting objects based on an initial image and the specified trajectory of one controlled object.
- A new Video Object Interaction (VOI) dataset with ground-truth bounding boxes and trajectories for all moving objects, and a novel Moving Object Consistency (MOC) metric, were developed to facilitate robust evaluation.

## Results

- C-Drag achieved superior quantitative performance on the VOI dataset, with an FVD score of **771.83**, FID of **95.87**, and an MOC score of **46.47**, outperforming baseline methods like DragNUWA (MOC **72.09**, a 35.5% improvement).
- The method demonstrated consistent outperformance across all three interaction categories in the VOI dataset: Collision and Chain Reaction, Gravity and Forces, and Levers and Mirrors.
- Qualitative results showed C-Drag generates more temporally consistent and physically plausible videos, accurately depicting multi-object interactions such as seesaw dynamics, coordinated bird motion, and mirror reflections, without the deformation or unrealistic movements observed in other methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Vision-Language Models combined with Chain-of-Thought reasoning can be effectively leveraged in a generative context to infer and predict complex multi-object dynamic interactions from an initial image and a single controlled trajectory.
> - A structured, multi-stage reasoning process (scene understanding, relationship inference, interaction prediction, iterative refinement, validation) significantly enhances the accuracy and plausibility of predicted trajectories for all interacting objects.
> - Accurate and detailed object perception, including class-agnostic segmentation and open-set detection, is fundamental for providing the precise input necessary for robust multi-object interaction reasoning.
