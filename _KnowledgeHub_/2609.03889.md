# FWBC-VLA: Force-Aware Whole-Body Compensation for Contact-Rich Loco-Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> FWBC-VLA is a force-aware framework for contact-rich loco-manipulation on wheeled-legged quadrupeds. HSR-Force, a dual-LSTM sensorless residual-torque estimator, infers contact strength and its trend from proprioception; these become force tokens cross-attended into a fine-tuned pi-0.5 action expert, and Jacobian-projected loads drive a bounded whole-body base-compensation residual. Real whiteboard wiping and door opening reach 64% and 52% final success.

## Problem

- Existing VLA models generate task-level actions from vision and language but cannot interpret the physical interaction forces those actions induce during contact.
- Whole-body controllers stabilize the robot yet cannot separate task-relevant interaction forces from external disturbances, exchanging only kinematic commands with the manipulation policy.
- Contact-aware policies rely on dedicated force/torque sensors that are arm-centric, ignore body-level effects, and are costly to retrofit onto existing platforms.

## Method

- **HSR-Force**: dual 200 Hz ==LSTM== experts (torque-motion history plus current multi-body state) fused by a fixed residual gate estimate free-motion torque, leaving directional six-channel external residuals.
- A two-channel contact descriptor (residual magnitude and its temporal delta) over 13 causal samples is ==GRU==-encoded into force tokens, ==cross-attended== into the ==pi-0.5== action expert's final hidden states with a learned presence gate.
- A **compensation generator** ==MLP== maps Jacobian-projected body-frame load, IMU posture deviation, and nominal base command to a tanh-bounded base-velocity residual added before the whole-body controller.

## Results

- HSR-Force reaches **0.15 N** zero-load force MAE and **0.97** touch AUC, beating NEXT (**0.28 N**, **0.95**), GMO-SI (**0.43 N**, **0.94**), and DF-MLP (**0.21 N**, **0.47**).
- Final-stage success is **64%** on whiteboard wiping and **52%** on door opening, **40** points above ForceVLA on both, and within **0-4** points of the ground-truth-force FWBC-GT variant.
- Ablation: force interface lifts average contact-stage success **12.0%** to **35.0%**; adding bounded compensation reaches **65%**, with the largest gain (**+52** points) on pushing a door with a closer.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Proprioceptive residual torque can substitute for force/torque hardware when the policy needs relative contact trend rather than a calibrated absolute wrench.
> - Fusing a history expert with a history-free state expert trades noise suppression against onset sensitivity; the gate keeps sustained contact from being absorbed as ordinary dynamics.
> - Contact awareness in the action head is insufficient alone: sustained high-load stages fail without an explicit body-level compensation channel feeding the controller.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic loco-manipulation, particularly for complex, contact-rich tasks, presents significant challenges in integrating high-level semantic action generation with low-level physical interaction control. Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have emerged as a promising approach for unifying perception, language understanding, and action generation, demonstrating capabilities from fixed-base manipulation to whole-body loco-manipulation. However, current VLA paradigms often operate primarily on kinematic commands, lacking explicit awareness of physical interactions. They may struggle to interpret the forces induced by their actions, leading to instability or task failure in contact-rich environments.

Concurrently, Whole-Body Control (WBC) policies are designed to stabilize robots during motion and interaction. Yet, a limitation of conventional WBC is its inability to distinguish between task-relevant interaction forces and forces arising from external disturbances. This lack of context can hinder effective reactive control during manipulation.

Some previous research has explored incorporating force/torque (F/T) sensor feedback into VLA models, demonstrating improvements in stability and precision for contact-rich manipulation. However, these methods typically focus on arm-centric control and often overlook the whole-body effects of manipulation forces. Furthermore, they rely on dedicated F/T sensors, which are not universally available on robotic platforms and can entail significant additional hardware costs and integration complexities. Sensorless force estimation methods have also been developed, aiming to infer external forces from proprioceptive signals, but their accuracy during dynamic whole-body motions with complex interactions remains a challenge.

This work addresses the gap between high-level VLA action generation and low-level physical control in contact-rich loco-manipulation, particularly for wheeled-legged robots. It aims to bridge this divide by introducing a sensorless, force-aware feedback mechanism that informs both the VLA policy and the WBC system. By providing explicit interaction awareness without requiring additional hardware, FWBC-VLA seeks to enhance the robot's ability to perform tasks involving sustained physical contact, coordinating manipulation actions with whole-body stabilization.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations for developing FWBC-VLA are centered on overcoming limitations in existing robotic control paradigms for contact-rich loco-manipulation, especially for wheeled-legged robots.

1. **Addressing the Gap in VLA Models for Physical Interaction**: Existing VLA models excel at generating task-level actions from visual and linguistic inputs but often do not explicitly interpret or react to the physical interactions (contact onset, sustained loading, release) induced by these actions. This absence of explicit physical feedback limits their effectiveness and robustness in tasks requiring precise force application or sustained contact. The motivation is to imbue VLA policies with direct, causal awareness of physical interactions to enable more nuanced and robust loco-manipulation.

2. **Enhancing Whole-Body Control with Contextual Force Information**: While WBC policies are crucial for maintaining robot stability, they typically do not differentiate between desired task-relevant interaction forces and unexpected external disturbances. This can lead to suboptimal or counterproductive compensatory movements. The objective is to provide the WBC policy with contextualized force estimates that distinguish between interaction loads and disturbances, allowing for more appropriate and task-aware body stabilization.

3. **Eliminating Reliance on Dedicated Force/Torque Sensors**: Many robotic platforms lack integrated F/T sensors, and retrofitting them is often costly, mechanically complex, and requires substantial calibration and integration effort. This hardware dependency restricts the applicability of force-aware control methods. A core motivation is to develop a sensorless approach that can infer physical interactions from the robot's existing proprioceptive signals (e.g., joint torques, encoder readings, IMU data). This would enable broader deployment of force-aware capabilities without hardware modifications.

4. **Enabling Robust Contact-Rich Loco-Manipulation for Wheeled-Legged Robots**: Wheeled-legged quadrupeds offer advantages in stability and terrain traversability for mobile manipulation, yet contact-rich tasks on these platforms are particularly challenging due to the propagation of end-effector forces throughout the robot's body. The objective is to provide a framework that allows these robots to execute contact-rich tasks (such as wiping or opening heavy doors) reliably by coordinating arm manipulation with dynamic whole-body stabilization, informed by explicit interaction feedback.

In summary, the key motivation is to create a comprehensive framework that integrates sensorless physical interaction awareness into both the high-level VLA action generation and low-level whole-body control of wheeled-legged robots, thereby enhancing their performance and robustness in contact-rich loco-manipulation tasks without requiring additional force-sensing hardware.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The FWBC-VLA framework integrates sensorless interaction estimation, interaction-conditioned VLA action generation, and whole-body compensation for wheeled-legged robots.

#### 3.1. WL&Arm Dataset

To train the VLA backbone and compensation module for wheeled-legged quadruped loco-manipulation, the researchers developed the WL&Arm Dataset.
- **Data Collection Method**: A Pico-based hybrid position-force teleoperation system was designed. This system allows operators to specify both tangential and normal end-effector (EE) force commands, which are logged as "force-intent" annotations. These annotations are used for dataset metadata and inspection but not directly as inputs for the estimator or VLA policy to prevent command-intent leakage.
- **Addressing Embodiment Mismatch**: To reduce the disparity between the target wheeled-legged robot and embodiments used to pretrain VLA models, the action outputs were defined as base velocity and steering angle, rather than joint-specific commands. This decouples VLA action prediction from the low-level wheel-leg controller while maintaining joint-level control execution.
- **Dataset Statistics**: The WL&Arm Dataset comprises over 5,000 teleoperation episodes. It includes 200 Hz joint data for contact estimation and 15 Hz loco-manipulation demonstrations. The principal task subsets are bottle pick-and-place (41%), whiteboard wiping (25%), and door opening (21%). Force calibration data (0.36 kg and 0.72 kg applied loads) were included for estimator training and evaluation.

#### 3.2. Sensorless Interaction Estimation (HSR-Force)

The core of the sensorless feedback mechanism is the History-State Residual Force (HSR-Force) Estimator, which operates at 200 Hz.
- **Torque Decomposition**: The measured arm-joint torque (τ_meas,t) is decomposed into torque from robot motion and internal dynamics (τ_free,t), environment-induced interaction residuals (τ_ext,t), and unmodeled effects (ϵ_t). The estimator focuses on predicting τ_free,t.
- **Dual Expert Network**: HSR-Force employs two compact LSTM networks:
 - **History Expert**: Uses a causal history of torque and motion to predict τ_free,hist,t, aiming to suppress free-motion prediction noise.
 - **State Expert**: Uses the current arm, base, leg, and IMU state (without torque history) to predict τ_free,state,t, which helps prevent sustained contact from being mistakenly learned as ordinary dynamics.
- **Fixed-Gate Fusion**: A fixed gate (α_t) dynamically combines the outputs of the two experts. This gate's value depends on the magnitude of the state residual (r_state,t = τ_meas,t - τ_free,state,t). When r_state,t is small (free motion), α_t approaches 0, favoring the history expert. When r_state,t is large (interaction), α_t approaches 1, favoring the state expert.
- **Residual Torque Output**: The raw estimated external joint torque (τ_ext,raw,t) is computed as the difference between the measured torque and the combined free-motion estimate (τ_meas,t - τ_free,t). All six residual channels are retained to preserve directional information for downstream processing.
- **Training**: The free-torque experts are trained on free-motion windows where τ_ext,t ≈ 0. Frames with sustained interaction, indicated by phase annotations, are masked during this training to ensure accurate learning of free-motion dynamics.

#### 3.3. Causal Physical Interaction Representation

The raw six-joint residual torque is summarized into a compact, causal representation for downstream use.
- **Contact Descriptor (d_int,t)**: This two-channel descriptor captures interaction strength and its temporal trend:
 - s_t: The L2 norm of the raw external torque estimate (∥τ_ext,raw,t∥_2), representing interaction strength.
 - ∆s_t: The temporal difference of s_t (s_t - s_t-1), indicating whether the interaction is increasing or decreasing.
- **Jacobian Projection**: For directional compensation, the estimated joint-torque residual is mapped to an equivalent six-dimensional interaction wrench at the end-effector (E_w_E,t) and re-expressed about the body origin (B_w_B,t) using damped least-squares Jacobian inversion. These wrenches provide directional and relative-load features.

#### 3.4. Interaction-Conditioned Action Generation

The VLA policy is made force-aware by incorporating the interaction representation.
- **Force Token Encoding**: A short history (K=13 samples, spanning 60 ms at 200 Hz) of the contact descriptors (d_int,t) is collected. A normalization layer followed by a GRU temporal encoder processes this history to produce "force tokens" (Z_int,t).
- **Late Fusion in VLA Action Expert**: The VLA policy is based on a pretrained π_0.5 backbone, which is fine-tuned on the WL&Arm dataset. Force tokens (Z_force,t) are fused into the action expert's final hidden states via cross-attention. A learned force-presence gate (g_t) and scaling factor (α_f) modulate this fusion. This mechanism allows the VLA policy to perceive force evolution and adapt its loco-manipulation action generation (arm, gripper, nominal base actions) accordingly, without propagating force information through the visual backbone.
- **Causal Synchronization**: Force tokens are generated using interaction measurements whose timestamps are not later than the current VLA policy query timestamp, ensuring causal deployment.

#### 3.5. Whole-Body Compensation Generation

A second pathway leverages the physical interaction interface to provide whole-body compensation, operating outside the VLA action expert.
- **Input to Compensation Generator**: This module receives the projected body-frame load proxy (F_body,t), IMU orientation and angular rate (∆η_imu,t, representing posture deviation from a free-motion reference), and the nominal base action (u_base,nom,t) from the VLA.
- **Residual Velocity Prediction**: A compact multilayer perceptron (MLP) predicts an unconstrained residual proposal (r_base,t) for planar velocity and yaw rate (∆v_x, ∆v_y, ∆ω_z).
- **Training and Bounding**: The compensation generator is trained with an ℓ_1 regression objective, where the target residual is the difference between the executed force-supported base command and the nominal base command. The final residual is bounded and passed through command shaping (deadband, low-pass filtering, slew-rate limits, clipping) before being added to the nominal base command for execution by the WBC. Stale or invalid signals reset the residual to zero. This compensation adjusts the robot's base motion to maintain stability under sustained external forces, differentiating between task-relevant loads and general disturbances.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were designed to evaluate the HSR-Force estimator's accuracy, the framework's performance in real-world contact-rich loco-manipulation, and the contribution of its individual components. Experiments were conducted on a DeepRobotics M20S wheeled-legged quadruped with a CM1 6-DoF arm and 1-DoF gripper, using RealSense D435i cameras.

#### 4.1. Force-Estimator Evaluation (Q1, Q2)

**Metrics**: Normalized Mean Absolute Error (N-MAE) for scalar force (Newtons), torque residual errors (All-free τ MAE, τ R90 S/D in N m), and Area Under the Receiver Operating Characteristic Curve (AUC) for contact detection (Touch AUC, Door-phase AUC).

**Q1: How accurately can HSR-Force estimate external interaction?**
- **Overall Performance**: HSR-Force achieved the lowest zero-load force error (0.15 N N-MAE) and the highest Touch AUC (0.97). It also exhibited low all-free torque MAE (0.37 N m) and competitive static/dynamic tail residuals (τ R90 S/D: 0.64/2.79 N m).
- **Comparison to Baselines**:
 - HSR-Force outperformed learned estimators like NEXT (0.28 N) and DF-MLP (0.21 N) in zero-load force error and demonstrated higher Touch AUC than NEXT (0.95), GMO-SI (0.94), and DF-MLP (0.47).
 - On known-load experiments, HSR-Force achieved the lowest frame Force MAE (1.09/1.21 N for static/dynamic) and lowest dynamic error among baselines, while GMO-SI showed the lowest static error.
- **Temporal Response**: Video-aligned results indicated that HSR-Force responded promptly near contact onset and remained active during sustained door loading, suggesting effective temporal tracking of interactions.

**Q2: How do the state and history heads and their fixed-gate fusion affect estimation performance?**
- **Ablation Study**:
 - **State only**: Higher zero-load force error (1.13 N) and torque residual errors, lower AUCs (0.92 Touch AUC).
 - **History only**: Improved zero-load error (0.93 N) and torque residuals compared to state-only, but still higher than the full model. Lower Touch AUC (0.87).
 - **Dual&Gate (Full HSR-Force)**: The fixed-gate fusion of both experts resulted in the lowest zero-load force error (0.25 N in ablation), competitive torque residuals (1.23/2.96 N m τ R90 S/D), and the best contact discrimination AUCs (0.94 Touch AUC, 0.83 Door-phase AUC) within the ablation studies. This demonstrated that the history expert reduces free-motion noise, the state expert improves sensitivity to contact onset, and their fixed-gate fusion provides robust performance across different conditions.

#### 4.2. Experiments in the Real World (Q3)

**Tasks**: Whiteboard wiping and door opening (with and without a door closer, requiring ~50N push force).
**Metrics**: Stage-wise success rates (%).
**Baselines**: OpenVLA, StarVLA, π_0.5, Gr00t N1.6 (without force feedback), ACP, ForceVLA, and FWBC-GT (with real F/T sensor ground truth).

**Q3: How does FWBC-VLA perform in real-world contact-rich loco-manipulation tasks, and what advantages does force-aware control offer?**
- **Whiteboard Wiping**: FWBC-VLA achieved the highest success rate at every stage, including 76% at the Wiping stage and 64% final success. This surpassed the strongest baselines (ACP: 44%, ForceVLA: 36% at Wiping) by significant margins (32 and 40 percentage points, respectively).
- **Door Opening**: FWBC-VLA also demonstrated superior performance, achieving 56% at Manipulate and 52% at both Push and Cross stages. It outperformed ForceVLA (24% at Manipulate, 12% at Push/Cross) by 28 and 40 percentage points.
- **Comparison to Ground Truth**: The success rates of FWBC-VLA were very close to those of FWBC-GT (using real F/T sensor data), indicating that the sensorless estimation is effective for contact-rich tasks.
- **Advantages of Force-Aware Control**: The results suggest that explicit interaction feedback enables better task progression and prevents failures observed in baselines (e.g., uneven contact or body drift during wiping), particularly in tasks involving sustained contact.

#### 4.3. Ablation of Force-Conditioned Compensation (Q4)

**Ablation Setup**: Progressively introduced components: Force-Aware Interface (FI – HSR-Force + force-token conditioning) and Bounded Body Compensation (BC – projected body-load proxy to base corrections).

**Q4: Which components contribute most significantly to the performance improvements of FWBC-VLA?**
- **Baseline (without Force)**: Achieved an average success rate of 12.0% across contact-critical stages.
- **Force-Aware Interface (FI only)**: Adding FI increased the average success rate to 35.0%. Notable gains were observed in handle pressing (from 28% to 52%), door pushing without closer (from 12% to 56%), and board cleaning (from 0% to 32%). This indicates that FI provides initial contact awareness for manipulation.
- **FWBC-VLA (FI + BC)**: Adding Bounded Body Compensation (BC) further improved the average success rate to 65.0%. This represents a 24.5 percentage point incremental gain over FI only. The benefit of BC was most pronounced in stages with sustained high loads: success rates increased by 52 percentage points when pushing a door with a closer (from 0% to 52%) and by 44 percentage points in board cleaning (from 32% to 76%). Stages with less sustained contact (handle pressing, door pushing without closer) showed marginal changes.

**Conclusion from Ablation**: These results indicate that the Force-Aware Interface provides necessary contact awareness for manipulation, while the Bounded Body Compensation is critical for maintaining stable whole-body behavior under sustained external forces, collectively leading to substantial performance improvements.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The FWBC-VLA framework represents a development in robotic loco-manipulation by integrating sensorless force awareness into both high-level action generation and low-level whole-body control. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enabling Force-Aware Control without Dedicated Hardware**: The development of HSR-Force, a sensorless residual-torque estimator, addresses a practical limitation in robotics by providing accurate estimates of physical interaction without the need for expensive and complex-to-integrate F/T sensors. This democratizes access to force-aware capabilities, allowing a wider range of existing robotic platforms to perform contact-rich tasks.

2. **Enhanced Robustness and Performance in Contact-Rich Tasks**: The experimental results demonstrate that explicit interaction feedback, derived from HSR-Force, significantly improves the success rates of wheeled-legged robots in complex, contact-rich loco-manipulation tasks such as whiteboard wiping and door opening with a heavy closer. This suggests that understanding contact onset, sustained loading, and release is crucial for stable and effective task execution, reducing failures related to body drift or insufficient force application.

3. **Bridging High-Level VLA and Low-Level WBC**: FWBC-VLA effectively bridges the gap between semantic VLA action generation and physical WBC. By conditioning the VLA action expert with force tokens and providing task-relevant body compensation to the WBC, the framework creates a dual feedback loop that coordinates manipulation actions with whole-body stabilization. This integrated approach allows robots to respond dynamically to physical interactions in a task-aware manner.

4. **Contribution to Data Collection for Loco-Manipulation**: The creation and upcoming public release of the WL&Arm Dataset, collected via a Pico-based hybrid position-force teleoperation system, provides a valuable resource for future research in contact-rich loco-manipulation. Its unique focus on capturing "force-intent" and addressing embodiment mismatch can facilitate the training and evaluation of other force-aware robotic policies.

5. **Implications for Generalist Robots**: The principles demonstrated in FWBC-VLA, particularly the sensorless force awareness and integrated compensation for whole-body effects, could be extended to other generalist robotic platforms, including humanoids and diverse mobile manipulators. This could contribute to the development of more capable and adaptable robots for real-world applications where physical interaction is unavoidable.

The limitations acknowledged by the authors, such as the need for further investigation into the impact of force estimation accuracy on task performance, indicate areas for future research. Nevertheless, FWBC-VLA proposes a viable path toward robust, hardware-agnostic, force-aware control for complex loco-manipulation, potentially influencing the design of future robotic systems and control strategies.
