# HumanoidBench: Simulated Humanoid Benchmark for Whole-Body Locomotion and Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> HumanoidBench introduces a high-fidelity, high-dimensional simulated benchmark for humanoid robots, featuring realistic models and a diverse suite of whole-body locomotion and manipulation tasks. It evaluates state-of-the-art reinforcement learning algorithms, demonstrating their current limitations and advocating for hierarchical approaches to tackle complex challenges.

## Problem

- Real-world humanoid hardware presents significant cost, fragility, and safety constraints, impeding rapid algorithmic development and testing.
- Existing robot learning benchmarks lack comprehensive integration of realistic whole-body locomotion and dexterous manipulation for humanoids.
- Prior simulated humanoid environments often rely on simplistic models, failing to capture the high-dimensional complexity and intricate physics of full humanoid systems.

- Researchers developed **HumanoidBench**, a ==MuJoCo-based== simulated environment featuring a realistic ==Unitree H1== humanoid model equipped with ==dexterous Shadow Hands==.
- The benchmark includes **27** diverse tasks, comprising **12** locomotion and **15** whole-body manipulation challenges, designed to test complex coordination.
- It offers high-dimensional observation (e.g., ==151D proprioception== + object states, ==448 taxel tactile sensing==) and a ==61-dimensional position-controlled action space== to reflect real humanoid complexity.

## Results

- State-of-the-art **RL** algorithms demonstrated **limited capability**, often failing to learn complex tasks or requiring **extensive training steps** for basic locomotion.
- The inclusion of dexterous hands and their corresponding **high-dimensional action space** drastically hindered learning across various tasks, even when hands were not directly involved.
- A **hierarchical RL** framework, incorporating pretrained low-level reaching policies, markedly enhanced performance for the **`push`** task and aided in grasping for the **`package`** task.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - State-of-the-art, flat reinforcement learning algorithms generally fail to solve most complex whole-body locomotion and manipulation tasks within HumanoidBench.
> - The high-dimensional action space introduced by dexterous hands, rather than merely observation complexity, poses a major challenge for algorithmic exploration and learning efficiency.
> - Structured approaches like Hierarchical Reinforcement Learning, utilizing robust pretrained low-level skills, can substantially improve performance on certain manipulation tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Humanoid robots, with their human-like morphology, are envisioned as versatile assistants capable of operating seamlessly in human-centric environments. However, a significant bottleneck in their development has been the inherent challenges of real-world experimentation: humanoid hardware is expensive, delicate, and often unsafe for rapid, iterative algorithmic testing. This limitation severely hinders the pace of algorithmic research in areas like whole-body control, dexterous manipulation, and complex locomotion.

The broader research landscape in robot learning has benefited immensely from standardized, simulated benchmarks. These benchmarks provide accessible, fast, safe, and inexpensive testbeds, akin to how platforms like Atari and DeepMind Control Suite have propelled DRL research in general. In robotics, several benchmarks exist:
- **Manipulation-focused benchmarks:** Environments like robosuite, MetaWorld, RLBench, MyoHand, Adroit, and FurnitureSim offer tasks primarily for single-arm or dexterous hand manipulation, often focused on quasi-static, short-horizon skills like pick-and-place. While some have introduced complex manipulation (e.g., FurnitureBench, BEHAVIOR, Habitat) or bimanual tasks (e.g., Bi-DexHands), they typically abstract away intricate low-level control for high-level planning or focus on floating hands rather than full humanoid systems.
- **Locomotion-focused benchmarks:** Benchmarks like MyoLeg, LocoMujoco, and DeepMind Control Suite's Humanoid tasks have accelerated research in robust humanoid locomotion. These environments have been instrumental in developing control algorithms that achieve impressive real-world locomotion.
- **Limited integrated approaches:** While some recent works have extended humanoid simulations to involve a degree of manipulation (e.g., tennis, soccer, ball manipulation, box moving), these efforts generally focus on demonstrating specific approaches on a narrow set of tasks. Crucially, they often employ simplistic humanoid models, leading to less accurate physics and collision handling, and lack the diversity and complexity required for a comprehensive evaluation of whole-body manipulation.

**HumanoidBench directly addresses these gaps** by introducing a *comprehensive, high-dimensional, simulated benchmark* specifically designed for humanoid robots. Its key differentiator is the integration of:
1. **Realistic Humanoid Model:** Leveraging validated models like the Unitree H1 with dexterous Shadow Hands, moving beyond simplistic representations.
2. **Diverse Task Suite:** Combining a wide range of locomotion and complex whole-body manipulation tasks that demand coordination across the entire robot body.
3. **High-Dimensionality:** Featuring high-dimensional observation and action spaces characteristic of real humanoid robots, including dexterous hands, which poses significant challenges for current learning algorithms.
4. **High-Fidelity Physics:** Built on the MuJoCo physics engine, known for its accuracy and speed, ensuring realistic interactions.

By providing such a benchmark, HumanoidBench aims to bridge the gap between isolated research in locomotion and manipulation, pushing the community towards developing integrated whole-body control strategies essential for deploying humanoids in complex, unstructured environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind HumanoidBench is to **accelerate algorithmic research in humanoid robotics**, which is currently impeded by the practical constraints of real-world hardware. To achieve this, the authors set forth several key objectives:

1. **Provide an Accessible and Economical Testbed:** To overcome the prohibitive costs, fragility, and safety concerns associated with real humanoid robot hardware, HumanoidBench aims to offer a simulated environment that is readily available, fast to run, safe for experimentation, and inexpensive. This allows researchers to rapidly prototype and verify algorithms.
2. **Introduce a High-Dimensional Simulation Environment:** To accurately reflect the complexity of real-world humanoids, the benchmark features a high-dimensional robot model, including a humanoid body and dexterous hands, within a high-fidelity physics engine (MuJoCo). This captures the intricate dynamics and large degrees of freedom inherent to these systems.
3. **Establish a Diverse Task Suite for Whole-Body Control:** The benchmark seeks to provide a wide variety of challenging tasks encompassing both locomotion and complex whole-body manipulation. These tasks are designed to require sophisticated coordination among various body parts and often involve long-horizon planning, mirroring everyday human activities.
4. **Create a Standardized Evaluation Platform:** By offering a consistent set of tasks and metrics, HumanoidBench aims to enable standardized evaluation of algorithmic progress in high-dimensional humanoid robot learning and control, fostering reproducibility and comparability across different research efforts.
5. **Identify and Highlight Current Algorithmic Challenges:** Through extensive benchmarking of state-of-the-art reinforcement learning (RL) algorithms, the authors intend to expose the limitations and specific challenges faced by current methods when applied to complex humanoid robots, thereby guiding future research directions. This includes challenges related to high-dimensional state/action spaces, exploration, and the integration of diverse skills.

In essence, HumanoidBench is motivated by the vision of humanoids assisting humans in daily life and serves as a critical infrastructure to push the boundaries of robot learning algorithms required to realize this vision.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of HumanoidBench focuses on creating a robust, diverse, and high-fidelity simulated environment for humanoid robot learning, followed by comprehensive benchmarking and specific ablation studies.

#### 3.1. Simulation Environment Design

- **Physics Engine:** The environment is built upon MuJoCo, known for its accurate and fast physics simulation capabilities, crucial for realistic robot dynamics.
- **Primary Robot Agent:** The benchmark primarily utilizes a Unitree H1 humanoid robot, chosen for its relative affordability and accurate simulation models. It is equipped with two dexterous Shadow Hands, which are well-regarded for their manipulation capabilities in both simulation and real-world applications.
- **Morphological Enhancements:** To align with future trends in humanoid design (e.g., slimmer, human-like hands), the cumbersome forearms of the Shadow Hands were removed, creating a more integrated, human-like morphology.
- **Additional Robot Configurations:** For broader utility, HumanoidBench also includes models for Unitree G1, Agility Robotics Digit, Robotiq 2F-85 parallel-jaw gripper, and the 13-DoF Unitree H1 hand, allowing for varied research contexts.
- **Observation Space:** The environment supports:
 - **Proprioceptive Data:** Joint angles and velocities of the robot (51 dimensions for the body, 50 for two Shadow Hands, totaling 151 dimensions with quaternions for floating base).
 - **Task-relevant Environment States:** Object poses and velocities for manipulation tasks.
 - **Egocentric Visual Observations:** Two cameras placed on the robot's head.
 - **Whole-Body Tactile Sensing:** Implemented using MuJoCo's tactile grid sensor, featuring 448 taxels distributed across the body (high resolution on hands, low on other parts), providing 3D contact force readings. This required non-trivial mesh adaptations and refinements using CoACD for finer contact resolution.
 - *Initial Benchmarking Focus:* The paper primarily focuses on state-based environments (proprioception + object states) to investigate control challenges, leaving multimodal perception for future work.
- **Action Space:** The humanoid robot is controlled via position control (default, also supports torque control) at 50 Hz. The action space is 61-dimensional (19 for the humanoid body, 42 for the two dexterous hands).

#### 3.2. Task Suite

HumanoidBench comprises 27 distinct tasks designed to challenge whole-body coordination:
- **12 Locomotion Tasks:** (e.g., `walk`, `stand`, `run`, `reach`, `hurdle`, `crawl`, `maze`, `sit`, `balance`, `stair`, `slide`, `pole`). These tasks are simpler, often serve as primitive skills, and focus on fundamental movement and balance.
- **15 Whole-Body Manipulation Tasks:** (e.g., `push`, `cabinet`, `highbar`, `door`, `truck` unloading, `cube` manipulation, `bookshelf` rearrangement, `basketball` catch-and-throw, `window` wiping, `spoon` stirring, `kitchen` tasks, `package` moving, `powerlift`, `room` organization, `insert` peg-in-hole). These are complex, long-horizon tasks requiring intricate coordination of the entire humanoid body, including the dexterous hands.
Each task includes detailed specifications for initialization, reward functions (dense and sparse components), and termination conditions.

#### 3.3. Benchmarking Strategy

- **Baseline Algorithms:** Four state-of-the-art RL algorithms were benchmarked:
 - **DreamerV3:** A model-based RL algorithm known for learning from imaginary model rollouts.
 - **TD-MPC2:** Another state-of-the-art model-based RL algorithm that incorporates online planning.
 - **SAC (Soft Actor-Critic):** A popular off-policy, model-free RL algorithm.
 - **PPO (Proximal Policy Optimization):** A widely used on-policy, model-free RL algorithm.
- **Evaluation Protocol:** Each algorithm was run for approximately 48 hours (corresponding to 10M environment steps for DreamerV3/SAC, and 2M for TD-MPC2), across three random seeds. Performance was evaluated based on learning curves (return over environment steps) and qualitative measures of task success. PPO was applied to a subset of tasks due to its inferior sample efficiency without massive parallelization.

#### 3.4. Ablation Studies and Hierarchical Reinforcement Learning (HRL)

To diagnose the challenges and explore potential solutions:
- **Impact of Hands and Action Space:** Performance was compared on tasks like `walk` and `push` with the full humanoid model (with dexterous hands), the model without hands, and the full model with fixed (zero-actuation) hands. This aimed to isolate the effect of added degrees of freedom and action space dimensionality.
- **Hierarchical RL Implementation:** The authors investigated an HRL approach for `push` and `package` tasks:
 - **Low-level Policy Pretraining:** Robust reaching policies (one-hand and two-hand) were pretrained independently. This was achieved using PPO with massive parallelization on MuJoCo MJX (thousands of environments) on a simplified H1 model (reduced collisions, no hands) to achieve high robustness, including force perturbations. These pretrained policies were then frozen.
 - **High-level Policy Training:** A high-level policy, using DreamerV3 or TD-MPC2, was trained to output setpoints for the frozen low-level policies, effectively controlling the robot by commanding reaching targets.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The benchmarking results and ablation studies reveal critical insights into the challenges of learning with high-dimensional humanoid robots.

#### 4.1. Performance of State-of-the-Art RL Algorithms

- **General Struggle:** Flat, end-to-end state-of-the-art RL algorithms (DreamerV3, TD-MPC2, SAC, PPO) largely struggle to learn most of the tasks in HumanoidBench. They consistently perform below the defined success thresholds, especially for tasks requiring long-horizon planning and intricate whole-body coordination.
- **Locomotion Difficulty:** Even fundamental locomotion tasks like `walk`, which are often solved with simpler agents, require a surprisingly large number of steps to learn with the HumanoidBench model.
- **Manipulation Difficulty:** Manipulation tasks present an even greater challenge. Policies often fail to learn beyond basic stabilization, indicating a severe struggle with acquiring complex manipulation skills, partly due to the prerequisite of robust locomotion.

#### 4.2. Impact of High-Dimensionality

- **Hands as a Bottleneck:** The presence of dexterous hands, with their additional degrees of freedom and larger action space, significantly degrades learning performance. For instance, on the `walk` task, performance dramatically decreases when hands are included, even though they are not directly used for walking.
- **Action Space Dimensionality:** The study confirmed that the increased dimensionality of the action space (from 19D without hands to 61D with hands) is a major contributor to the performance drop. When hands were present but their actuation fixed (reducing the active action space back to 19D), algorithms learned significantly faster, even with the increased observational complexity and mass. This highlights the exploration problem in high-dimensional action spaces.

#### 4.3. Efficacy of Hierarchical Reinforcement Learning (HRL)

- **Significant Improvement for Simpler Manipulation:** The hierarchical approach, leveraging robust pretrained low-level reaching policies, demonstrated a substantial performance boost on the `push` task. DreamerV3, in particular, achieved very high success rates, showcasing that introducing structure into the learning problem can be highly effective.
- **Partial Success for Complex Manipulation:** For the more challenging `package` task, the hierarchical approach improved the policy's ability to approach and grasp the package but still struggled with the subsequent lifting, suggesting that even more specialized or diverse low-level skills might be required for highly complex multi-stage tasks.

#### 4.4. Common Failure Scenarios

- **Short-Horizon Planning:** In tasks like `highbar`, agents often learn conservative behaviors to avoid termination (e.g., maintaining grip) but fail to execute the long-horizon, full-body rotation required for success.
- **Lack of Whole-Body Coordination:** The `door` task revealed that agents could unlock the door but struggled with the coordinated backward movement of the entire body necessary to pull the door open and traverse through it, indicating difficulties in integrating manipulation with locomotion.
- **Hard Exploration:** For `hurdle`, agents learned to run but failed to discover the jumping behavior needed to clear obstacles. Instead, they adopted conservative collision strategies to stabilize and avoid episode termination, highlighting a challenging exploration problem for novel, high-reward actions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

HumanoidBench represents a significant contribution to the field of robotics and reinforcement learning, with several profound implications:

1. **A Catalyst for Whole-Body Control Research:** By providing the first comprehensive, high-dimensional benchmark specifically designed for whole-body locomotion and manipulation with realistic humanoid models, HumanoidBench sets a "high bar" for the community. It is poised to become a critical tool for driving innovation in algorithms capable of handling the intricate dynamics and coordination required for general-purpose humanoid robots.
2. **Accelerated Algorithmic Development:** The accessibility, speed, and safety of the simulated environment dramatically lower the barrier to entry for researchers. This allows for rapid prototyping, iteration, and verification of new algorithms, potentially accelerating the pace of research in humanoid control by orders of magnitude compared to real-world experimentation.
3. **Clear Identification of Research Gaps:** The benchmark results clearly illustrate the limitations of current state-of-the-art RL algorithms when confronted with the complexity of humanoids. It precisely identifies key challenges such as high-dimensional exploration, long-horizon planning, and the coordination of diverse motor skills, providing a roadmap for future research efforts.
4. **Validation of Structured Learning Approaches:** The success of the hierarchical RL approach, even with simple low-level skills, strongly advocates for the utility of structured learning paradigms over flat, end-to-end methods for complex tasks. This will likely stimulate further research into more sophisticated hierarchical architectures, skill discovery, and compositionality in humanoid control.
5. **Foundation for Multimodal AI and Sim-to-Real Transfer:** While initial benchmarking focused on state-based observations, HumanoidBench's inclusion of egocentric vision and whole-body tactile sensing lays a crucial foundation for future research into multimodal perception and its interplay with control. Furthermore, its MuJoCo MJX integration and use of domain randomization for low-level policy training open avenues for more robust sim-to-real transfer studies, a critical step towards real-world deployment.
6. **Community Empowerment:** The open-source nature of HumanoidBench fosters community engagement, enabling researchers worldwide to contribute new tasks, robot models, and algorithms, thereby continually enriching the benchmark and its utility.

In summary, HumanoidBench is not merely another simulated environment; it is a meticulously designed scientific instrument that will enable the robotics community to confront the complexities of humanoid control head-on, fostering the development of algorithms that can ultimately lead to more capable and autonomous humanoid robots in human environments.
