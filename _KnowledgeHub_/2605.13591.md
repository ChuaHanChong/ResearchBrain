# Real2Sim: A Physics-driven and Editable Gaussian Splatting Framework for Autonomous Driving Scenes

> [!summary] Summary
> Real2Sim turns real driving clips into editable, physics-aware simulations by fusing 4D Gaussian Splatting with a differentiable Material Point Method solver. Background and each dynamic object are reconstructed as separate Gaussian clusters supporting translation, rotation, duplication; every Gaussian becomes an MPM particle with mass, friction, restitution, so collisions, deformation, and falls render realistically on Waymo Open Dataset scenes.

## Problem

- Manual driving-data collection costs tens of thousands of staff-hours, and conventional simulators leave a persistent reality gap when policies transfer to real deployment.
- Generative sensor-synthesis and diffusion world models still show rendering artifacts and fail to keep multi-view geometry spatially and temporally consistent across frames.
- Existing 4D Gaussian pipelines only replay recorded sensor data and edit primitives naively, so edited objects never react to forces, collisions, or post-impact dynamics.

## Method

- ==Street-Gaussians==-style scene construction splits a clip into a ==LiDAR-plus-SfM==-initialized background field and per-instance Gaussian clusters with learned time-varying object poses.
- A ==4D Fourier spherical-harmonics== basis encodes view-dependent color over time with few coefficients, preserving temporal coherence without storing per-frame harmonics parameters.
- A lightweight ==Material Point Method (MPM)== solver treats each Gaussian as a material particle with mass, rest volume, and deformation gradient, running ==P2G==, grid-force, velocity-update, ==G2P== steps that deform Gaussian covariances directly.

## Results

- Rendering on Waymo sequences 002 (**5** cameras, frames **50-100**, **505** images) and 031 (**3** cameras, frames **0-198**, **594** images) reaches **28.57** and **28.72** FPS with ground-truth-matching output.
- Single-vehicle wall collisions at **0.5 m/s** versus **2.5 m/s** produce mild versus severe frontal deformation; multi-vehicle tests at **3.5 m/s** show impact at frames **20** and **30** with lift and lateral push.
- Fall simulation with gravity **9.8**, Young's modulus **2e6 Pa**, density **200 kg/m3**, grid **200**, drops a vehicle onto a RANSAC-extracted road plane, contacting ground after roughly **0.5 s**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit point-based scene representations double as physics state: Gaussian kernels map directly onto material particles, so simulation needs no mesh extraction or remeshing.
> - Editing a reconstructed real scene yields more plausible, environment-consistent corner cases than inserting external asset libraries, which sacrifices realism for augmentation diversity.
> - Decoupling rendering from simulation is a real limitation: physics evolves a single-timestep snapshot, drifting from the trained Gaussian distribution and degrading rendered fidelity.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of reliable autonomous driving systems necessitates extensive, well-labeled datasets and robust models. Manual data collection for such systems is resource-intensive, often requiring tens of thousands of staff-hours for millions of time-synchronized images and 3D labels. This high cost and effort have driven research into alternative data generation methods.

Traditional simulation environments offer a controlled setting for generating data, but they often suffer from a persistent "reality gap" – a disparity between synthetic and real-world sensor data that can hinder the transferability of models trained in simulation to real-world deployment.

Recent advancements in generative frameworks, such as SurfelGAN and GAIA-2, have improved the visual fidelity of synthetic sensor data and introduced multi-agent interactions. However, these methods frequently encounter challenges with maintaining spatio-temporal consistency, producing rendering artifacts, and aligning multi-view outputs. Crucially, they typically lack physics-aware behavior, limiting their capacity to simulate plausible interactions and future events, which is essential for safety-critical autonomous driving scenarios.

Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) represent a significant step forward in high-fidelity image generation by encoding scenes as continuous volumetric functions. While NeRF models produce visually impressive results, their reliance on volumetric field evaluation makes them computationally slow, rendering them impractical for real-time, closed-loop simulation required by autonomous driving applications.

The emergence of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) has addressed some of NeRF's limitations by representing scenes as explicit collections of anisotropic Gaussian kernels. This explicit representation enables real-time rendering and direct scene manipulation, making it attractive for robotics and autonomous driving. Subsequent extensions, known as 4D Gaussian Splatting (4DGS), further enhance this capability by incorporating temporal deformation, allowing for continuous novel-time, novel-view synthesis in dynamic scenes with moving objects like vehicles and pedestrians.

Despite these advancements, existing 4DGS pipelines primarily function as recorders of observed sensor data. They permit naive editing of Gaussian primitives (e.g., position, orientation, mesh replacement) but lack the capability to simulate how objects would *physically* interact or what *could happen* in the future under modified simulation settings. This absence of physics-aware behavior prevents edited objects from reacting plausibly to forces or collisions, which is a critical limitation for testing complex scenarios such as multi-car crashes or near-misses. While some recent efforts have explored embedding kinematics and dynamics into Gaussian primitives, their application has not been extensively demonstrated in street-wide driving sequences.

Real2Sim addresses these limitations by integrating the high-fidelity, real-time rendering and explicit scene representation capabilities of 4D Gaussian Splatting with a differentiable physics solver. This integration aims to transform real-world driving scenes into editable, physics-aware simulations, thereby enabling the generation of diverse and realistic scenarios, including challenging corner cases, that current methods struggle to produce reliably.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the Real2Sim framework are rooted in the challenges of developing and validating autonomous driving systems:

- **Addressing Data Scarcity and Cost:** Autonomous driving models require vast quantities of diverse, high-quality, and well-labeled data for training and validation. Manually collecting and annotating such data from the real world is an extremely time-consuming, expensive, and resource-intensive process. The framework aims to provide a scalable and efficient alternative for generating this essential data.

- **Bridging the Reality Gap in Simulation:** Conventional simulators often exhibit a "sim-to-real gap," meaning models trained exclusively in synthetic environments may not perform optimally when deployed in the real world. While generative models have improved visual fidelity, they frequently lack the temporal and spatial consistency and physical realism necessary for robust autonomous driving data. Real2Sim seeks to bridge this gap by starting from real-world scenes and endowing them with physically plausible interactions.

- **Enabling Realistic and Flexible Scene Editing:** Existing radiance field methods and 4DGS frameworks can reconstruct dynamic scenes but offer limited capabilities for editing scenes in a physically consistent manner. The motivation is to allow users to modify driving scenarios (e.g., repositioning vehicles, altering trajectories) with a high degree of freedom while ensuring that these modifications adhere to physical laws, producing plausible and realistic outcomes.

- **Generating Challenging Corner Cases:** A significant challenge in autonomous driving development is the scarcity of data for "corner cases" – rare but critical events such as collisions, near-misses, or unusual traffic dynamics. These scenarios are vital for training robust models, but they are difficult and dangerous to collect in the real world. Furthermore, their rarity leads to long-tailed training distributions, which can negatively impact model performance. Real2Sim is specifically motivated to efficiently generate diverse and physically accurate corner-case data in a laboratory setting, circumventing the high costs and safety risks associated with real-world accident data collection.

- **Supporting Downstream Autonomous Driving Tasks:** The ultimate goal is to produce synthetic data that is directly applicable and beneficial to various downstream tasks in autonomous driving. This includes improving models for object detection, multi-object tracking, trajectory prediction, and training end-to-end driving policies by providing a rich, physics-aware, and editable data source.

In summary, the core objective of Real2Sim is to develop a unified, high-fidelity, and physics-aware simulation framework for autonomous driving scenes that enables detailed instance-level editing and realistic physical interactions, thereby providing a scalable solution for generating diverse and challenging training data, especially for corner cases.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Real2Sim proposes a unified framework that integrates 4D Gaussian Splatting (4DGS) with a differentiable Material Point Method (MPM) solver to reconstruct and simulate dynamic driving scenes with physical realism and editability. The methodology can be broken down into two main components: driving scene construction and physics-aware simulation.

**A. Driving Scene Construction**

The framework adopts a "Street Gaussians" methodology, which models a dynamic driving sequence as two distinct and interleaved Gaussian point clouds: one for the static background and one for each moving object instance.

1. **Background Representation:**
 - Static elements of the scene (e.g., roads, buildings, trees) are represented by a dense field of 3D Gaussians.
 - These background Gaussians are initialized from sparse LiDAR point clouds combined with outputs from Structure-from-Motion (SfM) techniques.
 - Each background Gaussian is parameterized by its mean position (μ), an anisotropic covariance matrix (Σ_b), an opacity weight (α_b), and view-dependent color coefficients (z_b) encoded using low-order spherical harmonics. This explicit form is intended to capture fine geometric detail efficiently for high-fidelity reconstruction and fast differentiable rendering.

2. **Dynamic Object Representation:**
 - Moving objects (e.g., vehicles, pedestrians) are individually segmented and tracked using an off-the-shelf detector.
 - Each dynamic object is represented by its own dedicated cluster of Gaussian primitives.
 - Initially, these Gaussian clusters are defined in the object's local coordinate frame. They are then transformed into world coordinates via learned object pose parameters (rotation R_t and translation T_t) that are allowed to evolve over time. This instance-aware separation is crucial for enabling individual object editing.
 - To capture continuous appearance variations of objects over time and space, a 4D Fourier spherical harmonics basis is introduced. This basis allows for the reconstruction of each object's spherical harmonics weights (z(t)) on the fly, ensuring temporal coherence and view-dependent effects without requiring per-frame parameters.

3. **Inference and Editing Foundation:**
 - During inference, the background and object point clouds are merged into a single point cloud and composited from front to back using their opacity weights.
 - The explicit, instance-aware nature of this representation forms the foundation for editing: because each object is a distinct Gaussian cluster with its own pose, users can manipulate them independently—relocating, rotating, scaling, or even replacing objects to create new traffic scenarios.

**B. Physics-aware Simulation**

To enable realistic physical interactions within these edited Gaussian scenes, Real2Sim integrates a Material Point Method (MPM) solver directly with the Gaussian representation.

1. **Gaussian as Material Particle:**
 - Each Gaussian primitive is treated as a material particle.
 - These particles are endowed with physical properties: mass (m_p), initial volume (V^0_p), current position (x_p), velocity (v_p), and a deformation gradient (F_p).

2. **MPM Integration Process:**
 - The MPM algorithm operates iteratively over discrete time steps (Δt).
 - **Particle-to-Grid (P2G):** At each time step, particle quantities (mass and momentum) are interpolated and transferred to a background Eulerian grid using C1 B-spline kernels.
 - **Compute Grid Forces:** Forces acting on the grid nodes are calculated. This involves computing the first Piola-Kirchhoff stress (P_p) for each particle, derived from a hyperelastic energy function (Ψ), and then aggregating these stresses onto the grid nodes. External forces (e.g., gravity) are also incorporated.
 - **Update Grid Velocities:** The velocities of the grid nodes are updated based on the computed forces.
 - **Grid-to-Particle (G2P):** The updated grid velocities are then interpolated back to the particles, updating their velocities (v_p) and positions (x_p). The deformation gradient (F_p) of each particle is also updated, reflecting the local deformation.

3. **Gaussian Deformation:**
 - Following the MPM integration, the Gaussian's mean (x_p(t)) and covariance (A_p(t)) are updated to reflect the physical deformation.
 - An affine transformation (ϕ_p(X, t)) is used as a first-order Fourier approximation to describe how a Gaussian deforms from its material-space mean (X_p) and covariance (A_p). The new mean and covariance evolve based on the updated deformation gradient F_p(t).
 - The opacity and color coefficients of the Gaussians are assumed to remain constant throughout the simulation.
 - The deformed Gaussian particles are then rendered using the standard splatting pipeline.

By unifying 3D Gaussian Splatting with the MPM solver, Real2Sim enables users to manipulate object poses and simulate physical interactions like collisions and post-impact trajectories directly on the Gaussian representation, eliminating the need for mesh extraction or remeshing. This approach is designed to generate diverse, physics-aware driving scenarios for downstream perception and control tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted using the Waymo Open Dataset, a large-scale, high-fidelity benchmark for autonomous driving tasks. All training and inference processes were performed on a single NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU with 24 GB of VRAM. The StreetGaussian model was trained for 50,000 iterations, with the physics simulation decoupled from the Gaussian model generation, where Gaussian point clouds from individual objects were loaded and evolved via MPM integration at each timestep.

**A. Scene Rendering and Reconstruction**

- **Capability:** Real2Sim demonstrated high-fidelity rendering and accurate reconstruction of dynamic urban driving scenes.
- **Evaluation:** Experiments on Waymo sequences 002 and 031, using multiple camera views, showed that the rendered images closely matched the ground truth.
- **Results:** The framework successfully reconstructed detailed scene point clouds by interpreting Gaussian centers as point locations, accurately recovering static elements such as road surfaces, trees, and walls, as well as dynamic objects. The independent modeling of dynamic objects and the static background was highlighted as a foundational aspect for subsequent editing and physics simulations. Fidelity extended to capturing fine details of vehicles and buildings, and reproducing environmental effects like shadows.

**B. Scene Editing**

- **Capability:** The explicit construction of rendered scenes using Gaussian primitives enabled arbitrary and on-demand scene editing.
- **Method:** Objects, represented by clusters of Gaussian primitives with time-varying rotations, positions, and poses, could be seamlessly modified by specifying new parameters.
- **Examples:** The experiments showcased three editing operations:
 - **Translation:** A black car was translated from the left lane to the center lane.
 - **Rotation:** A white car in the center lane was rotated by 15 degrees.
 - **Duplication:** The white car was duplicated and then translated along the x-direction.
- **Results:** These edits produced diverse road scenarios, indicating the framework's flexibility in generating new data. The authors noted that such direct editing of existing scene physics, as opposed to introducing external models, tends to yield more "realistic" and logically consistent scenarios.

**C. Physics-aware Simulation and Corner Case Generation**

- **Capability:** By treating each Gaussian primitive as a material particle endowed with physical properties and using an MPM solver, Real2Sim achieved physics-aware simulation, capturing object-object and object-environment interactions.
- **Physical Parameters:** Generic physical parameters (gravity, Poisson's Ratio, Young's Modulus, material density, grid resolution, material type "Jelly") were manually configured for the simulations. The authors acknowledged that these parameters were adjusted to emphasize collision responses and that precise replication of real-world vehicle physics was not achieved due to limited access to accurate material properties.

1. **Single Vehicle Collision:**
 - **Setup:** A white SUV from Sequence 031 was set with an initial velocity and directed towards a virtual collision plane.
 - **Results:** At an initial velocity of 0.5 m/s, the vehicle exhibited mild frontal deformation upon impact. When the initial velocity was increased to 2.5 m/s, a faster collision and more severe deformation were observed, demonstrating the system's ability to simulate kinetic energy release and deformation.

2. **Multi-vehicle Collision:**
 - **Setup:** Two white SUVs from Sequence 002 were assigned an initial velocity of 3.5 m/s, colliding with stationary vehicles ahead. Gravity was set to zero to keep vehicles on a horizontal plane.
 - **Results:** The simulation showed the right-hand SUV colliding with and lifting the vehicle ahead into midair. The left-hand SUV collided with its lead vehicle, pushing it forward before the lead vehicle moved laterally. This experiment demonstrated complex object-object interactions and post-impact trajectories.

3. **Fall Simulation:**
 - **Setup:** A vehicle was initially placed in midair and allowed to fall under gravity onto an extracted road surface from Sequence 002. The ground plane was extracted using either LiDAR or Gaussian point clouds with the RANSAC algorithm.
 - **Results:** The vehicle descended under gravity, contacted the ground, experienced a slight bounce, and eventually came to rest on the road surface. This illustrated object-environment interaction involving gravity and contact.

- **Overall Impact of Physics Simulation:** These experiments validated Real2Sim's capability to generate diverse, physics-aware driving scenarios, including complex corner cases that are rare in real-world datasets. This approach is presented as a method to efficiently create valuable training data for autonomous driving systems, mitigating the costs and risks associated with collecting such scenarios in reality.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Real2Sim represents a contribution to the field of autonomous driving data generation and simulation by integrating high-fidelity visual scene representation with physics-aware interaction capabilities. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Scalable and Efficient Data Generation:** The framework offers a laboratory-scale approach to generate vast amounts of diverse, realistic, and physics-aware driving scenarios. This capability has the potential to alleviate the resource-intensive and costly burden of manual real-world data collection and labeling, thereby accelerating the development and training of autonomous driving models.

- **Generation of Critical Corner Case Data:** A key impact of Real2Sim is its ability to synthesize challenging corner cases, such as multi-vehicle collisions or specific impact scenarios. These events are rare and dangerous to collect in the real world but are crucial for training robust and safe autonomous driving systems. By providing a controlled environment to generate such data, Real2Sim can help address the problem of long-tailed training distributions, leading to more resilient models.

- **Enhanced Realism and Editability:** By unifying 4D Gaussian Splatting with a differentiable Material Point Method solver, Real2Sim achieves both high visual fidelity and physically plausible interactions. The instance-level editing capabilities, combined with physics simulation, allow for the creation of logically consistent and diverse scenarios that can be tailored to specific research or testing needs, offering a level of control not typically available in purely generative or traditional simulation systems.

- **Support for Downstream Autonomous Driving Tasks:** The synthetic data generated by Real2Sim is designed to directly benefit a range of downstream tasks fundamental to autonomous driving. This includes training and evaluating models for object detection, multi-object tracking, motion prediction, and end-to-end driving policy learning. Access to rich, editable, and physics-aware data can lead to improved performance and generalization of these models.

**Limitations and Future Directions Identified by the Authors:**

The authors acknowledge several limitations that indicate areas for future research and development:

1. **Viewpoint Dependency of Gaussian Modeling:** The quality of Gaussian representations can degrade from unseen angles, leading to blurred or incomplete objects. Future work aims to address this by expanding datasets or leveraging diffusion models to refine and complete Gaussian representations, enhancing robustness to novel viewpoints.
2. **Decoupled Rendering and Physics Simulation:** Currently, rendering and physics simulation are decoupled, with the simulation relying on Gaussian snapshots. This can lead to deviations from the original Gaussian distribution and potential degradation of rendering quality over time. An objective for future work is to explore an end-to-end joint estimation framework to better integrate these components.
3. **Physical Parameter Calibration and Detailed Dynamics:** The current model treats each vehicle as a single soft body, limiting the simulation of intricate dynamics such as tire-road friction or internal vehicle component behavior. Future research plans to develop more sophisticated, decomposed Gaussian models that better reflect the complex structures and material properties of vehicles, allowing for more detailed and accurate physical simulations.

Despite these limitations, Real2Sim provides a foundation for creating scalable, realistic, and editable virtual environments from real-world data, holding significant promise for advancing autonomous driving research and development.
