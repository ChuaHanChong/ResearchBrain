# Attention from Action, for Action: Emergent Visual Bottlenecks for Policy Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Seeker is a lightweight readout over frozen DINOv3 patch features that learns where to look purely from action supervision. A task- and state-conditioned query iteratively attends to patches with gated multi-head attention, trained through a diffusion action loss; the resulting progression-aware region of interest is frozen and reused for RGB cropping, mask-guided augmentation, and point-cloud filtering, lifting real-robot in-domain success from 48.3% to 76.7%.

## Problem

- Region-of-interest interfaces built on gaze, object classes, affordance boxes, or vision-language grounding need external spatial labels that imitation datasets rarely contain.
- Label-free action-derived crops hand-design the action-to-region mapping via keyframe event thresholds, end-effector proxy points, and fixed crop scale, which break under trajectory variability.
- Fixed crops misalign when control-relevant evidence sits away from the end-effector, on tool contacts or inter-object relations, or shifts continuously with task progress.

## Method

- **Seeker** readout: ==FiLM== fuses a ==CLIP== task embedding with proprioception into an initial query that iteratively ==cross-attends== over frozen ==DINOv3== patch keys and values for two refinement steps.
- ==Query-dependent head gating== scores each attention head by dot product with the projected mean query, fusing per-head contexts and maps so different heads dominate as task stage changes.
- ==Coarse-to-fine attention trimming== with a ==head-weighted KL loss== tightens boxes; a ==top-p mass criterion== extracts mask and bounding box, supervised only by a ==diffusion== action-prediction objective.

## Results

- On six MimicGen tasks with **100** demos, Seeker averages **62.6%** success versus **42.6%** for the best crop baseline, beating RAVEN at **52.1%** and nearing privileged Oracle ROI at **64.2%**.
- Real xArm7 tasks: **76.7%** in-domain versus **48.3%** best baseline, **60.0%** versus **20.0%** under lighting and background shifts, a **78.2%** out-of-domain retention ratio.
- Frozen RGB regions filtering point clouds add **30.3** absolute points to DP3 over manual workspace cropping; ablation shows box-only FiLM conditioning drops **23.2** points versus explicit cropping.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Action streams are sufficient supervision for localizing control-relevant visual evidence; the emergent bottleneck switches between object-centric and interaction-centric focus without semantic labels.
> - The gain comes from physically cropping the input rather than telling the network where the box is, since low-resolution crops nearly match full-resolution ones.
> - Generative training objectives matter for attention quality, and coverage of spatial variation in demonstrations matters more than raw demonstration count.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Broader Research Landscape

This work is situated within the field of visuomotor learning for robotic manipulation, particularly focusing on improving data efficiency in complex visual environments. A prevalent challenge in imitation learning, especially for long-horizon tasks, is that decision-critical visual cues are often sparse and localized within a broader scene. This necessitates methods that can effectively direct the policy's attention to relevant regions of interest (ROIs).

Existing approaches to creating spatial bottlenecks, which restrict policy perception to task-relevant regions, broadly fall into two categories. The first relies on external annotations or auxiliary signals, such as human gaze data, object- or tool-centered crops, vision-language model (VLM) grounding, or recurring keypoints. While powerful, these methods often require additional labels not typically present in standard imitation datasets or may not precisely align with the control-dependent visual evidence needed for specific actions.

The second category, action-derived spatial bottlenecks, leverages the action stream intrinsic to imitation learning. These methods convert demonstrated actions into spatial targets, for instance, by projecting the end-effector at keyframes identified through gripper state changes or motion stops. Examples include Q-attention, PerAct, RVT, and RVT-2. While label-free, these approaches often depend on hand-designed heuristics for event timing, proxy points (e.g., end-effector position), and fixed crop scales. Such fixed designs can become restrictive under trajectory and task variability, potentially leading to misalignment when relevant visual evidence lies away from the chosen proxy or when interactions are continuous rather than discrete.

The presented research introduces Seeker, a module designed to learn these action-to-ROI mappings directly from observation-action streams, thereby addressing the limitations of hand-designed heuristics while maintaining a label-free approach. It aims to provide a control-dependent visual bottleneck that adapts to task progress and robot state, thereby enhancing data efficiency and robustness in visuomotor learning. This work is complementary to policy-level data efficiency strategies like reflection-consistent augmentation or equivariant policy learning, as it focuses on improving the visual input itself.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a method, termed Seeker, that can automatically learn and extract progression-aware visual regions of interest (ROIs) from observation-action streams, without requiring explicit spatial labels. This learned ROI serves as a dynamic spatial interface to enhance the data efficiency and robustness of downstream visuomotor policies.

The motivation for this objective stems from several identified limitations in current visuomotor learning paradigms:
1. **Sparsity of Decision-Critical Cues:** In visually complex environments, the most relevant visual information for making control decisions is often localized and can shift dynamically with the robot's state and task progress. Processing an entire high-resolution image for every decision is data-inefficient.
2. **Limitations of External Spatial Labels:** Many existing ROI methods rely on external spatial labels such as gaze, object classes, or affordance annotations. These labels are often costly to acquire, may not be available in standard imitation datasets, and might not directly correspond to the precise visual evidence required for a specific control decision.
3. **Rigidity of Action-Derived Heuristics:** Label-free alternatives that derive ROIs from action trajectories often depend on hand-designed heuristics. These heuristics make fixed choices about event timing (e.g., gripper state changes), proxy points (e.g., end-effector position), and crop scales. This fixed design can lead to misalignment when the necessary visual evidence is not centered on the end-effector, or when task progression involves continuous interactions rather than discrete events. Examples include continuous tool-object contact, where the relevant region is offset from the end-effector, or tasks lacking clear "keyframes."
4. **Need for Adaptive, Progression-Aware ROIs:** The visual evidence relevant for control is inherently control-dependent and can change continuously throughout a task. An ideal ROI should adapt its size, location, and focus as control demands evolve, shifting between object-, contact-, and relation-centric evidence rather than being fixed by semantic categories or simple heuristics.

To address these motivations, the authors propose Seeker, designed to learn attention from action. The key claims tested through this work are:
1. Demonstrating that action supervision, without spatial labels, can effectively recover policy-useful ROIs that closely approximate privileged oracle ROIs.
2. Verifying that these learned ROIs significantly improve the data efficiency of downstream policy learning, as measured by success rates across various tasks.
3. Establishing that the learned ROI is reusable across different input modalities (RGB, point clouds) and enhances robustness to appearance shifts (lighting, background changes) through mask-guided augmentation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Seeker is introduced as a module designed to learn task- and state-conditioned visual attention from observation-action streams. It operates as a readout over frozen DINOv3 patch tokens, iteratively refining a query to produce progression-aware ROIs without direct spatial supervision.

#### 3.1. Seeker Architecture

The core of Seeker is an iterative, gated cross-attention readout mechanism:
- **Robot-conditioned Query:** Seeker projects frozen DINOv3 patch features into attention keys and values. An initial query, $Q^{(0)}$, is dynamically generated by FiLM-conditioning a task embedding $Z$ with the proprioceptive robot state $s$ (comprising end-effector position and gripper state). This ensures the query is task- and state-aware from the outset.
- **Iterative Gated Readout:** Seeker refines the query through $T$ steps of gated cross-attention. At each step, the current query attends to the visual keys and values, producing attention maps ($A_h$) and contexts ($C_h$) for multiple heads ($H$). Instead of uniform averaging, Seeker learns query-dependent head weights ($\omega_h$) through a softmax operation over dot products between each head's context and a learned projection of the mean query. These weights fuse the per-head outputs into a combined visual context ($\tilde{C}$) and attention map ($\tilde{A}$), which then update the query via FiLM conditioning for the next iteration. This iterative process allows the ROI focus to evolve with task progress and visual evidence.
- **Output:** After $T$ iterations, the final $\tilde{C}$ serves as Seeker's compact visual context, and the final $\tilde{A}$, reshaped to the DINO patch grid, becomes the ROI map.

#### 3.2. Action-supervised ROI Interface

Seeker learns to produce ROIs solely from action supervision, without explicit spatial labels.
- **Training Objective:** During Seeker training, the final visual context ($\tilde{C}$) is concatenated with proprioception and a task embedding, then passed to a diffusion action head. This head is trained using a standard noise-prediction objective, providing a dense action-supervised signal that guides the ROI discovery. This diffusion head is solely for pretraining Seeker; it is discarded after training.
- **ROI Extraction:** The learned gated attention map ($\tilde{A}$) is converted into a patch-level ROI by selecting the smallest set of tokens whose cumulative attention mass exceeds a predefined top-$p$ threshold. This process generates both a tight bounding box and a coarse patch mask.
- **Downstream Applications:** The extracted ROIs are reused in three forms:
 1. **RGB Cropping:** The bounding box crops and resizes static third-person RGB views. To preserve absolute spatial information lost by cropping, policy features are FiLM-conditioned on the box's location and scale. Eye-in-hand views are not cropped.
 2. **Point-Cloud Filtering:** The image-plane bounding box filters projected 3D point-cloud points before downsampling, providing a localized 3D input without requiring retraining Seeker.
 3. **Mask-Guided Augmentation:** The patch mask selectively preserves task-relevant regions while perturbing task-irrelevant background appearance. This is used for domain generalization by blending original images with random textures based on the mask.

#### 3.3. Training Details

- **Coarse-to-Fine Attention Trimming:** To obtain compact and adaptive ROIs, a staged training scheme is employed. A "coarse Seeker" is initially trained for global localization. After a certain number of epochs ($\tau$), a "fine Seeker" branch is introduced, operating on patch tokens within the coarse ROI. After $2\tau$ epochs, an "attention trimming" mechanism is activated, suppressing out-of-box mass from the coarse attention map to align it with the fine ROI using a head-weighted KL divergence loss. Only the coarse branch is used for inference.
- **Iterative Refinement Benefit:** Iterative refinement of the query is used (two updates in all experiments) to allow the query to adapt to retrieved visual evidence, particularly in ambiguous cases where similar end-effector states might correspond to different visual targets.
- **Low-dimensional Inputs:** Inputs include end-effector translation, rotation, gripper state, a one-hot embodiment identifier, and CLIP-derived task embeddings. Only 3D translation and gripper state are used for Seeker's conditioning for stable convergence.
- **Pixel-level Perturbation:** A Random Overlay augmentation is applied from the start of training (probability 0.5) to prevent attention collapse and encourage robust ROI learning.
- **Sequential Training for Multi-View:** In multi-view settings, views are incorporated sequentially. Seeker is first trained on the third-person view, caching its context features. A multi-view Seeker is then initialized with these cached features and learns to incorporate additional views, ensuring the third-person representation remains consistent.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of Seeker was evaluated in both simulation and real-world environments across various tasks and conditions.

#### 4.1. Simulation Evaluations

The simulation study utilized six MimicGen tasks, covering diverse manipulation scenarios and spatial variance. A single multi-task Seeker was trained on 100 demonstrations per task and kept frozen.

##### 4.1.1. RGB Data Efficiency

- Seeker demonstrably improved average success rates. Compared to the best non-Seeker input-level baseline (RVT2-Crop at 42.6%), Seeker achieved an average success rate of 62.6%, representing a relative gain of +52.8%.
- Seeker's gains were consistent across tasks, showing the largest improvements on spatially ambiguous or contact-rich tasks like Three-Piece Assembly (+119.9%), Pick & Place (+40.6%), and Threading (+83.6%), where policies require localizing small, control-relevant regions.
- The performance of RVT2-Crop, which relies on fixed keyframe/proxy assumptions, was uneven, indicating its limitations in scenarios with continuous motion, noisy gripper-triggered keyframes, or interaction regions offset from the TCP proxy.
- Seeker surpassed the external state-of-the-art policy RAVEN on average (62.6% vs. 52.1%) and closely approached the performance of the privileged Oracle ROI (62.6% vs. 64.2%), despite not using scripted affordance boxes, spatial labels, gaze, or language grounding.

##### 4.1.2. Mask-Guided Augmentation for Background Generalization

- Seeker's frozen ROI masks were reused to guide appearance augmentation under shuffled tabletop textures. The Seeker-Guided augmentation preserved control-critical regions while perturbing task-irrelevant backgrounds.
- Seeker-Guided augmentation demonstrated effectiveness, particularly when trained on original-background demonstrations, and remained effective when combined with background-randomized training. This suggested that the action-grounded regions identified by Seeker generalize sufficiently across background changes to guide robust augmentation.
- Conversely, general visual diversity from background-randomized training alone did not always guarantee generalization, especially when additional texture complexity obscured fine interaction cues.

##### 4.1.3. Point-Cloud Transfer

- Seeker's image-plane ROI was evaluated as a spatial filter for point-cloud policies (DP3). The frozen RGB ROI filtered 3D points by image-plane projection, effectively localizing the 3D input.
- Adding Seeker ROI filtering on top of manual workspace cropping improved DP3 performance by an average of 30.3 absolute percentage points across three tasks (Pick & Place, Stack-3, Coffee Prep.), compared to manual cropping alone.
- This indicated that Seeker functions as a general spatial interface, transferable across sensing modalities, even when trained only from RGB action supervision. Point-cloud policies, however, generally still lagged image-based policies.

#### 4.2. Real-world Evaluations

Seeker was evaluated on three real-world multi-view RGB tasks (Coffee Transport, Table Cleanup, Board Assembly) using a UFactory xArm7.

- Seeker achieved an average in-domain success rate of 76.7%, a notable increase from the best baseline's (RVT2-Crop) 48.3%.
- Under challenging out-of-domain (OOD) conditions, including lighting and background shifts, Seeker's average success rate rose from 20.0% (RVT2-Crop) to 60.0%.
- Seeker demonstrated a retention ratio (Avg. OOD/Avg. ID) of 78.2%, indicating the robustness of its learned ROI under real-world appearance changes, including lighting shifts that could over-expose views.
- RVT2-Crop, based on action heuristics, exhibited failures in real-world scenarios due to inconsistent velocity profiles (leading to false-positive keyframes) or the absence of reliable gripper-state anchors in continuous motion tasks, causing misaligned crops. Seeker's learned, progression-aware ROIs mitigated these issues.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Training Objective for Seeker:** Diffusion-based action prediction proved most effective for training Seeker, yielding tighter and more stable ROI masks compared to direct regression (noisy attention) or other generative objectives like IMLE (cleaner but diffuse) and flow matching (closer to diffusion, but slightly less precise).
- **ROI Conditioning, Cropping, and Resolution:**
 - Explicit spatial cropping using the predicted ROI was found to be the primary benefit. A "low-res crop" variant, applying the normalized box to a downsampled image, nearly matched full Seeker performance.
 - "FiLM-only" conditioning, which used the predicted box location/scale to condition full-image CNN features without explicit cropping, was substantially weaker, suggesting that reducing the visual search space through cropping is critical.
 - The small remaining gap between low-res crop and full Seeker performance suggested that high-resolution ROI content provided a secondary benefit for fine-grained task stages.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents Seeker, a module that contributes a novel, label-free approach to generating progression-aware visual bottlenecks for visuomotor control. The significance and potential impact of this work include:

1. **Enhanced Data Efficiency:** By enabling policies to focus on task-relevant visual regions, Seeker significantly improves data efficiency in visuomotor learning. This can reduce the amount of expert demonstration data required to train competent robotic manipulation policies, making robot learning more accessible and scalable.
2. **Increased Robustness to Visual Variability:** The learned ROIs, adaptable to task progress and robot state, enhance policy robustness against variations in lighting and background. The ability to guide mask-based augmentation based on control-relevant regions allows policies to generalize better to unseen visual conditions without extensive data augmentation strategies.
3. **Cross-Modality Reusability:** Seeker's demonstration of transferring its image-plane ROI to filter 3D point clouds highlights its potential as a versatile spatial interface across different sensing modalities. This suggests that a single, action-supervised perception module could unify spatial attention for diverse robotic perception systems.
4. **Label-Free Operation:** A key advantage is its independence from external spatial labels, such as gaze, object annotations, or language grounding. This simplifies the data collection process for imitation learning, as only observation-action pairs are needed, avoiding the overhead and potential misalignment of additional semantic supervision.
5. **Addressing Limitations of Heuristics:** Seeker overcomes the rigidity of hand-designed action-derived crop heuristics by learning a dynamic mapping from observation-action streams. This allows it to adapt to continuous interactions, varied task stages, and cues located away from the end-effector, which are common challenges in complex manipulation tasks.
6. **Action-Grounded Visual Structure:** The work underscores that action supervision is a rich source of implicit control-aligned information for discerning visual relevance. It supports the notion that the visual structure recovered from action supervision is dependent on the coverage and variation present in the demonstrations, suggesting avenues for optimizing data collection.

Potential future impacts include the development of more general-purpose robot learning systems that can adapt to new tasks and environments with less human intervention, improved performance in complex real-world scenarios, and the creation of more interpretable robot behaviors by revealing the regions of visual focus during decision-making. The concept of an explicit, progression-aware, and action-supervised spatial bottleneck could become a standard component in architectures for visuomotor control.
