# Evolving-RL: End-to-End Optimization of Experience-Driven Self-Evolving Capability within Agents

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Xiaohongshu Inc. and Peking University developed Evolving-RL, a framework that jointly optimizes an LLM agent's capabilities for extracting and utilizing textual skills from past experiences. This approach fosters improved generalization to unseen tasks and enhances the agent's base policy by internalizing reusable procedural knowledge.

## Problem

- Large language models (LLMs) are static post-training and struggle to adapt continuously to novel, out-of-distribution (OOD) tasks and environments during deployment.
- Existing experience-driven self-evolving agents often decouple the optimization of experience extraction and utilization, leading to suboptimal performance or reliance on external quality control mechanisms.
- When reinforcement learning (RL) is applied to enhance experience utilization, it typically optimizes this phase in isolation, neglecting the intrinsic capability of the LLM to extract high-quality, reusable experience.

## Method

- **Evolving-RL** proposes an algorithmic framework that unifies the optimization of both experience extraction and utilization within a single, shared LLM policy.
- The framework employs an online skill generation and generalization-oriented evaluation process, producing coordinated supervisory signals for both the extractor and solver components.
- It utilizes Group-Normalized Policy Optimization (==GRPO==) for both components, incorporating a negative entropy coefficient in the extractor loss to bias skill generation towards more deterministic predictions and counter evaluation noise.

## Results

- Evolving-RL achieved a **96.0%** overall success rate on ALFWorld and **30.87%** action accuracy on Mind2Web (with skill injection), outperforming various prompt-based and RL-based baselines.
- On unseen ALFWorld tasks, the framework achieved an **88.6%** success rate (with skills), representing approximately a **98.7%** relative improvement over the GRPO (w/ skills) baseline.
- Ablation studies confirmed that co-evolution of extraction and utilization is critical for performance, showing that isolated optimization (solver-only or extractor-only) yields limited or overfit generalization gains.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Jointly optimizing skill extraction and utilization within a shared policy is essential for robust generalization to out-of-distribution tasks, overcoming limitations of isolated optimization.
> - Evolving-RL inherently functions as an experience-augmented RL algorithm, allowing the policy to internalize reusable procedural patterns and improve its base capabilities even without explicit skill injection at inference time.
> - The framework improves the intrinsic quality of extracted skills, making them broadly reusable and transferable to other policies rather than just inducing a policy-specific communication protocol.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Large language models (LLMs) have demonstrated proficiency across various tasks, including complex reasoning and autonomous agent decision-making. However, a fundamental limitation of these models is their static nature post-training, which hinders their ability to continuously adapt to novel, out-of-distribution (OOD) environments and tasks encountered during deployment. This limitation has motivated the development of "test-time self-evolution" techniques, particularly "experience-driven self-evolving agents."

Existing research in experience-driven self-evolution has largely focused on system-level design choices. This includes investigating various forms of experience representation (e.g., raw trajectories, workflows, executable skills, strategic principles) and developing mechanisms for autonomous experience extraction and efficient memory management. While these prompt-based methods have shown that injecting past experiences can enhance decision-making, their effectiveness is ultimately constrained by the underlying model's intrinsic capacities for abstraction, generalization, and in-context learning.

A more recent direction has involved the application of reinforcement learning (RL) to improve an LLM's ability to utilize experience. However, these methods typically optimize the experience utilization phase in isolation. They often rely on stronger external models or manually designed filtering heuristics to ensure the quality of the extracted experience, rather than optimizing the extraction process itself. This decoupled approach is identified as a limitation, as the quality of extracted experience directly influences the effectiveness of the RL dynamics for utilization. If extracted experience is noisy or unreliable, the policy may learn to ignore it, thus undermining the benefits of experience injection.

Evolving-RL addresses this identified gap by proposing an algorithmic framework that aims to jointly and end-to-end optimize both the experience extraction and experience utilization capabilities. By treating self-evolution as a unified process, this work positions itself as an advancement in the field of self-evolving agents and also contributes to experience-augmented reinforcement learning, where reusable patterns are internalized into model parameters. It contrasts with concurrent work that attributes co-evolution instability to parameter conflicts and opts for decoupled models, by demonstrating that stability can be achieved within a single unified model through specific controls.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an efficient algorithmic framework, named Evolving-RL, that jointly optimizes the experience extraction and utilization capabilities necessary for self-evolving agents. This objective is motivated by several identified limitations in the current landscape of LLM-based agents:

1. **Static Nature of LLMs:** Once trained, LLMs lack the inherent capacity for continuous adaptation to complex, unforeseen, and out-of-distribution tasks during deployment. This necessitates mechanisms for self-evolution.
2. **Bounded Effectiveness of Experience-Driven Agents:** While experience-driven agents aim to address the static nature by distilling and reusing past interactions, their performance is fundamentally limited by the underlying LLM's capacity to effectively abstract and generalize from these experiences. This includes the model's in-context learning abilities for both deriving and applying experience.
3. **Decoupled Optimization in Existing Methods:** Many current approaches to experience-driven self-evolution focus on system-level design (e.g., how experience is represented or managed) or only optimize the *utilization* of experience via reinforcement learning. They often neglect the inherent capability of the LLM to *extract* high-quality, reusable experience. When RL is applied, it typically enhances utilization in isolation, relying on external mechanisms or hand-crafted rules to guarantee the quality of extracted experience.
4. **Consequences of Decoupled Design:** The authors argue that extraction and utilization are not merely complementary but mutually constitutive. If experience extraction is not optimized alongside utilization, the quality of extracted experience can be inconsistent. Noisy or unreliable experience can negatively impact the reinforcement learning process for utilization, potentially leading the policy to converge to a degenerate behavior where it learns to ignore the provided, unreliable guidance. This undermines the core benefit of experience injection.

To address these motivations, Evolving-RL seeks to:
- Unify the optimization of experience extraction and utilization into a single, cohesive training paradigm.
- Center the learning process on experience extraction and rigorous evaluation, thereby generating two coordinated supervisory signals.
- Use these signals to optimize the extractor and solver components, which share the same underlying policy parameters, enabling their co-evolution.
- Instantiate experience as textual skills, which are procedural abstractions, to make the joint optimization tractable and interpretable.
- Demonstrate that this joint optimization leads to robust performance gains on out-of-distribution tasks and inherently functions as an experience-augmented RL algorithm, improving the base policy itself even without test-time skill injection.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Evolving-RL is designed as a unified algorithmic framework that jointly enhances both skill extraction and skill utilization capabilities within a single shared policy ($\pi_{\theta}$). The methodology centers on an online skill generation and evaluation process, producing coupled learning signals for optimizing both components.

**Roles and Shared Policy:**
The framework defines two main roles:
1. **Extractor:** Responsible for generating compact textual skills from completed source interactions. These skills abstract reusable decision rules, workflows, or error-handling strategies.
2. **Solver:** Takes a new task along with an injected skill and generates an action trajectory to complete the task.
Both the extractor and the solver are implemented using the same underlying Large Language Model policy, sharing its parameters.

**Rollout Structure:**
Each training iteration follows a specific sequence:
1. **Source Trajectory Rollout:** The solver, initially without any injected skill, interacts with a source task ($x_{src}$) sampled from the training distribution. This interaction produces a trajectory ($\tau$) and an environmental reward ($r_{src}$).
2. **Skill Extraction:** The obtained trajectory and reward form an "extraction state" ($s_e = (x_{src}, \tau, r_{src})$). Based on this state, the extractor component of the shared policy samples a set of N candidate textual skills, $E = \{e_1, \dots, e_N\}$.
3. **Generalization-Oriented Evaluation:**
 - **Downstream Task Retrieval:** A retrieval function (R) identifies K semantically related downstream tasks ($X_{ret}$) based on the embedding of the source task description. The source task itself is included in this set. Task embeddings are pre-computed using Qwen3-Embedding-4B.
 - **Skill-Conditioned Trajectories:** For each of the N candidate skills ($e_i$) and each of the K retrieved tasks ($x_j \in X_{ret}$), the solver generates a skill-conditioned trajectory ($t_{ij} \sim \pi_{\theta}(\cdot | x_j, e_i)$) and receives an environment reward ($r_{ij}$).
 - **Extractor Reward Calculation:** The reward for a candidate skill $e_i$ ($R_{e_i}$) is defined as its mean downstream performance across the K retrieved tasks: $R_{e_i} = \frac{1}{K} \sum_{j=1}^{K} r_{ij}$. This aggregates performance, promoting skills that are generally useful.

**Extractor Training:**
The extractor is optimized in an online manner. The N candidate skills from a single source interaction form a GRPO (Group-Normalized Policy Optimization) comparison group.
- **Advantage Estimation:** A group-normalized advantage $A_{e_i} = \frac{R_{e_i} - \text{mean}(\{R_{e_{i'}}\})}{\text{std}(\{R_{e_{i'}}\})}$ is computed for each skill, providing a relative measure of its quality within the group.
- **Loss Function ($L_e$):** The extractor loss is a GRPO-style objective incorporating an importance sampling ratio ($\rho_{e_i}$), KL regularization, and a specific entropy regularization term ($-\eta_e H(\pi_{\theta})$) with a *negative* coefficient. This negative coefficient is a key stability control mechanism, intended to penalize entropy growth and bias the extraction policy towards more deterministic predictions, countering the inherent high-entropy nature of textual skill generation and evaluation noise that could otherwise cause training collapse. A rule-based filter also assigns zero reward to skills containing abnormal characters.

**Solver Training:**
The solver's ability to utilize skills is optimized by reusing the N x K skill-conditioned trajectories collected during downstream evaluation.
- **Cross-Skill Group Advantage Estimation:** For each retrieved task $x_j$, the N skill-conditioned trajectories ($\{t_{ij}\}$), despite having heterogeneous prompts due to different injected skills, are grouped for comparison. A solver-side advantage $A_{s_{ij}} = \frac{r_{ij} - \text{mean}(\{r_{i'j}\})}{\text{std}(\{r_{i'j}\})}$ is computed. This normalization encourages the solver to exploit high-quality skills and develop robustness to spurious guidance.
- **Loss Function ($L_s$):** The solver loss is a GRPO-style objective that minimizes the clipped importance-sampled loss over all N x K trajectories, combined with a KL-divergence regularization term.
 - On Mind2Web, a turn-wise GRPO is applied, with step-level rewards ($r_{ij,t}$) for correct actions, while extractor rewards are computed using trajectory-level sums of these step rewards.

**Joint Objective and Co-Evolution Dynamics:**
The final training objective is a weighted combination of the extractor and solver losses: $L = \lambda_e L_e + \lambda_s L_s$. Since both losses operate on the same shared policy parameters, they are tightly coupled. This setup aims to:
- Incentivize the extractor to produce skills that are generally useful across diverse tasks.
- Train the solver with a realistic distribution of skill quality (both informative and noisy skills), fostering robustness and effective utilization.
- Create a closed loop where advancements in skill abstraction reinforce robust skill-conditioned execution, and vice-versa.

**Experimental Setup:**
- **Base Model:** Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct.
- **Benchmarks:** ALFWorld (text-based embodied tasks) and Mind2Web (grounded web navigation). Both benchmarks use explicit task-level splits (seen/unseen for ALFWorld; cross-task, cross-website, cross-domain for Mind2Web) to evaluate generalization.
- **Metrics:** Success Rate (ALFWorld, Mind2Web) and Action Accuracy (Mind2Web).
- **Baselines:** Prompt-based methods (ExpeL, Memento, ReasoningBank) and RL-based methods (GRPO, SkillRL).
- **Hardware:** 8 NVIDIA H800 GPUs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on ALFWorld and Mind2Web demonstrated that Evolving-RL consistently outperformed various baselines, supporting its claims regarding enhanced self-evolution and its efficacy as an experience-augmented RL algorithm.

**Overall Performance:**
- Evolving-RL exhibited superior performance across both benchmarks, confirming its ability to improve test-time self-evolution by strengthening both skill extraction and utilization.
- It also proved effective as an experience-augmented RL method, enhancing the underlying policy even without explicit skill injection during evaluation.

**ALFWorld Results (Table 1):**
- Evolving-RL (with skills) achieved the highest overall success rate of 96.0%, surpassing all baselines.
- The performance gains were particularly notable on *unseen* tasks, where Evolving-RL (w/ skills) achieved an 88.6% success rate, a significant increase from the GRPO (w/ skills) baseline of 44.6%. This represents approximately a 98.7% relative improvement.
- Even when skills were *not* injected at test time, Evolving-RL (w/o skills) achieved an 81.1% success rate on unseen tasks and an overall success rate of 93.1%, substantially outperforming GRPO baselines (33.7% and 79.9% respectively). This indicates that the training process enabled the policy to internalize reusable procedural patterns.

**Mind2Web Results (Table 2):**
- In the more challenging and noisy web environment of Mind2Web, Evolving-RL maintained a qualitative trend of improvement over GRPO.
- Evolving-RL (w/ skills) achieved an overall action accuracy of 30.87% and a success rate of 1.99%, representing the highest performance.
- Compared to GRPO (w/ skills), Evolving-RL (w/ skills) yielded relative improvements in action accuracy of 45.9% (cross-task), 42.8% (cross-website), and 29.4% (cross-domain).
- Similar to ALFWorld, Evolving-RL (w/o skills) also demonstrated superior performance over GRPO baselines, further supporting the internalization of experience.
- An observation was noted on the cross-website split where skill injection did not always lead to a clear improvement, potentially due to the substantial distribution shift making the extraction of useful skills difficult for these specific trajectories.

**Ablations and Analysis:**

1. **Drivers of Generalization Gains (Table 3):**
 - The **solver-only objective** (training utilization without co-evolving extraction) significantly improved generalization compared to GRPO, suggesting that exposure to experience-augmented contexts during RL training allows the policy to internalize procedural regularities. However, test-time skill injection offered no further benefit, implying the policy learned to become insensitive to potentially unreliable skills from a non-optimized extractor.
 - The **extractor-only objective** (optimizing extraction in isolation) showed some improvement in the base model's problem-solving capabilities, but the skill-extraction capability tended to overfit to seen trajectories, yielding minimal improvement on unseen tasks.
 - These findings collectively indicate that **co-evolution** is necessary to harmonize both components, overcoming the limitations of isolated optimization and achieving robust, generalizable performance.

2. **Overfitting to Skill Context (Figure 3):**
 - Evaluation with irrelevant skills showed performance comparable to the "no skill" baseline, without degradation.
 - Providing relevant skills, however, led to a distinct improvement.
 - These results suggest that the observed performance gains are genuinely due to the semantic content of the provided skills, rather than merely an artifact of overfitting to the presence or format of context prompts. The model also demonstrated robustness by effectively ignoring uninformative signals.

3. **Reusability of Extracted Skills (Table 4):**
 - Skills extracted by Evolving-RL-trained models exhibited strong transferability, leading to substantially larger performance gains when injected into *other* policies (e.g., the Base Model or a GRPO-trained model) compared to skills self-extracted by the evaluation policy itself.
 - For instance, injecting Evolving-RL skills into the Base Model improved its overall success rate from 45.5% to 60.4%.
 - This indicates that Evolving-RL enhances the *quality* of skill extraction itself, producing broadly reusable skills rather than merely inducing a private communication protocol between the extractor and solver within the same model.
 - A case study (Figure 5) further illustrated that Evolving-RL extracted concise, coherent, and procedurally correct skills, while the base model extracted skills contained procedural flaws and omitted critical actions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Evolving-RL presents a significant contribution to the development of autonomous agents, particularly those leveraging large language models. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Addressing LLM Static Limitations:** The work directly tackles a core limitation of LLMs: their inability to continuously adapt to new or out-of-distribution tasks post-training. By enabling agents to explicitly learn how to extract and utilize experience, Evolving-RL moves towards more dynamic and adaptable LLM-based systems.
2. **Unified Optimization Paradigm:** By proposing an end-to-end framework that jointly optimizes both experience extraction and utilization, Evolving-RL fills a critical gap in the existing literature. Prior work often decoupled these processes, leading to potential inconsistencies or suboptimal performance due to the reliance on unoptimized or external skill generation mechanisms. The coordinated co-evolution within a single shared policy is a novel and effective approach.
3. **Enhanced Generalization Capabilities:** The demonstrated significant performance gains, especially on unseen and out-of-distribution tasks across diverse environments like ALFWorld and Mind2Web, highlight the framework's ability to foster robust generalization. This is crucial for real-world deployment where agents must operate in dynamic and unpredictable settings.
4. **Dual Value as Experience-Augmented RL:** Beyond enabling explicit test-time self-evolution through skill injection, Evolving-RL inherently functions as an experience-augmented reinforcement learning algorithm. By repeatedly exposing the policy to experience-conditioned contexts during training, it allows the model to internalize reusable procedural patterns directly into its parameters. This means the underlying policy itself becomes more capable and generalizable, even without needing external skill injection during inference.
5. **Improved Skill Quality and Transferability:** The ablations confirmed that Evolving-RL enhances the intrinsic quality of extracted skills, making them broadly reusable across different policies. This is a vital outcome, indicating that the framework learns generalizable knowledge rather than just a policy-specific communication protocol. The ability to produce high-quality, transferable skills could accelerate the development of shared skill libraries for various agent architectures.
6. **Robustness to Imperfect Information:** The finding that the policy can effectively ignore irrelevant or uninformative skills without performance degradation demonstrates a degree of robustness. This is a practical advantage in real-world scenarios where extracted experiences may not always be perfectly applicable or high-quality.
7. **Foundation for Future Agent Evolution:** Evolving-RL provides a solid algorithmic foundation for future research in autonomous agent learning. The authors identify avenues for future work, such as integrating the framework with more sophisticated experience management and retrieval mechanisms, and reducing evaluation noise. These directions suggest a clear path for building increasingly intelligent and self-improving agents.

In summary, Evolving-RL advances the state of the art in LLM-based agents by providing a principled and effective method for end-to-end optimization of self-evolving capabilities, with implications for enhanced generalization, robustness, and the development of more capable artificial intelligence systems.
