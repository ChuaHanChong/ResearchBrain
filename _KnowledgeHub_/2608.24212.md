# NeoWorld-Pro: Programming Interactive Scenes from Monocular Images for Embodied Simulation

> [!summary] Summary
> NeoWorld-Pro turns a single RGB image into a simulation-ready interactive scene by having multimodal LLMs write executable Blender programs for object geometry, articulation and physics. Two physics-in-the-loop stages then refine the result: an object-level critic loop and a scene-level Cross-Entropy Method (CEM) search in Isaac Sim. It beats open-loop baselines, reaching 0% penetration and 100% stability on its benchmark.

## Problem

- Most prior image-to-URDF work reconstructs single assets and ignores scene-level structure, so inter-object spatial and physical dependencies needed for embodied interaction are not captured.
- Existing methods depend on curated asset libraries or category-level priors, which limits generalization to out-of-distribution objects and to hidden structures under heavy occlusion.
- Reconstruction pipelines are open-loop: simulation is used only post hoc, so articulation failures, penetration and unstable layouts are never corrected during generation.

## Method

- ==Procedural asset programming==: an ==MLLM Programmer== writes ==Blender== geometry, articulation and ==URDF==-export scripts per object crop, with runtime debugging and ==critic-refine loops==, and infers occluded structure from commonsense.
- ==Object-level physics loop==: URDFs are simulated in ==Isaac Sim== under free-fall and force perturbation, and a ==video MLLM critic== guides constrained edits to inertia, joint limits, damping and collisions.
- ==Scene-level CEM refinement==: a ==diagonal Gaussian== over per-object planar shift, yaw and log-scale is optimized with a reward mixing ==MLLM semantic score==, ==penetration penalty== and ==free-fall drift penalty==.

## Results

- Object level: best LPIPS (**0.1989**), CLIP (**0.9042**), Uni3D (**0.2812**) and F-scores (**25.77** and **67.06**). Joint miss rate falls to **11.83%** versus **22.45%** for Articraft, the strongest baseline.
- Scene level on the full benchmark: **0.00%** inter-object and part penetration and **100%** scene and object stability. VIGA gets **56.67%** scene penetration and **23.33%** scene stability. SAGE gets **6.67%** and **70.00%**.
- Ablations: full model reaches **92.85%** task success. Removing the simulator loop drops it to **44.05%**, removing the semantic reward to **48.81%**, and removing the penetration penalty to **76.47%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Executable code as the intermediate representation is editable, inspectable and simulatable, so MLLM commonsense can fill occluded structure without dataset-specific training.
> - Split refinement by variable type: MLLM code edits for discrete structure such as joints and inertia, and derivative-free simulator search for continuous pose and scale that MLLMs handle poorly.
> - Physics rewards and semantic scoring are complementary: simulator signals alone miss arrangement and affordances, while semantic scoring alone misses collisions and instability, so both are needed.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Embodied AI and robotics increasingly relies on high-quality, interactive simulation environments. These environments, often referred to as digital twins, necessitate not only accurate 3D geometry but also precise kinematic structures, realistic physical properties, and simulation-ready inter-object relationships to facilitate Real-to-Sim-to-Real transfer for robot learning. However, the manual construction of such intricate simulation assets is labor-intensive and does not scale to the diverse and complex environments encountered in the real world. Automating the transition from raw visual observations to physically functional and interactive simulation scenes has therefore become a significant area of research.

Existing research approaches in this domain face several limitations. Firstly, much of the prior work primarily focuses on single-asset image-to-URDF reconstruction, which often neglects scene-level modeling. This limits the capability to capture embodied interactions and the complex spatial and physical dependencies among multiple objects within a scene. Secondly, many current methods depend on extensive curated asset libraries or rigid category-level priors. This reliance imposes substantial data requirements and can hinder generalization to out-of-distribution scenarios, as models may struggle with novel object categories or configurations not present in their training data. Thirdly, and a notable constraint, existing pipelines are typically open-loop. These methods treat reconstruction as a unidirectional inference task, lacking a mechanism for corrective feedback. Consequently, physical inconsistencies in geometry, articulation, or scene composition often remain unresolved, leading to generated environments that are not consistently "simulation-ready" for high-precision downstream tasks. While recent efforts like NeoWorld aim to construct explorable virtual worlds from single images, their hybrid neural representations offer limited explicit control over critical simulation parameters like geometry, articulation, and physical properties.

NeoWorld-Pro positions itself within this landscape by addressing these identified bottlenecks. It proposes a framework that deviates from traditional dense reconstruction methods, which primarily focus on per-pixel alignment and unstructured geometric entities like point clouds or meshes. Instead, NeoWorld-Pro introduces a program-centric paradigm that prioritizes functional and physical plausibility. By leveraging the advanced capabilities of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) for code synthesis and incorporating a physics-in-the-loop refinement mechanism, the work aims to generate explicit, executable world representations. This approach is designed to overcome the limitations of dataset-specific priors and open-loop inference, thereby advancing the automation of creating robust and interactive simulation scenes from monocular imagery.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a framework, NeoWorld-Pro, that can transform a single RGB image into a high-quality, interactive, and simulation-ready 3D environment. This involves generating scenes that not only accurately mirror the visual appearance of the real world but also possess correct kinematic structures, physical properties, and functional inter-object relationships necessary for embodied AI and robotic applications.

The motivation behind NeoWorld-Pro stems from the critical need for enhanced simulation assets in the rapidly advancing fields of embodied AI and robotics. Specifically, the authors identify several key challenges that necessitate a novel approach:
- **Demand for Digital Twins:** The increasing sophistication of robot learning algorithms requires faithful digital twins that can accurately represent real-world physical interactions. Manually constructing these complex assets is prohibitively time-consuming and expensive, making an automated "Real-to-Sim" transition crucial.
- **Limitations of Current Reconstruction Methods:** Existing methods are constrained in three main areas:
 - **Lack of Scene-Level Interactivity:** Most approaches focus on reconstructing individual objects (image-to-URDF) rather than holistic scenes that capture the intricate physical and spatial dependencies required for embodied interaction among multiple objects.
 - **Dependency on Priors and Libraries:** Current systems often rely on extensive, curated asset libraries or rigid, category-specific priors. This limits their generalization capabilities, particularly when encountering novel or out-of-distribution objects and configurations.
 - **Open-Loop Inference:** A critical issue is the lack of feedback mechanisms in existing pipelines. By treating reconstruction as a one-way inference process, these methods cannot iteratively correct physical inconsistencies in geometry, articulation, or scene composition, often leading to environments that are not genuinely "simulation-ready" for precise tasks.
- **Need for Executable and Editable Representations:** Previous work, such as NeoWorld, utilized hybrid neural representations which, while capable of generating explorable virtual worlds, offered limited explicit control over simulation-critical properties. This motivated the development of an explicit, executable world representation that allows for structured editing and physics-based refinement.

In response to these motivations, NeoWorld-Pro aims to provide a solution by reformulating monocular scene reconstruction as procedural programming. The framework seeks to create executable programs that explicitly define object geometry, articulation, and physical properties, and then leverage a physics-in-the-loop mechanism to refine these programs, ensuring physically plausible articulations, valid object compositions, correct interactions, and accurate spatial relationships. This is intended to enable complex downstream tasks like stable stacking and fine-grained manipulation, which require a high degree of physical consistency in the simulated environment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

NeoWorld-Pro reformulates monocular scene reconstruction by shifting from unstructured geometric generation to a structured, program-centric paradigm. The methodology leverages Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) to synthesize executable programs, which are then refined through a physics-in-the-loop mechanism. The core idea is to represent scenes as compositions of executable programs, transforming visual observations into simulation-ready functional entities.

The framework processes a single RGB image (I) to generate a simulation-ready interactive scene, output as a collection of articulated object Universal Robot Description Format (URDF) models assembled within a Universal Scene Description (USD) stage (S). This process involves the inference of part-level geometry, kinematics, and spatial relationships, ensuring their mutual consistency and physical stability within a simulator.

The methodology is structured around two core innovations:
1. **Asset reconstruction via procedural code synthesis:** MLLMs are utilized to synthesize executable Blender Python programs as an intermediate representation. These programs are then compiled into standard URDF and USD assets. This program-centric approach offers executability for direct physical simulation, interpretability for MLLM-based inspection, and editability for iterative refinement. By drawing on the commonsense priors of MLLMs, NeoWorld-Pro can infer occluded internal structures and missing physical attributes without reliance on dataset-specific category priors.
2. **Physics-in-the-loop scene refinement:** This closed-loop mechanism addresses the physical validity and simulation readiness of the generated scene. It operates at two levels: object-level and scene-level. The generated programs are executed in a physics engine (e.g., Isaac Sim), and dynamic feedback such as collision events, instability, and articulation failures is collected. This feedback is then used by the MLLM to iteratively refine the code.

The NeoWorld-Pro pipeline consists of the following detailed steps:

#### 3.1. Object-Level Procedural Programming and Articulation Refinement

This stage focuses on reconstructing physically consistent articulated objects and their corresponding scene layout from a single RGB image, following a generate-critic-refine paradigm.

- **Step 1: Scene parsing:** An MLLM-Reasoner (e.g., Qwen3.6-Plus) processes the input image to predict: (i) the scene hierarchy tree, (ii) 2D and 3D bounding boxes for objects, and (iii) semantic attributes (e.g., whether an object acts as environmental support or is partially occluded). This joint prediction aims to use relational information (e.g., a cup on a table) to constrain object localization and layout estimation. An MLLM-Critic then evaluates these predictions for tightness, completeness, and hierarchical consistency. Object crops are then passed to the next stage.
- **Step 2: Procedural asset programming:** For each segmented object crop, an MLLM-Programmer (e.g., GPT-5.5) sequentially generates three types of executable code: (i) a Blender geometry program, (ii) an articulation program, and (iii) a URDF export script. Each generation step includes a runtime debugging loop to resolve execution failures, followed by a critic-refine loop where rendered outputs are compared against the input crop. This method prioritizes executable code over direct mesh generation, leveraging the MLLM's commonsense reasoning to infer occluded structures (e.g., a drawer cavity from its handle) without dataset-specific priors. The resulting URDFs are structurally valid and articulation-aware, but their physical behavior remains unverified.
- **Step 3: Object-level physics-in-the-loop refinement:** To ensure physical validity, the generated URDFs are simulated individually in Isaac Sim under two standardized physics scenarios: free-fall and force-perturbation. The free-fall simulation identifies structural issues like incorrect mass distributions, unstable collision geometries, object penetration, or unintended disassembly. The force-perturbation simulation verifies joint mobility and detects invalid articulation behavior. A video-based MLLM-Critic analyzes keyframes from these simulations to generate targeted refinement suggestions. The MLLM-Reasoner then performs localized edits, modifying inertial parameters, joint limits, damping coefficients, object origins, and collision geometries. This constrained refinement aims to preserve the validated kinematic topology and visual geometry while enhancing physical executability. At the conclusion of this step, each reconstructed object is individually physically consistent.

#### 3.2. Scene-Level Composition and CEM Refinement

This stage integrates the individually refined URDF assets into a complete and refined USD scene, addressing inter-object relationships.

- **Step 4: Scene composition:** The initial scene is constructed deterministically by traversing the predicted scene tree. Horizontal coordinates (x, y) are derived from the parsed spatial layout. Vertical coordinates are determined by aligning each child object's bottom surface with its parent's top surface. This strategy provides a geometrically consistent initialization, minimizing initial floating or inter-object penetration. Non-environment objects are instantiated as USD references, retaining their URDF articulation properties, while environment supports are static primitives. Despite this deterministic initialization, physical artifacts such as inter-object penetration, unstable stacking, scale inconsistency, or incorrect relative orientations can still arise.
- **Step 5: CEM-based scene refinement:** A continuous optimization process, utilizing the Cross-Entropy Method (CEM), refines scene assembly variables. For each non-environment object, a diagonal Gaussian distribution is maintained over a 4D perturbation vector z_i = (Δx_i, Δy_i, Δψ_i, log s_i), representing planar translation, yaw rotation, and isotropic log-scale in parent coordinates. The vertical axis is excluded to enforce scene-tree snapping constraints. In each iteration, N candidate layouts are sampled from the Gaussian distribution, instantiated in USD, and evaluated via free-fall rollouts in Isaac Sim. The evaluation uses a hybrid reward function:
 R(z) = w_sem * S_sem(z; I) - w_pen * D_pen(z) - w_drift * D_drift(z).
 Here, D_pen quantifies inter-object penetration using Signed Distance Function (SDF) values, and D_drift measures pose drift during free-fall, encouraging stacking stability. S_sem is a semantic score from an MLLM (e.g., GPT-5.5) that assesses rendered candidates against the input image, focusing on relational cues like depth ordering, relative scale, and spatial consistency. The top-K candidates (where K = ρN) are then used to update the mean and variance of the Gaussian distribution. This process iterates until convergence or a fixed budget. This two-level physics-in-the-loop framework jointly enforces articulation correctness and global spatial consistency, differentiating it from prior open-loop approaches.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates NeoWorld-Pro across object-level reconstruction, scene-level reconstruction and assembly, and downstream manipulation tasks, using both a public articulated-object benchmark and a newly constructed synthetic multi-object simulation benchmark.

#### 4.1. Object-Level Reconstruction Results of Articulated Assets

- **Qualitative Analysis:** Visual comparisons (Figure 3) demonstrate that NeoWorld-Pro achieves more accurate recovery of articulated joints and produces more faithful geometric reconstructions compared to existing methods such as PhysX-Anything, URDF-Anything+, Articulate-Anything, and Articraft. The generated objects exhibit higher fidelity in geometry, appearance, and articulation structure.
- **Quantitative Analysis (Table 1 & 2):**
 - **Appearance Evaluation:** NeoWorld-Pro achieved competitive SSIM scores (0.8894) and the best LPIPS (0.1989) and CLIP (0.9042) similarity scores, indicating strong photometric rendering fidelity and semantic similarity to the input images.
 - **Geometry Evaluation:** The method obtained the highest F-scores at both 0.01 (25.77) and 0.05 (67.06) thresholds, along with the second-lowest Chamfer distance (0.1809), signifying high mesh reconstruction quality. It also provided the strongest cross-modal 3D alignment according to Uni3D similarity (0.2812).
 - **Articulation Evaluation:** NeoWorld-Pro significantly improved joint inference quality (Table 2). It achieved a substantially lower joint miss rate (11.83%), more correct joint predictions, and reduced errors in joint type identification (18.78%), axis orientation (28.52%), and pivot position (0.19). This indicates a more accurate, complete, and physically consistent object-level understanding, benefiting from the program-centric and physics-in-the-loop refinement.

#### 4.2. Scene-Level Reconstruction and Assembly Results

- **Qualitative Analysis:** (Figure 4 & 5) NeoWorld-Pro demonstrated superior performance in reconstructing multi-object scenes. It preserved the complete set of foreground objects and more accurately recovered their relative scales, orientations, and pairwise spatial relationships across diverse environments (tabletop, appliance, workspace, and real-world images). Competing methods frequently omitted objects, substituted scene elements, or distorted the input layout, with these failures being more pronounced in complex scenes. The framework also showed generalization to real-world appearances, clutter, illumination variations, and partial occlusions, while maintaining functional articulations in the reconstructed movable components.
- **Quantitative Analysis (Table 3):**
 - **Physical Consistency:** NeoWorld-Pro achieved zero inter-object collisions (0.00% S-Pen) and zero part-level self-penetration (0.00% P-Pen). It maintained 100.00% stability at both scene and object levels (S-Stab, O-Stab) under gravity in simulation. This performance significantly surpassed baselines like VIGA, SAGE, and TabletopGen, which exhibited considerable penetration and stability issues.
 - **Semantic and Functional Fidelity:** The method achieved the highest CLIP (0.9128 on full benchmark) and DINOv2 (0.7855 on full benchmark) similarity scores, indicating strong semantic and visual consistency with the input images. MLLM-based judgments also rated NeoWorld-Pro highest in scene completeness (6.20), layout fidelity (5.35), and functional feasibility (5.58), confirming its ability to produce scenes that support intended physical interactions.

#### 4.3. Image-to-Policy Demonstration for Manipulation Tasks

The reconstructed scenes were validated through downstream manipulation tasks, such as the test-tube placement task (Figure 1). NeoWorld-Pro successfully reconstructed objects and their relative configurations. Using an MLLM to specify initial end-effector positions and displacements for manipulation in Isaac Sim, the framework demonstrated its ability to support physically grounded tasks. The iterative refinement of the scene layout, using task success as a feedback signal, highlighted that the reconstructed scenes enable successful manipulation, moving beyond mere visual resemblance.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies investigated the contribution of various components:
- **Object-Level Refinement:** Removing either the geometry or articulation critics reduced joint recovery success rates (e.g., 76.47% without geometry refine critic, 83.33% without articulation refine critic, compared to 92.85% for full NeoWorld-Pro), demonstrating their importance.
- **Scene-Level Refinement:**
 - Removing the MLLM-based semantic score (w/o S_sem) resulted in a lower success rate (48.81%), indicating that physical rewards alone are insufficient for capturing high-level semantics.
 - Operating in an open-loop manner (w/o simulator-critic), without closed-loop simulation feedback, significantly reduced success (44.05%), confirming that physical validation is essential for correcting errors in parsing and generation that lead to penetration, instability, and incorrect articulation.
 - Ablating the physical terms in the CEM reward function, such as the inter-object collision penalty (w/o D_pen, 76.47% success) or the free-fall stability penalty (w/o D_drift, 88.24% success), confirmed their role in enforcing collision-free placement and stable support relationships for task success.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

NeoWorld-Pro represents a notable contribution to the fields of embodied AI and robotics by advancing the automated generation of simulation-ready interactive scenes from monocular images. The significance of this work lies in its comprehensive approach to addressing key limitations of prior methods.

The primary contributions of this work are:
- **Novel Formulation of Scene Construction:** NeoWorld-Pro formulates monocular scene construction as MLLM-driven program synthesis. This approach reduces the reliance on extensive, specific asset databases and supervised category-level priors, which enhances the framework's scalability and generalization capabilities to novel or out-of-distribution scenarios.
- **Physics-in-the-Loop Refinement Pipeline:** The introduction of a two-level physics-in-the-loop scene refinement pipeline is a core innovation. By iteratively leveraging simulation feedback at both object and scene levels, the framework systematically improves intra-object articulations and inter-object relationships, ensuring physical plausibility and stability. This closed-loop mechanism is crucial for generating truly "simulation-ready" assets, distinguishing it from open-loop approaches that often produce physically inconsistent outputs.
- **Development of a Simulation Benchmark:** The construction of a new simulation benchmark, comprising 90 articulated object categories and 30 scenes in Universal Scene Description (USD) format, provides a valuable resource for the research community. This benchmark facilitates rigorous evaluation beyond mere reconstruction quality, including assessment of downstream manipulation tasks, which is essential for advancing embodied AI research.

The potential impact of NeoWorld-Pro is substantial:
- **Enhanced Embodied AI and Robotics Development:** By providing a method to automatically generate high-quality, physically consistent, and interactive simulation environments, NeoWorld-Pro can accelerate the development and testing of robot learning algorithms. This reduces the significant manual effort traditionally required to create such digital assets, making simulation-based research more accessible and scalable.
- **Improved Real-to-Sim Transfer:** The framework's emphasis on physical grounding and simulation readiness directly supports more effective "Real-to-Sim-to-Real" transfer, where policies learned in simulation can be more reliably deployed on physical robots.
- **Generalization to Diverse Scenarios:** The MLLM-driven program synthesis, combined with commonsense reasoning, enables the framework to infer occluded structures and physical attributes without requiring explicit training on every possible object category. This promotes greater generalization to novel and cluttered real-world scenes.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The program-centric representation offers an interpretable and editable format for generated scenes, which could serve as a foundation for more advanced scene understanding, editing, and interactive agent training.

Despite its advancements, NeoWorld-Pro has certain limitations. The current framework is primarily focused on rigid and articulated objects under quasi-static physics. It does not yet model highly deformable or continuous media, such as cloth, fluids, or granular materials, whose dynamics are considerably more complex and not readily represented by URDF-style structures. Additionally, the current evaluation is limited to scenes containing fewer than 10 objects. Addressing these limitations, by extending the framework to support deformable assets, richer material properties, and denser, more complex scenes, represents important directions for future research.
