# Better Reasoning with Less Data: Enhancing VLMs Through Unified Modality Scoring

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed SCALE, an automated pipeline that curates high-quality multimodal instruction tuning datasets by integrating unimodal and cross-modal quality assessments. This method allows Visual Language Models trained on less than 10% of the original data to achieve superior performance compared to models trained on full, uncurated datasets across various benchmarks.

## Problem

- Visual Language Model instruction tuning datasets suffer from noisy image-text correspondences.
- Existing datasets contain ambiguous or misleading textual descriptions, degrading overall data quality.
- Manually curating large-scale, high-quality multimodal datasets is prohibitively expensive and time-consuming.

## Method

- Introduced SCALE, a ==five-stage automated pipeline== for comprehensive data quality assessment, integrating ==unimodal (image and text) and multimodal alignment evaluations==.
- Utilized large language models (Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct) for predicting data tasks and rating text and multimodal quality.
- Employed a lightweight VLM (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) to generate ==general and task-specific image captions==, unifying visual and linguistic representations into a text-only format for streamlined multimodal comparison.

## Results

- Models fine-tuned on less than **10%** of SCALE-curated data consistently outperformed models trained on the full **500K** instruction tuning dataset.
- SCALE achieved an average score of **84.23** across various benchmarks, surpassing the full data baseline (**83.94**) and all other data selection baselines.
- The internal task prediction component using Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct achieved over **80%** accuracy for all defined sub-tasks, demonstrating its robustness in categorizing data.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Training VLMs on a significantly smaller, quality-curated subset of data can lead to superior performance compared to using the entire, uncurated dataset.
> - Unimodal data quality assessments (image-only or text-only) are insufficient and can bias selection by overlooking crucial, task-specific aspects or discarding valuable lower-fidelity samples.
> - Generated image captions effectively transform complex image-text multimodal alignment into a unified text modality, simplifying the evaluation process and improving efficiency.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancements in Visual Language Models (VLMs), largely driven by visual instruction tuning and other post-training techniques, have enabled impressive capabilities in interpreting visual content and following complex instructions. However, the effectiveness of these models is fundamentally tied to the **quality and scale of their training data**. This paper positions itself at the critical intersection of VLM development and data curation, addressing a key bottleneck in the field.

The broader research landscape for VLMs is characterized by:
- **The rise of large-scale datasets:** Projects like LLaVA, ShareGPT4Video, and Cambrian-1 aim to provide diverse and comprehensive visual-language pairs.
- **Challenges in data quality:** Despite efforts, these datasets frequently suffer from "noisy image-text correspondences" and "ambiguous or misleading text," as highlighted by the authors (Figure 1). This includes imprecise GPT-generated descriptions or misalignments in human-curated data, which can lead to misinterpretation and hinder model generalization.
- **Growing interest in data-centric AI:** There is a strong movement towards improving AI systems by focusing on data quality rather than solely model architecture or scale. This includes methods for dataset pruning, distillation, and influence-based data selection.

**Related work** discussed in the paper (Section 2) falls into several categories:
- **Unimodal Data Quality Evaluation:** Prior research has focused on assessing image quality (e.g., TracIn, MoSo, RDED) and text quality (e.g., removing duplicates, heuristic filtering, LESS, DS2) independently. While valuable, these methods often overlook the multimodal interplay.
- **Multi-Modality (Image & Text) SFT Data Quality Evaluation:** Efforts like CLIP, ALBEF, BLIP, and BLIP-2 have focused on aligning image and text embeddings, and on generating robust multimodal representations. Some recent works (ICM-Assistant, GeoReasoner) use prior knowledge to maintain dataset quality. However, these often concentrate on alignment *mechanisms* rather than systematic *data selection pipelines* that combine both unimodal and cross-modal quality assessments for instruction tuning.

This paper distinguishes itself by proposing **SCALE**, an automated, quality-driven data selection pipeline that explicitly integrates both single-modality data quality assessment and cross-modality alignment evaluation. Unlike previous methods that might focus on one aspect (e.g., embedding similarity), SCALE offers a holistic approach. A key innovation is its strategy to **unify visual and linguistic representations through generated captions**, effectively transferring the complex image-text multimodal alignment task into a more manageable text-only comparison. This approach directly addresses the "noisy alignments" and "ambiguous text" challenges, aiming to achieve "better reasoning with less data," a crucial goal for sustainable and efficient AI development. By focusing on identifying and selecting *high-quality examples* from potentially noisy, large datasets, SCALE fits perfectly within the data-centric AI paradigm and contributes directly to improving the robustness and performance of VLMs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The motivation for this research stems directly from two critical challenges observed in current VLM instruction tuning datasets:
1. **Noisy Image-Text Alignment:** Existing datasets often suffer from inaccurate or misaligned correspondences between images and their associated text. This can lead to models learning incorrect associations, propagating inaccurate knowledge, and ultimately hindering their understanding and generalization capabilities.
2. **Compromised Dataset Integrity due to Ambiguous or Misleading Text:** Beyond direct misalignment, the textual descriptions themselves can be ambiguous, vague, or misleading, obscuring the visual content and degrading the overall quality of the dataset. Poor image clarity or incoherent text further exacerbates this issue.

These challenges collectively mean that despite the availability of large-scale datasets, their **effectiveness is highly dependent on achieving precise recognition and accurate reasoning**, which is undermined by low-quality data. The high cost of curating such large, high-quality datasets manually further complicates the issue.

Given these motivations, the key objectives of the paper are:
- **To propose SCALE (Single modality data quality and Cross modality ALignment Evaluation):** A novel, quality-driven data selection pipeline designed specifically for VLM instruction tuning datasets.
- **To integrate a comprehensive cross-modality assessment framework:** This framework aims to assign each data entry to an appropriate vision-language task, generate diverse captions (general and task-specific), and then evaluate critical attributes like alignment, clarity, task rarity, text coherence, and image clarity based on these generated captions.
- **To demonstrate the "less is more" hypothesis:** Show that by scaling down original instruction tuning data significantly (to less than 10%), models trained on the SCALE-curated subset can surpass the performance of models trained on full datasets, especially in complex tasks like mathematical reasoning and discipline-specific knowledge.
- **To provide new insights into unimodal quality assessment:** Reveal that unimodal assessment methods alone are insufficient, often underestimating crucial samples or discarding valuable lower-quality instances that contribute to model robustness.
- **To offer a novel approach for multimodal alignment:** Highlight that appropriately generated image captions can efficiently transform the image-text multimodal task into a unified text modality, simplifying assessment.

In essence, the authors aim to build more robust, generalizable, and efficient VLMs by addressing the fundamental problem of data quality through an automated, intelligent curation process.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the paper's methodology is **SCALE (Single modality data quality and Cross modality ALignment Evaluation)**, an automated, quality-driven data selection pipeline for VLM instruction tuning datasets. The pipeline is designed to assess data quality comprehensively across both unimodal (image and text individually) and multimodal (image-text alignment) dimensions.

**A. Data-Quality Metrics:**
The authors define a set of metrics across three categories (Table 1):
- **Text Quality (Assessed by Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct):**
 - **Informativeness (INFO):** Measures clarity, contextual relevance, and novelty.
 - **Complexity (CPXT):** Evaluates syntactic and semantic sophistication.
 - **Completeness (CPLT):** Assesses if all expected information elements are covered.
- **Image Quality (Assessed by zhangzicheng/q-sit model):**
 - **Blur (BLUR):** Quantifies loss of sharpness.
 - **Noise (NOISE):** Measures random artifacts obscuring details.
- **Multimodal Quality (Generated via Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct for captions, rated by Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct):**
 - **Clarity (CLR):** Precision and unambiguity of text in conveying visual content.
 - **Relevance (REL):** Degree to which text accurately reflects and complements image content.
 - **Task Rarity (TR):** Novelty of the image-text pair's underlying task, promoting diversity.

**B. Data Selection Pipeline (5 Stages):**
The pipeline systematically processes each data entry (image-text pair) through five sequential stages (Figure 2):

1. **Unimodal Quality Assessment:**
 - **Image Quality Assessment (IQA):** The `zhangzicheng/q-sit` model is used to assess blurriness and noisiness of the image, yielding an image score ($S_I$).
 - **Text Quality Assessment (TQA):** The `Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct` model (a powerful LLM) is prompted to evaluate the informativeness, complexity, and completeness of the text, producing a text score ($S_T$).

2. **Data Task Prediction:**
 - To provide context for multimodal assessment, the `Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct` model is used to predict the specific task category and sub-task category of each data entry based on its text and a predefined list of task definitions (derived from MMBench). This stage uses few-shot prompting for calibration.

3. **Visual Understanding:**
 - A lightweight VLM, `Qwen2.5-VL-7B`, acts as a "captioner." It generates two types of captions for the image:
 - **General Caption ($T_{general}$):** Describes the overall scene, environment, background, and artistic style.
 - **Task-Specific Caption ($T_{specific}$):** Focuses on high-value aspects of the image relevant to the predicted task (e.g., human interactions, object locations).
 - This stage is crucial as it **unifies the visual and linguistic representations into a text-only format**, enabling direct comparison with the original text description.

4. **Multimodal Quality Assessment:**
 - The `Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct` model serves as a multimodal rating judge. It compares the original text entry (T) with the generated general ($T_{general}$) and task-specific ($T_{specific}$) captions.
 - It assesses the **Clarity** (ambiguity of text w.r.t. captions), **Relevance** (semantic alignment/factual errors), and **Task Rarity** (to promote diversity). This yields a multimodal score ($S_{MM}$).

5. **Data Ranking and Selection:**
 - A composite quality score ($S$) is calculated for each entry using a weighted sum: $S = 0.2S_I + 0.2S_T + 0.6S_{MM}$. The higher weight on multimodal quality reflects its importance in VLM instruction tuning.
 - Entries are sorted by $S$ in descending order, and the **top 10 percentile** are selected for downstream fine-tuning. This ensures a balanced emphasis on both unimodal and multimodal quality, resulting in a significantly reduced, yet higher-quality, dataset.

The systematic nature of SCALE, particularly the innovative use of LLMs for comprehensive unimodal and multimodal scoring, combined with VLM-generated captions to bridge modalities, forms a robust and automated approach to data curation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results powerfully validate SCALE's effectiveness in enhancing VLM performance with significantly less data.

1. **"Less is More" Hypothesis Confirmed:**
 - Models fine-tuned on a **less than 10% subset** of the original instruction tuning data, curated by SCALE, **surpassed the performance of models trained on the full 500K dataset**.
 - Specifically, SCALE achieved an average score of 84.23 across various benchmarks, outperforming the full data baseline (83.94) (Table 4).
 - Notable gains were observed in challenging tasks:
 - **CRPE Relation:** 77.0 (SCALE) vs. 76.5 (Full Data)
 - **LLaVA Wild:** 81.3 (SCALE) vs. 80.6 (Full Data)
 - **MME:** 2250.1 (SCALE) vs. 2249.0 (Full Data)
 - **ScienceQA:** 89.6 (SCALE) vs. 89.1 (Full Data)
 - **SeedBench:** 77.5 (SCALE) vs. 77.2 (Full Data)
 - This underscores the efficacy of quality-driven data curation, demonstrating that selecting high-quality data is more impactful than simply increasing dataset size.

2. **Superiority Over Baselines:**
 - SCALE consistently **outperformed all other baselines**, including Random Selection, ALBEF/BLIP/BLIP-2 Retrieve, and CLIP Similarity (Table 4).
 - For instance, on A-OKVQA, SCALE achieved 87.5%, significantly higher than Random Selection (86.2%), ALBEF Retrieve (81.4%), BLIP Retrieve (81.2%), BLIP-2 Retrieve (81.3%), and CLIP Similarity (85.1%).
 - This highlights that simply sampling, relying on basic embedding similarity, or using older retrieval methods is insufficient compared to SCALE's comprehensive quality assessment.

3. **Reliable Task Prediction:**
 - The task prediction component, utilizing `Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct` and MMBench annotations, achieved **over 80% accuracy for all defined sub-tasks**, with an average accuracy of approximately 87% (Table 3).
 - Tasks like 'Image Style', 'Image Scene', 'OCR', and 'Social Relation' even exceeded 90% accuracy, indicating the robustness of this internal pipeline stage.

4. **Pitfalls of Unimodal Evaluation:**
 - Ablation studies (Table 5) demonstrated that unimodal quality assessments (IQA only, TQA only, or even a simple (I+T)QA combination) **performed markedly worse** than the full SCALE pipeline.
 - For example, on A-OKVQA, IQA scored 80.7%, TQA 81.2%, and (I+T)QA 81.1%, all significantly lower than SCALE's 87.5%.
 - Qualitative examples (Figure 8) further illustrated why unimodal assessments are insufficient: they can undervalue task-specific relevance, disregard the importance of quality diversity for robustness, or misjudge instruction-following accuracy. This validates the paper's claim that relying solely on one modality introduces biases and can hinder performance.

5. **Efficacy of Unified Text Modality:**
 - The strong performance of SCALE, which leverages appropriately generated image captions to transfer the image-text multimodal alignment into a unified text modality, demonstrates this as an **efficient and effective approach** (Key Insight 4). This approach offers a powerful alternative to purely embedding-based or complex multimodal comparison methods for data evaluation.

Overall, the findings conclusively demonstrate SCALE's ability to create highly effective, smaller datasets, leading to improved VLM performance across a diverse range of tasks by meticulously assessing and prioritizing data quality and multimodal alignment.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The SCALE pipeline offers several significant contributions and has substantial potential impact on the research and application of Visual Language Models:

1. **Data Efficiency and Resource Democratization:** By demonstrating that VLMs fine-tuned on **less than 10% of the original data pool can surpass full-data baselines**, SCALE champions a "less-is-more" paradigm. This drastically reduces computational costs, energy consumption, and storage requirements associated with large-scale VLM training. This is critical for:
 - **Environmental Sustainability:** Reducing the carbon footprint of AI development.
 - **Democratization of AI:** Making advanced VLM training accessible to researchers and institutions with limited GPU resources, fostering innovation beyond large tech companies.
 - **Edge-Device Deployment:** Enabling the use of sophisticated multimodal models in resource-constrained environments like mobile health diagnostics or field robotics.

2. **Enhanced VLM Performance and Robustness:** The method directly addresses and mitigates the issues of noisy image-text alignments and ambiguous textual descriptions. By providing cleaner, higher-quality data, SCALE leads to:
 - **More Accurate Reasoning:** Especially in complex domains like mathematical reasoning and discipline-specific knowledge, where precise interpretation is paramount.
 - **Improved Generalization:** Models trained on curated data are less likely to overfit to noise and more capable of handling diverse and novel scenarios.

3. **Novel and Automated Data Curation Framework:** SCALE introduces the first **end-to-end automated pipeline** for quantifying and selecting data based on a holistic evaluation of textual coherence, visual fidelity, and multimodal alignment. This formalized framework provides a robust blueprint for future data curation efforts, moving beyond heuristic filtering or simple similarity metrics. Its modular nature also allows for adaptability and future improvements.

4. **Critical Insights into Multimodal Data Quality:** The study reveals key insights:
 - **Limitations of Unimodal Evaluation:** It empirically demonstrates that relying solely on image-only or text-only quality metrics introduces biases and can discard crucial "lower-fidelity" samples essential for model robustness or task-specific objectives. This challenges common practices and advocates for a truly multimodal assessment.
 - **Generated Captions as a Unified Modality Proxy:** The finding that appropriately generated image captions efficiently transfer complex image-text alignment into a unified text task is highly significant. This simplifies evaluation, opens new avenues for automated dataset labeling, cross-modal information retrieval, and alt-text generation for accessibility, making multimodal data processing more streamlined.

5. **Ethical Considerations and Future Directions:** The paper proactively discusses the **potential risks** of automated data selection, such as the amplification of existing biases from judge models, underrepresentation of minority cultures, and the misuse of efficient training paradigms for misinformation or surveillance. By encouraging the integration of bias mitigation, human-in-the-loop audits, and robustness checks, the authors contribute to the ongoing discourse on responsible AI development.

In conclusion, SCALE represents a significant step forward in data-centric AI for VLMs. It not only offers a practical solution for curating high-quality datasets more efficiently but also provides fundamental insights into the nature of multimodal data quality, paving the way for more powerful, robust, and ethically considered VLM applications.
