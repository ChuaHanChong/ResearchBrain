# VistaVLA: Geometry- and Semantic-Aware 3D Gaussian-Grounded VLA for Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> VistaVLA, a framework from Nanyang Technological University and A*STAR, endows robotic manipulation policies with an explicit, scene-level 3D representation that is both geometrically accurate and semantically grounded. It achieves a 22.8 percentage point increase in average success rate across real-world tasks and improves spatial generalization on the LIBERO-Pro-Swap benchmark from 1.7% to 12.2%.

## Problem

- Current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models primarily rely on 2D image observations, limiting their explicit 3D spatial reasoning for precise or contact-rich manipulation tasks.
- Existing 3D VLA approaches often focus on low-level structural geometry but lack high-level, open-vocabulary semantic features directly grounded in 3D space.
- Dense 3D scene representations are computationally intensive, making them impractical for real-time robotic control and often failing to provide a compact, action-relevant 3D context.

## Method

- Constructed a ==3D Gaussian-grounded cognitive representation== that links dense 3D geometry with high-level semantic features using ==3D Gaussian primitives==.
- Employed a two-stage ==Gaussian feature rendering== process, first stabilizing geometry with ==RGB-D rendering== and then distilling compact teacher features into Gaussians with ==multi-view supervision==.
- Developed a **Merge-then-Query (MtQ)** mechanism for parameter-free compression and query-based summarization of dense 3D-grounded semantic (GS) tokens into a fixed-length representation for VLA policy injection.

## Results

- Achieved the highest average success rate across seven real-world manipulation tasks, demonstrating a **22.8 percentage point** increase over the VLA-Adapter baseline.
- Exhibited improved robustness under spatial variations, with **9/10** successes in perturbed 'PlaceSponge' and **3/10** in 'OrganizeSponge' where baselines achieved **0/10**.
- Improved the average success rate on the LIBERO-Pro-Swap benchmark (spatial out-of-distribution) from **1.7%** (VLA-Adapter) to **12.2%**, surpassing other 2D and explicit 3D baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating a geometry- and semantics-aware 3D representation, built from 3D Gaussian primitives, provides VLA policies with a more robust and spatially aware understanding of the scene.
> - Efficiently compressing high-dimensional 3D-grounded semantic information into a compact set of tokens is crucial for real-time robotic control while preserving action-relevant context.
> - A multi-stage training approach combining RGB-D reconstruction with teacher-feature distillation effectively imbues 3D Gaussian primitives with both accurate geometry and high-level semantics from foundation models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represent a paradigm in robotic manipulation, enabling robots to translate natural language instructions and visual observations into control commands. Current VLA models predominantly rely on 2D image observations. This reliance often results in limitations concerning explicit scene-level 3D representation, which affects their ability to reason over spatial layouts and geometric constraints, particularly for precise or contact-rich manipulation tasks.

Recent developments have attempted to integrate explicit 3D cues, such as depth maps, point clouds, or sparse 3D coordinates, into VLA pipelines to enhance geometric awareness. While these methods can capture low-level structural geometry, they often lack high-level, open-vocabulary semantic features directly grounded in 3D space. This means they struggle to bind rich multimodal semantics to physical spatial structures, leading to observation-centric rather than scene-level representations. Consequently, dense 3D reasoning can remain computationally intensive and challenging for real-time robotic manipulation, as VLA policies may lack a compact, action-relevant 3D context.

This work addresses this gap by developing a framework that aims to provide both explicit 3D geometric structure and high-level semantic grounding within a unified, computationally efficient representation. It draws inspiration from human cognition, which is understood to utilize a "3D semantic cognitive map" to integrate spatial layouts with semantic context for viewpoint-invariant reasoning. By constructing a 3D scene representation that explicitly incorporates both geometry and semantics, this research positions itself as an advancement towards more robust and spatially aware robotic manipulation systems that can overcome the limitations of purely 2D or geometrically-focused 3D VLA approaches.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a VLA framework, named VistaVLA, that endows robotic manipulation policies with an explicit, scene-level 3D representation that is both geometrically accurate and semantically grounded. This objective is motivated by several identified limitations in existing VLA models:

- **Lack of Explicit 3D Reasoning in 2D VLAs:** Conventional VLA models primarily process 2D visual inputs. This approach inherently restricts their capacity for understanding and reasoning about the three-dimensional spatial relationships, object positions, and geometric constraints within a scene. Consequently, these models often exhibit failures in tasks requiring precise localization or complex interactions where 3D awareness is critical. The motivation is to move beyond 2D-centric perception to incorporate a robust 3D understanding.
- **Insufficient Semantic Grounding in Existing 3D VLAs:** While some prior work has integrated explicit 3D cues (e.g., depth maps, point clouds) into VLA systems, these methods typically focus on low-level structural geometry. They generally lack high-level, open-vocabulary semantic features directly anchored in 3D space. This deficiency means that while robots might perceive geometric shapes, they may not associate these shapes with meaningful object identities or functional roles in a consistent 3D context. The motivation is to bridge this gap by binding rich multimodal semantics directly to physical 3D structures.
- **Computational Intractability of Dense 3D Representations:** Constructing and processing dense 3D representations (e.g., raw point clouds or voxels) can be computationally expensive, making them impractical for real-time robotic control. Existing 3D VLA methods, by generating observation-centric rather than scene-level representations, often fail to provide a compact, action-relevant 3D context. This research is motivated to develop an efficient summarization mechanism that can distill vast amounts of 3D information into a manageable and actionable format for VLA policy learning.
- **Enhancing Robustness to Spatial Variations:** Robotic manipulation in real-world environments frequently encounters variations in object positions, orientations, and scene layouts. Current VLAs, particularly those lacking explicit 3D spatial reasoning, may struggle to generalize to such out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios. A key motivation is to improve the robustness and generalization capabilities of VLA models to spatial perturbations, mimicking the viewpoint-invariant reasoning observed in human cognition.

To address these motivations, VistaVLA aims to construct a 3D cognitive representation inspired by human perception, which integrates continuous spatial topology with semantic meaning. This "geometry- and semantics-aware" representation is built using 3D Gaussian primitives and is subsequently compressed into compact context tokens for efficient integration with VLA policy learning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VistaVLA operates as a two-stage framework designed to construct a geometry- and semantics-aware 3D cognitive representation and integrate it efficiently into a VLA policy.

**Stage I: 3D Gaussian-Grounded Cognitive Representation**

The first stage focuses on creating a scene-level representation that intrinsically links dense 3D geometry with high-level semantic features using 3D Gaussian primitives.

1. **Compact Teacher Feature Space:** To manage the computational cost of assigning high-dimensional foundation model features to a large number of Gaussian primitives, a compact teacher feature space is established. For each training frame, dense features from SigLIP2 (language-aligned semantics) and DINOv2-Large (robust visual descriptors) are extracted and concatenated. A lightweight auto-encoder is then trained to reconstruct these concatenated features from a 128-dimensional latent code. This 128-dimensional latent code serves as the compact teacher feature for subsequent Gaussian feature rendering.

2. **Two-Stage Gaussian Feature Rendering:** The scene is represented as a set of N Gaussian primitives, G = {g_i}. Each primitive g_i is defined by its center (μ_i), covariance (Σ_i), opacity (α_i), color (c_i), and a learnable semantic feature (z_i ∈ R^128). The Gaussian field is trained in two stages using RGB-D rendering supervision:
 - **Stage 1 (Geometric Stabilization):** The initial stage uses a loss function L_stage1 = λ_rgb L_rgb + λ_dep L_dep to establish stable scene geometry. L_rgb supervises RGB reconstruction, and L_dep constrains the rendered and intermediate depth.
 - **Stage 2 (Semantic Feature Distillation):** This stage maintains the RGB-D rendering objectives while adding teacher-feature supervision: L_stage2 = λ_rgb L_rgb + λ_dep L_dep + λ_feat L_feat. The L_feat component distills the compact 128-dimensional teacher features into the Gaussian primitives, without relaxing the geometric constraints learned in Stage 1. The rendered feature at each pixel (F̂_v(p)) is derived using an alpha-compositing rule, similar to 3D Gaussian Splatting, where the rendered feature is a weighted sum of the semantic features z_i of Gaussians covering that pixel. This process ensures that each z_i is optimized from teacher features observed across multiple viewpoints, thus aligning it with the 2D teacher feature space while its grounding is governed by the 3D primitive's geometry and visibility. During training, two calibrated RGB-D views are used as context, and a third view is rendered as a novel target to enforce cross-view consistency. At inference, the 3D Gaussian field is constructed online from live RGB observations and calibrated camera poses, eliminating the need for depth input or a third view.

3. **3D-Grounded GS Tokenization:** After training, the latent semantic feature (z_i) attached to each Gaussian primitive becomes its token value, forming a set of GS tokens T_gs = {z_i}. Each z_i is not an isolated 2D descriptor but is inherently associated with its underlying 3D Gaussian primitive. This grounding is ensured by the multi-view supervision and alpha-compositing process, making each GS token a multi-view semantic descriptor grounded in 3D space.

**Stage II: Compact GS Token Summarization and Policy Injection**

The initial set of GS tokens (N₀ ≈ 10⁵) is too large for real-time VLA processing. Stage II addresses this with the Merge-then-Query (MtQ) mechanism.

1. **Parameter-Free Compression:** This step reduces the dense GS token set (∼10⁵) to an intermediate size (N' ≈ 1000) without trainable parameters:
 - **Grid Reshaping and Pooling:** Dense GS tokens are reshaped into per-view grids, and stride-4 average pooling is applied to both semantic features and their associated 3D centers. This coarsely reduces the token count to approximately 7K.
 - **Morton-Guided Merging:** The remaining tokens are sorted by the Morton codes of their 3D centers, creating a spatially ordered sequence. This sequence is alternately split into an "anchor set" (A) and a "non-anchor set" (B). Each non-anchor token (u ∈ B) is matched to its most semantically similar anchor (a ∈ A) using cosine similarity. Only the top 50% most similar non-anchor tokens are merged with their respective anchors. Anchors are updated by size-weighted averaging of their features, centers, and aggregation sizes. This iterative process repeats until the token count reaches a target N' (e.g., 1000).

2. **Query-Based GS Summarization:** The compressed tokens (Z' ∈ R^(B×N'×d)) are further summarized into a fixed-length representation (N_q = 64) for VLA inference:
 - **Transformer Decoder:** Z' is projected into key and value features. N_q learnable query tokens (Q ∈ R^(B×N_q×d)) then attend to these features through a two-layer Transformer decoder.
 - **Projection:** The decoder output is projected to the hidden dimension (d_llm) of the downstream VLA backbone, yielding X_gs ∈ R^(B×N_q×d_llm). This X_gs forms the compact, 3D-grounded context tokens.

3. **Action Generation:** The VLA model (based on VLA-Adapter) formulates continuous action prediction as causal sequence modeling. The GS summary tokens (X_gs) are concatenated with 2D visual tokens (X_vis), language instruction tokens (X_lang), and action query tokens (X_act). This sequential arrangement allows the GS context to be language-conditioned before action decoding, and action query tokens to attend to all preceding context. The action head predicts the next K continuous actions (â_1:K) using the hidden states of action queries and context tokens, along with proprioceptive state (s). The policy is trained with an L1 regression objective. During real-robot inference, the first K_exec = 4 predicted actions are executed before replanning with new observations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of VistaVLA included real-world experiments, simulated tasks, and ablation studies, demonstrating its performance in robotic manipulation.

**Real-World Experiments:**

- **Task Performance:** VistaVLA was evaluated on seven real-world manipulation tasks, including "PlaceSponge," "StackBoxes," "StackBowls," "PlacePlate," "OrganizeSponge," "ThrowTrash," and "Wand&Cup." These tasks involved diverse object arrangements, interaction patterns, and temporal horizons.
 - VistaVLA achieved the highest average success rate across all tasks. Compared to the VLA-Adapter baseline, it demonstrated a 22.8 percentage point increase in average success rate.
 - The performance gains were more pronounced in tasks requiring precise spatial reasoning or complex interactions, where VistaVLA outperformed the baseline and a strong generalist policy (π_0.5) despite utilizing a smaller VLA backbone.
 - VLA-Adapter+Depth, an enhancement of the baseline with raw depth input, showed limited improvement over the RGB-only baseline, suggesting that raw depth alone is insufficient without semantic grounding in 3D.
- **Robustness under Spatial Variations:** The method was tested under perturbed conditions for "PlaceSponge" (height/depth variation) and "OrganizeSponge" (2D position variation).
 - For "PlaceSponge" with height/depth variation, VistaVLA achieved 9/10 successes, outperforming VLA-Adapter (6/10), VLA-Adapter+Depth (6/10), and π_0.5 (7/10).
 - For "OrganizeSponge" with position variation, VistaVLA was the only method to achieve non-zero success (3/10), while all baselines (VLA-Adapter, VLA-Adapter+Depth, SmolVLA, π_0.5) failed (0/10). This indicates improved robustness to spatial shifts, although large position perturbations remain challenging.

**Simulation Experiments:**

- **Standard LIBERO Benchmark:** VistaVLA maintained competitive performance on the standard LIBERO benchmark, achieving an average success rate of 96.05%. This result was noted as the best among methods without large-scale action-data pretraining, outperforming the VLA-Adapter baseline (94.1%) across Spatial, Object, Goal, and Long suites.
- **LIBERO-Pro-Swap (Spatial Out-of-Distribution):** On this benchmark, which tests generalization to unseen spatial configurations, VistaVLA demonstrated enhanced robustness.
 - It improved the average success rate from 1.7% (VLA-Adapter baseline) to 12.2%.
 - It also surpassed other 2D-input baselines (OpenVLA, UniVLA, π₀) and explicit 3D-input methods (OpenVLA-SF, Q-Depth VLA), which generally showed low success rates (0-5%). This indicates improved spatial generalization under constrained adaptation.

**Ablation Studies:**

- **Camera-view and Token Ablation:**
 - Comparing baseline variants with one, two, or three 2D camera views showed that adding more 2D views only moderately improved baseline performance (e.g., Base-1Cam: 4/10 on T1, Base-3Cam: 8/10 on T1).
 - VistaVLA with three cameras and 320 tokens (Vista-VLA-3Cam) achieved 9/10 on T1, outperforming the Base-3Cam with 768 tokens. This suggests that the performance gain is attributed to the proposed 3D spatial-semantic interface rather than merely an increased number of 2D visual tokens or a larger token budget.
- **Compression and Summarization Ablation:**
 - Variants of the GS token summarization module were tested: GS+FPS+Dec. (FPS downsampling), GS+Entropy+Dec. (entropy-based sampling), and GS+Merge+Linear (Morton-guided merge but no query decoder).
 - The full VistaVLA (using Morton-guided merging and query-based summarization) consistently outperformed these variants. For example, on T3 (StackBowls), full VistaVLA achieved 8/10, while GS+FPS+Dec. and GS+Entropy+Dec. both scored 0/10, and GS+Merge+Linear scored 0/10. This indicates that both geometry-aware token merging and query-based summarization contribute to preserving task-relevant 3D information in a compact form.
- **Query-Token Ablation:**
 - Testing different numbers of query tokens (16, 32, 64, 128, 256) on T3 (StackBowls) showed that 64 query tokens yielded the best result (8/10).
 - Increasing the number of queries beyond 64 did not lead to consistent gains, with 256 queries performing the worst (2/10). This suggests that an optimal number of query tokens is necessary for the two-layer Transformer network, as an excessively large set can introduce redundancy and complicate optimization of the summarization bottleneck.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

VistaVLA introduces a novel framework that contributes to the field of robotic manipulation by bridging a semantically grounded 3D scene representation with image-based policy networks. The primary significance lies in its ability to equip VLA models with an explicit, scene-level 3D understanding that integrates both geometric structure and high-level semantic information.

The potential impacts include:

- **Enhanced Spatial Reasoning and Precision:** By constructing 3D Gaussian-grounded cognitive representations, VistaVLA enables robots to reason more effectively about spatial layouts and geometric constraints. This is critical for complex manipulation tasks requiring high precision, contact-rich interactions, or understanding spatial relationships (e.g., "behind the mug"). The observed improvements in real-world success rates, particularly on spatially demanding tasks, indicate a direct positive impact on manipulation capabilities.
- **Improved Generalization and Robustness:** The framework's ability to maintain high performance under spatial variations (e.g., depth and position perturbations) and to generalize to out-of-distribution scene layouts (demonstrated in LIBERO-Pro-Swap) suggests a significant step towards more robust and adaptable robotic policies. This reduces the brittleness of current VLA models to minor environmental changes.
- **Computational Efficiency via Token Summarization:** The Merge-then-Query (MtQ) mechanism allows for a substantial reduction in the number of tokens (from ~10⁵ to 64) while preserving action-relevant 3D layouts and semantic consistency. This addresses a critical challenge in integrating dense 3D representations into real-time control systems, making the approach computationally tractable for practical robotic applications.
- **Biologically Inspired Cognitive Mapping:** The inspiration drawn from human 3D semantic cognitive maps positions this work towards developing more intuitive and intelligent embodied AI systems. Providing a multi-view-consistent and semantic 3D spatial interface could pave the way for more sophisticated scene understanding in robotics.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The authors acknowledge several limitations that define avenues for future research. The current evaluation is restricted to tabletop manipulation with a fixed robot platform and calibrated multi-view cameras. Broader deployment would necessitate robustness to larger workspace variations, dynamic occlusions, less reliable camera poses, and diverse robot embodiments. Future work could focus on relaxing strict calibration requirements, exploring manipulation-oriented feature teachers, and evaluating the framework in more varied real-world environments to enhance its generalizability and real-world applicability. Nevertheless, VistaVLA offers a generalizable interface for converting semantic Gaussian fields into compact policy-facing 3D context, providing a foundation for future advancements in spatially aware robotic control.
