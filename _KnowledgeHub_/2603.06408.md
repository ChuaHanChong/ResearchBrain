# Physical Simulator In-the-Loop Video Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework called Physical Simulator In-the-Loop Video Generation (PSIVG) introduces a training-free method to imbue AI-generated videos with physical consistency by integrating a physical simulator into the inference loop of pre-trained diffusion models. The approach achieved an 82.3% user preference for physical plausibility and demonstrated superior motion controllability with a SAM mIoU of 0.84 and a Corr. Pixel MSE of 0.007.

## Problem

- Current diffusion-based video generation models often produce content that violates fundamental physical laws, such as gravity, inertia, and collision dynamics.
- This lack of physical consistency leads to objects exhibiting erratic movements, inconsistent appearances, or disappearing, hindering perceived realism and limiting applicability in domains requiring accuracy.
- Existing generative models inherently lack explicit mechanisms to understand or enforce physical constraints, leading to visually rich but physically implausible outputs.

## Method

- A training-free, inference-time framework (**PSIVG**) integrates a physical simulator into the video diffusion process to enforce physical consistency.
- It employs a perception pipeline to reconstruct 3D object meshes and 4D scene information from an initial template video for physical simulation.
- An ==MPM==-based simulator generates physically accurate trajectories, which are then converted into guidance signals (e.g., hybrid ==optical flow==) for a video ==diffusion model==.
- A Test-Time Texture Consistency Optimization (**TTCO**) technique, utilizing pixel correspondence loss and prompt-based optimization, maintains object appearance consistency across frames.

## Results

- A user study found PSIVG videos were preferred for physical plausibility in 82.3% of comparisons, significantly outperforming five strong baseline methods.
- Quantitatively, PSIVG achieved superior motion controllability with a SAM mIoU of 0.84 and a Corr. Pixel MSE of 0.007, indicating accurate adherence to simulated trajectories and rotations.
- The framework maintained high general video quality, scoring well on VBench metrics such as Subject Consistency (0.95) and Motion Smoothness (0.99), demonstrating strong temporal coherence.
- Test-Time Texture Consistency Optimization (TTCO) improved Subject Consistency from 0.93 to 0.95, and prompt-based optimization for TTCO proved more effective than LoRA-based methods at preserving background quality.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating physical simulators directly into the inference loop of pre-trained video diffusion models effectively instills physical consistency without requiring model retraining.
> - A multi-stage perception pipeline is crucial for extracting coherent 3D object geometry and 4D scene dynamics from potentially inconsistent generated videos to feed a simulator.
> - Guiding video diffusion models with hybrid optical flow—combining simulator-generated physical motion with template video background details—achieves both physical accuracy and visual richness.
> - Test-Time Texture Consistency Optimization (TTCO) through localized prompt-based fine-tuning provides an efficient, training-free method to prevent object texture flickering and appearance drift during motion.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of video generation has witnessed astonishing progress in recent years, primarily driven by advancements in diffusion models and large-scale training datasets. Models like OpenAI's Sora (though not directly cited, its capabilities are a benchmark) and Google's Veo, along with models like CogVideoX and HunyuanVideo, can now produce videos of remarkable visual fidelity and diversity from text prompts. However, despite this visual realism, a critical and widely acknowledged limitation remains: these models often struggle to adhere to fundamental physical laws such as gravity, inertia, and collisions. Generated objects might float, vanish, or move in physically impossible ways, leading to a breakdown in perceived realism and limiting their applicability in domains where physical accuracy is paramount.

This paper directly addresses this critical gap. The broader research landscape can be categorized as follows:

- **Generative Video Models:** The foundation of this work builds upon the success of diffusion-based text-to-video (T2V) and image-to-video (I2V) models. PSIVG leverages the visual quality of these pre-trained models as a starting point (template video).
- **Physically Consistent Generation:** This is an emerging sub-field. Previous efforts have explored:
 - **2D Physics:** Early works coupled image-based simulators with image generators but were often limited to simplified 2D rigid-body assumptions, lacking true 3D understanding.
 - **3D Scene Understanding with Simulation:** More recent approaches, like PhysGen3D and WonderPlay, aim to reconstruct 3D representations from images for simulation, sometimes requiring user input or focusing on specific domains (e.g., cartoons in PhysAnimator). These works often create a simulative 3D environment from which videos are rendered, or use generated videos to refine a 3D scene. PSIVG differentiates itself by *refining* generated video directly using simulator guidance, rather than building a simulator-native rendering pipeline from scratch or relying heavily on user interaction for 3D reconstruction from a single image.
 - **LLM/Text-Based Physics:** Some studies use large language models (LLMs) to inject physics knowledge, either through physics-grounded prompts or by generating scripts for traditional 3D software (e.g., Blender). PSIVG’s approach is orthogonal, focusing on direct simulator integration rather than high-level reasoning.
 - **Physics-Aware Fine-tuning:** Concurrent works fine-tune diffusion models using physical forces or simulator-derived parameters. PSIVG stands out by being a **training-free, inference-time framework**. This is a significant distinction, as it allows for immediate application to existing pre-trained models without the need for extensive data collection or computationally intensive retraining. It acts as a "plug-and-play" physics guidance system for arbitrary diffusion models.
- **Controllable Video Generation:** Many methods aim to control video generation using additional inputs like masks, depth maps, trajectories, or optical flow. PSIVG integrates with these by generating precise, physically consistent control signals (optical flow, segmentation masks, pixel correspondences) from its simulator, which are then fed into existing controllable video generators (e.g., Go-with-the-Flow).

In essence, PSIVG positions itself as a crucial bridge between the visually rich but physically naive outputs of modern video diffusion models and the physically accurate but visually crude outputs of traditional simulators. It offers a novel paradigm of "simulation-in-the-loop" generation, enhancing realism and reliability without incurring significant training costs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for PSIVG stems from a recognized flaw in the otherwise impressive landscape of modern video generation: the lack of physical consistency. Despite their ability to produce highly realistic imagery, current diffusion-based video models frequently generate objects that violate basic laws of physics, such as gravity, inertia, and collision dynamics. Objects might exhibit erratic movements, inconsistent appearances across frames, or simply disappear, severely undermining the realism and trustworthiness of the generated content.

The paper identifies several key motivations for addressing this shortcoming:
- **Enhanced Visual Realism:** Physically consistent motion makes AI-generated videos more compelling and believable, which is crucial for commercial applications in entertainment, such as film production, virtual reality, and gaming, where high-quality visual effects often rely on accurate physics.
- **Improved Reliability for Safety-Critical Domains:** For applications like robotics and autonomous driving, AI-generated videos can serve as synthetic training data for agent models. If these videos lack physical plausibility, the agents trained on them may develop unsound decision-making capabilities in real-world scenarios. Ensuring physical adherence directly contributes to the reliability and safety of these systems.
- **Addressing a Fundamental Limitation of Current Models:** The authors observe that current video generation models, largely trained on denoising or reconstruction objectives, inherently lack an explicit understanding of physics. There is no built-in mechanism to enforce physical constraints, leading to the observed shortcomings.

Against this backdrop, the key objective of this work is to develop a novel framework that effectively integrates physical simulation into the video diffusion process. Specifically, the research aims to answer the question: **"How can we effectively incorporate information from the physical simulator into the video diffusion process to achieve physically consistent generation?"**

To achieve this overarching goal, PSIVG sets out with several specific objectives:
1. **Develop a "Physical Simulator In-the-loop Video Generation (PSIVG)" framework:** This involves creating an entire pipeline that starts with a text prompt, generates a template video, extracts scene information, runs a physical simulation, and then uses the simulation results to guide the final video generation.
2. **Design a robust perception pipeline:** This pipeline must accurately reconstruct 3D meshes of foreground objects and the 4D scene (including background geometry and camera motion) from a potentially inconsistent, generated template video, preparing it for simulation.
3. **Integrate an MPM-based physical simulator:** This includes setting up the simulation domain, inferring physical properties for objects, initializing their states, and performing forward simulation to generate physically accurate trajectories.
4. **Develop a mechanism to guide video generation with simulator outputs:** This involves converting the physical trajectories into a format consumable by video diffusion models (e.g., optical flow) to enforce physics-based motion while preserving visual quality.
5. **Propose a Test-Time Texture Consistency Optimization (TTCO) technique:** To address the persistent issue of texture flickering and appearance drift in moving objects, even with motion guidance, an additional optimization step is needed to maintain visual coherence without additional training data.

By achieving these objectives, PSIVG aims to deliver a system that produces videos that are not only visually appealing but also profoundly grounded in the laws of the physical world.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The PSIVG framework is a novel, multi-stage pipeline designed to integrate a physical simulator directly into the inference loop of pre-trained video diffusion models, ensuring physically consistent motion while preserving visual fidelity. The approach is training-free, operating entirely at inference time.

The methodology can be broken down into the following key steps.

#### 3.1. Template Video Generation

The process begins by leveraging a pre-trained video generator (e.g., CogVideoX-I2V-5B, HunyuanVideo-I2V) to create an initial "template video" from a given text prompt. This template video establishes the scene composition, camera movements, and object appearances and initial motions. While visually rich, this video is acknowledged to be physically inconsistent.

#### 3.2. Perception Pipeline (Section 3.2.1)

This crucial component bridges the 2D visual information from the template video to the 3D/4D requirements of a physical simulator.
- **Foreground Object Geometry:** For dynamic objects, off-the-shelf models are used for detection, grounding, and segmentation in each frame. A 3D mesh for each object is then reconstructed, preferably from the first frame (which often has the highest quality), using a single-image 3D mesh reconstruction tool (e.g., InstantMesh). This avoids inconsistencies that can arise from using multiple frames from a physically implausible generated video.
- **Background Scene Geometry and Camera Motion:** To obtain a stable background and camera trajectory, 4D reconstruction is performed using techniques like ViPE, which masks out foreground dynamics and applies bundle adjustment on keyframes. This yields 3D background geometry (aggregated static points) and camera poses, as well as rough initial object positions. Aggressive filtering is applied to enhance reliability.
- **Foreground Object Dynamics:** The initial state of objects, including linear and rotational velocities, is estimated. Linear velocity is calculated from 3D displacement between two keyframes (separated by ∆t). Rotational velocity is estimated by performing 2D feature matching (e.g., SuperGlue) between object instances in these keyframes and isolating rotational motion from the 2D flow field relative to the object's centroid. These components are combined to derive per-point instantaneous velocities.

#### 3.3. Physical Simulation (Section 3.2.2)

With the simulator-ready assets, a physical simulation is initialized and run.
- **Simulator Choice:** An MPM (Material Point Method)-based physical simulator (e.g., Taichi) is adopted for its ability to handle deformable materials and complex interactions.
- **Simulation Domain Setup:** An appropriate 3D bounding box is defined around the reconstructed foreground dynamics and background geometries to efficiently house the simulation. Scene geometries and camera parameters are scaled, rotated, and translated to fit this domain, and physical constants (gravity, Young's modulus) are adjusted accordingly.
- **Physical Property Estimation:** To assign realistic material properties, a large vision-language model (e.g., GPT-5) is queried. A hierarchical prompting framework infers qualitative material descriptors (e.g., composition, elasticity, roughness) from the first frame and text prompt, which are then mapped to numerical physical parameters like density and Young's modulus.
- **Simulate and Render:** The forward MPM simulation generates physically plausible, high-resolution particle-level trajectories in 3D. These are then rendered into RGB frames, segmentation masks, and pixel-to-pixel correspondences using Mitsuba, driven by the camera poses derived from the template video. The authors note that these rendered outputs, while physically accurate, lack photo-realism and are intended as guidance, not direct replacements for generated video.

#### 3.4. Physically-Consistent Video Generation (Section 3.3)

The simulator's physics-rich outputs are now used to guide a video diffusion model.
- **Guidance Mechanism:** The framework employs an optical flow-conditioned video generation model, Go-with-the-Flow (GwtF), as optical flow effectively encodes both trajectories and rotations.
- **Hybrid Optical Flow:** To combine physical accuracy with visual consistency, a hybrid optical flow field is created. RAFT optical flow is computed from two sources:
 - **Simulator-rendered RGB:** Provides physically consistent foreground motion.
 - **Original Template Video:** Preserves realistic background motion, camera dynamics, and complex details (e.g., water, foliage) that are difficult for the simulator to model.
 These flows are fused using segmentation masks. The resulting hybrid flow, along with the text prompt and the starting frame of the template video, is fed into the GwtF model to guide the generation process.

#### 3.5. Test-Time Texture Consistency Optimization (TTCO) (Section 3.3.1)

Despite effective motion guidance, existing models often suffer from texture flickering and appearance drift. TTCO addresses this with a lightweight, training-free optimization.
- **Objective:** To maintain consistent textures and colors for moving foreground objects across frames, especially during rotations.
- **Mechanism:** A pixel-correspondence loss (L\_tex) is applied. It compares the generated video frames with a "texture-consistent target" created by warping the first frame of the template video using the pixel-to-pixel correspondences provided by the physical simulator. The loss is an MSE applied only to foreground pixels, optionally using simulator-rendered object pixel values for large rotations.
- **Optimization Target:** To localize the optimization effect to foreground objects and prevent background degradation, the framework optimizes learnable residual tokens added to the text embeddings for object phrases, and feature-wise modulations within the DiT layers corresponding to object tokens. This is performed during the noisier, earlier diffusion steps to better influence texture generation.

The PSIVG pipeline thus creates a powerful synergy: leveraging the generative power of diffusion models for visual fidelity, the analytical precision of physical simulators for motion accuracy, and a clever test-time optimization for appearance coherence.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The comprehensive experiments conducted by the authors demonstrate that PSIVG significantly enhances the physical realism of generated videos while maintaining high visual quality and diversity. The evaluation covered both quantitative metrics and qualitative assessments, including a user study and ablation studies.

#### 4.1. Text-to-Video Generation Performance

- **Quantitative Results:**
 - **Superior Motion Controllability:** PSIVG consistently achieved the best performance on motion controllability metrics:
 - **SAM mIoU (↑):** Higher Intersection over Union for object masks, indicating better alignment with simulated trajectories (0.84 for PSIVG vs. 0.43-0.75 for baselines).
 - **Corr. Pixel MSE (↓):** Lower pixel correspondence error, signifying more accurate pixel-level motion and rotation adherence (0.007 for PSIVG vs. 0.012-0.032 for baselines).
 - **Maintained General Video Quality:** While prioritizing physics, PSIVG maintained strong performance on general quality metrics:
 - **CLIP Text (↑):** Comparable text-to-image similarity (0.35) with leading T2V models, indicating good alignment with the text prompt.
 - **CLIP Img (↑):** High similarity between consecutive frames (0.99), suggesting good temporal consistency.
 - **VBench Metrics:** Achieved high scores for Subject Consistency (0.95), Background Consistency (0.96), Motion Smoothness (0.99), and Temporal Flickering (0.97), comparable to or better than many baselines.
 - **Contrast with Baselines:** Existing T2V models (e.g., CogVideoX, HunyuanVideo, PISA variants) and controllable methods (e.g., MotionClone, SG-I2V) showed significantly weaker adherence to physical trajectories and rotations, even when conditioned with simulator-derived information. Some physics-aware baselines achieved high temporal consistency metrics but at the cost of minimal or static movement, failing to capture diverse, physically realistic dynamics.

- **Qualitative Results:**
 - Visual comparisons (e.g., Fig. 5) clearly illustrate PSIVG's ability to generate videos with physically consistent and temporally coherent motion (e.g., a bowling ball collision, a teddy bear dropping).
 - In stark contrast, baseline T2V models often produced visually appealing but physically implausible motions, such as objects floating, abruptly disappearing, or exhibiting chaotic, non-physical movements. Controllable methods often struggled with accurate trajectory following and maintaining object consistency, especially during rotations.

#### 4.2. User Study

A user study involving 32 participants revealed a strong preference for PSIVG. Participants were asked to choose the most physically plausible video from a set generated by PSIVG and five strong baseline methods. PSIVG was preferred in **82.3%** of the comparisons, significantly outperforming all baselines. This objective human evaluation strongly validates the perceived physical realism of PSIVG's generated content.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

- **Impact of TTCO (Test-Time Texture Consistency Optimization):**
 - Incorporating TTCO led to notable improvements: Corr. Pixel MSE decreased from 0.009 to 0.007 (better pixel-level alignment, especially rotations) and SAM mIoU slightly increased from 0.82 to 0.84 (more accurate trajectories).
 - Crucially, Subject Consistency (a VBench metric) improved from 0.93 to 0.95, confirming TTCO's effectiveness in enhancing the consistent appearance and texture of moving objects.

- **Impact of Prompt-Based Optimization Design:**
 - The study compared PSIVG's prompt-based optimization (optimizing residual tokens for text embeddings and feature modulations) against a LoRA-based fine-tuning approach for TTCO.
 - Prompt-based optimization consistently yielded higher quality results, particularly in preserving the background quality. LoRA-based methods often degraded the background. This highlights the advantage of PSIVG's lightweight, localized optimization strategy, which refines foreground object details without negatively impacting the overall scene. Optimizing spatio-temporal tokens directly, while attempted, resulted in undesirable grid-like artifacts.

In summary, the findings robustly demonstrate PSIVG's ability to generate videos that are quantitatively more physically consistent and qualitatively more believable than leading existing methods, validated by both objective metrics and human perception. The effectiveness of the TTCO component and the chosen prompt-based optimization strategy are also clearly established.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Physical Simulator In-the-loop Video Generation (PSIVG)" framework represents a significant advancement in the field of AI-generated content, pushing the boundaries of what is achievable in terms of physical realism and reliability in synthetic videos.

**Key Significance Points:**

- **Bridging a Critical Gap:** PSIVG successfully addresses the long-standing challenge of integrating physical consistency into high-fidelity video generation. While diffusion models excel at visual aesthetics, they inherently lack physics understanding. PSIVG provides a principled, effective method to imbue generated videos with physically plausible motion, making them more realistic and trustworthy.
- **Novel Training-Free, Inference-Time Paradigm:** A major contribution is the introduction of a training-free, inference-time framework. Unlike approaches that require extensive retraining or fine-tuning of generative models on physics-aware datasets, PSIVG can be directly applied to existing pre-trained video diffusion models. This dramatically lowers the barrier to entry for incorporating physics into generative pipelines, making it highly adaptable and efficient.
- **Enhanced Realism for Creative Industries:** The ability to generate physically consistent motion has profound implications for the entertainment industry. Film production, visual effects (VFX), virtual reality (VR), and gaming can all benefit from AI tools that can create realistic animations and interactions, reducing manual labor and enabling more complex, believable scenes.
- **Improved Reliability for Safety-Critical AI:** Perhaps even more critically, the framework's focus on physical accuracy enhances the reliability of AI-generated data for training in domains like robotics and autonomous driving. Agents trained on physically consistent synthetic data are more likely to make sound decisions and respond appropriately to real-world scenarios, thereby improving safety.
- **Advancing Controllable Generation:** By generating precise, physics-grounded control signals (e.g., optical flow, pixel correspondences), PSIVG provides a powerful mechanism for fine-grained control over object motion, beyond simple trajectory guidance. The perception pipeline, which transforms raw video into simulator-ready 3D/4D assets, is itself a notable engineering feat.
- **Innovation in Test-Time Optimization:** The proposed Test-Time Texture Consistency Optimization (TTCO) technique is an elegant solution to a common problem in generative models – appearance flickering and drift. Its localized, prompt-based optimization strategy effectively maintains object texture coherence without degrading the overall video quality, showcasing a clever way to inject targeted refinements without complex architectural changes or additional training data.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** PSIVG opens several avenues for future research. While the current work focuses on rigid-body and simple deformable (MPM) physics, extending it to handle more complex dynamics (e.g., articulated human bodies, vehicles, fluid simulations, soft body interactions beyond MPM's scope) would be a natural next step. Improving the robustness of the perception pipeline and integrating more advanced physical simulators are also clear directions.

**Potential Impact:**

The immediate impact of PSIVG will be observed in the increased realism and practical utility of AI-generated videos. By providing a method to imbue synthetic content with the fundamental laws of physics, PSIVG makes AI-generated worlds more believable, reliable, and functional. It sets a new standard for physically grounded generative AI, pushing towards a future where synthetic media is indistinguishable from reality, not just visually, but also dynamically. This could democratize high-quality content creation, accelerate training for intelligent agents, and deepen our understanding of how to integrate complex scientific principles into deep learning models.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
The authors acknowledge several limitations, which also highlight future research opportunities:
1. **Complexity of Agents:** Current reliance on MPM limits the handling of complex, articulated agents like humans or vehicles. Extending to more sophisticated physics engines and agent models is crucial.
2. **Perception Quality:** Inaccuracies in the initial object reconstruction and 4D scene lifting can propagate errors, suggesting a need for more robust perception pipelines.
3. **Inherited Generative Limitations:** PSIVG inherits limitations from the base video generation model (e.g., difficulty with very small or thin objects), indicating that continuous improvements in underlying generative architectures will also benefit PSIVG.

Despite these limitations, PSIVG marks a substantial step forward in integrating the rigor of physical simulation with the creative power of generative AI, promising a future of more realistic and useful AI-generated videos.
