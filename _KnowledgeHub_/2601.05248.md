# LaST$_{0}$: Latent Spatio-Temporal Chain-of-Thought for Robotic Vision-Language-Action Model

> [!summary] Summary
> LaST0, a Vision-Language-Action model, was developed to enhance robotic manipulation by employing a Latent Spatio-Temporal Chain-of-Thought within a dual-system Mixture-of-Transformers architecture. It demonstrates 15.4 Hz inference speed and achieves up to an 82% mean success rate on RLBench tasks, outperforming explicit Chain-of-Thought approaches.

## Problem

- Existing explicit Chain-of-Thought (CoT) VLA models suffer from high inference latency due to autoregressive generation, hindering real-time robotic manipulation and temporal consistency.
- Explicit reasoning, especially linguistic CoT, struggles to faithfully capture 'ineffable' physical attributes (e.g., precise spatial relationships, forces) crucial for robust physical interaction.
- Maintaining temporal consistency and coherence in actions for long-horizon robotic manipulation tasks is challenging for current methods.

## Method

- Introduced **LaST0**, a ==dual-system Vision-Language-Action model== based on a ==Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT)== architecture.
- Developed a ==Latent Spatio-Temporal Chain-of-Thought (LaST CoT)== that captures fine-grained physical and robotic dynamics through ==compact latent embeddings== from ==2D visual, 3D geometric, and robot proprioceptive states==.
- Implemented ==asynchronous frequency coordination== with a ==slow reasoning expert== generating LaST CoT at a low frequency and a ==fast acting expert== producing high-frequency actions conditioned on the most recent latent reasoning output.

## Results

- Achieved a mean success rate of **82%** across 10 RLBench manipulation tasks and a state-of-the-art **98.1%** on the LIBERO benchmark, consistently outperforming baselines.
- Demonstrated an inference speed of **15.4 Hz** on an NVIDIA 4090 GPU, **14x** faster than explicit CoT methods (e.g., CoT-VLA at **1.1 Hz**).
- Validated across real-world setups (Franka single/dual-arm, AgileX mobile, TienKung 2.0 humanoid hands), achieving a **72%** mean success rate on Franka platforms and showing improved long-horizon task coherence.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reasoning directly in a compact, continuous latent space can overcome the inference latency and representational bottlenecks of explicit linguistic or visual CoT for robotic manipulation.
> - Integrating multimodal latent features (2D visual, 3D geometric, and robot proprioceptive states) provides a richer, physically-grounded understanding of robot-environment interactions.
> - A dual-system Mixture-of-Transformers architecture effectively coordinates low-frequency deliberative reasoning with high-frequency reactive action generation, enhancing overall system robustness and efficiency.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper positions itself within the evolving field of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which integrate the semantic understanding and common-sense reasoning of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) with the low-level control capabilities of robotic policies. The overarching goal of VLA models is to create a unified framework for robotic agents to interpret human instructions and execute corresponding manipulation tasks in dynamic environments.

Recent advancements in VLA models have been inspired by the Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning paradigm. Existing CoT VLA methods primarily fall into two categories:
1. **Explicit Linguistic CoT**: These approaches generate linguistic reasoning traces or affordance representations to enhance manipulation stability and interpretability.
2. **Explicit Visual/Multimodal CoT**: These methods predict future visual observations or multimodal information (e.g., future images, object poses) to capture environmental dynamics.

However, the paper identifies two fundamental challenges with these explicit CoT VLA methods in the context of robotic manipulation:
1. **Inference Latency**: The autoregressive generation inherent in explicit reasoning introduces significant computational overhead, limiting real-time responsiveness and the ability to reason effectively along the temporal dimension. This undermines the temporal consistency required for closed-loop manipulation.
2. **Representational Bottleneck**: Explicit reasoning, especially when confined to the linguistic space, struggles to faithfully capture "ineffable" physical attributes (e.g., precise spatial relationships, force dynamics) that are critical for robust physical interaction in the real world.

Against this background, the work introduces "Latent CoT" as an emerging paradigm in general VLMs that addresses these limitations by performing multi-step inference directly in continuous latent spaces. This allows intermediate reasoning to be compact, implicit, and tightly integrated with downstream predictions. Prior work in embodied intelligence, such as LCDrive for autonomous driving and Thinkact for motion planning, has explored similar latent reasoning concepts.

LaST0 fits into this landscape by proposing a novel **Latent Spatio-Temporal Chain-of-Thought (LaST CoT)** specifically tailored for robotic manipulation. It aims to bridge the gap between efficient, real-time control and comprehensive, physically-grounded reasoning. By operating in a compact latent space, LaST0 seeks to overcome the latency and representational limitations of explicit CoT, while simultaneously capturing fine-grained physical and robotic dynamics and ensuring temporal coherence in manipulation tasks. The paper also introduces a dual-system architecture (Mixture-of-Transformers) to manage the distinct operational frequencies of reasoning and action experts efficiently.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model capable of efficient "reason-before-act" behavior for robotic manipulation, addressing the limitations inherent in existing explicit Chain-of-Thought (CoT) VLA approaches. The motivation behind LaST0 is multifaceted:

1. **Mitigate Inference Latency**: Current explicit CoT VLA models, which generate linguistic reasoning traces or predict future observations, rely on autoregressive generation. This process introduces non-negligible inference latency, thereby restricting the temporal resolution necessary for real-time robotic manipulation. This latency also hinders effective temporal reasoning, compromising the temporal consistency vital for closed-loop control. The objective is to enable faster inference speeds without sacrificing reasoning depth.

2. **Overcome Representational Bottlenecks in Physical Reasoning**: Explicit reasoning, particularly when confined to the linguistic space, is limited in its capacity to fully represent complex, ineffable physical attributes crucial for robotic interaction. Robots must reason about nuanced physical dynamics, such as precise geometric relationships, material properties, and contact forces, which are challenging to articulate verbally. The motivation is to create a reasoning space that can capture these fine-grained physical and robotic dynamics more faithfully.

3. **Enhance Temporal Consistency and Coherence**: Robotic manipulation tasks often require actions to be executed coherently over time, where past actions influence future possibilities. Existing explicit reasoning methods struggle to maintain temporal consistency, potentially leading to disjointed or unstable manipulation sequences. A key objective is to design a mechanism that propagates reasoning representations across time to form temporally consistent implicit reasoning trajectories, thereby improving action coherence.

4. **Develop an Efficient Dual-System Architecture**: The integration of sophisticated reasoning with high-frequency, low-level control presents an architectural challenge. Decoupling these two processes while maintaining seamless information flow is crucial for both efficiency and performance. The motivation is to propose a dual-system architecture that can efficiently coordinate a slow, deliberative reasoning process with a fast, reactive action generation process.

In essence, the paper aims to create a VLA model that reasons in a compact, physically-grounded latent space, enabling both efficient inference and rich, multimodal understanding of the environment and robot state, ultimately leading to more robust and generalizable robotic manipulation across a spectrum of tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

LaST0 introduces a dual-system VLA model architecture implemented via a Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) design, enabling efficient "reason-before-act" behavior through a Latent Spatio-Temporal Chain-of-Thought (LaST CoT).

#### 3.1. VLA Problem Formulation

The robotic manipulation task is framed as a probabilistic sequence decision-making problem. At each timestep $t$, the policy receives a natural language instruction $l_t$ and visual observations $I_t$ (RGB images). The objective is to generate an optimal action sequence $a_{t:t+H}$. The action space is defined within the Special Euclidean group SE(3). For single-arm configurations, a 7-DoF end-effector pose control is used ($[\Delta x, \Delta y, \Delta z]$, [roll, pitch, yaw], and gripper state). This is extended to 14-DoF for dual-arm configurations and includes base linear and angular velocities for mobile manipulation.

#### 3.2. LaST0 Architecture

- **Foundation Model**: LaST0 is initialized from Janus-Pro (Chen et al., 2025a), using DeepSeek-LLM 1.5B as its backbone.
- **Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT)**: The standard decoder-only transformer is repurposed into a unified MoT dual-system. This design integrates two specialized experts within the same latent space:
 - **Slow Reasoning Expert**: Operates at a low frequency, performing autoregressive latent inference to construct the LaST CoT from language and low-frequency visual inputs.
 - **Fast Acting Expert**: Operates at a high frequency, generating actions conditioned on high-frequency observations and periodically updated latent representations. These experts share a global self-attention context but possess task-specific parameters for non-embedding components (FFN, attention projections, Layer Normalizations).
- **Vision Encoder**: SigLIP-Large (Zhai et al., 2023) extracts semantic features from 384x384 RGB observations. These features serve as real-time contextual input for the MoT experts and as ground-truth targets for the visual component of the latent CoT.
- **Point Cloud Encoder (Training Only)**: Uni3D (Zhou et al., 2023b) is used during training to encode ground-truth point clouds into compact 3D features, providing geometric perception for the latent CoT space. It is not used during inference.
- **MLP Components**: Multi-Layer Perceptrons are used for:
 - A 3D Projector to align point cloud features for latent CoT supervision.
 - Components for the Flow Matching action policy (Black et al., 2024), including a timestep MLP, a noised-action MLP, and a projector MLP for the flow velocity field.

#### 3.3. Latent Spatio-Temporal Chain-of-Thought (LaST CoT)

The LaST CoT is designed to capture fine-grained physical and robotic dynamics efficiently:
- **Latent Embedding Construction**: For each future timestep $k$ within a horizon $H$, features are extracted from three modalities:
 - **2D Visual Latents ($z^v_k$)**: From future RGB frames $I_{t+k}$ using the frozen SigLIP-Large encoder.
 - **3D Geometric Latents ($z^p_k$)**: From future point clouds $P_{t+k}$ using the Uni3D encoder.
 - **Robot Proprioceptive Latents ($z^s_k$)**: From future robot states $s_{t+k}$ via an action tokenizer.
- **Token Efficiency**: Average pooling compresses feature maps of each modality into a single representative token, resulting in a compact set of embeddings $\{z^v_k, z^p_k, z^s_k\}$ per step.
- **Sequence Structure**: These tokens are organized in an interleaved, chronological order: $Z_{GT} = [z^v_1, z^p_1, z^s_1, \dots, z^v_H, z^p_H, z^s_H]$, preserving causal physical dependencies. Special tokens (`<latent start>`, `<latent end>`, `<latent pad>`) delineate the reasoning segment. During training, `<latent pad>` tokens are replaced with $Z_{GT}$ for teacher forcing. During inference, the slow expert autoregressively fills these positions.
- **Latent Supervision Strategy**: The slow reasoning expert is trained using continuous latent regression rather than discrete token likelihoods. A cosine similarity loss ($L_{latent}$) is employed to align the predicted latent states ($\hat{Z}$) with ground-truth representations ($Z_{GT}$), encouraging directional alignment in the latent space.

#### 3.4. Dual-System Coordination (Asynchronous Frequency Coordination)

- The reasoning expert and acting expert operate at decoupled frequencies, controlled by update ratios $\kappa$ (e.g., 2, 4, 8).
- The slow expert is activated only at sparse keyframes ($t \pmod \kappa = 0$) to perform latent CoT reasoning.
- The fast expert runs at the native control frequency at every timestep, generating actions conditioned on high-frequency observations ($I_{fast}$) and the most recent latent reasoning output from the slow expert.
- Crucially, due to the unified token sequence in the MoT architecture, the fast expert can efficiently attend to the linguistic goal and the cached Latent CoT tokens through a shared attention mechanism. Key-Value states computed by the slow expert are cached.

#### 3.5. Training Recipe

- **Large-Scale Robotic Pretraining**: LaST0 is pretrained on over 400K trajectories (28M frames) from diverse datasets (Open-X-Embodiment, DROID, RoboMIND). During pretraining, VGGT (Wang et al., 2025a) generates synthetic 3D point clouds to provide initial geometric latent inputs, ensuring the model learns spatial dynamics even without real-world depth sensors.
- **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)**: A joint SFT strategy optimizes both experts:
 - The slow reasoning expert minimizes the $L_{latent}$ loss.
 - The fast acting expert minimizes the standard Flow Matching loss $L_{flow}$ for action denoising.
 - The action expert is trained with randomly mixed fast-slow operating ratios (e.g., 1:1, 1:2, 1:4) to enable adaptive selection of execution frequencies during deployment and improve robustness.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of LaST0 involved extensive experiments in both simulation and real-world scenarios, focusing on manipulation performance and inference efficiency.

#### 4.1. Simulation Experiment (RLBench Benchmark)

- **Manipulation Performance**: LaST0-3.3B achieved a mean success rate of 82% across 10 RLBench manipulation tasks. This surpassed strong baselines, including HybridVLA-7B (74%), $\pi_{0.5}$-3B (65%), and CogACT-7B (61%). LaST0 attained the highest success rate on 7 out of 10 tasks.
- **Inference Efficiency**: LaST0 operated at an inference speed of 15.4 Hz on an NVIDIA 4090 GPU, which is significantly faster than explicit CoT methods like CoT-VLA (1.1 Hz) and competitive with $\pi_{0.5}$ (13.8 Hz).
- **Attention Analysis**: Attention heatmaps indicated that LaST0 demonstrated a concentrated attention pattern on manipulated objects and robot-object interaction areas, suggesting superior spatio-temporal understanding compared to explicit CoT variants or models without CoT.

#### 4.2. Simulation Experiment (LIBERO Benchmark)

- **Overall Performance**: LaST0 consistently outperformed all baselines with a state-of-the-art mean success rate of 98.1%.
- **Generalization**: Achieved near-saturation performance in the LIBERO-Spatial (99.2%) and LIBERO-Object (99.6%) suites, which assess generalization to novel layouts and objects.
- **Long-Horizon Tasks**: In the challenging LIBERO-Long suite, LaST0 achieved a 95.6% success rate, outperforming OpenVLA-OFT (94.5%) and $\pi_{0.5}$ (92.4%), demonstrating enhanced capability for long-horizon execution.
- **Latent vs. Explicit CoT**: The performance gap between LaST0 and CoT-VLA (81.1%) highlighted the advantage of compact latent reasoning in capturing physical dynamics and improving action execution stability.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies (on RLBench Tasks)

- **Importance of Latent CoT Modalities**: The study showed that each individual modality (image, point cloud, robot state latent) contributed to performance, with combinations providing further improvements. This validated the necessity of modeling comprehensive physical dynamics in the latent space.
- **Number of Latent Tokens**: Performance substantially improved from 68% (no latent tokens) to 82% (one token per modality). Further increasing token count yielded no significant accuracy gains, suggesting that a single token is sufficient for compact physical information encoding.
- **Temporal Coverage**: Increasing the temporal horizon of latent reasoning from 0 to 4 steps improved performance from 68% to 82%. Beyond 4 steps, further improvement was not significant, indicating an optimal temporal coverage.
- **Collaboration Frequency**: Training with mixed fast-slow operating ratios (1:1, 1:2, 1:4) resulted in the best performance (82% at 1:4 test ratio), indicating robust coordination between reasoning and action experts. Overly infrequent collaboration (1:8) led to performance degradation (74%).
- **Architecture Components**: A single-backbone implementation (without MoT dual-system) achieved a 74% success rate, 8% lower than LaST0, confirming the significance of the MoT design.
- **Modality Order and Latent Strides**: The chosen [Visual, Geometric, State] modality order performed best, though the model showed robustness to permutations. Sparse latent strides (e.g., 8) for dense data proved beneficial for capturing long-horizon dynamics.

#### 4.4. Real-World Experiment

- **Diverse Platforms**: LaST0 was evaluated on Franka single-arm and dual-arm setups, AgileX mobile manipulation platforms, and TienKung 2.0 humanoid dexterous hands.
- **Overall Performance**: Achieved a mean success rate of 72% ($\pm$3) on Franka platforms, outperforming SpatialVLA (41% $\pm$2), $\pi_{0.5}$ (59% $\pm$4), and CoT-VLA (50% $\pm$2).
- **Long-Horizon Tasks**: For multi-step tasks (e.g., "Place egg on bread"), LaST0 maintained higher success rates across consecutive steps (0.66 $\rightarrow$ 0.47 $\rightarrow$ 0.33) compared to $\pi_{0.5}$ (0.47 $\rightarrow$ 0.20 $\rightarrow$ 0.07), suggesting better preservation of coherent latent representations over extended horizons.
- **Generalizability**: Demonstrated precise navigation and coordinated dual-arm control in mobile manipulation, and successfully handled complex articulated object manipulation with dexterous hands, indicating generalizability beyond tabletop settings.
- **Qualitative Analysis**: Produced smooth, continuous motions and exhibited closed-loop capabilities, adapting to dynamic environmental changes (e.g., persistent erasing on a whiteboard with new marks).
- **Failure Analysis**: Common failure cases included manipulation height errors, object collisions in dual-arm tasks, and incorrect manipulation positions for dexterous hand tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

LaST0 presents advancements in the field of robotic Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, addressing identified limitations of prior Chain-of-Thought (CoT) approaches.

The primary significance stems from its demonstration of efficient "reason-before-act" behavior by employing a **Latent Spatio-Temporal Chain-of-Thought (LaST CoT)**. This approach aims to overcome the critical drawbacks of explicit CoT methods:
- **Reduced Inference Latency**: By performing reasoning in a compact latent space, LaST0 achieves significantly faster inference speeds (15.4 Hz), making real-time robotic control more feasible. This efficiency is crucial for responsive manipulation in dynamic environments.
- **Enhanced Representational Capacity**: The latent space, by incorporating multimodal information (2D visual, 3D geometric, and robot proprioceptive states), is designed to capture fine-grained physical and robotic dynamics that are difficult to articulate linguistically. This physically-grounded reasoning is intended to lead to more robust and accurate manipulation.
- **Improved Temporal Coherence**: The spatio-temporal nature of the latent CoT, which autoregressively models future dynamics, is designed to enable temporally consistent implicit reasoning. This feature is intended to result in more coherent and stable action generation in closed-loop manipulation tasks, particularly for long-horizon operations.

The **dual-system architecture** implemented via a Mixture-of-Transformers further contributes to the significance by effectively coordinating low-frequency deliberative reasoning with high-frequency action generation. This architectural design, coupled with asynchronous frequency coordination, allows for an adaptive balance between foresight and responsiveness, enhancing overall system robustness.

The empirical results, demonstrating state-of-the-art performance across a diverse range of simulation benchmarks (RLBench, LIBERO) and complex real-world tasks (tabletop, mobile, and dexterous hand manipulation), suggest the efficacy and generalizability of the LaST0 framework. Its superior performance in long-horizon tasks indicates an improved ability to maintain task progress and environmental state representations over extended periods.

Potential impacts of this work include:
- **Advancement of Robotic Foundation Models**: LaST0 contributes to the development of more capable and efficient foundation models for robotics, integrating advanced reasoning with practical control. This could serve as a building block for future robotic systems that demand sophisticated interaction with complex environments.
- **Broader Robot Deployment**: By enabling more reliable, efficient, and intelligent robot learning and control, LaST0 could facilitate the deployment of robots in a wider array of applications, from manufacturing and logistics to service robotics and domestic assistance, where responsive and adaptive manipulation is required.
- **Inspiration for Future Research**: The concept of latent spatio-temporal reasoning in multimodal contexts could inspire further research into physically-grounded AI, potentially leading to models that better understand and interact with the physical world. Future work involving explicit physical constraints, 3D relational graphs, and reinforcement learning for post-training could further enhance the model's capabilities and robustness.
