# Latent Thought Flow: Efficient Latent Reasoning in Large Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Latent Thought Flow (LTF), developed by researchers at Singapore Management University and Ant Group, enables Large Language Models to perform adaptive, efficient multi-step reasoning directly within their continuous latent space. The framework, which leverages GFlowNets, achieved an average accuracy improvement of 9.5% and a 27.2% reduction in reasoning length compared to existing latent reasoning methods across various LLM backbones and benchmarks.

## Problem

- Traditional Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting incurs a "linguistic space bottleneck" as intermediate thoughts must be decoded into discrete tokens, leading to high inference overhead and potentially redundant textual forms.
- Existing latent reasoning methods often learn deterministic reasoning paths or suffer from posterior collapse, lacking a principled mechanism to adaptively allocate sampling probability across diverse latent trajectories with varying correctness and computational cost.
- Balancing reasoning accuracy with computational efficiency remains a core challenge for LLMs in complex, multi-step tasks.

## Method

- **LTF** models reasoning as variable-length continuous trajectories within the LLM's latent space, allowing the model to adaptively determine the necessary internal computation for a given task.
- It adapts ==Generative Flow Networks (GFlowNets)== to learn a reward-proportional distribution over these continuous latent trajectories, where the probability of sampling a path is proportional to its combined utility (answer quality and computational cost).
- The framework introduces Entropy-Weighted Subtrajectory Balance (EW-SubTB) for refined credit assignment and Reference-Prior Regularization (RPR) to stabilize early exploration in the high-dimensional continuous latent space.

## Results

- LTF consistently improved average accuracy by **9.5%** and reduced average reasoning length by **27.2%** compared to strong latent reasoning baselines across LLaMA-**3.2** 1B/3B, LLaMA-**3.1** 8B, and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B backbones.
- The framework maintained superior performance even under extreme compression (reasoning length constrained to 1 step) on MATH and AQUA-RAT datasets, demonstrating efficient semantic information preservation.
- In transfer learning settings, LTF achieved the highest average accuracy on out-of-domain math datasets (GSM-Hard, SVAMP, MultiArith), outperforming baselines by up to **2.38%**, while maintaining concise reasoning lengths.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reasoning in LLMs can be effectively conceptualized and learned as an adaptive flow over continuous latent states, moving beyond discrete textual tokens or fixed-length latent steps.
> - GFlowNets can be successfully extended to continuous, high-dimensional latent spaces to learn stochastic generative policies that balance multiple objectives, such as accuracy and computational cost.
> - Entropy-aware weighting during credit assignment (EW-SubTB) lets GFlowNets better explore and concentrate mass on diverse, structured latent paths, while reference-prior regularization stabilizes learning and guides toward semantically meaningful latent states, especially early on.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Large Language Models (LLMs) are increasingly applied to complex tasks requiring multi-step reasoning, such as mathematical problem-solving, embodied planning, and code generation. Traditional methods like Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting facilitate this by decomposing problems into explicit textual rationales. However, this approach introduces a "linguistic space bottleneck" where each intermediate thought must be decoded into discrete tokens. This process incurs significant inference overhead and can constrain internal computation into potentially redundant textual forms, leading to an inherent trade-off between reasoning accuracy and computational efficiency.

Latent reasoning emerges as an alternative to mitigate this bottleneck by performing multi-step computation directly within the LLM's continuous hidden-state space. Existing latent reasoning methods, such as those employing pause tokens, stepwise internalization, compressed CoT, or continuous thought vectors, demonstrate that deliberation need not always be expressed linguistically. However, many current latent reasoning approaches predominantly learn deterministic reasoning paths, often through compression, distillation, or reward maximization under a fixed computational budget. They typically lack a principled mechanism for allocating sampling probability across diverse latent reasoning trajectories that may vary in correctness and computational cost. This deficiency can lead to issues like inheriting verbosity from training rationales (in maximum-likelihood objectives) or posterior collapse onto a few high-reward modes (in reinforcement learning).

"Latent Thought Flow" (LTF) addresses this gap by proposing a framework that views latent reasoning from a distributional perspective. It aims to develop a principled sampler capable of modeling the trade-offs between accuracy and computational cost, concentrating probability mass on concise and accurate trajectories while suppressing redundant or erroneous ones. This approach seeks to enable LLMs to dynamically allocate their computational budget for reasoning, moving beyond fixed or deterministic latent paths towards a more adaptive and efficient reasoning process.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop an efficient latent reasoning framework for LLMs that can adaptively balance reasoning accuracy and computational cost. The authors propose Latent Thought Flow (LTF) to achieve this by:

1. **Modeling Reasoning as Variable-Length Continuous Trajectories:** Instead of relying on fixed-length or deterministic intermediate steps, LTF aims to allow LLMs to determine "how much" internal reasoning is needed for a given task. This involves sampling continuous latent thought states (`z_t`) and adaptively deciding when to terminate the reasoning process (`⊥`).
2. **Learning a Reward-Proportional Distribution over Latent Trajectories:** The work posits that for a given question, there exists a manifold of plausible reasoning paths, only a sparse subset of which is both logically consistent and computationally efficient. The objective is not merely to find a single optimal path but to learn a distribution (`p*(τ | x, y) ∝ R_x,y(τ)`) where the probability of sampling a trajectory is proportional to its utility, which combines answer quality and computational cost. This addresses the limitations of deterministic paths and mode collapse in existing methods.
3. **Adapting GFlowNets for Continuous Latent Space Reasoning:** To realize the reward-proportional sampling, LTF leverages Generative Flow Networks (GFlowNets). GFlowNets are designed to learn stochastic generative policies whose terminal samples are proportional to an unnormalized reward, enabling the preservation of diverse high-utility solutions. A key motivation is to extend GFlowNets, traditionally applied to discrete textual or symbolic reasoning, to the continuous, high-dimensional latent space of LLMs.
4. **Addressing Technical Challenges in Continuous GFlowNet Implementation:** The move to continuous latent space introduces specific challenges:
 - **High-dimensional continuous vectors:** Standard discrete token-level balance equations are not directly applicable.
 - **Sparse answer-level supervision:** The utility of intermediate latent subtrajectories is only observable after the final answer is generated.
 - **Unconstrained exploration:** Unrestricted exploration in a continuous latent space can lead to semantically meaningless states.
5. **Improving Credit Assignment with Entropy-Weighted Subtrajectory Balance:** The motivation here is to refine how terminal rewards are propagated to intermediate latent steps. Uniform weighting of subtrajectories can be suboptimal. By considering the entropy of subtrajectories, the model can allocate stronger supervision to high-entropy paths (regions with richer information spread) to guide exploration more effectively.
6. **Anchoring Exploration with Reference-Prior Regularization:** To prevent unstable exploration in the vast continuous latent space during early training, the authors aim to introduce a mechanism that grounds latent thoughts to semantically meaningful regions, especially when teacher-generated rationales are available. This regularization serves as a warm-start for exploration.

In essence, the motivation is to develop a robust, adaptive, and efficient latent reasoning framework that leverages the strengths of distributional learning and GFlowNets to overcome the limitations of explicit CoT and existing latent reasoning methods, ultimately improving the accuracy-efficiency frontier for LLMs in complex tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Latent Thought Flow (LTF) is presented as a reward-proportional framework that models reasoning as an adaptive flow over continuous latent thought states. It comprises four main components:

1. **Variable-Length Latent Thought Trajectories:**
 - **Mechanism:** LTF replaces explicit textual rationales with an adaptive latent reasoning process. Given an input `x`, the model samples a variable-length latent thought trajectory `τ = (z_1:T, ⊥)`, where `z_t` are continuous thought states in the LLM's latent space, and `⊥` signifies an adaptive stopping decision (0 ≤ T ≤ T_max).
 - **Sampling Policy:** Each latent thought `z_t` is sampled from a variational latent reasoning process `q_φ(·|s_t)`. The policy `q_φ` is modeled as a Gaussian distribution, `N(μ_φ(s_t), diag(σ^2_φ(s_t)))`, where `s_t = (h_x, z_1:t)` is the current context state and `h_x` is the input embedding. The termination probability `π_⊥(s_t)` is given by `p_ψ(<eos_r>|s_t)`. This reparameterization allows gradients to flow through the latent computation.
 - **Answer Generation:** After sampling a trajectory `τ`, the LLM generates the final answer `y` conditioned on `x` and `τ` using its language head `p_ψ(y | x, τ)`.

2. **Reward-Proportional Target Distribution:**
 - **Utility Function:** For each training pair `(x, y)`, a non-negative accuracy-efficiency utility `R_x,y(τ) = V_x,y(τ) * exp(-λ_c * C(τ))` is assigned to a trajectory `τ`.
 - `V_x,y(τ)` (answer quality): Defined as `Ver(y, ŷ_τ) + exp(1/|y| * log p_ψ(y | x, τ))`, where `Ver` is task-specific accuracy and the second term provides a dense likelihood signal.
 - `C(τ)` (computation cost): By default, this is the length of the trajectory, `T`, favoring shorter paths. `λ_c` is a hyperparameter controlling the cost penalty.
 - **Target Distribution:** This utility induces a target distribution `p*(τ | x, y) = R_x,y(τ) / Z_R(x, y)`, where `Z_R` is an intractable normalization constant. The goal is to train the sampler `q_φ(τ | x)` to match this distribution, which allows multiple correct and efficient trajectories to coexist.

3. **Continuous GFlowNet Objective:**
 - **Adaptation for Continuous Space:** LTF extends GFlowNets to continuous latent thoughts. The forward transition density `P_F(s_t+1 | s_t)` for a non-terminal state `s_t+1` is `(1 - π_⊥(s_t)) * q_φ(z_t+1 | s_t)`.
 - **Flow Calculation:** The flow `F(s_t)` for an intermediate prefix `s_t` is derived from the reward of immediately stopping at `s_t`, `F(s_t) = R_x,y(s_t → ⊥) / π_⊥(s_t)`. This avoids the need for a separate learned flow estimator.
 - **Subtrajectory Balance (SubTB):** The SubTB objective `L_SubTB(x, y) = E_τ∼q_φ(·|s_0) [∑_(0≤i<j≤T) χ^2_i:j]` is used to enforce consistency over all subtrajectories, where `χ_i:j` is a residual derived from the GFlowNet balance condition involving flows and transition probabilities. This objective helps propagate terminal rewards to intermediate steps.
 - **Entropy-Weighted Subtrajectory Balance (EW-SubTB):** To improve credit assignment, LTF introduces `ω_i:j`, an entropy-aware weight for each subtrajectory. `ω_i:j` is defined using the relative entropy of latent subtrajectories, giving larger weights to high-entropy (more diverse/informative) paths. The empirical EW-SubTB objective is `L_flow(x, y) = 1/S * ∑_(s=1 to S) ∑_(0≤i<j≤T(s)) ω_(i:j)^(s) * (χ_(i:j)^(s))^2`. This reweighting focuses optimization on areas where the sampler needs stronger supervision without altering the target distribution.

4. **Reference-Prior Regularization:**
 - **Purpose:** To ground early exploration in the high-dimensional continuous latent space and prevent drift into semantically meaningless states.
 - **Mechanism:** When teacher-generated reference rationales `r` are available, a reference branch `p_ref_θ'(z_t | s_t-1)` (also a Gaussian) is used. The `L_prior` objective is `-E_(x,r)∼D [log p_ref_θ'(r | x)]`, which encourages the prior sampler to align with these reference traces. This regularizer is annealed during training.

**Training Objective and Inference:**
The final training objective `L = L_flow + λ_ans * L_ans + λ_prior * L_prior` combines:
- `L_flow`: The EW-SubTB loss for learning the reward-proportional latent sampler.
- `L_ans`: A cross-entropy loss for the final answer prediction, `L_ans(x, y) = -E_τ∼q_φ(·|H_x) [log p_ψ(y | τ)]`.
- `L_prior`: The reference-prior regularization.
During inference, the LLM samples latent thoughts using `q_φ(z_t+1 | s_t)` (Eq. 3) and then generates the answer without access to gold rationales. For efficiency, a single trajectory is used by default, but multiple latent trajectories can be sampled for larger test-time budgets.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents experimental results under finetuning and transfer learning settings across various LLM backbones (LLaMA-3.2 1B/3B, LLaMA-3.1 8B, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B) and reasoning datasets. Key findings include:

**Finetuning Performance:**
- **Accuracy-Efficiency Trade-off:** LTF consistently achieves improved accuracy-efficiency trade-offs compared to explicit Chain-of-Thought (CoT) and other latent reasoning baselines (iCoT, CODI, Coconut, SoftCoT++, CoLaR, ReGuLaR) on GSM8K-Aug, ASDiv-Aug, and DU datasets.
- **Quantitative Improvements:**
 - Compared to the strong latent reasoning baseline ReGuLaR, LTF (LLaMA-1B) improved average accuracy from 34.58% to 37.09% on GSM8K-Aug while reducing average reasoning length from 3.69 to 3.34.
 - Across all backbones, LTF improved accuracy by an average of 9.5% and reduced reasoning length by 27.2% compared to strong latent reasoning baselines.
 - Specifically, on LLaMA-3.1 8B, LTF improved average accuracy from 74.40% (ReGuLaR) to 77.08% while reducing reasoning length from 2.13 to 1.94.
 - LTF demonstrates adaptive allocation of reasoning budgets, achieving higher accuracy on ASDiv-Aug (94.02% from 92.00%) with a slight reduction in reasoning length (1.24 from 1.28).

**Extreme Compression Evaluation:**
- LTF maintains superior performance even under extreme compression, where reasoning length is often constrained to 1 step. On MATH and AQUA-RAT datasets, LTF consistently outperformed baselines across all LLM backbones.
- Compared to ReGuLaR, LTF showed average accuracy improvements of 2.72% on MATH and 3.61% on AQUA-RAT, indicating its ability to preserve task-relevant semantic information in highly compressed latent representations.

**Transfer Learning Performance:**
- On out-of-domain math datasets (GSM-Hard, SVAMP, MultiArith), LTF achieved the highest average accuracy across all backbones, outperforming ReGuLaR by 1.89% (LLaMA-1B), 1.84% (LLaMA-3B), 2.11% (LLaMA-8B), and 2.38% (DS-1.5B).
- LTF also maintained a more concise reasoning length than ReGuLaR and explicit CoT methods in all transfer learning settings, validating its generalization ability and compression efficiency.

**Ablation Studies:**
- **Entropy-Weighted Subtrajectory Balance (EW-SubTB):** Entropy weighting consistently improved accuracy with minimal impact on reasoning length. The gains increased with a larger rollout sample size `S` (e.g., +0.95% average accuracy for `S=20` with EW-SubTB), suggesting its effectiveness in credit assignment for diverse sampled subtrajectories.
- **Latent Thought Exploration Objective:** Comparing GFlowNet-based objectives (DB, TB, LTF) against a reinforcement learning baseline (GRPO), GFlowNet methods achieved substantially better accuracy-length trade-offs. LTF, with its EW-SubTB, attained the highest average accuracy (59.68%) while significantly reducing average reasoning length (1.91) compared to DB (55.98% Acc, 7.28 #L) and TB (56.80% Acc, 7.51 #L). This indicates better alignment of latent thought exploration with both reasoning effectiveness and conciseness.
- **Reference-Prior Regularization (RPR):** Ablation showed that RPR helps stabilize exploration and results in more concise reasoning paths. LTF with RPR improved average accuracy from 59.32% to 59.68% and reduced average reasoning length from 2.75 to 1.91 compared to LTF without RPR.
- **Test-Time Scaling:** Increasing the number of latent thought chains (`N`) during inference consistently improved accuracy without substantially increasing reasoning length. Increasing `N` from 1 to 10 improved average accuracy by 2.45% (from 59.68% to 62.13%) while reasoning length changed marginally (1.91 to 1.93). This demonstrates effective test-time scaling for accuracy.

**Analysis of Reasoning Trajectories (Appendix):**
- An analysis of average reasoning entropy showed that CoLaR had the lowest entropy (0.013), suggesting deterministic paths. ReGuLaR had higher entropy (0.019), indicating more stochasticity. LTF without entropy weighting had the highest entropy (0.030), potentially leading to overly stochastic and unstructured reasoning.
- LTF with EW-SubTB achieved a balanced entropy (0.024), lower than LTF w/o EW but higher than CoLaR and ReGuLaR. This suggests that EW-SubTB regulates exploration, fostering diverse yet structured latent reasoning paths, which contributes to its performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Latent Thought Flow (LTF) framework represents a development in efficient latent reasoning for Large Language Models. By modeling reasoning as variable-length continuous trajectories and leveraging a continuous GFlowNet objective, LTF addresses the limitations of explicit Chain-of-Thought (CoT) methods, particularly the linguistic space bottleneck and high inference overhead. The framework's ability to allocate probability mass across reasoning trajectories proportionally to both answer quality and computational cost offers a principled approach to managing the accuracy-efficiency trade-off inherent in LLM reasoning.

The introduction of the Entropy-Weighted Subtrajectory Balance objective contributes to more effective credit assignment, guiding the sampler to focus on informative and diverse latent thought states rather than redundant ones. The reference-prior regularizer enhances training stability by grounding early exploration in meaningful latent spaces.

The experimental results indicate that LTF can improve reasoning accuracy while simultaneously reducing computational reasoning length across various tasks and LLM backbones. Its performance under extreme compression and its generalization capabilities in transfer learning settings suggest its robustness and applicability to a broader range of complex reasoning problems. The observation that LTF can adaptively allocate computational budget and effectively scale during inference by sampling multiple compact latent trajectories holds implications for deploying LLMs in resource-constrained environments or applications requiring dynamic computational allocation.

Potential impacts include:
- **Enhanced Efficiency:** The reduction in reasoning length without significant accuracy loss could lead to more efficient LLM inference, reducing computational costs and energy consumption.
- **Improved Scalability:** By enabling adaptive and concise latent reasoning, LTF could facilitate the application of LLMs to more complex, multi-step tasks where explicit rationales would be prohibitively long or costly.
- **Robustness and Generalization:** The demonstrated performance in transfer learning suggests improved generalization capabilities for LLMs equipped with LTF, making them more adaptable to new problem domains.
- **Advancement in Latent Reasoning:** LTF contributes to the theoretical and practical understanding of how LLMs can perform internal computation more effectively, pushing the frontier of latent-space deliberation.

The authors note limitations, primarily focusing on textual tasks, suggesting future work in extending LTF to other modalities like vision and speech. Further theoretical exploration of its generalization ability is also indicated. Despite these, LTF offers a direction for developing more efficient, adaptive, and capable reasoning LLMs.
