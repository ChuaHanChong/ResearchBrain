# AEGIS: Exploring the Limit of World Knowledge Capabilities for Unified Mulitmodal Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at multiple Hong Kong universities and Huawei Research introduced AEGIS, a multi-task benchmark, and Deterministic Checklist-based Evaluation (DCE) protocol to rigorously assess the world knowledge and reasoning capabilities of Unified Multimodal Models (UMMs). The evaluation revealed that current UMMs struggle with complex reasoning, and their visual decoders often act as a bottleneck, even when the language component demonstrates understanding.

## Problem

- Existing multimodal benchmarks are typically siloed, focusing on single tasks and failing to evaluate UMMs' unified operations or inter-task performance gaps.
- Current evaluation methods lack fine-grained diagnostics, making it difficult to pinpoint whether model failures stem from understanding (LLM) or generative modules.
- Many benchmarks have limited scope and difficulty, often over-representing common questions and neglecting the broad world knowledge and complex reasoning required for real-world scenarios.

## Method

- Developed AEGIS, a comprehensive multi-task benchmark featuring ==1,050 manually verified questions== across ==21 topics and 6 reasoning types== (spatial, temporal, causal, comparative, analogical, logical) for visual understanding, generation, editing, and interleaved generation.
- Introduced a novel ==Deterministic Checklist-based Evaluation (DCE)== protocol that uses atomic, verifiable "Y/N" judgment questions, generated by an MLLM from keywords and reference responses, to objectively assess UMM outputs.
- Established a robust methodology that includes human-in-the-loop taxonomy construction, prompt generation (clear and reasoning-enhanced), and high-quality input/ground-truth annotation.

## Results

- Closed-source UMMs like Gemini Nano Banana and GPT-4o+GPT-Image-1 significantly outperformed open-source models, with Gemini Nano Banana achieving the highest scores (e.g., **64.5%** in STEM understanding, **42.6%** in STEM generation).
- The Deterministic Checklist-based Evaluation (DCE) protocol demonstrated high human consistency, achieving **90.7%** agreement across all tasks.
- Performance consistently degraded with increased task complexity and reasoning demands, with temporal and causal reasoning being the most challenging types for UMMs.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - UMMs possess more world knowledge than they can effectively apply when complex reasoning is required, indicating a reasoning bottleneck rather than a pure knowledge deficit.
> - The visual decoder acts as a significant bottleneck, often failing to render concepts correctly even when the LLM component provides accurate textual attributes.
> - Higher quality and more abundant training data, rather than just model scale, play a crucial role in enhancing UMMs' world knowledge capabilities.
