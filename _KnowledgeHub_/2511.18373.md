# MASS: Motion-Aware Spatial-Temporal Grounding for Physics Reasoning and Comprehension in Vision-Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> A new framework, MASS (Motion-Aware Spatial–temporal Grounding), and its accompanying benchmark, MASS-Bench, enable Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to better comprehend physics-driven reasoning in videos by injecting explicit spatial-temporal and motion cues. Models enhanced with MASS showed up to an 8.7% improvement over prior open-source state-of-the-art on physics-related tasks and matched or exceeded closed-source models on physical abnormality detection.

## Problem

- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) frequently struggle with complex physics-driven reasoning involving fine-grained 3D spatial layouts, intricate motion patterns, and precise temporal dynamics.
- Existing datasets lack the dense spatiotemporal and motion-level annotations required to train VLMs for robust physics-grounded reasoning.
- VLMs often rely on implicit priors, leading to physically implausible interpretations or overlooking abnormalities, especially in AI-generated content (AIGC) videos.

## Method

- Introduced ==MASS-Bench==, a comprehensive benchmark comprising 4,350 real-world and AIGC videos with 8,361 physics-related QA pairs and dense entity-level motion-grounded annotations.
- Developed MASS, a model-agnostic approach that injects ==explicit spatial-temporal signals== into the VLM's language space using ==depth-based 3D encoding==, visual grounding, and a motion tracker.
- Employed Reinforcement Fine-tuning (RFT) with ==Temporal Group Relative Policy Optimization (T-GRPO)== to strengthen cross-modal alignment and multi-stage physics reasoning in VLMs.

## Results

- MASS-augmented VLMs achieved up to an **8.7%** improvement over the previous best open-source model (Qwen3-VL-8B) on physics reasoning tasks.
- Qwen2.5-VL-7B + MASS demonstrated performance comparable to closed-source state-of-the-art VLMs like Gemini-2.5-Flash, and surpassed it in Physical Abnormality Detection.
- The framework also showed strong generalization, outperforming all baselines on general-purpose real-world video QA benchmarks such as MVBench.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit motion-aware spatial-temporal grounding is crucial for robust physics reasoning in VLMs, demonstrating more impact than simply increasing model scale.
> - High-quality, densely annotated datasets like MASS-Bench are essential for effectively training VLMs to perform physics-related comprehension and abnormality detection tasks.
> - Reinforcement Fine-tuning (RFT) significantly enhances VLMs' ability to integrate grounded visual cues with spatial-temporal structures for complex physics reasoning, outperforming Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT).

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have demonstrated proficiency in various standard video tasks, including captioning, event recognition, and scene understanding. However, the existing research landscape indicates a gap in their ability to perform robust physics-related reasoning, which necessitates a deeper understanding of motion dynamics, 3D spatial layouts, and complex temporal interactions. This limitation arises because physical principles, unlike explicit visual cues for tasks such as segmentation, must often be inferred from indirect and ambiguous visual evidence. Current VLMs frequently struggle to generalize across diverse physical processes and tend to rely on superficial correlations rathercribed in training data, especially due to the scarcity of datasets containing dense spatiotemporal and motion-level annotations.

The emergence of AI-generated content (AIGC) videos further exacerbates this challenge, as these videos frequently exhibit implausible trajectories, inconsistent depth, and temporally incoherent motion, leading VLMs to miss or hallucinate physics violations. Prior works have attempted to enhance VLM capabilities in spatial, temporal, and motion understanding through specialized models for motion grounding, visual grounding, and action analysis. However, a systematic integration of these fine-grained cues into VLMs for elevated, physics-centric reasoning has remained largely unexplored. Furthermore, existing physics-oriented datasets often provide coarse annotations, which limits the development of a mechanism-level understanding of physical phenomena in VLMs. This research positions itself to address these identified limitations by introducing a method to explicitly encode motion-aware spatial–temporal information and a benchmark designed to facilitate the development of VLMs capable of deeper physical comprehension and abnormality detection.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance Vision-Language Models' capabilities in physics reasoning and comprehension by enabling them to better process and understand motion dynamics and spatial interactions within videos. The work aims to bridge the gap between the structured dynamics of the physical world and the current perception, comprehension, and reasoning abilities of VLMs.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges observed in current VLM performance:
- **Struggle with Complex Physical Cues:** VLMs perform adequately on standard video tasks but demonstrate limitations when confronted with intricate visual cues involving intertwined 3D spatial layouts, motion patterns, and temporal dynamics crucial for physical understanding.
- **Inability to Internalize Physical Principles:** Robust physical understanding requires VLMs to not only perceive visual cues but also to internalize real-world physical principles and commonsense expectations about object behavior. Current models often fail to connect visual evidence with underlying physical dynamics.
- **Challenges in Inferring Physics from Indirect Evidence:** Unlike tasks with explicit, localized supervision (e.g., segmentation), real-world physics must be inferred from indirect and often ambiguous visual evidence, making it difficult for VLMs to generalize across diverse physical processes.
- **Data Scarcity for Physics Reasoning:** There is a lack of comprehensive datasets with dense spatiotemporal and motion-level annotations specifically tailored for physics understanding, which leads models to memorize superficial correlations rather than develop physics-grounded reasoning.
- **Problems with AIGC Videos:** VLMs frequently struggle with physics violations and implausible trajectories commonly found in AIGC videos, often hallucinating physically plausible outcomes rather than detecting abnormalities.
- **High Cost of Training from Scratch:** Training VLMs from scratch to address these issues is computationally intensive and time-consuming. A more data-efficient approach is needed to augment existing VLMs with structured spatial and temporal representations.
- **Limitations of Prior Physics Datasets:** Previous physics-oriented datasets typically rely on coarse annotations, which restricts their capacity to induce deep, mechanism-level physical understanding.

To address these motivations, the specific objectives of the research are:
- To propose **MASS (Motion-Aware Spatial–temporal Grounding)**, a model-agnostic approach that translates physical-world context cues into interpretable representations, injecting spatial–temporal signals into the VLM language space. This involves depth-based 3D encoding, visual grounding, and motion tracking for object dynamics.
- To contribute **MASS-Bench**, a comprehensive benchmark consisting of 4,350 real-world and AIGC videos and 8,361 free-form video question-answering pairs focused on physics-related comprehension tasks, complete with detailed annotations (visual detections, sub-segment grounding, and full-sequence 3D motion tracking).
- To strengthen cross-modal alignment and reasoning in VLMs by applying reinforcement fine-tuning to the MASS approach.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of this research involves the development of a novel benchmark, MASS-Bench, and a model-agnostic approach, MASS, designed to enhance Vision-Language Models' (VLMs) physics reasoning and comprehension abilities.

#### 3.1. MASS-Bench: A Motion-Grounded Physics Reasoning and Comprehension Benchmark

The benchmark was developed to provide fine-grained, spatially and temporally consistent annotations for entities across frames, along with a balanced mixture of examples that follow and violate physical laws.

- **Data Collection:** MASS-Bench comprises 4,350 videos and 8,361 free-form video question-answering (QA) pairs. Videos are sourced from real-world datasets (Motion-Sight, ActivityNet) and AIGC datasets (VideoPhy2, VideoHallu, COSMOS, Wan2.2, Sora). The dataset includes both positive (physics-compliant) and negative (physics-violating) demonstrations.
- **Question Types:** The dataset features two types of questions:
 - **Factual questions:** Assess models' grounding of real versus hypothetical scenarios, adherence to physical principles, or identification of abnormalities in AIGC videos.
 - **Critical-thinking questions:** Require inferring causes, intentions, or detecting physical/commonsense abnormalities beyond explicit annotations.
 - Existing multiple-choice and labeled physics tasks were re-annotated into open-ended QA pairs using Claude-4-Sonnet and verified by human experts.
- **Categorization:** Questions are grouped into five categories: Spatial Understanding (SU, 33.3%), Temporal Understanding (TU, 19.5%), Motion & Action Recognition (MAR, 14.4%), Physics Comprehension (PC, 15.6%), and Physical Abnormality Detection (PA, 17.1%).
- **Motion Grounding Annotations:** Each video-question-answer pair includes detailed annotations: temporal video segmentation (frame ranges), entity-level visual grounding (bounding boxes), full-video temporal profiles for each entity, and per-segment motion attributes (first/last 3D positions and 3D motion vectors).

#### 3.2. MASS: Model-Agnostic Approach for Spatial–temporal Grounding

The MASS approach augments VLMs with dedicated spatial and motion encoders to explicitly extract and represent key visual signals, integrating them into the VLM language space.

- **Entity-Centric Visual Grounding:**
 - **Semantic Extraction:** Key components are extracted from the probing question to narrow the target for visual grounding.
 - **Object Detection and Tracking:** Grounding-DINO is used to detect bounding boxes for queried entities. A dynamic temporal-resolution scheme selects appropriate sub-sequence lengths to improve object tracking and temporal coherence. SAM2 is applied to generate segmentation masks for grounded regions.
 - **Grounding Profile:** A time-aligned grounding profile is constructed for each detected entity.
- **Spatial Motion Feature Extraction:**
 - **Motion Tracking:** CoTracker3 tracks arbitrary point trajectories within grounded regions for each entity and its sub-sequences. This yields spatial deviations, averaged motion vectors, and first/last tracked positions.
 - **Depth Estimation:** Depth Anything V2 estimates per-frame depth to provide spatial awareness.
 - **3D Trajectory Projection:** Sampled RGB frames are aligned with their corresponding depth maps to project tracked motion vectors into 3D space, yielding 3D positions and full spatio-temporal motion trajectories for each entity.
- **Visual Feature Representation (Serialization):**
 - **Textual Conversion:** The extracted spatial–motion signals are converted into structured natural-language representations aligned with the tokenized questions. This includes 3D start/end positions, motion vectors, bounding boxes within sub-segments, and associated temporal indices. Predefined natural-language templates are used for this serialization.
 - **Integration with VLM:** These "motion-derived textual tokens" are appended to the VLM's textual input stream, providing explicit physical information in a modality the VLM can process.
- **Training Pipeline:**
 - **Post-training:** Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Fine-tuning (RFT) are explored to enhance VLM reasoning.
 - **Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Reasoning:** The training encourages CoT reasoning, which helps VLMs process complex temporal and physical concepts through intermediate reasoning steps.
 - **Reinforcement Fine-tuning (RFT):** Temporal Group Relative Policy Optimization (T-GRPO) is adopted, which extends vanilla GRPO with temporal-aware rewards. Additional rewards include ROUGE for semantic coherence and a format reward for proper CoT generation.
 - **Frozen Encoders:** During post-training, the spatial–temporal encoders are kept frozen, with parameter updates applied only to the Large Language Model (LLM) backbone.

#### 3.3. Evaluation Protocol

Performance is reported as accuracy (%). GPT-4.1-Mini is used as an automated judge to assess the factual correctness of model answers against ground-truth annotations for physics comprehension and reasoning questions. Ablation studies evaluate backbone models, post-training strategies, input modalities, training data composition, and noise robustness.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluation of MASS demonstrates notable advancements in physics reasoning and comprehension for Vision-Language Models (VLMs).

- **Overall Performance Enhancement:** VLMs augmented with MASS consistently outperform comparable and larger baselines, as well as prior state-of-the-art (SoTA) models, on the MASS-Bench dataset. For instance, Qwen2.5-VL-7B+MASS and LLaVA-OneVision-7B+MASS surpassed the previous best model, Qwen3-VL-8B, by 8.7% and 6.0%, respectively, in overall accuracy.
- **Competitive Performance with Closed-Source Models:** Qwen2.5-VL-7B+MASS achieved performance comparable to closed-source SoTA VLMs like Gemini-2.5-Flash in overall accuracy. Notably, it outperformed Gemini-2.5-Flash on the Physical Abnormality Detection (PA) category, which is considered a reasoning-heavy task.
- **Critical Role of Explicit Spatial–Temporal Signals:** The results indicate that providing explicit spatial–temporal representations is crucial for reliable physics reasoning. Standard VLMs, even larger ones, often miss these physical cues when relying solely on raw visual inputs. Scaling model parameters (e.g., from 8B to 32B–38B) yielded only marginal gains compared to the improvements observed with MASS integration.
- **Importance of High-Quality Training Data:** Models fine-tuned with GRPO but without the rich annotations of MASS-Bench (e.g., Video-R1, VideoChat-R1) did not show clear improvements on physics comprehension tasks compared to non-GRPO fine-tuned VLMs. This suggests that high-quality, densely annotated training data, including both positive and negative demonstrations, is essential for strengthening reasoning and counteracting language priors in physics-related tasks.
- **Ablation Study: The Role of Reasoning:**
 - **Reasoning Dominance:** Post-training with MASS resulted in substantial performance gains that exceeded the influence of the base model choice. Qwen2.5-VL-7B combined with MASS achieved a 9.6% improvement, while LLaVA-OneVision-7B with MASS showed a 13.5% gain. This indicates that physics comprehension, especially for challenging tasks like abnormality detection, relies more on integrated reasoning over visual physics cues and spatial–temporal structure than on raw model capacity.
 - **Complementary Modalities:** Evaluating different input modalities (Text-only, Visual-only, Both) demonstrated that combining video tokens with motion profiles yields the best results. Text-only input consistently underperformed visual-only, confirming that many questions are video-dependent. The motion profile serves as effective complementary context rather than a substitute for raw video.
 - **Effectiveness of RFT:** Reinforcement Fine-tuning (GRPO) consistently provided larger and more robust gains across both backbone models compared to Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT). GRPO achieved 61.93% on Qwen2.5-VL-7B and 59.24% on LLaVA-OneVision-7B, while SFT offered limited or even slightly reduced performance in some cases. This suggests that direct optimization of task-level rewards in GRPO is more effective for learning complex physical reasoning that depends on temporal evidence and causal consistency.
 - **Training Data and Module Ablations:**
 - Training with a balanced mix of real-world and synthetic videos (Both) yielded the best results, indicating that real videos provide natural variation while synthetic data explicitly highlight physical patterns. Training with only real-world or only synthetic data resulted in lower performance.
 - The use of 3D motion (depth-aware projection) was crucial, as replacing it with 2D motion lowered accuracy for both models, demonstrating the value of explicit depth information for scene understanding.
- **Robustness to Noise (Supplementary):** MASS-augmented models exhibited reasonable stability under 10% Gaussian perturbations applied to bounding box/position and 3D trajectory information. While slight accuracy drops were observed, the models maintained substantial performance gains over their baselines, indicating that moderate value-level perturbations are tolerated. Failures primarily occurred when noise reversed the sign of motion components.
- **Generalization to Real-world Video QA (Supplementary):** Qwen2.5-VL-7B+MASS showed improved performance on two external real-world benchmarks, MMVU and MVBench, and narrowed the gap to closed-source models. LLaVA-OneVision-7B+MASS, however, experienced a performance drop on these out-of-distribution tasks, suggesting sensitivity to detection and tracking quality in such scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a notable contribution to the field of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) by addressing their limitations in physics reasoning and comprehension. The significance and potential impact of this work are multi-faceted:

- **Advancement in VLM Capabilities:** The proposed MASS approach enables VLMs to move beyond superficial pattern recognition to achieve a more nuanced understanding of physical dynamics. By explicitly integrating motion-aware spatial–temporal signals into the VLM's language space, the research facilitates more robust reasoning about object behavior, interactions, and physical constraints. This represents a step towards closing the gap between human-level physical intuition and AI systems.
- **Novel and Comprehensive Benchmark:** The introduction of MASS-Bench is a significant contribution to the research community. This benchmark, with its dense spatiotemporal and motion-level annotations, balanced mix of real-world and AIGC videos, and focus on both factual and critical-thinking physics questions, provides a robust evaluation platform. It supports the development and rigorous assessment of VLMs capable of deeper physical understanding, including the crucial ability to detect physical abnormalities.
- **Model-Agnostic Approach:** MASS's model-agnostic nature allows it to be integrated with existing VLM architectures without requiring extensive retraining from scratch. This flexibility makes it a practical solution for enhancing a wide range of VLMs, democratizing access to improved physics reasoning capabilities across different models.
- **Improved AIGC Video Understanding:** The ability of MASS-augmented VLMs to identify and reason about physics violations in AIGC videos is particularly impactful. As AI-generated content becomes more prevalent, tools that can accurately evaluate the physical plausibility of synthetic media are essential for improving generative models and ensuring content quality and realism.
- **Foundational for Embodied AI and Robotics:** A deeper understanding of physical reasoning is fundamental for the development of more capable embodied AI systems, robots, and autonomous agents. The ability to predict object behavior, understand spatial interactions, and detect anomalies in real-world environments is crucial for tasks like manipulation, navigation, and human-robot collaboration.
- **Enhanced Cross-Modal Alignment:** The research demonstrates that reinforcement fine-tuning (RFT) can effectively strengthen cross-modal alignment between visual cues and language representations in the context of complex physical phenomena. This highlights a pathway for more effective learning and reasoning in multimodal AI systems.

While current limitations include challenges in highly crowded scenes, robust camera ego-motion handling, and persistent hallucinations in subtle cases, the work establishes a foundation for future advancements. Future research directions, such as improved long-range motion grounding, scalable multi-object tracking, and expanded training data diversity, are expected to further solidify the impact of motion-aware spatial–temporal grounding in achieving comprehensive physical understanding in AI.
