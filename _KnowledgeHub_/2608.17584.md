# HODAgent: Towards On-Demand, Responsive Humanoids for Physical World Human Interaction

> [!summary] Summary
> HODAgent is a System-2 embodied agent for humanoid service robots, using a semi-duplex architecture (Env-Interactor, Planner, Executor, hierarchical Memory) so the robot stays responsive during physical execution, retains task progress across interruptions, and grounds task closure in observed outcomes. It beats ReAct baselines by 9.8-18.9 points Joint Success in simulation and runs on a physical Unitree G1.

## Problem

- Vision-language-action and navigation models provide reusable skills but don't define how an evolving service goal should be managed when a new request arrives mid-execution.
- General LLM-agent frameworks (ReAct, AutoGen, LangGraph) give System-2-style reasoning but lack continuous robot-state events, skill-lifecycle cancellation, and physical outcome verification.
- Prior interactive/embodied benchmarks evaluate isolated dialogue or fixed-goal task execution, not coordinated interaction-planning-execution-review across changing requests mid-task.

## Method

- ==Semi-duplex architecture== with four decoupled modules (Env-Interactor using a multimodal Omni model, Planner, Executor, hierarchical Memory) letting interaction continue while robot action executes.
- ==Hierarchical Memory== across task-, subtask-, and action-level granularity, letting any module resume consistent state after interruption or replanning.
- Embodiment-independent observation/execution contract plus a workflow-conditioned ==User Agent== (four personas) and evidence-grounded evaluator (==Task Verifier== + ==Service Judge==) for interactive simulation benchmarking.

## Results

- On the 164-case interactive simulation benchmark, HODAgent beats ReAct in Joint Success by **9.8 points** (**84.8%** vs 75.0%) on Qwen3.6-35B-A3B and **18.9 points** (**91.5%** vs 72.6%) on Qwen3.6-27B.
- On physical Unitree G1, HODAgent passes **92%** of atomic-capability trials, **72%** of composite-capability trials, and **63.3%** of complete-task trials.
- Improves aggregate accuracy on general embodied benchmarks: EmbodiedBench-Navigation **49.3%->58.3%** (+9.0), ALFRED +0.7, Habitat +1.7, and VIGIL **20.1%->25.5%** (+5.4), with observer ablation showing consistent gains across all four.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling interaction from planning/execution lets a service agent stay conversationally available during long-horizon physical actions without blocking on motion completion.
> - Command receipt should not be conflated with task completion; outcome-grounded closure needs independent verification from observation evidence, not execution acknowledgment.
> - Generic task-decomposition and structured cross-step memory transfer across unrelated benchmarks (navigation, household planning, partial-observability progress tracking) without task-specific tuning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics has transitioned from fixed, task-specific automation to autonomous embodied systems that integrate perception, planning, control, and learning. Early influential work, such as Brooks's layered mobile-robot control, introduced reactive alternatives to monolithic sense-plan-act pipelines, while end-to-end visuomotor policies demonstrated joint learning of perception and control. This progression has led to robots adapting actions from sensory feedback in less constrained environments. Humanoid robots, with their human-like morphology and action space, extend this trajectory to interact with environments designed for people, exemplified by systems like the Honda humanoid program and the Atlas locomotion system, which leveraged optimization-based planning and whole-body control. More recent developments, such as GR00T N1, indicate an emerging trend of integrating foundation models for generalist humanoid skills, moving towards humanoids that combine versatile physical capabilities with advanced perception and decision-making.

Humanoid and humanlike robots are being deployed and evaluated in diverse service environments, including professional, domestic, socially assistive, and hospitality sectors. Reviews of humanoid service robots identify mechanical design, control, perception, and interaction as critical system capabilities. However, existing studies often focus on individual interaction functions or bounded tasks, frequently not addressing how a robot should incorporate a new user request while a physical task is already in progress. This limitation defines a gap in the ability of current systems to support "on-demand response," which is the capacity to accept and appropriately handle new requests during physical execution without losing goal context, valid progress, or pending work.

This work positions itself within the System-2/1/0 hierarchy, a functional abstraction separating high-level cognition (System-2) from reusable robot skills (System-1) and hardware-level execution (System-0). Recent advancements have primarily strengthened the System-1 boundary, with Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models (e.g., OpenVLA, Octo, SmolVLA) connecting language and perception to robot actions, and Vision-Language Navigation (VLN) models extending visual reasoning to navigation. Systems like SayCan and RT-2 ground language-level skill selection in learned affordances and transfer vision-language representations to robot control. While these models enhance policies and skill selection, they generally do not define how an evolving service goal should be managed when a request arrives during an active skill.

Conversely, LLM-powered agent systems (e.g., ReAct, AutoGen, LangGraph) offer System-2-like reasoning, task decomposition, memory, and tool orchestration in digital environments. Concepts like "harness engineering" emphasize the runtime surrounding a model, encompassing workflows, tools, memory, state, feedback, and verification. These systems provide robust orchestration mechanisms but often lack embodiment-specific execution semantics, such as continuous robot-state events, time-extended skill lifecycles with cancellation, physical outcome verification, and user interaction that remains available during active embodied actions.

HODAGENT addresses this complementary gap by providing a System-2 embodied agent that integrates the deliberative capabilities of digital agents with the physical execution requirements of embodied systems. It distinguishes itself from fixed-goal embodied task execution by preserving service goals and progress across interruptions, and from existing interactive/adaptive embodied agents (e.g., Inner Monologue, MonTA, REAL, HoloAgent-0, JoyAI-VL-Interaction) by focusing on maintaining coherence across an entire evolving service episode where requests may arrive during active skills. This includes coordination of responses, execution decisions, progress retention, plan revision, and outcome review.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research is to enable humanoid robots to operate effectively in service settings that require dynamic and responsive human interaction. Existing service robots commonly face challenges in seamlessly integrating new user requests or environmental changes into an ongoing physical task. This limitation prevents them from providing fluid and adaptive service in real-world scenarios.

To address this, the authors define human–humanoid interaction in service settings as a situated loop where a robot must:
1. **Interpret user intent:** Understand requests based on language, visual context, and current execution state.
2. **Respond appropriately:** Communicate and act in an embodied manner.
3. **Adapt active tasks:** Adjust behavior when user needs or the environment change.

This definition highlights the necessity for "on-demand response," which is the ability to handle new requests *during* physical execution—whether by replying, continuing, stopping, or replanning—without losing the current goal, valid progress, or pending work.

Realizing this interaction pattern requires three specific agent-level capabilities:

- **Grounded intent and situation understanding:** The robot must interpret requests in conjunction with visual observations, service context, and current execution state. This ensures that both verbal and physical responses are appropriate to the dynamic situation. For instance, understanding a request to "go to the map" must consider the robot's current location, its visual field, and any ongoing motion.
- **On-demand interaction and long-horizon task continuity:** The robot needs to remain receptive to user input while actively performing a physical task. It must preserve the overall episode goal, validly completed progress, and pending work if the user asks a question, interrupts an action, or modifies a requirement. This prevents the robot from becoming unresponsive or losing context when faced with dynamic user needs. An example is a user changing a destination during navigation; the robot must adapt without abandoning the entire interaction.
- **Execution-state awareness and outcome–grounded closure:** The robot must accurately track its execution and the physical consequences of its actions. It needs to distinguish between merely accepting a command and actually completing a physical task. If an outcome fails or is incomplete, the robot should be able to recover or replan. Crucially, if available evidence is insufficient to confirm success, the robot should retain the unresolved obligation rather than prematurely declaring completion. This capability ensures reliable and verifiable task completion.

The key objective of HODAGENT is to develop a System-2 embodied agent designed to meet these requirements. The agent's architecture aims to enable it to handle new requests during robot motion, retain completed and pending work, revise active behavior, and ground service closure in observable execution outcomes across both simulated and physical environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on the development of HODAGENT, a semi-duplex System-2 embodied agent, and its evaluation across simulation, physical robot deployment, and established benchmarks.

#### 3.1. HODAGENT Architecture

HODAGENT is structured around a semi-duplex architecture comprising four independently scheduled but coordinated modules:
- **Env-Interactor:** This module maintains continuous connection to the user and environment. It receives multimodal input (speech/text, visual observation) and current task state. Using a multimodal Omni model, it interprets these signals and determines the appropriate system response: direct conversational reply, initiation of the Planner for an embodied task, interruption of an active task for replanning, or ignoring irrelevant input. This separation of interaction routing from task planning allows for continuous processing of new input during robot motion, enabling on-demand responsiveness.
- **Planner:** Responsible for transforming routed task instructions into executable robot behavior, while retaining adaptability for revisions. It operates in a decomposition–planning–review cycle. Task decomposition breaks down complex goals into ordered, semantically meaningful subtasks. The Planner then generates short tool trajectories (e.g., whole-body control, speech) for the current subtask, incorporating user goals, progress, recent observations, memory, and execution feedback. Post-execution, it reviews the outcome, advances to the next subtask, or replans if needed. It also performs a final review against the original goal before declaring task completion, grounding closure in achieved states rather than just command receipts.
- **Executor:** This module translates planned tool trajectories into time-extended behavior on the selected platform. It dispatches actions, monitors their progress and lifecycle, and reports outcomes to the Planner and Memory. Its independence from planning allows robot control to continue while other modules process input or update context. It also handles interruptions, execution failures, and safety-related terminations. The Executor supports "streaming inference" by allowing the Planner to prepare the next plan towards the end of an ongoing non-terminal trajectory, reducing idle time between actions.
- **Hierarchical Memory:** This module maintains a shared representation of task history across three levels:
 - **Task-level:** Preserves overall goals and summaries of past tasks for long-term continuity.
 - **Subtask-level:** Records task decomposition, distinguishing completed, current, and remaining work, enabling resumption from consistent states.
 - **Action-level:** Retains recent observations, executed actions, and their outcomes for fine-grained decision-making and evaluation.
 Memory serves as the coordination medium, ensuring modules retrieve and update relevant context without requiring lockstep operation.

A shared agent-facing observation and execution contract connects HODAGENT to System-1 skills and System-0 backends, with platform-specific control and safety managed below this boundary.

#### 3.2. Interactive Embodied Evaluation in Simulation

To assess HODAGENT comprehensively, an interactive simulation framework was developed using Isaac Sim.
- **User Agent:** A case-constrained, state-conditioned User Agent simulates diverse user interactions using four personas (Novice-guided, Time-limited, Neutral-cooperative, Evidence-cautious) and a predefined interaction policy (e.g., ordered goal progression, interruption, recap, termination). It issues natural language requests based on observable execution progress, allowing for dynamic interactions like interruptions during active navigation.
- **Simulation Runtime:** Isaac Sim executes the agent's embodied tools. The system records dialogue, actions, navigation trajectories, and terminal states as an aligned "episode record."
- **Post-evaluation:** Two independent evaluators assess the episode record:
 - **Task Verifier:** A rule-based system that evaluates physical outcomes (target reach, visitation order, required actions, terminal location, final response).
 - **Service Judge:** Assesses interaction quality, including relevance, clarity, factual grounding, and appropriateness for the specified User Agent persona.
 - **Joint Success:** Requires both Task PASS and Judge PASS for the same episode, indicating both physical completion and satisfactory service interaction.
- **Evaluation Benchmark:** Consists of 164 cases across 22 indoor service environments (SAGE-3D), covering 39 routes. It includes five types of embodied interaction, with 15 cases specifically designed for execution-time interruption and resumption.
- **Baselines:** HODAGENT was compared against ReAct, an agent that selects responses or actions from the current request, observation, and previous action outcome.
- **Models:** Qwen3.6-35B-A3B and Qwen3.6-27B were used as backbone Vision-Language Models.

#### 3.3. Embodiment Integration (Physical Robot)

The HODAGENT design was instantiated on a physical Unitree G1 robot to demonstrate cross-embodiment feasibility.
- **Integration Architecture:** The shared HODAGENT (System-2) interacts with a G1-specific embodiment adapter (real-robot facade). This adapter maps agent actions to G1-compatible requests and converts physical feedback (camera images, joint/locomotion state, controller status) into the agent's representation. The adapter communicates with the GR00T bridge, which connects to SONIC, the robot's deployed whole-body control system (System-0).
- **Physical Capabilities:** The G1 system supports observing the environment, conversational interaction, bounded locomotion (translation, rotation), stopping ongoing motion, and a configured set of whole-body motions (e.g., waving). Safety boundaries, such as controller readiness checks, action limits, and preemption, are enforced below the agent level.
- **Progressive Physical-Robot Evaluation:** A three-level validation protocol was employed:
 - **Atomic capabilities:** Individual interaction and motion capabilities (e.g., speaking, turning, waving, halting).
 - **Composite capabilities:** Sequencing multiple capabilities (e.g., perception-to-response, speech during execution, move-then-wave, verbal correction during movement).
 - **Task capabilities:** Goal-directed, multi-step assistance episodes (e.g., finding a person of a specific color and high-fiving them, navigating to a person while bypassing an obstacle).
- **Setup:** Qwen3.6-27B served as the Planner backbone, and Qwen3-Omni for the Env-Interactor's multimodal model. All commands were given via user audio.

#### 3.4. General Ability Testing

To evaluate the generalization of HODAGENT's capabilities, it was tested on established embodied-agent benchmarks.
- **Benchmarks:** EmbodiedBench (EB-Navigation, EB-ALFRED, EB-Habitat for vision-conditioned planning and household tasks) and VIGIL (for inferring task progress from egocentric RGB observations and accurate self-termination under partial observability).
- **Adaptation:** A thin adapter converted benchmark-specific observations and actions to and from HODAGENT's visual-language interface. The agent used its generic protocol, including task decomposition, structured memory, multi-image visual context, and observer-generated state abstractions, without task-specific tuning.
- **Ablation Study:** An ablation compared the full agent with a variant omitting observer captions to quantify their contribution.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. Interactive Embodied Evaluation in Simulation

HODAGENT demonstrated improved performance compared to the ReAct baseline across all metrics on the proposed interactive simulation benchmark of 164 cases.
- With the **Qwen3.6-35B-A3B** backbone, HODAGENT achieved a Joint Success rate of **84.8%**, surpassing ReAct by **9.8 percentage points** (ReAct: 75.0%). HODAGENT also recorded 92.1% in Task PASS (ReAct: 87.2%) and 91.5% in Judge PASS (ReAct: 75.6%).
- Using the **Qwen3.6-27B** backbone, HODAGENT achieved a Joint Success rate of **91.5%**, outperforming ReAct by **18.9 percentage points** (ReAct: 72.6%). Corresponding Task PASS was 96.3% (ReAct: 75.0%) and Judge PASS was 94.5% (ReAct: 76.8%).
These results indicate that HODAGENT more consistently coordinates grounded physical execution with persona-aware service responses, while maintaining task context across evolving service episodes. Qualitative case studies further illustrated HODAGENT's ability to preserve unfinished obligations, respect user control during ongoing execution, and provide evidence-grounded responses.

#### 4.2. Embodiment Integration (Physical Unitree G1)

The physical evaluation on a Unitree G1 robot demonstrated the feasibility of operating the same agent design across embodiments.
- **Atomic capabilities:** HODAGENT achieved a 92% pass rate, with forward locomotion at 100%, and spoken interaction, left/right turning, whole-body waving, and halt/stand recovery all at 90%.
- **Composite capabilities:** The pass rate was 72%. Perception-to-response and move-forward-then-wave sequence reached 80%, speech during physical execution and continuous multi-action execution reached 70%, while verbal correction during movement was 60%.
- **Complete task capabilities:** The aggregate pass rate was 63.3%. Moving to a person, waving, and greeting achieved 80%, bypassing an obstacle before this sequence was 60%, and finding a person by clothing color and high-fiving them was 50%.
Failures were attributed to limitations of the G1's onboard sensing hardware (e.g., narrow camera FOV, audio inaccuracies) and model errors (e.g., losing action state over long sequences, action hallucination). The decreasing pass rate from atomic to complete task levels suggests challenges in coordinating capabilities over longer temporal horizons and in handling action handoff and preemption. A case study illustrated successful human-initiated termination during an active approach-and-wave task, demonstrating the agent's ability to stop and respond. Another case showed long-horizon interaction, including concurrent conversational requests, obstacle avoidance, and target reacquisition.

#### 4.3. General Ability Testing

HODAGENT's capabilities generalized to established embodied-agent benchmarks, improving aggregate performance over benchmark-original agents without task-specific adaptations.
- **EmbodiedBench (EB):** HODAGENT improved overall success in all three environments:
 - **EB-Navigation:** From 49.3% to **58.3%** (+9.0 percentage points), with notable gains in base (+13.4 pts) and long-horizon tasks (+23.4 pts).
 - **EB-ALFRED:** From 34.3% to **35.0%** (+0.7 percentage points), with the largest gain in complex instructions.
 - **EB-Habitat:** From 32.3% to **34.0%** (+1.7 percentage points), with improvements in commonsense, long-horizon, and spatial-relation tasks.
- **VIGIL:** HODAGENT improved overall accuracy from 20.1% to **25.5%** (+5.4 percentage points). Significant improvements were observed in distance approach (+24.0 pts), approach and interact (+8.0 pts), and state verification (+4.0 pts). These gains indicate the utility of structured cross-step memory and observation summaries for visual task execution and terminal condition recognition.
- **Observer Ablation:** An ablation study demonstrated that the inclusion of observer-generated captions improved aggregate accuracy across all evaluated benchmarks: +6.6 points on EB-Navigation, +4.7 points on EB-ALFRED, +0.7 points on EB-Habitat, and +2.3 points on VIGIL. This suggests that the observer contributes useful visual state abstraction.

These results collectively support the effectiveness of HODAGENT's design in coordinating grounded physical execution with responsive interaction and demonstrate the transferability of its underlying reasoning mechanisms to diverse embodied planning and state-understanding tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of embodied AI by addressing a gap in human-humanoid interaction: the need for on-demand responsiveness and task continuity during ongoing physical execution. HODAGENT's formulation of human-humanoid interaction as an evolving service loop, requiring grounded understanding, continuity under execution-time requests, and outcome-grounded closure, provides a framework for designing more adaptive and reliable service robots.

The development of the HODAGENT architecture, with its semi-duplex coordination of Env-Interactor, Planner, Executor, and hierarchical Memory, represents a methodological advance. This design enables the robot to process new requests and observations concurrently with physical action, preserving task context and progress across interruptions. This contrasts with traditional sequential sense-plan-act pipelines or digital agents that lack explicit embodiment semantics, allowing HODAGENT to maintain a coherent service relationship while actions unfold.

The shared observation and execution contract is a practical contribution, facilitating the deployment of the same high-level agent logic across both simulated and physical robot platforms. This allows for flexible integration, where platform-specific sensing, skills, control limits, and safety enforcement are adapted below a common interface, potentially reducing development effort for new robot embodiments. The progressive physical evaluation on the Unitree G1 provides initial evidence of this cross-embodiment feasibility, with pass rates demonstrating the agent's ability to execute individual and sequenced operations, albeit with challenges in longer-horizon coordination.

The quantitative results from the interactive simulation benchmark demonstrate HODAGENT's enhanced capability to achieve both physical task completion and satisfactory service interaction, outperforming a ReAct-style baseline. The consistent improvements in Joint Success indicate a more integrated approach to service quality. Furthermore, the evaluation on established general embodied benchmarks (EmbodiedBench and VIGIL) suggests that the principles of task decomposition, structured progress memory, multi-image context, and visual-state abstraction are broadly applicable, extending HODAGENT's utility beyond specific interactive service scenarios to general embodied planning and state understanding tasks. The observer ablation study validates the effectiveness of abstracting visual state information for improved performance.

The potential impact of this work is significant for the deployment of humanoid robots in service sectors such as reception, exhibition guidance, retail assistance, and facility navigation. Humanoids in these roles must reliably answer questions, accept corrections, and manage incomplete tasks during active motion. By enabling such adaptive behavior, HODAGENT could contribute to more natural, efficient, and user-friendly human-robot collaborations. The identified limitations and future work directions, such as latency analysis, broader physical evaluation, and model training from interaction traces, suggest a path toward further enhancing the robustness and generalization of these agents, fostering the development of continuously improving humanoid service robots.
