# From Real-Time Planning to Reliable Execution:Scalable Coordination for Heterogeneous Multi-Robot Fleets in Industrial Environments

> [!summary] Summary
> The SCALE framework from Shanghai Jiao Tong University enables real-time, robust coordination of heterogeneous robot fleets in industrial environments by integrating a novel conflict resolution mechanism with adaptive precedence graph-based execution. It achieved a sustained throughput of 40-55 tasks/min and a 45% reduction in average task response time in simulations, alongside successful real-world deployment across 30,000 tasks.

## Problem

- Traditional industrial coordination methods struggle with scalability, space utilization, and efficiency when managing large, heterogeneous robot fleets in high-density environments.
- Most Multi-Agent Path Finding (MAPF) solvers treat robots as point agents, failing to account for physical robot footprints, kinematic behaviors, and continuous motion conflicts, leading to real-world collision risks.
- Industrial operations are subject to uncertainties like communication delays and tracking errors, which can cause robots to deviate from planned paths, resulting in congestion, excessive waiting, and safety issues.

## Method

- An online reactive planning module leverages **Search-Integrated Conflict Discretization (SICD)** to convert complex geometric collision checks into efficient ==bitwise operations== for real-time, kinematically feasible multi-robot path planning.
- A resilience-guaranteed execution module utilizes a **Conjugate Action-Precedence Hypergraph (CAPH)** to adaptively adjust precedence relations between robot 'action capsules' for robust execution under real-world disturbances.
- The framework (**SCALE**) employs a ==bounded path-release strategy==, committing only a limited number of path steps, enabling adaptive and controllable scheduling for large-scale heterogeneous fleets.

## Results

- SICD-integrated LaCAM* achieved a **90.0%** success rate with continuous motion-conflict detection, a notable improvement over direct polygon-overlap methods (**23.2%**) and maintaining near graph-agent solver efficiency.
- CAPH reduced execution time by up to **18.4%** compared to non-switchable precedence methods and by approximately **110 seconds** versus greedy approaches in simulated disturbed environments, requiring **one-third** fewer dependency switches.
- System-level simulations showed SCALE achieving over **30%** higher throughput (**40-55** vs. **30-35** tasks/min) and **45%** faster response (**28s** vs. **51s**) than an industrial baseline, and a real-world 100-robot deployment completed ~**30,000** tasks with stable **700-1800 ms** edge computation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Geometric continuous motion conflicts for heterogeneous robots can be efficiently incorporated into discrete search through Search-Integrated Conflict Discretization (SICD), which maps swept footprints to compact bitmaps for fast bitwise collision checks.
> - Adaptive adjustment of robot precedence relations at the 'action capsule' level, as managed by the Conjugate Action-Precedence Hypergraph (CAPH) optimized via MILP, effectively mitigates the propagation of execution delays caused by real-world disturbances.
> - An integrated online reactive planning and robust execution approach, coupled with local conflict resolution and bounded path commitments, is essential for scalable and reliable coordination of heterogeneous fleets in dynamic industrial settings.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses the challenges of coordinating large-scale, heterogeneous robot fleets in dynamic industrial environments, such as intelligent manufacturing systems and warehousing logistics. This domain is increasingly adopting diverse robot types, including AGVs, forklifts, and mobile manipulators, which possess varied footprints, kinematic behaviors, and operational characteristics.

Current industrial coordination methods often rely on handcrafted heuristics, topological roadmaps, or predefined traffic zones. Techniques such as zone control, Petri-net supervisors, and glued-node reservation offer explicit safety logic and can be effective in specific, well-defined scenarios. However, these methods are noted for their limitations in scalability, often leading to conservative space utilization, reduced efficiency in high-density scenarios, and a lack of generality due to their reliance on manual layout designs and rules. They also struggle to manage dozens of tasks efficiently, with scheduling times potentially extending to tens of seconds.

Recent advancements in Multi-Agent Path Finding (MAPF) algorithms, including MAPF-LNS2 and LaCAM*, have demonstrated improved scalability and solution quality for path planning. However, most MAPF solvers operate under a graph-agent abstraction, treating robots as point agents that occupy discrete vertices or traverse edges at discrete time steps. This simplification creates a disparity when applied to real-world heterogeneous robots, whose occupied space is influenced by their rigid-body footprint, orientation, and nonholonomic kinematic constraints. While some approaches have attempted to incorporate more realistic assumptions, they often compromise real-time capability and scalability.

Furthermore, industrial environments are characterized by inherent uncertainties and disturbances, such as communication delays, tracking errors, human-robot interactions, and robot failures. These factors can cause robots to deviate from nominal planned paths, potentially leading to congestion, excessive waiting, and safety issues. Existing robust MAPF methods address these disturbances by reserving temporal margins or adjusting precedence relations. However, these methods typically characterize inter-robot interactions through pairwise vertex-occupancy conflicts or simplified vehicle models, which are insufficient for heterogeneous fleets.

This research aims to bridge these gaps by developing a coordination framework that integrates real-time, kinematically feasible path planning with robust, latency-resilient execution for heterogeneous robot fleets. It seeks to overcome the limitations of both traditional industrial scheduling and current MAPF approaches, particularly in handling real-world physical constraints and operational uncertainties.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a scalable coordination framework, named SCALE, that enables real-time path planning and robust execution for large-scale heterogeneous robot fleets operating in industrial environments. This objective is motivated by several critical challenges inherent in such systems:

1. **Heterogeneity and Density:** Industrial fleets comprise robots with diverse physical properties (footprint, kinematics, speed). Coordinating these robots in dense, shared workspaces necessitates path planning that accounts for these differences in real time, going beyond simple point-agent models to prevent collisions and deadlocks effectively. Existing methods often fail to adequately model continuous motion conflicts arising from varied robot geometries.

2. **Real-Time Performance:** In dynamic industrial settings, new tasks arrive continuously, and robot states change frequently. Scheduling needs to be performed efficiently and in real time. Traditional rule-based methods or complex global optimization approaches can incur prohibitive computational costs, leading to delays in task assignment and execution, which degrades overall system throughput.

3. **Execution Robustness under Uncertainty:** Planned paths are based on temporal assumptions that can be invalidated by real-world disturbances. Communication latency, tracking errors, human-robot interaction, or robot malfunctions can cause deviations, leading to unintended waiting, congestion, and potential deadlocks. Existing robust planning methods are often conservative or rely on simplified conflict models, making them less effective in accommodating significant or unpredictable delays in heterogeneous environments. The motivation is to design an execution strategy that can adapt dynamically to these uncertainties without requiring full replanning.

4. **Integration of Planning and Execution:** Current systems often separate path planning from execution control. This separation can lead to plans that are difficult to execute reliably or execution behaviors that deviate from optimal paths. The research is motivated by the need for an integrated approach where planning and execution are coupled as an online process, allowing for continuous adaptation and improvement while tasks are being performed.

To address these motivations, the specific objectives of the SCALE framework are:
- To develop a generalized coordination framework capable of managing large-scale heterogeneous robot fleets.
- To introduce a motion-induced conflict reduction mechanism (Search-Integrated Conflict Discretization, SICD) that facilitates real-time, kinematically feasible multi-robot path planning (MRPP) by efficiently handling continuous motion conflicts.
- To design a generalized data structure, the Conjugate Action-Precedence Hypergraph (CAPH), which adaptively adjusts precedence relations among robots to achieve robust and latency-resilient asynchronous execution under disturbances.
- To validate the practical feasibility and effectiveness of the proposed method through extensive simulations and real-world industrial deployments, demonstrating improvements in throughput and sustained collision-free, deadlock-free operation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The SCALE framework integrates an online reactive path planning module with a resilience-guaranteed path execution module. It operates on a bounded path-release strategy, committing only a limited number (K) of steps of the planned path to each robot per scheduling round, enabling controllable and adaptive scheduling.

**A. Online Reactive Path Conflict Resolution**

1. **Potential Conflict Robot Group Identification:**
 - The planner does not replan the entire fleet in every round. Instead, it identifies "local planning groups" of robots whose unreleased paths (within the K-step horizon) may conflict or form deadlocks.
 - This is achieved by incrementally augmenting the dependency structure from the previously constructed Conjugate Action-Precedence Hypergraph (CAPH) and checking for directed deadlock cycles (C_K).
 - Robot sets involved in these cycles are merged to form initial groups. Idle robots are added to a group if their current position conflicts with an active robot's horizon actions.
 - Local goals are assigned to guide robots within these groups: active robots either pursue their global task goal (if it's within the local coordination region and doesn't conflict with idle robots) or a vertex along their single-robot path that is outside the expanded conflict region. Idle robots are assigned nearby avoidance vertices that do not conflict with active horizon actions.

2. **Independent Robot Path Planning:**
 - Robots not part of a conflict group are planned independently using A* on a "guided graph."
 - This graph incorporates congestion awareness: edge costs are adjusted based on the flow count (number of paths passing through an edge), vertex-level congestion, and contraflow weight (detecting opposite-direction traffic). This guides robots towards low-congestion paths and avoids severe opposite-direction traffic.

3. **Search-Integrated Conflict Discretization (SICD) for MRPP:**
 - To address continuous motion conflicts of heterogeneous robots without incurring high computational cost, SICD is introduced.
 - Instead of repeatedly evaluating geometric polygon overlaps during discrete search, SICD maps the swept footprint (W_ϵ(a)) of each continuous action (a) to a graph-level occupancy set (O(a)) using pre-generated roadmap neighborhoods along the trajectory.
 - This occupancy set is then embedded into a compact bitmap b(a).
 - During search, when a new action a_t_i is considered for a state transition, its bitmap b_i is checked against an accumulated bitmap B (representing the combined occupancy of all already-accepted actions in the current round) using a bitwise AND operation. If (B & b_i) is zero, no collision is detected, and B is updated with a bitwise OR (B ← B ∨ b_i).
 - This approach converts complex geometric collision detection into efficient bitwise operations, significantly reducing the computational burden (from O(N² Lc_poly) to O(N|V|/w_m)) while preserving footprint-awareness.
 - A fallback strategy is implemented: if local MRPP fails to find a solution within a time limit, the group is split, the commitment horizon K is reduced, and the timeout relaxed for a retry.

**B. Conjugate Action-Precedence Hypergraph (CAPH)**

CAPH is designed for latency-resilient path execution by adaptively adjusting precedence relations.

1. **CPC Construction:**
 - The concept of an "action capsule" (Q_p:q_i) is introduced as a contiguous segment of a robot's action sequence.
 - Initial dependencies are derived from a Hybrid Action-Precedence Graph (HAPG), which defines action-level dependencies based on physical interactions.
 - A "conflict signature" (Γ_i←j(ℓ)) is defined for each action of robot j concerning conflicts with robot i's path.
 - "Maximal constant-signature intervals" on paths identify candidate capsule pairs (ω_ζ_ij).
 - A candidate capsule pair becomes a Conjugate Precedence Capsule (CPC) if at least one capsule contains multiple actions (i.e., is non-singleton), and its temporal orientation (which capsule precedes the other) can be switched without creating an immediate temporal contradiction. This switching ability is key to robustness.

2. **CAPH Definition:**
 - The CAPH is formally defined as H = (Q, Π_f, Ω_z), comprising action capsules (Q), fixed capsule precedences (Π_f), a set of CPCs (Ω), and binary variables (z) representing the orientation of each CPC.
 - CAPH induces an active precedence set Π(z) based on the chosen orientations. A CPC's orientation can only be switched if both capsules involved are in the "staged" state. Deadlock cycles in CAPH (C_K) indicate problematic configurations.

3. **Rolling Adjustment of CAPH:**
 - Inspired by receding-horizon control, CAPH is optimized through a Mixed-Integer Linear Program (MILP) in each scheduling round.
 - The MILP minimizes the cumulative predicted horizon-exit time for relevant unreleased capsules within the K-step horizon.
 - Constraints ensure capsule duration, fixed precedences, and conditional precedence for CPCs. Big-M constraints are used to model the binary switching variables z_ω.
 - A crucial condition is that a CPC's orientation can only be switched if both its constituent capsules are still "staged," preventing reversal of dependencies for actions already in progress or completed. This MILP ensures that the resulting active precedence set Π(z) remains acyclic, guaranteeing deadlock-free execution.
 - Theorem IV.7 formally establishes that the CAPH-based persistent path execution, when combined with paths planned by the SICD-enabled MRPP solver, is both collision-free and deadlock-free.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates SCALE across three dimensions: real-time MRPP performance (using SICD), robust path execution under external disturbances (using CAPH), and system-level lifelong coordination, including a real-world deployment.

**A. Performance of One-shot MRPP (SICD Evaluation):**
- **Conflict Detection Efficiency:** SICD was integrated into several MAPF solvers (LaCAM*, PIBT, PBS, ECBS, CBS). On an industrial roadmap graph with 2-20 robots (500ms timeout), LaCAM* with SICD solved 90.0% of instances, providing continuous motion-conflict detection (CCD). This was a significant improvement over direct polygon-overlap (PO) methods (23.2% success rate) and only a 9.7% reduction compared to the original graph-agent solver without CCD. This demonstrates SICD's ability to maintain high planning efficiency while incorporating physical collision awareness.
- **Scalability on Benchmarks:** On public MovingAI MAPF benchmarks with heterogeneous robot parameters (up to 300 robots, 30s timeout), SICD-based solvers largely preserved the original solver efficiency, with success rate reductions generally below 8% for robot densities under 0.20 and within 22% for denser settings. For up to 100 robots, SICD maintained performance close to solvers without CCD. In contrast, PO-based solvers struggled significantly, often failing even at low robot densities. SICD's median solving time remained close to the original search, while PO methods introduced substantial overhead due to geometric overlap tests.

**B. Performance of Path Execution under Uncertainty (CAPH Evaluation):**
- **Execution Time Reduction:** In scenarios with external disturbances (packet loss, controller delay, human factors) and varying robot group sizes (10-50 robots), CAPH consistently achieved the shortest execution times. For 50-robot groups, CAPH reduced execution time by up to 18.4% compared to the non-switchable Hybrid Action-Precedence Graph (HAPG) and by approximately 110 seconds compared to SG (a greedy segment-graph method). This highlights CAPH's effectiveness in mitigating delay propagation.
- **Efficient Dependency Switching:** CAPH achieved these improvements with significantly fewer dependency switches—about one-third the number used by SG across all tested cases. This indicates that CAPH's capsule-level dependency model enables more strategic and globally effective decisions, avoiding redundant or ineffective switches that could lead to negative outcomes or livelocks.

**C. System-level Simulation (Lifelong Coordination):**
- **Throughput:** A three-day simulation with 300 heterogeneous robots on a large industrial roadmap showed SCALE achieving a sustained high throughput of 40-55 tasks/min, peaking at 54 tasks/min. This represents over a 30% improvement compared to the industrial baseline CAEH (30-35 tasks/min). Another baseline, GNGW, experienced a complete collapse of throughput after approximately 30 hours due to accumulated deadlocks. SCALE successfully completed all tasks, unlike GNGW (which completed less than 40%).
- **Task Response Time:** SCALE demonstrated a more concentrated distribution of task response times, with an average of 28 seconds. This was a 45% reduction compared to CAEH (51s) and a 59% reduction compared to GNGW (69s), indicating more efficient task admission and faster robot dispatch.

**D. Industrial Deployment:**
- **Real-world Validation:** SCALE was deployed in a real-world warehousing factory with a fleet of 100 heterogeneous robots. Over a multi-day operation, approximately 30,000 tasks were completed.
- **Computational Feasibility:** The edge-side computation time for planning and authorization remained stable, ranging from 700-1800 ms per 10-second scheduling round. This demonstrates that the framework's computational demands are well within the operational cycle, allowing for real-time responsiveness in a demanding industrial setting.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant contribution to the field of multi-robot system coordination, particularly for industrial applications. The SCALE framework offers a comprehensive solution that addresses several critical limitations of existing approaches.

**Significance:**
- **Bridging the Reality Gap:** The work significantly advances the applicability of multi-robot coordination to real-world industrial environments by explicitly handling heterogeneity in robot kinematics and footprints through the Search-Integrated Conflict Discretization (SICD) mechanism. This is a notable departure from common point-agent abstractions in MAPF, enabling more physically accurate and safe planning.
- **Enhanced Robustness:** The Conjugate Action-Precedence Hypergraph (CAPH) and its rolling adjustment mechanism provide a robust solution for path execution under unavoidable industrial disturbances such as communication delays and human intervention. By adaptively adjusting precedence relations at a capsule level, CAPH mitigates the propagation of delays, ensuring system fluidity and preventing unnecessary waiting, which is a common issue in large-scale deployments.
- **Improved Efficiency and Scalability:** The online reactive planning architecture, coupled with SICD and CAPH, demonstrates substantial improvements in system throughput and reduced task response times for large-scale heterogeneous fleets. The system's ability to selectively identify and resolve local conflicts, rather than replanning globally, contributes to its scalability.
- **Integrated Planning and Execution:** By treating planning and execution as a coupled online process with bounded path commitments, the framework provides a pragmatic and controllable approach to scheduling dense industrial robot fleets. This integration ensures that plans are not only theoretically optimal but also practically executable and adaptable to dynamic changes.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Industrial Automation:** The successful validation through both extensive simulations and a multi-day real-world deployment in a warehouse environment indicates that SCALE is practically feasible and efficient. This could directly impact the efficiency and reliability of intelligent manufacturing, warehousing, and logistics operations globally, leading to higher automation levels, increased productivity, and reduced operational costs.
- **Future Research Directions:** The methodology, particularly SICD for conflict detection and CAPH for robust execution, could serve as foundational components for future research in multi-robot systems. The proposed extension to more general multi-robot trajectory planning, integrating continuous-time motion planning and execution, points towards a unified framework that could broaden the applicability of multi-robot systems across various domains beyond industrial warehouses.
- **Standardization of Coordination:** As industrial robot fleets become more diverse and complex, robust and scalable coordination frameworks like SCALE may contribute to the development of industry standards for multi-robot system management, ensuring safer and more efficient operations in shared human-robot workspaces.
