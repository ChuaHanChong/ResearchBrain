# Enhancing Policy Learning with World-Action Model

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at The Ohio State University developed the World-Action Model (WAM), an extension of world models that uses an inverse dynamics objective to regularize latent representations for action-relevant information. WAM achieved improved imagination quality with 8.7 times fewer training steps than DreamerV2, and policies trained with WAM demonstrated significantly higher success rates on robotic manipulation tasks, including a 15.9 percentage point average gain in behavioral cloning and a 13.0 percentage point average gain after model-based fine-tuning on the CALVIN benchmark.

## Problem

- Conventional world models primarily optimize for future observation prediction, leading to latent representations that may lack explicit action-relevant structure critical for effective robotic control.
- The asymmetry in training objectives means learned latent states often overlook fine-grained details about how actions influence the environment, impeding downstream policy learning.
- Existing solutions like unified action-world models often require significant architectural redesigns or reliance on large foundation models, limiting their practical applicability.

## Method

- The **World-Action Model (WAM)** augments the **DreamerV2** world model architecture by incorporating an ==inverse dynamics head==.
- This head predicts the action that caused a transition between two consecutive ==encoder embeddings==, explicitly regularizing the latent representations to capture ==action-relevant information==.
- After training, **WAM**'s ==action-regularized latent space== is used as a frozen simulator for efficient ==policy optimization==, enabling both ==behavioral cloning== and ==model-based reinforcement learning== fine-tuning.

## Results

- **WAM** achieved superior imagination quality compared to **DreamerV2**, demonstrating better PSNR (**22.10** vs 21.66), SSIM (**0.814** vs 0.807), LPIPS (**0.144** vs 0.149), and FVD (**10.82** vs 12.13) with **8.7x** fewer world model training steps.
- Policies trained with **WAM**'s representations on the CALVIN benchmark showed improved average behavioral cloning success (**61.7%** vs **45.8%** for DiWA) and higher average success after PPO fine-tuning (**92.8%** vs **79.8%** for DiWA).
- **WAM** demonstrated notable performance gains in tasks requiring precise control, such as closing a drawer (**+31.1 percentage points** in BC) and opening a drawer (**+22.26 percentage points** in fine-tuning) compared to the DiWA baseline.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly regularizing world model latent representations through an inverse dynamics objective compels the encoder to capture more precise and causally relevant information about environment dynamics.
> - Operating the inverse dynamics head on encoder embeddings, rather than higher-level RSSM features, ensures that action-aware structure is cascaded through the recurrent model, benefiting both posterior and prior states for imagined rollouts.
> - This lightweight, complementary augmentation to existing world model backbones significantly enhances the quality of learned representations and improves downstream policy learning without a complete architectural overhaul.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

World models have emerged as a significant component in the field of robotic control and reinforcement learning. These models facilitate learning environment dynamics by compressing high-dimensional observations into compact latent spaces. This compression allows agents to plan and refine behaviors through simulated rollouts within the learned model, thereby reducing the need for extensive and costly real-world interactions. Early work demonstrated the capability of recurrent neural networks to model latent dynamics, which was further advanced by models like PlaNet and the Dreamer series (DreamerV2, DreamerV3) through the introduction of the Recurrent State-Space Model (RSSM) and categorical latent representations. These advancements showed that policies trained within learned world models could achieve substantial performance on robotic manipulation tasks and even transfer to physical hardware.

The conventional approach to training these world models primarily focuses on predicting future observations, using actions solely as conditioning inputs. This design implies that the latent representations learned by the world model are optimized for pixel prediction and reconstruction fidelity, rather than explicitly capturing information related to how actions influence state transitions. This asymmetry can limit the utility of these representations for downstream policy learning, where understanding the action-relevant structure is crucial for effective control.

Recent research has begun to address this limitation. Some approaches, such as unified action-world models (e.g., WorldVLA), integrate action generation directly into the architecture, often relying on large foundation models and requiring significant architectural redesigns.

This work presents an alternative and complementary direction. Instead of fundamentally redesigning the world model architecture, the authors propose to augment the training objective of an existing world model (DreamerV2) with an inverse dynamics component. This approach seeks to improve the quality of the learned latent representations by explicitly regularizing them towards action-relevant information, without necessitating a complete architectural overhaul or reliance on very large models. This strategy aligns with prior work in self-supervised representation learning, where inverse dynamics models have been shown to produce representations focused on controllable aspects of the environment. The paper positions itself as an enhancement to the representation learning capabilities of existing world model backbones, with direct implications for sample-efficient policy learning in robotics.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the effectiveness of policy learning in robotic manipulation tasks by improving the quality of latent representations learned by world models. The authors specifically aim to achieve this by regularizing these representations to capture action-relevant structure.

The motivation for this objective stems from several identified limitations of conventional world models:

- **Asymmetry in Training Objective:** Traditional world models, such as DreamerV2, are predominantly trained to predict future visual observations. While actions are provided as input to condition these predictions, the latent states (e.g., `z_t`) generated by the world model are optimized mainly for pixel reconstruction accuracy and KL regularization. There is no explicit objective that compels these latent states to encode information directly pertinent to the actions that drive state transitions.
- **Lack of Action-Relevant Structure:** The absence of an explicit action-prediction objective means that the learned representations might overlook or discard fine-grained details about how an agent's actions influence the environment. This information is considered critical for downstream control tasks, where a policy needs to understand the causal link between its actions and the resulting environmental changes. For instance, in manipulation tasks, subtle differences in gripper position or force are directly linked to task success, and these nuances might not be sufficiently captured if the representation is only optimized for visual fidelity.
- **Suboptimal Policy Input:** In model-based policy learning pipelines (e.g., DiWA), the policy directly uses the world model's latent states as input. If these latents lack explicit action-relevant information, the policy's ability to learn effective control strategies can be impeded, potentially leading to lower success rates or requiring more extensive training.
- **Preference for Lightweight Extension:** While some recent research explores unified action-world models that jointly generate actions and observations, these often involve significant architectural redesigns and may rely on large foundation models. The authors motivate a complementary approach that seeks to improve existing world model representations through a lightweight training objective augmentation, rather than a fundamental architectural overhaul. This allows for leveraging the established robustness and efficiency of existing world model backbones.
- **Inspiration from Self-Supervised Learning:** The concept of inverse dynamics models has been successfully applied in self-supervised representation learning. By requiring a model to predict the action that caused a transition between consecutive states, these models learn representations that inherently focus on controllable aspects of the environment, filtering out task-irrelevant distractors. This principle motivates its application to world model training.

By addressing these points, the research aims to demonstrate that explicitly incorporating an inverse dynamics objective can compel the world model's encoder to capture more precise and causally relevant information about environment dynamics, leading to stronger, more sample-efficient policy learning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors introduce the World-Action Model (WAM), which extends the conventional world model architecture of DreamerV2 by incorporating an inverse dynamics objective. This modification aims to regularize the learned latent representations towards capturing action-relevant information.

**A. World Model Backbone**
WAM is built upon the Recurrent State-Space Model (RSSM) architecture derived from DreamerV2.
1. **Encoder:** A dual-stream Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) processes observations, which include static and gripper camera images, alongside proprioceptive state data. This encoder outputs embeddings `e_t` ∈ R^1554.
2. **RSSM Dynamics:** The RSSM models latent dynamics through a recurrent process. It maintains a deterministic recurrent state `h_t`, and a stochastic categorical variable `z_t` (32x32) representing the latent state. The model predicts future latent states (`z_t` and `h_t`) based on past states and actions.
 - `h_t = f_φ(h_{t-1}, z_{t-1}, a_{t-1})` (deterministic state transition)
 - `z_t ~ q_φ(z_t | h_t, e_t)` (posterior over stochastic state given observation embedding)
 - `ẑ_t ~ p_φ(z_t | h_t)` (prior over stochastic state)
3. **Decoder:** A decoder reconstructs the future observation `ô_t` from the combined latent feature `f_t = [h_t; z_t]` ∈ R^2048.

**B. Action-Regularized World Model (WAM)**
The core innovation of WAM is the addition of an inverse dynamics head to the DreamerV2 architecture.
1. **Inverse Dynamics Head:** This component, denoted as `M_action_θ`, is a three-layer Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) represented by `ψ`. It is designed to predict the action `â_t` that caused a transition between two consecutive encoder embeddings: `â_t = ψ([e_t; e_{t+1}])`. The embeddings `e_t` and `e_{t+1}` are concatenated as input.
2. **Cascading Effect:** A critical design aspect is that the inverse dynamics head operates on the encoder embeddings `e_t`, rather than the RSSM features `f_t`. This is because `f_t` already incorporates `a_{t-1}` through the GRU, which would make action prediction trivial. By regularizing `e_t` directly, the action-aware structure learned by the encoder is propagated forward. This action-relevant information shapes the posterior `z_t`, which the KL divergence loss then transfers to the prior `ẑ_t`. This chain ensures that the improved representations in `ẑ_t` are available for generating imagined rollouts and conditioning the downstream policy.
3. **Training Objective:** WAM is trained end-to-end using a combined loss function:
 `L_WAM = λ_KL * L_KL + λ_img * L_recon + λ_act * L_action`
 - `L_KL`: KL divergence between the posterior `q_φ(z_t | h_t, e_t)` and the prior `p_φ(z_t | h_t)`.
 - `L_recon`: L2 reconstruction loss between the ground-truth observation `o_t` and the predicted observation `ô_t`.
 - `L_action`: L1 loss between the predicted action `â_t` and the ground-truth action `a_t`.
 The coefficients `λ_KL`, `λ_img`, and `λ_act` are tuned to balance these objectives.

**C. Enhancing Downstream Policy Learning with WAM**
After WAM is trained and frozen, it serves as a learned simulator for policy optimization in its latent space. This process involves two stages:
1. **Behavioral Cloning (BC):**
 - WAM's frozen encoder and RSSM are used to extract latent features `f_t` from expert demonstrations `D_exp`.
 - A DiffusionMLP policy `π_θ(a_t | f_t)` is trained via behavioral cloning. This policy generates actions by iteratively denoising Gaussian noise.
 - The objective `L_BC` minimizes the difference between the denoised actions and the target actions.
 - The authors emphasize that WAM's action-regularized `f_t` directly benefits this stage by providing richer action-relevant information to the diffusion policy.
2. **Offline Policy Fine-tuning:**
 - The BC-pretrained policy is further refined using DPPO (Diffusion Policy Policy Optimization) entirely within WAM's frozen latent space, eliminating the need for real-world interactions.
 - During imagined rollouts, the policy relies on WAM's prior `ẑ_t` to generate latent states. The cascading effect of WAM ensures that these imagined rollouts benefit from action-aware representations.
 - A binary reward classifier `R_ψ` is trained on WAM features to provide task-completion signals within the latent space.
 - The policy is updated using a clipped PPO surrogate objective. Behavioral cloning regularization is applied during rollouts to mitigate catastrophic forgetting.

**D. Experimental Setup**
Experiments are conducted on the CALVIN benchmark (Environment D), a tabletop manipulation benchmark featuring a 7-DoF Franka Emika Panda robot. The dataset comprises 6 hours of teleoperated play data (~500K transitions). Observations consist of 64x64 RGB images from static and gripper cameras, along with proprioceptive state. WAM is trained for 230K gradient steps using a batch size of 500 and a sequence length of 50. Loss weights are `λ_KL = 3.0`, `λ_img = 1.0`, and `λ_act = 1000.0`. Baselines include DreamerV2 for generation quality comparison and DiWA for policy learning comparison, using identical policy architectures and training procedures where appropriate.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on the CALVIN benchmark addressed two primary questions: whether action regularization enhances WAM's performance compared to standard world models, and whether WAM's representations improve downstream policy learning.

**A. Action Regularization Enhances WAM**
1. **Generation Quality Comparison:** WAM was compared against DreamerV2 for imagination quality. The models performed 50-step open-loop imagination rollouts conditioned on initial ground-truth observations and actions.
 - **Quantitative Metrics (Table I):** WAM consistently outperformed DreamerV2 across standard video prediction metrics:
 - **PSNR (Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio):** WAM achieved 22.10 ± 2.22, compared to DreamerV2's 21.66 ± 2.20 (higher is better).
 - **SSIM (Structural Similarity Index Measure):** WAM achieved 0.814 ± 0.061, compared to DreamerV2's 0.807 ± 0.067 (higher is better).
 - **LPIPS (Learned Perceptual Image Patch Similarity):** WAM achieved 0.144 ± 0.072, compared to DreamerV2's 0.149 ± 0.073 (lower is better).
 - **FVD (Frechet Video Distance):** WAM achieved 10.82, compared to DreamerV2's 12.13 (lower is better).
 - **Qualitative Observations (Figure 3):** WAM produced more realistic future state predictions over the entire rollout horizon, exhibiting better preservation of object shapes, finer details, and more accurate colors compared to DreamerV2, which showed color drift and distorted object shapes.
 - **Training Efficiency:** These improvements were achieved with WAM being trained for 230K gradient steps, approximately 8.7 times fewer steps than the 2M steps used by the baseline DreamerV2. The authors attribute these gains to the inverse dynamics head, which compels the encoder to capture action-relevant and causal structures in its latent representations.

**B. Enhancing Policy Learning with WAM**
WAM's impact on downstream policy learning was evaluated against the DiWA baseline, both using identical DiffusionMLP policy architectures and training pipelines.

1. **Behavioral Cloning (BC) Results (Table III & Figure 4):**
 - WAM demonstrated improved average behavioral cloning success rates. WAM achieved an average success rate of 61.7% across eight CALVIN tasks, outperforming DiWA's 45.8% by 15.9 percentage points.
 - WAM showed superior performance on 7 out of 8 tasks. Notable gains were observed in tasks involving articulated objects where precise control is critical:
 - `close drawer`: WAM 89.7% vs. DiWA 58.6% (+31.1 percentage points).
 - `move slider right`: WAM 82.8% vs. DiWA 51.7% (+31.1 percentage points).
 - `open drawer`: WAM 73.3% vs. DiWA 53.3% (+20.0 percentage points).
 - The task `turn on led` showed a marginal decrease for WAM (41.4%) compared to DiWA (44.8%).
 - The evaluation curves (Figure 4) indicated that WAM policies converged faster and reached higher success rates during BC training. These results suggest that WAM's action-regularized latent features provide a more informative input for imitation learning.

2. **Diffusion Policy Fine-tuning Results (Table IV):**
 - After 800 iterations of model-based PPO fine-tuning within the frozen world model, WAM maintained its superior performance.
 - WAM achieved an average success rate of 92.8% across all eight tasks, surpassing DiWA's 79.8% by 13.0 percentage points.
 - WAM outperformed DiWA on every individual task. Two tasks, `turn on lightbulb` and `turn off LED`, reached 100% success with WAM.
 - Significant improvements for WAM included:
 - `open drawer`: WAM 96.7% vs. DiWA 74.44% (+22.26 percentage points).
 - `turn on led`: WAM 96.6% vs. DiWA 86.21% (+10.39 percentage points).
 - The combined results from BC and PPO fine-tuning indicate that WAM's latent representations benefit both offline imitation and online policy adaptation processes, yielding higher average success rates with 8.7x fewer world model training steps than the baseline.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents the World-Action Model (WAM) as a contribution to the field of model-based reinforcement learning and robotic control. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Improved Representation Learning:** The introduction of an inverse dynamics objective as a regularization mechanism is a notable methodological contribution. It demonstrates that explicitly training a world model to predict actions from latent state transitions, in addition to predicting future observations, leads to richer and more action-relevant latent representations. This addresses a limitation of conventional world models, where representations optimized solely for pixel prediction might neglect information critical for control.
- **Enhanced Downstream Policy Performance:** The most direct impact is on the performance of downstream policies. WAM consistently improved success rates in both behavioral cloning and model-based reinforcement learning fine-tuning across a challenging set of manipulation tasks. The observed gains of 15.9 percentage points in average BC success and 13.0 percentage points in average PPO fine-tuning success suggest that the improved representations translate into more effective and robust control policies.
- **Sample Efficiency:** WAM achieved its superior performance while requiring significantly fewer training steps for the world model itself (8.7 times fewer than the baseline DreamerV2). This indicates that the action regularization not only improves representation quality but also potentially makes the world model learning process more efficient in terms of data utilization. Increased sample efficiency is a critical factor in robotics, where real-world data collection is often expensive and time-consuming.
- **Lightweight and Complementary Approach:** Unlike approaches that necessitate significant architectural redesigns or reliance on large foundation models, WAM is a lightweight extension to existing world model architectures like DreamerV2. This makes it a practical and accessible method for improving the capabilities of established model-based reinforcement learning frameworks. It suggests that enhancing the training objective can be a powerful alternative to fundamental architectural changes.
- **Broader Applicability:** The principle of action regularization could be applied to other RSSM-based world models or generative models in different domains, potentially leading to more informative latent spaces for various control and planning tasks. This could extend the utility of world models beyond their current scope, particularly in scenarios where understanding the causal link between actions and environmental changes is paramount.
- **Advancement in Robotic Manipulation:** By enabling policies to achieve higher success rates on complex tasks like object manipulation, WAM contributes to the advancement of autonomous robotics. The ability to learn policies that can precisely control articulated objects, as evidenced by significant gains in "close drawer" and "move slider" tasks, indicates progress toward more dexterous and reliable robotic agents.
