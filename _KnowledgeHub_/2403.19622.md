# RH20T-P: A Primitive-Level Robotic Dataset Towards Composable Generalization Agents

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers introduce RH20T-P, a primitive-level robotic manipulation dataset with 38,000 annotated clips that include explicit spatial information for motion-based skills. This dataset, coupled with a plan-execute paradigm, enables the RA-P baseline to achieve 67.5-80% execution success on out-of-distribution tasks across novel scenes, objects, and skills.

## Problem

- Robotic manipulation agents struggle to achieve robust generalization to out-of-distribution tasks in real-world environments due to limitations in existing datasets.
- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) can decompose complex tasks but often fail to provide the precise, fine-grained primitive-level spatial information (e.g., exact trajectories, destinations) needed for reliable low-level robot execution.
- Current robotic manipulation datasets often lack primitive-level skill annotations and precise spatial grounding, which hinders the training of truly Composable Generalization Agents (CGAs).

## Method

- Developed RH20T-P, a large-scale dataset featuring approximately 38,000 video clips meticulously annotated with primitive skills (e.g., move, pick, push) and diverse primitive-level spatial information (destination, direction, trajectory) extracted from 7-DoF teleoperation records.
- Standardized a 'plan-execute' paradigm for CGAs, which structurally separates task planning (predicting primitive decisions), motion planning (predicting spatial information), and low-level control (executing robot actions).
- Implemented RA-P, an exemplar CGA baseline, utilizing LLaVA for task planning, a Deformable DETR model for motion planning (localizing 2D pixel coordinates and depth), and a combination of hard-coded and policy-based (ACT) low-level controllers.

## Results

- The RH20T-P dataset contains 38,000+ annotated clips, covers 67 diverse manipulation tasks, defines 10+ primitive skills, and includes over 16 million frames, providing fine-grained primitive and spatial annotations.
- The RA-P baseline demonstrated robust generalization, achieving 80% execution success on novel scenes, 70% on novel objects and compositions, and 67.5% on novel skills in out-of-distribution tasks.
- In contrast, VLM-only motion planners (e.g., GPT-4V) exhibited high planning accuracy (95-100%) but low execution success rates (12.5-35%), highlighting their inability to generate precise spatial grounding for physical robot interaction.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating explicit primitive-level spatial information into datasets is crucial for bridging the gap between high-level VLM planning and robust low-level robotic execution.
> - A decoupled 'plan-execute' architecture, where task planning, motion planning, and low-level control are handled by specialized components, significantly enhances generalizability compared to end-to-end or imitation-only approaches.
> - Well-designed, template-based primitive skills enable agents to achieve composable generalization, allowing them to effectively combine learned actions to tackle entirely novel tasks and scenarios.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation faces a fundamental challenge: achieving robust generalization to out-of-distribution tasks in real-world environments. Traditional approaches, such as imitation learning or reinforcement learning, often struggle when encountering novel tasks or objects not extensively covered in their training data. Collecting comprehensive datasets for every conceivable real-world scenario is impractical, making generalizable robotic agents elusive.

Recently, Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have shown significant promise by enabling robots to understand multimodal instructions and decompose complex, compounded tasks into a sequence of simpler, primitive-level skills. This paradigm, termed "composable generalization," allows a robot to learn a set of basic skills and then combine them to solve entirely novel, more complex tasks. For example, a "throw" action might be composed of "move, pick, move, open." This approach is seen as a crucial step towards mitigating the unpredictability and intricacy of open-world robotic manipulation.

However, the current landscape of VLM-based task planners (e.g., PaLM-E, VILA, GPT4Robotics) exhibits significant limitations. While they excel at high-level task decomposition, they often struggle to provide the fine-grained, primitive-level spatial information (e.g., precise trajectories, destinations, or directions) necessary for successful execution by low-level controllers. Existing robotic manipulation datasets further exacerbate this issue:
- Many datasets lack any textual annotations.
- Those with annotations often provide only coarse, task-level language descriptions, which are insufficient for training agents to understand and execute discrete primitive skills.
- Even datasets with "hindsight free-form language" for video clips offer granularity that can still be too ambiguous for robust low-level control.

This critical absence of primitive-level data, coupled with precise spatial knowledge, has hampered the development of truly effective Composable Generalization Agents (CGAs). The RH20T-P work directly addresses this gap by providing a meticulously annotated dataset, thereby filling a crucial need for the community and laying the groundwork for more advanced and generalizable robotic manipulation agents.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to advance the field of robotic manipulation towards achieving generalizability in solving out-of-distribution tasks. The authors identify a critical bottleneck in the current development of Composable Generalization Agents (CGAs): the lack of suitable data and standardized approaches for handling primitive-level skills.

Specifically, the key motivations and objectives are:

1. **Address the deficiency in primitive skill design:** The paper argues that current approaches for defining primitive skills, particularly those relying on free-form natural language, are often too coarse-grained. This lack of precision makes it difficult for low-level controllers to accurately interpret and execute tasks, thereby hindering the composable generalization paradigm.
2. **Overcome the scarcity of primitive-level data annotations:** The community lacks datasets that provide fine-grained annotations of primitive skills, especially those explicitly linking skills with corresponding spatial information. This data gap prevents the effective training of both task planners (to decompose tasks into primitive sequences) and motion planners/low-level controllers (to execute these primitives reliably).
3. **Enable reliable primitive-level spatial information prediction:** While VLMs can plan tasks, they struggle to generate the precise spatial grounding (e.g., exact 3D coordinates, trajectories) needed for robust physical execution. This deficiency often leads to low success rates in real-world scenarios. The authors are motivated to provide this spatial knowledge directly within the dataset.
4. **Propose RH20T-P:** The core objective is to collect and meticulously annotate RH20T-P, a primitive-level robotic manipulation dataset. This dataset is designed to contain a rich set of meticulously defined primitive skills and diverse primitive-level spatial knowledge, making it a foundational resource for building generalizable CGAs in real-world scenarios.
5. **Standardize a plan-execute CGA paradigm:** Beyond the dataset, the authors aim to propose a structured "plan-execute" framework for CGAs. This includes distinct components for task planning, motion planning, and low-level control, providing a blueprint for how future agents can leverage primitive-level data.
6. **Implement and validate a baseline (RA-P):** To demonstrate the utility and effectiveness of RH20T-P, the authors motivate the implementation of an exemplar baseline CGA, RA-P (RobotAgent-Primitive). This baseline serves to validate that the proposed dataset can indeed offer composable generalization ability to robotic manipulation agents, particularly in solving unseen tasks.

In essence, the paper is driven by the need to bridge the gap between high-level AI planning and robust real-world robotic execution by providing the foundational data and a conceptual framework for primitive-level, spatially-aware robotic skills.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers around the creation of the RH20T-P dataset and the standardization and implementation of a plan-execute paradigm for Composable Generalization Agents (CGAs).

#### 3.1. RH20T-P Dataset Creation

The RH20T-P dataset is built upon RH20T, a large-scale real-world robot manipulation dataset.
- **Data Sampling:** A subset of tasks from RH20T, deemed suitable for CGAs and requiring visual reasoning (e.g., wiping a table, arranging chessboard pieces), was sampled.
- **Hierarchical and Scalable Primitive Skills Design:**
 - **Definition:** Primitive skills are defined from the robot arm's perspective, focusing on changes in motion and gripper state. This avoids the ambiguity of overly coarse free-form language.
 - **Categories:**
 - **Motion-based Skills:** Describe robot arm movements (e.g., `move`, `push`, `pull`, `press`, `rotate`). Crucially, these skills are augmented with various forms of spatial information (destination, direction, trajectory) extracted from teleoperation records, a key differentiator from prior datasets. This provides precise grounding, preventing ambiguities in execution.
 - **Gripper-based Skills:** Pertain to gripper operations (e.g., `pick`, `place`, `open/close the gripper`).
 - **Primitive Skill Template:** A template format like "pick the {attribute} {object}" is used to allow flexible interaction with diverse objects and attributes, avoiding the need to define an exhaustive list of skill-object compositions.
 - **Other Skills:** `done` and `reset` are included to indicate task completion and robot arm reset.
- **Hindsight Primitive-level Annotation:**
 - **Segmentation:** Annotators manually watched complete RH20T episodes and segmented them into approximately 38,000 video clips.
 - **Skill Annotation:** Each clip was annotated with its corresponding primitive skill and object/attribute placeholders.
 - **Spatial Information Generation:** The 7-DoF teleoperation records from RH20T were used to automatically generate primitive-level spatial information (destination, direction, trajectory) for motion-based skills.
 - **Scene Description:** GPT-4V was employed to generate detailed captions for each episode, which were then human-inspected to remove hallucinations, providing rich contextual information within the dataset.
- **Dataset Statistics:** RH20T-P comprises 38K+ annotated clips, covers 67 diverse manipulation tasks, defines 10+ primitive skills, and contains over 16 million frames.

#### 3.2. Plan-execute CGA Paradigm

The paper standardizes a "plan-execute" paradigm (Figure 4) for CGAs, consisting of three main components:
1. **Task Planner:** Given a language task description (P), historical decisions, current visual observations (Iᵢ), and the robot arm's position (qᵢ), the Task Planner predicts the next primitive-level decision (πᵢ₊₁).
2. **Motion Planner:** If the predicted decision (πᵢ₊₁) is a motion-based skill, a Motion Planner is invoked to predict the corresponding primitive-level spatial information (mᵢ₊₁), such as a destination coordinate or trajectory.
3. **Low-level Controller:** This component maps the primitive decision (πᵢ₊₁) and its associated spatial information (mᵢ₊₁) to a sequence of robot actions ({aᵢ₊₁}). These actions are then executed by the robot arm, leading to a new state.
This process iterates until the Task Planner signals task completion. During training, each transition in a RH20T-P clip provides the ground truth for planning and execution.

#### 3.3. RA-P Baseline Implementation

To validate the RH20T-P dataset, the authors implement RA-P (RobotAgent-Primitive), an exemplar CGA baseline following the proposed paradigm.
- **Task Planner:** LLaVA (a 7B Vision-Language Model) is fine-tuned on an instruction-following dataset derived from RH20T-P. A "Chain-of-Thought" inference strategy (describing the scene before decision-making) is also utilized to mitigate perceptual errors.
- **Motion Planner:** A Deformable DETR model is used to localize the next destination as a 2D pixel coordinate (x, y) and depth (d). A special `<pos>` token embedded in the Task Planner's output triggers the Motion Planner. The predicted (x, y, d) is then converted into a 3D point in the real world using camera calibration.
- **Low-level Controller:**
 - **Hard-coded controllers:** For basic primitive skills like `move` and `open/close`, simple hard-coded logic is used. For motion-based skills, trajectories are interpolated between the current robot position and the 3D point predicted by the Motion Planner.
 - **Policy-based controllers:** For complex interaction skills (e.g., `pick`, `push`, `pull`, `press`), individual ACTs (Action Chunking with Transformers) are trained using the 7-DoF control sequences from the corresponding RH20T-P clips.
- **Training and Inference Details:** RA-P is trained on 8 NVIDIA A100 GPUs. The DETR motion planner is further fine-tuned on a small dataset from the evaluation environment to adapt to new sensors. Inference is deployed on a single NVIDIA A100 GPU, with a communication module facilitating interaction with the robot arm, following the iterative plan-execute cycle.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides compelling findings regarding the utility of RH20T-P and the effectiveness of the proposed CGA paradigm and RA-P baseline.

#### 4.1. Dataset Characteristics

- **Scale and Diversity:** RH20T-P contains approximately 38,000 video clips, encompassing 67 diverse manipulation tasks in real-world scenarios. These tasks are decomposed into over 10 meticulously designed primitive skills. The dataset includes 16 million frames, providing a rich source of visual and operational data.
- **Fine-grained Annotation:** Each clip is manually annotated with primitive skills, objects, and attributes, providing fine-grained supervisory signals.
- **Spatial Information:** A crucial contribution is the inclusion of primitive-level spatial information (destination, direction, trajectory) extracted from 7-DoF teleoperation records, which directly addresses the gap in existing datasets.
- **Contextual Descriptions:** GPT-4V-generated and human-inspected captions for each episode provide rich semantic context for the scenes.

#### 4.2. Generalization Performance

The RA-P baseline was evaluated on 8 out-of-distribution novel tasks across three incremental levels of generalization, with 10 trials per task:
1. **Novel Scenes:** Tasks in unseen environments (e.g., different backgrounds, tablecloths).
2. **Novel Objects & Compositions:** Tasks involving unseen objects or novel combinations of known skills and objects.
3. **Novel Skills:** Tasks requiring skills never encountered during training (e.g., wiping, throwing, stacking).

The results (Table 2) demonstrate significant improvements over baselines:

- **Comparison to Imitation-based Methods (ACT):**
 - ACT (without fine-tuning on evaluation tasks) showed consistently poor execution success rates (5-15%), struggling even with basic tasks due to sensitivity to initial robot arm positions.
 - ACT (with fine-tuning on evaluation tasks) saw improvement but remained limited (15-40%), indicating that direct imitation learning alone struggles with out-of-distribution generalization.
 - RA-P's approach of decoupling motion planning enabled its primitive-level controllers to operate more effectively near target objects, leading to substantial performance gains.

- **Comparison to VLM-only Motion Planning (GPT-4V):**
 - Agents using GPT-4V for both task and motion planning achieved high planning accuracy (95-100%).
 - However, their execution success rates were significantly lower (12.5-35%). This stark disparity highlights GPT-4V's difficulty in generating reliable primitive-level spatial information (even with ICL), leading to imprecise positioning and execution failures.

- **RA-P (ours) Performance:**
 - RA-P achieved high planning accuracy (85-100%) and substantially higher execution success rates across all generalization levels:
 - **Novel Scenes:** 80% execution success.
 - **Novel Objects & Compositions:** 70% execution success.
 - **Novel Skills:** 67.5% execution success.
 - These results validate that RH20T-P's well-designed primitive skills and rich spatial information enable robust composable generalization, especially in tackling novel skills by combining known primitives.

#### 4.3. Qualitative Analysis and Insights

- **Robustness to Object Distractions:** RA-P demonstrated the ability to distinguish target objects from clutter and successfully execute tasks like "Throw garbage" (Figure 6c).
- **Failure Cases (Figure 6d):** Analysis revealed that failures primarily stemmed from low-level controller issues (e.g., failing to pick), localization deviations from the DETR motion planner (especially in cluttered scenes), and perceptual errors by the LLaVA VLM leading to incorrect subsequent positioning.
- **Data Scaling:** Experiments on a simple online benchmark showed that increasing the amount of RH20T-P data for fine-tuning significantly improved both task planning accuracy and destination prediction recall (Table 3), indicating that the dataset's potential is far from saturated.
- **Inference Time:** The deployed RA-P system exhibited acceptable inference times, with the planner inference accounting for roughly 8% of the total cycle, suggesting feasibility for real-world applications (Figure 7). Asynchronous communication is noted as a future optimization.
- **Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Inference:** By having the VLM first describe the scene, CoT inference effectively reduced some perceptual errors, although the 7B model occasionally produced hallucinations (Figure 8, Appendix A).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of RH20T-P represents a significant advancement in the field of robotic manipulation, offering a critical resource for developing more generalizable and intelligent robotic agents.

1. **Addressing a Core Gap:** RH20T-P is the first dataset to provide meticulously designed primitive skills along with diverse primitive-level spatial knowledge, directly filling a crucial void in existing robotic manipulation datasets. This precise, fine-grained annotation, especially the integration of spatial information from teleoperation, allows for a more robust grounding of high-level plans to low-level physical actions.
2. **Facilitating Composable Generalization:** The dataset, coupled with the proposed plan-execute CGA paradigm, provides a strong foundation for researchers to develop and evaluate agents capable of composable generalization. By enabling agents to master novel tasks through the composition of learned primitive skills, this work pushes beyond the limitations of task-specific training.
3. **Improved Real-World Generalization:** The experimental validation with the RA-P baseline unequivocally demonstrates that agents trained on RH20T-P can achieve robust generalization across novel scenes, objects, and, most importantly, novel skills. This capability is paramount for deploying robots in dynamic, unstructured real-world environments where encountering unforeseen tasks is common.
4. **Catalyst for Future Research:** RH20T-P is poised to accelerate research in several key areas:
 - **Advanced Task and Motion Planners:** The rich annotations will enable the development of more sophisticated task planners that can accurately decompose complex goals and more robust motion planners that can precisely ground these primitives in space.
 - **Specialized Low-level Controllers:** The fine-grained primitive skill data allows for the training of highly specialized and efficient low-level controllers for each primitive.
 - **Scaling and Integration of VLMs:** The dataset provides a benchmark for integrating larger and more capable Vision-Language Models into robotic systems, evaluating their ability to interpret instructions and generate spatially-aware plans.
5. **Practical Implications:** The ability for robots to learn novel skills through composition will lead to more adaptable and versatile robotic systems in various applications, from industrial settings to domestic assistance, reducing the need for extensive retraining for every new task.

**Limitations and Future Work:** The authors acknowledge that while RH20T-P is pioneering, there is significant potential for further data accumulation to push the boundaries of composable generalization. They also plan to explore more sophisticated planning systems (e.g., those from) and robust motion planning strategies based on directions or trajectories, beyond the current destination-based approach, to overcome existing computational resource constraints.

**Potential Social Impact:** The authors note the potential for negative social impacts due to violent elements (e.g., using a knife) present in the source RH20T dataset. However, they argue these risks are controllable given RH20T's public release, emphasizing responsible development and deployment of agents trained on such data.

In conclusion, RH20T-P marks a pivotal step towards building robotic systems that are truly capable of learning and adapting to the complexities of the real world, setting a new standard for data-driven research in robotic manipulation.
