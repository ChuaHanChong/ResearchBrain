# Planning with Diffusion for Flexible Behavior Synthesis

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from UC Berkeley and MIT developed Diffuser, a novel approach using denoising diffusion probabilistic models to synthesize entire state-action trajectories for control. The method unifies learned dynamics and planning, demonstrating high performance on long-horizon, sparse-reward tasks and strong test-time flexibility across various robotic and locomotion benchmarks.

## Problem

- Traditional model-based reinforcement learning (MBRL) often fails because powerful trajectory optimizers exploit inaccuracies in learned dynamics models, leading to suboptimal or 'adversarial' plans.
- Current MBRL approaches struggle with long planning horizons and sparse reward signals, as compounding errors in sequential prediction make global coherence difficult.
- Learning separate dynamics models and then using them with classical planners limits flexibility and reusability, often requiring retraining for new tasks or constraints.

## Method

- Introduces **Diffuser**, a ==denoising diffusion probabilistic model==, to directly generate entire ==state-action trajectories== non-autoregressively, unifying modeling and planning.
- Frames planning as ==guided sampling== from the learned trajectory distribution, using a separate ==reward predictor== for ==classifier-guided sampling== or treating goal-conditioning as an ==inpainting== task.
- Employs a fully convolutional ==U-Net==-like architecture for the diffusion model, allowing for variable-length plans and ==warm-starting== to reduce computational costs for online execution.

## Results

- Diffuser achieved scores over **100** in Maze2D environments, significantly outperforming prior model-free offline RL algorithms (MPPI, CQL, IQL) on long-horizon, sparse-reward tasks.
- A single trained Diffuser model demonstrated strong test-time flexibility across various block stacking tasks, adapting to new objectives by modifying only the guidance function.
- The approach achieved competitive performance on D4RL locomotion benchmarks (HalfCheetah, Hopper, Walker2d), often outperforming other model-based and return-conditioning methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating trajectory modeling and planning into a single generative process, rather than treating them separately, leads to more robust and less exploitable learned planners.
> - Diffusion probabilistic models can effectively learn distributions over entire trajectories, enabling non-autoregressive, flexible, and guided behavior synthesis.
> - The iterative denoising process of diffusion models allows for global coherence in generated plans to emerge from local consistency constraints, supporting long-horizon and sparse-reward tasks.
