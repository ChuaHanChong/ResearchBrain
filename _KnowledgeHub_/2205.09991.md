# Planning with Diffusion for Flexible Behavior Synthesis

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from UC Berkeley and MIT developed Diffuser, a novel approach using denoising diffusion probabilistic models to synthesize entire state-action trajectories for control. The method unifies learned dynamics and planning, demonstrating high performance on long-horizon, sparse-reward tasks and strong test-time flexibility across various robotic and locomotion benchmarks.

## Problem

- Traditional model-based reinforcement learning (MBRL) often fails because powerful trajectory optimizers exploit inaccuracies in learned dynamics models, leading to suboptimal or 'adversarial' plans.
- Current MBRL approaches struggle with long planning horizons and sparse reward signals, as compounding errors in sequential prediction make global coherence difficult.
- Learning separate dynamics models and then using them with classical planners limits flexibility and reusability, often requiring retraining for new tasks or constraints.

## Method

- Introduces **Diffuser**, a ==denoising diffusion probabilistic model==, to directly generate entire ==state-action trajectories== non-autoregressively, unifying modeling and planning.
- Frames planning as ==guided sampling== from the learned trajectory distribution, using a separate ==reward predictor== for ==classifier-guided sampling== or treating goal-conditioning as an ==inpainting== task.
- Employs a fully convolutional ==U-Net==-like architecture for the diffusion model, allowing for variable-length plans and ==warm-starting== to reduce computational costs for online execution.

## Results

- Diffuser achieved scores over **100** in Maze2D environments, significantly outperforming prior model-free offline RL algorithms (MPPI, CQL, IQL) on long-horizon, sparse-reward tasks.
- A single trained Diffuser model demonstrated strong test-time flexibility across various block stacking tasks, adapting to new objectives by modifying only the guidance function.
- The approach achieved competitive performance on D4RL locomotion benchmarks (HalfCheetah, Hopper, Walker2d), often outperforming other model-based and return-conditioning methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating trajectory modeling and planning into a single generative process, rather than treating them separately, leads to more robust and less exploitable learned planners.
> - Diffusion probabilistic models can effectively learn distributions over entire trajectories, enabling non-autoregressive, flexible, and guided behavior synthesis.
> - The iterative denoising process of diffusion models allows for global coherence in generated plans to emerge from local consistency constraints, supporting long-horizon and sparse-reward tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research sits at the intersection of several rapidly evolving fields within artificial intelligence: model-based reinforcement learning (MBRL), deep generative models (specifically diffusion models), and offline reinforcement learning.

Traditionally, MBRL approaches involve learning an explicit dynamics model of the environment (how states evolve given actions) and then using this model with classical trajectory optimization techniques (e.g., Model Predictive Control (MPC), Cross-Entropy Method (CEM)) to plan actions. However, the authors highlight a critical empirical shortcoming: learned models are often imperfect, and powerful trajectory optimizers can exploit these inaccuracies, leading to "adversarial examples" or plans that are theoretically optimal according to the faulty model but perform poorly in the real environment. This has often pushed contemporary MBRL algorithms towards hybrid approaches that integrate elements from model-free methods, such as value functions or policy gradients, thereby reducing their reliance on pure trajectory optimization.

The paper aims to disrupt this paradigm by proposing a "tighter coupling between modeling and planning." Instead of treating the learned model as a black box to be plugged into a separate planning algorithm, they propose to integrate planning directly into the generative modeling process. This shifts the focus from learning accurate single-step dynamics (which compound errors over long horizons) to directly learning distributions over entire *trajectories*.

This is where **diffusion probabilistic models** come into play. Diffusion models have recently revolutionized generative AI, demonstrating state-of-the-art results in image, audio, and text synthesis. They operate by iteratively denoising data from a simple noise distribution, effectively learning to reverse a gradual corruption process. This paper is one of the first, if not the first, to successfully apply this powerful class of generative models to the domain of sequential decision-making and reinforcement learning. By framing trajectory optimization as an iterative denoising process, the authors leverage the inherent capabilities of diffusion models for conditional generation, flexibility, and generalization.

Furthermore, the work significantly contributes to **offline reinforcement learning**. Offline RL focuses on learning policies from pre-collected, static datasets of interactions without further online environment exploration. This setting is crucial for real-world applications where data collection can be expensive or unsafe. The ability of Diffuser to learn from heterogeneous data and generate high-quality plans without online interaction is a significant advantage in this landscape, providing a strong alternative to traditional model-free offline RL algorithms.

Finally, the paper touches upon the "control-as-inference" framework, where reinforcement learning problems are reinterpreted as probabilistic inference tasks. This connection allows them to leverage techniques like classifier-guided sampling (common in diffusion models for class-conditional generation) to incorporate reward functions into the planning process, further unifying the modeling and planning steps.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to fundamentally rethink the relationship between learned models and planning in reinforcement learning, specifically addressing the empirical shortcomings of current model-based RL approaches. The core motivation stems from the observation that traditional model-based RL, which typically learns an approximate dynamics model and then uses classical trajectory optimizers, often fails. This failure occurs because the optimizers, designed for ground-truth dynamics, can exploit small inaccuracies in learned models, leading to plans that resemble "adversarial examples" rather than optimal behaviors.

To overcome this, the authors propose a paradigm where:
- **Learning and Planning are Unified:** The central goal is to "fold as much of the trajectory optimization pipeline as possible into the modeling problem, such that sampling from the model and planning with it become nearly identical." This means the model isn't just a predictor; it *is* the planner.
- **Direct Trajectory Generation:** Instead of focusing on single-step dynamics prediction, the model should directly generate entire, coherent trajectories. This avoids the compounding errors inherent in autoregressive prediction over long horizons.
- **Reward Agnostic Modeling:** The learned model should be independent of the specific reward function. This allows a single trained model to be repurposed for multiple tasks or objectives without retraining, enhancing flexibility and reusability.
- **Flexible Conditioning:** The model should natively support diverse planning objectives, such as maximizing rewards, reaching specific goals, or satisfying complex constraints, through flexible conditioning mechanisms at test time.
- **Robustness and Generalization:** The resulting planner should be resistant to the myopic failure modes of standard shooting-based algorithms, scale gracefully to long planning horizons with sparse rewards, and exhibit strong generalization capabilities by composing familiar subsequences into novel behaviors.

In essence, the motivation is to create a more robust, flexible, and capable data-driven planner that leverages the power of modern deep generative models to directly synthesize effective behaviors, rather than relying on an imperfect separation between dynamics modeling and trajectory optimization.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the proposed methodology is **Diffuser**, a denoising diffusion probabilistic model specifically designed for trajectory data. This approach represents a significant departure from conventional model-based RL.

**A. Trajectory Representation and Model Architecture:**
- **Joint State-Action Trajectories:** Diffuser models complete trajectories `τ = (s0, a0, s1, a1..., sT, aT)` as a two-dimensional array of interleaved states and actions. This joint representation is crucial because, for planning, actions are as important as states, and the model needs to understand their interplay over time.
- **Non-Autoregressive Generation:** Unlike traditional dynamics models that predict states sequentially (s_t+1 from s_t, a_t), Diffuser predicts all timesteps of a plan *concurrently*. This non-autoregressive nature is essential because planning often involves "anti-causal" dependencies (e.g., decisions in the present are conditioned on future goals).
- **Temporal Locality:** Despite concurrent prediction, Diffuser maintains a form of temporal locality. Its architecture, inspired by U-Nets used in image processing, employs one-dimensional temporal convolutions. This means each denoising step primarily enforces local consistency (dependencies between nearby timesteps). Global coherence emerges over many iterative denoising steps.
- **Variable-Length Plans:** A key architectural advantage is that Diffuser is fully convolutional in the "horizon" dimension. This allows the planning horizon `T` to be determined by the input noise dimensionality at inference time, rather than being fixed by the model architecture during training. This enables flexible generation of plans of varying lengths.
- **Training:** Diffuser is trained by minimizing a simplified objective (noise prediction) common in diffusion models, where it learns to predict the noise added to a noisy trajectory `τ_i` to recover the original clean trajectory `τ_0`.

**B. Planning as Guided Sampling:**
The true innovation lies in how Diffuser uses its generative capabilities for planning. This is achieved through two main strategies:

- **Reinforcement Learning as Classifier-Guided Sampling:**
 - Leveraging the "control-as-inference" framework, the problem of maximizing reward is reinterpreted as sampling from a perturbed distribution `p̃(τ) ∝ p(τ)h(τ)`, where `p(τ)` is the trajectory distribution learned by Diffuser, and `h(τ)` encodes the reward or optimality information.
 - This is implemented by training a separate "reward predictor" model `Jφ` (a shallow network) to estimate the cumulative reward of a given trajectory.
 - During the iterative denoising process, the mean `μ` of the reverse diffusion step is modified by adding a gradient term `αΣ∇J(μ)`. This gradient "guides" the trajectory generation towards regions of high reward, analogous to how classifier gradients guide image generation in diffusion models.
 - The current state is incorporated as a hard constraint by "inpainting" it into the initial part of the trajectory during each denoising step, ensuring plans start from the correct location.

- **Goal-Conditioned RL as Inpainting:**
 - For tasks where the objective is to satisfy constraints (e.g., reach a specific goal state), the problem is framed as an "inpainting" task.
 - Given the 2D trajectory representation, specific state or action constraints (e.g., `s_0 = current_state`, `s_T = goal_state`) are treated as "observed pixels."
 - The diffusion model then "fills in" the unobserved parts of the trajectory in a way consistent with these constraints.
 - In practice, this involves sampling from the unperturbed reverse process and then directly replacing the sampled values at constrained timesteps with the specified conditioning values.

**C. Practical Considerations:**
- **Warm-Starting:** To mitigate the computational cost of iterative denoising for online execution (where a new plan might be needed at each step), the authors propose warm-starting. This involves taking a previously generated plan, adding a small amount of noise (limited forward diffusion steps), and then denoising from this partially noisy state for fewer steps to generate an updated plan. This significantly reduces planning latency.

In summary, Diffuser unifies learned dynamics, behavior generation, and planning into a single, cohesive framework. By operating on entire trajectories and leveraging the iterative, gradient-based sampling properties of diffusion models, it bypasses the traditional pitfalls of explicit dynamics model exploitation and offers flexible, guided behavior synthesis.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of Diffuser focuses on demonstrating its capabilities across several key areas: long-horizon planning, multi-task generalization, test-time flexibility, and performance in standard offline reinforcement learning benchmarks.

**A. Long Horizon Multi-Task Planning (Maze2D Environments):**
- **Superior Performance:** Diffuser significantly outperforms prior model-free offline RL algorithms (MPPI, CQL, IQL) in the Maze2D environments, which are characterized by sparse rewards and require hundreds of steps to reach a goal. Diffuser consistently achieves scores over 100, indicating it finds optimal paths that expert policies would take, something prior methods struggle with due to credit assignment difficulties.
- **Robustness to Sparse Rewards:** The results demonstrate that Diffuser's learned planning procedure effectively handles sparse reward settings, unlike shooting-based approaches that often fail in such scenarios.
- **Multi-Task Capability:** In the Multi2D variant, where goal locations are randomized at the start of each episode, Diffuser maintains its high performance simply by changing the conditioning goal. It does not require retraining, showcasing its natural multi-task planning capability. In contrast, the best model-free baseline (IQL) suffers a substantial performance drop when adapted to the multi-task setting.
- **Beyond Perfect Dynamics:** Interestingly, MPPI, a classical planning method using *ground-truth* dynamics, performs poorly compared to Diffuser. This highlights that even with perfect dynamics, long-horizon planning can be challenging, and Diffuser's integrated approach provides benefits beyond mere predictive accuracy.

**B. Test-time Flexibility (Block Stacking Tasks):**
- **Strong Generalization:** Diffuser demonstrates remarkable test-time flexibility across three challenging block stacking tasks: Unconditional Stacking, Conditional Stacking (specified order), and Rearrangement (novel arrangements). It substantially outperforms BCQ and CQL, especially in conditional settings that require flexible behavior generation in novel states.
- **Single Model, Multiple Tasks:** A single trained Diffuser model is sufficient for all block-stacking tasks; only the perturbation function `h(τ)` is modified to guide the planning process towards different objectives (e.g., matching a final state, enforcing contact constraints). This showcases its ability to adapt to new goals and constraints without architectural changes or retraining.

**C. Offline Reinforcement Learning (D4RL Locomotion Suite):**
- **Competitive Performance:** On the standard D4RL locomotion benchmarks (HalfCheetah, Hopper, Walker2d), Diffuser achieves competitive performance compared to a wide array of prior algorithms, including state-of-the-art model-free, return-conditioning, and model-based methods. While not consistently outperforming the absolute best single-task specialized offline RL techniques, it holds its own, particularly outperforming some other model-based methods (MOReL, MBOP) and return-conditioning approaches (DT).
- **Coupled Design is Key:** Crucially, the authors found that using Diffuser solely as a *dynamics model* within conventional trajectory optimizers (like MPPI) yielded performance no better than random. This strongly supports their central hypothesis: the effectiveness of Diffuser stems from the *coupled* nature of its modeling and planning processes, not merely from superior open-loop predictive accuracy of trajectories.

**D. Runtime Considerations (Warm-Starting Diffusion):**
- **Improved Efficiency:** While iterative denoising can be slow, the authors demonstrate that warm-starting the planning process (by re-using previous plans and running fewer denoising steps) can significantly reduce runtime. For instance, using one-tenth the number of diffusion steps results in only a modest drop in performance, making the approach more practical for real-time control.

Overall, the results paint a picture of Diffuser as a highly capable and versatile data-driven planner. Its key strengths lie in its ability to handle long-horizon, sparse-reward problems, adapt flexibly to new tasks and constraints without retraining, and robustly learn from offline data, all while mitigating the exploitation issues common in traditional MBRL.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Diffuser represents a significant step forward in the field of model-based reinforcement learning and deep generative modeling for control. Its implications and potential impact are multifaceted:

- **A New Paradigm for Model-Based RL:** Diffuser challenges the conventional abstraction barrier between learned dynamics models and classical planners. By tightly coupling modeling and planning into a unified, iterative generative process, it offers a novel paradigm that appears more robust to model inaccuracies and less prone to the "adversarial example" problem that has plagued traditional MBRL. This could inspire a new wave of research exploring deep integration of model learning and decision-making.

- **Pioneering Application of Diffusion Models in RL:** This work is a pioneering effort in applying the powerful framework of diffusion probabilistic models to sequential decision-making and reinforcement learning. Given the immense success of diffusion models in image, audio, and text generation, their effective translation to complex control problems opens up a vast new avenue for research. Future work could explore variants of diffusion models, different guidance mechanisms, or even hybrid approaches that combine diffusion with other generative paradigms for more efficient or accurate behavior synthesis.

- **Enhanced Flexibility and Generalization:** The ability of Diffuser to adapt to new reward functions and constraints at test time, without retraining the core model, is a major advantage. This "task compositionality" and "temporal compositionality" (stitching together known subsequences) are critical for developing more general-purpose AI agents that can solve a wide variety of tasks in dynamic environments. This flexibility contrasts sharply with many model-free methods that require retraining for each new task.

- **Addressing Long-Horizon and Sparse Reward Challenges:** The impressive performance in sparse-reward, long-horizon tasks like Maze2D highlights Diffuser's capability to overcome fundamental challenges in RL. Traditional methods often struggle with credit assignment and exploration in such settings. Diffuser's trajectory-level generation and iterative refinement intrinsically support long-term planning.

- **Advancements in Offline Reinforcement Learning:** As a strong performer in offline RL benchmarks and exhibiting robust behavior synthesis from demonstration data (block stacking), Diffuser offers a compelling new approach for learning from static datasets. This is crucial for real-world applications where online interaction is costly or impossible, paving the way for more practical and deployable RL systems.

- **Insights into Behavior Synthesis:** The paper provides valuable insights into how complex behaviors can emerge from local consistency constraints through an iterative denoising process. This could inform our understanding of how biological systems or intelligent agents might compose novel actions from learned primitives.

While the computational cost of iterative denoising remains a practical challenge for online deployment, the proposed warm-starting technique offers a promising path towards mitigating this. The paper's success firmly establishes diffusion models as a powerful tool for behavior synthesis, potentially inspiring a new generation of robust, flexible, and generalizable learning-based control systems.
