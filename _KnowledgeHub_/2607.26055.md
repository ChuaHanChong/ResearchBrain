# $π\mathbf{R}^2$: Reactive Real-time Flow Policies

> [!summary] Summary
> The πR^2 framework enables reactive, real-time control for generalist manipulation policies that typically face high latency from large vision-language models and multi-step action prediction. It achieves this by disentangling sensory conditioning and introducing a latency-adaptive flow schedule, leading to up to 30% increased success rates in real-world dexterous manipulation tasks compared to baseline methods.

## Problem

- Modern generalist robotic manipulation policies suffer from limited reactivity due to open-loop execution of action chunks, making them unresponsive to dynamic sensory input.
- The computational cost of large vision-language backbones and multiple denoising steps introduces significant latency in the perception-to-action pipeline, impeding frequent replanning.
- Existing action-chunking policies struggle to maintain smooth, continuous motion and adapt to variable inference delays in practical deployment scenarios.

## Method

- A ==Proprioception-Reactive Diffusion Forcing== mechanism disentangles policy conditioning into a slow channel (vision-language, processed asynchronously) and a fast channel (proprioception, processed synchronously), enabling high-frequency action emission with single denoising steps.
- A ==Latency-Adaptive Flow Schedule== employs a ==three-region staircase noise schedule==, treating in-flight actions as inpainting conditions and adjusting to variable inference delays to maintain smooth and coherent actions.
- The policy is trained to tolerate staleness in slow-channel features by introducing artificial delays and learned embeddings, allowing it to adapt to measured visual/language latency during deployment.

## Results

- In simulation, πR^2 achieved **0.43-0.45** success rates on a reactive cube reorientation task, outperforming baselines (**0.33-0.36** success rates) under realistic latency, while maintaining an effective action delay of one control tick.
- Real-world experiments on an xArm6 with GR00T-N1.7 showed πR^2 improving success rates by **20% to 30%** on contact-rich tasks like 'Tidy Up Book' (**12/20** successes vs. **8/20** for the best baseline).
- πR^2 demonstrated qualitatively smoother and more accurate motions, improved failure recovery, and immediate, precise fingertip force modulation based on live feedback, unlike baselines which often reacted late and overshot desired forces.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling conditioning based on modality update rates allows for real-time reactive control using fresh proprioceptive input, even when integrating computationally intensive, asynchronously updated vision-language models.
> - Amortizing the denoising budget across control ticks (one Neural Function Evaluation per call) combined with a latency-adaptive noise schedule can achieve high-frequency action emission without sacrificing policy expressiveness or temporal consistency.
> - Explicitly accounting for and training against variable inference delays and feature staleness enhances policy robustness and enables seamless operation in dynamic real-world robotic environments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

Recent advancements in robotic manipulation have led to the development of "robotics foundation models." These models typically incorporate three key design choices:
1. **Large pre-trained backbones:** Utilizing vision-language models (VLMs) for rich, generalizable representations from visual and language inputs.
2. **Expressive policy architectures:** Employing diffusion or flow matching models to capture complex, multi-modal action distributions observed in human demonstrations.
3. **Action chunking:** Predicting multiple future actions jointly to improve temporal consistency and provide a richer training signal.

While these innovations have enabled generalizable and scalable imitation learning, they introduce significant limitations in terms of reactivity and latency. Current policies execute "chunks" of actions in an open-loop manner, meaning they cannot react to new sensory input that arrives during the execution of a chunk. Replanning more frequently would address this, but the computational demands of large backbones combined with multiple denoising steps in diffusion/flow matching make the "perception-to-action" pipeline too slow. This latency results in stale committed actions, making these policies suboptimal for dynamic, closed-loop control tasks.

This work addresses this core challenge by proposing a framework, πR2, that aims to restore reactivity and real-time performance to these generalist manipulation policies. It seeks to do so without abandoning the beneficial design principles of large pre-trained backbones, expressive multi-modal policies, and multi-action prediction.

Related work has explored similar challenges. Action chunking policies, such as those based on Diffusion Policy and flow matching, improve temporal coherence but suffer from open-loop execution for dynamic tasks. Generalist manipulation policies like GR00T N1 leverage large VLMs, exacerbating the latency problem. Methods for real-time execution of action-chunking policies, such as RTC and Streaming Diffusion Policy, have been introduced. RTC focuses on maintaining continuity across asynchronous chunks through inpainting, while Streaming Diffusion Policy emits actions incrementally with variable noise. However, these prior approaches do not fully address the bottleneck of repeatedly conditioning on large semantic backbones or effectively managing variable inference delays. Streaming Diffusion Policy, for instance, often relies on compact visuomotor policies and synchronous operation. Concurrent work like FASTER also uses diffusion-forcing-inspired schedules and action-prefix conditioning but processes the backbone once per chunk, conditioning all actions on a single observation, thus improving latency and smoothness without enhancing reactivity to mid-execution sensory input.

πR2 distinguishes itself by:
1. Disentangling conditioning into fast (proprioception) and slow (vision-language) channels, allowing high-frequency proprioceptive updates within an action chunk while tolerating asynchronous VLM updates.
2. Introducing a latency-adaptive flow schedule that treats in-flight actions as inpainting conditioning and enables single-denoising-step action emission, allowing the model to adapt to varying hardware latency and maintain smooth, temporally coherent outputs.

By addressing these specific aspects, πR2 aims to bridge the gap between the generality and scalability of modern robotics foundation models and the real-time reactivity required for complex, dynamic manipulation tasks.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable reactive, real-time policy inference for generalist manipulation policies, specifically those built upon large pretrained backbones, expressive multi-modal architectures (like diffusion/flow matching), and action chunking. The authors identify several motivations and challenges that drive this objective:

1. **Addressing the reactivity deficit in current generalist policies:** Modern policies, while powerful in terms of generalization and scalability, suffer from limited reactivity. This is because they typically predict a "chunk" of future actions that are then executed open-loop. During this open-loop execution, the robot cannot incorporate new sensory information, making it unresponsive to unexpected changes or dynamic environments. This lack of reactivity is particularly detrimental for tasks requiring fast, smooth, and precise control, such as contact-rich manipulation.

2. **Overcoming the latency bottleneck of perception-to-action pipelines:** The computational cost associated with current policies is a major impediment to frequent replanning. The pipeline involves:
 - Processing visual and language inputs through large vision-language backbones (VLMs).
 - Performing multiple denoising iterations for flow matching or diffusion policy inference.
 These steps cumulatively introduce significant latency, making it infeasible to re-predict actions with every new sensory observation. Consequently, actions committed to by the policy become "stale," further reducing effective reactivity.

3. **Retaining the benefits of existing successful design choices:** The authors explicitly state their goal is not to discard the effective components of current generalist policies (large backbones, expressive multi-modal policies, multi-action prediction) but to integrate reactivity and real-time performance within this framework. This implies a motivation to refine, rather than replace, the current paradigm.

4. **Exploiting modality-specific update rates:** A key motivation stems from the observation that different sensory modalities provide information at vastly different update rates and serve different control purposes. Proprioceptive signals (joint positions, velocities, torques) can be acquired and processed much faster than visual or linguistic inputs. Furthermore, proprioception is often sufficient for local, reactive corrections, while vision and language provide broader contextual guidance. The authors are motivated to leverage this asymmetry to improve reactive control.

5. **Managing variable inference delays:** Practical deployment environments introduce variable inference delays due to factors like GPU bandwidth, network latency, and computational load fluctuations. Existing methods often assume instantaneous inference or fixed delays, leading to issues like jerky motions or discontinuities at chunk boundaries when these assumptions are violated. A motivation is to develop a policy that can seamlessly adapt to and tolerate such real-world, variable latency.

In summary, the core motivation is to bridge the performance gap between the impressive generalization capabilities of robot foundation models and the practical requirements of real-world dynamic manipulation, specifically by making these models reactive and capable of real-time inference without compromising their underlying architectural strengths.

### 3. Methodology and approach

The πR2 framework modifies existing large-scale flow policies to enhance reactivity and reduce inference latency. It builds upon flow matching and diffusion forcing and introduces two main contributions: Proprioception-Reactive Diffusion Forcing and a Latency-Adaptive Flow Schedule.

#### 3.1. Preliminary Concepts

**Flow Matching and Action Chunking Policy:**
The work is based on flow matching, which learns a velocity field to transport samples from a noise distribution to a data distribution. In the context of action chunking policies, the data `x1` is a chunk of `H` future actions `(at, ..., a(t+H))` conditioned on an observation `ot`. This chunk is generated through `K` denoising steps, and the robot executes the first `h <= H` actions before replanning. A key limitation is that all `H` actions share a single noise level, and the chunk is executed open-loop, limiting reactivity.

**Diffusion Forcing and Streaming Diffusion:** Diffusion forcing extends flow matching by assigning an independent noise level `τp` ∈ to each chunk position `p`. This allows for closed-loop action prediction by conditioning successive denoising steps on progressively more recent observations within a single chunk. Streaming Diffusion is an example that uses a linearly increasing noise schedule. πR2 adopts diffusion forcing but focuses on enabling it for large-scale flow policies (VLAs) where high latency is inherent.

#### 3.2. Proprioception-Reactive Diffusion Forcing

This component addresses the computational bottleneck of large VLM backbones.
- **Problem:** Processing the full observation `ot = (st, It, Tt)` (proprioception, image, text) through a large VLM and multiple DiT denoising steps (e.g., 60 ms for VLM + 80 ms for DiT = 140 ms for GR00T-N1.7) creates significant per-call latency.
- **Solution:** The DiT's conditioning is disentangled into two channels:
 1. **Slow Channel:** Vision and language features from the VLM and image/text encoders. These are processed asynchronously in a background thread and cached. They provide coarse spatial and task guidance.
 2. **Fast Channel:** Proprioceptive signals (joint positions, end-effector pose, etc.). These are retrieved and processed orders of magnitude faster and are "fresh every tick." They provide fine motor refinement.
- **Mechanism:** The DiT action head runs only one denoising step per call, using the fresh proprioceptive input and the most recently cached slow features. This reduces the per-call computational cost of the action head to a single Neural Function Evaluation (NFE), independent of the backbone size.
- **Training for Staleness Tolerance:** To account for the latency `d_vlm` (age of the cached slow feature), the slow channel is artificially delayed during training (`d_vlm` ~ Uniform{0..., `d_max_vlm`}). A learned embedding, indexed by the integer delay, is added to the slow representation, allowing the policy to adapt to the measured `d_vlm` at deployment.

#### 3.3. Latency-Adaptive Flow Schedule

This component enables the policy to operate effectively under variable inference delays (`d`) that arise from practical constraints (e.g., GPU bandwidth, network latency).
- **Problem:** Naive linear noise schedules (like in Streaming Diffusion) assume `d = 0` (instantaneous inference) and lead to chunk boundary issues when `d > 0`.
- **Solution: Three-region Staircase Schedule:** πR2 employs a per-position noise schedule `τ*^d` for a target inference delay `d`, which consists of three regions within an action chunk of length `H`:
 1. **Front Region [0, d):** These `d` positions correspond to "in-flight" actions already committed to the robot. They are clamped clean (`τp = 1`) and serve as inpainting conditioning for the new prediction.
 2. **Interior Region [d, H-d):** This is a linear ramp where noise levels decrease from clean to noisy as `p` increases. Actions in this region are candidates for emission.
 3. **Tail Region [H-d, H):** These `d` positions are pure noise (`τp = 0`), appended at the back of the chunk.
- **Training:**
 - At each batch, `d` is sampled uniformly (`d` ~ Uniform{1..., `d_max`}).
 - `τ*^d` is constructed.
 - The front `d` slots are filled with ground-truth actions and excluded from the loss calculation, mirroring inference-time inpainting.
 - The interior and tail positions are noised according to the schedule and contribute to the MSE loss.
 - Symmetric jitter is added to `τp` to absorb small deviations in `d`.
 - With a probability (e.g., 0.2), the model is also trained on a standard flow schedule (single `τ` across all positions, no mask), allowing it to denoise a full chunk from pure noise for buffer warm-start at episode beginning.
- **Compatibility:** This approach requires minimal architectural changes to existing DiT-style flow matching action heads: the AdaLN conditioning becomes per-position (`(γp, βp)` per chunk position) instead of shared. The VLM backbone and slow/fast feature paths remain unchanged, allowing fine-tuning from pretrained VLAs.
- **Inference Cycle:**
 1. **Warm-up:** At episode start, the buffer is initialized with a full chunk denoised from pure noise (using the standard-flow branch) and then re-noised to match `τ*^d` for the initial `d`.
 2. **Per-call Denoising:** In each call, one denoising step (Euler step) is applied. Per-position advances `Δτp` are chosen such that the schedule shifts right by `d` slots.
 3. **Action Emission:** Positions `[d, 2d)` reach `τ=1` and are emitted as clean actions.
 4. **Buffer Update:** The buffer then slides by `d` positions, and `d` fresh-noise slots (`τ=0`) are appended at the back. This maintains the staircase schedule. If `d` changes, the `Δτp` pattern adapts, pulling the buffer towards the new `τ*^d` over a few calls.

Together, these modifications enable πR2 to achieve reactive, real-time control by decoupling slow and fast modalities and robustly handling variable inference delays.

### 4. Main findings and results

The effectiveness of πR2 was evaluated through both simulation and real-world experiments, demonstrating improvements in reactivity and success rates over baseline methods.

#### 4.1. Simulation Experiments (Leap Cube Reorientation Task)

**Setup:** Experiments were conducted on the Leap Cube Reorientation task in MuJoCo Playground, a task requiring reactive control. Policies were state-based with a prediction chunk length `H=16`.

#### 4.1.1. Execution-horizon (h) sweep without inference delay

- **Objective:** To assess the trade-off between execution horizon `h` and performance, and to determine if πR2 ’s amortized denoising (1 NFE/call, `h=1`) matches the reactivity of standard flow with small `h`.
- **Results:** Standard flow performance decreased as `h` increased, confirming that executing more actions open-loop negatively impacts reactive tasks. πR2, while using only one denoising step per call and emitting one action (`h=1`), achieved performance comparable to standard flow with `h=1` or `h=2`. This indicates that more frequent updates with fresh observations are beneficial, and amortizing the denoising budget across calls does not sacrifice quality.

#### 4.1.2. Deployment under VLA-level inference delay

- **Objective:** To evaluate πR2 under realistic latency constraints, mirroring those of a GR00T-N1.7 VLA.
- **Baselines:** Naive-async, Train-Time RTC, and πR2 without asynchronous fast/slow channel processing.
- **Effective Delays:**
 - Naive-async and Train-Time RTC incurred an end-to-end inference delay of approximately `1.75 d0` (where `d0` is the cost of 4 denoising steps).
 - πR2 w/o async achieved `1.0 d0` delay.
 - πR2 w/ async (proprioception-reactive) achieved an effective proprioception delay of `0.25 d0` (i.e., `d=1` control tick), with the visual delay `d_vis` varying.
- **Results:** πR2 with asynchronous processing consistently outperformed all baselines across different `d0` settings (0.43, 0.42, 0.45 success rates vs. 0.33-0.36 for baselines). The performance margin widened with increasing delay. The ability of πR2 to maintain a low action delay (`d=1`) while gracefully degrading with increasing visual delay (`d_vis`) supports the hypothesis that vision provides coarse guidance, while up-to-date proprioception drives precise manipulation.

#### 4.2. Real World Experiments (xArm6 + XHand with GR00T-N1.7)

**Setup:** Experiments were conducted on a real xArm6 + XHand robotic platform, using a fine-tuned GR00T-N1.7 model. Control and demonstration collection occurred at 25 Hz (1 tick ≈ 40 ms). An RTX A5000 GPU was used for inference, resulting in baseline delays `d=4-5` ticks and πR2 delays `d=1-2` ticks. Proprioceptive state included arm/hand joint angles, torques, and fingertip forces.

**Tasks:** Four contact-rich, dexterous, and reactive manipulation tasks were used: Don’t Spill, Tidy Up Book, Insert Box, and Catch Book. These tasks demand high reactivity for success.

**Baselines:** Flow Synchronous, Flow Naive Async (dense, TE), and Flow Train-Time RTC. All were fine-tuned from GR00T-N1.7.

**Results:**
- **Overall Performance:** πR2 consistently outperformed all baselines across all tasks and metrics (success rate and partial-progress score). It achieved absolute improvements of approximately 20% to 30% in success rate on reactivity-critical tasks (Tidy Up Book, Insert Box, Catch Book). For instance, on Tidy Up Book, πR2 achieved a 12/20 success rate compared to 8/20 for Train-Time RTC.
- **Qualitative Observations:** πR2 produced smoother and more accurate motions, and demonstrated better failure recovery capabilities. Synchronous inference led to jittery motion and failures due to robot pauses. Naive asynchronous inference improved smoothness but sacrificed precision. Train-Time RTC, while the strongest baseline, struggled to recover from failures due to the bias from previously generated in-flight actions.
- **Reactivity Analysis (Fingertip Force Control):** Detailed analysis of fingertip contact force showed that πR2 modulated its grip based on live force feedback, stopping precisely (e.g., around 50 N on Tidy Up Book). In contrast, Train-Time RTC reacted late to contact, overshooting desired forces (e.g., to ~120 N on Tidy Up Book), often leading to task failure (e.g., dropping the book). This immediate force modulation capability highlights πR2 's enhanced reactivity to proprioceptive feedback. Similar patterns were observed across other tasks, demonstrating real-time response to contact events.

In conclusion, the experimental results demonstrate that πR2 successfully enhances the reactivity and real-time performance of large-scale VLA policies, leading to substantial improvements in task success rates in both simulated and real-world dynamic manipulation scenarios.

### 5. Significance and potential impact

The research presented in "πR2: Reactive Real-time Flow Policies" offers several significant contributions and has the potential for considerable impact within the field of robotic manipulation, particularly concerning the deployment of advanced foundation models.

**Core Significance:** The primary significance lies in enabling generalist manipulation policies, which rely on large pre-trained backbones, expressive multi-modal policy architectures, and action chunking, to operate in a reactive and real-time manner. Historically, these powerful models have been constrained by high inference latency and an inability to respond to dynamic sensory input mid-execution. πR2 directly addresses this fundamental trade-off, demonstrating that the benefits of generalizable, scalable imitation learning can be retained while overcoming limitations in dynamic control.

**Key Achievements and Implications:**
1. **Enhanced Reactivity:** By disentangling conditioning into fast (proprioception) and slow (vision-language) channels, πR2 allows the policy to react to fresh proprioceptive input at every control tick (e.g., every 40 ms on the xArm6), even with a large VLM backbone running asynchronously. This enables closed-loop control within action chunks, crucial for tasks requiring fine motor skills, precision, and contact-rich interactions.
2. **Real-time Performance with Large Models:** The latency-adaptive flow schedule and single-step action emission significantly reduce the effective per-call delay. πR2 achieves roughly 4× faster closed-loop replanning than baseline policies (25 Hz on an A5000 GPU with GR00T-N1.7), making high-frequency control possible with computationally intensive foundation models. This is critical for tasks like dynamically grasping objects, reorienting delicate items without spilling, or reacting to unexpected object movements.
3. **Robustness to Variable Latency:** The latency-adaptive flow schedule enables the policy to intrinsically adapt to varying hardware and network latencies. By treating in-flight actions as inpainting conditions and adjusting the noise schedule, it maintains smooth and temporally coherent motion, preventing jerky or discontinuous actions often seen in policies that do not account for variable delays.
4. **Compatibility and Practicality:** A notable aspect is the minimal modification required for existing architectures. πR2 can be fine-tuned from pretrained VLAs (such as GR00T-N1.7) without altering the backbone architecture. This facilitates straightforward integration into existing robotic learning pipelines and maximizes the utility of already-trained large models.
5. **Improved Task Success:** The experimental results, showing up to 23% improvement in simulation and 30% in real-world manipulation tasks over strong baselines, quantitatively demonstrate the practical value of πR2. This indicates that reactive, real-time control is a critical factor for successful deployment of robot foundation models in complex and dynamic environments.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Broader Adoption of Foundation Models:** By making large, generalist models more practical and reliable for dynamic tasks, πR2 could accelerate their adoption in real-world robotics applications, moving beyond static pick-and-place to more complex and interactive scenarios.
- **Advancements in Dexterous Manipulation:** The ability to react instantly to contact and proprioceptive feedback is transformative for dexterous manipulation, enabling robots to perform intricate tasks involving precise force control, object reorientation, and responsive grasping.
- **Future Research Directions:** This work opens avenues for future research, such as exploring dedicated architectural designs that further emphasize proprioceptive features or developing methods to address external sources of latency (e.g., communication delays).

In essence, πR2 represents a step towards realizing the full potential of robot foundation models by equipping them with the real-time reactive capabilities necessary to operate robustly and effectively in the physical world.
