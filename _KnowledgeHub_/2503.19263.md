# DWIM: Towards Tool-aware Visual Reasoning via Discrepancy-aware Workflow Generation & Instruct-Masking Tuning

> [!summary] Summary
> DWIM enables Large Language Model (LLM) agents to perform tool-aware visual reasoning by addressing the challenges of noisy perception tools and data scarcity. The framework achieves state-of-the-art performance with an average absolute improvement of over 9.73% on various visual reasoning datasets and demonstrates a 10% lower failure rate due to tool errors in human evaluation compared to frozen LLMs.

## Problem

- Existing compositional visual reasoning methods rely on frozen LLMs that lack deep understanding of specific visual tools' capabilities and limitations, hindering optimal workflow generation.
- Fine-tuning LLMs for visual reasoning is challenging due to data scarcity and the inherent inaccuracies of perception tools, which lead to noisy training data unsuitable for standard fine-tuning.
- Current approaches inefficiently utilize training data by discarding workflows that fail due to tool errors, preventing models from learning how to recover from imperfections.

## Method

- An =="Auto-Exploring Agentic Framework"== that, during training workflow generation, leverages ground truth to enable the LLM to identify factual discrepancies from tool feedback and generate =="Rethink" actions== to explore alternative tool usages.
- A =="Discrepancy-aware Training Workflow Generation"== method that dynamically refines actions when tool errors are detected, increasing the success rate of workflows and generating a larger, more effective dataset.
- =="Instruct-Masking Fine-tuning"==, a semantic masking strategy that flags actions as effective or ineffective; it masks effective actions for reproduction while unmasking ineffective actions and "Rethink" descriptions to explicitly teach the model error recovery.

## Results

- Achieved state-of-the-art performance with an average absolute improvement of over **9.73%** across six diverse visual reasoning datasets (e.g., GQA, SugarCREPE, RefCOCO) and exhibited strong zero-shot cross-dataset generalization with a **4.8%** improvement over baselines.
- Reduced average tool utilization per successful sample to 1.74, significantly lower than baselines (2.49-3.26), indicating more precise and effective tool usage, and showed improved performance across various open-source LLM backbones (>**12.4%** absolute gain).
- Ablation studies confirmed that Discrepancy-aware Workflow Generation boosted data utilization by ~**17%** and Instruct-Masking Fine-tuning yielded up to **16.3%** absolute performance gain over naive SFT, validating the efficacy of both core components.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LLMs can be trained to intelligently assess the effectiveness of visual perception tools, detect discrepancies in their outputs, and self-correct through iterative reasoning, rather than merely chaining tools.
> - Generating training data that explicitly accounts for tool imperfections and recovery mechanisms significantly improves an LLM's robustness and tool awareness in complex visual environments.
> - A fine-tuning approach that selectively reinforces successful actions while also exposing the model to failures and recovery strategies (via instruct-masking) leads to more resilient and efficient tool utilization.
