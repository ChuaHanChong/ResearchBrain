# DWIM: Towards Tool-aware Visual Reasoning via Discrepancy-aware Workflow Generation & Instruct-Masking Tuning

> [!summary] Summary
> DWIM enables Large Language Model (LLM) agents to perform tool-aware visual reasoning by addressing the challenges of noisy perception tools and data scarcity. The framework achieves state-of-the-art performance with an average absolute improvement of over 9.73% on various visual reasoning datasets and demonstrates a 10% lower failure rate due to tool errors in human evaluation compared to frozen LLMs.

## Problem

- Existing compositional visual reasoning methods rely on frozen LLMs that lack deep understanding of specific visual tools' capabilities and limitations, hindering optimal workflow generation.
- Fine-tuning LLMs for visual reasoning is challenging due to data scarcity and the inherent inaccuracies of perception tools, which lead to noisy training data unsuitable for standard fine-tuning.
- Current approaches inefficiently utilize training data by discarding workflows that fail due to tool errors, preventing models from learning how to recover from imperfections.

## Method

- An =="Auto-Exploring Agentic Framework"== that, during training workflow generation, leverages ground truth to enable the LLM to identify factual discrepancies from tool feedback and generate =="Rethink" actions== to explore alternative tool usages.
- A =="Discrepancy-aware Training Workflow Generation"== method that dynamically refines actions when tool errors are detected, increasing the success rate of workflows and generating a larger, more effective dataset.
- =="Instruct-Masking Fine-tuning"==, a semantic masking strategy that flags actions as effective or ineffective; it masks effective actions for reproduction while unmasking ineffective actions and "Rethink" descriptions to explicitly teach the model error recovery.

## Results

- Achieved state-of-the-art performance with an average absolute improvement of over **9.73%** across six diverse visual reasoning datasets (e.g., GQA, SugarCREPE, RefCOCO) and exhibited strong zero-shot cross-dataset generalization with a **4.8%** improvement over baselines.
- Reduced average tool utilization per successful sample to 1.74, significantly lower than baselines (2.49-3.26), indicating more precise and effective tool usage, and showed improved performance across various open-source LLM backbones (>**12.4%** absolute gain).
- Ablation studies confirmed that Discrepancy-aware Workflow Generation boosted data utilization by ~**17%** and Instruct-Masking Fine-tuning yielded up to **16.3%** absolute performance gain over naive SFT, validating the efficacy of both core components.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LLMs can be trained to intelligently assess the effectiveness of visual perception tools, detect discrepancies in their outputs, and self-correct through iterative reasoning, rather than merely chaining tools.
> - Generating training data that explicitly accounts for tool imperfections and recovery mechanisms significantly improves an LLM's robustness and tool awareness in complex visual environments.
> - A fine-tuning approach that selectively reinforces successful actions while also exposing the model to failures and recovery strategies (via instruct-masking) leads to more resilient and efficient tool utilization.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Visual Reasoning (VR) stands as a cornerstone of artificial intelligence, enabling machines to interpret and analyze visual information akin to human cognitive processes. It underpins a wide array of applications, from multimodal learning and autonomous driving to medical image analysis. Despite significant strides, VR remains inherently challenging due to the complexity of visual scenes, the vast diversity of reasoning tasks (e.g., commonsense reasoning, visual question answering, grounding), and the need for robust generalization capabilities.

Historically, VR solutions often relied on **end-to-end (E2E) vision-language models**. While powerful, these models demand immense annotated datasets and considerable computational resources, limiting their scalability and efficiency, particularly for novel or complex tasks.

In recent years, a paradigm shift has occurred towards **compositional approaches**, also known as workflow-based or tool-aware methods. These methods leverage the impressive reasoning abilities of Large Language Models (LLMs) by integrating them with specialized perception tools. LLMs act as "planners" or "reasoners," decomposing complex visual tasks into smaller, manageable sub-tasks and generating structured workflows or code to utilize various tools (e.g., object detectors, image captioning models, external knowledge bases). Prominent examples in this space include VisProg, ViperGPT, Chameleon, IdealGPT, HYDRA, and VisRep. These approaches have shown promise in improving performance by breaking down complex problems and enhancing transparency.

However, the existing compositional VR landscape faces critical limitations, which DWIM specifically aims to address:

1. **Limited Tool Awareness in Frozen LLMs:** Most compositional methods rely on *frozen, pre-trained LLMs* (e.g., GPT variants) without further fine-tuning for the visual domain. While these LLMs possess strong general reasoning, they are not explicitly trained to deeply understand the capabilities and limitations of specific visual tools, nor to generate optimal tool-utilization workflows. Their effectiveness largely depends on in-context learning examples, which can quickly saturate and offer diminishing returns.
2. **Challenges in Fine-tuning LLMs for VR:** Unlike other domains (e.g., mathematical reasoning, text-based games) where LLMs can be effectively fine-tuned on large, often synthetically generated, and reliable datasets, VR presents unique hurdles:
 - **Data Scarcity:** Generating large datasets of high-quality, annotated visual reasoning workflows is difficult and expensive.
 - **Imperfect Tools and Noisy Workflows:** Crucially, perception tools in VR (often deep learning models) are prone to inaccuracies and factual errors. A logically sound workflow might fail in practice due to a tool's misstep, leading to "noisy" training data where correct reasoning is obscured by execution errors. Existing fine-tuning methods (like supervised fine-tuning or reinforcement learning) are not well-equipped to handle such noise, potentially leading the model to learn ineffective tool usage or discard valuable data.
 - **Single-Turn vs. Multi-Turn Reasoning:** Many prior works operate in a single-turn fashion, limiting the LLM's ability to iteratively refine its actions based on environmental feedback, which is vital for complex reasoning.

DWIM directly positions itself to overcome these challenges. It aims to develop a tool-aware LLM agent specifically for VR by tackling the issues of efficient, reliable workflow generation from imperfect tools and effective fine-tuning on noisy workflow data. By introducing "discrepancy-aware workflow generation" and "instruct-masking fine-tuning," DWIM innovates on how LLMs learn to interact with visual environments, moving beyond merely chaining tools to intelligently understanding their operational effectiveness and recovering from their failures. This work represents a significant step towards creating more robust, adaptable, and human-like visual reasoning agents that can effectively navigate the complexities and uncertainties of the real visual world.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind DWIM stems from the recognized performance bottleneck in existing compositional visual reasoning (VR) approaches: the inherent lack of tool awareness in frozen Large Language Models (LLMs) when applied to the visual domain. While LLMs excel at general reasoning and planning, their effectiveness in VR is significantly hampered by several factors unique to visual tasks and perception tools.

The paper articulates several key objectives to address this overarching problem:

1. **Enhance LLM Tool Awareness for Visual Reasoning:** The primary objective is to make LLMs "tool-aware," enabling them to not only plan sequences of tool usage but also to deeply understand each tool's capabilities, anticipate potential failures, and adapt their strategies accordingly. This goes beyond mere integration to intelligent utilization.
2. **Develop an Efficient and Robust Workflow Generation Method for VR:** Traditional workflow generation methods, which rely on existing datasets or discard workflows that yield incorrect outcomes, are inefficient and unsuitable for VR. They fail to account for the common inaccuracies of perception tools, leading to a shortage of viable training data. DWIM aims to create a method that can:
 - **Assess tool usage effectiveness in real-time:** Identify when a tool provides inaccurate or unexpected feedback.
 - **Refine actions with alternatives:** When discrepancies are detected, guide the LLM to generate alternative or corrective actions.
 - **Increase workflow success rates and data utilization:** By dynamically exploring and refining workflows, generate a larger pool of successful and effective workflows suitable for training, thereby optimizing the use of valuable training data.
3. **Design a Fine-tuning Strategy Robust to Noisy VR Workflows:** Standard supervised fine-tuning (SFT) or reinforcement learning (RL) methods, when applied naively to VR, can inadvertently reinforce ineffective tool usage or struggle with learning from workflows corrupted by tool errors. The objective is to devise a fine-tuning mechanism that:
 - **Learns effectively from noisy data:** Identify and focus on effective actions within a workflow, rather than blindly cloning entire sequences that might contain errors.
 - **Guides the model to recognize tool failures:** Expose the model to instances where tools fail or provide incorrect information, allowing it to learn from these "negative" examples.
 - **Enhance task planning and practical solution generation:** Improve the LLM's ability to plan tool usage more accurately and generate solutions that are practical and resilient to real-world tool imperfections.

In essence, DWIM is motivated by the desire to bridge the gap between the powerful symbolic reasoning capabilities of LLMs and the messy, uncertain reality of visual perception. By addressing the challenges of data scarcity and tool unreliability specific to VR, the authors aim to unlock the full potential of compositional approaches, leading to more intelligent, robust, and human-like visual reasoning agents that can operate effectively with less human intervention and greater autonomy.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DWIM introduces a novel two-pronged approach to tackle the challenges of tool-aware visual reasoning: (i) **Discrepancy-aware training Workflow generation** and (ii) **Instruct-Masking fine-tuning**. These two components work synergistically to improve LLM tool awareness and robustness in VR.

#### 3.1. Task Formulation

The paper formalizes a visual reasoning task where an agentic LLM (π_θ) processes a visual input (v) and a textual query (q) to produce an answer (y). The core idea is to generate a step-by-step workflow (ω = {ω_1:T}) of actions. Each action (ω_t) at step *t* interacts with the environment, producing feedback (ϕ(ω_t)), which then updates the environmental context (e_t). Unlike single-turn systems, DWIM emphasizes a multi-turn approach, where the LLM's action generation is conditioned on the accumulated environmental feedback (e_t-1), enabling incremental reasoning. The initial environment (e_0) includes the visual-textual query pair and documentation for the tool library (δ). The objective is to optimize the LLM's parameters (θ) to accurately generate these workflows.

#### 3.2. Workflow Generation with Agentic Framework (Discrepancy-aware)

DWIM utilizes an "Auto-Exploring Agentic Framework," inspired by prior work like ReAct and CodeAct, but enhanced for VR. This framework is characterized by its dynamic, multi-turn interaction capabilities, diverging from traditional one-step planning. The LLM agent can generate three types of actions:
- **〈Thought〉:** For reasoning, analyzing environmental information, and determining the next optimal step or refining a previous one.
- **〈Code〉:** For execution, involving the use of perception tools (e.g., BLIP2, GroundingDINO) via Python APIs to gather additional visual information. This updates the environmental feedback (e_t).
- **〈Done〉:** To conclude the task once the query is answered.

The critical innovation here is **Discrepancy-aware Training Workflow Generation**. During the training workflow generation phase, the ground truth answer (*y*) is provided to the LLM as prior knowledge. This allows the LLM to:
1. **Recognize Discrepancies:** By comparing the environmental feedback (e_t) from tool execution with the expected outcome (informed by *y*), the LLM can identify factual errors or unexpected information, even if the executed action was logically sound.
2. **Generate "Rethink" Actions:** If a discrepancy is detected, the LLM generates a special "Rethink" 〈Thought〉 action (ω_Rethink). This action provides a natural language description of the discrepancy and suggests an alternative or refined action for the subsequent step. This dynamic refinement process allows the agent to explore different tool usages and pathways.
3. **Optimize Data Utilization:** This iterative refinement increases the success rate of workflow generation, as the agent can recover from tool errors and find valid solutions. Consequently, it yields a larger dataset (D_ω) of effective and viable workflows suitable for training, overcoming the data scarcity issue prevalent in VR.

This approach is crucial because, unlike tools in math or text domains that are often deterministic, visual perception tools are inherently imperfect. Discrepancy-aware generation ensures that the LLM learns from these imperfections rather than being misled by them, generating a more robust and cleaner training dataset.

#### 3.3. Instruct-Masking Fine-tuning

After collecting workflows via the discrepancy-aware generation process, DWIM employs a novel fine-tuning method called **Instruct-Masking**. This method addresses the challenge of training on potentially noisy workflows by selectively learning from effective actions.
1. **Action Flagging:** Based on the discrepancy-aware recognition during workflow generation, each action (ω_t) in a collected workflow is flagged as either effective (κ_t = 1) or ineffective (κ_t = 0). For example, tool usages that lead to factual errors or directly precede a "Rethink" action are flagged as ineffective.
2. **Semantic Masking:** Unlike token-level masking in models like BERT, Instruct-Masking operates at a *semantic level*. Specifically, *effective actions* (e.g., successful tool usage) are masked.
3. **Instruction Tuning:** The LLM is then *instructed* to reproduce these masked effective actions iteratively. The loss is computed between the original and the LLM-generated action.
4. **Learning from Failures:** Crucially, *ineffective tool usages* and their corresponding "Rethink" descriptions (which highlight the discrepancy and refinement thought) are *unmasked*. This exposes the model to examples of tool failures and the reasoning processes for recovery.
5. **Behavioral Cloning:** The model is fine-tuned using a reward-weighted negative log-likelihood loss, essentially applying behavioral cloning. This encourages the LLM to clone only effective actions and planning strategies, thereby enhancing tool awareness and preventing it from learning from noisy or erroneous steps.

By combining discrepancy-aware workflow generation with instruct-masking fine-tuning, DWIM creates a powerful feedback loop. The generation process yields better training data by handling tool imperfections, and the fine-tuning process effectively learns from this data, making the LLM more adept at tool selection, usage, and error recovery in the complex visual reasoning domain.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted in the paper demonstrate that DWIM achieves state-of-the-art performance across various visual reasoning (VR) tasks and exhibits strong generalization capabilities. The key findings are summarized below:

1. **State-of-the-Art Performance Across Diverse VR Tasks:**
 - DWIM consistently outperforms existing state-of-the-art compositional VR methods (VisRep, HYDRA) and several end-to-end (E2E) models across six distinct VR datasets: GQA (VCR), OKVQA (EKVQA), SugarCREPE (VLCU), NaturalBench (VASA), RefCOCO (GD), and TallyQA (CCQ).
 - It achieves an average absolute improvement of over **9.73%** compared to existing compositional approaches. For instance, on GQA, DWIM scores 69.3% compared to HYDRA's 62.3%; on SugarCREPE, it reaches 74.6% versus VisRep's 58.2%; and on RefCOCO, it achieves 82.7%, significantly higher than the next best 63.0%.
 - Notably, DWIM achieves these results with **0-shot in-context learning examples** during evaluation, unlike baselines that rely on 10-shot examples. This highlights its superior ability to learn effectively during training and generalize.

2. **Strong Generalization Ability:**
 - When trained on the GQA dataset and tested on other unseen datasets (OKVQA, A-OKVQA, SugarCREPE, Winoground) without further training, DWIM demonstrates robust zero-shot cross-dataset generalization.
 - It achieves an approximate **4.8% improvement** over HYDRA and VisRep in generalization tasks, showcasing its ability to transfer learned tool awareness and reasoning patterns to new problem settings.

3. **Enhanced Tool Awareness and Efficiency:**
 - **Reduced Dependency on Human Prompt Engineering:** Experiments show that while frozen LLMs improve with more in-context learning examples, their performance quickly saturates. DWIM, by contrast, significantly outperforms these frozen models, even with minimal training data, implying that fine-tuning with its proposed methods is more effective than extensive prompt engineering.
 - **Effectiveness with Complete Tool Libraries:** When evaluated using a complete tool library (instead of task-specific ones), DWIM shows significantly smaller performance drops compared to other methods. This indicates that DWIM is more adept at selecting and utilizing the optimal tools from a larger, more complex selection space, demonstrating superior tool awareness.
 - **Improved Tool Utilization Efficiency:** DWIM exhibits a lower average tool utilization per successful sample (1.74) compared to Frozen few-shot (2.87), HYDRA (3.26), and VisRep (2.49). This signifies that DWIM uses tools more precisely and effectively, achieving correct answers with fewer unnecessary actions.
 - **Human Evaluation of Tool Awareness (Supplementary):** Human evaluations reveal that DWIM-generated workflows have a 10% lower failure rate due to tool errors and are less likely to be logically incorrect compared to frozen LLMs, further confirming its enhanced understanding and usage of tools.

4. **Ablation Studies Validate Key Components:**
 - **Discrepancy-aware Training Workflow Generation:** This component is crucial. It boosts "data utilization" (the proportion of training data points generating correct workflows) by **17.4% on GQA and 17.1% on SugarCREPE**. This translates to absolute performance gains of **11.3% on GQA and 13.9% on SugarCREPE**, proving its effectiveness in generating a larger and more viable training dataset.
 - **Instruct-Masking Fine-tuning:** This method also shows significant contributions. When applied to workflows generated by a standard method, it yields a 4.3% absolute performance improvement on GQA and 2.9% on SugarCREPE over naive SFT. When combined with discrepancy-aware generation, the gains are even more pronounced (**14.5% on GQA and 16.3% on SugarCREPE**). Furthermore, Instruct-Masking outperforms variants like Random-Masking and Masking-W-Rethink, confirming its targeted approach to learning effective actions while understanding failures.

5. **Generality Across LLM Backbones:**
 - DWIM's effectiveness is not limited to a single LLM. It consistently improves the absolute performance of various open-source models (LLaMa-3.1-8B, Mistral-v0.2/0.3-7B, Qwen2.5 variants) by over **12.4%**, demonstrating its broad applicability and robustness as a general enhancement method for tool-aware LLMs.

In summary, DWIM's empirical results strongly support its claim of providing a more generalizable and robust solution for compositional visual reasoning. Its ability to effectively learn from noisy visual environments, optimize data utilization, and enhance LLM tool awareness leads to significant performance improvements across the board.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in DWIM offers several significant contributions and holds substantial potential impact across the fields of artificial intelligence, computer vision, and natural language processing.

1. **Advancing Tool-Aware LLMs for Visual Reasoning:** DWIM directly addresses a critical and under-tackled problem: the lack of robust tool awareness in Large Language Models when applied to complex visual reasoning tasks, especially when confronting imperfect, real-world perception tools. By enabling LLMs to intelligently assess tool usage, detect discrepancies, and refine their actions, DWIM pushes the frontier of truly "tool-aware" AI agents. This is a crucial step toward building more autonomous and intelligent systems that can reason and act effectively in dynamic visual environments.

2. **Efficient and Robust Training Data Generation:** The proposed "discrepancy-aware training workflow generation" method is a groundbreaking innovation. It tackles the significant challenges of data scarcity and noise in VR by:
 - **Maximizing Data Utilization:** Instead of discarding workflows that fail due to tool errors (a common practice), DWIM learns to recover from these failures, converting what would be wasted data into valuable training examples. This is paramount for domains where high-quality annotated workflows are expensive to obtain.
 - **Generating Cleaner, More Effective Training Data:** By enabling the LLM to self-correct and explore alternative tool usages when discrepancies arise, the generated workflows are inherently more robust and representative of successful reasoning paths, even with imperfect tools. This reduces the need for extensive human curation of workflows.

3. **Novel and Effective Fine-tuning Strategy:** The "instruct-masking fine-tuning" method provides a powerful paradigm for learning from noisy, multi-modal data. By selectively masking effective actions and explicitly exposing the model to ineffective actions and "rethink" processes, it teaches the LLM not just *what to do*, but also *what not to do* and *how to recover from errors*. This is a more sophisticated learning mechanism than traditional supervised fine-tuning and has broad implications for training agents in environments with uncertain feedback.

4. **Reduced Reliance on Human Engineering:** DWIM significantly lessens the dependence on tedious and often saturated human prompt engineering and in-context learning examples. By automating the generation of effective training data and developing a robust fine-tuning process, it shifts the burden from human annotators and engineers to the learning capabilities of the model itself. This scalability is essential for deploying AI agents in complex, open-ended scenarios.

5. **Broad Applicability and Generalizability:** The demonstrated state-of-the-art performance across a diverse range of visual reasoning tasks (VCR, EKVQA, VLCU, VASA, GD, CCQ), coupled with strong zero-shot generalization to unseen datasets and compatibility with various LLM backbones, highlights the method's versatility. This suggests DWIM could serve as a foundational component for a wide array of future multimodal AI systems.

6. **Impact on Real-World Applications:** The involvement of Building 4.0 CRC and DARPA funding indicates a strong orientation towards practical impact. DWIM's advancements could directly benefit critical applications such as:
 - **Autonomous Systems:** Improved visual reasoning for autonomous vehicles, drones, and robots, enabling them to make better decisions in dynamic and uncertain environments.
 - **Medical Imaging:** More accurate interpretation and diagnosis from medical images, assisting clinicians.
 - **Industrial Automation:** Enhancing complex visual inspection, quality control, and operational planning in manufacturing and construction.
 - **Human-Computer Interaction:** Enabling more intuitive and intelligent visual assistants that understand complex visual queries.

In conclusion, DWIM represents a significant step forward in making LLM-based agents more capable, robust, and autonomous in visual reasoning. By cleverly addressing the unique challenges of tool imperfections and data efficiency in multimodal learning, it provides a robust framework that could profoundly impact the development of next-generation AI systems capable of human-like visual understanding and decision-making.
