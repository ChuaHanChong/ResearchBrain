# RL-GSBridge: 3D Gaussian Splatting Based Real2Sim2Real Method for Robotic Manipulation Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Shanghai Jiao Tong University and collaborators introduce RL-GSBridge, a real-to-sim-to-real framework utilizing 3D Gaussian Splatting (3D GS) with a novel soft mesh binding mechanism to create highly realistic simulation environments. This enables zero-shot transfer of deep reinforcement learning policies for robotic manipulation tasks to real robots, achieving an average success rate drop of only 6.6% during sim-to-real transfer across various grasping scenarios and demonstrating superior rendering quality over existing methods.

## Problem

- The 'sim-to-real gap' poses a major challenge for transferring policies learned in simulated environments to real-world robotic systems, limiting practical applications of reinforcement learning.
- Traditional sim-to-real methods often require extensive augmented data or large, complex models, leading to inefficiency and high computational demands for specific robotic tasks.
- Visual and geometric inconsistencies between simulators and the real world can lead to significant policy transfer failures in vision-based robotic manipulation tasks.

## Method

- RL-GSBridge leverages 3D Gaussian Splatting to reconstruct real-world scenes into high-fidelity virtual environments, effectively bridging the visual perception gap.
- A novel 'soft mesh binding' method is introduced for 3D GS, allowing Gaussian units to 'float' along mesh normals for increased optimization flexibility and improved rendering quality, while maintaining geometric accuracy.
- The framework integrates physics dynamics-based GS editing, synchronizing real-time object pose changes from a physics simulator (Pybullet) with the rendered 3D GS scene for dynamic visual consistency during RL training.

## Results

- Achieved robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer for grasping tasks, demonstrating an average success rate drop of only 6.6% across diverse objects and backgrounds, significantly outperforming an RL-sim baseline (which saw an 80% drop).
- Successfully enabled a pick-and-place task with a 68.75% success rate in simulation and 71.87% in the real world, indicating consistent policy performance.
- The soft mesh binding method consistently outperformed hard mesh binding (GaMeS) in rendering quality across metrics, e.g., improving SSIM from 0.894 to 0.989 and PSNR from 26.53 dB to 35.46 dB for a 'Banana' object.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - High visual fidelity in simulation, achieved through 3D Gaussian Splatting, is critical for enabling reliable zero-shot sim-to-real transfer in vision-based robotic manipulation.
> - The proposed soft mesh binding for 3D GS enhances rendering quality and provides greater flexibility for Gaussian optimization compared to rigid mesh-bound methods, evidenced by superior SSIM, PSNR, and LPIPS scores.
> - Integrating the editable 3D GS representation with a physics simulator allows for real-time dynamic updates of the visual scene, creating a consistent and effective training environment for deep reinforcement learning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a significant challenge in robotics: the "sim-to-real gap," which refers to the difficulty of transferring policies learned in simulated environments to real-world robotic systems. This gap limits the practical application of reinforcement learning (RL) in robotics, despite the advantages of simulation for safety, cost, and efficiency.

Existing Sim-to-Real methods often involve techniques such as domain randomization, which expands training data distribution in simulation to cover real-world variations, or domain adaptation, which aims to find a unified feature space between simulation and reality. Other approaches include meta-learning for adaptation to new tasks and distillation learning for transferring knowledge from expert networks. However, the authors note that many of these methods either require extensive augmented data or large, generalized learning models, leading to inefficiency for specific tasks.

The emergence of radiance field reconstruction methods, particularly Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) and 3D Gaussian Splatting (3D GS), has opened new avenues for creating visually realistic simulated environments. NeRF, while capable of high-fidelity novel view synthesis, is often constrained by slow training and rendering speeds. 3D Gaussian Splatting, introduced more recently, offers explicit scene representation, significantly faster training, and real-time rendering capabilities, making it potentially more suitable for dynamic robotic applications.

This research integrates 3D Gaussian Splatting into the deep reinforcement learning (DRL) simulation pipeline to create a "virtual-reality" platform. This differentiates it from prior works like NeRF2Real, which uses NeRF to convert background meshes into a simulator, and other 3D GS applications focusing on drone navigation (Quach et al.) or general manipulation (ManiGaussian). RL-GSBridge specifically leverages the explicit and editable nature of 3D GS for both foreground objects and backgrounds, with a focus on robotic arm manipulation tasks that involve complex interactions. The introduction of a novel soft mesh binding mechanism for GS further positions this work within the evolving landscape of radiance field applications for robotics, aiming to enhance visual realism and geometric accuracy simultaneously.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel real-to-sim-to-real framework, termed RL-GSBridge, that facilitates zero-shot transfer of vision-based deep reinforcement learning policies from simulation to real-world robotic manipulation tasks. The framework aims to overcome the sim-to-real gap by constructing highly realistic simulation environments using 3D Gaussian Splatting.

The motivation for this work stems from several key challenges and opportunities:

- **Inefficiency of Current Sim-to-Real Methods**: Traditional Sim-to-Real approaches often demand large quantities of augmented data or large, complex learning models to generalize across the simulation and reality domains. This inefficiency increases the burden during the training phase, especially for specific robotic tasks. The authors seek a framework that achieves satisfactory Sim-to-Real performance without such extensive computational or data requirements.
- **Bridging the Visual Perception Gap**: For vision-based robot tasks, inconsistencies between visual perception in the simulator and the physical contact geometry in the real world can lead to significant policy transfer failures. Achieving realistic rendering results while maintaining an accurate geometric representation of objects is crucial to avoid "illusions" that hinder policy performance. The motivation is to create a training environment where visual observations closely match those of the real world.
- **Leveraging Advances in Radiance Field Reconstruction**: The recent progress in radiance field-based methods, particularly 3D Gaussian Splatting, offers a promising direction for constructing highly realistic 3D scenes from multi-view images. This technology enables the creation of visually rich environments using only consumer-grade cameras, which is more accessible and less costly than traditional 3D scanning or CAD modeling techniques. The authors are motivated to explore how these high-fidelity reconstruction capabilities can be integrated into the conventional RL simulation pipeline.
- **Enhancing Flexibility and Accuracy of GS Models**: Existing mesh-based Gaussian Splatting methods, such as GaMeS, ensure geometric accuracy by binding Gaussians to mesh surfaces but might limit the flexibility of Gaussian optimization, potentially introducing rendering defects if the mesh model is not perfectly accurate. A motivation is to improve upon these methods by allowing more flexibility while preserving geometric integrity, thereby enhancing rendering quality and potentially enabling the handling of non-rigid objects.
- **Integrating Physics Dynamics with Realistic Rendering**: Robotic manipulation inherently involves physical interactions. The simulation environment must accurately reflect both the visual and dynamic aspects of these interactions. Therefore, a key motivation is to synchronize the high-fidelity visual rendering of 3D GS with the real-time physical dynamics from a simulator, ensuring that the visual scene updates in conjunction with object movements and contacts.

Through these objectives, the authors aim to provide a robust, efficient, and visually realistic simulation platform that enables the training of effective robotic manipulation policies directly transferable to the real world without further fine-tuning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The RL-GSBridge framework operates in two primary stages: Real2Sim and Sim2Real, designed to create a realistic simulation environment and then leverage it for zero-shot policy transfer.

**A. Real2Sim: Building Simulator with Soft Mesh Binding GS**

This stage focuses on reconstructing real-world scenarios into a high-fidelity virtual environment.
1. **Real-world Data Preparation**:
 - **Data Collection**: Monocular cameras or mobile phones are used to capture 1-2 minute videos of target objects on a desktop-level operating platform.
 - **Image Processing**: Approximately 200 keyframes are extracted from the videos. COLMAP is employed to estimate intrinsic and extrinsic camera parameters for these frames.
 - **Object Segmentation**: An off-the-shelf segmenter, SAM-track, is utilized to efficiently extract target objects from the images.
 - **Mesh Model Generation**: The open-source package openMVS is used to generate geometrically accurate mesh models, which serve as prior geometric information for the Gaussian Splatting process.

2. **Real2Sim Modeling by Soft Mesh Binding GS**:
 - **Baseline (GaMeS) Review**: Traditional 3D Gaussian Splatting (Vanilla GS) optimizes parameters (position, rotation, scale, opacity, color) of individual Gaussian units. GaMeS extends this by constraining Gaussians within triangular faces of a mesh model, binding their mean vectors as convex combinations of mesh vertices. While this ensures geometric accuracy, the authors identify it as "hard mesh binding" due to its rigidity, which can limit Gaussian unit optimization if the mesh is imprecise, potentially introducing rendering artifacts.
 - **Proposed Soft Mesh Binding**: To address the limitations of hard binding, the authors introduce a soft mesh binding method. This approach modifies the mean vector (μ_i) of each Gaussian unit (g_i) by adding a component along the normal vector (v_n) of the mesh surface, alongside the existing convex combination of mesh vertices (v1, v2, v3):
 μ_i = α_i1 * v1 + α_i2 * v2 + α_i3 * v3 + α_in * v_n
 Here, α_i1, α_i2, α_i3, and α_in are learnable weight parameters, with α_in constrained within the range of [-1, 1]. This modification allows Gaussian units to "float" within a certain range along the normal, offering greater flexibility during optimization. This flexibility is designed to enhance rendering quality, enable smoother Gaussian distribution on object surfaces, and provide tolerance for mesh inaccuracies, while still maintaining the link to the mesh geometry for controllable editing. The method also demonstrates potential for representing non-rigid objects.

3. **Physic Dynamics-Based GS Editing**:
 - **Alignment**: Initial visual GS models ({G_k}) and geometric mesh models ({M_k}) are aligned within the simulator using RANSAC plane regression and manual adjustments.
 - **Dynamic Synchronization**: The system reads real-time pose changes (rotation quaternion q_k and homogeneous transformation matrix T_k) of each object from the physics simulator.
 - **Gaussian Parameter Update**: For each Gaussian unit (g_i_k) in an object's GS model ({G_k}), its position (μ_i_k) and rotation (r_i_k) parameters are updated based on the simulator's pose information:
 μ_i_k = T_k * μ_i_k
 r_i_k = q_k * r_i_k
 For local non-rigid transformations, Gaussians are updated based on the soft mesh binding equation.
 - **Scene Rendering**: All updated Gaussian sets are concatenated to form the complete scene Gaussian model (G_scene). Rasterization is then used to render G_scene, generating visually synchronized views that reflect the physical interactions in the simulator.

**B. Sim2Real: Training in Simulation and Zero-Shot Transfer**

This stage involves policy learning in the GS-rendered simulation environment and direct deployment to a real robot.
1. **Reinforcement Learning Setup**:
 - **Simulator**: Pybullet is used as the underlying physics simulation platform.
 - **RL Algorithm**: Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) is employed for policy learning. To address the challenge of sparse rewards in complex robotic tasks, the authors introduce a variation called SACwB (SAC with Baseline), inspired by DDPGwB. SACwB integrates a lightly designed baseline controller to guide the learning process, which helps accelerate convergence and avoid exploring ineffective action spaces without requiring complex reward engineering.
 - **SACwB Mechanics**: The target Q-value (y_t) in SACwB's critic loss is modified to take the maximum of the Q-values evaluated for both the actor's proposed action and the baseline controller's action. An additional behavior cloning loss (L_bc) supervises the actor network's mean action outputs using the baseline controller's actions, with a decaying probability (λ) that gradually reduces over training.

2. **Policy Training Pipeline**:
 - **Inputs**: The RL agent receives first-person perspective RGB images rendered by the physics dynamics-based GS renderer, along with the proprioceptive state of the robotic arm.
 - **Action Execution**: The RL algorithm outputs actions to the Pybullet simulator, which in turn provides state feedback for actor-critic learning.
 - **Visual Fidelity Enhancement**: To improve robustness and bridge the sim-to-real gap, random noise is added to the rendered GS images, and certain image attribute parameters are randomly varied during training.

3. **Zero-Shot Real-World Deployment**:
 - After training, the actor network is directly deployed on a physical KUKA iiwa robot arm without any fine-tuning.
 - The real robot's eye-in-hand camera provides real-world observations as visual input to the trained policy.
 - The policy then outputs end-effector positions and gripper states as executable actions for the robot.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate the efficacy of RL-GSBridge in achieving zero-shot sim-to-real transfer for robotic manipulation tasks.

**A. Grasping Task Results**

1. **Comparison with RL-sim Baseline**:
 - **Setup**: The RL-GSBridge framework was compared against a baseline method, RL-sim, where policies were trained using images rendered from standard mesh models shaded within the PyBullet simulator. Experiments involved grasping a "Small cube" and a "Bear" on a foam pad background.
 - **Results (Table I)**: RL-sim exhibited a substantial drop in success rates when transferred from simulation to the real world (an average decrease of 80% for the two objects), indicating a significant sim-to-real gap due to visual discrepancies. In contrast, RL-GSBridge maintained a consistent and high success rate across both simulated and real environments. For the "Small cube," the success rate was 96.88% in both sim and real. For the "Bear," the real-world success rate (100.00%) surpassed the simulation rate (87.50%), suggesting that the realistic GS rendering potentially compensated for suboptimal physics simulation of the bear's soft, unstructured material.

2. **Performance Across Various Scenarios**:
 - **Setup**: RL-GSBridge was tested on diverse objects ("Cake," "Banana," "Small cube," "Bear") and two different desktop backgrounds ("Foam Pad" and "Tablecloth") for the grasping task.
 - **Results (Table II)**: The framework demonstrated robustness, experiencing an average success rate drop of only 6.6% during sim-to-real transfer across all complex scenarios. This indicates effective bridging of the perception gap. A notable decrease was observed for grasping the "Bear" on a "Tablecloth" background (87.50% in sim to 75.00% in real). This was attributed to similar texture features between the brown bear and the brown tablecloth, making visual perception more challenging.

3. **Behavior Consistency**:
 - **Observation (Fig. 4)**: Visual comparisons showed that the robotic arm's behavior in real-world tasks, when trained with RL-GSBridge, closely mirrored its behavior in simulation. This consistency was not observed with the RL-sim baseline, which exhibited significant behavioral differences between simulation and reality.

**B. Pick-and-Place Task Results**

1. **Setup**: The framework was evaluated on a pick-and-place task involving placing a "Cake" onto a "Plate."
2. **Results (Table III)**: RL-GSBridge achieved a success rate of 68.75% in simulation and 71.87% in the real world. The slight increase in the real-world success rate (4.54%) was attributed to differences in how minor physical contacts are handled. In simulation, any excessive contact during placement might be registered as a failure, whereas, in the real world, some non-detrimental contacts are tolerated as long as the task is successfully completed.

**C. 3D Gaussian Splatting Rendering Results**

1. **Soft Mesh Binding vs. GaMeS**:
 - **Setup**: The proposed soft mesh binding method was quantitatively and qualitatively compared with the hard mesh binding approach of GaMeS for various foreground objects ("Banana," "Bear," "Cake," "Small cube") and backgrounds ("Foam Pad," "Tablecloth," "Plate").
 - **Quantitative Metrics (Table IV)**: The soft mesh binding method consistently outperformed GaMeS across common rendering metrics:
 - **SSIM (Structural Similarity Index)**: Higher values (e.g., Banana: 0.989 vs. 0.894 for GaMeS), indicating better structural similarity to ground truth images.
 - **PSNR (Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio)**: Higher values (e.g., Banana: 35.46 dB vs. 26.53 dB for GaMeS), indicating lower reconstruction error.
 - **LPIPS (Learned Perceptual Image Patch Similarity)**: Lower values (e.g., Banana: 0.026 vs. 0.049 for GaMeS), indicating higher perceptual similarity to ground truth.
 These results indicate that the proposed soft mesh binding method achieved superior rendering quality.
 - **Qualitative Visualization (Fig. 5)**: Visual comparisons confirmed that the soft mesh binding method produced fewer artifacts and rendered more detailed textures compared to GaMeS.
 - **Non-rigid Object Editing (Fig. 6)**: The soft binding constraint GS modeling method demonstrated consistent results in editing a non-rigid toy bear, suggesting potential applicability for deformable object manipulation, although comprehensive experiments in this area were noted as future work due to PyBullet's limitations in simulating soft objects.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The RL-GSBridge framework offers a significant contribution to the field of robotic learning, particularly in addressing the long-standing sim-to-real gap. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Enabling Reliable Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer**: The core impact is the demonstration of reliable zero-shot transfer of policies learned in simulation to real-world robots for specific manipulation tasks. This capability is crucial for accelerating the development and deployment of robotic systems, as it reduces the need for costly, time-consuming, and potentially unsafe real-world training and fine-tuning.
- **Enhanced Simulation Realism with Accessibility**: By integrating 3D Gaussian Splatting, RL-GSBridge provides a method for constructing visually highly realistic simulation environments. This realism is paramount for vision-based tasks, where discrepancies between simulated and real visual observations can critically undermine policy performance. Crucially, this high fidelity is achieved using only consumer-grade cameras, making the approach more accessible and cost-effective than traditional methods relying on expensive 3D scanning equipment or expert CAD modeling.
- **Advancement in Radiance Field Applications for Robotics**: The work pioneers the application of 3D Gaussian Splatting, a relatively new radiance field technique, as a core component for robotic simulation. This motivates further research into leveraging such explicit and editable scene representations for more complex robotic scenarios.
- **Improved 3D Gaussian Splatting Methodology**: The introduction of a soft mesh binding strategy for 3D GS represents an improvement over existing mesh-bound methods. By allowing more flexibility in Gaussian optimization while maintaining geometric accuracy, it enhances rendering quality, reduces artifacts, and potentially extends the utility of GS to more challenging scenarios, such as the representation and manipulation of non-rigid objects.
- **Robustness Across Diverse Scenarios**: The validation of RL-GSBridge across various objects, textures, and backgrounds for both grasping and pick-and-place tasks underscores its robustness. The minimal drop in success rates during sim-to-real transfer demonstrates the framework's ability to maintain stable performance in complex real-world conditions.
- **Foundation for Future Research**: RL-GSBridge establishes a foundation for several promising future research directions. These include exploring the simulation of realistic lighting conditions, integrating the framework with advanced large-scale policy models for broader generalization, and combining it with more sophisticated perception learning methods. The identified potential for handling deformable objects using the soft mesh binding approach also opens up new avenues for advanced robotic manipulation.

In conclusion, RL-GSBridge provides a robust and visually faithful simulation platform that significantly reduces the perception gap between simulation and reality, thereby contributing to safer, more efficient, and more scalable development of robotic manipulation capabilities.
