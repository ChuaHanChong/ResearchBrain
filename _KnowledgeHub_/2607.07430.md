# Immersive Social Interaction with VR and LLM-Assisted Humanoids

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at New York University Abu Dhabi developed a humanoid teleoperation system that integrates VR hand tracking for manipulation with LLM-assisted voice commands for locomotion, enabling immersive social interaction. The system demonstrated improved task completion times and success rates, with expert users completing object pick tasks in 22 seconds with 90% success, and also collects multi-modal data for training future autonomous robot behaviors.

## Problem

- Existing humanoid teleoperation systems are often complex, non-user-friendly, and cognitively demanding, particularly for locomotion in unstructured environments.
- There is a growing need for innovative solutions to address social isolation and mobility limitations, especially among older adults, requiring enhanced remote connection capabilities.
- Current teleoperation systems lack integrated capabilities for collecting diverse human-robot interaction data, which is essential for developing future autonomous robot behaviors.

## Method

- A comprehensive teleoperation system was developed for a Unitree H1 humanoid robot, utilizing an Apple Vision Pro for operator interaction.
- Natural voice commands, processed by ==GPT-4==, were integrated for high-level locomotion control, while ==VR hand tracking== enabled precise manipulation of dexterous hands.
- The system provides real-time bidirectional audio for social interaction and is designed to collect multi-modal data (e.g., ego-centric images, voice commands, joint angles) for ==imitation learning== and downstream autonomy tasks.

## Results

- Expert users achieved higher task success rates (**90%** for object pick, **80%** for social interaction) and significantly reduced completion times compared to novice users (e.g., **22 seconds** vs. **52 seconds** for object pick).
- The system uniquely offers voice-controlled locomotion and explicit social interaction capabilities, distinguishing it from several existing humanoid teleoperation approaches.
- The collected multi-modal data, including ego-centric images, voice/text commands, and robot joint angles, provides a valuable foundation for training advanced autonomous robot behaviors through imitation learning and other methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining voice-controlled locomotion with VR hand tracking provides a more intuitive and less cognitively demanding teleoperation interface, improving accessibility for diverse users.
> - The integrated system effectively facilitates immersive social interaction and telepresence through bidirectional audio, addressing a critical need for remote social engagement.
> - The inherent multi-modal data collection capability of the system is crucial for generating rich datasets that can significantly advance embodied action reasoning and imitation learning for future autonomous robots.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of human-robot interaction (HRI) is experiencing growth driven by demand in various sectors, including elder care, social engagement, and search-and-rescue operations. Teleoperation systems for humanoid robots are a significant area within HRI, offering potential solutions for remote presence and task execution.

Existing teleoperation systems face several challenges. One prominent issue is the global rise in social isolation and mobility limitations, particularly among homebound older adults, which necessitates innovative methods for remote connection and engagement. In parallel, teleoperated robots are considered for hazardous environments, such as search-and-rescue, where direct human presence is unsafe. However, these systems frequently encounter difficulties with locomotion control, especially in complex or unstructured settings. Traditional teleoperation interfaces are often described as not user-friendly and cognitively demanding, requiring operators to manage numerous robot joints simultaneously.

This research contributes to the development of more intuitive and capable teleoperation systems by integrating virtual reality (VR) for manipulation and large language model (LLM)-assisted voice commands for locomotion. The work aims to address the limitations of existing interfaces by reducing the cognitive load on operators and enhancing the robot's ability to navigate and interact in human-centric environments. By focusing on whole-body control and multi-modal data collection, this system positions itself as a step towards more autonomous and socially integrated humanoid robots, building upon previous efforts in bilateral teleoperation, humanoid control surveys, and user-centered interface design, while introducing a novel combination of control modalities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from observed limitations in current humanoid teleoperation systems and the increasing societal need for advanced human-robot interaction.

**Motivation:**
- **Addressing Social Isolation and Mobility Challenges:** The paper highlights the growing global aging population and the associated issues of social isolation and reduced mobility among older adults. There is a demand for innovative solutions that enable these individuals to maintain external connections, participate in social activities, and preserve independence. Conversational telepresence and teleoperation robots are identified as promising tools to meet this need.
- **Enhancing Safety in Hazardous Environments:** Beyond social applications, teleoperation systems hold potential for deployment in dangerous scenarios, such as search-and-rescue operations, where human intervention carries significant risks. Improving robot control and autonomy in such environments is a key driver.
- **Overcoming Teleoperation Interface Limitations:** Traditional teleoperated robotic systems are characterized by challenges in locomotion control, particularly in complex and unstructured environments. Furthermore, their interfaces are often described as non-user-friendly and cognitively demanding, requiring operators to manage multiple robot joints simultaneously. This complexity limits broader adoption and efficiency.
- **Facilitating Data Collection for Future Autonomy:** The authors recognize the value of collecting diverse human-robot interaction data to train future autonomous robot behaviors. Current systems often lack integrated capabilities for multi-modal data acquisition during teleoperation.

**Key Objectives:**
Based on these motivations, the authors established several objectives for their work:
- **Develop a Versatile Humanoid Teleoperation System:** The overarching goal was to create a comprehensive system that allows for whole-body control of a humanoid robot.
- **Integrate Voice Commands for Locomotion:** To simplify navigation and reduce cognitive load, an objective was to enable users to control robot locomotion using natural voice commands.
- **Implement VR Hand Tracking for Manipulation:** The system aimed to leverage VR technology, specifically hand tracking, to provide an intuitive interface for precise robot manipulation tasks.
- **Enable Social Interaction Capabilities:** A key objective was to facilitate natural human-humanoid interactions, including bidirectional audio communication, to support social engagement and telepresence.
- **Support Multi-modal Data Collection:** The system was designed to collect various forms of data (ego-centric images, voice/text commands, joint angles, eye movements) during teleoperation, which could then be utilized for training robots via imitation learning and other advanced autonomy approaches.
- **Lay Groundwork for Human-Humanoid Collaboration:** The proposed interface aimed to support more complex scenarios, such as cooperative manipulation, beyond the immediate tasks studied.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research presents a humanoid teleoperation system comprising three main functional components: voice-controlled locomotion, teleoperated manipulation, and enhanced social interaction. This integrated framework also facilitates multi-modal data collection for subsequent task learning.

**Overall System Architecture:**
The system is designed to provide real-time whole-body control of a humanoid robot. The user interacts via an Apple Vision Pro, which streams ego-centric images, processes voice commands for locomotion and social interaction, and tracks hand movements for manipulation. This input is then translated into robot actions, while various data streams are simultaneously recorded.

**A. Voice Commands for High-level Locomotion:**
1. **Visual Input:** The operator receives ego-centric images streamed from the robot with a resolution of 640 × 480 via the Apple Vision Pro, providing visual context for navigation.
2. **Voice Input and Transcription:** The user issues locomotion commands verbally. Deepgram is employed for real-time speech-to-text transcription of these commands.
3. **Command Parsing and Reasoning:** GPT-4 is utilized for its reasoning capabilities to parse the transcribed text into high-level control commands. These commands include functions such as `move(x, y)` (for translation), `rotate(angle)` (for turning), `stop()`, and `stand()`.
4. **Execution and Stabilization:** The parsed high-level functions are then called for robot navigation. The robot's bipedal locomotion is based on a pre-trained deep reinforcement learning model, which aims to produce robust locomotion policies. The authors note challenges related to the robot's bipedal morphology requiring inherent stabilization mechanisms.
5. **Uncertainty Mitigation:** To address instances where GPT-4 might misinterpret voice commands, a verification step is implemented. If the system detects uncertainty in the command interpretation, it prompts the user for confirmation or clarification before execution.
6. **Scene Description (Optional):** The system offers an on-demand scene description generated using GPT-4’s vision capabilities. This feature is disabled by default to optimize computational resources and inference speed.

**B. Teleoperation for Manipulation:**
1. **Hardware and Tracking:** The Apple Vision Pro and the VisionPro Teleop framework are used to stream the human operator’s wrist and finger poses in SE(3) (Special Euclidean group, representing rigid body transformations in 3D space) to a server installed on the robot.
2. **End Effectors:** The robot is equipped with Inspire Robotics dexterous hands as end effectors.
3. **Retargeting and Mapping:** The streamed human pose data is then re-targeted and re-mapped to control the robot’s arms and fingers. Specifically, the system controls 4 degrees of freedom (DoF) for each arm and 6 DoF for the fingers of each hand.
4. **Inverse Kinematics and Control:** Human wrist poses are transformed into the robot’s coordinate frame. Inverse kinematics from Pinocchio, is used to compute the required joint angles for the robot's arms and fingers. These joint angles are then passed through a Proportional-Derivative (PD) controller to enable the robot’s arms to mimic the operator’s movements.

**C. Social Interaction:**
1. **Bidirectional Audio:** Real-time, bidirectional audio transmission is implemented using ROS 1 (Robot Operating System). ROS nodes on both the operator's side and the robot enable the operator to hear the robot’s environment and to communicate verbally with people surrounding the robot via its speakers.
2. **Situational Awareness:** This audio setup is designed to enhance the operator’s situational awareness and support effective telepresence. The authors indicate that while egocentric view alone is insufficient for comprehensive navigation, future work plans to integrate an additional waist-mounted camera to improve situational awareness further.

**D. Multi-modal Data Collection:**
The system is designed to record various types of data during teleoperation, including:
- Ego-centric images (from the robot's perspective).
- Voice/text commands issued by the operator.
- Robot body joint angles (19 DoF).
- Robot hand joint angles (12 DoF).
- Operator eye movements.
This collected data is intended for use in training robots through imitation learning and other downstream autonomy tasks.

**Experiment Setup:**
The system was evaluated using specific hardware and tasks:
- **Hardware:** Apple Vision Pro for operator interface, Inspire Robotics Dexterous Hands for manipulation, and a Unitree H1 Humanoid Robot as the teleoperated platform.
- **Tasks:**
 - **Manipulation Task (Pick and Place Bottle):** The robot was teleoperated to pick up a bottle, randomly placed on a table, and then place it into a designated box. This task aimed to assess the robot’s precision in manipulation and its locomotion control for navigation, grasping, and transport.
 - **Social Interaction Task (Passing Cube):** This task required the robot to verbally request a cube from one person, then navigate to another person, and hand over the cube based on verbal instructions. This evaluated the robot’s ability to coordinate locomotion, manipulation, and social communication cues.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of the developed humanoid teleoperation system focused on its capabilities in locomotion, manipulation, and social interaction, with an emphasis on comparing performance between novice and expert users, and contrasting the system's features with existing teleoperation methods.

**A. Performance Comparison of Novice vs. Expert Users (Table I):**
The study assessed the success rate and time taken for two specific tasks: "Object Pick" (a manipulation task) and "Social Interaction" (passing a cube).
- **Object Pick Task:**
 - Novice users achieved a success rate of 0.8 (80%) with an average task completion time of 52 seconds.
 - Expert users demonstrated a higher success rate of 0.9 (90%) and significantly reduced completion time, averaging 22 seconds.
- **Social Interaction Task:**
 - Novice users completed this task with a success rate of 0.7 (70%), taking an average of 326 seconds.
 - Expert users again showed improved performance with a 0.8 (80%) success rate and a much faster average completion time of 158 seconds.

These results indicate that while the system is usable by novice operators, proficiency and efficiency improve with experience, as evidenced by higher success rates and reduced task completion times for expert users across both manipulation and social interaction tasks.

**B. Comparison of Humanoid Teleoperation Methods (Table II):**
The paper presents a qualitative comparison of its method against several existing humanoid teleoperation approaches: Open Television, Human Plus, and Human to Humanoid.
- **Locomotion:** The presented method uniquely supports voice-controlled locomotion, a feature not explicitly indicated in the compared existing approaches.
- **Manipulation:** All listed methods, including the presented one, support manipulation capabilities.
- **Social Interaction:** The presented method supports social interaction capabilities, whereas Open Television, Human Plus, and Human to Humanoid are not explicitly stated to support this feature in the comparison.

This comparison highlights that the integration of voice-controlled locomotion and explicit social interaction capabilities distinguishes the presented system from the compared prior works.

**C. Implications and System Advantages:**
The combination of features within the system offers several advantages:
- **Enhanced Accessibility and Reduced Cognitive Load:** The integration of voice-controlled locomotion and VR hand tracking is presented as making the system more accessible and less physically demanding for operators. This is considered particularly beneficial for specific user groups, such as homebound older adults who may have mobility limitations or experience cognitive fatigue with complex interfaces.
- **Suitability for Challenging Environments:** The intuitive control scheme and comprehensive capabilities are noted as advantageous for applications in confined or hazardous environments where ease of operation and precise control are critical.
- **Foundation for Downstream Autonomy:** A significant finding relates to the system's capacity for multi-modal data collection. The concurrent recording of aligned voice/text instructions, egocentric observations, and executed robot actions provides a valuable dataset. This data can serve as priors for various downstream autonomy tasks, including:
 - **Imitation Learning:** Direct training of robots to replicate human-demonstrated behaviors.
 - **Embodied Action Reasoning:** Developing models for understanding and executing complex tasks based on multi-modal inputs.
 - **3D Scene Reconstruction:** Utilizing egocentric RGB streams to support high-fidelity 3D scene reconstruction, potentially employing techniques like Gaussian Splatting, for enhanced perception and mapping capabilities.

In summary, the results demonstrate the feasibility and comparative advantages of the integrated teleoperation system, particularly in terms of user experience and its potential as a data generation platform for future autonomous robot development.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant contribution to the field of human-robot interaction by integrating advanced control modalities for humanoid teleoperation. Its significance and potential impact can be understood in several key areas:

**1. Addressing Limitations in Human-Robot Interaction:**
The system directly addresses several limitations inherent in existing teleoperation systems. By combining voice-controlled locomotion with VR-based hand tracking, it aims to reduce the cognitive burden on human operators and simplify control, moving beyond traditional, often complex, multi-joint control interfaces. This intuitive, whole-body control interface has the potential to make teleoperation more accessible to a broader user base.

**2. Enhancing Social Engagement and Telepresence:**
A core aspect of this work is the emphasis on social interaction capabilities, facilitated by bidirectional audio transmission. This enables the operator to not only perceive the robot's environment but also to communicate directly with individuals in that environment through the robot. This enhanced telepresence is particularly impactful for applications aimed at mitigating social isolation, such as providing remote social engagement for homebound older adults. It offers a tangible mechanism for maintaining connections and participation in social activities, thus improving well-being.

**3. Expanding Applications in Critical Domains:**
The versatility of the system extends its potential impact to critical domains beyond social interaction:
- **Elderly Care:** By enabling intuitive remote interaction, the system can offer solutions for elder care, allowing family members or caregivers to interact with older adults remotely, provide assistance, and combat loneliness.
- **Hazardous Environments:** The ability to control a humanoid robot for locomotion and manipulation in complex environments, with reduced cognitive load on the operator, is highly relevant for tasks in dangerous settings like search-and-rescue operations. This could minimize human exposure to risk while allowing for human-level dexterity and decision-making in challenging situations.

**4. Advancing Robot Autonomy through Data Collection:**
Perhaps one of the most significant long-term impacts is the system's inherent capacity for multi-modal data collection during teleoperation. The simultaneous recording of ego-centric visual data, voice commands, text instructions, and the robot's executed actions creates a rich dataset. This data is invaluable for training future robot intelligence through imitation learning, allowing robots to learn complex behaviors directly from human demonstrations. Beyond imitation learning, this aligned multi-modal data can facilitate:
- **Embodied Action Reasoning:** Training models that allow robots to understand and reason about tasks in a more human-like, context-aware manner.
- **Advanced Perception and Mapping:** Utilizing visual data for high-fidelity 3D scene reconstruction, which can significantly improve a robot's understanding of its environment for navigation and interaction.

**5. Guiding Future Research Directions:**
The work also lays a foundation for future research. The authors identify areas for refinement, such as enhancing the social interaction module and improving multi-modal data processing. This suggests that the developed framework serves not only as a functional system but also as a platform for continued advancements in robot behavior learning and human-humanoid collaboration. The presented approach contributes to the trajectory towards more autonomous, intelligent, and socially integrated humanoid robots capable of interacting naturally and effectively in diverse human environments.
