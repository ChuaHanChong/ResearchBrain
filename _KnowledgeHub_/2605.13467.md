# PDCR: Perception-Decomposed Confidence Reward for Vision-Language Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Perception-Decomposed Confidence Reward (PDCR) is a framework that dynamically categorizes vision-language reasoning steps into visual perception or textual reasoning, applying skill-specific reward normalization to mitigate "mixture-induced signal degradation." The approach improves average accuracy by up to 0.7% over naive dense-reward baselines on various V-L reasoning tasks and reduces visual hallucinations.

## Problem

- Pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs) struggle with multi-step reasoning due to sparse, outcome-based rewards in standard Reinforcement Learning, leading to a credit assignment problem.
- Applying global confidence-based rewards directly to V-L reasoning is suboptimal because these tasks involve heterogeneous skills (visual perception and textual reasoning) with differing statistical properties, causing signal degradation.
- Existing fine-grained supervision methods are either resource-intensive, prone to misalignment, or do not effectively address the multimodal nature of V-L tasks with internal signals.

## Method

- The framework employs an unsupervised skill decomposition method based on a "==Visual Dependence Score==," which quantifies a step's reliance on visual input using log-likelihood ratios.
- Otsu's method dynamically clusters these scores to categorize each reasoning step as either visual perception or textual reasoning without requiring external labels.
- **PDCR** then computes separate, intra-cluster normalized advantage signals for each identified skill, ensuring stable and well-scaled rewards specific to visual or textual steps.

## Results

- PDCR consistently outperformed existing baselines, achieving average accuracy improvements of up to **0.7%** over naive dense-reward PACR and **1.4%** over sparse-reward GRPO on Qwen2.5-VL-**7B**.
- The framework demonstrated superior performance on complex understanding tasks like MMMU-Pro and MMMU, and mathematical reasoning tasks such as MathVerse.
- Qualitative analysis showed that PDCR effectively mitigated visual hallucinations by guiding models to more accurately ground reasoning in visual input, leading to correct final answers.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - V-L reasoning is a heterogeneous process with distinct visual perception and textual reasoning skills, where visual steps are sparse (~30%) and textual steps are dense (~70%).
> - Applying global normalization for model-intrinsic rewards to V-L tasks leads to "mixture-induced signal degradation," particularly for sparse visual steps, due to the statistical dominance of textual steps.
> - Decomposing rewards based on identified skills and normalizing them within their respective clusters provides more stable and effective training signals, achievable unsupervised using model-internal signals.
