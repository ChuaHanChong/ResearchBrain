# PDCR: Perception-Decomposed Confidence Reward for Vision-Language Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Perception-Decomposed Confidence Reward (PDCR) is a framework that dynamically categorizes vision-language reasoning steps into visual perception or textual reasoning, applying skill-specific reward normalization to mitigate "mixture-induced signal degradation." The approach improves average accuracy by up to 0.7% over naive dense-reward baselines on various V-L reasoning tasks and reduces visual hallucinations.

## Problem

- Pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs) struggle with multi-step reasoning due to sparse, outcome-based rewards in standard Reinforcement Learning, leading to a credit assignment problem.
- Applying global confidence-based rewards directly to V-L reasoning is suboptimal because these tasks involve heterogeneous skills (visual perception and textual reasoning) with differing statistical properties, causing signal degradation.
- Existing fine-grained supervision methods are either resource-intensive, prone to misalignment, or do not effectively address the multimodal nature of V-L tasks with internal signals.

## Method

- The framework employs an unsupervised skill decomposition method based on a "==Visual Dependence Score==," which quantifies a step's reliance on visual input using log-likelihood ratios.
- Otsu's method dynamically clusters these scores to categorize each reasoning step as either visual perception or textual reasoning without requiring external labels.
- **PDCR** then computes separate, intra-cluster normalized advantage signals for each identified skill, ensuring stable and well-scaled rewards specific to visual or textual steps.

## Results

- PDCR consistently outperformed existing baselines, achieving average accuracy improvements of up to **0.7%** over naive dense-reward PACR and **1.4%** over sparse-reward GRPO on Qwen2.5-VL-**7B**.
- The framework demonstrated superior performance on complex understanding tasks like MMMU-Pro and MMMU, and mathematical reasoning tasks such as MathVerse.
- Qualitative analysis showed that PDCR effectively mitigated visual hallucinations by guiding models to more accurately ground reasoning in visual input, leading to correct final answers.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - V-L reasoning is a heterogeneous process with distinct visual perception and textual reasoning skills, where visual steps are sparse (~30%) and textual steps are dense (~70%).
> - Applying global normalization for model-intrinsic rewards to V-L tasks leads to "mixture-induced signal degradation," particularly for sparse visual steps, due to the statistical dominance of textual steps.
> - Decomposing rewards based on identified skills and normalizing them within their respective clusters provides more stable and effective training signals, achievable unsupervised using model-internal signals.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses challenges in enhancing the reasoning capabilities of pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs), a prominent area in artificial intelligence research. Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) has emerged as a method for improving these capabilities by using a programmatically checkable metric, such as the correctness of a final answer, as a reward signal. However, a limitation of standard RLVR is its reliance on a sparse, outcome-based reward, which offers little guidance for intermediate steps in multi-step reasoning tasks, thereby creating a credit assignment problem.

To overcome this, recent research has explored fine-grained, step-level supervision. One approach involves training external Process Reward Models (PRMs), which provide dense, step-level signals. However, PRMs typically require substantial data and computational resources for training and can be susceptible to misalignment. An alternative approach, particularly in the language domain, is to use model-intrinsic signals, such as rewarding the growth of confidence in the ground-truth answer. The Progressively Ascending Confidence Reward (PACR) framework is an example of this, providing step-level guidance using the model's own probabilities without needing an external reward model.

This paper identifies a gap in the application of such model-intrinsic rewards to vision-language (V-L) reasoning. While effective for unimodal text, directly applying a global confidence-based reward to V-L tasks is presented as suboptimal. The core reason is the heterogeneous nature of V-L reasoning, which combines distinct visual perception steps with textual reasoning steps. This heterogeneity leads to a "mixture-induced signal degradation" when a single, global normalization strategy is applied to reward signals, as the sparse visual steps can be statistically distorted by the more predominant textual steps.

The work differentiates itself from existing V-L decomposition approaches, which often focus on generating explicit textual descriptions (e.g., image captions) to serve as a basis for reward. Instead, this research aims to decompose the original reasoning steps directly through an internal, process-level signal, arguing that visual and linguistic capabilities are distinct skills that benefit from independent normalization. This aligns with a broader research trend in decomposed reward modeling, where complex signals are broken down for more effective learning, seen in domains such as multi-reward systems or tool-use agent tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observation that V-L reasoning is not a homogeneous cognitive process. Instead, it is a heterogeneous combination of two functionally distinct high-level skills: visual perception, which involves extracting information from images, and textual reasoning, which involves logical deduction or inference based on that information. The authors empirically validate this distinction through analysis of model attention patterns, showing that visual perception steps exhibit high attention to visual tokens, while textual reasoning steps primarily attend to preceding text tokens. Furthermore, they observe a statistical imbalance: visual perception steps are sparse (approximately 30% of total steps), while textual reasoning steps are dense (approximately 70%).

The critical problem identified by the authors is that this heterogeneity and statistical imbalance lead to "mixture-induced signal degradation" when a naive, globally-normalized model-intrinsic reward, like that used in PACR, is applied to V-L reasoning. The global normalization, based on statistics overwhelmingly dominated by textual reasoning steps, compresses and misaligns the advantage distribution for sparse visual perception steps. This can result in an ill-scaled or unstable training signal for these critical visual actions, hindering effective learning.

Given this problem, the key objectives of this work are:

1. **To empirically validate the existence and impact of mixture-induced signal degradation** in V-L reasoning when using global model-intrinsic rewards, showing that skill heterogeneity renders such rewards suboptimal.
2. **To propose an unsupervised skill decomposition method** that can automatically and dynamically categorize individual reasoning steps into either visual perception or textual reasoning. This method should leverage a model-internal signal without requiring external labels or complex annotations during training.
3. **To introduce Perception-Decomposed Confidence Reward (PDCR)**, a novel framework that aligns the reward structure with the heterogeneous nature of V-L reasoning. PDCR aims to resolve the signal degradation problem by computing separate, intra-cluster normalized advantages for each identified skill.
4. **To demonstrate through extensive experimentation** that the PDCR framework consistently outperforms existing sparse-reward baselines and naive, globally-normalized dense-reward formulations on a range of V-L reasoning benchmarks, validating the efficacy of the decomposed reward signal.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology is the Perception-Decomposed Confidence Reward (PDCR) framework, designed to address the challenges of heterogeneous skills in V-L reasoning. PDCR operates in two main stages: Unsupervised Skill Decomposition and Perception-Decomposed Advantage Calculation.

#### 3.1. Unsupervised Skill Decomposition

To provide a distinct reward signal for different types of reasoning steps, PDCR first categorizes each step `h^(i)_k` within a generated trajectory `τ^(i)` as either visual perception or textual reasoning. This is achieved without external labels or human supervision.

- **Visual Dependence Score (V_k)**: A model-internal signal is introduced to quantify a step's reliance on the visual input. This score `V_k` is defined as the log-likelihood ratio between the probability of generating the step `h^(i)_k` given the actual visual input `I` and its probability given a non-informative, blank (white) image `I_white`.
 - `V^(i)_k = log π_θ(h^(i)_k | I, q, H^(i)_<k) - log π_θ(h^(i)_k | I_white, q, H^(i)_<k)`
 - A high `V_k` suggests heavy reliance on the visual input, indicating a visual perception step. A score near zero implies the step was predominantly driven by the textual prefix, marking it as a textual reasoning step.
 - An ablation study on visual perturbation strategies confirmed that methods effectively destroying visual semantics (white image, Gaussian blur, Gaussian noise) yielded high decomposition accuracy, while spatial transformations (rotation) were insufficient, validating the choice of a white image for its efficiency and effectiveness.

- **Optimal Clustering (Otsu's Method)**: To dynamically identify an optimal threshold `c*` for separating visual perception and textual reasoning steps, Otsu's method is applied. This classic 1D clustering algorithm finds a threshold that minimizes the total intra-cluster variance within the distribution of all visual dependence scores `V`. The selected `c*` partitions the set of all step indices `I` into two disjoint subsets: `I_visual` for visual perception steps (`V_k ≥ c*`) and `I_textual` for textual reasoning steps (`V_k < c*`).
 - Validation against a "Top-K" baseline, which classifies a fixed percentage of steps as visual perception, demonstrated that Otsu's method achieves higher classification accuracy (76.2% vs. 67.5% for the best Top-K configuration) against human-annotated ground truth labels. This indicates that the dynamic, data-driven thresholding is more robust and accurate for skill decomposition.

#### 3.2. Perception-Decomposed Advantage Calculation

Following the skill decomposition, PDCR computes a separate, normalized advantage for each skill cluster. This directly addresses the "mixture-induced signal degradation" problem by ensuring that the reward for a specific skill is evaluated only against its peers.

- **Discounted Returns (G_k)**: The process begins by calculating the stepwise ground-truth confidence gains `g^(i)_k` (change in log-probability of the ground-truth answer) for each step, similar to the PACR framework. These gains are then converted into discounted returns `G^(i)_k`.
- **Intra-Cluster Normalization**: Instead of a global normalization, `G^(i)_k` values are normalized using min-max scaling within their respective skill clusters:
 - For visual steps `(i, k) ∈ I_visual`, the advantage `A^(i)_V,k` is computed by normalizing `G^(i)_k` against the minimum and maximum discounted returns found *only within the `I_visual` set*.
 - For textual steps `(i, k) ∈ I_textual`, the advantage `A^(i)_T,k` is computed by normalizing `G^(i)_k` against the minimum and maximum discounted returns found *only within the `I_textual` set*.
 - This ensures that both sparse visual perception steps and dense textual reasoning steps receive stable, well-scaled reward signals, evaluated against appropriate statistical baselines.

#### 3.3. Final PDCR Advantage

The total advantage `A^(i)_total,k` for any given step `h^(i)_k` is a weighted sum of the sparse outcome advantage `A^(i)_O` (derived from the correctness of the final answer, as in standard GRPO) and its corresponding decomposed process advantage `A^(i)_decomposed,k`.

- `A^(i)_total,k = λ_O * A^(i)_O + λ_P * A^(i)_decomposed,k`
- `A^(i)_decomposed,k` is `A^(i)_V,k` if the step is classified as visual, and `A^(i)_T,k` if it is textual.
- This combined and decomposed advantage signal is then used during the policy update for all tokens comprising the step `h^(i)_k`.

#### 3.4. Training Details

Experiments utilized Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct, and Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct models, trained on the Vision-SR1 dataset (approximately 47K examples covering mathematical reasoning, commonsense knowledge, and general visual understanding). The training followed the R1-Zero style using the EasyR1 framework, applying RLVR directly without a Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) stage. A format reward was integrated, assigning a bonus for reasoning processes correctly enclosed within `<think>` and `</think>` tags and the final answer within `\boxed{}`. Hyperparameters were kept consistent across all methods for fair comparison.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of PDCR against various baselines demonstrated consistent performance improvements across multiple V-L reasoning benchmarks.

- **Overall Performance**:
 - On the Qwen2.5-VL-7B backbone, PDCR achieved an average accuracy of 52.9, outperforming the naive dense-reward baseline PACR (52.2), the sparse-reward GRPO (51.5), and the stabilized GRPO variant DAPO (52.0).
 - Similar trends were observed with the Qwen2.5-VL-3B backbone, where PDCR scored an average of 45.2, surpassing PACR (44.4) and GRPO (43.6).
 - Further evaluation on the Qwen3-VL-8B backbone confirmed generalization, with PDCR achieving an average score of 59.1, exceeding PACR (58.5) and GRPO (58.3).
 - PDCR consistently showed improvements over PACR, particularly in complex understanding tasks like MMMU-Pro and MMMU, and in mathematical reasoning tasks such as MathVerse.

- **Comparison to DAPO**: PDCR's consistent outperformance of DAPO, a baseline designed to mitigate the vanishing advantage problem of GRPO, indicates that the benefits of PDCR extend beyond mere training signal stabilization. The decomposed reward structure contributes a high-quality, meaningful signal that enhances learning.

- **Efficacy of Skill Decomposition**:
 - An ablation study comparing full PDCR with a "Random Decomposition" baseline validated the importance of meaningful skill decomposition. The Random Decomposition baseline, which randomly assigned steps to visual or textual clusters before applying decomposed normalization, performed significantly worse than full PDCR (e.g., 52.3 vs. 52.9 on 7B model) and only marginally better than naive PACR. This indicates that merely decomposing the reward is insufficient; the accurate identification and separation of heterogeneous skills are crucial for PDCR's effectiveness.

- **Training Dynamics, Cost, and Efficiency**:
 - Training dynamics showed that PDCR converged faster and achieved higher final accuracy throughout the training process compared to all baselines.
 - Computational overhead analysis revealed that PDCR incurred a slightly higher training cost per step than PACR (which itself is about 1.5x more costly than GRPO), primarily due to an additional forward pass for calculating the Visual Dependence Score.
 - However, this increased training cost was offset by a significant gain in inference-time efficiency. Both PDCR and PACR learned to produce substantially shorter and more concise reasoning traces over time compared to GRPO, leading to faster inference.

- **Qualitative Results**:
 - Qualitative comparisons highlighted a key benefit of PDCR: its ability to mitigate visual hallucinations. Baseline models frequently exhibited a failure mode where they generated logically coherent reasoning paths founded on incorrect visual extractions. In contrast, PDCR guided the model to more accurately ground its reasoning in the visual input, providing a robust foundation for subsequent logical inference and leading to correct final answers in tasks involving spatial estimation, counting, and geometric reasoning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work makes a contribution to the field of vision-language reasoning by directly addressing the inherent heterogeneity of V-L tasks within the reinforcement learning framework. The introduction of Perception-Decomposed Confidence Reward (PDCR) and its unsupervised skill decomposition mechanism offers a method for providing tailored, stable training signals for distinct cognitive processes.

The significance and potential impact of this research include:

- **Improved V-L Reasoning Performance**: PDCR demonstrates consistent and notable improvements over strong sparse-reward and naive dense-reward baselines across various V-L benchmarks and model scales. This suggests a more effective way to train VLMs to perform complex, multi-step reasoning.
- **Mitigation of Visual Hallucinations**: A key qualitative finding is PDCR's role in reducing visual hallucinations. By providing a more precise reward signal for visual perception steps, the model is guided to ground its reasoning more accurately in the visual input, which can enhance the trustworthiness and reliability of VLM outputs.
- **Scalable and Self-Contained Solution**: The unsupervised skill decomposition relies on model-internal signals (Visual Dependence Score and Otsu's method) without requiring external annotations or the training of separate, costly reward models. This makes the PDCR framework a scalable and practical solution for VLM training, reducing the overhead typically associated with fine-grained supervision.
- **Enhanced Training and Inference Efficiency**: While PDCR involves a higher computational cost per training step, this investment leads to models that generate more concise and efficient reasoning traces during inference. This trade-off is often favorable in real-world deployment scenarios where inference speed and resource consumption are critical.
- **Broader Implications for Multi-Skill Agents**: The principle of dynamically decomposing and normalizing reward signals based on identified underlying skills could be transferable to other complex multimodal tasks or agentic systems that involve a mix of heterogeneous capabilities, offering a new paradigm for reinforcement learning with structured rewards.

**Limitations and Future Work**:
The primary limitation identified is the computational overhead during training, although this is balanced by inference efficiency. Future work could explore more computationally efficient methods for calculating confidence and visual dependence scores, such as approximations based on internal model activations. Additionally, the current binary skill decomposition (visual vs. textual) simplifies the reality of potentially hybrid reasoning steps. Future research could investigate soft-clustering methods or the identification of more nuanced skill types to capture these complexities more accurately.
