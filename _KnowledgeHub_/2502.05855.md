# DexVLA: Vision-Language Model with Plug-In Diffusion Expert for General Robot Control

> [!summary] Summary
> DexVLA introduces a vision-language-action model for general robot control that integrates a billion-parameter diffusion-based action expert with a vision-language model, enabling robots to perform complex, long-horizon, and dexterous tasks across diverse embodiments with high data efficiency. It achieves superior performance in tasks like shirt folding and laundry folding, outperforming state-of-the-art baselines without external high-level planning.

## Problem

- Current state-of-the-art Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for general robot control demand prohibitively large and costly datasets.
- Existing VLA architectures often suffer from an imbalance, with large vision-language models but underdeveloped action space representations, hindering complex dexterous skill execution and cross-embodiment generalization.
- Many advanced VLAs require external high-level policies (e.g., SayCan) to decompose long-horizon tasks, preventing truly end-to-end autonomous systems.

## Method

- **DexVLA** introduces a novel architecture integrating a 2B parameter VLM with a significantly scaled, billion-parameter diffusion-based action expert, enhancing motor command generation capacity.
- It employs a three-stage embodied curriculum learning strategy for efficient training: cross-embodiment pre-training of the diffusion expert, embodiment-specific alignment, and task-specific adaptation with substep reasoning.
- The model is trained to generate internal sub-step reasoning as intermediate language outputs, allowing the VLM to act as an implicit high-level policy for autonomous task decomposition without external planners.

## Results

- DexVLA achieved a score of **0.92** on shirt folding, while baselines scored near 0, demonstrating superior acquisition of intricate manipulation skills.
- It learned dexterous skills on novel embodiments with fewer than 100 demonstrations, achieving an average score of **0.90** for tasks like drink pouring and packing, substantially outperforming other VLA models.
- The model surpassed baselines like 
p0 on complex long-horizon tasks, achieving **0.4** on laundry folding (compared to 
p0's **0.2**) and outperforming 
p0 by **0.08** points on 'table bussing (hard),' without external policy modules.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Scaling the action expert to a billion parameters is crucial for learning diverse and complex motor skills, significantly outperforming smaller action experts.
> - A structured, three-stage embodied curriculum learning strategy dramatically improves data efficiency and generalization across tasks and embodiments.
> - Training the VLM to generate internal sub-step reasoning enables the model to effectively handle long-horizon tasks, disentangle action spaces, and achieve superior performance compared to explicit planning approaches.
