# DexVLA: Vision-Language Model with Plug-In Diffusion Expert for General Robot Control

> [!summary] Summary
> DexVLA introduces a vision-language-action model for general robot control that integrates a billion-parameter diffusion-based action expert with a vision-language model, enabling robots to perform complex, long-horizon, and dexterous tasks across diverse embodiments with high data efficiency. It achieves superior performance in tasks like shirt folding and laundry folding, outperforming state-of-the-art baselines without external high-level planning.

## Problem

- Current state-of-the-art Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for general robot control demand prohibitively large and costly datasets.
- Existing VLA architectures often suffer from an imbalance, with large vision-language models but underdeveloped action space representations, hindering complex dexterous skill execution and cross-embodiment generalization.
- Many advanced VLAs require external high-level policies (e.g., SayCan) to decompose long-horizon tasks, preventing truly end-to-end autonomous systems.

## Method

- **DexVLA** introduces a novel architecture integrating a 2B parameter VLM with a significantly scaled, billion-parameter diffusion-based action expert, enhancing motor command generation capacity.
- It employs a three-stage embodied curriculum learning strategy for efficient training: cross-embodiment pre-training of the diffusion expert, embodiment-specific alignment, and task-specific adaptation with substep reasoning.
- The model is trained to generate internal sub-step reasoning as intermediate language outputs, allowing the VLM to act as an implicit high-level policy for autonomous task decomposition without external planners.

## Results

- DexVLA achieved a score of **0.92** on shirt folding, while baselines scored near 0, demonstrating superior acquisition of intricate manipulation skills.
- It learned dexterous skills on novel embodiments with fewer than 100 demonstrations, achieving an average score of **0.90** for tasks like drink pouring and packing, substantially outperforming other VLA models.
- The model surpassed baselines like 
p0 on complex long-horizon tasks, achieving **0.4** on laundry folding (compared to 
p0's **0.2**) and outperforming 
p0 by **0.08** points on 'table bussing (hard),' without external policy modules.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Scaling the action expert to a billion parameters is crucial for learning diverse and complex motor skills, significantly outperforming smaller action experts.
> - A structured, three-stage embodied curriculum learning strategy dramatically improves data efficiency and generalization across tasks and embodiments.
> - Training the VLM to generate internal sub-step reasoning enables the model to effectively handle long-horizon tasks, disentangle action spaces, and achieve superior performance compared to explicit planning approaches.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of robots capable of performing diverse tasks across varied environments is a central, long-standing challenge in robotics. The current research landscape is increasingly dominated by Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which aim to imbue robots with generalizable skills by leveraging large-scale vision-language models (VLMs) pre-trained on internet-scale data. These VLAs, exemplified by models like RT-2, OpenVLA, and π0, demonstrate promise in interpreting language commands and generalizing to novel objects and environments.

However, the field faces two persistent bottlenecks that DexVLA directly addresses:
1. **Data Scarcity:** State-of-the-art VLA models often require immense datasets (e.g., Open-X Embodiment, 10,000-hour datasets) collected through costly and labor-intensive human demonstrations. This high data demand limits the accessibility and scalability of generalist robot training.
2. **Architectural Imbalance:** Many current VLA models prioritize scaling the VLM component (e.g., OpenVLA with 7B parameters, π0 with 3B parameters) for enhanced visual and linguistic understanding. While beneficial for perception, the action space representation and the component responsible for generating motor commands often remain relatively underdeveloped. This creates a disconnect between the VLM's high-level understanding and the embodied, sensorimotor context of robotic action, hindering the execution of complex dexterous skills. Furthermore, even advanced VLAs like π0 often rely on external high-level policies (e.g., SayCan) to decompose long-horizon tasks into manageable sub-goals, preventing a truly end-to-end autonomous system.

DexVLA enters this landscape by proposing a paradigm shift. Instead of solely focusing on the VLM's scale, it emphasizes the development of a *billion-parameter diffusion-based action expert* as a plug-in component. Diffusion models, which have revolutionized visual generation, have also shown strong capabilities in robot learning for modeling multimodal action distributions (e.g., Diffusion Policy). DexVLA leverages this strength by significantly scaling the action expert and integrating it with a VLM. By introducing a novel embodied curriculum learning strategy and incorporating sub-step reasoning directly into the model's training, DexVLA aims to overcome the architectural imbalance and integrate high-level planning capabilities *within* the VLA itself. This approach seeks to enable more data-efficient training, improve generalization, and empower robots to execute complex, long-horizon, and dexterous tasks across diverse embodiments without external guidance. Thus, DexVLA positions itself as a significant advancement towards more capable, versatile, and autonomous robot foundation models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind DexVLA is to overcome the fundamental challenges in robot learning to enable versatile robots that can perform diverse tasks across varied and dynamic environments. This entails not just task completion but also adaptability to language commands, environmental constraints, and unexpected disruptions.

The authors identify two critical bottlenecks hindering the realization of omnipotent robot foundation models:
1. **Costly Data Acquisition:** The prohibitive cost and labor intensity of collecting massive datasets required by current state-of-the-art VLA models.
2. **Architectural Disparity:** The current emphasis on scaling the Vision-Language Model (VLM) component, leading to an imbalance where the action representation remains a significant limitation, especially for complex motor skills and cross-embodiment generalization. Current VLMs struggle to translate their vast internet-scale knowledge into precise physical actions and often require external high-level policies for complex task decomposition.

Against this backdrop, DexVLA sets forth several key objectives:
- **Enhance Data Efficiency:** To enable VLAs to learn effectively from smaller, more manageable datasets, reducing the reliance on prohibitively expensive data collection efforts.
- **Improve Generalization Capabilities:** To allow robots to generalize learned skills across different tasks, novel objects, and, critically, diverse robot embodiments (e.g., single-arm, bimanual, dexterous hand).
- **Master Complex, Long-Horizon Tasks:** To empower robots to autonomously complete multi-stage, intricate tasks, such as laundry folding, directly from high-level language prompts, without external task decomposition.
- **Scale the Action Expert:** To address the architectural imbalance by significantly scaling the diffusion-based action expert to a billion parameters, thereby enhancing its capacity to learn and generate intricate motor skills.
- **Integrate High-Level Reasoning:** To enable the VLA model to inherently interpret task states and generate internal sub-step reasoning, effectively serving as an "implicit high-level policy" to guide action generation, eliminating the need for separate planning modules like SayCan.
- **Achieve Cost-Efficient Training and Fast Inference:** To develop a framework that is practical for research and deployment, demonstrating efficient training resource utilization and real-time inference speeds.

By addressing these objectives, DexVLA aims to push the boundaries of current VLA capabilities, paving the way for more intelligent, autonomous, and broadly applicable robotic systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DexVLA's methodology centers on a novel framework that integrates a sophisticated vision-language model (VLM) with a significantly scaled diffusion-based action expert, guided by an innovative embodied curriculum learning strategy.

#### 3.1. Model Architecture

The core of DexVLA is built upon a transformer language model backbone, specifically Qwen2-VL (a 2B parameter VLM), which serves as the base VLM.
- **Vision-Language Model (VLM):** Image observations from multiple camera views are processed by image encoders and projected into the same embedding space as language tokens. The VLM generates two types of outputs:
 - **Reasoning Tokens:** These represent the model's high-level understanding and internal task decomposition.
 - **Action Tokens:** These are passed to the action expert for generating motor commands.
- **Projection Module:** A module consisting of two linear layers with LayerNorm acts as a connector, transforming the VLM's embedding space to align with the input requirements of the action expert.
- **FiLM Layers:** The reasoning tokens generated by the VLM are injected into the policy (diffusion expert) using FiLM (Feature-wise Linear Modulation) layers, which scale and shift the parameters. This allows the VLM's autonomous reasoning to directly guide the action generation process.
- **Billion-Parameter Diffusion Expert:** This is a key innovation. Recognizing the limitations of conventional action experts, DexVLA utilizes a diffusion-based expert scaled to *one billion parameters*, a substantial increase over typical multi-million parameter experts. It is based on the Scale Diffusion Policy (ScaleDP), a Transformer-architecture variant of Diffusion Policy. To facilitate cross-embodiment learning, it employs a multi-head output architecture, where each head is responsible for a specific robot configuration, similar to Octo.
- **Training Objectives:** The overall training loss is a weighted combination of diffusion loss (L_diff) and next-token prediction loss (L_ntp), with α=1. This ensures the model primarily focuses on action prediction based on reasoning and instructions.

#### 3.2. Embodied Curriculum Learning

A three-stage training strategy is central to DexVLA's data efficiency and generalization:

- **Stage 1: Cross-Embodiment Pre-training:**
 - **Goal:** Learn low-level, generalizable motor skills that are embodiment-agnostic.
 - **Process:** Only the diffusion expert is pre-trained in this stage. It is temporarily decoupled from the VLM. A ResNet-50 serves as the image encoder (randomly initialized), and DistilBERT as the language embedding model, with embeddings integrated via FiLM layers.
 - **Rationale:** This stage focuses on building a robust action generation capability, "warming up" the diffusion expert's vast parameters and equipping it with foundational motor skills from diverse cross-embodiment data. It's crucial for optimizing the large diffusion expert.

- **Stage 2: Embodiment-Specific Alignment:**
 - **Goal:** Adapt the learned motor skills and align the abstract vision-language representations from the VLM with the physical constraints of a specific robot embodiment.
 - **Process:** The VLM, the projection layer, and the diffusion expert are jointly trained on *embodiment-specific data*. Importantly, the VLM's visual encoder is frozen. This stage bridges the high-level VLM understanding with the target robot's motor control space.
 - **Rationale:** This stage enables the model to effectively "adapt to its body," allowing it to perform a variety of tasks on a specific robot (e.g., shirt folding) even before task-specific fine-tuning.

- **Stage 3: Task-Specific Adaptation (Post-training):**
 - **Goal:** Refine the model's ability to execute complex, dexterity-demanding downstream tasks and generalize to novel objects.
 - **Process:** The model is fine-tuned on high-quality, expert demonstrations for specific complex tasks.
 - **Key Innovation - Substep Reasoning:** For long-horizon tasks, demonstrations are annotated with detailed sub-step instructions (e.g., breaking "fold the shirt" into "smooth wrinkles," "align sleeves"). Crucially, these sub-step reasonings are not used as direct instructional inputs but as *intermediate language outputs* that the model is compelled to learn and generate. This approach transforms the VLM backbone into an "implicit high-level policy model" that internally interprets task states and guides action generation, eliminating the need for external planners like SayCan. This disentangles the action space, preventing parameter conflicts in shared parameter spaces.

#### 3.3. Real-World Evaluation Setup

Experiments are conducted on four diverse robot configurations: Franka with gripper, Franka with dexterous hand (12 DoF), Bimanual UR5e, and Bimanual AgileX, covering tasks like bin picking, shirt folding, laundry folding, drink pouring, and sorting. The training dataset consists of approximately 100 hours of data across 91 distinct tasks, mostly short-horizon, contrasting with the long-horizon evaluation tasks to highlight generalization.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

DexVLA demonstrates significant advancements across multiple evaluation fronts, consistently outperforming state-of-the-art baselines.

1. **Superior Performance Without Task-Specific Adaptation (After Stage 2 Training):**
 - DexVLA significantly outperforms OpenVLA, Octo, and Diffusion Policy on complex tasks like "shirt folding," "bin picking easy," and "table bussing easy."
 - For "shirt folding," DexVLA achieved a score of 0.92, whereas all baselines struggled to complete any steps, scoring near 0. This highlights DexVLA's ability to learn intricate manipulation skills directly from embodiment-specific alignment without further task-specific fine-tuning.

2. **Efficient Learning of Dexterous Skills on New Embodiments:**
 - When learning skills on *novel embodiments* (Franka with dexterous hand, bimanual UR5e) not encountered during initial pre-training (Stage 1 or 2), DexVLA excelled.
 - With fewer than 100 demonstrations, DexVLA achieved an impressive average score of 0.90 across "drink pouring" and "packing" tasks.
 - It substantially outperformed extensively pre-trained VLA models (OpenVLA, Octo) and task-specific methods (Diffusion Policy), demonstrating its adaptability and data efficiency for acquiring new, dexterous skills on unfamiliar hardware.

3. **Mastery of Complex, Long-Horizon Tasks with Direct Prompting:**
 - DexVLA consistently outperformed OpenVLA, Octo, and π0 on challenging multi-stage tasks like "laundry folding" and "bussing table (hard)," using only direct language prompts.
 - For "laundry folding," DexVLA achieved a score of 0.4, significantly higher than π0's 0.2, showcasing its ability to handle highly complicated scenarios autonomously.
 - It also surpassed π0 by 0.08 points on "table bussing (hard)." Additional experiments showed DexVLA achieving 0.8 on "dryer unloading" and nearly 3x higher scores on "sorting" and "bin picking (hard)" compared to Octo and OpenVLA. This demonstrates DexVLA's capacity to integrate high-level planning internally, negating the need for external policy modules like SayCan.

4. **Ablation Studies Validate Core Components:**
 - **Three-Stage Training:** Ablations confirmed the criticality of each stage. Without Stage 1 pre-training, the model completely failed to learn, highlighting its role in "warming up" the large diffusion expert and building foundational skills. Stage 3 (task-specific adaptation) was crucial for complex tasks, with performance on laundry folding dropping from 0.4 to 0 without it.
 - **Size of Diffusion Expert:** A larger (1B parameter) diffusion expert was shown to be superior. It significantly outperformed smaller experts (93M UNet, 410M Diffusion Expert), achieving 0.92 vs. 0.17 and 0.63 respectively on shirt folding. This indicates that scaling the action expert's capacity is vital for learning diverse and complex motor skills.
 - **Substep Reasoning:** Training without sub-step reasoning led to a drastic performance drop (e.g., from 0.92 to 0.07 on shirt folding, and complete failure in other cases). This confirms that sub-step reasoning helps the model learn a disentangled action space, avoiding parameter conflicts and improving generalization for long-horizon tasks.
 - **Implicit vs. Explicit Sub-step Reasoning:** DexVLA's *implicit* sub-step reasoning (internally generated by the VLM) was found to be superior to explicit SayCan-based planning, achieving 0.70 vs. 0.58 on "bussing table (hard)." This is attributed to adaptive state-space segmentation and effective action space disentanglement.

5. **Visual Generalization and Zero-Shot Cross-Embodiment Transfer:**
 - DexVLA demonstrated effective visual generalization to novel objects and scenes (e.g., unseen shirts, cups, environments) maintaining high success rates.
 - Intriguingly, it achieved 60% success in a zero-shot cross-embodiment transfer scenario, deploying a gripper-trained model to a dexterous hand for bin-picking with novel objects, despite significant visual and kinematic differences. This highlights the robustness of its learned visual and camera-view representations.

6. **Training Cost Efficiency:**
 - Stage 1 training (only the diffusion expert) was found to be 2.78 times faster than training the entire VLA model, emphasizing the cost-efficiency of the proposed curriculum learning strategy.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

DexVLA represents a significant leap forward in the field of general robot control, addressing several critical bottlenecks that hinder the deployment of versatile robotic systems. Its key significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Pioneering a Balanced VLA Architecture:** By advocating for and successfully implementing a *billion-parameter diffusion expert* as a plug-in component, DexVLA rebalances the architectural focus in VLA research. It demonstrates that scaling the action generation capability is as crucial as, if not more important than, scaling the vision-language understanding alone for robust, dexterous manipulation. This paradigm shift could inspire future VLA designs to invest more heavily in the motor control aspect.
- **Enabling Truly End-to-End Robotic Autonomy:** The ability of DexVLA to tackle complex, long-horizon tasks like laundry folding with *direct language prompting and internal sub-step reasoning*, without relying on external high-level planning modules (like SayCan), is a major breakthrough. This moves robotics closer to truly autonomous, intuitive human-robot interaction where robots can interpret abstract commands and self-decompose tasks, simplifying deployment and increasing flexibility in unstructured environments.
- **Enhanced Dexterity and Generalization:** The superior performance across diverse embodiments, novel objects, and complex tasks underscores DexVLA's ability to learn and generalize intricate motor skills. This is vital for real-world applications where robots encounter variability in objects, environments, and robot morphologies. The impressive results on learning dexterous skills on novel embodiments with limited data (fewer than 100 demonstrations) highlight its data efficiency and adaptability.
- **Practical Applicability for Industry:** The involvement of Midea Group and the focus on cost-efficient training and faster inference (60Hz on a single A6000 GPU) signal a strong drive towards practical, deployable solutions. The framework's ability to handle diverse robot types and learn quickly on new embodiments makes it highly relevant for industrial automation, logistics, and potentially household robotics, where versatility and adaptability are paramount.
- **Foundation for Future Generalist Robots:** DexVLA's embodied curriculum learning strategy provides a robust and efficient pathway for training complex neural networks for robotics. This structured approach, mimicking how humans acquire skills, not only boosts performance but also offers a principled method for leveraging heterogeneous robotic data. This could form a foundational training methodology for future generalist robot policies.
- **Impact on Imitation Learning and Diffusion Models:** The work validates the potential of large-scale diffusion models for robotic control, especially when integrated intelligently within a VLA framework. The findings regarding the necessity of a large action expert and the efficacy of sub-step reasoning will inform future research in imitation learning, task decomposition, and behavior generation for robotics.
- **Advancing Zero-Shot Capabilities:** While not fully dexterous, the demonstrated zero-shot cross-embodiment transfer from a gripper to a dexterous hand is a promising indicator of the model's robustness to hardware changes. Further development in this area could significantly reduce the engineering effort required to adapt robots to new tasks or hardware.

In conclusion, DexVLA significantly advances the state-of-the-art in VLA models for general robot control by offering a balanced architectural design, an efficient training paradigm, and unprecedented capabilities in dexterous and long-horizon task execution. Its impact is likely to be felt across academic research and industrial applications, pushing the boundaries of what autonomous robots can achieve.
