# HALO-WA: Hybrid-Attention Latent-Guided Online Reinforcement Learning for World-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> HALO-WA (Hybrid-Attention Latent-Guided Online Reinforcement Learning for World-Action Models) introduces a framework for efficient online adaptation of large, pre-trained World-Action (WA) models, enabling precision control in real-world robotic manipulation tasks. The approach improved average success rates across four real-world precision tasks from 26.4% (WA-base) to 87.1% and significantly reduced human intervention during training.

## Problem

- World-Action (WA) models, while capable of long-horizon actions, suffer from a "precision deployment gap" in real-world tasks, failing in final stages due to accumulated errors.
- Directly applying online reinforcement learning to large WA models is computationally intensive, slow, and prone to overfitting with limited real-world interaction data.
- Existing online adaptation methods for foundation models often do not optimally leverage the distributed latent features critical for precision correction within WA models' unique internal architecture.

## Method

- **HALO-WA** freezes the large WA model backbone and trains a lightweight ==actor-critic adapter== for online action refinement, operating within a cloud-robot collaborative closed-loop training architecture.
- The adapter utilizes a ==hybrid-attention mechanism==, combining self-attention for robot state and WA reference action tokens with cross-attention to selectively read task-relevant information from WA visual and action-generation latent features.
- Online training employs a ==TD3== variant with ==behavior cloning regularization==, learning from robot execution and human interventions to refine chunk-level actions while leveraging the WA model's initial action priors.

## Results

- **HALO-WA** achieved an average success rate of **87.1%** across four real-world precision manipulation tasks, significantly outperforming the WA-base (**26.4%**) and the strongest baseline (RL-token-like adapter, **67.9%**).
- During online training, the human intervention rate rapidly decreased across all tasks, approaching zero in later stages, indicating robust autonomous adaptation.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical role of both the hybrid-attention mechanism and the effective utilization of WA latent-guided representations for achieving high precision.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Effectively leveraging distributed latent features and action priors from pre-trained World-Action models is crucial for achieving high-precision online adaptation in real-world robotic tasks.
> - A hybrid-attention architecture, which intelligently fuses state-action structure with conditional reading from latent memory, is particularly effective for online policy refinement with limited interaction data.
> - Combining online reinforcement learning with behavior regularization, guided by a strong pre-trained prior, can rapidly adapt large models for precise real-world robotic manipulation with minimal human intervention.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has seen advancements with the development of World-Action (WA) models. These models are designed to generate long-horizon action chunks, conditioned on visual observations, robot states, and task specifications, aiming for general-purpose robotic control. Prior research, such as Cosmos Policy, GigaWorld-Policy, Fast-WAM, and Causal World Modeling, has explored coupling video world modeling with action generation, demonstrating capabilities in visuomotor control and planning. However, these models are typically trained offline and exhibit a "precision deployment gap" when applied to real-world precision tasks. This gap arises from the accumulation of small errors in calibration, perception, control, or contact dynamics during execution, leading to failures in the final stages of tasks like alignment or insertion, which offline training alone struggles to address.

Online reinforcement learning (RL) and interactive imitation learning represent a distinct area of research focused on enabling robots to learn and adapt directly within their operational environments. Methods like DAgger, HG-DAgger, and HIL-SERL leverage real-time interaction, expert feedback, or human intervention to refine policies, particularly in contact-rich scenarios where pre-trained policies are vulnerable. More recently, approaches like RL Token have explored combining frozen robotic foundation models, such as Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, with lightweight online RL to improve real-world deployment. These methods often involve extracting compact representations or utilizing reference actions from the foundation models to guide smaller, adaptable policies.

HALO-WA positions itself within this landscape by addressing the specific challenge of adapting WA models for precision manipulation using online RL. It acknowledges the limitations of existing online RL methods when applied to WA models: directly updating large WA models is computationally intensive and prone to overfitting, while training policies from scratch discards valuable pre-trained knowledge. Furthermore, it distinguishes WA models from VLA models, noting that WA models distribute task-relevant information within their variational autoencoder (VAE) or world-action latent features, rather than solely through their final action output or compact tokens. Therefore, HALO-WA seeks to develop a "WA-native adaptation mechanism" that preserves the general capabilities and priors of the WA model while exploiting its distributed latent features to enable precise, online action correction. This work aims to bridge the identified precision deployment gap by enabling fast online adaptation to real-world errors without extensive re-training of the base WA model.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable fast and effective online reinforcement learning for precision control in robotic manipulation, specifically for systems powered by World-Action (WA) models, while preserving the general capabilities and pre-trained knowledge embedded within these models.

This objective is driven by several motivations:

1. **Addressing the Precision Deployment Gap in WA Models:** World-Action models are capable of generating long-horizon action chunks, which is beneficial for general-purpose robotic manipulation. However, when these models are deployed on real robots for precision tasks, they frequently encounter failures in the final stages, such as the last few millimeters of alignment or insertion. These failures are attributed to real-world errors in calibration, perception, control, or contact dynamics, which are difficult to account for adequately during offline training. The motivation is to overcome this "precision deployment gap" by allowing the robot to adapt to these specific, real-world errors through online learning.

2. **Inefficiency of Direct Online RL on Large WA Models:** Directly applying online reinforcement learning to a large-scale WA model is impractical. The process of performing full-parameter updates on such large models is computationally expensive, leads to slow adaptation, and is susceptible to overfitting, especially given the typically limited interaction data available from real-robot training. This motivates the development of a lightweight and efficient adaptation mechanism.

3. **Preservation of Pre-trained WA Priors:** While training a smaller, specialized policy from scratch could be an alternative, it results in the loss of valuable visual, temporal, and behavioral priors that are already encoded within the pre-trained WA model. The motivation is to leverage this existing knowledge as a robust foundation, rather than discarding it, to accelerate learning and improve robustness.

4. **Limitations of Existing Adaptation Mechanisms for WA Models:** Current approaches for combining frozen robotic foundation models with online RL, such as those designed for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, often rely on policy fine-tuning, action residual learning, or compact token readout interfaces. WA models possess a different internal action generation structure, where critical information for end-stage alignment and contact is often distributed across variational autoencoder (VAE) or world-action latent features. Therefore, existing VLA-centric adaptation methods may not optimally exploit these distributed latent features for precise action correction in WA models. This motivates the need for a WA-native adaptation mechanism that can effectively utilize these internal representations.

In summary, the motivation behind HALO-WA is to develop an online learning framework that allows WA models to refine their actions for high-precision tasks in real-world environments. This framework must be efficient, leverage the WA model's inherent knowledge, and be specifically designed to interact with the unique internal architecture of WA models to achieve targeted, fine-grained corrections.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

HALO-WA (Hybrid-Attention Latent-Guided Online Reinforcement Learning for World-Action Models) is designed as a lightweight, online adaptation framework that operates on top of a pre-trained World-Action (WA) model. The core methodology involves freezing the large WA backbone and training a smaller actor-critic adapter to refine its action outputs for precision manipulation tasks.

#### 3.1. Overall Framework

The framework functions within a cloud-robot collaborative closed-loop training architecture:
1. **WA Model Inference:** At each time step $t$, the robot captures multi-view observations $o_t$, its narrow proprioceptive state $s_{r,t}$, and task instructions $p$. The frozen WA model ($\pi_{WA}$) generates a reference action chunk $\tilde{a}_{t:t+H-1}$ of length $H$.
2. **Latent Feature Extraction:** Concurrently, latent features $Z_{WA,t}$ are extracted from the visual encoding and action generation processes of the WA model.
3. **Adapter State Formulation:** The actor-critic adapter uses a comprehensive state representation $s_t$, which combines the narrow robot state $s_{r,t}$, the first $C$ actions of the WA reference action chunk $\tilde{a}_{t:t+C-1}$ (where $C \le H$), and the extracted WA latent features $Z_{WA,t}$.
4. **Refined Action Generation:** The lightweight actor ($\pi_\theta$) of HALO-WA outputs a refined action chunk $a^\pi_{t:t+C-1}$ based on this augmented state $s_t$.
5. **Robot Execution and Data Collection:** The robot executes the refined action chunk. If human intervention occurs due to an error, the human-executed actions override the policy's output. All actually executed actions, along with the corresponding states, rewards, and next states, are stored in a replay buffer.
6. **Cloud-Side Online Training:** The cloud infrastructure performs WA inference, adapter forward inference, and online updates. It utilizes the collected data in the replay buffer to continuously update the actor-critic adapter.

This design aims to preserve the global action prior from the WA model while enabling fine-grained, localized action refinement through the adapter, leveraging the internal WA representations.

#### 3.2. Hybrid-Attention Actor-Critic Adapter

The adapter is engineered with a hybrid-attention mechanism to integrate diverse inputs:
1. **Query-Side Tokens:** The robot state $s_{r,t}$ and the WA reference action chunk $\tilde{a}_{t:t+C-1}$ are encoded as query-side state-action tokens, $q_t$.
2. **Latent Memory Tokens:** The WA visual latent features $Z_{WA,t}$ are encoded as latent memory tokens, $m_t$.

- **Actor Architecture:**
 - **Self-Attention:** The state-action tokens ($q_t$) first pass through a self-attention module. This step models the structural and temporal relationships within the current robot state and the WA reference action chunk, preserving the temporal consistency of the generated refined action chunk.
 - **Cross-Attention:** Subsequently, the actor uses cross-attention, where $q_t$ serve as queries and $m_t$ serve as keys/values. This mechanism allows the actor to selectively retrieve task-relevant information from the WA latent features (e.g., target position, object geometry, potential contact regions), conditioned on the current state and action prior.
 - **Output:** The actor then outputs the refined action chunk $a^\pi_{t:t+C-1}$.

- **Critic Architecture:**
 - The critic employs a similar architecture but additionally receives a *candidate* action chunk $a_{t:t+C-1}$ alongside the state-action tokens $q_t$.
 - These inputs, including the candidate action, form the query-side tokens.
 - Self-attention models the relationship among the state, reference action, and candidate action.
 - Cross-attention reads from the WA latent memory $m_t$.
 - The resulting representation is fed into twin Q heads for value estimation, which helps mitigate Q-value overestimation, a common issue in DRL (e.g., as addressed by TD3).

This hybrid-attention design allows the policy to dynamically leverage WA priors when reliable, and to deviate for targeted corrections when errors occur, enhancing local precision while maintaining the long-horizon capabilities of the WA model.

#### 3.3. Online Training Objective

HALO-WA trains the actor-critic adapter using a variant of TD3 (Twin-Delayed Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient) combined with behavior regularization:
1. **Data Collection:** Transitions, including the current state ($s_t$), the actually executed action chunk ($a_{t:t+C-1}$), reward ($R_t$), next state ($s_{t+C}$), and terminal flag, are stored in a replay buffer. Human intervention actions, when they occur, are recorded as the executed actions to ensure data consistency.
2. **Critic Update:** The critic is updated using a chunk-level TD target ($y_t$). This target is computed using the accumulated reward $R_t$ over the chunk, a discount factor $\gamma$, and the minimum Q-value estimated by the target critics for the next action chunk $a'_{t+C:t+2C-1}$ generated by the target actor. The critic loss ($L_Q$) minimizes the squared difference between the estimated Q-values and $y_t$.
3. **Actor Update:** The actor's objective ($L_\pi$) comprises two terms:
 - **Reinforcement Learning Term:** Maximizes the action value estimated by the critic, driving the policy towards higher-reward behaviors.
 - **Behavior Cloning (BC) Regularization:** A regularization term (weighted by $\lambda_{BC}$) minimizes the squared difference between the actor's output and a reference action $a^{ref}_{t:t+C-1}$. This reference action can originate from the WA model's output, actions from successful trajectories, or human intervention actions. This term stabilizes early online learning, prevents the actor from drifting into unsafe or suboptimal regions when data is sparse or the critic is inaccurate, and maintains adherence to valuable prior behaviors.

This combined objective allows HALO-WA to learn from real successes, failures, and human guidance, refining the WA output while leveraging its initial action priors.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

- **Hardware:** Real-world experiments use a Songling ALOHA dual-arm robot with three RGB cameras (one global, two wrist-view). A cloud-robot setup employs two A800 GPUs for WA inference, adapter training, and policy execution.
- **Warm-up:** Before real-world online training, the actor is warmed up via supervised fine-tuning (SFT) with a BC loss to provide a basic ability to follow WA reference actions, mitigating random actions in early stages.
- **Tasks:** Four real-world precision manipulation tasks (Stick Insertion, Ethernet Insertion, Block Assembly, Power Plug Insertion) and two RoboTwin simulation tasks (Click Bell, Beat Block Hammer) are used for evaluation.
- **Baselines:** HALO-WA is compared against WA-base (a fine-tuned GigaWorld Policy), Probe-Learn-Distill (residual correction), HG-DAgger (interactive imitation learning), and an RL-token-like adapter (compact token compression).
- **Metrics:** Success Rate (SR), Episode Length (EL), and Intervention Rate during training.
- **Online Training Time:** Real-world tasks require 45-75 minutes of online training.
- **Rewards:** Sparse success/failure rewards are used for all tasks.
- **Hyperparameters:** Key parameters include a chunk length of 12 and 14-dimensional actions. Asynchronous rollout-training is used. A demonstration buffer is mixed with online replay data at a 1:1 sampling ratio. UTD ratio is 5, critic-to-actor update ratio is 2, BC regularization coefficient is 50. Learning rates are $2.5 \times 10^{-5}$ for the actor and $3.0 \times 10^{-5}$ for the critic.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of HALO-WA involved real-world precision manipulation tasks and simulation experiments, along with ablation studies.

#### 4.1. Real-World Results

HALO-WA demonstrated substantial improvements in performance on four real-world precision manipulation tasks (Stick Insertion, Ethernet Insertion, Block Assembly, Power Plug Insertion) compared to baseline methods.

- **Success Rate and Episode Length:** HALO-WA achieved an average success rate of 87.1%, which represents a significant increase from the 26.4% achieved by WA-base. The method also reduced the average episode length from 327.7 steps for WA-base to 267.0 steps, indicating more efficient task completion.
 - Specifically, HALO-WA obtained success rates of 94.3% (Stick Insertion), 97.1% (Ethernet Insertion), 71.4% (Block Assembly), and 85.7% (Power Plug Insertion).
- **Comparison with Baselines:**
 - HALO-WA consistently outperformed other adaptation baselines. Probe-Learn-Distill achieved an average success rate of 52.1%, HG-DAgger reached 62.9%, and the RL-token-like adapter, identified as the strongest baseline, achieved 67.9%.
 - The paper attributes HALO-WA's superior performance to its capacity for chunk-level action refinement and its effective utilization of distributed WA latent features, which provides more comprehensive information compared to local residual corrections or compact token representations.
- **Intervention Rate:** During online training, HALO-WA rapidly decreased the human intervention rate across all tasks. The intervention rate approached zero in the later stages of training, suggesting that the policy progressively learned autonomous end-stage correction without human assistance. This indicates the framework's ability to adapt and improve independently over time.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies

Two sets of ablation studies were conducted on the Stick Insertion task to analyze the contributions of key components: hybrid attention and WA latent-guided representation.

- **Effect of Hybrid Attention:**
 - Direct concatenation of features resulted in a 28.6% success rate, indicating that a naive combination of high-dimensional inputs is ineffective.
 - A CNN encoder, which compresses WA latents, improved the success rate to 74.3%, demonstrating that WA latents contain useful spatial information. However, static compression limited its performance.
 - Cross-attention alone achieved 57.1%, and self-attention alone achieved 62.9%.
 - Hybrid attention, as implemented in HALO-WA, achieved the highest success rate of 94.3%. This result supports the design choice of combining self-attention for modeling state-action structure with cross-attention for conditional reading of latent memory, particularly under limited real-world interaction data.
- **Necessity of WA Latent-Guided Representation:**
 - Variants using a raw image encoder (25.7% SR) or no latent input (22.9% SR) performed worse than WA-base, indicating that learning visual features from scratch or removing latent information is insufficient within short online training periods.
 - Using WA intermediate features resulted in a 57.1% success rate, confirming the utility of internal WA representations but showing them to be less effective than the action-generation latents used by HALO-WA.
 - The full HALO-WA method, leveraging WA latent features, achieved 94.3% success rate, affirming the critical role of effectively utilizing WA latent features for precision improvement.

#### 4.3. Simulation Experiments

To facilitate reproducibility, HALO-WA was also evaluated on two RoboTwin simulation tasks (Click Bell and Beat Block Hammer) without human intervention.

- **Click Bell:** HALO-WA improved the success rate from 54.0% for WA-base to 97.0%, a relative improvement of 79.6%. Episode length was reduced by 33.8%.
- **Beat Block Hammer:** For this more complex task, HALO-WA improved the success rate from 22.0% to 45.0%, representing a 104.5% relative improvement. Episode length was reduced by 31.6%.

These simulation results indicate that HALO-WA can achieve online adaptation and improve performance even in the absence of human corrective signals, relying solely on autonomous exploration.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

HALO-WA contributes to the field of robotic manipulation by addressing the precision deployment gap inherent in World-Action (WA) models. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enabling Real-World Precision for WA Models:** The work demonstrates a method for WA models to overcome their limitations in executing fine-grained, contact-rich tasks in real-world environments. By substantially improving success rates in precision manipulation tasks (e.g., insertion, assembly) with minimal online training, HALO-WA facilitates the practical deployment of these complex models where high accuracy is critical.

2. **Efficient and Lightweight Online Adaptation:** HALO-WA provides an efficient approach to integrate online reinforcement learning with large, pre-trained WA models. By freezing the computationally intensive WA backbone and training only a lightweight actor-critic adapter, it avoids the high computational costs, slow adaptation, and overfitting issues associated with full-parameter updates of large foundation models. This efficiency makes online adaptation more feasible for real-world robotic systems with limited training budgets.

3. **Novel Utilization of Internal Model Representations:** The framework introduces a "WA-native" adaptation mechanism that effectively leverages the distributed latent features and action priors generated by the WA model. This approach is distinct from methods designed for VLA models and highlights the importance of understanding and exploiting the internal architecture of specific foundation models for optimal adaptation. The hybrid-attention design specifically demonstrates a method for intelligently fusing disparate information sources (robot state, WA reference actions, and WA latent features).

4. **Robustness and Generalization:** The ability to learn from real-world interaction, including human interventions and autonomous exploration, allows HALO-WA to adapt to system-specific errors (calibration, perception, contact dynamics) that are difficult to model offline. The consistent performance improvements across diverse real-world and simulation tasks suggest a degree of robustness in its adaptation capability.

5. **Accelerating Robotic Learning:** By using the WA model as a strong action prior and employing behavior cloning regularization, HALO-WA can stabilize early online training and guide exploration, potentially accelerating the learning process compared to training a policy from scratch. The rapid reduction in human intervention rates observed during training supports this aspect.

6. **Future Research Directions:** The paper identifies several limitations, such as the focus on insertion/alignment tasks, reliance on the WA model's initial competence, and the absence of explicit contact force or tactile feedback modeling. These limitations delineate clear avenues for future research, including evaluating generalization across more diverse tasks and object types, enhancing robustness in scenarios where the base WA model is less reliable, and incorporating multi-modal sensing for more complex contact-rich manipulations.

Overall, HALO-WA represents a step in making large-scale world-action models more practical and reliable for real-world precision robotic manipulation, thereby extending their utility beyond coarse motion planning to fine-grained, robust execution. The provision of supplementary simulation experiments and code release contributes to the reproducibility and further development of this research direction.
