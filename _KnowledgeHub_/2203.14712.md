# Assembly101: A Large-Scale Multi-View Video Dataset for Understanding Procedural Activities

> [!summary] Summary
> Assembly101, a large-scale multi-view video dataset, provides 513 hours of synchronized static and egocentric video of unscripted assembly and disassembly tasks, complete with multi-granular action labels, 3D hand poses, and novel mistake annotations. This resource aims to advance research in action understanding and procedural learning for augmented reality applications.

## Problem

- Existing multi-step activity datasets are often domain-specific (e.g., kitchen) or small-scale for assembly tasks, limiting generalization to diverse real-world scenarios.
- Many datasets lack synchronized multi-view recordings (static and egocentric), detailed 3D hand pose annotations, and specific labels for procedural mistakes and corrections, which are crucial for embodied AR systems.
- Instructional videos from online platforms are often 'produced' with mixed viewpoints, fast-forwarding, or unrelated narration, making them unsuitable for continuous, unscripted observation required by smart assistants.

## Method

- Developed Assembly101, a dataset featuring 513 hours of synchronized video from 8 static and 4 egocentric cameras, capturing 53 participants performing unscripted assembly and disassembly of 101 unique toys.
- Provided multi-granular annotations including over 1 million fine-grained action segments, 100,000 coarse action segments, 18 million 3D hand poses, mistake labels ('correct', 'mistake', 'correction'), and participant skill levels.
- Established benchmarks for fine-grained action recognition and anticipation, coarse temporal action segmentation, and a novel mistake detection task using state-of-the-art baselines.

## Results

- Static camera views consistently outperformed egocentric views across all tasks, achieving 16.2% higher overall accuracy for action recognition and 6.5% higher mean frame-wise accuracy for temporal action segmentation.
- Action recognition performance showed a substantial 37% gap between head and tail action classes, with object recognition in tail classes experiencing a 42% drop.
- The novel mistake detection task proved challenging, yielding a recall of 46.6% for 'mistakes' and 29.6% for 'corrections' using video features, with further degradation in early prediction settings.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A significant domain gap exists between static and egocentric viewpoints, indicating challenges for cross-view transfer learning and requiring robust approaches for AR applications.
> - Procedural activities exhibit a pronounced long-tail distribution of actions, particularly for objects, making recognition and anticipation of less frequent actions difficult.
> - 3D hand pose information demonstrates effectiveness in encoding movement (higher verb accuracy) and less sensitivity to domain gaps from pre-training compared to appearance-based video features, highlighting its complementary value.
