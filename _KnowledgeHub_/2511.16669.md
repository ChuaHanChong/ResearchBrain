# Video-as-Answer: Predict and Generate Next Video Event with Joint-GRPO

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from City University of Hong Kong and Kuaishou Technology developed VANS, a model for Video-Next-Event Prediction (VNEP) that generates dynamic video answers to contextual questions, moving beyond text-only responses. Utilizing a novel Joint-GRPO reinforcement learning strategy to align a VLM and VDM, VANS demonstrates superior performance in generating semantically accurate and visually coherent videos, achieving a lower Fréchet Video Distance (78.32) and higher CLIP-V (0.8021) on procedural benchmarks compared to existing methods.

## Problem

- Existing next-event prediction (NEP) primarily offers textual answers, failing to visually demonstrate physical actions, spatial layouts, or temporal sequences, which are crucial for "how-to" scenarios.
- Current video generation models are largely confined to entertainment, underutilizing video's capacity for practical, intuitive explanations.
- Cascaded Vision-Language Models (VLM) and Video Diffusion Models (VDM) often suffer from semantic-to-visual misalignment, leading to text descriptions that are visually unexecutable or inconsistent.

## Method

- Developed **VANS** (Video-as-Answer ==Next-Event Predictor== and Generator System), which tightly integrates a Vision-Language Model (VLM) for instruction-grounded reasoning with a Video Diffusion Model (VDM) for generating dynamic video responses.
- Implemented Joint-GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization), a two-stage reinforcement learning strategy, to effectively align the VLM and VDM, ensuring the VLM produces visualization-friendly captions and the VDM generates context-faithful videos.
- Constructed VANS-Data-100K, a novel large-scale dataset comprising 100,000 video-question-answer triplets, tailored for training and evaluating models in Video-Next-Event Prediction.

## Results

- VANS, utilizing Joint-GRPO, established state-of-the-art performance on both procedural and predictive VNEP benchmarks, consistently surpassing cascaded and unified baseline models.
- Quantitatively, VANS (Joint-GRPO) achieved a Fréchet Video Distance (FVD) of 78.32 and a CLIP-V of **0.8021** on procedural tasks, a substantial improvement over the best cascaded baseline's FVD of 110.54 and CLIP-V of **0.7102**.
- Ablation studies validated the efficacy of the Joint-GRPO strategy's two-stage optimization and joint reward functions, demonstrating its necessity for robust VLM-VDM alignment and preventing issues like reward hacking.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Shifting from textual to video-based answers (VNEP) fundamentally enhances AI's utility for tasks requiring visual demonstration, such as procedural learning and "how-to" guides.
> - Overcoming the semantic-to-visual misalignment between reasoning (VLM) and generation (VDM) is crucial for producing coherent and faithful video responses.
> - A two-stage reinforcement learning strategy, Joint-GRPO, effectively co-adapts the VLM and VDM, enabling them to mutually reinforce each other's capabilities and bridge the cross-modal gap.
