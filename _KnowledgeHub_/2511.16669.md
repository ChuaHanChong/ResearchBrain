# Video-as-Answer: Predict and Generate Next Video Event with Joint-GRPO

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from City University of Hong Kong and Kuaishou Technology developed VANS, a model for Video-Next-Event Prediction (VNEP) that generates dynamic video answers to contextual questions, moving beyond text-only responses. Utilizing a novel Joint-GRPO reinforcement learning strategy to align a VLM and VDM, VANS demonstrates superior performance in generating semantically accurate and visually coherent videos, achieving a lower Fréchet Video Distance (78.32) and higher CLIP-V (0.8021) on procedural benchmarks compared to existing methods.

## Problem

- Existing next-event prediction (NEP) primarily offers textual answers, failing to visually demonstrate physical actions, spatial layouts, or temporal sequences, which are crucial for "how-to" scenarios.
- Current video generation models are largely confined to entertainment, underutilizing video's capacity for practical, intuitive explanations.
- Cascaded Vision-Language Models (VLM) and Video Diffusion Models (VDM) often suffer from semantic-to-visual misalignment, leading to text descriptions that are visually unexecutable or inconsistent.

## Method

- Developed **VANS** (Video-as-Answer ==Next-Event Predictor== and Generator System), which tightly integrates a Vision-Language Model (VLM) for instruction-grounded reasoning with a Video Diffusion Model (VDM) for generating dynamic video responses.
- Implemented Joint-GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization), a two-stage reinforcement learning strategy, to effectively align the VLM and VDM, ensuring the VLM produces visualization-friendly captions and the VDM generates context-faithful videos.
- Constructed VANS-Data-100K, a novel large-scale dataset comprising 100,000 video-question-answer triplets, tailored for training and evaluating models in Video-Next-Event Prediction.

## Results

- VANS, utilizing Joint-GRPO, established state-of-the-art performance on both procedural and predictive VNEP benchmarks, consistently surpassing cascaded and unified baseline models.
- Quantitatively, VANS (Joint-GRPO) achieved a Fréchet Video Distance (FVD) of 78.32 and a CLIP-V of **0.8021** on procedural tasks, a substantial improvement over the best cascaded baseline's FVD of 110.54 and CLIP-V of **0.7102**.
- Ablation studies validated the efficacy of the Joint-GRPO strategy's two-stage optimization and joint reward functions, demonstrating its necessity for robust VLM-VDM alignment and preventing issues like reward hacking.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Shifting from textual to video-based answers (VNEP) fundamentally enhances AI's utility for tasks requiring visual demonstration, such as procedural learning and "how-to" guides.
> - Overcoming the semantic-to-visual misalignment between reasoning (VLM) and generation (VDM) is crucial for producing coherent and faithful video responses.
> - A two-stage reinforcement learning strategy, Joint-GRPO, effectively co-adapts the VLM and VDM, enabling them to mutually reinforce each other's capabilities and bridge the cross-modal gap.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of artificial intelligence has witnessed a revolution driven by large language models (LLMs), which have found impactful applications across diverse real-world domains such as healthcare and education. However, the paper identifies a significant disparity: while text-based generative AI thrives in practical utility, video generation models remain largely confined to entertainment or highly specialized tasks. This work directly addresses this "missed opportunity" by leveraging video's unique capacity to convey rich, dynamic physical-world information that is often difficult to communicate through text alone.

The research builds upon and significantly extends the established task of **Next-Event Prediction (NEP)**. Traditionally, NEP involves taking an input video and a procedural or predictive question to forecast the subsequent event, with the answer modality being exclusively textual. Existing efforts in textual NEP, supported by benchmarks like VLEP, MVP, and V1-33K, have utilized various techniques from event understanding and commonsense reasoning to advanced Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and reinforcement learning. However, a fundamental limitation persists: these models can "tell" what happens next but cannot "show" it.

This paper pioneers **Video-Next-Event Prediction (VNEP)**, a novel paradigm that fundamentally shifts the answer modality from textual descriptions to dynamic video demonstrations. This shift from "telling" to "showing" is crucial for tasks requiring intuitive and customized answers, particularly in procedural learning where spatial layout, motion, and temporal ordering are paramount (e.g., demonstrating how to tie a knot or perform a cooking step). Unlike simple video extension tasks, which merely predict future frames based on spatio-temporal patterns, VNEP demands deep event-conditioned reasoning, requiring the model to comprehend multimodal input, reason causally or procedurally, and then generate a video that is both visually coherent and semantically faithful to the inferred event.

The authors highlight the limitations of current approaches to VNEP:
1. **Cascaded Pipelines:** A straightforward approach combining a VLM for prediction and a Video Diffusion Model (VDM) for generation often suffers from "semantic-to-visual misalignment." The VLM might produce linguistically correct text that is visually unrealistic or unexecutable by the VDM, leading to divergent outputs.
2. **Unified Models:** While attempting to align understanding and generation within a single model, these often face a capability trade-off, excelling in one aspect at the expense of the other.

The paper's contribution to the broader landscape is therefore multi-faceted: it introduces a critical new task (VNEP), provides a novel architectural solution (VANS) that intelligently integrates specialized models, proposes a sophisticated training methodology (Joint-GRPO) to overcome inter-model alignment challenges, and establishes a foundational dataset (VANS-Data-100K) necessary for advancing research in this area. This work positions itself at the intersection of multimodal AI, generative models, and reinforcement learning, pushing towards more intuitive and practically applicable AI solutions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind this research stems from the inherent superiority of video over text for conveying certain types of information, especially those involving physical actions, spatial relationships, and temporal sequences. While language models have excelled in abstract reasoning and information retrieval, they fall short when tasked with demonstrating "how-to" scenarios or visualizing "what-if" predictions in a tangible, intuitive manner. The paper highlights this gap, citing examples like teaching someone to tie a tie using only text versus a video demonstration. The current confinement of video generation to entertainment is seen as a significant missed opportunity.

Based on this motivation, the key objectives of the research are:

1. **Pioneer Video-Next-Event Prediction (VNEP):** The primary objective is to introduce and formalize VNEP as a novel task that extends next-event reasoning from generating textual descriptions to producing dynamic video demonstrations. This paradigm shift aims to unlock more intuitive and customized answers, particularly beneficial for procedural learning and creative exploration.
2. **Develop a Robust Model for VNEP (VANS):** To address the complex requirements of VNEP, the authors aim to design and implement a model, named VANS, capable of:
 - Comprehending multimodal input (video and question).
 - Performing instruction-conditioned reasoning to predict the next event.
 - Generating video responses that maintain both visual consistency with the input and semantic faithfulness to the predicted event.
3. **Overcome Semantic-to-Visual Misalignment:** A critical objective is to resolve the fundamental challenge faced by cascaded VLM-VDM pipelines, where the VLM's text output may be unsuitable for effective video generation by the VDM. The goal is to achieve a tight integration where both models mutually reinforce each other's capabilities.
4. **Introduce an Effective Alignment Strategy (Joint-GRPO):** To achieve the necessary alignment and synergy between the VLM and VDM, the paper aims to propose and implement a novel reinforcement learning strategy called Joint-GRPO. This strategy is designed to orchestrate both models as a cohesive unit, driven by a shared reward that optimizes for both semantic accuracy in captions and visual plausibility in generated videos.
5. **Create a Dedicated VNEP Dataset (VANS-Data-100K):** Recognizing the absence of suitable datasets for this novel task, a crucial objective is to construct a large-scale, high-quality dataset of video-question-answer triplets (VANS-Data-100K) to facilitate supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning for VNEP models.

In essence, the research aims to move beyond merely describing future events to visually *demonstrating* them, thereby significantly enhancing the utility and intuitiveness of AI in understanding and interacting with the physical world.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The VANS (Video-as-Answer Next-Event Predictor and Generator System) model employs a sophisticated methodology to tackle the challenges of VNEP, primarily by integrating a Vision-Language Model (VLM) with a Video Diffusion Model (VDM) through a novel reinforcement learning strategy called Joint-GRPO.

**Overall VANS Architecture:**
VANS operates as a tightly integrated system:
- **VLM (Qwen2.5-VL-3B initialized):** Receives the tokenized input question and high-level visual features (from a ViT encoder) extracted from the input video. Its primary role is to perform instruction-grounded reasoning, producing a textual caption that describes the predicted next event. This caption acts as the semantic guide for the VDM.
- **VDM (Wan-2.1-1.3B initialized):** Takes the VLM's generated caption as semantic conditioning and low-level visual cues. These cues are derived from *n* sampled input frames, tokenized by a VAE encoder, and concatenated into the VDM's conditioning latent space. The VDM then generates the novel video frames for the predicted event, aiming for fine-grained visual correspondence and scene consistency.

**Addressing the Semantic-to-Visual Gap with Joint-GRPO:**
The core innovation of VANS lies in its **Joint-GRPO** (Group Relative Policy Optimization) strategy, a post-training reinforcement learning approach designed to align the VLM and VDM. This contrasts with independent optimization or naive joint training, both of which suffer from significant limitations (semantic-visual misalignment or training instability due to reward attribution ambiguity). Joint-GRPO co-steers the VLM and VDM through a structured **two-stage optimization process**:

**Stage 1: Visualization-Friendly VLM Tuning**
- **Objective:** To train the VLM (π_VLM) to produce captions that are not only semantically accurate but also visually plausible and easily executable by the VDM. The VDM is kept frozen in this stage.
- **Process:** For a given input video and question, the VLM generates a group of *G* candidate textual captions. Each caption is then fed to the frozen VDM to generate a corresponding output video.
- **Joint Reward (r1):** A composite reward guides the VLM:
 - `r_f` (Format Reward): Encourages adherence to a "reason-then-answer" template.
 - `r_t1` (Text Fidelity): Measures the ROUGE-L similarity between the generated caption and the ground-truth caption, ensuring linguistic accuracy.
 - `r_v1` (Video Fidelity): Evaluates the visual coherence and quality of the VDM-generated video (conditioned on the VLM's caption) against the ground-truth video using CLIP Similarity.
- **Impact:** This stage forces the VLM to internalize the VDM's visual synthesis capabilities and limitations, leading to captions that are "visualization-friendly."

**Stage 2: Context-Faithful VDM Adaptation**
- **Objective:** To adapt the VDM (π_VDM) to faithfully render the (now improved) captions from the VLM while meticulously preserving visual consistency with the input video context. The VLM is frozen as an anchor model in this stage.
- **Process:** The VLM from Stage 1 generates a high-quality "anchor caption" (s_anchor). This caption, along with the input video's VAE tokens, conditions the VDM to generate *G* output videos.
- **Joint Reward (r2):** A composite reward guides the VDM:
 - `r_v2` (Video Fidelity): Measures the visual quality and consistency of the generated video with the ground-truth video using CLIP Similarity.
 - `r_c2` (Semantic Alignment): Evaluates the semantic consistency between the generated output video and the VLM's anchor caption using CLIPScore.
- **Impact:** This stage compels the VDM to strictly adhere to the event described in the anchor caption, preventing reward hacking (e.g., generating static frames) and ensuring faithful, contextually coherent video generation.

**VANS-Data-100K Dataset Creation:**
Given the lack of suitable datasets for VNEP, the authors constructed VANS-Data-100K:
- **Data Sources:** High-resolution procedural videos from COIN and YouCook2, and predictive videos from general-scene datasets (e.g., ActivityNet, V1-33K, Video-Holmes) and YouTube.
- **Curation Pipeline (using Gemini-2.5-Flash):**
 1. **Raw Data Collection:** Gathering videos for both procedural and predictive scenarios.
 2. **Shot Split:** Segmenting raw videos into coherent clips (using ground-truth timestamps for procedural, shot-boundary detection for predictive), filtering clips shorter than 3 seconds.
 3. **Clip Selection:** Identifying optimal 3-5 second clips that best represent an action or event.
 4. **QA Pair Generation:** Creating diverse questions, chain-of-thought reasoning, and ground-truth text/video answers for each (input video, question) pair, with self-checks for logical soundness.
- **Dataset Composition:** 30K procedural and 70K predictive samples. A subset of 1K high-quality samples is manually curated for the RL post-training phase.

This comprehensive methodology, integrating specialized models with a carefully designed RL strategy and a bespoke dataset, forms the backbone of VANS's ability to perform complex VNEP tasks effectively.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of VANS demonstrates its superior performance across both quantitative and qualitative metrics for Video-Next-Event Prediction (VNEP).

**Quantitative Comparisons:**
- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** VANS (Joint-GRPO) consistently achieves the highest scores across all evaluated metrics on both procedural and predictive benchmarks.
 - For **procedural benchmarks**, VANS (Joint-GRPO) achieves a ROUGE-L of 0.3631 (textual accuracy) and a CLIP-V of 0.8021 (video visual coherence), significantly outperforming the best cascaded baseline (Gemini-FilmWeaver: ROUGE-L 0.2802, CLIP-V 0.7102) and the unified model (Omni-Video: ROUGE-L 0.1075, CLIP-V 0.6293). The Fréchet Video Distance (FVD), a measure of video generation quality, is also substantially lower (78.32 vs. Gemini-FilmWeaver's 110.54), indicating higher quality and realism.
 - Similar trends are observed for **predictive benchmarks**, with VANS (Joint-GRPO) achieving ROUGE-L 0.3058, FVD 86.85, and CLIP-V 0.7872, again demonstrating clear superiority.
- **Effectiveness of Joint-GRPO:** The post-training Joint-GRPO strategy provides a significant boost over the supervised fine-tuned (SFT) version of VANS. For instance, on procedural benchmarks, ROUGE-L increases from 0.2812 (SFT) to 0.3631 (Joint-GRPO), and CLIP-V from 0.7655 to 0.8021. This clearly validates the effectiveness of the proposed RL strategy in aligning the VLM and VDM.
- **Baseline Limitations:** Video extension models like Video-GPT perform poorly in textual consistency (low CLIP-T score) as they lack event-conditioned reasoning. Cascaded pipelines show better textual prediction but struggle with video quality and semantic alignment due to the VLM-VDM disconnect.

**Qualitative Comparisons:**
- Visual examples highlight the weaknesses of baseline models, which often misinterpret events (e.g., misinterpreting a quarrel as a fight) or generate visually inconsistent characters and scenes.
- VANS (SFT) improves reasoning but still exhibits limitations, such as "semantic hallucination" (VLM generating non-existent information) and semantic-visual misalignment (text describing one action, video showing a subtly different one, like "adding cheese" vs. "pouring").
- VANS (Joint-GRPO) effectively resolves these issues, demonstrating precise event prediction and faithful, high-quality video generation. For example, it correctly translates "sprinkle cheese" into a visual action that matches "shower," showcasing fine-grained alignment.

**Ablation Studies:**
The ablation studies rigorously validate the design choices of Joint-GRPO:
- **Joint vs. Isolated Optimization:** Joint-GRPO significantly outperforms variants where GRPO is applied only to the VLM or VDM, or where separately optimized models are simply cascaded. This confirms that co-adaptation is crucial for bridging the semantic-to-visual gap.
- **Staged Training:** The two-stage design is critical. An "all-in-one" variant suffers from training instability and ambiguous reward signals. Relying only on Stage 1 (VLM tuning) leads to captions and videos that are less coherent.
- **Reward Component Analysis:** Each component of the joint reward functions (`r_f`, `r_t1`, `r_v1` in Stage 1; `r_v2`, `r_c2` in Stage 2) is shown to be essential. Removing `r_t1` (text fidelity) reduces caption accuracy, while removing `r_v1` (video fidelity) harms visual consistency. In Stage 2, omitting `r_c2` (semantic alignment) leads to "reward hacking" (e.g., static frames), and removing `r_v2` (video fidelity) degrades output coherence.

**Generalization Capabilities:**
- **Multi-Future Prediction:** VANS demonstrates the ability to generate distinct, contextually appropriate video answers from the same input video, conditioned on different hypothetical questions (e.g., showing a realistic "coughing" reaction vs. an exaggerated "smoke exhaling" reaction for getting burned). This highlights its capacity to explore multiple potential futures.
- **Reasoning Image-to-Video (I2V) Generation:** The model successfully generalizes to I2V tasks by treating a single image as a static video. For example, given an image of a banana and the instruction "leave the bananas for a week," VANS accurately predicts and visualizes the temporal evolution of the banana ripening, outperforming baselines.

**Human Evaluation:**
A human evaluation involving 30 participants confirmed the objective findings. VANS (Joint-GRPO) received the highest ratings across semantic correctness, visual consistency, and overall satisfaction, further reinforcing its superior performance and human-perceived quality over all baselines and its SFT-only version.

In summary, the main findings unequivocally establish VANS with Joint-GRPO as the leading solution for VNEP, driven by its innovative architecture, effective alignment strategy, and high-quality dataset.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research marks a significant advancement in the field of multimodal AI, with profound implications across various domains. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Pioneering a New Paradigm for AI Interaction:** By introducing Video-Next-Event Prediction (VNEP), this work fundamentally shifts how AI can respond to user queries, moving beyond textual descriptions to dynamic visual demonstrations. This "show, don't just tell" approach makes AI responses inherently more intuitive, comprehensive, and engaging, particularly for tasks involving physical actions or visual predictions. This could transform human-AI interaction interfaces in numerous applications.

2. **Revolutionizing Procedural Learning and "How-To" Guides:** The ability of VANS to generate customized video answers for procedural questions has immense potential for education, training, and everyday tasks. Imagine an AI assistant that not only tells you "how to tie a Windsor knot" but actually *shows* you, adapting the demonstration to the specific tie configuration, color, and tightness from your input video. This personalization and clarity can dramatically improve learning efficacy and user satisfaction compared to generic text or static image instructions.

3. **Enhancing Creative Content Generation:** For content creators, game developers, or animators, VANS could serve as a powerful tool for ideation and visualization. By predicting and generating subsequent video events, it can aid in storyboarding, visualizing hypothetical scenarios, or even creating interactive narratives, pushing the boundaries of automated content creation beyond simple text-to-video generation.

4. **Advancing Multimodal AI Integration and Alignment:** The Joint-GRPO strategy offers a robust solution for integrating and aligning distinct specialized AI models (VLM for reasoning, VDM for generation). This addresses a pervasive challenge in multimodal AI, where semantic understanding often struggles to translate seamlessly into high-fidelity generation. The success of Joint-GRPO provides a blueprint for future research in orchestrating complex AI systems to achieve coherent cross-modal outputs.

5. **Foundational Contribution to Research:** The introduction of VNEP as a task, coupled with the creation of the VANS-Data-100K dataset, provides a critical foundation for future research in this area. Researchers now have a benchmark, a robust methodology, and a dedicated dataset to build upon, accelerating progress in video understanding, next-event prediction, and multimodal generative AI. The release of code further ensures reproducibility and facilitates community engagement.

6. **Real-World Application Potential through Industry Partnership:** Backed by Kuaishou Technology, this research has direct avenues for real-world deployment. Kuaishou's platform, with its vast user base and focus on short-form video content, could integrate VNEP capabilities to offer innovative features like personalized video tutorials, interactive content creation tools, or advanced AI-powered content recommendations, thereby enhancing user experience and driving platform engagement.

7. **Broader Impact on Intelligent Systems:** Beyond specific applications, the principles and techniques developed in this work could contribute to more generally intelligent systems. The ability of VANS to perform complex reasoning, synthesize novel visual content, and adapt to diverse contexts brings us closer to AI that can perceive, understand, and interact with the physical world in a more human-like, intuitive manner. This could have implications for robotics (e.g., predicting consequences of actions), virtual assistants, and even scientific simulation.

In conclusion, "Video-as-Answer" is not just an incremental improvement but a conceptual leap in how AI can serve human needs, promising to unlock new possibilities for intuitive interaction, personalized learning, and creative expression through dynamic video.
