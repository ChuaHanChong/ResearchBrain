# Mimic Intent, Not Just Trajectories

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework called MINT (Mimic Intent, Not just Trajectories) introduces a Spectrally Disentangled Action Tokenizer to explicitly separate behavioral intent from execution details in robot manipulation, achieving state-of-the-art performance across multiple benchmarks and enabling one-shot skill transfer through intent token injection.

## Problem

- Existing imitation learning methods, particularly Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, struggle with generalization and skill transfer due to overfitting to superficial correlations in demonstration data.
- These models tend to mimic raw action trajectories without explicitly capturing the underlying high-level behavioral intent.
- Previous action tokenization approaches primarily function as data compression mechanisms, often lacking explicit constraints to imbue tokens with semantically interpretable meaning like intent.

## Method

- The **MINT** framework introduces a ==Spectrally Disentangled Action Tokenizer (SDAT)== that learns multi-scale discrete action representations by leveraging frequency-domain decomposition and scale-wise spectral reconstruction.
- SDAT explicitly forces the coarsest action tokens to capture low-frequency global behavioral intent, while finer scales encode high-frequency execution details, enforced through a ==scale-wise spectral loss==.
- A MINT policy learns to generate actions by ==autoregressively predicting== these multi-scale tokens, and an ==intent-based action ensemble== during inference ensures temporal consistency by weighting predictions based on intent compatibility.

## Results

- MINT achieves state-of-the-art or superior performance across LIBERO, CALVIN, and MetaWorld benchmarks, with the MINT-4B variant **nearly tripling** success rates on challenging MetaWorld tasks and demonstrating robust stability on CALVIN.
- The framework exhibits strong generalization to various environmental distribution shifts on LIBERO-PLUS and successfully performs one-shot skill transfer in both simulation and real-world robot experiments via intent token injection.
- Ablation studies confirm the efficacy of scale-wise spectral decomposition in disentangling intent, leading to performance gains (e.g., **93.4%** on LIBERO-Long) and improved learning efficiency compared to baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly disentangling low-frequency behavioral intent from high-frequency execution details in action representations significantly improves generalization and robustness of robot manipulation policies.
> - Injecting a learned intent token from a single demonstration enables effective one-shot skill transfer to novel tasks, layouts, or extended-horizon sequences, outperforming language-based transfer.
> - Scale-wise spectral decomposition in action tokenization is crucial for creating semantically meaningful and organized latent spaces for behavioral intent, fostering more interpretable and adaptable robot control.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Imitation learning (IL) has demonstrated capabilities in robot manipulation, particularly with the advent of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. These models map visual observations and language instructions directly to control commands, achieving proficiency in tasks such as object rearrangement and dexterous manipulation. However, a limitation of many existing VLA models is their struggle to adapt to environmental changes and transfer skills effectively to novel situations. This is attributed by the authors to a tendency to mimic raw trajectories without capturing the underlying behavioral intent, leading to overfitting to superficial correlations in demonstration data.

The concept of action tokenization has emerged as an approach to address this by transforming continuous action trajectories into discrete latent representations. This aligns with the idea that action semantics can be structured and compositional. While some tokenization methods exist, they often function primarily as compression mechanisms without explicit constraints to imbue tokens with interpretable semantic meaning, such as intent. Even hierarchical tokenization approaches have not consistently enforced that coarse-grained representations capture high-level intent.

This work, "Mimic Intent, Not Just Trajectories" (MINT), positions itself within this landscape by proposing an explicit method to disentangle behavioral intent from execution details. It aims to advance action tokenization beyond mere data compression by introducing a mechanism that structurally separates low-frequency, global behavioral intent from high-frequency, fine-grained execution information within action representations. By doing so, MINT seeks to improve the generalization, robustness, and transferability of learned robot manipulation policies, addressing a core limitation in current state-of-the-art imitation learning methodologies.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the generalization and skill transfer capabilities of imitation learning agents, particularly those based on Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. The authors identify a core limitation in existing VLA approaches: they learn to replicate observed trajectories as raw signals without explicitly modeling the underlying reasons or "intent" behind those actions. This deficiency leads to policies that overfit to specific demonstration contexts and exhibit poor adaptation to environmental variations or novel task instances.

To address this, the key motivations and objectives driving the MINT framework are:

1. **Disentangling Intent from Execution**: The central motivation is to explicitly separate high-level behavioral intent from low-level execution details within an end-to-end imitation learning framework. The authors hypothesize that low-frequency components of an action trajectory represent global intent (e.g., "pick up," "move forward"), while high-frequency components encode fine-grained movements and reactive adjustments.
2. **Developing Semantically Meaningful Action Tokens**: A specific objective is to move beyond action tokenization methods that primarily function as data compression. MINT aims to create discrete action tokens where the coarsest scale explicitly captures global intent, and finer scales encode residual execution information. This is intended to align the token space with interpretable behavioral concepts.
3. **Improving Generalization and Robustness**: By disentangling intent, the framework seeks to enable policies to generalize more robustly to environmental disturbances, changes in object layouts, and variations in task specifications, as the core intent remains stable despite execution-level perturbations.
4. **Facilitating Efficient Skill Transfer**: A significant motivation is to enable efficient, ideally one-shot, transfer of learned skills. The abstract "Intent token" is hypothesized to provide a compact and reusable task specification, allowing a policy to adapt to novel tasks or environments by simply injecting the intent from a single demonstration.
5. **Boosting Learning Efficiency**: The progressive coarse-to-fine structure of the action tokens is intended to induce an intent-to-execution reasoning process. This hierarchical prediction in the latent space is expected to improve sample efficiency during training and stabilize the generation of long-horizon action sequences.
6. **Ensuring Physical Executability**: While abstracting intent, the methodology also aims to ensure that the decoded action trajectories remain physically executable and retain the precision required for dexterous manipulation tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The MINT (Mimic Intent, Not just Trajectories) framework operates in two distinct stages: the Spectrally Disentangled Action Tokenizer (SDAT) and the MINT policy learning phase.

**Stage 1: Spectrally Disentangled Action Tokenizer (SDAT)**
SDAT is designed to learn multi-scale discrete representations of action trajectories, explicitly disentangling behavioral intent from execution details through spectral decomposition.

- **Action Encoding and Frequency Domain Transformation**:
 - Continuous action sequences `A` (horizon `H`, dimension `D`) are segmented into overlapping chunks.
 - An action encoder `E` maps each chunk into a compressed latent embedding `f`.
 - A spectrum decoder `Dspec` reconstructs the action sequence `Â` and converts it into a frequency-domain representation `F` using the Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) applied along the temporal dimension.
- **Multi-Scale Residual Quantization**:
 - SDAT utilizes a Multi-Scale Residual Quantization scheme to decompose the initial latent embedding `f^(0)` into a multi-scale discrete representation `S = {s1, ..., sK}`.
 - Each `sk` is a discrete token map at a specific resolution `lk`, representing quantized features at scale `k`. The resolutions `l1` to `lK` progressively increase, with `lK` being the full latent resolution.
 - A shared codebook `Z` containing `V` code vectors is used across all scales.
 - Quantization is performed recursively: at each scale `k`, `f^(k)` is interpolated to `lk`, quantized to `sk`, and then mapped to an embedding `zk`. The residual feature is updated as `f^(k+1) = f^(k) - φk(zk)`.
 - The coarsest scale (`s1`) is designed to capture global, low-frequency structure (the "Intent token"), while finer scales (`s2` to `sK`) capture residual, high-frequency details (the "Execution tokens").
- **Scale-wise Spectral Reconstruction (Key Mechanism for Disentanglement)**:
 - To enforce the disentanglement, SDAT is trained with a progressive reconstruction objective in the frequency domain.
 - `f̂(k)` represents the cumulative latent approximation up to scale `k`, formed by summing the quantized residuals `Σ φi(Lookup(Z, si))` for `i=1` to `k`.
 - Each `f̂(k)` is decoded by `Dspec` into a progressively refined action sequence `Â(k)`, which is then transformed to the frequency domain as `F(k) = DCT(Â(k))`.
 - A scale-wise spectral loss `L_freq. = Σ λk ||F - F(k)||²` enforces consistency between the ground-truth frequency representation `F = DCT(A)` and each partial reconstruction `F(k)`. This objective forces earlier scales (e.g., `s1`) to capture dominant low-frequency components, while later scales specialize in high-frequency residuals.
- **Overall Training Objective**: The full SDAT loss combines `L_freq.` with standard VQ-VAE codebook and commitment losses, plus an auxiliary L1 time-domain reconstruction loss (`α ||A - Â||1`) to ensure detailed execution fidelity.
- **Implementation Details**: The action encoder and spectrum decoder use 1D CNN architectures. Modality-specific MLPs and Group CNNs handle heterogeneous action spaces (translation, rotation, gripper states). Exponential Moving Average (EMA) is used for codebook updates. The binary gripper dimension is explicitly excluded from DCT and spectral reconstruction.

**Stage 2: MINT Policy Learning**
The MINT policy learns to generate actions by predicting the multi-scale discrete action tokens produced by SDAT.

- **Inputs**: The policy receives current visual observations, language instructions, and robot proprioceptive states.
- **Next-Scale Autoregressive Modeling**:
 - The policy models the joint distribution over tokens `S = {s1, ..., sK}` autoregressively across scales: `p(s1, ..., sK) = Π p(sk | s<k)`.
 - The action expert component generates all tokens within a given scale `sk` in parallel, conditioned on coarser-scale token maps `s<k` and scale-specific positional embeddings.
 - A hybrid attention mask ensures that tokens at scale `k` can only attend to token maps from coarser or equal scales (`s<=k`).
 - The policy is optimized using a cross-entropy loss against ground-truth token maps.
- **Intent-Based Action Ensemble**:
 - During inference, to ensure temporal consistency and smooth behavioral transitions, the final action `at` is derived through an ensemble of overlapping predictions.
 - Adaptive weights `w_intent_h` for each prediction are calculated based on the cosine similarity between the current intent token `s(t)1` and historical intent tokens `s(t-h)1`.
- **Model Architectures**:
 - **MINT-30M**: A lightweight (30M parameters) decoder-only Transformer, trained from scratch. It uses frozen SigLIP and DINOv2 backbones for visual input and a frozen BERT encoder for language, with language features injected via FiLM layers.
 - **MINT-4B**: A large-scale policy built upon a pretrained PaliGemma-2.6B VLM backbone (SigLIP vision encoder, Gemma-2B LLM). It incorporates a randomly initialized, 300M-parameter transformer-based action expert trained from scratch.
- **One-Shot Skill Transfer (MINT-Zero)**: In a language-free variant, a single intent token `s1` is extracted from a demonstration and directly injected into the policy. The policy then autoregressively generates the remaining execution tokens conditioned on this fixed intent, enabling transfer to new tasks or environments without additional training.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents empirical evaluations of the MINT framework across various simulated manipulation benchmarks and on a real robotic system.

**Performance Comparison:**
- **State-of-the-Art Performance**: MINT consistently matches or surpasses the performance of current state-of-the-art approaches across the LIBERO, CALVIN, and MetaWorld benchmarks.
- **Efficiency of Smaller Models**: The MINT-30M variant, trained from scratch without large-scale pre-training, demonstrates strong performance, surpassing larger, pretrained models such as OpenVLA and $\pi_0$ on LIBERO.
- **Enhanced Large-Scale Performance**: The pre-trained MINT-4B variant outperforms $\pi_{0.5}$ on LIBERO and significantly improves upon $\pi_0$ on MetaWorld, including nearly tripling the success rate in the most challenging "Very Hard" MetaWorld tasks. On CALVIN, MINT-4B exhibits superior stability in long-horizon compositional tasks.

**Generalization:**
- **Robustness to Distribution Shifts**: MINT demonstrates robust generalization capabilities on the LIBERO-PLUS benchmark, which evaluates performance under various distribution shifts (e.g., camera viewpoints, robot initial states, language instructions, lighting, background, sensor noise, object layout).
- **Superiority over Baselines**: Both MINT-30M and MINT-4B outperform prior baselines under these perturbations, with notable gains observed for camera viewpoint variations.
- **Finetuning Effectiveness**: When fine-tuned on the LIBERO-PLUS dataset (MINT-4B+), the model further amplifies its advantages, suggesting that the MINT framework leverages diverse data more effectively for generalization.

**One-Shot Transfer via Intent Token Injection:**
- **Limitations of Traditional Transfer**: Standard one-shot fine-tuning with language-based task specifications (MINT-30M) showed limited success in transferring to new layouts or extended-horizon tasks, sometimes leading to policy divergence or failure.
- **Effectiveness of Intent Injection**: In contrast, intent-based task specification (MINT-Zero-30M), where an intent token from a single demonstration is injected into the policy, enabled effective one-shot transfer. This approach yielded high success rates across novel tasks, new layouts, and extended-horizon sequences, indicating that the explicit intent representation provides a more grounded and execution-aligned task specification than language alone.

**Real-World Experiments:**
- **Physical System Transfer**: MINT-4B was evaluated on a 6-DOF robotic arm for tasks like "Place Banana," "Stack Blocks," and "Insert Marker," as well as a zero-shot "Stack Cups" task.
- **Outperformance on Real Robot**: MINT-4B significantly outperformed baseline policies (ACT, $\pi_0$, $\pi^*_{0.5}$) on seen tasks, demonstrating superior precision for tasks like coaxial alignment.
- **Zero-Shot Generalization in Reality**: For the unseen "Stack Cups" task, MINT effectively generalized the shared "stacking" intent from a similar seen task ("Stack Blocks"), substantially outperforming baselines that overfitted to specific object instances. This was achieved with a relatively small number of demonstrations (approximately 20 per task).

**Ablation Studies:**
- **Efficacy of Scale-Wise Spectral Decomposition**: Qualitative analysis using t-SNE visualizations showed that the spectral objective organized the latent space into coherent behavioral clusters for intent tokens (S1), unlike the fragmented clusters produced by standard time-domain reconstruction. Quantitatively, the scale-wise spectral loss yielded notable performance gains (e.g., 93.4% on LIBERO-Long) compared to terminal losses or scale-wise time-domain constraints, confirming its role in disentangling intent from execution details.
- **Impact of Intent-Based Action Ensemble**: The intent-based action ensemble consistently outperformed both temporal and action-based ensembling strategies. By dynamically modulating aggregation weights based on intent compatibility, it improved action stability and task success rates (93.2% on LIBERO-Long, 4.57 average sequence length on CALVIN).
- **Learning Efficiency**: MINT converged significantly faster and demonstrated improved data efficiency compared to baseline methods, both with and without pre-training.
- **Hyperparameter Ablation**: Ablations on the number of spectral scales and action chunk horizon indicated that moderate multi-scale configurations and intermediate action horizons (e.g., 16 timesteps) provided the best balance between expressiveness and optimization stability.

**Reconstruction Accuracy and Intent Token Analysis**:
- Reconstruction error consistently decreased with an increasing number of spectral scales, and visualizations demonstrated faithful trajectory reconstructions that preserved global structure while allowing for fine-grained adjustments.
- Extended visualizations of the S1 (Intent) latent space across LIBERO and CALVIN confirmed the formation of distinct, semantically coherent clusters (e.g., "pick up," "move left," "rotation"), indicating a stable and generalized representation of behavioral abstractions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The MINT framework introduces a methodological advancement in imitation learning by explicitly addressing the entanglement of high-level intent and low-level execution in robot control. Its significance and potential impact are multi-faceted:

1. **Enhanced Generalization and Robustness**: By disentangling intent, MINT enables policies to generalize more effectively across diverse environmental variations and disturbances. This improves the reliability of robot systems in unstructured and changing environments, moving beyond task-specific overfitting. The demonstrated robustness on benchmarks like LIBERO-PLUS indicates a step towards more adaptable robotic agents.

2. **Efficient Skill Transfer**: The capability for one-shot skill transfer via intent token injection represents a notable step towards reducing the data requirements for deploying robots in new tasks. Instead of extensive retraining for each new scenario, extracting and injecting an abstract intent token from a single demonstration allows for rapid adaptation. This could significantly lower the barrier to entry for robot learning in real-world applications and accelerate the development of versatile robotic skills.

3. **Principled Action Representation**: The Spectrally Disentangled Action Tokenizer (SDAT) provides a novel and principled approach to action tokenization. By leveraging frequency domain decomposition and scale-wise reconstruction constraints, it generates action tokens that are not merely compressed representations but semantically meaningful abstractions. This explicit structuring of action space is a departure from previous methods and offers a foundation for more interpretable and manipulable robot behaviors.

4. **Improved Learning Efficiency**: The progressive intent-to-execution reasoning enabled by the multi-scale token hierarchy contributes to faster convergence and better data efficiency during training. This is a practical benefit for robot learning, which often relies on costly and time-consuming data collection.

5. **Foundation for Future Research**:
 - **Compositional Capabilities**: The existence of distinct, clustered intent tokens suggests possibilities for synthesizing novel, long-horizon behaviors through the recombination or manipulation of these discrete intent representations in a zero-shot manner. This could lead to robots learning to perform complex tasks not explicitly seen in demonstrations.
 - **Bridging Data Gaps**: The current reliance on trajectory demonstrations to learn intent highlights a future direction to explore methods that can derive a richer and more diverse set of intents from broader data sources, potentially including large-scale network data or multimodal inputs.
 - **Interpretability and Debugging**: The semantically grounded intent tokens could offer enhanced interpretability of robot policies. Understanding "what" the robot intends to do at a high level, rather than just observing low-level actions, could aid in debugging, policy analysis, and human-robot collaboration.

In summary, MINT presents a robust framework that improves the adaptability, efficiency, and generalization of robot manipulation policies by focusing on behavioral intent. This approach has implications for developing more autonomous, versatile, and user-friendly robotic systems capable of operating effectively in diverse and dynamic environments.
