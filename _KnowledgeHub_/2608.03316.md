# Any-OPD: Heterogeneous On-Policy Distillation for Flow-Matching Models via Representation-Space Bridging

> [!summary] Summary
> Any-OPD introduces a framework for on-policy knowledge distillation between heterogeneous latent flow-matching models, bridging architectural and latent space differences via an external representation space. This allowed a 2.5B student model to surpass a 12B teacher's performance on aesthetic, image, and pick scores while maintaining compositional accuracy.

## Problem

- State-of-the-art generative models are often too large (e.g., 12B parameters) for deployment in resource-constrained environments due to high latency and memory demands.
- Existing on-policy distillation methods require homogeneity between teacher and student models, assuming shared VAEs, architectures, and noise schedules.
- The 'no shared space' (different VAEs) and 'no shared clock' (different noise schedules) problems prevent effective knowledge transfer between truly heterogeneous models, making direct latent or pixel-wise supervision problematic.

## Method

- **Any-OPD** employs a two-stage process: an initial offline '==anchoring==' phase to align the student's output distribution with the teacher's manifold, followed by ==on-policy distillation==.
- It addresses the 'no shared space' problem by coupling models in a frozen, external, model-agnostic representation space (==DINOv2 embeddings==) rather than direct latent or pixel space.
- The 'no shared clock' issue is resolved by aligning trajectories based on continuous noise levels instead of discrete solver step indices, ensuring comparable states are supervised.

## Results

- The **2.5B** SD3.5-Medium student, distilled using Any-OPD, improved over its baseline and surpassed the **12B** FLUX.1-dev teacher on DrawBench metrics including Aesthetic Score (**5.788** vs **5.765**), ImageReward (**1.116** vs **0.971**), and PickScore (**0.884** vs **0.878**).
- Ablation studies confirmed the DINOv2 CLS objective significantly outperformed latent MSE and LPIPS, and demonstrated the necessity of the anchoring stage and adequate teacher refinement strength.
- The framework successfully distilled knowledge from a different heterogeneous teacher (Z-Image) without hyperparameter changes, showcasing its generality and 'any-teacher' capability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Using a high-level, coordinate-free embedding from an external vision model (DINOv2 CLS token) provides a robust and stable objective for comparing heterogeneous model outputs, tolerating stochastic local variations from teacher refinement.
> - Aligning teacher-student interactions by continuous noise levels, rather than discrete solver steps, effectively enables on-policy learning despite differing internal noise schedules.
> - An initial offline anchoring phase is crucial for establishing the student's foundational generation quality, allowing the subsequent on-policy distillation to focus purely on per-sample refinement.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of generative artificial intelligence, particularly text-to-image synthesis, has seen significant advancements with models like FLUX.1-dev and SD3.5-Medium achieving high-resolution and high-fidelity image generation. Many of these state-of-the-art models are built upon flow-matching or diffusion principles. However, a common characteristic of these high-performing models is their substantial size, often comprising billions of parameters (e.g., FLUX.1-dev with 12B parameters). This scale presents challenges for deployment in resource-constrained environments, where latency and memory are critical considerations.

Knowledge distillation, a technique where a smaller "student" model is trained to emulate the behavior of a larger "teacher" model, is a common approach to address this challenge. Among distillation methods, on-policy distillation (OPD) has emerged as particularly effective. In OPD, the student model learns by generating its own samples and receiving feedback or corrections from the teacher on these self-generated states. This approach helps mitigate exposure bias, a common issue in purely offline imitation learning where the student is only trained on a fixed dataset generated by the teacher, and may encounter out-of-distribution states during its own inference. Existing OPD schemes, as applied to language models and iterative image generation models like diffusion or flow-matching models, have historically relied on a fundamental assumption: the teacher and student models are *homogeneous*. This implies they share the same Variational Autoencoder (VAE) for latent space representation, possess similar architectural designs, and operate on a common timestep or noise schedule.

This homogeneity assumption, while simplifying distillation, significantly restricts its applicability. In practical scenarios, the most powerful available teacher model might originate from a different research group or model family than the desired student model, leading to fundamental differences in their internal components (VAE, architecture, noise schedule). This paper addresses this specific limitation by formulating and providing a solution for *heterogeneous on-policy distillation*. It aims to bridge the gap that current methods cannot, allowing distillation between models that "share nothing but pixels," thereby expanding the utility of knowledge distillation across diverse model ecosystems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop a framework, termed Any-OPD (any teacher, any student, on-policy distillation), that enables effective on-policy distillation between *arbitrary pairs of latent flow-matching generative models*, even when these models exhibit significant heterogeneity. This includes differences in their VAEs, architectural designs, and noise schedules.

The motivation for this work arises from several detailed observations and challenges:

1. **Deployment Constraints of Large Generative Models:** State-of-the-art text-to-image models, such as FLUX.1-dev, achieve high fidelity at the cost of substantial computational resources due to their large parameter counts (e.g., 12 billion parameters for the denoising transformer alone). This makes them impractical for applications with strict latency or memory requirements. Knowledge distillation is a natural solution for creating smaller, more efficient models.

2. **Limitations of Existing On-Policy Distillation Methods:** While on-policy distillation is recognized for its effectiveness in reducing exposure bias and improving student performance, current methods (e.g., DiffusionOPD, Flow-OPD) presuppose homogeneity between the teacher and student. This assumption is critical because:
 - **Shared Latent Space:** Existing methods often assume a shared VAE, allowing teacher predictions in the latent space to serve directly as regression targets for the student.
 - **Shared Architecture:** Similar architectures enable internal feature matching, providing additional supervision signals.
 - **Shared Timestep Grid:** A common noise schedule ensures that "the same step" corresponds to an equivalent noise level for both models, facilitating direct alignment of trajectories.

3. **The Heterogeneity Problem in Real-World Scenarios:** The critical motivation for Any-OPD is the recognition that the "strongest teacher available" and the "student one wishes to deploy" often originate from different model families or development pipelines. This inherent heterogeneity renders existing distillation techniques inapplicable. The paper identifies two specific axes along which this heterogeneity causes breakdown:
 - **No Shared Representation ("No Shared Space"):**
 - When VAEs differ, a teacher's latent representation is fundamentally incompatible as a direct target in the student's latent coordinate system.
 - Architectural differences preclude direct internal feature matching.
 - Even decoding both models to pixel space and applying pixel-wise losses (e.g., MSE) is problematic. Teacher refinement is inherently a stochastic re-synthesis process; it preserves high-level semantics but re-draws local details. A pixel-wise loss against such a stochastic target tends to regress towards a blurred conditional mean or even leads to training collapse.
 - **No Shared Trajectory Alignment ("No Shared Clock"):**
 - Different flow-matching models often employ distinct noise schedules or "shift functions." Consequently, identical solver step indices (e.g., step 10) do not necessarily correspond to the same noise level across teacher and student. Index-aligned supervision would therefore pair states from incomparable noise regimes, leading to ineffective or detrimental gradient signals.

In summary, the key motivation is to overcome these fundamental representational and temporal mismatches to enable effective knowledge transfer from large, state-of-the-art but heterogeneous teachers to smaller, efficient students, thereby expanding the practical applicability of on-policy distillation in generative AI.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Any-OPD is a novel framework designed for on-policy distillation between heterogeneous latent flow-matching generative models. It addresses the challenges of "no shared space" and "no shared clock" through a multi-stage approach, treating the teacher primarily as a black-box sampler.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries: Flow Matching and Latent Generators

The work builds on Flow Matching, which trains a velocity field $v_\theta$ to transport noise to data along an Ordinary Differential Equation (ODE). The training objective minimizes the difference between the predicted velocity and the true velocity given by the optimal transport interpolation between a data point $x_0$ and a noise point $x_1$. For inference, a discrete Euler schedule with $N$ steps and model-specific noise levels $\sigma_i$ is used. Latent generators (like SD3, FLUX) consist of an encoder (E), a decoder (D), a velocity field (v), and a noise schedule ($\{\sigma_i\}$). The student is denoted $S = (E_S, D_S, v_\theta, \{\sigma_S_i\})$ and the frozen teacher $T = (E_T, D_T, v_\phi, \{\sigma_T_i\})$, emphasizing their disjoint latent spaces ($Z_S \ne Z_T$).

#### 3.2. Addressing Heterogeneity

Any-OPD resolves the identified problems:
- **No Shared Space:** The student and teacher are coupled not in their internal latent spaces or pixel space directly, but through a *third, external, model-agnostic representation space*.
- **No Shared Clock:** Trajectory alignment is performed by matching *continuous noise levels* rather than discrete step indices.
- **Distribution Gap:** An initial "anchoring" phase transfers the teacher's output distribution into the student's latent space to provide a good starting point for on-policy refinement.

#### 3.3. Stage 1: Anchoring the Student on the Teacher’s Manifold

This offline stage aims to close the distribution-level gap, allowing the student to produce images consistent with the teacher's style and quality, without requiring direct sample-to-sample correspondence.
1. **Teacher Sample Generation:** Given a text prompt $c$, the teacher $T$ generates a high-quality image $x_T = D_T(\text{Sample}_T(c))$.
2. **Student Re-encoding:** This teacher-generated image $x_T$ is then encoded using the *student's own VAE encoder*: $z^\star = E_S(x_T)$. This projects the teacher's output into the student's latent coordinate system.
3. **Flow-Matching Training:** The student's velocity field $v_\theta$ is trained using a standard flow-matching objective ($L_{WS}$). This involves drawing a noise level $\sigma_S_m$ from the student's schedule, noising $z^\star$ to $z_m = (1-\sigma_S_m)z^\star + \sigma_S_m \epsilon$, and minimizing the difference between $v_\theta(z_m, \sigma_S_m, c)$ and the true velocity $(\epsilon - z^\star)/\sigma_S_m$. This process ensures the student learns to generate images of the teacher's quality within its own latent space, without requiring on-policy interaction.

#### 3.4. Stage 2: On-Policy Distillation Between Any Teacher and Any Student

This stage focuses on per-sample error correction, leveraging the teacher to refine student-generated samples. It operates around a "noise-and-denoise" map $\Pi^\sigma_T(x)$, which perturbs an image $x$ to a noise level $\sigma$ and then denoises it using the teacher's velocity field. This effectively projects $x$ onto the teacher's image manifold.
Each training iteration involves three core steps:

1. **Projecting the On-Policy Sample:**
 - **Student Rollout:** The student model $S$ generates an image $x_S$ by integrating its velocity field $v_\theta$ from pure noise $z_0 \sim N(0, I)$ through its $N_S$ steps, *without gradients*. This produces an on-policy sample $x_S = D_S(z_{N_S})$. An intermediate latent state $z_j$ is cached for later gradient propagation.
 - **Teacher Refinement Initiation:** A projection radius is determined by choosing a teacher start index $k = N_T - r$ (where $r$ is a randomly sampled integer from a range). This corresponds to a specific noise level $\sigma_T_k$.
 - **Format Conversion and Re-noising:** The student's output image $x_S$ is converted into the teacher's latent space by $z_{T_{N_T}} = E_T(x_S)$. This latent is then re-noised to $\sigma_T_k$: $z_{T_k} = (1-\sigma_T_k)z_{T_{N_T}} + \sigma_T_k \epsilon$.
 - **Teacher Denoising:** The teacher then denoises $z_{T_k}$ back to a clean image $x_{ref} = \Pi^{\sigma_{T_k}}_T(x_S)$ using its own $N_T$ steps and velocity field $v_\phi$. This $x_{ref}$ serves as the refined target.

2. **Routing the Correction by Noise Level:**
 - **Noise Level Alignment:** To ensure the student's gradient addresses the correct scales, the process identifies the student's solver step $j$ where its noise level $\sigma_S_j$ is approximately equal to or just above the teacher's refinement starting noise level $\sigma_{T_k}$. This is represented as $j = \max \{i : \sigma_S_i \ge \sigma_{T_k}\}$.
 - **Gradient Flow Segment:** The student's velocity field $v_\theta$ is re-evaluated (with gradients enabled) from the cached latent $z_j$ up to $N_S$ steps to produce $\hat{z}_{N_S}$. Critically, $z_j$ is treated as a constant, ensuring the gradient corrects the student's *actual* trajectory from that point, rather than an ideal one.

3. **Coupling the Models in Representation Space:**
 - **Decoding:** The student's refined latent $\hat{z}_{N_S}$ is decoded by $D_S$ to $\hat{x}_S$. The teacher's refined image $x_{ref}$ is also available.
 - **Representation Comparison:** Both $\hat{x}_S$ and $x_{ref}$ are fed into a *frozen, model-agnostic DINOv2-Base model* ($f$). The CLS (class token) embeddings from DINOv2 are extracted.
 - **Loss Function:** The on-policy distillation loss ($L_{OPD}$) is defined as $1 - \text{cos}(f(\hat{x}_S), f(x_{ref}))$. This cosine similarity loss measures the semantic and structural similarity between the student's refined output and the teacher's projection, while being robust to local pixel variations. Gradients flow through the student's decoder $D_S$ into its velocity field $v_\theta$.

#### 3.5. Experimental Setup

- **Models:** Student: SD3.5-Medium (2.5B params, SD3 VAE, static shift 3.0, guidance 4.5). Teacher: FLUX.1-dev (12B params, FLUX VAE, dynamic shifting, guidance 3.5). This pair represents maximal heterogeneity.
- **Training:** Only LoRA adapters (rank 32, $\alpha=64$) in the student transformer are trained; other student components are frozen. Training uses Pick-a-Pic prompts. Anchoring runs for 400 steps (M=10 noise levels, 50-step Euler for teacher targets at 512x512). On-policy training runs for 800 steps (N_S = N_T = 20 steps, 512x512).
- **Evaluation:** Models evaluated at 1024x1024 with their standard inference settings (FLUX: 50-step Euler; SD3.5-M and Any-OPD: 40-step Euler). Metrics include Aesthetic Score, ImageReward, PickScore, HPSv3, UnifiedReward, UnifiedReward2 on DrawBench, and overall scores on GenEval and DPG-Bench.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate the effectiveness of Any-OPD in distilling knowledge from a heterogeneous, larger teacher model to a smaller student model, leading to substantial improvements in image generation quality.

#### 4.1. Main Quantitative Results (Table 1)

- **Student Performance Improvement:** The 2.5B SD3.5-Medium student model, when distilled with Any-OPD, significantly improves across all six preference-oriented metrics on the DrawBench benchmark compared to its baseline initialization.
 - Aesthetic Score increases from 5.383 to 5.788.
 - ImageReward improves from 0.922 to 1.116.
 - PickScore rises from 0.866 to 0.884.
 - HPSv3 increases from 9.12 to 10.97.
 - UnifiedReward and UnifiedReward2 also show increases (3.23 to 3.31 and 3.21 to 3.26, respectively).
- **Teacher-Surpassing Performance:** The distilled 2.5B student not only improves over its baseline but also *exceeds* its 12B FLUX.1-dev teacher on Aesthetic Score (5.788 vs. 5.765), ImageReward (1.116 vs. 0.971), and PickScore (0.884 vs. 0.878). It also substantially closes the gap on HPSv3 and UnifiedReward scores, approaching the teacher's performance at approximately one-fifth of its size.
- **Compositional Accuracy:** The distilled student maintains compositional accuracy, with the DPG-Bench score showing a minor increase (84.58 to 84.71).
- **Failure of Baselines:** Direct latent regression, a naive alternative for cross-VAE distillation, failed to train the student model at all, underscoring the necessity of Any-OPD's approach.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Results (Figure 3)

Qualitative comparisons on DrawBench prompts reveal that Any-OPD leads to visually superior images. The distilled student produces outputs with cleaner textures, more coherent lighting, and finer structural details, while preserving the compositional layout generated by the student's initial rollout. This is consistent with the projection-based objective, where the teacher refines the "execution" quality while retaining the student's "composition."

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Objective Function (Figure 5):**
 - **Latent MSE:** Collapsed within the first few training steps, validating that coordinate-wise regression across heterogeneous VAE boundaries is unstable and ineffective.
 - **LPIPS:** Showed initial improvement but degraded later in training, suggesting its patch-local features are still sensitive to the stochastic re-synthesis introduced by the teacher.
 - **DINOv2 CLS:** All DINOv2 variants (CLS, multi-layer CLS, CLS+Patch) remained stable throughout training, with the plain CLS token performing comparably or better than richer variants. This confirms that a global, coordinate-free embedding from an external vision model is crucial and sufficient for heterogeneous comparison. The results indicate a clear hierarchy: MSE < LPIPS < DINOv2, reflecting increasing robustness to local inconsistencies.
- **Anchoring Stage (Figure 6):**
 - The quality of the initial anchoring stage directly correlates with the final performance of the on-policy distillation. Models started from a well-anchored checkpoint (Anchor-400) consistently outperformed those with less (Anchor-100) or no anchoring. This establishes anchoring as a prerequisite that sets the student's initial distribution, allowing OPD to focus on per-sample refinements.
- **Teacher Refinement Strength (Table 2):**
 - Stronger teacher refinement (starting the teacher's denoising process from noisier levels, indicated by larger 'r' values) generally yielded better results. A "medium-low" refinement strength ($r \in [0, 10)$) underperformed, indicating insufficient signal from the teacher. The range $r \in$ was identified as the most effective balance.
- **Teacher Refinement Steps (Figure 4):**
 - The teacher's denoising budget (number of steps, $N_T$) directly impacts the transferable quality. Using fewer steps ($N_T=10$) for teacher refinement resulted in noticeable blur in the refined targets, which propagated to the student's outputs. More steps ($N_T=20$) produced sharper targets and a correspondingly sharper student.
- **Rollout Stochasticity (Figure 7):**
 - Replacing the deterministic Euler student rollout with stochastic Coefficients-Preserving Sampling (CPS) degraded performance. Deterministic Euler (stochasticity parameter $\eta=0$) was consistently superior. This suggests that introducing stochasticity during the student's rollout decouples the supervised trajectory from the deployed one, hindering effective on-policy learning.

#### 4.4. Generalization: Swapping the Teacher (Table 3)

To demonstrate the "any-teacher" capability, FLUX.1-dev was replaced with Z-Image (6B parameters, different VAE, shift 6, guidance 5.0) as the teacher, without any changes to the Any-OPD procedure or hyperparameters.
- The student, Any-OPD-Z, improved on all reported metrics (ImageReward, PickScore, HPSv3, GenEval, DPG-Bench) compared to the SD3.5-Medium baseline.
- Notably, the gains mirrored the new teacher's strengths: Z-Image is strong on compositional benchmarks like GenEval and DPG-Bench, and Any-OPD-Z inherited these improvements, confirming the method's flexibility and teacher-agnostic nature.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Any-OPD framework represents a significant advancement in the field of knowledge distillation for generative models, with notable conceptual and practical implications:

1. **Conceptual Breakthrough in On-Policy Distillation:** Any-OPD is presented as the first framework to successfully enable on-policy distillation between *heterogeneous* flow-matching generative models. It fundamentally challenges and resolves the implicit assumptions of shared latent spaces, architectures, and timestep grids that have constrained previous on-policy methods. By decoupling the teacher and student through a shared, external representation space (DINOv2) and aligning trajectories via noise levels rather than solver indices, it addresses core incompatibilities.

2. **Expanded Applicability and Practical Utility:** This work significantly broadens the practical applicability of knowledge distillation. It allows researchers and practitioners to leverage the most powerful and advanced generative models as teachers, irrespective of their internal design or model family, to distill knowledge into smaller, more efficient student models suitable for diverse deployment scenarios with limited computational resources (e.g., edge devices, applications requiring low latency). The ability to distill "any teacher" to "any student" unlocks greater flexibility in model optimization pipelines.

3. **Efficiency and Performance Gains:** The distillation of a 12B FLUX.1-dev teacher into a 2.5B SD3.5-Medium student, resulting in a model that approaches or surpasses the teacher's performance on critical preference metrics (Aesthetic Score, ImageReward, PickScore) at one-fifth the size, demonstrates substantial efficiency gains. This outcome is crucial for practical applications where quality must be maintained while reducing computational footprint.

4. **Robustness and Generality by Design:** The architecture of Any-OPD, treating the teacher as a black-box sampler and relying on a model-agnostic feature extractor (frozen DINOv2) for comparison, inherently imbues the framework with high robustness and generality. This is validated by the successful "teacher swapping" experiment with Z-Image, where the method required no modifications, demonstrating its true "any-teacher" capability.

5. **Separation of Concerns:** The two-stage approach (offline anchoring followed by on-policy distillation) intelligently separates the task of aligning output distributions from the task of correcting per-sample errors. This modularity likely contributes to the method's stability and effectiveness.

6. **Future Research Directions:** The paper also suggests promising avenues for future work, such as exploring the combination of complementary teachers to create even more capable student models and extending Any-OPD to other latent generative modalities beyond images, given that the underlying formulation is not specific to visual data beyond the choice of feature extractor. This indicates a potential for broader impact across different generative AI domains.
