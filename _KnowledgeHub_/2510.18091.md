# Accelerating Vision Transformers with Adaptive Patch Sizes

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Carnegie Mellon University and KAIST developed Adaptive Patch Transformers (APT) to accelerate Vision Transformers by dynamically adjusting patch sizes based on image content. This approach yields substantial speedups in both training and inference across various visual tasks while fully maintaining or slightly improving downstream performance.

## Problem

- Vision Transformers (ViTs) suffer from quadratic computational complexity due to self-attention, making them slow and expensive for high-resolution images.
- Standard ViTs use fixed-size image patches, wasting computation on visually homogeneous or redundant regions and failing to allocate finer-grained attention to complex areas.
- Existing token reduction methods often introduce practical inefficiencies (e.g., irregular shapes, padding) or require extensive fine-tuning, limiting their real-world applicability and training speedups.

## Method

- ==APT== implements a ==content-aware, multi-scale patchification strategy==, using entropy to dynamically assign larger patches to homogeneous regions and smaller patches to detailed areas.
- A novel ==patch aggregation mechanism== employs a zero-initialized MLP to convert variable-sized patches into uniform embeddings, enabling APT to rapidly adapt to pre-trained ViT models with minimal fine-tuning.
- Dynamic token counts are efficiently handled through ==sequence packing and block-diagonal attention masks==, which are optimized by modern attention backends like FlashAttention.

## Results

- Achieved up to an **86%** speedup in ImageNet training for ViT-L at 448x448 resolution and up to **23%** throughput increase for LLaVA-1.5-13B on VQA benchmarks, maintaining or exceeding baseline performance.
- Reduced input tokens by **30%** and attained a **30%** speedup in object detection (COCO) and an **11%** speedup in semantic segmentation (ADE20K) using EVA-02 backbones, matching baseline accuracy.
- APT effectively allocates patches, using fewer tokens for homogeneous regions and more for complex areas, confirming its content-aware processing and achieving rapid convergence (**1 epoch**) for fine-tuning pre-trained models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Adopting a variable-granularity, content-aware tokenization strategy, similar to those in NLP, can drastically improve the computational efficiency of Vision Transformers.
> - A zero-initialized MLP enables a "plug-and-play" capability, allowing APT to be applied to existing pre-trained ViT backbones and converge in as little as one fine-tuning epoch.
> - Input-level token reduction, when guided by image content, provides more robust and practical speedups compared to fixed-ratio or layer-level token merging/pruning methods.
