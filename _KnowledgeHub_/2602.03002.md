# RPL: Learning Robust Humanoid Perceptive Locomotion on Challenging Terrains

> [!summary] Summary
> The RPL framework introduces a two-stage training method for humanoid robots, enabling robust multi-directional locomotion across challenging terrains while carrying payloads. It utilizes an efficient multi-depth rendering system and novel robustification techniques, achieving stable real-world bidirectional traversal on diverse surfaces like stairs and stepping stones with a 2kg payload.

## Problem

- Existing humanoid locomotion methods lack robustness for multi-directional movement on complex terrains, often failing beyond forward motion.
- Single-camera perception is brittle due to self-occlusions and limited field of view, hindering comprehensive terrain awareness and generalization to unseen geometries.
- Current simulation environments are computationally expensive for training multi-view, vision-based policies at scale, and policies struggle with real-world deployment due to the sim-to-real gap, especially with varying terrain widths and payloads.

## Method

- A two-stage training framework (RPL) is proposed, first training terrain-specialized "expert" policies with privileged height map information.
- These experts are then distilled into a unified visual policy using multi-view depth camera observations, enabled by an efficient NVIDIA Warp-based multi-depth rendering system that casts rays against both dynamic robot and static terrain meshes.
- Novel robustification techniques, Depth Feature Scaling based on Velocity Commands (DFSV) and Random Side Masking (RSM), are introduced during distillation to enhance generalization to asymmetric visual inputs and unseen terrain widths.

## Results

- The multi-depth rendering pipeline achieved a 5x speedup over existing methods, enabling efficient training for complex multi-camera perceptive policies by supporting ray-casting against dynamic robot meshes and static terrain meshes.
- RPL demonstrated robust real-world bidirectional locomotion on challenging terrains, including 20° slopes, varying staircases, and stepping stones with 60 cm gaps, using a Unitree G1 humanoid robot.
- The robot maintained stable locomotion while performing loco-manipulation tasks, successfully carrying a 2 kg payload throughout a 50m terrain course, with the policy running onboard at 50 Hz.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Multi-view depth cameras are crucial for robust multi-directional and omnidirectional locomotion, significantly outperforming single-camera setups on complex terrains.
> - A CNN+Transformer architecture effectively fuses multi-view depth inputs for the student policy, achieving lower distillation loss and better generalization compared to other architectures.
> - Specialized robustification techniques like DFSV and RSM are vital for bridging the sim-to-real gap, allowing policies to generalize to out-of-distribution terrain geometries and handle asymmetric visual inputs effectively.
