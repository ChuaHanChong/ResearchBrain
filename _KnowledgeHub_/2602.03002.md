# RPL: Learning Robust Humanoid Perceptive Locomotion on Challenging Terrains

> [!summary] Summary
> The RPL framework introduces a two-stage training method for humanoid robots, enabling robust multi-directional locomotion across challenging terrains while carrying payloads. It utilizes an efficient multi-depth rendering system and novel robustification techniques, achieving stable real-world bidirectional traversal on diverse surfaces like stairs and stepping stones with a 2kg payload.

## Problem

- Existing humanoid locomotion methods lack robustness for multi-directional movement on complex terrains, often failing beyond forward motion.
- Single-camera perception is brittle due to self-occlusions and limited field of view, hindering comprehensive terrain awareness and generalization to unseen geometries.
- Current simulation environments are computationally expensive for training multi-view, vision-based policies at scale, and policies struggle with real-world deployment due to the sim-to-real gap, especially with varying terrain widths and payloads.

## Method

- A two-stage training framework (RPL) is proposed, first training terrain-specialized "expert" policies with privileged height map information.
- These experts are then distilled into a unified visual policy using multi-view depth camera observations, enabled by an efficient NVIDIA Warp-based multi-depth rendering system that casts rays against both dynamic robot and static terrain meshes.
- Novel robustification techniques, Depth Feature Scaling based on Velocity Commands (DFSV) and Random Side Masking (RSM), are introduced during distillation to enhance generalization to asymmetric visual inputs and unseen terrain widths.

## Results

- The multi-depth rendering pipeline achieved a 5x speedup over existing methods, enabling efficient training for complex multi-camera perceptive policies by supporting ray-casting against dynamic robot meshes and static terrain meshes.
- RPL demonstrated robust real-world bidirectional locomotion on challenging terrains, including 20° slopes, varying staircases, and stepping stones with 60 cm gaps, using a Unitree G1 humanoid robot.
- The robot maintained stable locomotion while performing loco-manipulation tasks, successfully carrying a 2 kg payload throughout a 50m terrain course, with the policy running onboard at 50 Hz.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Multi-view depth cameras are crucial for robust multi-directional and omnidirectional locomotion, significantly outperforming single-camera setups on complex terrains.
> - A CNN+Transformer architecture effectively fuses multi-view depth inputs for the student policy, achieving lower distillation loss and better generalization compared to other architectures.
> - Specialized robustification techniques like DFSV and RSM are vital for bridging the sim-to-real gap, allowing policies to generalize to out-of-distribution terrain geometries and handle asymmetric visual inputs effectively.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of humanoid robotics has seen remarkable progress in recent years, particularly in achieving agile motion tracking and dynamic whole-body control, often leveraging advanced control algorithms and reinforcement learning techniques. However, several critical challenges persist, especially concerning robust perceptive locomotion in unstructured and complex environments.

**Existing Strengths:**
- Humanoids can perform impressive agile motions, sometimes exceeding human capabilities, as evidenced by work on dynamic balance and intricate whole-body tasks.
- Perceptive locomotion for legged robots, including humanoids, has made strides using various sensing modalities like LiDAR and depth cameras.

**Identified Gaps and Limitations in Prior Work:**
1. **Limited Directionality:** Most existing perceptive locomotion methods primarily focus on forward movement. Achieving robust *multi-directional* (bidirectional or omnidirectional) locomotion, particularly on complex terrains, remains underexplored.
2. **Perception Robustness:** Many approaches rely on a single forward-facing camera, which is brittle when facing dynamic self-occlusions (e.g., from upper-body motions during manipulation) or when diverse terrains require views from multiple angles.
3. **Asymmetric Visual Inputs:** Handling and effectively utilizing asymmetric visual inputs from multiple cameras observing distinct terrain structures is a significant challenge.
4. **Mapping-Based vs. End-to-End Vision:** While mapping-based methods (e.g., LiDAR-based elevation maps) can support complex terrains, they depend on accurate calibration and robust online state estimation, which can introduce noise and latency. End-to-end vision-based methods (e.g., depth cameras) reduce this dependency but often lack robustness for multi-directional tasks.
5. **Sim-to-Real Gap & Generalization:** Policies often struggle to generalize to unseen real-world terrain geometries (e.g., varying widths) beyond their training distributions.
6. **Loco-Manipulation with Disturbances:** Few studies evaluate robustness under combined locomotion and manipulation tasks, such as carrying payloads, which introduce significant dynamic disturbances.
7. **Simulation Scalability:** Realistic multi-view depth rendering in existing simulators is computationally expensive, hindering scalable training for complex perceptive policies.

**RPL's Contribution to the Landscape:**
"RPL" directly addresses these critical limitations. It pushes the boundaries of humanoid perceptive locomotion by focusing on:
- **Robust Multi-directional Locomotion:** A core focus is enabling stable forward and backward (and even omnidirectional in simulation) traversal.
- **Multi-View Depth Perception:** Utilizing multiple depth cameras to provide comprehensive terrain coverage and handle self-occlusions.
- **Scalable Simulation:** Introducing an efficient multi-depth rendering system that overcomes computational bottlenecks in simulating realistic multi-camera setups.
- **Generalization to Unseen Terrains:** Developing novel techniques (DFSV, RSM) specifically to improve generalization to varying terrain widths and asymmetric visual inputs, thus bridging the sim-to-real gap more effectively.
- **Loco-Manipulation with Payloads:** Explicitly demonstrating robustness while carrying significant payloads, making the humanoid more capable for practical tasks.

By integrating these advancements, RPL positions itself as a significant step towards unlocking the full potential of humanoids as versatile robots capable of navigating and interacting with complex, unstructured human environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching motivation for "RPL" stems from the immense potential of humanoid robots to perform diverse human tasks. However, realizing this potential requires humanoids to possess a defining capability: robust legged mobility that allows them to traverse a wide range of challenging terrains while simultaneously performing meaningful manipulation tasks, such as carrying objects.

The authors identify several critical limitations in current humanoid perceptive locomotion methods that prevent this vision from becoming a reality:
- **Lack of Robust Multi-Directional Locomotion:** Most state-of-the-art approaches excel at forward movement but become brittle or fail when attempting to move backward, sideways, or perform turning maneuvers on complex terrains. This severely limits a robot's operational flexibility in real-world scenarios.
- **Vulnerability to Asymmetric Perception and Self-Occlusion:** Existing systems often rely on a single, forward-facing camera. This setup is inherently limited because it cannot adequately perceive terrains behind or to the sides, nor can it cope with dynamic self-occlusions introduced by upper-body manipulation (e.g., when the robot bends down or moves its arms). Robust perception requires a broader, multi-directional view.
- **Poor Generalization to Unseen Terrain Variations:** Policies trained in simulation often struggle when deployed on real-world terrains with geometries (e.g., widths of stairs or gaps between stepping stones) that differ from the training distribution. This issue of out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization is a major hurdle for practical deployment.
- **Lack of Robustness to Loco-Manipulation Disturbances:** The ability to carry payloads or actively manipulate objects while walking introduces significant dynamic disturbances to the robot's balance and locomotion. Current methods frequently do not explicitly address or demonstrate robustness under such combined loco-manipulation tasks.
- **Computational Bottlenecks in Simulation:** Training complex, vision-based policies, especially those using multiple cameras, requires massive amounts of simulated data. Existing depth rendering pipelines in simulators are often too slow and computationally expensive to support the scale needed for multi-view perceptive learning.

To overcome these challenges, the key objectives of the RPL framework are:
1. **Enable Robust Multi-Directional Locomotion:** Develop a learning framework that allows humanoid robots to traverse challenging terrains effectively in forward, backward, and potentially omnidirectional movements.
2. **Achieve Robustness with Payloads:** Ensure the robot can maintain stable locomotion even when carrying significant payloads, thereby supporting combined loco-manipulation tasks.
3. **Integrate Multi-View Perceptive Capabilities:** Design a policy that can robustly fuse and utilize visual information from multiple depth cameras to achieve comprehensive terrain awareness, mitigating issues of occlusions and limited fields of view.
4. **Enhance Generalization to Unseen Terrain Geometries:** Introduce novel techniques during policy distillation to improve the robot's ability to navigate terrains with varying and unseen widths, thus enhancing sim-to-real transfer.
5. **Develop a Scalable Simulation Infrastructure:** Create an efficient multi-depth rendering system within the simulator to enable fast and realistic generation of multi-view depth data, crucial for scalable policy training.

By pursuing these objectives, RPL aims to significantly advance the capabilities of humanoid robots, moving them closer to becoming truly versatile and autonomous agents in complex, human-centric environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RPL introduces a novel two-stage training framework designed to impart robust multi-directional perceptive locomotion skills to humanoid robots. The methodology combines expert policy training with advanced distillation techniques, supported by a highly efficient simulation environment and specialized robustification methods.

**A. Stage 1: Training Multiple Terrain Experts via Height Map**
The initial stage focuses on creating high-quality "teacher" policies, specialized for different challenging terrains. These experts are trained using privileged information, specifically a height map, which provides a clean and direct representation of the terrain geometry.

- **Dual-Agent Formulation:** Building upon the FALCON framework, RPL uses a dual-agent structure: a lower-body locomotion policy and an upper-body manipulation policy. Both agents observe shared proprioceptive history and are conditioned on task-specific goals (locomotion goals like linear/angular velocity, root height; and manipulation goals like upper-body joint targets). Perception (height map) is primarily fed to the lower-body policy, recognizing its main role in legged locomotion.
- **Privileged Observation:** Experts receive a privileged height map as their perceptual input. This height map is a local grid (1.6m × 1.0m with 0.1m resolution) providing precise terrain geometry.
- **Training Algorithm:** Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) is used, coupled with asymmetric actor-critic training (critics access additional privileged information like root linear velocities and end-effector forces). A force curriculum is applied, tailored to address payloads on the end-effector. Symmetry data augmentation is utilized to encourage symmetric gait patterns.
- **Diverse Terrain Settings:** Experts are trained on four challenging terrain types:
 - **Slopes:** Ramps up to 37° inclination.
 - **Stairs Up/Down:** Ascending and descending staircases with varying step lengths (0.25–0.30m) and heights (0.05–0.27m).
 - **Stepping Stones:** Discrete, column-shaped footholds (0.25–0.40m diameter) separated by large gaps (0.05–0.70m) and height variations up to 0.05m. These are particularly challenging given the robot's foot length (0.21m).
- **Specialized Reward Design:** Beyond standard dual-agent rewards, custom rewards are introduced for stability and precision on specific terrains:
 - **Foot Edge Penalty:** For stepping stones, penalizes foot contacts on terrain edges to encourage centered placements.
 - **Foothold Penalty:** For stairs, penalizes foot placements resulting in partial or empty support, ensuring stable contact.
 - **Torso Orientation Tracking:** Rewards tracking a target torso orientation, coordinating waist and hip joints for a wide range of motion.

**B. Stage 2: Distillation into Unified Visual Policy**
Once the terrain-specialized experts are trained, their knowledge is distilled into a single, unified visual policy that uses multi-view depth camera observations.

- **Distillation Formulation:** The student policy, $\pi_{vis}^l$, takes noisy proprioception and multi-view depth inputs $D_t = \{d_t^{(i)}\}_{i=1}^{N_{cam}}$, learning to mimic the actions of the expert policies, $\pi_{exp,k}^l$, which use noise-free proprioception and privileged height maps $H_t$. DAgger is employed to minimize an action regression loss.
- **Policy Architecture:** The student policy uses CNN backbones to encode each depth input, which are then fused into a single visual feature. This is combined with proprioceptive observations and task goals, and fed into a transformer policy that predicts actions for the low-level PD controller.
- **Efficient Multi-Depth System Simulation:** A critical component for scalable training.
 - **NVIDIA Warp-based:** Implemented using NVIDIA Warp for GPU-efficient parallel ray casting.
 - **Dynamic and Static Meshes:** Crucially, it ray-casts against both dynamic robot meshes (to account for self-occlusions) and static terrain meshes. This is achieved by transforming world-frame rays into body-local coordinates for dynamic meshes, avoiding costly per-frame mesh refitting.
 - **Massively Parallel:** All ray-mesh queries are executed in a single fused Warp kernel, parallelized across environments, cameras, and pixels, and captured in a CUDA graph for low-overhead replay.
 - **Realistic Sensor Modeling:** Incorporates realistic depth sensor latency, Gaussian noise, and dropout during distillation to improve sim-to-real transfer.
- **Robustification Techniques for Generalization:** Two novel techniques are introduced during distillation to improve robustness to out-of-distribution scenarios:
 - **Depth Feature Scaling Based on Velocity Commands (DFSV):** Addresses asymmetric visual inputs from multiple cameras. It adaptively modulates the importance of features from each camera based on how well its viewing direction aligns with the commanded linear velocity. Cameras aligned with the movement direction are emphasized, while irrelevant ones are suppressed, without requiring learned attention parameters.
 - **Random Side Masking (RSM):** Enhances generalization to unseen terrain widths. During training, peripheral regions of depth images are stochastically occluded with random noise. This forces the policy to rely on the central visible region, making it more robust to narrower terrains than seen during training. Masking probabilities are terrain-specific to retain crucial peripheral information for terrains like stepping stones.

The combination of expert knowledge distillation, an efficient simulation environment, and targeted robustification techniques forms the core of RPL's approach to achieving robust multi-directional perceptive locomotion.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The authors conducted extensive experiments in both simulation and the real world to validate RPL's effectiveness, addressing four key research questions.

**Q1: Scalable Multi-Depth Rendering Performance**
- **Efficiency:** RPL's multi-depth rendering pipeline demonstrates superior efficiency. It achieved a **5x speedup** over IsaacSim Warp (the most efficient existing method) while uniquely supporting ray-casting against *both dynamic robot meshes and static terrain meshes*. This is crucial for realistic self-occlusion modeling during loco-manipulation. The system also showed lower VRAM usage and faster iteration times as the number of cameras increased compared to baselines.
- **Necessity of Multi-Cameras:** Experiments comparing different numbers of depth cameras ($N_{cam}$=1, 2, 4) confirmed that multiple cameras are essential for robust multi-directional and omnidirectional locomotion. Performance degraded significantly with fewer cameras, especially on challenging terrains like stepping stones, where a wider field of view is critical for sparse footholds. A front-back dual camera setup ($N_{cam}$=2) was found sufficient for robust bidirectional locomotion.

**Q2: Training Architecture and Distillation Ablations**
- **Optimal Architecture:** A comparison of different backbone architectures for the student policy revealed that the **CNN+Transformer** architecture achieved the lowest distillation loss. This outperformed CNN+MLP, CNN+RNN, and U-Net+Transformer. The transformer's attention-based fusion of multi-view depth inputs was more effective than direct concatenation (CNN+MLP/RNN) or pixel-wise height map reconstruction (U-Net+Transformer), especially given its compatibility with techniques like Random Side Masking.

**Q3: Robustness to Asymmetric Depth Observations and Unseen Terrain Widths**
- **OOD Generalization:** While all policy variants (RPL, RPL w/o RSM, RPL w/o DFSV) showed similar distillation losses during training, their performance diverged significantly in out-of-distribution (OOD) deployment scenarios.
- **Impact of RSM:** Policies without Random Side Masking (RPL w/o RSM) struggled to generalize to stairs with unseen narrow widths during backward descent, leading to instability. RPL, with RSM, maintained stable traversal.
- **Impact of DFSV:** Policies without Depth Feature Scaling based on Velocity Commands (RPL w/o DFSV) failed when faced with asymmetric multi-view inputs (e.g., front camera seeing stairs, rear camera seeing stepping stones during forward descent). The policy was distracted by irrelevant visual cues. RPL, with DFSV, effectively filtered these distractions, ensuring robust traversal.
- **Joint Effectiveness:** RSM and DFSV together were critical for robust bidirectional locomotion under OOD terrain geometries and challenging asymmetric perception scenarios.

**Q4: Robust Real-World Bidirectional Locomotion with Payloads**
- **Real-World Validation:** Extensive experiments were conducted on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot on a self-designed 50m terrain course and a curved building staircase.
- **Challenging Terrains:** The robot demonstrated robust and stable bidirectional locomotion (forward and backward) across:
 - 20° slopes.
 - Staircases with varying step lengths (22 cm, 25 cm, 30 cm) and 20 cm height, including a curved staircase requiring turning.
 - Stepping stones (25 cm × 25 cm) separated by large 60 cm gaps. These terrains were highly challenging given the robot's 21 cm foot length.
- **Payload Robustness:** RPL enabled the robot to perform whole-body loco-manipulation tasks, specifically bending down to pick up and transport a **2 kg payload** throughout the entire terrain course, demonstrating high robustness to payload-induced disturbances.
- **Onboard Deployment:** The depth-based transformer policy ran effectively at 50 Hz on an onboard NVIDIA Jetson Orin NX 16GB. Asynchronous processing for depth capture, policy inference, and teleoperation (using shared memory for IK targets from Apple Vision Pro) significantly reduced latency, enabling stable real-time control.

In summary, the results convincingly demonstrate that RPL achieves its objectives, providing a robust, multi-directional, perceptive locomotion solution for humanoids that generalizes to challenging, unseen real-world conditions and can handle significant external disturbances like payloads.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in "RPL" marks a significant advancement in the field of humanoid robotics, with substantial implications for future development and deployment.

#### 5.1. Significance and Key Contributions

1. **Enabling Robust Multi-Directional Perceptive Locomotion:** RPL directly addresses a critical gap in humanoid capabilities by enabling robust bidirectional (and simulated omnidirectional) locomotion on diverse, challenging terrains. This moves humanoids beyond mere forward walking and significantly enhances their operational flexibility in complex environments.
2. **Addressing the Multi-View Perception Challenge:** The framework demonstrates how to effectively utilize and fuse information from multiple depth cameras, handling asymmetric visual inputs and dynamic self-occlusions introduced by the robot's own body movements during manipulation. This is crucial for comprehensive environmental awareness.
3. **Novel Robustification Techniques for Generalization:** The introduction of Depth Feature Scaling based on Velocity Commands (DFSV) and Random Side Masking (RSM) are key methodological innovations. These techniques specifically target out-of-distribution generalization, allowing policies trained in simulation to perform robustly on real-world terrains with unseen geometries (e.g., varying widths) and dynamic, asymmetric visual conditions. This directly contributes to bridging the persistent sim-to-real gap.
4. **Development of a Highly Efficient Simulation Infrastructure:** The creation of a 5x faster multi-depth rendering system that supports ray-casting against both dynamic robot meshes and static terrain meshes is a major technical contribution. This scalable and realistic simulation environment significantly lowers the barrier for training complex vision-based policies, benefiting not only RPL but also future research in embodied AI and robot learning.
5. **Demonstrated Robustness under Loco-Manipulation with Payloads:** The successful real-world demonstration of stable locomotion while carrying a 2kg payload across challenging terrains highlights RPL's practical utility. This capability is essential for humanoids to perform real-world tasks that involve both navigation and object interaction, such as logistics, household chores, or disaster response.
6. **Real-World Validation on a Physical Humanoid:** The extensive long-horizon experiments on the Unitree G1 robot provide strong empirical evidence of the framework's effectiveness and robustness in physical deployment, not just simulation. This real-world success is a crucial benchmark in robotics research.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Accelerating Humanoid Robot Deployment:** By overcoming key limitations in robust, multi-directional locomotion and generalization, RPL paves the way for wider deployment of humanoids in unstructured environments, including factories, warehouses, homes, and outdoor settings.
- **Enabling Complex Human-Robot Interaction:** Robots capable of robustly navigating and manipulating objects can more effectively assist humans in various tasks, leading to advancements in service robotics, elderly care, and collaborative work.
- **Advancing Sim-to-Real Transfer Techniques:** The proposed techniques (DFSV, RSM) offer general strategies for improving policy robustness and generalization, which can be applied to other robot learning problems, accelerating the development cycle for new robotic capabilities.
- **Fostering Further Research in Perceptive Locomotion:** The efficient multi-depth rendering system provides a powerful tool for researchers, enabling the training of more sophisticated perceptive policies with realistic visual inputs at an unprecedented scale. This can lead to new breakthroughs in vision-based control.
- **Foundation for More Autonomous and Intelligent Robots:** RPL contributes to building robots that are more autonomous, adaptable, and intelligent by enabling them to perceive and react to their environment in a more sophisticated and robust manner.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
Despite its strengths, the authors acknowledge two main limitations: (1) real-world sideways locomotion was not fully demonstrated for all terrains, suggesting that achieving omnidirectional expert-level performance for the distilled policy remains a challenge; and (2) the policy primarily relies on fixed viewpoints and does not explicitly learn active exploration or viewpoint selection. These limitations present clear avenues for future research, such as developing more general omnidirectional control and integrating active perception strategies.

Overall, RPL represents a significant step forward in making humanoid robots more capable, robust, and versatile, bringing them closer to realizing their potential as general-purpose robots in human-centric environments.
