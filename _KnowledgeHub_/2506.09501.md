# Understanding and Mitigating Numerical Sources of Nondeterminism in LLM Inference

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Rice University, University of Minnesota, and Adobe Inc. systematically investigated numerical precision as a source of non-determinism in Large Language Model (LLM) inference. They demonstrated that 16-bit precision (BF16) leads to substantial variability in outputs and performance, even under greedy decoding, and proposed LayerCast, a hybrid precision inference pipeline that achieves FP32-level reproducibility with reduced memory usage.

## Problem

- LLM inference, even in supposedly deterministic settings like greedy decoding with fixed random seeds, often produces inconsistent outputs across different hardware and system configurations.
- Existing LLM evaluation practices frequently overlook the impact of numerical precision, leading to unreliable benchmark scores and difficulties in distinguishing true model improvements from random variations.
- Inconsistent LLM outputs due to subtle numerical variations pose risks to the reliability, safety, and fairness of LLMs deployed in critical real-world applications.

## Method

- Conducted a comprehensive empirical study across various LLMs, benchmarks, ==hardware configurations== (GPU types, counts, batch sizes), and precision settings (BF16, FP16, FP32).
- Analyzed the underlying numerical mechanisms, identifying the non-associative nature of floating-point arithmetic and minimal logit gaps in reasoning models as key drivers of non-determinism.
- Developed LayerCast, a lightweight hybrid precision inference pipeline that stores model weights in BF16 but performs all computations in FP32, aiming for numerical stability with memory efficiency.

## Results

- BF16 precision introduced up to **9%** variation in accuracy and up to 9,000 tokens difference in response length for reasoning models across different system configurations, while FP32 consistently yielded near-perfect reproducibility.
- The proposed LayerCast method achieved reproducibility levels nearly identical to full FP32 inference (e.g., zero standard deviation in accuracy) while reducing memory usage by approximately **34%** compared to FP32.
- The observed reproducibility challenges generalized across larger models (Qwen3-**32B**), diverse reasoning tasks (GPQA Diamond), and different inference backends (vLLM and HuggingFace Transformers), confirming the broad applicability of the findings.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Greedy decoding with 16-bit precision (BF16) is not deterministic, leading to notable variance in LLM outputs and performance, particularly for reasoning models where top-1 and top-2 token probabilities are very close.
> - Numerical precision, specifically the non-associativity of floating-point operations in parallel computing, is a fundamental and often overlooked source of non-reproducibility in LLM inference.
> - Runtime configurations such as GPU count, batch size, and specific GPU architecture can significantly amplify numerical non-determinism, especially when using lower precision formats like BF16.
