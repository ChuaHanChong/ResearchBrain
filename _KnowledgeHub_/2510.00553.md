# On Predictability of Reinforcement Learning Dynamics for Large Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> This research analyzes how large language models evolve during reinforcement learning (RL) training, revealing that parameter updates are dominated by a single, linearly evolving low-rank subspace. Leveraging these findings, the AlphaRL framework accelerates RL training by up to 2.5 times, preserving over 96% of the model's final reasoning performance.

## Problem

- The internal mechanisms of RL-guided parameter updates in LLMs, crucial for enhancing reasoning, are largely unexamined.
- Current interpretability efforts focus on post-hoc analysis of trained models, providing limited insight into the dynamic process of capability acquisition.
- The computational expense and time required for RL training of LLMs are substantial, necessitating more efficient training paradigms.

## Method

- Systematically analyzed the parameter update matrix (ΔW) during RL training using ==Singular Value Decomposition== (SVD) to identify and track dominant update directions.
- Investigated the evolution of the Rank-1 subspace (largest singular value and vectors) across training checkpoints using dimensionality reduction and linear regression.
- Developed AlphaRL, an acceleration framework that predicts optimal parameter updates early in training based on the identified low-rank dominance and linear dynamics.

## Results

- The Rank-1 Subspace successfully recovered over **99%** of reasoning performance improvements achieved by fully RL-trained models.
- AlphaRL, the proposed plug-in acceleration framework, achieved up to 2.5 times speedup in RL training time while retaining over **96%** of the final reasoning capability.
- The identified properties and AlphaRL demonstrated strong generalizability across 13 diverse LLMs (**7B**-**32B** parameters), 10 advanced RL algorithms, and 6 reasoning benchmarks, including adversarial self-play.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - RL-induced parameter updates exhibit 'Rank-1 Dominance,' where the single largest singular subspace of the update matrix (ΔW) recovers over 99% of reasoning performance gains.
> - The dominant Rank-1 subspace evolves with 'Rank-1 Linear Dynamics,' demonstrating a highly predictable linear trajectory throughout RL training, enabling accurate performance extrapolation.
> - Unlike SFT and DIST, RL updates are characterized by smaller magnitude, higher concentration in low-rank subspaces, and minimal shifts in embedding space, indicating targeted high-level reasoning optimization.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancement in the reasoning capabilities of large language models (LLMs) is substantially attributed to reinforcement learning (RL) based training paradigms. While the effectiveness of RL in enhancing LLM performance is empirically evident, the underlying parameter dynamics during RL training remain largely unexamined. Existing research predominantly focuses on post-hoc interpretability, analyzing models after training completion through methods such as neuron attribution, circuit analysis, and sparse autoencoders. These approaches explain the outcomes of training but do not thoroughly investigate the dynamic process of learning itself.

This work addresses a gap in the current understanding by shifting the focus to the RL training process itself. It aims to demystify the "black box" nature of RL-induced parameter updates, exploring the principles governing these updates and their connection to the emergence of reasoning capabilities in LLMs. By doing so, the research moves beyond merely explaining the end state of a trained model to providing insights into the evolutionary trajectory of its parameters. This approach contrasts with other training paradigms like supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and distillation (DIST), which are shown to induce different parameter update characteristics. The identified properties are presented as a means to develop a more principled, interpretable, and efficient training framework for LLMs, potentially bridging the gap between empirical scaling laws and theoretical understandings of how new capabilities emerge. The proposed acceleration framework, AlphaRL, is positioned as a complement to existing optimization techniques, suggesting its potential to enhance overall RL training efficiency for LLMs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to illuminate the internal mechanisms of reinforcement learning within large language models by investigating the parameter dynamics during training. Specifically, the study aims to answer two fundamental questions:
1. Are RL-guided parameter updates governed by consistent and identifiable principles?
2. How do these underlying principles contribute to the emergence and enhancement of reasoning capabilities in LLMs?

The motivation for addressing these questions stems from several key observations and challenges in the field:
- **Reliance on RL for Reasoning:** Recent breakthroughs in LLM reasoning are heavily reliant on RL techniques, underscoring the importance of this training paradigm. However, the precise manner in which RL translates into enhanced reasoning at the parameter level is not well understood.
- **Limited Process-Oriented Interpretability:** Current interpretability efforts largely concentrate on the static analysis of fully trained models. This post-hoc approach offers insights into the final state but provides limited understanding of the dynamic process by which reasoning abilities are acquired and refined. A deeper understanding of the training process itself is crucial for principled model development and optimization.
- **Optimizing RL Paradigms:** A clearer understanding of parameter dynamics can inform and improve the design of future RL algorithms for LLMs, potentially leading to more efficient, stable, and performant training.
- **Shedding Light on Capability Emergence:** By studying how parameters evolve, the research seeks to uncover fundamental principles related to how complex capabilities like reasoning emerge in large neural networks, contributing to a broader theoretical understanding of LLMs.
- **Practical Acceleration:** Beyond theoretical understanding, the motivation also includes developing practical tools to accelerate the computationally intensive RL training process, making it more accessible and sustainable for large-scale applications. The goal is to provide a "free lunch" acceleration method that integrates seamlessly with existing RL algorithms without requiring additional modules or hyperparameter tuning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a multi-faceted approach to analyze the parameter dynamics of LLMs during RL training, combining mathematical tools, extensive experimentation, and the development of a practical acceleration framework.

**Parameter Update Analysis:**
The core of the methodology involves a step-wise analysis of the parameter update matrix, denoted as Δ*W*. This matrix represents the difference in parameters between an RL-trained model and its initial base model.
- **Singular Value Decomposition (SVD):** SVD is applied to Δ*W* at various training checkpoints. The focus is on the *Rank-1 Subspace*, which is defined by retaining only the largest singular value (σ₁) and its corresponding left (u₁) and right (v₁) singular vectors.
- **Consistent Update Strength:** To ensure fair comparison, the Rank-1 update matrix (Δ*W*^(1)) is rescaled to match the L2 norm of the full Δ*W*.
- **Rank-k% Subspace Evaluation:** In addition to Rank-1, the collective effect of the top *k*% of singular subspaces is also investigated to understand the broader distribution of update information.
- **Evaluation Model:** The performance of the Rank-1 subspace (or Rank-k%) is evaluated by adding its rescaled components to the base model parameters.

**Dynamics of Rank-1 Subspace:**
To track how the Rank-1 Subspace evolves during training:
- **Update Trajectory Construction:** For each module of the LLM, a sequence of u₁ vectors is collected across multiple training checkpoints, forming a module’s update trajectory (U₁ = {u₁^(t)}).
- **Dimensionality Reduction:** Principal Component Analysis (PCA) is initially applied to reduce the dimensionality of these high-dimensional u₁ vectors to 50 principal components. Subsequently, t-SNE is used on these components to create two-dimensional visualizations of the trajectories, facilitating pattern identification.
- **Partial Least Squares (PLS) Regression:** PLS regression is used to quantify the linearity between the module’s Rank-1 trajectory (u₁^(t)) and the corresponding reasoning accuracy (y^(t)) at each checkpoint. The R² statistic measures the strength of this linear relationship.

**Control Experiments and Ablation Studies:**
- **Comparison with SFT and DIST:** The identified properties are validated as specific to RL by performing control experiments using Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT) and Distillation (DIST) on the same LLMs and tasks. These non-RL methods serve as baselines to demonstrate that Rank-1 dominance and linear dynamics are distinctive characteristics of RL.
- **Ablation Study on Subspace Contributions:** The relative contributions of different individual singular subspaces beyond Rank-1 are investigated to confirm the unique importance of the dominant direction.
- **Scaling Factor Experiment:** The impact of scaling the magnitude of the Rank-1 update (λ Δ*W*^(1)) on performance is examined to understand the relationship between update strength and reasoning gains.

**Analysis of Underlying Causes:**
The study investigates potential causes for Rank-1 Dominance by:
- **Comparing Update Norms:** Quantifying the L2 norm of Δ*W* for RL, SFT, and DIST to compare the magnitude of parameter changes.
- **Analyzing Norm Proportions:** Examining the fraction of the total Δ*W* norm captured by the unscaled Rank-1 and Rank-1% subspaces.
- **Embedding Space Shifts:** Visualizing the distribution shifts in token embeddings induced by RL, SFT, and DIST using PCA and t-SNE to understand their impact on lower-level representations.
- **External Manifestations:** Analyzing properties of reasoning chains (CoTs) generated by RL-trained models, such as the proportion of greedy/non-greedy tokens and perplexity, and performing prefix token experiments to understand where Rank-1 influence is most critical.

**AlphaRL Acceleration Framework:**
Leveraging the established properties, AlphaRL is proposed as a plug-in acceleration scheme:
- **Prediction Mechanism:** AlphaRL uses an early training window to calculate the initial Rank-1 Subspace of Δ*W* and its linear growth rate. It then extrapolates this information to predict the final parameter update required to achieve a target reasoning performance. The scaled Rank-1 vectors (α^(t)σ₁^(t)u₁^(t)) are used with PLS regression to establish a linear relationship with relative accuracy.
- **Inversion and Update:** Given a target relative accuracy (e.g., y\* = 1 for full model performance), AlphaRL inverts the linear relationship to obtain the corresponding update vector, which is then combined with the right singular vector (v₁) to form the new Rank-1 update for each module.

**Experimental Scope:**
The methodology is applied and validated through extensive experiments:
- **LLMs:** 13 diverse models, ranging from 7B to 32B parameters (e.g., Qwen3, Llama3, GLM4).
- **RL Algorithms:** 10 advanced RL and fine-tuning algorithms (e.g., PPO, RLOO, GRPO, Dr.GRPO, DAPO, as well as DIST, SFT, DPO, On-Policy Distillation, and Spiral).
- **Reasoning Benchmarks:** 6 standard reasoning benchmarks (e.g., AIME24, AIME25, MATH-500, Minerva, GPQA, GSM8K).
- **Training Details:** Detailed experimental setups, including hyperparameters, batch sizes, and optimizer settings, are provided in the appendix to ensure reproducibility.
- **Adversarial Self-Play:** Experiments in adversarial self-play settings (Kuhn Poker Games) are also conducted to assess generalizability beyond reasoning tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research identifies two fundamental properties of RL-induced parameter updates in LLMs and demonstrates their application in accelerating training.

#### 4.1. Property 1: Rank-1 Dominance

- **Performance Recovery:** The top singular subspace (Rank-1 Subspace) of the parameter update matrix (Δ*W*) is nearly sufficient to recover reasoning improvements induced by RL. Across extensive experiments, retaining only the Rank-1 component of Δ*W* recovered over 99% of the performance gains achieved by fully RL-trained models. In some instances (e.g., RLOO, GRPO, and DAPO on MATH-500), the Rank-1 reconstructed model even matched or slightly surpassed the performance of the fully trained model.
- **Persistence Through Training:** This dominance is not limited to the final converged state but holds throughout the entire RL training process. Even at intermediate checkpoints, the Rank-1 Subspace captures the majority of reasoning improvements.
- **RL-Specific Phenomenon:** Control experiments with Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT) and Distillation (DIST) showed that these methods exhibit a strong dependence on higher-rank subspaces, requiring more components to achieve performance gains. This indicates that Rank-1 Dominance is a distinctive characteristic of RL training for LLMs.
- **Singular Subspace Contribution:** An ablation study confirmed that the Rank-1 Subspace significantly outperforms other individual singular subspaces in contributing to reasoning enhancement. Its performance declines as singular values decrease.
- **Scaling Behavior:** Performance increases rapidly with the scaling factor (λ) applied to the Rank-1 update, then saturates, peaking slightly below the full update’s L2 norm. This suggests that the core contribution of Rank-1 is primarily determined by its magnitude.

#### 4.2. Analysis of Rank-1 Dominance

- **Update Magnitude and Concentration:** RL updates involve parameter changes that are one to two orders of magnitude smaller than those in DIST and SFT. Despite smaller overall changes, RL updates are highly concentrated, with the unscaled Rank-1 and Rank-1% subspaces occupying a larger fraction of the total update norm compared to SFT and DIST.
- **Minimal Embedding Space Shift:** RL methods induce minimal distribution shifts in the LLM's embedding space, in contrast to DIST and SFT which cause noticeable global deviations. This implies that RL primarily optimizes high-level information flow and reasoning pathways, rather than fundamentally altering lower-level representations.
- **Approximate Low-Rank Property:** The study identifies a universal approximate low-rank property of Δ*W* specific to RL, a characteristic absent in SFT and DIST. This low-rank structure is hypothesized to contribute to RL-trained models' superior properties, such as minimizing catastrophic forgetting and improving generalization.
- **Critical Token Adjustments:** The Rank-1 Subspace captures critical token-level adjustments in reasoning chains. Analysis shows that RL introduces signals at a small number of critical positions, effectively activating latent reasoning patterns already present in the base model. These critical positions often correspond to initial-answer tokens (e.g., "Alright") or reasoning-transition tokens (e.g., "Wait").
- **Prefix Guidance:** Injecting only the Rank-1 information significantly reduces the number of "non-greedy" tokens (where the base model's prediction differs from the RL model's). Furthermore, guiding the base model with only the first 20 tokens (approximately the first sentence) generated by the Rank-1 model allows the base model to approach the performance of the full parameter update, indicating the early importance of Rank-1 in establishing solution strategies.

#### 4.3. Property 2: Rank-1 Linear Dynamics

- **Linear Evolution:** The dominant Rank-1 Subspace evolves approximately linearly throughout the RL training process. Visualizations of the u₁ vector trajectories exhibit smooth, nearly linear patterns, with color gradients aligned to training progress.
- **High Predictability:** Partial Least Squares (PLS) regression reveals a strong linear relationship between the Rank-1 update directions and corresponding reasoning performance, with R² values exceeding 0.96 for some modules. This linearity enables accurate prediction of later states from early-stage checkpoints, with average prediction errors less than 5%.
- **Module Importance:** The linearity (R² values) of Rank-1 Subspace evolution correlates with the functional roles of different modules. MLP modules, particularly in mid-to-high layers, generally exhibit higher R² values, suggesting they are key regions where RL allocates effective capacity for reasoning. Self-attention modules tend to have lower R² values, indicating noisier update signals. The R² value is shown to effectively quantify module-level contributions to performance.

#### 4.4. AlphaRL Acceleration Framework

- **Significant Speedup:** Based on these properties, AlphaRL, a plug-in acceleration framework, achieved up to 2.5× speedup in RL training time.
- **Performance Retention:** AlphaRL maintained high reasoning performance, retaining greater than 96% of the final reasoning capability compared to fully trained models. In some cases, AlphaRL-enhanced models at 40% training stages matched or surpassed the performance of fully trained models.
- **Consistency in Responses:** AlphaRL-enhanced models not only performed comparably in metrics but also exhibited similar reasoning patterns and approaches in their generated responses to fully trained models.
- **Generalizability:** AlphaRL demonstrated stable extrapolation behavior and significant improvements across various LLM sizes (7B to 32B), diverse RL algorithms (RLOO, GRPO, DAPO), and even in adversarial self-play settings (Kuhn Poker Games), highlighting its broad applicability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the understanding and practical application of reinforcement learning in large language models in several significant ways:

- **Enhanced Interpretability of RL in LLMs:** By identifying "Rank-1 Dominance" and "Rank-1 Linear Dynamics," the work provides a predictive and interpretable lens into the complex RL training process. It challenges the common perception of RL as a black-box optimization procedure, offering insights into how reasoning capabilities emerge and evolve at the parameter level. This move from post-hoc analysis to process-oriented understanding can foster more principled model design.
- **Efficient LLM Training Acceleration:** The proposed AlphaRL framework offers a substantial practical benefit by accelerating the computationally expensive RL training process by up to 2.5 times while maintaining over 96% of the final reasoning performance. This "free lunch" acceleration method is presented as a plug-and-play solution that requires no additional modules, hyperparameter tuning, or human intervention. Its orthogonality to existing acceleration paradigms suggests that it can be multiplicatively combined with other techniques, leading to even greater efficiencies. This efficiency is particularly crucial for the development and deployment of increasingly large and complex LLMs.
- **Bridging Empirical and Theoretical Gaps:** The identified low-dimensional, predictable core mechanism governing RL dynamics opens new avenues for bridging the gap between empirical scaling laws observed in LLMs and a deeper theoretical understanding of how intelligence and specific capabilities emerge in these models. It provides a basis for future theoretical work to formalize these empirical observations.
- **Versatile and Practical Tool:** The robust generalizability of these properties across diverse LLMs (from 7B to 32B parameters), numerous advanced RL algorithms, and even beyond standard reasoning tasks to adversarial self-play settings, positions AlphaRL as a versatile and practical tool for large-scale RL. This broad applicability suggests its potential impact across various domains requiring efficient and high-performance LLM training.
- **Future Research Directions:** The findings naturally suggest several promising future research avenues, including incorporating neuron attribution and causal tracing for more rigorous theoretical models, exploring advanced nonlinear forecasting methods for AlphaRL, and applying the framework to high-cost scenarios such as large-scale agents or multimodal training. Furthermore, the Rank-1 properties could be exploited for dynamic training monitoring and as a reward signal for reverse optimization, potentially leading to more flexible low-rank control methods.
