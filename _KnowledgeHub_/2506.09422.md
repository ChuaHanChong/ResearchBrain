# Time-Unified Diffusion Policy with Action Discrimination for Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Xi'an Jiaotong University developed the Time-Unified Diffusion Policy (TUDP), a new diffusion-based model for robotic manipulation that improves action generation efficiency and accuracy. TUDP achieved an 82.6% average success rate in RLBench simulations and 51/60 success in real-robot tasks, requiring fewer iterations compared to previous methods.

## Problem

- Existing diffusion policies for robotic manipulation require a large number of iterative denoising steps, leading to high computational costs and slow real-time responses.
- Conventional diffusion policies learn distinct velocity fields for each denoising timestep, making training complex and potentially reducing action accuracy, especially with limited demonstration data.
- Diffusion models can struggle with ambiguity in complex tasks where a noisy action might correspond to multiple successful outcomes, causing confusion and less precise denoising.

## Method

- Developed a **Time-Unified** ==Diffusion Policy (TUDP)== that uses a single, time-unified velocity field for denoising, simplifying the learning task compared to time-varying approaches.
- Integrated an Action Discrimination Network to predict 'action scores' for noisy actions, providing explicit guidance for distinguishing successful action neighborhoods.
- Utilized a two-stage action-wise training method to first train the discrimination network and then use its insights to inform the time-unified diffusion network.

## Results

- Achieved an average success rate of **82.6%** across 18 diverse RLBench tasks in simulation, establishing state-of-the-art performance with an average ranking of **1.9**.
- Demonstrated significantly higher success rates with fewer denoising iterations compared to baseline diffusion models, indicating improved efficiency and potential for near real-time robotic responses.
- Successfully completed 51 out of 60 real-robot manipulation tasks on a 6-DoF UR5 arm, learning effectively from a limited dataset of 58 demonstrations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Simplifying the denoising process to a time-unified velocity field, rather than time-varying, makes the model easier to train and allows for faster convergence to successful actions.
> - Explicitly integrating action discrimination capabilities helps the model resolve ambiguities when multiple successful actions are possible, leading to more precise denoising.
> - The action correlation weight "lambda", informed by the discrimination network, effectively guides the noisy action towards the correct successful target while suppressing conflicting directions from other potential successful actions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic manipulation is a significant area of research within embodied intelligence, characterized by diverse real-world applications requiring robots to predict appropriate next-step actions based on current observations. Complex manipulation tasks often involve multiple possible successful actions, necessitating robust methods for modeling action distributions. Generative models, particularly diffusion models, have emerged as effective tools for imitation learning in robotic manipulation due to their training robustness and ability to model complex data distributions.

However, existing diffusion-based policy methods face two primary challenges:
1. **Computational Efficiency:** They typically require a large number of iterative denoising steps (e.g., around 100 iterations for 3D diffusion-based policies), which translates to significant time consumption and hinders real-time robotic responses. Prior efforts to accelerate diffusion policies, such as Retrieval-Enhanced Asymmetric Diffusion (READ) and ManiCM (using consistency distillation), have introduced drawbacks like poor task generalization or dependence on teacher models and accuracy compromises. FlowPolicy, using flow matching, also aims for more efficient denoising paths.
2. **Training Complexity and Accuracy:** Current diffusion policies model a time-varying action denoising process, where different velocity fields are learned for each denoising timestep. This temporal complexity increases the difficulty of model training, especially with limited robot demonstrations, and can lead to suboptimal action accuracy. Specifically, the model may struggle to discern the specific successful action corresponding to a given noisy input, particularly at early denoising stages where noisy actions are nearly indistinguishable regardless of their target.

This research introduces the Time-Unified Diffusion Policy (TUDP) to address these limitations. TUDP aims to improve both the efficiency and accuracy of robot action generation by fundamentally altering the denoising process. Instead of a time-varying approach, it proposes a time-unified velocity field and integrates action discrimination capabilities. This positions TUDP as an advancement over existing diffusion policy methods by attempting to mitigate their core inefficiencies and training difficulties without relying on external databases or distillation from teacher models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a diffusion-based policy for robotic manipulation that can generate robot actions efficiently and accurately.

The motivation for pursuing this objective stems from two detailed problems observed in existing diffusion policy methods:

1. **Difficulty in Determining the Corresponding Successful Action:** In complex manipulation scenarios, a single noisy action, especially at the initial stages of denoising where it closely resembles a standard Gaussian distribution, might be associated with multiple possible successful actions. This ambiguity can cause the diffusion model to become "confused," leading to unclear denoising directions. The resulting interference among potential successful actions increases the time required for denoising and can compromise the accuracy of the generated robot actions, potentially pushing them away from any truly successful outcome.

2. **Difficulty in Learning the Time-Varying Action Denoising Process:** Conventional diffusion policies establish a distinct velocity field for each denoising timestep, creating a "time-varying" denoising process. This necessitates the model to learn denoising capabilities across a continuous spectrum of denoising times. Such a requirement significantly increases the complexity of model training. When robot demonstrations are limited, the inherent complexity of fitting these time-varying velocity fields can indirectly reduce the precision and accuracy of the action denoising performed by the diffusion policy.

To address these difficulties, the authors propose TUDP, which is motivated by two core ideas:
- Building a **time-unified velocity field** that is simpler to fit and converges efficiently to successful actions, especially by emphasizing the neighborhood of successful actions.
- Integrating **action recognition capabilities** to provide additional discrimination information, clarifying denoising directions and improving accuracy.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Time-Unified Diffusion Policy (TUDP) is designed to achieve efficient and accurate robotic manipulation through a novel time-unified denoising process. The method operates within a keyframe-based manipulation framework, where each keyframe captures scene observations and corresponding label actions. Robot actions are represented as end-effector postures, comprising translation, rotation, and gripper opening state.

**Data Preparation and Feature Extraction:**
Multi-modal scene observations (RGB-D images from multiple views, text instructions, and historical robot actions as proprioception) are processed using pre-trained, frozen encoders. A CLIP image encoder processes multi-view images, a CLIP text encoder processes text instructions, and rotary position embedding processes proprioception. These features are then aligned and fused through two parallel multimodal alignment modules.

**Two Core Network Branches:**
The fused multimodal features feed into two main components:
1. **Action Discrimination Network:**
 - **Purpose:** To predict an "action score" `s` for a given noisy action `y` based on the scene observation `x`. This score indicates whether `y` is within the "neighborhood" of a successful action `ˆy_i`.
 - **Mechanism:** It learns to determine if a noisy action `y` is within a radius `l` of a successful action `ˆy_i`. The target label `ˆs` is defined by a continuous negative exponential function that is 1 when `∥y - ˆy∥ < l` and approaches 0 otherwise, to manage sensitivity near the boundary.
 - **Role in Training:** This network is trained in the first stage of the action-wise training method and its output (action score) is used to inform the unified diffusion network in the second stage. It does not participate in inference.

2. **Unified Diffusion Network:**
 - **Purpose:** To predict the action noise `ϵ` that needs to be removed from a noisy action `y` to guide it towards a successful action, given the scene observation `x`.
 - **Key Innovation:** Unlike traditional diffusion models that take denoising time `t` as input, this network operates on a *time-unified* velocity field, meaning it learns a single noise prediction function `ϵ(x, y)` that is independent of `t`. This simplifies the learning task.
 - **Inference:** During inference, this network iteratively denoises an initial noisy action `y_0` by repeatedly computing `ϵ = ϵ_ϑ(x, y_t)` and updating `y_{t+1} = y_t - ϵ`. An early termination mechanism stops iteration if the change in action `∥y_{t+1} - y_t∥` falls below a threshold `δ`.

**Time-Unified Velocity Field Design:**
The core of TUDP lies in its novel time-unified velocity field.
- **Initial Concept:** A simple conditional velocity field `ε(y|ˆy) = v * (y - ˆy) / max{v, ∥y - ˆy∥}` guides noisy action `y` towards a label action `ˆy` with a velocity limit `v`.
- **Problem with Simple Merging:** If multiple successful actions exist, a simple average of `ε(y|ˆy_i)` could result in a non-zero velocity field at successful action points (`ε(ˆy_i) ≠ 0`), leading to deviations.
- **Solution: Action Correlation Weight `λ`:** To mitigate interference from other successful actions, a correlation weight `λ(y, ˆy_i)` is introduced. This `λ` is 0 if `y` is within the neighborhood `l` of *any other* successful action `ˆy_j` (where `j ≠ i`), and 1 otherwise. This ensures that when a noisy action is close to a specific successful action `ˆy_i`, the velocity field predominantly guides it towards `ˆy_i`, while suppressing conflicting directions from other `ˆy_j`.
- **Final Time-Unified Velocity Field:** The overall velocity field `ϵ(y)` is constructed as a weighted sum of conditional velocity fields, incorporating `λ`: `ϵ(y) = Σ (1/k) * p(y|ˆy_i) * λ(y, ˆy_i) * ε(y|ˆy_i)`. This design ensures that the action space converges accurately to successful actions with fewer iterations.

**Action-wise Training Method (Two-Stage):**
TUDP employs a two-stage training process (Algorithm 2) to optimize both the action discrimination and unified diffusion networks. Noisy actions for training are sampled from a conditional Gaussian distribution `p(y|ˆy)` centered around the label action `ˆy` with variance `σ`.

1. **Phase 1: Action Discrimination Training:**
 - The action discrimination network `s_θ(x, y)` is trained to predict the action score `s`.
 - The loss function `L_score = E[∥s_θ(x, y) - ˆs(y, ˆy)∥]` optimizes `θ` to accurately predict `ˆs`, which is 1 if `y` is near `ˆy` and 0 otherwise.

2. **Phase 2: Time-Unified Diffusion Training:**
 - The unified diffusion network `ϵ_ϑ(x, y)` is trained.
 - The *already trained* action discrimination network `s_θ` is used to approximate the correlation weight `λ(y, ˆy) = 1 - s_θ(x, y) * sgn(∥ˆy - y∥ - l)`. This `λ` acts as a dynamic weight for the target conditional velocity field `ε(y|ˆy)`.
 - The action loss `L_action = E[∥ϵ_ϑ(x, y) - λ(y, ˆy)ε(y|ˆy)∥]` optimizes `ϑ` to learn the time-unified velocity field.
 - A total loss `L_noise = L_action + w_open * L_open` includes a gripper opening loss `L_open` for the binary gripper state.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates TUDP across various simulation benchmarks and real-robot experiments, demonstrating its effectiveness in achieving both high accuracy and efficiency in robotic manipulation.

**Simulation Results on RLBench:**

- **Multi-view Setting:**
 - TUDP achieved a state-of-the-art (SOTA) average success rate of **82.6%** across 18 diverse RLBench tasks, with an average performance ranking of **1.9**.
 - It outperformed existing SOTA methods, including RVT2 and 3D Diffuser Actor, achieving the best performance on 8 tasks and suboptimal performance on 6 tasks.
 - Specifically, TUDP demonstrated significant improvements over the diffusion-based 3D Diffuser Actor on tasks involving multiple successful actions.
 - Some tasks showed performance degradation, which the authors suggest might be attributable to multi-task training.

- **Single-view Setting:**
 - In a more constrained scenario with only a front camera view across 10 tasks, TUDP again achieved the highest success rate of **83.8%** and the best average ranking of **1.4**.
 - It showed performance improvements on 5 tasks, confirming its robustness in single-view scenarios.

- **Success Rate at Different Iterations:**
 - A critical finding was TUDP's ability to maintain high success rates even with *fewer denoising iterations*. Compared to the 3D Diffuser Actor, TUDP exhibited a significantly smaller decrease in average success rate when the number of iterations was reduced, indicating efficient action generation and near real-time responsiveness. This confirms the method's core claim regarding efficiency.
 - A velocity limitation `v=0.5` was observed to reduce the efficiency of iterative denoising, leading to suboptimal performance at low iterations.

- **Visualization of Velocity Fields:**
 - Visualizations of the velocity fields in a complex "stack maroon blocks" task illustrated TUDP's effectiveness. The time-unified velocity field of TUDP, with velocity limitation, demonstrated accurate iterative denoising. In contrast, the time-varying velocity field of the 3D Diffuser Actor was less precise at initial timesteps and exhibited a smaller accurate region at final timesteps. TUDP's consistent and accurate guidance from any noisy action to a successful one was evident.

**Ablation Experiments:**

- **Impact of Components:**
 - **Action-wise training:** This component was identified as fundamental. Abandoning it led to a significant decrease in average success rate from 82.6% to 79.1%, highlighting the importance of explicit action discrimination.
 - **Velocity Limitation:** It improved the accuracy of noise prediction but could increase inference time for simpler tasks.
 - **Early Termination:** This mechanism, while shortening the iterative denoising process, showed no significant impact on the average success rate (81.8% vs 82.6%), particularly when velocity limitation was enabled.

- **Hyperparameter Sensitivity:**
 - **Neighborhood Radius (`l`):** An optimal neighborhood radius of `l = 0.1` achieved the highest average success rate (82.0%). Both larger radii (confusing successful actions) and smaller radii (increasing learning difficulty) led to reduced performance.
 - **Gaussian Variance (`σ`) for Noisy Actions:** A variance of `σ = 0.5` yielded the best average success rate (81.9%). Smaller variances (`σ = 0.05`) limited the policy network's focus to immediate successful actions, while larger variances (`σ = 1`) made it harder to account for random noisy actions across the wider action space.

**Real-Robot Results:**

- TUDP was evaluated on a 6-DoF UR5 robotic arm with a 2F-140 gripper and a single Azure Kinect RGB-D camera across six language-conditioned tasks.
- From a limited dataset of 58 demonstrations, TUDP achieved a **51/60 success rate** across 10 episodes per task.
- It demonstrated satisfactory performance on tasks requiring high precision, such as "put cylinder into box" and "stack blocks." These results indicate that TUDP can effectively learn precise real-world manipulation tasks from restricted demonstrations and achieve reliable execution.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Time-Unified Diffusion Policy (TUDP) represents a notable contribution to the field of robotic manipulation, primarily by addressing critical limitations of existing diffusion-based policies regarding efficiency and training complexity.

**Key Significance:**

- **Enhanced Efficiency and Accuracy:** TUDP successfully balances the trade-off between the speed of action generation and the precision of the generated actions. Its ability to achieve high success rates with fewer denoising iterations is a significant advancement for real-time robotic applications, where rapid decision-making is crucial.
- **Simplified Policy Learning:** By introducing a time-unified velocity field, TUDP simplifies the learning problem compared to traditional time-varying diffusion policies. This design reduces the complexity of model training, making it potentially more robust even with limited demonstration data.
- **Improved Action Discrimination:** The integration of an action discrimination network and an action-wise training method allows TUDP to implicitly learn to discern successful actions. This capability helps in resolving ambiguity when multiple successful actions are possible, leading to clearer denoising directions and improved accuracy.
- **Robust Performance Across Scenarios:** TUDP demonstrated state-of-the-art performance in diverse simulation settings (multi-view and single-view, various tasks) and validated its effectiveness on real-world robotic platforms with limited demonstrations. This breadth of validation suggests its generalizability and practical applicability.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Real-time Robotic Systems:** The improved inference speed makes TUDP a strong candidate for real-time or near real-time reactive robotic systems in manufacturing, logistics, or service robotics, where quick responses to dynamic environments are essential.
- **More Accessible Policy Learning:** The simplification of the diffusion policy training process could make it more accessible for practitioners with less extensive datasets or computational resources.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The concepts of a time-unified velocity field and explicit action discrimination within a diffusion framework could inspire new directions for developing more efficient, accurate, and robust generative models for various robot learning tasks beyond manipulation.
- **Complex Task Execution:** TUDP's demonstrated ability to handle tasks with multiple successful actions and high precision requirements could lead to more sophisticated and adaptive robotic behaviors in unstructured environments.

**Limitations (as identified by the authors):**
The efficacy of TUDP is contingent on certain prior assumptions, such as the setting of the neighborhood radius of successful actions, which might restrict its applicability in dense interaction scenarios. Additionally, the method involves several hyperparameters that require careful tuning, increasing the complexity of model configuration. These aspects represent avenues for future refinement to broaden TUDP's applicability and ease of use.
