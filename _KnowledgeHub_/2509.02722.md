# Planning with Reasoning using Vision Language World Model

> [!summary] Summary
> The Vision Language World Model (VLWM) introduces a foundation model for language-based world modeling from natural videos, enabling high-level task planning. It leverages natural language as an abstract world state representation and achieves state-of-the-art performance on visual planning benchmarks and human-preferred plans in evaluations.

## Problem

- Traditional world models are limited to low-level, continuous control and struggle with high-level task planning that requires semantic and temporal abstraction.
- Existing LLMs lack direct grounding in sensory experience, leading to hallucinations in real-world planning, while VLMs are not designed for action-conditioned world state prediction.
- Generating future observations (e.g., raw pixels) is computationally inefficient for long-horizon roll-outs and often captures task-irrelevant details.

## Method

- A ==Vision Language World Model (VLWM)== is developed, using natural language as an abstract world state representation, trained on a large corpus of natural videos.
- A two-stage data generation pipeline compresses raw video into a =="Tree of Captions"== and extracts structured goal-plan trajectories using LLM self-refinement.
- A ==dual-system planning approach== combines efficient reactive plan generation (System-1) with reflective reasoning via cost minimization using a ==self-supervised critic== (System-2).

## Results

- Achieved new state-of-the-art performance on Visual Planning for Assistance (VPA) benchmarks, with absolute gains of **+3.2%** in Success Rate and **+3.9%** in Mean Accuracy.
- Secured the highest Elo score (**1261**) in human evaluations with PlannerArena, demonstrating superior plan quality, and System-2 planning improved Elo by **+27%** over System-1.
- Attained a state-of-the-art BLEU-4 score of **55.6** on the RoboVQA benchmark, and the self-supervised critic model achieved **98.4%** accuracy in goal achievement detection on in-domain data.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Natural language provides an efficient, semantically rich, and interpretable interface for abstract world states, effectively bridging the gap between perception and reasoning.
> - Combining fast reactive planning with a deliberative, cost-minimizing reflective system significantly improves plan quality and robustness in complex, long-horizon tasks.
> - Self-supervised training of a critic model enables objective evaluation of candidate plans without relying on explicit, engineered reward functions or perfect demonstrations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of intelligent agents capable of complex, long-horizon planning remains a significant challenge in AI. Traditional world models have shown strong performance in low-level, continuous control tasks (e.g., robotics, autonomous driving) by predicting raw sensory data or latent embeddings. However, extending these to **high-level task planning, characterized by semantic and temporal abstraction**, is largely underdeveloped. This paper directly addresses this critical gap.

The authors position VLWM against several existing paradigms:
- **Prompting-based LLMs:** While versatile, LLMs lack direct grounding in sensory experience, making their planning abilities limited and prone to hallucination in real-world contexts.
- **Visual-Language Models (VLMs):** Primarily focused on visual perception, VLMs are not inherently designed for action-conditioned prediction of world-state transitions.
- **Simulation environments:** Learning from simulations, though useful, struggles to scale to the diversity and complexity of real-world activities.
- **Generative world models (e.g., diffusion models):** These models generate future observations (e.g., pixels), but this approach is often ill-posed due to partial observability and uncertainty. More critically, it is computationally inefficient for long-horizon roll-outs and captures task-irrelevant details.
- **JEPA-based world models (e.g., DINO-WM, V-JEPA2):** While employing abstract latent embeddings for prediction, these have predominantly focused on low-level motion planning. Their latent spaces, while compact, lack the interpretability and seamless integration with human knowledge that language offers.

VLWM's key contribution to the landscape is its novel approach of using **natural language as an abstract world state representation** within a world model. This offers several advantages:
1. **Semantic Abstraction:** Language inherently provides high-level semantic meaning, which is crucial for understanding complex tasks.
2. **Computational Efficiency:** Generating language is significantly more efficient than generating raw sensory data.
3. **Interpretability:** Language-based states are intuitive and interpretable, allowing for easier human oversight and integration with human feedback.
4. **Leveraging LLM/VLM Ecosystems:** It enables seamless integration with the vast prior knowledge and engineering tools developed for language models.

Furthermore, the paper introduces a **dual-system planning paradigm** (System-1 reactive and System-2 reflective planning), drawing inspiration from cognitive science. This differentiates it from purely reactive imitation learning approaches or traditional model-predictive control schemes, which may lack explicit reasoning. The self-supervised training of the critic for cost minimization is also a significant advancement, enabling robust planning without reliance on manually engineered rewards or perfect demonstrations, thus pushing the boundaries of learning from reward-free offline data. The work aligns with the broader trend of building more general-purpose AI agents that can reason, plan, and assist humans in diverse real-world scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the Vision Language World Model (VLWM) are driven by the ambition to develop more capable and human-aligned AI agents for complex, real-world tasks. Specifically, the paper aims to:

1. **Develop a High-Level World Model for Abstract Planning:** The central objective is to create a world model that can understand and reason about actions with *semantic and temporal abstraction*. Existing world models are largely confined to low-level, continuous control, leaving a critical gap in enabling AI to optimize plans for tasks involving conceptual understanding and long-horizon sequences of actions.
2. **Utilize Natural Language as an Abstract World State Representation:** Motivated by the limitations of pixel-based or latent embedding world models (computational inefficiency, lack of interpretability, entanglement with task-irrelevant details), the authors aim to leverage natural language as an intuitive, efficient, and interpretable representation for world states and their changes.
3. **Enable Both Reactive and Reflective Planning:** The work seeks to provide a versatile planning framework that supports both "system-1" reactive plan generation (fast, direct policy decoding) and "system-2" reflective planning (deliberative reasoning via cost minimization). This dual-mode design aims to combine efficiency with the capacity for foresight, evaluation of alternatives, and correction of suboptimal decisions.
4. **Learn from Large-Scale, Uncurated Natural Videos:** A key motivation is to train the world model directly from massive, reward-free offline data, specifically uncurated natural videos. This approach aims to circumvent the scalability issues of learning from simulations or the need for explicit, verifiable rewards in reinforcement learning.
5. **Address the "Impossible Triangle" in Video-to-Text Extraction:** The authors are motivated to overcome the challenge of simultaneously achieving high spatial resolution, long temporal horizons, and the use of large VLMs for extracting structured language representations from videos, proposing an efficient two-stage abstraction pipeline.
6. **Develop a Self-Supervised Critic for Cost Evaluation:** To facilitate reflective planning, an objective is to train a critic model in a self-supervised manner that can accurately assess the desirability of candidate action plans by measuring semantic distance to the desired goal state, without requiring explicit human annotations for rewards.

The overarching motivation is to unlock a wide range of practical applications, such as enabling AI agents in wearable devices to assist humans in complex tasks (e.g., cooking, repair) and empowering embodied agents to autonomously pursue long-horizon goals with human-like reasoning capabilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The VLWM framework is designed to perceive environments visually and predict world evolution using language-based abstraction. It encompasses a unique data generation pipeline, a specific model architecture, and two distinct planning modes.

#### 3.1. Vision-Language World Modeling Data Generation

To overcome the challenges of extracting high-level semantic information from raw video at scale, VLWM employs a two-stage abstraction pipeline:

1. **Compress Video into Tree of Captions:**
 - **Challenge:** Directly processing entire videos with large VLMs for fine-grained, long-horizon understanding is computationally infeasible.
 - **Approach:** Raw videos are first compressed into a hierarchical "Tree of Captions."
 - **Process:**
 - Video features are extracted using a state-of-the-art Perception Encoder (PE-G14).
 - Hierarchical agglomerative clustering is applied to these features to segment the video into coherent units, adaptively determining the tree structure. Segments with minimal increases in within-segment feature variance are merged, ensuring semantic coherence.
 - Detailed captions for each video segment (nodes in the tree, excluding very short ones) are generated using PerceptionLM-3B.
 - **Benefit:** This significantly reduces data volume (e.g., 1.1 TB Ego4D videos to <900 MB captions) while preserving essential semantic information for later processing.

2. **Extract Plans with LLM Self-Refine:**
 - **Challenge:** Generating structured, high-quality goal-plan descriptions requires sophisticated reasoning and adherence to specific formats.
 - **Approach:** Structured textual representations (goal description, goal interpretation, action descriptions, world states) are extracted from the Tree of Captions using LLMs.
 - **Process:**
 - The Tree of Captions is linearized using a depth-first search (DFS) traversal order to be compatible with LLM input formats.
 - An LLM (Llama-4 Maverick) is prompted with the linearized captions, detailed output requirements, and examples to generate an initial draft.
 - An iterative **Self-Refine** procedure (Madaan et al., 2023) is applied. In each iteration, the LLM provides feedback on the current draft, identifies problems against predefined requirements (e.g., action granularity, state change detail, logical coherence), and produces a revised version. This process is repeated for a set number of iterations to optimize quality.
 - Additional information like speech transcripts (for web videos) and expert commentary (for EgoExo4D) is provided to the LLM during this stage to enhance video understanding.
 - **Output Structure:** The extracted data includes:
 - **Goal description:** High-level summary (e.g., "cook tomato and eggs").
 - **Goal interpretation:** Contextual explanation of initial and expected final world states.
 - **Action descriptions:** Clear, concise, imperative sentences for execution (e.g., "Preheat the skillet on the stove").
 - **World states:** Detailed, internal explanations of the consequences of each action, serving as an information bottleneck for reasoning (e.g., "This action prepares the skillet...").
 - **Data Sources:** The generation process is applied to a large corpus of web instructional videos (COIN, CrossTask, YouCook2, HowTo100M) and egocentric recordings (EPIC-KITCHENS-100, EgoExo4D), totaling 180k videos and yielding 1.2 million goal-plan trajectories. Additionally, text-only reasoning paths from NaturalReasoning are repurposed.

#### 3.2. Training of Vision Language World Model (VLWM)

- **Model Architecture:** VLWM is initialized from PerceptionLM-8B, inheriting strong visual perception and language capabilities. It is a JEPA-style world model, but it predicts abstract language representations of future world states rather than raw pixels or latent embeddings.
- **Training Task:** The model is trained to predict the future (goal, interpretation, and an interleaved sequence of action-state pairs) conditioned on a configuration prompt and visual/textual context from past observations (Eq. 1).
 `[config, context] -> [goal, interpretation, <A0, ∆S0>..., <AN, ∆SN>]`
- **Loss Function:** The model optimizes a cross-entropy loss for next-token prediction, effectively learning goal inference, action anticipation, and action-conditioned world state dynamics prediction.
- **Context:** Visual context includes 32 frames at 448x448 resolution.
- **Scale:** Trained on 12 nodes of 8xH100 GPUs for 5 days.

#### 3.3. Planning with Reasoning (Dual-System Approach)

VLWM supports two planning modes:

1. **System-1 Reactive Planning:**
 - **Mechanism:** Direct text completion. Given the config, context, and goal, VLWM autoregressively generates a sequence of action-state pairs.
 - **Characteristics:** Computationally efficient, well-suited for short-horizon or in-domain tasks. World state descriptions act as "internal chains of thought" to track progress.
 - **Limitation:** Lacks foresight and reflection; decisions are irreversible, leading to potential error accumulation or suboptimal plans (especially from imperfect training demonstrations).

2. **System-2 Reflective Planning by Cost Minimization:**
 - **Mechanism:** Combines the VLWM with a critic module to evaluate multiple predicted futures, enabling a reasoning process of searching for optimal plans.
 - **Actor:** Can be the VLWM itself or an external module, proposing candidate action sequences.
 - **VLWM Simulation:** For each candidate action sequence, VLWM simulates the resulting world states.
 - **Critic Evaluation:** A separate critic module evaluates the desirability (cost) of these simulated trajectories against the desired goal. The planner then selects the candidate sequence that minimizes this cost.

#### 3.4. Learning the Critic from Self-supervision (VLWM-critic-1B)

- **Objective:** To quantify the semantic distance between predicted world states and the desired goal state.
- **Model:** A language model (initialized from Llama-3.2-1B) trained with a self-supervised objective.
- **Training Signals (ranking loss):**
 1. **Progress vs. Distractor:** Samples are constructed by appending either valid next steps (`C_good`) or irrelevant distractor steps (`C_bad`) to a base partial trajectory (`C_base`). The critic is trained to satisfy `C_good < C_base < C_bad`.
 2. **Temporal Coherence:** Negative samples are generated by randomly shuffling steps in a base trajectory (`C_shuffled`). The critic is trained to enforce `C_base < C_shuffled`.
- **Loss Function:** A ranking loss (Eq. 2) with a fixed margin and a cost-centering regularization term.
- **Data Sources:** VLWM progress data (HowTo100M, NaturalReasoning), preference tuning datasets (UltraFeedback, Orca DPO, Math-Step-DPO), and triplet-based datasets for learning semantic similarity (MS-MARCO, SQUAD, HotPotQA, NaturalQuestions, FEVER). This diverse data augments the critic's ability to generalize semantic quality assessment.
- **Scale:** Trained for one epoch with a batch size of 128 (2.7k steps) on a single node of 8xH100 GPUs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The VLWM demonstrates state-of-the-art performance across various benchmarks, showcasing its capabilities in high-level planning, grounded reasoning, and goal achievement detection.

#### 4.1. Visual Planning for Assistance (VPA) Benchmarks

- **Evaluation:** VLWM-8B was fine-tuned and evaluated on the COIN and CrossTask datasets for procedural planning, predicting the next T (3 or 4) high-level steps. Metrics included Success Rate (SR), Mean Accuracy (mAcc), and Mean IoU (mIoU).
- **Results:** VLWM-8B consistently achieved new state-of-the-art performance, outperforming previous baselines, including larger models like VidAssist (70B LLM).
 - Averaged across the four settings (COIN T=3, T=4; CrossTask T=3, T=4), VLWM delivered absolute gains of **+3.2% in SR, +3.9% in mAcc, and +2.9 points in mIoU** compared to the next best model.
 - For instance, on COIN T=3, VLWM achieved 27.9 SR, 50.1 mAcc, and 69.3 mIoU, compared to VidAssist's 21.8 SR, 44.4 mAcc, and 64.4 mIoU.

#### 4.2. Human Evaluation with PlannerArena

- **Methodology:** A novel Elo-based human evaluation framework, inspired by ChatbotArena, where human evaluators choose the preferred plan between two anonymous models given a video context and textual goal.
- **Models Compared:** VLWM System-2 (cost-minimizing critic), VLWM System-1 (cost-maximizing critic, essentially random/suboptimal policy), Llama-4-Maverick, Qwen2.5VL, PerceptionLM, and Ground Truth plans.
- **Results:**
 - **VLWM System-2 achieved the highest Elo score (1261)** by a significant margin, demonstrating its superior plan quality based on human preference.
 - Llama-4-Maverick was the second most preferred (1099 Elo).
 - Even VLWM System-1 (cost-maximizing) (992 Elo) was generally preferred over ground truth and other VLMs, suggesting a strong base generation capability.
 - Crucially, the evaluation highlighted the **inadequacy of current procedural planning datasets' ground truth annotations**, which often have low quality. Ground truth plans had varying win rates (e.g., 69.5% on EgoExo4D, but only 43.6% on COIN and 42.2% on CrossTask), emphasizing the need for human preference-based evaluation.
 - System-2 planning improved the Elo score by **+27% upon System-1**, underscoring the value of reflective planning.

#### 4.3. RoboVQA

- **Evaluation:** VLWM-8B was tested on the RoboVQA benchmark, which assesses robotics-focused visual question answering requiring understanding of complex visual scenes and coherent action sequences. BLEU scores were used.
- **Results:** VLWM achieved a BLEU-1 score of 74.2, ranking within the top two models and outperforming strong specialized robotic LLMs like ThinkVLA-3B (62.4) and ThinkAct (69.1), and competing closely with RoboBrain-7B (72.1). Notably, VLWM achieved the **highest BLEU-4 score of 55.6**, surpassing RoboBrain’s 55.1. This indicates VLWM's robust generalization and ability to effectively integrate visual and language information for grounded reasoning in embodied settings, despite not being specialized for robotic data.

#### 4.4. Critic Evaluations

Intrinsic evaluations confirmed the critic model's intended behavior.

- **Goal Achievement Detection:**
 - **Task:** Identify the step at which a goal is achieved by evaluating costs for partial trajectories with valid and distractor steps.
 - **Results:** VLWM-critic-1B significantly outperformed baseline semantic similarity models (Qwen3-Embedding, Qwen3-Reranker) on most subsets. It achieved **98.4% accuracy on in-domain VLWM-Instruct data** and 92.7% on unseen VLWM-Ego data. On OGP datasets, it showed a clear advantage over baselines (e.g., 72.9% vs. 65.6% on OGP-Robot), even with 8x fewer parameters.
 - **Ablation Studies:** Removing goal interpretations or world state descriptions from the critic's input consistently reduced performance, with a more significant drop on out-of-domain (OOD) data (e.g., 26.1% drop on EK-100 without states), highlighting their importance for effective generalization.
 - **Cost Curves:** Visualizations showed VLWM-critic-1B producing cleaner "energy landscapes" with minimum costs accurately aligning with goal completion, unlike noisy baseline behaviors.

- **Procedural Planning on WorldPrediction-PP:**
 - **Task:** Identify the correctly ordered sequence of actions from shuffled distractors, given initial/final visual states.
 - **Results:** VLWM-critic-1B established a new **state-of-the-art with 45.4% accuracy**. This was a zero-shot scenario, demonstrating the critic's ability to generalize to new goal descriptions and action representations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Vision Language World Model (VLWM) represents a significant advancement in the field of AI, particularly in the domain of high-level planning and embodied intelligence. Its contributions have several key significances and potential impacts:

1. **Bridging Perception and Reasoning with Language:** The core innovation of using natural language as an abstract representation for world states effectively bridges the gap between perception-focused VLMs and reasoning-centric LLMs. This provides a highly interpretable, semantically rich, and computationally efficient interface for AI agents to understand and interact with complex environments, moving beyond raw pixel manipulation or opaque latent embeddings.
2. **Enabling Robust High-Level Planning:** By learning directly from large-scale natural videos and predicting abstract language-based state changes, VLWM addresses the long-standing challenge of high-level task planning that involves semantic and temporal abstraction. Its state-of-the-art performance on benchmarks like VPA and WorldPrediction-PP demonstrates a new level of capability in generating coherent, long-horizon plans.
3. **Advancing Dual-System AI Architectures:** The integration of System-1 reactive planning (fast, policy decoding) with System-2 reflective planning (deliberative, cost-minimization based reasoning) marks a crucial step towards more human-like intelligence. The demonstrated +27% Elo improvement of System-2 over System-1 highlights the power of internal trial-and-error reasoning within a learned world model, allowing for foresight, error correction, and optimization in complex scenarios.
4. **Scalable Learning from Uncurated Data:** The efficient two-stage data generation pipeline (Tree of Captions and LLM Self-Refine) is pivotal. It enables the extraction of structured, high-quality goal-plan knowledge from massive, uncurated video datasets, circumventing the need for expensive, domain-specific human annotations or simulated environments. This unlocks unprecedented scalability for training world models on real-world data.
5. **Self-Supervised Cost Modeling:** The novel approach to training a critic model through self-supervision to assess semantic distance to a goal is highly impactful. It provides a robust, generalizable mechanism for evaluating plan desirability without relying on explicit, engineered reward functions, which are often scarce and difficult to define for high-level tasks. This further enhances the autonomy and adaptability of AI agents.
6. **Improved Human-AI Collaboration and Trust:** The interpretability offered by language-based world states means that humans can understand *why* an AI agent is proposing a particular action or plan, fostering greater trust and enabling more effective collaboration. This is crucial for applications like AI assistants in wearable devices or embodied agents working alongside humans.
7. **Catalyst for Embodied AI:** The strong performance on RoboVQA suggests that VLWM has significant potential to guide robotic agents effectively in realistic, multi-embodiment settings. By providing grounded, high-level reasoning and planning, VLWM can empower embodied AI systems to tackle more intricate and purposeful tasks in the physical world.
8. **Open Science Contribution:** The commitment to open-sourcing models and data will undoubtedly accelerate research and development in language-based world modeling, allowing the broader scientific community to build upon these foundations.

In conclusion, VLWM pushes the boundaries of AI by offering a powerful and interpretable framework for high-level world modeling and planning. Its ability to learn from vast amounts of raw video data and reason with language holds immense promise for developing more intelligent, adaptable, and trustworthy AI systems capable of assisting humans and operating autonomously in complex, real-world environments.
