# Canonical Representation and Force-Based Pretraining of 3D Tactile for Dexterous Visuo-Tactile Policy Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> This work makes 3D tactile data from distributed sensors usable for dexterous manipulation. Each taxel is canonicalized into a per-sensor unit frame and paired with the sensor's 6D pose and 3D force, encoded by a graph network pretrained with masked local-force and net-force prediction. Plugged into a DinoV2 diffusion policy, it reaches 78% average success on four real-world contact-rich tasks.

## Problem

- Distributed tactile sensors cover large areas of dexterous hands, producing high-dimensional input with no standardized large datasets or strong pretrained 3D tactile backbones.
- Converting tactile readings into 2D images to reuse visual backbones distorts and discards spatial relationships among taxels within a sensor and across sensors.
- Existing graph-based tactile encoders use only a subset of 3D information, position or force, are task-specific, and need large data to learn useful features.

## Method

- ==Canonical tactile representation==: each taxel's coordinate is normalized into a per-sensor unit frame by diagonal length, concatenated with the sensor origin's 6D pose and the taxel's 3D force.
- A ==GraphMAE==-based graph network encodes the 12D per-taxel set as a 4-neighbourhood graph, pretrained on self-collected robotic play data rather than task demonstrations.
- Two force-based ==self-supervised objectives==: ==masked local-force reconstruction== with MSE on masked taxels, then re-encoding the filled-in forces to regress the analytically computed ==net force==.

## Results

- Averaged **78%** success across open box, reorientation, flip and assembly, over T-DEX at **63%**, vision-only diffusion policy at **53%**, MLP-encoded HATO at **48%**, and a 9D graph baseline at **0%**.
- Ablations: removing canonical representation collapses performance to **0%** even with pretraining; keeping it without pretraining gives **48%**; dropping net-force prediction falls to **28%**, dropping local-force to **48%**.
- Freezing sensor poses or zeroing forces both make the flip task fail entirely; on unseen objects the policy succeeds **5/8** on open box and **6/8** on flip.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Normalizing sensor coordinates into a shared unit frame both aligns heterogeneous sensor layouts and amplifies millimetre-scale taxel spacing so encoders can discriminate neighbours.
> - Representation choice can dominate pretraining: with indistinguishable taxel features, self-supervised pretraining recovers nothing, while good representation alone already yields workable policies.
> - Pretraining targets should mirror the downstream physics; predicting net force from local forces mattered more than local reconstruction for object-moving tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation is rapidly advancing, with a strong focus on enabling robots to perform complex, contact-rich tasks in unstructured environments. Human-level dexterity, essential for integrating robots into daily life, heavily relies on robust perception, particularly tactile sensing. This paper directly addresses a significant challenge in this domain: effectively utilizing high-dimensional 3D tactile data from distributed sensors on dexterous hands.

**Challenges in Tactile Sensing for Dexterous Manipulation:**
- **High Dimensionality:** Distributed tactile sensors, like those covering dexterous hands, generate a large amount of data (many taxels, each with multi-axis force readings), making feature learning complex.
- **Lack of Standardized Datasets:** Unlike vision, there are no large, standardized 3D tactile datasets or strong pretrained backbones. This hinders the development of generalizable tactile encoders.
- **Information Loss in 2D Conversion:** Many previous works simplify 3D tactile data by converting it into 2D images to leverage powerful visual backbones (e.g., T-DEX). However, this transformation inherently loses spatial relationships between taxels, especially those on different parts of a multi-fingered hand.
- **Limitations of Graph Neural Networks (GNNs):** While GNNs have been applied to represent 3D tactile data, existing methods often use only a subset of the available information (e.g., 3D position or 3D force) and require large amounts of data to learn effective features without robust pretraining strategies tailored for 3D.
- **Sim-to-Real Gap:** Learning policies in simulation is efficient for data generation but often suffers from a significant gap when transferring to real-world tactile data, limiting the fidelity of tactile feedback used.

**Contribution to the Landscape:**
This work directly tackles the limitations of current approaches by focusing on **3D tactile data** and introducing novel solutions for its representation and pretraining.
- **Addressing 3D Data Complexity:** Instead of converting to 2D, the authors propose a "canonical representation" that preserves and enhances the inherent spatial relationships of 3D tactile data. This is a crucial departure from 2D image-based methods and offers a more faithful representation of physical contact.
- **Bridging the Pretraining Gap:** Recognizing the absence of strong pretrained backbones for 3D tactile data, the paper introduces a "force-based self-supervised pretraining task." This leverages "play data" (unlabeled robotic interaction data) to learn meaningful features, similar to how large language models or visual models are pretrained, but specifically for the unique characteristics of tactile forces. This is a significant improvement over purely task-specific learning, which is data-intensive, and provides a strategy for intra-modal pretraining of 3D tactile data, contrasting with common cross-modal pretraining efforts.
- **Enhancing Dexterous Manipulation:** By improving 3D tactile feature learning, the paper aims to enable more precise and robust control for fine-grained, contact-rich dexterous manipulation tasks, which are often challenging for vision-only or less sophisticated tactile systems.

In essence, this research pushes the boundaries of how robots perceive and interact with objects through touch, moving beyond simplified representations and towards more robust, data-efficient learning paradigms for complex 3D tactile information, thereby enhancing robot dexterity in real-world scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching goal of this research is to **overcome the significant challenges associated with learning effective features from high-dimensional 3D tactile data for dexterous robotic manipulation policies.** The authors are motivated by the critical role of tactile sensing in enabling robots to perform fine-grained, contact-rich tasks, akin to human manipulation capabilities.

The key objectives can be broken down as follows:

1. **Develop an Effective Canonical Representation for 3D Tactile Data:**
 - **Motivation:** Existing methods either lose spatial information by converting 3D tactile data to 2D images or struggle with the vast, unaligned feature space of raw 3D tactile readings (e.g., 9D pose and force per taxel). The sparse distribution of taxels and small inter-taxel distances within a sensor also make it difficult for neural networks to distinguish local features.
 - **Objective:** To propose a representation that aligns features across differently distributed sensors, reduces the overall feature space complexity, amplifies relative distances between taxels for better local feature capture, and preserves the inherent spatial relationships of 3D tactile data.

2. **Introduce a Force-Based Self-Supervised Pretraining Strategy for 3D Tactile Data:**
 - **Motivation:** The high dimensionality of tactile data and the lack of large, standardized datasets mean that learning effective tactile features from scratch for specific tasks is inefficient and data-intensive. Humans instinctively use force for manipulation, suggesting that force-related features are crucial.
 - **Objective:** To design a pretraining task that encourages the tactile encoder to learn features critical for manipulation, specifically related to both **local forces** (how individual taxels interact) and **net forces** (the overall force applied by the hand). This pretraining aims to improve the efficiency and effectiveness of downstream policy learning.

3. **Integrate and Validate the Proposed Methods in a Real-World Dexterous Manipulation System:**
 - **Motivation:** The ultimate test of any robotic perception or control method is its performance in real-world scenarios, especially for complex dexterous tasks that demand high robustness and precision.
 - **Objective:** To demonstrate the effectiveness and robustness of the canonical representation and force-based pretraining by integrating the pretrained tactile encoder into an imitation learning framework (Diffusion Policy) and evaluating its performance on a set of challenging, fine-grained, contact-rich tasks using a real dexterous robotic hand.

By achieving these objectives, the authors aim to significantly advance the state-of-the-art in robot tactile perception and control, paving the way for more capable and versatile robotic manipulators.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose a multi-faceted approach consisting of a novel 3D tactile representation, a self-supervised pretraining strategy, and its integration into a visuo-tactile policy learning framework.

#### 3.1. A. Canonical Tactile Representation

To address the challenges of high-dimensional and sparsely distributed 3D tactile data, the authors introduce a novel canonical representation:

1. **Raw Data:** For each taxel of the distributed tactile sensor, the system obtains a 3D force vector ($F \in R^3$) and a 6D pose ($P \in R^6$) through forward kinematics. This forms a 9D raw representation per taxel.
2. **Canonicalization:** The core idea is to normalize the coordinates of each taxel *within its respective sensor* into a unit frame (e.g., ranging from -1 to 1 along each axis). This is achieved by dividing the taxel's coordinates by the diagonal length of the sensor's coordinate system.
 - **Benefits:**
 - **Feature Alignment:** Canonicalization aligns the features of different sensors, even if they have slightly varying taxel distributions, making them consistently representable.
 - **Feature Space Reduction:** By unifying the coordinate frames, it effectively reduces the complexity of the overall feature space that the neural network needs to learn.
 - **Enhanced Discriminability:** It amplifies the relative distances between taxels within the same sensor, making individual taxel features more distinguishable for the neural network and facilitating the capture of localized features.
3. **Complete Representation:** The canonicalized 3D position of each taxel ($T \in R^3$) is combined with the 6D pose of its *sensor's origin* relative to the hand's base ($P_s$). Together with the 3D force ($F$), this forms a 12D representation for each taxel ($R = [P_s, T, F]$). This explicitly captures both intra-sensor (canonicalized $T$) and inter-sensor (global $P_s$) spatial relationships.
4. **Graph Neural Network (GNN) Encoding:** To handle the inherent sparsity and irregular distribution of taxels across the hand, the proposed 12D taxel representations are organized as a set $S = \{R_1..., R_n\}$. A graph $G = (S, E)$ is constructed, where nodes are the taxels (with their 12D features) and edges $E$ are defined by the 4-neighborhood of each tactile node. A GNN (specifically, Graph Attention Networks for the baseline GNN, and GraphMAE for pretraining and then fine-tuning in the proposed method) is then used to encode this graph into a compact tactile feature vector.

#### 3.2. B. Force-Based Self-Supervised Pretraining

To ensure the tactile encoder learns features relevant to manipulation, the authors propose a two-part force-based self-supervised pretraining strategy using "play data" (unlabeled interaction data collected by the robot):

1. **Masked Local Force Prediction:**
 - **Goal:** To enable the encoder to learn local force relationships and features of individual taxels.
 - **Mechanism:** During pretraining, a random portion of the tactile forces for certain taxels is masked out. The masked tactile data is fed into the encoder, which then outputs a latent representation. A decoder reconstructs the original force values for only the masked taxels.
 - **Loss:** Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss is calculated between the reconstructed and original masked force values. This forces the encoder to understand how local forces propagate and relate to each other.

2. **Net Force Prediction:**
 - **Goal:** To help the encoder understand the relationship between local forces and the overall "net force" applied by the hand, which is crucial for object manipulation.
 - **Mechanism:** First, the local force prediction task is performed. The *predicted* force values for the masked taxels are then substituted back into the original tactile data. This "modified" tactile data (with reconstructed masked forces) is then re-encoded by the *same* encoder. An MLP then predicts the total net force ($F_{nP}$) applied by the hand, calculated from the original taxel poses and forces ($F_{nG}$).
 - **Loss:** MSE loss is calculated between the predicted net force ($F_{nP}$) and the ground truth net force ($F_{nG}$).
 - **Shared Encoder:** Both tasks share the same encoder, ensuring that it learns features relevant to both fine-grained local interactions and global force application. The backbone used for pretraining is GraphMAE.

#### 3.3. C. Visuo-Tactile Policy Learning

After pretraining, the tactile encoder is integrated into an imitation learning framework for downstream dexterous manipulation tasks:

1. **Policy Backbone:** The authors adopt Diffusion Policy due to its ability to model complex action distributions.
2. **Multimodal Input:**
 - **Vision:** A pretrained DinoV2 is used as the visual backbone for robust visual feature extraction.
 - **Tactile:** The *pretrained* tactile encoder (from GraphMAE with canonical representation) processes the 3D tactile data. This encoder is fine-tuned during policy learning.
 - **Proprioception:** Robot state (3D arm position, 4D arm quaternion, 16D hand joint positions) is also included.
3. **Feature Fusion:** The tactile features are concatenated with visual features and robot proprioceptive state to form the complete input for the Diffusion Policy.
4. **Action Space:** The policy predicts target actions (3D translation, 4D quaternion for arm end-effector, and 16D joint positions for the hand) rather than states, as these actions implicitly capture the force usage vital for manipulation.
5. **Robot System:** The experiments are conducted on a real robotic system comprising a JAKA MiniCobo 6-DoF arm and a 16-DoF Leap Hand equipped with PaXini distributed tactile sensors (3x5 taxel array per sensor, measuring tri-axial forces). A RealSense D415 camera provides visual input. Teleoperation with HaMeR and Dexpilot is used for expert demonstration collection.

This comprehensive methodology, combining a novel representation with a targeted pretraining strategy, aims to unlock the full potential of 3D tactile sensing for advanced robotic dexterity.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted on a real robot system across four challenging, fine-grained, contact-rich dexterous manipulation tasks: Open Box, Reorientation, Flip, and Assembly. Each method was evaluated over 10 experiment runs per task, with a maximum of 600 steps.

#### 4.1. A. Manipulation Policy Comparison

The proposed method achieved the highest average success rate across all tasks (78%), significantly outperforming several baselines:

- **Ours:** 78% (90% Open Box, 70% Reorientation, 80% Flip, 70% Assembly)
- **DP (Diffusion Policy w/o tactile):** 53%
- **HATO (MLP for flattened tactile force):** 48%
- **T-DEX (2D tactile image + BYOL pretraining):** 63%
- **GNN (9D tactile + GNN w/o pretraining):** 0%

**Key Observations:**
- **Tactile Importance:** While DP (vision-only) performs reasonably well on Open Box and Reorientation, it struggles with highly contact-rich and occluded tasks like Flip. The superior performance of methods utilizing tactile feedback, especially Ours and T-DEX, highlights its critical role.
- **3D Tactile vs. 2D Tactile:** T-DEX, which converts 3D tactile to 2D images, performs better than DP and HATO, showing the benefit of pretraining on tactile data. However, it still struggles with the Flip task (40% success rate) compared to "Ours" (80%), suggesting that preserving 3D spatial information is crucial for tasks requiring precise coordination and stable grasping.
- **Difficulty of Raw 3D Tactile:** The GNN baseline, using raw 9D tactile representation without pretraining or canonicalization, consistently failed (0% success). This strongly validates the authors' initial premise about the difficulty of learning directly from unorganized 3D tactile data. HATO, which only uses flattened 3D force values, performs slightly better, indicating that even without spatial arrangement, force information is somewhat useful, but raw 3D spatial + force data is too complex without proper processing.

#### 4.2. B. Importance of Canonical Representation and Pretraining (Ablation Studies)

Ablation studies confirmed the critical role of both proposed components:

- **Ours w/o CR & PRE (Canonical Representation & Pretraining):** 0% Avg. Success. This variant is essentially the GNN baseline.
- **Ours w/o CR (Canonical Representation):** 0% Avg. Success. Even with force-based pretraining, policies fail if the canonical representation is not used. The analysis revealed that without canonicalization, the tactile encoder outputs similar features for taxels within the same sensor, failing to capture fine-grained differences, leading to erratic hand movements.
- **Ours w/o PRE (Pretraining):** 48% Avg. Success. While canonical representation alone significantly improves performance over the GNN baseline (from 0% to 48%), adding force-based pretraining boosts the average success rate to 78%, underscoring the efficiency gains from pretraining.

These results unequivocally establish the necessity of the canonical representation for effective 3D tactile feature learning and the substantial benefits of the force-based pretraining strategy.

#### 4.3. C. Effect of Force-Based Pretraining Tasks

Further ablation on the pretraining tasks revealed that both components are essential:

- **Ours w/o NF (Net Force Prediction):** 28% Avg. Success. Omitting net force prediction leads to a significant drop in performance across all tasks.
- **Ours w/o LF (Masked Local Force Prediction):** 48% Avg. Success. Omitting local force prediction also reduces performance.
- **Observation:** The results suggest that net force prediction is slightly more critical for overall task success than local force prediction. However, both contribute synergistically, ensuring the encoder learns features for both fine-grained local interactions and global force application.

#### 4.4. D. Role of Spatial Information and Force Information

Ablation studies on the downstream policy demonstrated how different types of tactile information are leveraged:

- **Spatial Ablation (fixed 6D pose):** The robot failed to flip the cap, with the thumb oscillating randomly after reaching the object, indicating that dynamic spatial information is crucial for forming correct gross hand poses.
- **Force Ablation (all forces set to zero):** The robot also failed the flip task, struggling with unstable grasps or continuous, ineffective adjustments, highlighting the importance of force feedback for fine-grained manipulation and stable contact.
- **Conclusion:** The policy effectively uses spatial information for gross hand poses and force information for precise, fine-grained adjustments during manipulation.

#### 4.5. E. Generalization to Unseen Objects

The method showed promising generalization capabilities on unseen objects with diverse color, geometry, and dynamics:

- **Open Box:** 5 out of 8 successes. Failures were attributed to significantly different friction properties.
- **Flip:** 6 out of 8 successes.
- **Conclusion:** The learned policy demonstrates good generalization to novel objects, indicating that the learned tactile features are robust and transferable.

Overall, the findings strongly support the authors' hypotheses, demonstrating that their canonical representation and force-based pretraining strategy significantly enhance 3D tactile feature learning, leading to superior performance in real-world dexterous manipulation tasks and improved generalization.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents significant advancements in the field of robotic tactile perception and dexterous manipulation, with substantial potential impact:

1. **Enabling Advanced Dexterity with 3D Tactile Sensing:** The most immediate impact is on empowering robots with more sophisticated tactile perception. By providing a robust method to process high-dimensional 3D tactile data, this work moves beyond the limitations of vision-only systems or simplified 2D tactile representations. This is crucial for tasks requiring delicate touch, precise force control, and intricate object interaction, bringing robots closer to human-level dexterity.

2. **Addressing the Data Scarcity Problem:** The introduction of a force-based self-supervised pretraining strategy is a critical step towards mitigating the challenge of data scarcity in 3D tactile sensing. By leveraging readily collectible "play data," robots can learn meaningful tactile features without requiring vast amounts of labeled, task-specific demonstrations. This significantly reduces the overhead of training and makes 3D tactile integration more feasible for various applications.

3. **Improved Feature Learning for GNNs in Robotics:** The proposed canonical representation specifically enhances the utility of Graph Neural Networks for tactile data. By aligning sensor readings and amplifying local differences, it transforms complex raw 3D tactile data into a more digestible format for GNNs, which are well-suited for irregular graph structures. This could inspire similar representation learning strategies for other sparse, high-dimensional sensor data in robotics.

4. **Foundation for Future Research in Multimodal Perception:** This work lays a strong foundation for future research in truly multimodal robotic perception, where vision, tactile, and proprioceptive inputs are seamlessly integrated and leveraged. The validated effectiveness of combining DinoV2 for vision and a carefully designed tactile encoder provides a template for building robust perception systems.

5. **Practical Applications and Robustness:** The real-world experiments on challenging manipulation tasks (e.g., flipping a bottle cap, intricate assembly) demonstrate the practical applicability and robustness of the proposed methods. The ability to generalize to unseen objects further boosts confidence in its real-world utility for diverse settings, such as manufacturing, service robotics, and even surgical applications.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The authors acknowledge that the policy shows limited generalization when encountering objects with "significantly different shapes and dynamics." This points to an exciting direction for future work: developing quick adaptation mechanisms using tactile feedback to handle a broader range of object properties. This could involve few-shot learning, meta-learning, or advanced transfer learning techniques that leverage the powerful tactile features learned through their pretraining.

In conclusion, this paper makes a substantial contribution by tackling the fundamental challenges of 3D tactile data processing. Its innovative canonical representation and force-based pretraining strategy promise to significantly enhance the capabilities of dexterous robots, driving progress towards more intelligent and adaptable robotic systems.
