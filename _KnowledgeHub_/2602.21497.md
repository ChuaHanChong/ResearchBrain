# See It, Say It, Sorted: An Iterative Training-Free Framework for Visually-Grounded Multimodal Reasoning in LVLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> The "See It, Say It, Sorted" (ECRD) framework improves visually-grounded multimodal reasoning in Large Vision-Language Models by iteratively supervising each reasoning step with dynamically generated textual visual evidence. This training-free approach effectively reduces visual hallucination propagation, achieving up to 29.5% relative gains on reasoning benchmarks without costly retraining.

## Problem

- Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) frequently suffer from visual hallucination propagation, especially during extended Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning, leading to incorrect final answers despite logical coherence.
- Current methods to mitigate hallucination are primarily training-based, demanding significant computational resources, extensive curated data, and complex reward designs, making them costly and model-specific.
- A lightweight, generalizable, and training-free approach is lacking to effectively enhance visual grounding at test time across diverse LVLM architectures.

## Method

- An iterative, training-free, and plug-and-play framework, ==Evidence-Constrained Reweighting Decoding (ECRD)==, is introduced to enhance visual grounding at test time without modifying base LVLMs.
- A ==Distribution Supervisor== adaptively reweights the base model's next-token probabilities using an "Evidence Pool" of dynamically growing textual visual observations.
- An ==Uncertainty-Triggered Visual Decider==, a lightweight VLM, is invoked on-demand when the base model's confidence is low, providing specific textual "micro-observations" to ground subsequent reasoning steps.

## Results

- ECRD consistently improved reasoning accuracy on the TreeBench benchmark, achieving relative gains of **16.5%** to **29.5%** across various open-source LVLM backbones (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL, LLaVA-OneVision).
- It surpassed the performance of several closed-source models (e.g., Gemini-2.5-Flash, GPT-4o) and training-based visual-grounded reasoning models on TreeBench, all without additional training.
- The framework enhanced the reasoning-perception balance on RH-Bench, increasing the RH-AUC from **0.51** to **0.58**, and showed generalizable improvements across five diverse multimodal benchmarks like OCRBench and HallusionBench.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Visual hallucination in LVLMs can be effectively mitigated at test time by dynamically integrating semantic textual evidence into the decoding process without costly retraining.
> - An adaptive negotiation mechanism, where the visual evidence's influence scales with the base model's uncertainty, is crucial for balancing the LVLM's fluent generation with robust visual grounding.
> - Employing a lightweight, specialized visual decider only when the model exhibits low confidence allows for targeted visual clarification, preventing error propagation with minimal computational overhead.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) has progressed significantly, enabling models to generate detailed Chain-of-Thought (CoT) responses for various multimodal reasoning tasks. This capability allows models to articulate their reasoning process, which can lead to solving complex problems. However, this advancement introduces a challenge: the potential for visual hallucination propagation. In multimodal contexts, particularly when generating long CoT sequences, models can produce intermediate reasoning steps that are inconsistent with the visual evidence presented in the image. Once a hallucination occurs, subsequent reasoning steps, even if logically sound, can lead to an incorrect final answer because they build upon a flawed premise. This phenomenon is often attributed to language priors, where the model's textual context and knowledge can sometimes override or dilute subtle visual cues, especially as the textual context lengthens during inference-time decoding.

Existing research has attempted to address this issue by training models to integrate visual information more actively into their reasoning process. Solutions often involve reinforcement learning (RL) or preference optimization pipelines, where models learn a policy to "think with images" by calling visual tools, such as zooming into or cropping specific image regions, and re-injecting these pixel-level observations back into the reasoning context. Examples of such approaches include PixelReasoner and DeepEyes. While these methods have demonstrated effectiveness in improving visual grounding, they present several limitations. They typically require extensive curated data, careful reward design, and substantial computational resources for training. Furthermore, these RL-based pipelines are often model-specific, tightly coupled to particular backbone architectures, which limits their generalizability and accessibility, especially as model scales continue to increase. The learned policies intertwine spatial exploration with CoT generation, leading to repeated encoding of cropped views and potential increases in latency.

This research introduces an alternative approach that positions itself as a lightweight, training-free, and model-agnostic framework. Instead of learning to look at training time, this work proposes to supervise each reasoning step at test time with visual evidence. This approach aims to address the identified limitations of RL-based methods by offering a plug-and-play solution that can be applied to existing LVLM backbones without requiring additional training or architectural modifications, thereby broadening the accessibility and applicability of visually-grounded reasoning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an iterative, training-free framework for visually-grounded multimodal reasoning in LVLMs that effectively mitigates visual hallucination propagation during CoT generation. The framework aims to ensure that every token generated during the reasoning process is justified by corresponding visual evidence.

The motivation behind this objective stems from several identified challenges and limitations in the current state of LVLM reasoning:

1. **Vulnerability to Visual Hallucination Propagation:** LVLMs, despite their advanced reasoning capabilities, are prone to generating "fluent but visually inconsistent" reasoning chains. This occurs when an early reasoning step diverges from visual facts, leading to a cascade of errors. The ability to "think more" often correlates with "seeing less" as language priors tend to dominate visual grounding during long-horizon token generation. The authors aim to directly tackle this core problem, which undermines the reliability and trustworthiness of LVLM outputs in visually-intensive tasks.

2. **Limitations of Existing RL-based Solutions:** Current methods that try to enforce visual grounding, such as those employing reinforcement learning or preference optimization (e.g., PixelReasoner, DeepEyes), are resource-intensive. They demand curated datasets, complex reward function design, significant computational power for training, and are often model-specific. This high cost and lack of generalizability hinder their widespread adoption and application across diverse LVLM architectures. The authors are motivated to bypass these training-related challenges by developing a solution that is economical and adaptable.

3. **Need for a Lightweight and Generalizable Approach:** There is a need for a more accessible method that can enhance visual grounding without imposing substantial overhead or requiring modifications to the base model's architecture. A "plug-and-play" framework that works with frozen LVLMs would significantly improve accessibility and utility for the broader research community and practical applications. The authors aim to create a system that can wrap around existing models, making it inherently transferable and robust across different scales and backbones.

4. **Ensuring Visual Certainty at Each Reasoning Step:** The core motivation is to instill a mechanism that supervises each token selection at test time. This involves dynamically assessing whether the model has sufficient visual certainty to proceed. If uncertainty is detected, the system should actively seek and incorporate additional visual evidence. This step-by-step grounding ensures that reasoning remains tethered to the visual input, preventing premature commitment to visually inconsistent paths.

By addressing these points, the research endeavors to improve reasoning accuracy, reduce hallucination rates, and foster a better balance between linguistic reasoning and visual perception in LVLMs, all within a practical and efficient framework.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, termed Evidence-Constrained Reweighting Decoding (ECRD), is an iterative, training-free, and plug-and-play method designed to enhance visually-grounded multimodal reasoning in LVLMs. It operates by supervising token selection at each decoding step with dynamic visual evidence.

#### 3.1. Visual Description Grounded Decoding (VDGD) as a Baseline

The methodology builds upon principles from Visual Description Grounded Decoding (VDGD). In VDGD, a global textual description of the image is first generated. During subsequent decoding, candidate tokens are selected from the top-k plausible tokens based on a knee truncation of the probability distribution. These candidates are then evaluated against prefixes of the global description using KL divergence, with the minimum deviation determining the token's preference. However, the authors identify limitations in VDGD, such as its reliance on a static caption, lack of self-adaptiveness, and its method of overwriting base model logits, which does not preserve the model's calibrated confidence.

#### 3.2. Distribution Supervisor

ECRD introduces a "Distribution Supervisor" that refines and extends VDGD's approach. This supervisor makes two key changes:
- **Evidence Scoring:** Instead of VDGD's min-over-prefix KL divergence, ECRD calculates a mean-over-prefix probability (`q_E(w)`) for each candidate token `w` against an evidence sentence `E`. This averaging across prefixes provides a more robust measure of support. Given a pool of multiple evidence sentences (`E_i`), their supports are averaged to produce an aggregate evidence score `S_i(w)`. These scores are then normalized over the candidate set (`C_i`) to form an evidence-induced distribution `r_i(w)`.
- **Negotiated Reweighting:** The supervisor mixes the base LVLM's probability distribution (`p_i`) with the evidence-induced distribution (`r_i`). To ensure consistency in scale, `r_i` is first mass-matched (`~r_i`) to `p_i` within the candidate set `C_i`, such that the total probability mass assigned to `C_i` in `~r_i` equals that in `p_i`. The final mixed distribution (`p_mix_i(w)`) is a weighted average of `p_i` and `~r_i`. The adaptive weight `alpha_i` is set to `p_i(1)` (the top probability of the base model). This design allows the base distribution to dominate when the model is confident (large `p_i(1)`) and gives more weight to evidence during "hallucination-critical moments" when `p_i(1)` is small, thereby making the mixture responsive to local uncertainty.

#### 3.3. Dynamic Evidence Pool and the Visual Decider

A core component of ECRD is the dynamic management of a textual evidence pool and the invocation of a "Visual Decider."
- **Uncertainty-Triggered Invocation:** After the negotiated reweighting, the supervisor inspects the "negotiated margin" (`Delta_i = p_mix(1) - p_mix(2)`). If this margin is below a predefined hyperparameter threshold `delta` (and the candidate set `k*` is greater than 1), the system identifies a probable hallucination trigger and invokes the Visual Decider. Otherwise, the token with the highest `p_mix(1)` is directly selected.
- **Visual Decider Functionality:** When invoked, the Visual Decider (instantiated by GRIT, built on Qwen2.5-VL-3B) receives the image, the current textual prefix (tail of the CoT), and the candidate tokens (`C_i`). Crucially, it does not receive the original question, focusing solely on resolving the ambiguity of the current step. The decider generates a concise "micro-observation" as a natural language sentence (`E_i`), potentially with associated image coordinates for interpretability. It also outputs a chosen token `w*` from `C_i`.
- **Evidence Pool Management:** The chosen token `w*` is forced for the current step, and the generated evidence sentence `E_i` is appended to the dynamic evidence pool (`E_i+1`). This pool is initialized with a global textual description (`d_global`) and grows only "on demand" as the decider is invoked.
- **Textual Evidence Representation:** A deliberate design choice is to represent evidence solely in text. This keeps the evidence compact, semantic, and model-native, residing in the same token space as the decoder. This textual form allows it to be scored efficiently without repeatedly revisiting and re-encoding image pixels (as in zoom-in pipelines), significantly reducing computational overhead and making the intervention lightweight and interpretable. The coordinates generated by GRIT are stored as annotations but do not directly participate in scoring steps. This approach ensures that fine-grained visual cues, once captured as text, can be reused downstream to stabilize subsequent reasoning steps.

ECRD's design is training-free and transferable, wrapping around a frozen LVLM. It is cost-aware, as additional visual computation occurs only when most likely to prevent a hallucination. The textual representation of evidence enables semantic composition and reuse across the entire reasoning sequence.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research conducted extensive experiments across multiple LVLM backbones and benchmarks to evaluate the effectiveness of ECRD.

#### 4.1. Performance on TreeBench

- ECRD consistently improved performance across all tested open-source general backbones and scales (Qwen2.5-VL, LLaVA-OneVision, InternVL3, ranging from 7B to 78B).
- For example, on Qwen2.5-VL-7B, the overall accuracy increased from 37.0% to 47.9% (a 10.9 percentage point absolute gain). Similar absolute gains (typically +4-8 points) were observed on larger models.
- The method achieved relative improvements of 16.5%–29.5% on TreeBench.
- Sub-categories requiring precise visual facts, such as OCR, Physical State, and Comparison, showed the most significant benefits.
- ECRD, when applied to Qwen2.5-VL-7B, surpassed existing RLHF-based visually-grounded reasoning models like DeepEyes-7B and Pixel-Reasoner-7B, and approached the performance of TreeVGR-7B, notably without requiring any additional training.
- The framework also outperformed several strong closed models such as Gemini-2.5-Flash and GPT-4o, though it trailed Gemini-2.5-Pro and o3.
- Compared to other training-free baselines (Woodpecker, ViperGPT, ControlMLLM) and standard decoding alternatives (beam search, self-consistency, diverse sampling) on Qwen2.5-VL-7B, ECRD demonstrated a marked performance advantage.

#### 4.2. Performance on RH-Bench

- On RH-Bench, ECRD applied to Qwen2.5-VL-7B improved Reasoning accuracy from 39.6% to 46.4% and Perception accuracy from 50.2% to 57.1%.
- The RH-AUC metric, which quantifies the trade-off between reasoning length and hallucination, increased from 0.51 to 0.58. This indicates that ECRD better maintains accuracy over longer reasoning chains and achieves a more favorable balance between reasoning and hallucination.

#### 4.3. Performance on General Multimodal Benchmarks

- ECRD demonstrated broad, task-general gains across five diverse multimodal benchmarks (V*Bench, MathVista, ChartQA, OCRBench, HallusionBench) on both Qwen2.5-VL-7B and LLaVA-OneVision-7B.
- V*Bench overall accuracy increased by several points, and MathVista and ChartQA showed steady improvements.
- OCRBench saw substantial improvements of approximately +8 to +12 points, and HallusionBench improved by roughly +8 to +11 points. These gains suggest that ECRD effectively reduces visually induced errors that would otherwise propagate through the reasoning process.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study

- An ablation study on Qwen2.5-VL-7B for TreeBench revealed the contributions of ECRD's components:
 - GRIT-3B (the visual decider model) alone yielded low accuracy, indicating that ECRD's gains are due to its decoding design rather than simply using a stronger perception model.
 - The "supervisor" component, incorporating the mean-over-prefix evidence scoring, provided a stable performance boost even without dynamic decider calls.
 - The inclusion of the dynamic visual decider (especially the grounding-oriented GRIT-3B) yielded the full ECRD gains, with larger margins observed in visually consequential categories. This suggests that both the supervisor and the visual decider are necessary and complementary for the framework's overall effectiveness.

#### 4.5. Efficiency Analysis and Uncertainty Threshold (δ)

- ECRD's computational overhead consists of lightweight evidence scoring and heavier visual decider calls.
- Analysis of the uncertainty threshold `δ` (which triggers decider calls) showed that increasing `δ` monotonically increases the average number of decider calls per question (`r`) and improves accuracy.
- Accuracy curves exhibited an "elbow" around `δ ≈ 0.08`, where most attainable gains were achieved with a relatively low number of decider calls. Beyond this point, further increases in `δ` primarily increased `r` and latency with diminishing returns in accuracy.
- This `δ = 0.08` value was selected as the default, representing a balance between cost and accuracy.
- A latency model (`T(δ) ≈ t_0 + l_0 * r(δ)`) confirmed that end-to-end time is a function of base time (`t_0`) and the marginal latency of a single decider call (`l_0`) multiplied by the call rate.

#### 4.6. Qualitative Analysis and Gain Breakdown

- Qualitative examples demonstrated ECRD's ability to correct common failure modes. This included cases where the "negotiated reweighting" by the supervisor resolved ambiguity using existing textual evidence, and cases where the "visual decider" intervened to provide mid-chain visual grounding or directly supply final answers when uncertainty persisted.
- A breakdown of ECRD's +10.9-point gain on TreeBench with Qwen2.5-VL-7B indicated that 11.4% of the gain came from decider calls directly providing final answers, 18.2% from decider calls injecting mid-chain visual grounding, and the remainder from the supervisor's reweighting and the cumulative benefits of the expanding evidence pool.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework that addresses a critical challenge in Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs): the propagation of visual hallucinations during complex multimodal reasoning. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Mitigation of Visual Hallucinations:** The primary impact is the demonstrated ability to substantially reduce hallucination rates and improve the visual grounding of LVLMs. By ensuring that each reasoning step is iteratively supervised by visual evidence, the framework enhances the reliability and trustworthiness of AI-generated CoT explanations and final answers in visually-intensive tasks.

- **Training-Free and Plug-and-Play Design:** A key contribution is the development of a framework that does not require additional training, finetuning, or policy optimization of the base LVLM. This "training-free" and "plug-and-play" nature makes ECRD highly accessible and easily deployable. It can be wrapped around existing, frozen LVLM backbones, significantly lowering the barrier to incorporating visually-grounded reasoning capabilities into diverse architectures and scales. This contrasts with previous costly and model-specific RL-based approaches.

- **Model Agnosticism and Generalizability:** The framework's ability to operate across different LVLM backbones (Qwen2.5-VL, LLaVA-OneVision, InternVL3) and scales (7B to 78B) without specific modifications highlights its generalizability. This indicates that the proposed approach is not tied to particular model designs but rather offers a generic mechanism for improving multimodal reasoning.

- **Cost-Aware Efficiency:** ECRD's uncertainty-triggered invocation of the visual decider ensures that additional computational resources are expended only when ambiguity is high and intervention is most likely to prevent a hallucination. This "on-demand" approach yields a favorable cost-accuracy trade-off, making it practical for deployment in scenarios with varying computational budgets.

- **Textual Evidence Management:** The innovative use of textual micro-observations for evidence, rather than raw pixel crops, provides several advantages. Textual evidence is compact, semantic, and naturally composable within the model's token space. This design reduces computational overhead, allows for reusable visual cues throughout the reasoning chain, and offers enhanced interpretability by providing a human-readable trail of visual justification.

- **Advancement in Multimodal Reasoning:** By stabilizing the balance between language priors and visual perception, ECRD contributes to the development of more robust multimodal AI systems. Its improvements across diverse benchmarks (TreeBench, RH-Bench, V*Bench, MathVista, ChartQA, OCRBench, HallusionBench) suggest a broad applicability in enhancing various aspects of multimodal competence, including attributes, spatial reasoning, and complex logical operations over visual information.

In summary, this research offers a practical and effective method for enhancing the visual grounding of LVLMs, with the potential to significantly improve their reliability and broaden their applicability in real-world scenarios requiring trustworthy multimodal reasoning.
