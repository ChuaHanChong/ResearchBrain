# Combinatorial Rising Bandits

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers introduced the Combinatorial Rising Bandit (CRB) framework to model online learning scenarios involving combinatorial actions and dynamically improving rewards that are partially shared among components. They developed the Combinatorial Rising Upper Confidence Bound (CRUCB) algorithm, which demonstrated near-optimal regret bounds theoretically and empirically outperformed existing baselines in both synthetic and deep reinforcement learning environments.

## Problem

- Existing combinatorial online learning models assume stationary reward distributions, failing to capture scenarios where rewards improve over time.
- Traditional rising bandit models lack combinatorial action structures and the 'partially shared enhancement' where improvements in sub-components benefit multiple complex actions.
- Real-world applications in robotics, network routing, and recommendation systems require models that account for both combinatorial actions and rising, shared rewards simultaneously.

## Method

- The research formalizes the ==Combinatorial Rising Bandit== (CRB) framework, defining problems with combinatorial super arms, rising and concave base arm rewards, and monotone expected reward functions.
- It proposes the Combinatorial Rising Upper Confidence Bound (**CRUCB**) algorithm, which uses a 'Future-UCB' index to optimistically estimate future base arm outcomes by considering recent averages, predicted improvements, and an adaptive exploration bonus.
- CRUCB selects super arms by maximizing the expected reward based on these optimistic future estimates, using a modular solver for various combinatorial structures.

## Results

- CRUCB consistently achieved lower cumulative regret across various synthetic combinatorial tasks, including Online Shortest Path and k-MAX problems, outperforming five state-of-the-art baselines.
- The algorithm demonstrated superior performance in complex deep reinforcement learning AntMaze environments, maintaining robustness even when initial reward growth violated strict concavity assumptions.
- Visualizations confirmed CRUCB's efficient exploration strategies, showing that it quickly identified and exploited optimal paths by leveraging shared improvements, unlike baselines that exhibited inefficient or overly broad exploration patterns.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Constant policies, which can be optimal in non-combinatorial rising bandits, are generally sub-optimal in CRB due to the complex dependencies introduced by partially shared enhancement among base arms.
> - The proposed Future-UCB index, with its adaptive sliding window, effectively balances exploration and exploitation by robustly estimating future rising rewards and their increments for base arms.
> - CRUCB's regret upper bounds closely match the derived theoretical lower bounds for the CRB problem, demonstrating its near-optimality and adaptivity to varying reward growth patterns without requiring prior knowledge of instance difficulty.
