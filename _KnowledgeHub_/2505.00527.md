# DeCo: Task Decomposition and Skill Composition for Zero-Shot Generalization in Long-Horizon 3D Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> A model-agnostic framework called DeCo was developed to enhance zero-shot generalization for long-horizon 3D manipulation tasks by enabling imitation learning models to decompose tasks into reusable atomic skills and flexibly compose them. It improved success rates on novel compositional tasks, reaching up to 66.67% in simulation and 53.33% in real-world robot experiments for various baseline models.

## Problem

- Multi-task imitation learning (IL) models exhibit limited generalization to novel, long-horizon 3D manipulation tasks, even when tasks are compositions of individually learned skills.
- Current models often lack the ability to autonomously decompose complex instructions into known atomic skills, and then retrieve, schedule, and smoothly compose them.
- A gap persists between high-level semantic plans generated by Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and flexible, robust low-level robotic execution, hindering effective skill composition.

## Method

- The framework employs physical interaction-based task decomposition and skill learning, breaking down demonstrations into modular atomic tasks based on gripper-object contact events.
- It utilizes VLM-guided planning and skill scheduling, where a VLM generates a sequence of atomic skills from a high-level instruction, which the IL model then executes.
- A spatially-aware skill chaining module ensures smooth, collision-free transitions between sequential atomic skills by generating intermediary chaining poses.

## Results

- DeCo-enhanced IL models achieved substantially higher average success rates (up to **66.67%**) on 12 novel long-horizon tasks in simulation, compared to baseline models which failed (**0.00**-**0.14%** success).
- In real-world robot experiments, RVT-2+DeCo demonstrated a **53.33%** average success rate on 9 novel long-horizon tasks, while the baseline RVT-2 achieved **0%**.
- Ablation studies confirmed that decomposition based on 'full physical interactions' and the spatially-aware skill chaining module are critical for DeCo's performance in compositional generalization.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decomposing complex manipulation tasks into 'atomic skills' defined by consistent physical gripper-object interactions facilitates more effective learning and zero-shot recomposition for novel long-horizon tasks.
> - Integrating high-level VLM planning with low-level, pose-supervised IL policy execution, combined with dynamic skill scheduling, bridges the semantic-to-action gap in complex manipulation.
> - Spatially-aware transition planning between atomic skills is crucial for robust, collision-free execution and for mitigating policy distribution shifts, especially in real-world settings.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Imitation Learning (IL) has become a prevalent approach for robotic manipulation, allowing robots to acquire diverse skills from visual demonstrations and language instructions. Recent advancements have focused on developing language-conditioned multi-task control policies to operate in unstructured 3D environments. However, a significant challenge within this field is the limited generalization capabilities of current multi-task IL models, particularly when confronted with novel, long-horizon 3D manipulation tasks. These models often struggle even when new tasks are merely sequential compositions of skills they have previously learned individually. For example, a robot might understand "open drawer," "put block in opened drawer," and "close drawer," but fail to execute the combined instruction "put block into the closed drawer and then close drawer." This indicates a fundamental limitation in the models' ability to autonomously decompose novel tasks, retrieve relevant skills, schedule their execution, and compose them effectively.

Existing research has explored the use of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to generate subtasks for long-horizon operations. These approaches typically involve VLMs parsing high-level instructions into natural language plans, executable code, spatial keypoints, or affordance maps. While VLMs show promise in high-level planning, a persistent challenge remains in bridging the gap between these high-level semantic plans and flexible, low-level execution. Often, the low-level tasks generated are restricted to simple motion planning or pre-trained, fixed skills. The semantic decomposition provided by VLMs frequently does not align directly with the physical skill space of the robot, limiting effective skill composition and hindering zero-shot generalization performance on complex 3D manipulation tasks. This gap between high-level understanding and low-level physical action execution currently undermines the real-world applicability and scalability of multi-task IL models.

The work presented in this paper directly addresses this gap by proposing DeCo, a model-agnostic framework. DeCo aims to enhance zero-shot generalization for compositional long-horizon 3D manipulation tasks by enabling IL models to decompose tasks into reusable atomic skills, flexibly schedule them, and execute skill sequences without additional training. It introduces a structured approach to defining and learning atomic skills based on physical gripper-object interactions, ensuring that the decomposed subtasks are directly compatible with low-level robot control policies. By integrating VLM-guided planning with a spatially-aware skill chaining mechanism, DeCo seeks to establish a more robust and scalable method for robots to handle novel, complex manipulation sequences that can be synthesized from a library of learned atomic actions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to investigate how long-horizon 3D manipulation tasks can be decomposed into learned skills in a manner that allows multi-task imitation learning (IL) models to interpret task structure, plan accordingly, and successfully complete novel, compositional tasks without requiring additional training.

The motivation for this objective stems from several limitations observed in current multi-task IL models:
- **Limited Generalization to Compositional Tasks:** Despite the ability of IL models to learn diverse skills, they frequently exhibit poor generalization to novel long-horizon tasks, even when these tasks are logically composed of previously learned individual skills. For instance, a robot might learn to "open a drawer," "place an item," and "close a drawer" as separate actions, but struggle with a composite instruction like "place the item in the closed drawer and then close the drawer."
- **Inadequate Task Decomposition and Skill Composition:** Current models often lack the inherent capability to decompose complex, unseen instructions into a sequence of known, executable atomic skills. Concurrently, they struggle to retrieve, schedule, and smoothly compose these learned skills into a coherent execution plan. This inability to understand the underlying compositional structure of tasks severely limits their utility in dynamic, real-world environments.
- **Mismatch Between High-Level Planning and Low-Level Execution:** While Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have advanced the ability to generate high-level plans or subtasks, a persistent challenge lies in effectively translating these semantic plans into robust, low-level robotic actions. Existing VLM-driven approaches often rely on predefined action primitives or simple motion planning, which may not align with the physical skill space learned by IL policies, thereby restricting flexibility and zero-shot generalization.
- **Enhancing Real-World Applicability and Scalability:** The aforementioned limitations significantly hinder the practical applicability and scalability of multi-task IL models in complex real-world scenarios. A framework that enables zero-shot generalization to novel compositional tasks would greatly improve the autonomy and adaptability of robotic systems, reducing the need for extensive retraining for every new task variation.

To address these motivations, the researchers proposed DeCo, a model-agnostic framework designed to equip multi-task IL models with the necessary capabilities for zero-shot generalization in compositional long-horizon 3D manipulation. The framework aims to enable robots to interpret task structure, generate plans, and execute sequences of atomic skills effectively without prior exposure to the exact task composition.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The DeCo (Task Decomposition and Skill Composition) framework is designed to enhance the zero-shot generalization of multi-task Imitation Learning (IL) models to novel, compositional long-horizon 3D manipulation tasks. It comprises three primary components: Physical Interaction-based Task Decomposition and Skill Learning, VLM-Guided Planning and Skill Scheduling, and Spatially-Aware Skill Chaining.

#### 3.1. Physical Interaction-based Task Decomposition and Skill Learning

DeCo’s approach to skill learning begins with a novel task decomposition strategy inspired by human hand-object interactions. It processes raw IL demonstrations by analyzing the physical interactions between the robotic gripper and objects.
- **Interaction Definition**: A physical interaction is defined as a contact event between the gripper and an object, identified by changes in the gripper's openness. A "cycle" is a single change (open to closed or closed to open). A "full interaction" (p_full) involves two cycles (open → closed → open), while a "half interaction" (p_half) is a single cycle.
- **Atomic Task Creation**: Original training demonstrations (T_o) are decomposed into a set of "modular and reusable atomic tasks" (T_a). The primary decomposition method uses full physical interactions (p_full) to ensure consistent and complete subtasks. Each resulting atomic task T_a_i is paired with a natural language instruction (ℓ_a_i) and a goal pose, which is the end-effector pose in the robot base frame at the final keyframe of the demonstration. This forms an atomic instruction library (L_a).
- **Skill Learning**: A multi-task IL model (M) is then trained on this dataset of physically consistent atomic tasks. This training enables the model to learn a policy (π_a_θ*) that maps observations (RGB-D) and atomic instructions (ℓ_a_i) to 6-DoF end-effector poses and a 1-DoF gripper state.

#### 3.2. VLM-Guided Planning and Skill Scheduling

Once the IL model has acquired various atomic skills, DeCo facilitates zero-shot generalization to novel long-horizon tasks through VLM-guided planning and dynamic skill scheduling.
- **High-Level Planning**: Given a novel, compositional task (T_new) specified by a language instruction and observed RGB images, the system queries a Vision-Language Model (VLM), specifically GPT-4o, along with the pre-built atomic instruction library (L_a). The VLM leverages its reasoning capabilities to retrieve relevant atomic instructions from L_a and generate an ordered sequence of skills, effectively creating a task plan.
- **Low-Level Execution**: The multi-task IL model (M) is responsible for the low-level execution of each atomic skill within the planned sequence. DeCo provides goal-pose supervision and manages the scheduling of skills.
- **Dynamic Scheduling and Monitoring**: The system continuously monitors the robot’s current pose relative to the goal pose of the actively executing atomic skill. Upon successful completion of an atomic skill—indicated by the robot reaching the goal pose, which corresponds to a full gripper interaction cycle—DeCo triggers the execution of the next skill in the VLM-generated plan. This pose-based, closed-loop monitoring ensures reliable skill termination and flexible composition without altering the underlying IL policy.

#### 3.3. Spatially-Aware Skill Chaining

To ensure smooth and collision-free transitions between sequentially executed atomic skills, DeCo incorporates a spatially-aware skill chaining module.
- **Problem Addressed**: Learned skills often have distinct start and goal poses, leading to spatial discontinuities between successive skills. Traditional motion planners, while collision-free, may generate trajectories that fall into low-confidence regions of the next policy's learned state distribution, potentially causing execution failures.
- **Module Function**: When the current skill completes and the next skill is scheduled, the module receives the current skill's goal pose, the predicted start pose for the next skill, and the scene's point cloud.
- **Collision-Free Chaining Poses**: This module, adapted from Voxposer, generates a sequence of "chaining poses" that bridge the gap between the two skills. These poses are designed to be collision-free and to keep the robot's trajectory within a distribution compatible with the learned policies, mitigating policy distribution shifts.
- **Motion Planning**: The robot then executes an RRT-based (Rapidly-exploring Random Tree) motion planning algorithm over these generated chaining poses to achieve a safe and continuous transition between the sequential skills.

#### 3.4. DeCoBench Benchmark

To systematically evaluate zero-shot compositional generalization, the researchers introduce DeCoBench, a benchmark built upon RLBench.
- **Domains Covered**: Object Rearrangement with Drawer, Object Rearrangement with Cupboard, and Rubbish Cleanup.
- **Task Structure**: Four source tasks are decomposed into 10 reusable atomic skills based on physical interaction analysis (e.g., "open drawer," "put item in drawer"). These atomic skills are then recomposed into 12 novel long-horizon tasks for testing. These test tasks vary in complexity (e.g., 4-cycle, 6-cycle, 10-cycle tasks) and include cross-domain compositions (e.g., "transfer box" between drawer and cupboard).

#### 3.5. Experimental Setup

- **Baseline Models**: DeCo was integrated and evaluated with three representative multi-task IL models: RVT-2 (multi-view robotic transformer), 3DDA (3D scene representation with diffusion-based policy), and ARP (Chunking Causal Transformer for autoregressive action sequences).
- **Simulation**: Experiments were conducted on DeCoBench. Demonstrations were generated using scripted policies. Each baseline and its DeCo-enhanced variant were trained on 50 demonstrations per atomic task and evaluated on 20 test demonstrations per compositional task, with three random seeds.
- **Real-world**: Real-robot validation used a Franka Emika Panda robot with an Intel RealSense D435i camera. RVT-2 and RVT-2+DeCo were trained on 6 atomic tasks (16 variations via kinesthetic teaching) and tested on 9 novel long-horizon tasks (30 variations) in zero-shot, with 10 executions per task with randomized object placements.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of DeCo involved both simulated experiments on DeCoBench and real-world robot trials, assessing its impact on the generalization capabilities of various multi-task IL models.

#### 4.1. Performance on Atomic Tasks (Simulation)

Table I demonstrates the performance of baseline IL models and their DeCo-enhanced variants on 10 atomic tasks within DeCoBench.
- **Baseline Performance**: All three baseline models (RVT-2, 3DDA, ARP) exhibited high average success rates on atomic tasks (91.83%, 98.00%, and 94.67%, respectively). This indicates their proficiency in learning individual, well-defined skills when provided with ground-truth instructions.
- **DeCo-Enhanced Performance**: DeCo-enhanced models showed a slight decrease in average success rates on atomic tasks (RVT-2+DeCo 86.80%, 3DDA+DeCo 96.00%, ARP+DeCo 91.67%). This reduction is attributed to the Vision-Language Model (VLM) component's involvement in atomic task planning, which can introduce instability through visual-semantic grounding errors, such as state estimation hallucinations leading to over-planning or instruction distribution shifts from VLM-generated commands.

#### 4.2. Generalization Performance on Novel Long-Horizon Tasks (Simulation)

Table II highlights DeCo's core contribution: zero-shot generalization to novel, compositional long-horizon tasks.
- **Baseline Failure on Long-Horizon Tasks**: The baseline models, despite their high performance on atomic tasks, largely failed to generalize to the 12 novel long-horizon tasks, achieving success rates of 0.00% for RVT-2 and 3DDA, and a negligible 0.14% for ARP. This failure is due to their inability to reason about unseen temporal dependencies, infer necessary pre-conditions (e.g., opening a drawer before placing an item), and address perceptual aliasing in tasks involving repeated states.
- **Significant Improvement with DeCo**: DeCo substantially improved the generalization performance of all integrated IL models:
 - RVT-2+DeCo: Success rate increased from 0.00% to 66.67% (a 66.67% improvement).
 - 3DDA+DeCo: Success rate increased from 0.00% to 21.53% (a 21.53% improvement).
 - ARP+DeCo: Success rate increased from 0.14% to 58.06% (a 57.92% improvement).
- These results validate DeCo's model-agnostic design and its effectiveness in enabling zero-shot generalization by scheduling and composing learned atomic skills. The paper also notes that the overall generalization improvement is bounded by the inherent capabilities of the base IL model and the reasoning abilities of the VLM. For example, 3DDA+DeCo and ARP+DeCo struggled with specific compositional tasks like "Sweep and Drop" or "Retrieve and Sweep," indicating challenges in visual processing within novel compositional contexts despite accurate VLM planning.

#### 4.3. Impact of Atomic Task Design

Table III presents a comparison of RVT-2+DeCo (trained on atomic tasks) against RVT-2 trained directly on a subset of 6 long-horizon tasks.
- RVT-2+DeCo achieved a 53.89% higher success rate on 6 novel long-horizon tasks (those not seen by either model during training).
- Across all 12 long-horizon tasks, RVT-2+DeCo still showed an overall improvement of 14.31% compared to RVT-2 trained on 6 long-horizon tasks.
- These findings suggest that DeCo's atomic task training methodology is more effective in supporting skill acquisition and enhancing generalization to novel compositional tasks than direct training on a limited set of long-horizon tasks.

#### 4.4. Real-Robot Evaluations

Real-world experiments (Table IV) validated DeCo's practical effectiveness using a Franka Emika Panda robot.
- RVT-2, trained on 6 atomic tasks, achieved a 0% success rate on 9 novel long-horizon tasks.
- RVT-2+DeCo achieved an average success rate of 53.33% on these same 9 novel long-horizon tasks.
- This demonstrates DeCo's capability for zero-shot generalization in real-world settings.
- **System Robustness**: The system exhibited robustness to minor perturbations like object replacement or small drawer displacements, successfully completing tasks as long as the goal pose was reachable. However, it failed to recover from significant object displacement, indicating a lack of a re-planning mechanism in such scenarios.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies

Figure 7 summarizes three ablation studies on DeCo's heuristic settings, primarily using the RVT-2+DeCo model.
- **Half vs. Full Interaction (Figure 7a)**: DeCo-enhanced models performed better when atomic tasks were decomposed based on "full physical interactions" (open → closed → open) compared to "half interactions." Full interactions yielded clearer and more consistent subtasks, providing stronger learning signals and improving generalization. Half interactions often resulted in overly fine-grained fragments, increasing task interference and hindering policy learning. ARP+DeCo was most affected by the half-interaction setting, while RVT-2+DeCo and 3DDA+DeCo showed more stable performance.
- **Effect of Training Demonstration Number (Figure 7b)**: Increasing the number of demonstrations per atomic task improved the performance of the IL model on both atomic and long-horizon tasks. This indicates a direct correlation between the quality of atomic skill learning and overall compositional generalization within DeCo.
- **Effect of Spatially-Aware Skill Chaining (Figure 7c)**: Disabling the spatially-aware skill chaining module (Chaining Poses Num = 0) led to a significant performance drop, often resulting in collisions due to poor transition planning. Enabling the module consistently improved task success, highlighting its importance for smooth and safe transitions between skills, regardless of the precise number of chaining poses. The study used a heuristic of 6 chaining poses for DeCo.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The DeCo framework addresses a fundamental limitation in robotic imitation learning: the inability of current multi-task IL models to generalize zero-shot to novel, compositional long-horizon manipulation tasks. By introducing a structured approach to task decomposition and skill composition, this research makes several significant contributions and has substantial potential impact.

#### 5.1. Significance

- **Enhanced Zero-Shot Generalization:** DeCo provides a model-agnostic framework that demonstrably equips diverse multi-task IL models with zero-shot generalization capabilities for complex, unseen long-horizon tasks. This is achieved by decomposing IL demonstrations into modular, reusable "atomic tasks" based on physical gripper-object interactions, allowing models to learn fundamental skills that can be recomposed.
- **Bridging High-Level Planning and Low-Level Execution:** The framework successfully integrates Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for high-level semantic planning with low-level IL policy execution. This directly addresses a critical gap in the field, where VLM-generated plans often struggled to translate into flexible, robust robotic actions. DeCo enables VLMs to parse high-level instructions, retrieve relevant atomic skills from a learned library, and dynamically schedule their execution by the IL model.
- **Robust Skill Chaining for Seamless Transitions:** The introduction of a spatially-aware skill chaining module is crucial for practical implementation. This module ensures smooth, collision-free transitions between sequentially executed atomic skills by generating chaining poses that connect the goal of one skill to the start of the next while considering the scene geometry and policy compatibility. This enhances the reliability and safety of long-horizon task execution.
- **Standardized Benchmarking:** The development of DeCoBench provides a new, systematic benchmark for evaluating compositional generalization in long-horizon manipulation. This benchmark is valuable for future research, offering a consistent environment for comparing different approaches to skill learning and composition.
- **Empirical Validation:** Extensive evaluations in both simulated environments and real-world robotic setups, across three different multi-task IL models, provide strong empirical evidence for DeCo's effectiveness and practical applicability. The substantial improvements in success rates demonstrate its capacity to significantly advance the state of the art in robotic manipulation.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

- **Increased Robotic Autonomy and Flexibility:** DeCo's ability to enable robots to perform novel, complex tasks by composing basic learned skills without additional training can significantly increase the autonomy and flexibility of robotic systems. This reduces the need for constant human intervention or extensive re-training for every new task variation.
- **Broader Real-World Application:** The framework makes multi-task IL models more scalable and robust for deployment in various real-world scenarios, particularly in unstructured 3D environments where tasks often involve sequential and compositional actions. This could impact areas such as industrial automation, service robotics, and domestic assistance.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** DeCo provides a strong foundation for future research in several interconnected areas, including more sophisticated task decomposition strategies, advanced VLM-robot interaction for planning and reasoning, closed-loop re-planning mechanisms for enhanced robustness, and the exploration of diverse end-effector types and manipulation modalities (e.g., tactile sensing for dexterous control, non-prehensile manipulation).
- **More Efficient Skill Acquisition:** By focusing on learning reusable atomic skills, the approach promotes more data-efficient skill acquisition. Once a set of atomic skills is learned, a vast number of composite tasks can be addressed without needing new demonstrations for each specific long-horizon sequence.

#### 5.3. Limitations and Future Work

The paper acknowledges several limitations. The system's dependencies on its components mean that VLM visual-semantic grounding errors can lead to failures in atomic task planning, such as over-planning. Additionally, the generalization capabilities on long-horizon tasks are ultimately constrained by the visual robustness of the base IL model in novel contexts. The current task decomposition is tailored for claw-like end-effectors, limiting applicability to more dexterous or non-prehensile manipulation. The real-world experiments also highlighted a lack of a re-planning mechanism when significant object displacement occurs. Future work includes exploring tactile modalities for more versatile control, incorporating closed-loop re-planning for enhanced robustness to environmental changes, and parameterizing primitives to improve scalability.
