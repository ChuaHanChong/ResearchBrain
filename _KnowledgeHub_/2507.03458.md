# Helping CLIP See Both the Forest and the Trees: A Decomposition and Description Approach

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Tianjin University and collaborators developed 'Decomposition and Description' (D&D), a method to enhance Vision-Language Models like CLIP by integrating fine-grained local visual semantics with detailed textual descriptions. D&D achieves an average top-1 accuracy improvement of 1.85% in zero-shot classification and up to 1.95% in test-time adaptation over strong baselines.

## Problem

- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) like CLIP exhibit a strong inherent bias towards global image patterns, hindering their effective processing of fine-grained local semantics.
- A widely held assumption that VLMs inherently recognize local visual details when provided with descriptive text was found to be flawed.
- Existing VLM adaptation strategies struggle to effectively integrate both global image patterns and fine-grained local attributes for improved understanding.

## Method

- Introduces =="Decomposition and Description" (D&D)==, a plug-and-play approach that decomposes images into multiple stochastic local regions using ==multi-crop augmentation==.
- Utilizes Large Language Models (LLMs) to generate sets of fine-grained textual descriptors for each candidate class.
- Employs ==Earth Mover's Distance (EMD)== to compute set-level similarity between the set of local visual features and the set of textual descriptors, finding optimal latent correspondences.

## Results

- Achieved an average top-1 accuracy of **60.62%** in zero-shot classification across 11 datasets, representing a **1.85%** improvement over Zero-Shot CLIP and **1.17%** over CALIP.
- Improved few-shot learning performance, reaching an average top-1 accuracy of **71.46%** (16-shot setting) when integrated with Tip-Adapter, surpassing original Tip-Adapter by **1.14%**.
- Attained state-of-the-art test-time adaptation, outperforming the strongest baseline TDA by **1.95%** (**69.48%** vs. **67.53%** average accuracy with ViT-B/16 backbone) across cross-domain benchmarks, with a notable **+27.87%** on Aircraft.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Empirical evidence disproves the common assumption that VLMs inherently process fine-grained local details, revealing a fundamental bias towards global semantics.
> - Explicitly decomposing visual inputs into local regions and aligning them with detailed textual descriptions via EMD effectively activates CLIP's latent capacity for localized feature analysis.
> - The D&D method is highly adaptable and can be integrated into existing VLM adaptation paradigms (zero-shot, few-shot, test-time adaptation) without architectural modifications.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of Vision-Language Models (VLMs), prompt engineering, and advanced adaptation techniques for deep learning models. VLMs, particularly Contrastive Language-Image Pre-training (CLIP), have revolutionized cross-modal understanding by aligning visual and textual representations in a shared embedding space, demonstrating remarkable zero-shot generalization capabilities. Their success has led to widespread adoption in tasks such as image classification, object detection, and semantic segmentation, even extending to open-world scenarios.

A significant line of research in VLM adaptation focuses on "prompt engineering," where carefully crafted text prompts guide the model's understanding. More recently, this has evolved to incorporate Large Language Models (LLMs) to generate rich, category-specific attribute descriptors (e.g., "fluffy tails," "blue irises") to enhance traditional coarse-grained prompts. The underlying assumption in many of these approaches is that VLMs inherently possess the capability to recognize localized visual details, and augmenting text prompts simply helps surface this latent ability for improved classification.

This paper critically challenges and empirically disproves this pervasive assumption. It reveals a fundamental limitation of CLIP: its strong inherent bias towards global image patterns, which hinders its effective processing of fine-grained local semantics, even when these details are explicitly described in the text prompts. This finding is crucial because it highlights a bottleneck in current VLM architectures and adaptation strategies.

To address this, the proposed method, "Decomposition and Description" (D&D), offers a novel solution that integrates with existing VLM adaptation paradigms like few-shot learning (e.g., Tip-Adapter) and test-time adaptation (e.g., TDA). By focusing on activating CLIP's *latent* capacity for localized feature analysis, D&D aims to bridge the gap between global and local understanding. The method also strategically incorporates Optimal Transport (specifically Earth Mover's Distance) a technique known for geometrically aligning structured data and quantifying dissimilarity between distributions, to effectively compare sets of visual features with sets of textual descriptions, moving beyond simple one-to-one cosine similarity. In essence, this work pushes the boundaries of VLM capabilities by rectifying an overlooked architectural bias, thereby making these powerful models more robust and discriminative for fine-grained tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this research stems from a critical empirical observation: Vision-Language Models (VLMs) like CLIP, despite their impressive zero-shot generalization, exhibit a significant bias towards global image patterns and struggle to effectively leverage fine-grained local semantic details. While previous research and conventional prompt engineering strategies often assume that VLMs inherently recognize localized visual attributes (e.g., "a red beak," "striped wings") and that enriching text prompts with such descriptors would naturally enhance classification, the authors' systematic experiments reveal this assumption to be flawed.

The motivation is meticulously built upon several experimental findings:
1. **Limited Perception with Descriptor-Only Prompts:** CLIP's classification accuracy drops sharply when presented with descriptor-only prompts (e.g., "a red beak and striped wings") compared to label-only prompts (e.g., "a photo of a bird"), indicating its predominant reliance on global category labels rather than local attributes.
2. **Poor Integration of Global and Fine-grained Details:** When evaluated under a strict criterion where both the global label and its corresponding descriptor must be correctly identified, hybrid prompts yield extremely low accuracy. This suggests CLIP struggles to integrate global and fine-grained information.
3. **Insensitivity to Descriptor Perturbations:** A similarity-level analysis reveals that CLIP's predictions are far more sensitive to changes in global labels than to changes in local descriptors. Mismatched descriptors (e.g., "a bird with blue wheels") have minimal impact on similarity scores compared to label perturbations. This further corroborates CLIP's prioritization of global context over fine-grained details.

Given these fundamental limitations, the **key objective** of this work is to develop a simple, effective, and plug-and-play solution that enables CLIP to "See Both the Forest and the Trees"—that is, to simultaneously capture and integrate both global image patterns and fine-grained local semantics. By addressing CLIP's inherent bias towards global representations, the authors aim to activate its latent capacity for localized feature analysis, thereby enhancing its discriminative power across various tasks and settings.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, named "Decomposition and Description" (D&D), is a plug-and-play solution designed to overcome CLIP's bias towards global features and improve its perception of fine-grained local semantics. It fundamentally alters how visual and textual information are prepared and compared within the VLM framework.

**Core Idea:**
D&D decomposes images into a set of local regions and describes classes using a set of fine-grained textual prompts. It then employs Earth Mover's Distance (EMD) to compute the similarity between these two sets, enabling a more nuanced, set-level alignment that considers optimal correspondences between local visual features and textual descriptors.

#### 3.1. Zero-shot Classification Approach

1. **Visual Decomposition (Seeing the Trees):** For an input image, D&D employs stochastic multi-crop augmentation during inference. This process generates multiple randomly cropped regions (e.g., 9 patches, 10–75% of original scale). Each cropped region is then encoded by CLIP's image encoder, resulting in a *set* of localized visual features, $X = \{v_1, v_2..., v_M\}$. This explicit cropping strategy forces the model to focus on partial regions, effectively constraining its receptive field and recalibrating its attention mechanism away from solely global patterns.
2. **Textual Description (Describing the Trees):** Building upon existing methods, Large Language Models (LLMs) are used to generate a *set* of fine-grained semantic descriptors for each candidate class. For a class $c$, this results in a set of descriptors, $D_c = \{d_1, d_2..., d_N\}$. These descriptors are designed to capture various attributes or parts relevant to the class.
3. **Set-level Similarity with Earth Mover's Distance (EMD):** Rather than computing cosine similarity between single global embeddings, D&D calculates the similarity between the *set* of image features ($X$) and the *set* of text descriptors ($D_c$) using EMD. EMD quantifies the minimal cost to transform one distribution (visual features) into another (text descriptions), inherently finding the optimal latent correspondences between elements of the sets. The grounding cost $C_{m,n}$ between a visual patch $v_m$ and a descriptor $d_n$ is defined as $1 - \phi(v_m, d_n)$, where $\phi$ is cosine similarity. The class with the minimal EMD to the image patch set is chosen as the prediction. The Sinkhorn distance is used to efficiently approximate EMD.

#### 3.2. Few-shot Learning and Test-Time Adaptation Approach

D&D is designed to be plug-and-play, enhancing existing adaptation methods like Tip-Adapter and TDA by incorporating local-aware processing.
1. **Constructing Local-aware Caches:**
 - For a training image $x_i$ and its label $y_i$, multiple random crops are performed, similar to the zero-shot setting.
 - For each descriptor $d_k$ associated with class $y_i$, the visual feature $v_{i,h}$ from the cropped regions that has the highest cosine similarity to $d_k$ is selected.
 - These selected visual features are aggregated into a set $V_i$ for each training image.
 - A class-specific cache $M_c$ is then constructed by collecting all such feature sets $V_i$ from training images belonging to class $c$. This cache now stores local-aware representations.
2. **Integrating Knowledge from Dual Modalities with EMD:**
 - For a test image $x$, it is also multi-cropped into a local feature set $X = \{v_1, v_2..., v_M\}$.
 - For each class $c$, the EMD is computed between the test image's local feature set $X$ and each feature set $V \in M_c$ in the class-specific cache. These EMDs are summed to get $EMD_c$.
 - An affinity score $A_c$ for each class is derived from $EMD_c$ (e.g., $exp(-\beta \cdot EMD_c)$).
 - Finally, the adapter's weight computation is refined by combining this affinity score $A_c$ with the zero-shot textual similarity, which is also calculated using EMD between the test image's local feature set $X$ and the class's full descriptor set $D_c$. The class with the highest fused similarity score is predicted.

This comprehensive approach allows D&D to consistently integrate fine-grained local visual information with detailed textual descriptions, thus "seeing both the forest and the trees" more effectively than prior methods.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted across zero-shot, few-shot, and test-time adaptation scenarios consistently demonstrate the effectiveness and superior performance of the proposed D&D method. The studies utilized 11 common image classification datasets and various CLIP backbones (ResNet-50 and ViT-B/16).

#### 4.1. Zero-shot Classification (Table 2)

- D&D significantly outperforms baseline methods like Zero-Shot CLIP and CALIP. It achieves an average top-1 accuracy of 60.62%, an improvement of 1.85% over Zero-Shot CLIP (58.77%) and 1.17% over CALIP (59.45%) across 11 datasets.
- The method shows strong improvements on fine-grained tasks (e.g., Pets, Flowers, FGVC) with an average gain of +1.71%, and a notable +5% on SUN-397. This highlights D&D's enhanced capability in cross-modal alignment by better utilizing local features.

#### 4.2. Few-shot Learning (Table 3 & 6)

- When integrated with Tip-Adapter (Tip+D&D), the method achieves the highest average top-1 accuracy of 71.46% (for 16-shot setting), surpassing the original Tip-Adapter (70.32%) and other baselines like Linear-probe (71.10%) and Tip-X (71.40%).
- The full results (Table 6) show consistent improvements across all datasets and various shot settings (1, 2, 4, 8, 16 shots), demonstrating D&D's robustness and effectiveness in low-data regimes. For example, for 1-shot, Tip+D&D improved average accuracy from 62.33% to 64.49%.

#### 4.3. Test-time Adaptation (Table 4 & 7)

- TDA+D&D achieves state-of-the-art average performance on cross-domain benchmarks with both ViT-B/16 and ResNet-50 backbones.
- With a ViT-B/16 backbone (Table 4), it outperforms the strongest baseline TDA by 1.95% (69.48% vs. 67.53%) and shows improvements on 9 out of 10 tasks.
- Remarkable gains are observed on datasets requiring detailed differentiation, such as Aircraft (+27.87%) and significant improvements on DTD and UCF101, showcasing robust generalization across diverse domains.
- With a ResNet-50 backbone (Table 7), TDA+D&D again achieves the highest average accuracy of 62.25%, demonstrating consistent robustness across different CLIP architectures.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study (Table 5)

The ablation studies provide crucial insights into D&D's core contributions:
- **Core Contribution Isolation (D vs. D&D):** Merely enhancing CLIP with detailed textual descriptions (CLIP+D) yields marginal gains (or even slight drops) on fine-grained tasks like FGVC (-0.12%) because generic descriptors don't effectively discriminate subtle inter-class differences. In contrast, D&D (CLIP+D&D), by combining descriptions with random cropping and EMD, achieves a +1.95% improvement over CLIP+D on FGVC, validating that the local decomposition and EMD-based alignment are critical for fine-grained understanding.
- **Alternative Classification Methods (D+R vs. D&D):** Random cropping combined with feature averaging (CLIP+D+R) shows no gains on tasks like UCF101, as averaging dilutes crucial spatiotemporal context. D&D's use of EMD for optimal alignment between image crops and text descriptions preserves this coherence, leading to superior performance on UCF101 (62.32% vs. 61.99% for D+R) and consistently outperforming CLIP+D+R across datasets.

In summary, the results comprehensively validate that D&D effectively addresses CLIP's inherent bias towards global semantics, enabling it to "See Both the Forest and the Trees" and achieve state-of-the-art performance across diverse VLM adaptation settings.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Decomposition and Description" (D&D) approach makes several significant contributions and holds substantial potential impact across the field of Vision-Language Models and their applications.

#### 5.1. Fundamental Insight into VLM Behavior

The most profound significance of this work lies in its empirical disproof of a widely held assumption: that VLMs like CLIP inherently recognize fine-grained local visual details when provided with descriptive text. By systematically demonstrating CLIP's strong bias towards global semantics and its limited perception of localized descriptors, the paper uncovers a critical architectural limitation. This insight is fundamental, guiding future research toward more targeted improvements rather than relying on unproven latent capabilities. It highlights that "seeing the trees" doesn't come naturally to CLIP and requires explicit intervention.

#### 5.2. Novel and Effective Plug-and-Play Solution

D&D offers a simple yet highly effective and plug-and-play solution to mitigate CLIP's global bias. By leveraging stochastic multi-crop augmentation to decompose images into local regions and combining this with LLM-generated descriptions and Earth Mover's Distance (EMD) for set-level alignment, D&D reactivates CLIP's latent capacity for fine-grained analysis. Its plug-and-play nature means it can be easily integrated into existing VLM adaptation frameworks (zero-shot, few-shot, test-time adaptation) without requiring architectural modifications or extensive retraining, making it highly practical for researchers and practitioners.

#### 5.3. Enhanced Performance Across Diverse Settings

The consistent state-of-the-art performance across 11 diverse datasets and three challenging evaluation settings (zero-shot, few-shot, test-time adaptation) underscores the robustness and generalizability of D&D. Significant improvements, particularly on fine-grained classification tasks and cross-domain adaptation benchmarks, demonstrate its ability to extract more discriminative knowledge. This advancement makes VLMs more reliable and accurate in scenarios where subtle visual cues are crucial.

#### 5.4. Broader Practical Implications

- **Fine-Grained Classification:** D&D can significantly boost performance in applications requiring detailed object or scene differentiation, such as medical image analysis (identifying specific pathologies), remote sensing (classifying land cover types), specialized industrial inspection (detecting minute defects), or biological classification (distinguishing subspecies).
- **Robustness to Domain Shift:** By improving local feature perception, D&D enhances the model's ability to adapt to new domains where global features might change, but local attributes remain consistent. This is crucial for real-world deployment of VLMs.
- **Efficient Adaptation:** For few-shot and test-time adaptation, D&D offers an efficient way to improve VLM performance with limited labeled data or during inference, reducing the need for large-scale fine-tuning.

#### 5.5. Future Research Directions

The authors themselves suggest promising avenues for future work, directly stemming from the limitations and insights of D&D:
- **Integration with Semantic Segmentation:** Combining D&D with models like SAM (Segment Anything Model) could enable more precise localization of descriptor-relevant image regions. This would move beyond random cropping to *semantically guided* cropping, further enhancing the alignment between visual parts and textual descriptions.
- **Explicit Part-Text Alignment Supervision during Pre-training:** Recognizing that CLIP's pretraining objective currently lacks explicit supervision for part-text alignment, future VLM architectures could be co-trained with localized contrastive losses. This would aim to build an inherent capability for fine-grained understanding directly into the foundation model, rather than relying on inference-time activation.

In conclusion, D&D not only provides a powerful, practical solution to a critical VLM limitation but also deepens our fundamental understanding of how these models perceive and integrate visual and linguistic information, paving the way for the development of more intelligent and perceptually rich multimodal AI systems.
