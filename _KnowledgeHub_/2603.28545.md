# ManipArena: Comprehensive Real-world Evaluation of Reasoning-Oriented Generalist Robot Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> ManipArena introduces a standardized, real-world evaluation framework for reasoning-oriented generalist robot manipulation models, including VLA and world models. It features 20 diverse tasks and systematic out-of-distribution evaluation, revealing that current models achieve a maximum of 42.7% success and exhibit complementary strengths, with no single architecture excelling across all capabilities.

## Problem

- Existing robot evaluation largely relies on simulators, creating a "reality gap" due to uncaptured real-world complexities like perception noise, latency, and contact dynamics.
- Real-world evaluations are fragmented across different robot platforms and custom environments, hindering fair comparison and reproducibility across research groups.
- Current benchmarks often emphasize basic motor skills or task memorization, lacking focus on complex spatial, semantic, and sequential reasoning required for truly generalist agents.

## Method

- ManipArena provides a standardized real-world evaluation framework featuring 20 diverse reasoning-oriented tasks for both tabletop and mobile manipulation.
- It employs a server-side inference architecture, a controlled green-screen environment, and a single bimanual robot platform (X2Robot) to ensure reproducible and fair comparisons.
- The framework integrates systematic diversity design for training data and stratified out-of-distribution (OOD) evaluation to rigorously test generalization, alongside rich sensory diagnostics and partial credit scoring for granular analysis.

## Results

- Baseline models achieved a maximum total score of 640.5 out of 1,500 (42.7%) across 15 tabletop tasks, indicating substantial room for improvement in current generalist manipulation capabilities.
- Unified multi-task VLA (OneModel) showed a +109% increase in semantic tasks (e.g., sort_headphone) but a -79% decrease in procedural tasks (e.g., put_items_into_drawer) compared to single-task VLA.
- World Action Models demonstrated stronger spatial invariance and more graceful degradation on compound OOD tasks, but struggled with fine-grained precision, consistently scoring low (e.g., 3/10) on tasks like insert_wireline.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A "one-model-for-all-tasks" VLA approach shows multi-task gains in semantic recognition but can lead to significant performance degradation in task-specific procedural reasoning due to catastrophic forgetting.
> - VLA and World Action Models exhibit complementary capabilities: WAMs excel at coarse manipulation and spatial robustness, while VLAs perform better in fine-grained precision and semantic understanding when language explicitly guides.
> - The benchmark highlights that force-sensitive fine manipulation (e.g., pouring, insertion) and complex sequential reasoning remain significant open challenges for current generalist models, as current approaches struggle to achieve high scores.
