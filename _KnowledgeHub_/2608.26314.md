# Dispersive Forward Tree Search for Optimal Control: Coverage, Complexity, and Computation

> [!summary] Summary
> DFT* is a steering-free kinodynamic planner for differentially flat nonlinear systems. It grows a breadth-first forward tree of locally dispersive control commands, giving deterministic finite-sample near-optimality, and applies cost-conditioned dominance pruning to cut tree size from exponential to polynomial in the horizon. On Dynobench it beats state-of-the-art planners on solution cost at comparable embedded-tier times, and its receding-horizon variant flies collision-free in cluttered dynamic scenes.

## Problem

- Steering-based sampling planners require state-to-state boundary value problem solutions, rarely available for nonlinear platforms, confining their strong optimality guarantees to linear or otherwise steerable systems.
- Forward-propagation planners avoid steering but carry only asymptotic or probabilistic guarantees; no finite-sample characterization exists of how sampled commands cover the dynamically feasible trajectory space.
- Deterministic grid propagation needs sampling resolution exponential in the planning horizon, while motion-primitive graph search leaves trajectory discontinuities that demand post-hoc optimization repair.

## Method

- ==Locally dispersive command sets==: finite flat-input families with cardinality bounds, a general ==pushforward construction== for control-affine systems, plus efficient hand-designed samplers for unicycle, trailer car and quadrotor.
- **DFT***: breadth-first forward tree expansion over dispersive commands with ==cost-conditioned dominance pruning== on quantized state cells, implemented as fused GPU kernels that sample, propagate and grade rollouts.
- **WWDFT***: receding-horizon DFT* that rejects rollouts violating depth-matched worst-case obstacle reachable-ball predicates, yielding a proven clearance guarantee on every committed trajectory segment.

## Results

- On Dynobench quadrotor tasks DFT* cuts first-solution cost from iDb-A*'s **6.58 s** to **2.50 s** on block and **4.62 s** to **2.00 s** on window, where SST* and RRT*+TO fail all **five** runs.
- Against FLASK's reported baselines DFT* returns the shortest paths, unicycle bugtrap **9.77 m** versus **11.51 m** for iDb-A*, in **73 ms** at embedded tier and **7 ms** on an RTX 4090.
- In dynamic corridors WWDFT* achieves **100%** collision-free success with zero constraint violation over **30** runs and **14.25-15.35 s** travel, versus FAPP's **40-60%** success and **39.3%** velocity overshoot.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dispersion, not randomness, is the lever for finite-sample guarantees: deterministic covering arguments yield non-asymptotic bounds where probabilistic Voronoi-bias sampling yields only limiting statements.
> - Cost-conditioned dominance pruning turns an exponential coverage requirement into polynomial work, so certified coverage and tractable search need not be traded against each other.
> - Caution: generic dispersive constructions are sample-inefficient; exploiting per-platform structure when designing command sets is what makes such theory deployable at low sample counts.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper positions itself within the field of kinodynamic motion planning, specifically addressing sampling-based algorithms that employ forward propagation of control commands. This domain is crucial for enabling autonomous systems to navigate complex environments while respecting their dynamic constraints.

Current sampling-based kinodynamic planners broadly fall into two categories:
- **Configuration-space sampling with Boundary Value Problem (BVP) steering:** Algorithms like PRM*, RRT*, and FMT* (and their kinodynamic extensions) typically sample states in the configuration space and rely on solving BVPs to connect these states. This category benefits from strong theoretical guarantees, including asymptotic optimality and, more recently, deterministic finite-sample performance guarantees (e.g., gPRM by Janson et al., 2018). However, solving optimal state-to-state BVPs for nonlinear systems is computationally challenging and often intractable.
- **Control-space sampling with forward propagation:** This approach evades the BVP requirement by sampling control commands and propagating the system's dynamics forward. Examples include the original kinodynamic RRT, SST, and GLC. While these methods offer probabilistic completeness or asymptotic optimality, they have historically lacked non-asymptotic analysis, including convergence rates and deterministic finite-sample near-optimality guarantees. The central difficulty in this category has been quantifying how effectively a finite set of sampled commands covers the resulting space of dynamically feasible trajectories.

This research directly addresses this identified gap within the control-space sampling and forward propagation paradigm. By focusing on the class of differentially flat nonlinear systems, the authors aim to provide the missing deterministic, finite-sample theoretical guarantees for this type of planner.

The work also relates to other planning approaches:
- **Graph search over motion primitives:** Methods like iDb-A* and those based on state lattices precompute or generate optimal motion segments (primitives) and search through them. These can be effective but may be limited by the primitive library's coverage or the computational cost of generating optimal primitives.
- **Optimization-based methods:** Trajectory optimization frameworks (e.g., TrajOpt, GuSTO, Crocoddyl) compute locally optimal trajectories under full system dynamics. These provide high-quality solutions but are often expensive and sensitive to initial guesses, making them less suitable for real-time reactive planning without reduced-order models.
- **Parallel computation:** The increasing availability of parallel hardware (e.g., GPUs) has been leveraged by modern sampling-based planners (e.g., Kino-PAX, FLASK). This paper also integrates hardware parallelism into its algorithmic design.

In summary, this paper seeks to advance the theoretical foundations and practical performance of forward propagation-based kinodynamic planners, particularly for nonlinear systems, by introducing deterministic finite-sample guarantees and an efficient, parallelizable algorithm.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objective of this research is to develop a propagation-based kinodynamic planner that offers deterministic, non-asymptotic guarantees on solution quality for a finite sampling budget. This contrasts with existing forward propagation methods that primarily offer probabilistic or asymptotic guarantees, leaving their finite-sample behavior largely uncharacterized.

The motivations driving this research are multi-faceted:

- **Bridging the Theoretical Gap:** A significant motivation is to elevate the theoretical rigor of forward propagation-based planners to a level comparable to that of state-to-state steering methods. While steering-based planners have established non-asymptotic, and even deterministic finite-sample, optimality guarantees, forward propagation methods have historically lacked such strong assurances. The authors aim to fill this gap, particularly for nonlinear systems where optimal steering is difficult.
- **Leveraging Differential Flatness:** The paper is specifically motivated by the properties of differentially flat nonlinear systems. This large class of systems allows for a finite-dimensional representation (flat output space) that simplifies the complex dynamics, making the problem of characterizing trajectory coverage more tractable. By working in this flat output space, the authors can construct "locally dispersive command sets" that do not require challenging point-to-point steering.
- **Quantifying Trajectory Coverage:** A central challenge in forward propagation is determining how well a finite collection of sampled control commands "covers" the space of all dynamically feasible trajectories. The research aims to precisely quantify this coverage, thereby enabling deterministic bounds on solution quality.
- **Computational Tractability of Optimal Control:** The theoretical analysis initially indicates that direct forward tree expansion to cover a trajectory class might lead to a tree size exponentially large in the problem horizon. A key motivation is to develop methods to mitigate this computational explosion, specifically through a pruning procedure, to make the algorithm practically deployable while retaining theoretical guarantees.
- **Practical Performance and Real-time Application:** Beyond theoretical contributions, a strong motivation is to develop an algorithm, Dispersive Forward Tree search (DFT*), that is competitive with, or superior to, existing state-of-the-art kinodynamic planners in terms of solution quality and computation time. Furthermore, the authors aim to demonstrate its applicability for real-time planning in dynamic environments on embedded-tier processors, which is crucial for autonomous robotics. This involves developing an online, receding-horizon variant (WWDFT*).

In essence, the paper is motivated by the desire to provide a theoretically sound, computationally efficient, and practically performant framework for kinodynamic motion planning for a broad class of nonlinear systems, moving beyond probabilistic assurances to offer deterministic guarantees on solution quality.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology involves a blend of theoretical development rooted in control theory and functional analysis, coupled with algorithmic design and practical implementation tailored for parallel computation.

**A. Foundations in Differential Flatness and Metric Entropy:**
The work is based on the properties of **differentially flat nonlinear systems**, which permit a transformation from the high-dimensional state and control space to a lower-dimensional "flat output" space. This transformation simplifies the dynamics and allows for a more tractable representation of trajectories. The optimal control problem is recast into this flat reformulation.
The concept of **dispersion** from metric entropy theory is central. It quantifies how well a finite set of points (or trajectories) covers a given space. The **Sobolev metric** is used to measure distances between trajectories in the flat output jet space.

**B. Dispersive Local Sampling:**
- **Definition of Dispersive Command Sets:** A command set is deemed δ-dispersive if its local rollouts (trajectories over a unit time window) from any state cover a class of M-smooth (bounded jet and flat input) and ε-eroded (control inputs remain within U with a margin ε) trajectories within a distance δ.
- **Construction for Control-Affine Systems:** For control-affine systems, the input map (Ψ_u) is shown to be affine in the flat input. This property enables a "pullback" mechanism: sampling flat inputs on a lattice (grid) in subwindows of the control horizon, then checking if the induced physical control remains within the eroded input set. This procedure provides a constructive path to theoretically guarantee the existence and cardinality bounds of δ-dispersive command sets.
- **Platform-Specific Pushforward Designs:** In practice, the universal pullback construction can be computationally inefficient due to low acceptance rates of commands. Therefore, for specific platforms (unicycle 1st/2nd order, car with trailer, quadrotor), the authors design more efficient "pushforward" command sets. These directly sample admissible control signals and propagate them, providing better practical performance while still yielding analytical dispersion estimates.

**C. Coverage and Optimality Theory:**
- **Margin Correction Mechanism:** A key theoretical innovation to overcome the accumulation of errors typical in forward propagation. It demonstrates that if a reference trajectory is ε-eroded (i.e., its control commands have spare authority), a command set can "correct" deviations. A "corrector control" ensures that a trajectory starting from a deviated state can re-converge to the reference state at the end of a time window, preventing exponential error growth over longer horizons.
- **Eroded Coverage:** This mechanism allows the authors to certify that a forward tree built with δ_A-dispersive commands covers an ε-eroded, M-smooth reference trajectory within a tube of width O(δ_A), uniformly over the entire planning horizon.
- **Eroded Optimality:** Assuming Lipschitz continuity of cost functionals and predicates, the framework guarantees that a trajectory found by the search will be feasible (satisfy predicates with a margin) and its cost will be within O(δ_A) of the optimal cost for the class of M-smooth, ε-eroded, and γ-robust reference trajectories.

**D. Complexity Management through Dominance Pruning:**
- **Exponential Complexity:** The paper theoretically demonstrates that covering the *entire* M-smooth trajectory class with deterministic guarantees inherently requires a tree size that grows exponentially with the planning horizon. This renders a brute-force search intractable.
- **Dominance Pruning Procedure:** To address this, a novel spatio-temporal dominance pruning procedure is introduced. This method is applicable to problems with temporally aggregating costs and separating predicates (e.g., additive costs, static obstacles, running extrema).
 - **Mechanism:** At each depth of the tree, the algorithm quantizes the state space (or flat output jet space) into cells of a given radius `s`. For each occupied cell, only the node with the minimum accumulated cost (running summary) is retained, while others are pruned.
 - **Result:** This pruning procedure reduces the tree size from exponential to polynomial in the problem horizon, while the optimality guarantee is maintained, albeit with a slightly increased certified gap (O(δ_A + s)).

**E. Algorithm and Implementation (DFT\* and WWDFT\*):**
- **DFT\* (Dispersive Forward Tree Search):** Implemented as a breadth-first expansion of the forward tree. Its data-parallel structure (independent evaluation of commands at each node, followed by pruning) is highly suitable for **GPU-accelerated parallelization** using fused CUDA kernels for sampling, propagation, and grading trajectories.
- **Algorithmic Variants:** For specific problem structures, variants like DFT*-Static (for static predicates, pruning globally across depths) and DFT-A* (for problems with consistent cost-to-go heuristics) are presented to further improve computational efficiency.
- **WWDFT\* (WarpWalker-DFT\*):** An online, **receding-horizon** implementation of DFT* for dynamic environments. It iteratively solves a fixed-horizon OCP from the current state, committing to the first segment of the best found trajectory. It includes mechanisms for safety assurance, given assumptions on obstacle dynamics and execution accuracy.

**F. Evaluation:**
The methodology includes extensive evaluation:
- **Offline Benchmarking:** DFT* is benchmarked against leading kinodynamic planners (iDb-A*, SST*, RRT*+TO, FLASK) on various platforms (unicycle, car with trailer, quadrotor) from the Dynobench suite. Metrics include solution cost, wall time, and impact of post-optimization.
- **Online Benchmarking:** WWDFT* is evaluated for real-time planning in dynamic, cluttered environments against state-of-the-art trajectory planners (FAPP, MIGHTY). This evaluates collision-free success rates, travel times, and adherence to differential constraints for both reduced-order and full 3D quadrotor models.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yields several key findings and results across theoretical contributions, algorithmic performance, and practical application:

**A. Theoretical Foundations and Guarantees:**
- **Deterministic Finite-Sample Near-Optimality:** The paper successfully develops a theoretical framework that provides the first deterministic, finite-sample near-optimality guarantees for kinodynamic planning based on forward propagation, specifically for differentially flat nonlinear systems.
- **Dispersive Command Sets:** It establishes the existence and complexity bounds for locally dispersive command sets. For control-affine systems, a general mechanism based on lattice quantization is provided for their construction, along with proofs for margin transfer and dispersion realization.
- **Eroded Coverage and Optimality:** The concept of "margin correction" is introduced, demonstrating that for ε-eroded reference trajectories, a forward tree constructed with δ_A-dispersive commands can track the reference within an O(δ_A) tube throughout the horizon. This directly translates to an O(δ_A)-eroded optimality guarantee for problems with Lipschitz continuous costs and predicates, stating that a feasible trajectory can be found whose cost is within O(δ_A) of the optimal cost for sufficiently robust solutions.
- **Complexity Analysis and Pruning:** The paper rigorously shows that achieving full coverage of the M-smooth trajectory class necessitates a tree size exponential in the planning horizon. However, it introduces a "dominance pruning" procedure that, for a specified class of temporally aggregating costs and separating predicates, reduces the tree's complexity to polynomial in the horizon. This pruning allows for a tractable search while still maintaining the near-optimality certificate, with the optimality gap increasing by a term related to the pruning radius `s`.

**B. DFT\* Algorithmic Performance:**
- **Superior Solution Quality:** On the Dynobench suite of kinodynamic planning problems, DFT* consistently delivers competitive, and often substantially better, solution quality (lower trajectory cost/time) compared to leading sampling-based planners like iDb-A*, SST*, RRT*+TO, and FLASK. For example, on several unicycle and quadrotor tasks, DFT* achieves significantly lower costs.
- **Efficient Computation on Embedded Hardware:** DFT* achieves these performance levels with comparable solution times when deployed on embedded-tier processors (e.g., emulating Jetson Orin Nano/AGX Orin).
- **Scalable Parallelization:** The algorithm demonstrates significant acceleration with increased parallel compute. Wall times shrink by up to 15x when scaled to a full desktop GPU (RTX 4090), highlighting its design for parallel hardware.
- **Post-Optimization Benefits:** While DFT* solutions are inherently continuous, seeding a non-linear optimizer (Crocoddyl) with DFT* outputs further refines trajectory costs and closes terminal goal tolerances, often maintaining the initial cost advantage over other planners.

**C. WWDFT\* Online Planning Performance:**
- **Real-time Planning in Dynamic Environments:** The receding-horizon implementation, WWDFT*, successfully demonstrates safety-assured real-time planning for a quadrotor in dynamic and cluttered environments.
- **High Success Rates and Fast Travel Times:** In benchmarking against FAPP and MIGHTY, WWDFT* achieves 100% collision-free success rates across various obstacle densities (up to 200 obstacles) and reports the fastest aggregate travel times.
- **Constraint Adherence and Safety Assurance:** Crucially, WWDFT* maintains safety (collision avoidance) and adheres to all differential constraints (velocity, acceleration, jerk, body rates, tilt angle) throughout execution. This contrasts with one of the baseline methods (FAPP), which exhibited constraint violations and obstacle penetrations in several trials.
- **Applicability to Reduced-Order and Full Models:** WWDFT* performs effectively on both a reduced-order triple integrator model and the full 3D quadrotor dynamics, demonstrating its versatility.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes significantly to the field of robotic motion planning, particularly for complex autonomous systems, with potential impacts across theoretical and practical domains:

- **Advancement in Sampling-Based Planning Theory:** The paper fills a long-standing theoretical gap by providing the first deterministic, non-asymptotic near-optimality guarantees for forward propagation-based kinodynamic planners. This provides a robust theoretical foundation for a class of algorithms that are widely used but whose performance limits at finite samples were previously less understood. This shift from probabilistic to deterministic guarantees offers stronger assurances for safety-critical applications.
- **Enabling Robust Control for Nonlinear Systems:** By leveraging the concept of differential flatness, the work provides a powerful framework to address optimal control problems for a broad and important class of nonlinear systems, which are common in robotics (e.g., aerial vehicles, manipulators). This extends the applicability of rigorously certified sampling-based methods to systems where traditional BVP-based steering is impractical.
- **Improved Performance for Kinodynamic Planning:** The DFT* algorithm demonstrates a tangible improvement in solution quality compared to established state-of-the-art planners while maintaining competitive or superior computation times. This suggests DFT* could become a preferred method for high-performance kinodynamic planning in various applications.
- **Facilitating Real-time Autonomous Navigation:** The online, receding-horizon implementation (WWDFT*) showcases the practical viability of this approach for real-time, safety-assured navigation in dynamic and cluttered environments. This is a critical capability for advanced autonomous robots, enabling them to operate safely and efficiently in unpredictable real-world settings.
- **Optimized for Modern Parallel Hardware:** The explicit design of DFT* for massive parallelization on GPUs significantly enhances its computational efficiency. This makes the algorithm highly compatible with modern embedded processors, pushing the boundaries of what is achievable on resource-constrained platforms for complex planning tasks.
- **Systematic Dispersive Sampler Design:** The work provides a systematic methodology for constructing dispersive command sets, demonstrating how to leverage system-specific structures for efficient sampling. This guidance is valuable for practitioners aiming to deploy certified planning algorithms.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The established theoretical framework and algorithmic advancements open several avenues for future research, including extensions to broader classes of nonlinear systems, integration with learning-based methods for real-time planning, and further optimization of command set design and pruning strategies. The explicit characterization of coverage and complexity provides a strong basis for further analysis and improvements.

In summary, this research not only advances the theoretical understanding of sampling-based kinodynamic planning but also delivers a high-performance, real-time capable algorithm with strong safety assurances, addressing key challenges in autonomous system navigation.
