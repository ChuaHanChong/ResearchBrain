# VLA-Precision: Asymmetric Co-Bootstrapping for Efficient Real-World Online RL of Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> VLA-Precision is a real-world online RL framework for post-training large flow-based VLAs on high-precision manipulation. Its ACoB algorithm couples intervention-driven behavior cloning with progressively calibrated critics that yield relative action advantages under a frozen reference, while the ACoB-Stream actor-learner architecture reuses frozen-prefix KV contexts. It reaches 98.3% mean success on nine chemistry tasks after 45.8 minutes of online training per task.

## Problem

- Simulation-based RL for VLA post-training is cheap and parallel, but visual and contact-dynamics sim-to-real gaps block reliable transfer to physical robots.
- Existing real-world RL methods regularize updates with behavior cloning yet still maximize absolute Q values, so optimistic value errors induce policy drift.
- Replay-intensive optimization recomputes frozen VLA prefixes per sample and synchronizes full models, collapsing throughput and sample efficiency at large-VLA scale.

## Method

- **ACoB** critic: an ensemble of value-advantage-decomposed critics trained by ==TD global return propagation== plus a ==state-matched preference-ranking loss== placing human corrections above the overwritten policy proposal.
- ==Relative-advantage policy improvement==: per-critic paired advantage gaps against a frozen Stage-I reference, optimized via a ==softplus soft-margin loss== and mixed with ==flow-matching== BC and reference regularization.
- **ACoB-Stream**: four-stage experience-policy loop with ==frozen-prefix KV reuse==, deduplicated disk-backed sliding-window persistence, objective-aligned context retrieval with prefetch, and trainable-==LoRA==-only policy publication.

## Results

- **98.3%** mean success over nine chemistry tasks in **45.8 min**/task and **27.6 s** episodes, versus **67.8%** for finetuned pi-0.5 and **10.0%** for Robo-Dopamine.
- ACoB-Stream completes **15,666** critic-to-actor cycles in **150** minutes: **10.95x** the no-KV-cache throughput, **90.9%** lower mean latency, and **1.09x** a CPU-RAM alternative.
- Ablations cut autonomous success from **96.25%** to **8.75%** without relative advantage, **20.00%** without actor BC, and **26.25%** without critic preference ranking.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - When critics are young, comparing actions relatively beats maximizing absolute Q, since paired differences cancel value scale and optimistic estimation error.
> - A human correction carries two signals: a BC target and a ranking preference over the proposal it overwrote, which TD alone leaves overvalued.
> - At large model scale the dominant online-RL cost is frozen-prefix recomputation and full-model synchronization, not the RL objective itself.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has seen significant advancements with the emergence of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. These models, pretrained on vast heterogeneous multimodal datasets, exhibit broad transferability and can perform diverse tasks with limited task-specific demonstrations. However, a recurring limitation of existing VLAs is their unreliability in tasks demanding high precision and repeatability, where even minor residual errors can lead to failures.

Traditional approaches like behavior cloning (BC) learn directly from demonstrations, but their performance is bounded by the quality and distribution of the demonstrations, leading to compounding errors when deviations occur. Reinforcement learning (RL), in contrast, offers a path to surpass demonstration-imposed ceilings by leveraging temporal credit assignment and continuous policy optimization through interaction-derived return signals.

The application of RL to VLA models has been explored in both simulation and real-world settings. Simulation-based RL allows for scalable and failure-tolerant interaction, but the "sim-to-real" gap often limits the reliability of transferring policies to physical robots. Real-world RL, by directly optimizing policies through physical interaction, enables adaptation to real-world dynamics and sensor noise. However, real-world VLA-RL faces two primary challenges:
1. **Algorithmic Stability:** Unreliable value estimates during online RL can induce policy drift, especially when fine-tuning large, complex VLA models. While methods employing BC constraints from demonstrations or interventions can stabilize exploration, they often provide limited improvement in the accuracy of value estimation.
2. **System Efficiency:** The substantial computational overhead associated with large VLA models constrains training throughput and overall online RL efficiency. Existing systems for compact robot policies do not scale effectively to large VLAs, and current VLA-specific solutions often involve replay-intensive optimization, externalized policy improvements, or rely on hardware scale-out rather than computational efficiency improvements per update.

This research introduces VLA-Precision to specifically address both the algorithmic stability and system efficiency bottlenecks in the context of real-world online RL for large VLAs. It aims to develop a stable RL algorithm that can improve VLA performance beyond demonstration limits and an efficient system architecture to handle the computational demands of large models in continuous online training. This work contributes to making precise and reliable real-world robotic manipulation with large VLA models more practical and achievable.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an efficient and reliable framework for applying real-world online reinforcement learning to large Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. This framework, termed VLA-Precision, is designed to enable autonomous trial-and-error improvement beyond the limitations of demonstration data, particularly for tasks requiring high precision and repeatability in real-world environments.

The motivation for VLA-Precision stems from two critical bottlenecks identified in current real-world online RL approaches for large VLAs:

1. **Addressing Algorithmic Instability due to Unreliable Value Signals:**
 - **Problem:** Large pretrained VLA models, while broadly capable, often exhibit residual errors that lead to intermittent failures in high-precision tasks. Applying online RL to refine these models can lead to policy drift if the value estimates derived from real-world interaction are unreliable. Traditional regularization techniques, such as behavior cloning (BC) from demonstrations, can constrain policy updates but do not inherently improve the accuracy of value estimation. This leaves RL updates vulnerable to errors in the value function, hindering stable and consistent improvement.
 - **Motivation:** There is a need for an RL algorithm that can robustly improve VLA performance without compromising their existing task-specific competence or introducing instability. This requires mechanisms to:
 - Rapidly incorporate successful and human-executed actions to accelerate early policy improvement.
 - Continuously calibrate value estimates as autonomous experience accumulates.
 - Guide policy updates with reliable signals that suppress drift and leverage learned competence effectively.
 - **Objective:** To propose the Asymmetric Co-Bootstrapping (ACoB) algorithm, which couples rapid behavioral learning with progressive value calibration across timescales to overcome demonstration-based performance limits while suppressing value-induced policy drift.

2. **Mitigating System Inefficiency due to Large-VLA Computational Overhead:**
 - **Problem:** The computational cost of operating large VLA models, especially the frozen multimodal prefix, creates a significant overhead. This overhead impacts training throughput and reduces the overall sample efficiency of real-world online RL, where interaction with physical robots is inherently slow and costly. Existing asynchronous actor-learner systems, while effective for compact policies, struggle with the scale-dependent costs of large VLAs, including expensive model forward passes, large KV (Key-Value) cache accumulation, high-latency data access, and burdensome full-model synchronization. These inefficiencies prevent new experience from promptly influencing the deployed policy, thereby slowing down the learning loop.
 - **Motivation:** There is a need for an architectural solution that can efficiently manage the computational and memory demands of large VLAs in a continuous online RL loop. This requires design principles that:
 - Minimize redundant computation of invariant model parts.
 - Efficiently manage and access large volumes of experience data.
 - Reduce overhead associated with policy synchronization and deployment.
 - **Objective:** To develop ACoB-Stream, a closed-loop experience–policy architecture guided by invariant-state decoupling and on-demand streaming. This architecture aims to deliver substantial improvements in throughput and computational efficiency for real-world online RL of large VLAs.

By addressing these two core challenges, VLA-Precision aims to unlock the full potential of online RL for fine-tuning large VLAs, enabling them to reliably perform high-precision robotic manipulation tasks in real-world environments.

### 3. Methodology and approach

VLA-Precision is presented as an efficient real-world online RL framework consisting of a two-stage post-training pipeline, the Asymmetric Co-Bootstrapping (ACoB) algorithm for policy optimization, and the ACoB-Stream architecture for system efficiency.

#### 3.1. VLA-Precision Problem Formulation and Pipeline

The framework begins with a two-stage post-training pipeline:
- **Stage I: Full-Parameter Imitation Learning:** A pretrained VLA model (e.g., π0.5) undergoes full-parameter fine-tuning on task-specific demonstrations. This establishes an initial task-specific policy prior, denoted as ΘIL.
- **Stage II: Real-World Online RL:** The online RL process is initialized from ΘIL. During this stage, the multimodal prefix parameters (Θf) and the core action-expert parameters (ψ) inherited from Stage I are frozen. Only a small set of trainable LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) parameters (θ) within the action expert are optimized. The original Stage-I action expert serves as a frozen reference (θ0). This stage is formulated as a closed-loop experience–policy optimization problem within an asynchronous actor-learner system.
 - **Actor Process:** Deploys the current policy (¯θn) to interact with the real world, generating trajectories (τn). It collects online experience, including raw transitions, effective human corrections, and multimodal prefix contexts, which are stored in an experience pool (En) comprising a replay buffer (R), a correction buffer (C), and a context buffer (K).
 - **Learner Process:** Optimizes the trainable parameters (θn) using the ACoB algorithm over sampled batches (Bn) from En, producing updated parameters (θ n+1) that are then deployed to the actor (¯θ n+1).

The robot's state (st) comprises visual observations (ot), robot state (qt), and task instruction (ℓ). Actions are H-step action chunks (at), and rewards are cumulative over the chunk horizon.

#### 3.2. Asymmetric Co-Bootstrapping (ACoB) Algorithm

ACoB addresses algorithmic stability by coupling rapid behavioral learning with progressive value calibration.

- **Progressive Value Calibration (Critic Optimization):**
 - **Value Model:** An ensemble of K task-specific critics is used. Each critic decomposes the action-value function Q(ω, ba) into a state-value V(ω) and an advantage A(ω, ba), where ω is the visual-proprioceptive state and ba is the flattened critic representation of the action chunk.
 - **Global Return Propagation (LTD):** Uses temporal-difference (TD) learning to propagate long-horizon returns. For each executed transition (ξt), a TD target (yt) is constructed using the minimum Q-value from the target critic ensemble, bootstrapping from the next state's action inferred by the current policy. This objective grounds the critic in cumulative rewards.
 - **Local Preference Ranking (Lrank):** Addresses situations where human interventions correct policy proposals. If a human correction (ahum) is applied over a policy's proposal (aprop), this module ensures that the corrected action's value (aexec) is ranked higher than the original proposal's value. This is achieved through a margin-based loss applied to the advantage component (A) for state-matched pairs of executed and proposed actions, resolving local credit ambiguity.
 - **Combined Critic Objective:** Lcritic = LTD
 - λrankLrank, where λrank balances the two signals.

- **Relative-Advantage Policy Improvement (Action Expert Optimization):**
 - **Relative-Advantage Improvement (Lrel):** Instead of directly maximizing absolute Q-values, which can amplify errors, ACoB uses paired advantage comparisons. For a given state, the action expert generates an action (aθ), and this is compared against a baseline. The baseline (bt,k) is defined as the advantage of the frozen Stage-I reference action (aref), potentially boosted by the advantage of the original policy proposal (aprop) if a human intervention occurred. A pessimistic relative advantage (∆At) is computed as the minimum advantage gap across the critic ensemble. An Lrel objective, a smooth soft-margin loss, encourages the current policy's action to be better than the baseline.
 - **Flow-Matching Behavior Cloning (LBC):** Rapidly incorporates high-quality actions into the policy. This loss is applied over task demonstrations, successful online executions, and effective human corrections (selected by a behavioral mask χbeh). It uses a flow-matching objective to learn the underlying velocity field of action generation.
 - **Reference Regularization (Lref):** To prevent policy drift and preserve the competence learned in Stage I, the trainable LoRA parameters (θ) are regularized towards the frozen Stage-I reference parameters (θ0). This term penalizes deviations in body-action coordinates from the reference.
 - **Combined Action-Expert Objective:** LAE = wBCLBC
 - wrelLrel
 - wrefLref, where wBC, wrel, and wref are nonnegative weights.

This asymmetric co-bootstrapping loop ensures that rapid behavioral learning improves policy performance and experience quality, while progressively calibrated value estimates provide stable guidance for autonomous policy improvement.

#### 3.3. ACoB-Stream Architecture

ACoB-Stream addresses system efficiency for large VLAs using two design principles: invariant-state decoupling and on-demand streaming, across four stages of state management.

- **1. Experience-Context Formation:**
 - During actor inference, the frozen VLM prefix (Θf) generates KV caches for each observation. These contexts are compacted (removing inactive positions) and stored in a disk-backed context buffer (K).
 - Transition records in replay (R) and correction (C) buffers store compact identifiers that reference these contexts in K, avoiding redundant re-computation of prefixes during learner updates.
- **2. Experience-Context Persistence:**
 - To manage the large footprint of accumulated prefix contexts, K is disk-backed, and contexts are stored once.
 - For replay sampling, a sliding-window mechanism is used, bounding the active dataset to improve Linux page-cache reuse, reducing disk-to-CPU transfers. Correction sampling remains independent.
- **3. Experience-Context Access:**
 - Context retrieval is aligned with the optimization objectives. Critic updates primarily retrieve successor contexts for bootstrapping, while action-expert updates retrieve both current and successor contexts.
 - Background CPU workers batch sample transitions, retrieve required contexts from K by identifiers, and assemble host batches. Device workers asynchronously transfer these to the GPU, minimizing GPU idle time.
- **4. Policy-State Synchronization:**
 - To reduce deployment latency, the learner only publishes the updated, trainable LoRA parameters (θ) to the actor.
 - The actor merges these updated parameters into its local VLA (where invariant VLA state is already resident) and atomically replaces the active policy state, allowing rollouts to continue on the last valid version during preparation.

#### 3.4. Real-World Training and Deployment

- **Teleoperation and Data Collection:** Utilizes an active isomorphic master interface for UR5e (6-DoF, kinematically isomorphic, with load-aware graded actuation and load-decoupled joint structure) for continuous, compliant interaction, and an incremental Cartesian keyboard interface for high-precision, fine adjustments in specific tasks.
- **State–Action Representation:** Employs a step-wise delta task-space action representation, where single-step end-effector increments (δxcmd, gcmd) are used. This facilitates stable optimization and fine-motion learning.
- **Robot Deployment:** Employs robot-specific Cartesian impedance controllers for UR5e (force-mode wrench control) and Franka (torque-level control) to ensure safe and precise physical interaction.

### 4. Main findings and results

The evaluation of VLA-Precision was conducted on nine high-precision chemistry manipulation tasks across four categories (contact-rich, contact-light, contact-free, bimanual coordination) using four robot embodiments.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance and Online RL Training Dynamics

- **High Success Rate and Efficiency:** VLA-Precision achieved a mean success rate of 98.3% across all nine tasks after an average of 45.8 minutes of online training per task. Successful trials were completed in an average of 27.6 seconds. This includes 177 successful trials out of 180 held-out trials, with 100% success on seven tasks and at least 90% on every task.
- **Superiority over Baselines:**
 - Compared to demonstration-only fine-tuned VLAs (π0.5 and π0), VLA-Precision improved average success by 30.5% and 38.9%, respectively, and improved average execution speed by 8.7% and 15.9%.
 - Against the strongest real-world RL baseline, Robo-Dopamine, VLA-Precision improved average success by 88.3% and average execution speed by 61.2%.
- **Addressing Baseline Limitations:** HIL-SERL, ConRFT, and Robo-Dopamine, while demonstrating some capabilities, showed limited average success rates (2.8% to 10.0%) and high intervention rates on the challenging task suite. This indicates their vulnerability to optimistic value errors and policy drift when attempting direct Q-maximization or lacking robust generalization. Fine-tuning π0 and π0.5 improved success but were still bounded by the demonstration-only adaptation.
- **Online Training Metrics (Fig. 7):** VLA-Precision consistently led in autonomous success rate (average 98.0%), effective success rate (average 99.0%), and maintained low cumulative interventions (average 2,517.4) and intervention rates (average 0.024%). This performance profile demonstrated the efficacy of asymmetric co-bootstrapping in rapidly absorbing corrections and guiding stable autonomous improvement.

#### 4.2. Offline VLA Test Performance

- On five representative tasks, VLA-Precision succeeded in 99 out of 100 trials, compared to 61 for π0.5 and 50 for π0, improving average success by 38 and 49 percentage points respectively.
- VLA-Precision achieved 100% success on all four single-arm tasks and 95% on the bimanual task, demonstrating robust execution under pose randomization and precision demands.
- It also reduced successful trial completion time, averaging 26.65 s, compared to 29.32 s for π0.5 and 32.05 s for π0. These results indicate that online RL with VLA-Precision effectively strengthens the VLA policy beyond what demonstration-only adaptation can achieve.

#### 4.3. Throughput and Efficiency Evaluation of ACoB-Stream

- ACoB-Stream achieved the highest update throughput (1.7407 CTA cycles/s) and the lowest mean critic-to-actor (CTA) cycle latency (0.574 s), completing 15,666 CTA cycles in 150 minutes.
- **Impact of KV Cache:** Compared to the 'No KV Cache' baseline, ACoB-Stream delivered 10.95× higher throughput and reduced mean latency by 90.9%, confirming the significant computational value of cross-update frozen-prefix reuse.
- **Impact of Sliding-Window Sampling:** Relative to 'Disk Full Random', ACoB-Stream provided 1.53× higher throughput and 34.5% lower mean latency. The 'Disk Full Random' baseline's throughput degraded significantly as the replay buffer grew, highlighting the importance of sliding-window sampling for disk locality.
- **Impact of Objective-Aligned Access:** ACoB-Stream's objective-aligned retrieval and on-demand prefetch provided 1.80× higher throughput and 44.4% lower mean latency compared to 'Naive Context Access'.
- **Comparison to Dynamic CPU RAM:** ACoB-Stream also outperformed a competitive 'Dynamic CPU RAM' alternative by 1.09× throughput and 8.0% lower latency, while having a less complex memory management system.

#### 4.4. Ablation Experiments for the ACoB Algorithm

Ablation studies on four representative tasks confirmed the contribution of each ACoB component:
- **W/o critic preference:** Removing state-matched preference ranking resulted in a 70.00-point drop in autonomous success and increased interventions, demonstrating its role in resolving local credit ambiguity and calibrating value estimates.
- **W/o actor BC:** Removing intervention-guided behavior cloning led to a 76.25-point drop in autonomous success and increased interventions, confirming its importance for rapid behavior absorption and improved experience quality.
- **W/o relative advantage:** Replacing relative-advantage policy improvement with direct Q-maximization caused the largest degradation: an 87.50-point drop in success and the highest intervention rate (24.93%). This indicated that direct Q-maximization is highly susceptible to absolute value scale and optimistic critic errors, while relative advantage is crucial for stable policy improvement and preventing drift.

#### 4.5. Action-Horizon Analysis

An analysis of step-wise delta action representation showed that increasing the action horizon (He) from 3 to 6 reduced average autonomous success from 58.3% to 6.7% and increased relative action-prediction RMSE by 29.3%. This highlighted the limitation of step-wise deltas for longer horizons due to recursive error accumulation, motivating the consideration of chunk-wise deltas for such scenarios.

### 5. Significance and potential impact

This work presents a framework that makes significant contributions to the field of robotic manipulation, particularly for tasks requiring high precision and reliability, and offers several potential impacts:

- **Enabling High-Precision Real-World Robotic Manipulation:** VLA-Precision enables large VLA models to achieve high levels of precision and repeatability in complex tasks within real-world environments. The demonstrated success in challenging chemistry manipulation tasks, which demand millimeter and submillimeter accuracy, indicates its potential for applications in sensitive domains like laboratory automation, manufacturing, and intricate assembly, where small errors can have significant consequences.

- **Advancing Real-World Online Reinforcement Learning for Large VLAs:** The framework directly addresses critical bottlenecks that have hindered the practical application of online RL to large VLA models: algorithmic stability and system efficiency. By providing a robust algorithm (ACoB) and an efficient architecture (ACoB-Stream), VLA-Precision makes real-world online refinement of large, pretrained models more feasible and effective.

- **Improved Policy Stability and Robustness:** The Asymmetric Co-Bootstrapping (ACoB) algorithm's ability to couple rapid behavioral learning with progressive value calibration is crucial. It allows policies to improve beyond the limits of initial demonstrations while suppressing value-induced policy drift. This stability is paramount in real-world scenarios, where unstable policies can lead to equipment damage, unsafe interactions, or task failures. The integration of human corrections, relative advantage learning, and reference regularization contributes to creating a more reliable and resilient learning process.

- **Enhanced Computational Efficiency:** ACoB-Stream's innovations in state management—including invariant-state decoupling, on-demand streaming, and optimized data access—significantly boost training throughput and computational efficiency. This translates to faster iteration cycles for policy improvement, reducing the overall time and computational resources required for real-world deployment, especially with resource-intensive large models. The 10.9x throughput improvement demonstrated is a substantial practical benefit.

- **Bridging the Gap between Broad Capability and Precision:** Existing VLA models offer broad capabilities but often lack the precision required for specialized tasks. VLA-Precision provides a methodology to bridge this gap, demonstrating that online RL can effectively refine these broad capabilities into precise, reliable execution without eroding the underlying generalization.

- **Foundational Work for Future Robot Learning:** The insights gained from VLA-Precision, particularly regarding action space design (step-wise vs. chunk-wise deltas for long-horizon tasks) and strategies for managing online learning complexity, lay groundwork for future research. It highlights important directions such as extending to multi-task real-world RL for VLAs (allowing skill transfer and reducing data collection) and tackling even more complex long-horizon bimanual manipulation challenges.

In summary, VLA-Precision represents a step forward in making real-world autonomous manipulation with powerful VLA models more practical, precise, and reliable, opening avenues for their deployment in a wider range of demanding applications.
