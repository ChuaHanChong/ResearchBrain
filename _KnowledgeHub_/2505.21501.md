# Vision Transformers with Self-Distilled Registers

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the University of Hong Kong developed Post Hoc Registers (PH-Reg), an efficient self-distillation framework to incorporate register tokens into pre-trained Vision Transformers without full retraining. This method successfully reduces artifact tokens, leading to enhanced performance in dense prediction tasks, including improved mean Intersection over Union on semantic segmentation benchmarks and better RMSE for depth estimation.

## Problem

- Vision Transformers (ViTs) generate "artifact tokens" – anomalous features incongruent with local semantics – hindering performance in fine-grained tasks.
- Integrating artifact-mitigating register tokens into large, pre-trained ViTs typically necessitates a computationally expensive full model retraining.
- Prior denoising approaches for ViT features suffer from significant computational overhead or limitations in their applicability.

## Method

- An efficient ==self-distillation framework, PH-Reg==, employs a teacher-student model architecture, both initialized from a single pre-trained Vision Transformer.
- The teacher model generates clean, artifact-free dense feature representations using a novel, ==gradient-free test-time augmentation (TTA)== scheme involving inverse transformation and aggregation.
- The student network incorporates a small set of randomly initialized, ==learnable register tokens== and selectively unfreezes a minimal subset of parameters for optimization.

## Results

- PH-Reg consistently achieved superior performance in zero-shot open-vocabulary semantic segmentation, outperforming all compared methods on **7 out of 8** benchmarks with the highest mean IoU.
- Significant and consistent improvements were observed for linear probe-based semantic segmentation and monocular depth estimation across diverse ViT backbones, with PH-Reg yielding, for instance, a **+**5.04%** mIoU** gain on VOC21 for CLIP.
- The framework demonstrated notable efficiency, reducing training time by over **58.9%** and requiring no additional storage for distillation targets, while also consuming approximately **10%** fewer FLOPs and parameters during inference compared to prior approaches.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Artifact tokens in Vision Transformers vary in magnitude across different models (e.g., lower norm in OpenAI CLIP, higher norm in DINOv2), challenging a uniform characterization.
> - The combined approach of learnable register tokens within the student and gradient-free test-time augmentation for teacher denoising provides a synergistic and effective artifact mitigation strategy.
> - Optimal performance is achieved by selectively unfreezing only a small subset of the student model's parameters, including positional embeddings, the patch embedding layer, and the final transformer block, in addition to the new register tokens.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision Transformers (ViTs) have rapidly become the predominant architecture for a wide array of visual processing tasks, demonstrating remarkable scalability and performance across classification, detection, and segmentation. Unlike traditional Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), ViTs process images by breaking them into patches and applying self-attention mechanisms, enabling global feature interactions without strong, built-in locality inductive biases. This design allows for more flexible representation learning and impressive emergent capabilities, such as zero-shot open-vocabulary segmentation from models like CLIP or rich semantic features learned through self-supervised methods like DINOv2.

However, the very flexibility that grants ViTs their power can also lead to a critical issue: the emergence of "artifact tokens." These are anomalous features that are often incongruous with local image semantics, failing to correspond to meaningful image structures. Such artifacts significantly degrade ViT performance in tasks requiring fine-grained localization or structural coherence, a limitation that has been an active area of research.

Previous attempts to mitigate these artifact tokens generally fall into a few categories:
- **Architectural Modifications:** Introducing "register tokens" into the ViT architecture is a proven method to absorb these artifacts. However, this approach traditionally requires retraining the entire model from scratch, which is computationally prohibitive for large, pre-trained foundation models.
- **Denoising Methods:** Other works, such as DVT, have proposed denoising ViT features using neural fields or similar techniques. While effective, these methods often involve computationally intensive gradient-based optimization per image or assume static artifact components, which may not always hold true.
- **Training-Free Attention Modifications:** Concurrent lines of work (e.g.,) focus on modifying attention mechanisms during inference to improve open-vocabulary segmentation, often without requiring additional training.

This paper, "Vision Transformers with Self-Distilled Registers" (PH-Reg), directly addresses the critical gap left by these existing solutions. It recognizes the impracticality of retraining large pre-trained ViTs from scratch to incorporate register tokens. Instead, PH-Reg offers an innovative *post-hoc* and efficient self-distillation framework. By enabling the integration of register tokens into existing models without full retraining or extensive labeled data, PH-Reg significantly enhances the utility and applicability of the vast ecosystem of pre-trained ViTs. It stands out by providing a robust, efficient, and broadly applicable solution to a fundamental limitation of modern Vision Transformers, making dense, artifact-free representations more accessible for a wide range of downstream tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an efficient and effective method for integrating register tokens into *existing* large-scale, pre-trained Vision Transformers to mitigate the presence of artifact tokens, without necessitating a complete retraining of the model or the use of additional labeled data.

This objective is driven by several key motivations:
1. **Addressing Artifact Tokens:** Vision Transformers, despite their impressive capabilities, inherently suffer from "artifact tokens" – outlier features that lack local semantic coherence. These artifacts are particularly detrimental to tasks requiring fine-grained spatial understanding, such as semantic segmentation and depth prediction, where precise localization is paramount.
2. **Leveraging Existing Pre-trained Models:** There is a vast and growing ecosystem of powerful, pre-trained ViTs (e.g., OpenAI CLIP, DINOv2) that have been trained on massive datasets, often at immense computational cost. Incorporating architectural improvements like register tokens, which are known to effectively reduce artifacts, typically requires training these models from scratch. This is computationally infeasible and time-consuming given their size and the resources already invested in their pre-training. The authors are motivated to unlock the benefits of registers for these models in a practical manner.
3. **Efficiency and Accessibility:** Existing denoising methods often involve significant computational overhead (e.g., neural field-based approaches requiring gradient optimization per image) or make assumptions about the artifacts (e.g., stationary nature) that may not always hold. There is a clear need for a method that can efficiently denoise dense features without incurring high computational costs or requiring specialized labeled datasets.
4. **Maintaining Model Utility:** The goal is not just to remove artifacts but to do so while preserving the original utility and emergent capabilities of the pre-trained ViTs, especially for zero-shot tasks and language-based dense queries. Any solution must avoid introducing undesirable distribution shifts or disrupting the model's fundamental strengths.

By proposing Post Hoc Registers (PH-Reg), the authors aim to provide a "plug-and-play" solution that significantly enhances the quality of dense feature representations in pre-existing ViTs, thereby improving their performance on critical downstream tasks with minimal overhead and no need for additional human-annotated data.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The PH-Reg framework is an efficient self-distillation method designed to integrate register tokens into existing pre-trained Vision Transformers without full retraining or labeled data. It operates by setting up a teacher-student network paradigm, both initialized from the same pre-trained ViT.

#### 3.1. Efficient Denoising of Teacher Representations

The core of the teacher network's role is to generate clean, artifact-free dense feature representations to guide the student. Unlike prior work that relies on computationally expensive neural fields for denoising, PH-Reg introduces a novel, gradient-free test-time augmentation (TTA) scheme:
- **Augmentation Strategy:** Given an input image `I`, `n` random augmentation parameters (θ_i) are sampled. Each θ_i comprises horizontal/vertical offsets (x_i, y_i) and a boolean horizontal flip (flip_i). The offsets are integer multiples of the ViT's patch size for efficient indexing.
- **Feature Generation:** The teacher model, which remains frozen and unmodified (except for task-specific adaptations like NACLIP for CLIP in zero-shot tasks), processes each augmented image (I_i) to produce dense features (F_i).
- **Inverse Transformation and Aggregation:** The generated features (F_i) are then restored to their original image locations using an inverse transform (T^-1(F_i, C_i)). These restored features are additively accumulated across all `n` augmentations for each spatial location.
- **Mean Computation:** Finally, a dimension-wise sample mean is computed for the accumulated features at each location. This average represents the "denoised" target feature map, which is mathematically proven to be the optimal minimizer under MSE loss for a discrete grid of representations.
- **Efficiency:** This TTA-based denoising process is significantly faster (e.g., less than 200ms) than neural field-based methods, as it avoids gradient-based optimization.

#### 3.2. Design of the Student Network

The student network is designed to be a minimally invasive enhancement of the original ViT, focusing on computational efficiency:
- **Initialization:** The student is initialized with the *same* pre-trained weights as the teacher.
- **Register Tokens:** A small number (`m`) of randomly initialized, learnable register tokens are added. These tokens participate in the self-attention process alongside the image patch tokens and the [CLS] token, but are discarded at the output, implicitly "absorbing" artifacts.
- **Selective Unfreezing:** To preserve the vast knowledge encoded in the pre-trained weights, only a *small subset* of the student's parameters are unfrozen and optimized during distillation. Through ablation studies, the authors identify that optimizing the register tokens, positional embeddings, the convolutional patch embedding layer, and the final transformer block (containing the self-attention mechanism) yields optimal results. The majority of the ViT's weights remain frozen.

#### 3.3. Learning and Optimization of the Student

The student learns from the denoised representations of the teacher through a multi-objective distillation strategy:
- **Loss Function:** The total loss (Loss_total) combines two components:
 - **Cosine Similarity Loss:** `1 - cossim(target, predicted)` ensures directional alignment between the student's output features (F_student) and the teacher's denoised targets (F_teacher).
 - **Mean Squared Error (MSE) Loss:** `MSE(target, predicted)` ensures magnitude alignment between the student and teacher features.
- **Data Requirements:** The entire self-distillation process requires only unlabeled images, making it highly practical and scalable for diverse datasets.

In summary, PH-Reg leverages the power of pre-trained models by efficiently generating clean feature targets via TTA, then distills this denoised knowledge into a student model augmented with a small set of learnable register tokens and selectively unfrozen weights. This approach minimizes computational overhead while significantly improving feature representation quality.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides compelling evidence that PH-Reg effectively mitigates artifact tokens in Vision Transformers, leading to quantifiable improvements across various dense prediction tasks.

#### 4.1. Effectiveness in Reducing Artifact Tokens

- **Qualitative Visualization:** UMAP visualizations of dense features (Figures 1, 4) clearly show that PH-Reg produces significantly cleaner and more spatially consistent feature maps compared to baselines like MaskCLIP, SCLIP, and NACLIP. Heatmaps generated from CLIP text queries demonstrate that PH-Reg results in more fine-grained, semantically aligned localizations.
- **Artifact Nature:** The study reveals that artifact tokens do not consistently exhibit high magnitude across all ViT models. In OpenAI CLIP and OpenCLIP, artifacts often have lower norms, while in DFN-CLIP and DINOv2, they are higher norm (Figure 6). PH-Reg effectively reduces the variance of patch norms and outliers, regardless of whether artifacts are initially low or high magnitude (Figure 7).

#### 4.2. Quantitative Performance Improvements

- **Zero-shot Open-vocabulary Semantic Segmentation:**
 - On 8 benchmarks (Table 1), PH-Reg consistently outperforms all compared methods (including MaskCLIP, SCLIP, NACLIP, ClearCLIP, and DVT-enhanced versions) on 7 out of 8 datasets, achieving the highest mean Intersection over Union (mIoU). For instance, it achieves 63.01% mIoU on VOC21, a notable improvement.
 - Further evaluation using Pearson correlation (Table S.4) also shows PH-Reg outperforming baselines, with an average performance of 0.404 across 7 datasets, highlighting improved consistency of dense feature representations.
- **Linear Probe Based Segmentation & Depth:**
 - PH-Reg demonstrates significant and consistent improvements when applied to various pre-trained ViT backbones (OpenAI CLIP, OpenCLIP, DFN-CLIP, DINOv2) for both semantic segmentation (PASCAL VOC 2012, ADE20K) and monocular depth estimation (NYUv2-Depth) (Table 2).
 - It outperforms DVT-denoised versions for most CLIP-based models, and achieves competitive or superior results for DINOv2 with less parameter overhead. For example, PH-Reg yields a +5.04% mIoU gain on VOC21 for CLIP and improves DINOv2's RMSE for depth estimation.

#### 4.3. Efficiency Analysis

- **Training Time:** PH-Reg significantly reduces training time compared to DVT. For DINOv2 ViT-B, PH-Reg trains in 9000 minutes (150 hours) compared to DVT's 21908 minutes (365.1 hours), representing a saving of over 58.9% (Table S.6). This is largely due to PH-Reg's gradient-free denoising.
- **Space Usage:** PH-Reg requires no additional storage space as it computes distillation targets on the fly, a stark contrast to DVT which requires terabytes of intermediate neural fields.
- **Inference Cost:** During inference, PH-Reg utilizes approximately 10% fewer FLOPs and parameters compared to DVT (Table S.7), demonstrating its efficiency in deployment.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies and Architectural Insights

- **Number of Registers:** Increasing the number of register tokens (from 0 to 16) improves cosine similarity, with performance gains diminishing beyond 8-16 registers. 16 registers were chosen for subsequent experiments (Figure 5a).
- **Unfreezing Strategy:** Unlocking positional embeddings, the convolutional patch embedding layer, and the final transformer block (in addition to registers) yields the best performance. The study suggests that positional embeddings alone do not fully account for artifact tokens (Figure 5a).
- **Number of Augmentations:** Increasing the number of augmentations for teacher denoising improves feature alignment, with 10 augmentations providing efficient high-quality features (Figure 5b).
- **Component Contribution:** Both the register tokens and the denoising process applied to the teacher model contribute roughly equally to the overall performance improvements, demonstrating the synergistic effect of the full PH-Reg pipeline (Table 3).
- **Shifting Ratio:** An optimal shifting ratio of 15% for augmentations in the denoising process yields the best average performance across datasets (Table 4).

Overall, the findings strongly support PH-Reg as an effective, efficient, and broadly applicable method for improving the dense feature representations of pre-trained Vision Transformers, making them more robust for fine-grained tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Post Hoc Registers (PH-Reg) represents a significant advancement in the practical application and ongoing development of Vision Transformers, with several profound implications and potential impacts:

1. **Enhanced Utility of Existing Foundation Models:** PH-Reg directly addresses a critical challenge in the widespread deployment of large, pre-trained ViTs (e.g., CLIP, DINOv2). By enabling the post-hoc integration of artifact-mitigating register tokens without the need for costly and time-consuming retraining from scratch, PH-Reg significantly extends the lifespan and practical utility of these models. This allows researchers and practitioners to leverage their immense pre-trained knowledge for fine-grained tasks without having to invest in developing new models or retraining existing ones.

2. **Improved Performance in Dense Prediction Tasks:** The consistent and substantial performance gains demonstrated across zero-shot semantic segmentation, linear probe segmentation, and monocular depth estimation highlight PH-Reg's ability to produce cleaner, more semantically coherent dense feature maps. This directly translates to more accurate and reliable outputs for applications requiring precise spatial understanding, such as autonomous driving, robotics, medical image analysis, and augmented reality.

3. **Computational Efficiency and Accessibility:** PH-Reg's self-distillation framework, which utilizes unlabeled data and an efficient, gradient-free test-time augmentation for denoising, offers significant advantages in terms of computational resources. It substantially reduces training time and eliminates the need for vast storage of intermediate data compared to previous methods like DVT. This makes advanced ViT feature enhancement more accessible to researchers and organizations with limited computational budgets, democratizing access to high-quality visual representations.

4. **Broader Applicability and Generalizability:** The method's effectiveness across various ViT backbones (OpenAI CLIP, OpenCLIP, DFN-CLIP, DINOv2) and diverse dense prediction tasks underscores its robust and generalizable nature. This suggests that PH-Reg can serve as a universal enhancement module for a wide range of existing and future ViT-based architectures.

5. **Deeper Insights into ViT Artifacts:** The observation that artifact token norms are not uniformly high across all ViT models (being lower-norm in some CLIP variants) provides valuable new insights into the underlying mechanisms and dynamics of artifact emergence. This finding challenges previous assumptions and opens new avenues for theoretical investigation into why and how these inconsistencies arise, potentially leading to more sophisticated mitigation strategies in the future.

6. **Catalyst for Future Research:** By providing a powerful and efficient tool for cleaning ViT features, PH-Reg can accelerate research in areas that heavily rely on robust dense visual descriptors. This includes, but is not limited to, open-vocabulary learning, scene understanding, visual reasoning, and even cross-modal tasks where fine-grained visual alignment is crucial. The public release of the code further supports reproducibility and fosters community engagement.

In conclusion, PH-Reg offers an elegant and highly practical solution to a pervasive problem in Vision Transformers, significantly advancing their utility and efficiency for dense prediction tasks. Its impact is poised to be felt across both academic research and real-world applications of computer vision.
