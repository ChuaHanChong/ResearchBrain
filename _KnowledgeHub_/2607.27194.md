# VidMap: Exploiting Temporal Structure for Video-Based Structure-from-Motion

> [!summary] Summary
> VidMap is an offline Structure-from-Motion framework that leverages temporal structure and global optimization to reconstruct 3D environments and camera poses from uncalibrated videos. It integrates dense matching, robust loop closure, and monocular depth priors to achieve higher accuracy and robustness, outperforming state-of-the-art methods with up to 10 percentage points higher W-AUC on challenging datasets.

## Problem

- Existing SLAM systems, while processing incrementally, suffer from error accumulation (drift) and sensitivity to transient failures, often requiring known camera intrinsics.
- Traditional Structure-from-Motion (SfM) systems, designed for global optimization, treat video frames as an unordered set, making them vulnerable to visual symmetries and unable to leverage temporal continuity.
- A robust solution is needed to accurately recover camera calibration and metric poses from arbitrary, unconstrained videos to facilitate large-scale training data generation.

## Method

- **VidMap** develops a hybrid framework that integrates strong sequential constraints from video into an offline global optimization ==Structure-from-Motion (SfM)== pipeline.
- Implements a video-aware extraction phase utilizing ==dense matching==, multi-flow drift correction for keyframe tracking, and incorporates learned monocular depth priors for geometric regularization.
- Extends a global SfM framework with ==provenance-dependent robust losses== for sequential versus loop closure observations, and jointly optimizes poses, 3D points, intrinsics, and per-image depth scales.

## Results

- VidMap consistently outperformed all state-of-the-art SLAM and SfM baselines on challenging datasets (LaMAR, CroCoDL), achieving up to **10 percentage points** higher Windowed Area Under the Curve (W-AUC) for translation error in uncalibrated settings.
- Demonstrated robust and accurate joint estimation of camera intrinsics, with performance in uncalibrated settings nearly matching ground-truth calibrated scenarios.
- Ablation studies confirmed that metric monocular depth priors and provenance-dependent robust losses are critical components for maintaining accuracy and robustness against drift and visual aliasing.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating video's temporal structure into a global SfM optimization framework significantly enhances robustness against drift and visual ambiguities, especially for unconstrained video.
> - Incorporating metric monocular depth priors within global positioning and bundle adjustment stages is crucial for mitigating scale drift and improving accuracy in geometrically challenging scenarios.
> - Differentiating between sequential and loop closure observations with provenance-dependent robust losses effectively manages errors from visual aliasing, leading to more stable optimization.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of camera pose and calibration estimation from visual data is primarily addressed by two dominant paradigms: Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) and Structure-from-Motion (SfM). Both aim to reconstruct 3D environments and camera trajectories, but they operate under different assumptions and optimization strategies.

SLAM systems are typically designed for real-time, incremental operation. They process images sequentially, building and updating a map and camera poses causally. This design allows for real-time applications but makes SLAM sensitive to initial conditions, prone to error accumulation (drift) over time, and susceptible to unrecoverable failures when tracking is lost due to challenging conditions like degenerate motions or occlusions. Many SLAM systems also assume known camera intrinsics. Recent advancements in SLAM incorporate learned components for improved robustness in feature tracking, depth estimation, and 3D surface estimation, but the fundamental causal and incremental architecture remains.

In contrast, SfM systems are designed for offline processing, considering all available data (images) globally. This allows for optimal initialization and global optimization, which generally leads to higher accuracy and the ability to jointly optimize camera calibration. However, traditional SfM often treats image sets as unordered, neglecting the temporal continuity inherent in videos. This makes SfM less robust to visual symmetries and unable to adapt its computation or keyframing to motion dynamics. Incremental SfM methods exist but still do not fully leverage the sequential nature of video in the same manner as SLAM for specific challenges.

The broader landscape also includes feedforward learned algorithms that directly predict poses, calibration, and 3D structure. While robust to sparse views and low visual overlap, these methods often lack the geometric precision, scalability, and long-term consistency required for extended video sequences, frequently suffering from drift.

VidMap aims to bridge the identified gap between SLAM and SfM by integrating the strengths of both. It seeks to leverage the strong sequential constraints of video, traditionally exploited by SLAM, within the global optimization framework of offline SfM. This approach intends to overcome the limitations of each paradigm: SLAM's sensitivity to transient failures and drift, and SfM's vulnerability to visual symmetries due to its lack of temporal awareness. The work positions itself as a system that combines temporal structure awareness, dense image matching, reliable loop closure, and metric monocular depth priors within a global optimization framework to achieve robust and accurate metric reconstruction of long, uncalibrated videos.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a system capable of accurately recovering camera calibration and metric poses for arbitrary, unconstrained videos, enabling the creation of large-scale training data for navigation and scene understanding applications. The existing limitations of current approaches serve as the core motivation for this work.

Specifically, the key objectives and motivations include:

1. **Overcoming SLAM's Limitations:**
 - **Sensitivity to Initialization and Transient Failures:** SLAM's causal, incremental nature means errors introduced early propagate and compound, leading to drift. Transient failures (e.g., due to degenerate motion, occlusions, or textureless environments) often result in unrecoverable tracking loss. VidMap is motivated to address this by adopting a global optimization strategy that can recover from local inconsistencies.
 - **Real-time Optimization Constraints:** SLAM systems are often over-optimized for real-time operation, which can limit the exploration of alternative designs and the integration of advanced computer vision techniques. VidMap, being an offline system, can leverage more computationally intensive, accurate methods.
 - **Requirement for Known Camera Calibration:** Many SLAM systems require accurate camera intrinsics. A key motivation for VidMap is to handle uncalibrated videos, jointly estimating intrinsics alongside poses and structure.

2. **Addressing SfM's Limitations:**
 - **Lack of Temporal Awareness:** Traditional SfM typically disregards image ordering, treating video frames as an unordered set. This prevents it from exploiting the temporal continuity of videos, making it less robust to visual symmetries and unable to adapt to motion dynamics. VidMap seeks to integrate temporal ordering as a first-class citizen to improve robustness against ambiguities and aliasing.
 - **Robustness to Visual Symmetries and Extreme Motions:** Without leveraging sequential constraints, SfM can struggle with visual symmetries (e.g., repetitive patterns in indoor environments) which can lead to incorrect data associations. VidMap is motivated to use strong sequential constraints to disambiguate such scenarios.
 - **Dense Matching Incompatibility:** Historically, dense, pixel-wise matching has been challenging to integrate with the discrete tracks required by SfM. VidMap aims to leverage recent advances in wide-baseline dense image matching within its framework.

3. **Enhancing Robustness and Accuracy for Challenging Videos:**
 - **Arbitrary, Long, Uncalibrated Videos:** Existing systems often fail on videos captured by non-expert users, featuring degenerate and fast motions, visual symmetries, texture-less environments, or unknown calibration. VidMap is motivated to provide a solution that is robust across these challenging conditions.
 - **Mitigating Scale Drift and Degenerate Configurations:** Multi-view geometric constraints alone can be insufficient in certain degenerate configurations (e.g., pure rotation, forward motion, planar scenes). VidMap's objective is to augment global optimization with metric monocular depth priors to provide additional constraints, thereby mitigating scale drift and enhancing robustness in such scenarios.

By combining the strengths of both paradigms—SLAM's sequential constraints and SfM's global optimization—VidMap aims to deliver a significantly more robust and accurate approach for camera pose and 3D scene reconstruction from unconstrained videos, without requiring prior camera calibration.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VidMap is a reconstruction framework designed to leverage the temporal structure of videos within a global SfM optimization pipeline. It operates in two main phases: video-aware extraction and global mapping. The system aims to estimate camera poses ($T_i \in SE(3)$) for selected keyframes, camera intrinsics ($K_i$), and a sparse 3D point cloud ($X = \{X_k\}$) from an uncalibrated video $I = \{I_1, \dots, I_N\}$.

#### 3.1. Video-Aware Extraction

This phase adapts SLAM's sequential tracking and keyframing concepts for offline dense matching. It is non-causal, allowing for multiple passes over the data.

- **Video Feature Matching:**
 - **Dense Matching:** VidMap utilizes a coarse-to-fine dense matcher (e.g., RoMa v2) to generate dense flow fields ($W_{a \to b}$), per-pixel certainty ($c_{a \to b}$), and localization covariance ($\Sigma_{a \to b}$) between image pairs. This allows for correspondence sampling even in textureless regions.
 - **Tracking:** Sparse keypoints are propagated across consecutive frames using the dense flow fields. The localization covariance accumulates additively along sequential tracks.
 - **Keyframe Detection:** Keyframes are adaptively selected based on observed motion. A new keyframe is inserted when a sufficient fraction of tracked keypoints move beyond a threshold distance or are lost.
 - **Track Construction:** To reduce drift, a multi-flow drift correction mechanism is employed. For a new keyframe $K_i$, in addition to the sequential prediction from $K_{i-1}$, direct flows from a window of $W$ preceding keyframes ($J_i$) to $K_i$ are also evaluated. The candidate prediction with the minimum covariance trace is selected, but only if it is consistent with the sequential prediction. Otherwise, the sequential prediction is retained.
 - **Track Filtering:** Tracks are terminated if their certainty drops below a threshold. Epipolar filtering removes tracks with large errors in a given pair, and spatially clustered tracks are probabilistically thinned for uniform coverage.
- **Depth Estimation:** For each selected keyframe, a monocular depth map is estimated using a learned depth model, providing a depth prior ($m_{ik}$) and uncertainty ($\sigma_{ik}$) for each observed pixel. These priors are used to regularize degenerate geometry and scale drift.

#### 3.2. Loop Closure

Loop closures provide long-term constraints but can be prone to errors due to visual aliasing.

- **Retrieval and Matching:** Global descriptors (e.g., MegaLoc) are used to retrieve top candidate loop closure pairs. These pairs are matched using the dense matcher and geometrically verified.
- **Track Establishment:** Unlike standard SfM, VidMap builds tracks exclusively from sequential edges. Loop closure correspondences are attached as separate observations with a provenance label (`lc`), distinct from sequential observations (`seq`). This preserves temporal trust and enables provenance-dependent robust losses.

#### 3.3. Uncalibrated Videos

For uncalibrated videos, focal lengths are estimated and refined in two stages before global mapping.

- **Focal Length Estimation:** GeoCalib is used to independently estimate focal length per keyframe. The most confident estimates are then used for joint optimization of a single focal length across multiple views.
- **Intrinsics Refinement:** The shared intrinsics are further refined via view graph calibration, which estimates focal lengths from fundamental matrices of well-conditioned pairs. If insufficient well-conditioned pairs exist, the GeoCalib estimate is used as a fallback. These refined intrinsics serve as an initialization for bundle adjustment.

#### 3.4. Global Mapping

VidMap extends the GLOMAP global SfM framework with new optimization machinery.

- **Rotation Averaging:** Pairwise relative rotations ($\tilde{R}_{ij}$) are estimated. Global orientations ($\{R_i\}$) are then optimized by minimizing geodesic distances. Importantly, provenance-dependent losses are applied: sequential edges receive Huber loss, while loop closure edges receive Cauchy loss to downweight erroneous closures.
- **Global Positioning (GP) with Depth Priors:** This stage jointly optimizes camera centers ($\{c_i\}$), 3D points ($\{X_k\}$), and per-image depth scale factors ($\{s_i\}$). The objective combines:
 - A **bearing residual** ($e^{GP}_{ik}$) penalizing angular discrepancy between observed camera rays and 3D points.
 - A **depth residual** ($r^{depth}_{ik}$) comparing optimized depth to the scaled monocular depth prior ($s_i \cdot m_{ik}$).
 - A **scale regularizer** ($r^{scale}_i$) for the scale factors.
 Provenance-dependent robust losses are also applied here, with different loss functions for sequential (`seq`) and loop closure (`lc`) observations. Observation covariances are propagated from 2D pixel space to 3D bearing space for weighting.
- **Bundle Adjustment (BA):** This final stage refines poses, 3D points, intrinsics, and per-image depth scales. The objective minimizes reprojection error and incorporates depth and scale residuals. By this stage, the reconstruction is near convergence, and inconsistent loop closure observations have been filtered, allowing for uniform robust losses across all observations. Depth scales are optimized here, unlike methods that fix them after incremental reconstruction.
- **Robust Optimization:** Graduated non-convexity is used, progressively tightening robust losses across iterations in GP and BA. Depth-match inconsistencies are flagged, and corresponding depth residuals switch to Cauchy loss if the depth ratio exceeds a threshold.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

VidMap was evaluated against state-of-the-art SLAM and SfM approaches, both classical and learned, on four diverse and challenging datasets: LaMAR, CroCoDL, ETH3D-SLAM, and EuRoC. The evaluations were performed in both calibrated (ground-truth intrinsics provided) and uncalibrated (intrinsics estimated) settings.

#### 4.1. Performance on Challenging Datasets (LaMAR and CroCoDL)

- **LaMAR Dataset (Table 1):**
 - VidMap significantly outperformed all existing approaches in both calibrated and uncalibrated settings across various window lengths (10m, 25m, 50m, 100m, and full trajectory), as measured by Windowed Area Under the Curve (W-AUC) of the translation error.
 - For example, in the uncalibrated setting, VidMap achieved W-AUCs ranging from 91.9% (10m) to 88.5% (full trajectory), while the next best, ViPE, ranged from 86.6% to 76.6%.
 - On challenging sequences with geometric degeneracies (narrow baselines, forward motion, textureless surfaces), classical methods (GLOMAP, DPV-SLAM) exhibited significantly more drift. Approaches leveraging metric depth priors (ViPE, MegaSaM) showed improved robustness but still fell short of VidMap's precision.
 - VidMap's accuracy in the uncalibrated setting was nearly identical to its performance in the ground-truth calibrated setting, demonstrating its ability to estimate accurate calibration.

- **CroCoDL Dataset (Table 2):**
 - VidMap demonstrated strong generalization to out-of-domain videos from disaster-site buildings (rubble, collapsed structures, varying lighting). It outperformed all baselines by a large margin on both phone and robot sequences, without retraining or domain-specific adaptation.
 - For phone sequences (uncalibrated), VidMap achieved W-AUCs from 94.0% (10m) to 95.5% (full), compared to DA3-Long (86.2% to 88.9%).
 - For robot sequences (uncalibrated), VidMap achieved W-AUCs from 91.4% (10m) to 80.3% (full), significantly outperforming other methods (e.g., DA3-Long at 79.5% to 78.3%, or ViPE at 59.5% to 71.0%).
 - Qualitative comparisons (Fig. 6, 13, 15) showed VidMap reconstructing scenes without drift, while others suffered from drift or tracking loss.

#### 4.2. Performance on Established Benchmarks (ETH3D-SLAM and EuRoC)

- **ETH3D-SLAM (Table 3):**
 - VidMap outperformed all baselines in both calibrated and uncalibrated settings, especially at stricter error thresholds (e.g., 5cm and 10cm).
 - In the uncalibrated setting, VidMap achieved AUCs of 51.4% (5cm) and 68.3% (10cm), notably higher than the next best, MegaSaM (38.4% and 53.9%).
 - In the calibrated setting, VidMap (69.4% and 79.2%) still slightly outperformed DPV-SLAM (62.3% and 72.5%).
- **EuRoC (Table 3):**
 - On EuRoC, which features grayscale fisheye cameras and motion blur, VidMap's performance was competitive with the best baselines, particularly in the calibrated setting (23.0% at 5cm, 54.6% at 10cm, comparable to DPV-SLAM and DROID-W).
 - In the uncalibrated setting, VidMap showed a significant advantage (11.5% at 5cm, 31.6% at 10cm), highlighting the strength of its calibration estimation. The larger gap between calibrated and uncalibrated results for EuRoC suggested that calibration remains a primary bottleneck for precision in this challenging dataset.

#### 4.3. Detailed Analysis

- **Ablation Study (Table 4, Fig. 8):**
 - **Depth Priors:** Removing depth priors in either Global Positioning (GP) or Bundle Adjustment (BA) led to significant drops in accuracy, particularly over longer horizons on LaMAR. This confirms their critical role in preventing degenerate estimates under complex motion. "No scale optimization" (fixing monocular metric scale) and "No metric scale" (discarding the metric prior) also impaired robustness. VidMap's use of metric monocular depth as a soft prior with per-image scale optimization was shown to be effective.
 - **Temporal Structure:** Removing loop closure edges resulted in large drift, especially on ETH3D-SLAM. Disregarding provenance (treating sequential and loop closure edges equally) led to catastrophic failure on LaMAR due to visual aliasing. This highlights the importance of provenance-dependent robust losses.
- **Runtime (Fig. 7):**
 - VidMap demonstrated efficient handling of long videos, with runtime scaling linearly with keyframes and practical memory usage.
 - It was found to be comparably efficient to GLOMAP and substantially faster than COLMAP.
 - Motion-aware keyframing effectively balances speed and accuracy, retaining accuracy while reducing computational cost compared to denser keyframing.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Results (Fig. 9, 10, 11, 12)

Qualitative visual comparisons consistently illustrated VidMap's ability to reconstruct trajectories and 3D scenes accurately without drift, even in the presence of pure rotations, textureless regions, local symmetries, and difficult transitions, where other methods often failed or produced fragmented/drifted reconstructions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The VidMap framework represents a notable advancement in the field of 3D reconstruction from unconstrained video, offering a robust and accurate solution that addresses long-standing challenges in both SLAM and SfM paradigms. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key contributions:

1. **Enhanced Robustness and Accuracy for Unconstrained Videos:** VidMap demonstrates significantly higher robustness and accuracy compared to existing state-of-the-art SLAM and SfM systems, including those based on learned methods. This is particularly evident on challenging, real-world datasets featuring complex motions, visual symmetries, textureless environments, and uncalibrated cameras. This improved performance can unlock the potential of billions of unconstrained videos for downstream applications.

2. **Bridging the SLAM-SfM Divide:** By combining the temporal awareness and strong sequential constraints typically found in causal SLAM with the flexibility and global optimization capabilities of offline SfM, VidMap offers a hybrid paradigm. This approach effectively mitigates the limitations of each individual system (e.g., SLAM's drift, SfM's vulnerability to visual aliasing), suggesting a new direction for visual odometry and mapping research.

3. **Effective Integration of Modern CV Techniques:** The system effectively leverages recent advancements in wide-baseline dense image matching, reliable loop closure (with provenance-aware handling), and metric monocular depth priors. This integration enhances robustness in conditions where traditional geometric methods struggle (e.g., pure rotations, forward motion, textureless regions).

4. **Robust Uncalibrated Reconstruction:** The ability of VidMap to jointly estimate accurate camera intrinsics alongside poses and 3D structure, without requiring prior calibration, is a significant practical advantage. This feature makes it highly applicable to consumer-grade video data where calibration information is often unavailable or unreliable.

5. **Scalability and Efficiency:** Despite its global optimization nature, VidMap is designed for efficiency, demonstrating linear scaling with the number of keyframes and practical memory usage. This makes it suitable for processing long video sequences, which is crucial for large-scale scene mapping.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Large-Scale Training Data Generation:** The ability to reliably recover metric camera poses and calibration from any unconstrained video can facilitate the generation of vast amounts of high-quality training data for machine learning models in navigation, scene understanding, and other computer vision tasks. This could accelerate progress in autonomous systems and spatial AI.
- **Democratization of 3D Reconstruction:** By handling arbitrary, unconstrained videos from consumer devices, VidMap could democratize 3D reconstruction, enabling non-expert users to create accurate 3D models of environments from their personal video footage.
- **Augmented Reality (AR) and Virtual Reality (VR):** Accurate and robust camera tracking and 3D mapping are foundational for AR/VR applications. VidMap's advancements could lead to more stable and immersive AR/VR experiences, especially in diverse and dynamic real-world settings.
- **Robotics and Autonomous Systems:** Improved mapping and localization from visual streams are critical for robots operating in complex and unknown environments (e.g., disaster response, exploration). VidMap's robustness to out-of-distribution environments, as demonstrated on CroCoDL, is particularly relevant here.
- **Digital Twins and Generative Scene Modeling:** The creation of highly accurate 3D models from abundant video streams could serve as a basis for generating detailed digital twins of real-world environments and for advancing generative scene modeling research.

While the paper acknowledges limitations such as challenges with extreme forward motion, complex interactions between weak geometry and visual aliasing, and the inherent dependency on learned priors, VidMap represents a substantial step forward for monocular reconstruction, paving the way for converting the world's video content into precise spatial understanding.
