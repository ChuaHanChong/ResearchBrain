# The invariant extended Kalman filter as a stable observer

> [!summary] Summary
> A theoretical analysis establishes the local asymptotic stability of the Invariant Extended Kalman Filter (IEKF) as a deterministic non-linear observer for systems on Lie groups, addressing a long-standing challenge in non-linear state estimation. The IEKF demonstrates consistent convergence in simulations for mobile robotics and inertial navigation, outperforming the conventional Extended Kalman Filter which can diverge under certain conditions.

## Problem

- Designing robust and stable non-linear observers, as general and robust methods like the linear Kalman filter are absent for non-linear systems.
- The conventional Extended Kalman Filter (EKF) lacks theoretical guarantees for stability and optimality, often diverging with large initial errors or significant non-linearities due to its reliance on small error linear approximations.
- Proving EKF stability is challenging, often requiring strong, non-trivial assumptions about the Kalman covariance matrix or specific estimated trajectory behaviors that are difficult to verify in practice.

## Method

- Characterized a generalized class of continuous-time dynamical systems on matrix Lie groups for which the invariant estimation error (left or right) propagates autonomously, independent of the true state trajectory.
- Demonstrated that for this class of systems, the Lie logarithm of the invariant estimation error evolves according to an exact linear differential equation, even for arbitrarily large errors.
- Formulated the IEKF and derived its gain computation using standard Riccati equations based on this exact linear error model, enabling a rigorous stability analysis.

## Results

- Provided rigorous proof of local asymptotic stability for the IEKF around *any* system trajectory under standard and verifiable conditions (e.g., observability of the linearized system around the true state), offering theoretical guarantees previously lacking for non-linear Kalman-type filters.
- Demonstrated in non-holonomic car localization simulations that the IEKF exhibits superior robustness to large initial heading errors, maintaining rapid error convergence where the EKF's performance degrades significantly.
- Showcased in inertial navigation simulations that the IEKF reliably converges in challenging scenarios (e.g., tight process noise tuning) where the EKF diverges due to its gains decreasing too rapidly in the non-linear transitory phase, confirming IEKF's superior robustness and accuracy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The identification of a broader class of systems on Lie groups where invariant errors have autonomous dynamics, significantly expanding the applicability of invariant observer design beyond strictly left-invariant systems.
> - The discovery that the Lie logarithm of the invariant error in these systems exhibits *exact linear* dynamics, transforming a complex non-linear problem into an equivalent linear one for stability analysis.
> - The ability to leverage well-established stability theorems for linear, time-varying Kalman filters to prove the local asymptotic stability of the IEKF, bypassing the complexities and restrictive assumptions typically required for EKF stability analysis.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The design of non-linear observers, which estimate the internal state of a system based on observed outputs, presents a significant challenge in control theory and engineering. Unlike linear systems, for which general and robust observer design methods exist (e.g., the Kalman filter), a universal approach for non-linear systems remains elusive. A common strategy involves linearization techniques, such as the Extended Kalman Filter (EKF), which uses Kalman equations to stabilize a linearized version of the estimation error. However, the EKF, despite its widespread adoption, possesses inherent limitations. Its performance and stability heavily rely on the assumption that the estimation error remains sufficiently small for the linear approximation to be valid. When this assumption is violated, particularly due to large initial errors or significant non-linearities, the EKF can diverge. Proving local stability for the EKF is complex because the linearized error equation is typically time-varying and depends on the unknown true state. Existing EKF stability analyses often rely on strong, non-trivial assumptions about the Kalman covariance matrix, which are difficult to verify in practice.

This research paper situates itself within the evolving field of non-linear observer design on Lie groups, specifically building upon the theory of symmetry-preserving observers and invariant Kalman filtering. Lie groups provide a natural mathematical framework for representing states in various physical systems, such as attitude, position, and velocity, commonly encountered in robotics and navigation. A key feature of invariant observers for left-invariant systems on Lie groups is that the estimation error, when appropriately defined (e.g., as a left-invariant error), evolves autonomously, meaning its dynamics do not depend on the true state trajectory. This property simplifies observer design and analysis. Prior work in invariant Kalman filtering (IEKF) has demonstrated promising practical performance, particularly for attitude estimation, but lacked comprehensive theoretical guarantees for its stability as a deterministic non-linear observer. This paper aims to bridge this gap by extending the theoretical foundation of IEKF, providing formal stability proofs under conditions analogous to those for linear systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to rigorously analyze the convergence properties of the Invariant Extended Kalman Filter (IEKF) when deployed as a deterministic non-linear observer for continuous-time systems with discrete observations on Lie groups. This analysis aims to establish theoretical guarantees for the IEKF's stability, a property that the standard EKF typically lacks in non-linear contexts.

The motivation for this work stems from several critical limitations of the conventional Extended Kalman Filter (EKF):

- **Lack of Theoretical Guarantees**: Despite its popularity and practical utility, the EKF does not possess guaranteed optimality or widespread convergence properties for non-linear systems. Its performance is often "aleatory," meaning it is not uniformly reliable across different applications or operating conditions.
- **Dependence on Small Error Assumption**: The EKF's core mechanism relies on linearizing the system dynamics around the current state estimate. This linearization is valid only when the estimation error is sufficiently small. If the estimate deviates significantly from the true state, the linearized model becomes inaccurate, leading to an unadapted Kalman gain that can amplify the error and cause filter divergence.
- **Challenging Stability Proofs**: Proving the local stability of the EKF in a non-linear setting is challenging because the linearized error dynamics are time-varying and depend on the unknown true state. Existing stability results for EKF often necessitate strong assumptions, such as the eigenvalues of the Kalman covariance matrix being bounded, which are difficult to verify a priori or maintain during operation.
- **Empirical Performance of IEKF**: Previous studies and practical implementations have indicated that the IEKF exhibits good behavior and superior performance compared to the EKF in specific applications, particularly in attitude estimation. However, this observed robustness lacked a comprehensive theoretical explanation or formal convergence proofs.

To address these motivations, the paper sets forth several specific objectives:

- **Generalize Autonomous Error Property**: To characterize a broader class of systems on Lie groups, beyond strictly left-invariant systems, for which the left- or right-invariant estimation error equation is autonomous (i.e., its dynamics depend only on the error itself, not the true state trajectory). This generalization aims to expand the applicability of invariant observer theory.
- **Discover Log-Linear Error Dynamics**: To demonstrate that for this newly characterized class of systems, a suitably defined non-linear function of the estimation error—specifically, its Lie logarithm—obeys an *exact linear* differential equation. This "log-linear" property is crucial as it transforms a complex non-linear problem into an equivalent linear one, significantly simplifying stability analysis.
- **Prove IEKF Local Stability**: To leverage the log-linear error property to prove the local asymptotic stability of the IEKF around *any* system trajectory. The goal is to show that the IEKF can achieve this under standard, verifiable conditions, similar to those established for linear Kalman filters (e.g., observability of the linearized system around the true state), without the demanding assumptions typically required for EKF stability proofs.
- **Illustrate and Validate with Examples**: To demonstrate the practical utility and robustness of the IEKF through relevant engineering examples, specifically a mobile robotics scenario (non-holonomic car localization) and an inertial navigation problem (UAV state estimation). Simulations are used to compare the IEKF's performance against the conventional EKF, especially in challenging scenarios where the EKF might diverge.

By achieving these objectives, the research aims to provide a robust theoretical foundation for the IEKF, positioning it as a reliable and stable non-linear observer for a significant class of dynamical systems on Lie groups, thereby advancing the field of non-linear control and state estimation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this paper involves a theoretical development of invariant observer properties on Lie groups, a detailed formulation of the IEKF, and a rigorous stability analysis, supported by practical examples and simulations.

#### 3.1. Characterization of Systems with Autonomous Error Propagation

The paper begins by considering dynamical systems whose states evolve on a matrix Lie group $G$. The core idea of invariant observers is that the estimation error (defined as a left- or right-invariant quantity, e.g., $\eta_L = \chi_t^{-1} \bar{\chi}_t$) can evolve autonomously.
- **Definition of Invariant Errors**: Left-invariant error $\eta_L = \chi_t^{-1} \bar{\chi}_t$ and right-invariant error $\eta_R = \bar{\chi}_t \chi_t^{-1}$ are introduced to quantify the discrepancy between two trajectories $\chi_t$ and $\bar{\chi}_t$.
- **Equivalence Condition (Theorem 1)**: The paper establishes that the following three conditions are equivalent: (i) the left-invariant error has state-trajectory independent propagation, (ii) the right-invariant error has state-trajectory independent propagation, and (iii) the system dynamics $f_u(\chi_t)$ satisfy the specific property: $f_u(ab) = f_u(a)b + a f_u(b) - a f_u(Id)b$ for all group elements $a, b \in G$. This condition characterizes a broader class of systems than solely left-invariant systems, providing explicit forms for the autonomous error dynamics $\dot{\eta} = g_u(\eta)$.

#### 3.2. Log-Linear Property of Error Propagation (Theorem 2)

A significant theoretical contribution is the "log-linear property" for systems satisfying the conditions of Theorem 1.
- **Lie Logarithm**: The concept of mapping elements of the Lie group $G$ to its Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$ (which is a vector space) via the Lie logarithm is central. The error $\eta$ is expressed as $\eta = \exp(\xi)$, where $\xi \in \mathbb{R}^{\text{dim } \mathfrak{g}}$ is a vector representation of the error.
- **Linear Error Dynamics**: The paper proves that for the identified class of systems, the Lie logarithm of the invariant error, $\xi_t$, evolves according to an *exact linear* differential equation: $\dot{\xi}_t = A_u(t) \xi_t$. This holds even for arbitrarily large errors, a substantial departure from the EKF where linearization is only valid for small errors. The matrix $A_u(t)$ is derived from the first-order approximation of $g_u(\exp(\xi))$.

#### 3.3. IEKF Formulation and Gain Tuning

The Invariant Extended Kalman Filter (IEKF) is formulated for this class of systems, considering continuous-time dynamics and discrete observations.
- **System Model**: The underlying system is defined by $\dot{\chi}_t = f_{u_t}(\chi_t)$ with observations of the form $Y_n = \chi_{t_n} d_i$ (left-invariant) or $Y_n = \chi_{t_n}^{-1} d_i$ (right-invariant), where $d_i$ are known vectors.
- **IEKF Structure**: The IEKF consists of a continuous propagation step and a discrete update step. The propagation of the state estimate $\hat{\chi}_t$ follows the system dynamics. The update uses a gain $L_n$ to correct the estimate based on the innovation, which is formed by the difference between the actual measurement and the predicted measurement, expressed in an invariant form.
- **"Noisy" System and Linearization**: To tune the IEKF gains, the authors introduce an "associated noisy system." This involves adding multiplicative process noise (on the Lie group) and additive measurement noise. The dynamics of the invariant estimation error (represented by its Lie logarithm $\xi_t$) are then linearized. The crucial aspect here is that the *exact* linear error propagation of $\xi_t$ during the propagation step (due to Theorem 2) is used, simplifying the analysis significantly compared to the EKF.
- **Riccati Equation for Gain Computation**: The linearized "noisy" error dynamics and measurements are used to compute the Kalman gain $L_n$ and the covariance matrix $P_t$ via standard Riccati equations, similar to a linear Kalman filter. This allows the user to tune the IEKF using interpretable process noise ($\hat{Q}_t$) and measurement noise ($\hat{N}_n$) covariance matrices.

#### 3.4. Stability Analysis (Theorem 4)

The paper's main theoretical achievement is the proof of the IEKF's local asymptotic stability.
- **Definition of Stability**: Asymptotic stability is defined as the ability of the observer to converge to the true state trajectory when initialized within a certain radius.
- **Leveraging Linear Results**: The proof leverages the log-linear property of the error, which allows the use of established stability conditions for linear, time-varying Kalman filters (specifically, the Deyst and Price Theorem, 1968). This theorem provides sufficient conditions (related to controllability and observability of the linear system, and bounds on noise covariances) for the Kalman filter to be an asymptotically stable observer.
- **Key Insight**: The fundamental difference from EKF stability analyses is that the IEKF's error propagation (in terms of $\xi_t$) is *exactly* linear, eliminating higher-order terms that complicate EKF analysis. The Riccati equation for the IEKF depends on the estimate only through the noise covariance matrices, which are shown to affect stability in a minor, bounded way. This enables the application of linear stability theorems to the linearized model around the *true* state, which is a stronger and more verifiable condition than requiring the estimated trajectory to be well-behaved.

#### 3.5. Illustrative Examples

Two practical engineering examples are presented to demonstrate the applicability and performance of the IEKF:
- **Simplified Car Example**: A 2D non-holonomic car estimating its position and orientation using odometer and GPS/landmark measurements. The system is embedded in the SE(2) Lie group. IEKF equations are derived, and local stability is proven under specific conditions (e.g., sufficient displacement for GPS, two distinct observed points for landmarks). Simulations compare IEKF and EKF, showing IEKF's superior robustness to large initial errors.
- **Inertial Navigation on Flat Earth**: A UAV estimating attitude, velocity, and position using accelerometers, gyrometers, and relative observations of known landmarks. This system is embedded in the SE2(3) Lie group, a "double homogeneous" matrix group. The paper explicitly shows that this system fits the generalized framework for autonomous error equations. Stability is proven if three non-collinear points are observed. Simulations highlight EKF's divergence under tight process noise tuning (common in high-precision inertial navigation) while IEKF maintains convergence and accuracy.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents several key findings and results, advancing the theoretical understanding and practical applicability of the Invariant Extended Kalman Filter (IEKF).

- **Generalized Class of Systems with Autonomous Error Evolution (Theorem 1)**: The paper identifies and characterizes a broad class of continuous-time dynamical systems on matrix Lie groups for which the left- or right-invariant estimation error propagates autonomously. This class is defined by a specific condition on the system dynamics, $f_u(ab) = f_u(a)b + a f_u(b) - a f_u(Id)b$. This finding is significant because it extends the applicability of invariant observer theory beyond the traditionally assumed left-invariant systems, encompassing a wider range of physical systems (e.g., the full inertial navigation problem).
- **Log-Linear Property of the Invariant Error (Theorem 2, Proposition 2)**: For the newly characterized class of systems, the paper demonstrates a fundamental and surprising property: the Lie logarithm of the invariant estimation error (denoted $\xi_t$) evolves according to an *exact linear* differential equation, $\dot{\xi}_t = A_u(t) \xi_t$. This linearity holds true even for arbitrarily large errors, a stark contrast to the standard EKF where linear approximations are valid only for small errors. This discovery simplifies the stability analysis of non-linear observers, transforming it into an equivalent linear problem.
- **IEKF Local Asymptotic Stability Guarantees (Theorem 4, Theorem 5)**: Leveraging the log-linear error property, the paper provides a rigorous proof that the IEKF, when used as a deterministic non-linear observer for this class of systems, is locally asymptotically stable around *any* system trajectory. This stability is achieved under standard and verifiable conditions, identical to those required for the stability of a linear Kalman filter (e.g., observability/reachability of the linearized system around the *true* state). This is a critical result, as it offers theoretical guarantees that the standard EKF typically lacks, where stability often depends on highly non-trivial assumptions about the estimated trajectory. The convergence radius is also shown to be trajectory-independent.
- **Practical Validation in Mobile Robotics (Proposition 3, Proposition 4)**:
 - For a non-holonomic car model on the SE(2) Lie group, the IEKF is shown to be an asymptotically stable observer for both GPS-like position measurements (under sufficient displacement) and range-and-bearing landmark measurements (if at least two distinct points are observed).
 - Simulations indicate that the IEKF consistently outperforms the EKF, particularly when confronted with large initial heading errors, where the EKF's performance degrades significantly, while the IEKF maintains rapid error convergence.
- **Application and Validation in Inertial Navigation (Proposition 5, Theorem 6)**:
 - The paper demonstrates that the complex problem of 3D attitude, velocity, and position estimation from inertial sensors and landmark measurements (a full navigation problem) fits within the generalized framework, despite the system not being strictly left- or right-invariant.
 - The IEKF for this system, operating on the SE$_2$(3) Lie group, is proven to be an asymptotically stable observer under the condition that three non-collinear points are observed.
 - Simulations highlight a critical robustness issue with the EKF: it can diverge in challenging scenarios (e.g., when process noise covariance is set very low, common in high-precision inertial navigation) because its gains decrease too rapidly during the non-linear transitory phase. In contrast, the IEKF with identical tuning reliably converges to the true state, demonstrating its superior robustness and reliability in situations where the EKF fails. Even with "robust tuning" (inflating EKF's process noise), the IEKF still exhibits faster convergence.

In summary, the paper's main findings include a new characterization of systems amenable to invariant observer design, the discovery of an exact log-linear error dynamic for these systems, and the subsequent proof of guaranteed local asymptotic stability for the IEKF. These theoretical advances are validated through compelling simulations in relevant robotics and navigation applications, showcasing the IEKF's superior performance and robustness over the EKF.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research holds significant theoretical and practical implications for the field of non-linear state estimation and control, particularly for systems evolving on Lie groups.

**Theoretical Significance:**
- **Rigorous Stability Guarantees**: The paper provides a rigorous theoretical foundation for the stability of the Invariant Extended Kalman Filter (IEKF), a property that has been largely absent for non-linear Kalman-type filters. By proving local asymptotic stability for the IEKF under standard linear conditions (observability/reachability of the linearized system around the *true* state), it addresses a long-standing challenge in non-linear observer design.
- **Generalized Applicability of Invariant Observers**: The characterization of a broader class of systems (beyond strict left-invariant systems) for which invariant errors exhibit autonomous dynamics significantly expands the theoretical reach of invariant observer design. This allows more complex, real-world systems, such as full inertial navigation, to be rigorously analyzed within this framework.
- **Discovery of Log-Linear Error Dynamics**: The identification of the "log-linear" property – where the Lie logarithm of the non-linear error evolves according to an *exact linear* differential equation – is a fundamental theoretical contribution. This transforms a challenging non-linear problem into a solvable linear one, simplifying analysis and paving the way for new observer design techniques for this class of systems.

**Practical Impact:**
- **Enhanced Reliability and Robustness**: For critical applications like mobile robotics and aerial/space navigation, the IEKF offers a demonstrably more reliable and robust alternative to the conventional EKF. The simulations clearly illustrate that the EKF can diverge in challenging scenarios (e.g., large initial errors, specific noise tunings common in high-precision systems), while the IEKF consistently converges due to its appropriate handling of system non-linearities and its guaranteed stability.
- **Improved Design Confidence**: Engineers can design and implement IEKFs with greater confidence, knowing that their local convergence properties are theoretically guaranteed under verifiable conditions. This contrasts with the EKF, where performance is often achieved through extensive, application-specific tuning and robustness checks, sometimes involving artificial inflation of noise parameters that diminish the filter's interpretability.
- **Efficient Tuning**: The IEKF maintains a similar tuning methodology and computational load to the EKF, making its adoption practical for engineers already familiar with Kalman filtering principles. The interpretability of the covariance matrices as sensor noise characteristics is preserved, unlike in "robustly tuned" EKFs.
- **Broader Application Scope**: The explicit demonstrations for a non-holonomic car and a full 3D inertial navigation system (attitude, velocity, position) highlight the IEKF's applicability to complex, high-dimensional state estimation problems. This could lead to more accurate and dependable navigation systems for unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs), autonomous ground vehicles, and other robotic platforms.
- **Foundation for Future Research**: The theoretical insights, particularly the log-linear error property, provide a foundation for further research into stability analysis of other non-linear observers, adaptive control on Lie groups, and even the development of new optimal filters for this class of non-linear systems.

In conclusion, this paper significantly advances the theory of non-linear observers by providing robust stability guarantees for the IEKF. Its findings position the IEKF as a theoretically sound and practically superior alternative to the EKF for a broad and important class of systems on Lie groups, with the potential to enhance the reliability and performance of state estimation in numerous engineering applications.
