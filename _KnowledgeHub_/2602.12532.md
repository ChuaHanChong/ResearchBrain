# CRAFT: Adapting VLA Models to Contact-rich Manipulation via Force-aware Curriculum Fine-tuning

> [!summary] Summary
> CRAFT is a lightweight, model-agnostic fine-tuning framework that adapts pretrained VLA models to contact-rich manipulation. It inserts a variational information bottleneck after the vision-language encoder, using an exponentially annealed KL penalty so the policy first learns from low-entropy joint-torque proprioception, then reintegrates perception. On five real-world tasks it lifts average success by 35.36 points on pi-0 and 25.66 on RDT.

## Problem

- VLA models rely on high-entropy vision and language inputs, which cannot capture subtle contact interactions needed for precise alignment and stable force regulation.
- Most VLA policies lack explicit force or tactile feedback, missing low-entropy signals revealing slip or resistance, producing unstable, imprecise, or unsafe contact behavior.
- Prior force-aware policies add tactile inputs or force experts but lack a unified mechanism to rebalance modality entropy, so force signals stay underutilized.

## Method

- ==Variational information bottleneck== placed after the vision-language encoder compresses visual and language embeddings into Gaussian latents, penalized by ==KL divergence== against a standard normal prior.
- **CRAFT**'s ==force-aware curriculum fine-tuning== anneals the VIB weight by ==exponential decay==, forcing early reliance on torque before progressively restoring full multimodal information flow.
- Homologous leader-follower teleoperation rig, built from low-cost force-controlled servos and an ==impedance-controlled== Franka arm, records synchronized dual-camera video, joint torques, and actions without extra sensors.

## Results

- Average success across five contact-rich tasks rises from **25.32%** to **60.68%** for pi-0 and from **22.66%** to **48.32%** for RDT, beating the FACTR baseline.
- On out-of-distribution object and task variants, average success improves from **22.50%** to **58.75%**, with rolling plasticine color variants jumping from **10%** to **60%**.
- Ablation on three tasks: base **20.0%**, adding VIB alone **46.7%**, adding torque proprioception with VIB **56.7%**, confirming both components contribute.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - When modalities differ sharply in entropy, compressing the dominant one during early training is an effective way to force a policy to exploit weaker signals.
> - Joint torque under impedance control encodes tracking error, inertial discrepancy, and external contact, so it is a richer proprioceptive input than joint position.
> - Information-bottleneck curricula are architecture-agnostic plug-ins, transferring across flow-based and diffusion-based policies without touching pretrained encoders.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of large-scale foundation models, robotic control, and multimodal learning, specifically addressing the limitations of current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models in contact-rich manipulation tasks.

The emergence of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and Large Language Models (LLMs) has revolutionized AI, extending their powerful capabilities in perception, reasoning, and grounding to robotics. This has given rise to the VLA paradigm, which integrates these pre-trained visual and language representations with robot learning systems to enable robots to execute general instructions. Pioneering works like RT-1/RT-2, SayCan, and OpenVLA have demonstrated impressive zero-shot generalization capabilities, allowing robots to adapt to novel objects, instructions, and environments. More advanced approaches such as RDT and $\pi^0$ further highlight the potential of unifying large-scale multimodal pre-training with scalable robot control across diverse tasks and architectures.

However, despite these significant advancements, a critical bottleneck remains: **contact-rich manipulation**. Tasks requiring precise insertions, deformable object handling, or continuous surface wiping demand fine alignment, stable contact maintenance, and dynamic force regulation. Current VLA models predominantly rely on high-entropy visual and language inputs, which are often insufficient for capturing the subtle physical interactions at the point of contact. The lack of explicit force or tactile feedback, which provides low-entropy but task-critical signals about resistance or slip, leads to unstable, imprecise, or unsafe behaviors in these contact-rich scenarios.

Prior works have attempted to bridge this gap:
- **Reactive Diffusion Policy (RDP)** uses a slow-fast visual-tactile policy learning scheme.
- **VLA-Touch** integrates dual-level tactile feedback to complement visual and language inputs.
- **ForceVLA** employs a force-aware mixture-of-experts architecture.
- **FACTR** introduces a curriculum learning strategy that heuristically corrupts visual inputs to prioritize force signals.

While these methods represent progress, they are often limited by the fundamental challenge of **modality entropy imbalance** – high-entropy visual and language signals tend to overshadow the critical, but low-entropy, force signals. This imbalance prevents models from robustly leveraging force information. CRAFT distinguishes itself by offering a theoretically grounded mechanism, the Variational Information Bottleneck (VIB), to explicitly regulate multimodal information flow, rather than relying on heuristic input degradation. By doing so, CRAFT addresses the core challenge of balancing multimodal contributions systematically, ensuring that force features are robustly integrated into the latent space before comprehensive perceptual data is reintroduced. This positions CRAFT as a significant advancement towards building scalable, generalizable, and truly force-aware VLA models for complex real-world manipulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **enhance existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models to effectively perform contact-rich manipulation tasks** that require precise alignment, stable contact maintenance, and adaptive force handling.

The motivation stems from two critical limitations of current VLA models:
1. **Over-reliance on high-entropy perception:** Traditional VLA models, trained primarily on visual and linguistic data, tend to over-rely on these high-entropy inputs. While useful for general scene understanding, these modalities struggle to provide the fine-grained information necessary for subtle physical interactions in contact-rich scenarios (e.g., detecting precise contact points or small resistances). This leads to failures in tasks requiring high precision and compliance.
2. **Underutilization of critical force signals:** Force or tactile feedback offers low-entropy, direct, and unambiguous information about physical contact and interaction. However, due to the sheer volume and complexity of visual and language data, VLA models often relegate force signals to a secondary role or ignore them entirely. This "modality entropy imbalance" prevents the models from developing stable and force-aware control strategies.

To address these challenges, the authors propose **CRAFT (Contact-rich manipulation via Force-Aware Curriculum Fine-tuning)**. The core motivation is to develop a lightweight, model-agnostic, and principled framework that can:
- **Systematically balance multimodal information:** Specifically, to prevent high-entropy vision and language from overshadowing low-entropy, but critical, force signals.
- **Encourage early prioritization of force signals:** To enable the model to first learn robust, contact-aware behaviors.
- **Progressively integrate full multimodal information:** To ensure that rich perceptual capabilities are not lost once force-aware behaviors are established.
- **Facilitate scalable and generalizable force-aware VLA learning:** By providing a unified mechanism that can be applied to diverse pre-trained VLA architectures.

Ultimately, the goal is to enable robots to perform complex real-world tasks like delicate insertions, precise surface wiping, and careful handling of deformable objects with the robustness and adaptability currently lacking in general VLA models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

CRAFT introduces a novel force-aware curriculum fine-tuning framework that strategically leverages a variational information bottleneck (VIB) to address the modality entropy imbalance in VLA models for contact-rich manipulation.

**A. Homologous Leader–Follower Teleoperation System for Data Collection**
- **System Design:** The authors developed a teleoperation system inspired by HACTS and FACTR. It uses an off-the-shelf Franka Emika Panda robot as the "follower" arm, without requiring additional sensors. A "leader" arm, built from low-cost servo motors with built-in force control, is operated by a human.
- **Force Feedback:** Real-time contact forces measured at the follower arm are fed back to the leader, allowing the human operator to naturally perceive interaction forces and provide high-quality demonstrations. This bidirectional synchronization ensures accurate and reliable force data collection.
- **Robot Control:** The follower robot operates under impedance control, which modulates compliance during physical interaction, ensuring safety and stable contact.
- **Force Modality:** Crucially, **joint torques** are used as the force modality. Unlike joint positions, torques inherently capture richer force-aware information, including task-level deviations, dynamic discrepancies, and external interaction forces ($\tau_{ext}$), making them a more informative proprioceptive input.
- **Visual Observations:** Two synchronized cameras provide visual data: a wrist-mounted camera for first-person views and a static third-person camera for environmental context.
- **Data Synchronization:** All sensory modalities (multi-view images, proprioceptive states, joint torques, and robot actions) are temporally synchronized, forming a comprehensive multimodal dataset for force-aware policy learning.

**B. Force-aware Curriculum Fine-tuning**
The core of CRAFT lies in its curriculum fine-tuning strategy, which mitigates the tendency of VLA models to over-rely on high-entropy visual and language signals.

- **Variational Information Bottleneck (VIB):**
 - **Placement:** A VIB module is inserted immediately after the vision and language encoders.
 - **Purpose:** The VIB module's role is to temporarily restrict the information flow from high-entropy visual ($F_V$) and language ($F_L$) embeddings by compressing them into compact latent features ($F_c$).
 - **Mechanism:** It achieves this by minimizing the mutual information between the original and compact features, formulated as a Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence loss ($L_{VIB}$) against a standard normal prior. This encourages the VIB to discard irrelevant or redundant information while retaining task-relevant aspects.
 - **Reparameterization Trick:** During fine-tuning, compact features are sampled via the reparameterization trick, allowing gradients to flow through the stochastic sampling process.
- **Force as Proprioception (Joint Torques):**
 - CRAFT explicitly uses joint torques ($\tau$) rather than standard joint positions ($q$) as the proprioceptive input.
 - **Rationale:** As detailed in the method, joint torque in an impedance-controlled robot intrinsically encodes more information:
 - Current kinematic state ($q, \dot{q}, \ddot{q}$).
 - Deviations from desired trajectories ($q_d - q$).
 - Dynamic discrepancies (inertial effects).
 - Crucially, **external interaction forces ($\tau_{ext}$)**.
 - This makes torque a richer, low-entropy, and more task-relevant signal for contact-rich manipulation compared to positions alone, which primarily capture internal robot state.
- **VIB Weight Schedule (Curriculum Learning):**
 - To implement the "force-first-then-multimodal" strategy, CRAFT employs a dynamic weight schedule for the VIB regularization term ($\lambda_{VIB}(t)$) in the total loss ($L_{total} = L_{task} + \lambda_{VIB}(t) \cdot L_{VIB}$).
 - **Early Training:** Initially, $\lambda_{VIB}$ is set high ($\lambda_{init}$). This imposes a strong penalty on the information content of visual and language features, forcing the policy to prioritize and learn from the low-entropy joint torque signals. This enables the acquisition of stable, contact-aware behaviors.
 - **Progressive Relaxation:** As training progresses, $\lambda_{VIB}(t)$ gradually decreases following an exponential decay schedule ($\lambda_{init} \cdot \exp(-t/T_{decay})$). This relaxation slowly "releases the bottleneck," allowing the model to reintegrate richer visual and linguistic information, which complements the already learned force-sensitive representations.

**C. Implementation**
- CRAFT is designed as a lightweight, plug-in fine-tuning module, making it model-agnostic.
- It was implemented and validated on two representative VLA models: the flow-based $\pi^0$ and the diffusion-based RDT.
- The VIB module is added after the pre-trained vision-language encoder, and joint torques are introduced as proprioceptive inputs. This allows seamless integration without modifying the base VLA architecture.
- The framework is generalized such that the VIB can apply to any low-entropy, task-critical modality, beyond just joint torques (e.g., tactile feedback).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted across five challenging real-world contact-rich tasks demonstrate the effectiveness, generalizability, and robustness of the CRAFT framework.

**A. Main Results (Enhanced Performance)**
- **Consistent Performance Improvement:** CRAFT significantly enhances the performance of VLA models in contact-rich manipulation.
 - Integrating CRAFT with the $\pi^0$-base model yielded an average increase of **35.36%** in task success rates across five tasks. For example, in the "Wipe Whiteboard" task, success improved remarkably from 33.3% to 66.7%.
 - RDT + CRAFT outperformed baseline RDT, achieving an average improvement of **25.66%** points. Notably, "Rolling Plasticine" saw a **50%** gain (from 8.3% to 58.3%).
- **Superior to Baselines:** Both CRAFT-enhanced models (especially $\pi^0$-base + CRAFT) consistently achieved higher success rates compared to baseline VLA models ($\pi^0$-base, RDT) and even a force-aware baseline (FACTR).
- **Enabling Force-aware Control:** Qualitative analysis (Fig. 5) illustrated that baseline VLA models often misalign and fail in tasks like USB insertion, whereas CRAFT-enhanced models continuously adjust their motion based on real-time contact force signals, leading to successful execution. Similarly, in "Flip Carton," CRAFT effectively uses force signals to guide actions for stable manipulation, preventing failure observed in the baseline.
- **Broad Applicability:** CRAFT is demonstrated to be compatible with different VLA architectures (flow-based $\pi^0$ and diffusion-based RDT). This highlights its lightweight, plug-and-play design and its ability to enhance force awareness without requiring fundamental architectural changes to the base VLA model.

**B. Generalization Studies (Robustness to OOD Settings)**
- CRAFT-enhanced VLA models exhibited stronger generalization capabilities to unseen objects and novel task variations, which are critical for real-world deployment.
- **Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Variants:** The evaluation included:
 - **Object-level:** Flipping different cartons, rolling plasticine of different colors.
 - **Task-level:** USB insertion with varied socket placement height and visual changes, whiteboard wiping at different angles.
- **Significant Generalization Gains:** Across these OOD settings, CRAFT consistently improved success rates, achieving an average increase from 22.50% (without CRAFT) to **58.75%** (with CRAFT), a remarkable **36.25%** improvement.
 - For "USB Insertion" (Task-level variant), success improved from 1/10 (10%) to 4/10 (40%).
 - "Flip Carton" (Object-level variant) saw an increase from 10/20 (50%) to 15/20 (75%).
 - "Wipe Whiteboard" (Task-level variant) improved from 2/10 (20%) to 6/10 (60%).
 - "Rolling Plasticine" (Object-level variant) demonstrated a substantial jump from 2/20 (10%) to 12/20 (60%).
- These results confirm that the force-aware curriculum and torque-centered representations enable the model to focus on critical external torques and better understand task-relevant visual/language cues, enhancing robustness.

**C. Ablation Studies (Component Contribution)**
- Ablation experiments on the $\pi^0$-base model focused on three representative tasks (USB Insertion, Wipe Whiteboard, Rolling Plasticine) demonstrated the individual contributions of CRAFT's components:
 - **"+ VIB" (VIB with joint positions as proprioception):** Incorporating the VIB layer alone significantly improved average success rates from 20.0% (w/o CRAFT) to **46.7%**. This validates the benefit of constraining high-entropy visual and language embeddings to force the model to rely more on proprioceptive information.
 - **"+ VIB & Force" (Full CRAFT: VIB with joint torques as proprioception):** Combining VIB with joint torques as proprioception further boosted the average success rate to **56.7%**. This confirms the critical role of using joint torques, which provide richer, task-relevant force feedback, for robust contact-rich manipulation.

In summary, the findings robustly support CRAFT's ability to transform VLA models into highly capable agents for contact-rich manipulation, through its principled integration of force awareness via a curriculum-based information bottleneck.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The CRAFT framework represents a significant advancement in the field of robotic manipulation, particularly for tasks requiring physical interaction and precise force control. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enabling Robust Contact-Rich Manipulation:** This work directly addresses a critical weakness of current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which often struggle with tasks demanding precise contact, stable interaction, and deformable object manipulation. By effectively integrating and prioritizing force signals, CRAFT empowers VLA models to perform these challenging tasks with unprecedented success rates and stability. This is crucial for deploying robots in diverse real-world scenarios, from manufacturing and assembly to healthcare and domestic assistance.

2. **Principled Modality Balancing:** CRAFT introduces a theoretically grounded approach to overcome the "modality entropy imbalance" problem. By leveraging the Variational Information Bottleneck (VIB) with a curriculum fine-tuning strategy, the framework offers a systematic way to manage the flow of information from high-entropy (vision, language) and low-entropy but critical (force) modalities. This is a more robust and adaptive solution compared to previous heuristic methods for feature degradation, providing a clearer path for VLA models to learn multimodal representations effectively.

3. **Generalizability and Broad Applicability:** The design of CRAFT as a lightweight, model-agnostic, and plug-and-play fine-tuning module is highly impactful. Its demonstrated effectiveness across different VLA architectures (flow-based $\pi^0$ and diffusion-based RDT) highlights its versatility. This means that existing and future VLA models can readily adopt CRAFT to gain force-awareness without requiring extensive architectural redesigns or prohibitive retraining costs, thus accelerating the development of more capable general-purpose robotic agents.

4. **Improved Generalization to Unseen Scenarios:** The rigorous generalization studies, including unseen objects and novel task variations, demonstrate CRAFT's ability to enhance robustness in out-of-distribution settings. This is a vital characteristic for practical robotic systems, as they frequently encounter novel situations. By learning more fundamental, force-aware representations, CRAFT-enhanced models are less prone to failures when confronted with minor changes in objects or task contexts.

5. **Cost-Effective Data Collection:** The homologous leader-follower teleoperation system, built on off-the-shelf robot arms without additional specialized sensors, significantly reduces the barrier to collecting high-quality, synchronized multimodal data (vision, language, and force). This accessibility allows more researchers and practitioners to generate the necessary datasets for training force-aware policies, fostering further innovation in the field.

6. **Advancing Human-Robot Interaction and Safety:** The ability to precisely control forces and maintain stable contact inherently leads to safer and more natural human-robot interaction. Robots equipped with CRAFT could potentially perform delicate tasks alongside humans or in unstructured environments with reduced risk of damage to objects or injury to humans.

In essence, CRAFT provides a crucial piece of the puzzle for building truly general-purpose robots that can seamlessly transition between high-level semantic understanding and fine-grained physical interaction. Its impact extends to making VLA models more practical, robust, and capable for a wider range of real-world applications, paving the way for the next generation of intelligent robotic systems.
