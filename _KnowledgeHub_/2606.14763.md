# Bayesian Optimization for Learning Nonlinear MPC in Autonomous Agent Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> A map-free navigation framework for autonomous agents, particularly quadrupedal robots, integrates a LiDAR-based Gaussian occupancy representation with A* planning and a nonlinear Model Predictive Control (MPC) tracker. The system leverages Tree-structured Parzen Estimators (TPE) for Bayesian Optimization to automatically tune 11 MPC cost function parameters, achieving a 38.7% reduction in path length and a 53.0% reduction in time to goal across diverse simulated environments, with parameters successfully transferring to real hardware.

## Problem

- Autonomous navigation for mobile robots, especially legged platforms in dynamic, unknown, and cluttered environments, faces challenges like jerky execution and poor generalization due to decoupled planning and control.
- The real-time deployment of Model Predictive Control (MPC) is constrained by the computational efficiency of environment representation for obstacle avoidance and the labor-intensive, often suboptimal manual tuning of MPC cost function weights.
- Existing navigation approaches often require extensive pre-built maps or struggle with transferring learned policies from simulation to real hardware (sim-to-real gap).

## Method

- A hierarchical, modular navigation stack was developed, integrating a reactive, semi-persistent LiDAR-based Gaussian occupancy grid for local environment representation with an A* planner for local path generation.
- A nonlinear ==Model Predictive Control== (**MPC**) tracker, formulated with CasADi and IPOPT, generates dynamically feasible velocity setpoints using a smooth sigmoid obstacle barrier function for collision avoidance.
- An offline Bayesian Optimization module, based on Tree-structured Parzen Estimators (TPE), systematically tunes an 11-dimensional vector of MPC hyperparameters to optimize a composite performance score across multiple navigation scenarios.

## Results

- Bayesian Optimization (BO)-tuned parameters reduced the average path length by **38.7%**, average MPC solve time by **25.3%**, and average time to goal by **53.0%** across three simulated environments, improving the overall success rate from **65.0%** to **90.0%**.
- The BO-tuned configuration demonstrated strong generalization to an unseen, out-of-distribution warehouse environment, achieving a **44.5%** reduction in path length without prior exposure during the tuning phase.
- Hardware deployment on a Unitree Go2 quadruped robot in a real laboratory environment validated the sim-to-real transfer, with BO-tuned parameters improving the success rate from 5/10 to 8/10 without any additional on-robot tuning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Automating MPC parameter tuning with Bayesian Optimization using Tree-structured Parzen Estimators significantly improves navigation performance and robustness across diverse, unseen environments, outperforming manual tuning methods.
> - Parameters optimized in simulation for a complex nonlinear MPC system effectively transfer to real-world quadruped robots (Unitree Go2) without requiring additional on-hardware tuning, addressing the sim-to-real gap for navigation.
> - Control jerk and control effort parameters exhibit the highest sensitivity in the MPC formulation, indicating their primary role in solution stability, and Bayesian Optimization contributes to a smoother optimization landscape with reduced overall parameter sensitivity.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Autonomous navigation for mobile robots, particularly legged platforms operating in dynamic, unknown, and cluttered environments, presents a persistent challenge in robotics. Traditional navigation architectures often decouple global planning from low-level control. This decoupling can lead to issues such as jerky execution, violation of dynamic constraints, and poor generalization to novel scenarios due to a lack of integrated consideration for robot dynamics during planning.

Model Predictive Control (MPC) is an established framework that addresses these limitations by simultaneously optimizing path following and constraint satisfaction over a receding horizon. However, the real-time deployment of MPC is historically constrained by two primary factors:
1. **Computational efficiency of environment representation:** Generating obstacle-free trajectories requires an efficient and frequently updated environmental model.
2. **Parameter sensitivity and tuning:** The performance of MPC is critically dependent on a set of cost function weights that balance objectives such as tracking accuracy, control effort, and safety. Manual tuning of these parameters is labor-intensive, often suboptimal, and may not generalize well across different environments or tasks.

Within the context of legged robot navigation, various approaches exist, including deep learning-based terrain estimation combined with classical planning, or model-free reinforcement learning for end-to-end navigation. These often suffer from the need for extensive pre-built maps or difficulties in transferring learned policies from simulation to real hardware (sim-to-real gap). The proposed work adopts a high-level abstraction for leg dynamics, treating the robot as a holonomic mobile agent through an existing locomotion controller, which simplifies the navigation problem at the planning and control layers.

For local navigation, fast sampling-based planners like RRT* and its adaptive variants are common. This work utilizes a grid-based A* planner, but the framework is designed to be agnostic to the specific path planning algorithm.

In MPC for mobile robots, nonlinear formulations are used to capture complex kinematic models, requiring efficient Nonlinear Programming (NLP) solvers such as CasADi with IPOPT. Obstacle avoidance is typically implemented through hard distance constraints or smooth barrier functions, with differentiable sigmoid penalties being a common choice to maintain NLP tractability.

To address the challenge of MPC parameter tuning, Bayesian Optimization (BO) has emerged as a standard method for black-box hyperparameter optimization in robotics. Frameworks like COAt-MPC provide performance-guaranteed BO for MPC, and specific acquisition strategies like Tree-structured Parzen Estimators (TPE) are recognized for their efficiency in high-dimensional mixed search spaces. This research builds upon this trend by employing TPE-based Bayesian Optimization to automate the tuning of MPC parameters, aiming to overcome the limitations of manual tuning and improve generalization.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop and validate a robust, map-free navigation framework for autonomous agents, specifically quadrupedal robots, operating in dynamic and unknown environments. The framework aims to address two core limitations frequently encountered in the real-time deployment of Model Predictive Control (MPC) for such tasks:

1. **Computational efficiency of environment representation for obstacle avoidance:** Real-time navigation necessitates a fast and adaptive method to represent the immediate environment for collision prevention. Traditional mapping approaches can be computationally intensive or require prior environmental knowledge, which is often unavailable in unknown settings. The motivation here is to develop a reactive, local environment model that can be rapidly updated from sensor data.

2. **Robust and generalized tuning of MPC cost function weights:** The performance, stability, and safety of an MPC system are highly sensitive to its internal parameters, such as weights for tracking accuracy, control effort, and obstacle avoidance. Manual tuning is a laborious, iterative process that often yields suboptimal parameters specific to a particular environment or task, leading to poor generalization. The motivation is to automate this tuning process to find near-optimal parameter configurations that are robust and transferable across diverse environments, thereby reducing development time and improving overall system reliability.

To achieve these objectives, the work proposes several key contributions:
- **Development of a map-free, rolling-horizon navigation stack:** This involves integrating a LiDAR-based Gaussian occupancy representation with A* planning and a nonlinear MPC formulation. This setup allows for reactive planning in unknown environments without relying on global, persistent maps.
- **Implementation of a Bayesian Optimization framework for MPC parameter tuning:** The goal is to systematically identify optimal controller parameters by leveraging Tree-structured Parzen Estimators (TPE) to efficiently search a high-dimensional parameter space. This aims to overcome the limitations of manual tuning.
- **Validation of simulation-to-real transferability:** A crucial objective is to demonstrate that parameters optimized in simulation can effectively transfer to a physical robot without significant performance degradation or requiring further on-hardware tuning. This seeks to confirm the practical utility of the proposed offline optimization approach.
- **Analysis of parameter sensitivity:** By employing a Gaussian Process surrogate, the research aims to provide insights into the optimization landscape and understand which MPC parameters have the most significant impact on navigation performance, contributing to a deeper understanding of the system's behavior.

In summary, the motivation stems from the need for more autonomous, robust, and easily deployable navigation solutions for mobile robots, especially legged platforms, by addressing the challenges of real-time environmental awareness and critical parameter tuning in MPC-based control systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed navigation framework is a hierarchical, modular stack designed for general-purpose autonomous agent navigation. It consists of five interconnected modules that communicate via ROS 2 topics, enabling real-time operation.

#### 3.1. A. System Architecture

1. **Odometry Bridge:** This module converts raw Extended Kalman Filter (EKF) odometry data into a unified pose representation for the robot.
2. **Mapping Module:** Maintains two distinct representations:
 - **Reactive Semi-Persistent Gaussian Occupancy Grid:** A local, square grid (default 10x10 m, 0.25 m/cell resolution) centered on the robot, rebuilt from scratch at each replanning cycle using only the current LiDAR point cloud. Cell occupancy probability is derived from the minimum Euclidean distance to LiDAR returns using a standard normal Cumulative Distribution Function (CDF), with a Gaussian spread parameter (σ = 0.05 m) controlling obstacle inflation. Cells with occupancy probability ≥ 0.45 are treated as hard obstacles; others incur a soft traversal penalty.
 - **Topological Navigation Graph:** A persistent graph G=(V, E) where nodes represent semantically meaningful regions and edges denote navigable transitions. Edges are weighted by traversal costs estimated from the current occupancy grid. New nodes are added as the robot explores previously unvisited areas, and the graph is updated online.
3. **Global-to-Local Planning Layer:** Uses the Navigation Graph to provide high-level waypoints to a rolling-horizon A* planner. The A* planner operates on the local occupancy grid at 1 Hz. For distant goals, the local planning target is either the goal itself if it's within the grid, or a point on the grid boundary along the ray from the robot to the global goal. If the local target is an occupied cell, a Breadth-First Search (BFS) finds the nearest free cell as a proxy.
 - **A* Path Planning:** Executes on an 8-connected grid. The edge traversal cost `g(n → n')` incorporates a move cost (`c_move`) and a soft obstacle penalty weighted by the cell's occupancy probability (`w_obs * P(n')`). The heuristic `h(n)` is the Euclidean distance to the goal. Paths are resampled at uniform spacing (0.20 m) and smoothed with a 5-point moving-average kernel.
4. **Nonlinear MPC Tracker:** Formulated with CasADi and solved with IPOPT, this module generates dynamically feasible velocity setpoints. It converts MPC lookahead states into body-frame `/cmd_vel` commands for the CHAMP locomotion layer.
5. **Offline Bayesian Optimization Module:** Based on Tree-structured Parzen Estimator (TPE) and Gaussian Processes (GP), this module tunes the MPC hyperparameters offline to improve trajectory efficiency and tracking performance.

#### 3.2. B. MPC Formulation

1. **State, Control, and Dynamics:** The robot is modeled as a holonomic kinematic agent in a 2D plane with state `x_k = [p_x, p_y, ψ]^T` (position and heading) and control input `u_k = [v_x, v_y, ω]^T` (body-frame velocities). Discrete-time dynamics are approximated using Forward Euler integration with a step `Δt = 0.1 s`.
2. **Objective Function:** The MPC solves a finite-horizon Nonlinear Program (NLP) over `N = 50` steps (a 5-second horizon) at 10 Hz. The objective function `J` minimizes a weighted sum of:
 - Terminal tracking error (`e_N^T Q_T e_N`).
 - Per-step tracking error (`e_k^T Q e_k`) relative to the A*-derived reference trajectory.
 - Control effort (`u_k^T R u_k`).
 - Jerk regularization (`R_jerk ||u_k - u_{k-1}||^2`) to ensure smooth commands.
 - Smooth obstacle cost (`J_obs(x_k)`).
 The NLP is compiled once using CasADi's symbolic differentiation, and only numerical parameters are updated at runtime.
3. **Sigmoid Obstacle Barrier:** Obstacle avoidance is implemented as a smooth cost penalty `J_obs(x_k)` using a logistic sigmoid barrier function. It is evaluated based on the `M = 12` nearest LiDAR returns within a 3-meter radius. The function form `W / (1 + exp(α (d(x_k, p_j) - r)))` (with a numerically stable `tanh` variant) depends on the barrier height `W`, steepness `α`, and safety radius `r`. A small epsilon ensures differentiability at zero distance.
4. **Reference Trajectory and Warm Starting:** The reference trajectory `x_ref,k` is generated by advancing along the smoothed A* path at a constant cruise speed `v_ref`. Reference position is linearly interpolated, and reference yaw is derived from the path tangent. The previous IPOPT solution is shifted and used as a warm-start for the next iteration, improving computational efficiency.

#### 3.3. C. Bayesian Optimization for MPC Tuning

1. **Composite Performance Score:** The MPC parameter vector `θ` is 11-dimensional, including weights for position tracking (`Q_x`, `Q_y`), yaw tracking (`Q_ψ`), terminal cost (`Q_T`), control effort (`R_v_x`, `R_v_y`, `R_ω`), jerk regularization (`R_jerk`), obstacle cost (`W_obs`), and obstacle shaping parameters (`α_obs`, `r_obs`). The search space `Θ` is defined by predefined lower and upper bounds for each parameter.
 The composite score `J(θ)` is an aggregation of scores from multiple navigation scenarios (`s ∈ S`) with associated weights (`w_s`). Each scenario score `J_s` integrates five metrics:
 - **Goal Progress (ϕ):** Fraction of initial distance closed towards the goal.
 - **Path Efficiency (η):** Ratio of straight-line distance to total traversed arc length.
 - **Control Smoothness (s):** Exponentiated negative mean second-order finite difference of the command sequence (jerk proxy).
 - **Obstacle Avoidance (O):** Based on fractions of time spent in "danger" (<0.3m) and "warning" (0.3-0.6m) zones from LiDAR returns, and mean clearance.
 - **Time Efficiency (τ):** Ratio of reference time to elapsed time (only if goal reached).
 Two scoring branches are used: one for successful goal attainment and another for failed attempts (prioritizing goal progress and obstacle avoidance).
2. **Tree-Structured Parzen Estimator (TPE):** This offline Bayesian Optimization algorithm is used for its efficiency in high-dimensional mixed parameter spaces. TPE partitions observed scores at a γ = 0.15 quantile threshold. It models two marginal densities: `ℓ(θ)` for parameters yielding scores below the threshold and `g(θ)` for scores above. Each density is a mixture of truncated Gaussians. The acquisition function is the Expected Improvement ratio `EI(θ) ∝ ℓ(θ) / g(θ)`. The process starts with `N_rand = 20` random trials (cold start), then iteratively refits densities, samples 20 candidates from `ℓ(θ)`, selects the one maximizing `ℓ/g`, evaluates `J(θ)`, and adds it to the dataset. This continues for `N_max = 120` trials.
3. **GP Surrogate for Sensitivity Analysis:** An ARD (Automatic Relevance Determination) Matérn-5/2 Gaussian Process is fitted to all collected observations. This surrogate model allows for interpretability by learning length scales (`ℓ_i`) for each parameter. The normalized inverse length-scale (`ℓ̃_i`) quantifies parameter sensitivity: shorter length scales indicate parameters to which the score landscape is more sensitive.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation involved a Unitree Go2 quadruped robot, using the CHAMP hierarchical controller and a 3D LiDAR. The system runs on a Jetson Orin Nano, with simulations conducted in Gazebo Fortress.

#### 4.1. A. Experimental Setup

Four environments were used:
- **E1 (Open) & E2 (Indoor Office):** In-distribution training environments for offline Bayesian Optimization. E1 is sparse, E2 is confined with complex passages.
- **E3 (Warehouse):** Held-out, out-of-distribution test environment with dense obstacles and narrow corridors.
- **E4 (Real Room):** A real-world laboratory room with furniture and dynamic obstacles (people), used for sim-to-real testing.

The evaluation process included:
- **Phase 0 (Baseline):** Manually tuned MPC parameters based on limited simulation trials.
- **Phase 1 (Offline BO):** 120 BO trials on E1 and E2 to identify `θ*BO`.
- **Phase 2 (Generalization):** Evaluation of baseline and BO-tuned configurations on the unseen E3.
- **Phase 3 (Sim-to-Real):** Limited tests in the real environment E4.

Evaluation metrics reported were: Success Rate (SR, %), Time to Goal (TTG, s), Path Length (PL, m), and average MPC Solve Time (TSM, ms).

#### 4.2. B. Navigation Performance

The results, summarized in Table I, indicate substantial improvements with the BO-tuned parameters compared to the hand-tuned baseline.

- **Baseline Performance:** The hand-tuned baseline performed adequately in the open environment (E1: PL = 9.64 m, TSM = 40.85 ms) but showed degradation in more confined settings (E2: PL = 17.92 m, TSM = 46.78 ms), suggesting its sensitivity to obstacle density.
- **BO-Tuned Performance (In-distribution):** Offline Bayesian Optimization on E1 and E2 consistently improved all metrics across these environments.
 - In E2 (Indoor Office), path length was reduced by 32.3% (from 17.92 m to 12.13 m), and average MPC solve time decreased by 49.2% (from 46.78 s to 23.77 s). These improvements suggest that BO-tuned parameters lead to shorter and more computationally efficient trajectories.
- **Generalization to Unseen Environment (E3):** The BO-tuned configuration demonstrated strong generalization to the unseen warehouse environment (E3). Path length dropped by 44.5% (from 73.97 m to 41.07 m) despite E3 not being used during the tuning phase. This indicates robust out-of-distribution transferability of the optimized parameters. Figure 3 illustrates that the MPC objective function values were consistently lower for the BO-tuned configuration in E3, suggesting better task performance due to tighter path tracking and reduced obstacle costs. Figure 4 provides a visual comparison of trajectories in E3, showing the BO-tuned MPC following a shorter and smoother path.
- **Aggregated Performance:** Across E1-E3, BO reduced average path length by 38.7%, average MPC solve time by 25.3%, and time to goal by 53.0% (from 200.37 s to 94.25 s). The overall success rate improved from 65.0% (13/20 trials) to 90.0% (18/20 trials). These gains confirm that optimizing across environments with contrasting difficulties produces robust and generalizing parameter configurations.
- **Sim-to-Real Transfer (E4):** Hardware deployment in the real laboratory room (E4) validated the sim-to-real transfer. The success rate improved from 5/10 to 8/10 using the `θ*BO` parameters, without any additional on-robot or online tuning. This confirms that simulation-tuned parameters effectively transfer to physical hardware and real-world obstacles.

#### 4.3. C. MPC Parameter Sensitivity

Analysis of MPC parameter importance using the GP-based sensitivity estimation (Figure 5) revealed:
- **Highest Sensitivity:** Control jerk (`R_jerk`) and control effort (`R_v`) exhibited the highest sensitivity, indicating that dynamic regularization plays a primary role in solution stability.
- **Moderate Sensitivity:** Obstacle weight (`W`) and solver-time related effects followed.
- **Low Sensitivity:** Tracking terms (`Q_xy`, `Q_ψ`) and the failure penalty showed limited influence.
- **Reduced Sensitivity Post-BO:** Compared to the baseline, BO reduced the average sensitivity across parameters by 42.3%, suggesting a smoother optimization landscape that contributes to improved robustness and generalization. The BO convergence plot in Figure 5 demonstrates the optimization process stabilizing and finding better scores over trials.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a navigation framework that significantly contributes to the field of autonomous robotics, particularly for legged platforms operating in complex, unknown environments.

**Key areas of significance include:**

1. **Automated MPC Parameter Tuning:** The successful application of Tree-structured Parzen Estimator (TPE)-based Bayesian Optimization for tuning an 11-dimensional MPC parameter space demonstrates a robust methodology for overcoming the challenges of manual parameter tuning. This automation reduces the expert effort required for system setup and tuning, allowing for more efficient deployment of MPC-based controllers.
2. **Enhanced Navigation Performance and Efficiency:** The demonstrated improvements in path length (38.7% reduction), MPC solve time (25.3% reduction), time to goal (53.0% reduction), and success rate (from 65.0% to 90.0%) across diverse simulated environments highlight the practical benefits of the BO-tuned parameters. These gains translate directly into more efficient, faster, and safer autonomous navigation.
3. **Robust Generalization:** The ability of parameters tuned in specific training environments (E1, E2) to generalize effectively to an unseen, more complex environment (E3) is significant. This indicates that the optimized parameters are not merely overfit to training scenarios but capture underlying dynamics and constraints applicable to a broader range of operating conditions. This robustness is critical for deploying robots in varied real-world settings.
4. **Effective Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The successful transfer of simulation-tuned parameters to a physical quadruped robot (Unitree Go2) in a real laboratory environment (E4) without additional hardware-specific tuning is a crucial finding. This confirms the practical utility of simulation-based optimization for real-world robotic applications, bridging a common gap in robotics research.
5. **Modular and Platform-Agnostic Architecture:** The framework's modular design, leveraging ROS 2 and abstracting lower-level locomotion (via CHAMP controller), makes it highly adaptable. Its platform-agnostic nature suggests it could be extended to other mobile robots, including wheeled platforms or different legged systems, by simply interfacing with their respective low-level controllers.
6. **Insights into Parameter Sensitivity:** The use of a Gaussian Process surrogate for sensitivity analysis provides valuable insights into which MPC parameters are most critical for overall performance. Identifying control jerk and control effort as highly sensitive, for instance, can guide future design and optimization efforts, focusing resources on the most impactful parameters. The observed reduction in average sensitivity post-BO also suggests a more stable and less brittle control system.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Accelerated Robotic Deployment:** By automating tuning and demonstrating effective sim-to-real transfer, the work has the potential to significantly accelerate the development and deployment cycle for autonomous robots, especially in applications where environments are dynamic and varied (e.g., logistics, inspection, search and rescue).
- **Improved Reliability and Safety:** More robust and generalized navigation capabilities contribute to increased reliability and safety of autonomous systems operating in human-centric or hazardous environments.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The framework serves as a strong foundation for future advancements, as outlined by the authors. This includes online Bayesian Optimization for continual adaptation, integration of deep learning (DGCNN) for map-adaptive parameter conditioning, and interfacing with Visual Language Action (VLA) models for high-level, language-conditioned goal decomposition. These extensions could lead to more intelligent, adaptable, and intuitive robot navigation systems.
- **Contribution to Legged Robotics:** Specifically for legged robots, the ability to abstract complex leg dynamics and use a kinematic model with robust MPC planning provides a practical approach to high-level navigation, complementing ongoing research in whole-body control and locomotion.
