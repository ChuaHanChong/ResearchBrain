# More Thought, Less Accuracy? On the Dual Nature of Reasoning in Vision-Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers identified a "dual nature" in reasoning for Vision-Language Models (VLMs), where extended reasoning can degrade visual perception, a phenomenon termed "visual forgetting". Their proposed Vision-Anchored Policy Optimization (VAPO) method addresses this by explicitly anchoring reasoning to visual evidence, leading to state-of-the-art performance and more reliable multimodal outputs across various benchmarks.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language Models (VLMs) exhibit a "dual nature" where prolonged reasoning enhances logical inference but often impairs perceptual grounding, challenging the assumption that more thought consistently leads to more accuracy.
- Models experience "visual forgetting," progressively decreasing attention to visual input as reasoning length increases, leading to errors on basic visual recognition tasks they were initially capable of solving.
- A significant portion of VLM reasoning errors, over 55%, are perception-based (e.g., misreading charts, hallucination), and a third of these are recoverable by terminating reasoning earlier.

## Method

- The study introduces **VISION**-==ANCHORED POLICY OPTIMIZATION== (VAPO), a policy gradient algorithm built upon GRPO, designed to intrinsically steer reasoning towards visually grounded trajectories.
- VAPO generates independent, verifiable visual claims about an image and embeds "visual anchors" along the reasoning path, prompting the VLM to judge claim veracity at intermediate steps.
- A novel perception reward (RpercR_{perc}Rperc) is incorporated into the training objective, conditionally applied only when the VLM's final answer is correct, to incentivize sustained visual grounding and counteract visual forgetting without reward hacking.

## Results

- VAPO-Thinker-**7B** achieved new state-of-the-art results, improving by an average of **2%** on mathematical benchmarks and **3.2%** on general-purpose benchmarks compared to previous methods, significantly outperforming strong baselines including proprietary models.
- VAPO fundamentally rectifies visual forgetting, demonstrating sustained higher visual attention and steadily increasing accuracy even in later reasoning steps, unlike baselines where accuracy plateaus or declines.
- Error analysis confirmed that perception errors dominate VLM reasoning failures, accounting for over **55%** of errors, with **32.35%** being recoverable, directly supporting the "visual forgetting" hypothesis and validating VAPO's target problem.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Multimodal reasoning in VLMs possesses a dual nature where gains in logical inference can be counterbalanced by a degradation in perceptual grounding, particularly with extended reasoning.
> - The primary cause of perceptual degradation during VLM reasoning is "visual forgetting," characterized by a marked decline in attention to visual tokens as the reasoning process unfolds.
> - Explicitly anchoring the reasoning process to visual evidence via a tailored training-time reward mechanism is essential to prevent visual forgetting and achieve sustained, accurate multimodal reasoning without compromising perception.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of artificial intelligence has seen significant advancements in Large Language Models (LLMs), particularly in their reasoning capabilities. Early methods like Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting demonstrated that explicit step-by-step explanations could enhance problem-solving in complex tasks such as mathematics and code generation. More recent developments, including Reinforcement Learning (RL) based techniques like Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) and the integration of cognitive behaviors such as self-reflection, have further expanded these capabilities, leading to practices like Test-Time Scaling (TTS) where longer reasoning is often assumed to yield better performance.

Building on the success of reasoning in LLMs, a growing body of research has aimed to extend these capabilities to Vision-Language Models (VLMs). This extension involves adapting RL-based training or Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) to multimodal settings, enabling VLMs to tackle challenging visual tasks like geometry, navigation, image classification, and detection with promising results and improved generalization. However, limitations have begun to emerge. Prior studies have indicated that explicit reasoning in VLMs sometimes provides only marginal gains compared to direct answering. Furthermore, observations have been made where RL training in multimodal contexts can lead to a "collapse," with models favoring shorter reasoning paths as accuracy improves. These findings suggest that the assumption of consistent performance improvement through extended reasoning, prevalent in text-only LLMs, may not universally apply to VLMs.

This paper systematically investigates these limitations, specifically exploring the trade-offs involved in multimodal reasoning. It addresses the previously unexplored "dual nature" of reasoning in VLMs, where enhanced logical inference may coincide with degraded perceptual grounding. By identifying "visual forgetting" as a core underlying issue, the work aims to offer a fundamental solution, distinguishing itself from prior efforts that primarily focused on test-time remedies for text bias or modality imbalance in VLMs. The proposed method, VISION-ANCHORED POLICY OPTIMIZATION (VAPO), represents a training-based approach to intrinsically mitigate this problem, aiming to overcome the bottlenecks of existing reasoning models and advance the reliability of multimodal reasoning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to systematically investigate the impact of reasoning on the accuracy of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and address identified limitations. The work is motivated by an observation that, despite the widespread adoption and perceived benefits of reasoning in LLMs and its extension to VLMs, its universal efficacy in multimodal contexts remains questionable.

Specifically, the paper identifies a "dual nature" of multimodal reasoning:
1. **Enhancement of Logical Inference:** Reasoning demonstrably strengthens a VLM's ability to perform logical deductions and solve complex problems.
2. **Impairment of Perceptual Grounding:** Counterintuitively, longer reasoning processes may progressively weaken the model's capacity for accurate visual recognition, leading to errors on basic visual questions that the model was initially capable of answering correctly.

This phenomenon is illustrated through an "early decision mode" analysis, revealing that initial reasoning steps yield significant gains, but these advantages plateau or even reverse with prolonged thought. A detailed error analysis of failure cases indicates that perception errors, such as misreading charts or visual hallucinations, constitute the dominant category of errors (over 50%). A substantial portion of these perception errors are found to be "recoverable" by terminating reasoning earlier, suggesting that extended reasoning actively misleads the model. This leads to the central hypothesis: "the harder the model thinks, the worse the model sees."

To further investigate the root cause of this perceptual decline, the authors examine the evolution of attention to visual tokens during reasoning. They observe a marked decrease in visual attention as reasoning progresses, a phenomenon termed "visual forgetting." This suggests that models become increasingly reliant on their internal thought processes and less on the visual input over time.

The key motivations are therefore:
- To empirically demonstrate and characterize the "dual nature" of reasoning in VLMs, challenging the assumption that "more thought" consistently leads to "more accuracy" in multimodal settings.
- To identify "visual forgetting" as the underlying mechanism for the observed degradation in perceptual grounding during prolonged reasoning.
- To develop a principled, training-based method that explicitly counteracts visual forgetting and steers the reasoning process toward visually grounded trajectories, thereby unlocking the full potential of multimodal reasoning without compromising perceptual accuracy.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a multi-faceted approach involving empirical analysis, hypothesis validation through interventions, and the proposal of a novel training algorithm.

#### 3.1. Initial Analysis of Reasoning's Dual Nature

The study began by analyzing how accuracy evolves in existing multimodal reasoning models as reasoning length increases.
- **Model Selection:** Three representative VLMs were selected: R1-OneVision, VLAA-Thinker, and Vision-R1, spanning both SFT-then-RL and RL-only training pipelines.
- **Benchmark Selection:** Evaluation was conducted on a diverse set of benchmarks, categorized into "reasoning-centric" (MathVerse, LogicVista) and "vision-intensive" (MMStar, HallusionBench).
- **Early Decision Mode:** To quantify the contribution of individual reasoning segments, an "early decision mode" was introduced. This involved prompting the model to halt reasoning at intermediate logical breakpoints and provide an immediate answer. This allowed for tracking accuracy as reasoning progressed from direct answering (0% reasoning) to full reasoning (100% reasoning).
- **Error Analysis:** A comprehensive error analysis was performed on Vision-R1's incorrect responses under full reasoning. Errors were classified into three categories: Perception Error (e.g., chart misreading, hallucination), Logical Error (e.g., arithmetic inaccuracies), and Knowledge Error (e.g., commonsense violations). GPT-5 was utilized for automated error categorization. The concept of "recoverable" errors was introduced, defining instances where a correct answer could have been produced at an earlier reasoning step.

#### 3.2. Investigation and Validation of Visual Forgetting

To pinpoint the cause of perceptual decline, the researchers tracked the evolution of visual attention.
- **Visual Attention Tracking:** The attention ratio assigned to visual tokens at each generation step was monitored to serve as a proxy for the model's reliance on visual information.
- **Inference-Level Interventions:** Two interventions were designed to validate the "visual forgetting" hypothesis:
 - **Visual Replay:** The input image was reintroduced to the models at regular intervals during reasoning.
 - **Focus Prompt:** Models were explicitly prompted with instructions (e.g., "I need to see the image") to attend to visual input at selected steps.

#### 3.3. Proposed Method: VISION-ANCHORED POLICY OPTIMIZATION (VAPO)

VAPO is a policy gradient algorithm designed to explicitly steer the reasoning process toward visually grounded trajectories. It builds upon the standard Reinforcement Learning framework, typically Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO).
- **GRPO Foundation:** The paper briefly outlines the GRPO objective, which maximizes reward by comparing generated candidate sequences using a token-level advantage derived from normalized sequence-level rewards.
- **Visual Claim Generation:** To assess perceptual capability, a set of visual claims about the input image are generated using GPT-5. These claims are designed to be:
 - **Balanced:** Containing an equal number of correct and incorrect claims.
 - **Independent:** Verifiable solely from visual input, without relying on the main question or prior reasoning context.
 - **Concise and Verifiable:** Factual statements about clearly visible details, no more than 20 words, no hedging terms.
 - **Filtering and Deduplication:** A rule-based mechanism ensures quality and diversity, removing semantically similar or purely low-level claims (e.g., only color/counting).
- **Inserting Visual Anchors:** During the reasoning rollout, a sequence of "visual anchors" is embedded along the reasoning path. At each anchor, a sampled visual claim is appended to the current reasoning prefix, and the VLM is prompted to judge its veracity (yes/no). This provides a binary score for perceptual accuracy at that specific point. Anchor positions are randomly distributed throughout the trajectory, aligned with logical boundaries.
- **Perception Reward ($R_{perc}$):** This reward quantifies the model's overall perceptual grounding. It is an aggregation of scores from all anchor points, weighted with a "late-emphasis" factor ($w_k = \exp(\beta \cdot \frac{a_k}{T})$). The late-emphasis ($\beta$) assigns greater importance to later anchors, targeting the stages where visual forgetting is more pronounced.
- **Final Reward ($R_i$):** The total reward for a generated sequence $o_i$ combines the standard accuracy reward ($R_{acc}$), format reward ($R_{fmt}$), and the perception reward ($R_{perc}$). Critically, the perception reward is conditionally applied only when $R_{acc} = 1$ (i.e., the final answer is correct) to prevent reward hacking where models might achieve high perception scores by producing short, incorrect reasoning. $R_i = R_{acc} + R_{fmt} + \gamma \cdot \mathbf{1}[R_{acc}=1] \cdot R_{perc}$, where $\gamma$ is the weight of the perception reward.
- **Training Configuration:** Models are trained on the ViRL39K dataset using Qwen2.5-VL (3B and 7B parameters) as the base VLM. Standard GRPO hyperparameters (clip ratio $\epsilon$, KL penalty $\lambda$) are used, with VAPO-specific parameters (anchor number $K=20$, late-emphasis weight $\beta=1.5$, perception reward weight $\gamma=0.1$) tuned via ablation studies.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yields several key findings demonstrating the dual nature of reasoning in VLMs, the identification of visual forgetting as a root cause, and the effectiveness of the proposed VAPO method.

#### 4.1. The Dual Nature of Reasoning in VLMs

- **Accuracy vs. Reasoning Length:** Analysis of existing VLMs (R1-OneVision, VLAA-Thinker, Vision-R1) revealed that while early-stage reasoning provides substantial accuracy gains, these benefits tend to saturate and may even reverse in later steps. This decline was particularly evident in vision-intensive benchmarks like MMStar and HallusionBench, where accuracy dropped by over 2% from its peak with prolonged reasoning (Figure 2A).
- **Dominance of Perception Errors:** A detailed error analysis of Vision-R1's failures under full reasoning indicated that perception errors, such as chart misreading or hallucination, were the most prevalent category, accounting for over 55.23% of all errors. Logical errors comprised 33.05%, and knowledge errors 11.72% (Figure 2B).
- **Recoverable Perception Errors:** A significant portion of these perception errors (32.35%) could be rectified by adopting an "early decision mode," suggesting that models were initially capable of producing correct answers but were misled by extended reasoning. This supports the hypothesis that prolonged thought can degrade perceptual grounding (Figure 2B, 2C).

#### 4.2. Identification and Validation of Visual Forgetting

- **Declining Visual Attention:** Tracking the attention ratio assigned to visual tokens showed a marked decrease as reasoning progressed in vanilla VLM reasoning, eventually reaching negligible levels (Figure 3A). This indicated that models increasingly relied on generated textual context rather than the visual input.
- **Effectiveness of Interventions:** Two inference-level remedies, "visual replay" (reintroducing the image) and "focus prompt" (explicitly prompting visual attention), successfully triggered sharp increases in visual attention and alleviated the performance degradation caused by prolonged reasoning. Visual replay, in particular, improved accuracy by approximately 1.5% over vanilla reasoning, empirically validating visual forgetting as a central limiting factor (Figure 3A, 3B).

#### 4.3. Performance of VISION-ANCHORED POLICY OPTIMIZATION (VAPO)

- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** VAPO-Thinker-7B consistently outperformed strong baselines, including proprietary models like GPT-5-Thinking and Gemini-2.5-Pro, and open-source models like Vision-R1-7B.
 - On mathematical benchmarks, VAPO-Thinker-7B achieved an average improvement of 2% over Vision-R1-7B (49.1% to 51.1%) (Table 1).
 - On general-purpose benchmarks, it surpassed the previous best results by 3.2% (59.9% to 63.1%), establishing a new state of the art. The gains were more pronounced on vision-intensive tasks like MMStar and HallusionBench (Table 2).
- **Fundamental Rectification vs. Inference-Level Remedies:** VAPO-Thinker-7B achieved substantially higher performance than baselines augmented with visual replay or focus prompt (e.g., 53.8% vs. 51.7% for Vision-R1 with visual replay on average across selected benchmarks, Table 3). This indicates that VAPO offers a fundamental, training-based solution to visual forgetting, rather than a superficial inference-time fix.
- **Enhanced Perceptual Capability and Sustained Attention:** VAPO-Thinker-7B exhibited a gentler decline in visual attention and sustained consistently higher levels of attention to visual tokens throughout the reasoning process compared to baselines (Figure 5A). This translated into steadily increasing accuracy even in later reasoning steps, fully leveraging the benefits of extended reasoning without compromising perception (Figure 5B).
- **Improved Perception Reward During Training:** Training curves showed a steady increase in both accuracy and the perception reward, indicating that the model progressively allocated greater attention to visual input and improved its ability to verify visual claims (Figure 6).

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies and Further Analyses

- **Anchor Number (K):** Increasing the number of visual anchors improved average accuracy, with performance saturating around K=20 (Figure 7, Table 29), suggesting denser sampling provides a more reliable assessment of perceptual capability.
- **Late-Emphasis Weight ($\beta$):** Performance peaked at $\beta=1.5$, where later anchors were given more weight, confirming the strategy of targeting visual forgetting in later reasoning stages (Figure 7, Table 30).
- **Perception Reward Weight ($\gamma$):** An optimal $\gamma$ of 0.1 was found, with mathematical tasks favoring smaller values and vision-intensive tasks benefiting from more aggressive settings (Table 5).
- **Data Augmentation vs. Reward Mechanism:** Directly augmenting GRPO with synthetic examples derived from visual claims yielded only marginal gains, confirming that VAPO's performance is primarily due to its reward-driven, visually grounded reasoning, not merely data augmentation (Table 6).
- **Visual Claim Design:** The quality and nature of visual claims were crucial; non-visually-dependent or question-aligned claims performed worse, highlighting the necessity for claims that purely assess visual grounding (Table 7).
- **Computational Efficiency:** VAPO introduced minimal additional training overhead (approximately 30 minutes for 2 epochs) compared to GRPO or DAPO, while yielding substantially higher performance gains. Visual claim generation was a one-time process taking about half an hour (Table 11, 12).
- **Generalizability:** VAPO was effective with open-source models for claim generation (Table 15) and showed substantial improvements when applied to different base architectures like Qwen3-VL (Table 16), indicating broad applicability. It also generalized well to fine-grained perception and spatial reasoning tasks (Table 17).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research provides a systematic investigation into the behavior of reasoning in Vision-Language Models (VLMs), identifying a fundamental limitation and proposing a method to address it.

The primary significance lies in the empirical demonstration and characterization of the "dual nature" of multimodal reasoning. The finding that prolonged reasoning can degrade perceptual grounding, leading to "visual forgetting" and recognition failures on otherwise basic visual questions, challenges the prevalent assumption of monotonic performance gains with increased reasoning depth, particularly in multimodal contexts. This insight is crucial for understanding the limitations of current VLM architectures and training paradigms. It highlights that the integration of reasoning into VLMs is not a universally beneficial strategy without careful consideration of its perceptual side effects.

The proposed **VISION-ANCHORED POLICY OPTIMIZATION (VAPO)** offers a principled and effective solution to this identified problem. By explicitly anchoring the reasoning process in visual evidence through a novel perception reward mechanism, VAPO fundamentally rectifies the model's tendency to disregard visual input over time. The achievement of new state-of-the-art results across a diverse range of mathematical and general-purpose benchmarks, especially on vision-intensive tasks, demonstrates the practical efficacy of VAPO. Its ability to outperform inference-level remedies suggests a deeper, intrinsic improvement in VLM behavior rather than a superficial fix. The minimal computational overhead of VAPO further enhances its practicality and potential for widespread adoption.

**Potential impacts of this work include:**

- **Improved VLM Reliability and Trustworthiness:** By mitigating visual forgetting and ensuring that reasoning remains grounded in visual evidence, VAPO can reduce instances of visual hallucination and factually incorrect statements. This can enhance the reliability and trustworthiness of VLMs in critical applications where accurate interpretation of visual information is paramount.
- **Enhanced Safety of AI Systems:** Ungrounded reasoning, especially in safety-critical domains (e.g., autonomous driving, medical imaging), can lead to hazardous decisions. By promoting visually anchored reasoning, VAPO contributes to building safer and more dependable AI systems.
- **Advancement of Multimodal AI Research:** This work shifts focus from merely extending reasoning capabilities to VLMs to critically evaluating and refining these capabilities within the multimodal context. It opens new avenues for research into more sophisticated mechanisms for balancing logical inference with perceptual grounding, potentially inspiring novel VLM architectures or training objectives.
- **Guidance for VLM Development:** The findings provide clear guidance for developers and researchers working on VLMs, emphasizing the need to explicitly address visual grounding during reasoning training. This could lead to the development of more robust and capable next-generation VLMs.

**Limitations identified by the authors and areas for future impact include:**

- **Visual Claim Quality:** The quality of generated visual claims (currently from GPT-5) impacts VAPO's effectiveness. Further improvements in claim generation or human-curated claims could enhance performance.
- **Hyperparameter Sensitivity:** VAPO introduces new hyperparameters (K, $\beta$, $\gamma$) that require tuning, and optimal settings may vary by task. Research into adaptive parameter adjustment could be beneficial.
- **Generalization to Complex Multimodal Tasks:** While promising results are shown for single-image and initial multi-image tasks, extending VAPO to complex multi-image reasoning or video-based tasks presents further research opportunities.

Overall, this research offers a significant contribution by uncovering a critical weakness in current multimodal reasoning approaches and providing an effective, computationally efficient solution. Its implications extend to improving the accuracy, safety, and reliability of VLMs across various applications.
