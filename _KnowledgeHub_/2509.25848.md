# More Thought, Less Accuracy? On the Dual Nature of Reasoning in Vision-Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers identified a "dual nature" in reasoning for Vision-Language Models (VLMs), where extended reasoning can degrade visual perception, a phenomenon termed "visual forgetting". Their proposed Vision-Anchored Policy Optimization (VAPO) method addresses this by explicitly anchoring reasoning to visual evidence, leading to state-of-the-art performance and more reliable multimodal outputs across various benchmarks.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language Models (VLMs) exhibit a "dual nature" where prolonged reasoning enhances logical inference but often impairs perceptual grounding, challenging the assumption that more thought consistently leads to more accuracy.
- Models experience "visual forgetting," progressively decreasing attention to visual input as reasoning length increases, leading to errors on basic visual recognition tasks they were initially capable of solving.
- A significant portion of VLM reasoning errors, over 55%, are perception-based (e.g., misreading charts, hallucination), and a third of these are recoverable by terminating reasoning earlier.

## Method

- The study introduces **VISION**-==ANCHORED POLICY OPTIMIZATION== (VAPO), a policy gradient algorithm built upon GRPO, designed to intrinsically steer reasoning towards visually grounded trajectories.
- VAPO generates independent, verifiable visual claims about an image and embeds "visual anchors" along the reasoning path, prompting the VLM to judge claim veracity at intermediate steps.
- A novel perception reward (RpercR_{perc}Rperc​) is incorporated into the training objective, conditionally applied only when the VLM's final answer is correct, to incentivize sustained visual grounding and counteract visual forgetting without reward hacking.

## Results

- VAPO-Thinker-**7B** achieved new state-of-the-art results, improving by an average of **2%** on mathematical benchmarks and **3.2%** on general-purpose benchmarks compared to previous methods, significantly outperforming strong baselines including proprietary models.
- VAPO fundamentally rectifies visual forgetting, demonstrating sustained higher visual attention and steadily increasing accuracy even in later reasoning steps, unlike baselines where accuracy plateaus or declines.
- Error analysis confirmed that perception errors dominate VLM reasoning failures, accounting for over **55%** of errors, with **32.35%** being recoverable, directly supporting the "visual forgetting" hypothesis and validating VAPO's target problem.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Multimodal reasoning in VLMs possesses a dual nature where gains in logical inference can be counterbalanced by a degradation in perceptual grounding, particularly with extended reasoning.
> - The primary cause of perceptual degradation during VLM reasoning is "visual forgetting," characterized by a marked decline in attention to visual tokens as the reasoning process unfolds.
> - Explicitly anchoring the reasoning process to visual evidence via a tailored training-time reward mechanism is essential to prevent visual forgetting and achieve sustained, accurate multimodal reasoning without compromising perception.
