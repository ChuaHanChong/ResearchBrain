# TD-TOG Dataset: Benchmarking Zero-Shot and One-Shot Task-Oriented Grasping for Object Generalization

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces the TD-TOG dataset, a real-world RGB-D resource for task-oriented grasping, and benchmarks zero-shot and one-shot learning approaches for object generalization. The study found one-shot learning excels at fine-grained subcategory differentiation and robustness in multi-object scenes, while zero-shot methods offer reduced annotation effort.

## Problem

- A scarcity of comprehensive, high-quality real-world datasets specifically designed for training and benchmarking Task-Oriented Grasping (TOG) solutions.
- The challenge of enabling TOG solutions to generalize effectively to previously unseen objects and tasks without requiring extensive retraining.
- Current TOG evaluation metrics often overlook the crucial ability of models to differentiate between visually similar object subcategories, which is vital for many practical applications.

## Method

- Introduced the **TD-TOG** dataset, a meticulously ==hand-annotated real-world RGB-D dataset== featuring object masks, affordance masks, planar grasps, and textual descriptions across 30 object categories and 120 subcategories.
- Proposed **Binary-TOG**, a novel ==modular framework== that integrates zero-shot object recognition (using ==CLIP== and ==SAM== with a new ==Size Subset Filtering== algorithm) with one-shot affordance recognition (using AffCorrs) to generalize to new objects and tasks.
- Conducted a comprehensive comparative analysis by benchmarking Binary-TOG and **OS-TOG** (one-shot object recognition) against a traditional baseline (Standard-TOG) on the new TD-TOG dataset and through real-world robotic experiments.

## Results

- OS-TOG achieved a **70.9%** real-world Task-Oriented Grasp (TOG) success rate for unknown object categories, outperforming Binary-TOG's **52.7%** and Standard-TOG's **44.1%** in these novel scenarios.
- OS-TOG consistently outperformed Binary-TOG in differentiating object subcategories, achieving F1 scores of **0.806** (known categories) and **0.819** (novel categories), compared to Binary-TOG's **0.714** and **0.682** respectively.
- The novel Size Subset Filtering (SSF) algorithm significantly improved the performance of the SAM segmentation model by **192.8%** in AP50 and **148.2%** in IoU, enhancing object and affordance mask quality for both generalization frameworks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The TD-TOG dataset effectively supports the training of grasp detection models, achieving an 84.0% average grasp accuracy for both known and novel objects.
> - One-shot learning (OS-TOG) demonstrates superior robustness in cluttered multi-object scenes and significantly outperforms zero-shot learning in differentiating between visually similar object subcategories.
> - Zero-shot object recognition, while reducing annotation effort by leveraging textual prompts, can struggle with uncommon objects or subtle visual variations that deviate from its pre-training data biases.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated within the critical and rapidly evolving field of **Task-Oriented Grasping (TOG)** in robotics. TOG aims to enable robots to not only grasp objects stably but also to do so in a manner that facilitates a subsequent task, such such as grasping a hammer by its handle for a user handover or by its head for hammering. This capability is fundamental for advancements in diverse robotic domains, including industrial automation, domestic assistance, and assistive robotics.

The paper identifies two major gaps in the current TOG landscape that it seeks to address:
1. **Limited Availability and Quality of TOG Datasets:** Existing literature reveals a scarcity of comprehensive datasets specifically designed for training and benchmarking TOG solutions. Many available datasets are synthetic, which may not accurately reflect real-world complexities, or they suffer from incomplete annotations (lacking object masks, affordance masks, or grasp configurations) or artifacts that hinder model performance. The demand for real-world, multi-object TOG datasets with high-quality, complete manual annotations is high, as these better represent the environments where robots operate.
2. **Challenges in Object Generalization and Evaluation:** A primary challenge for autonomous and scalable TOG solutions is their ability to adapt and manipulate *previously unseen objects* without extensive retraining. While one-shot learning approaches (e.g., OS-TOG, a previous work by the lead author) have shown promise in generalizing to new object categories and tasks, the emergence of powerful foundation models and **zero-shot learning** techniques (e.g., CLIP, SAM) has opened new avenues for generalization through semantic descriptions like textual prompts. The comparative effectiveness of these two approaches in TOG, particularly in multi-object scenes and for fine-grained object differentiation, remains underexplored. Furthermore, current TOG evaluation metrics often overlook the crucial ability of models to distinguish between object *subcategories* (e.g., different types of mugs or screws), which is vital for many practical applications.

This work directly addresses these gaps by:
- **Introducing TD-TOG:** A novel, real-world, hand-annotated RGB-D dataset designed to provide the high-quality, comprehensive annotations (object masks, affordance masks, planar grasps, textual descriptions) necessary for training and benchmarking advanced TOG solutions, including those leveraging one-shot and zero-shot learning. It explicitly accounts for multi-object scenes and highly detailed masks (e.g., holes in objects), which existing datasets often lack.
- **Proposing Binary-TOG:** A new modular TOG framework that integrates both zero-shot learning (for object recognition using textual prompts) and one-shot learning (for affordance recognition using visual references). This framework is designed to generalize to new objects and tasks without retraining, contributing to the development of adaptable robotic systems. Crucially, its modular design allows for a clearer comparison of generalization capabilities with one-shot methods and aids in error tracing, contrasting with more opaque end-to-end approaches.
- **Benchmarking Generalization:** The paper provides a rigorous comparative analysis between Binary-TOG (zero-shot object recognition) and OS-TOG (one-shot object recognition), along with a traditional baseline (Standard-TOG). This comparison highlights the strengths and limitations of one-shot and zero-shot approaches in generalizing to novel object categories and, importantly, differentiating between object subcategories, a novel evaluation challenge introduced in this work. This analysis aims to guide future research and inform the selection of appropriate generalization techniques for different application domains.

By providing a new, high-quality dataset and a comparative study of generalization methods, this research significantly advances the tools and understanding needed to develop more robust, adaptable, and practical robotic grasping systems for unstructured environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching motivation for this research stems from the pressing need for autonomous robotic systems that can perform complex manipulation tasks in diverse, unstructured environments. A critical prerequisite for such systems is **Task-Oriented Grasping (TOG)**, where robots must predict grasps that not only secure an object but also facilitate a specific subsequent task.

The authors identify several specific motivations and translate them into key objectives:

1. **Addressing Data Scarcity and Quality for TOG:** The introduction section highlights that "existing literature reveals there is a limited availability of TOG datasets for training and benchmarking." These datasets are often synthetic, suffer from annotation artifacts, or lack the complete set of annotations (object masks, affordance masks, and grasps) required for robust TOG model development.
 - **Objective 1:** To introduce the **Top-down Task-oriented Grasping (TD-TOG) dataset**, a hand-annotated, real-world RGB-D dataset with detailed object masks, affordances, and planar rectangular grasps across 30 object categories and 120 subcategories. This dataset aims to overcome the limitations of existing resources by providing high-quality, comprehensive annotations suitable for training and evaluating TOG solutions.

2. **Enabling Object Generalization without Retraining:** A significant challenge for TOG solutions is adapting to and manipulating previously unseen objects. Traditional methods often require extensive retraining for new object categories, limiting scalability. The paper builds on previous work (OS-TOG) that utilized one-shot learning but recognizes the emerging potential of zero-shot learning with foundation models (like LLMs, CLIP, SAM).
 - **Objective 2:** To propose a novel TOG framework, **Binary-TOG**, which modifies the one-shot object recognition module from OS-TOG to leverage zero-shot learning. This enables Binary-TOG to identify objects in multi-object scenes from textual prompts and generalize to new objects and tasks without requiring retraining of its underlying models.

3. **Benchmarking and Understanding Generalization Capabilities:** There is a need for a clear understanding of the strengths and limitations of different generalization approaches (one-shot vs. zero-shot) in TOG. This comparison is crucial for guiding future research and deploying appropriate solutions in various applications.
 - **Objective 3:** To conduct a comprehensive **comparative analysis** between Binary-TOG (zero-shot object recognition) and the previous OS-TOG (one-shot object recognition). This analysis aims to highlight the capabilities of each approach in object generalization, particularly in differentiating between object subcategories, and to identify their suitability for distinct use cases.

4. **Addressing Overlooked Evaluation Aspects:** The authors note that existing TOG literature largely overlooks the ability of solutions to differentiate between object *subcategories* (e.g., blue round mug vs. orange square mug), rather than just broad categories (mug, hammer). This fine-grained distinction is vital for industrial assembly (bolts, screws) or personalized assistive robotics.
 - **Objective 4:** To introduce a **novel evaluation challenge** within the TD-TOG dataset specifically designed to assess a TOG solution’s ability to distinguish between object subcategories.

5. **Demonstrating Real-World Applicability:** Beyond theoretical evaluation, validating framework performance on physical robotic platforms is essential to confirm practical utility.
 - **Objective 5:** To conduct **real-world experiments with a robotic arm** to demonstrate Binary-TOG’s ability to execute TOG predictions on target objects and tasks in multi-object RGB-D scenes of household objects and tools.

In summary, the core motivation is to advance robotic manipulation by providing both a high-quality, real-world dataset and innovative, generalizable TOG frameworks, along with a thorough comparative analysis and new evaluation paradigms, to enable robots to effectively interact with novel objects in complex environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a multi-faceted methodology encompassing dataset creation, novel framework development, and comprehensive evaluation through both simulated and real-world experiments.

#### 3.1. TD-TOG Dataset Creation

The core of the data contribution is the **Top-Down Task-Oriented Grasping (TD-TOG) dataset**:
- **Data Characteristics:** Comprises 1,449 real-world RGB-D scenes of household objects (480x640px). It features 30 unique object categories, 120 object subcategories (4 variations per category), and 16 affordances mapped to 21 tasks. In total, it has 4,101 annotated object instances, 31,248 grasp rectangles, and 8,337 affordance masks.
- **Annotation Details:** Each object is meticulously hand-annotated with:
 - **Object masks:** Precise segmentation masks, even accounting for holes within objects (e.g., scissor handles).
 - **Affordance masks:** Binary masks indicating task-suitable regions.
 - **Planar rectangular grasps:** Represented as 5D vectors (x, y, w, h, θ), commonly used for top-down grasping with parallel-jaw grippers.
 - **Textual descriptions:** Semantic information (e.g., "round thin paintbrush with wooden handle and light bristles").
- **Annotation Process:** Manual annotation using Labelme for masks and LabelImg2 for grasps. To reduce human effort, two pre-labeling algorithms were developed:
 - **Affordance-refining algorithm:** Refines rough affordance masks by extracting portions overlapping with detailed object masks.
 - **Auto-labeling algorithm:** Leverages reference dataset annotations to automatically label grasp rectangles and rough affordance masks in new scenes, followed by manual validation.
- **Dataset Structure:** Divided into three main sets:
 - **Reference Set:** 120 single-object scenes, one for each subcategory, with full annotations.
 - **Training Set:** 750 multi-object scenes (1-5 distinct objects) featuring 15 "known" object categories (45 subcategories).
 - **Testing Set:**
 - **Category Split (225 scenes):** Evaluates general TOG performance on different levels of novelty: KC-KSC (known category, known subcategory), KC-USC (known category, unknown subcategory), and UC-USC (unknown category, unknown subcategory).
 - **Subcategory Split (354 scenes):** A novel challenge evaluating the ability to differentiate between object subcategories. Scenes contain 2-4 objects of the same category but different subcategories, divided into KC (known categories) and UC (unknown categories).
- **Data Collection:** RGB-D scenes were captured using a 7-DoF Franka Emika robotic arm equipped with a D415 Intel RealSense camera in a controlled laboratory setting (Figure 5).

#### 3.2. Task-Oriented Grasping Frameworks

The paper develops and evaluates three distinct modular TOG frameworks: Binary-TOG, OS-TOG, and Standard-TOG. All operate on the formal definition of TOG: given an RGB image, a target object, and a target task, identify a grasp configuration on the target object that satisfies task-specific rules (e.g., grasp handle, avoid head).

- **1. Binary-TOG (Novel Framework):** A modular framework designed for generalization without retraining.
 - **Knowledge Base (K):** For each object, stores (RGB image, object mask, affordance masks, grasp rectangles, textual description).
 - **Modules:**
 - **Zero-shot Object Segmentation:** Uses **Segment Anything Model (SAM)** (ViT-L backbone) to generate candidate segmentation masks. A novel **Size Subset Filtering (SSF) algorithm** post-processes SAM outputs to remove noise, small artifacts, large background regions, and redundant overlapping masks, empirically tuned for optimal performance.
 - **Zero-shot Object Recognition:** Retrieves a textual description of the target object from K. Uses **Contrastive Language-Image Pretraining (CLIP)** (ViT-B/32) to compare embeddings of segmented scene objects with the textual prompt, identifying the target object.
 - **One-shot Affordance Recognition:** Determines the target affordance based on the task rule. Retrieves reference image, affordance mask, and object mask from K. Uses **AffCorrs** (a one-shot affordance recognition model) to identify the task-suitable region on the recognized target object. An **Affordance Alignment (AA) module** rotates reference images to align with the scene object's orientation for better matching.
 - **Zero-shot Task-Oriented Grasp Module:** Uses **S2A-Net** (trained on TD-TOG) to generate candidate grasp rectangles. Filters grasps to retain only those whose center falls within the task-suitable region identified by the affordance recognition module, selecting the highest-confidence grasp.

- **2. OS-TOG (Previous Work, Improved):** Based on the authors' prior work (Holomjova et al., 2023), it shares a similar modular architecture and generalization goal but differs in object recognition.
 - **Knowledge Base (K):** Similar to Binary-TOG but relies on visual references for object recognition.
 - **One-shot Object Recognition:** Re-implementation of **N-net** (Zeng et al., 2019). It compares visual features of segmented scene objects with a reference RGB image and object mask from K to identify the target object. It also adopts the improved SAM + SSF segmentation module.

- **3. Standard-TOG (Baseline):** A traditional TOG framework representing solutions without one-shot or zero-shot generalization.
 - **No Knowledge Base for Generalization:** Its recognition modules are standard neural networks trained on fixed categories.
 - **Standard Object Recognition:** Uses a **ResNet-50** trained on TD-TOG to classify segmented objects into known categories.
 - **Standard Affordance Recognition:** Uses a **Mask R-CNN** trained on TD-TOG to detect affordances.
 - **Grasp Detection:** Uses S2A-Net, similar to the other frameworks.

#### 3.3. Evaluation Metrics and Experiments

- **Dataset Experiments (TD-TOG):**
 - **Metrics:** Object segmentation (mean Average Precision (AP), Intersection over Union (IoU)), Object Recognition (F1 score), Affordance Recognition (F1 score), Grasp Accuracy (IoU > 25% with ground-truth grasp, θ angle difference < 30°), Task-oriented Grasp (TG) Accuracy (grasp accuracy + grasp center in ground-truth task-relevant region).
 - **Procedure:** Frameworks evaluated on Category Split (general performance across varying object novelty) and Subcategory Split (ability to differentiate between visually similar subcategories).
- **Real-world Experiments:**
 - **Setup:** Franka Emika robotic arm (Figure 5) in 9 unique scenes with 5 object instances. Two trials per object/task/framework.
 - **Metrics:** Success rates for each module (Object Segmentation, Object Recognition, Affordance Recognition, Grasp Detection, TOG Detection), Grasp Success Rate, and overall TOG Success Rate (physical execution). Inference and grasp execution times were also recorded.

The comprehensive methodological design, including a meticulously annotated real-world dataset, novel modular frameworks, and rigorous multi-level evaluation, allows for a robust comparative analysis of generalization techniques in TOG.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several significant findings across dataset effectiveness, framework performance, subcategory differentiation, and real-world applicability:

#### 4.1. Effectiveness of the TD-TOG Dataset

- **Grasp Detection Performance:** The S2A-Net grasp detection model, when trained on TD-TOG, achieved high average per-object grasp accuracies: 88.9% (KC-KSC), 83.1% (KC-USC), and 80.0% (UC-USC). This demonstrates an overall grasp accuracy of 84.0% across all subsplits of the category split, confirming TD-TOG's effectiveness in training models for predicting grasps in both known and novel scenes/object categories.
- **Segmentation Improvement:** The novel Size Subset Filtering (SSF) module significantly enhanced the performance of the SAM segmentation model, leading to an average increase of 192.8% in AP50 and 148.2% in IoU score. This highlights SSF's critical role in removing noise and irrelevant segment masks, which is crucial for subsequent recognition steps.

#### 4.2. Framework Performance and Generalization (TD-TOG Dataset)

- **Generalization Capabilities:**
 - **Standard-TOG** showed a significant drop in Task-Oriented Grasp (TOG) accuracy when moving from known objects (KC-KSC) to novel subcategories (KC-USC), emphasizing its limited ability to generalize. It was unable to participate in the UC-USC (unknown categories) subsplit without retraining. Its average TOG accuracy for known objects was 90.8%, dropping to 59.1% for novel subcategories.
 - **Binary-TOG and OS-TOG** maintained relatively high TOG accuracy across novel object categories and subcategories, validating the generalization capabilities of one-shot and zero-shot techniques.
- **Performance Comparison in Single vs. Multi-Object Scenes:**
 - **Single-Object Scenes (Table IV):** Binary-TOG generally outperformed OS-TOG and Standard-TOG, achieving the highest TOG accuracy across all category subsplits (e.g., 90.9% for KC-KSC, 81.8% for KC-USC, 69.8% for UC-USC). This suggests strong performance when the target object is clearly isolated.
 - **Multi-Object Scenes (Table V):** OS-TOG demonstrated better performance in multi-object scenes across all subsplits (e.g., 83.9% for KC-KSC, 70.8% for KC-USC, 63.3% for UC-USC) compared to Binary-TOG (82.6% for KC-KSC, 69.5% for KC-USC, 54.5% for UC-USC). This indicates that OS-TOG is more robust to the presence of distractor objects.
- **Zero-shot Object Recognition Limitations:** Binary-TOG's object recognition (using CLIP) sometimes struggled with "uncommon objects and subtle variations" (e.g., specific types of glue, grater, spatula, paint roller, peeler) that deviate from ordinary forms. This is attributed to potential biases or gaps in its large-scale pre-training data.

#### 4.3. Differentiating Between Object Subcategories

- **Challenge Difficulty:** The subcategory split proved to be a more difficult challenge than merely distinguishing between object categories. Both Binary-TOG and OS-TOG showed a decrease in object recognition performance (F1 score) in the subcategory split compared to the category split.
- **OS-TOG Superiority:** OS-TOG consistently outperformed Binary-TOG in differentiating between object subcategories, for both known categories (KC subsplit: OS-TOG F1 = 0.806 vs. Binary-TOG F1 = 0.714) and novel categories (UC subsplit: OS-TOG F1 = 0.819 vs. Binary-TOG F1 = 0.682). This suggests OS-TOG's visual-based one-shot learning is superior for fine-grained distinction and does not overfit to training instances.
- **Binary-TOG's Specific Struggles:** Binary-TOG showed particular difficulty distinguishing visually similar subcategories like different types of screws (F1 = 0.360 vs. OS-TOG's 0.617) and vitamins, even with detailed textual descriptions. This highlights a limitation of current foundation models in capturing fine-grained visual details from generalized semantic embeddings.

#### 4.4. Real-World Experiments

- **Overall TOG Success Rates (Table X):**
 - **Binary-TOG:** Achieved 64.8% TOG success rate for KC-USC objects and 52.7% for UC-USC objects.
 - **OS-TOG:** Achieved 65.3% TOG success rate for KC-USC objects and 70.9% for UC-USC objects.
 - OS-TOG demonstrated superior performance, especially in generalizing to novel object *categories* (UC-USC) in real-world scenarios.
 - Standard-TOG had a significantly lower success rate (44.1% for KC-USC).
- **Inference and Grasp Times:** Both Binary-TOG and OS-TOG had comparable average inference times (8.4-9.3 seconds for Binary-TOG, 8.8-9.8 seconds for OS-TOG) and average grasp execution times (~8.9 seconds), indicating similar operational efficiency despite performance differences.
- **Failure Analysis:**
 - **Object Recognition Failures (Binary-TOG):** In real-world UC-USC, Binary-TOG struggled with objects like screws, paint rollers, and peelers, often matching them to partial segmentations of other objects. This issue, linked to the zero-shot model focusing on prominent features, could hinder correct affordance identification.
 - **Grasp Prediction Failures:** Some TOG failures were due to a lack of grasp candidates on the task-relevant region.
 - **Grasp Execution Failures:** Other failures were attributed to unstable grasps (object slipping) or the robot hitting joint constraints. Minor discrepancies in object placement between trials could also influence outcomes.

In summary, while zero-shot learning offers significant advantages in reducing annotation effort and generalizing to novel categories, one-shot learning (specifically OS-TOG) currently demonstrates greater robustness in cluttered multi-object scenes and superior ability in distinguishing between visually similar object subcategories, both in dataset and real-world evaluations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research makes several significant contributions with substantial potential impact on the fields of robotics, computer vision, and artificial intelligence, particularly in the domain of autonomous manipulation.

#### 5.1. Impact of the TD-TOG Dataset

The introduction of the **TD-TOG dataset** is a critical advancement:
- **Addresses a Core Research Gap:** It directly fills the urgent need for a real-world, multi-object, RGB-D dataset with comprehensive, high-quality, and manually annotated data. Its inclusion of detailed object masks (even accounting for holes), affordance masks, planar grasps, and textual descriptions makes it uniquely suitable for training and evaluating advanced TOG solutions, especially those relying on generalization techniques.
- **Reliable Benchmarking:** By providing a standardized and realistic benchmark, TD-TOG significantly reduces the reliance on costly, time-consuming, and often inconsistent real-world robotic trials for early-stage evaluation. Researchers can use this dataset to reliably estimate their system's real-world performance in a controlled setting.
- **Drives New Research Directions:** The dataset's detailed annotations and diverse object coverage will foster the development of more sophisticated perception and grasping models. Its explicit structure for evaluating object subcategory differentiation introduces a crucial new benchmark that pushes the boundaries of fine-grained object recognition in robotics.

#### 5.2. Impact of the Binary-TOG Framework and Comparative Analysis

- **Advancing Generalizable Robotics:** **Binary-TOG** demonstrates the viability of integrating zero-shot learning for object recognition into a modular TOG framework. This signifies a major step towards truly generalizable robots that can interact with novel objects based solely on textual prompts, drastically reducing the need for extensive prior visual data or retraining. This is particularly impactful for applications where rapid deployment with new objects is required.
- **Enhanced Interpretability:** The modular design of Binary-TOG, in contrast to end-to-end approaches, improves interpretability and facilitates error tracing. This is invaluable for researchers and developers in identifying performance bottlenecks and directing future improvements more effectively.
- **Informing Design Choices for TOG Solutions:** The comprehensive comparative analysis between one-shot and zero-shot learning is perhaps the most significant conceptual contribution. It provides crucial insights into the trade-offs:
 - **One-shot learning (e.g., OS-TOG)** offers higher accuracy, especially for fine-grained subcategory differentiation and robustness in cluttered multi-object scenes. This makes it highly suitable for industrial settings (e.g., manufacturing, assembly) where precision is paramount, and obtaining a single visual reference for new components is feasible.
 - **Zero-shot learning (e.g., Binary-TOG)** reduces the manual annotation effort by relying on textual descriptions, making it more practical for domestic or assistive applications where user convenience and ease of describing objects are critical, even with a slight trade-off in accuracy or sensitivity to subtle object variations.
 - This nuanced understanding will guide researchers and practitioners in selecting the most appropriate generalization strategy based on their specific application domain, data availability, and performance requirements.

#### 5.3. Impact of the Subcategory Differentiation Challenge

- **Real-World Relevance:** The introduction of the subcategory differentiation challenge is highly significant. It addresses a gap in existing evaluations by focusing on a capability essential for many real-world scenarios (e.g., distinguishing different types of screws for assembly, or personalized items in assistive robotics).
- **Highlighting Limitations and Driving Innovation:** This new challenge effectively exposed limitations in both one-shot and zero-shot approaches, particularly their struggles with visually similar subcategories. This motivates future research into incorporating complementary information, such as textual recognition components for brand names or semantic object properties (e.g., size, weight), to enhance fine-grained differentiation.

#### 5.4. Future Directions and Broader Impact

The identified future work, such as extending to 6D grasp poses, incorporating semantic object properties for improved stability, exploring alternative end-effectors, and investigating zero-shot affordance recognition, outlines a clear roadmap for advancing the field. This research lays a robust foundation for developing more intelligent, adaptable, and robust robotic systems capable of performing a wider array of complex manipulation tasks in dynamic, real-world environments. The public release of the dataset, code, and models will undoubtedly accelerate research and innovation within the robotics community.
