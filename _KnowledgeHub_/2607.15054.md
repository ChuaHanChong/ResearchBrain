# Beyond Single Expert: Harmonizing Diverse Visual Priors in MLLMs for Spatial Understanding

> [!summary] Summary
> ViPS, a framework developed at The University of Hong Kong, enhances Multimodal Large Language Models' spatial understanding by efficiently integrating diverse visual priors from multiple foundation models. It utilizes lightweight proxy networks to approximate distinct expert knowledge and a dynamic fusion mechanism to adaptively combine these priors, improving performance on both spatial reasoning and 3D understanding benchmarks with minimal additional inference cost.

## Problem

- Existing MLLM approaches for spatial understanding predominantly rely on prior knowledge extracted from a single external foundation model.
- Different foundation models possess distinct specializations in capturing various aspects of spatial information, which single-expert integration fails to fully leverage.
- Directly incorporating multiple heavy foundation models for prior extraction would incur prohibitive computational overhead during inference.

## Method

- The ==Efficient Prior Proxy== component uses a single robust base vision encoder and lightweight ==MLP proxy networks== to efficiently generate multiple distinct prior representations without running full foundation models during inference.
- The ==Dynamic Prior Fusion== mechanism employs ==zero-initialized convolutions== for stable integration and a ==context-aware weighting== scheme (based on the text query) to adaptively combine these diverse priors and inject them into the MLLM.
- The framework integrates knowledge from five diverse foundation models (VGGT, DepthAnything3, TraceAnything, Wan2.1, RADIO) to cover a broad spectrum of spatial information.

## Results

- ViPS achieved an average accuracy of **63.8%** on the VSI-Bench for spatial reasoning tasks, outperforming the previous state-of-the-art spatial-enhanced model (VLM-3R) by **6.6 percentage points**.
- The framework demonstrated leading performance on 3D spatial understanding benchmarks, including **64.6%** Acc@0.25 on ScanRefer and new top scores on embodied question answering tasks (**107.9 CIDEr** on ScanQA and **62.5% EM** on SQA3D).
- Ablation studies confirmed that the full ViPS framework, with its dynamic multi-prior injection and efficient proxy networks, consistently yielded the highest scores across metrics while maintaining an inference cost comparable to using only a single prior.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Diverse foundation models specialize in distinct, complementary aspects of spatial information, confirming the value of a multi-expert integration approach over single-expert methods.
> - Lightweight proxy networks can effectively distill and emulate the unique knowledge of multiple heavy foundation models, enabling efficient multi-prior integration with a negligible increase in inference cost.
> - Context-aware dynamic weighting and zero-initialized convolutions are critical for harmoniously integrating heterogeneous prior features, ensuring stable training and preventing performance degradation from disparate feature distributions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) represent a significant advancement in artificial intelligence, demonstrating capabilities in bridging the reasoning prowess of Large Language Models (LLMs) with sensory inputs, particularly visual and point-cloud data. This integration has shown substantial promise in complex domains such as spatial understanding, which is fundamental for applications like robotic navigation and embodied intelligence.

A recognized approach to enhance the spatial awareness of MLLMs involves incorporating prior knowledge extracted from pre-trained foundation models. Existing research in this area typically focuses on leveraging features from a *single* external expert model, either as auxiliary inputs or through alignment of latent representations via distillation or feature injection. While this paradigm has proven effective in augmenting MLLMs with domain-specific knowledge, it implicitly assumes that a single expert can provide all necessary spatial insights.

This paper identifies a limitation in this prevailing "single-expert" approach. It posits that different foundation models, having been trained on varied data and objectives, encapsulate distinct types of spatial priors. Consequently, these models likely offer complementary advantages across diverse spatial understanding tasks. The presented work directly addresses this gap by investigating how to effectively and synergistically integrate knowledge from *multiple* diverse foundation models into MLLMs. By proposing a framework that harmonizes these complementary strengths, the authors aim to move beyond the limitations of single-expert systems, thus contributing to the development of more comprehensive and robust MLLMs for spatial reasoning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from an observation that existing MLLM spatial understanding approaches predominantly rely on prior knowledge from a single foundation model. The authors hypothesize that different foundation models possess distinct specializations in capturing various aspects of spatial information. This hypothesis led to two core questions that frame the objectives of the paper:

1. **Do different foundation models exhibit distinct specializations across various spatial understanding tasks when serving as priors for MLLMs?**
2. **How can expert knowledge from multiple foundation models be synergistically integrated into MLLMs?**

To address the first question, the authors conducted an empirical study. They integrated priors from a diverse set of five foundation models—VGGT, DepthAnything3, TraceAnything, Wan2.1, and RADIO—into MLLMs. These models were selected for their varied training paradigms and the distinct representations they are expected to capture (e.g., general visual-geometric features, precise monocular depth cues, object-centric motion, spatio-temporal dynamics, and robust generic visual representations). The evaluation spanned a wide range of spatial understanding tasks, including fine-grained spatial reasoning benchmarks like VSI-Bench and 3D spatial understanding benchmarks from the ScanNet-series (ScanRefer, Multi3DRefer, Scan2Cap, ScanQA, SQA3D).

The results of this preliminary study provided the motivation for the proposed framework. Visualizations of prior features using t-SNE demonstrated that representations from distinct foundation models clustered into disparate regions in the latent space, indicating unique information encoding. Furthermore, activation heatmaps revealed that different models focused on varying structural and semantic cues within identical scenes. Critically, performance evaluations across tasks showed that no single foundation model consistently achieved optimal performance across all metrics; instead, different models exhibited strengths in different sub-tasks. This empirical evidence confirmed the hypothesis that diverse visual priors are highly complementary, underscoring the necessity for a multi-model integration approach.

Having established the value of diverse priors, the authors then considered the challenge of their integration (the second core question). A straightforward approach, such as a naive ensemble of multiple foundation models, was identified as problematic due to two main drawbacks:
- **Prohibitive Computational Overhead:** Extracting priors from each foundation model would require an independent forward pass during inference, leading to a linear increase in computational cost with the number of integrated models. This limitation hinders scalability and real-world deployment.
- **Ineffective Prioritization and Potential Confusion:** A simple ensemble would not prioritize the most relevant prior knowledge for a given task. Moreover, the inherent distribution disparities among features from different foundation models could confuse the MLLM, potentially degrading overall performance rather than enhancing it.

Motivated by these empirical findings and identified challenges, the paper proposes ViPS (Visual Priors for Spatial understanding), a novel multi-model prior framework designed to efficiently extract and adaptively harmonize diverse prior knowledge from multiple foundation models for comprehensive spatial reasoning within MLLMs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed ViPS framework is designed to integrate diverse visual priors into MLLMs efficiently and harmoniously. It comprises two main components: the Efficient Prior Proxy and the Dynamic Prior Fusion mechanism.

**Overall Framework Structure:**
Upon receiving a visual input (e.g., a video clip or multi-view images) and a textual task instruction, the framework first processes the visual sequence through a standard vision encoder (e.g., SigLIP), generating base visual embeddings. These embeddings, along with the textual instruction, are then processed by the MLLM. ViPS augments this process by introducing additional prior knowledge.

**1. Efficient Prior Proxy:**
The objective of this component is to generate multiple foundational priors without incurring the computational cost of running multiple full foundation models independently during inference.
- **Base Model and Unified Representation:** A single robust vision encoder serves as a "base model" (E_base), which is typically one of the chosen foundation models (e.g., VGGT). This base model processes the visual input to extract a unified foundational representation (F_base). This representation is intended to contain sufficient low- and mid-level visual and geometric features shared across various specialized models.
- **Lightweight Proxy Networks:** To approximate the distinct knowledge of K target foundation models (E_1..., E_K), K lightweight proxy networks (ϕ_1..., ϕ_K) are instantiated. Each proxy is implemented as a simple Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP). Each ϕ_k takes the unified F_base as input and processes it to generate an estimated prior representation F^k_prior, specific to the k-th foundation model.
- **Alignment Loss during Training:** To ensure the fidelity of these estimated priors, the proxy networks are supervised during training. Ground-truth prior features (F^k_gt) are obtained by running the original K distinct foundation models (E_k) on the visual input. An L2 loss (L_alignment) is then applied to enforce strict alignment between each proxy's output (F^k_prior) and its corresponding ground-truth prior (F^k_gt).
- **Efficiency Rationale:** This design leverages the insight that different foundation models share common underlying visual semantics. By distilling distinct high-level prior semantics from a robust base representation using shallow MLPs, the framework avoids the computational bottleneck of K independent forward passes of heavy foundation models during inference. This results in an inference cost comparable to using a single foundation model.

**2. Dynamic Prior Fusion:**
This component is responsible for harmoniously integrating the diverse, efficiently generated priors (F^1_prior..., F^K_prior) into the MLLM in a context-aware manner.
- **Zero-Initialized Convolutions:** Direct addition of heterogeneous priors can confuse the MLLM, particularly in early training stages due to disparate feature distributions. To address this, ViPS introduces independent zero-initialized convolutional layers (ZeroConv_k) for each prior branch. Initially, these convolutions output zeros, preserving the MLLM's original representations. As training progresses, they gradually learn to scale up the prior injection, ensuring progressive and stable integration without disrupting early learning. The processed prior is denoted as F̃^k_prior = ZeroConv_k(F^k_prior).
- **Context-Aware Weighting Mechanism:** To adaptively prioritize relevant priors for a given task, a dynamic weighting mechanism is employed. The representation of the final token of the input text query (x_text_last) is extracted, as it encapsulates the aggregated semantics of the instruction. This context vector is fed into an MLP (MLP_weight) to compute K-dimensional logits. A Softmax activation is then applied to these logits to obtain normalized fusion weights (w), where each w_k corresponds to the importance of the k-th prior.
- **Harmonized Multi-Expert Prior:** The dynamic weights are used to linearly combine the processed priors (F̃^k_prior), yielding the overall harmonized multi-expert prior (F̂_prior = Σ w_k ⋅ F̃^k_prior).
- **Injection into MLLM:** This fused representation (F̂_prior) is then element-wise added to the corresponding projected visual embeddings (image tokens) of the MLLM. The paper specifies that this dynamic prior fusion mechanism is uniformly applied across five layers of the MLLM, ensuring deep integration of diverse prior knowledge.

**Implementation Details:**
The base MLLMs used include Qwen2-VL-7B and Qwen3-VL-8B. The vision encoder is initialized with SigLIP. The five foundation models for priors are VGGT, DepthAnything3, TraceAnything, Wan2.1, and RADIO, with VGGT serving as the default base model for the Efficient Prior Proxy. Video inputs are uniformly sampled to 32 frames. Training involves two phases: first, training the Efficient Prior Proxy with the alignment loss, and then freezing the proxies and fine-tuning the MLLM using Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) for the LLM backbone.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The ViPS framework was evaluated on a comprehensive set of spatial understanding benchmarks, encompassing both fine-grained spatial reasoning and 3D spatial understanding tasks.

**Spatial Reasoning on VSI-Bench:**
- ViPS achieved an average accuracy of 63.8% across the eight spatial reasoning sub-tasks (Object Counting, Absolute Distance, Object Size, Room Size, Relative Distance, Relative Direction, Route Planning, Appearance Order).
- This performance surpassed the previous state-of-the-art spatial-enhanced model, VLM-3R, which scored 57.2%.
- ViPS demonstrated leading performance in most categories, including Object Counting (71.5%), Absolute Distance (60.8%), Object Size (70.7%), Room Size (71.6%), Relative Distance (65.5%), Relative Direction (84.7%), and Route Planning (51.0%). While it slightly trailed VG-LLM-8B in "Appearance Order," its overall dominance across a broad spectrum of reasoning tasks validated the benefits of integrating multiple priors dynamically.

**3D Spatial Understanding on ScanNet-series Benchmarks:**
ViPS was evaluated across five ScanNet-series benchmarks covering 3D visual grounding (ScanRefer, Multi3DRefer), dense captioning (Scan2Cap), and embodied question answering (ScanQA, SQA3D).
- **Visual Grounding:** ViPS achieved a top score of 64.6% Acc@0.25 on ScanRefer and 62.0% F1@0.25 on Multi3DRefer, outperforming models like Vega-3D.
- **Dense Captioning:** ViPS achieved 85.5 CIDEr@0.5 on Scan2Cap, remaining highly competitive despite slightly trailing 3DRS (86.1 CIDEr@0.5).
- **Embodied Question Answering:** ViPS set new leading performance with 107.9 CIDEr and 31.6% EM on ScanQA, and 62.5% EM on SQA3D, surpassing Vega-3D and other generalist 3D-LLMs.
These results indicated that ViPS effectively harnesses diverse foundation priors for complex 3D spatial understanding tasks, confirming its robustness across various downstream applications.

**Ablation Studies:**
- **Effectiveness of Individual Visual Priors:** Experiments showed that introducing each individual foundation model prior (e.g., RADIO, DepthAnything3, TraceAnything, VGGT, Wan2.1) yielded distinct performance gains over a baseline MLLM without prior injection. The full ViPS framework, integrating all five priors, consistently achieved the highest scores across all metrics, confirming the effectiveness and strong complementarity of harmonizing heterogeneous visual knowledge.
- **Effectiveness of Dynamic Prior Injection:**
 - **Without Zero-initialized Convolutions ("w/o Zero-init"):** Removing the zero-initialized convolutions resulted in a severe performance drop compared to the full ViPS. This confirmed that zero-initialization is crucial for stable and progressive prior injection, preventing the diverse and potentially misaligned prior distributions from disrupting the MLLM during early training.
 - **Vanilla Addition (Static Fusion):** Replacing the dynamic, context-aware weighting with a simple static feature summation degraded performance. This validated the efficacy of the dynamic weighting mechanism in adapting prior selection to the input query.
- **Effectiveness of Efficient Prior Proxy:**
 - **Comparison to Ground-Truth Priors ("w/ GT Priors"):** An upper-bound setting, where ground-truth features from all five foundation models were directly used (incurring ~5x parameter overhead and inference latency), yielded marginally higher performance than ViPS. However, ViPS achieved nearly comparable performance with only a 1x inference cost and parameter overhead, demonstrating its efficiency and accuracy. The reported estimation error (0.252 in cosine distance) between estimated and ground-truth priors was minimal.
 - **Without Alignment Loss ("w/o L_alignment"):** Even without the explicit L2 alignment loss, the prior proxies still provided considerable improvements over the baseline, suggesting that they inherently act as specialized projectors from the base representation into distinct feature spaces beneficial for the MLLM.

**Analysis of Dynamic Prior Weights:**
Visualization of the learned fusion weights revealed that ViPS dynamically adapted its reliance on different foundation models based on the specific downstream task (e.g., ScanQA, ScanRefer) and even across distinct question types within the same dataset (e.g., VSI-Bench). This confirmed the framework's ability to perform context-aware prior selection, aligning with the initial motivation that different models specialize in different types of spatial information.

**Robustness to Base Model Choice (Appendix):**
Further experiments in the appendix demonstrated that substituting the default VGGT base model in the Efficient Prior Proxy with other foundation models (e.g., Wan, TraceAnything, DepthAnything, RADIO) yielded comparable and consistently strong performance. This indicated the robustness and flexibility of the ViPS framework, showing it is not strictly dependent on a single specific base model choice.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The ViPS framework represents a methodological advancement in enhancing the spatial understanding capabilities of MLLMs. Its significance is multifaceted:

- **Empirical Validation of Prior Complementarity:** The work empirically demonstrates that diverse foundation models offer distinct and complementary spatial knowledge, challenging the efficacy of single-expert prior injection paradigms. This finding provides a new direction for integrating specialized vision models into MLLMs.
- **Efficiency in Multi-Prior Integration:** The Efficient Prior Proxy addresses a critical computational bottleneck associated with leveraging multiple heavy foundation models. By distilling knowledge into lightweight proxies, ViPS enables the integration of numerous priors with minimal inference overhead, comparable to using a single encoder. This efficiency is crucial for deploying sophisticated MLLMs in practical applications.
- **Harmonious and Context-Aware Fusion:** The Dynamic Prior Fusion mechanism, with its context-aware weighting and zero-initialized convolutions, provides a robust method for integrating heterogeneous priors. It ensures that the MLLM receives the most relevant spatial information for a given task, while maintaining training stability and preventing confusion from disparate feature distributions.
- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** By successfully harmonizing diverse visual priors, ViPS establishes new state-of-the-art performance across multiple complex spatial reasoning and 3D spatial understanding benchmarks, indicating its generalizability and effectiveness.

**Potential Impact:**
The advancements introduced by ViPS have the potential for significant positive impacts across various domains:
- **Enhanced Embodied AI and Robotics:** More accurate and comprehensive spatial understanding can lead to the development of more intelligent and capable embodied agents, such as autonomous robots that can navigate complex real-world environments, perform intricate manipulation tasks, and interact more naturally with humans based on a deeper understanding of their surroundings.
- **Accessibility and Assistive Technologies:** The ability to efficiently perceive and reason about 3D spaces could be transformative for assistive applications, potentially aiding visually impaired individuals in navigating unfamiliar environments more safely and independently.
- **Resource-Constrained Deployments:** The efficiency gains from the Efficient Prior Proxy could enable the deployment of advanced 3D perception systems on edge devices with limited computational resources, broadening the accessibility and applicability of MLLMs in real-world scenarios.

**Limitations and Ethical Considerations:**
While promising, the technology also presents potential ethical concerns. The highly accurate 3D spatial understanding capabilities could be misused for unauthorized surveillance or the invasive reconstruction of private spaces, posing significant privacy risks.
- **Mitigation:** The authors suggest that future real-world applications must incorporate robust anonymization pipelines (e.g., obfuscating human faces and sensitive documents) and enforce strict access controls, transparent user consent mechanisms, and adherence to data protection regulations (e.g., GDPR) to safeguard individual privacy.

**Future Work:**
The authors acknowledge several limitations that could be addressed in future research:
- **Scaling:** Current experiments are constrained by computational resources, limiting the base MLLM size and training data volume. Scaling up these aspects could lead to further performance enhancements.
- **Internalization of Geometric Knowledge:** Relying on external visual priors is considered a suboptimal workaround. A more fundamental approach would be to internalize geometric knowledge directly during the MLLM pre-training phase through more diverse spatial datasets, fine-grained 3D sub-tasks, and tailored loss functions.
- **Real-World Validation:** The current evaluations are confined to offline benchmarks. Testing and validating spatial-aware MLLMs in real-world robotics and physical embodied AI scenarios remains a crucial next step.
