# One-Policy-Fits-All: Geometry-Aware Action Latents for Cross-Embodiment Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> The One-Policy-Fits-All (OPFA) framework introduces a method to learn a single visuomotor policy capable of cross-embodiment manipulation across diverse robotic end-effectors, including simple grippers and complex dexterous hands. It achieves this by developing a geometry-aware latent representation and a unified decoder, demonstrating robust generalization and few-shot learning capabilities in both simulated and real-world tasks.

## Problem

- Robotic manipulation data is inherently coupled with specific end-effector morphology and kinematics, leading to significant variations in action spaces and hindering policy generalization across different robots.
- The substantial cost and time required to collect new, embodiment-specific data for each new robot limits scalability and creates data scarcity for learning robust manipulation skills.
- Existing cross-embodiment learning approaches for multifingered hands often rely on embodiment-specific decoders or human motion priors, which restrict data efficiency and can introduce action conflicts due to structural discrepancies.

## Method

- A Geometry-Aware Latent Representation (GaLR) is learned in an annotation-free manner by encoding the end-effector's 3D point cloud using multi-scale local structure encoding with KPConv and global gesture perception via a geometric transformer with semantic positional embeddings.
- A unified decoder, distinct from embodiment-specific decoders, predicts joint angles for a hypothetical 'universal hand model,' from which only the physically existing joints are selected for a specific embodiment, optimized end-to-end with the GaLR training framework.
- The learned GaLR is integrated into a visuomotor policy (e.g., Diffusion Policy 3) to enable cross-embodiment co-training, where the policy learns to map visual observations directly to the GaLR representation of target actions.

## Results

- OPFA demonstrated strong generalization across 9 end-effectors on a spray-picking task, showing an average improvement of 19.8% in success rate over 'Naive Co-training' in spatial generalization scenarios.
- Exhibited robust few-shot learning ability, achieving high success rates (e.g., XHand surpassed 80%) with only 8 new trajectories for a banana-picking task, outperforming baselines that struggled with limited data or faced training conflicts.
- Validated in real-world experiments with 4 different end-effectors across 7 diverse tasks, consistently achieving high success rates and outperforming baseline methods, particularly on challenging long-horizon and fine-motor control tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Directly encoding the end-effector's geometric structure into a latent action representation effectively decouples the visuomotor policy from specific physical morphologies, enabling joint training across a wide range of robot hands.
> - A unified decoder that predicts actions for a conceptual 'universal hand model' and then retargets them to specific embodiments allows for shared knowledge transfer without the need for separate, less data-efficient decoders.
> - The use of semantic positional embeddings within the geometric transformer enhances global gesture perception by providing a consistent structural understanding across diverse end-effector designs, aiding in robust latent space learning.
