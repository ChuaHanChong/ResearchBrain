# TAR: Teacher-Aligned Representations via Contrastive Learning for Quadrupedal Locomotion

> [!summary] Summary
> A novel framework, Teacher-Aligned Representations (TAR), leverages contrastive learning and privileged information to train quadrupedal locomotion policies for proprioceptive-only robots. This method achieves accelerated training and superior generalization in out-of-distribution scenarios, demonstrating effective zero-shot sim-to-real transfer and robust performance on a Unitree Go2 robot.

## Problem

- Existing teacher-student paradigms for quadrupedal locomotion face representation misalignment between the privileged teacher and proprioceptive-only student due to differing sensory inputs.
- Behavioral cloning methods often suffer from covariate shift and fail to generalize effectively to out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios, even with memory-augmented architectures.
- Policies trained in simulation struggle with real-world adaptation and continual fine-tuning due to the unavailability of privileged information during deployment, hindering long-term autonomy.

## Method

- The **Teacher-Aligned Representations (TAR)** framework integrates ==self-supervised contrastive learning== with ==privileged information== to guide the student's latent representation learning, moving beyond direct feature regression.
- It employs a ==triplet loss== formulation where the teacher's next-state latent serves as an anchor, a ==forward dynamics model== predicts the student's positive sample, and a ==task-informed negative sampling== strategy from diverse environmental conditions is utilized.
- The architecture is designed for deployability, ==removing the teacher encoder post-simulation== to enable continual learning and fine-tuning with only proprioceptive inputs in real-world settings.

## Results

- TAR achieved peak performance in approximately **50% less training time** compared to state-of-the-art baselines and surpassed a fully privileged Teacher model by **4.1%** in return.
- Demonstrated superior generalization in OOD scenarios, achieving a **74% lower** combined error than the expert Teacher and maintaining robust performance from **5,000 to 12,500 iterations** without degradation.
- Achieved successful zero-shot sim-to-real transfer on a Unitree Go2 robot, completing trials across diverse terrains, with payloads up to **12 kg**, absorbing **150 N** lateral pushes, and navigating steps, outperforming vendor-provided controllers.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Leveraging privileged information through self-supervised contrastive learning can create robust, physics-grounded latent representations for proprioceptive-only policies, sensitive to physical variations while preserving gait periodicity.
> - Task-informed negative sampling, combined with privileged information, is crucial for structuring the latent space, making representations discriminative and enhancing generalization to OOD scenarios.
> - Modular architecture design, where gradient flow from the actor policy is blocked from the student encoder and velocity estimator, helps prevent representation collapse and promotes stable learning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of quadrupedal locomotion control has significantly advanced through the application of Reinforcement Learning (RL), enabling robots to learn complex and adaptive motion skills. A common paradigm in this area is the teacher-student framework, designed to address the challenge of partial observability inherent in real-world robotic control. In this setup, a "teacher" policy is trained with privileged information (e.g., full environmental state, internal robot parameters), and a "student" policy subsequently learns to mimic the teacher's behavior using only proprioceptive (internal body) sensory inputs, which are the only data available during real-world deployment.

However, existing teacher-student approaches face several limitations. A primary concern is the representation misalignment between the teacher's latent space, which benefits from rich privileged observations, and the student's latent space, which must infer critical information from limited proprioceptive data. This misalignment often arises when the student is trained via behavioral cloning, leading to discrepancies that can accumulate and degrade performance, a phenomenon known as covariate shift. Furthermore, while techniques such as N-step observation history or memory-augmented architectures (e.g., GRUs, LSTMs, TCNs) enhance temporal modeling, they frequently prove insufficient for reconstructing the necessary latent representations when faced with out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios. The simulation-to-reality (Sim2Real) gap also poses a persistent challenge, despite efforts in domain randomization. Critically, the absence of privileged information in real-world deployment hinders long-term autonomy and adaptive fine-tuning of the policy.

This research contributes to the landscape by proposing an alternative to direct feature regression in teacher-student learning. It integrates self-supervised contrastive learning with privileged information to develop robust, task-relevant latent spaces for the proprioceptive-only student. This approach aims to address the aforementioned issues by improving representation alignment, enhancing generalization capabilities to OOD scenarios, and enabling continual learning in real-world settings without requiring privileged observations post-simulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this work stems from the identified limitations within current teacher-student paradigms for quadrupedal locomotion via RL. These limitations include:

1. **Representation Misalignment:** The fundamental difference in sensory input available to the teacher (privileged, exteroceptive, and latent state variables) versus the student (proprioceptive-only) leads to distinct latent representations. When a student policy is trained through behavioral cloning, it attempts to regress to a feature space constructed using richer modalities, which can result in a mismatch and poor generalization when deployed in the real world.
2. **Covariate Shift:** Direct behavioral cloning can cause small errors in the student's trajectory to compound over time during deployment. This divergence from the teacher's optimal behavior leads to performance degradation, especially in dynamic and varied environments.
3. **Lack of Deployable Adaptation:** Once trained in simulation, policies often struggle to adapt or fine-tune on physical robots because the privileged information used during training is unavailable. This restricts the robot's ability for long-term autonomy and continuous learning in unstructured real-world environments.
4. **Limited Generalization to OOD Scenarios:** While domain randomization helps bridge the Sim2Real gap, it often operates within limited parameter ranges. Existing methods, even with memory-augmented architectures, frequently fail to reconstruct robust latent representations necessary for unseen and challenging OOD scenarios.

To address these challenges, the key objectives of this research are:

- **To propose an efficient representation alignment method:** The goal is to leverage privileged information in simulation to guide self-supervised representation learning for the student. This alignment aims to mitigate representation misalignment without direct teacher-student regression, thereby improving sample efficiency and enhancing generalization to OOD scenarios.
- **To develop a robust adaptation and negative sampling strategy:** This objective involves incorporating a task-informed negative sampling strategy during contrastive learning. The aim is to improve the structure of the latent space, making representations more discriminative and robust to environmental variations, further supported by privileged information.
- **To enable deployable and off-policy compatible learning:** The final objective is to design an architecture that eliminates the reliance on privileged observations after simulation training. This would allow student representations to be aligned through contrastive learning, facilitating real-world fine-tuning and continual adaptation, making the policies suitable for long-term autonomous deployment.

By achieving these objectives, the authors aim to improve the generalization capabilities, training efficiency, and real-world adaptability of quadrupedal locomotion policies.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, Teacher-Aligned Representations via Contrastive Learning (TAR), is designed to train sample-efficient quadrupedal locomotion policies that generalize from simulation to reality. The methodology is structured in two main phases: simulation training with privileged information and real-world deployment with continual learning.

**A. Reinforcement Learning Formulation**
The policy is trained using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), a standard on-policy reinforcement learning algorithm.

- **State Space (`s_t`)**:
 - **Proprioceptive-only input (`O_t`)**: This input, along with a recurrent hidden state (`h_{t-1}`), is fed to the policy network. It includes base angular velocity, projected gravity, linear velocity commands, joint positions, joint velocities, and previous actions. This recurrent state is intended to capture temporal dependencies.
 - **Privileged information (`O_priv_t`)**: This expanded input is used by the critic network during training. It includes all policy inputs at a single timestep, augmented with base linear velocity, height scan, base external force, foot contact states, contact friction coefficient, and payload mass.
- **Action Space (`a_t`)**: Represents the target joint torques applied to the robot's actuators.
- **Reward Function and Domain Randomization**: The training utilizes a specific reward function and applies domain randomization (varying friction, restitution, payload, external forces, joint initialization) to enhance sim-to-real transfer.

**B. Training Framework with Privileged Information (Simulation Phase)**
The core of the TAR framework (depicted in Figure 3) involves a teacher encoder, a student encoder, a forward dynamics model, and a velocity estimator, all operating within a PPO-based training loop.

1. **PPO-Based Policy Optimization (`L_PPO`)**:
 - The actor and critic networks are optimized using the PPO objective.
 - The actor policy (`π`) is conditioned on the student's latent representation (`Z_S_t`) and an estimated base velocity (`ˆv_t`).
 - **Velocity Estimator (`f_V`)**: A learned model that estimates base velocity (`ˆv_t`) from the student's latent (`Z_S_t`) and a 4-step history of proprioceptive observations (`O_{t-3:t}`). It is trained using a Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss (`L_vel`) against the ground-truth velocity (`v_true_t`). To prevent interference with policy learning, gradients from the estimator are blocked from propagating to the policy.

2. **Contrastive Representation Learning with Triplet Loss (`L_triplet`)**:
 - Instead of direct regression, a triplet loss formulation is used to enforce structured representation learning for the student.
 - **Anchor (`Z_T_{t+1}`)**: The latent representation of the next state generated by the *teacher encoder* (`f_T`) from *privileged information* (`S_{t+1}`).
 - **Positive Sample (`~Z+_{t+1}`)**: The student’s *predicted* next latent representation. This is generated by a forward dynamics model (`f_D`) that takes the student's current latent (`f_S(O_t, h_{t-1})`) and the current action (`a_t`) as input.
 - **Negative Sample (`Z-_{t+1}`)**: A latent representation sampled from rollouts under *different environmental conditions* (e.g., varying friction, mass, perturbations) from the replay buffer. This is a task-informed negative sampling strategy, ensuring semantic contrast and robustness to environmental dynamics.
 - The triplet loss pulls the student's next-state prediction (`~Z+_{t+1}`) closer to the teacher's informative latent anchor (`Z_T_{t+1}`) while pushing it away from the environmentally diverse negative sample (`Z-_{t+1}`).

3. **Student and Critic Encoder Optimization**:
 - The critic encoder is jointly optimized with the PPO value loss and the triplet loss (`L_critic = L_value + λ_{triplet}L_{triplet}`).
 - The student encoder is updated using gradients solely from the forward dynamics model via the triplet loss (`θ_S ← θ_S - λ∇_{θ_S}L_{triplet}`).
 - Gradient flow from the actor policy is explicitly blocked from reaching both the student encoder and the velocity estimator to prevent representation collapse and promote modular, stable learning.

**C. Deployable Architecture for Real-World Fine-Tuning (Deployment Phase)**
For real-world deployment and continual learning without privileged information (depicted in Figure 4), the teacher encoder is removed.

- **Self-Generated Samples**: Positive and negative samples are generated exclusively from the student encoder (`f_S`).
- **Positive Pair**: `Z+_{t+1} = f_S(O_{t-h+1:t+1})`.
- **Negative Sample**: `Z-_{t+1} = f_S(O^j_{t-h+1:t+1})`, where `j ≠ i` (i.e., from past rollouts of different agents or contexts).
- This design ensures temporal consistency in the latent space and enables continual learning and fine-tuning in dynamic, non-stationary real-world environments, making it compatible with off-policy RL algorithms like Soft Actor Critic (SAC).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research conducted evaluations in both simulation using NVIDIA Isaac Sim and real-world deployment on a Unitree Go2 robot.

**A. Training Performance Analysis**
The TAR method was compared against four of its variants and three state-of-the-art baselines: HIM, SLR, and a privileged "Teacher" model. A weighted metric combining normalized terrain levels, mean reward, and episode length was used for evaluation.

- **Accelerated Training**: TAR achieved peak performance in approximately 50% less training time compared to state-of-the-art baselines.
- **Superior Return**: TAR consistently demonstrated the highest performance, surpassing the fully privileged Teacher after 12,000 iterations and achieving a 4.1% higher return.
- **Variant Performance**: The MLP variant of TAR achieved comparable results, reaching 95.5% of TAR's final performance. HIM reached 63.9%, and SLR reached 58.7% of TAR's performance, respectively.

**B. Generalization and Robustness Evaluation**
Generalization was assessed using a composite metric across In-Distribution (ID) and Out-of-Distribution (OOD) test cases, combining normalized mean linear/angular velocity errors and failure counts. ID tests included normal ranges of friction and payload, while OOD tests involved higher payload mass (15 kg) and increased maximum linear velocity (2.0 m/s).

- **OOD Generalization**: TAR consistently outperformed all other variants, particularly in OOD scenarios. It achieved peak performance at 7,500 iterations, with a 74% lower combined error than the expert Teacher, 39.8% lower than SLR, and 42.2% lower than HIM.
- **Sustained Performance**: TAR maintained high performance from 5,000 to 12,500 iterations without degradation, indicating robust learning.
- **Component Contributions**: Ablation studies quantified the contribution of different TAR components:
 - Privileged information: Led to a 28.2% performance gain.
 - Improved negative sampling: Contributed an 8.0% boost.
 - Recurrent policy: Accounted for 3.8%.
 - Velocity estimation: Provided a 0.82% improvement.

**C. Impact of Privileged Information and Representation Learning**
A significant performance degradation in OOD settings was observed for methods trained without privileged information (SLR, Ours w/o Priv, and Ours w/o Priv Vel). This indicates that privileged information is crucial for developing robust policies capable of generalizing to unseen scenarios. However, models trained *exclusively* with privileged information (e.g., the 1-step MLP Teacher) did not outperform TAR, suggesting that self-supervised representation learning is essential for encoding environment dynamics and temporal dependencies effectively. TAR's approach of balancing privileged information with self-supervised learning resulted in improved training efficiency and generalization while preserving real-world adaptability.

**D. Encoder t-SNE Visualization**
T-SNE visualizations of the student encoder’s latent space demonstrated that the representations formed a ring-like manifold under isolated variations of friction, external force, and payload, even when these parameters were outside the training randomization ranges. This indicates that the encoder learned to preserve the periodic nature of the gait while remaining sensitive to meaningful physical variations, suggesting the development of physics-grounded embeddings rather than merely memorizing training distributions.

**E. Hardware Validation and Robustness Evaluation**
The trained policy was deployed on a Unitree Go2 robot and evaluated through various robustness tests:

- **Diverse Terrains**: Successfully traversed slippery flooring, high-friction rubber mats, deformable foam, coarse asphalt, and dense vegetation.
- **Payload Transport**: Maintained stability with payload masses ranging from 0 to 12 kg, approaching the robot's mechanical limits.
- **External Perturbations**: Absorbed lateral pushes up to 150 N while trotting at 1.0 m/s.
- **Step Navigation**: Navigated vertical steps from +30 cm to -60 cm.
- **Actuator Degradation (OOD)**: Maintained stability and performance even when the commanded torque of one joint was reduced to 10% of its nominal value.
- The policy consistently completed all trials with stable performance, avoiding joint torque saturation and emergency shutdowns. In contrast, the vendor-provided baseline controller showed degraded performance and was unable to handle several of these scenarios. The robot also demonstrated agile bidirectional stepping and maintained stability with partial leg obstruction. These results support strong sim-to-real generalization in a zero-shot setting.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework that addresses several challenges in applying reinforcement learning to quadrupedal locomotion, particularly concerning representation misalignment between privileged teachers and proprioceptive-only students, and the limitations in real-world generalization and adaptation.

The **significance** of TAR lies in its demonstration that leveraging privileged information through self-supervised contrastive learning can yield robust and generalizable locomotion policies. The findings indicate that this approach:

- **Enhances Training Efficiency**: By aligning representations via contrastive objectives, TAR achieved optimal performance in significantly less training time compared to existing methods, suggesting a more sample-efficient learning process.
- **Improves Generalization and Robustness**: The method demonstrated superior performance in both in-distribution and out-of-distribution scenarios, including extreme environmental conditions, varied payloads, and simulated hardware failures. This suggests that the learned latent spaces are more structured and discriminative, enabling the student policy to implicitly infer unobserved environmental dynamics.
- **Facilitates Real-World Adaptability**: A critical aspect is the architecture's ability to transition into a proprioceptive-only learning regime during deployment. This feature eliminates the post-simulation reliance on privileged observations, thereby enabling continual fine-tuning and adaptation in unstructured real-world environments, a capability that traditional teacher-student approaches often lack.
- **Provides Structured Latent Representations**: The t-SNE visualizations illustrate that the student encoder learns physics-grounded embeddings sensitive to relevant physical variations while preserving gait periodicity, which is indicative of a robust and meaningful representation of the robot's state and environment.

The **potential impact** of this work is multifaceted:

- **Advancement in Legged Robotics**: The framework contributes to the development of more autonomous and capable legged robots that can operate reliably in complex, unknown, and changing real-world environments without constant human intervention or re-training.
- **Improved Sim-to-Real Transfer**: By explicitly addressing representation misalignment and leveraging robust self-supervised learning, the method enhances the reliability of transferring policies from simulation to physical hardware in a zero-shot manner.
- **Foundations for Continual Learning**: The deployable architecture sets a precedent for long-term autonomous agents capable of adapting to novel conditions and continuously improving their performance throughout their operational lifespan.
- **Broader RL Applications**: The principles of teacher-aligned contrastive representation learning could potentially be extended to other robotic control tasks or domains where partial observability and sim-to-real transfer are significant challenges.

Future work outlined by the authors includes extending the framework to off-policy RL paradigms, integrating advanced fine-tuning strategies for real-world continual learning, and evaluating performance across diverse robotic morphologies, indicating a clear path for further development and broader applicability.
