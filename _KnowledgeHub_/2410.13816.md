# Steering Your Generalists: Improving Robotic Foundation Models via Value Guidance

> [!summary] Summary
> V-GPS re-ranks action proposals sampled from any frozen generalist robot policy using a language-conditioned Q-function trained via offline RL (Cal-QL), steering deployment-time behavior without fine-tuning. Applied to five policies across two embodiments and twelve tasks, it lifts real-world success by 82.8% on average.

## Problem

- Generalist policies trained on mixed-quality demonstration data inherit imprecise manipulation such as failed grasps and premature drops, despite strong semantic generalization.
- Fine-tuning a deployed generalist policy to fix precision issues needs costly teleoperated data collection and delicate hyperparameter tuning to avoid losing generality.
- Prior offline RL value functions require Gaussian policy heads or access to policy weights, so they cannot steer expressive black-box generalist architectures at test time.

## Method

- **Value-Guided Policy Steering (V-GPS)** trains a single language-conditioned ==Q-function== Q_theta(s,a,l) offline via ==Cal-QL==, using a ResNet-34 image encoder with ==FiLM conditioning== on frozen MUSE language embeddings.
- ==Sparse binary reward labeling== marks only the last H=3 transitions of each demonstration as success, converting unlabeled language-annotated trajectories into a form trainable by any ==offline RL== algorithm.
- ==Test-time action re-ranking== samples K candidate actions from the frozen generalist policy per step and selects one via ==temperature-controlled softmax== over the Q-function's scores, requiring no policy weight access.

## Results

- On **6** real WidowX tasks V-GPS raised Octo-small-1.5's average success rate from **0.24** to **0.44**, an **82.8%** relative improvement, with per-scene gains up to **+100%**.
- In SIMPLER simulation across **12** tasks, the same single value function improved all **five** tested policies (Octo-small/base/1.5, RT1-X, OpenVLA) on average success rate versus their unsteered baselines.
- On the eggplant-in-basket task requiring precise grasp and obstacle avoidance, V-GPS delivered the largest gains for every policy, and it also mitigated Octo-small-1.5's checkpoint-induced performance regression.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A single offline-trained value function can transfer across distinct policy architectures and embodiments as a plug-and-play test-time module, decoupling capability improvement from policy retraining.
> - Sampling-based re-ranking with a value function, effective for LLM reasoning, generalizes to multi-step real-world robotic control from raw pixels despite stochastic dynamics.
> - Precision failures like premature or delayed release concentrate value-function benefit at short critical decision windows, suggesting deployment-time correction is cheaper than fixing the base policy's whole trajectory distribution.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of "foundation models" in robotics, analogous to large language models (LLMs) and vision-language models (VLMs), represents a major paradigm shift. These generalist robotic policies, trained on massive and diverse datasets (e.g., Open-X-Embodiment, OXE), aim to control a variety of robots across a wide range of tasks and environments using a single model. This approach offers unprecedented generalization capabilities, enabling robots to acquire broad repertoires of manipulation skills and adapt to new instructions or scenes.

However, a significant challenge arises from the nature of the training data. Such large datasets are inherently of "mixed quality," often containing suboptimal or imperfect human demonstrations. While current state-of-the-art imitation learning methods (e.g., RT-X, Octo, OpenVLA) excel at replicating the *distribution* of these demonstrations and achieving strong *semantic generalization* (understanding what to do), they frequently fall short in terms of *precision* and *robustness*. Common failure modes include imprecise grasping, premature object release, or difficulty with environmental distribution shifts (e.g., changes in table texture or object properties).

Existing solutions to improve robotic policies from mixed-quality data include offline reinforcement learning (RL), which, in principle, can learn more optimal behaviors than pure imitation learning. However, traditional offline RL methods typically train or fine-tune a policy, often limiting its architecture or requiring complex hyperparameter tuning to integrate with expressive, pre-trained generalist models. Fine-tuning these massive generalist models is also computationally expensive, data-intensive (requiring new high-quality data for each adaptation), and can lead to "catastrophic forgetting" of their broad capabilities.

This paper addresses a crucial gap by proposing a method that enhances the precision and robustness of these pre-trained generalist policies *at deployment time*, without altering their weights or requiring costly fine-tuning. It draws inspiration from the success of sampling-based action selection and value-guided decoding in LLMs, where a separate "verifier" or value function re-ranks proposals from a generative model. While similar ideas have been explored in simulated robotic environments or for high-level robotic reasoning, this work is the first to demonstrate its effectiveness for low-level action selection in diverse, high-dimensional, *real-world* generalist robotic tasks. By offering a modular, "plug-and-play" solution, V-GPS fits into the landscape as a crucial post-training enhancement tool that can unlock the full potential of robotic foundation models in practical applications.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind V-GPS stems from the observed limitations of current generalist robotic policies:
- **Imprecision and Lack of Robustness:** Despite their impressive generalization to varied tasks and environments, generalist policies often struggle with fine-grained manipulation, such as executing precise grasps or accurately timing object releases. This leads to failures even when the policy semantically understands the task.
- **Sensitivity to Distribution Shifts:** These policies can be fragile when encountering minor, extraneous changes in the environment, such as variations in table texture or camera pose, which they might not have perfectly covered in their diverse but finite training data.
- **Impracticality of Fine-Tuning:** Refining generalist policies through fine-tuning is often infeasible due to several factors:
 - **Cost of Data Collection:** Each adaptation cycle requires collecting new, high-quality, task-specific data.
 - **Computational Expense:** Fine-tuning very large models (e.g., 7B parameters for OpenVLA) is prohibitively expensive.
 - **Loss of Generalist Capabilities:** Aggressive fine-tuning can lead to "catastrophic forgetting," where the model loses its broad generalization abilities.
 - **Black-Box Access:** Many powerful generalist models might only be accessible via an API, precluding direct weight manipulation.

Given these challenges, the **key objective** of this work is to develop a general, broadly applicable, and modular approach to enhance the performance of pre-trained generalist robot policies at deployment time. Specifically, the authors aim to:
- **Improve Precision and Robustness:** Address the observed failure modes related to imprecise manipulation and fragility to environmental shifts.
- **Preserve Generalist Capabilities:** Achieve improvements without requiring fine-tuning or even access to the internal weights of the generalist policy.
- **Enable Plug-and-Play Integration:** Design a method that can seamlessly combine with a wide range of existing, off-the-shelf generalist policies and work across different robotic embodiments.
- **Utilize Value Functions for Action Steering:** Demonstrate that re-ranking multiple action proposals from a generalist policy using a value function, a technique proven effective in LLMs, can be successfully adapted for multi-step robotic manipulation from raw pixel observations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, **Value-Guided Policy Steering (V-GPS)**, operates in two main phases: training a language-conditioned value function via offline RL and then deploying it to re-rank actions from any generalist policy at test time.

#### 3.1. Training: Learning Value Function via Offline RL Pre-training

The core of V-GPS is a language-conditioned Q-function, $Q_\theta(s, a, l)$, which predicts the expected long-term discounted return for taking action $a$ in state $s$ given a language instruction $l$.
1. **Reward Function Design:** Since standard robotic datasets lack explicit reward annotations, the authors devise a sparse binary reward scheme. The last $H=3$ transitions of each successful demonstration trajectory are assigned a positive reward (+1, later shifted to 0 for training stability), indicating task completion. All other transitions receive a negative reward (0, later shifted to -1). This encourages the value function to identify actions leading to task success.
2. **Offline RL Algorithm:** The value function is trained using an offline RL algorithm designed to learn from static, pre-collected datasets. The primary choice is **Cal-QL (Calibrated Q-learning)**. Cal-QL is selected for two key reasons:
 - It aims to approximate the "best in support" optimal value function, which is crucial for effective action re-ranking.
 - Its conservative objective makes it robust to out-of-distribution (OOD) actions, a desirable property when evaluating diverse action proposals from a generalist policy.
 - (In Appendix A, IQL is also shown to be effective, demonstrating flexibility in algorithm choice.)
3. **Model Architecture:** The Q-function $Q_\theta(s, a, l)$ employs a **ResNet-34** as its image encoder. Language conditioning is achieved using **FiLM (Feature-wise Linear Modulation)**, where a frozen **MUSE encoder** processes the language instruction and modulates the features at every block of the ResNet. The final image features are concatenated with the 7-dimensional action vector and passed through a multi-layer perceptron (MLP) to output the scalar Q-value.
4. **Training Data:** To ensure generalizability across different robots and tasks, the value function is pre-trained on a mixed dataset comprising **Bridge V2** and **Fractal** datasets. These datasets include a wide variety of language-annotated manipulation demonstrations from different environments and robot embodiments (e.g., WidowX, Google Robot).

#### 3.2. Deployment: Test-Time Action Re-Ranking

Once the value function $Q_\theta$ is trained, it is used to "steer" any pre-trained generalist policy $\pi$ at deployment time without needing to modify $\pi$'s weights.
1. **Action Proposal Generation:** At each time step, given the current observation $s_t$ and language prompt $l$, the generalist policy $\pi$ is queried to sample $K$ diverse action candidates, $\{a_1, \ldots, a_K\}$. This requires the policy to be stochastic or capable of generating multiple samples (e.g., diffusion models or by re-running inference).
2. **Value-Guided Re-Ranking:** Each of the $K$ proposed actions is then evaluated by the trained Q-function $Q_\theta(s_t, a_i, l)$, yielding a Q-value score for each action.
3. **Action Selection and Execution:** The final action $a_t$ to be executed is chosen based on these Q-values. The method offers flexibility:
 - **Greedy Selection:** $a_t = \arg\max_{a_i} Q_\theta(s_t, a_i, l)$, picking the action with the highest estimated value.
 - **Softmax Sampling:** $a_t \sim \text{Softmax}\left(\frac{Q_\theta(s_t, a_1)}{\beta}, \ldots, \frac{Q_\theta(s_t, a_K)}{\beta}\right)$, where $\beta$ is a temperature parameter controlling the sharpness of the distribution, allowing a balance between exploration and exploitation.

This modular design means V-GPS can operate with generalist policies even if they are black-box (API-only access), as long as they can propose multiple actions. The same single value function is used across all policies and embodiments, highlighting its broad applicability.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of V-GPS is extensive, covering both real-world and simulated environments, multiple robotic embodiments, and a diverse set of tasks, using five state-of-the-art generalist policies.

#### 4.1. Addressing Failure Modes of Generalist Policies

- **Real-world Evaluation (WidowX Robot, 6 tasks across 3 scenes):** V-GPS consistently and significantly improved the performance of the Octo-small-1.5 policy.
 - **Imprecise Grasping:** For the "put pepper in pot" task, where precision is critical due to a slippery object, V-GPS doubled the success rate from 15% to 35%.
 - **Untimely Object Release:** In tasks like "put mushroom on cloth," Octo-small-1.5 often released objects prematurely. V-GPS increased success from 35% to 70% by guiding the policy to hold the object until correctly positioned. Similarly, for "put sushi in pot," where Octo tended to hold too long, V-GPS tripled success from 10% to 30%.
 - **Overall Real-World Improvement:** V-GPS achieved an impressive **+82.8% average improvement** in success rates across all 6 real-world tasks.

#### 4.2. Performance Across Various Generalist Policies and Embodiments

- **Simulation Evaluation (SIMPLER environment, 12 tasks, WidowX & Google Robot):** V-GPS enhanced the performance of *all five* tested generalist policies: Octo-small, Octo-base, Octo-small-1.5, RT1-X, and OpenVLA.
 - **Consistent Improvement:** On average, V-GPS led to improved success rates for every policy across both WidowX and Google Robot embodiments, using the *same single value function*.
 - **Challenging Tasks:** V-GPS showed the largest benefits on complex tasks like "put eggplant in basket," which involves significant height differences, an obstructing wall, and a slippery object, where policies without V-GPS struggled considerably.
 - **Robustness to Policy Variation:** Notably, Octo-small-1.5 showed surprisingly poor performance in SIMPLER initially, but combining it with V-GPS largely mitigated this degradation, suggesting V-GPS can stabilize performance variations due to different policy checkpoints or training recipes.

#### 4.3. Ablations and Comparisons

- **Comparison to Fine-tuning (Appendix E):** V-GPS demonstrated superior performance compared to fine-tuning the Octo-small model on the Bridge dataset or training policies from scratch on the same dataset. This highlights V-GPS's advantage in preserving generalist capabilities while still improving task-specific performance.
- **Impact of Dataset Size (Appendix F):** The value function remained effective even when trained on smaller subsets (50% and 10%) of the offline dataset, indicating good data efficiency for value function learning.
- **Inference Overhead (Appendix G):** V-GPS introduced a manageable inference time overhead (e.g., 1.28x for K=10, 1.59x for K=50) compared to the base policy, which was not deemed a significant slowdown for typical robotic control frequencies (e.g., 5Hz for WidowX).
- **Necessity of Both Components (Appendix J):** Ablation studies confirmed that both the well-trained value function and the high-quality action proposals from the generalist policy are crucial. Randomly selecting actions from the generalist policy showed no improvement, and a random policy guided by V-GPS achieved zero success.
- **Comparison to Offline RL Actors (Appendix I):** Standalone actors derived from Cal-QL or IQL failed to perform the tasks, often struggling with basic gripper actions. This underscores the synergy of V-GPS, which leverages the generalist policy's expressive action generation alongside the value function's refined guidance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

V-GPS represents a significant advancement in the deployment and utility of generalist robotic foundation models, addressing a critical bottleneck in their real-world application. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Enhanced Reliability and Performance:** By demonstrably improving the precision and robustness of state-of-the-art generalist policies across diverse tasks and embodiments, V-GPS brings these powerful models closer to reliable real-world deployment. This directly tackles the "last mile" problem of converting broad semantic understanding into precise physical execution.
- **Modularity and Accessibility:** The "plug-and-play" nature of V-GPS is a game-changer. It allows for performance enhancement of *any* pre-trained generalist policy, even those with black-box access, without requiring costly and complex fine-tuning. This dramatically lowers the barrier for researchers and practitioners to improve existing and future foundation models. The fact that a single, cross-embodiment value function can steer multiple distinct policies is particularly powerful.
- **Leveraging Offline RL for Deployment:** The work effectively bridges the gap between theoretical advantages of offline RL (learning from suboptimal data) and practical benefits for online robotic control. It shows that offline RL-trained value functions can serve as effective "critics" or "steerers" for expressive, imitation-learned policies, a novel application at the low-level action space for real-world robotics.
- **Cross-Domain Innovation:** By successfully adapting value-guided decoding techniques from large language models to complex embodied robotic tasks, V-GPS highlights a promising direction for cross-domain transfer of AI methodologies, suggesting that principles for improving reasoning and decision-making in one domain can find analogues in another.
- **Reduced Development Costs:** By obviating the need for extensive fine-tuning and new data collection for each task refinement, V-GPS can significantly reduce the computational and human effort costs associated with deploying and maintaining advanced robotic systems.
- **Path for Future Research:** The paper opens several avenues for future work, including exploring more compute-optimal ways to balance policy sampling and value function querying, and scaling value function architectures and training data to further enhance their robustness to completely unseen objects or language instructions (out-of-distribution generalization).

In conclusion, V-GPS provides a practical, robust, and generalizable method for augmenting the capabilities of robotic foundation models, making them more precise and reliable in real-world scenarios without compromising their broad generalization. This work is a crucial step towards making general-purpose robots a practical reality.
