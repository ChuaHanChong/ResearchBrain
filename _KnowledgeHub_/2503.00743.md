# Quality-Driven Curation of Remote Sensing Vision-Language Data via Learned Scoring Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Nanjing University developed `ScoreRS`, the first open-source learned scoring model and preference dataset specifically designed for assessing the quality of remote sensing (RS) vision-language data. This model enables quality-driven data curation, leading to improved VLM performance, with fine-tuning on top 30% of `ScoreRS`-ranked data yielding up to 7% higher classification accuracy and `ScoreRS` as a Best-of-N selector pushing LHRS-Bench accuracy past 70%.

## Problem

- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) face significant challenges in interpreting Remote Sensing (RS) images due to substantial domain shifts and a scarcity of large-scale, high-quality RS-specific vision-language data.
- Current methods for creating RS vision-language data (e.g., rule-based or synthetic generation) often introduce quality issues such as informational sparsity, semantic inconsistency, or hallucinations.
- A robust, automated framework for systematically assessing and curating the quality of synthetically generated RS vision-language data is lacking, hindering performance improvements in RS-specific VLMs.

## Method

- A comprehensive ==quality framework== was established for RS vision-language data, defining five crucial dimensions: relevance, specificity & detail, completeness, clarity & fluency, and semantic richness.
- The first large-scale, RS-specific preference datasets were meticulously constructed for both image-captions (ICPD: 72K pairs) and vision instructions (VIPD: 59K pairs) using diverse VLM generations and GPT-4o for quality judgments.
- A novel parameter-wise learned scoring model, `**ScoreRS**`, was developed and trained using a three-stage progressive strategy on these preference datasets to automatically assess the quality of RS vision-language data.

## Results

- Fine-tuning CLIP-ViT-L/14 with the top **30%** of data ranked by `ScoreRS` achieved a **7%** improvement in average classification accuracy and **2%** in average retrieval recall on the RemoteCLIP dataset, surpassing full-data training.
- Integrating `ScoreRS` as a reward model in Reinforcement Learning for Qwen2VL-**7B**-RS improved performance by over **5%** on VG-DIOR and **2%** on LHRS-Bench compared to the base model.
- As a Best-of-N selector at test time, `ScoreRS` consistently enhanced VLM performance across multiple models and tasks, enabling the challenging LHRS-Bench dataset accuracy to exceed **70%** for the first time.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The quality of vision-language data, rather than just its volume, is a critical bottleneck for advancing Vision-Language Models in the remote sensing domain.
> - A learned scoring model, trained on human-aligned preference data generated with advanced LLMs, can effectively learn and predict fine-grained quality distinctions unique to RS imagery and text.
> - Quality-driven data curation, by filtering and selecting high-quality subsets using `ScoreRS`, can lead to superior VLM performance, validating the "less is more" principle in the context of RS data.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The advancement of artificial intelligence, particularly in visual understanding, has greatly benefited from large-scale, diverse, and high-quality training data. Vision-Language Models (VLMs), from foundational models like CLIP to modern integrations with Large Language Models (LLMs), have shown remarkable success in interpreting natural images. However, the application of these powerful models to Remote Sensing (RS) images faces significant challenges due to **substantial domain shifts in data distributions** and, crucially, a **scarcity of large-scale, high-quality RS-specific vision-language data**. Unlike natural images, which benefit from abundant web-crawled image-text pairs, RS imagery lacks such readily available interleaved data.

Existing approaches to bridge this gap primarily fall into two categories:
1. **Rule-based methods:** These involve selecting RS data from general datasets or constructing pairs using predefined templates (e.g., OpenStreetMap tags). While useful, they often produce informationally sparse or semantically inconsistent data.
2. **Synthetic data generation:** Leveraging flagship VLMs (e.g., GPT-4, Gemini) to automatically generate descriptions or question-answer pairs for RS images. This method, while scalable, often introduces **quality concerns** such as hallucinations, factual inaccuracies, or responses that misrepresent visual content, ultimately propagating errors and limiting VLM performance in RS domains.

The broader landscape thus identifies a critical bottleneck: **the absence of a robust, automated framework for systematically assessing and curating the quality of synthetically generated RS vision-language data.** This paper directly addresses this gap. Previous works have focused on data volume and VLM architecture, but the authors contend that *data quality* is a fundamental, underexplored problem hindering further progress in RS-specific VLMs. By proposing a learned scoring model for quality assessment, this research pioneers a systematic approach to data curation, allowing for the "less is more" principle to be effectively applied in a domain where data quality has often been suboptimal. This work shifts the focus from merely increasing data volume to intelligently selecting and refining it, aligning with recent trends in general VLM research that emphasize quality over sheer quantity.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observed limitations of current Vision-Language Models (VLMs) in interpreting remote sensing (RS) images, which the authors attribute to a critical bottleneck: the **scarcity of large-scale, high-quality RS vision-language training data**. Existing methods for RS data creation, whether rule-based or VLM-generated, often introduce significant quality issues such as informational sparsity, semantic inconsistency, or hallucinatory content. This lack of robust quality control directly constrains improvements in RS-specific VLM performance.

To address this fundamental problem, the paper sets forth several key objectives:

1. **Establish a Quality Framework:** To define and formalize what constitutes "high-quality" text for RS images. This involves outlining five crucial dimensions: relevance, specificity & detail, completeness, clarity & fluency, and semantic richness.
2. **Construct RS-Specific Preference Datasets:** To create the first large-scale, RS-specific preference datasets for both image-captions and vision instructions. These datasets encode human-like preferences regarding text quality, serving as the foundation for training an automated scoring model.
3. **Develop a Learned Quality Scoring Model (ScoreRS):** To propose and train `ScoreRS`, a novel parameter-wise learned scoring model specifically designed for automated quality assessment of RS vision-language data.
4. **Validate Quality-Driven Data Curation:** To empirically demonstrate that fine-tuning VLMs on a *subset* of data, rigorously ranked and selected by `ScoreRS`, can achieve superior performance compared to training on the complete, uncurated dataset or data filtered by simpler metrics like CLIP-score.
5. **Explore Practical Applications of ScoreRS:** To validate `ScoreRS`'s utility beyond data selection, specifically as a reward model in Reinforcement Learning (RL) for VLM training and as a Best-of-N (BoN) selector for improving test-time VLM performance.

In essence, the overarching goal is to systematically tackle the data quality problem in RS vision-language tasks, thereby unlocking the full potential of VLMs for remote sensing image understanding and facilitating more advanced, domain-specific applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology centers on developing `ScoreRS`, a learned quality scoring model, through a multi-stage process encompassing quality definition, preference data construction, and specialized training.

#### 3.1. Quality Dimensions for RS Vision-Language Data

The authors begin by meticulously defining five critical dimensions that characterize high-quality RS vision-language data:
- **Relevance:** Accuracy of text description to visual content, avoiding hallucination.
- **Specificity & Detail:** Granularity and precision of information provided.
- **Completeness:** Coverage of all salient and important features in the image.
- **Clarity & Fluency:** Grammatical correctness, structure, and understandability of language.
- **Semantic Richness:** Use of domain-specific terminology and concepts relevant to RS applications.
Each dimension is assigned a 5-point scoring system for both image-captions and instruction samples, forming the basis for human and model-based preference judgments.

#### 3.2. Preference Data Construction

Given the absence of RS-specific preference datasets, the authors meticulously construct two types of large-scale preference data:
- **Image-Caption Preference Dataset (ICPD):**
 - **Image Source:** 1.15 million orthorectified RS images from the LHRS-Align dataset, deduplicated to 76K distinctive images using SSCD copy detection to ensure representativeness.
 - **Caption Generation:** Captions for each image are generated using a diverse set of VLMs: RS-specific LHRS-Bot-Nova and general-purpose Qwen2VL-7B and InternVL-2.5-8B, using consistent prompts and sampling parameters.
 - **Judgment:** GPT-4o evaluates these generated captions based on the five defined quality dimensions. The highest-scoring caption for each image is designated as positive (T$^{+}$), and others as negative (T$^{-}$). A dataset of 72K image-caption preference pairs is formed, with GPT-4o's reliability validated against human judgments (92.6% agreement).
- **Vision Instruction Preference Dataset (VIPD):**
 - **Instruction Aggregation & Deduplication:** Vision instruction data is aggregated from GeoChat, LHRS-Instruct, and SkysenseGPT. A two-stage similarity-based filtering (text and image similarity) reduces redundancy, resulting in 112K diverse samples.
 - **Close-ended Questions:** For questions with definitive answers, Qwen2VL-7B generates an answer ($\hat{y}$). If $\hat{y}$ differs from the provided answer ($y$), $y$ is treated as T$^{+}$ and $\hat{y}$ as T$^{-}$.
 - **Open-ended Questions:** LHRS-Bot-Nova and Qwen2VL-7B generate answers. Qwen2VL-72B evaluates these, along with the source dataset answer, using the defined quality dimensions, designating the highest-scoring as T$^{+}$ and others as T$^{-}$.
 - **RS-Specific Questions:** To enhance domain specificity, 33K additional preference pairs are created by manually crafting five questions across 12 expert-defined RS image analysis categories, rephrasing them with Qwen2.5-13B, generating answers with LHRS-Bot-Nova and Qwen2VL-7B, and finally judging with GPT-4o.
 - This process yields 26K + 33K = 59K vision instruction preference pairs.

#### 3.3. ScoreRS Training and Application

- **ScoreRS Training:**
 - **Initialization:** `ScoreRS` is initialized with Qwen2VL-7B, with its language head replaced by a linear layer to output a scalar quality score.
 - **Three-Stage Progressive Training:**
 1. **Stage 1 (Text-only):** The value head is trained on a pure text preference dataset (UltraFeedback) for robust initialization.
 2. **Stage 2 (Image-Caption):** The Vision Transformer (ViT) and value head are unfrozen and trained on the ICPD to enhance RS image understanding.
 3. **Stage 3 (Vision Instruction):** The LLM component is unfrozen, and full-parameter training is conducted using the VIPD and the RLHF-V dataset.
 - **Loss Function:** The Bradley-Terry model is used, minimizing empirical negative log-likelihood loss for pairwise preferences.
- **Applications:**
 - **Data Selection:** `ScoreRS` ranks vision-language data, allowing for the selection of top-quality samples for VLM fine-tuning.
 - **Reinforcement Learning (RL) Reward Model:** For Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) training, `ScoreRS` provides continuous rewards for open-ended questions based on a reference answer, while rule-based binary rewards are used for close-ended questions.
 - **Best-of-N (BoN) Selector:** At test time, `ScoreRS` selects the highest-scoring answer from multiple VLM-generated candidates.

This comprehensive approach allows `ScoreRS` to learn fine-grained quality distinctions tailored to the unique characteristics of RS data, enabling more effective curation and utilization of vision-language resources in the remote sensing domain.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents compelling empirical evidence validating the effectiveness of `ScoreRS` across various applications.

#### 4.1. Vision-Language Data Selection for VLM Training

- **CLIP Fine-tuning:** When fine-tuning CLIP-ViT-L/14 on the RemoteCLIP dataset, models trained with the **top 30% of data ranked by `ScoreRS`** achieved superior accuracy compared to both full-data fine-tuning and CLIP-score-based ranking. Specifically, `ScoreRS` filtering yielded a 7% improvement in average classification accuracy and a 2% improvement in average retrieval recall compared to baseline CLIP. It also consistently outperformed CLIP-score filtering. Similar benefits were observed when applying `ScoreRS` to the Skyscript dataset, where full-data fine-tuning even degraded performance compared to the original CLIP, highlighting the necessity of quality control.
- **Large VLM Fine-tuning (Qwen2VL-7B):** Fine-tuning Qwen2VL-7B with RS image-caption data and vision instruction datasets from VHM also demonstrated `ScoreRS`'s efficacy. Using the **top 30% of image-caption data** filtered by `ScoreRS` resulted in the best performance, yielding a 1% improvement on LHRS-Bench. For vision instruction data, a more moderate filtering (top 60%) led to substantial improvements: 3% on classification, 5% on VQA, and 0.36% on grounding and LHRS-Bench benchmarks, consistently outperforming CLIP-score-based selection. The final Qwen2VL-7B-RS model, trained on `ScoreRS`-filtered data (30% pretraining, 60% instruction), achieved comparable or superior performance to leading RS-specific VLMs and outperformed general-purpose models on the challenging LHRS-Bench.

#### 4.2. Reinforcement Learning Training

- `ScoreRS` was successfully integrated as a reward model for GRPO-based RL training of the Qwen2VL-7B-RS model. Direct RL training using `ScoreRS` (Qwen2VL-7B-RS-Zero) improved performance by 0.2% on LHRS-Bench and 1% on VG-DIOR.
- Further, a multi-stage approach involving initial supervised fine-tuning with manually refined reasoning data, followed by RL training with `ScoreRS` (Qwen2VL-7B-RS-R1), yielded significant improvements: **over 5% on VG-DIOR and 2% on LHRS-Bench** compared to the base Qwen2VL-7B-RS, and consistently outperformed variants trained without `ScoreRS`-based rewards. This demonstrates `ScoreRS`'s effectiveness in guiding models to learn more reasonable reasoning patterns.

#### 4.3. Best-of-N (BoN) Selection

- As a BoN selector at test time, `ScoreRS` consistently enhanced evaluation performance across multiple models (LHRS-Bot-Nova, Qwen2VL-7B, Qwen2VL-7B-RS-R1, InternVL3-8B, Qwen2.5VL-7B) for both complex perception (VG-DIOR) and holistic understanding (LHRS-Bench) tasks.
- Notably, `ScoreRS` enabled accuracy on the challenging LHRS-Bench dataset to **exceed 70% for the first time**, significantly outperforming CLIP-score as a selector and even majority voting, especially for tasks involving bounding box predictions where majority voting struggles.

#### 4.4. Ablation Analysis

- Ablation studies confirmed the criticality of both the preference dataset composition and the multi-stage training strategy. Training with the complete preference dataset achieved the highest reward accuracy. Omitting any subset degraded performance, even on different domains.
- The multi-stage training strategy improved reward accuracy by **over 10%** compared to joint training.
- Larger model sizes for `ScoreRS` (7B vs. 2B) consistently yielded better performance, aligning with scaling laws for reward models.
- Initializing `ScoreRS` with a high-quality RS-specific VLM (one trained on `ScoreRS`-filtered data) provided additional benefits compared to a general VLM or one trained on uncurated data, supporting the hypothesis that good quality base models are important for effective scoring.

These findings collectively underscore the current suboptimal quality of RS vision-language data and establish `ScoreRS` as a robust, versatile, and highly effective tool for quality assessment, data curation, and performance enhancement in RS-specific VLMs.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research makes several significant contributions with far-reaching potential impacts on the remote sensing (RS) community and the broader field of Vision-Language Models (VLMs).

First, the paper addresses a fundamental bottleneck in the development of RS-specific VLMs: the **suboptimal quality of existing RS vision-language datasets**. By formally establishing a **framework for defining text quality** across five crucial dimensions (relevance, specificity & detail, completeness, clarity & fluency, and semantic richness), the authors provide a much-needed objective standard for evaluating RS image descriptions and instructions. This framework is a critical first step towards more rigorous and systematic data curation in a domain historically hampered by data scarcity and heterogeneity.

Second, the introduction of the **first large-scale, open-source RS-specific preference dataset** (comprising both image-caption and vision instruction pairs) and the accompanying **`ScoreRS` model** is a major breakthrough. `ScoreRS` is the first open-source learned scoring model tailored to the unique characteristics and complexities of RS vision-language data. This resource will be invaluable for future research, enabling other researchers to reproduce, extend, and build upon this work, fostering collective progress in the field.

Third, the empirical results unequivocally demonstrate the **power of quality-driven data curation**. The finding that fine-tuning VLMs on just the *top 30% of data ranked by `ScoreRS` outperforms training on complete datasets or even CLIP-score-filtered data* strongly validates the "less is more" principle in the RS domain. This highlights that focusing on quality over sheer data volume is crucial for maximizing VLM performance for RS tasks. This paradigm shift will encourage more efficient and effective utilization of computational resources and data collection efforts, moving away from brute-force scaling.

Finally, the versatility of `ScoreRS` is a key takeaway. Its successful application as both a **reward model in Reinforcement Learning** and a **Best-of-N selector** at test time showcases its broad utility. By integrating `ScoreRS` into RL pipelines, models can be guided to generate higher-quality, more relevant, and semantically richer responses for open-ended RS queries. As a BoN selector, it offers a practical and effective way to boost the performance of existing VLMs without extensive retraining, providing immediate benefits for RS image interpretation tasks. The achievement of over 70% accuracy on the challenging LHRS-Bench benchmark for the first time with BoN selection is a testament to this impact.

The authors anticipate that this study will catalyze a greater emphasis on vision-language data quality control and strategic data utilization within the RS community. This foundational work paves the way for developing more robust, reliable, and capable RS-specific VLMs that can transition from basic perception tasks to more advanced applications such as disaster analysis, urban planning, and environmental monitoring, ultimately enabling more sophisticated human-AI interaction in critical geological information analysis.
