# SafeVLA: Towards Safety Alignment of Vision-Language-Action Model via Constrained Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Peking University developed SafeVLA, an Integrated Safety Approach (ISA) that systematically embeds explicit safety constraints into Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models using a Constrained Markov Decision Process framework. This method reduced cumulative safety costs by 83.58% on average while simultaneously increasing task success rates by 3.85% across navigation and manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Existing safety mechanisms for LLMs/VLMs primarily address abstract risks (e.g., harmful content) and are insufficient for the physical safety challenges of VLA models in real-world environments.
- Current VLA training paradigms (imitation learning, standard RL) do not explicitly integrate safety constraints, leading to a lack of mechanisms to prevent unsafe physical behaviors.
- Generalist VLA models, while capable, face extreme safety challenges due to their physical interaction with complex and unpredictable environments, amplifying risks of damage or injury.

## Method

- Formulated VLA safety alignment as a ==Constrained Markov Decision Process== (**CMDP**), where the objective is to maximize task reward while satisfying explicit safety constraints.
- Introduced an Integrated Safety Approach (**ISA**) pipeline that systematically models safety predicates, elicits risks through a diverse benchmark (**Safety-CHORES**), constrains policies via a ==Lagrangian Safe Reinforcement Learning== method, and assures safety through multi-dimensional evaluation.
- Translated state-action and trajectory-level safety predicates into cost signals, iteratively updating VLA model parameters and Lagrange multipliers to prioritize safety adherence before maximizing task performance.

## Results

- ISA achieved an **83.58%** average reduction in cumulative safety costs and a **3.85%** average increase in task success rate compared to state-of-the-art task-focused RL baselines.
- Qualitative analysis showed ISA eliminated trajectories with extremely high safety costs (e.g., >10), effectively mitigating long-tail risks and reducing the upper bound of unsafe behavior severity to approximately 1/35th.
- ISA-aligned policies demonstrated robustness to out-of-distribution visual perturbations and maintained significantly lower safety costs in extreme failure scenarios where task completion was impossible (e.g., FLaRe incurred **32x** higher CC than ISA), exhibiting cautious exploration.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly embedding safety constraints into VLA models is achievable without compromising task performance, with ISA demonstrating improved task success rates alongside substantial safety gains.
> - Rich risk elicitation environments, like the novel Safety-CHORES benchmark, are crucial for effectively training VLA models to prevent a wide range of unsafe behaviors and long-tail risks.
> - The dynamic adjustment of Lagrange multipliers in a CMDP framework enables a superior trade-off between safety and task performance compared to fixed penalty coefficients, ensuring cost limits are adhered to while maintaining high success rates.
