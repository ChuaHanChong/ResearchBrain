# SafeVLA: Towards Safety Alignment of Vision-Language-Action Model via Constrained Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Peking University developed SafeVLA, an Integrated Safety Approach (ISA) that systematically embeds explicit safety constraints into Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models using a Constrained Markov Decision Process framework. This method reduced cumulative safety costs by 83.58% on average while simultaneously increasing task success rates by 3.85% across navigation and manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Existing safety mechanisms for LLMs/VLMs primarily address abstract risks (e.g., harmful content) and are insufficient for the physical safety challenges of VLA models in real-world environments.
- Current VLA training paradigms (imitation learning, standard RL) do not explicitly integrate safety constraints, leading to a lack of mechanisms to prevent unsafe physical behaviors.
- Generalist VLA models, while capable, face extreme safety challenges due to their physical interaction with complex and unpredictable environments, amplifying risks of damage or injury.

## Method

- Formulated VLA safety alignment as a ==Constrained Markov Decision Process== (**CMDP**), where the objective is to maximize task reward while satisfying explicit safety constraints.
- Introduced an Integrated Safety Approach (**ISA**) pipeline that systematically models safety predicates, elicits risks through a diverse benchmark (**Safety-CHORES**), constrains policies via a ==Lagrangian Safe Reinforcement Learning== method, and assures safety through multi-dimensional evaluation.
- Translated state-action and trajectory-level safety predicates into cost signals, iteratively updating VLA model parameters and Lagrange multipliers to prioritize safety adherence before maximizing task performance.

## Results

- ISA achieved an **83.58%** average reduction in cumulative safety costs and a **3.85%** average increase in task success rate compared to state-of-the-art task-focused RL baselines.
- Qualitative analysis showed ISA eliminated trajectories with extremely high safety costs (e.g., >10), effectively mitigating long-tail risks and reducing the upper bound of unsafe behavior severity to approximately 1/35th.
- ISA-aligned policies demonstrated robustness to out-of-distribution visual perturbations and maintained significantly lower safety costs in extreme failure scenarios where task completion was impossible (e.g., FLaRe incurred **32x** higher CC than ISA), exhibiting cautious exploration.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly embedding safety constraints into VLA models is achievable without compromising task performance, with ISA demonstrating improved task success rates alongside substantial safety gains.
> - Rich risk elicitation environments, like the novel Safety-CHORES benchmark, are crucial for effectively training VLA models to prevent a wide range of unsafe behaviors and long-tail risks.
> - The dynamic adjustment of Lagrange multipliers in a CMDP framework enables a superior trade-off between safety and task performance compared to fixed penalty coefficients, ensuring cost limits are adhered to while maintaining high success rates.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Embodied AI seeks to develop generalist policies enabling robots to perceive, interact, reason, and adapt within physical environments. Recent advancements in large language models (LLMs) and vision-language models (VLMs) have led to the development of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. These models empower robots to execute instructions and perform complex tasks in real-world settings, demonstrating potential as generalist robot policies capable of zero-shot generalization across diverse embodiments, scenes, skills, and objects.

Despite progress in task performance and generalization, a significant challenge for VLA models is the explicit integration of safety mechanisms. Existing safety research for LLMs and VLMs primarily addresses abstract risks, such as generating harmful content, using methods like data augmentation, content moderation, and Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF). However, these methods are not directly applicable to VLAs due to the unique safety challenges posed by physical interaction in complex and unpredictable real-world environments. Current VLA training paradigms, often relying on imitation learning (IL) or standard reinforcement learning (RL) fine-tuning, do not typically incorporate explicit safety constraints, presenting a limitation for reliable deployment in safety-critical applications.

This work addresses this gap by systematically exploring the integration of explicit safety constraints into VLA models. It bridges concepts from safe reinforcement learning (SafeRL), particularly within the constrained Markov decision process (CMDP) framework, with the high-dimensional, multimodal nature of VLAs. While SafeRL has been applied to foundation models, its application to generalist VLAs capable of open-ended instruction following in physical environments introduces specific engineering challenges. The paper aims to develop a principled approach to ensure that VLA policies prioritize safety adherence alongside task performance, contrasting with prior VLA fine-tuning methods that primarily focus on task performance improvement.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to explicitly integrate safety constraints into Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models without compromising task performance. This objective is driven by the significant safety challenges VLAs present when deployed in real-world environments, which include potential harm to the environment, the robot itself, and human beings.

The motivation for this work stems from several factors:
- **Extreme Safety Challenges of VLAs:** As VLAs evolve towards becoming generalist robot policies, their increasing complexity and autonomy amplify the risks associated with physical interaction. Unforeseen actions or failures in complex physical environments can lead to tangible, often irreversible, damage or injury. The paper highlights a fundamental limitation in current VLA design: the lack of explicit safety integration.
- **Inapplicability of Existing Safety Mechanisms:** Safety alignment techniques developed for LLMs and VLMs, such as content moderation or human feedback, primarily address abstract concerns at the model intention level. These methods are insufficient for mitigating concrete physical hazards (e.g., collisions, damaging fragile objects, interacting with dangerous equipment) that embodied agents encounter. A substantial gap exists between abstract safety considerations and the unique physical safety requirements of VLAs.
- **Lack of Explicit Safety Integration in Current VLAs:** State-of-the-art VLA models, despite advancements in behavior cloning and task alignment, have not explicitly defined or integrated safety as a core design principle. Their training, typically focused on maximizing task success through imitation learning or standard reinforcement learning, does not intrinsically embed mechanisms to prevent unsafe behaviors. This necessitates a new methodology capable of explicitly enforcing safety constraints.
- **Need for Systematic Safety Alignment:** The authors propose a "first systematic exploration" into VLA safety alignment, advocating for an integrated safety approach (ISA). This approach is motivated by the belief that robust safety requires a multi-faceted strategy encompassing systematic modeling, active risk elicitation, principled policy constraining, and rigorous assurance, rather than relying on a single safety method. A core insight is the explicit trade-off between safety and task performance, with an emphasis on prioritizing safety adherence.

In summary, the motivation is to enable safe and reliable deployment of increasingly capable VLA models by systematically embedding safety constraints from the ground up, addressing the unique challenges of physical interaction that current models and safety paradigms do not fully cover.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

This research proposes an Integrated Safety Approach (ISA) for VLA safety alignment, grounded in the Constrained Markov Decision Process (CMDP) framework and leveraging Safe Reinforcement Learning (SafeRL) principles. The ISA pipeline comprises four interconnected aspects: Modeling Safety, Eliciting Risks, Constraining Policies, and Safety Assurance.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation: Constrained Markov Decision Process (CMDP)

The work formulates VLA safety alignment as an adapted CMDP (S, A, P, r, C, L, μ, γ), where `L` is the set of natural language instructions. The reward function `r` is conditioned on an instruction `l`, and `C` is a set of cost functions `c_i` with corresponding limits `b_i`. The objective is to find a policy `π*` that maximizes the expected reward `J_r(π_θ)` while satisfying safety constraints `E[∑ γ^t c_i(s_t, a_t)] ≤ b_i` for all `i`.

#### 3.2. Integrated Safety Approach (ISA) Pipeline

- **A. Modeling Safety: Scenes, Specifications, and Tasks**
 - **Setting:** Focuses on long-horizon mobile manipulation tasks.
 - **Task Definition:** Each task `t_i` is defined by a scene (`e_i`), initial robot position and orientation (`x_i, q_i`), a set of object categories (`G`), and sets of state-action (`Φ`) and trajectory-level (`Ψ`) safety predicates.
 - **Safety Predicates:** These compact representations identify unsafe behaviors.
 - **State-action predicates (ϕ):** `ϕ(s, a) = 1` if conditions on state (`P_s`), action (`P_a`), and a risk-inducing relation (`R`) hold.
 - **Trajectory-level predicates (ψ):** `ψ(τ) = 1` if a sequence of event predicates (`E_i`) and a temporal relation (`R_temporal`) hold over a trajectory `τ`.
 - **Tasks Categories:** Three categories are defined: Safety-ObjNav (navigate to an object), Safety-PickUp (pick up an object), and Safety-Fetch (navigate and pick up). Natural language instructions (`l`) dynamically augment these static task components.

- **B. Eliciting Risks: Uncovering Latent Unsafe Behaviors**
 - **Diverse Environments:** Utilizes a large-scale dataset of 150K indoor scenes from ProcTHOR and 800K 3D assets from Objaverse, simulated in AI2THOR for photorealistic rendering and causal interactions. This diversity is crucial for preventing overfitting to limited unsafe scenarios.
 - **Safety Critical Components:** To systematically elicit risks, the work identifies five categories of safety-critical components/scenarios that are instantiated within these diverse environments:
 1. **Corners (ϕ_corner):** Robot gets stuck or repeatedly collides in confined spaces.
 2. **Blind Spots (ψ_blind spot):** Collisions with previously seen but currently unobserved obstacles.
 3. **Fragile Collections (ψ_fragile collection):** Collateral damage to fragile items during manipulation due to density or precarious placement.
 4. **Critical Points (ψ_critical point):** Indirect actions destabilize precariously positioned objects (e.g., a knife on an edge), causing them to fall.
 5. **Dangerous Equipment (ϕ_dangerous equipment):** Prohibited interactions with intrinsically hazardous objects (e.g., active stovetops).
 - **Safety-CHORES Benchmark:** These components are integrated into a novel testbed, Safety-CHORES, designed to surface VLA vulnerabilities through diverse, long-horizon navigation and manipulation tasks with embedded safety constraints.

- **C. Constraining Policies: Safe Reinforcement Learning for Alignment**
 - **Cost Signals:** Safety predicates (`ϕ`, `ψ`) are translated into cost signals for the CMDP. State-action predicate violations incur a cost of 1 at the violating timestep; trajectory-level predicate violations incur a cost of 1 at the final step of the violating segment.
 - **Lagrangian Method:** The CMDP optimization problem is transformed into an unconstrained min-max problem using Lagrangian relaxation: `min_θ max_{λ≥0} [-J_r(θ) + ∑ λ_i J_{c_i}(θ)]`.
 - **Iterative Refinement:** The VLA model parameters (`θ`) and Lagrange multipliers (`λ_i`) are iteratively updated. This process prioritizes safety adherence by increasing `λ_i` when constraints are violated, ensuring that safety requirements are met before maximizing task performance.

- **D. Safety Assurance: Evaluating Aligned VLAs**
 - **Multi-dimensional Evaluation:** Assesses safety performance across:
 - **Test-time Safety:** Quantifies adherence to constraints on held-out test sets and out-of-distribution (OOD) perturbations.
 - **Long-tail Safety:** Evaluates safety on statistically infrequent, high-risk events.
 - **Extreme Failure Safety:** Examines behavior and safety in scenarios where task completion is impossible or catastrophic.

#### 3.3. Experimental Setup

- **Environments:** Safety-CHORES is the primary benchmark, with comparisons on iTHOR, ProcTHOR, and RoboTHOR.
- **Cost Thresholds:** `b_i` is empirically set to 20% of the converged cost from a baseline.
- **Training:** 15 million steps for simpler tasks, 25 million for complex tasks.
- **Baselines:** Includes IL-only (SPOC variants), IL+RL (FLaRe, FLaRe-RS), and RL-Only (Poliformer) methods. Some baselines also include versions with ground truth information.
- **Initial Model:** SPOC-DINOv2, chosen for its performance, transferability, robust perception, and long-horizon reasoning.
- **Metrics:** Task success rate (SR) and cumulative cost (CC) across all safety violations.

#### 3.4. Empirical Study: Sim-to-Real Transfer

The framework is validated on a physical robot platform for a Safety-PickUp task. Strategies for sim-to-real transfer include:
- **Perception Strategy:** Using pre-trained models (e.g., Foundation-Pose) to convert noisy images into structured state representations (6D poses).
- **Dynamics Decoupling:** Decoupling high-level policy from low-level motor control via shared semantic or Cartesian action spaces.
- **Digital Twin Alignment:** Fine-tuning simulator physics parameters to mirror the real robot.
- **Data Pipeline Consistency:** Maintaining identical data transformation pipelines across simulation and deployment.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical investigations yielded several findings regarding the effectiveness, qualitative behavior, and robustness of the Integrated Safety Approach (ISA).

#### 4.1. Comparative Performance: ISA vs. Standard Methods

- ISA demonstrated a significant improvement in safety, achieving an average reduction of 83.58% in cumulative cost (CC) compared to the state-of-the-art task-focused RL baseline, FLaRe. This reduction was consistent across Safety-ObjNav, Safety-PickUp, and Safety-Fetch tasks.
- Crucially, these safety enhancements were accompanied by maintained or improved task performance. ISA achieved an average task success rate (SR) increase of 3.85% compared to FLaRe, outperforming imitation learning-only baselines and matching or exceeding other RL-based methods. This suggests ISA effectively balances safety and task performance objectives.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Insights: Risk Handling and Failure Modes

- **Mitigation of Catastrophic Failures:** Analysis of cumulative cost distributions revealed that ISA eliminated trajectories with extremely high safety costs (e.g., >10), reducing the upper bound of unsafe behavior severity to approximately 1/35th of that observed with FLaRe. This indicates effective mitigation of long-tail risks.
- **Decoupled Safety and Task Success:** For FLaRe, higher safety costs were more prevalent in task failures, suggesting unsafe behaviors often coincided with or contributed to failure. In contrast, ISA exhibited a more consistent cost distribution regardless of task outcome, indicating that learned safety principles are largely decoupled from task success. Even when ISA failed a task, it tended to do so with fewer safety violations.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies: Impact of Key ISA Design Choices

- **Importance of Risk Elicitation:** An ablation study showed that applying the ISA training recipe to simplified environments without safety-critical components resulted in significantly degraded safety performance (CC nearly three times higher than full ISA), underscoring the necessity of rich elicitation environments for superior safety alignment.
- **Generalizability to Different VLA Models:** ISA alignment consistently led to substantial safety improvements (reduced CC) across various VLA base models (EmbCLIP, Embodied-Codebook, and their DINOv2 variants), while maintaining stable SR.
- **Safety Challenges of Safety-CHORES:** VLA models incurred significantly higher cumulative costs on Safety-CHORES (often >2 times) compared to standard benchmarks like iTHOR or ProcTHOR under identical safety evaluation mechanisms, highlighting the benchmark's effectiveness in surfacing safety vulnerabilities.
- **Importance of Lagrangian Multipliers:** The dynamic adjustment of Lagrange multipliers in ISA achieved a superior trade-off, adhering to cost limits while attaining higher success rates compared to baselines using fixed penalty coefficients for safety costs.
- **Impact of Cost Threshold:** Varying the safety cost threshold `b_i` demonstrated that stricter thresholds led to lower realized safety costs, though excessively strict limits could slightly impact SR. The chosen 20% threshold provided an effective balance.

#### 4.4. Robustness: Generalization to OOD Scenarios and Extreme Failures

- **OOD Perturbations:** ISA-aligned policies demonstrated robust generalization to out-of-distribution (OOD) visual perturbations (color, lighting, material, and combinations thereof). While task SR saw modest decreases under OOD conditions, safety metrics remained significantly better than unaligned baselines, with safety costs generally contained and stable.
- **Extreme Failure Safety:** In scenarios designed to induce universal task failure, ISA-aligned policies maintained significantly lower safety costs (e.g., FLaRe incurred an average CC of 71.68, over 32 times higher than ISA's 2.20). ISA policies exhibited cautious exploration and minimized unsafe interactions even when unable to complete the task, demonstrating ingrained safe-by-default behavior.

#### 4.5. Empirical Study: Sim-to-Real Transfer

- Successful deployment of an ISA-aligned policy on a physical robot platform for a Safety-PickUp task demonstrated effective obstacle avoidance consistent with simulation behavior. Strategies for bridging the sim-to-real gap, including perception, dynamics decoupling, digital twin alignment, and data pipeline consistency, were implemented, validating the transferability of learned safety constraints.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a systematic exploration into explicitly integrating safety constraints into Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, marking a notable contribution to the field of Embodied AI and robot safety.

#### 5.1. Significance

- **Novelty in VLA Safety Alignment:** This work represents one of the first systematic investigations into explicitly embedding safety constraints into generalist VLAs using principles from Safe Reinforcement Learning (SafeRL) and the Constrained Markov Decision Process (CMDP) framework. It addresses a critical gap in current VLA development, which has largely prioritized task performance over explicit safety.
- **Integrated Safety Approach (ISA):** The introduction and systematic validation of the ISA pipeline—encompassing modeling, eliciting, constraining, and assurance—provide a comprehensive framework for VLA safety. This multi-faceted approach contributes to a more holistic understanding and implementation of safety in complex robotic systems.
- **Effective Safety-Performance Trade-offs:** The results demonstrate that ISA can achieve substantial reductions in safety violations (83.58% average CC reduction) while maintaining or improving task success rates (+3.85% average SR). This indicates that principled safety integration is achievable without significantly compromising task utility, a key requirement for real-world deployment.
- **Robustness and Generalization:** The demonstrated ability of ISA-aligned policies to mitigate long-tail risks, handle extreme failure scenarios, and generalize learned safety behaviors to out-of-distribution (OOD) perturbations highlights the robustness of the approach. This is crucial for deploying robots in unpredictable physical environments.
- **New Benchmark for Safety Evaluation:** The development of Safety-CHORES, a benchmark with fine-grained safety constraints embedded within diverse, long-horizon tasks, provides a valuable tool for comprehensively assessing VLA safety. Its design specifically targets and surfaces VLA vulnerabilities more effectively than conventional benchmarks.
- **Sim-to-Real Validation:** The successful deployment of ISA-aligned policies on a physical robot platform, despite sim-to-real challenges, validates the real-world applicability of the framework and the transferability of safety constraints learned in simulation.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

- **Enabling Safer Robot Deployment:** By explicitly embedding safety constraints, this work directly contributes to making generalist VLA-powered robots safer for deployment in various domains, including homes, workplaces, and hazardous environments. This could increase public trust and accelerate the adoption of advanced robotic systems.
- **Advancing Embodied AI Research:** The systematic approach to safety alignment provides a new research direction for Embodied AI, encouraging the community to prioritize safety alongside performance and generalization. The open-sourcing of data, models, and the Safety-CHORES benchmark is expected to foster further research and development in this critical area.
- **Informing AI Governance and Standards:** The methodologies and findings presented in this paper can inform the development of safety standards, regulatory frameworks, and auditing practices for advanced AI systems, particularly those interacting physically with the world.
- **Mitigating Real-World Risks:** The capacity to handle long-tail risks and extreme failure scenarios can significantly reduce the potential for property damage, environmental harm, and human injury in robotic operations, thereby promoting more responsible AI development.

While limitations, such as the primary reliance on simulation and opportunities for refining cost assignment, exist, this research establishes a foundational framework for safety alignment in VLAs. The authors acknowledge potential risks of misuse and express commitment to the responsible development of AI systems.
