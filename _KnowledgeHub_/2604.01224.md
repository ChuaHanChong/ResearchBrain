# Functional Force-Aware Retargeting from Virtual Human Demos to Soft Robot Policies

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Carnegie Mellon University and Meta Reality Labs developed SoftAct, a contact force-guided framework for transferring human manipulation skills from virtual reality demonstrations to non-anthropomorphic soft robots. This approach, which explicitly models contact geometry and force distribution, achieved a 55% reduction in low-level tracking error and up to 70% higher task success rates in simulation and real-world zero-shot deployments compared to kinematic-only methods.

## Problem

- Soft robotic hands often exhibit extreme embodiment mismatch with human hands, featuring non-anthropomorphic designs, underactuation, and nonlinear deformation, making direct kinematic imitation ineffective for skill transfer.
- Traditional kinematic-only retargeting methods fail to capture the functional intent and unique compliance of soft robots in contact-rich manipulation tasks, leading to unstable interactions or task failures.
- Collecting dense and precise contact force information from real-world human demonstrations is technologically challenging and often noisy, hindering robust policy learning for soft robots.

## Method

- Introduced **SoftAct**, a ==two-stage force-aware retargeting algorithm== that explicitly optimizes for contact patches and force distributions, moving beyond kinematic similarity as the primary transferable representation.
- Utilized a high-fidelity immersive virtual reality (VR) environment to collect detailed human demonstrations, capturing full hand kinematics, object poses, dense contact patches, and corresponding contact forces.
- Implemented a ==learned low-level controller== for the pneumatic soft robot hand, translating desired fingertip trajectories into chamber pressure commands via an optimized ==inverse model== to handle nonlinearities and compliance.

## Results

- The learned low-level pressure controller for single soft fingers achieved a **55%** reduction in Root Mean Square Error (RMSE), tracking planar trajectories with an RMSE of **2.28 mm** compared to **5.05 mm** for the best baseline.
- Full SoftAct consistently reduced object-level translational error by **55–75%** and rotational error by up to **80%** in simulation manipulation tasks (e.g., light bulb twisting reduced rotational error from **18.2°** to **2.9°**) compared to kinematic baselines.
- SoftAct demonstrated a **30–70%** increase in task success rates in simulation and consistently outperformed kinematic baselines in real-world zero-shot deployments across various contact-rich tasks (e.g., **85%** vs **35%** for paper cup pouring, **95%** vs **30%** for light bulb screwing).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly modeling and transferring contact forces and their spatial distributions is essential for soft robots to reproduce the functional intent of manipulation, enabling them to adapt to their unique morphology rather than merely imitating human kinematics.
> - A two-stage retargeting process, combining offline force-balanced finger assignment with online geodesic-weighted contact-informed refinement, effectively bridges the embodiment mismatch between humans and non-anthropomorphic soft robots.
> - Optimizing a learned inverse model at execution time provides a robust and precise low-level control mechanism for compliant, underactuated soft fingers, significantly improving trajectory tracking accuracy.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research contributes to the fields of soft robotics, dexterous manipulation, learning from demonstration (LfD), and human-to-robot motion retargeting. Soft robot hands are recognized for their intrinsic compliance, safety, and robustness in contact-rich tasks, offering advantages over rigid manipulators. However, their unique characteristics, such as nonlinear, underactuated dynamics, deformation-based motion, and often non-anthropomorphic morphologies, pose significant challenges for policy learning and control.

Traditional LfD and motion retargeting methods often assume a meaningful kinematic mapping and direct finger-to-finger correspondence between human and robot hands. These approaches, which map human joint trajectories, fingertip positions, or full hand poses to robot configurations via inverse kinematics, heuristic rules, or learned pose-to-pose mappings, have been effective for anthropomorphic rigid robots with similar structures. However, they are generally inadequate for non-anthropomorphic soft robotic hands due to substantial embodiment mismatch, where direct kinematic imitation can fail to preserve the functional intent of manipulation.

Prior work has explored incorporating contact information to generalize manipulation strategies. However, these methods typically require explicit mapping between hand surfaces to align contact patches, which still limits their application primarily to anthropomorphic target embodiments. Furthermore, controlling soft and underactuated hands is challenging due to their compliance, limited controllability, and nonlinear actuation.

The presented work addresses these limitations by moving beyond kinematic imitation and surface correspondence. It proposes a contact force-guided framework that explicitly reasons about contact forces and distributions, treating them as the primary transferable representation. This approach aims to bridge the embodiment mismatch gap, enabling skill transfer to soft robots that differ significantly from human hands in morphology and actuation. By focusing on the functional intent derived from contact forces, it seeks to enable soft robots to discover robot-specific manipulation strategies that may diverge from human motion but achieve similar task outcomes.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable soft robot hands to perform human-like manipulation skills by explicitly incorporating contact force information during the skill transfer process. The motivation for this objective stems from several challenges inherent in soft robotics and existing learning-from-demonstration (LfD) paradigms:

1. **Extreme Embodiment Mismatch:** Soft robotic hands often feature non-anthropomorphic designs, underactuated systems, and nonlinear deformation-based motion, which fundamentally differ from human hands with their jointed kinematics. This mismatch renders direct kinematic imitation or traditional pose-to-pose retargeting methods ineffective, as they struggle to find a meaningful correspondence between human and robot configurations. The current methods often fail to reproduce the functional aspects of manipulation, even when geometric similarity is approximated.

2. **Limitations of Kinematic-Only Retargeting:** Conventional retargeting strategies focus on replicating human joint trajectories or end-effector poses. While successful for anthropomorphic rigid robots, these methods do not account for the unique compliance and actuation mechanisms of soft robots. They often lead to unstable interactions or task failures when applied to systems with different affordances and control complexities. The paper highlights that geometric similarity alone is insufficient to capture the functional intent of manipulation, especially in contact-rich scenarios.

3. **Need for Robust Control for Soft Robots:** Controlling soft robots is intrinsically challenging due to their nonlinear dynamics, inherent underactuation, and complex pressure-deformation relationships. Precise fingertip positioning and force regulation, crucial for dexterous manipulation, are difficult to achieve with analytical models or linear controllers. This necessitates robust low-level control mechanisms that can accurately execute desired actions despite the robot's inherent compliance and nonlinearities.

4. **Difficulty of Collecting Real-World Contact Data:** Acquiring dense and detailed contact force information in real-world human demonstrations is technologically challenging and often noisy. Leveraging virtual reality (VR) environments offers a controlled means to capture rich, precise, and noise-free contact geometry and force data, which can then be utilized to inform robot policies.

To address these motivations, the research sets out to:
- Develop a **contact force-guided LfD framework** capable of transferring human manipulation skills to non-anthropomorphic soft robotic hands.
- Introduce a **two-stage force-aware retargeting algorithm** that moves beyond kinematic similarity by explicitly optimizing contact patches and force distributions. This involves attributing demonstrated contact forces to individual human fingers and proportionally allocating robot fingers, followed by online refinement using geodesic-weighted contact influence.
- Implement a **learned low-level controller** specifically designed for the highly nonlinear pneumatic soft robot hand, enabling accurate trajectory tracking.
- Construct an **end-to-end system** for deploying policies derived from VR datasets onto a physical pneumatic soft hand, and evaluate its performance across various contact-rich manipulation tasks.

By pursuing these objectives, the research aims to demonstrate that explicitly modeling contact geometry and force distribution is essential for effective skill transfer to soft robotic hands, thereby enabling them to naturally accommodate embodiment mismatch and nonlinear compliance for stable and functional manipulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The SoftAct framework is designed for transferring human manipulation skills to non-anthropomorphic soft robotic hands by explicitly considering contact forces. The methodology comprises several integrated components: a custom soft robot manipulator, a finite-element-based simulation environment, a VR-based human demonstration collection system, a two-stage force-aware retargeting algorithm, a diffusion policy learning approach, and a learned low-level soft finger controller.

**A. Soft Robot Manipulator**
The custom manipulator features a rigid–soft hybrid architecture. Each soft finger is constructed from Ecoflex 00–30 elastomer, providing compliance, and contains internal rigid strain-limiting structures to enhance controllability and repeatability by constraining undesired deformation modes. Each finger is actuated by three radially arranged pneumatic chambers. Differential pressurization of these chambers enables planar and limited out-of-plane bending. The soft fingers are mounted on a rigid end-effector base attached to a 7-DoF robotic arm (Franka Emika Panda), allowing independent control of global arm motion and local finger deformation. Internal pressure to the chambers is controlled at 200 Hz with proportional pressure regulators.

**B. Simulation Environment**
Retargeting and evaluation are conducted in a custom finite-element-based (FEM) physics engine that supports coupled rigid and soft body dynamics.
- **Soft Finger Model:** Each soft finger is modeled as a deformable FEM body using a stable Neo-Hookean material formulation, with parameters chosen to approximate the physical elastomer.
- **Rigid Spine Approximation:** To enable controllable deformation, each finger incorporates an internal rigid spine, approximated as a chain of capsule-shaped rigid bodies with spherical joints. This backbone supports smooth bending.
- **Soft–Rigid Coupling:** The rigid spine is coupled to the deformable mesh via distributed soft-to-rigid virtual spring constraints, transmitting spine motion to the soft body.
- **Actuation Abstraction:** Pneumatic actuation is abstracted by applying torques to the rotational degrees of freedom of the spine capsules, inducing articulated bending that approximates the physical system's deformation.
- **Collision Handling:** Collisions between fingers and manipulated objects, as well as between different fingers' spines, are enabled, while self-collision within a finger and between a spine and its enclosing soft body are disabled for numerical stability.

**C. Human Demonstration Collection in Virtual Reality (VR)**
An immersive VR environment, built using Unreal Engine, serves as a high-fidelity data collection platform.
- **Setup:** Participants perform manipulation tasks using their right hand, interacting with virtual objects. OptiTrack motion capture (ten cameras, 24 markers) tracks human hand pose, which is mapped to a kinematic hand model in VR. Object poses are simultaneously recorded.
- **Data Captured:** For each timestep, the system logs full hand kinematics, object poses, dense contact patches (spatial distributions of contact points on the hand surface), and corresponding contact force magnitudes and directions. This provides detailed contact-level information difficult to acquire in the real world.

**D. Force-Aware Retargeting Algorithm**
SoftAct employs a two-stage retargeting process, treating contact forces as the primary transferable representation:

- **Stage 1: Force-balanced Finger Assignment (Offline):**
 - **Geodesic Distance:** Distances on the hand surface are measured intrinsically using mesh geodesics, ensuring contact influence propagates along the physical surface rather than through free space.
 - **Geodesic Force Diffusion:** Contact forces from demonstrations are diffused over the hand surface using a geodesic heat kernel.
 - **Per-Finger Load Estimation:** Heat values are accumulated over time and summed within each human skeleton finger region to estimate total contact load ($F_r$).
 - **Load-Balanced Allocation:** Robot fingers are allocated to human fingers proportionally to balance the contact load by solving an optimization problem that minimizes the maximum per-finger load, allowing many-to-one mappings.
 - **Workspace-Aware Finger Matching:** An assignment cost (combining Euclidean distance of workspaces and a penalty for non-overlapping workspaces) determines the optimal mapping between robot and skeleton fingertips using the Hungarian algorithm. This mapping remains fixed.

- **Stage 2: Retargeting with Contact-informed Refinement (Online):**
 - During execution, the robot follows a baseline end-effector trajectory while each soft finger tracks its assigned human skeleton fingertip.
 - **Geodesic Contact Weighting:** For each demonstrated contact, a geodesic-weighted influence ($w_{ij}$) is computed based on its force magnitude and geodesic distance to the current robot fingertip position.
 - **Fingertip Adjustment Rule:** An adjustment vector ($\delta_i$) is calculated as a weighted average of the differences between demonstrated contact locations and the current robot fingertip position, capped by a maximum adjustment. This refinement allows each robot finger to adapt its target online based on demonstrated contact geometry and force distribution.

**E. Policy Learning**
A diffusion policy is trained to imitate the functionally retargeted trajectories ($D_r$).
- **Observation Space:** Robot proprioceptive state and object poses.
- **Action Space:** Composite actions including end-effector displacement, end-effector orientation update, and soft-finger commands (torques for simulation).
- **Architecture:** A one-dimensional conditional UNet backbone with three downsampling stages. Conditioning is applied globally using concatenated observations.
- **Training:** DDPM-style diffusion process (100 steps) trained with AdamW for 3000 epochs, predicting noise residuals.
- **Inference:** Action sequences are generated using 100 diffusion denoising steps and executed in a receding-horizon fashion.

**F. Soft Finger Control (Low-Level)**
For real-world deployment, a learned low-level controller translates desired fingertip trajectories into pneumatic chamber pressure commands.
- **Forward Kinematic Model:** An MLP ($f_{MLP}$) is trained to map three chamber pressures ($p$) to fingertip displacement ($d$). This model captures the nonlinear compliant behavior.
- **Inverse Control through Optimization:** At runtime, desired planar fingertip displacements ($d_{ref}$) are converted into pressure commands ($p_{ref}$) by inverting the learned forward model via gradient-based optimization. The optimization minimizes the squared difference between the predicted and reference planar displacements, with pressures re-parameterized using a sigmoid function to enforce actuator limits.
- **Execution:** Optimization is warm-started from the previous solution, solved using Adam for a fixed number of iterations, and resulting pressures are commanded to hardware at each waypoint.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of SoftAct encompassed low-level fingertip trajectory tracking, object-level trajectory tracking in simulation, and policy-level task success rates in both simulation and real-world zero-shot deployments.

**A. Low-Level Trajectory Tracking**
The SoftAct low-level pressure controller for a single soft finger demonstrated superior performance in tracking planar reference trajectories (square, circular, triangular, rectangular) compared to three baselines: Direct KNN, Direct MLP, and Direct Linear controllers.
- **Quantitative Improvement:** SoftAct achieved a Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) of 2.28 mm, significantly lower than the strongest baseline (Direct Linear) at 5.05 mm. This represents a 55% reduction in RMSE. The mean tracking error was reduced by 48% (from 3.97 mm to 2.06 mm), and the maximum error was substantially decreased (from 10.89 mm to 5.47 mm).
- **Stability and Precision:** The results indicated that optimizing a learned inverse model at execution time produced more accurate and stable trajectory tracking with reduced variance compared to direct prediction methods. This validates the effectiveness of SoftAct’s low-level control module for precise, contact-sensitive manipulation.

**B. Object Trajectory Tracking in Simulation**
Object-level manipulation performance was evaluated by comparing executed object trajectories against ground-truth demonstrations for tasks such as paper cup pouring, light bulb insertion, and light bulb screwing. Three retargeting conditions were compared: kinematic-only baseline, SoftAct-stage 1 (force-balanced finger assignment only), and full SoftAct (with contact-informed refinement).
- **Overall Outperformance:** Full SoftAct consistently outperformed the kinematic-only baseline across all evaluated tasks, reducing translational error by approximately 55–75% and rotational error by up to 80%.
- **Impact of Refinement Stage:** While SoftAct-stage 1 improved performance over pure kinematic retargeting, the full SoftAct method with contact-informed refinement consistently achieved better results. This finding suggests that online adaptation based on contact geometry and force distribution is critical and cannot be fully recovered by offline force balancing alone.
- **Task-Specific Gains:** The most significant gains were observed in constrained insertion and rotational tasks (e.g., Light Bulb Insertion, Light Bulb Twisting, Bottle Unscrewing), where maintaining stable contact is paramount and kinematic retargeting often led to accumulated drift. For instance, in Light Bulb Twisting, SoftAct reduced rotational error from 18.2° to 2.9°.

**C. Policy Evaluation**
Policy-level performance was assessed on six contact-rich manipulation tasks (light bulb insertion, light bulb twisting, cup pouring, marker grabbing, bottle unscrewing, box reorienting) in both simulation and real-world zero-shot deployments.
- **Simulation Success Rates:** In simulation, SoftAct demonstrated consistent and substantial improvements over the kinematic-only baseline, increasing task success rates by roughly 30–70%. For example, in Light Bulb Insertion, SoftAct achieved a 47% success rate compared to 0% for the kinematic baseline. In Box Reorienting, SoftAct achieved 90% success versus 30% for the baseline.
- **Real-World Zero-Shot Deployment:** SoftAct consistently outperformed the kinematic baseline in zero-shot real-world deployment across all evaluated tasks (paper cup pouring, light bulb screwing, box reorienting), achieving notable relative gains in success rates. For example, SoftAct achieved 85% success in Paper Cup Pouring compared to 35% for the kinematic baseline, and 95% in Light Bulb Screwing compared to 30%.
- **Contact-Centric Importance:** These results indicate that explicitly modeling contact geometry and force distribution during retargeting is essential for reliable manipulation with non-anthropomorphic soft robotic hands. The observed real-world performance suggests that this contact-centric approach improves sim-to-real transfer and allows the robot to adapt to its unique morphology and actuation, rather than merely imitating human kinematics.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The presented work, SoftAct, introduces a contact force-guided framework for transferring human manipulation skills to non-anthropomorphic soft robotic hands. Its significance lies in addressing a fundamental challenge in robotics: bridging extreme embodiment mismatch between human demonstrators and robotic platforms, particularly those with compliant and nonlinear characteristics.

The key aspects contributing to its significance and potential impact include:

1. **Enabling Dexterous Manipulation for Soft Robots:** By providing a robust method for transferring complex human manipulation skills, SoftAct expands the capabilities of soft robotic hands. It moves beyond simple grasping to facilitate dexterous, contact-rich interactions, which are crucial for tasks in unstructured environments, human-robot collaboration, and handling fragile objects. This could lead to wider adoption of soft robots in applications requiring delicate or adaptive manipulation.

2. **Novel Retargeting Paradigm:** The shift from kinematic imitation to a force-aware, contact-centric representation is a substantial contribution. By explicitly reasoning about contact geometry and force distribution, SoftAct allows soft robots to reproduce the *functional intent* of human demonstrations rather than merely mimicking motion. This is particularly impactful for non-anthropomorphic robots, as it enables them to leverage their unique morphology and compliance to achieve task goals, potentially discovering robot-specific, effective manipulation strategies that differ from human execution.

3. **Robustness to Embodiment Mismatch:** The framework directly tackles the problem of severe kinematic and morphological differences between human and soft robot hands. The two-stage retargeting process, combining offline force-balanced finger assignment with online geodesic-weighted contact refinement, provides a mechanism to adapt manipulation strategies across diverse embodiments. This broadens the applicability of learning from demonstration to a wider range of robotic systems, including highly specialized or biologically inspired designs.

4. **Improved Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The consistent gains in task success rates in real-world zero-shot deployment, following training in simulation from VR demonstrations, highlight the framework's effectiveness in facilitating sim-to-real transfer. By focusing on fundamental physical interactions (contact forces), the policies learned in simulation generalize better to the complexities and uncertainties of the real world. This reduces the need for extensive real-world data collection and fine-tuning, accelerating robot deployment.

5. **Integration of Advanced Technologies:** The system integrates immersive virtual reality for high-fidelity data collection, finite-element-based simulation for detailed physical modeling, and diffusion policies for robust control. This showcases a comprehensive approach to tackling complex robotics problems by combining state-of-the-art tools across different domains.

6. **Foundation for Future Research:** The work opens several avenues for future research. Extending the approach to visually estimated contact forces would increase its real-world applicability beyond motion capture. Scaling to longer-horizon, multi-stage manipulation tasks and developing vision-based student policies from the current state-based teacher policies are identified as important directions, addressing current limitations and pushing the boundaries of autonomous soft robot manipulation.

In summary, SoftAct represents a significant step towards enabling soft robots to learn and execute complex manipulation skills from human demonstrations, specifically by re-framing the skill transfer problem around contact forces rather than kinematics. This approach has the potential to enhance the versatility and autonomy of soft robotic systems across various industrial, medical, and service applications where robust, compliant, and adaptive manipulation is required.
