# ARM-FM: Automated Reward Machines via Foundation Models for Compositional Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> The ARM-FM framework leverages Foundation Models to automatically generate Language-Aligned Reward Machines (LARMs) from natural language, providing structured reward signals for reinforcement learning. This approach enables agents to solve complex, long-horizon tasks across diverse environments and demonstrates zero-shot generalization to novel compositions of learned sub-goals.

## Problem

- Designing effective, non-sparse reward functions for complex reinforcement learning (RL) tasks is challenging and can lead to sparse rewards or reward hacking.
- Communicating complex human objectives to RL agents in a structured, actionable manner is difficult, limiting RL's application to real-world problems.
- The manual design of Reward Machines (RMs) for compositional reward specification requires significant expert knowledge, constraining their practical utility and reusability.

## Method

- **ARM-FM** employs an iterative self-improvement loop using generator and critic Foundation Models (e.g., GPT-4o) to automatically construct Language-Aligned ==Reward Machines== (LARMs) from natural language task descriptions.
- LARMs include the automaton structure, executable Python code for labeling functions, and natural language descriptions (with embeddings) for each subtask.
- The agent's policy is conditioned on both the environment state and the language embedding of the current LARM state, and the total reward combines environmental rewards with structured RM rewards to provide a dense learning signal.

## Results

- ARM-FM agents consistently achieved near-perfect rewards on complex, long-horizon sparse-reward tasks (e.g., MiniGrid UnlockToUnlock, KeyCorridor), substantially outperforming baseline RL methods which made minimal progress.
- The framework successfully enabled task completion in a 3D procedurally generated environment (Craftium) and achieved higher success rates in continuous control robotics tasks (Meta-World), demonstrating scalability across diverse environments.
- The compositional structure of LARMs facilitated robust zero-shot generalization to novel task compositions and maintained high performance when trained on up to **10** simultaneous tasks in XLand-MiniGrid.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Foundation Models can effectively bridge the gap between abstract natural language task understanding and the concrete, structured reward signals required for effective RL agent learning.
> - Language embeddings of Reward Machine states create a semantically grounded shared skill space, enabling significant knowledge transfer, efficient multi-task learning, and zero-shot generalization across diverse tasks.
> - Automating the generation of Reward Machines through Foundation Models provides a principled, interpretable, and verifiable approach to decomposing complex, long-horizon tasks into manageable sub-goals for RL.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The design of effective reward functions remains a significant challenge in reinforcement learning (RL), limiting its application to complex, real-world problems. Existing issues include the difficulty of designing dense reward functions, which can lead to sparse rewards that provide insufficient learning signals or dense rewards that are susceptible to "reward hacking" by agents. Communicating complex objectives to RL agents in a structured and actionable manner is a recognized problem.

Foundation Models (FMs) have demonstrated capabilities in interpreting and decomposing tasks from natural language. However, a gap exists in translating this abstract understanding into the concrete, structured reward signals that RL agents require for effective learning. High-level plans generated by FMs often lack the granular feedback necessary for grounding and skill acquisition in RL environments.

Reward Machines (RMs) offer an automata-based formalism for reward specification. They decompose tasks into a finite automaton of sub-goals, providing a compositional structure for rewards and policies. This structure is considered more verifiable and principled than monolithic reward functions. Historically, the practical application of RMs has been constrained by the complexity and expert knowledge required for their manual design, often limiting them to task-specific applications.

This work positions itself at the intersection of FMs and RMs, aiming to leverage the reasoning and code-generation capabilities of FMs to automate the design and construction of RMs. The goal is to bridge the gap between abstract human intent, as expressed in natural language, and the concrete, structured learning signals required by RL agents. It seeks to unlock the potential of RMs for broader applicability by automating their generation, thereby addressing the long-standing challenge of reward function design in RL. The research also extends previous work on RMs by integrating language embeddings into the RM states, creating a mechanism for knowledge sharing and generalization across subtasks. In contrast to other FM-driven approaches that either use FMs directly as agents, provide auxiliary signals, or synthesize automata from demonstrations, ARM-FM focuses on generating semantically grounded automata directly from natural language to structure learning in RL.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to address the challenge of designing effective reward functions for complex RL tasks by automating the process through the use of Foundation Models. The work aims to translate high-level natural language task descriptions into structured, actionable reward signals for RL agents.

The key motivations driving this research are:
- **Overcoming Reward Specification Challenges:** Reward function design is identified as a central impediment to the widespread applicability of RL. Sparse rewards provide insufficient learning signals, while hand-crafted dense rewards can be prone to "reward hacking" and may not align with the true objective.
- **Leveraging Foundation Model Capabilities:** Foundation Models (FMs) demonstrate strong capabilities in understanding and decomposing natural language. The research is motivated by the potential to harness these capabilities for automated task interpretation and translation into RL-compatible formats.
- **Automating Reward Machine Design:** Reward Machines (RMs) provide a compositional, verifiable structure for reward specification, decomposing complex tasks into sub-goals. However, their manual design is often complex and requires expert knowledge. Automating this process using FMs is a key motivation to expand their practical utility.
- **Bridging the Semantic Gap:** There is a recognized gap between the abstract understanding of FMs and the concrete, structured feedback required by RL agents. The research aims to bridge this by using FMs to generate RMs, which provide a grounded, structured representation of objectives.
- **Enabling Generalization and Skill Reuse:** Traditional RM applications often treat states as isolated symbols, limiting knowledge transfer. The paper is motivated to introduce a mechanism (language embeddings of RM states) to create a shared skill space, fostering generalization and efficient policy transfer across related tasks.
- **Solving Complex, Long-Horizon Tasks:** Many real-world RL problems involve long-horizon tasks with sparse rewards, which are intractable for standard RL methods. The research is motivated to develop a framework that can convert such sparse rewards into dense, structured learning signals to improve sample efficiency and enable solutions for these challenging tasks.

Specifically, the paper outlines three primary contributions that serve as its objectives:
1. **Automated Generation of Language-Aligned Reward Machines (LARMs):** To develop a framework that uses FMs to automatically generate complete task specifications, including the automaton structure, executable labeling functions, and natural language instructions for each subtask, directly from natural language.
2. **Creation of a Shared Skill Space:** To introduce a method that leverages the language-aligned nature of LARMs to create a shared skill space, facilitating effective experience reuse and policy transfer across related tasks.
3. **Empirical Validation of Effectiveness:** To provide extensive empirical evidence demonstrating that the proposed approach can solve complex, long-horizon tasks across multiple domains, including grid worlds, 3D environments, and robotics with continuous control, and to show evidence of zero-shot generalization.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Automated Reward Machines via Foundation Models (ARM-FM) framework is designed for automated reward design in RL. It translates natural-language task descriptions into structured task representations for RL training. The framework comprises two main components: Language-Aligned Reward Machines (LARMs) and their integration into the RL training loop.

#### 3.1. Language-Aligned Reward Machines (LARMs)

The foundation of ARM-FM is the LARM, which extends the concept of a Reward Machine (RM).
- **Reward Machine (RM) Formalism:** An RM is formally defined as a tuple ⟨U, u_I, Σ, δ, R, F, L⟩, where:
 - U is a finite set of RM states, each representing a subtask.
 - u_I is the initial state.
 - Σ is a finite set of symbols representing events.
 - δ: U × Σ → U is the deterministic transition function.
 - R: U × S × A × S → ℝ is the RM reward function.
 - F ⊆ U is the set of final RM states.
 - L: S × A → Σ is the labeling function, connecting MDP states and actions to RM event symbols.
- **LARM Definition:** LARMs are RMs augmented with:
 - Natural-language instructions (l_u) for each RM state u.
 - An embedding function ϕ(·) that maps these language instructions to an embedding vector (z_u ∈ ℝᵈ). These embeddings are intended to create a semantically grounded skill space for policies.
- **Automated Construction of LARMs:** LARMs are automatically constructed from language and image-based task descriptions through an iterative prompting process involving Foundation Models.
 - **Self-Improvement Loop:** The framework employs N rounds of self-improvement using paired generator and critic FMs. The generator FM initially produces an RM specification, executable Python code for labeling functions, and natural language descriptions for each RM state. The critic FM evaluates these outputs.
 - **Optional Human Intervention:** A human operator can optionally intervene by approving the FM's output or providing corrective feedback during the refinement process (as detailed in Appendix A.4). This human feedback is integrated into the self-improvement loop.
 - **Components Generated:** For each task, the FM generates the RM specification (plaintext automaton), the Python code for the labeling functions (L), and the natural language descriptions (l_u) and their corresponding embeddings (z_u) for each RM state.
 - **FM Used:** GPT-4o (Hurst et al., 2024) was used to generate LARM components for most tasks, with various open-source FMs employed for ablation studies on XLand-MiniGrid tasks.

#### 3.2. Reinforcement Learning with LARMs

The generated LARMs are integrated into the RL training procedure:
- **Augmented State Space:** The agent operates within an augmented state space, which is the cross-product of the MDP state and the RM state (S × U).
- **Policy Conditioning:** The agent's policy, π, is conditioned on both the environment state (s_t) and the language embedding of the current LARM state (z_u_t), i.e., π(s_t, z_u_t). This conditioning is intended to enable generalization and skill reuse by providing the agent with awareness of its active sub-goal.
- **Combined Reward Signal:** During training, after executing an action (a_t), the agent observes an environment reward (R_t). The labeling function L(s_{t+1}, a_t) determines if an event occurred, potentially triggering a LARM transition (u_{t+1} = δ(u_t, L(s_{t+1}, a_t))) and yielding an additional RM reward (R_RM_t). The total reward for learning is the sum: R_total_t = R_t + R_RM_t. This provides a dense, structured learning signal.
- **Theoretical Guarantees:** The paper states that the framework yields theoretical guarantees, ensuring that the generated reward structure preserves the optimal policy of the original sparse task, provided the LARM contains no positive reward cycles and the final task reward is sufficiently dominant (as discussed in Appendix A.5).
- **Base RL Algorithms:** The framework integrates with standard RL algorithms. DQN (Mnih et al., 2013) was used for MiniGrid and BabyAI environments, PPO (Schulman et al., 2017) for Craftium, and SAC (Haarnoja et al., 2018) for Meta-World.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluation of ARM-FM was conducted across a diverse suite of challenging environments, focusing on long-horizon planning, generalization, and scalability.

#### 4.1. Performance on Sparse Reward Tasks (MiniGrid and BabyAI)

- ARM-FM consistently outperformed baseline methods, including DQN, DQN with Intrinsic Curiosity Module (ICM), and ReAct, across various MiniGrid tasks such as DoorKey (fixed and procedurally generated maps), UnlockToUnlock, BlockedUnlockPickup, and KeyCorridor.
- In complex, long-horizon tasks (UnlockToUnlock, BlockedUnlockPickup, KeyCorridor), ARM-FM agents achieved near-perfect rewards, while all baselines failed to make significant progress.
- Analysis indicated that the LARM effectively transforms sparse environmental rewards into dense, structured learning signals, guiding the agent through sequential sub-goals and bridging the credit assignment gap.

#### 4.2. Scalability to Complex 3D Environments (Craftium)

- In a Minecraft-based resource-gathering task within a procedurally generated 3D environment (Craftium), a PPO agent augmented with an FM-generated LARM consistently completed the task (mining a diamond).
- In contrast, a baseline PPO agent made minimal progress. This result demonstrated ARM-FM's ability to handle increased action dimensionality and visual complexity by decomposing high-level goals into progressive subtasks and providing intermediate rewards.

#### 4.3. Applicability to Robotic Manipulation (Meta-World)

- ARM-FM was applied to continuous control tasks in the Meta-World benchmark, automating reward engineering for robotic manipulation.
- Using SAC as the base agent, the method achieved higher success rates compared to learning from sparse rewards alone, indicating its capacity to provide richer learning signals in these domains.

#### 4.4. Generalization through Language Embeddings (XLand-MiniGrid)

- **Ablation Study on Multi-Task Learning:** An ablation study using Rainbow DQN on XLand-MiniGrid demonstrated that both the structured rewards from the LARM and the language embeddings of the current RM state are essential for learning robust, multi-task policies.
 - The baseline agent failed to generalize as the number of simultaneous tasks increased.
 - Providing only state embeddings offered a weak signal that degraded quickly.
 - Providing only LARM rewards enabled multi-task learning but led to struggles due to the policy's unawareness of the active sub-goal.
 - The full ARM-FM method, combining both dense LARM rewards and state embeddings, maintained high performance even when trained on 10 simultaneous tasks.
- **Zero-Shot Generalization:** The compositional structure of LARMs enabled zero-shot generalization to novel, unseen tasks composed of previously encountered sub-goals. An agent trained on tasks A and B successfully solved a new composite task C without additional training, by reusing learned skills based on the semantic proximity of the sub-goal embeddings.

#### 4.5. In-Depth Analysis of ARM-FM Components

- **FM Generation Quality:** An evaluation of 1,000 XLand-MiniGrid tasks showed a scaling trend where larger foundation models (e.g., Qwen3-32B) more reliably generated syntactically correct RM structures and executable Python labeling functions. Different models exhibited varying strengths in generating structure versus code.
- **Semantic Structure of State Embeddings:** PCA visualization of state instruction embeddings from 1,000 tasks revealed a coherent semantic structure. Embeddings clustered distinctly for instructions corresponding to the start, middle, and end stages of tasks. Instructions with similar meanings from different tasks also clustered together, supporting the foundation for skill transfer.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The ARM-FM framework presents a method for bridging the gap between the semantic reasoning capabilities of foundation models and the low-level control requirements of reinforcement learning agents. Its central contribution, the automated generation of Language-Aligned Reward Machines (LARMs) from natural language, has several significant implications:

- **Automated Reward Engineering:** The framework automates a historically challenging and labor-intensive aspect of RL—reward function design. This could significantly lower the barrier to entry for developing RL solutions in complex domains, allowing non-experts to specify tasks in natural language.
- **Enhanced Sample Efficiency and Task Solvability:** By transforming sparse environmental rewards into dense, structured learning signals, ARM-FM enables RL agents to solve long-horizon tasks that are intractable for standard RL methods. This leads to improved sample efficiency and expands the range of problems that RL can address.
- **Generalization and Skill Transfer:** The introduction of language embeddings for RM states is a notable advancement. It transforms the reward machine from a static plan into a compositional library of reusable skills, allowing policies to share knowledge across semantically similar subtasks. This capability facilitates efficient multi-task learning and enables zero-shot generalization to novel task compositions.
- **Scalability to Diverse Environments:** The empirical results demonstrate the framework's effectiveness across varied environments, from 2D grid worlds to complex 3D procedurally generated environments and continuous control robotics. This suggests broad applicability across different levels of environmental complexity and action spaces.
- **Interpretable and Refinable Task Specifications:** The modular, language-based structure of LARMs offers an interpretable interface for task specifications. FMs generate accurate plans, agents learn generalizable skills, and human operators can easily inspect and refine the generated task definitions. This human-in-the-loop capability could enhance trust and control in autonomous systems.
- **Foundation for Hierarchical RL:** The compositional nature of LARMs provides a principled path toward hierarchical reinforcement learning, where complex tasks are naturally decomposed into manageable sub-goals, fostering modular policy development and learning.

While the framework benefits from optional human verification during LARM generation, future work could explore automated self-correction mechanisms to reduce or eliminate this dependence. Ultimately, this research contributes to the development of a new class of RL agents that can translate high-level human intent and FM-generated plans into competent, generalizable, and interpretable behaviors, potentially broadening the impact of RL in real-world applications.
