# MoRE: Unlocking Scalability in Reinforcement Learning for Quadruped Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers integrate mixture-of-experts architectures with reinforcement learning to create MoRE, a vision-language-action model for quadruped robots that learns from mixed-quality data while achieving superior performance across six different skills compared to baseline methods in both simulated and real-world environments.

## Problem

- Current vision-language-action models for robots rely heavily on high-quality expert demonstration data, which is expensive and time-consuming to collect
- Existing approaches struggle to effectively learn from mixed-quality data including failed trajectories, limiting scalability

## Method

- Develop **MoRE** architecture combining multiple LoRA expert modules within a multimodal LLM backbone
- Implement reinforcement learning-based training objective to learn from both successful and failed trajectories
- Utilize conservative regularization for stable training with mixed-quality offline data

## Results

- Outperforms baseline methods across six different quadruped robot skills in simulation
- Successfully transfers to real-world deployment on Unitree Go2 robot
- Ablation studies confirm importance of both MoE architecture and RL training components
- Demonstrates improved generalization to out-of-distribution scenarios

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Mixture-of-experts architecture enables specialization for different robotic tasks while maintaining generalization
> - RL-based training allows effective use of suboptimal data without compromising model performance
> - Conservative regularization helps prevent overconfident predictions when learning from failed examples

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses a crucial challenge in robotics: **developing versatile and generalizable robots capable of performing complex tasks in real-world environments based on vision and language input.** The research directly builds upon and contributes to several active areas within the broader robotics and AI landscape:

- **Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Models:** The paper squarely fits within the growing field of VLA models, which aim to create robots that can understand visual inputs and natural language instructions to execute actions. It tackles the limitations of current VLA models, which primarily rely on imitation learning with high-quality expert data, which is expensive to collect.
- **Multi-Modal Large Language Models (MLLMs):** The work leverages the power of MLLMs as controllers for robots. The integration of MLLMs into robotics is a prominent trend, as these models possess extensive knowledge about the world and can process complex instructions. The paper explores how to effectively adapt these models to robotic tasks.
- **Reinforcement Learning (RL) in Robotics:** The paper advocates for the use of reinforcement learning to fine-tune VLA models, particularly for learning from mixed-quality data. It attempts to overcome the limitations of imitation learning which relies heavily on expert data, which is limited in scope and expensive to collect. This aligns with the broader trend of using RL to improve robot autonomy and adaptability.
- **Mixture of Experts (MoE) Architectures:** The research innovatively applies MoE architectures to VLA models for quadruped robots. While MoE has been explored in other domains of MLLMs such as Vision Question Answering (VQA), its application to robotics is relatively novel.
- **Quadruped Robotics:** The paper focuses on quadruped robots, which are gaining prominence due to their ability to navigate complex and unstructured terrains. Enhancing the autonomy and versatility of quadruped robots is a key research goal.

The paper positions itself within this landscape by:

- **Addressing the limitations of imitation learning:** Current VLA models often rely on high-quality expert data, which is costly to obtain and limits scalability.
- **Exploring the benefits of RL for VLA models:** The paper proposes using RL to learn from mixed-quality data, including suboptimal trajectories.
- **Investigating the suitability of MoE architectures for robotics:** The paper explores whether MoE can enhance the performance and generalization capabilities of VLA models in robotics.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The paper employs a multi-faceted methodology, combining model architecture design, reinforcement learning, and experimental validation:

1. **Model Architecture:**
 - **MoRE (Mixture of Robotic Experts):** The core of the approach is a novel VLA model called MoRE. It builds upon a dense multi-modal large language model (MLLM) using the Fuyu-8B architecture.
 - **Mixture of LoRA Experts:** MoRE incorporates multiple Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) modules as distinct experts within the MLLM. This forms a sparse-activated mixture-of-experts model, which enables the model to specialize in different tasks.
 - **Input:** The model receives RGB images and natural language instructions as inputs.
 - **Output:** It directly outputs control commands for quadruped robots.

2. **Reinforcement Learning Training:**
 - **RL-based Training Objective:** The model is trained using a reinforcement learning-based training objective as a Q-function. This allows the model to learn from mixed-quality data, including suboptimal trajectories.
 - **Offline RL:** The training is performed offline, using a pre-collected dataset of trajectories.
 - **Loss Function:** A specific loss function, based on the Bellman equation, is used to train the model as a Q-function. It also includes conservative regularization terms to ensure training stability.

3. **Experimental Validation:**
 - **Simulation Experiments:** Extensive experiments are conducted in simulation using NVIDIA Isaac Gym.
 - **Real-World Experiments:** The model is also tested in real-world scenarios using a Unitree Go2 quadruped robot.
 - **Datasets:** The experiments use a mixed-quality dataset consisting of expert data (successful tasks) and suboptimal data (failed tasks).
 - **Baselines:** The model is compared against state-of-the-art approaches, including QUART, VC-1, and CLIP.
 - **Ablation Studies:** Ablation studies are performed to evaluate the impact of different components of the model, such as the RL loss and MoE modules.
