# CIG-RL: Curiosity-Driven Information-Guided Reinforcement Learning for Source Term Estimation in Uncertain Environments

> [!summary] Summary
> CIG-RL is a deep reinforcement learning framework for gas source term estimation that pairs curiosity-driven exploration of particle-filter belief transitions with an uncertainty-adaptive active perception reward blending mutual information and distance to the estimated source. Trained with soft actor-critic under high measurement noise, it reaches 98.3% and 95.0% success rates in simulation and 100%/90% in real CO2 experiments.

## Problem

- Information-theoretic search like Infotaxis computes expected entropy reduction over every action candidate, incurring heavy online cost that limits long-horizon planning and real-time use.
- End-to-end deep RL plume trackers lack explicit source estimators, so they cannot judge when the search is complete or reliably report the source location.
- Estimator-equipped RL methods use random exploration or pure belief-uncertainty reduction, which is fragile when noisy measurements bias the belief toward wrong source hypotheses.

## Method

- ==Curiosity network== with feature extractor, ==forward and inverse heads== encodes ==GMM== belief features; forward prediction error on the next latent belief becomes the auxiliary exploration reward.
- ==Uncertainty-adaptive active perception reward== adds ==mutual information== to a source-distance term weighted by inverse ==particle-filter== variance, shifting from exploration to approach as belief sharpens.
- ==Decoupled sampling==: extrinsic reward is replayed from the buffer while the curiosity reward is recomputed online, preventing stale high-novelty values from driving excessive late-stage exploration.

## Results

- Across two high-noise settings CIG-RL scores **98.3%**/**95.0%** success with **80.2** m/**86.2** m mean travel, beating GMM IG-RL (**91.6%**/**86.0%**) and MVG-RDDPG (**90.6%**/**84.0%**).
- Ablations: dropping both components falls to **86.0%**/**82.5%**; removing variance adaptation or decoupled sampling keeps success near **95%** but raises travel distance to about **88-96 m**.
- Real CO2 trials give **100%** and **90%** success with **26.4** and **30.8** steps versus Infotaxis **70%**/**60%**, at **83 ms** per decision against Infotaxis **420 ms**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Curiosity computed in belief space, not observation space, gives an exploration signal that stays meaningful when raw sensor readings are dominated by noise.
> - Letting posterior variance arbitrate between information gathering and goal approach yields automatic exploration-exploitation scheduling without hand-tuned decay schedules.
> - Intrinsic rewards should be recomputed rather than replayed; stale novelty values stored in a buffer bias late training toward wasteful over-exploration.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses the problem of Source Term Estimation (STE), which involves identifying key properties of gas sources, such as location and release strength, using autonomous mobile sensors. This problem is challenging due to the invisible nature of gas leakage and the influence of turbulent atmospheric conditions. The broader research landscape for STE involves several approaches:

- **Information-Theoretic Approaches:** Methods like Infotaxis and Entrotaxis employ Bayesian inference for source estimation and information theory for action selection, aiming to maximize information gain or entropy reduction. While robust to noisy measurements, these methods often incur substantial computational costs due to the online calculation of expected information gain for all action candidates, which can limit their applicability for real-time operations and long-horizon planning.

- **End-to-End Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) Approaches:** DRL offers fast decision-making and the ability to learn non-myopic search policies offline. Several studies have adopted DRL for rapid source search, often incorporating memory networks (e.g., LSTM, GRU) to leverage historical measurements or using advanced DRL algorithms like Dueling DQN with CFD simulations. A common limitation of these end-to-end approaches is the absence of an explicit source estimator, which hinders reliable determination of search completion and precise source localization.

- **DRL with Explicit Source Estimators:** To overcome the limitations of end-to-end DRL, a subset of DRL-based STE research integrates explicit source estimators, typically particle filters, into the framework. Examples include PC-DQN, MVG-RDDPG, and AGDC, which focus on belief representation and search termination. However, these methods often rely on random exploration, which can be sample-inefficient and may not effectively acquire informative observations in noisy environments. Furthermore, some prior work in this category has primarily been evaluated under relatively low environmental noise conditions, limiting demonstrated robustness in more challenging scenarios. More recent efforts, such as AID-RL and GMM IG-RL, have attempted to incorporate information-theoretic principles into DRL rewards to guide policy learning by reducing uncertainty. Nevertheless, the reliance solely on current belief uncertainty reduction can be insufficient when belief states are distorted by severe noise, highlighting a need for more effective exploration strategies that consider belief state transitions.

This paper positions itself within the DRL with explicit source estimators category. It aims to address the limitations of existing DRL-based STE methods by focusing on improving policy robustness through a novel exploration strategy driven by the novelty of *belief state transitions* and an adaptively balanced information-gathering reward, particularly under highly noisy environmental conditions. It also contributes to the real-world validation gap in this research area.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL)-based framework that facilitates robust and efficient source term estimation (STE) using mobile sensors, especially in environments characterized by high noise and uncertainty.

The motivation for this work arises from several identified limitations in existing STE methodologies:

- **Computational Burden of Information-Theoretic Methods:** Traditional information-theoretic approaches, while robust in handling noisy measurements, demand substantial computational resources for online action selection. This computational cost restricts their application in real-time scenarios and for planning over extended horizons, thereby motivating the search for more efficient decision-making frameworks.

- **Limitations of End-to-End DRL in STE:** While DRL offers fast decision-making capabilities, many end-to-end DRL approaches for STE do not explicitly model the source term. This absence makes it challenging to ascertain when the search is complete or to reliably estimate the precise source location, which are critical requirements for practical applications involving hazardous gas releases.

- **Ineffective Exploration in DRL with Source Estimators:** Current DRL-based STE frameworks that integrate explicit source estimators (e.g., particle filters) often struggle to maintain policy robustness under highly noisy conditions. These methods frequently rely on random exploration, which is inefficient and lacks mechanisms to guide the agent toward informative belief states. When operating with noisy measurements, the particle filter-based belief states, which form the basis for agent decision-making, can become biased toward incorrect source hypotheses. Therefore, an effective strategy for exploring belief state transitions during training is crucial to prevent policy instability.

- **Insufficient Guidance from Uncertainty Reduction Alone:** While recent DRL methods have incorporated information gain to guide policy learning by reducing uncertainty, this approach can be limited. If the belief state itself is distorted by noisy measurements, merely reducing the uncertainty in that potentially flawed belief may not lead to robust policy learning or accurate source identification without a complementary mechanism for effective exploration of the belief space.

- **Lack of Real-World Validation:** A significant gap in the literature is the limited real-world validation of DRL-based STE methods that incorporate explicit source estimation. This leaves their practical robustness and feasibility insufficiently verified under authentic, challenging conditions.

To address these motivations, the specific objectives of the proposed research are:
1. To introduce a **curiosity-driven belief space exploration framework** that encourages the agent to actively explore novel and insufficiently learned belief state transitions, thereby enhancing policy robustness in noisy environments.
2. To design an **uncertainty-adaptive active perception reward** mechanism that intelligently balances information gathering (exploration) and source-directed approach (exploitation) based on the level of uncertainty in the estimated source term, facilitating efficient search under noisy conditions.
3. To conduct comprehensive **simulations under high-noise conditions and real-world experiments** with CO2 leakages to rigorously validate the effectiveness, robustness, and practical applicability of the proposed framework.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology, Curiosity-Driven Information-Guided Reinforcement Learning (CIG-RL), is a DRL-based framework designed for robust source search in noisy environments. It integrates a particle filter for source estimation, a GMM-based belief state representation, a curiosity-driven exploration module, and an uncertainty-adaptive active perception reward within a Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) learning framework.

**A. Problem Formulation and Models:**
- **Source Term:** The hazardous gas source is defined by its location p_s = [x_s, y_s]^T and release strength q_s, forming the source term vector θ_s = [p_s^T, q_s]^T. The study focuses on localizing p_s.
- **Gas Dispersion Model:** An isotropic plume model is employed to describe gas concentration C(p_t|θ_s) at a sensing position p_t, incorporating particle lifetime τ, diffusivity D, average wind speed V, and wind direction χ.
- **Sensor Model:** Sensor measurements z_t are modeled with Gaussian noise, including environmental noise (ν_env) and sensor noise (ν_sensor), where σ_sensor is proportional to the mean gas concentration. The likelihood p(z_t|θ_s) is defined using the total noise standard deviation σ_T.
- **Particle Filter:** A particle filter is used for online source term estimation, representing the probability distribution p(θ_t|z_1:t) using N_p weighted particles. Particle weights are updated sequentially based on measurement likelihood, and resampling with Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) is applied to mitigate degeneracy and impoverishment.

**B. State and Action:**
- **State (s_t):** Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) clustering is applied to the particles from the particle filter to extract a compact belief state representation (b_t). The belief state b_t consists of the GMM means (M_t), diagonal terms of the covariance matrices (Σ_t,diag), mixing weights (Π_t), and the mean of all particles (m_t). To handle label-switching ambiguity, GMM components are consistently ordered. The full state s_t combines the belief state and the agent's current position (p_t).
- **Action (a_t):** The agent's action space is parameterized by its heading direction. The movement distance (Δd) is fixed for each step, determining the next sensing position p_t+1.

**C. Curiosity-Driven Belief Space Exploration (Auxiliary Reward):**
- **Motivation:** To encourage exploration of novel belief state transitions, especially critical in noisy environments where beliefs can be misleading.
- **Curiosity Network:** This module comprises a feature extractor (ξ_feat), a forward network (ξ_fwd), and an inverse network (ξ_inv).
 - The feature extractor maps the particle filter-based belief state (b_t) to a latent belief representation (φ(b_t)).
 - The forward network predicts the next latent belief (ˆφ(b_t+1)) from the current latent belief (φ(b_t)), agent position (p_t), and chosen action (a_t). The prediction error between the actual and predicted next latent beliefs forms the forward network loss (L_fwd).
 - The inverse network predicts the action (ˆa_t) that led to the transition between consecutive latent beliefs, with its prediction error forming the inverse network loss (L_inv).
- **Training:** The curiosity network is trained by minimizing a weighted sum of L_fwd and L_inv.
- **Auxiliary Reward (r_a_t):** The forward network loss (L_fwd) is utilized as an auxiliary reward for the DRL agent. A high L_fwd indicates an insufficiently learned or novel belief transition, thereby encouraging the agent to explore such regions of the belief space. This reward naturally diminishes as the forward network improves its predictions.

**D. Uncertainty-Adaptive Active Perception Reward (Extrinsic Reward Component):**
- **Motivation:** To guide the agent efficiently towards the source by balancing information gathering (exploration) and source approach (exploitation) in an adaptive manner under uncertainty.
- **Components:**
 - **Mutual Information (I(p_t+1)):** Based on Shannon's entropy reduction, this term encourages the policy to reduce the uncertainty of the estimated source term by seeking informative measurements.
 - **Distance Term (d_t):** Represents the Euclidean distance between the agent's current position and the estimated source location (ˆp_t,s), encouraging direct movement towards the source.
- **Adaptive Balancing:** The balance between the mutual information and distance terms is adaptively adjusted by the inverse of the trace of the particle filter's covariance matrix (tr(Cov(θ_t))⁻¹).
 - When covariance is high (high uncertainty), the mutual information term is weighted more heavily, promoting information gathering.
 - As covariance decreases (reduced uncertainty), the distance term gains more influence, guiding the agent to approach the estimated source location.
- **Total Extrinsic Reward (r_e_t):** Comprises a sparse positive reward for successful source finding (+10), the step-wise active perception reward (r_t,step), and a penalty for moving outside the search boundary (-5).

**E. Learning Framework (CIG-RL):**
- **Reinforcement Learning Algorithm:** Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) is chosen for its off-policy learning capability and entropy maximization.
- **Total Reward (r_total_t):** The extrinsic active perception reward (r_e_t) and the auxiliary curiosity reward (r_a_t) are combined as r_total_t = r_e_t + η * r_a_t, where η is a weighting parameter.
- **Replay Buffer:** Stores tuples (s_t, a_t, r_e_t, s_t+1). A **decoupled sampling strategy** is employed: the extrinsic reward r_e_t is replayed from the buffer, while the auxiliary reward r_a_t is computed online by the current curiosity network, not replayed. This prevents bias from reusing outdated high curiosity values and ensures continuous learning of novel transitions.
- **Network Updates:** The critic network (Q-network) is updated using r_total_t to minimize the squared difference between predicted and target Q-values. The actor network is updated to maximize the Q-value while considering policy entropy. Automatic entropy tuning adjusts the temperature parameter α.
- **Termination Criteria:** Simulations terminate after 300 steps or when the particle filter's standard deviation falls below 0.1. A successful search is declared if the estimated source is within 3m (training) or 1m (evaluation) of the true source.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** Success Rate (SR), Mean Travel Distance (MTD), and Computation Time (CT) for real-world experiments.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides evidence of the proposed CIG-RL framework's robustness, efficiency, and practical applicability through numerical simulations and real-world experiments.

**A. Numerical Simulation Results:**
Simulations were conducted in two high-noise environments (Env 1: σ_env = 0.4, β = 0.25; Env 2: σ_env = 0.5, β = 0.4), representing substantially higher noise levels than typically used in prior studies. CIG-RL was compared against both information-theoretic (Infotaxis, Entrotaxis) and DRL-based (PC-DQN, MVG-RDDPG, AGDC, AID-RL, GMM IG-RL) methods.

- **Superior Performance:** CIG-RL achieved the highest success rates (SR) and lowest mean travel distances (MTD) across both challenging environments.
 - Env 1: SR of 98.3%, MTD of 80.2 m.
 - Env 2: SR of 95.0%, MTD of 86.2 m.
 - This performance surpassed all baselines, including MVG-RDDPG (SR 90.6%, MTD 91.8m in Env 1) and GMM IG-RL (SR 91.6%, MTD 95.8m in Env 1), indicating enhanced robustness and efficiency under high noise.
- **Training Robustness:** Training curves demonstrated that CIG-RL exhibited lower SR variability and consistently achieved higher SR during training compared to other DRL baselines, suggesting stable and robust policy learning.
- **Curiosity Behavior:** The auxiliary reward (forward network loss) was initially high but consistently decreased over training episodes, converging to a low value. This indicates that the belief-based curiosity mechanism effectively guided exploration of novel belief transitions and stabilized as the network learned to predict these transitions.

**B. Ablation Study:**
An ablation analysis evaluated the contributions of the curiosity-driven exploration and the uncertainty-adaptive active perception reward.
- **Combined Importance:** Removing both components significantly degraded performance (e.g., SR dropped to 86.0% in Env 1), highlighting their joint importance for robust policy learning.
- **Individual Contribution:** Removing either curiosity or the active perception reward individually also resulted in lower SR compared to the full CIG-RL, confirming their complementary roles.
- **Uncertainty-Adaptive Reward:** Ablation of the particle filter variance from the active perception reward led to increased MTD, demonstrating the critical role of adaptively balancing information gathering and source approach for search efficiency.
- **Decoupled Sampling:** The decoupled sampling strategy for the auxiliary reward (not replaying old curiosity values) outperformed a variant that replayed auxiliary rewards, suggesting that reusing previously high curiosity values can lead to inefficient excessive exploration in later training stages.

**C. Sensitivity Analysis:**
A sensitivity analysis on the weighting factor (η) for the auxiliary reward revealed that an optimal balance is crucial.
- Low η (e.g., 0.5): Insufficient exploration, leading to degraded SR.
- High η (e.g., 10): Excessive exploration in early training, increasing MTD.
- η = 5 yielded the best overall performance, balancing exploration and exploitation.

**D. Real-World Experiments:**
Real-world experiments were conducted using a mobile sensor platform with a CO2 tank as the source in two gym environments (Env A and Env B, with Env B being more challenging due to sparse measurements). CIG-RL was compared with Infotaxis and MVG-RDDPG.
- **High Success Rate and Efficiency:** CIG-RL achieved superior success rates and required fewer steps (SN) to locate the source.
 - Env A: CIG-RL achieved 100% SR with 26.4 SN, outperforming MVG-RDDPG (80% SR, 34.8 SN) and Infotaxis (70% SR, 49.3 SN).
 - Env B: CIG-RL achieved 90% SR with 30.8 SN, compared to MVG-RDDPG (60% SR, 48.6 SN) and Infotaxis (60% SR, 61.3 SN).
- **Computational Efficiency:** CIG-RL maintained practical computation times (CT) for one-step decision-making (82.6 ms in Env A, 83.8 ms in Env B), comparable to MVG-RDDPG and significantly faster than Infotaxis (over 400 ms), demonstrating its suitability for real-time deployment.
- **Optimized Trajectories:** Search trajectories showed that CIG-RL generated more efficient paths to the source, avoiding the oscillatory movements observed in Infotaxis and MVG-RDDPG, and leading to faster particle filter convergence.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of autonomous source term estimation by proposing a novel reinforcement learning framework that addresses critical challenges in uncertain and noisy environments. The significance and potential impact of this work are multifaceted:

- **Enhanced Robustness and Efficiency in STE:** The primary impact is the demonstration of a DRL framework that can robustly and efficiently estimate gas source terms even under severe environmental and sensor noise. By combining curiosity-driven exploration with an uncertainty-adaptive reward mechanism, the method provides a more reliable approach to navigating complex and uncertain gas dispersion landscapes compared to existing methods.

- **Improved Exploration Strategies in DRL for State Estimation:** The introduction of curiosity-driven exploration in the belief space, specifically using prediction error of latent belief transitions, offers a novel strategy for DRL agents operating in environments where raw observations are noisy and belief states are complex. The decoupled sampling strategy for auxiliary rewards further refines exploration by preventing the reuse of outdated curiosity signals, contributing to more effective and efficient learning.

- **Adaptive Policy Guidance:** The uncertainty-adaptive active perception reward provides a principled way to balance the trade-off between information gathering and direct source approach. This adaptive mechanism, responsive to the reliability of the source estimate (particle filter variance), allows the agent to dynamically prioritize exploration or exploitation, which is crucial for optimizing search in evolving uncertainty conditions.

- **Practical Applicability for Real-World Problems:** The rigorous validation through both high-noise simulations and real-world CO2 leakage experiments is a significant aspect of this work. The results confirm the feasibility and practical utility of CIG-RL, demonstrating its ability to operate effectively with real sensors, turbulent gas plumes, and under time constraints. This makes it a promising candidate for deployment in actual hazardous gas release scenarios.

- **Foundation for Advanced Autonomous Systems:** The CIG-RL framework provides a robust foundation for developing next-generation autonomous systems for environmental monitoring, emergency response, and industrial safety. Its capability to rapidly and accurately locate gas leaks can reduce risks to human personnel and mitigate environmental damage.

- **Extensibility to Complex Scenarios:** The paper suggests future work on extending the framework to multi-agent systems, indicating its potential for scalability and applicability in even more complex and larger-scale environments, further enhancing the efficiency and coverage of search operations.
