# DiffCoTune: Differentiable Co-Tuning for Cross-domain Robot Control

> [!summary] Summary
> The DiffCoTune framework, developed by researchers from USC and the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign, introduces an automated, gradient-based method for simultaneously tuning robot controller parameters and simulator parameters. This co-tuning approach addresses the sim-to-real gap, improving controller performance in deployment domains by up to 60% in some tasks, with only a few real-world rollouts.

## Problem

- Robot controllers designed in simulators often exhibit performance degradation when transferred to physical robots due to unmodeled dynamics and discrepancies, known as the "sim-to-real gap."
- Bridging this gap traditionally requires time-consuming and inefficient manual or ad-hoc tuning of controller parameters.
- Existing methods like system identification or controller adaptation in isolation often have limitations, such as leading to conservative performance or struggling with high-dimensional parameter spaces and biased gradients.

## Method

- **DiffCoTune** proposes an iterative, ==gradient-based co-tuning== framework that simultaneously adapts parameters of a ==differentiable simulator== and a ==differentiable controller== in an intermediate tuning domain.
- It defines two objective functions: a ==system identification objective== to match closed-loop dynamics of the model to the target system, and a ==task objective== for controller performance in the model.
- Two update strategies are offered: a ==Combined== strategy for joint optimization and a more robust ==Split-Alternate== strategy that iteratively refines simulator parameters based on real-world rollouts, then tunes controller parameters in the refined simulator.

## Results

- DiffCoTune improved cartpole stabilization performance, transforming an under-damped response to critically damped, and reduced steady-state velocity error and overshoot for DNN controllers with extreme mass discrepancies.
- For quadruped velocity tracking, DiffCoTune Split-Alternate prevented falls and improved performance by **60%** in sim-to-sim transfer and enabled a Unitree Go1 robot to travel **1.04m** further in 5 seconds during high-speed real-world motion.
- The framework enabled a bipedal robot to achieve a **34 cm** jump on hardware, correcting torso pitch and foot placement for stable landing, where an untuned controller failed and controller-only tuning resulted in a fall.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Simultaneous co-tuning of both simulator and controller parameters is essential for robust cross-domain transfer, especially in the presence of significant model uncertainty, outperforming controller-only tuning.
> - Gradient-based tuning methods, leveraging differentiable simulators, are significantly more efficient than gradient-free alternatives like Bayesian Optimization, achieving comparable solutions with substantially fewer real-world rollouts.
> - An iterative data collection and tuning scheme, where real-world rollouts from a progressively tuned controller inform subsequent updates, leads to greater performance improvements than a one-shot "sysId-then-tune" approach.
