# ViPRA: Video Prediction for Robot Actions

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Carnegie Mellon University and Skild AI developed ViPRA, a framework that enables continuous robot control by leveraging large-scale actionless videos. The system uses motion-centric latent actions and multimodal pretraining to achieve high success rates on complex manipulation tasks in both simulation and real-world environments, requiring minimal labeled data for adaptation and generating smooth, high-frequency actions.

## Problem

- Robot learning relies on extensive, costly expert demonstrations, limiting scalability and preventing the use of abundant actionless video data.
- Existing video prediction models and latent action approaches often lack the ability to directly translate visual understanding into precise, high-frequency continuous robot actions or struggle with generalization.
- Prior methods for robot learning from videos often incur high inference costs or fail to explicitly model fine-grained physical dynamics, hindering real-time dexterous control.

## Method

- **ViPRA** introduces a three-stage framework: (1) ==Latent Action Learning== from actionless videos, (2) ==Multimodal Pretraining== that integrates these latents with a video-language model, and (3) ==Continuous Adaptation via Flow Matching==.
- It extracts ==motion-centric latent actions== that encapsulate how an environment changes and jointly predicts future visual states alongside these latent actions to build a robust prior of scene dynamics.
- The framework adapts to continuous robot actions using a ==flow matching decoder==, which, combined with ==action chunking==, generates smooth, high-frequency (up to 22 Hz) control outputs from minimal labeled demonstrations.

## Results

- ViPRA achieved an average success rate of **69.8%** (discrete) and **62.5%** (continuous) on the SIMPLER benchmark, surpassing strong baselines like LAPA, OpenVLA, and UniVLA.
- On the LIBERO Long benchmark, ViPRA attained a **79%** success rate on long-horizon, multi-stage manipulation tasks, demonstrating effective transfer of motion-centric latent pretraining.
- In real-world single-arm manipulation, ViPRA-FM achieved an average success rate of **54.1%**, outperforming π0 and Scratch-FM, and showed effective transfer to challenging bimanual tasks with minimal finetuning data.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Jointly predicting both future visual states ("what changes") and motion-centric latent actions ("how the scene evolves") during pretraining is crucial for learning grounded and transferable control priors.
> - Pretraining on a diverse corpus of human and robot videos, rather than solely robot data, enhances generalization capabilities across different robot embodiments.
> - Utilizing flow matching for continuous action generation, coupled with action chunking, provides stable and high-frequency control, essential for dexterous real-world manipulation, outperforming discrete action policies.
