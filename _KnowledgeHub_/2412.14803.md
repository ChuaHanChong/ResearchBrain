# Video Prediction Policy: A Generalist Robot Policy with Predictive Visual Representations

> [!summary] Summary
> Video Prediction Policy (VPP) introduces a generalist robot policy that leverages predictive visual representations extracted from a fine-tuned video diffusion model in a single forward pass. This approach enables the policy to implicitly learn inverse dynamics from rough future predictions, achieving an average task completion length of 4.33 on the CALVIN ABC→D benchmark and robust generalization across various real-world manipulation tasks with Franka Panda and dexterous hands.

## Problem

- Existing visual representations for generalist robot policies often focus on static information, neglecting the crucial temporal dynamics necessary for complex, embodied tasks.
- Prior methods leveraging future prediction for robot control typically suffer from low control frequencies or sub-optimal prediction quality due to multi-step generative processes.
- Developing generalist robot policies that can robustly generalize across a wide array of tasks and environments, including dexterous manipulation and unseen scenarios, remains a challenge.

## Method

- Develops a two-stage approach: first, fine-tuning an open-sourced large ==video foundation model (SVD)== into a ==text-guided video prediction (TVP)== model using a diverse blend of internet-scale human and robot manipulation videos.
- Utilizes the frozen TVP model as a ==predictive visual encoder== by extracting internal representations from a single, efficient ==forward pass==, rather than generating full video sequences.
- Employs a ==Video Former== module to condense aggregated multi-layer features from the TVP model and condition a ==Diffusion Policy== head for high-frequency, closed-loop action generation.

## Results

- Achieves an average task completion length of **4.33** on the CALVIN ABC→D benchmark, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art by **41.5%** (over GR-1), and outperforms GR-1 even when trained with only **10%** of the data.
- Attains an average success rate of **0.682** across 50 MetaWorld tasks, demonstrating a **10.8%** absolute improvement over the strongest baseline (GR-1).
- Exhibits strong real-world generalization on unseen objects and tasks with a Franka Panda robot (**0.73** success rate for unseen tasks) and an Xarm with a 12-degree Xhand dexterous hand (**0.68** for challenging tool-use tasks).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Internal representations of video diffusion models (VDMs), even from a single forward pass, implicitly encode rich spatio-temporal dynamics that are highly effective for robot control.
> - A downstream policy can implicitly learn inverse dynamics by tracking robot movements within these predictive visual representations, simplifying action generation.
> - Combining large-scale internet video pre-training with diverse robot manipulation datasets enables exceptional generalization and data efficiency for robot policies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of generalist robot policies capable of performing a wide array of tasks is a rapidly evolving and critical area of robotics research. Traditional approaches often rely on task-specific policies or extensive demonstrations, limiting their adaptability. The field is moving towards "foundation models" for robotics, mirroring the success of large language models (LLMs) and vision transformers (ViTs) in other AI domains.

This paper positions itself at the intersection of several key research trends:

- **Generalist Robot Policies:** Inspired by recent successes like RT-2 and Octo, the paper acknowledges the importance of vision encoders for capturing information from pixel observations. The challenge lies in creating policies that can generalize across tasks and environments.
- **Visual Representation Learning for Robotics:** A substantial body of work focuses on pre-training vision encoders using self-supervised learning (SSL) techniques (e.g., contrastive learning, image reconstruction, masked autoencoders) on internet-scale image and video datasets (e.g., R3M, VC-1, Voltron). However, the authors argue that these methods often capture primarily *static* information or operate on individual images/pairs, thus neglecting the crucial *dynamic* aspects inherent in sequential embodied tasks.
- **Future Prediction for Embodied Control:** Researchers have explored leveraging future visual predictions to inform robot actions (e.g., SuSIE, UniPi, GR-1). While promising, these methods often suffer from limitations such as low control frequency (due to time-consuming denoising steps) or sub-optimal prediction quality, especially for long-horizon or complex dynamics. GR-1, a notable prior work, generates frames and actions autoregressively but does not leverage pre-trained video foundation models to their full potential.
- **Visual Representations within Diffusion Models:** More recently, diffusion models, originally designed for generative tasks (image and video synthesis), have been explored as powerful vision encoders. Studies have shown that representations learned within image diffusion models are versatile for discriminative and embodied tasks. However, the specific utility of representations *within video diffusion models* for *sequential embodied control* has been less explored.

"Video Prediction Policy" (VPP) addresses these limitations by proposing a novel paradigm. It leverages the inherent capability of powerful video diffusion models (VDMs) to predict future frames, arguing that the *internal representations* of these VDMs implicitly encode both static and dynamic information crucial for robot control. Unlike prior works that might use a VDM to *generate* a target image or a full video sequence, VPP uses the VDM as a *predictive visual encoder* by performing only a *single forward pass*. This innovative approach aims to unlock the "predictive property" of VDM representations, enabling high-frequency, closed-loop control while benefiting from the extensive physical world knowledge embedded in large-scale video pre-training. VPP directly competes with state-of-the-art generalist policies and future-prediction methods, aiming to surpass them by effectively integrating dynamic understanding into robotic policy learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind the Video Prediction Policy (VPP) stems from a critical observation about existing approaches in generalist robotics: while visual representations are paramount, current methods often fall short in capturing the *dynamic* information essential for complex, embodied tasks.

The key objectives and motivations can be summarized as follows:

1. **Address the limitations of static visual representations:** Traditional vision encoders, often pre-trained on single images or pairs, excel at capturing static object information but frequently neglect the temporal dynamics crucial for robots interacting with their environment. The authors hypothesize that this oversight limits the generalization and performance of generalist robotic policies.
2. **Leverage the inherent predictive power of Video Diffusion Models (VDMs):** VDMs have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in predicting future frames and understanding physical dynamics. The authors propose a key hypothesis: VDMs, by their very nature, generate internal visual representations that intrinsically combine current static information with rich, predicted future dynamics. These "predictive visual representations" are hypothesized to be uniquely valuable for guiding robot action learning.
3. **Enable Implicit Inverse Dynamics Learning:** The central insight is that if a video model can accurately predict future scenarios for diverse tasks, a downstream policy can implicitly learn the inverse dynamics by tracking the robot's movements *within* these predictive representations. This allows the policy to generate appropriate actions without needing an explicit, complex inverse dynamics model.
4. **Transfer Generalization Capabilities:** By fine-tuning pre-trained video foundation models on robotic and human manipulation data, the goal is to transfer the vast generalization capabilities of these large video models directly to the robotic policy. This aims to allow the policy to succeed even with limited robot-specific demonstrations.
5. **Achieve High-Frequency, Closed-Loop Control:** Unlike some prior works that involve time-consuming multi-step denoising to generate explicit future images, VPP aims to use the VDM as a *vision encoder* in a single, efficient forward pass. This design choice is motivated by the need for high control frequency in real-world closed-loop robot operation, circumventing latency issues inherent in full video generation.
6. **Develop a Generalist Robot Policy:** The ultimate objective is to build a robust generalist policy that can solve a wide variety of tasks in both simulated and real-world environments, outperforming current state-of-the-art methods in terms of success rate and generalization, particularly for long-horizon and dexterous manipulation tasks.

In essence, VPP seeks to bridge the gap between powerful video generation capabilities and robust robotic control by reinterpreting video prediction models not just as generators, but as sophisticated encoders of spatio-temporal dynamics, thereby providing a more informative and efficient visual input for action learning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Video Prediction Policy (VPP) employs a novel two-stage learning process designed to harness the predictive capabilities of video diffusion models for generalist robot control.

**Stage 1: Text-guided Video Prediction (TVP) Model for Robot Manipulation**

The first stage focuses on developing a specialized "Manipulation TVP Model" that can accurately predict future video sequences relevant to robot manipulation, leveraging large-scale prior knowledge.

1. **Foundation Model Selection:** The authors start with the open-sourced **Stable Video Diffusion (SVD)** model, a large video foundation model with 1.5 billion parameters, known for its general video generation capabilities.
2. **Augmentation for Controllability:**
 - The base SVD model, which typically conditions only on an initial frame, is augmented to incorporate **CLIP (Contrastive Language-Image Pre-training) language features** via cross-attention layers. This allows the model to be guided by textual instructions, making it "text-guided."
 - The output video resolution is adjusted to `16x256x256` (16 frames, 256x256 pixels) for efficiency, while preserving the core SVD architecture.
 - The initial observation (`s_0`) is concatenated channel-wise with each predicted frame as a condition for the diffusion process.
3. **Fine-tuning on Manipulation Datasets:** The augmented TVP model (`V_θ`) is fine-tuned using a diffusion objective to reconstruct full video sequences. Crucially, this fine-tuning is performed on a diverse blend of datasets:
 - **Internet Human Manipulation Data (D_H):** e.g., Something-something-v2, providing broad human-centric manipulation dynamics.
 - **Internet Robot Manipulation Data (D_R):** e.g., RT-1, Bridge, BC-Z, Taco-Play, Jaco-Play, providing robot-specific manipulation data.
 - **Self-Collected Datasets (D_C):** e.g., Calvin, MetaWorld, Franka Panda, Dexterous Hand, focusing on the specific downstream tasks.
 - **Dataset Balancing:** To account for varying quality and scale, different sampling ratios (`λ`) are applied to each dataset type during training, similar to approaches in large-scale robotic datasets like Octo. This ensures a balanced influence and comprehensive learning of physical knowledge relevant to manipulation.
4. **Freezing the TVP Model:** Once fine-tuned, the Manipulation TVP model is frozen and used as a fixed component in the subsequent action learning stage.

**Stage 2: Action Learning Conditioned on Predictive Visual Representation**

The second stage focuses on learning the robot policy itself, using the frozen TVP model as a powerful visual encoder.

1. **TVP Model as a "Vision Encoder":** Instead of performing a full, time-consuming multi-step denoising process to generate a clear video, VPP utilizes the TVP model to produce "predictive visual representations" from just a *single forward step*. The authors argue that even this single step, while not yielding a perfectly clear video, provides sufficient "rough trajectory of future states and valuable guidance" for policy learning. This design choice is critical for achieving high control frequency (7-10 Hz). The current image (`s_0`) is concatenated with a noised latent (`q(x_t'|x_0)`) and fed into the TVP model.
2. **Feature Aggregation Module:**
 - The TVP model, as a diffusion model, contains multiple up-sampling layers. Previous work suggests that features from these intermediate layers are more effective representations.
 - VPP proposes an automatic method to aggregate features (`L_m`) from multiple up-sampling layers (e.g., `m` layers). Each layer's feature map is first linearly interpolated to a consistent spatial dimension (`W_p x H_p`).
 - These interpolated features (`L'_m`) are then concatenated along the channel dimension to form a final high-dimensional "predictive visual representation" (`F_p`). This aggregation avoids manual layer selection and leverages a richer set of features.
 - For multi-camera setups (e.g., third-view and wrist camera), representations are predicted independently for each view.
3. **Video Former:**
 - The aggregated predictive representation (`F_p`) is still high-dimensional (a sequence of image features). To condense this information into a fixed number of tokens suitable for policy learning, a **Video Former** module is designed.
 - It initializes learnable tokens (`Q`) and processes them through **spatial-temporal attention** (similar to architectures like DiT or Perceiver) on the aggregated features (`F_p`). This allows the Video Former to efficiently consolidate information across spatial locations, time steps (the predicted future sequence), and multiple camera views into a compact set of tokens (`Q''`).
4. **Action Generation (Diffusion Policy Head):**
 - The compressed tokens (`Q''`) from the Video Former, along with the CLIP language embedding (`l_emb`), serve as conditions for a **Diffusion Policy** head.
 - The Diffusion Policy (based on a Diffusion Transformer, DiT) learns to denoise a noisy action sequence (`a_k`) back to the original action sequence (`a_0`). This process aims to reconstruct the correct robot actions that align with the observed and predicted visual dynamics, implicitly learning the inverse dynamics model.
 - The policy is trained to minimize a diffusion loss, effectively learning a denoiser (`D_ψ`).

By decoupling video prediction from action generation and leveraging the internal representations of VDMs, VPP aims to achieve both state-of-the-art generalization and practical control frequencies.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on both simulated and real-world robotic tasks demonstrate that the Video Prediction Policy (VPP) consistently outperforms existing state-of-the-art methods.

#### 4.1. Simulation Results

- **CALVIN ABC→D Benchmark (Long-horizon generalization):**
 - VPP achieves a remarkable **average task completion length of 4.33**, significantly surpassing the previous state-of-the-art (SOTA) method, Robo-Uniview (3.65), and GR-1 (3.06). This represents a **41.5% improvement** over GR-1 (as stated in the abstract).
 - Even when trained with only **10% of the annotated Calvin ABC data**, VPP achieves an average length of 3.25, which *exceeds* the performance of GR-1 using 100% of the data, highlighting its exceptional data efficiency and generalization capabilities derived from pre-trained video models.
- **MetaWorld Benchmark (Multi-task dexterity):**
 - VPP achieves an average success rate of **0.682** across 50 diverse manipulation tasks.
 - This is a **10.8% absolute improvement** over the strongest baseline, GR-1 (0.574), demonstrating VPP's superior precision and dexterity for complex tasks.
- **Visualization of Predictive Representations:** Visualizations confirm that even a single-step forward pass through the TVP model provides valuable information about future physical evolution, including object and robot arm movements, despite the lack of precise textures and details. This supports the core hypothesis that these "rough" predictions are sufficient for effective policy learning.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies (Calvin ABC→D)

- **Effectiveness of Predictive Visual Representations:** Replacing VPP's VDM-based encoder with other pre-trained vision encoders (Stable-VAE, VC-1, Voltron) leads to a significant performance drop (e.g., from 4.33 to 2.58 for Stable-VAE, and even lower for others), confirming the superiority of VDM-derived predictive representations.
- **Effectiveness of Video Pre-training and Internet Manipulation Datasets:**
 - Removing co-trained internet manipulation data results in a performance decrease (4.33 to 3.97).
 - Removing the pre-trained SVD model entirely (training TVP from scratch) leads to a substantial drop to 1.63, demonstrating the critical role of large-scale pre-training.
 - Even without fine-tuning on Calvin video data alone, performance declines (3.31), emphasizing the cumulative benefit of all data sources.
- **Effectiveness of Video Former:** Removing the Video Former module (directly connecting TVP features to Diffusion Policy) causes a decrease in performance (4.33 to 3.86) and nearly triples inference time, validating its importance for both accuracy and computational efficiency.
- **Effectiveness of Feature Aggregation Module:** Replacing the aggregated multi-layer features with only the final layer's features leads to a performance drop (4.33 to 3.60), indicating that aggregating information from various up-sampling layers is beneficial for capturing comprehensive representations.

#### 4.3. Real-World Experiments

- **Franka Panda Robot Arm (30+ tasks):** VPP achieves high success rates (0.85 for seen tasks, 0.73 for unseen tasks), significantly outperforming Diffusion Policy (0.42, 0.25), Susie (0.56, 0.46), and GR-1 (0.52, 0.38) in both categories.
- **Xarm with 12-degree Xhand Dexterous Hand (100+ tasks):** VPP demonstrates superior performance with the dexterous hand, achieving 0.75 for seen tasks, 0.60 for unseen tasks, and notably **0.68 for challenging tool-use tasks**. This compares favorably to baselines (e.g., GR-1 at 0.32, 0.15, 0.15 respectively).
- **Generalization Analysis:** VPP exhibits strong generalization to unseen objects and tasks (e.g., tennis ball, pouring Coca-Cola, using a spoon, none of which were in its collected dataset). This is attributed to two factors: (1) the TVP model's ability to make accurate visual predictions for novel scenarios due to internet-scale pre-training, and (2) the low-level policy learning a robust inverse dynamics model that implicitly tracks robot movement within the predicted future, reducing reliance on explicit knowledge of new objects.

Overall, the findings strongly support the VPP hypothesis, demonstrating that leveraging predictive visual representations from fine-tuned video diffusion models is highly effective for learning robust, generalizable, and efficient robot policies across a wide range of complexities and environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Video Prediction Policy (VPP) represents a significant advancement in the field of generalist robotics, with several key implications and potential impacts:

1. **Paradigm Shift in Visual Representation Learning for Robotics:** VPP pioneers the idea of using video diffusion models not just for generating videos, but as powerful *predictive visual encoders* for embodied control. By extracting dynamic, future-aware representations from a single, efficient forward pass, it moves beyond static image features and costly full-video denoising, enabling a new class of vision-based robot policies.
2. **State-of-the-Art Generalization and Performance:** The consistent and substantial improvements over previous state-of-the-art methods in challenging benchmarks (Calvin, MetaWorld) and real-world settings (Franka Panda, Dexterous Hand) underscore VPP's effectiveness. Its ability to perform well with only 10% of training data on Calvin demonstrates remarkable data efficiency, which is critical for real-world robotic deployment where large, diverse datasets are expensive to collect.
3. **Enhanced Real-World Applicability:** Achieving high control frequencies (7-10 Hz) on consumer-level GPUs is crucial for responsive and stable closed-loop robot operation. This practical efficiency, combined with its strong performance on dexterous manipulation and novel tool-use tasks, makes VPP a highly promising candidate for deployment in complex real-world scenarios.
4. **Implicit Inverse Dynamics Learning:** The insight that policies can implicitly learn inverse dynamics by tracking robot movements within predicted future representations simplifies the policy learning problem. This approach potentially bypasses the need for explicit, often brittle, inverse kinematics or dynamics models, leveraging the robust implicit understanding of physics within large video models.
5. **Unlocking the Full Potential of Video Foundation Models:** VPP highlights the vast, untapped potential of video generation models for embodied AI. As video models continue to grow in scale and sophistication, VPP provides a blueprint for how their rich understanding of physical dynamics can be directly channeled into practical robotic intelligence, beyond just synthetic content generation.
6. **Addressing Key Challenges in Generalist Robotics:** The work directly tackles the core challenges of generalization to unseen tasks, objects, and environments, as well as enabling dexterous manipulation and complex tool use. By effectively combining internet-scale data with robot-specific fine-tuning, VPP pushes the boundaries of what generalist robot policies can achieve.

In conclusion, VPP represents a significant step towards building truly generalist robots that can flexibly and robustly operate in diverse, dynamic environments. Its novel integration of predictive visual representations from video diffusion models paves the way for future research at the intersection of generative AI and embodied intelligence, promising more capable, adaptable, and efficient robotic systems.
