# Being-M0.5: A Real-Time Controllable Vision-Language-Motion Model

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers introduce Being-M0.5, a vision-language-motion model that achieves real-time controllable motion generation, supported by the large-scale HuMo100M dataset. The model delivers state-of-the-art performance across nine motion tasks, including fine-grained part-level control and inference speeds exceeding 20 frames per second.

## Problem

- Existing motion generators struggle with comprehensive controllability, including responding to diverse commands, arbitrary initial poses, long-term sequences, unseen scenarios, and precise part-level control.
- Practical deployment of motion generation is limited by slow inference speeds, preventing real-time applications.
- A lack of large-scale, high-quality multimodal datasets with fine-grained and long-term annotations impedes the development of robust vision-language-motion models.

## Method

- ==Introduced HuMo100M==, a multimodal human motion dataset with over 5 million sequences and 100 million instructional instances, featuring part-level and long-term annotations.
- Developed **Being-M0.5**, a 7B-parameter Vision-Language-Motion Model, incorporating a multi-stage training curriculum and a novel Part-aware Residual Quantization (PRQ) for fine-grained control.
- Optimized for real-time performance through specific design choices, including the HuMo263 motion feature for efficient data representation and a frame-by-frame decoding strategy.

## Results

- Being-M0.5-PRQ4 demonstrated state-of-the-art performance across nine motion generation tasks, including a **60%** FID improvement (**0.056**) on HumanML3D for T2M and significantly lower FID (**1.25**) on HuMo-I2PM for part-level control.
- The model achieved real-time inference speeds exceeding 20 FPS on all tested GPUs, reaching up to **28.9** FPS on H100, enabled by optimized design choices.
- Part-aware Residual Quantization (PRQ) enabled precise part-level motion control, evidenced by a **76%** success rate in left-right exchange tests, and comprehensive multi-task training improved overall model controllability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Comprehensive multi-task pretraining on a large-scale, diverse dataset like HuMo100M is crucial for achieving robust and controllable motion generation across various tasks.
> - The Part-aware Residual Quantization (PRQ) method, with its decomposition into part-specific features, enables precise, fine-grained control over individual body parts while preserving motion quality.
> - Achieving real-time inference requires specific architectural optimizations, including an efficient motion feature representation like HuMo263 and a frame-by-frame decoding strategy.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Human motion generation is a field with applications in areas such as video games, film production, and humanoid robotics. Early methods for motion generation, often text-to-motion (T2M) or action-to-motion, faced challenges in producing realistic and diverse outputs, leading to the development of stochastic techniques like Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) and Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs). Recent advancements have integrated large language models (LLMs) to enhance understanding of human intent, as seen in models like MotionGPT and MotionLLM, which explore multi-turn conversational generation and unified frameworks for motion comprehension.

A parallel development in artificial intelligence is the emergence of Vision-Language Models (VLMs), such as BLIP-2 and LLaVA, which leverage multimodal (visual and textual) contexts for various tasks. Inspired by these, Vision-Language-Motion Models (VLMMs) have been developed to incorporate human motion data, sometimes including visual cues for improved motion understanding.

Despite these advancements, the field encounters significant limitations:
1. **Controllability:** Existing motion generators often lack the ability to handle diverse user commands, accommodate arbitrary initial poses, generate long-term or novel motion sequences, and provide precise part-level control. Domain-specific models perform well within their trained scenarios but generalize poorly beyond them.
2. **Real-time Inference:** Practical deployment of motion generation technologies is often hindered by slow inference speeds.
3. **Data Scarcity:** High-quality, large-scale multimodal datasets specifically for motion generation, especially those with fine-grained annotations and long-term sequences, are limited. While some efforts have begun to extract motions from web videos, the effective utilization of this data, particularly beyond simple scaling, remains a challenge.
4. **Motion Tokenization:** Effective representation of motion data is crucial. Vector quantization (VQ) and its variants (Residual Quantization, Hierarchical Quantization, Finite Scalar Quantization) have improved encoding, but fine-grained, part-level control with corresponding annotations and evaluation benchmarks has largely been overlooked.

This research addresses these gaps by proposing a novel VLMM designed for real-time, comprehensive controllability and by introducing a large-scale, extensively annotated dataset, HuMo100M, to facilitate this development. It aims to bridge the gap between advanced multimodal understanding and practical, efficient motion generation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a real-time, controllable Vision-Language-Motion Model (VLMM) that achieves state-of-the-art performance across multiple motion generation tasks. This objective is driven by the identified limitations of existing VLMMs and the need for more practical and adaptable motion generation technologies.

Specifically, the motivations and objectives are detailed as follows:

1. **Addressing the Controllability Bottleneck:** The researchers identify a main bottleneck in existing VLMMs: inadequate controllability, which manifests in five key aspects. The work aims to overcome each of these:
 - **Inadequate Response to Diverse Human Commands:** Current models often struggle to effectively interpret and execute arbitrary natural language instructions. The objective is to enhance the model's ability to follow a broad range of user commands.
 - **Limited Pose Initialization Capabilities:** Existing VLMMs are frequently constrained to generating motion from fixed initial poses, limiting their adaptability. The goal is to enable the model to generate coherent motion from arbitrary or random initial pose configurations.
 - **Poor Performance on Long-Term Sequences:** Human activities are typically continuous and extended. The research aims to enable the generation of seamless, long-duration motion sequences, moving beyond isolated short clips.
 - **Insufficient Handling of Unseen Scenarios:** Generalization to novel motion patterns not present in training data is a significant challenge. The objective is to improve the model's ability to generate motions for previously unseen actions or scenarios.
 - **Lack of Fine-Grained Control Over Individual Body Parts:** Current models typically rely on whole-body representations, hindering precise control over specific anatomical regions. A key motivation is to achieve fine-grained, part-level control over human motion, such as controlling individual limbs.

2. **Enabling Real-Time Performance:** Practical deployment of motion generation technologies requires high inference speeds. The research is motivated by the need to balance model performance with computational efficiency, particularly for real-time applications, a topic that has received limited attention in prior work.

3. **Constructing a Comprehensive Motion Dataset:** A major limitation in motion generation research is the scarcity of high-quality, large-scale motion datasets. The researchers are motivated to create HuMo100M, the largest and most comprehensive human motion dataset to date, to provide the necessary data scale and diversity, including specific annotations that address the identified controllability gaps. This includes:
 - Part-level annotations for fine-grained control.
 - Long-term motion sequences to facilitate extended generation.
 - Text-aligned visual clips to leverage weak supervision from web videos.

4. **Developing Novel Architectural Innovations:** To achieve fine-grained control, particularly at the body part level, the research is motivated to develop a new motion tokenization technique. The existing reliance on single code embeddings for the entire body and the absence of part-specific annotations necessitate an innovative approach.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research involves the creation of a large-scale multimodal dataset, HuMo100M, and the development of a novel Vision-Language-Motion Model (VLMM), Being-M0.5, alongside specific architectural and design choices aimed at achieving real-time, controllable motion generation.

#### 3.1. The HuMo100M Dataset

HuMo100M is presented as the largest multimodal human motion dataset to date, comprising over 5 million self-collected motion sequences and 100 million multi-task instructional instances. Its construction involved a multi-stage data curation pipeline:

- **Data Curation:** Over 20 million videos were collected from public datasets and online platforms.
 - **Content Filtering:** Initial filtering used video metadata to retain human-related content.
 - **Human Tracking and Segmentation:** YOLO-based tracking identified and extracted human-centric segments, followed by video segmentation to maintain spatiotemporal consistency of motion sequences.
 - **Quality Assessment:** A pre-trained 2D keypoint detector was used to assess occlusion levels by filtering based on confidence thresholds and minimum visible keypoints. Short motion sequences were removed to ensure sufficient temporal context.
 - **SMPL Parameter Extraction:** SMPL parameters were extracted using WHAM to regress 3D human motion.
 - **Motion Quality Enhancement:** An RL-based policy, PHC, was applied for further enhancement.
- **Key Advantages and Innovations:**
 - **Part-Level Text Description:** Augments motion sequences with limb-specific and rule-based descriptions, generated using Gemini-1.5-Pro and PoseScript. This provides fine-grained supervision for specific anatomical regions, complementing traditional body-level annotations.
 - **Long-Term Motion Sequence:** Addresses the limitation of short-duration clips by developing two motion concatenation methods:
 - *Interpolation-based Concatenation:* Uses orientation alignment, global coordinate alignment, and spherical linear interpolation (Slerp) with a neutral standing pose to create smooth transitions between sequences.
 - *Learning-based Concatenation:* Employs a fine-tuned text-to-motion model for generating contextually appropriate transitions using a masking approach during training. A hybrid framework combines these two methods during inference.
 - **Text-Aligned Visual Clips:** Integrates visual cues from web videos, providing weak supervision and rich contextual information to enhance motion understanding and compensate for noisy or incomplete motion data, especially beneficial for internet-sourced videos.

#### 3.2. Being-M0.5 Model Architecture

Being-M0.5 is a 7B-parameter Vision-Language-Motion Model built on the LLaVA-video-7B framework.

- **Core Components:**
 - A 400M visual encoder (SigLIP).
 - A 2-layer MLP for visual feature projection.
 - A 7B LLaMA-2-chat backbone.
- **Visual Processing:** A slow-fast strategy is adopted to efficiently process extended video sequences, reducing visual token complexity while preserving temporal information.
- **Motion Representation:** Human motion is conceptualized as a structured language, with a motion tokenizer Q quantizing continuous motion data into discrete tokens. Special boundary tokens `<MOT>` and `</MOT>` delineate motion sequences within text.
- **Training Curriculum:** The model follows a three-stage training curriculum:
 1. **Motion-text alignment:** Establishes correspondence between motion tokens and the LLM's representational space.
 2. **Vision-text-motion alignment:** Integrates all three modalities (vision, text, motion) into a unified framework.
 3. **Motion instruction tuning:** Enhances responsiveness to diverse user commands and improves controllability.
- **Optimization:** The model is optimized using the standard next-token prediction objective via negative log-likelihood.

#### 3.3. Controllable Motion Generation

The research addresses controllability through comprehensive multi-task pretraining and architectural innovations.

- **Multi-Task Motion Pretraining:** Five fundamental dimensions of controllability are defined and addressed through specialized instructional tasks and systematic data curation:
 - **Random Instruction Control:** Introduced the Instruct-to-Motion (I2M) task with a comprehensive instruction template library to enhance command responsiveness.
 - **Random Pose Initialization Control:** Developed the Motion Prediction and In-between (MPI) task, where the model predicts missing segments from motion sequences extracted from diverse initial poses.
 - **Long-Term Motion Control:** Utilized concatenated long-form motion sequences from HuMo100M with the Instruct-to-LongMotion (I2LM) task to generate extended, temporally coherent motions.
 - **Unseen Motion Control:** Expanded motion data coverage through large-scale web video collection and multi-task training design, introducing the Instruct-to-Unseen (I2U) task for generalization evaluation.
 - **Random Part Control:** Proposed the Instruct-to-PartMotion (I2PM) task, leveraging part-level annotations to generate motion for specific body regions while maintaining overall coherence.

- **Part-aware Residual Quantization (PRQ):**
 - Inspired by residual vector quantization (RQ), PRQ decomposes each motion feature into part-specific features for five anatomical regions: left arm, right arm, left leg, right leg, and torso. Shared joint representations (pelvis, spine) are maintained across parts.
 - These part features are encoded into latent vector sequences, which are then quantized using a shared motion codebook across K+1 quantization layers, iteratively refining residuals.
 - The decoder reconstructs motion by mapping quantized latent sequences back to part-level motion space and aggregating them.
 - **Innovations:** Finer-grained control over five distinct anatomical regions, rich part-level textual supervision, and a shared motion codebook that enhances inter-limb coordination and reduces joint reconstruction errors. The loss function includes motion reconstruction and latent embedding objectives.

#### 3.4. Design Choices for Real-time Generation

Computational efficiency is a core consideration for real-time applications.

- **LLM Backbone Selection:** LLaMA-2-7B was chosen as the backbone after evaluating trade-offs between model capacity and inference latency, demonstrating an optimal balance.
- **Motion Feature (HuMo263):** A novel motion feature, HuMo263, is proposed, based directly on SMPL model parameters. It comprises relative 6D rotations of key joints, root node parameters, redundant joint position information from SMPL forward kinematics, and foot contact states. This avoids the computationally intensive inverse kinematics (IK) calculations required by HM3D263, reducing latency and information loss.
- **Visual Resolution and Temporal Duration:** Being-M0.5 operates at 224x224 resolution and processes up to 64 input frames, balancing visual fidelity and inference speed.
- **Tokenization Architecture and Decoding Strategy:** Four PRQ layers were found to achieve optimal quality-efficiency balance. Crucially, a frame-by-frame decoding strategy is employed, enabling progressive motion output generation and improving system responsiveness, in contrast to conventional layer-by-layer approaches that introduce significant delays.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents extensive experimental validation demonstrating the capabilities of HuMo100M and Being-M0.5.

#### 4.1. Dataset Contributions

HuMo100M is established as the largest multimodal motion dataset, encompassing over 5 million motion sequences and 100 million instructional instances. It features fine-grained, long-form, and part-level annotations, which address critical gaps in existing datasets. Statistical analysis confirms its diverse sequence lengths and instance counts.

#### 4.2. Multi-Task Benchmarking Performance

Being-M0.5 was benchmarked against state-of-the-art methods across nine distinct motion generation tasks, with "Being-M0.5\*" denoting the model trained on the full HuMo100M dataset.

- **Text-to-Motion (T2M):**
 - On HumanML3D, Being-M0.5 using standard VQ shows superior performance (FID 0.141) compared to existing methods like MotionGPT (FID 0.542).
 - Integrating PRQ (Being-M0.5-PRQ4) further improves performance, reducing FID to 0.056 (a 60% improvement) and achieving MMDist of 2.865, outperforming other advanced VQ variants like SCaMo-FSQ (FID 0.101) and MoMask-RQ6 (FID 0.045) despite using a smaller codebook.
 - Consistent improvements were observed on the KIT-ML benchmark.
- **Instruct-to-Motion (I2M):** On the HuMo-I2M testbed, Being-M0.5-PRQ4 achieved an FID of 0.148, significantly outperforming prior LLM-based approaches like MotionGPT (FID 0.314) and T2M-GPT (FID 0.682). This improvement is attributed to enhanced motion instruction tuning and PRQ.
- **Instruct-to-Unseen (I2U):** Performance on the HuMo-Unseen testbed showed a reduction in FID from 65.04 (HumanML3D trained) to 8.65 (HuMo100M trained), emphasizing the importance of large-scale data for generalization to novel motion patterns.
- **Instruct-to-PartMotion (I2PM):** On the HuMo-I2PM benchmark, Being-M0.5 demonstrated significantly lower FID (1.25) compared to previous SoTA (7.82), attributed to its part-aware motion encoding. Quantitative evaluation using a left-right exchange test yielded a 76% success rate, surpassing ParCo (64%) and T2M (46%).
- **Instruct-to-LongMotion (I2LM):** Being-M0.5 achieved an FID of 0.18 on HuMo-I2LM, outperforming previous SoTA (0.21), indicating improved capacity for generating extended, continuous motion sequences.
- **Other Benchmarks:** Consistent superior performance was observed across motion prediction (AMASS, 3DPW, HuMo-MPI), motion in-between (AMASS), action-to-motion (UESTC), and motion-to-text (HumanML3D) tasks.

#### 4.3. Motion Quantization Configuration Analysis

A comparison with previous motion tokenizers demonstrated PRQ's effectiveness:
- PRQ substantially outperformed lookup-free approaches (2D-LFQ, FSQ) while using a significantly smaller codebook (1.5% the size), particularly evident on the large-scale HuMo100M testbed.
- PRQ surpassed RQ-VAE performance even with fewer quantization layers due to its part-level encoding mechanism.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical role of shared joints across body parts; PRQ with shared joints outperformed the variant without (PRQ w/o Shared) in FID (0.007 vs. 0.042 on HumanML3D).
- PRQ’s architectural structure and part labels were found to be complementary, as PRQ without part labels performed worse than the corresponding RQ variant.
- Increasing PRQ layer depth improved reconstruction but degraded generation performance, likely due to increased decoding complexity with the frame-by-frame strategy.

#### 4.4. Time Efficiency

Being-M0.5, when utilizing a 4-bit quantized 7B-parameter LLaMA-2 backbone, achieved real-time inference speeds.
- It sustained a minimum of 20 FPS across all tested GPUs (RTX3090, RTXA6000, A800, RTX4090, A100, H100), reaching a peak of 28.9 FPS on the H100.
- The HuMo263 motion feature, based directly on SMPL parameters, eliminated the computational overhead of inverse kinematics (IK) required by other feature formats, contributing to consistent real-time performance.
- The frame-by-frame decoding strategy of PRQ was essential for system responsiveness, preventing the delays associated with conventional layer-by-layer generation.

#### 4.5. Part-level Controllability

Being-M0.5's part-aware residual quantization, which decomposes the human body into five distinct part-level tokens, enabled precise part-specific motion control. This design facilitated pretraining on fine-grained motion-text pairs and allowed selective decoding of individual body parts. Comparative analysis showed superior performance over existing part-specific text-to-motion methods on HumanML3D, with an improved FID of 0.056 compared to ParCo (0.109) and Fg-T2M++ (0.089).

#### 4.6. Ablation Studies and Further Discussion

- **Visual Modality Benefit:** Vision-text-motion alignment consistently improved performance (FID 5.791 vs. 7.053 without visual alignment on HuMo-T2M), indicating that visual cues provide valuable supervisory signals, especially for noisy web video data.
- **Multi-Task Training Benefit:** Multi-task training substantially enhanced controllability. The full multi-task configuration (T2M+I2M+MPI+I2PM+I2LM) yielded the best FID (5.791 on HuMo-T2M), with each task contributing to specific controllability dimensions.
- **Motion Tokenizer Selection:** The choice of PRQ4 balanced generation performance (R@# and MMDist) with decoding efficiency, indicating a trade-off between reconstruction accuracy (which can benefit from more layers) and LLM-based decoding complexity.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents Being-M0.5 as the first reported real-time, controllable Vision-Language-Motion Model, achieving state-of-the-art performance across a comprehensive set of motion generation benchmarks. Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

1. **Advancement in Controllability:** By systematically addressing five key dimensions of controllability—diverse instructions, arbitrary pose initialization, long-term sequences, unseen scenarios, and fine-grained part-level control—the work advances the capabilities of motion generation beyond previous limitations. This enhances the practical utility of motion synthesis systems.

2. **New Standard for Motion Datasets:** The introduction of HuMo100M, the largest multimodal motion dataset with 5 million sequences and 100 million instructional instances, directly addresses the chronic scarcity of high-quality motion data. Its unique part-level annotations, long-term sequences, and text-aligned visual clips provide a rich resource for training more sophisticated and controllable VLMMs, potentially catalyzing future research in data-driven motion generation.

3. **Real-Time Practicality:** The meticulous design choices for real-time inference, including the optimized LLaMA-2-7B backbone, the HuMo263 motion feature (which bypasses computationally expensive Inverse Kinematics), and the frame-by-frame decoding strategy of PRQ, enable Being-M0.5 to operate at practical speeds (over 20 FPS). This overcomes a significant bottleneck for real-world deployment in applications requiring immediate responsiveness.

4. **Fine-Grained Motion Control:** The novel Part-aware Residual Quantization (PRQ) method is a key innovation, enabling precise and granular control over individual body parts. This capability, supported by specific part-level textual supervision, opens avenues for more nuanced and detailed animation workflows, critical for realistic character control in interactive environments and professional content creation.

5. **Unified Multimodal Understanding:** The integration of vision, language, and motion modalities within a unified framework, particularly by leveraging text-aligned visual clips, enhances the model's understanding of motion from noisy web videos. This multimodal approach broadens the scope of applications, potentially enabling motion estimation directly from video inputs.

6. **Guidance for Future Development:** The comprehensive efficiency analysis and systematic ablation studies provide valuable design insights and computational considerations for the development of future practical motion generators. This includes understanding the trade-offs in LLM backbone selection, motion feature representations, and tokenization architectures.

Overall, Being-M0.5 and HuMo100M represent contributions that could accelerate the adoption of motion generation technologies in industries such as video games, film production, virtual reality, and humanoid robotics, by providing tools that are both powerful in their generative capabilities and practical in their real-time performance and controllability.
