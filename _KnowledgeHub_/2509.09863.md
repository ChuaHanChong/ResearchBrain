# Off Policy Lyapunov Stability in Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the University of Victoria developed a framework for learning neural Lyapunov functions using off-policy data, enhancing sample efficiency and enabling stability guarantees in reinforcement learning. This approach, when integrated with algorithms like Soft Actor-Critic and Proximal Policy Optimization, produced more stable and performant controllers for robotic tasks while reducing Lyapunov condition violations.

## Problem

- Traditional reinforcement learning (RL) methods often lack formal stability guarantees, posing safety challenges for real-world robotic applications.
- Deriving Lyapunov functions for non-linear systems in RL is analytically challenging, and existing neural Lyapunov function learning typically requires on-policy data, limiting sample efficiency.
- Low sample efficiency in on-policy learning presents a practical barrier in robotic applications where data collection is costly, time-consuming, or hazardous.

## Method

- The paper introduces an ==action-dependent neural Lyapunov function== Lη(s, a) that accounts for both state and action, extending stability certification to off-policy data.
- A modified ==Lyapunov risk function== and ==Lie derivative== are defined, explicitly incorporating the current policy's actions in successor states to leverage off-policy transitions for learning.
- The learned off-policy Lyapunov function is integrated into standard RL algorithms (Soft Actor-Critic as **LSAC** and Proximal Policy Optimization as **LPPO**) by augmenting their policy loss or advantage function to penalize instability.

## Results

- LSAC achieved superior sample efficiency on the inverted pendulum task, reaching higher rewards in fewer training steps and stabilizing the pendulum closest to equilibrium with minimal oscillation.
- LSAC demonstrated a significantly lower percentage of Lyapunov decreasing condition violations (**0.84%**) compared to other stable RL methods (e.g., POLYC at **13%**, LAC at **51%**).
- LPPO exhibited faster convergence and more accurate trajectory tracking for the quadrotor task compared to standard PPO and Policy-Conditioned Lyapunov (POLYC) baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Leveraging an action-dependent Lyapunov function allows for the effective use of off-policy data, enabling more data-efficient learning of stability certificates.
> - Modifying the Lyapunov risk to depend on the current policy's actions in successor states is critical for correctly evaluating stability conditions with off-policy data.
> - Integrating a Lyapunov term directly into the RL agent's policy optimization objective (via policy loss or advantage) can steer learning towards stable control policies without reward reshaping.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) has demonstrated considerable success in controlling complex robotic systems, particularly those characterized by non-linear dynamics and high-dimensional state spaces, where classical control methods may encounter difficulties. However, the application of DRL in real-world robotics presents challenges, primarily concerning safety and sample efficiency. The process of collecting trial-and-error samples can be unsafe for physical robots, necessitating mechanisms to ensure system safety and efficient learning from data.

Stability is a fundamental requirement for the safe and reliable operation of controlled systems. Unstable systems exhibit unpredictable behavior and can pose risks. Traditional sample-based Reinforcement Learning (RL) techniques generally lack the capacity to provide formal stability guarantees for the policies they learn. More recent advancements in RL research have aimed to address this limitation by integrating Lyapunov stability concepts into robot learning frameworks. This typically involves the computation or learning of a Lyapunov function for the system's task error in a closed-loop configuration with the RL agent. The existence of such a function is a sufficient condition for stability.

A significant challenge in applying Lyapunov stability analysis to non-linear systems, particularly in the context of RL, is the absence of a systematic analytical method for deriving Lyapunov functions. The RL community has explored several approaches to overcome this:
- In **model-based RL**, stability can be directly guaranteed through Lyapunov functions derived from system models or ensured through safety certificates provided by control barrier functions.
- In **model-free RL**, stability is often approached by incorporating backup safe controllers, or by using candidate Lyapunov functions, frequently the value function, which necessitates reshaping the reward function into a cost. Alternatively, neural Lyapunov functions can be learned directly by minimizing a Lyapunov risk loss function, which penalizes deviations from Lyapunov conditions without requiring reward reshaping or restricting the candidate function.

A key distinction among these methods lies in their data efficiency. While approaches using a candidate Lyapunov function can leverage off-policy data to improve sample efficiency, the direct learning of neural Lyapunov functions has largely been limited to on-policy data. This on-policy constraint can reduce sample efficiency, which is a critical consideration in many real-world applications where data collection is costly or hazardous. This paper aims to bridge this gap by introducing a method for learning neural Lyapunov functions directly using off-policy data, thereby enhancing sample efficiency while preserving the benefits of direct Lyapunov function learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this research stems from the inherent limitations of existing methods for learning neural Lyapunov functions in Reinforcement Learning. Specifically, while neural Lyapunov functions can offer effective stability certification without requiring modifications to the reward function or predefining a candidate function, their learning process has historically been restricted to on-policy data. This on-policy requirement leads to reduced sample efficiency, as it necessitates collecting new data from the current policy, preventing the reuse of data generated by older policies. In robotic applications, where data collection can be expensive, time-consuming, or unsafe, sample inefficiency presents a significant practical barrier.

To address this, the paper sets out the following key objectives:
1. **Develop a framework for off-policy Lyapunov function learning:** The central objective is to propose a novel method that allows a neural Lyapunov function to be learned directly using off-policy data. This extends previous work that was limited to on-policy learning, aiming to improve data utilization.
2. **Integrate off-policy Lyapunov functions into state-of-the-art RL algorithms:** The research intends to demonstrate how these newly developed off-policy Lyapunov functions can be effectively incorporated into established Deep Reinforcement Learning algorithms. Specifically, it focuses on Soft Actor-Critic (SAC), an off-policy algorithm, and Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), an on-policy algorithm, to guide them towards learning stable control policies.
3. **Illustrate enhanced sample efficiency and performance:** Through empirical validation, the paper aims to show that the proposed framework, when integrated with RL algorithms, can improve sample efficiency without compromising control performance, in comparison to existing stable RL methods.

By achieving these objectives, the research seeks to advance the field of safe Reinforcement Learning by making stability guarantees more accessible and data-efficient, thereby facilitating the broader adoption of DRL in safety-critical robotic systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology proposed in this paper extends the concept of learning neural Lyapunov functions to incorporate off-policy data. The approach builds upon existing frameworks for neural Lyapunov functions and integrates them into both off-policy and on-policy RL algorithms.

**Preliminaries:**
The paper begins by reviewing the definitions of Lyapunov stability and the Lyapunov stability criterion for a closed-loop system `˙x(t) = f(x(t), u(x(t)))`. It also introduces the concept of neural Lyapunov functions, where a neural network `Lθ` is trained to minimize a Lyapunov risk `J_L_θ` over an on-policy dataset. This risk function includes terms penalizing negative Lyapunov values, positive Lie derivatives (indicating instability), and non-zero values at equilibrium. The Lie derivative `L_f L_θ(x) = ∇L · f(x(t), u(x(t)))` is approximated using a finite difference derivative `(L(s') - L(s)) / Δt` for discrete-time systems.

**Learning Off-Policy Lyapunov Functions:**
The core contribution is the extension of the Lyapunov risk function to accommodate off-policy data. This is achieved in two main steps:
1. **Action-Dependent Lyapunov Function:** Unlike previous methods where the Lyapunov function `Lθ` depended only on the state `s`, the proposed method learns a neural Lyapunov function `Lη(s, a)` that is dependent on both the state `s` and the action `a`. This design is inspired by approaches where the RL action-value function `Q(s, a)` serves as a Lyapunov candidate.
2. **Modified Lyapunov Risk and Lie Derivative:** The Lyapunov risk `J_L(η)` (Equation 5) is redefined for an off-policy dataset `D`. The finite difference Lie derivative `L_f,Δt L_η` (Equation 6) is also modified to explicitly depend on the action `a` taken in state `s` and the action `π(s')` that the *current policy* `π` would take in the next state `s'`. This explicit dependence on the current policy's action in the successor state is crucial for handling off-policy data, as the transition `(s, a, r, s')` might have been generated by an older policy, but the stability condition needs to be evaluated with respect to the *current* policy's behavior. The equilibrium value `Lη(sG, π(sG))` is also policy-dependent.
3. **Expected Lyapunov Function:** To verify the Lyapunov conditions, the expected value of `Lη(s, a)` over actions under the current policy `π` is considered: `Lη(s) = E_a∼π Lη(s, a)` (Equation 7). The paper argues that if `Lη(s, a) > 0` and `Lη(sG, π(sG)) = 0`, then `Lη(s) > 0` and `Lη(sG) = 0`. Furthermore, `L_f,Δt Lη(s, a)) < 0` is considered sufficient for `Lη(s)` to decrease along system trajectories.

**Practical Changes:**
A hyperparameter `μ ∈ R>0` is introduced into the Lyapunov risk function (Equation 8) to define a minimum desired rate of decrease for the Lyapunov function: `max(0, L_f,Δt L_η + μ)`. This allows for control over the steepness of the Lyapunov function's decay. While this modification makes the loss function non-zero at equilibrium (by design, it cannot decrease further than `μ`), the paper clarifies that `J_L(η)` (Equation 8) is used for training, and `J_L(η)` (Equation 5) (without `μ`) is used to certify stability.

**Learning Stable Policies:**
The learned off-policy Lyapunov function is integrated into two prominent RL algorithms:

1. **Lyapunov Soft Actor-Critic (LSAC):**
 - SAC's policy loss `J_π(ϕ)` (Equation 9) is augmented with a term that penalizes violations of the Lyapunov decreasing condition.
 - The modified policy loss `J_π(ϕ)` (Equation 10) includes `β max(0, L_f,Δt L_η + μ)`, where `β` is a Lyapunov temperature hyperparameter. This term applies a penalty if the Lie derivative of the Lyapunov function, after accounting for the minimum decrease rate `μ`, is positive. If the Lyapunov conditions are met (i.e., the Lie derivative is negative by at least `μ`), no penalty is applied, preventing bias.

2. **Lyapunov Proximal Policy Optimization (LPPO):**
 - For on-policy algorithms like PPO, the Lyapunov function is learned using the off-policy framework (Equation 8), but with on-policy data.
 - The PPO advantage `ˆA_t` is augmented into `ˆA_β` (Equation 12) by adding `β min(0, -(L_f,Δt L_η + μ))`. This modifies the advantage to incorporate the stability requirement; a negative Lie derivative contributes positively to the advantage, encouraging stable actions.
 - The policy loss `J_π(ϕ)` (Equation 13) then uses this augmented advantage `ˆA_β`.

**Stability Certification:**
Stability is certified either by the convergence of the modified Lyapunov loss (Equation 8) to zero (if `μ` is small enough or if `μ` is considered as a target for decay), or by evaluating the Almost Lyapunov Conditions, which allow for a small, bounded number of violations near the equilibrium.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper evaluates the proposed off-policy Lyapunov function learning framework through numerical experiments on two control tasks: an inverted pendulum and a quadrotor.

#### 4.1. Inverted Pendulum Experiment

The inverted pendulum task, utilizing the Pendulum-v1 environment, involved training the proposed Lyapunov Soft Actor-Critic (LSAC) against standard Soft Actor-Critic (SAC), Lyapunov Actor-Critic (LAC), Policy-Conditioned Lyapunov (POLYC), and Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO).

- **Training Rewards (Figure 2a):** LSAC demonstrated superior sample efficiency by achieving the highest reward within the fewest training steps, indicating faster convergence compared to the other algorithms over 10 random seeds.
- **Sample Trajectory (Figure 2c):** After training, LSAC stabilized the pendulum closest to the upright equilibrium position (θ = 0) with minimal oscillation. POLYC also achieved stabilization near equilibrium but exhibited more noise in its trajectory. SAC and LAC stabilized the pendulum with low noise but further away from the desired equilibrium.
- **Lyapunov Violations (Figure 3):** An analysis of the learned Lyapunov functions revealed that LSAC incurred the lowest percentage of violations of the Lyapunov decreasing condition (0.84%) along simulated trajectories. In contrast, POLYC showed 13% violations, and LAC exhibited 51% violations. This suggests that the Lyapunov function learned by LSAC better satisfied the theoretical stability conditions, potentially implying a larger region of attraction.

#### 4.2. Quadrotor Trajectory Tracking Experiment

Given that previous studies indicated difficulty for off-policy methods like SAC and LAC in producing meaningful quadrotor controllers, this experiment focused on comparing the proposed Lyapunov Proximal Policy Optimization (LPPO) with POLYC and PPO for a trajectory tracking task using a Mujoco-simulated quadrotor.

- **Training Rewards (Figure 4):** LPPO and POLYC achieved comparable maximum rewards, which were slightly higher than those attained by PPO. Notably, LPPO converged faster than POLYC, indicating its enhanced sample efficiency in this complex control task.
- **Trajectory Tracking Performance (Figure 5):** LPPO demonstrated the most accurate tracking of the reference trajectory among the compared algorithms. POLYC also tracked the trajectory accurately for the majority of the episode. PPO was capable of tracking the trajectory but with larger errors compared to both LPPO and POLYC.

**Summary of Results:**
The experimental results indicate that the proposed off-policy Lyapunov function learning methods (LSAC and LPPO) can lead to improved sample efficiency and better control performance compared to several existing stable and non-stable RL algorithms. This improvement is observed in both a pendulum swing-up/stabilization task and a quadrotor trajectory tracking task. The reduced number of Lyapunov condition violations by LSAC also suggests that the learned Lyapunov functions provide a more robust certificate of stability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in this paper offers several significant contributions and potential impacts on the field of Reinforcement Learning and robotic control:

**Advancement in Safe Reinforcement Learning:** The primary significance lies in addressing a critical limitation within existing methods for incorporating stability guarantees into deep reinforcement learning. By enabling the direct learning of neural Lyapunov functions using off-policy data, the paper enhances the practicality of stable RL. This allows for more effective utilization of historical data, which is particularly valuable in safety-critical applications where data collection through trial-and-error can be hazardous or expensive.

**Improved Sample Efficiency:** The empirical results demonstrate that the proposed off-policy Lyapunov-based algorithms (LSAC and LPPO) achieve higher sample efficiency compared to their counterparts. This means that stable policies can be learned with less interaction with the environment, which is a key factor in accelerating the development and deployment of RL agents in real-world robotic systems.

**Enhanced Control Performance and Stability:** The experiments on the inverted pendulum and quadrotor tasks show that the proposed methods not only learn stable policies but also achieve superior or comparable control performance. For instance, LSAC stabilized the pendulum closer to equilibrium with less noise, and LPPO demonstrated more accurate quadrotor trajectory tracking. The fewer violations of Lyapunov conditions observed with LSAC further support the robustness of the learned stability certificates.

**Broader Applicability:** The framework's ability to integrate with both off-policy (SAC) and on-policy (PPO) RL algorithms highlights its versatility. This suggests that the benefits of off-policy Lyapunov function learning can be extended across a wider range of RL paradigms, making stability considerations more accessible to different types of agents and tasks.

**Potential for Real-World Deployment:** By providing a more data-efficient and robust method for certifying stability, this work moves closer to enabling the safe deployment of DRL controllers in physical robotic systems. Guaranteed stability is a prerequisite for safety, making this research relevant for applications in autonomous vehicles, industrial robotics, and human-robot interaction.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
The paper acknowledges several limitations that outline important areas for future work:
- **Physical Validation:** The current work is based solely on simulations. Testing the proposed algorithms in physical environments is crucial to validate their robustness and performance in real-world conditions.
- **Hyperparameter Tuning:** The introduction of two new hyperparameters (minimum rate of decrease `μ` and Lyapunov temperature `β`) necessitates further systematic investigation, such as hyperparameter sweeps, to understand their impact and optimize their values.
- **Dependence on Underlying Algorithms:** The success of LSAC and LPPO is contingent on the performance of their foundational algorithms (SAC and PPO).
- **Bias in Off-Policy Data:** While the paper proposes a method to address bias from data collected by previous policies, a detailed analysis of the impact of this bias and exploration of techniques like importance sampling are suggested for further research.
- **Theoretical Guarantees:** The work currently lacks formal theoretical stability guarantees for the proposed algorithms, which would strengthen the claims of certified stability. Developing these guarantees is identified as a significant direction for future research.

Despite these limitations, the work represents a step towards making deep reinforcement learning more reliable and applicable in safety-critical domains, by providing a method for efficient and direct learning of stability certificates.
