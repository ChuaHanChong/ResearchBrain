# SEINE: Short-to-Long Video Diffusion Model for Generative Transition and Prediction

> [!summary] Summary
> The SEINE model introduces a short-to-long video diffusion framework for generating creative transitions between diverse scenes and predicting extended video sequences. It extends a pre-trained text-to-video model, quantitatively surpassing traditional morphing and diffusion-based baselines in human evaluations and objective metrics for transition quality, and maintains visual coherence in long-form video generation.

## Problem

- Existing video generation models produce short, 'shot-level' clips, which are insufficient for creating 'story-level' narratives composed of multiple distinct scenes.
- Traditional video editing transitions lack the flexibility and creativity to generate diverse and imaginative connections between semantically different scenes.
- Generating long videos with temporal coherence and semantic consistency across extended durations remains challenging, with existing models often facing issues like temporal degradation.

## Method

- **SEINE** extends a pre-trained text-to-video ==diffusion== model with a ==random mask module== to enable selective conditioning on visible frames for generating unseen frames.
- The model is trained using a ==U-Net== architecture to predict noise for entire corrupted latent codes, conditioned on both masked (unseen) and unmasked (visible) frames, along with a textual description.
- This random-mask design allows the model to perform generative transitions by providing initial and final scenes as visible frames, and to execute ==auto-regressive video prediction== by using preceding frames as conditions.

## Results

- SEINE quantitatively outperformed traditional and diffusion-based transition methods, achieving higher **CLIPSIM-text (0.2726)**, **CLIPSIM-Scenes (0.7740)**, and **CLIPSIM-frames (0.9675)** scores.
- Human evaluation showed a strong preference for SEINE's generative transitions, with it being favored over Morphing, VQGAN-based, and SD-based methods in **83% to 92%** of cases.
- For long video prediction, SEINE maintained stable and visually coherent generation for **1024-frame** videos, surpassing state-of-the-art TATS qualitatively and achieving lower FVD scores after fine-tuning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A random mask module can effectively enable a diffusion model to control which frames are generated versus conditioned upon, facilitating versatile short-to-long video generation tasks.
> - Training a diffusion model on partial, masked video inputs (visible and unseen frames) allows it to learn robust mechanisms for bridging semantic and temporal gaps between diverse scenes.
> - Extending pre-trained text-to-video diffusion models, rather than training from scratch, provides a strong foundation for advanced generative tasks like story-level video creation and complex transitions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of video generation has experienced notable advancements, particularly with the emergence of diffusion models. These models have demonstrated capability in generating realistic video content. However, existing methods primarily produce "shot-level" videos, which are typically short clips lasting a few seconds and depicting a single scene. These short outputs are often insufficient for applications requiring longer, coherent narratives, such as cinematic or industrial video productions, which demand "story-level" video generation.

Traditional video editing employs techniques like dissolves, fades, and wipes to transition between scenes. These methods rely on predefined algorithms and often lack the flexibility and creativity to generate diverse and imaginative connections between semantically different scenes. While image morphing techniques can create smooth transitions by finding pixel-level similarities, they are limited in generating novel content or semantic changes aligned with a descriptive narrative.

Concurrently, research in long video generation has explored various approaches, including Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), Variational Auto-Encoders (VAEs), transformer-based models for sequential token generation, and hierarchical latent diffusion models. Despite these efforts, generating "story-level" long videos that maintain temporal coherence and semantic consistency across multiple distinct scenes remains a challenge. Existing models often face issues such as temporal degradation or difficulty in managing the conceptual flow across extended durations.

This work, SEINE, positions itself within this landscape by addressing the gap between short, single-scene video generation and the need for coherent, multi-scene "story-level" narratives. It introduces the problem of "generative transition" as a means to seamlessly connect diverse video shots and extends this capability to long video prediction. By focusing on generating intermediate frames that bridge two distinct scenes based on textual descriptions, SEINE aims to provide a generative solution for complex video editing tasks that exceed the capabilities of traditional or current shot-level video generation methods. It builds upon and extends pre-trained text-to-video diffusion models to facilitate the creation of longer, structured video content.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a short-to-long (S2L) video diffusion model, named SEINE, capable of generating high-quality long videos. This encompasses two main functionalities: creating smooth and creative transitions between different scenes, and enabling the prediction of varying lengths of shot-level videos.

The motivation for this work arises from several limitations observed in current video generation technologies:
1. **Limited Scope of Existing Models**: Current AI-generated videos are predominantly "shot-level," meaning they are short clips focusing on a single scene. This contrasts with the requirements of cinematic or industrial video productions, which typically involve "story-level" videos composed of multiple distinct scenes interconnected through transitions.
2. **Inflexibility of Traditional Transitions**: Conventional transition techniques (e.g., dissolves, fades, wipes) are based on predefined algorithms. These methods lack the flexibility to generate creative or contextually rich transitions that dynamically evolve between scenes, as often seen in professional film production. There is a need for a generative approach that can produce imaginative intermediate shots to smoothly connect different visual narratives.
3. **Absence of Generative Transition Task**: The problem of "generative transition," which involves producing diverse and creative video segments to bridge two distinct scenes, has been less explored in the research community. This task requires not only visual fidelity but also semantic relevance to the source and target scenes, temporal coherence within the generated sequence, and alignment with textual descriptions.
4. **Demand for Longer, Coherent Videos**: Creating extended video sequences that maintain visual and semantic consistency over time remains a significant challenge. Enabling reliable auto-regressive video prediction is crucial for synthesizing "story-level" narratives, where individual shots can vary in length and contribute to a larger storyline.

To address these motivations, the specific objectives of SEINE are:
- To design a diffusion model capable of automatically generating creative transitions between two given scene images, guided by textual descriptions.
- To ensure that these generated transition videos maintain visual coherence and quality, exhibiting properties of temporal smoothness, semantic resemblance to the initial and final scenes, and consistency with the provided text.
- To extend the core model's capabilities to related tasks, specifically image-to-video animation (animating a static image) and auto-regressive video prediction (generating subsequent frames to extend a video).
- To propose a set of evaluation criteria—temporal consistency, semantic similarity, and video-text semantic alignment—for comprehensively assessing the quality of generative transitions, thereby establishing a benchmark for this new task.

By achieving these objectives, the research aims to contribute to the creation of more sophisticated AI tools for video content generation, bridging the gap between isolated short clips and integrated, narrative-driven long videos.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, SEINE, is a short-to-long (S2L) video diffusion model designed for generative transition and prediction tasks. It operates by extending a pre-trained text-to-video (T2V) diffusion model with a random mask module.

**Foundation Model**:
SEINE is initialized from LaVie-base, a component of the cascaded LaVie framework. LaVie-base itself is built upon a pre-trained Stable Diffusion model, which performs image diffusion in a compressed latent space of an autoencoder. Stable Diffusion learns to predict noise added to a latent code (`z_t`) conditioned on a textual description (`c`), using a U-Net architecture. LaVie-base extends this by incorporating a temporal attention module and undergoing joint fine-tuning on both image and video datasets, enabling it to generate visually and temporally consistent videos.

**Core Architecture: Short-to-Long Video Diffusion Model**:
The central component of SEINE is its diffusion-based architecture augmented with a random mask module, enabling it to generate unseen frames based on visible conditional images or videos.
1. **Latent Representation**: An N-frame input video, `x_0`, is first encoded into its latent representation, `z_0`, using a pre-trained variational auto-encoder (VAE). `z_0` has dimensions `n × c × h × w`, where `n` is the number of frames, and `c, h, w` are the channel, height, and width of the latent code.
2. **Noise Corruption**: During training, `z_0` is corrupted by Gaussian noise to produce `z_t`, following the standard diffusion process: `z_t = √¯α_t * z_0 + √1 − ¯α_t * ϵ`, where `ϵ` is initial Gaussian noise.
3. **Random Mask Module**: To introduce conditioning flexibility for generating unseen frames, a binary mask `m` is applied.
 - `m` is a vector of `n` elements, with each element being either 0 (masked/unseen) or 1 (visible/conditional).
 - `m` is broadcast to `˜m` to match the dimensions of the latent code (`n × c × h × w`).
 - A masked latent code, `˜z_0 = z_0 ⊙ m`, is created, effectively preserving only the visible frames in their uncorrupted latent form.
 - The model then takes a concatenated conditional input `˜z_t = [z_t; m; ˜z_0]`. This input combines the noisy latent code, the mask itself (to inform the model which frames are masked), and the uncorrupted latent codes of the visible frames.
 - The binary mask `m` is sampled stochastically during training using a Bernoulli distribution `m ~ Bernoulli(p)`. The probability `p` is set to 0.15, aiming for an average of approximately two to three visible frames per sequence, which is suitable for learning transitions.
4. **Training Objective**: The model, represented by a U-Net with parameters `θ`, is trained to predict the noise `ϵ` of the entire corrupted latent code. The loss function is `L = E[||ϵ − ϵ_θ(˜z_t, t, c)||]`, where `c` is the textual description. This objective enables the model to learn the underlying noise distribution for both masked and unmasked frames, conditioned on the visible frames and the text prompt.

**Applications of SEINE**:
The random-mask based design allows SEINE to be applied to various video generation tasks:
- **Generative Transition**: To generate intermediate frames between two scenes, the first and last frames of a desired sequence are provided as input. Their corresponding mask elements are set to 1, while all intermediate frames' mask elements are set to 0. A textual description `c` guides the content and style of the transition.
- **Auto-regressive Video Prediction**: For generating long videos, the model recursively uses the last few frames of a previously generated video segment as conditional inputs. The mask elements corresponding to these input frames are 1, and subsequent frames to be predicted have mask elements of 0. This iterative process allows for the creation of extended video sequences.
- **Image-to-Video Animation**: A single reference image is treated as the first frame. Its mask element is set to 1, and all subsequent mask elements are set to 0. The model then animates this image into a video sequence.

**Implementation Details**:
SEINE is initialized from the pre-trained LaVie-base model, with the random mask layer being randomly initialized. It is primarily trained on the WebVid10M dataset, supplemented by internal watermark-free datasets to mitigate watermark generation. The model is trained on videos of 320x512 resolution with 16 frames but is capable of generating videos of arbitrary aspect ratios during inference.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of SEINE focuses on its performance in generative transition and long video prediction tasks, comparing it against both traditional and contemporary methods.

**Evaluation Criteria for Generative Transition**:
To assess the quality of generative transitions, the authors proposed three criteria:
1. **Temporal Coherence**: Assesses the smoothness and naturalness of visual changes across consecutive frames in the transition.
2. **Semantic Similarity**: Evaluates how well the semantic content of generated intermediate frames relates to the initial and final scene images, and if this similarity changes gradually.
3. **Video-text Alignment**: Measures the semantic correlation between the generated video frames and the provided textual description for the transition.

**Comparison Methods**:
For generative transition, SEINE was compared against:
- **Morphing**: A traditional technique that smoothly transforms one image into another, often by finding pixel-level correspondences.
- **VQGAN-based Transition**: Involves interpolating latent codes from the encoder of a VQGAN, which serves as a latent code layer for diffusion models.
- **SD-based Transition**: Utilizes Stable Diffusion by performing DDIM inversion on the two given images to obtain latent codes, then interpolating these latent codes for denoising to generate intermediate frames.

**Qualitative Comparison (Generative Transition)**:
Visual comparisons indicated that:
- VQGAN-based and Morphing methods often resulted in transparent blending artifacts when connecting disparate scenes. While Morphing could handle object deformations by matching key points, it struggled with scene-level changes (e.g., cityscape to sky) where simple blending might still occur.
- SD-based methods avoided simple blending but demonstrated a lack of sufficient continuity in their latent codes, leading to inconsistencies in the generated intermediate images.
- SEINE produced transitions with smooth object motion and introduced plausible, creative phenomena (e.g., a scene obscured by smoke before revealing the next scene), preserving object deformations while integrating realistic elements.

**Quantitative Comparison (Generative Transition)**:
Experiments used the MSR-VTT dataset, with the first and 64th frames serving as reference scene images. Metrics included:
- **CLIPSIM-text**: CLIP text-image similarity between generated video and text prompt.
- **CLIPSIM-Scenes**: CLIP image similarity between each generated frame and the two given scene images.
- **CLIPSIM-frames**: CLIP score between neighboring generated frames.

| Methods | CLIPSIM-text (↑) | CLIPSIM-Scenes (↑) | CLIPSIM-frames (↑) |
| :---------------------- | :--------------- | :----------------- | :----------------- |
| Morphing | 0.2535 | 0.7707 | 0.9569 |
| VQGAN-based Transition | 0.2528 | 0.7389 | 0.9542 |
| SD-based Transition | 0.2665 | 0.6080 | 0.8809 |
| **Ours** | **0.2726** | **0.7740** | **0.9675** |

SEINE generally outperformed the comparison algorithms across all metrics. Although CLIPSIM-Scenes and CLIPSIM-frames scores were not significantly higher than some baselines, this was attributed to blending methods potentially yielding high coherence scores despite ghosting artifacts.

**Human Evaluation (Generative Transition)**:
A human preference study involving 10 raters comparing 100 randomly sampled video pairs showed SEINE was preferred over:
- Morphing in 83% of cases.
- VQGAN-based method in 85% of cases.
- SD-based method in 92% of cases.

**Diverse and Controllable Transition Generation**:
SEINE demonstrated the ability to generate diverse transitions from identical prompts and scenes by varying random seeds. It also allowed for controllable transitions through textual descriptions, such as camera movements (e.g., zoom-in/zoom-out to create occlusions).

**Comparison for Video Prediction (Long Video Generation)**:
SEINE was compared against TATS, a state-of-the-art method for long video generation, on the UCF-101 dataset for generating 1024-frame videos.
- **Qualitative Results**: TATS showed degradation, grid-like artifacts, and visual collapse over time in generated sequences. SEINE maintained stable and visually coherent video generation throughout the sequence.
- **Quantitative Results (FVD)**: SEINE, in a zero-shot setting, initially exhibited higher FVD scores than TATS due to data distribution differences between its training set (WebVid10M) and UCF-101. However, as video length increased and TATS degraded, SEINE's FVD score became lower. After fine-tuning on UCF-101, SEINE consistently outperformed TATS.

**Additional Applications**:
- **Long Video Generation**: SEINE effectively generated multi-tens-frame videos through auto-regressive prediction, maintaining visual quality and semantic coherence.
- **Image-to-Video Animation**: The model animated static images (from Midjourney and Stable Diffusion XL) into videos. It produced results comparable to the online API Gen-2, despite not being specifically trained for this task, and supported various image aspect ratios.

**Failure Cases**:
- **Watermarks**: Due to the presence of watermarks in the WebVid10M training dataset, generated videos occasionally exhibited watermarks, particularly in auto-regressive prediction where they could persist.
- **Abrupt Transitions**: Transitions between highly dissimilar scenes lacking common elements could appear abrupt or visually inconsistent. The authors suggest a multi-step approach involving image editing for such cases.
- **Text-video Unalignment**: Issues arose when there was a lack of inherent connection between the unmasked input image and the textual caption, potentially leading to discontinuities or difficulties in aligning the generated content with the text prompt.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The SEINE model represents a contribution to the field of generative artificial intelligence for video production, particularly by addressing the challenges of creating coherent, long-form video content.

**Introduction of Generative Transition**: The work formally introduces and provides a solution for the task of "generative transition." This moves beyond predefined algorithmic transitions towards a model that can dynamically and creatively connect disparate scenes based on textual input, which is a closer approximation of artistic video editing processes. This capability has the potential to enhance flexibility and creativity in AI-driven video synthesis.

**Enabling Story-Level Video Generation**: By facilitating seamless transitions between scenes and supporting auto-regressive video prediction, SEINE contributes to bridging the gap between short, isolated video clips and integrated, "story-level" long videos. This is significant for applications requiring narrative continuity and extended visual storytelling.

**Versatile Application Framework**: The random-mask diffusion model proposed in SEINE demonstrates versatility across multiple video generation tasks, including scene transitions, long video prediction, and image-to-video animation. This flexibility suggests a broad applicability in various creative and industrial contexts.

**Novel Evaluation Criteria**: The establishment of specific evaluation criteria for generative transitions—temporal coherence, semantic similarity, and video-text semantic alignment—provides a framework for future research and standardized assessment in this emerging area.

**Potential Impact**:
- **Filmmaking and Content Creation**: SEINE could empower filmmakers, animators, and content creators with tools to rapidly prototype complex scene transitions and extend narrative sequences, potentially reducing post-production time and enabling more experimental visual storytelling.
- **Video Games and Virtual Environments**: The ability to generate dynamic transitions and predict long video sequences could be applied to creating more fluid and immersive experiences in video games, virtual reality, and interactive media.
- **Artistic Expression**: The model offers a new medium for artists to explore dynamic visual transformations and create generative video art that responds to textual prompts.
- **Research Advancement**: This work provides a foundation for future research in conditional video generation, particularly concerning long-term coherence, complex scene understanding, and multimodal control.

**Ethical Considerations**: The authors acknowledge the ethical implications common to text-to-image and text-to-video diffusion models, including the potential for training data biases to be reflected in generated content. Responsible deployment and further research into bias mitigation are implicit considerations.

Future work will focus on enhancing the alignment between textual descriptions and conditional images to improve the overall effectiveness of generation, addressing one of the identified limitations. Continued efforts to mitigate issues such as watermarks from training data would also improve the practical utility of such models.
