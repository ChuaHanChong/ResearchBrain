# Reinforcement Learning for Versatile, Dynamic, and Robust Bipedal Locomotion Control

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at UC Berkeley, Simon Fraser University, and Mila developed a deep reinforcement learning framework for bipedal locomotion, enabling the Cassie robot to perform diverse and dynamic skills like sustained running and various jumps with zero-shot sim-to-real transfer. The approach leverages a novel dual-history policy architecture and a multi-stage training curriculum, resulting in robust performance and adaptability to real-world conditions over extended periods.

## Problem

- Developing robust, agile, and versatile locomotion controllers for high-dimensional, human-sized bipedal robots is challenging due to their inherent instability, complex dynamics, and the difficulty of zero-shot sim-to-real transfer.
- Traditional model-based control methods struggle with the complexity and discontinuities of bipedal contact dynamics and are typically less adaptable to diverse, aperiodic skills or real-world uncertainties without extensive tuning.
- Prior reinforcement learning approaches for bipeds often focused on single skills, required significant hand-engineering, or faced major hurdles in robustly transferring policies from simulation to real hardware without fine-tuning.

## Method

- A unified deep reinforcement learning framework employs a 'dual-history' control policy architecture that feeds both a short (0.1s) and long (2s, via 1D CNN encoder) history of robot input/output to a base MLP network.
- The policy directly outputs desired motor positions for joint-level PD controllers, which was empirically found to be more stable and effective than residual actions for bipedal locomotion.
- A multi-stage training curriculum is utilized, starting with single-task learning, followed by extensive task randomization to promote versatility, and finally dynamics randomization to bridge the sim-to-real gap for robust zero-shot transfer.

## Results

- The proposed framework enabled Cassie to achieve state-of-the-art bipedal locomotion with zero-shot sim-to-real transfer, including sustained 400m running (average **2.15** m/s) and record-breaking 100m sprint (**4.2** m/s peak).
- Cassie performed diverse dynamic jumps (e.g., 1.4m standing long jump, 0.44m high jump, multi-axis jumps), accurately tracking commanded landing positions and demonstrating in-air and post-landing corrective hops.
- The controller exhibited extreme robustness to untrained uneven terrains (stairs, slopes) and various external perturbations, consistently maintaining performance for over 492 days of hardware use without retraining, outperforming a model-based controller in recovery.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A dual-history policy architecture, combining short-term direct I/O history with long-term I/O history processed by a CNN encoder, provides optimal performance for state estimation and real-time control in partially observable bipedal dynamics.
> - Task randomization, training a policy on a diverse range of commanded tasks, acts as an orthogonal and critical source of robustness, enabling the robot to generalize and comply with unforeseen disturbances through learned versatile behaviors.
> - Reinforcement learning can implicitly learn complex contact planning and long-horizon recovery behaviors for bipedal robots, adapting to time-varying events and time-invariant dynamics shifts through its latent representations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of robust and agile locomotion for bipedal robots is a long-standing challenge in robotics, crucial for enabling robots to operate effectively in human-centric environments. This paper positions itself at the forefront of this challenge by proposing a unified deep reinforcement learning (RL) framework.

Historically, bipedal locomotion control has largely been dominated by two main paradigms:
1. **Model-Based Optimal Control (OC):** This approach relies on precise mathematical models of robot dynamics. Methods like Hybrid Zero Dynamics (HZD) and various forms of Model Predictive Control (MPC) (e.g., using simplified models like the Linear Inverted Pendulum (LIP)) have been successful in generating stable and efficient gaits. However, OC often struggles with the inherent complexity, high dimensionality, and highly discontinuous contact dynamics of bipedal robots. It typically requires extensive offline computation, careful tuning, and is less adaptable to unknown or changing dynamics, often needing pre-defined contact sequences and being highly task-specific. Extending OC from periodic (walking) to aperiodic (jumping) or highly dynamic (running) skills, especially in 3D, presents significant challenges.
2. **Model-Free Reinforcement Learning (RL):** Recent advancements in deep RL have revolutionized legged locomotion control, particularly for quadrupedal robots (e.g., Spot, ANYmal). RL offers the advantage of learning directly from interaction with the environment, implicitly handling complex dynamics and contact forces without explicit modeling. However, applying these successes directly to bipedal robots like Cassie is challenging due to their inherent instability and underactuation. Previous RL efforts for bipeds often focused on single skills or required significant hand-engineering (e.g., residual policies, limited history, task-specific policies, or extensive real-world fine-tuning). Sim-to-real transfer remains a major hurdle, often addressed through dynamics randomization or policy distillation, but their efficacy for complex bipedal skills has been debated.

This work critically addresses several limitations of prior research:
- **Generality Across Diverse Skills:** Unlike many previous RL papers that focus on a single locomotion skill (e.g., only walking or only jumping), this paper aims for a *general* control solution that encompasses periodic (walking, running) and aperiodic (jumping, standing) dynamic skills within a single framework. This unified approach reduces the need for developing separate, specialized controllers.
- **Robust Sim-to-Real Transfer:** A core contribution is the demonstration of *zero-shot transfer* from simulation to the real Cassie robot without any real-world tuning. This is achieved through a novel policy architecture and a structured multi-stage training framework, addressing a persistent bottleneck in robotics research.
- **Leveraging Robot History:** The paper delves into the optimal way to integrate robot observation history into RL policies. It challenges conventional approaches (e.g., short history, state-only history, policy distillation) by introducing a "dual-history" architecture, arguing that explicit short-term I/O history complements long-term I/O history for better real-time control and system identification.
- **Sources of Robustness:** Beyond common dynamics randomization, the paper empirically investigates and highlights *task randomization* as an "orthogonal" and critical source of robustness. By training a versatile policy capable of diverse tasks, the robot develops generalization capabilities that enable robust recovery from unexpected disturbances, even if not explicitly trained for those specific perturbations. This is a significant insight, suggesting that diversity in tasks can be as important as diversity in simulated dynamics for real-world robustness.

In essence, this research pushes the boundaries of bipedal locomotion by proposing a highly effective, RL-centric solution that combines architectural innovations, a structured training curriculum, and a deep understanding of adaptability and robustness. It offers a general recipe for achieving agile, robust, and versatile bipedal control that surpasses previous benchmarks for human-sized robots like Cassie.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching goal of this research is to advance the capabilities of bipedal robots to reliably operate in diverse human environments. The paper identifies a significant bottleneck in achieving this objective: the development of control solutions for diverse, agile, and robust legged locomotion skills for high-dimensional, human-sized bipedal robots.

The key objectives and motivations can be broken down as follows:

- **Develop a General Control Framework:** The primary aim is to create a single, unified control framework that can enable a wide range of dynamic bipedal locomotion skills. This includes periodic skills like walking and running, as well as aperiodic skills such as jumping and robust standing. The motivation is to move beyond specialized controllers for individual skills, offering a more scalable and comprehensive solution.
- **Unlock Full Potential of High-Dimensional Bipedal Robots:** Human-sized, torque-controlled bipedal robots possess complex, nonlinear, and underactuated dynamics. While challenging, these characteristics also offer the potential for highly agile and dynamic maneuvers. The objective is to develop a framework that can "unlock" this full potential, enabling complex actions with minimal manual prescription.
- **Achieve Robustness to Uncertainty:** Real-world robot deployment involves inherent uncertainties, including modeling errors, sensor noise, time-varying dynamics (e.g., wear and tear), and unexpected external disturbances. A key objective is to develop controllers that are robust to these factors, enabling direct transfer from simulation to a physical robot without further tuning (zero-shot transfer).
- **Empirical Understanding of RL's Advantages:** The paper is motivated by a desire to provide a detailed empirical analysis of *why* and *how* RL-based controllers achieve adaptivity and robustness in legged locomotion. This involves investigating the benefits of using robot I/O history and identifying different sources of robustness (dynamics randomization vs. task randomization).
- **Demonstrate Novel Bipedal Capabilities:** A practical motivation is to push the state-of-the-art in real-world bipedal robot performance. This includes achieving records for endurance (400-meter dash), speed (100-meter dash), and agility (diverse long jumps, high jumps, complex multi-axis jumps, and robust recovery maneuvers) on a human-sized robot like Cassie.

In summary, the motivation stems from the inherent difficulty of controlling bipedal robots dynamically and robustly in diverse settings. The authors aim to tackle this by thoroughly investigating and leveraging deep reinforcement learning, not just as a black-box solution, but by analyzing its core mechanisms for adaptability and robustness.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core methodology revolves around a novel deep reinforcement learning framework, specifically designed for torque-controlled human-sized bipedal robots like Cassie. The approach is characterized by its innovative control policy architecture and a structured multi-stage training strategy.

#### 3.1. Robot Model and Problem Formulation

- **Cassie Robot:** The experimental platform is Cassie, a human-sized bipedal robot with 20 degrees of freedom (DoFs), 10 of which are actuated (motors), and 2 passive spring-connected joints per leg. Its dynamics are underactuated due to a floating base.
- **Observable States:** The robot operates under partial observability, accessing only reliable sensor data: motor positions and velocities, base orientation from an IMU, and estimated base linear velocity.
- **POMDP Formulation:** Legged locomotion control is formulated as a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP). The policy, $\pi(a_t|o_{t:t-h}, a_{t-1:t-h-1}, c_t)$, maps the robot’s I/O history and a given command ($c_t$) to an action ($a_t$).
- **Action Space:** The policy directly outputs desired motor positions ($\mathbf{q}^d_m \in \mathbb{R}^{10}$) for the robot's actuated joints. These actions are smoothed by a Low Pass Filter (LPF) and then used by joint-level PD controllers operating at a higher frequency (2 kHz, while policy runs at 33 Hz). This choice is shown to be superior to residual actions.
- **Task Parameterization:** Different locomotion tasks are parameterized by a command vector ($c_t$), which varies by skill (e.g., desired linear/angular velocities and height for walking/running, target positions/orientations/elevations for jumping).

#### 3.2. Dual-History Control Policy Architecture

- **Core Innovation:** The most notable architectural innovation is the "dual-history" approach for processing robot input/output (I/O) history. This involves two streams of information:
 - **Short I/O History:** A brief, 4-timestep history ($\approx$ 0.1 seconds) of observable states ($o$) and previous actions ($a$) is directly fed into the base Multilayer Perceptron (MLP) network. This provides immediate, recent feedback crucial for real-time control.
 - **Long I/O History:** A longer, 2-second history (66 pairs of I/O data) is processed by a 1D Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) encoder. The CNN extracts a latent representation from this temporal sequence, which is then fed into the base MLP. This long history is designed to aid in system identification and state estimation, especially for ballistic movements during flight phases.
- **Reference Motion Integration:** A preview of skill-specific reference motion ($\mathbf{q}^r_t$) (e.g., desired motor positions sampled ahead in time) and the current command ($c_t$) are also provided as input to the base MLP.
- **General Structure:** The same policy architecture (MLP base + 1D CNN encoder) is used for all skills (standing, walking, running, jumping), requiring only changes in reference motion and commands.

#### 3.3. Multi-Stage Training Framework

The training process employs a structured curriculum in simulation using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), designed to achieve versatility and robustness, enabling zero-shot sim-to-real transfer.
- **Stage 1: Single-Task Training:** The robot learns a fundamental locomotion skill from scratch, focusing on a fixed command (e.g., walking forward, jumping in place). The primary goal is to master the skill itself and avoid conservative behaviors. Motion tracking reward terms are prioritized.
- **Stage 2: Task Randomization:** Once the skill is acquired, diverse commands are introduced. The robot is trained on a wide range of tasks within that skill (e.g., varied speeds, directions, turning angles for walking/running; varied landing positions/elevations/turns for jumping). This stage develops a "versatile policy" and fosters task generalization. For periodic skills, a sub-stage is added to learn transitions to/from standing. Task completion reward terms are emphasized.
- **Stage 3: Dynamics Randomization:** To bridge the sim-to-real gap, extensive randomization of simulation dynamics parameters is applied. This includes ground friction, joint damping, spring stiffness, link mass/inertia, CoM positions, motor PD gains, sensor noise, and communication delays. Optional external perturbations and randomized terrains can also be introduced for specific skills (though carefully, as they can hinder learning for highly dynamic skills like jumping). This stage enhances policy robustness to real-world uncertainties.
- **Reward Function:** A weighted sum of three main terms:
 - **Motion Tracking:** Encourages following reference motion (motor positions, pelvis/foot height).
 - **Task Completion:** Incentivizes achieving commanded goals (pelvis position/velocity, orientation/angular rate).
 - **Smoothing:** Discourages jerky movements (foot impact, torque, motor velocity, joint acceleration, action change).
 Weights are adjusted across stages and skills to reflect learning priorities (e.g., motion tracking initially, then task completion; higher foot height tracking for skills with flight phases).
- **Episode Design:** Consistent duration (76 seconds), with commands randomized at random intervals in Stage 2. Early termination conditions are used to guide learning, such as exceeding foot height tracking tolerance (especially for flight phases) or task completion tolerance, pushing the robot towards desired behaviors.

#### 3.4. Training Details

- **Simulator:** MuJoCo, based on `cassie-mujoco-sim`.
- **Algorithm:** Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO).
- **Hyperparameters:** Consistent PPO hyperparameters across all skills, with training iterations varying by stage and skill complexity.

This comprehensive methodology, combining architectural novelty with a rigorous training curriculum, allows the system to learn complex, dynamic, and robust bipedal locomotion skills directly from simulation, enabling high-performance deployment on real hardware.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents several key findings across architectural advantages, sources of adaptivity and robustness, and extensive real-world demonstrations.

#### 4.1. Advantages of Policy Architecture (Ablation Studies - Section VII)

Through extensive simulation benchmarks and real-world in-place walking experiments, the study identified optimal design choices for the RL policy:
- **Action Space:** Directly specifying desired motor positions outperforms using a "residual" action added to a reference motion. Residual policies consistently showed worse learning performance and instability in real-world transfer.
- **Observation Input:** Providing a history of both robot *inputs* (actions) and *outputs* (observable states) significantly outperforms using only state-only history. This "I/O history" is crucial for system identification and state estimation in partial observability.
- **History Length & Dual-History:**
 - Using *only* a long I/O history (e.g., 2 seconds) did not clearly outperform policies with *only* a short I/O history (e.g., 4 timesteps).
 - The proposed **dual-history approach** (explicitly providing *both* short I/O history to the base MLP *and* a long I/O history through a CNN encoder) yielded the best learning performance across all skills and minimal sim-to-real degradation. The short history provides critical real-time feedback, complementing the generalized information from the long history.
 - Increasing history length from 1s to 3s generally improved performance, but beyond that, gains saturated or even hindered learning.
- **Training Strategy:** End-to-end training of the base policy and history encoder (as in their proposed method) showed superior performance compared to policy distillation methods like RMA/Teacher-Student. Policy distillation often led to significant performance degradation, especially for dynamic skills like running, and required substantially more training samples even with fine-tuning (A-RMA).
- **Real-world In-place Walking:** Policies trained with the proposed dual-history, end-to-end approach demonstrated minimal drift and better stabilization compared to all baselines on the real Cassie robot, highlighting superior adaptivity to real hardware dynamics.

#### 4.2. Source of Adaptivity (Latent Embedding Analysis - Section VIII)

The study analyzed the latent representations produced by the long I/O history encoder, revealing the mechanism of adaptivity:
- **Time-Varying Events:** The latent embedding successfully captured and adapted to time-varying events like contact phases (e.g., distinct latent signals correlating with left/right foot impact forces in running, or separate take-off/landing signals in jumping) and external perturbations. This implicit contact estimation is a significant emergent capability.
- **Time-Invariant Dynamics Shifts:** The latent embedding showed distinct changes corresponding to specific variations in robot dynamics parameters (e.g., CoM position, link mass, PD gains, friction). This indicates the policy's ability to implicitly estimate and adapt to these time-invariant changes.
- **Noise Filtering:** The encoder effectively filtered out significant levels of simulated measurement noise, showing minimal impact on the latent embedding.
- **Performance Maintenance:** Despite substantial changes in dynamics or external events, the control policy maintained consistent performance (low task completion and motion tracking errors), demonstrating its adaptive nature derived from the I/O history.

#### 4.3. Source of Robustness (Task Randomization - Section IX)

The research identifies task randomization as a crucial, orthogonal source of robustness, distinct from dynamics randomization:
- **Generalization through Versatility:** Policies trained across a diverse range of tasks (task randomization) exhibited significantly higher robustness and compliance to out-of-distribution disturbances compared to single-task policies, even if the latter were trained with simulated perturbations.
- **Behavioral Compliance:** Versatile policies could adapt their gait or employ different learned skills (e.g., lateral walking to counter a side push, backward walking to compensate for a forward CoM shift) to recover from disturbances, rather than strictly adhering to the commanded task. This "compliant" behavior is a hallmark of robust generalization.
- **Real-world Robust Standing:** A compelling real-world case study demonstrated that a versatile walking policy (also trained for standing) could recover from strong pushes by initiating walking steps, intelligently breaking contact and leveraging its learned locomotion repertoire, unlike standing-only policies that simply fell. This extends to transitions from running (side-stepping for recovery) and jumping (corrective hops after unstable landings).

#### 4.4. Extensive Real-World Demonstrations (Section X)

The proposed framework enabled Cassie to perform a wide range of state-of-the-art bipedal locomotion skills with zero-shot sim-to-real transfer:
- **Walking:**
 - Reliable tracking of variable commands (sagittal/lateral velocity, height, turning) with low tracking errors.
 - **Remarkable consistency over time:** The same controller maintained high performance after over 492 days of hardware use without any tuning, demonstrating extreme adaptivity to hardware wear and tear.
 - Fast walking (up to 1.14 m/s forward, -0.5 m/s backward) and seamless transitions to/from standing.
 - Robustness to untrained uneven terrains (stairs, slopes) and various external perturbations (impulse, persistent pushes), often demonstrating intelligent, compliant recoveries. Outperformed a manufacturer-provided model-based controller in perturbation recovery.
- **Running:**
 - **400-meter dash:** Completed in 2 min 34 s, with an average estimated speed of 2.15 m/s (peak 3.54 m/s), consistently turning, demonstrating sustained dynamic running.
 - **100-meter dash:** Fastest time of 27.06 s, reaching estimated peak speeds of 4.2 m/s, showcasing aggressive acceleration from standing and stable high-speed running with noticeable flight phases.
 - Tracking of variable sagittal/lateral velocities and sharp turns (90 degrees in 5 steps, 2 seconds) even without specific training for sharp turns.
 - Running on uneven terrains (up to 10° slopes) while maintaining flight phases, demonstrating adaptation to ground elevation changes.
 - Robust to impulse perturbations during high-speed running.
- **Jumping:**
 - **Flat-ground policy:** Performed diverse jumps to target landing locations, including in-place turns (60°), backward (0.3m), forward (1.4m), lateral (0.3m), and complex multi-axis jumps (forward + lateral + turn).
 - **Discrete-terrain policy:** Successfully jumped onto elevated platforms (up to 0.44m high, 0.88m forward), and performed a record-breaking 1.4-meter standing long jump.
 - Robustness to in-air perturbations: The robot initiated corrective hops mid-air or after landing to regain stability, demonstrating generalization from learned diverse jump tasks.
 - **Emergent Contact Planning:** Across all skills, the robot frequently demonstrated its ability to spontaneously break or make contact (e.g., corrective hops, prolonged double-support in walking) to maintain stability, effectively learning contact sequences online without explicit human definition.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research marks a significant step forward in the field of bipedal robot locomotion control, offering profound implications and potential impact:

1. **Advancing Bipedal Robot Capabilities:** The work pushes the boundaries of agility, versatility, and robustness for human-sized bipedal robots. The demonstrated capabilities – including sustained 400m running, record-breaking long and high jumps, and consistent performance over prolonged periods without retraining – establish new benchmarks for real-world bipedal robot performance. This demonstrates the feasibility of deploying highly dynamic bipedal robots in complex, unstructured environments.

2. **Robust Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer:** Achieving highly dynamic and diverse bipedal skills with zero-shot transfer from simulation is a critical breakthrough. It dramatically reduces the prohibitive costs and risks associated with real-world robot training, making it more practical to develop and deploy advanced robot behaviors. This capability is vital for accelerating research and development in robotics.

3. **Novel Insights into RL for Control:** The paper provides crucial empirical insights into the underlying mechanisms of reinforcement learning for complex robotic control:
 - **Importance of I/O History:** It definitively demonstrates the superior performance of using both robot input and output history, especially the novel "dual-history" architecture, for enhanced adaptivity and state estimation. This offers a new best practice for designing RL observations for dynamic systems.
 - **Task Randomization as a Robustness Source:** By highlighting task randomization as a powerful, distinct source of robustness (orthogonal to dynamics randomization), the work offers a new paradigm for training highly generalized and compliant controllers. This suggests that teaching a robot to perform a wide variety of tasks implicitly equips it with better disturbance rejection capabilities.

4. **Enabling Emergent Behaviors:** The observation that the RL policies learn to autonomously manage contact sequences and execute long-horizon recovery maneuvers (e.g., complex walking steps to recover from a push, corrective hops after unstable jumps) without explicit programming or external planning is highly significant. This suggests that RL can implicitly perform sophisticated motion and contact planning, a long-standing challenge in model-based control.

5. **Foundation for Future Research:** This work provides a solid foundation for several promising future research directions:
 - **Humanoid Robots:** The framework's success with Cassie directly translates to humanoid robots, paving the way for more agile and robust human-like locomotion that can leverage upper-body dynamics.
 - **Perceptive Locomotion:** The proposed architecture can be readily extended to integrate visual sensors (e.g., depth cameras) alongside proprioceptive I/O history, enabling robots to adapt to unknown terrains and navigate dynamic environments.
 - **Loco-Manipulation:** Combining robust bipedal locomotion with bimanual manipulation capabilities would open up new frontiers for robots to perform complex tasks in human environments, such as walking while carrying objects or interacting with objects in dynamic settings.
 - **Unified Policies:** While this work focuses on skill-specific versatile policies, the uniform architecture and training approach lay groundwork for developing truly unified control policies that seamlessly transition between many distinct skills, potentially leveraging techniques like continual RL or large offline datasets.

In essence, this paper doesn't just present impressive results; it offers a deeper understanding of how to effectively apply deep reinforcement learning to solve one of the most challenging problems in robotics. Its findings and demonstrated capabilities will likely influence the design of future bipedal robots and their controllers, pushing closer to the vision of versatile, autonomous robots operating safely and effectively in our world.
