# PRISM-DP: Spatial Pose-based Observations for Diffusion-Policies via Segmentation, Mesh Generation, and Pose Tracking

> [!summary] Summary
> The PRISM-DP framework introduces an automated pipeline that leverages learning-based mesh generation and 6D pose estimation to provide structured observations for diffusion policies, enabling compact robot manipulation policies to consistently outperform larger image-based counterparts in both simulated and real-world tasks.

## Problem

- High-dimensional raw image inputs for robot diffusion policies necessitate very large models, leading to significant computational inefficiencies during training and inference.
- Obtaining accurate and scalable 6D spatial poses of objects in real-world, open-set environments is challenging, often requiring specialized hardware or labor-intensive manual mesh creation.
- Advanced learning-based pose estimation and tracking systems, while robust, still rely on pre-existing, manually reconstructed 3D object meshes, creating a manual bottleneck that limits scalability.

## Method

- **PRISM-DP** reformulates robot observations from high-dimensional raw RGB images to low-dimensional, task-relevant 6D spatial poses of objects, combined with robot proprioceptive states.
- The framework integrates a five-step automated pipeline, starting from RGB-D camera inputs, to generate and track object poses: segmentation (SAM2), learning-based 3D mesh generation (Meshy), initial pose estimation, and continuous pose tracking (FoundationPose).
- Generated meshes are cached and then utilized by a unified pose estimation and tracking model to continuously update object poses across entire demonstrations, eliminating manual mesh creation.

## Results

- In simulation, PRISM-DP policies consistently outperformed image-conditioned policies of equivalent capacity and matched the performance of policies using ground-truth meshes, while also outperforming significantly larger image-based models on complex tasks.
- Real-world experiments showed PRISM-DP policies performing on par with ground-truth mesh-based policies and substantially outperforming even large image-based diffusion policies across block stacking and drawer interaction tasks.
- Analysis of generated meshes demonstrated excellent Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) scores, indicating strong perceptual alignment with ground-truth meshes, and resulting in very low positional errors during pose estimation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Automating the 3D mesh generation process via learning-based models like Meshy effectively removes the manual bottleneck, making pose-based policy learning scalable and practical for diverse environments.
> - Compact diffusion policies conditioned on automatically derived 6D object poses achieve robust performance comparable to or exceeding policies trained with ground-truth poses or meshes, and consistently outperform larger image-based policies.
> - The perceptual quality of learning-generated meshes is sufficient for accurate downstream pose tracking, indicating robustness of pose estimation systems like FoundationPose to minor geometrical discrepancies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic control and manipulation increasingly leverages learning-based policies, with diffusion policies emerging as a prominent approach for generating robot motions. These policies typically learn to denoise action-space trajectories by conditioning on observations from the environment. A common practice involves using raw RGB image streams as observations, primarily due to their accessibility and generality. However, these high-dimensional image inputs often contain substantial amounts of task-irrelevant information. This characteristic necessitates the use of large models with hundreds of millions of parameters to effectively extract meaningful patterns from demonstration data, leading to significant computational demands during both training and inference.

In contrast to high-dimensional visual inputs, structured observations, such such as the 6D spatial poses (position and orientation) of key objects, offer a more compact and lower-dimensional representation of the environment. Policies conditioned on these structured observations can be trained with fewer parameters, leading to more compact and computationally efficient models. Despite this advantage, a significant challenge in deploying pose-based policies in real-world, open-set environments is the accurate and scalable acquisition of object poses. Traditional methods often rely on specialized hardware (e.g., motion capture systems, markers like AprilTags) or labor-intensive manual processes, such as pre-scanning objects to create 3D meshes for model-based pose estimation.

Recent advancements in learning-based 6D pose estimation and tracking, exemplified by frameworks like FoundationPose, have addressed some of these limitations by offering unified pipelines for pose estimation and continuous tracking from RGB-D video streams. While these methods reduce the need for external tracking setups, they typically depend on manually reconstructed or hand-crafted object meshes as input. This dependency creates a bottleneck, as manual mesh creation limits scalability, particularly in environments where novel objects frequently appear or object composition changes across tasks. Prior attempts to integrate such pose estimation techniques into diffusion policy frameworks have been constrained by this manual preprocessing requirement.

PRISM-DP positions itself within this landscape by addressing the critical gap of automating the mesh generation process. By integrating state-of-the-art segmentation, mesh generation, and unified pose estimation/tracking models, PRISM-DP aims to enable the benefits of compact pose-based diffusion policies without the manual burden of mesh creation, thus improving scalability and practical applicability in diverse real-world scenarios. It seeks to bridge the gap between high-performance pose tracking technologies and the demands of scalable, automated robot policy learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable the learning of compact and efficient diffusion policies for robot manipulation by directly utilizing the spatial poses of task-relevant objects as observations, rather than relying on high-dimensional raw RGB images.

This objective is motivated by several key challenges in current robot learning paradigms:

1. **Computational Inefficiency of Image-Based Policies:** Diffusion policies, when conditioned on raw RGB images, face the inherent challenge of high-dimensional input data. These images contain vast amounts of information, much of which is irrelevant to the specific task at hand. Extracting meaningful patterns from this noisy, high-dimensional data typically requires very large neural networks with numerous parameters (often hundreds of millions). This leads to substantial computational costs for training and inference, making these policies resource-intensive and potentially slower to deploy.

2. **Difficulty in Acquiring Accurate Structured Observations:** While compact diffusion policies can be trained using low-dimensional, structured observations such as 6D object poses, obtaining these poses accurately and reliably in real-world, open-set environments remains a significant obstacle. Existing solutions often demand specialized hardware (e.g., motion capture systems, fiducial markers), extensive manual annotation, or cumbersome offline preprocessing steps. These requirements limit the practicality, generality, and scalability of pose-based approaches.

3. **Dependency on Manual Mesh Creation in Advanced Pose Tracking Systems:** Contemporary learning-based 6D pose estimation and tracking frameworks, such as FoundationPose, have demonstrated robust performance in estimating and tracking object poses from RGB-D data. However, a critical limitation is their reliance on pre-existing, manually reconstructed, or hand-crafted 3D object meshes as input. This manual mesh creation process is time-consuming, requires specialized expertise, and is difficult to scale for dynamic or open-set environments where objects may be novel or frequently change. This dependency effectively reintroduces a manual bottleneck, hindering the widespread adoption of these advanced pose tracking methods for automated robot policy learning.

PRISM-DP directly addresses these motivations by proposing a comprehensive framework that automates the entire pipeline from raw camera inputs to structured pose observations. Crucially, by integrating a mesh generation model, the framework eliminates the need for manual mesh creation, thereby overcoming the scalability issues associated with previous pose-based policy learning approaches. This automation is intended to make compact, pose-based diffusion policies more practical and scalable for real-world robotic applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PRISM-DP aims to learn a compact and efficient receding horizon policy, $\pi_\theta$, that predicts a future action sequence $a_{t:t+H_p}$ conditioned on a history of past observations $o_{t-H_o:t}$. In contrast to common diffusion policies that use raw RGB images ($I_t \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times 3}$) as observations, PRISM-DP reformulates the observation $o_t$ to include low-dimensional, task-relevant information: $o_t = [s_t, \{\hat{T}^i_t\}_{i=1}^J]$, where $s_t \in \mathbb{R}^{d_s}$ is the robot's proprioceptive state, and $\{\hat{T}^i_t\}_{i=1}^J$ is a set of $J$ estimated spatial poses for the task-relevant objects at time $t$.

The core challenge addressed by PRISM-DP is inferring these object poses for all objects and time steps without specialized setups or extensive manual effort. The approach, illustrated in Fig. 2, takes four primary inputs:
1. A sequence of RGB images $I_t \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times 3}$.
2. A sequence of depth images $D_t \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W}$.
3. A sequence of robot states $s_t$.
4. The camera intrinsics $K \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 3}$.

The methodology involves a five-step process to convert these inputs into a sequence of object pose estimates:

1. **Initial Object Visibility and Selection:** For each target object $i \in \{1, \ldots, J\}$, a user manually identifies the frame index $F_i$ where the object first appears clearly in the RGB image $I_{F_i}$. The system assumes that once visible, the object remains in view for the remainder of the demonstration. If an object is not visible at the start, its poses are initially set to null.

2. **Segmentation:** A segmentation model is applied to $I_{F_i}$ to produce a binary mask $M_i \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W}$ for each target object at its frame of first visibility. This mask delineates the pixels belonging to the object. For the system implementation, Segment Anything 2 (SAM2) is used, with the user providing "point prompts" by clicking on the target objects.

3. **Mesh Generation:** Each segmented image ($I_{F_i} \ast M_i$, where $\ast$ denotes element-wise multiplication to black out non-object pixels) is passed to a mesh generation model to create a 3D triangular mesh $\mathcal{M}_i$. This step is crucial for eliminating manual mesh creation. The Meshy model is employed for its ability to produce high-fidelity 3D outputs from masked images. These generated meshes are cached for subsequent use.

4. **Initial Pose Estimation:** An approximate 6D pose $\hat{T}^i_{F_i}$ is calculated for each target object at its frame of first visibility. This is achieved using a pose estimator that takes the RGB image $I_{F_i}$, depth image $D_{F_i}$, the generated mesh $\mathcal{M}_i$, and camera intrinsics $K$ as input. FoundationPose is used for this purpose.

5. **Pose Tracking:** The initial pose estimates $\hat{T}^i_{F_i}$ are then used to bootstrap a pose tracking model for each object. This model continuously updates the object's pose $\hat{T}^i_t$ across subsequent frames ($t \in \{F_i, \ldots, T\}$), using the previous pose estimate $\hat{T}^i_{t-1}$, current RGB and depth images ($I_t, D_t$), the generated mesh $\mathcal{M}_i$, and camera intrinsics $K$. FoundationPose provides this unified pose estimation and tracking functionality.

The output of this five-step process is a sequence of estimated 6D poses for all task-relevant objects over the entire demonstration. These pose estimates at each time step $t$ are concatenated with the corresponding robot state $s_t$ to form the low-dimensional observation $o_t$. This sequence of observations is then used to train a diffusion policy.

**Diffusion Policy Training:** The policy $\pi_\theta$ is instantiated as a diffusion model that learns to denoise action sequences. A forward noising process gradually corrupts clean action data $a_{t:t+H_p}$ with Gaussian noise. The denoising network $\epsilon_\theta(x_k, k, y)$ is trained to predict the noise component $\epsilon$ in a noisy sample $x_k$ (representing a noisy action sequence), conditioned on the timestep $k$ and the observation history $y$ (which is $o_{t-H_o:t}$ in PRISM-DP). The training objective is to minimize the mean squared error loss between the predicted noise and the ground-truth noise.

**System Implementation Details:**
- **Segmentation:** Segment Anything 2 (SAM2) is used, with user-provided point prompts for object selection.
- **Mesh Generation:** The Meshy model is employed to generate 3D meshes from masked RGB images.
- **Pose Estimation & Tracking:** FoundationPose serves as the unified framework for both initial pose estimation and continuous pose tracking.
- **Operational Speed:** The system operates at a 10 Hz control frequency, ensuring real-time performance without significant latency.
- **Initialization:** Object poses are null until first visibility, then initialized via SAM2 prompts and FoundationPose, followed by tracking. Experiments are initialized from random states to demonstrate generalizability.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of PRISM-DP was evaluated through extensive experiments in both simulated and real-world environments across various robotic manipulation tasks.

**A. Simulation Experiments:**
Experiments were conducted on five Robosuite tasks (Lift, Can, Square, Stack, Mug) using a simulated Franka Panda arm. Various baselines were compared, including Transformer-based (DP-T) and UNet-based (DP-U) diffusion policies trained with:
- Ground-truth object poses (GTP).
- Raw RGB images (Img) – standard capacity and larger variants (L Img).
- Estimated 6D poses using ground-truth meshes (GTM).
The proposed method, PRISM-DP, was evaluated with both Transformer (PRISM-DP-T) and UNet (PRISM-DP-U) architectures.

**Key results (Table I):**
- **Performance vs. Image-based Policies:** PRISM-DP consistently outperformed image-conditioned diffusion policies with equivalent network capacity (DP-T Img, DP-U Img) in terms of success rate across all tasks, while also demonstrating better training efficiency (lower training time per epoch).
- **Performance vs. Ground-Truth Mesh Policies:** PRISM-DP achieved performance comparable to diffusion policies trained with poses obtained from ground-truth meshes (DP-T GTM, DP-U GTM). This finding indicates that the learning-based mesh generation component of PRISM-DP is sufficiently accurate for downstream pose tracking and policy learning.
- **Performance vs. Large Image-based Policies:** Even against significantly larger image-based policies (DP-T-L Img, DP-U-L Img, 8 to 11 times larger parameter counts), PRISM-DP maintained superior performance on more complex tasks (Stack, Square, Mug). While the larger image models closed the performance gap on simpler tasks (Lift, Can), this came at a substantial computational cost.
- **Performance vs. Ground-Truth Pose Policies:** PRISM-DP matched the performance of policies trained with perfectly accurate ground-truth poses (DP-T GTP, DP-U GTP) on simpler single-object tasks (Lift, Can). A slight performance drop was observed on tasks with multiple dynamic objects (Stack, Square, Mug), attributed to PRISM-DP's reliance on learned perception modules (segmentation, mesh generation, pose estimation) compared to the idealized ground-truth input.

**Mesh and Pose Quality Analysis (Fig. 4):**
- **Mesh Quality:** Generated meshes exhibited excellent Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) scores, indicating strong perceptual alignment with ground-truth meshes. Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR) scores were in the upper-mid range, suggesting minor pixel-level discrepancies, but the low FID implied these differences were not semantically significant for the learning-based FoundationPose.
- **Pose Estimation Accuracy:** The high perceptual quality of the generated meshes resulted in extremely low positional errors during pose estimation. Although orientation errors appeared comparatively higher, these were primarily due to consistent differences in the canonical orientation between generated meshes and ground-truth CAD models. FoundationPose tracks pose updates relative to the given mesh geometry, so these stable offsets did not degrade downstream policy performance.

**B. Real-World Experiments:**
Real-world experiments were conducted on a dual-arm robotic system with two tasks: block stacking (Block Stack.) and drawer interaction (Drawer Inter.). Baselines included image-based policies (standard and large variants) and policies using ground-truth meshes for pose estimation. Ground-truth pose baselines were excluded as they are not available in real-world settings.

**Key results (Fig. 5):**
- **Consistency with Simulation:** Real-world results corroborated simulation findings. PRISM-DP (T and U variants) performed on par with policies using poses derived from ground-truth meshes (DP-T GTM, DP-U GTM).
- **Outperformance of Image-based Policies:** Small-capacity image-based diffusion policies (DP-T Img, DP-U Img) completely failed in both real-world tasks, likely due to increased noise and complexity in real-world RGB observations overwhelming their limited capacity.
- **Superiority over Large Image-based Policies:** PRISM-DP significantly outperformed even the larger image-based diffusion policies (DP-T-L Img, DP-U-L Img) in both tasks, with a more pronounced performance gap observed in the more complex drawer interaction task. This reinforces the conclusion that scaling image-based policies for complex real-world tasks remains challenging and less efficient than using structured pose observations.

Overall, the main findings demonstrate that PRISM-DP enables the learning of compact diffusion policies that achieve robust performance, comparable to or exceeding policies trained with ground-truth information (meshes), and consistently outperforming image-based policies—even larger ones—in both simulated and real-world environments. This underscores the value of automated, structured pose observations for efficient and effective robot policy learning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The PRISM-DP framework represents a notable contribution to the field of robot learning and manipulation, offering several significant advancements and potential impacts:

**Significance:**
1. **Enabling Compact and Efficient Diffusion Policies:** PRISM-DP addresses the fundamental challenge of high-dimensional observation spaces in diffusion policies. By successfully demonstrating that compact policies can be effectively learned using low-dimensional, structured object poses, the work reduces the computational burden associated with large, image-conditioned models. This is significant for developing more resource-efficient and faster-to-deploy robotic systems.
2. **Automation of Mesh Generation:** A crucial bottleneck in utilizing model-based 6D pose estimation and tracking (e.g., FoundationPose) has been the manual creation or acquisition of 3D object meshes. PRISM-DP's integration of learning-based mesh generation (via Meshy) eliminates this manual preprocessing step. This automation vastly improves the scalability and practicality of deploying pose-based manipulation systems in open-set, real-world environments where novel objects are common or object compositions frequently change.
3. **Demonstrated Real-World Outperformance of Pose-based Policies:** To the authors' knowledge, this work is the first to conclusively show that diffusion policies conditioned on pose-based observations can consistently outperform policies conditioned on raw image observations in real-world settings, even when compared against significantly larger image-based models. This finding challenges the prevailing paradigm of exclusively relying on raw vision for robot learning and highlights the strong value proposition of structured, semantically rich observations.
4. **Robustness to Perceptual Noise:** The analysis of mesh and pose quality demonstrates that even with minor perceptual discrepancies in generated meshes, the overall system maintains high accuracy in pose estimation, particularly in position. This indicates a degree of robustness in FoundationPose to the output of generative mesh models, allowing for effective downstream policy learning.

**Potential Impact:**
1. **Enhanced Scalability and Deployability:** By automating the perception pipeline from raw sensor data to task-relevant object poses, PRISM-DP makes advanced robotic manipulation more accessible and scalable. This could lead to wider deployment of robots in dynamic environments such as warehouses, factories, and service robotics, where objects vary.
2. **Reduced Computational Costs:** The ability to train high-performing policies with significantly fewer parameters than large image-based models implies lower hardware requirements, faster training times, and reduced energy consumption. This could democratize access to advanced robot learning capabilities and foster sustainable AI development in robotics.
3. **New Directions for Research:** The consistent outperformance of pose-based policies over raw image-based policies, especially in real-world scenarios, suggests that future research might focus more heavily on robust and generalizable perception modules that provide structured representations. This could encourage innovation in integrated perception-action systems and representations for robot learning.
4. **More Robust and Generalizable Robotic Systems:** By abstracting away irrelevant visual information and focusing on key object poses, PRISM-DP could contribute to the development of robot policies that are more robust to environmental variations (e.g., lighting changes, background clutter) and more easily generalizable to new tasks involving familiar objects.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
Despite these advancements, the paper acknowledges limitations that present avenues for future research. The generated meshes, while sufficient for pose tracking, may have suboptimal topology (e.g., excessive triangles), potentially increasing memory usage and latency in certain applications. Furthermore, the current method is restricted to rigid-body motion, meaning it cannot model or interact with non-rigid deformations such as folding cloth or manipulating deformable objects. Addressing these challenges will be critical for extending PRISM-DP's applicability to even more complex and dynamic manipulation scenarios.
