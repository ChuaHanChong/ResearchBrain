# SPACE: Swarm Pheromone Fields for Adaptive Collision-Aware Exploration

> [!summary] Summary
> The SPACE framework introduces a decentralized, bio-inspired stigmergic coordination system for massive robot swarms exploring unknown indoor environments, achieving an 8- to 17-fold reduction in inter-robot collision rates on congested real-world floorplans while maintaining near time-optimal coverage efficiency for teams of up to 256 ground robots.

## Problem

- Large multi-robot swarms exploring unknown indoor environments suffer from significant congestion, leading to increased inter-robot interference and collisions, which degrades overall performance.
- Existing multi-robot exploration benchmarks primarily focus on efficiency metrics (e.g., coverage time), largely overlooking or not explicitly measuring the inter-robot collision rate for massive swarms.
- Centralized coordination methods become impractical for massive swarms due to communication and computation bottlenecks, necessitating scalable decentralized alternatives.

## Method

- The **SPACE** system employs a ==two-layer control architecture==: a shared stigmergic field for global coordination and a local reactive controller for immediate close-range safety.
- It leverages a shared occupancy grid to maintain three scalar fields: a ==frontier-distance field== for exploration, an ==explore pheromone== to prevent revisiting, and a ==density field== to disperse robots from crowded areas.
- Each robot combines local gradients from these fields into a ==steering vector==, which is then augmented by short-range ==reactive obstacle and neighbor avoidance== behaviors for final actuation.

## Results

- The SPACE system reduced inter-robot contact rates by **8- to 17-fold** on HouseExpo homes and **4- to 7-fold** on KTH building floors compared to a greedy nearest-frontier planner, achieving statistically significant safety improvements (**p < 0.001**).
- SPACE maintained coverage times within approximately **2%** of the fastest uncoordinated baseline at congested swarm sizes (**N=64 to 256**), indicating substantial safety gains with minimal impact on exploration efficiency.
- The explore pheromone component in SPACE was specifically shown to reduce contact rates by an additional factor of **1.5 to 5.0** by minimizing redundant exploration and distributing the swarm.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating inter-robot contact rate as a primary metric provides a more comprehensive evaluation of massive swarm exploration, revealing a critical safety-efficiency tradeoff often overlooked.
> - Decentralized, bio-inspired stigmergic coordination, mediated by environment-based scalar fields, offers a scalable solution to manage congestion and safety in large robot teams without centralized control.
> - For massive indoor robot swarms, the primary practical benefit of coordination strategies is a substantial reduction in inter-robot contact rates (improved safety), rather than a significant increase in exploration speed.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research contributes to the field of multi-robot systems, specifically focusing on large-scale multi-robot exploration in unknown environments. The broader landscape of multi-robot exploration emphasizes efficiency, often measured by coverage time, in applications such as search and rescue, inspection, and mapping. Common approaches include frontier-based exploration, which directs robots to the boundary between known and unknown space, and next-best-view planners that select informative viewpoints. Recent decentralized systems like RACER and TARE have shown high efficiency, though typically validated with smaller teams or single robots. Learning-based explorers also contribute by training policies for goal selection.

A recurring challenge in multi-robot exploration, particularly at scale, is congestion. As the number of robots increases, they tend to crowd passages and frontiers, leading to increased interference and collisions. Prior studies have described this phenomenon as a concave performance curve, where adding more robots beyond an optimal swarm size can lead to diminishing returns or even performance degradation due to congestion. A significant gap in existing research and exploration benchmarks is the explicit measurement and reporting of inter-robot collision rates, especially when evaluating massive robot swarms. While reactive collision avoidance techniques are well-developed for multi-robot navigation in known environments, their integration into large-scale, unknown environment exploration, with an explicit focus on scalability and safety metrics, remains an area requiring further investigation.

This paper addresses this gap by introducing a system designed to manage the safety-efficiency tradeoff inherent in massive robot swarms. It draws inspiration from biomimetic principles, specifically stigmergy, a coordination mechanism observed in social insects like ants. Ants utilize pheromone trails for decentralized coordination, which allows large colonies to perform complex tasks without central control or direct communication. By adopting this environment-mediated coordination, the work seeks to develop a scalable solution that circumvents the per-robot communication and computation bottlenecks typically associated with centralized coordination in very large robot teams. This positions the work as an advancement in decentralized, bio-inspired swarm robotics for robust exploration in congested conditions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to investigate and improve the safety-efficiency tradeoff for massive ground robot swarms engaged in exploring unknown indoor environments. This involves developing a coordination strategy that can manage teams ranging from tens to hundreds of robots effectively, ensuring both rapid coverage of the environment and a low rate of inter-robot contacts.

The motivation for this work stems from several observations regarding current multi-robot exploration practices:
1. **Scaling Challenges:** While an intuitive expectation is that larger robot teams explore faster, practical applications reveal that increasing the number of robots (N) beyond a certain point introduces significant congestion. Robots may interfere with each other, crowd bottlenecks like doorways, and spend an increasing proportion of their time on collision avoidance rather than new exploration. This leads to a performance curve where efficiency gains diminish or reverse at high robot densities.
2. **Overlooked Safety Metrics:** The majority of existing multi-robot exploration research and standardized benchmarks primarily focus on efficiency metrics, such as coverage time. The inter-robot collision rate, which is a direct measure of safety and a critical factor for the robustness and longevity of a swarm, is largely unmeasured, especially as swarm size increases. The authors argue that this contact rate grows quickly in dense swarms and therefore requires direct measurement and optimization.
3. **Need for Scalable Coordination:** Centralized coordination methods, which rely on extensive per-robot communication and computation, become impractical and create bottlenecks for massive swarms. Decentralized, environment-mediated coordination, such as stigmergy, offers a scalable alternative inspired by natural systems. The motivation is to leverage this bio-inspired approach to maintain coordination without incurring high communication or computational overheads.

Based on these motivations, the specific objectives articulated by the authors are:
- To establish a comprehensive evaluation framework that explicitly incorporates both safety (inter-robot collision rate) and efficiency (coverage time) metrics for massive indoor swarm exploration on real building floorplans (up to 256 ground robots). This aims to characterize the empirical Pareto frontier of this tradeoff, which has been omitted in prior benchmarks.
- To design and implement SPACE, a bio-inspired stigmergic coordination system. This system utilizes a decentralized dual-pheromone field to achieve low collision rates as the swarm scales, while incurring minimal costs in exploration efficiency.
- To analyze the fundamental impact of coordination strategies at scale. By comparing SPACE with various baselines, the research aims to determine whether the practical gain from coordination in massive swarms primarily manifests as a reduction in contact rates (improved safety) or an increase in exploration throughput (higher speed). The authors hypothesize that the main benefit will be improved safety.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The SPACE system is structured as a two-layer control system operating in a decentralized, environment-mediated manner, drawing inspiration from stigmergic coordination in social insects.

**A. System Architecture: Two Layers**
1. **Layer 1: Shared Stigmergic Field:** This layer provides global coordination cues, guiding robots towards unexplored areas and dispersing them from already visited or crowded regions.
2. **Layer 2: Local Reactive Controller:** This layer ensures immediate close-range safety by handling obstacle and neighbor avoidance, acting as a reactive safeguard against collisions. This separation ensures that the system does not rely solely on the field for close-range safety in dense environments.

**B. Shared Map and Three Scalar Fields**
The swarm maintains a shared occupancy grid `M` of the environment, where each cell is marked as UNKNOWN, FREE, or OBSTACLE. Robots update `M` by revealing cells within their sensing radius. Crucially, `M` also serves as the medium for three scalar fields that mediate coordination:

1. **Frontier-distance field (D_f):**
 - **Purpose:** Guides robots towards unexplored areas, acting as the primary efficiency driver.
 - **Mechanism:** `D_f` represents the breadth-first distance from the nearest frontier cell (a free cell adjacent to an unknown cell) over traversable paths.
 - **Update:** Recomputed periodically (every `T_f` ticks) using a multi-source Breadth-First Search (BFS) starting from all frontier cells.
 - **Guidance:** Robots are attracted to the negative gradient (`-∇D_f`), effectively pulling them towards the closest reachable frontier.

2. **Explore pheromone (ϕ_e):**
 - **Purpose:** Prevents redundant revisiting of already explored areas, analogous to ant "no-entry" pheromones.
 - **Mechanism:** Each time a robot senses a free cell, a unit deposit is added to `ϕ_e` at that cell. The field then decays exponentially over time by a factor `γ_e` per tick.
 - **Guidance:** Robots are repelled by the negative gradient (`-∇ϕ_e`), encouraging them to move away from recently explored and heavily pheromone-marked regions.

3. **Density field (ρ):**
 - **Purpose:** Disperses robots from crowded regions to pre-empt collisions.
 - **Mechanism:** Each robot deposits a unit value at its current cell. This field decays rapidly over time by a factor `γ_ρ` per tick, where `γ_ρ` is significantly smaller than `γ_e`. This rapid decay ensures `ρ` reflects momentary crowding.
 - **Guidance:** Robots are repelled by the negative gradient (`-∇ρ`), pushing them away from areas with high robot concentrations.

**C. Decentralized Field Guidance (Layer 1 Output)**
Each robot `i` at its current cell `c_i` reads only the local gradients of these three fields. These gradients are combined into a world-frame steering vector `g_i`:
`g_i = w_f * unit_vector(-∇D_f) + w_e * unit_vector(-∇ϕ_e) + w_ρ * unit_vector(-∇ρ)`
Here, `w_f`, `w_e`, and `w_ρ` are fixed weights. The repellent weights (`w_e`, `w_ρ`) are deliberately bounded to ensure the frontier-seeking drive is never completely overridden, mitigating failure modes observed in purely repellent stigmergy. Gradients are computed using a four-neighbor finite difference, treating obstacle or unreachable cells as flat surfaces.

**D. Reactive Safety and Actuation (Layer 2)**
Building upon the field guidance `g_i`, each robot incorporates short-range reactive behaviors using its range-and-bearing (RAB) sensor and proximity sensors. The final actuation command `u_i` is calculated as:
`u_i = R(θ_i)g_i + w_n * Σ_j∈N_i a(d_ij) * unit_vector(-b̂_ij) + w_o * r_i`
Where `R(θ_i)` rotates `g_i` into the robot's local frame, `N_i` refers to detected neighbors, `a(d_ij)` is a range-decreasing weight for neighbor repulsion, `b̂_ij` is the bearing to neighbor `j`, `w_n` is the neighbor avoidance strength, `w_o` is the obstacle avoidance strength, and `r_i` is a vector from proximity sensors. The resulting `u_i` is then translated into differential wheel speeds, clamped to a maximum velocity. This reactive layer, including parameters like `w_n` and `d_n`, is held identical across all methods tested, isolating the impact of the coordination strategy.

**E. Experimental Setup**
- **Simulator:** ARGoS was used with a 2D dynamics engine and a foot-bot model, featuring differential steering, RAB sensors, proximity sensors, and positioning.
- **Environments:** Evaluations were performed on 16 real home layouts from the HouseExpo dataset and 8 campus building floors from the KTH dataset. These floorplans were rasterized into occupancy grids, with doorways configured to connect rooms. Swarms began clustered in an open area and explored outwards.
- **Swarm Sizes:** Robot populations ranged from 16 to 256.
- **Replication:** 20 random seeds were used for HouseExpo maps, and 8 for KTH maps, to ensure statistical robustness.
- **Metrics:**
 - **Coverage Time (T_cov):** Ticks required to reach 95% coverage of the reachable free space.
 - **Contact Rate:** Number of robot pairs within a specified contact clearance, normalized per robot per 100 ticks (excluding an initial startup phase).
 - **Path Length per Covered Area:** Measures movement efficiency.
- **Baselines:** SPACE was compared against:
 - **Frontier:** An uncoordinated nearest-frontier planner (sets `w_e=w_ρ=0`).
 - **MMPF:** A potential-field dispersion planner (sets `w_e=0`, uses density field only).
 - **Alloc:** A MinPos-style frontier allocation method.
 - **Flow:** A handed lane-formation rule.
 - **Random:** A correlated random walk.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of SPACE on real building floorplans revealed consistent performance advantages regarding safety, while maintaining high efficiency in exploration.

**A. Safety and Efficiency Across Swarm Size**
- **Pareto Frontier Performance:** SPACE consistently occupied the empirical Pareto frontier in the coverage-time versus contact-rate plane across all tested swarm sizes and datasets. This indicates that it achieved the lowest contact rates while keeping coverage time near-optimal.
- **Significant Contact Rate Reduction:**
 - On the HouseExpo homes, at congested swarm sizes (N=64, 128, 256), the greedy nearest-frontier planner exhibited contact rates of 0.046, 0.477, and 3.501, respectively. In contrast, SPACE maintained significantly lower rates of 0.003, 0.044, and 0.435. This represents an 8- to 17-fold reduction in inter-robot contacts.
 - Similarly, on the KTH building floors, the greedy planner's contact rates were 0.181, 0.953, and 3.855, while SPACE achieved 0.026, 0.215, and 0.954. This translates to a 4- to 7-fold reduction in contacts.
 - The absolute number of avoided contacts was substantial, with SPACE preventing approximately 5,000 robot-contact instances per mission on HouseExpo and over 20,000 on KTH at N=256 compared to the greedy planner. These safety gains were statistically significant (p < 0.001).
- **Coverage Time Performance:**
 - For smaller swarm sizes (e.g., N=16 on HouseExpo), SPACE was faster than the greedy nearest-frontier planner (e.g., 1565 ticks vs. 2523, a 38% improvement) by reducing redundant exploration.
 - At congested swarm sizes (N=64, 128, 256), SPACE maintained coverage times within approximately two percent of the greedy nearest-frontier planner. For instance, at N=256 on HouseExpo, SPACE achieved 654 ticks compared to the greedy planner's 641 ticks. This indicates that SPACE provides substantial safety improvements with minimal impact on exploration speed, which is near time-optimal for coverage.
 - No other tested method was found to be both faster and safer than SPACE at any congested swarm size.

**B. Impact of the Explore Pheromone**
- By comparing SPACE (which includes the explore pheromone `ϕ_e`) with the MMPF baseline (which uses only the density field `ρ` for dispersion), the research demonstrated the isolated contribution of the `ϕ_e` term. The addition of the explore pheromone in SPACE reduced the contact rate by a factor of 2.1 to 5.0 on HouseExpo homes and 1.5 to 1.8 on KTH floors at congested swarm sizes. This indicates that `ϕ_e` effectively spreads the team across distinct regions and minimizes redundant revisiting, contributing significantly to improved safety.

**C. What Coordination Changes at Scale**
- **Greedy Planner's Efficiency:** The greedy nearest-frontier rule was found to be near time-optimal for coverage, suggesting that simply moving to the closest unexplored boundary is an efficient strategy for covering an unknown area quickly.
- **Limited Speed Gain from Coordination:** Other coordinated baselines, such as MinPos frontier allocation and handed lane formation, did not demonstrate significant improvements in coverage speed over the greedy planner. MinPos, for example, sometimes led to longer detours without increasing throughput.
- **Primary Benefit: Safety:** The results consistently indicated that the primary practical gain from coordination in massive indoor swarms is a substantial reduction in the inter-robot contact rate, rather than an increase in exploration speed. This suggests that coordination mechanisms are most effective at mitigating congestion-related safety issues.
- **Movement Efficiency:** The path length per covered area remained consistent across all methods (within a few percent), implying that the differences in contact rates were due to how robots dispersed and interacted, not how efficiently they moved overall.
- **Dataset Specifics:** While SPACE generally dominated, on the long-corridor KTH floors, handed lane formation achieved similar contact rates to SPACE (though with higher coverage times), indicating joint Pareto-optimality in that specific environment. However, SPACE retained its dominance in the more cluttered HouseExpo homes.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research provides a systematic analysis of the safety-efficiency tradeoff in massive multi-robot exploration, addressing a critical gap in the field by explicitly quantifying and optimizing inter-robot contact rates at unprecedented scales for indoor ground swarms. The development of SPACE, a decentralized and bio-inspired stigmergic coordination system, offers a practical and scalable solution for managing large robot teams in complex, unknown environments.

The significance and potential impact of this work include:
- **Redefining Swarm Exploration Benchmarks:** By integrating inter-robot contact rate as a primary metric alongside coverage time, the research establishes a more comprehensive framework for evaluating the performance of massive robot swarms. This can inform future benchmark designs and encourage the development of safety-aware exploration algorithms.
- **Enhancing Robustness and Reliability:** The demonstrated ability of SPACE to significantly reduce inter-robot contacts (by 4 to 17 times) without compromising exploration speed is crucial for practical applications. Fewer collisions translate to reduced wear and tear, lower energy consumption for collision avoidance, and improved operational uptime for individual robots, thereby enhancing the overall robustness and reliability of the swarm.
- **Scalability for Real-World Deployments:** SPACE's decentralized nature, relying on local sensing and an environment-mediated shared field, ensures that per-robot computation remains independent of swarm size. This makes it a highly scalable solution suitable for deploying hundreds of robots where centralized control or extensive communication would be infeasible, opening possibilities for applications in large-scale search and rescue, disaster response, and industrial inspection.
- **Validation of Biomimetic Principles:** The success of SPACE validates the utility of stigmergic coordination, drawing insights from ant foraging behavior (attractive frontier pheromones, repellent explore and density pheromones). This contributes to the broader understanding of how decentralized, biologically inspired mechanisms can solve complex engineering problems.
- **Fundamental Insights into Swarm Dynamics:** The finding that coordination in dense indoor swarms primarily improves safety rather than accelerating coverage time offers a valuable insight into the fundamental dynamics of large-scale robot exploration. It suggests that while greedy frontier exploration can be time-efficient, sophisticated coordination is essential for maintaining operational integrity and preventing system degradation due to congestion.
- **Future Research Directions:** The work lays a foundation for future advancements, such as exploring learned predictors for frontier fields to anticipate unseen structures, extending the approach to three-dimensional environments for aerial swarms, and conducting real-world hardware deployments to address practical challenges like localization noise and limited communication ranges.

In conclusion, this research provides a significant step towards enabling safer and more robust deployment of massive robot swarms, particularly in confined and cluttered indoor settings, by offering a scalable, efficient, and collision-aware exploration strategy.
