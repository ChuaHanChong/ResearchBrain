# TC-IDM: Grounding Video Generation for Executable Zero-shot Robot Motion

> [!summary] Summary
> The TC-IDM framework presents a "plan-and-translate" approach that bridges the gap between high-level visual plans from generative world models and low-level robot actions. It leverages a tool-centric intermediate representation to enable robust zero-shot robot motion generalization across diverse manipulation tasks, including those with deformable objects and different robot embodiments.

## Problem

- Translating high-level pixel-based visual plans from generative world models into precise, physically executable robot actions, known as the "planning-action chasm."
- End-to-end Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models heavily depend on large, robot-specific datasets and struggle with out-of-distribution tasks, long-horizon planning, and explicit reasoning.
- Existing inverse dynamics methods often rely on brittle pixel tracking or unstable object states, leading to failures under occlusions, rapid motion, or with deformable objects.

## Method

- A "plan-and-translate" paradigm is introduced, comprising a Spatiotemporal Prediction stage to generate a visual-geometric understanding and a Decoupled Action Translation stage for robot control.
- The Decoupled Action Translation core explicitly separates vision-driven gripper internal control (1-DoF) from geometry-grounded 6-DoF end-effector motion.
- It uses a tool-centric intermediate representation by tracking the 3D motion of the robot's end-effector and analytically recovering 6-DoF rigid-body poses, bypassing reliance on potentially unstable object states.

## Results

- Achieves superior real-world manipulation success rates, with an average of 61.11% across tasks when integrated with various video generative models, significantly outperforming existing inverse dynamics baselines.
- Demonstrates robust zero-shot generalization across diverse scenarios, including novel camera viewpoints, deformable object manipulation (38.46% success rate for cloth removal), and complex long-horizon tasks (e.g., 6-step hoodie folding).
- Successfully transfers learned motion priors to different robot embodiments, from a single-arm Franka to a dual-arm UR robot, and to dexterous hands, showcasing embodiment-agnostic motion representation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Anchoring robot control to the stable, well-defined 3D motion of the end-effector (tool) provides a more robust and generalizable approach than tracking manipulated objects or raw pixels.
> - Decoupling the gripper's internal state control from the 6-DoF pose trajectory of the Tool Center Point allows for a modular, interpretable, and robust action translation pipeline.
> - Pre-trained foundation models (e.g., world models for video generation, DINOv3 for semantics, SAM3 for masking, 3D Point Tracker for motion) can be effectively integrated to achieve strong zero-shot generalization without extensive robot-specific data.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of embodied AI and robotic control is undergoing a transformative period, largely driven by advancements in large-scale AI models. Historically, robotic control relied on meticulously engineered models or task-specific learning algorithms. The advent of large-scale vision-language models (VLMs) has shifted the paradigm towards leveraging their powerful perceptual and reasoning capabilities for robotic tasks.

The current research landscape presents two primary approaches to achieving general-purpose robot control:

1. **End-to-End Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Models:** Exemplified by models like OpenVLA, π0, and the RT series, these approaches directly map multimodal inputs (vision and language) to low-level robot actions. They have demonstrated remarkable performance on tasks within their training distribution, benefiting from vast, high-quality robotic datasets (e.g., BridgeData, Open X-Embodiment). However, a critical limitation is their reliance on such extensive and often domain-specific data, which restricts their generalization capabilities to out-of-distribution (OOD) tasks, unseen environments, or long-horizon, compositional reasoning. They often lack an explicit planning mechanism, making complex sequential tasks challenging.

2. **Generative World Models for Visual Planning:** A promising alternative involves leveraging generative models, particularly video-based world models (e.g., Sora, Kling, Cosmos, WoW), as high-level planners. These models can synthesize high-fidelity, physically plausible future video sequences, providing "visual foresight" for complex manipulations. This paradigm allows agents to internalize intricate dynamics and simulate potential future states in pixel space, addressing many challenges previously considered intractable for robotics.

**The "Planning-Action Chasm" and TC-IDM's Niche:**
Despite the impressive visual foresight offered by generative world models, a significant "last-mile" challenge persists: translating their pixel-level plans into physically executable robot actions. The raw RGB video output of a world model does not inherently constitute precise robotic control primitives. Such visual synthesis often suffers from noise, physical hallucinations (e.g., objects moving unrealistically), or kinematic inconsistencies, primarily because it lacks grounding in the robot's specific morphology and actuation limits. Existing methods attempting to track object states from videos (e.g., AVDC, VidBot, Novaflow) are often brittle, failing under occlusions, rapid motion, or when dealing with deformable objects, whose states are notoriously difficult to define and track reliably.

TC-IDM directly addresses this "planning-action chasm." It positions itself as a robust bridge between the high-level, pixel-based planning of generative world models and the low-level, physically consistent trajectories required for robot control. Unlike prior inverse dynamics models that rely on full pixel tracking or unstable object states, TC-IDM introduces a novel "tool-centric" intermediate representation. By focusing on the stable, well-defined motion of the robot's end-effector (tool), as envisioned by the world model, it overcomes the brittleness of pixel-level translation, significantly enhancing generalization and robustness. This approach represents a crucial step towards realizing general-purpose embodied AI where robots can learn to perform complex tasks by simply "imagining" them through video generation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind TC-IDM stems from the fundamental challenge of deploying sophisticated visual planning capabilities of generative world models onto physical robots. While these world models can synthesize remarkable future scenarios, translating these high-level pixel plans into precise, low-level robot actions remains a formidable barrier. The authors identify this as the "planning-action chasm," a critical gap that prevents world models from driving truly general-purpose embodied AI.

Specifically, the key objectives and motivations driving the development of TC-IDM are:

- **Bridging the Visual-to-Action Gap:** The overarching objective is to develop a robust and reliable mechanism to translate high-level visual plans generated by world models into executable robot control signals. Current methods often struggle with noise, physical inconsistencies, and lack of robot-specific grounding in visual predictions.
- **Overcoming Limitations of VLA Models:** To move beyond the limitations of end-to-end VLA models, which, despite their power, heavily depend on large, robot-specific datasets and struggle with out-of-distribution tasks, long-horizon planning, and explicit reasoning. TC-IDM seeks to leverage the planning capabilities of world models to enhance generalization without extensive task-specific robot data.
- **Establishing a Stable Intermediate Representation:** To find a robust intermediate representation that can effectively link high-level visual foresight with low-level physical control. Traditional approaches tracking arbitrary object states from video are often fragile, especially with occlusions, fast motions, or deformable objects. The motivation is to anchor the control to something inherently stable and well-defined.
- **Enabling Deformable Object Manipulation:** To allow robots to manipulate deformable objects (like cloth) effectively. Current methods struggle with defining and tracking the complex, ever-changing state of non-rigid materials, making precise manipulation difficult. TC-IDM aims to bypass this complexity by focusing on the tool's interaction rather than the object's deformation.
- **Achieving Viewpoint Invariance:** To design a control system that is robust to variations in camera viewpoints, which is crucial for real-world deployment where camera positions might change or differ from training scenarios. Representing motion in a world coordinate frame addresses this.
- **Supporting Diverse and Long-Horizon Tasks:** To facilitate the execution of complex, multi-stage tasks by leveraging the compositional planning capabilities of world models while ensuring the physical feasibility of each sub-action.
- **Demonstrating Zero-Shot Generalization:** To showcase strong generalization capabilities across various real-world scenarios, including novel tasks, unseen environments, different camera setups, deformable objects, and even different robot embodiments, without requiring task-specific fine-tuning.

In essence, TC-IDM is motivated by the desire to unlock the full potential of generative world models for robotics, transforming their imaginative foresight into reliable, physically grounded actions for general-purpose, adaptable robotic agents.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

TC-IDM employs a novel "plan-and-translate" paradigm, which systematically bridges the high-level visual planning of generative world models with low-level robot control. The core insight lies in anchoring the control policy to the stable, well-defined motion of the robot's end-effector (tool), rather than relying on the often unstable state of manipulated objects or raw pixels. The overall architecture is depicted in Fig. 2 and comprises two main stages: Spatiotemporal Prediction and Decoupled Action Translation.

**Stage 1: Spatiotemporal Prediction**
This initial stage generates a comprehensive visual-geometric understanding of the imagined future:

1. **Video Prediction:** A video-generative world model (e.g., WoW, Cosmos, Kling) takes an initial RGB frame ($I_{0}^{rgb}$) and a text instruction (L) as input. It then generates an imagined RGB video sequence ($V_{rgb-gen} = \{I_{t}^{rgb-gen}\}_{t=0}^{T}$) that serves as the high-level visual plan for the robot. Crucially, the initial frame ensures the robot's end-effector is visible to facilitate subsequent pose acquisition.
2. **Spatial Estimation:** To ground the pixel-level video in 3D geometry, the method utilizes a series of perception modules:
 - **Depth and Camera Pose Estimation:** VGGT (Visual Geometry Grounded Transformer) is used to generate an initial depth map sequence ($\{\tilde{D}_t\}_{t=1}^T$) and corresponding relative camera poses ($\{P_{rel_t}\}_{t=1}^T$).
 - **Metric Alignment:** A reference metric depth map ($D_{0}^{metric}$) is obtained from a raw depth measurement refined by a completion network. This is then used to linearly align the predicted depth maps by computing a scale and shift factor. The scaled relative camera poses are aligned to the known ground truth initial camera pose ($P_{0}^{gt}$) to yield a fully metric camera trajectory ($\{P_{metric_t}\}_{t=1}^T$) and depth sequence ($\{D_{metric_t}\}_{t=1}^T$). This step is critical for providing a consistent 3D context for action translation.

**Stage 2: Decoupled Action Translation (TC-IDM Core)**
This stage is the core contribution, explicitly decomposing robot action into two complementary and decoupled streams:

1. **Vision-Driven State Generation (for Gripper/Tool Internal Control):**
 - **Semantic Feature Extraction:** A frozen, pre-trained visual encoder, DINOv3, which is known for its strong dense correspondence capabilities, processes the generated RGB video ($V_{rgb-gen}$). This extracts a sequence of temporally consistent semantic embeddings ($F_{dino} = \{f_t^{dino}\}_{t=0}^T$). These features capture high-level task semantics like object identity, contact states, and phase transitions.
 - **Gripper MLP Head (1-DoF Gripper Control):** A lightweight Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP), termed `GripperHead`, takes these semantic features ($f_t^{dino}$) and predicts a continuous grasping aperture parameter ($A_{gripper}$). This controls the opening and closing of the robot's gripper. This stream is responsible for the internal state of the end-effector.

2. **Geometry-Grounded Gesture Generation (for 6-DoF End-Effector Motion):**
 - **Dense Gripper Trajectory Extraction:** The aligned spatiotemporal prediction ($V_{spatial} = \{I_{t}^{rgb-gen}, D_{t}^{metric}\}_{t=0}^{T}$) is used. SAM3 (Segment Anything Model 3) generates a dense gripper mask ($M_t^{gripper}$) for each frame, isolating the end-effector. A 3D Point Tracker (SpatialTrackerv2) then leverages the RGB-D input and gripper mask to extract dense 3D point trajectories across time ($T_{dense}$). These trajectories represent the 3D motion of points on the gripper surface.
 - **Rigid-Body Motion Prior:** To mitigate generative artifacts and ensure stable geometric representation, the system evaluates the consistency of each dense trajectory under a rigid transformation. It retains the top-K trajectories (K=10) that best satisfy this rigid-body assumption, yielding $T_{gripper}$.
 - **Rigid-Body Pose Recovery for 6-DoF Control:** Instead of learning a separate action decoder, this branch analytically recovers executable robot actions. Assuming locally rigid gripper motion, it estimates the relative 6-DoF rigid-body transformation ($R_t, p_t \in SE(3)$) that best aligns the filtered 3D trajectories between consecutive frames. This recovered transformation directly defines the 6-DoF end-effector action ($A_{t+1}^{TCP}$) to be executed (position and orientation changes). This stream is responsible for the global pose trajectory of the Tool Center Point (TCP).

**Final Robot Control:** The outputs from both decoupled streams—the gripper action ($A_{gripper}$) and the end-effector action ($A_{TCP}$)—are concatenated to form the complete robot control vector. This modular and decoupled design is robust, flexible, and adaptive, allowing TC-IDM to handle a wide range of tasks and end-effectors while being robust to viewpoint changes and deformable objects.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents a comprehensive set of quantitative and qualitative experiments in both simulated and real-world environments to validate TC-IDM's performance and generalization capabilities.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Manipulation Experiments (Real-World Replay on Ground Truth Video)

This set of experiments evaluates the inverse dynamics model's ability to accurately reproduce actions from a given visual plan (ground-truth video replay), disentangling IDM performance from world model generation quality. Tasks are categorized into Easy, Medium, and Hard based on DoF and precision constraints.

- **Performance Comparison:** TC-IDM significantly outperforms all baseline inverse dynamics models (ResNet-MLPs, AVDC, AnyPos, 2DtrackerIDM) across all difficulty levels.
 - It achieves near-perfect performance on Easy tasks (10/10 for "Bread to Plate"), showcasing robust basic control.
 - On Medium tasks, TC-IDM consistently outperforms, demonstrating better handling of simple collision avoidance and 4-DoF control.
 - Crucially, on Hard tasks (e.g., "Hang Cup," "Insert Chopsticks"), which require fine-grained 6-DoF coordination and high precision, TC-IDM's success rate is substantially higher than baselines. For instance, it achieves 7/15 on "Hang Cup" compared to 2/15 for AnyPos and 0/15 for AVDC.
- **Ablation Insights:** The results highlight that static appearance cues (ResNet-MLPs) are insufficient for accurate inverse dynamics, and even 2D trackers (2DtrackerIDM) struggle without geometric or depth-aware reasoning on complex tasks. AnyPos, while better than AVDC, still suffers from temporal drift on long-horizon interactions. TC-IDM's approach of leveraging 3D motion tracking and rigid-body constraints proves superior for stability and accuracy. The stated 93.3% replay accuracy on Easy tasks represents the upper bound for IDM performance in this setup.

#### 4.2. Manipulation Evaluation Across Video World Models

This section evaluates TC-IDM when integrated with various state-of-the-art video generation models for real-world execution.

- **Consistent Superiority:** TC-IDM consistently achieves the highest success rates across all difficulty levels and when paired with diverse video generative models (e.g., π0, CogVideo-IDM, Kling-IDM, Cosmos2-IDM, WoW-cosmos2-IDM*, WoW-wan-IDM*).
- **Robustness to World Model Variability:** The performance margin is particularly significant on Hard tasks, indicating TC-IDM's robustness to variations in the visual dynamics and potential artifacts produced by different generative models.
- **Overall Success:** The world model with TC-IDM achieves an average success rate of 61.11% across all tasks, with 77.7% on simple tasks, showcasing its practical applicability.

#### 4.3. Generalization Experiments (Zero-Shot)

These experiments rigorously test TC-IDM's ability to generalize to unseen scenarios without any task-specific fine-tuning.

- **Error Range Analysis (Dart-Placing):** TC-IDM demonstrates high plan-to-execution fidelity, consistently placing a dart within 4 cm of the target. This significantly outperforms VLA baselines, which often fail to hit the target, highlighting TC-IDM's strong zero-shot execution capability by leveraging internet-pretrained world models.
- **Generalization Across Camera Variations:** The model, trained on Intel Realsense D457, successfully generalizes to unseen camera domains (Apple Pro, Realsense D435i) without finetuning. Despite substantial visual shifts (color, illumination, viewpoint), generated trajectories remain consistent, proving robustness to distribution shifts.
- **Generalization of Deformable Objects:** TC-IDM achieves a 38.46% success rate on zero-shot deformable object tasks (e.g., cloth removal). It reliably identifies grasp regions and predicts robust trajectories for cloth, even though it was trained exclusively on rigid objects. This is a crucial finding for real-world adaptability.
- **Long-Horizon Generalization:** The system successfully completes a challenging six-step hoodie-folding task in a zero-shot manner (Fig. 4). This demonstrates coherent multi-step control and alignment with the intended plan, even with substantial state changes after each action.
- **Cross-Embodiment Experiment:** Motion priors learned on a single-arm Franka robot effectively transfer to a real dual-arm UR robot for a xylophone-playing task (Fig. 8), achieving high spatial precision and temporal stability. This suggests the learning of robust, embodiment-invariant motion representations.

#### 4.4. Human-to-Dexterous Hand Migration

TC-IDM's modularity allows for adaptation to dexterous hands by remapping executor states, keeping upstream modules unchanged.

- **Dexterous Hand Compatibility:** A pipeline integrating HaMeR for human hand state estimation and a retargeting network for embodiment-specific joint angles enables zero-shot transfer.
- **Demonstrated Tasks:** Successfully transfers motions to BrainCo (balloon hitting) and Inspire-Robots (cloth removal) dexterous hands, further validating the embodiment-agnostic nature of the learned motion representation.

In summary, TC-IDM establishes a new, strong baseline for deploying generative world models on physical robots, showcasing remarkable performance and zero-shot generalization across a wide array of challenging manipulation scenarios, including those involving deformable objects and diverse embodiments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The TC-IDM framework represents a significant advancement in embodied AI and robotic manipulation, with several profound implications and potential impacts:

1. **Bridging the "Last-Mile" Gap for World Models:** The most immediate and critical impact is its effective solution to the "planning-action chasm." By translating pixel-level visual plans from generative world models into physically executable robot actions, TC-IDM unlocks the immense potential of visual foresight for real-world robotics. This paves the way for world models, often trained on vast internet-scale video data, to directly inform and guide robot behavior, reducing the reliance on costly, robot-specific datasets.

2. **Enhanced Zero-Shot Generalization:** TC-IDM demonstrates unprecedented zero-shot generalization capabilities across a remarkable range of scenarios:
 - **New Tasks and Environments:** Robots can perform tasks not explicitly seen during training.
 - **Camera Viewpoint Variations:** Robustness to different camera models and positions enhances real-world applicability.
 - **Deformable Object Manipulation:** The ability to handle deformable objects (like cloth) effectively, without complex state modeling, is a long-standing challenge in robotics. TC-IDM's success in this area opens up vast possibilities for domestic, industrial, and medical applications.
 - **Long-Horizon and Compositional Tasks:** The framework enables robots to execute complex, multi-step tasks by leveraging the world model's planning, a key aspect for truly autonomous agents.
 - **Cross-Embodiment Transfer:** The successful transfer of learned motions to different robot platforms (e.g., Franka to UR, human hand to dexterous hands) indicates the learning of abstract, embodiment-agnostic representations, which is crucial for scalable robot learning.

3. **A Robust and Interpretable "Plan-and-Translate" Paradigm:**
 - **Decoupled Design:** The separation of gripper state control (vision-driven) from 6-DoF TCP pose recovery (geometry-grounded) offers a modular and more interpretable approach compared to monolithic end-to-end systems. This allows for easier debugging and targeted improvements.
 - **Physical Grounding:** The analytical recovery of 6-DoF actions through rigid-body alignment of the tool's 3D trajectories ensures physical consistency and reduces the occurrence of "physical hallucinations" often found in purely pixel-based or learned action decoders.

4. **Leveraging Foundation Models:** TC-IDM effectively integrates several powerful pre-trained foundation models (world models, DINOv3, SAM3, 3D Point Tracker). This strategy allows the system to benefit from vast pre-training knowledge, reducing the need for extensive robot-specific data collection and promoting scalability. This aligns with the broader trend in AI of building upon large, general-purpose models.

5. **Setting a New Baseline for Embodied Control:** The strong quantitative results, significantly outperforming existing VLA-style baselines and other inverse dynamics models, establish TC-IDM as a new, compelling benchmark for deploying generative world models on physical robots. This will undoubtedly inspire further research in grounding visual plans.

6. **Towards General-Purpose Robotic Intelligence:** By making generative imagination directly executable, TC-IDM contributes significantly to the vision of general-purpose embodied AI. Robots can potentially learn from a much broader range of visual data (e.g., human videos, synthetic environments) and translate that understanding into actionable plans, leading to more adaptable, autonomous, and versatile robotic systems capable of operating in complex, unstructured real-world environments.

In conclusion, TC-IDM provides a robust, generalizable, and highly impactful framework that bridges a critical gap in embodied AI. Its ability to turn visual imagination into precise, physically grounded robot actions, especially in zero-shot and challenging scenarios, marks a significant step forward for the field of robotics.
