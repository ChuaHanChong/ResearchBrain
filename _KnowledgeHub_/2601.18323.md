# TC-IDM: Grounding Video Generation for Executable Zero-shot Robot Motion

> [!summary] Summary
> The TC-IDM framework presents a "plan-and-translate" approach that bridges the gap between high-level visual plans from generative world models and low-level robot actions. It leverages a tool-centric intermediate representation to enable robust zero-shot robot motion generalization across diverse manipulation tasks, including those with deformable objects and different robot embodiments.

## Problem

- Translating high-level pixel-based visual plans from generative world models into precise, physically executable robot actions, known as the "planning-action chasm."
- End-to-end Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models heavily depend on large, robot-specific datasets and struggle with out-of-distribution tasks, long-horizon planning, and explicit reasoning.
- Existing inverse dynamics methods often rely on brittle pixel tracking or unstable object states, leading to failures under occlusions, rapid motion, or with deformable objects.

## Method

- A "plan-and-translate" paradigm is introduced, comprising a Spatiotemporal Prediction stage to generate a visual-geometric understanding and a Decoupled Action Translation stage for robot control.
- The Decoupled Action Translation core explicitly separates vision-driven gripper internal control (1-DoF) from geometry-grounded 6-DoF end-effector motion.
- It uses a tool-centric intermediate representation by tracking the 3D motion of the robot's end-effector and analytically recovering 6-DoF rigid-body poses, bypassing reliance on potentially unstable object states.

## Results

- Achieves superior real-world manipulation success rates, with an average of 61.11% across tasks when integrated with various video generative models, significantly outperforming existing inverse dynamics baselines.
- Demonstrates robust zero-shot generalization across diverse scenarios, including novel camera viewpoints, deformable object manipulation (38.46% success rate for cloth removal), and complex long-horizon tasks (e.g., 6-step hoodie folding).
- Successfully transfers learned motion priors to different robot embodiments, from a single-arm Franka to a dual-arm UR robot, and to dexterous hands, showcasing embodiment-agnostic motion representation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Anchoring robot control to the stable, well-defined 3D motion of the end-effector (tool) provides a more robust and generalizable approach than tracking manipulated objects or raw pixels.
> - Decoupling the gripper's internal state control from the 6-DoF pose trajectory of the Tool Center Point allows for a modular, interpretable, and robust action translation pipeline.
> - Pre-trained foundation models (e.g., world models for video generation, DINOv3 for semantics, SAM3 for masking, 3D Point Tracker for motion) can be effectively integrated to achieve strong zero-shot generalization without extensive robot-specific data.
