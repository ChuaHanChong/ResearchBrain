# HERMES: A Unified Self-Driving World Model for Simultaneous 3D Scene Understanding and Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> HERMES is a unified driving world model that jointly performs 3D scene understanding and future scene generation via a shared BEV backbone processed by an LLM. World queries carry causally-attended world knowledge from text into a current-to-future link that generates future BEV features, rendered into point clouds. It cuts generation Chamfer error by 32.4% and lifts CIDEr by 8.0% over specialist baselines.

## Problem

- Existing driving world models predict future scene evolution but cannot interpret or describe the environment, answer questions, or provide contextual reasoning about the scene.
- Driving VLMs handle scene understanding and VQA well but lack any predictive capability for how the scene evolves, limiting decision-making usefulness.
- Naively sharing BEV features between separate understanding and generation heads fails to exploit interactions between the two tasks and hinders joint optimization.

## Method

- **HERMES**'s ==BEV-based world tokenizer== encodes multi-view images via ==CLIP== and ==BEVFormer v2== then downsamples and flattens into compact tokens feeding directly into the ==LLM==.
- ==World queries== are initialized via ==max-pooling== the BEV feature, enriched with ego-motion and frame embeddings, and updated with world knowledge through the LLM's ==causal attention==.
- ==Current-to-future link== uses stacked ==cross-attention blocks== where encoded BEV queries against world-knowledge-enhanced queries, followed by a shared ==BEV-to-point volumetric SDF Render==.

## Results

- HERMES reduces 3-second point cloud Chamfer distance by about **32%** compared to ViDAR, despite using only current-frame multi-view input versus ViDAR's 3-second history.
- On OmniDrive-nuScenes, HERMES beats OmniDrive by **8.0%** on CIDEr while also outperforming on METEOR and ROUGE understanding metrics.
- Ablations show world queries cut 3s Chamfer distance by **10%** versus removing them, and max-pooling query initialization beats attention and average pooling.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Joint training of understanding and generation over a shared BEV representation can beat both single-task specialists and a naive separated-unification baseline on generation.
> - A compact query-based bridge, not full feature sharing, is what lets world knowledge flow from language understanding into spatial generation without token-length blowup.
> - Tying an auxiliary current-frame reconstruction loss to the shared encoder regularizes representations and helps downstream future prediction at no added inference cost.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of autonomous driving research is rapidly advancing, with significant progress in both perception and planning. Within this landscape, "world models" have emerged as a critical component, enabling vehicles to predict future environmental states and facilitate proactive decision-making. HERMES positions itself at the intersection of two previously distinct, yet equally important, sub-fields:

- **Driving World Models (DWMs) for Generation:** Prior DWMs, whether in 2D (video generation) or 3D (point cloud or occupancy prediction), have primarily focused on *generating* future scene evolutions. These models excel at forecasting how objects and the environment will change over time, which is vital for trajectory planning and risk assessment. Examples include GAIA-1, VISTA, and ViDAR. However, a significant limitation of these models is their lack of explicit *scene understanding* – they can predict "what will happen" but struggle to interpret "what is happening," answer complex queries, or provide contextual reasoning.

- **Large Language Models (LLMs) for Driving for Understanding:** Concurrently, the advent of powerful vision-language models (VLMs) and LLMs has led to their application in autonomous driving for *scene understanding*. These models leverage vast world knowledge and causal reasoning to interpret driving environments, generate descriptive captions, answer visual questions (VQA), and even assist in decision-making by explaining scenarios. DriveGPT4, DriveLM, and OmniDrive are examples of such efforts. While these models provide rich contextual understanding, they inherently lack the *predictive capabilities* required to forecast future environmental dynamics.

**The Research Gap and HERMES's Contribution:** The core motivation behind HERMES is to address the crucial gap between these two specialized areas. Autonomous driving systems require a holistic understanding that encompasses *both* interpreting the current 3D environment and accurately predicting its future evolution. Relying on separate models for these tasks leads to suboptimal performance due to fragmented information and missed opportunities for synergistic interaction.

HERMES is presented as the **first unified self-driving world model** that seamlessly integrates 3D scene understanding and future scene generation. By doing so, it pioneers a new paradigm that leverages the strengths of generative DWMs (predictive accuracy) and LLM-based understanding (interpretability and reasoning). This unification promises to create a more comprehensive, intelligent, and robust autonomous driving system that can not only react to its environment but also truly comprehend and anticipate it. This work represents a significant step towards creating more "intelligent" and interpretable world models for autonomous agents, moving beyond mere prediction to true situational awareness.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of the HERMES research is to **develop a unified Driving World Model (DWM) capable of simultaneously performing 3D scene understanding and future scene evolution (generation) within autonomous driving scenarios.**

This overarching goal is driven by several critical motivations:

1. **Addressing the Limitations of Existing DWMs:** Current DWMs primarily focus on generative prediction (e.g., forecasting point clouds or occupancy maps) and excel at simulating future environmental changes. However, they lack the ability to *interpret* the scene, provide high-level descriptions, answer complex questions, or reason about contextual information. This interpretability gap makes them "black boxes" from a decision-making perspective.
2. **Overcoming the Shortcomings of Driving LLMs:** While recent LLMs and VLMs applied to autonomous driving scenarios demonstrate remarkable capabilities in scene understanding, visual question answering (VQA), and generating textual descriptions, they are inherently limited to the *current* state of the environment. They do not possess the predictive power to forecast how the 3D scene will evolve over time, which is indispensable for proactive driving decisions.
3. **Meeting the Holistic Needs of Autonomous Driving:** Robust autonomous driving systems demand a comprehensive understanding of the driving environment that integrates both *what is happening* (understanding) and *what will happen* (prediction/generation). Without this unification, vehicles cannot make fully informed decisions, anticipate risks, or optimize long-term plans effectively. Separating these tasks leads to a fragmented view of the world and hinders optimal performance.
4. **Enabling Cross-Task Knowledge Transfer:** The researchers hypothesize that understanding and generation are inherently linked, and a unified model can leverage synergistic interactions between these tasks. Knowledge gained from interpreting the current scene (e.g., identifying objects, traffic rules) should inform and enrich future predictions, and vice-versa.
5. **Tackling Technical Challenges for Unification:** The motivation also includes addressing specific technical hurdles in building such a unified model:
 - **Large Spatiality in Multi-View Data:** Autonomous driving datasets often involve multiple high-resolution camera views, which generate an enormous number of tokens. Directly feeding these to LLMs would exceed token limits and fail to capture inter-view geometric relationships.
 - **Effective Integration of Understanding and Generation:** A naive approach of simply sharing features between separate understanding and generation modules would likely fail to capitalize on the potential for deep interaction and knowledge transfer between these tasks.

In essence, HERMES is motivated by the fundamental need for autonomous vehicles to not just *see* and *predict*, but to *comprehend* their complex, dynamic world in a truly intelligent and integrated manner.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

HERMES proposes a novel, unified framework designed to seamlessly integrate 3D scene understanding and future scene evolution (generation) in autonomous driving. The methodology centers around a Bird’s-Eye View (BEV) representation, a Large Language Model (LLM), and "world queries" for cross-task knowledge transfer.

#### 3.1. Overall Architecture

The HERMES pipeline takes multi-view input images ($I_t$) at time $t$. These images are processed by a BEV-based tokenizer, fed into an LLM for scene understanding and future prediction, and finally rendered into point clouds for both the current and future time steps ($P_t$ to $P_{t+\Delta t}$).

#### 3.2. World Tokenizer and Render

- **BEV-based World Tokenizer:**
 - **Purpose:** To encode multi-view images into a compact, continuous BEV representation ($F_t$) that preserves geometric relationships and rich semantic information, making it suitable for LLM processing.
 - **Mechanism:** Multi-view images ($I_t$) are first passed through a pre-trained **CLIP image encoder** (e.g., OpenCLIP ConNext-L) to extract high-level visual features. These features are then aggregated into a BEV representation using a **single-frame BEVFormer v2** backbone.
 - **Compression:** The resulting BEV feature ($F_{bev}^t \in \mathbb{R}^{w \times h \times c}$) is often too large for LLMs. HERMES employs a down-sampling block (reducing by 2x) and then flattens and projects it to $F_t \in \mathbb{R}^{L_{bev} \times C}$, effectively compressing the visual information while retaining crucial spatial details. This addresses the LLM's token length limitations (e.g., 2,500 tokens for 50x50 BEV vs. ~47,000 for raw views).
- **BEV-to-Point Render:**
 - **Purpose:** To convert the processed BEV features back into 3D point clouds, enabling geometric scene representation.
 - **Mechanism:** This module (based on differentiable volume rendering, similar to NeRF/NeuS) takes the down-sampled BEV features (or encoded BEV from the LLM, $B_t$) and up-samples them. It then reshapes the 2D BEV feature to introduce a height dimension, converting it into a 3D volumetric feature ($F_{vol}^t$). A shallow MLP predicts Signed Distance Function (SDF) values for sampled points along rays, and these SDFs are used to compute rendered depths, which form the point cloud ($P_t$).

#### 3.3. Unification via Large Language Model (LLM) and World Queries

The core of HERMES's unification lies in how the LLM processes visual and textual information, and how "world queries" facilitate knowledge transfer.

- **Large Language Model (LLM):** HERMES utilizes a powerful LLM, specifically **InternVL2**, as its central processing unit.
 - **Understanding:** For scene understanding, the flattened BEV feature ($F_t$) is projected into the LLM's feature space. User instructions (prompts) are tokenized into text tokens ($T$). The LLM then performs **Next Token Prediction (NTP)**, generating textual responses (scene descriptions, VQA answers) by leveraging its internal world knowledge and the visual context provided by $F_t$.
 - **Generation (Crucial Role of World Queries):** To enable future scene generation, HERMES introduces a novel **world query** mechanism:
 - **Initialization:** $\Delta t$ groups of world queries ($Q_w \in \mathbb{R}^{(\Delta t \times n) \times C}$) are initialized. Each group ($Q_i$) is derived from the *max pooling* of the raw BEV feature ($F_t$), capturing peak responses and salient information.
 - **Contextual Enrichment:** To enable controllable future generation, each query group ($Q_i$) is infused with **ego-motion condition** ($e_{t+i}$), representing planned future positions and headings, and a **frame embedding (FE)** to indicate the target prediction frame.
 - **LLM Integration:** These enriched world queries ($Q_w$) are appended to the LLM's input sequence, alongside the flattened BEV ($F_t$) and text tokens ($T$). The LLM's **causal attention mechanism** allows the world queries to access and integrate world knowledge from the textual understanding process.
 - **Current to Future Link:** After LLM processing, an "current to future link" module (composed of 3 cross-attention blocks) is used. It takes the encoded current BEV ($B_t$) from the LLM output as a query, and the processed world queries (for each future scene) as keys and values. This module effectively injects world knowledge and current scene context into the *future* BEV features ($B_{t+1}, \dots, B_{t+\Delta t}$).
- **Shared Render:** The encoded current BEV ($B_t$) and the generated future BEV features ($B_{t+1}, \dots, B_{t+\Delta t}$) are then passed to the *same* BEV-to-Point Render to generate the current point cloud ($P_t$, as an auxiliary task) and the future point clouds ($P_{t+1}, \dots, P_{t+\Delta t}$).

#### 3.4. Training Objectives

HERMES is trained with a combined loss:
- **Next Token Prediction Loss ($L_N$):** Standard cross-entropy loss for the LLM's language modeling task, maximizing the likelihood of ground-truth text tokens.
- **Depth Loss ($L_D$):** An L1 loss applied to the depths of the rendered point clouds, comparing predicted depths against ground-truth LiDAR depths. This loss is weighted for different future frames ($\lambda_i$).
- **Total Loss:** $L = L_N + 10L_D$, with a higher weight on depth loss to emphasize geometric accuracy.

#### 3.5. Training Stages

The training is structured in three stages:
1. **Tokenizer Training:** The world tokenizer (encoder) and Render are trained to convert images to current point clouds.
2. **BEV-Text Alignment and Refinement:** Establishes vision-language alignment for the LLM. Data augmentation (masking views) and LoRA fine-tuning are used.
3. **Understanding and Generation Unification:** Integrates future generation modules and trains the entire HERMES framework using comprehensive datasets.

This meticulously designed methodology allows HERMES to bridge the gap between understanding and generation by strategically leveraging BEV representations for spatial compression and geometric preservation, and ingeniously employing world queries within an LLM to facilitate knowledge transfer across tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

HERMES demonstrates compelling results, achieving state-of-the-art (SOTA) performance in both 3D scene understanding and future scene generation, highlighting the effectiveness of its unified approach.

#### 4.1. SOTA Performance on Both Tasks

- **Future Point Cloud Generation:** HERMES significantly outperforms existing generation-specialist models. It achieves a **32.4% reduction in Chamfer Distance** (a metric for point cloud precision) for 3-second point cloud prediction compared to ViDAR, the previous SOTA method. Notably, HERMES achieves this using only current multi-view inputs, whereas some competitors like ViDAR utilize a 3-second history horizon, showcasing HERMES's strong predictive power from a single time step.
- **3D Scene Understanding:** HERMES also achieves highly competitive results in scene understanding tasks, specifically in caption quality. It **outperforms OmniDrive (a SOTA understanding specialist) by 8.0% on the CIDEr metric** and shows superior performance on METEOR and ROUGE metrics. This is significant, as OmniDrive leverages extensive 2D pre-training data and 3D object supervision, while HERMES achieves its understanding capabilities through its unified BEV representation.

#### 4.2. Advantage of Unified Approach

- **Synergistic Interaction:** When compared to a "Separated unification" baseline (where BEV features are shared but understanding and generation models are separate), HERMES yields significantly better generation results while maintaining strong understanding performance. This empirically validates the core hypothesis that integrating these tasks within a unified framework, particularly through the world query mechanism, enables beneficial cross-task knowledge transfer and optimizes overall performance.

#### 4.3. Key Ablation Study Insights

- **Understanding and Generation Interaction:** Experiments confirm that the fully unified HERMES architecture generally outperforms models trained solely on one task or a separated unification approach, demonstrating the synergistic benefits.
- **Effectiveness of World Queries:** The introduction of "world queries" is crucial for future generation capabilities, leading to a **10% reduction in Chamfer Distance** for 3-second point cloud prediction. While there's a minor 1% decrease in understanding (CIDEr), this is attributed to increased optimization complexity, and the overall benefit for generation is substantial. Processing world queries through the LLM is shown to further enhance generative performance, confirming their role in effective world knowledge transfer.
- **Generation Length:** As the number of predicted future frames increases (e.g., from 1s to 3s), there is a slight drop in generation performance, indicating an ongoing optimization challenge for longer-term predictions.
- **Auxiliary Task (Current Point Cloud Prediction):** Predicting the current frame's point cloud ($P_t$) serves as an effective auxiliary task. It regularizes the encoded BEV features from the LLM, enhancing future generation results without adding extra inference burden, proving to be a practical addition.
- **Source of World Queries:** Max pooling is found to be the most effective method for initializing world queries from the flattened BEV features, as it better captures salient "peak responses" compared to average or attention pooling, which might be diluted by background noise.
- **Flattened BEV Size:** A spatial resolution of 50x50 for the flattened BEV ($F_t$) significantly improves both understanding (7.3% CIDEr increase) and immediate generation (10% Chamfer Distance reduction for 0s) compared to 25x25. This highlights the importance of retaining sufficient spatial information for both tasks, balancing detail with LLM token limits.
- **LLM Scaling Potential:** Experiments with different LLM sizes (0.8B, 1.8B, 3.8B parameters) consistently show that scaling up the LLM leads to gains in both 3D scene understanding and point cloud generation, suggesting further benefits from larger models with broader world knowledge.
- **Number of World Queries:** An optimal number of 4 world queries per group for future generation yielded the best results; increasing this number led to performance decline, likely due to redundancy and optimization complexity.
- **Baseline Comparison (Copy&Paste):** HERMES significantly outperforms a simple "Copy&Paste" baseline for future point cloud generation, which merely duplicates the current point cloud. This validates that HERMES genuinely learns to predict dynamic scene evolution rather than static reproduction.
- **NuScenes-QA VQA Performance:** HERMES achieves a superior VQA accuracy of 61.9% on the NuScenes-QA benchmark, outperforming LLaVA and even LiDAR-based methods. This reinforces its strong 3D scene understanding capabilities despite not using explicit 3D object detection supervision.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Results

Qualitative examples demonstrate HERMES's ability to accurately predict future scene evolution (e.g., a van maintaining its distance, building corners moving backward) and provide detailed, contextually relevant textual descriptions of the driving environment (e.g., identifying "Starbucks," describing road conditions). Furthermore, it shows potential for controllable scene generation by conditioning predictions on ego-motion commands like "stop" or "turn right."

In summary, the main findings robustly confirm that HERMES successfully unifies 3D scene understanding and generation, achieving leading performance in both domains. The detailed ablations provide insights into the critical components contributing to its success, particularly the BEV representation and the world query mechanism for knowledge transfer.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of HERMES represents a significant advancement in the field of autonomous driving research, with far-reaching implications and potential impacts:

1. **Pioneering a Unified World Model Paradigm:** HERMES is the first model to successfully unify 3D scene understanding and future scene generation within a single framework. This shifts the paradigm from specialized, siloed systems to a holistic, integrated approach, paving the way for more comprehensive and intelligent autonomous agents. It demonstrates the feasibility and benefits of combining previously disparate research directions.

2. **Enhanced Autonomous Driving Decision-Making:** By providing both a deep interpretation of the current environment and accurate predictions of its future evolution, HERMES equips autonomous vehicles with a more robust and complete understanding of their operational context. This enables more informed, proactive decision-making, improved risk assessment, optimized trajectory planning, and safer navigation in complex and dynamic scenarios.

3. **Increased Interpretability and Reasoning:** The integration of Large Language Models introduces high-level interpretability and reasoning capabilities to DWMs, which traditionally function more as "black boxes" focused purely on prediction. HERMES can explain *why* certain predictions are made or *what* elements contribute to a specific scene, enhancing trust and auditability in autonomous systems. This is critical for safety validation and human-AI interaction.

4. **Novel Methodological Contributions:** The paper introduces several innovative techniques that could influence future research:
 - **BEV-based Tokenization for LLMs:** Effectively compressing multi-view spatial information into a BEV representation for LLM input, overcoming token limits while preserving crucial geometric and semantic details. This provides a template for integrating rich visual data into large language models for complex spatial reasoning.
 - **World Queries for Cross-Task Knowledge Transfer:** The concept of "world queries" explicitly links world knowledge derived from textual understanding with the generation task. This mechanism is key to ensuring that future predictions are not just geometrically accurate but also contextually intelligent and linguistically informed.

5. **Foundation for Future Research and Development:** HERMES opens up numerous avenues for subsequent research:
 - **Integration of More Perception Tasks:** The unified framework could be extended to incorporate other vital autonomous driving perception tasks, such as 3D object detection, tracking, and motion forecasting, creating an even more comprehensive world model.
 - **Multi-Modality Generation:** While HERMES focuses on point cloud generation, future work could explore generating other modalities, such as future images or semantic maps, offering richer predictive outputs.
 - **Improved Controllability and Simulation:** The demonstrated ability to condition future generation on ego-motion commands (e.g., "stop," "turn right") highlights its potential as a sophisticated world simulator for training and testing autonomous driving policies.
 - **Scalability and Efficiency:** Further research can focus on optimizing the efficiency and scalability of such unified models, especially given the computational demands of LLMs and 3D rendering.

6. **Accelerating Adoption:** The public release of the model and code will democratize access to this advanced framework, fostering further innovation and accelerating the development of more capable and intelligent autonomous driving systems across the research community and industry.

In conclusion, HERMES marks a pivotal moment in autonomous driving research by bridging a long-standing gap. Its ability to simultaneously understand and generate 3D scenes, coupled with its novel architectural components, positions it as a foundational work that will undoubtedly inspire and enable the next generation of truly intelligent and context-aware self-driving vehicles.
