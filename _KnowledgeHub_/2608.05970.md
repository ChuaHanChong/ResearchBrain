# SkillMemo: Expert-guided Skill Memory Framework for Compositional Embodied Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> The SkillMemo framework introduces an expert-guided skill memory for compositional embodied manipulation, implicitly decomposing long-horizon demonstrations into atomic skills and storing their functional knowledge in a dynamic memory. This approach enables improved compositional generalization and higher success rates across various simulated and real-world robotic tasks.

## Problem

- Existing visuomotor policies, such as Diffusion Policy (DP) and Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, suffer from monolithic learning due to limited large-scale embodied trajectory datasets, leading to poor compositional generalization.
- Current memory-augmented policies primarily store unstructured or holistic visual-semantic representations of past experiences, lacking explicit mechanisms for identifying and reusing behaviorally salient skill-level features.
- These limitations hinder robust performance in out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios that require synthesizing novel behaviors from combinations of known objects, environments, or instructions.

## Method

- ==Expert-Guided Trajectory Segmentation (EGTS)== uses a Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architecture, regularized by a synergy-based information loss (L_PID), to implicitly partition long-horizon demonstrations into latent atomic skills.
- A ==Skill-Level Memory Architecture (SLMA)== stores compact key-value pairs for each discovered skill, with keys representing centroid features of skill segments and values encoding the expert activation profiles (gating weights) required to execute the skill.
- During inference, **SkillMemo** dynamically retrieves the most relevant skill primitives from memory based on current observations and fuses their stored expert activation profiles with the policy's predicted gating weights, providing contextual guidance for action prediction.

## Results

- SkillMemo consistently improved task success rates across simulation benchmarks, achieving **64.6%** average success with a DP backbone (**3.4%** higher than base DP) and **98.0%** with a π0.5 VLA backbone (**1.2%** higher than base π0.5).
- The framework demonstrated strong zero-shot compositional generalization on unseen LIBERO tasks; for example, a model trained on LIBERO-Goal achieved **75.6%** on the unseen Spatial suite, surpassing a baseline explicitly trained on Spatial (**72.1%**).
- In real-world UR5e robotic manipulation, SkillMemo enhanced performance on contact-rich tasks, showing improvements such as **7.5%** (from 82.5% to 90.0%) on 'Corn in Pot' and **12.5%** (from 62.5% to 75.0%) on 'Butter in Pot', and superior generalization to novel object-receptacle combinations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Implicitly decomposing complex behavioral trajectories into atomic skills via an MoE architecture allows for fine-grained policy specialization and structured skill reuse, outperforming monolithic approaches.
> - Storing functional knowledge of atomic skills as expert activation profiles within a dynamic memory bank enables more efficient and context-aware retrieval compared to unstructured memory representations.
> - Fusing retrieved skill-level priors with the current policy's predictions during inference provides robust guidance, allowing embodied agents to synthesize novel behaviors by composing known primitives for enhanced compositional generalization.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of embodied visuomotor models, encompassing approaches such as Diffusion Policy (DP) and Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, has exhibited advancements in robotic manipulation tasks. These models often leverage large-scale vision-language pretraining, followed by fine-tuning on embodied trajectory datasets. Despite their performance on established benchmarks, a fundamental constraint persists: the limited availability of extensive embodied trajectory datasets. This scarcity typically results in learned policies that are monolithic, meaning they do not inherently decompose behaviors into reusable skill structures. Consequently, these policies often demonstrate reduced compositional generalization capabilities, particularly in out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios involving novel combinations of known objects, receptacles, or instructions.

Prior research has attempted to address these limitations through various avenues, including robotic reinforcement learning, the development of motion primitive libraries, and memory-augmented policies. Memory-augmented methods, in particular, have shown promise for addressing long-horizon generalization challenges. For instance, MemoryVLA integrates a dual-stream Perceptual-Cognitive Memory Bank to facilitate context-aware decision-making, while HAMLET aims to manage non-Markovian dependencies by encoding interaction history into temporal moment tokens.

However, existing memory-augmented approaches primarily store unstructured or holistic visual-semantic representations of past experiences. They often lack an explicit mechanism for identifying, storing, and retrieving behaviorally salient *skill-level* features. This limitation leads to inefficient reuse of prior experience and a diminished capacity for compositional generalization when confronted with tasks that require recombining fundamental actions. The proposed SkillMemo framework positions itself within this landscape by introducing a mechanism that implicitly decomposes long-horizon demonstrations into atomic skills and integrates these skill-level features into a dynamic, retrievable memory system. This approach aims to provide a more structured form of experiential knowledge, thereby enhancing compositional generalization beyond the capabilities of current visuomotor and memory-augmented models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance robust compositional generalization for embodied manipulation systems. This overarching goal is pursued through several specific aims designed to overcome limitations in current visuomotor policies.

The core motivation stems from the observation that while Diffusion Policy (DP) and Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have demonstrated promising performance, their real-world applicability is constrained by the high cost and scarcity of large-scale embodied trajectory datasets. Training with insufficient data typically leads to the development of monolithic policies. These policies process entire behavioral trajectories as undifferentiated sequences, failing to capture underlying, reusable skill structures. This deficiency renders them ineffective when confronted with out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios, where novel compositions of familiar objects, environments, or instructions demand an agent capable of synthesizing new behaviors from known primitives. Such monolithic approaches cannot effectively generalize to unseen task configurations.

Specifically, the paper identifies a gap in existing memory-augmented policies. While these methods incorporate memory to some extent, they often store unstructured or holistic representations of past experiences. This means they do not explicitly decompose complex tasks into discrete, semantically meaningful atomic skills, nor do they provide a structured mechanism for memorizing and retrieving these individual skill-level features. Consequently, their capacity for efficient reuse of prior experience and robust compositional generalization in novel tasks remains limited.

To address these challenges, the key objectives are:
1. **Implicit Skill Decomposition:** To develop a method that can implicitly partition long-horizon robotic demonstrations into a set of latent atomic skills. This aims to enable fine-grained specialization of the policy's network capacity and facilitate structured skill reuse.
2. **Skill-Level Memory Integration:** To design and implement a dynamic episodic memory architecture capable of storing compact, retrievable representations of these learned atomic skills. This memory should organize skills as key-value pairs, where the value encapsulates the functional knowledge (e.g., expert activation profiles) required to execute the skill.
3. **Context-Aware Skill Retrieval and Fusion:** To establish a mechanism for dynamically retrieving the most relevant skill primitives from the memory bank during inference, and to effectively fuse these retrieved priors with the model's current action prediction process. This fusion is intended to provide robust contextual guidance, enabling the policy to synthesize novel behaviors by composing known primitives, thereby improving compositional generalization.

By achieving these objectives, the research aims to enable embodied agents to learn more efficiently from limited demonstrations, decompose complex tasks into manageable sub-components, and generalize more effectively to novel, unseen task configurations by leveraging a structured memory of atomic skills.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The SkillMemo framework is designed to enhance compositional generalization in embodied manipulation by implicitly decomposing long-horizon demonstrations into latent atomic skills and integrating these skill-level features into a dynamic episodic memory. The framework comprises three main components: Expert-Guided Trajectory Segmentation, Skill-Level Memory Architecture, and Memory Update.

#### 3.1. Expert-Guided Trajectory Segmentation (EGTS)

The EGTS module addresses the limitation of conventional visuomotor models that use monolithic networks, which often lead to poor compositionality. Instead, SkillMemo structurally decomposes entire trajectories into a sequence of latent atomic skills. This decomposition allows for specialized network capacity and facilitates skill reuse.

- **Implicit Partitioning:** Unlike explicit segmentation schemes that might fail to capture all skill boundaries, EGTS employs an implicit, data-driven partitioning method. This is realized through a Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architecture.
- **MoE Architecture:** The MoE consists of *N* expert networks, {E₁..., E_N}, and a gating network, *G*. Given an intermediate feature representation *h_t* at time *t*, the MoE layer computes the output *y_t* as a weighted sum of expert outputs: *y_t = Σ(g_i(h_t) ⋅ E_i(h_t))*. Here, *g_i(h_t)* represents the gating weight for the *i*-th expert. The gating network implicitly defines temporal segmentation boundaries by modulating expert activations, meaning each expert *E_i* is intended to specialize in a recurring local motion primitive.
- **Skill Specialization and Composition:** To encourage distinct specialization and emergent skill composition among experts, the model is regularized using a synergy-based information loss (L_PID) derived from Partial Information Decomposition (PID) theory. This loss encourages the joint representation of experts to contain more task-relevant information than the sum of individual expert contributions, fostering complementary skill learning. Additionally, expert load-balancing losses and top-k routing strategies are employed to prevent expert collapse and ensure balanced expert utilization.

#### 3.2. Skill-Level Memory Architecture (SLMA)

The SLMA is designed to leverage the atomic skills discovered by EGTS for solving novel compositional tasks, moving beyond purely reactive policies. It stores, retrieves, and composes previously learned skill primitives.

- **Memory Storage:** The memory bank *M* is organized as a collection of Key-Value pairs:
 - **Memory Key (k_m):** For each atomic skill segment *s_k* discovered by EGTS, a compact centroid feature is computed. This key is an average of the latent trajectory features *h_t* over the segment: *k_m = (1/L) Σ_{t∈τ_m} h_t*. This temporal aggregation ensures efficient storage and retrieval by representing the most characteristic features of the skill.
 - **Memory Value (v_m):** The value stores the functional knowledge to reproduce the skill, specifically the complete sequence of gating weights across all timesteps within the segment: *v_m = {g(h_t) | t∈τ_m}*. These gating weights govern expert activation and implicitly define the skill's category.
 The final memory bank is *M = {(k_m, v_m)}* up to *M_max* stored skill primitives.

- **Memory Retrieval:** During inference, the stored procedural knowledge is used to enhance policy execution:
 1. **Similarity Calculation:** The latent feature *q_t* of the current observation is compared with every memory key *k_m* using cosine similarity: *s(q_t, k_m) = (q_t ⋅ k_m) / (||q_t|| ||k_m||)*.
 2. **Top-N Retrieval:** The top-N memory entries with the highest similarity scores are retrieved, forming *M_top = {(k_n, v_n)}*.
 3. **Reliability Check:** An average similarity score of the retrieved set is compared against a predefined confidence threshold *δ*. If the retrieval is deemed reliable, the process continues.
 4. **Policy Augmentation:** The current gating weights *g(q_t)* (predicted by the MoE) are refined by fusing them with the retrieved expert activation profiles (the gating distributions from *v_n*). This is done through a weighted average: *g'(q_t) = λg(q_t) + (1-λ) (1/N) Σ_{n=1}^N v_n[t']*, where *λ* controls the strength of memory intervention and *v_n[t']* is the aligned timestep within the retrieved skill sequence. This fusion enables the model to synthesize novel behaviors by composing known primitives.

#### 3.3. Memory Update

To support lifelong learning and manage a finite-capacity memory bank (*M_max*), a dynamic memory update mechanism is implemented. When the memory capacity is reached, a prioritized pruning strategy discards low-utility information based on three criteria:
1. **Outdated Timestamps:** Memories with the oldest timestamps are removed.
2. **Unused Entries:** Memories that have not been successfully retrieved are prioritized for removal.
3. **Low Reliability:** Memories that consistently fail the reliability threshold *δ* during retrieval, indicating low quality or irrelevance, are discarded.
This strategy maintains a compact, relevant, and diverse memory bank conducive to effective skill composition.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of SkillMemo was conducted through ablation studies, simulated environment tests with Diffusion Policy (DP) and Vision-Language-Action (VLA) backbones, and real-world robot manipulation tasks.

#### 4.1. Ablation Studies

- **Component-wise Analysis:** Experiments demonstrated that incrementally adding the Expert-Guided Trajectory Segmentation (EGTS) module and the Skill-Level Memory Architecture (SLMA) module consistently improved task success rates. Integrating EGTS alone provided improvements over the vanilla backbone, while further incorporating SLMA yielded additional gains, particularly for tasks requiring compositional reasoning. For instance, on the LIBERO benchmark, integrating both components significantly improved performance over the UniAct backbone (e.g., from 68.7% to 73.4% on Goal tasks, 72.1% to 80.2% on Spatial tasks, and 75.7% to 79.8% on Object tasks). Similar trends were observed for the DP backbone on Push-T, BlockPush, and Kitchen tasks.
- **Impact of Expert Number:** Investigation into the number of experts (*N*) in the MoE module revealed a trade-off between accuracy and inference time. Increasing *N* generally improved success rates, but diminishing returns were observed beyond *N=5*. For example, increasing *N* from 5 to 10 for UniAct on the LIBERO-Goal benchmark improved success from 71.1% to 72.7%, while inference time substantially increased from 3.93s to 7.94s. Consequently, *N=5* was selected as the default configuration.
- **Expert Activation Analysis:** Visualizations of expert routing weights during task execution indicated that the gating coefficients dynamically shifted and exhibited pronounced peaks aligned with critical action phases (e.g., grasping a bowl, turning on a stove). This temporal correlation supported the claim that the MoE mechanism implicitly specializes individual experts in distinct, semantically meaningful motion primitives without explicit action labels.

#### 4.2. Simulated Evaluation

- **DP Backbone Benchmarks:** SkillMemo demonstrated consistent performance improvements over state-of-the-art Diffusion Policy baselines (DP, SDP, CP, IMLE Policy, STEP) across Push-T, UR3 Block Push, and Franka Kitchen environments. SkillMemo achieved the highest average success rate of 64.6%, outperforming the base DP by 3.4% and the IMLE Policy by 1.8%.
- **VLA Backbone Benchmarks:** On the LIBERO benchmark, SkillMemo consistently enhanced the performance of various VLA architectures (UniAct, UniVLA, and π0.5). Notably, when integrated with π0.5, SkillMemo achieved an average success rate of 98.0%, surpassing the base π0.5 by 1.2% (98.0% vs. 96.8%) and MemoryVLA by 1.5% (98.0% vs. 96.5%). This indicated that skill-level memory contributed to maximizing VLA performance.
- **Unseen Task Generalization:** A zero-shot cross-suite evaluation on LIBERO demonstrated strong compositional generalization. Models trained with SkillMemo on one subset (e.g., LIBERO-Goal) were evaluated on other unseen subsets (e.g., LIBERO-Spatial, LIBERO-Object). SkillMemo trained on LIBERO-Goal achieved a 75.6% success rate on the unseen Spatial suite, which exceeded the performance of a baseline model explicitly trained on the Spatial suite (72.1%). This result suggested that the memory bank facilitated compositional reuse of skill primitives for tasks outside the training distribution.

#### 4.3. Real-World Evaluation

- **Single-Task Performance:** Deployed on a UR5e robotic manipulator, SkillMemo consistently outperformed the base Diffusion Policy across five contact-rich manipulation tasks. For instance, SkillMemo improved the success rate on "Corn in Pot" by 7.5% (from 82.5% to 90.0%) and on "Butter in Pot" by 12.5% (from 62.5% to 75.0%).
- **Unseen Task Generalization:** In compositional real-world scenarios, where models were trained on a limited subset of object-receptacle pairings and tested on novel combinations, SkillMemo significantly outperformed DP. For example, for the unseen task "Strawberry on Plate," SkillMemo achieved a 75.0% success rate compared to DP's 65.0%. This result further supported the framework's ability to retrieve and fuse atomic skills from diverse training experiences to generalize to unseen task configurations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The SkillMemo framework represents an advancement in addressing the limitations of existing visuomotor models in robotic manipulation, particularly concerning data scarcity and compositional generalization. By introducing a mechanism for the implicit decomposition, structured storage, and dynamic retrieval of atomic skill primitives, the research provides a pathway for embodied agents to acquire and leverage experiential knowledge more effectively.

The significance of this work is demonstrated through several aspects:
- **Enhanced Generalization:** The framework consistently improves performance across diverse backbone architectures (Diffusion Policy and Vision-Language-Action models) on both simulation benchmarks and real-world robotic tasks. Crucially, its ability to generalize to unseen task configurations, as evidenced by zero-shot cross-suite evaluations and novel object-receptacle combinations, suggests a step towards more adaptable and robust robotic systems. This capacity for compositional generalization can enable robots to perform tasks by recombining known skills, rather than requiring re-training for every novel permutation.
- **Structured Knowledge Representation:** SkillMemo moves beyond holistic memory approaches by focusing on skill-level features. The Expert-Guided Trajectory Segmentation using a Mixture-of-Experts architecture implicitly discovers and specializes in distinct atomic skills. This structured representation of behavioral knowledge allows for more precise and efficient reuse.
- **Data Efficiency Potential:** By enabling policies to synthesize new behaviors from a library of learned skills, SkillMemo could potentially mitigate the demand for vast, diverse demonstration datasets. This has practical implications for reducing the cost and effort associated with robot learning in various applications.
- **Advancement in Embodied AI:** The framework contributes to the development of more intelligent embodied agents capable of a form of experiential reasoning. By endowing robots with a dynamic, retrievable memory of how to perform fundamental actions, it moves closer to agents that can learn, adapt, and operate in complex, open-world environments with greater autonomy and flexibility.

The potential impact of SkillMemo is in facilitating the deployment of robots in real-world scenarios that demand compositional intelligence and adaptability. Applications could span manufacturing, service robotics, and assistive technologies, where robots need to handle novel object arrangements or execute tasks that are variations of previously learned ones, without extensive re-programming or re-demonstration.
