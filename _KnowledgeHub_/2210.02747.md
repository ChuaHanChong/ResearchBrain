# Flow Matching for Generative Modeling

> [!summary] Summary
> Flow Matching introduces a simulation-free method for scalably training Continuous Normalizing Flows (CNFs) by leveraging a conditional objective with identical gradients to the marginal objective. The approach achieves state-of-the-art performance on ImageNet, particularly with Optimal Transport paths, leading to faster training convergence and significantly more efficient sample generation through fewer function evaluations.

## Problem

- Continuous Normalizing Flows (CNFs) have historically been computationally prohibitive to train on high-dimensional data due to expensive numerical Ordinary Differential Equation (ODE) simulations.
- Diffusion models, while successful, are limited by their specific stochastic processes, often resulting in circuitous probability paths, long training times, and slow sampling.
- Existing simulation-free training methods for CNFs often suffer from intractable integrals or biased gradients, preventing their widespread adoption.

## Method

- **Flow Matching (FM)** directly regresses a neural network-parameterized ==vector field== to a predefined target vector field, circumventing the need for computationally intensive ==ODE simulations== during training.
- **Conditional Flow Matching (CFM)** defines the target ==probability path== and vector field in terms of conditional distributions, making the training objective tractable through sampling from conditional paths (its gradient is identical to the marginal FM objective).
- The framework is flexible, supporting various conditional paths, including a novel application of ==Optimal Transport (OT)== conditional paths which lead to efficient, straight-line generative trajectories.

## Results

- Flow Matching with Optimal Transport (FM-OT) paths achieved state-of-the-art results on ImageNet, with FID of **14.45** and NLL of **3.31 BPD** on ImageNet 64x64, outperforming diffusion-based baselines.
- FM-OT demonstrated significantly faster training convergence, reaching lower FID values in fewer epochs compared to baselines, and required substantially fewer Network Function Evaluations (NFE) for sampling (e.g., average **138 NFE** on ImageNet 64x64 vs. **264** for DDPM).
- FM-OT generated samples with straighter trajectories and superior numerical accuracy at low NFE, requiring roughly **60% fewer NFEs** to achieve the same error threshold as diffusion models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The gradients of the marginal Flow Matching objective and the tractable Conditional Flow Matching objective are identical, enabling efficient, simulation-free training of Continuous Normalizing Flows (CNFs).
> - Optimal Transport (OT) displacement interpolation provides a highly efficient and effective family of probability paths for generative modeling, leading to simpler vector fields and straight-line trajectories.
> - Flow Matching offers a generalized framework for training CNFs that is not restricted to specific stochastic processes like diffusion, allowing for exploration of diverse and efficient generative trajectories.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of deep generative models has seen explosive growth and remarkable advancements, particularly in image generation, with models like GANs, VAEs, and more recently, diffusion models achieving unprecedented realism and quality.

**Diffusion Models:** The recent influx of impressive generative capabilities, as seen in models like DALL-E 2 (Ramesh et al., 2022) and Stable Diffusion (Rombach et al., 2022), is largely attributable to the success of diffusion-based models (Ho et al., 2020; Song et al., 2020b). These models frame image generation as a gradual denoising process, exhibiting stable training dynamics and high sample quality. However, their reliance on simple diffusion processes imposes limitations:
 - **Confined Probability Paths:** The specific stochastic differential equations (SDEs) governing diffusion processes restrict the types of probability paths that can be modeled, often leading to circuitous routes between noise and data.
 - **Long Training Times:** The inherent nature of diffusion processes, particularly those that approximate a true noise distribution, often requires extensive training iterations.
 - **Slow Sampling:** While stable during training, generating samples from diffusion models typically involves many sequential steps (integrating an SDE or its corresponding ODE), leading to slow inference. Specialized sampling methods (e.g., Song et al., 2020a; Zhang & Chen, 2022) are often required to accelerate this process.

**Continuous Normalizing Flows (CNFs):** As introduced by Chen et al. (2018) via Neural ODEs, CNFs offer a general and deterministic framework for generative modeling. CNFs define a continuous-time transformation from a simple prior distribution (e.g., Gaussian noise) to a complex data distribution by learning a time-dependent vector field. They are theoretically powerful, capable of modeling arbitrary probability paths, and importantly, can encompass the probability paths generated by diffusion processes (Song et al., 2021). However, CNFs have historically faced significant scalability challenges:
 - **Expensive Training:** Traditional maximum likelihood training for CNFs (e.g., Grathwohl et al., 2018) requires computationally intensive numerical ODE simulations for both forward and backward propagation, making them impractical for high-dimensional data like images.
 - **Intractable or Biased Simulation-Free Methods:** While some prior works attempted simulation-free CNF training, they often involved intractable integrals (Rozen et al., 2021) or suffered from biased gradients in stochastic settings (Ben-Hamu et al., 2022).

**Flow Matching's Position:** This paper introduces Flow Matching (FM) as a paradigm shift that aims to combine the best aspects of both diffusion models and CNFs while overcoming their respective limitations. FM offers an efficient, simulation-free approach to training CNFs that is:
 - **Scalable:** It addresses the core scalability issue of CNF training, enabling their application to large image datasets.
 - **General:** Unlike diffusion models, FM is not restricted to specific stochastic processes. It can train CNFs with *arbitrary* desired probability paths, providing much greater flexibility.
 - **Robust:** The paper demonstrates that even when employing diffusion-like paths, FM provides a more stable and robust training alternative to traditional score matching methods used for diffusion models.
 - **Efficient:** By enabling more direct and efficient probability paths (like Optimal Transport paths), FM leads to faster training convergence and significantly faster and more reliable sample generation using off-the-shelf ODE solvers.

In essence, Flow Matching positions itself as a principled and practical alternative to diffusion models by leveraging the theoretical elegance of CNFs. It generalizes beyond the specific family of diffusion processes, opening up a broader space of generative trajectories that are empirically shown to be more efficient and performant. It effectively "breaks the barriers for scalable CNF training beyond diffusion," as stated by the authors, moving the field towards more flexible and computationally efficient continuous generative models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the "FlowMatching for Generative Modeling" paper can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enable Scalable Training of Continuous Normalizing Flows (CNFs):** The core motivation is to overcome the long-standing challenge of training CNFs at scale, particularly for high-dimensional data like images. Previous CNF training methods, especially maximum likelihood estimation, involved expensive numerical ODE simulations, which made them computationally prohibitive compared to other generative models. The authors aim to introduce a "simulation-free" approach to address this bottleneck.

2. **Generalize Beyond Diffusion Probability Paths:** While diffusion models have achieved remarkable success, their underlying stochastic processes (and thus the probability paths they model) are somewhat confined. The authors are motivated to explore more general and potentially more efficient probability paths within the CNF framework. Specifically, they want to move beyond the implicit definition of paths through stochastic differential equations (SDEs) and directly define and learn vector fields that generate desired distributions.

3. **Provide a More Robust and Stable Alternative for Diffusion Model Training:** Even for the specific case of diffusion paths, the authors aim to show that Flow Matching offers a superior training methodology compared to existing score matching techniques. This suggests a motivation to improve the training dynamics and final performance of models operating on diffusion-like principles.

4. **Introduce and Leverage Optimal Transport (OT) Probability Paths:** A significant motivation is to demonstrate the practical utility of defining CNF training with non-diffusion probability paths. The paper specifically highlights Optimal Transport displacement interpolation as a promising candidate. The hypothesis is that these paths, characterized by straight-line trajectories and potentially simpler dynamics, will lead to more efficient training and faster, higher-quality sample generation.

5. **Improve Computational Efficiency for Both Training and Sampling:** A critical practical goal is to reduce the computational cost associated with deep generative models. This includes accelerating the training process (requiring fewer iterations to converge or achieve better performance) and significantly speeding up the inference/sampling process (requiring fewer function evaluations from the learned model).

In essence, the paper is motivated by the desire to unlock the full potential of CNFs as highly flexible and deterministic generative models by making them computationally tractable and by exploring a broader, more efficient family of generative processes than currently offered by conventional diffusion models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Flow Matching (FM) framework introduces a novel, simulation-free approach for training Continuous Normalizing Flows (CNFs) by directly regressing a neural network-parameterized vector field to a predefined target vector field.

#### 3.1. Continuous Normalizing Flows (CNFs)

CNFs model a time-dependent vector field, $v_t(x;\theta)$, parameterized by a neural network with parameters $\theta$. This vector field defines an Ordinary Differential Equation (ODE):
$$ \frac{d}{dt} \phi_t(x) = v_t(\phi_t(x)) $$
$$ \phi_0(x) = x $$
This ODE describes a "flow" $\phi_t$ that transforms a simple prior distribution $p_0$ (e.g., standard Gaussian noise) into a complex target distribution $p_1$ (approximating the data distribution $q$) via the push-forward operation $p_t = [\phi_t]_* p_0$. The key challenge is learning $v_t$ such that $\phi_1$ effectively maps $p_0$ to $p_1$.

#### 3.2. Flow Matching (FM) Objective

The core idea of Flow Matching is to directly match the learned vector field $v_t(x)$ to a pre-defined target vector field $u_t(x)$ that is known to generate the desired probability path $p_t(x)$. The Flow Matching (FM) objective is defined as a mean squared error:
$$ L_{\text{FM}}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{t, p_t(x)} \|v_t(x) - u_t(x)\|^2 $$
where $t \sim U$ and $x \sim p_t(x)$.
The primary hurdle for this objective is its intractability: neither the target probability path $p_t(x)$ nor its corresponding vector field $u_t(x)$ are typically known in closed form or easily sampled from, especially since $p_t(x)$ depends on the unknown data distribution $q(x_1)$.

#### 3.3. Conditional Flow Matching (CFM)

To address the intractability of the FM objective, the authors introduce a crucial innovation: **Conditional Flow Matching (CFM)**.
The key insight is to define the target probability path $p_t(x)$ and vector field $u_t(x)$ in terms of *conditional* distributions and vector fields.
1. **Conditional Path Construction:** For each data sample $x_1 \sim q(x_1)$, a *conditional probability path* $p_t(x|x_1)$ is defined such that $p_0(x|x_1)$ is a simple noise distribution (e.g., $N(x|0,I)$) and $p_1(x|x_1)$ is concentrated around $x_1$ (e.g., $N(x|x_1, \sigma_{\min}^2 I)$).
2. **Marginalization:** The *marginal probability path* $p_t(x)$ is then defined by marginalizing over the data distribution: $p_t(x) = \int p_t(x|x_1)q(x_1)dx_1$. This ensures that at $t=1$, $p_1(x)$ approximates the data distribution $q(x_1)$.
3. **Marginal Vector Field:** Similarly, a *marginal vector field* $u_t(x)$ is defined as a weighted average of *conditional vector fields* $u_t(x|x_1)$, which generate $p_t(x|x_1)$: $u_t(x) = \int u_t(x|x_1) \frac{p_t(x|x_1)q(x_1)}{p_t(x)} dx_1$.
 - **Theorem 1 (Key Observation 1):** The authors prove that this marginal vector field $u_t(x)$ indeed generates the marginal probability path $p_t(x)$.
4. **CFM Objective Equivalence:** Despite the marginal forms still being intractable, the **Conditional Flow Matching (CFM) objective** is proposed:
 $$ L_{\text{CFM}}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{t, q(x_1), p_t(x|x_1)} \|v_t(x) - u_t(x|x_1)\|^2 $$
 This objective is tractable because it only requires sampling $t$, a data point $x_1$, and then a point $x$ from the *conditional* path $p_t(x|x_1)$, for which $u_t(x|x_1)$ can be defined in closed form.
 - **Theorem 2 (Key Observation 2):** Critically, the authors prove that $L_{\text{CFM}}$ and $L_{\text{FM}}$ have *identical gradients* with respect to $\theta$. This means optimizing the tractable CFM objective effectively trains the CNF to match the true (intractable) marginal probability path.

#### 3.4. Conditional Probability Paths and Vector Fields

The CFM objective is flexible with the choice of conditional paths. The paper focuses on a general family of Gaussian conditional probability paths:
$$ p_t(x|x_1) = N(x|\mu_t(x_1), \sigma_t(x_1)^2 I) $$
where $\mu_t(x_1)$ and $\sigma_t(x_1)$ are time-dependent mean and standard deviation functions, with boundary conditions $\mu_0=0, \sigma_0=1$ (noise) and $\mu_1=x_1, \sigma_1=\sigma_{\min}$ (data).
- **Theorem 3:** The authors derive the unique vector field $u_t(x|x_1)$ that generates this Gaussian conditional path:
 $$ u_t(x|x_1) = \frac{\sigma_t'(x_1)}{\sigma_t(x_1)}(x - \mu_t(x_1)) + \mu_t'(x_1) $$
 where prime denotes derivative with respect to time.

#### 3.5. Special Instances of Gaussian Conditional Paths

The flexibility of the framework allows defining different types of conditional paths:

##### 3.5.1. Diffusion Conditional Vector Fields

The authors show that existing diffusion processes (like Variance Exploding (VE) and Variance Preserving (VP) diffusion) can be cast as specific instances of this Gaussian conditional path family. By plugging in the $\mu_t(x_1)$ and $\sigma_t(x_1)$ functions corresponding to these diffusion types, the derived $u_t(x|x_1)$ precisely matches the vector fields used in deterministic probability flows for diffusion models. This demonstrates that FM provides a more robust and stable alternative for training models that *are* diffusion-like, even on their native paths, compared to traditional score matching.

##### 3.5.2. Optimal Transport (OT) Conditional Vector Fields

A novel and particularly interesting choice introduced by this work is the Optimal Transport (OT) conditional path. This path is defined by a simple linear interpolation of the mean and standard deviation:
$$ \mu_t(x) = tx_1 $$
$$ \sigma_t(x) = 1 - (1-\sigma_{\min})t $$
This leads to a significantly simpler conditional vector field $u_t(x|x_1) = \frac{x_1 - (1-\sigma_{\min})x}{1-(1-\sigma_{\min})t}$. This path is optimal in the sense that the corresponding conditional flow $\psi_t(x) = (1-(1-\sigma_{\min})t)x + tx_1$ is an Optimal Transport displacement map between two Gaussians. Intuitively, particles following this flow move in straight line trajectories with constant speed, unlike the curved and often "overshooting" paths of diffusion processes. This simplicity is hypothesized to translate to faster training, faster generation, and better generalization.

In summary, the methodology pivots on the Conditional Flow Matching objective, which leverages the mathematical equivalence of gradients between marginal and conditional forms. This allows for efficient, simulation-free training of CNFs while providing the unprecedented flexibility to define and train on diverse probability paths, with Optimal Transport paths highlighted as a highly efficient and performant novel choice.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments validate Flow Matching's efficacy, particularly with Optimal Transport (OT) paths, across various image generation tasks and datasets.

#### 4.1. Density Modeling and Sample Quality on ImageNet

- **Superior Performance:** On CIFAR-10 and ImageNet (32x32, 64x64, 128x128), Flow Matching with OT paths (FM-OT) consistently achieved the best results across all quantitative measures compared to competing diffusion-based methods (DDPM, Score Matching (SM), Score Flow (SF)).
 - **Negative Log-Likelihood (NLL):** FM-OT yielded the lowest NLL (in Bits Per Dimension, BPD), indicating a better density estimation of the underlying data distribution. For example, on ImageNet 64x64, FM-OT achieved 3.31 BPD compared to DDPM's 3.32, SM's 3.40, and SF's 3.36.
 - **Sample Quality (FID):** FM-OT also produced the lowest Frechet Inception Distance (FID), signifying higher quality and diversity of generated samples. On ImageNet 64x64, FM-OT achieved an FID of 14.45, outperforming DDPM (17.36), SM (19.74), and SF (24.95).
- **State-of-the-Art on ImageNet-128:** For unconditional ImageNet 128x128 generation, FM-OT achieved an FID of 20.9, which is competitive with or superior to many prior GAN-based and diffusion-based models (e.g., BigGAN (Uncond.) at 25.3, PGMGAN at 21.7).
- **Faster Training Convergence:** FM, especially with OT paths, demonstrated significantly faster convergence during training. Image quality (FID) improved more rapidly with FM-OT compared to baselines (Figure 5), reaching lower FID values in fewer epochs/iterations. For instance, ImageNet-128 training used 500k iterations for FM compared to 4.36m for Dhariwal & Nichol (2021), with less image throughput. The sampling cost (NFE) also remained stable throughout training for FM, unlike score matching.

#### 4.2. Sampling Efficiency

- **Fewer Function Evaluations (NFE):** FM-OT models consistently required fewer Network Function Evaluations (NFE) during inference to generate samples, indicating higher computational efficiency. For example, on ImageNet 64x64, FM-OT had an average NFE of 138, compared to DDPM (264), SM (441), and SF (601).
- **Straight Line Trajectories:** Qualitatively, the generated sample paths for FM-OT models showed straighter trajectories and initiated image generation much earlier in the process compared to diffusion paths (Figure 6, Figure 4 left). Diffusion paths tend to remove noise visibly only towards the end of the path, sometimes with "overshoots" and "backtracking," which is avoided by the direct nature of OT paths.
- **Improved Numerical Accuracy and Sample Quality at Low NFE:** When using fixed-step ODE solvers, FM-OT models produced better numerical error (lower per-pixel MSE compared to high-NFE solutions) and better FID scores at very low NFE values ($\le 100$). This indicates a superior trade-off between computational cost and sample quality, requiring roughly 60% fewer NFEs to achieve the same error threshold as diffusion models.

#### 4.3. Conditional Sampling from Low-Resolution Images

- **Image Super-Resolution:** The paper demonstrated Flow Matching's applicability to conditional generation tasks, specifically upsampling images from 64x64 to 256x256.
- **Competitive Results:** FM-OT achieved an FID of 3.4 for super-resolution, significantly outperforming a regression baseline (15.21) and substantially improving upon SR3 (Saharia et al., 2022) which achieved 5.2. While PSNR and SSIM were similar to SR3, the better FID and IS (200.8 vs 80.1 for SR3) suggest superior overall generation quality, aligning with the argument that FID/IS are better indicators of generation quality.

In summary, Flow Matching, particularly with Optimal Transport probability paths, is shown to be a highly effective and efficient generative modeling paradigm. It delivers state-of-the-art performance in both likelihood and sample quality, while also offering faster training convergence and significantly more efficient sample generation, directly addressing key limitations of existing generative models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Flow Matching (FM) marks a significant advancement in the field of deep generative modeling, with several far-reaching implications and potential impacts:

1. **Unlocking Scalable CNF Training:** For years, Continuous Normalizing Flows (CNFs) have been theoretically appealing due to their exact likelihood computation and deterministic generation process, but their practical application was severely limited by the computational cost of training (due to expensive ODE simulations). Flow Matching, by providing an efficient, simulation-free training framework with unbiased gradients, effectively removes this barrier. This is a monumental step that enables CNFs to compete with and even surpass state-of-the-art diffusion models on complex, high-dimensional data like images, as demonstrated on ImageNet.

2. **Generalization Beyond Diffusion Processes:** A core contribution of FM is its ability to train CNFs with arbitrary, user-defined probability paths, moving beyond the strict constraints of diffusion processes. This flexibility is critical. While diffusion models have been highly successful, they rely on a specific family of stochastic processes. Flow Matching opens the door to exploring a much richer and potentially more efficient space of generative trajectories.

3. **Introduction of Optimal Transport Paths:** The successful application of Optimal Transport (OT) displacement interpolation as a concrete, highly performant probability path is a major highlight. OT paths' inherent "straight-line" trajectories lead to simpler vector fields that are easier for neural networks to learn and enable more efficient and faster sample generation. This demonstrates the power of directly designing probability paths based on mathematical principles (like optimal transport) rather than being constrained by the properties of stochastic processes. This could inspire the discovery and utilization of other novel and efficient probability paths in the future.

4. **Improved Efficiency in Training and Sampling:**
 - **Faster Training Convergence:** The empirical evidence shows that FM models converge faster than diffusion models, requiring fewer training iterations to achieve superior performance. This translates directly to reduced computational resources and energy consumption, addressing growing concerns about the environmental impact of large AI models.
 - **Faster and More Reliable Sampling:** FM, especially with OT paths, allows for significantly faster inference times (lower NFE) while maintaining high sample quality. The ability to use off-the-shelf numerical ODE solvers effectively further simplifies implementation and broadens accessibility. The straight-line trajectories and more uniform noise reduction lead to more robust and predictable sampling, reducing issues like "overshooting" seen in some diffusion trajectories.

5. **A Unifying Framework:** Flow Matching offers an alternative, and often superior, way to train models that resemble diffusion processes while also generalizing beyond them. This provides a more unified and principled approach to continuous-time generative modeling, potentially simplifying the landscape of research in this area.

6. **Broader Applicability:** While demonstrated on image generation and super-resolution, the fundamental principles of Flow Matching are general. Its ability to efficiently learn complex probability transformations could extend to other data modalities (e.g., audio, video, 3D data, time series) and tasks (e.g., inverse problems, scientific simulations, drug discovery) where modeling high-dimensional data distributions is crucial.

In conclusion, Flow Matching represents a significant leap forward for CNFs, establishing them as a highly competitive and versatile class of generative models. By marrying theoretical elegance with practical efficiency, it not only pushes the boundaries of generative AI but also paves the way for more energy-efficient and flexible approaches to modeling complex data distributions.
