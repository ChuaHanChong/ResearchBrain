# Lift3D Foundation Policy: Lifting 2D Large-Scale Pretrained Models for Robust 3D Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Lift3D, developed by researchers from Peking University, BAAI, and CUHK, introduces a two-stage framework that effectively lifts large-scale pretrained 2D foundation models to achieve robust 3D robotic manipulation. The framework significantly outperforms prior state-of-the-art methods in simulation, improving success rates by up to 21.3% on benchmarks like Adroit, and demonstrates high success rates in real-world tasks with limited demonstration data.

## Problem

- Traditional vision-based robotic manipulation policies, often 2D-centric, struggle with comprehensive understanding of 3D spatial relationships and complex structures.
- Direct 3D encoding methods for robotics face challenges due to the scarcity of large-scale 3D robotic datasets and foundational models, along with high computational costs.
- Existing modality transformation approaches (e.g., lifting 2D to 3D or projecting 3D to 2D) incur a loss of critical spatial information, hindering precise 3D manipulation.

## Method

- A two-stage training process enhances transformer-based 2D foundation models (e.g., DINOV2, CLIP) to achieve robust 3D manipulation capabilities.
- Stage 1 develops implicit 3D robotic representation using a task-aware masked autoencoder (MAE) that reconstructs depth information for task-relevant 'affordance regions' and utilizes distillation to preserve pretrained 2D knowledge.
- Stage 2 constructs explicit 3D robotic representation via a '2D Model-lifting Strategy' that tokenizes 3D point clouds and projects their 3D coordinates onto virtual planes to leverage the 2D foundation model's pretrained positional embeddings, enabling direct 3D input for imitation learning.

## Results

- Lift3D (DINOV2) achieved an **84.5%** average success rate on MetaWorld, an **18.2%** improvement over previous state-of-the-art (SOTA) DP3, and Lift3D (CLIP) reached **88.0%** on Adroit, a **21.3%** improvement over DP3.
- In real-world experiments on a Franka arm, Lift3D demonstrated strong performance with only 30 episodes of demonstration data per task, achieving **90%** success for 'place bottle at rack' and **85%** for 'pour water'.
- Ablation studies confirmed that depth reconstruction, affordance-guided masking, and leveraging pretrained 2D positional embeddings are crucial for performance, with the model scaling effectively with larger 2D foundation models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large-scale pretrained 2D foundation models can be systematically enhanced to acquire robust 3D robotic manipulation capabilities without extensive 3D datasets.
> - Implicit 3D understanding can be instilled in 2D models by training a task-aware masked autoencoder to reconstruct depth information in task-relevant regions.
> - Pretrained 2D positional embeddings can be effectively repurposed through a '2D Model-lifting Strategy' to enable 2D vision transformers to directly process and encode 3D point cloud data for manipulation policies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation heavily relies on a robot's ability to perceive and interact with its 3D environment. Traditionally, vision-based manipulation policies have often relied on 2D image inputs to predict 3D end-effector poses, typically through reinforcement or imitation learning. While effective for simpler tasks, these 2D-centric approaches inherently struggle with a comprehensive understanding of spatial relationships and intricate 3D structures in the physical world.

Recent research has increasingly shifted towards explicit extraction of 3D feature representations, broadly categorized into two main streams:
1. **Direct 3D Encoding:** Methods that directly encode 3D data, such as point clouds (e.g., using PointNet++, PointNext). While offering direct 3D perception, these approaches are often hampered by the scarcity of large-scale robotic 3D datasets and foundational models specifically designed for 3D. Furthermore, processing raw 3D or voxelized features is computationally intensive, limiting scalability and real-world practicality.
2. **Modality Transformation:** Approaches that transform data modalities, such as lifting pretrained 2D features into 3D space or projecting 3D point clouds into multi-view 2D images for input into powerful 2D pretrained models (e.g., CLIP, DINOV2). While these methods leverage the vast knowledge embedded in large-scale 2D foundation models, they inevitably incur a loss of spatial information during the transformation process, thereby hindering the robot's ability to fully grasp complex 3D relationships.

Lift3D enters this landscape by addressing the fundamental question: "Can we develop a 3D policy model that integrates large-scale pretrained knowledge while incorporating complete 3D spatial data input?" It positions itself as a novel framework that bridges the gap between the rich representations learned by 2D foundation models and the critical need for 3D geometric understanding in robotic manipulation. Unlike existing methods, Lift3D proposes a progressive enhancement of 2D foundation models, first implicitly and then explicitly, to achieve robust 3D manipulation capabilities without sacrificing spatial information during the crucial imitation learning phase. This distinct two-stage approach aims to harness the best of both worlds: the generalization power of 2D vision transformers and the precise spatial awareness provided by 3D data.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind Lift3D stems from the critical need for robots to deeply understand 3D geometric information for performing complex manipulation tasks. This understanding involves perceiving the 3D environment, reasoning about spatial relationships, and interacting with intricate spatial configurations. The authors identify key limitations in existing approaches that motivate their proposed framework:
1. **Lack of Large-Scale 3D Robotic Data and Foundational Models:** While 2D foundation models benefit from massive datasets, comparable resources for 3D robotic data and robust 3D foundational models are scarce. This limits the generalization capabilities of 3D-native approaches.
2. **High Computational Costs of 3D Processing:** Direct processing of 3D features (e.g., voxels or raw point clouds) often incurs significant computational overhead, posing challenges for scalability and real-time robotic applications.
3. **Spatial Information Loss During Modality Transformation:** Methods that convert 3D data to 2D views or lift 2D features to 3D invariably lose some critical spatial detail, compromising the robot's ability to fully grasp 3D relationships.

Given these challenges, the overarching objective of the Lift3D framework is to develop a 3D policy model that effectively integrates the vast pretrained knowledge of large-scale 2D foundation models (like DINOV2 or CLIP) with comprehensive 3D spatial data input, while minimizing the aforementioned spatial information loss and computational burden.

More specifically, the key objectives are:
- **Enhance Implicit 3D Robotic Representation:** To imbue 2D foundation models with an inherent understanding of 3D spatial properties, even when operating on 2D images. This is achieved by focusing the model's attention on task-relevant affordance regions and training it to reconstruct depth information.
- **Construct Explicit 3D Robotic Representation:** To enable 2D foundation models to directly process and encode 3D point cloud data. This aims to leverage the pretrained knowledge of the 2D model's positional embeddings to interpret 3D spatial relationships without relying on cumbersome modality transformations during the manipulation policy learning.
- **Achieve Robust 3D Manipulation Policies:** To develop a policy that consistently outperforms state-of-the-art methods across diverse simulation and real-world manipulation tasks, demonstrating strong generalization capabilities to novel objects, scenes, and conditions.

By pursuing these objectives, Lift3D aims to deliver a robust, efficient, and generalizable solution for vision-based robotic manipulation in complex 3D environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Lift3D framework proposes a two-stage training process designed to progressively enhance transformer-based 2D foundation models (such as DINOV2 and CLIP) with robust 3D manipulation capabilities. It strategically improves both implicit and explicit 3D robotic representations.

**Stage 1: Implicit 3D Robotic Representation through Task-aware Masked Autoencoder (MAE)**
This stage aims to instill 3D spatial awareness into the 2D foundation model by leveraging large-scale unlabeled datasets.
- **Data Preparation:** The training dataset for this stage comprises approximately 1 million paired image, depth, and text description samples, sourced from large-scale robotic manipulation datasets like Open X-Embodiment.
- **Task-aware Masking Strategy:** Unlike conventional MAEs that randomly mask image patches, Lift3D employs a "task-aware" masking approach. It uses a multimodal model (e.g., CLIP) to generate image attention maps based on task-specific text descriptions (e.g., "Robot arm take the red bowl..."). These attention maps are then back-projected onto the 2D input image to guide the masking process, prioritizing task-relevant "affordance regions." A threshold is applied to attention values to distinguish foreground (affordance) from background tokens. Random background tokens are also masked to reach a high masking ratio (e.g., 75%), consistent with standard MAE practices.
- **Depth Reconstruction Target:** Instead of reconstructing low-level RGB information, the MAE is trained to reconstruct depth information for the masked tokens (both affordance and randomly masked background patches). This direct reconstruction of 3D geometric information is crucial for enhancing the 2D foundation model's implicit understanding of the 3D environment.
- **Distillation Loss:** To preserve the powerful inherent capabilities of the pretrained 2D foundation model and mitigate catastrophic forgetting, a distillation loss is introduced. This loss constrains the output features of the visible tokens from the fine-tuned model to remain similar to those generated by the original, off-the-shelf pretrained model.
- **Training:** During this self-supervised fine-tuning stage, an injected adapter (LoRA) and a dedicated decoder are trained using a combined reconstruction (L1 loss on depth) and distillation (L1 loss on features) objective.

**Stage 2: Explicit 3D Robotic Representation through 2D Model-lifting Strategy**
After the 2D foundation model has gained implicit 3D awareness, this stage enables it to directly process and comprehend 3D point cloud data for imitation learning. The core challenge is to leverage the 2D model's powerful pretrained positional embeddings (PEs) for 3D data without introducing semantic discrepancies or losing pretrained knowledge.
- **3D Tokenization:** Raw input point clouds (N points with 6 features: XYZ, RGB) are first transformed into a set of high-dimensional 3D tokens (e.g., 128 tokens, each 768-dimensional) using a lightweight 3D tokenizer. This tokenizer employs farthest point sampling for downsampling, k-Nearest Neighbor for local aggregation, and learnable linear layers for feature encoding. Each 3D token also retains its 3D coordinate.
- **Positional Mapping to 2D PEs:** The key innovation lies here. Instead of creating new 3D PEs, the 3D coordinates of each 3D token are projected onto multiple virtual planes (e.g., 6 faces of a cube). This establishes a positional correspondence between each 3D token and the pretrained 2D positional embeddings of the virtual planes.
- **Unified 3D Positional Indicator:** For each 3D token, the corresponding 2D PEs from all virtual planes are averaged to create a unified 3D positional indicator (PE_3D). This allows the 2D foundation model to effectively "understand" the 3D position of each token using its existing 2D positional knowledge, providing diverse positional relations.
- **Input to 2D Foundation Model:** The generated 3D tokens are then combined with these derived 3D PEs and fed into the (LoRA-adapted) 2D foundation model. This enables the model to encode 3D point cloud data using its large-scale pretrained knowledge without explicit modality transformations during the imitation learning inference.
- **Policy Head:** A simple three-layer Multilayer Perceptron (MLP) acts as the policy head, taking the output features from the 2D foundation model and predicting the 7-DoF end-effector pose (3-DoF translation, 3-DoF rotation, 1-DoF gripper state).
- **Training:** During this stage, the parameters of the 2D foundation model are frozen (except for the injected adapter), and only the 3D tokenizer, injected adapter, and policy head are updated.
- **Supervision Loss (L_explicit):** A combined loss function is used, including Mean Squared Error (MSE) for translation, cosine similarity for rotation, and Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE) for the gripper state.

This progressive, two-stage approach allows Lift3D to leverage the strengths of large-scale 2D foundation models while explicitly addressing the unique requirements of 3D robotic perception and manipulation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

Lift3D demonstrates compelling performance across extensive evaluations in both simulation and real-world scenarios, consistently outperforming existing state-of-the-art methods.

**Simulation Experiments:**
The framework was rigorously tested on over 30 tasks across three widely-used manipulation simulation benchmarks: MetaWorld, Adroit (MuJoCo), and RLBench (CoppeliaSim). The visual inputs included 224x224 RGB images and single-view point clouds (1,024 points). Baselines included 2D robotic representation methods (CLIP, R3M, VC-1), 3D representation methods (PointNet, PointNet++, PointNeXt, SPA), and 3D policy methods (DP3, RVT-2).
- **MetaWorld & Adroit:** Lift3D (using DINOV2 or CLIP as backbone) significantly surpassed all baselines. For MetaWorld, Lift3D (DINOV2) achieved an average success rate of 84.5%, representing an 18.2% improvement over the previous SOTA 3D policy (DP3, 65.3%). Similar gains were observed on the Adroit benchmark, with Lift3D (CLIP) reaching an 88.0% mean success rate, a 21.3% improvement over DP3. This highlights Lift3D's robust manipulation capabilities and deeper understanding of 3D spatial awareness, even for complex dexterous hand tasks.
- **RLBench:** Lift3D consistently outperformed 2D and 3D representation methods. Notably, even with single-view point cloud input, Lift3D achieved a 72.6% average success rate, surpassing RVT-2 with single-view input (65.3%) and showing comparable results to RVT-2 with four-view input (67.3%). This demonstrates the practical efficacy of Lift3D with minimal sensory input.

**Real-World Experiments:**
Evaluations were conducted on a Franka Research 3 arm with a UMI gripper across ten diverse manipulation tasks. With only 30 episodes of demonstration data per task, Lift3D achieved impressive success rates.
- **Performance:** For tasks like "place bottle at rack," Lift3D achieved a 90% success rate, indicating accurate 3D position and rotation prediction. For "pour water," it achieved 85%, showcasing its ability to handle complex action sequences and precise gripper rotations. Even for challenging tasks involving deformable objects like "wipe table," Lift3D maintained a 40% success rate, outperforming other methods.
- **Qualitative Results:** Visualizations confirmed Lift3D's ability to accurately predict continuous 7-DoF end-effector poses, enabling smooth and successful task completion trajectories.

**Ablation Studies:**
Detailed ablation studies confirmed the efficacy of Lift3D's core components:
- **Task-aware MAE:** Depth reconstruction proved crucial for performance, outperforming RGB reconstruction. The affordance-guided masking strategy significantly improved success rates compared to random masking, demonstrating the benefit of focusing on task-relevant regions. Visual token distillation was essential to prevent catastrophic forgetting and retain pretrained knowledge.
- **2D Model-lifting Strategy:** Explicit point cloud encoding via the lifting strategy led to substantial performance gains compared to image input, underscoring the importance of 3D spatial information. The implicit 3D representation learned in Stage 1 synergistically facilitated subsequent explicit 3D imitation learning. Critically, leveraging the *pretrained* 2D positional embeddings was found to be superior to using newly injected learnable PEs, validating the strategy's effectiveness in utilizing large-scale 2D pretrained knowledge.
- **Virtual Planes & Parameters:** Using six virtual planes (cube projection) provided the best performance for positional mapping. The LoRA parameter update strategy proved efficient and effective, achieving strong results with minimal updated parameters (1.01M, ~1% of total model). A 3-layer 3D tokenizer struck the optimal balance between accuracy and computational efficiency.

**Generalization Capabilities:**
Lift3D exhibited strong real-world generalization across three distinct test scenarios:
- **Different manipulated instances:** Smallest accuracy loss, attributed to the semantic understanding of pretrained 2D foundation models.
- **Complex background scenes:** Maintained a manipulation success rate above 50%, the smallest drop among methods, due to effective use of pretrained knowledge and affordance-guided masking.
- **Different lighting conditions:** Only an average accuracy drop of 20%, demonstrating robust 3D robotic representation despite lighting variations affecting both RGB and depth data.

**Model Scalability:**
As observed in computer vision, Lift3D's performance improved with larger 2D foundation models. Replacing DINOV2-ViT-base (86M parameters) with ViT-large (304M) and ViT-giant (1B) significantly increased success rates (from 28% to 48% and 58% respectively on a very hard MetaWorld task) and accelerated convergence, validating the scalability of the Lift3D policy.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Lift3D represents a significant advancement in the field of robotic manipulation, particularly at the intersection of large-scale foundation models and 3D perception. Its core contribution lies in successfully bridging the gap between powerful 2D vision models and the critical need for comprehensive 3D understanding in physical world interactions.

The framework's primary significance stems from several aspects:
- **Effective Integration of 2D and 3D Knowledge:** Lift3D provides a novel and effective mechanism to leverage the vast, generalizable knowledge embedded in large-scale 2D foundation models for 3D robotic tasks. By systematically enhancing both implicit and explicit 3D representations, it enables robots to "see" and "understand" the 3D world through the lens of models trained on unprecedented amounts of 2D visual data.
- **Mitigation of Data Scarcity and Computational Costs:** By building upon existing 2D foundation models and employing efficient 3D tokenization and positional mapping strategies, Lift3D significantly reduces the reliance on large-scale, costly-to-acquire 3D robotic datasets. Furthermore, it minimizes the computational overhead typically associated with training entirely new 3D models or performing complex modality transformations during real-time policy execution.
- **Robustness and Generalization:** The extensive experimental results, especially in real-world settings with limited demonstrations and under challenging generalization scenarios (novel objects, backgrounds, lighting), highlight Lift3D's remarkable robustness. This strong generalization ability is crucial for deploying robots in unstructured and dynamic environments.
- **Modularity and Scalability:** The modular design of Lift3D allows for easy adaptation to different 2D foundation models and policy heads. Its demonstrated scalability with larger foundation models suggests that future advancements in 2D vision models will directly translate to more capable 3D robotic manipulation policies.

The potential impact of Lift3D is substantial:
- **Advancement in Embodied AI:** It pushes the frontier of embodied AI by equipping robots with a more profound understanding of their physical surroundings, paving the way for more autonomous and intelligent robotic agents.
- **Democratization of Robot Learning:** By reducing the data requirements and computational burden for training effective 3D policies, Lift3D could make advanced robotic manipulation more accessible to researchers and developers without access to massive, specialized 3D datasets and computational resources.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The paper identifies a clear path for future work, notably the integration of Lift3D with language models to create more sophisticated 3D Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. This would enable robots to understand and execute commands given in natural language, leading to more intuitive human-robot interaction. Further improvements in handling sparse point clouds also represent a promising avenue.

In conclusion, Lift3D offers a compelling solution to a long-standing challenge in robotics, effectively bridging the gap between the power of 2D foundation models and the indispensable need for 3D spatial intelligence, thus making a significant contribution towards the development of truly robust and adaptable robotic manipulators.
