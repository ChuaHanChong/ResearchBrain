# Learning Force-Regulated Manipulation with a Low-Cost Tactile-Force-Controlled Gripper

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of Virginia and Columbia University developed a low-cost, force-controlled gripper (TF-Gripper) with integrated tactile sensing and a novel policy framework, RETAF. This framework enables robots to learn reactive force-regulated manipulation, achieving an average stable grasp rate of 68% and a task success rate of 60% across five force-sensitive tasks, outperforming existing position-controlled and non-reactive methods.

## Problem

- Manipulating fragile, soft, or deformable objects is challenging for robots, as it requires precise force regulation that traditional position-controlled grippers cannot robustly provide.
- Commercial force-controlled grippers are often expensive, have high minimum force thresholds, and lack broad compatibility, limiting research into delicate object interaction.
- Learning effective force-regulated policies is difficult due to the frequency mismatch between slow policy updates and fast contact dynamics, and the challenge of stably integrating sparse tactile signals with dense visual observations.

## Method

- Developed a low-cost ($150) TF-Gripper with integrated piezo-resistive tactile sensors and a precise force control range (0.45-45 N) suitable for delicate objects.
- Designed a low-cost teleoperation device that directly records human-applied grasping forces, enabling the collection of high-fidelity ground-truth force data for training.
- Introduced RETAF, a policy framework that decouples manipulation into a low-frequency Base Policy (arm pose, gripper open/close) and a high-frequency (80 Hz+) Force Adaptation Policy, which uses wrist-view vision and tactile input for reactive force regulation during contact.

## Results

- Direct force control consistently led to higher stable grasp rates across all evaluated policies, with RETAF achieving an average stable grasp rate of 68% compared to 38% for the Diffusion Policy baseline.
- RETAF outperformed non-reactive baselines, achieving an average task success rate of 60% on five force-sensitive tasks, whereas Diffusion Policy with tactile achieved 20%, and ViTac-MAE achieved 30%.
- RETAF demonstrated versatility by consistently improving reach, stable grasp, and task success rates when integrated with different base policies (e.g., increasing stable grasp rate from 30% to 90% for cherry tomato picking with a VLA model base).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Direct force control significantly improves grasp stability and overall task performance for force-sensitive objects compared to traditional position control.
> - Decoupling high-frequency reactive force adaptation from lower-frequency arm pose prediction effectively addresses the frequency mismatch problem and enables stable learning of force-regulated policies.
> - Effective integration of tactile sensing for reactive adjustments is crucial for precise force-regulated manipulation, enhancing a policy's ability to adapt to contact dynamics.
