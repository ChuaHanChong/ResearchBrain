# Moto: Latent Motion Token as the Bridging Language for Learning Robot Manipulation from Videos

> [!summary] Summary
> Moto presents a framework for robot manipulation learning that effectively utilizes large-scale, unlabeled video data by introducing a novel concept of latent motion tokens as a bridging language. This approach enables unsupervised generative pre-training of a robot policy, which is then fine-tuned for precise control, demonstrating superior data efficiency, robustness in real-world tasks, and competitive performance against larger models.

## Problem

- Robot manipulation faces a significant challenge with data scarcity, particularly for action-labeled data, which is expensive and laborious to acquire.
- Traditional methods for robot learning often rely on extensive human demonstrations or trial-and-error in reinforcement learning, limiting scalability and generalization.
- Prior video-based robot learning often focuses on raw pixel values or static visual features, potentially overlooking the critical dynamic motion information necessary for effective control and transferability.

## Method

- A Latent Motion Tokenizer, based on a VQ-==VAE== architecture with a ==transformer== encoder and ViT decoder, is developed to convert consecutive video frames into discrete, compact sequences of latent motion tokens.
- A GPT-style transformer, **Moto**-GPT, is pre-trained autoregressively on these extracted latent motion token sequences from vast video datasets, learning diverse motion priors conditioned on initial frames and language instructions.
- A co-fine-tuning strategy is employed to adapt the pre-trained **Moto**-GPT for precise robot control by introducing action query tokens and an MLP-based action head, combining motion token prediction with ground-truth robot action loss.

## Results

- Latent motion tokens proved semantically interpretable, enabling a classifier to predict CALVIN task labels with **79.7%** accuracy using only 7 motion token chunks.
- Fine-tuned Moto-GPT achieved a **61.4%** overall success rate on the SIMPLER benchmark, outperforming a baseline without motion tokens (**48.0%**) and performing competitively with larger VLM-based models.
- Moto-GPT demonstrated substantial data efficiency, achieving a **52.5%** success rate on CALVIN with only **1%** of action-labeled data (where the baseline achieved **0%**), and improved real-world robot task success from **23.33%** to **60%** with increased robustness to distractors and novel objects.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Latent motion tokens serve as an interpretable, hardware-agnostic 'language of motion,' effectively distilling fine-grained visual dynamics from videos and acting as a compact semantic representation.
> - Unsupervised generative pre-training on these motion token sequences allows Moto-GPT to learn rich motion priors, including semantics, trajectory rationality, and future anticipation, crucial for robust robot policies.
> - A co-fine-tuning approach, simultaneously predicting motion tokens and continuous robot actions, efficiently transfers abstract motion knowledge to precise low-level control, significantly improving data efficiency during fine-tuning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robot manipulation has long been constrained by the high cost and scarcity of action-labeled data. Traditional methods require meticulous human demonstrations or extensive trial-and-error in reinforcement learning, both of which are time-consuming and difficult to scale. In parallel, recent breakthroughs in Natural Language Processing (NLP), driven by Large Language Models (LLMs) pre-trained on vast text corpora through autoregressive prediction, have demonstrated remarkable generalization capabilities. This success has inspired researchers to explore analogous pre-training paradigms for robotics.

This paper directly addresses the challenge of data scarcity by proposing to leverage the abundance of readily available, interaction-rich video data as a "corpus" for robot learning. Prior work in "Robot Learning from Videos" has explored various avenues, including contrastive learning with egocentric videos to enhance visual representations and generating videos as intermediate plans for low-level control. More recent efforts have focused on generative video pre-training for end-to-end policy models, often using pixel values or patch-level tokens for autoregressive prediction.

Moto differentiates itself from these preceding works by emphasizing *motion-related knowledge* rather than raw pixel values or static frame features. The authors argue that human skill acquisition relies on observing dynamic changes (motion), which are inherently tied to low-level actions and are hardware-agnostic, making them highly transferable to robot control. This perspective positions Moto as a refinement of video-based robot learning, moving beyond generic visual features to focus on the kinematic and dynamic information crucial for manipulation.

Furthermore, Moto interacts with the emerging landscape of "Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Models." These models aim to generate robot actions from observations and language instructions, often using transformer architectures. While some VLA models fine-tune policies from powerful pre-trained vision-language models (VLMs) or train generalist models on diverse action-labeled robot data, Moto offers a novel pre-training strategy that enriches VLAs with motion priors learned from *unsupervised* video data. By converting video content into "latent motion token sequences," Moto establishes a bridging "language" that is conceptually similar to tokens in LLMs, allowing for autoregressive pre-training to absorb a wealth of motion-related prior knowledge. This approach aims to provide VLA models with a richer understanding of interaction dynamics, leading to more robust and data-efficient robot policies.

The work also relates to concurrent efforts on learning "latent actions" from videos, as seen in Genie, LAPO, DynaMo, LAPA, and IGOR. However, Moto distinguishes itself by pre-training an end-to-end policy model to *autoregressively predict a trajectory of latent motion tokens* for future video clips, offering a more natural modeling of sequential visual motions and a comprehensive analysis of its policy effectiveness. This places Moto at the forefront of research aiming to leverage internet-scale video data for building more generalist and capable robot agents.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation behind Moto stems from the disparity between the success of Large Language Models (LLMs) in NLP and the persistent challenges in robot learning. LLMs thrive on massive, easily accessible text corpora, learning rich semantic and syntactic priors through unsupervised generative pre-training. Robotics, however, struggles with data scarcity, particularly for action-labeled data, which is expensive and laborious to collect.

Given this context, the authors pose a fundamental question: **Can a similar generative pre-training approach, leveraging the abundant video data rich in interaction-related knowledge, be effectively applied to enhance robot learning?**

To achieve this, the paper sets out several key objectives:

1. **Identify an effective representation for autoregressive pre-training on video data:** The core challenge is to distill meaningful, transferable knowledge from raw video pixels. The authors hypothesize that focusing on *motion-related knowledge*, rather than just static visual details, is crucial. This is because motion is directly tied to low-level robot actions, is hardware-agnostic, and aligns with how humans learn skills by observing dynamic changes.
2. **Develop a mechanism to convert video content into a learnable "language" of motion:** This involves creating a representation that captures visual dynamics between frames in an unsupervised, compact, and discrete manner, analogous to words or tokens in language models. This representation, termed "Latent Motion Tokens," should serve as a bridging interface.
3. **Pre-train a generative model (Moto-GPT) on these latent motion token sequences:** The goal is to enable the model to learn diverse motion priors, including motion semantics, trajectory rationality, and future anticipation, through an autoregressive prediction objective, without requiring explicit action annotations.
4. **Devise a seamless strategy to transfer these learned motion priors to real robot control tasks:** The pre-trained knowledge must be effectively adapted to produce precise, low-level robot actions. This requires bridging the abstract motion token space with the continuous action space of robots.
5. **Validate the interpretability, utility of learned priors, and performance of the fine-tuned robot policy:** The research aims to systematically demonstrate that latent motion tokens are interpretable, that Moto-GPT indeed learns useful motion priors, and that the fine-tuned policy achieves superior robustness and efficiency on standard robot manipulation benchmarks, especially in data-limited scenarios and real-world deployments.
6. **Explore cross-embodiment knowledge transfer:** A secondary but significant objective is to investigate whether motion knowledge learned from large-scale human videos can be effectively transferred to robot manipulation, further expanding the potential data sources for robot learning.

In essence, the motivation is to unlock the potential of vast unlabeled video data to overcome the data bottleneck in robotics, creating more capable, generalist, and data-efficient robot policies by treating motion as a fundamental, transferable "language."

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Moto proposes a three-stage methodology to bridge generative pre-training on video data with precise robot control using latent motion tokens.

#### 3.1. Latent Motion Tokenizer

The first stage focuses on learning a discrete, compact representation of visual motion between successive video frames. This **Latent Motion Tokenizer** acts as an unsupervised "encoder-decoder" system:

- **Architecture:** It employs a VQ-VAE (Vector Quantized Variational AutoEncoder) based design.
 - **Encoder (M-Former):** This multi-layer transformer takes as input the last-layer patch features of two consecutive video frames, `o_t-1` (previous frame) and `o_t` (current frame), extracted using a frozen pre-trained ViT (Vision Transformer) encoder. Eight learnable query embeddings are concatenated with these patch features. The M-Former processes these inputs through self-attention layers.
 - **VQ Codebook:** The output query features from the M-Former are then quantized using a VQ codebook with a vocabulary size of 128, producing discrete latent motion tokens. This quantization step is crucial for creating a discrete "language."
 - **Decoder (ViT Decoder):** This component is designed to reconstruct `o_t` from `o_t-1` and the generated latent motion tokens. It takes linearly embedded patches of `o_t-1` and an MLP-projected compact embedding of the concatenated quantized motion tokens. This conditional embedding acts as an information bottleneck, forcing the decoder to capture the *changes* (motions) necessary to transform `o_t-1` into `o_t`.
- **Training:** The tokenizer is optimized using a standard VQ-VAE objective, including reconstruction loss (MSE between `o_t` and reconstructed `o_t`), vector quantization loss, and commitment loss. To ensure significant visual differences for motion learning, videos are downsampled by a certain rate (e.g., one frame every three or five original frames).
- **Outcome:** Once trained, the Latent Motion Tokenizer is frozen. It converts any pair of consecutive video frames into a sequence of discrete latent motion tokens, effectively creating a unified sequential motion representation. The decoder can also function as a "simulator" to visualize environmental changes based on initial observation and motion tokens.

#### 3.2. Motion Token Autoregressive Pre-training (Moto-GPT)

The second stage involves pre-training a generative model, **Moto-GPT**, on sequences of latent motion tokens extracted from videos:

- **Input:** For a video clip, features from the initial frame (`o_0`) are extracted using a frozen pre-trained ViT, and features from a corresponding language instruction (if available) are extracted using a frozen pre-trained T5 model. These visual and text features serve as input prompts. The sequence of latent motion tokens (chunks from `(o_0, o_1)`, `(o_1, o_2)`..., `(o_T-1, o_T)`) forms the main autoregressive sequence.
- **Architecture:** Moto-GPT employs a GPT-style transformer, a powerful architecture for sequence-to-sequence learning.
- **Objective:** The model is pre-trained with a next-motion-token prediction objective. It maximizes the likelihood of the ground-truth latent motion token sequence, conditioned on the language instruction, the initial video frame, and the preceding motion tokens.
- **Outcome:** Through this large-scale unsupervised pre-training on video data, Moto-GPT learns rich motion priors, including an understanding of motion semantics, trajectory rationality, and the ability to anticipate future movements.

#### 3.3. Co-fine-tuning for Robot Manipulation

The final stage adapts the pre-trained Moto-GPT for precise, low-level robot control:

- **Bridging the Gap:** While pre-trained Moto-GPT can generate plausible motion token trajectories, a gap remains for generating precise real robot actions. To bridge this, "action query tokens" are introduced during fine-tuning.
- **Input Modification:** At each time step, `N` action query tokens (where `N` is the number of robot actions between two video frames) are appended after the latent motion token chunk in Moto-GPT's input.
- **Action Head:** An MLP-based action head is added. It projects the output hidden states of these action query tokens into the real robot action space, predicting continuous components (e.g., positional `∆x`, rotational `∆θ` using Smooth-L1 loss) and binary components (e.g., gripper open/close `∆grip` using Binary Cross Entropy loss).
- **Co-fine-tuning Loss:** The fine-tuning objective is a combination of two loss terms:
 - **Motion Token Prediction Loss (`L_motion`):** The original next-motion-token prediction objective is retained. This ensures that Moto-GPT continues to utilize and preserve the learned motion priors from pre-training.
 - **Action Loss (`L_action`):** The loss from the action head, calculated against ground-truth robot actions.
 - The total fine-tuning loss is `L_ft = L_motion + L_action`.
- **Attention Masking:**
 - To maintain consistency with pre-training, latent motion tokens are prevented from attending to the newly inserted action query tokens.
 - A random masking (50%) is applied to attention from action query tokens to latent motion tokens. This allows for knowledge transfer while preventing over-reliance on ground-truth conditions and improving inference efficiency. During inference, if latent motion tokens are not to be generated, padding tokens can serve as placeholders, blocking attention from action queries.
- **Outcome:** This co-fine-tuning strategy effectively transfers the abstract intentions learned as motion priors into precise action execution, resulting in a robust and data-efficient robot policy.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted validate Moto's effectiveness across three key questions: the interpretability of latent motion tokens, the utility of learned motion priors, and the performance of the fine-tuned robot policy.

#### 4.1. Q1: Latent Motion Token as an Interpretable Motion Language

- **Authentic Reconstruction:** The Latent Motion Tokenizer successfully reconstructs subsequent frames from initial frames and motion tokens, demonstrating its ability to capture fine-grained dynamics. The decoder effectively acts as a simulator to visualize environmental changes.
- **Controllability and Consistency:** Qualitative examples (Fig. 4, Fig. 5) show that different latent motion token chunks produce distinct motion orientations and scales, while identical chunks applied to different initial observations (even across human and robot embodiments) yield semantically consistent effects. This highlights the controllability and generalizability of the motion language.
- **Semantic Interpretability:** Quantitatively, a video classifier using an initial frame's visual features combined with 7 latent motion token chunks (for 7 subsequent frames) achieved 79.7% accuracy in predicting semantic labels for CALVIN tasks (Table 1). This is comparable to using full visual features from all 8 frames, yet it significantly reduces input dimensionality (from 196 to 8 tokens per frame), confirming that latent motion tokens provide a compact, expressive, and semantically interpretable representation of visual motions.

#### 4.2. Q2: Moto-GPT as a Useful Motion Prior Learner

- **Trajectory Prediction:** Pre-trained Moto-GPT demonstrates the ability to predict plausible future motion trajectories based on initial observations and language instructions (Fig. 6). This is crucial for planning and anticipation in robot tasks, even for complex actions like "rotation" and "stacking." The model achieves reasonable top-K prediction accuracy for ground-truth latent motion tokens (Table 8).
- **Trajectory Rationality Assessment:** Moto-GPT can effectively distinguish successful robot trajectories from failures and random attempts by evaluating their autoregressive log-likelihood (Fig. 7). This suggests that Moto's log-likelihood can serve as a valuable reward signal for trajectory rationality, measuring how well a sequence of actions aligns with learned motion priors and exhibits temporal consistency.

#### 4.3. Q3: Moto-GPT as an Effective Robot Policy

- **Overall Performance on Benchmarks:**
 - On the **SIMPLER** benchmark (Table 2), fine-tuned Moto-GPT significantly outperforms "Moto w/o Motion Token" (trained from scratch without latent motion tokens), achieving an overall success rate of 61.4% compared to 48.0%. Notably, Moto-GPT, with only 98M parameters for its GPT backbone and no action labels during pre-training, performs competitively with much larger VLM-based models like RT-2-X (PaLI-X 55B) and OpenVLA (Prismatic-7B).
 - On the challenging **CALVIN (ABC→D)** benchmark for long-horizon tasks in unseen environments (Table 3), Moto-GPT (Avg. Len. 3.10) again outperforms "Moto w/o Motion Token" (Avg. Len. 2.14). It also achieves competitive results compared to other baselines that use richer inputs (e.g., GR-1 uses gripper RGB and proprioceptive states) while Moto-GPT relies solely on static RGB images. This finding supports the argument that focusing on motion dynamics is more effective than raw pixel prediction for learning from videos.

- **Real-world Robot Experiments:** Moto-GPT demonstrates superior performance in real-world tasks (pick-place banana, close laptop, disassembly), consistently outperforming "Moto w/o Motion Token" (Fig. 8). The average success rate improved from 23.33% to 60%. Importantly, Moto-GPT showed increased robustness under generalization evaluations, improving performance by 20% with visual distractors and 30% with novel objects.

- **Learning from Human Videos and Cross-Embodiment Transfer:** A preliminary study combining OXE robot videos with human videos from SSV2 (Something-Something V2) dataset shows that additional human video pre-training further boosts Moto-GPT's performance (Fig. 9), specifically by +17.9% on the "Move Near" task. This highlights the potential for leveraging Internet-scale human videos. Furthermore, latent motion tokens act as a unified "language" to translate human movements into semantically aligned robot actions (Fig. 10), enabling cross-embodiment knowledge transfer.

- **Data Efficiency:** Moto-GPT demonstrates significant data efficiency during fine-tuning. With only 1% of action-labeled data, it achieves a 52.5% success rate on CALVIN, whereas "Moto w/o Motion Token" achieves 0% (Fig. 11). This underscores the benefits of pre-training on large, unlabeled video datasets to reduce the need for expensive supervised robot data.

- **Ablation Studies on Fine-tuning:** Ablations (Fig. 12) confirm the importance of Moto's co-fine-tuning strategy. "Moto-IML" (excluding motion token prediction loss during fine-tuning) and "Moto-DM" (omitting latent motion tokens from input during fine-tuning) both perform worse than the full Moto-GPT. This indicates that retaining latent motion tokens in the input sequence and continuing to predict them during fine-tuning are crucial for preserving and effectively transferring the learned motion priors.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Moto represents a significant advancement in robot learning, primarily by addressing the persistent challenge of data scarcity in robotics through a novel, LLM-inspired approach. Its core contributions and potential impacts are multi-faceted:

1. **Bridging Unsupervised Video Learning and Precise Robot Control:** The introduction of Latent Motion Tokens as a discrete "language" is a breakthrough. It enables the unsupervised extraction of rich motion priors from abundant video data (robot or human) and provides a direct interface for fine-tuning into precise robot actions. This mechanism effectively bridges the gap between high-level visual understanding and low-level control, a critical step toward more capable and generalist robot policies.

2. **Hardware-Agnostic Motion Priors:** By focusing on abstract motion dynamics rather than hardware-specific action labels or raw pixel values, Moto learns priors that are intrinsically hardware-agnostic. This greatly enhances the transferability of learned knowledge, not just across different robot tasks but potentially across different robot embodiments and even from human demonstrations to robots. This generalization capability is key to scaling robot learning beyond narrowly defined tasks.

3. **Data Efficiency and Scalability:** Moto's ability to learn effectively with limited action-labeled data during fine-tuning is highly impactful. By pre-training on readily available, large-scale video datasets, the expensive and laborious process of collecting expert robot demonstrations is significantly reduced. This opens the door to leveraging vast internet-scale video resources, including human interaction videos, to continuously enhance robot intelligence. The demonstrated cross-embodiment transfer from human videos further amplifies this potential.

4. **Enhanced Robot Robustness and Generalization:** The experiments show that Moto-GPT fine-tuned policies exhibit superior robustness in real-world scenarios, particularly when facing novel objects or visual distractors. This indicates that the learned motion priors contribute to a more fundamental understanding of interaction dynamics, making the robot less brittle to environmental variations. Its competitive performance against larger, more heavily pre-trained models, despite using fewer parameters and less supervised data, underscores its efficiency and effectiveness.

5. **New Avenues for Robot Learning:** Beyond direct policy learning, Moto's capacity to assess trajectory rationality through log-likelihood suggests its potential as a novel reward function for reinforcement learning. Furthermore, its decoder acting as a "simulator" for visualizing environmental changes could aid in planning and exploration. This framework could pave the way for more sophisticated world models and planning agents in robotics.

In conclusion, Moto proposes a robust and scalable paradigm for robot learning that moves beyond the limitations of traditional supervised methods. By transforming raw video into an interpretable "language of motion," it provides robots with a foundational understanding of dynamics, setting the stage for more adaptive, versatile, and intelligent autonomous systems that can learn efficiently from the visual world around them. Future work will undoubtedly focus on scaling the pre-training datasets and exploring a broader range of robotic applications, further solidifying Moto's impact on the field.
