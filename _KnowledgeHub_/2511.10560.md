# OmniVGGT: Omni-Modality Driven Visual Geometry Grounded Transformer

> [!summary] Summary
> OmniVGGT, a 3D foundation model developed by researchers including those from HKUST and NTU, integrates an arbitrary number of geometric modalities like depth maps and camera parameters, resulting in superior performance across 3D perception tasks and improved spatial reasoning for robotic manipulation.

## Problem

- Existing 3D foundation models primarily rely on RGB images, neglecting the rich geometric cues (e.g., depth, intrinsics, poses) readily available in many real-world applications.
- Prior multimodal 3D perception methods are limited in their flexibility, often handling only a fixed, small number of inputs rather than an arbitrary combination of diverse geometric modalities.
- Directly injecting global geometric information, such as camera parameters, into large foundation models can destabilize their learned feature representations and hinder generalization.

## Method

- A novel ==GeoAdapter== module is introduced to effectively incorporate auxiliary geometric cues: a Camera Adapter uses zero-initialized convolutions for stable injection of camera parameters, while a Depth Adapter directly adds depth tokens.
- A ==stochastic multimodal fusion== strategy is employed during training, where the model randomly samples the presence and proportion of auxiliary modalities, enabling robustness to arbitrary input combinations at inference.
- The architecture builds upon a Visual Geometry Grounded Transformer (VGGT) backbone, utilizing a multi-task training objective that includes losses for camera parameters, depth, and 3D point maps to promote geometric consistency.

## Results

- OmniVGGT achieves state-of-the-art performance across monocular and multi-view depth estimation, camera pose estimation, and 3D reconstruction tasks, consistently outperforming previous methods in both RGB-only and multimodal settings.
- Performance scales with the availability of auxiliary information; for example, **100%** depth injection on Sintel significantly reduces Abs. Rel error, and camera injection boosts AUC@30° for pose estimation.
- Integrating OmniVGGT's 3D spatial tokens into a Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model substantially enhances robotic manipulation capabilities on the CALVIN dataset, outperforming point-cloud-based and RGB-only baselines while maintaining high inference efficiency (approximately **30x** faster than Pow3R).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Implementing distinct injection mechanisms for global (camera parameters via ZeroConv) and dense (depth via direct addition) geometric cues is crucial for stable and effective integration into 3D foundation models.
> - A stochastic multimodal training strategy enables a single model to leverage an arbitrary number and combination of geometric inputs, making it adaptable to diverse real-world data availability scenarios.
> - Incorporating diverse geometric modalities through OmniVGGT leads to more accurate and generalized 3D spatial representations, which not only boosts performance on core 3D tasks but also enhances spatial reasoning in downstream applications like robotics.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research on OmniVGGT is situated at the intersection of general 3D foundation models and multimodal learning, addressing a critical gap in the current landscape of 3D vision.

**Evolution from Task-Specific to General 3D Models:** Traditionally, 3D vision tasks like monocular depth estimation, multi-view stereo, and camera pose estimation were tackled by specialized algorithms. Recent advancements, mirroring the success of Large Language Models (LLMs) like ChatGPT in NLP, have seen the emergence of "general 3D foundation models" (e.g., DUSt3R, MASt3R, VGGT). These models aim to learn unified 3D representations applicable across diverse downstream tasks using feed-forward architectures, moving away from per-scene optimization required by methods like NeRF or 3D Gaussian Splatting. OmniVGGT builds directly upon this paradigm, specifically extending the capabilities of VGGT (Visual Geometry Grounded Transformer).

**Addressing the RGB-Only Limitation:** A significant limitation of most existing 3D foundation models, including VGGT, is their reliance solely on RGB images as input. This overlooks the wealth of readily available geometric cues—such as depth maps, camera intrinsics, and camera poses—that are common in real-world 3D vision applications (e.g., RGB-D sensors in VR/AR, LiDAR in autonomous driving, known camera parameters in robotics). The authors explicitly state that while general solutions unify tasks, they typically "overlook efforts to unify diverse input modalities."

**Advancing Multimodal 3D Perception:** Prior attempts to incorporate multimodal inputs, such as Pow3R, have been limited in their flexibility, often handling at most two inputs (e.g., an RGB pair and a depth pair). OmniVGGT pushes beyond these limitations by introducing a framework capable of benefiting from an *arbitrary number* of various geometric modalities during both training and inference. This flexibility is crucial because real-world scenarios rarely provide a fixed, predefined set of auxiliary data; instead, applications might have access to some, all, or none of these cues in varying combinations. By enabling this "omnimodality," OmniVGGT represents a significant step towards more robust and universally applicable 3D perception systems, enhancing the fidelity of 3D scene understanding and reconstruction.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objectives and motivations behind the development of OmniVGGT are multifaceted, driven by the desire to overcome the limitations of current 3D foundation models and enhance their applicability to complex real-world scenarios.

**Primary Objective:** To develop a novel framework, OmniVGGT, that can effectively leverage an arbitrary number of auxiliary geometric modalities (such as depth maps, camera intrinsics, and camera extrinsics/poses) during both training and inference, thereby improving the accuracy and robustness of 3D geometric reconstruction and related downstream tasks.

**Key Motivations:**
1. **Exploiting Underutilized Geometric Cues:** Existing 3D foundation models predominantly rely on RGB-only inputs, neglecting readily available and information-rich geometric cues. The authors are motivated to integrate these diverse modalities to unlock superior 3D understanding.
2. **Enhancing Flexibility for Real-World Applications:** Real-world 3D vision applications (e.g., VR/AR, autonomous driving, robotics) often provide heterogeneous and variable sets of auxiliary geometric information. Current models lack the flexibility to seamlessly integrate an arbitrary number and combination of these inputs. OmniVGGT aims to provide this crucial adaptability.
3. **Improving Spatial Representation and Generalization:** Directly injecting camera parameters or depth maps can destabilize the feature space of large-scale foundation models. The authors seek a method to progressively and stably inject this information, preserving the model’s robust high-quality feature representations and promoting learning of more generalized and robust 3D spatial representations.
4. **Boosting Performance Across Diverse 3D Tasks:** The goal is not just to integrate modalities but to demonstrate consistent and significant performance improvements across a wide range of 3D vision tasks, including depth estimation (monocular and multi-view), camera pose estimation, and 3D scene reconstruction.
5. **Practical Utility in Downstream Robotic Applications:** To highlight its real-world impact, OmniVGGT is motivated to demonstrate its value in enhancing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robotic manipulation, where accurate spatial understanding and absolute pose prediction are critical.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

OmniVGGT extends the Visual Geometry Grounded Transformer (VGGT) by introducing a flexible input scheme capable of processing an arbitrary number of geometric modalities. The methodology is characterized by two main innovations: the GeoAdapter and a stochastic multimodal fusion strategy.

**Core Architecture (Building on VGGT):**
OmniVGGT inherits the fundamental structure of VGGT, which directly infers 3D attributes from an image set. Inputs are initially tokenized using a DINO backbone to create spatial tokens. These are concatenated with learnable camera and register tokens and then processed by an Alternating-Attention (AA) encoder (L layers), which captures both intra-image and inter-view information. Finally, dense prediction heads (DPT heads) generate depth maps, 3D point maps, and confidence maps, while a separate head predicts camera intrinsics and extrinsics.

#### 3.1. GeoAdapter for Stochastic Multimodal Inputs

The GeoAdapter is a lightweight, two-part module designed to effectively incorporate geometric cues without disrupting the foundation model's learned representations.

- **Camera Adapter:**
 - **Normalization:** Camera intrinsics and poses (rotation and translation) are first aligned to a common coordinate system (relative to the first camera) and then normalized using an average distance scale factor.
 - **Encoding:** The normalized parameters are parameterized into a feature vector (quaternion for rotation, translation vector, field of view) and fed into dedicated camera encoders (E_cam_l) for each AA block.
 - **Injection with Zero-Convolution:** To prevent destabilization of the foundation model’s feature space, especially for global attributes like camera pose, a **zero-initialized convolution (ZeroConv)** layer is applied to the auxiliary camera tokens. This "progressively injects" information, meaning it starts with minimal impact and gradually learns to integrate the geometric data during training, ensuring stability.
 - **Placeholder Tokens:** For images lacking camera information, zero vectors (e_plh_c) act as placeholder tokens, allowing the model to handle incomplete input sets.

- **Depth Adapter:**
 - **Normalization and Concatenation:** Auxiliary depth maps are normalized batch-wise (using valid pixels identified by a mask) and concatenated with their corresponding masks along the channel dimension.
 - **Encoding:** The combined depth and mask information is fed into a dedicated depth encoder (E_dpt), which uses a convolutional layer to tokenize and align dimensions with spatial tokens.
 - **Direct Injection:** Through ablation studies, the authors found that applying an additional ZeroConv layer to the depth branch was redundant and detrimental. Therefore, auxiliary depth tokens are **directly added** to the corresponding spatial tokens. This likely works because depth provides dense, per-pixel spatial cues that are more compatible with existing spatial token representations compared to the global camera parameters.
 - **Placeholder Tokens:** A separate depth placeholder token (e_plh_d) is used for images without reference depth information.

#### 3.2. Stochastic Multimodal Fusion Strategy

This novel training regimen is crucial for enabling OmniVGGT to accept *arbitrary numbers and combinations* of input modalities at inference time.
- During training, for each image sequence, the network stochastically samples:
 - A number `Q` of images to receive ground-truth camera parameters (assigned to the first `Q` images).
 - A number `O` of images to receive ground-truth depth maps (assigned to randomly selected indices).
- This random sampling ensures that the model learns to be robust to various partial-information scenarios, rather than overfitting to specific auxiliary cues or requiring all modalities to be present.
- Crucially, a subset of training batches (with a probability `p%`) is processed using *only raw RGB images*, ensuring the model performs well even when no auxiliary information is available (auxiliary-free case).

**Training Objective:**
The overall training objective is a multi-task loss, `L = L_camera + L_depth + L_pmap`, following VGGT:
- `L_camera`: L1 regression loss for predicted camera parameters against ground truth.
- `L_depth` and `L_pmap`: Confidence-aware regression loss augmented with a gradient-based term to promote local geometric consistency for depth and point map predictions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

OmniVGGT demonstrates state-of-the-art performance across a wide array of 3D vision tasks, showcasing its effectiveness both with and without auxiliary inputs, and its significant practical utility in robotics.

#### 4.1. Auxiliary Information Guidance (Zero-shot on Sintel, ARKitScenes, OmniWorld-Game)

- **RGB-Only Baseline Improvement:** OmniVGGT outperforms the vanilla VGGT baseline even in the auxiliary-free setting, indicating that its architectural changes (GeoAdapter and stochastic fusion) lead to better generalized spatial representations inherently. For example, on Sintel, it reduces Abs Rel error and increases `δ < 1.25` for depth, and improves RRA@5° for pose.
- **Scalability with Auxiliary Inputs:** Performance consistently and significantly improves as the proportion of available ground-truth camera or depth information increases. For instance, on Sintel, 100% depth injection reduces Abs. Rel error by 0.452 and 100% camera injection boosts AUC@30° by 14.52, validating the effective integration of cues.
- **Cross-Modality Benefit:** Injecting auxiliary depth information also leads to notable gains in pose estimation, and vice-versa, demonstrating that OmniVGGT enhances the *accuracy of spatial representations* rather than merely learning direct input-output mappings.

#### 4.2. Depth Estimation

- **Monocular Depth Estimation (Sintel, Bonn, NYU-v2):** OmniVGGT achieves superior performance (e.g., 0.5 and 1.0 improvement in `δ < 1.25` on Sintel and NYU-v2 respectively) over the baseline in the RGB-only setting. With auxiliary depth (w/ D), it approaches near-perfect accuracy (99.9% in `δ < 1.25`) on Bonn and NYU-v2, significantly outperforming Pow3R w/ D.
- **Multi-View Depth Estimation (ScanNet, ETH3D, DTU, Tanks and Temples):** Consistently outperforms prior approaches in both accuracy and robustness, even without auxiliary information. With auxiliary depth supervision (w/ D), it yields pronounced improvements, achieving near-perfect correlation on ETH3D and DTU.

#### 4.3. Camera Pose Estimation (Co3Dv2, RealEstate10K)

- **RGB-Only SOTA:** In the RGB-only setting, OmniVGGT surpasses state-of-the-art methods, including VGGT and all other baselines.
- **Significant Gains with Auxiliary Inputs:** With auxiliary inputs (K+RT), it demonstrates consistent performance gains, outperforming the advanced auxiliary-aware method Pow3R by up to 16% on Re10K.
- **Efficiency:** Achieves this superior performance while being approximately 30x faster than Pow3R, highlighting unprecedented flexibility and efficiency in handling arbitrary auxiliary inputs.

#### 4.4. 3D Reconstruction (7-Scenes)

- **Competitive RGB-Only:** Achieves competitive performance with existing state-of-the-art VGGT in the RGB-only setting.
- **Substantial Boost with Auxiliary Inputs:** Dramatically boosts performance when auxiliary inputs (depth and camera parameters) are available. For example, the inclusion of camera parameters (OmniVGGT w/ (K+RT)) yields a remarkable 65.4% performance gain (Acc metric from 0.104 to 0.036) over the baseline. This demonstrates robustness in challenging scenarios like extreme image sparsity or non-overlapping viewpoints.

#### 4.5. Application on Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Model (CALVIN dataset)

- **Enhanced Spatial Understanding:** Integrating OmniVGGT's richer 3D spatial tokens into Kosmos-VLA (a VLA model) significantly enhances its spatial reasoning capabilities for robot manipulation.
- **Outperforms Baselines:** The enhanced VLA model outperforms vanilla point-cloud-based baselines and even the Kosmos-VLA w/ RGB baseline (by 0.43 in Avg. Len under zero-shot ABC→D setting).
- **Consistent Gains with Depth Input:** Further benefits are observed when auxiliary depth inputs are provided, leading to consistent gains on robotic tasks (e.g., 0.04 improvement in Avg. Len over point-cloud-based RGB-D baseline in ABCD→D setting).

#### 4.6. Architecture Ablation (GeoAdapter)

- Ablation studies confirm the efficacy of the specific GeoAdapter design choices:
 - Directly replacing tokens (variant a) or single-layer injection (variant b) yield suboptimal results.
 - Applying ZeroConv to depth (variant c) is detrimental, suppressing meaningful depth information.
 - The chosen design (ZeroConv for camera parameters, direct addition for depth) effectively preserves and integrates information, leading to accurate spatial representations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

OmniVGGT represents a substantial advancement in the field of 3D computer vision, particularly for the development of general 3D foundation models. Its significance and potential impact span several key areas:

1. **Enabling True Omni-Modality in 3D Foundation Models:** The most significant contribution is its ability to flexibly incorporate an *arbitrary number and combination* of diverse geometric modalities. This moves beyond previous limitations of RGB-only inputs or fixed pairwise multimodal inputs, paving the way for more versatile and robust 3D models that can adapt to the heterogeneous data availability in real-world scenarios.

2. **Enhanced Robustness and Accuracy:** By leveraging rich geometric cues, OmniVGGT achieves state-of-the-art performance across a broad spectrum of 3D perception tasks (depth estimation, camera pose, 3D reconstruction). The consistent improvements, even in challenging conditions like sparse or non-overlapping views, highlight its superior spatial understanding capabilities.

3. **Broadening Applicability Across Domains:** The model's flexibility and enhanced performance have direct implications for various applications:
 - **Robotics:** Demonstrated improvement in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robot manipulation suggests more capable and adaptable robots with better spatial reasoning and precise interaction with their environment.
 - **Augmented/Virtual Reality (AR/VR):** The ability to use RGB-D data and camera parameters directly supports enhanced 3D reconstruction and spatial awareness for immersive experiences.
 - **Autonomous Driving:** The capacity to integrate LiDAR data (represented as depth/point clouds) alongside camera inputs can lead to more robust perception systems for navigation and object detection.

4. **Foundation for Future General 3D AI:** OmniVGGT’s architecture, particularly the GeoAdapter and stochastic fusion strategy, provides a powerful blueprint for future research in general 3D AI. It establishes a robust method for integrating heterogeneous sensor data, which is crucial for building comprehensive "world models" that mirror human-like perception across multiple senses.

5. **Efficiency and Practicality:** Despite its enhanced multimodal capabilities, OmniVGGT maintains high inference efficiency, operating significantly faster than some prior multimodal approaches. This efficiency is critical for real-time applications and scalable deployments.

In conclusion, OmniVGGT addresses a fundamental challenge in 3D vision by creating a truly omni-modal 3D foundation model. Its innovative methodology and compelling empirical results position it as a pivotal development, promising to accelerate the creation of more intelligent and adaptable AI systems capable of perceiving and interacting with the 3D world with unprecedented accuracy and flexibility.
