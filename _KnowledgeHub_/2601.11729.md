# SpaRRTa: A Synthetic Benchmark for Evaluating Spatial Intelligence in Visual Foundation Models

> [!summary] Summary
> SpaRRTa, a new synthetic benchmark, evaluates Visual Foundation Models' abstract spatial reasoning abilities by requiring them to identify relative object positions from egocentric and allocentric viewpoints. It reveals that spatial information is distributed within local patch features, and while 3D supervision enhances this, allocentric perspective-taking remains difficult for current models.

## Problem

- Visual Foundation Models (VFMs) lack robust, abstract spatial reasoning, hindering their application in embodied and interactive AI systems.
- Existing benchmarks primarily assess metric 3D perception (e.g., depth, camera pose), not qualitative, relational spatial understanding.
- It is unclear if VFMs truly gain general spatial awareness or merely overfit to specific geometric training objectives.

## Method

- A synthetic benchmark called ==SpaRRTa== was created to specifically evaluate abstract, relational spatial understanding in VFMs, differentiating it from metric 3D tasks.
- ==Unreal Engine 5== was used to generate photorealistic images with precise control over object placement, camera views, and ground-truth spatial annotations across diverse scenes.
- Two distinct spatial reasoning tasks were defined: ==SpaRRTa-ego== for egocentric relations and ==SpaRRTa-allo== for allocentric relations, which necessitates implicit perspective-taking from a third object's viewpoint.

## Results

- Efficient Probing consistently achieved superior performance across all models, confirming that structured aggregation of local patch features is critical for effective spatial reasoning.
- VGGT, a 3D-supervised model, significantly surpassed DINO-v2 in accuracy when evaluated with AbMILP and Efficient Probing, indicating that 3D supervision enhances localized geometric structure.
- All evaluated models exhibited substantially lower accuracy on the allocentric spatial reasoning task compared to the egocentric task, highlighting the challenge of perspective-taking.
- A positive correlation (R² "**0.40**) was observed between SpaRRTa performance and 3D geometric tasks, while correlation with semantic classification benchmarks was negligible (R² "**.00**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Spatial information critical for relational understanding is predominantly embedded within local patch features of VFM representations, often obscured by global pooling methods.
> - Explicit 3D supervision reorganizes VFM internal representations by increasing cross-object attention and enhancing patch-level geometric structure, making spatial details more accessible to sophisticated probes.
> - Abstract allocentric reasoning, which demands implicit perspective-taking, poses a significantly greater challenge for current VFMs compared to egocentric spatial understanding.
> - VFM spatial intelligence is a distinct capability, showing strong correlation with other 3D geometric tasks but negligible correlation with high-level semantic classification.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancements in Visual Foundation Models (VFMs), such as DINO and CLIP, have revolutionized semantic image understanding, leading to widespread adoption in tasks like image classification, object recognition, and multimodal learning. However, as these models transition from static perception to deployment in embodied and interactive AI systems (e.g., robotics, autonomous navigation), their inherent limitations in spatial reasoning become a critical bottleneck. Effective interaction with the physical world demands more than just recognizing objects; it requires a nuanced understanding of their three-dimensional spatial relationships, relative positions, and how these relations change from different perspectives.

Existing efforts to enhance VFMs' spatial awareness typically involve incorporating 3D tasks like depth estimation, camera pose estimation, or surface normal prediction into their training. While these methods show some improvements in specific metric 3D tasks, their performance remains inconsistent across a broader range of spatial challenges. This raises a fundamental question: Do contemporary VFMs acquire a *general, abstract notion of spatial awareness*, or do they merely *overfit* to the specific, quantifiable geometric objectives used during their training?

This paper addresses a crucial gap in the research landscape by introducing SpaRRTa, a benchmark specifically designed to evaluate *abstract, relational, human-like spatial understanding* rather than precise metric 3D prediction or high-level semantic recognition. It complements existing benchmarks in several ways:
- **Contrast with Metric 3D Benchmarks:** Unlike Probe3D or Mid-Level Vision Probe, which focus on quantitative 3D outputs (depth, surface normals), SpaRRTa probes a more fundamental, qualitative understanding of "where" objects are relative to each other and a given viewpoint. This disentangles relational spatial logic from the exact measurement of 3D properties.
- **Distinction from Task-Driven Suites:** While benchmarks like VC-1 and SPA evaluate 3D awareness through downstream success in navigation or manipulation tasks, SpaRRTa focuses on the *representational knowledge* within VFMs, assessing their intrinsic spatial encoding capabilities independently of task-specific policy learning.
- **Orthogonality to Semantic Benchmarks:** The paper explicitly demonstrates that SpaRRTa is orthogonal to fine-grained semantic classification benchmarks (e.g., FGVC Aircraft, Flowers102). High semantic accuracy does not guarantee strong spatial reasoning, highlighting that spatial intelligence is a distinct capability axis.
- **Leveraging Synthetic Data for Controlled Evaluation:** Building upon the trend of using synthetic environments (like Habitat-Sim and FlySearch with Unreal Engine 5), SpaRRTa uses Unreal Engine 5 to generate photorealistic, fully controllable scenes. This offers precise ground-truth spatial annotations and the ability to systematically vary object arrangements and viewpoints—a level of control prohibitively expensive in real-world datasets like ScanNet.
- **Exploring Probing Mechanisms:** The work systematically investigates various probing techniques (linear probing, AbMILP, efficient probing) to understand *how* spatial information is encoded within VFM latent representations, specifically whether it's globally aggregated or present in local patch features. This sheds light on the internal mechanisms of Vision Transformers, contributing to the broader understanding of their representational power beyond typical classification tasks.

In summary, SpaRRTa provides a novel, controlled, and diagnostically valuable tool for assessing a crucial, yet under-evaluated, aspect of VFM intelligence: their abstract spatial awareness. This work is pivotal for guiding the development of the next generation of VFMs that can genuinely support robust embodied and interactive AI applications.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind introducing SpaRRTa stems from the evolving role of Visual Foundation Models (VFMs) in real-world, interactive applications. As VFMs move beyond static image analysis into embodied systems that interact with physical environments, their capabilities must extend beyond mere semantic understanding to include robust spatial reasoning. The authors observe that while current VFMs excel at semantic tasks, their performance in 3D perception remains inconsistent, raising a fundamental question: Do these models truly possess a general spatial awareness, or do they merely overfit to specific 3D objectives introduced during training?

To address this critical question and bridge the gap between semantic understanding and functional spatial intelligence, the authors set forth the following key objectives:

1. **Introduce a Novel Benchmark for Relational Spatial Awareness:** To create the **Spatial Relation Recognition Task (SpaRRTa)**, a benchmark designed specifically to evaluate VFMs' ability to identify the relative positions of objects within an image. Unlike traditional 3D benchmarks that focus on precise metric predictions (e.g., depth, surface normals), SpaRRTa aims to probe a fundamental capability underpinning more advanced forms of human-like spatial understanding, focusing on qualitative relations like "left," "right," "front," and "back."

2. **Develop a Photorealistic and Controllable Synthetic Data Generation System:** To build a robust data generation system using **Unreal Engine 5**. This system is intended to produce an arbitrary number of photorealistic images with diverse scenes and fully controllable object arrangements. The full control over object placement, camera viewpoints, and scene parameters is crucial for generating precise, unambiguous spatial annotations that would be impossible to acquire at scale from real-world data.

3. **Define Standardized Challenge Sets for Different Spatial Reasoning Paradigms:** To establish two distinct variants of the SpaRRTa task:
 - **SpaRRTa-ego (Egocentric):** Where the camera serves as the viewpoint, assessing directly observable spatial relations.
 - **SpaRRTa-allo (Allocentric):** Where a selected third object (e.g., a human) acts as the viewpoint, requiring the VFM to perform implicit perspective-taking and decouple spatial relations from its own visual input. This variant aims to challenge the model's ability for more abstract spatial reasoning.

4. **Benchmark State-of-the-Art VFMs and Analyze Their Spatial Reasoning:** To evaluate a broad range of contemporary VFMs (including self-supervised, supervised, and vision-language models) using the SpaRRTa benchmark. This objective aims to reveal significant disparities in their spatial reasoning abilities and to understand which architectural choices or pre-training objectives contribute most to spatial awareness.

5. **Provide Insights into the Mechanisms of Spatial Awareness:** To conduct an in-depth analysis of VFM internal representations, particularly focusing on how spatial information is encoded and structured within their latent spaces. This includes investigating the effectiveness of different probing mechanisms (global vs. patch-level aggregation) and analyzing attention dynamics within Vision Transformers to understand how information flows and is reorganized to support spatial tasks.

Ultimately, the motivation is to offer SpaRRTa as a **useful diagnostic tool** to guide the development of future spatially aware visual models, which are indispensable for creating truly intelligent embodied and interactive AI systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The SpaRRTa methodology employs a two-stage evaluation framework: synthetic data generation and offline model probing, ensuring rigorous and controlled assessment of spatial awareness in VFMs.

#### 3.1. Synthetic Data Generation

The data generation pipeline is built upon **Unreal Engine 5 (UE5)**, a state-of-the-art rendering engine that provides photorealistic quality and performance. This synthetic approach offers full control over scene content, object placement, and ground-truth annotation, which is a significant advantage over real-world datasets.

1. **Scenario Generation:**
 - **Set Stage:** An evaluation controller defines the scene by selecting one of five diverse environments (Forest, Desert, Winter Town, Bridge, City) and a specific set of 3D assets. These assets are curated to be semantically coherent with their environments (e.g., camels in the desert) and statistically prominent in common pre-training datasets like ImageNet, ensuring semantic recognition is not the primary challenge. Critically, source objects are isotropic (e.g., a tree, rock) to simplify relative position definition. Objects (source, target, and a human viewpoint for allocentric tasks) are randomly positioned on the ground, with physics-aware placement via raycasting to handle uneven terrain.
 - **Set Camera:** The camera position is sampled, and its orientation is dynamically locked to center the relevant objects, ensuring they are visible within the camera's frustum. Visibility and occlusion control (using AABB overlap checks) prevent objects from being cut off or hidden, guaranteeing clear scene composition.
 - **Geometric Ambiguity Control:** To ensure mathematically rigorous and unambiguous ground-truth labels, a strict rejection sampling strategy is employed. Scenarios where the target object falls within ±15° angular regions along the diagonals (e.g., between "front" and "left") relative to the viewpoint's forward direction are discarded. This ensures that all retained samples have indisputable ground-truth labels ({left, right, front, back}).

2. **Image and Ground Truth Capture:**
 - The UE5 rendering pipeline synthesizes high-fidelity 2D RGB images.
 - Simultaneously, the UnrealCV plugin is used to capture precise 6-DoF positional information (x,y,z, roll, pitch, yaw) of all objects and the camera, along with pixel-perfect ground-truth segmentation masks.
 - This metadata is used to derive the exact spatial relation label for each image.
 - **Dataset Size:** 5,000 images per environment for egocentric tasks and 10,000 images per environment for allocentric tasks to mitigate overfitting, given the higher complexity of the latter.

#### 3.2. Spatial Relation Recognition Task (SpaRRTa) Definition

The task involves a four-way classification of the spatial direction from a "source" object to a "target" object relative to a specified "viewpoint."

- **Egocentric Variant (SpaRRTa-ego):** The camera itself serves as the viewpoint. The model predicts where the target lies relative to the source *as seen by the camera*. This directly assesses spatial relations observable from the input image.
- **Allocentric Variant (SpaRRTa-allo):** A third object (a human agent present in every scene) is designated as the viewpoint. The model must predict the spatial relation from the source to the target *from the human's perspective*. This is significantly more challenging as it requires implicit perspective-taking, decoupling the spatial relation from the model's own visual input.

#### 3.3. Representation Probing

Once data is generated, visual models are evaluated with frozen backbones to assess their intrinsic pre-trained representations.

1. **Evaluated Visual Foundation Models (VFMs):** A diverse suite of VFMs, predominantly using a ViT-Base/16 backbone (with ViT-L/14 exceptions for VGGT and DINO-v2 (+reg)), are benchmarked. These include:
 - **Joint-Embedding Architectures (JEA):** DINO, DINO-v2, DINOv3.
 - **Masked Image Modeling (MIM):** MAE, MaskFeat, SPA, CroCo, CroCoV2.
 - **Supervised & Weakly Supervised:** VGGT (3D supervised), DeiT (ImageNet supervised), CLIP (image-text contrastive).

2. **Probing Mechanisms:** Three types of lightweight probing heads are attached to the final transformer block's output features:
 - **Linear Probing (LP) with Global Average Pooling (GAP):** Aggregates all patch tokens into a single global vector, assuming uniform spatial information.
 - **Attention-Based Multiple Instance Learning Pooling (AbMILP):** Learns scalar importance weights for each patch, selectively aggregating relevant regions.
 - **Efficient Probing:** Employs multiple learnable query vectors that attend directly to the input feature space, allowing specialized attention to different objects or scene components.

3. **Evaluation Protocol:**
 - For each VFM, environment, and specific object triple (source, target, viewpoint), a dedicated probe is trained.
 - Datasets are split 80/10/10 for training, validation, and testing.
 - Probes are trained for a specified number of epochs (1000 for LP, 500 for AbMILP/Efficient Probing) using AdamW optimizer and cosine decay scheduler.
 - Results are reported as test accuracies, averaged over 2 random seeds and 3 distinct object triples per environment, ensuring robustness.

#### 3.4. Analysis of Spatial Representation Mechanisms

To understand *how* spatial representations are formed, the attention mechanisms of DINO-v2 (+reg) and VGGT (chosen due to VGGT's initialization from DINO-v2 and subsequent 3D fine-tuning) are analyzed.
- **Methodology:** Attention weights are computed from source object patches (or the [CLS] token) to all other regions (other objects, background, [CLS] token, register tokens) across all heads and layers.
- **Focus:** Quantifying the layer-wise evolution of these mean attention scores, especially the redirection of attention from object-to-self to cross-object dependencies.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of Visual Foundation Models (VFMs) on SpaRRTa reveals several critical insights into their spatial reasoning capabilities:

1. **Spatial Information Resides in Dense Patch Representations:**
 - A consistent performance hierarchy is observed across all models: Linear Probing (GAP) < AbMILP < Efficient Probing. This indicates that **spatial information is primarily encoded in local patch features** and is largely diluted or obscured by global pooling methods (like GAP).
 - Efficient Probing, with its multi-query attention mechanisms, consistently yields substantial gains, demonstrating its ability to selectively emphasize and aggregate information from relevant patches corresponding to source, target, and viewpoint objects. This confirms the importance of structured aggregation for uncovering spatial knowledge.

2. **Impact of 3D Supervision on Patch-Level Structure:**
 - VGGT, a model trained with explicit 3D supervision, does not outperform DINO-v2 (its parent model initialized for semantic tasks) under linear probing. However, VGGT significantly surpasses DINO-v2 when using AbMILP and Efficient Probing.
 - This suggests that 3D supervision enriches the **patch-level geometric structure** of representations, making precise spatial information more available, but this information is largely inaccessible to global pooling, requiring spatially-aware probes to extract it.

3. **Allocentric Reasoning: A Significant Challenge:**
 - Accuracy on the **allocentric variant of SpaRRTa is consistently and substantially lower** than on the egocentric variant across all models, probing strategies, and environments.
 - This performance gap underscores the difficulty of perspective-taking, which requires models to reason about spatial relations from a non-camera viewpoint, demanding a more abstract understanding of object arrangement.

4. **Consistent Hierarchy of Spatial Performance:**
 - A stable ranking of models emerges: **CLIP and DeiT consistently exhibit the lowest performance**, indicating limited spatial reasoning support.
 - The **DINO family (DINO-v2, DINOv3), pretrained on large-scale datasets, shows strong and consistent performance**.
 - **VGGT (with efficient probing) and MAE** also achieve competitive performance, with MAE notable for its pixel-level reconstruction objective. This suggests that models preserving rich, structured patch-level features are more adept at spatial reasoning.

5. **Environmental Complexity Affects Spatial Reasoning:**
 - Models achieve **higher accuracy in visually homogeneous environments** (e.g., Desert, Forest) and **lower performance in cluttered scenes** (e.g., City, Winter Town). This indicates that background complexity and visual noise interfere with identifying relevant patches for spatial relation reasoning, highlighting a general sensitivity of VFMs to environmental clutter.

6. **SpaRRTa Correlates with 3D Geometry, Not Semantics:**
 - A significant **positive correlation (R² ≈ 0.40)** is found between SpaRRTa accuracy and performance on 3D mid-level geometric tasks (depth estimation, camera pose estimation). This validates SpaRRTa's grounding in latent geometric representations.
 - In contrast, there is a **negligible correlation (R² ≈ 0.00)** between SpaRRTa and high-level semantic classification benchmarks. This confirms that SpaRRTa targets a distinct spatial skill set, separate from semantic recognition.
 - The overall correlation matrix shows a clear clustering of spatial tasks (SpaRRTa-ego, SpaRRTa-allo, camera pose, depth estimation), distinct from semantic classification, reinforcing 3D awareness as an independent capability.

7. **3D Supervision Induces Relational Attention Reorganization:**
 - Analysis of attention dynamics in VGGT versus DINO-v2 reveals a **fundamental reorganization of information flow in deeper layers (15-24) of VGGT**.
 - VGGT's 3D supervision leads to a **reduction in object-to-self attention and a sharper increase in cross-object attention**. This signifies a shift from object-centric feature extraction to actively encoding spatial dependencies between distinct entities.
 - For VGGT, the `[CLS]` token's attention budget shifts towards "register tokens," and object-relative spatial details are distributed across local patch tokens. This explains why VGGT underperforms DINO-v2 with linear probing on `[CLS]` but surpasses it with efficient probing, which aggregates distributed patch information.

8. **Spatial Information Peaks in Late-Intermediate Layers:**
 - Layer-wise probing shows that spatial reasoning accuracy **improves through initial layers and peaks in late-intermediate layers (e.g., Layer 18-20 for ViT-L)**, rather than at the final output layer. The final layers of VFMs, often optimized for high-level semantic abstraction, tend to discard precise geometric details.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of SpaRRTa marks a significant contribution to the field of visual AI, particularly in bridging the gap between current Visual Foundation Models (VFMs) and their requirements for robust embodied intelligence. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Addressing a Critical Gap in VFM Evaluation:** SpaRRTa uniquely targets *abstract, relational spatial awareness*, a capability distinct from both precise metric 3D estimation and high-level semantic understanding. By demonstrating that existing benchmarks do not sufficiently cover this aspect of intelligence, SpaRRTa fills a crucial void, offering a more holistic evaluation of VFM capabilities essential for interacting with the physical world.

2. **A Powerful Diagnostic Tool:** The benchmark serves as an invaluable diagnostic instrument. Researchers can use SpaRRTa to pinpoint specific strengths and weaknesses of VFMs in spatial reasoning, understand *where* spatial information is encoded (e.g., patch-level vs. global features, intermediate vs. final layers), and *how* different pre-training objectives (e.g., 2D self-supervision vs. explicit 3D supervision) influence these representations. This detailed understanding goes beyond mere performance metrics, offering insights into the underlying mechanisms.

3. **Guiding Future VFM Development:** The insights gleaned from SpaRRTa can directly inform the design and training of future VFMs. For instance, the findings emphasize the importance of retaining rich, structured patch-level features and employing spatially structured probing mechanisms. Developers can leverage these findings to:
 - **Design better architectures:** Prioritizing mechanisms that preserve local geometric detail.
 - **Refine pre-training objectives:** Developing objectives that explicitly foster abstract spatial reasoning and perspective-taking, beyond just metric 3D prediction.
 - **Optimize feature extraction:** Potentially extracting features from late-intermediate layers for tasks requiring precise spatial understanding, rather than defaulting to the final layer.

4. **Advancing Embodied and Interactive AI:** The core motivation for SpaRRTa is to enable VFMs to serve as more capable perception systems for embodied agents. By providing a benchmark that measures a VFM's ability to reason about relative object positions and adapt to different viewpoints, SpaRRTa directly contributes to the development of robots and autonomous systems that can navigate, manipulate objects, and make decisions more intelligently in complex, dynamic environments. This capability is fundamental for human-like interaction and autonomy.

5. **Understanding Vision Transformer Mechanisms:** The detailed attention analysis of models like DINO-v2 and VGGT offers fundamental insights into how different training regimes reorganize information flow within Vision Transformers. The observation of increased cross-object attention in 3D-supervised models, coupled with the differing efficacy of probing global vs. local tokens, deepens our understanding of how these powerful models encode and process complex visual information.

6. **Fostering Open Science and Reproducibility:** The commitment to publicly release the complete SpaRRTa dataset (including synthetic images and geometric metadata) and the source code for the Unreal Engine data generation pipeline and evaluation framework is highly significant. This promotes transparency, enables reproducibility, and encourages wider adoption and further research within the community, ensuring its impact extends beyond this initial publication.

In conclusion, SpaRRTa is more than just another benchmark; it's a foundational tool that addresses a critical, under-explored dimension of visual intelligence. Its adoption promises to accelerate the development of VFMs that are not only semantically aware but also spatially intelligent, bringing us closer to truly general-purpose visual AI systems.
