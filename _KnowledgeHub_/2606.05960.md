# Towards a Data Flywheel for Embodied Intelligence in Logistics

> [!summary] Summary
> This research outlines a "logistics data flywheel" framework to enhance embodied intelligence in industrial logistics by converting daily operations into reusable data assets. It introduces WM-DAgger, an initial method that uses World Models to generate reliable synthetic recovery data, resulting in significantly improved few-shot imitation learning performance across various robot manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Scaling embodied intelligence in industrial logistics faces challenges due to the high cost and limited diversity of training data from laboratory settings.
- Current robot learning struggles to cover the full spectrum of manipulation cases, especially infrequent "long-tail" events, under stringent industrial reliability requirements.
- Acquiring sufficient real human demonstrations for complex recovery behaviors in industrial settings is often impractical and expensive.

## Method

- The paper proposes a "logistics data flywheel" framework to create a self-improving embodied intelligence system by continuously converting industrial operations into data, generating supervision, and incorporating deployment feedback.
- WM-DAgger is introduced as a method to synthesize recovery trajectories using ==World Model==s, reducing the reliance on human-in-the-loop supervision for out-of-distribution states.
- The WM-DAgger framework incorporates corrective action synthesis to generate task-oriented recovery and consistency-guided filtering to ensure the physical plausibility and task relevance of synthetic data.

## Results

- WM-DAgger achieved a **93.3%** success rate on a soft bag pushing task with only 5 expert demonstrations, substantially outperforming Standard Behavioral Cloning (**26.7%**) and Diffusion-based Data Augmentation (**40.0%**).
- On a pick-and-place task using 20 demonstrations, WM-DAgger attained an **84.4%** success rate for seen objects and **70.0%** for unseen objects, demonstrating robustness to novel object instances.
- The framework consistently improved performance across diverse manipulation tasks, including Ballot Insertion (**73.3%**) and Towel Folding (**46.7%**), showing its applicability to rigid, deformable, and contact-rich interactions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Industrial logistics environments inherently generate large volumes of diverse, real-world embodied data that can be effectively leveraged for robot learning if properly structured.
> - World Models can function as reliable data engines, synthesizing valuable out-of-distribution recovery supervision that significantly enhances policy robustness with limited real demonstrations.
> - A joint approach to data generation and validation, such as consistency-guided filtering, is crucial for mitigating issues like hallucination in synthetic data and ensuring its utility for policy learning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Embodied intelligence, involving robots that interact with the physical world, is undergoing a transition from laboratory research to industrial deployment. The logistics industry is identified as a key application sector where robust and adaptable robots are needed to perform tasks such as parcel manipulation, which often involves handling diverse, long-tail cases under stringent reliability and throughput requirements.

Historically, robotic systems have relied on traditional perception-planning-control pipelines, which often involve manually designing task-specific heuristic rules. Recent advancements in robot learning, including imitation learning, reinforcement learning, and vision-language-action (VLA) models, suggest an alternative paradigm. These learning-based approaches aim to enable robots to acquire flexible and dexterous manipulation skills directly from data, moving towards end-to-end policies.

However, a fundamental challenge in scaling this data-driven paradigm is the availability and management of embodied data. Current robot learning systems utilize various data sources, such as human teleoperation, portable and embodiment-agnostic demonstrations (e.g., UMI-style), cross-embodiment robot datasets, and unlabeled human videos. Additionally, World Models, which learn action-conditioned dynamics and predict future observations, offer a complementary method for synthesizing additional training trajectories and facilitating learning from imagined interactions.

Despite these efforts, many existing data schemes primarily operate within laboratory-level demonstration tasks and curated benchmarks. While such data is typically well-structured and aligned with policy learning objectives, its high collection cost and limited coverage of the complex, long-tail operational scenarios encountered in real-world industrial settings pose significant bottlenecks for widespread deployment of embodied agents.

This research positions itself within this landscape by addressing the limitations of laboratory-centric data collection and leveraging the unique data generation capabilities of industrial logistics environments. It recognizes that real logistics sites continuously produce a large volume of diverse embodied data—including human operation videos, labeled demonstrations, robot execution logs, failure cases, and system-level performance metrics—that is generated naturally during daily operations. This data is characterized by its scale, diversity, low collection cost, and capture of "in-the-wild" physical interactions under actual operational constraints, encompassing various parcel types, workspace layouts, and human manipulation strategies. The work aims to bridge the gap between this abundant, unstructured industrial data and the structured data requirements of advanced robot learning techniques, particularly focusing on how this data can be effectively collected, organized, generated, validated, and reused to build robust and continually improving embodied intelligence systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the growing demand for embodied intelligence in industrial settings, specifically within the logistics sector. While robot learning shows promise for flexible manipulation, its scalability in real-world industrial environments is constrained by the limitations of current data acquisition and utilization strategies. The logistics industry, with its continuous and diverse operational data generation, presents a unique opportunity to overcome these data-centric challenges.

The overarching objective of this research is to construct a **logistics data flywheel**. This framework aims to convert the daily operations of industrial logistics into reusable data assets, utilize advanced models to generate reliable supervision for complex or infrequent manipulation cases, and feed deployment feedback back into the system for continuous policy improvement. This closed-loop approach is designed to foster a self-improving embodied intelligence system.

To achieve this overarching goal, the research identifies and seeks to address three specific research questions (RQs):

- **RQ1: How can legacy logistics automation data be converted into reusable embodied learning datasets?**
 - **Motivation:** Industrial logistics sites already generate vast quantities of data from traditional automation systems. Although this data was not originally intended for robot learning, it contains rich information about machine actions and environmental dynamics.
 - **Objective:** To develop methods for aligning heterogeneous industrial data sources into structured observation-action-outcome sequences that are suitable for training advanced models like World Models and Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, and for evaluating policies, especially for long-tail manipulation scenarios.

- **RQ2: How can World Models generate reliable supervision for robust policy learning from limited demonstrations?**
 - **Motivation:** Acquiring real human demonstrations is often costly and impractical to cover the full spectrum of manipulation cases, particularly infrequent "long-tail" events like grasp failures and necessary recovery behaviors. World Models have the capacity to synthesize additional trajectories beyond the limited state coverage of real demonstrations, potentially addressing this gap. However, data generated by World Models can suffer from hallucinations, physical inconsistencies, or task-irrelevant actions, which could mislead policy learning.
 - **Objective:** To devise strategies that enable World Models to serve as reliable data engines. This involves generating task-useful, out-of-distribution supervision data for robust policy learning, while simultaneously developing mechanisms to validate and filter out unreliable or misleading synthetic data. This is particularly relevant for handling complex parcel manipulation scenarios where failures are difficult to fully demonstrate.

- **RQ3: How can logistics World Foundation Models be built and continuously improved through deployment feedback?**
 - **Motivation:** Large-scale unlabeled logistics videos capture diverse parcel flows, human manipulation strategies, and complex physical interactions, offering a rich source for general dynamics learning. Concurrently, labeled demonstrations and robot rollouts provide action supervision, albeit at a smaller scale. Integrating these multi-modal data streams and enabling continuous learning from real-world deployment outcomes is crucial for developing highly capable and adaptable robot policies.
 - **Objective:** To establish a continuous learning loop that combines large-scale video-only pretraining, action-conditioned post-training using labeled data, and feedback from real-world robot deployments. This feedback mechanism should allow World Models and robot policies to learn from both historical operational data and the results of their own actions in the field, leading to sustained improvement.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research proposes an overarching **logistics industry embodied data flywheel** as its framework. This flywheel conceptualizes a closed-loop system where data collection from industrial operations, data generation using World Models, and feedback from deployment are interconnected and continuously reinforce each other.

As an initial result towards addressing these research questions, particularly RQ2 concerning reliable supervision from World Models, the paper introduces **WM-DAgger**. This framework addresses the challenge of compounding errors in imitation learning, where small prediction errors can lead a robot into out-of-distribution (OOD) states, potentially resulting in failures. Traditional data aggregation methods like DAgger involve human experts providing recovery demonstrations during policy rollouts, which is often difficult to scale in cost-sensitive industrial environments.

WM-DAgger leverages World Models to synthesize recovery trajectories, thereby reducing the reliance on human-in-the-loop supervision. The methodology involves the following steps:

1. **World Model Training:** An action-conditioned World Model is initially trained using a combination of limited real demonstrations and exploratory interaction data. This model learns to predict future visual observations based on current observations and candidate robot actions.

2. **Synthetic Recovery Data Generation:** The trained World Model is then used to generate synthetic recovery trajectories. The utility of this synthetic data for downstream policy learning depends on its task relevance and physical plausibility. WM-DAgger incorporates two mechanisms to ensure this:
 - **Corrective Action Synthesis:** This module generates task-oriented recovery trajectories. It operates by intentionally perturbing expert demonstrations to create nearby OOD states. Subsequently, the World Model is guided to predict actions that bring the robot back towards the manifold of successful expert trajectories. This process ensures that the synthesized actions align with the task objective of recovery.
 - **Consistency-Guided Filtering:** To mitigate the issues of hallucination or physically implausible transitions often associated with generated data, this module filters out unreliable synthetic trajectories. It compares the terminal frames of generated video-action trajectories with corresponding real demonstration frames. If the generated terminal frames are visually inconsistent with the expected real outcomes, the entire synthetic trajectory is discarded before being used for policy training.

3. **Policy Training:** The filtered, high-quality synthetic recovery data is then aggregated with the initial set of real demonstrations. This combined dataset is used to train a more robust imitation learning policy, which is better equipped to handle OOD states and recover from potential errors.

Building upon WM-DAgger, the research outlines future directions to fully realize the logistics data flywheel:

- **Transforming Legacy Automation Data (Addressing RQ1):** Future work will focus on converting heterogeneous data from traditional logistics automation systems into structured, reusable learning datasets. This involves aligning multi-sourced data into coherent observation-action-outcome sequences, segmenting long operational videos into meaningful interaction units (e.g., parcel arrival, clustering, grasping), and enriching these segments with lightweight metadata such as parcel type, workspace layout, and action outcomes. The goal is to establish an industrial logistics benchmark suitable for World Model pretraining, action-conditioned prediction, weakly supervised representation learning, and long-tail policy evaluation.

- **Developing World Foundation Models with Deployment Feedback (Addressing RQ3):** This involves a multi-stage training and continuous improvement process. Logistics World Models will be pretrained using large-scale unlabeled operational videos to learn general dynamics. Subsequently, they will be post-trained using labeled demonstrations and aligned robot or automation actions to acquire action-conditioned prediction capabilities. These models are intended to support advanced functionalities like synthetic data generation, imagined rollouts, and World Action Models. A crucial component is the integration of deployment feedback to close the learning loop. Successful robot rollouts will be reused for offline fine-tuning, while failed rollouts will be analyzed to generate targeted recovery data and facilitate outcome-conditioned post-training, thus enabling continuous improvement of both the World Models and the robot policies.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The initial results of this research are demonstrated through the **WM-DAgger** framework, which focuses on generating reliable recovery supervision for robust imitation learning. Real-world experiments were conducted across various manipulation tasks to evaluate WM-DAgger's efficacy.

The key findings are summarized by the success rates achieved on different evaluation tasks, as presented in the paper's Table 1:

- **Soft Bag Pushing Task (related to logistics parcel handling):**
 - With only **5 expert demonstrations**, WM-DAgger achieved a **93.3% success rate**. This represents a substantial improvement compared to Standard Behavioral Cloning (26.7%) and Diffusion-based Data Augmentation (DMD) (40.0%).
 - With **20 expert demonstrations**, WM-DAgger maintained high performance at **96.7% success rate**, again outperforming Standard BC (30.0%) and DMD (56.7%).

- **Pick-and-Place Task:**
 - For **seen objects**, with 20 demonstrations, WM-DAgger achieved an **84.4% success rate**, significantly higher than Standard BC (11.1%) and DMD (32.2%).
 - For **unseen objects**, with 20 demonstrations, WM-DAgger attained a **70.0% success rate**, demonstrating robustness to novel object instances, compared to Standard BC (5.0%) and DMD (11.8%).

- **Other Manipulation Tasks (with 20 demonstrations):**
 - **Ballot Insertion:** WM-DAgger achieved a **73.3% success rate**, outperforming Standard BC (13.3%) and DMD (26.7%).
 - **Towel Folding (deformable object manipulation):** WM-DAgger achieved a **46.7% success rate**, which was an improvement over Standard BC (0.0%) and DMD (10.0%), indicating its utility even in complex deformable object tasks.

These results indicate that WM-DAgger can substantially improve few-shot imitation learning performance across a range of manipulation tasks, including those involving rigid objects, deformable objects, and contact-rich interactions. The framework's ability to synthesize large quantities of useful recovery data (1500 generated data points in each experimental setup) for improving policy robustness from limited real demonstrations was consistently observed.

The implications of these findings are that World Models can indeed serve as effective data engines for industrial embodied intelligence. Furthermore, the success of WM-DAgger highlights the importance of jointly considering data generation and data validation. In industrial settings, the value of synthetic embodied data is realized when it leads to measurable improvements in the target policy's behavior under real deployment conditions, and WM-DAgger demonstrated this capability by filtering generated data for consistency and relevance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of embodied intelligence by proposing a systematic framework, the "logistics data flywheel," designed to facilitate the transition of learning-based robotic systems from laboratory environments to industrial deployment. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Enabling Scalable Embodied Intelligence in Industry:** The work directly addresses a critical bottleneck in deploying embodied AI in industrial logistics: the scarcity and cost of obtaining diverse, high-quality, and robust training data for long-tail scenarios. By proposing a framework to convert routine industrial operations into reusable data assets, the research offers a pathway for scaling robot learning in real-world settings that generate abundant, albeit often unstructured, operational data.

- **Leveraging Industrial Data for Robot Learning:** The research highlights the unique opportunity presented by the logistics industry, where large volumes of in-the-wild multimodal data are generated naturally. By devising methods to transform this legacy and operational data into valuable learning resources, the project aims to unlock a previously underutilized resource for robot policy improvement.

- **Advancing Data-Centric AI for Robotics:** The WM-DAgger framework demonstrates a concrete method for generating reliable synthetic data for out-of-distribution recovery, a challenging aspect of robot robustness. This approach reduces the reliance on costly human-in-the-loop data collection, aligning with the needs of industrial scalability. The emphasis on both data generation *and* validation is critical for ensuring the utility of synthetic data in safety-critical applications.

- **Developing Robust Robot Policies:** By improving performance on few-shot imitation learning and enhancing recovery capabilities, the research contributes to the development of more robust and reliable robot policies. This is crucial for industrial applications where robots must operate consistently and autonomously, minimizing downtime due to unexpected or novel situations. The results from WM-DAgger on diverse manipulation tasks suggest a generalizable approach to improving robot dexterity.

- **Foundational for Continuous Learning Systems:** The proposed "data flywheel" and "logistics World Foundation Models" represent a vision for continuous learning systems in robotics. By integrating operational data collection, World Model-based generation, and deployment feedback into a closed loop, the research lays the groundwork for robotic systems that can adapt and improve over time, learning from both historical data and real-world experience. This self-improving capability could lead to significant reductions in operational costs and increases in efficiency in logistics and potentially other industrial sectors.

- **Broader Applicability:** While grounded in logistics, the principles of leveraging existing operational data, synthesizing reliable training data with World Models, and establishing a data flywheel for continuous improvement could be transferable to other industrial domains seeking to deploy embodied AI, such as manufacturing, agriculture, or hazardous environment exploration.
