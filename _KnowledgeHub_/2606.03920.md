# Benchmarking Visual State Tracking in Multimodal Video Understanding

> [!summary] Summary
> A new benchmark, VSTAT, evaluates Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) on continuous visual state tracking within videos, revealing a significant performance gap where human accuracy reaches 90.5% compared to 44.4% for the top proprietary MLLM and 35.1% for the best open-source MLLM. The study identifies visual perception, rather than reasoning, as the primary bottleneck for MLLMs in these tasks.

## Problem

- Existing MLLM video benchmarks do not adequately assess models' ability to track continuous dynamics and evolving states over extended video durations.
- Many current benchmarks allow MLLMs to infer answers from isolated keyframes or end states, negating the need for continuous perception and integration of events throughout a video.
- Prior attempts to evaluate visual state tracking were limited to highly simplified synthetic tasks, lacking the diversity and complexity of real-world scenarios.

## Method

- The **VSTAT** benchmark was developed, featuring 834 video clips (synthetic and real-world) and 1,500 questions designed to explicitly require continuous ==visual state tracking== without visual shortcuts.
- VSTAT questions and videos are categorized by 'State Complexity' (e.g., tracking counts, locations, attributes, with atomic, sequence, set, or dictionary structures) and 'Perceptual Complexity' (e.g., occlusion, camera motion).
- Diagnostic analyses, including comparing performance on temporally stretched videos, comparing video input to text-only state transcriptions, and analyzing MLLM thinking traces, were conducted to pinpoint failure modes.

## Results

- Human performance on VSTAT averaged **90.5%** accuracy, substantially exceeding MLLM performance.
- The top-performing proprietary MLLM (Gemini-3.1 Pro) achieved **44.4%** accuracy, while the best open-source model (LLaVA-OV-2-8B) reached **35.1%**, often performing below the **37.8%** 'Chance Level (Frequency)' baseline.
- Preliminary evaluations showed that current agentic frameworks (e.g., AVP, Claude Code, Codex) achieved near chance-level accuracy on a VSTAT subset, failing to resolve the underlying perceptual challenges.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Visual perception is the fundamental bottleneck for MLLMs in visual state tracking; models perform near-perfectly with text transcriptions of events but poorly with original video input, even on short clips.
> - Increasing model size, leveraging motion-grounded training, or employing 'thinking modes' do not consistently improve MLLM performance on VSTAT, sometimes leading to degradation or increased hallucination.
> - The dominant failure modes for MLLMs include incorrect event recognition (over 50% of errors), followed by issues in consistently associating events with specific entities across frames.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of multimodal video understanding has experienced substantial advancements, largely driven by the development of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). These models have demonstrated capabilities in semantic understanding and action recognition across various video benchmarks. However, the paper identifies a limitation within the current evaluation paradigms: most existing benchmarks do not adequately assess an MLLM's ability to track continuous dynamics and evolving states over extended video durations.

Current benchmarks often allow models to infer answers from a small subset of keyframes, salient moments, or visible end states, without necessitating continuous perception or the integration of events throughout the entire video stream. While some recent efforts have explored this gap, they are often restricted to highly simplified synthetic tasks, such as the shell game, and lack diversity in real-world scenarios.

This work addresses this specific limitation by introducing VSTAT, a video-based benchmark explicitly designed to diagnose and evaluate the visual state tracking capability of MLLMs. VSTAT extends beyond pixel-level object tracking to encompass the tracking of underlying latent state representations that evolve continuously within a video stream. By focusing on tasks that explicitly require continuous perception and integration of events, VSTAT aims to reveal fundamental limitations in current MLLMs that are not apparent in existing evaluations, thereby complementing the broader landscape of video understanding benchmarks. It positions itself as a diagnostic tool to drive advancements in MLLM architectures and training methodologies, particularly concerning their ability to reason about continuous visual processes, a critical aspect for real-world applications such as robotics and assistive agents.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce and utilize VSTAT, a novel benchmark, to diagnose the visual state tracking capabilities of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) in video understanding. This objective is motivated by several detailed considerations:

#### 2.1. Fundamental Human Capacity vs. MLLM Limitations

The authors observe that humans naturally engage in continuous visual state tracking when interpreting videos. For instance, watching a basketball game involves continuously updating scores, tracking player actions, and associating events with specific entities. This capacity is considered fundamental to how humans learn from and reason about visual demonstrations. In contrast, it is largely unexplored whether current MLLMs possess a similar ability to understand continuous dynamics and track evolving states throughout a procedural video. The research aims to clarify this discrepancy and measure the performance gap.

#### 2.2. Shortcomings of Existing Video Understanding Benchmarks

A significant motivation for VSTAT stems from the limitations identified in current MLLM video benchmarks. Many existing benchmarks, despite indicating strong MLLM performance, do not explicitly require continuous state tracking. Models can often infer answers by focusing on isolated keyframes, salient moments, or the final state of a video, without needing to process and integrate information across the entire temporal sequence. This allows MLLMs to achieve high scores without genuinely understanding how states evolve over time. The paper argues that such benchmarks do not genuinely reflect an MLLM's capacity for continuous visual reasoning, leaving a crucial capability unevaluated.

#### 2.3. Need for Diverse and Challenging State Tracking Scenarios

Prior attempts to evaluate state tracking have been limited to a few simple synthetic tasks, such as variations of the shell game. While these tasks illustrate the concept, they do not cover the diversity and complexity of real-world scenarios or the varied perceptual challenges that arise. The motivation is to create a benchmark that:
- **Avoids visual shortcuts**: Questions are designed such that answers cannot be derived from single frames or short segments, compelling models to engage in continuous perception.
- **Covers diverse state complexities**: Tasks require tracking different types of information, such as counts, locations, and attributes, and involve various data structures for these states (atomic, sequence, set, dictionary).
- **Includes varied perceptual challenges**: Videos contain real-world complexities like occlusion, camera motion, homogeneity of entities, symbolic decoding, multi-entity attribution, and event ambiguity, which make visual perception difficult.

#### 2.4. Diagnostic Tool for MLLM Development

Beyond mere evaluation, a key motivation is to use VSTAT as a diagnostic tool. By exposing specific failure modes and identifying underlying bottlenecks, the benchmark aims to guide future research and development in MLLM architectures, pre-training strategies, and post-training techniques. The goal is to move beyond simply measuring overall performance to understanding *why* MLLMs fail on complex, continuous video understanding tasks, thereby facilitating the creation of more robust and human-like MLLMs. This includes distinguishing between failures in visual perception versus reasoning capabilities.

#### 2.5. Relevance to Real-World Applications

The ability to continuously track visual states is critical for various real-world applications. Examples include robotics, where continuous perception is essential for long-horizon manipulation tasks; surveillance systems that require tracking events and entities over time; and assistive agents that need to understand dynamic environments. The paper posits that addressing the gap in visual state tracking is crucial for developing MLLMs that can operate effectively in such complex, dynamic environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces the Visual STAte Tracking benchmark (VSTAT) and employs a systematic approach to evaluate and diagnose the visual state tracking capabilities of MLLMs.

#### 3.1. VSTAT Benchmark Design

VSTAT adopts a standard question-answering (QA) format: an MLLM receives a video stream and a question, then outputs an answer. The core design principle is that answers cannot be inferred from single keyframes or short segments, compelling models to process the entire video and continuously track and update relevant information.

- **Data Curation**:
 - **Scale**: The benchmark comprises 834 video clips paired with 1,500 questions.
 - **Sources**:
 - **Synthetic Videos**: 450 clips were generated from 9 distinct 3D environments using Blender software (e.g., rolling die, Americano making, tightening bolts, rotating shell game). These allow for controlled manipulation of events and states.
 - **Real-World Videos**: 304 clips were collected from YouTube and 80 clips were self-recorded in scripted settings. These cover diverse procedural processes (e.g., Rubik's cube, basketball, cooking, order packing) and exhibit real-world perceptual challenges like continuous camera movement and occlusions.
- **Question-Answer Generation**:
 - **No Visual Shortcuts**: Questions are explicitly designed to prevent answers from being derived from sparse visual information, requiring full temporal understanding.
 - **Diverse Query Types**: Questions vary in the type of information to be tracked (e.g., locations, counts, attributes like characters) and the structure of the state (e.g., atomic values, sequences, sets, or dictionaries).
 - **"One Video, Multiple Questions"**: Multiple questions are posed for each video, allowing for comprehensive analysis of different aspects of visual state tracking.
 - **Format**: Includes numerical questions (NQs) and multiple-choice questions (MCQs) with carefully designed distractors.
 - **Human-in-the-Loop Verification**: All videos, questions, answers, and category labels are manually annotated and reviewed through a verification protocol to ensure accuracy and reduce ambiguity.
- **Taxonomy for Analysis**: Each video-question pair is labeled along two axes to facilitate detailed performance breakdown:
 - **State Complexity**:
 - *Element Type*: Count, Location, Attribute.
 - *Structure*: Atomic (single value), Sequence (ordered series), Set (unordered subset), Dictionary (map of entities to values).
 - **Perceptual Complexity**: Occlusion, Camera motion, Homogeneity, Symbolic decoding, Multi-entity attribution, Event ambiguity.

#### 3.2. Evaluation Setup

- **Models Evaluated**:
 - **Proprietary API Models**: Gemini-3.1 Pro and Gemini-3.0 Flash (with low and high thinking levels).
 - **Open-Source Models**: Qwen3VL, Cambrian-S, MiMo-VL, InternVL3.5, LLaVA-OV, Molmo2 (varying model sizes and thinking modes, where applicable).
 - **Agentic Frameworks**: AVP (video agent), Claude Code, and Codex (coding agents) for a preliminary case study.
- **Metrics**: The average of accuracy on MCQs and Mean Relative Accuracy (MRA) on NQs is reported.
- **Frame Handling**: Open-source models were evaluated by sweeping maximum frame budgets (16, 32, 64, 128 uniformly sampled frames), reporting the best score. Proprietary models used a MEDIUM resolution setting. LLaVA-OV-2 with a codec video backend was also tested.
- **Baselines**:
 - **Chance Level (Random)**: Random selection accuracy for MCQs.
 - **Chance Level (Frequency)**: Performance achieved by always selecting the most frequent answer.
 - **Human Performance**: Measured by authors (not involved in question construction) to establish an upper bound and gauge task difficulty.

#### 3.3. Diagnostic Analysis Approaches

- **Impact of Frame Subsampling**: Compared MLLM performance on original synthetic videos (5 seconds) against temporally stretched versions (where each event spans more frames) to check if frame subsampling causes event ambiguity.
- **Perception vs. Reasoning Disentanglement**: For simple synthetic tasks, MLLM performance was compared under two conditions: original video input and a text-only transcription of visible states and events. This aimed to isolate whether failures stemmed from visual perception or reasoning.
- **Failure Mode Analysis**: The thinking traces of Gemini-3.1 Pro were analyzed against the video streams in failure cases to identify recurring patterns. Three main failure modes were identified: event recognition, entity association, and state update. A quantitative error analysis was conducted.
- **Agentic Framework Study**: A small subset of VSTAT (39 video-question pairs) was used to evaluate whether agentic approaches (video agents, coding agents) could improve performance. Strict sandbox protocols were implemented to prevent contamination for coding agents.

This comprehensive methodology allows for both a broad performance evaluation of MLLMs on a challenging new benchmark and a granular diagnostic analysis of their underlying limitations in visual state tracking.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of MLLMs on VSTAT revealed several key findings regarding their visual state tracking capabilities.

#### 4.1. Substantial Performance Gap Between MLLMs and Humans

- Humans achieved an average accuracy of 90.5% on VSTAT, significantly exceeding MLLM performance.
- State-of-the-art MLLMs performed modestly above or, in many cases, below the "Chance Level (Frequency)" baseline (37.8%). The top-performing proprietary model, Gemini-3.1 Pro (low), achieved 44.4%, while the best open-source model, LLaVA-OV-2-8B, reached 35.1%. This indicates a considerable gap between MLLM and human capabilities in continuous visual state tracking.
- An exception was tasks requiring tracking of "sequence" states, which were the most challenging for humans but the best-performing (smallest gap) category for MLLMs.

#### 4.2. Limited Efficacy of Increased Model Size and Motion-Grounding

- Most open-source models performed below the Chance-Level (Frequency) baseline across various frame budgets.
- Improvements with increased model size were marginal, with some models (Molmo2, InternVL3.5) even showing slight degradation.
- Models specifically trained with motion-grounded codec streams (LLaVA-OV-2) or pixel-space object tracking data (Molmo2) did not demonstrate substantial improvements over other open-source MLLMs on VSTAT. This suggests that VSTAT evaluates a more complex form of state tracking that goes beyond these targeted objectives, requiring the tracking of latent state representations.

#### 4.3. Thinking Mode Does Not Reliably Improve Performance

- Enabling or increasing "thinking levels" or "thinking modes" in MLLMs (e.g., Gemini-3.1 Pro, Gemini-3.0 Flash, Qwen3VL-8B, InternVL3.5-8B) did not consistently improve performance; it often led to a slight degradation. For instance, Qwen3VL-8B dropped from 33.2% to 28.2% with thinking enabled.
- Analysis of thinking traces suggested that for tasks with higher perceptual complexity, a larger thinking budget could increase the likelihood of hallucination in these models.

#### 4.4. Visual Perception as the Primary Bottleneck

- **Not Frame Subsampling**: Temporally stretching videos (duplicating frames to ensure events are fully visible even at low sampling rates) resulted in only marginal performance improvement for Gemini-3.1 Pro. This indicates that event ambiguity from frame subsampling is not the primary cause of MLLM failures.
- **Perception vs. Reasoning Experiment**: On simple synthetic tasks where events could be manually transcribed into text, MLLMs performed almost perfectly when given text transcriptions. However, their performance dropped to near-chance levels when given the original video input, even for short 5-second videos. This stark contrast strongly suggests that the fundamental bottleneck lies in the visual perception of events from the continuous video stream, rather than in the model's reasoning capabilities once relevant information is extracted. The authors emphasize that such manual transcription is infeasible for most complex VSTAT tasks.

#### 4.5. Identified Failure Modes in Visual State Tracking

Analysis of Gemini-3.1 Pro's thinking traces identified three recurring failure modes:
- **Event Recognition (Dominant)**: Over 50% of failures stemmed from the model incorrectly recognizing events or failing to extract the corresponding state information from the video. Examples include misidentifying cup swaps in a shell game or hallucinating entire event traces. This suggests a core limitation in low-level visual processing.
- **Entity Association**: MLLMs struggled with consistently associating events with specific entities, especially when objects appeared visually similar or when camera motion disrupted spatial reference frames (e.g., distinguishing volleyball players in identical uniforms or tracking objects amidst continuous camera rotation).
- **State Update**: In some instances, models correctly recognized events and maintained entity associations but failed to use this information to accurately update the tracked state for question answering. This was less frequent, likely due to the advanced reasoning capabilities of current MLLMs once the correct visual information is perceived.

#### 4.6. Agentic Frameworks Do Not Resolve Failures

- Preliminary evaluation on a subset of VSTAT showed that recent agentic frameworks (AVP, Claude Code, Codex) did not significantly improve performance, achieving near chance-level accuracy.
- Coding agents (Claude Code, Codex) often spent considerable time and tokens, producing inconsistent intermediate results that led to incorrect answers. They required extensive sandboxing to prevent "contamination" from external data sources.
- Video agents (AVP) tended to "commit too early" to initial observations, sampling videos at fixed low frame rates without verification, thus failing to mitigate the underlying perceptual issues.

These findings collectively highlight visual perception of continuous events as a major, unresolved challenge for current MLLMs in video understanding, even with advanced reasoning and agentic approaches.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of VSTAT and the findings from its evaluation carry several significant implications and potential impacts on the field of multimodal AI research and its applications.

#### 5.1. Diagnostic Tool for MLLM Limitations

VSTAT's primary significance lies in its role as a diagnostic benchmark. It systematically exposes a crucial capability gap in current MLLMs: the inability to perform robust visual state tracking over continuous video streams. By designing tasks that explicitly prevent visual shortcuts and demand continuous temporal reasoning, VSTAT highlights limitations that existing, less stringent benchmarks might overlook. This diagnostic capability is essential for pinpointing specific areas where MLLMs need improvement.

#### 5.2. Guiding Future Research in MLLM Development

The research identifies visual perception as the primary bottleneck for MLLMs in state tracking, rather than reasoning capabilities. This finding is impactful because it directs future research efforts. Instead of solely focusing on enhancing the linguistic reasoning modules of MLLMs, the community can now prioritize developing more sophisticated visual encoders and temporal integration mechanisms that can:
- Accurately recognize subtle events within continuous visual streams.
- Maintain consistent entity associations despite visual similarities, occlusions, or camera movements.
- Robustly extract and update task-relevant latent state representations.
This guidance could lead to novel pre-training objectives and architectural designs for MLLMs.

#### 5.3. Advancing Real-World Applications

The capacity for visual state tracking is fundamental for numerous practical applications that involve dynamic visual environments:
- **Robotics**: For robots to perform long-horizon manipulation tasks, they need to continuously track object states, environmental changes, and task progress. VSTAT directly tests this capability, pushing MLLMs towards more reliable embodied intelligence.
- **Sports Analytics**: Tracking scores, player statistics, and event sequences in sports videos requires robust state tracking, which VSTAT evaluates.
- **Medical Video Analysis**: Monitoring physiological changes or procedural steps in surgical or diagnostic videos could benefit from improved MLLM state tracking.
- **Assistive Technologies**: Agents designed to assist humans in complex tasks would require continuous awareness of the user's and environment's states.
By driving improvements in visual state tracking, VSTAT contributes directly to the feasibility and reliability of these applications.

#### 5.4. Complementing Existing Benchmarks

VSTAT fills a critical void in the MLLM evaluation landscape. Unlike benchmarks that focus on general video understanding, event recognition, or knowledge reasoning, VSTAT specifically targets the continuous tracking of evolving latent states. Its diverse blend of synthetic and real-world videos, coupled with a comprehensive taxonomy of state and perceptual complexities, offers a more holistic assessment of temporal visual intelligence than previous, more limited state-tracking attempts.

#### 5.5. Highlighting Limitations of Current Agentic Frameworks

The finding that even advanced agentic frameworks do not trivially resolve VSTAT's challenges is significant. It suggests that simply wrapping MLLMs in planning or coding loops is insufficient if the underlying visual perception components are weak. This calls for further research into how agentic control can be integrated with more robust low-level visual processing capabilities, or how agents can explicitly address perceptual uncertainty and limitations.

#### 5.6. Promoting Open Science

The authors have open-sourced VSTAT, including its videos, questions, answers, and evaluation code. This promotes transparency, reproducibility, and collaborative research, enabling the broader community to use, extend, and build upon this work.

#### 5.7. Future Research Directions

The paper's discussion of limitations, such as the reliance on textual thinking traces, suggests avenues for future work, including vision-centric analyses of intermediate visual representations and the development of MLLMs capable of tackling even longer-form, more complex video streams. The identified risk of models overfitting to specific benchmark patterns also underscores the continuous need for diverse, out-of-distribution evaluation.

In conclusion, VSTAT makes a substantial contribution by providing a challenging, diagnostic benchmark that uncovers fundamental perceptual limitations in contemporary MLLMs regarding continuous visual state tracking. Its impact is expected to foster targeted research efforts, accelerate the development of more capable and reliable multimodal AI systems, and broaden the scope of MLLM applications in dynamic real-world environments.
