# Real-Time Gait Adaptation for Quadrupeds using Model Predictive Control and Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> This research from the Indian Institute of Science introduces a framework combining Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) control with a learned Dreamer module to enable real-time, continuous gait adaptation for quadrupedal robots. The method allows dynamic selection and smooth transitions between gaits, achieving up to 36.48% lower Cost of Transport compared to fixed-gait baselines in simulations.

## Problem

- Fixed-gait policies used in traditional MPC and many RL approaches limit quadrupedal robots' adaptability to varying speeds and terrains, as no single gait is optimal across all conditions.
- Traditional Model Predictive Control (MPC) relies on predefined gait parameters and accurate dynamic models, which restricts its flexibility and real-time online adaptation in complex environments.
- Model-free Reinforcement Learning (RL) often converges to a single dominant gait, lacking the diversity and continuous modulation needed for truly adaptive and energy-efficient locomotion.

## Method

- A two-stage framework integrates offline training of an expert RL policy and a Dreamer module (learning dynamics, reward, policy, and value models) with online deployment.
- During online deployment, a ==Model Predictive Path Integral== (MPPI) algorithm leverages the learned Dreamer module to jointly optimize both control actions and continuous gait parameters in real-time over a finite horizon.
- Gait parameters are represented as a [3x1] vector, encoding phase offsets between leg pairs, allowing for continuous modulation and smooth transitions through a wrap function and momentum-based updates.

## Results

- The framework successfully enabled continuous gait adaptation, with the robot tracking desired forward velocities smoothly while demonstrating stable, cyclic joint trajectories and continuous interlimb coordination changes.
- A significant reduction in energy consumption was observed, with the proposed method achieving up to **36.48%** lower Cost of Transport across varying target speeds compared to fixed-gait RL baselines.
- The controller achieved an inference frequency of approximately 330 Hz on an NVIDIA RTX 3080 GPU, suggesting computational feasibility for potential hardware deployment on moderately powered systems.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - No single fixed gait (trot, pace, bound, pronk) performs optimally across all commanded velocities for quadrupedal robots, highlighting the inefficiency of non-adaptive locomotion.
> - Integrating model-based planning with learned models allows for real-time, continuous adaptation of gait parameters, addressing the limitations of both fixed-gait MPC and single-gait RL policies.
> - By jointly optimizing control actions and continuous gait parameters, the system can dynamically adapt interlimb coordination to context-specific demands, improving both tracking accuracy and energy efficiency.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Real-Time Gait Adaptation for Quadrupeds using Model Predictive Control and Reinforcement Learning

#### 1.1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Quadrupedal robots have demonstrated increasing agility and versatility, replicating dynamic motions observed in animals. However, a persistent challenge in achieving truly adaptable locomotion across varied environments lies in the ability to dynamically select and transition between different gaits (e.g., walking, trotting, galloping) in real-time. Animals instinctively adjust their gaits based on factors such as energy minimization, dynamic stability, and robustness to perturbations. Current robotic systems often fall short in replicating this adaptive behavior.

Traditional Model Predictive Control (MPC) approaches have been widely employed to generate dynamically consistent and task-specific motion for quadrupedal robots. These methods typically rely on predefined gait parameters, such as contact sequences or timing offsets, which limits their flexibility when faced with changing task demands or terrains. Furthermore, MPC requires accurate dynamic models and can be computationally intensive, hindering its suitability for online adaptation in complex or unknown environments.

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has emerged as a data-driven alternative, enabling robots to learn control policies directly from interaction without requiring an explicit model. While RL can produce emergent locomotion behaviors, designing reward functions that yield diverse and stable gaits has proven challenging. Simple reward structures can lead to unstable or repetitive behaviors, while complex ones may inadvertently bias the policy towards specific, singular gaits. This often results in policies that converge to a single dominant gait, such as trotting or walking, which may be suboptimal for various conditions.

Hierarchical RL methods have attempted to address the issue of multi-gait behaviors by decomposing control into a high-level gait selector and a low-level motion controller. While these architectures can improve behavioral structure and interpretability, they often interpolate between similar symmetric gaits and may struggle to generalize beyond a limited set of predefined patterns.

More recently, hybrid strategies combining RL with model-based control, such as Dreamer-like architectures, have shown promise in complex dynamical systems. These frameworks learn a dynamics model, reward estimator, policy, and value function for long-horizon control. During inference, they often warm-start trajectory optimization using the learned RL policy to enhance convergence and adaptability. However, these hybrid approaches often inherit the core limitation of RL-based policies, which is a tendency for the underlying policy to converge to a single gait, thereby limiting true gait diversity.

The presented work aims to bridge these gaps by developing a unified framework that combines the structured planning capabilities of model predictive control with the adaptive learning abilities of reinforcement learning. The objective is to enable continuous, real-time gait adaptation by jointly optimizing both control actions and gait parameters, an capability not comprehensively provided by existing end-to-end RL, classical MPC, gait library-based, or existing Dreamer-based hybrid methods.

#### 1.2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an optimization framework that facilitates real-time, continuous gait adaptation for quadrupedal robots. This framework aims to enable robots to dynamically select and smoothly transition between gaits based on environmental context and task demands, akin to how animals adapt their locomotion.

The motivation for this work stems from several limitations observed in current quadrupedal locomotion research:

- **Suboptimal Performance of Fixed Gaits:** Most existing research efforts for multi-terrain locomotion often result in singular gait policies (e.g., trotting or walking). An ablation study performed by the authors demonstrates that no single gait (trot, pace, bound, pronk) performs optimally across all commanded velocities in terms of energy consumption and tracking accuracy. This highlights the inefficiency of employing a fixed gait, as a specific gait might be energy-efficient at one speed but highly inefficient at another.
- **Lack of Adaptability in Traditional MPC:** While Model Predictive Control is effective for generating dynamically consistent motion, it typically relies on *a priori* defined gait parameters. This fixed-gait assumption limits the robot's flexibility when faced with varying task demands, dynamic environments, or unexpected terrain changes. MPC's dependence on accurate dynamic models and its computational demands further restrict its online adaptability.
- **Limited Gait Diversity in Model-Free RL:** Model-free Reinforcement Learning offers adaptability by learning policies directly from interaction. However, designing reward functions to elicit diverse and stable gaits remains challenging, often leading policies to converge to a single, dominant behavior. This prevents the emergence of a broad range of context-appropriate gaits.
- **Constraints of Hierarchical RL:** Hierarchical RL methods attempt to manage multi-gait behaviors but often interpolate between similar symmetric gaits and struggle to generalize beyond predefined patterns. This limits their ability to generate truly novel or continuously modulated gaits.
- **Inherent Single-Gait Bias in Hybrid RL+MPC Architectures:** Even advanced hybrid approaches that combine RL with planning, such as those leveraging Dreamer-like architectures, often inherit the single-gait limitation from their underlying RL policies. While these methods improve performance, they typically do not explicitly address the joint optimization of actions and continuous gait parameters.

To address these limitations, the authors propose an integrated framework designed to:
- Enable continuous, rather than discrete, gait modulation.
- Perform real-time joint optimization of both control actions and gait parameters.
- Achieve diverse gaits and smooth, uninterrupted transitions between them.
- Improve energy efficiency and velocity tracking across a broad range of operating conditions.
- Integrate the structural benefits of model-based planning with the adaptive capabilities of data-driven learning within a single, unified framework.

#### 1.3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework integrates Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) control with a learned Dreamer module to achieve real-time gait adaptation for quadrupedal robots. The methodology involves a two-stage pipeline: offline training and online deployment.

**A. Offline Training**

The training phase focuses on learning the necessary components for deployment-time planning.
- **Problem Formulation:** Quadruped locomotion is modeled as an infinite-horizon discounted Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP).
- **RL Policy Training:** An Actor-Critic framework, specifically Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), is used to train an expert RL policy. This policy is conditioned on manually specified gait parameters, adopting the structure from the "Walk These Ways" framework, which allows for gait-dependent behaviors.
- **Observation and Action Space:** Observations include joint positions and velocities, timing reference variables, commanded gait, projected gravity, and previous two actions. The action space consists of joint-angle perturbations applied to nominal joint angles, which are then processed by an actuator network to compute torques.
- **Reward (RL Training):** The RL policy is trained using a reward structure from "Walk These Ways," which combines task rewards (e.g., velocity tracking) with auxiliary terms to encourage stable and gait-specific locomotion, including posture maintenance and desired contact schedules.
- **Dreamer Module Training:** In parallel with the RL policy, a Dreamer module (Eθ) is trained using supervised learning. This module learns the following components crucial for deployment:
 - **Policy (πθ):** A cloned policy trained to imitate the expert RL policy. This policy takes a 30-step observation history as input and outputs an action, serving to warm-start the MPPI planner during deployment.
 - **Dynamics Model (Dθ):** Learns to predict the next observation given the current observation and action, trained from environment interactions.
 - **Reward Model (Rθ):** A learned reward function designed to capture general performance metrics such as velocity tracking and energy efficiency, without bias towards specific gaits. It takes observation and action as input.
 - **Value Function (Vθ):** Estimates the expected cumulative reward under the learned reward model (Rθ), enabling infinite-horizon approximation during deployment without extensive rollouts. This function is distinct from the RL critic, being specifically designed for planning purposes.
 - **Adaptation Module:** Trained to estimate privileged information from observation history, assisting the actor during deployment.

**B. Online Deployment (MPPI Planning with Gait Optimization)**

During deployment, the MPPI algorithm leverages the learned Dreamer module to optimize both control actions and continuous gait parameters in real-time.
- **Optimization Problem:** At each time step (t), the MPPI planner solves an optimization problem to find the optimal action sequence (at:t+H) and gait commands (gcmd t:t+H) over a finite horizon (H). The objective is to maximize the total expected return, which comprises a sum of learned rewards (Rθ) over the horizon and a terminal value prediction (Vθ) for infinite-horizon planning, subject to the learned dynamics (Dθ).
- **MPPI Algorithm:**
 1. **Warm-Start:** At the beginning of each planning cycle (or if t=0), the Dreamer policy (πθ) and dynamics model (Dθ) are used to generate initial trajectories. This serves to warm-start the mean (μ) and variance (σ) of the Gaussian distribution from which MPPI samples actions and gaits.
 2. **Sampling Trajectories:** A set of N trajectories (action sequences and corresponding gait command sequences) are sampled from the current Gaussian distribution.
 3. **Rollouts and Reward Calculation:** For each sampled trajectory, rollouts are generated using the Dreamer dynamics model (Dθ). The total return (Rj) for each trajectory is computed using the Dreamer reward model (Rθ) and the learned value function (Vθ) for bootstrapping the terminal state.
 4. **Reward Formulation (Deployment):** The deployment-time reward includes:
 - rvel (velocity tracking)
 - renergy (energy efficiency)
 - rang (angular velocity penalty)
 - rcont (action continuity penalty)
 - rdiv (actor divergence penalty): Encourages sampled actions to stay close to the cloned Dreamer policy's output.
 - rgait (gait continuity penalty): Penalizes abrupt changes in gait commands to promote smooth transitions.
 5. **Elite Selection:** The top-performing M_elite trajectories, based on their computed returns, are selected.
 6. **Distribution Update:** A new Gaussian distribution (mean and variance) is fitted to these elite trajectories. A momentum-based update rule (β) is used to combine the new elite distribution with the previous distribution, ensuring stability.
 7. **Action Execution:** The first action (and corresponding gait command) from the updated Gaussian distribution's mean is applied to the robot.
- **Gait Parameter Normalization:** Gait parameters are represented as a [3x1] vector, with each element being a scalar in the range. This representation encodes phase offsets between leg pairs, allowing for continuous modulation of gait structures. A wrap function (˜gcmd = gcmd % 1.0) is applied to ensure smooth and bounded gait variation.

The integrated architecture provides a framework where the robot learns a diverse set of gait-conditioned behaviors offline, and then, during deployment, intelligently adapts its gait in real-time through an optimization process guided by the learned models.

#### 1.4. Main Findings and Results

The framework was evaluated through simulations using the Unitree Go1 quadruped robot in NVIDIA’s Isaac Gym simulator.

**A. Ablation Study on Fixed Gaits**
An ablation study was conducted to assess the performance of four major fixed gaits (trot, pace, bound, and pronk) across a range of commanded velocities (0.5 m/s to 2.0 m/s).
- **Observation:** No single fixed gait consistently exhibited optimal performance across all speeds. Trotting demonstrated consistently low linear velocity errors and nearly constant Cost of Transport (CoT) at lower speeds (0.5 m/s to 1.5 m/s). In contrast, pronking showed improved energy efficiency at higher speeds while maintaining competitive tracking accuracy, indicating its suitability for faster locomotion.
- **Conclusion:** These results support the motivation for adaptive gait selection, as different gaits are optimal for different speed regimes and conditions.

**B. Gait Adaptation Performance**
The framework's ability to adapt gaits was evaluated under a continuously increasing forward velocity command.
- **Velocity Tracking:** The robot successfully tracked the desired forward velocity throughout the episode, demonstrating smooth acceleration.
- **Motion Stability:** Joint position trajectories remained cyclic and smooth, indicating stable locomotion even as the gait evolved.
- **Continuous Gait Transitions:** Visualization of foot contact patterns revealed continuous adaptation of interlimb coordination with increasing velocity. The planner generated context-specific gaits without relying on discrete gait labels.
- **Transition Smoothness:** Velocity tracking performance was maintained during transitions, with no observable degradation in motion stability or quality, and joint trajectories remained smooth.
- **Context-Specific Adaptation:** The planner demonstrated the ability to adapt to alternative coordination patterns beyond conventional trotting based on the context, suggesting that continuous gait modulation can better achieve tracking accuracy and energy efficiency.

**C. Energy Consumption**
The energy efficiency of the proposed gait adaptation method was compared against fixed-gait RL baselines using the Cost of Transport (CoT).
- **CoT Definition:** CoT was defined as |P| / (m ⋅ g ⋅ v), where |P| is average power, m is robot mass, g is gravitational acceleration, and v is average forward velocity.
- **Result:** The proposed method consistently achieved a lower Cost of Transport across all commanded velocities (0.5 m/s, 1.0 m/s, 1.5 m/s, 2.0 m/s) when compared to fixed-gait RL policies (trotting, pacing, bounding, pronking). Specifically, the energy consumption was reduced by up to 36.48% across varying target speeds, and remained 15-20% lower consistently compared to the most efficient individual fixed gaits in each velocity range.
- **Implication:** This indicates that dynamic gait adaptation significantly improves energy efficiency by avoiding the inefficiencies of using a single gait across varied operational speeds.

**D. Computational Feasibility**
- **Training Time:** Training the RL policy with the Dreamer module required approximately 5–6 hours on an NVIDIA A6000 GPU.
- **Inference Frequency:** During inference, the controller achieved a frequency of approximately 330 Hz on an NVIDIA RTX 3080 GPU.
- **Hardware Deployment Potential:** These computational metrics suggest the framework possesses the potential for smooth hardware deployment on moderately powered systems, although onboard GPU computation remains a requirement.

#### 1.5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework that addresses a notable challenge in quadrupedal robot locomotion: the need for real-time, continuous, and adaptive gait selection. By integrating Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) control with a learned Dreamer module, the work moves beyond the limitations of fixed-gait policies inherent in traditional MPC and many model-free RL approaches.

The significance of this work lies in several key contributions:

- **Enhanced Adaptability and Robustness:** The ability to jointly optimize actions and continuous gait parameters allows the robot to adapt dynamically to varying task demands, such as changes in target speed. This enhances the robot's versatility and robustness in diverse operational environments, without requiring manual pre-programming or discrete gait libraries.
- **Improved Energy Efficiency:** The consistent reduction in Cost of Transport across a range of velocities, up to 36.48% compared to fixed-gait baselines, indicates a significant improvement in energy efficiency. This has substantial practical implications for extending battery life and increasing operational duration of quadrupedal robots in real-world applications.
- **Smooth and Seamless Transitions:** The framework enables smooth and continuous transitions between gaits, as evidenced by stable joint trajectories and reliable velocity tracking during adaptation. This is critical for maintaining dynamic stability and preventing disruptive behaviors, contributing to safer and more agile robot operation.
- **Unified and Modular Framework:** The proposed architecture provides a unified approach that leverages the strengths of both model-based planning (MPPI) and data-driven learning (Dreamer). Its modular design allows for integration with various gait-conditioned RL algorithms, potentially broadening its applicability.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The demonstrated computational feasibility for real-time inference in simulation suggests potential for hardware deployment. This work lays a foundation for future advancements in multi-terrain locomotion, where robots must continuously adapt their gaits to uneven and rough surfaces. Future research directions, such as incorporating visual inputs for predictive planning and integrating structured models like Lagrangian Neural Networks, are identified to further enhance generalization and reliability for real-world deployment.

In conclusion, this research advances the state of the art in quadrupedal locomotion by enabling intelligent, energy-efficient, and adaptable gait control. The framework contributes to the development of more autonomous and versatile robots capable of performing complex tasks in dynamic and unstructured environments.
