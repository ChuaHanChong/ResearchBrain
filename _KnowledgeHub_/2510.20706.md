# Real-Time Gait Adaptation for Quadrupeds using Model Predictive Control and Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> This research from the Indian Institute of Science introduces a framework combining Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) control with a learned Dreamer module to enable real-time, continuous gait adaptation for quadrupedal robots. The method allows dynamic selection and smooth transitions between gaits, achieving up to 36.48% lower Cost of Transport compared to fixed-gait baselines in simulations.

## Problem

- Fixed-gait policies used in traditional MPC and many RL approaches limit quadrupedal robots' adaptability to varying speeds and terrains, as no single gait is optimal across all conditions.
- Traditional Model Predictive Control (MPC) relies on predefined gait parameters and accurate dynamic models, which restricts its flexibility and real-time online adaptation in complex environments.
- Model-free Reinforcement Learning (RL) often converges to a single dominant gait, lacking the diversity and continuous modulation needed for truly adaptive and energy-efficient locomotion.

## Method

- A two-stage framework integrates offline training of an expert RL policy and a Dreamer module (learning dynamics, reward, policy, and value models) with online deployment.
- During online deployment, a ==Model Predictive Path Integral== (MPPI) algorithm leverages the learned Dreamer module to jointly optimize both control actions and continuous gait parameters in real-time over a finite horizon.
- Gait parameters are represented as a [3x1] vector, encoding phase offsets between leg pairs, allowing for continuous modulation and smooth transitions through a wrap function and momentum-based updates.

## Results

- The framework successfully enabled continuous gait adaptation, with the robot tracking desired forward velocities smoothly while demonstrating stable, cyclic joint trajectories and continuous interlimb coordination changes.
- A significant reduction in energy consumption was observed, with the proposed method achieving up to **36.48%** lower Cost of Transport across varying target speeds compared to fixed-gait RL baselines.
- The controller achieved an inference frequency of approximately 330 Hz on an NVIDIA RTX 3080 GPU, suggesting computational feasibility for potential hardware deployment on moderately powered systems.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - No single fixed gait (trot, pace, bound, pronk) performs optimally across all commanded velocities for quadrupedal robots, highlighting the inefficiency of non-adaptive locomotion.
> - Integrating model-based planning with learned models allows for real-time, continuous adaptation of gait parameters, addressing the limitations of both fixed-gait MPC and single-gait RL policies.
> - By jointly optimizing control actions and continuous gait parameters, the system can dynamically adapt interlimb coordination to context-specific demands, improving both tracking accuracy and energy efficiency.
