# E$^2$DT: Efficient and Effective Decision Transformer with Experience-Aware Sampling for Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> The E²DT framework improves Decision Transformer policies for robotic manipulation by integrating an active, model-guided experience selection mechanism that leverages k-Determinantal Point Processes to balance sample quality and diversity. This approach led to higher success rates and faster learning in diverse simulation and real-world tasks, for instance, boosting success on Nut Assembly to 55.6% from the standard DT's 31.7%.

## Problem

- Decision Transformers (DT) operate as passive learners, making their performance heavily dependent on the quality and coverage of their training data for effective robotic manipulation.
- Standard uniform experience replay in DT leads to poor sample efficiency due to redundant data, a failure to prioritize valuable trajectories, and reduced overall learning effectiveness.
- Existing reinforcement learning approaches struggle with long-horizon dependencies, efficient exploration, and the effective utilization of collected samples, impeding scalability to complex real-world robot applications.

## Method

- Developed the **E²DT** framework, which transforms ==Decision Transformers== from passive data consumers into active participants via a closed-loop, model-cooperative ==experience selection== mechanism.
- The system evaluates experience quality using a composite score incorporating ==return quantiles==, ==predictive uncertainty==, and ==inverse frequency== of stages, while diversity is measured in the DT's ==behavioral latent representation space==.
- A ==k-Determinantal Point Process (k-DPP)== is applied to a novel ==quality-diversity joint kernel== to select an optimal subset of experiences, followed by a ==debiased mixed replay== strategy to mitigate sampling bias during training.

## Results

- E²DT consistently achieved **higher final success rates** and demonstrated faster early learning across diverse long-horizon tasks in RoboSuite and ManiSkill2 simulation benchmarks.
- On the Nut Assembly task, E²DT reached a **55.6%** success rate, outperforming standard DT (**31.7%**) and a quality-only selection strategy (**49.6%**), confirming the importance of balancing quality and diversity.
- In real-world robotic manipulation with an Elephant Robotics myCobot arm, E²DT demonstrated higher mean success rates, such as **82.1%** for High-Shelf Placement compared to the standard DT's **52.4%**, with fewer training episodes.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Balancing experience quality (utility for policy improvement) and diversity (coverage of the state-action space) is essential for enhancing Decision Transformer learning efficiency and effectiveness in long-horizon tasks.
> - Quantifying diversity in a behavioral latent representation space, derived from the Decision Transformer's encoder, is more effective than raw physical state space for capturing relevant behavioral differences and avoiding spurious diversity.
> - A principled approach, combining multi-dimensional quality signals with latent-space diversity through a k-Determinantal Point Process (k-DPP) on a joint kernel, enables active selection of high-value, non-redundant experiences.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of reinforcement learning (RL) has demonstrated promise in developing autonomous decision-making and control for robotic manipulation. However, existing RL approaches face persistent challenges, particularly in addressing long-horizon dependencies, achieving efficient exploration, and effectively utilizing collected samples. These limitations collectively impede the scalability of RL to complex, real-world robotic applications.

Sequence modeling approaches, with the Decision Transformer (DT) being a notable example, have emerged as a paradigm for handling long-term reward dependencies. DT conditions action selection on the expected return-to-go, which allows it to capture extended task structures. Despite this capability, DT operates as a passive learner. Its performance is substantially influenced by the quality and coverage of its training data. When coupled with conventional uniform experience replay mechanisms, DT frequently suffers from data redundancy, fails to prioritize highly valuable trajectories, and exhibits reduced learning efficiency and overall effectiveness.

In the broader context of RL, the challenge of sample efficiency and effective exploration has led to various research directions, including prioritized experience replay, intrinsic motivation, and model-based RL. Prioritized replay methods aim to focus learning on samples that are deemed more important (e.g., those with high temporal difference errors). However, these methods often rely on local heuristics and may not consider the long-term utility or diversity of experiences in the context of sequence modeling. Exploration strategies often employ methods like curiosity-driven exploration or count-based bonuses to encourage visiting novel states, but integrating these effectively with a passive, sequence-based model like DT remains an area of active research. Furthermore, for long-horizon tasks, merely prioritizing high-reward trajectories can lead to suboptimal policies by ignoring critical, low-reward but informative sub-tasks or states.

This work positions itself at the intersection of sequence-based RL and active experience management. It addresses the inherent limitations of DT by transforming it from a passive data consumer into an active participant in shaping its own training distribution. By developing an experience-aware sampling mechanism, E²DT seeks to overcome the inefficiencies of uniform replay and the potential shortcomings of purely heuristic prioritization methods, thereby enhancing DT's capacity for robust learning in complex, long-horizon robotic manipulation tasks. The integration of quality and diversity criteria within a Determinantal Point Process (DPP) framework provides a principled, non-heuristic approach to experience selection, differentiating this work from many prior art methods that often focus on only one dimension (e.g., error-based prioritization for quality, or novelty for diversity).

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observed limitations of the Decision Transformer (DT) in reinforcement learning for robotic manipulation. While DT is effective at modeling long-horizon dependencies by conditioning action selection on return-to-go, its performance is heavily reliant on the training data's coverage and quality. Standard DT implementations, which typically use uniform experience replay, are characterized by:
- **Poor Sample Efficiency**: Redundant experiences are replayed equally, leading to inefficient use of collected data.
- **Limited Exploration**: Without an active mechanism to prioritize novel or uncertain experiences, the model may struggle to explore the state-action space adequately.
- **Reduced Overall Effectiveness**: The inability to distinguish valuable experiences from less informative ones can hinder policy convergence and optimality, particularly in long-horizon tasks with sparse rewards or critical sub-tasks.
- **Delayed Policy Convergence**: Excessive or unfocused exploration can prevent the policy from stabilizing.

To address these limitations, the authors identify a core issue: the passive data-receiving mode of the standard DT model. They propose that maximizing DT's learning efficiency requires equipping it with an active, model-cooperative experience selection mechanism. Such a mechanism should move beyond short-sighted heuristics and evaluate the usefulness of experience from a long-term perspective, balancing two orthogonal objectives:

1. **Quality**: Prioritizing experiences that directly refine the policy and possess high return potential, including those that are currently challenging for the policy to predict or execute correctly.
2. **Diversity**: Ensuring broad coverage of the state-action space to prevent distribution collapse, where the policy overfits to a narrow set of experiences, and premature convergence to suboptimal solutions.

Based on this motivation, the research sets out to achieve three key objectives:

1. **Defining "High-Quality" Experience**: The work aims to define a comprehensive notion of "high-quality" experience that transcends simple return maximization. This definition incorporates both the immediate utility for policy improvement (e.g., high-return trajectories or regions of high uncertainty for the current policy) and the need for broad state-action space coverage to prevent distribution collapse.
2. **Developing Reliable Quality Signals**: The active experience selector must leverage the policy's internal signals rather than relying on ad hoc or external heuristics. This allows the DT model to identify valuable experiences based on its current knowledge and predictive capabilities, making the selection process self-guided and adaptive.
3. **Balancing Optimality and Efficiency**: The proposed selection algorithm must be theoretically principled to ensure robust learning outcomes, while also remaining computationally efficient. This is crucial because finding an optimal subset from a large replay buffer is generally intractable, necessitating a practical yet effective solution.

In summary, the overarching goal is to design an active experience selection framework for DT, guided by the model’s own signals, that efficiently balances quality and diversity while remaining computationally feasible, ultimately maximizing learning efficiency and effectiveness in long-horizon robotic manipulation tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The E²DT framework is designed to address the sample inefficiency of the Decision Transformer (DT) by integrating an active, model-guided experience selection mechanism. This closed-loop system consists of three main stages: (i) signal extraction for defining valuable samples, (ii) construction of a quality–diversity joint kernel, and (iii) subset selection via k-Determinantal Point Process (k-DPP) and debiased training.

**A. Diversity and Quality Evaluation**

The first stage focuses on quantifying the value of experiences from the perspective of the current DT policy, based on both their quality (utility for policy improvement) and diversity (coverage of the state-action space).

- **Diversity Evaluation**:
 - The method addresses the issue of "spurious diversity" in raw physical state space, where minor kinematic variations might obscure behavioral similarity.
 - Diversity is measured in a *behavioral latent representation space* induced by the DT's encoder, `f_θ`. For a trajectory window `w = (s_t:t+H, a_t:t+H-1, R_hat_t:t+H-1)`, the DT encoder extracts a latent embedding vector `z(w) = f_θ(w)`. These embeddings capture high-level behavioral intent, filtering out irrelevant physical details.
 - Similarity `S_ij` between two windows `i` and `j` in this latent space is computed using an RBF (Gaussian) kernel: `S_ij = exp(- ||z_i - z_j||^2 / (2σ^2))`. The bandwidth `σ` is set using the median-of-pairwise-distances heuristic.

- **Quality Evaluation**:
 - Quality is not solely defined by final return but by the marginal contribution of an experience to the current learning stage. A multi-dimensional composite quality score `q(w)` is computed as a weighted sum: `q(w) = α * RTG(w)_hat + β * eu(w) + ζ * eρ(w)`, where `α + β + ζ = 1`.
 - **Exploitation (High-Value Samples via Return Quantiles)**: This component promotes learning from successful patterns. The discounted return `G(w)` for a window is calculated. To make this robust to outliers and scaling, the *quantile* of `G(w)` within the candidate pool `C` is used as a normalized score, denoted `RTG(w)_hat`. This focuses on experiences that are "relatively strong" within the current data.
 - **Exploration (Cognitive Boundaries via Uncertainty)**: This component guides the model to explore its "blind spots." Predictive uncertainty `u(w)` for a window is estimated by performing multiple stochastic forward passes (e.g., MC-Dropout) and calculating the trace of the covariance of the predictive means. This `u(w)` is then normalized (`eu(w)`) to quantify regions where the model is most uncertain.
 - **Coverage (Rare Stages via Inverse Frequency)**: To address class imbalance in long-horizon tasks (where common behaviors might overshadow critical rare sub-tasks), stage coverage `eρ(w)` is introduced. Each window is assigned a stage label `ϕ(w)` (from environment annotations or unsupervised clustering of latent embeddings). The inverse-frequency score `eρ(w)` is `1 - freq(ϕ(w)) / max_c freq(c)`, which up-weights underrepresented but pivotal sub-tasks.

**B. Constructing the Quality–Diversity Joint Kernel**

The individual quality scores `q_i` and the pairwise similarity matrix `S` are fused into a unified L-ensemble joint kernel `L`.
- The kernel is defined as `L = Q^(1/2)SQ^(1/2) + λI`, where `Q` is a diagonal matrix with `q_i` on its diagonal, and `λI` is a regularization term.
- The `(i, j)`-th entry of `L` becomes `L_ij = sqrt(q_i) * S_ij * sqrt(q_j)`. This formulation modulates the latent-space similarity `S_ij` by the geometric mean of the qualities of samples `i` and `j`. This means low-quality samples have a diminished influence on the global diversity structure, while high-quality samples amplify their association strength.
- Geometrically, maximizing the determinant of a submatrix of `L` (as done by DPP) implies selecting a set of samples whose corresponding vectors in the kernel space are both "long" (high quality) and "mutually wide-angled" (high diversity), achieving the desired balance.

**C. k-DPP Based Sample Selection and Debiased Training**

- **Subset Selection**: Given the joint kernel `L`, a k-DPP (Determinantal Point Process) is used to select a fixed-size subset `Y` of `k` trajectory windows from the candidate pool `C`. The selection aims to maximize `log det(L_Y)`, which corresponds to finding the subset with maximum a posteriori (MAP) probability. A greedy MAP procedure is employed, incrementally adding candidates with the largest marginal gain to the set, effectively selecting high-quality and non-redundant samples.
- **Debiasing via Importance Sampling**: Training only on the selected subset `Y` introduces sampling bias because `p_sel(w) ≠ p_D(w)`. To mitigate this, a "Debiased Mixed Replay" strategy is used. A mini-batch `B` is constructed by drawing a fraction `η` of samples uniformly from the selected subset `Y` and the remaining `1 - η` from the global replay buffer `D`. Importance weights `ω_i` are then applied to each sample `i` in `B` to correct for the sampling bias. The weight `ω_i` is proportional to `1/p_i`, where `p_i` is the probability of sampling `i` under the mixed replay strategy.
- **DT Update**: The Decision Transformer policy parameters `θ` are updated by minimizing an importance-weighted DT loss on the mini-batch `B`, ensuring that gradients are unbiased estimators under the full data distribution while benefiting from the prioritized samples.

This comprehensive methodology allows E²DT to dynamically shape its training distribution by actively selecting experiences that are both informative for policy improvement and diverse for robust exploration, all guided by the internal signals of the DT model itself.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of E²DT was conducted across a range of challenging robotic manipulation benchmarks in both simulation and real-robot settings, focusing on learning efficiency, coverage, diversity, and final task success. The method was compared against several representative baselines, including uniform DT, HER, SynthER, MAPLE, Relo, and SkillTree.

**A. RoboSuite Simulation Benchmarks**
E²DT was tested on four long-horizon tasks from RoboSuite: Block Stacking, Nut Assembly, Door Opening, and Pick-and-Place.
- **Performance:** E²DT consistently achieved higher final success rates and demonstrated faster early learning compared to all baselines across all tasks. For instance, on Nut Assembly, E²DT achieved a success rate of 55.6% compared to the standard DT's 31.7% and MAPLE's 42.1%. Similar improvements were observed in Block Stacking (79.8% vs. DT's 60.1%), Door Opening (65.2% vs. DT's 46.8%), and Pick-and-Place (73.7% vs. DT's 58.0%).
- **Key Observations:** The gains were most pronounced in tasks characterized by rare but critical phases, such as the precise alignment required in Nut Assembly or the latch release in Door Opening. The authors attributed this to the diversity measured in the DT latent space preventing "spurious diversity" in raw state space, and the quality branch (combining RTG quantiles, uncertainty, and stage coverage) effectively focusing training on high-value, complementary experiences.

**B. ManiSkill2 Simulation Benchmarks**
Four vision-based, long-horizon tasks from ManiSkill2 were used: Pick Cube, Pick YCB, Stack Cube, and Turn Faucet.
- **Performance:** E²DT again achieved superior final success rates and faster early improvement across all four tasks. This performance was consistent with the RoboSuite results, demonstrating its applicability to vision-based control.
- **Key Observations:** The benefits were particularly noticeable in tasks like Stack and Turn Faucet, which involve rare but critical sub-stages (e.g., precise alignment for stacking, initiating rotation for faucet turning). The k-DPP mechanism, guided by the joint kernel, was observed to increase coverage of these critical sub-stages without introducing redundancy, leading to quicker convergence and higher success rates.

**C. Real-World Robotic Manipulation (Elephant Robotics myCobot arm)**
E²DT was deployed on a physical 6-DoF Elephant Robotics myCobot arm for three long-horizon tasks: High-Shelf Placement, Stacking, and Target Grasping.
- **Performance:** E²DT demonstrated higher mean success rates and faster learning with fewer episodes compared to baselines in the real-world environment. For example, in High-Shelf Placement, E²DT achieved 82.1% success compared to DT's 52.4% and SkillTree's 69.8%. Similar trends were seen in Stacking (85.6% vs. DT's 56.1%) and Target Grasping (83.4% vs. DT's 54.7%).
- **Key Observations:** The quality branch (RTG quantiles, predictive uncertainty, stage coverage) effectively directed updates to crucial phases like grasp onset, lift-to-transport transitions, and final alignment. The use of DT's latent space for diversity measurement prevented "spurious diversity" that could arise from raw image inputs, contributing to robust performance in physical interaction.

**D. Ablation Study and Sensitivity Analysis**
An ablation study was conducted on the Nut Assembly task to investigate the contributions of E²DT's components.
- **Full E²DT:** Achieved 55.6% success with 10.1% redundancy and 0.87 diversity (normalized log det(S_Y)).
- **Quality-Only:** (Greedy selection based on `q(w)`) resulted in 49.6% success, but with significantly higher redundancy (32.9%) and lower diversity (0.46). This indicates that solely prioritizing quality without diversity leads to over-focusing on local high-return regions and redundant sampling.
- **Diversity-Only:** (k-DPP based purely on DT-latent space diversity, constant `q_i`) yielded 43.8% success, with lower redundancy (13.4%) and high diversity (0.89). While maintaining breadth, it under-utilized high-value samples, harming efficiency and overall convergence.
- **Uniform Replay:** Performed lowest with 31.2% success, highest redundancy (42.3%), and lowest diversity (0.40).
- **Conclusion:** These results empirically confirmed that the joint enforcement of both quality and diversity via k-DPP on the proposed `L = Q^(1/2)SQ^(1/2) + λI` kernel is essential for achieving optimal efficiency and robustness.
- **Sensitivity:** The method showed stable performance across reasonable ranges for the `k/N` ratio (0.08 to 0.20) and the mixed replay ratio `η` (0.6 to 0.8). The pairwise-median heuristic for the RBF kernel bandwidth `σ` was robust, and the refresh period `K` could be balanced between computational cost and buffer-drift tracking (5k to 15k steps).

Overall, the findings consistently demonstrate that E²DT provides substantial improvements in sample efficiency, convergence speed, and final task success rates across diverse and challenging robotic manipulation scenarios, both simulated and real-world.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The E²DT framework represents a significant advancement in the application of Decision Transformers to robotic manipulation tasks, particularly those involving long-horizon planning and complex interaction.

**Significance:**
- **Addressing DT Limitations:** The work directly tackles the core limitations of Decision Transformers, namely their passive learning nature and susceptibility to poor data distribution when using uniform experience replay. By enabling DT to actively guide its own experience selection, E²DT transforms it into a more adaptive and efficient learning agent.
- **Principled Experience Selection:** Instead of relying on ad hoc heuristics, E²DT introduces a theoretically principled approach for active experience sampling based on k-Determinantal Point Processes (k-DPP). The development of a novel quality–diversity joint kernel, which mathematically fuses individual sample quality with pairwise diversity in the DT's latent space, provides a robust and interpretable mechanism for prioritizing informative experiences.
- **Balanced Learning Objectives:** The framework effectively balances exploitation (learning from high-return, valuable experiences) with exploration (covering uncertain and rare but critical states) and coverage (addressing data imbalance in long-horizon tasks). This multi-faceted approach ensures that learning is both efficient in converging to optimal policies and effective in maintaining robust generalization across the task space.
- **Model-Guided Signals:** By deriving quality and diversity signals directly from the DT's internal latent embeddings and predictive uncertainties, the method ensures that the experience selection is cooperative with the policy's current knowledge and learning needs, making the process self-improving.
- **Empirical Validation:** The consistent performance gains demonstrated across diverse simulation benchmarks (RoboSuite, ManiSkill2) and, crucially, on a real-world robotic platform, provide strong empirical evidence for the method's effectiveness and robustness in practical settings. The ablation studies further confirm the critical role of each proposed component.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Accelerated Robotic Learning:** E²DT's ability to significantly improve sample efficiency and accelerate convergence can reduce the immense data collection requirements typically associated with complex robotic tasks. This directly translates to faster development cycles and reduced computational costs in both research and industrial applications.
- **Enhanced Robot Autonomy:** By enabling robots to learn more robust and generalizable policies for long-horizon tasks, E²DT contributes to advancing robot autonomy. This can lead to robots capable of performing more complex sequences of operations in unstructured environments with greater reliability.
- **Broader Adoption of RL in Robotics:** The framework provides a more reliable and efficient pathway for applying advanced sequence-based RL methods like DT to real-world robotic problems, potentially broadening their adoption beyond academic research.
- **Foundational for Future Research:** The principled approach to quality–diversity weighting and k-DPP-based sampling in the context of sequence models could serve as a foundation for future research in active learning, curriculum learning, and intelligent data management within RL, especially for other Transformer-based architectures.

In conclusion, E²DT offers a principled and empirically validated path toward robust long-horizon robotic learning by intelligently coupling policy learning with experience-aware sampling. This work is poised to positively impact the efficiency and effectiveness of developing autonomous robotic systems capable of complex manipulation tasks.
