# VG-CoT: Towards Trustworthy Visual Reasoning via Grounded Chain-of-Thought

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Chung-Ang University developed VG-CoT, a new dataset and benchmark for training Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) to perform trustworthy visual reasoning. This work utilizes a fully automated pipeline to generate multi-step reasoning explicitly grounded in visual evidence, which improved rationale quality and answer accuracy across various LVLMs, with LLaVA-1.5-7B's rationale quality increasing by 11.2% and answer accuracy by 13.8%.

## Problem

- Existing vision-language datasets lack the fine-grained, local region-based understanding required for advanced applications and often rely on costly manual annotation.
- Current multimodal Chain-of-Thought (CoT) approaches often lack explicit grounding between reasoning steps and specific visual evidence within images (e.g., spatial coordinates).
- Evaluation benchmarks primarily assess the accuracy of the final answer, failing to provide insights into the trustworthiness or visual grounding of the reasoning process itself.

## Method

- A fully automated three-stage pipeline generates the **VG-CoT** dataset, leveraging ==YOLO==, ==PaddleOCR==, ==Grounding DINO==, and ==GPT-4o== to extract visual evidence and generate visually-grounded rationales.
- The dataset explicitly links each logical deduction in the reasoning process to its corresponding ==visual evidence== — object and text bounding box coordinates — for transparent and verifiable reasoning.
- A comprehensive benchmark evaluates LVLMs across three dimensions: ==Rationale Quality (RQ)==, ==Answer Accuracy (AA)==, and ==Reasoning-Answer Alignment (RAA)==, assessed using GPT-4o as an evaluator.

## Results

- Fine-tuning with the VG-CoT dataset consistently improved Rationale Quality across all tested LVLMs, with LLaVA-1.5-7B's overall RQ increasing from **72.2** to **83.4**.
- Answer Accuracy also improved for all models after fine-tuning; for example, LLaVA-1.5-7B's AA rose from **48.7** to **62.5**, and Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct's AA increased from **68.5** to **73.6**.
- Analysis of visual evidence prediction accuracy showed that achieving high spatial precision (mAP@0.75) for bounding box localization, especially for small text regions, remains a significant challenge, with performance dropping from mAP@0.5 of **48.7** to mAP@0.75 of **33.6** for LLaVA-1.5-13B.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Training LVLMs with visually grounded rationales consistently enhances the logical coherence and evidence-based nature of their reasoning across various tasks.
> - Precisely grounding reasoning steps to specific spatial coordinates (visual evidence) is inherently more challenging for LVLMs compared to generating logically coherent text, as indicated by lower 'Visual Evidence' sub-scores.
> - The automated pipeline is effective for scalable generation of high-quality, visually grounded reasoning data, addressing limitations of manual annotation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) has progressed significantly, enabling models to process and understand both visual and linguistic information. Initially, research focused on comprehensive, image-level understanding. However, the current trajectory is shifting towards requiring more precise, local region-based understanding. This fine-grained capability is considered crucial for advanced applications such as autonomous driving, robotics, and medical image analysis, which necessitate accurate identification of specific image regions and interpretation of their spatial and semantic relationships.

Existing vision-language datasets, while effective for image-level understanding, often lack the granularity required to train models for detailed attribute-based or inter-object relational reasoning. Datasets that do incorporate local region information typically rely on extensive manual annotation, leading to limitations in scalability, cost-efficiency, and diversity. This manual approach becomes particularly restrictive for tasks demanding complex visual relationships or multi-step reasoning.

Furthermore, a significant challenge in current multimodal Chain-of-Thought (CoT) approaches is the lack of explicit grounding between the reasoning steps and specific visual evidence within the image. This absence of spatial coordinates for objects or text referenced in the rationale makes it difficult to verify if a model's logic is genuinely derived from the visual content or merely produces plausible text based on linguistic patterns. Consequently, existing evaluation benchmarks primarily assess the accuracy of the final answer, failing to provide insights into the trustworthiness of the reasoning process itself. This limitation prevents a comprehensive assessment of whether a model utilizes correct visual evidence as its rationale.

This research addresses these gaps by proposing a scalable, automated method for generating a dataset that explicitly links multi-step reasoning to precise visual evidence, thereby enabling a more rigorous and trustworthy evaluation of LVLM reasoning capabilities. It contributes to the evolving landscape by moving beyond simple answer accuracy to evaluating the fidelity and logical coherence of the reasoning process grounded in visual cues.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from two critical limitations in the current development and evaluation of LVLMs: the scarcity of scalable, explicitly grounded reasoning datasets and the inadequacy of existing evaluation benchmarks for assessing model trustworthiness.

The key objectives are as follows:

1. **Develop a Scalable and Cost-Efficient Dataset for Trustworthy Visual Reasoning:** The existing reliance on manual annotation for datasets that incorporate local region information severely limits their scalability and cost-efficiency. This research aims to overcome these limitations by proposing a fully automated pipeline for dataset construction. This pipeline is designed to generate multi-step reasoning processes explicitly aligned with precise visual evidence (spatial coordinates of objects and text) within images, ensuring that the model's logic is grounded in actual visual content. This objective seeks to provide a foundation for training LVLMs that can perform fine-grained, evidence-based reasoning.

2. **Ensure Explicit Alignment Between Reasoning Steps and Visual Evidence:** A core issue with previous multimodal Chain-of-Thought (CoT) datasets is the absence of a direct link between reasoning steps and the specific visual evidence (e.g., bounding box locations of objects or text) that supports those steps. This prevents verification of whether the model's reasoning is faithful to the image content. The research aims to construct a dataset where every logical deduction in the reasoning process is explicitly linked to its corresponding visual evidence, thereby enabling a more transparent and verifiable reasoning path.

3. **Establish a Comprehensive Benchmark for Trustworthy LVLM Evaluation:** Current evaluation methods for LVLMs predominantly focus on the accuracy of the final answer, neglecting the quality and reliability of the intermediate reasoning process. This overlooks whether models arrive at correct answers through sound, evidence-based logic or through spurious correlations or hallucinations. This study aims to introduce a new benchmark that moves beyond simple answer accuracy to comprehensively assess three complementary dimensions: Rationale Quality, Answer Accuracy, and Reasoning-Answer Alignment. This new benchmark is intended to enable a deeper analysis of the logical soundness and visual grounding of a model's reasoning.

4. **Provide Insights into LVLMs' Visual Evidence Utilization:** By fine-tuning representative LVLMs on the proposed dataset and evaluating them with the new benchmark, the research seeks to analyze their capabilities in leveraging visual evidence for reasoning. This objective aims to identify current strengths, weaknesses, and potential bottlenecks in how LVLMs integrate visual perception with linguistic reasoning, thereby guiding future research directions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces the Visual Grounding Chain-of-Thought (VG-CoT) dataset and a new benchmark. The VG-CoT dataset is constructed through a fully automated three-stage pipeline, leveraging state-of-the-art models for visual evidence extraction and text generation.

#### 3.1. VG-CoT Data Generation Pipeline

The pipeline processes raw data sourced from three distinct tasks to cover a wide range of reasoning scenarios: Scene-text VQA (TextVQA) for text-intensive reasoning, General VQA (Visual7W) for object recognition, and Relation VQA (GQA) for complex inter-object relational reasoning.

1. **Stage 1: Multi-Granularity Visual Evidence Extraction.**
 - **Objective:** To extract comprehensive initial visual evidence (objects and text) from source images.
 - **Approach:**
 - **Object-level Evidence:** The YOLO model (You Only Look Once) is employed to detect and generate bounding boxes for key objects within the images.
 - **Text-level Evidence:** PaddleOCR is used to extract both the location (bounding boxes) and the textual content of any text present in the image, handling complex visual environments.
 - **Supplementary Data:** For the GQA dataset, the intrinsic scene graph information already provided by the dataset is utilized as additional initial evidence.

2. **Stage 2: Visually-Grounded CoT Generation.**
 - **Objective:** To generate step-by-step reasoning paths that explicitly cite the extracted visual evidence.
 - **Approach:** GPT-4o, a large multimodal model, is provided with the original image, question, ground-truth answer, and the visual evidence (object and text bounding boxes and their labels/content) extracted in Stage 1.
 - **Prompt Engineering:** A specific prompt is designed (detailed in Table 1 of the paper) to instruct GPT-4o. This prompt explicitly requires the model to:
 - Generate a step-by-step reasoning process.
 - Directly link each logical deduction to the spatial coordinates (visual evidence) obtained in Stage 1.
 - Explain why certain elements are *not* the answer or why other options do not apply, encouraging comparative reasoning and higher plausibility.
 - Ensure the reasoning is evidence-based and moves beyond simple text generation.

3. **Stage 3: Rationale-Driven Grounding Refinement.**
 - **Objective:** To refine and extend the visual evidence based on the nuanced references within the GPT-4o-generated rationale.
 - **Approach:**
 - **Noun Extraction:** Key object nouns are extracted from the CoT sentences generated in Stage 2 using natural language processing tools (e.g., NLTK).
 - **Open-Set Detection:** These extracted nouns serve as queries for Grounding DINO, an open-set object detector. This allows for precise textual grounding of specific objects or concepts mentioned in the rationale that might not have been captured by the general detectors in Stage 1.
 - **Final Alignment:** The refined visual evidence (bounding boxes) is then explicitly linked back to each corresponding reasoning step, establishing a clear and precise alignment between the textual rationale and the actual image coordinates.

This fully automated pipeline aims to achieve high scalability and cost-efficiency while ensuring consistent quality and detailed visual grounding of the VG-CoT dataset. The dataset comprises 13,826 samples, with a distribution across General VQA (4,000), Relation VQA (6,600), and Scene-text VQA (3,226), and contains a total of 51,025 object bounding boxes and 4,782 OCR bounding boxes.

#### 3.2. Benchmark Construction

To evaluate LVLMs beyond mere answer accuracy, a comprehensive benchmark is established, structured around three core dimensions:

1. **Rationale Quality (RQ):** Assessed by GPT-4o acting as an automated evaluator, scoring reasoning on a 1 to 5 scale. It encompasses four sub-metrics:
 - **Visual Evidence:** Measures the extent to which the model correctly utilizes object and text location information.
 - **Coherence:** Evaluates the logical flow and consistency between reasoning steps.
 - **Completeness:** Determines if all necessary logical deductions are present for the conclusion.
 - **Overall:** The combined average score of the three preceding metrics.

2. **Answer Accuracy (AA):** A standard metric that evaluates whether the model's final predicted answer matches the ground truth.

3. **Reasoning-Answer Alignment (RAA):** Measures the correlation between the quality of the intermediate reasoning and the correctness of the final answer, comprising two sub-metrics:
 - **Consistency Score:** Calculates the proportion of samples where reasoning quality aligns logically with answer correctness. A match occurs if excellent reasoning (RQ $\ge$ 4) leads to a correct answer (AA = 1), or if poor reasoning (RQ $\le$ 3) leads to an incorrect answer (AA = 0).
 - **Faithful Score:** Assesses the reliability of correct predictions by measuring the proportion of correct answers specifically among samples that exhibit excellent reasoning (RQ $\ge$ 4).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

Experiments were conducted by fine-tuning four representative LVLMs: LLaVA-1.5 (7B and 13B), Qwen2-VL-7B-Instruct, and Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct, on the VG-CoT dataset. The evaluation utilized the proposed multi-dimensional benchmark.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance on VG-CoT Benchmark

- **Consistent Improvements:** Fine-tuning with the VG-CoT dataset consistently led to improvements across most evaluation metrics for all tested LVLMs (Table 2).
 - **Rationale Quality (RQ):** All models showed notable gains in overall Rationale Quality. For instance, LLaVA-1.5-7B's overall RQ increased from 72.2 to 83.4, and Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct improved from 88.0 to 89.5. This indicates that training with visually grounded rationales enhances the logical and evidence-based nature of reasoning. Sub-metrics like Coherence and Completeness also showed consistent increases.
 - **Answer Accuracy (AA):** Answer Accuracy also improved across all models after fine-tuning. LLaVA-1.5-7B's AA rose from 48.7 to 62.5, and Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct's AA increased from 68.5 to 73.6.
 - **Reasoning-Answer Alignment (RAA):** Most models showed improvements in Consistency and Faithful Scores, suggesting better alignment between the quality of reasoning and the correctness of answers. For example, Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct's Faithful Score increased from 70.0 to 75.7.

- **Specific Observations and Bottlenecks:**
 - **Visual Evidence Score:** The "Visual Evidence" sub-metric within Rationale Quality consistently remained lower than Coherence and Completeness scores across all models. This suggests that precisely grounding reasoning steps to specific spatial coordinates is inherently more challenging than generating logically coherent text.
 - **Consistency Anomaly:** LLaVA-1.5-13B exhibited a slight decrease in Consistency Score (from 66.9 to 65.6) after fine-tuning, implying that while its overall rationale quality and answer accuracy improved, there were instances of disconnect between its reasoning and final answer. This highlights the complexity of ensuring full alignment.

#### 4.2. Task-Specific Fine-Tuning Effects

- **Broad Applicability:** VG-CoT fine-tuning demonstrated benefits across all three distinct reasoning tasks (Table 3), confirming its general applicability.
 - **General VQA and Relation VQA:** Models, particularly LLaVA-1.5-7B, showed substantial improvements in both Rationale Quality and Answer Accuracy for General VQA (e.g., LLaVA-1.5-7B's AA from 51.8 to 69.2) and Relation VQA (e.g., LLaVA-1.5-7B's AA from 53.4 to 64.3). This suggests that VG-CoT is effective in training models to reason about spatial and relational contexts.
 - **Scene-text VQA:** In the text-heavy Scene-text VQA task, models also showed significant gains in Rationale Quality (e.g., LLaVA-1.5-7B's RQ from 63.8 to 86.4), although Answer Accuracy improvements were generally less pronounced compared to other tasks.
 - **Advanced Models:** Even highly capable, instruction-tuned models like Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct saw improvements, with its General VQA Answer Accuracy increasing from 79.4 to 84.4, underscoring the value of VG-CoT for diverse model architectures.

#### 4.3. Analysis of Visual Evidence Prediction Accuracy

- **Grounding Challenges:** An analysis of predicted visual evidence using Mean Average Precision (mAP) for bounding box localization (Table 4) revealed insights into grounding capabilities.
 - **Model Capacity:** Increased model capacity correlated with better grounding ability, as LLaVA-1.5-13B consistently outperformed LLaVA-1.5-7B (average mAP@0.5 of 48.7 vs. 44.5).
 - **Task Specificity:** Models achieved their highest mAP scores in Relation VQA (54.9 mAP@0.5 for LLaVA-1.5-13B), indicating better localization of larger, more distinct relational objects. Conversely, performance was significantly lower in the Scene-text task (32.4 mAP@0.5 for LLaVA-1.5-13B), quantitatively demonstrating the difficulty in precisely locating small and dense text regions.
 - **Precision Demands:** A notable drop in performance was observed with a stricter Intersection over Union (IoU) threshold. For instance, LLaVA-1.5-13B's average mAP dropped from 48.7 (mAP@0.5) to 33.6 (mAP@0.75). This indicates that while models can approximate the general location of relevant visual evidence, achieving high spatial precision remains a challenge requiring further investigation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This study contributes to the advancement of Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) by addressing critical limitations in data availability and evaluation methodologies.

1. **Enabling Trustworthy Reasoning:** The proposed VG-CoT dataset offers a novel resource for training LVLMs to perform trustworthy, evidence-based reasoning. By explicitly aligning each step of the reasoning process with real visual evidence (object and text bounding boxes), it allows models to learn to ground their logic in perceptible image content rather than relying solely on linguistic patterns or biases. This is significant for applications requiring high reliability and explainability, such as medical diagnostics or autonomous systems.

2. **Scalable Data Generation:** The fully automated three-stage pipeline for VG-CoT construction represents a practical solution to the scalability and cost-efficiency challenges associated with manually annotated datasets. This automation facilitates the creation of large-scale, high-quality datasets that would otherwise be prohibitive, thereby accelerating research and development in visually grounded reasoning.

3. **Comprehensive Evaluation Benchmark:** The introduction of a new benchmark that goes beyond simple answer accuracy to measure Rationale Quality and Reasoning-Answer Alignment provides a more holistic framework for evaluating LVLMs. This benchmark, leveraging an LLM (GPT-4o) as an evaluator, enables a deeper assessment of a model's logical coherence, completeness, and faithful utilization of visual evidence. This allows researchers to identify specific strengths and weaknesses in reasoning capabilities, fostering the development of more robust and transparent models.

4. **Guiding Future Research:** The experimental results, which demonstrate consistent improvements across various LVLMs and tasks, validate the effectiveness of the VG-CoT dataset. Simultaneously, the identified challenges, particularly in precise spatial grounding for small text regions and the drop in performance with stricter IoU thresholds, highlight specific areas for future research. This provides clear directions for improving LVLM architectures and training strategies to enhance fine-grained visual evidence utilization.

In summary, this work provides both a core dataset and an evaluation methodology that can contribute to fostering more trustworthy and explainable LVLMs. Its impact is expected to extend to the broader research community by providing tools and insights necessary for building vision-language models that not only provide correct answers but also demonstrate sound, verifiable reasoning grounded in visual facts.
