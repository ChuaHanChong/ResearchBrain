# Towards Fast, Memory-based and Data-Efficient Vision-Language Policy

> [!summary] Summary
> LiteVLP is a lightweight vision-language policy built on a 1B-parameter VLM, fine-tuned via conversation-style visuomotor instruction tuning with a multi-observation compression module for token reduction. It outperforms state-of-the-art vision-language policies on VIMA-Bench using only 1.2% of training data, with 6.8x faster inference and 18.8% higher success on long-horizon tasks.

## Problem

- Fine-tuning large pretrained VLMs on robotic data causes frequent domain shifts from mismatched web-pretraining versus robotic data modalities, destabilizing training.
- Existing vision-language policies have limited capacity to retain and use past or future experiences, hurting performance on long-horizon manipulation.
- Large backbone parameter counts in prior VLA models yield high inference latency, restricting real-world deployment feasibility.

## Method

- ==Memory-based visuomotor instruction tuning== fine-tunes a 1B-parameter ==InternVL2== backbone on a small conversation-style dataset, explicitly appending prior actions and multi-image history for long-horizon memory.
- ==Multi-observation compression (MOC)== module adapts ==run-length tokenization== to detect static image patches across consecutive frames and replaces them with a shared patch plus learnable length embedding, cutting image tokens roughly 60%.
- Overall **LiteVLP** architecture interleaves MOC-compressed image tokens with text instructions through a projector into an LLM that outputs 2D end-effector coordinates and rotation angle extracted via a text-parsing action extractor.

## Results

- On VIMA-Bench with only **1.2%** of training data, LiteVLP-m reaches **84.2%**/**78.1%**/**75.4%** success at L1/L2/L3, close to full-data VIMA's **81.5%**/**81.5%**/**78.7%** and beating LLaRA-7B and Mipha-3B baselines trained on the same small dataset.
- On three long-horizon tasks (rearrangement, reasoning, constraints) LiteVLP-m averages **83.4%** success, beating CoTDiffusion's **64.6%** by **18.8** points, attributed to its historical and future-goal memory.
- MOC ablation shows near-unchanged accuracy (**79.2%** vs **79.6%** average) while cutting training time by **47.5%** and boosting inference speed by **34.1%**; training took **6.1** hours on 4 RTX 3090s versus **24** hours for VIMA and **21** hours for LLaRA-7B.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - High structural alignment between fine-tuning data format and pretraining data format reduces domain shift more effectively than simply scaling model size.
> - Redundant static image patches across consecutive observations are a target for compression without accuracy loss, generalizable beyond this specific robotic setup.
> - Aggregating multi-image tokens together rather than interleaving them with text preserves structured input representation and materially improves downstream success rates.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

Robot control systems have long sought to achieve human-like understanding of visual inputs and natural language instructions. Recent advances in Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have shown remarkable potential in connecting these domains, enabling robots to interpret complex instructions and visual scenes. However, implementing VLMs in practical robotics applications faces significant obstacles that limit their real-world utility.

The research paper "Towards Fast, Memory-based and Data-Efficient Vision-Language Policy" by Haoxuan Li and colleagues from Huazhong University of Science and Technology addresses these challenges by introducing LiteVLP, a lightweight and efficient vision-language policy model. The model achieves the impressive feat of maintaining high performance while significantly reducing computational requirements and training data needs.

### 2. Challenges in VLM-Based Robotics

Traditional approaches to robot control often struggle with understanding complex visual scenes and natural language instructions. Large Vision-Language Models (VLMs) offer promising solutions but face three critical limitations:

1. **Computational Cost**: Large VLMs require substantial computational resources, making real-time robot control challenging or impossible.

2. **Domain Adaptation**: Pre-trained VLMs typically learn from web data, creating a domain shift when applied to robot manipulation tasks.

3. **Memory Limitations**: Many VLMs process only current observations without effectively leveraging historical context or future goals.

These challenges have prevented the widespread adoption of VLMs in practical robotic applications, despite their theoretical potential. The ability to process historical context and future goals is particularly critical for successful long-horizon robotic tasks that require planning several steps ahead.

### 3. LiteVLP Architecture

LiteVLP adopts a thoughtfully designed architecture to address these limitations while maintaining high performance. The system uses a 1B-parameter InternVL2-1B model as its backbone, considerably smaller than many state-of-the-art alternatives that often exceed 7B parameters.

The architecture consists of several key components:

1. **Vision Transformer (ViT)**: Processes image inputs and extracts visual features.

2. **Text Tokenizer**: Converts natural language instructions into tokens.

3. **MLP Projector**: Aligns visual and textual features in a shared embedding space.

4. **Large Language Model (LLM)**: Integrates multimodal information and generates robot actions.

5. **Multi-Observation Compression (MOC)**: A novel module that efficiently processes multiple sequential observations.

The model accepts multiple images as input (past, current, and future states) along with textual instructions, enabling it to understand the task context comprehensively. The output consists of position coordinates (x, y) and rotation angle (θ) for robot actions.

### 4. Multi-Observation Compression

A key innovation in LiteVLP is the Multi-Observation Compression (MOC) module, which addresses the challenge of efficiently processing multiple sequential observations.

Traditional approaches that simply concatenate multiple image tokens often lead to quadratic computational complexity, making them impractical for real-time robot control. The MOC module tackles this by identifying and removing redundant information across sequential images.

The compression process works as follows:

1. Identify static patches across consecutive frames.
2. Replace redundant patches with the first occurrence.
3. Add a length embedding to indicate how long each patch persists in the sequence.

This approach is particularly effective because robotic scenes often contain many static elements, with only a few objects moving between frames. The mathematical representation of this process can be expressed as:

$$p_i^{compressed} =
\begin{cases}
p_i, & \text{if } sim(p_i, p_{i-1}) < \text{threshold} \\
\emptyset, & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}$$

Where $p_i$ represents the image patch at position $i$, and $sim$ is a similarity function.

The MOC module significantly reduces the number of image tokens that need processing, decreasing both training time and inference latency without compromising model performance.

### 5. Visuomotor Instruction Tuning

LiteVLP employs a specialized fine-tuning approach called visuomotor instruction tuning to effectively adapt pre-trained VLMs to robotic tasks. This approach is inspired by the LLaRA framework but modified for the specific requirements of robot control.

The training process involves:

1. **Data Preparation**: Converting robotic demonstration data into a conversation-style format that interleaves visual observations with textual instructions.

2. **Object Detection and Description**: Using an object detector to identify and describe objects in the scenes, creating rich contextual information.

3. **Data Fusion**: Combining task descriptions with object information to create comprehensive prompts.

This approach enables the model to learn efficient representations of robot manipulation tasks using limited data. The training process uses the D-inBC-8k dataset with the following optimization parameters:

- Optimizer: AdamW
- Learning rate: 2e-5
- Weight decay: 0.01
- Batch size: 32
- Warm-up ratio: 0.03

During training, the vision encoder parameters are frozen to preserve the pre-trained visual knowledge, while the MLP layers, LLM, and MOC module parameters are fine-tuned.

### 6. Experimental Results

LiteVLP was evaluated on the VIMA-Bench dataset, which includes a diverse range of robotic manipulation tasks with varying difficulty levels (L1, L2, and L3). The model demonstrated impressive performance across all difficulty levels:

- 84.2% success rate on L1 tasks
- 78.1% success rate on L2 tasks
- 75.4% success rate on L3 tasks

What makes these results particularly notable is that LiteVLP achieves this performance with significantly fewer parameters and less training data than competing models. For example, it outperforms or matches models like LLaRA-7B and Mipha-3B while using only a 1B-parameter backbone.

### 7. Performance Analysis

The most impressive aspect of LiteVLP is its exceptional efficiency compared to alternative approaches:

1. **Training Efficiency**: LiteVLP reduces training time by 17.9 hours compared to VIMA and 14.9 hours compared to LLaRA-7B, while achieving comparable success rates.

2. **Inference Speed**: The model achieves 6.8 times lower inference latency than LLaRA, making it suitable for real-time robot control applications.

3. **Parameter Efficiency**: With just 1B parameters, LiteVLP requires significantly less memory than 7B+ models, making it deployable on more modest hardware.

4. **Data Efficiency**: The model achieves competitive performance with limited training data (D-inBC-8k), demonstrating effective transfer learning from the pre-trained VLM.

The efficiency benefits are largely attributed to two key innovations:

1. The lightweight InternVL2-1B backbone, which provides a solid foundation with fewer parameters.
2. The Multi-Observation Compression module, which substantially reduces the computational complexity of processing multiple sequential images.

### 8. Long-Horizon Tasks and Robustness

LiteVLP excels particularly in two critical areas: long-horizon manipulation tasks and robustness to environmental variations.

For long-horizon tasks, which require planning and executing multiple steps in sequence, LiteVLP outperforms baseline models by an average of 18.8%. This improvement is attributed to the model's ability to effectively process both past observations and future goal states, providing rich contextual information for decision-making.

The model also demonstrates impressive robustness to object location inaccuracies. As shown in Figure 6, LiteVLP-m (which uses multiple image inputs) maintains much better performance under increasing levels of random noise compared to LiteVLP-s (which uses single image input). At the highest noise level of 0.8, LiteVLP-m experiences only a 9.6% performance drop, while LiteVLP-s suffers a 22.5% decline.

This robustness is crucial for real-world robotic applications, where perfect perception is rarely achievable, and environmental variations are common.

### 9. Conclusion and Impact

LiteVLP represents a significant advancement in making Vision-Language Models practical for robotic applications. By addressing the key challenges of computational efficiency, memory integration, and domain adaptation, it brings us closer to robots that can effectively understand and execute complex natural language instructions.

The key contributions of this research include:

1. A lightweight VLM-based policy that achieves competitive performance with significantly reduced computational requirements.
2. The novel Multi-Observation Compression module that efficiently processes multiple sequential observations.
3. A visuomotor instruction tuning approach that effectively adapts pre-trained VLMs to robotic tasks with limited data.

The potential impact extends beyond academic research to practical applications in manufacturing, healthcare, logistics, and personal assistance, where robots need to understand and respond to complex instructions and environments. The 6.8× inference speedup is particularly significant, as it makes real-time control feasible on more modest hardware platforms.

As Vision-Language Models continue to evolve, approaches like LiteVLP that focus on practical efficiency rather than just increasing model size will be crucial for bridging the gap between laboratory demonstrations and real-world robotic applications. This research represents an important step toward more capable, responsive, and practical vision-language-guided robotic systems.
