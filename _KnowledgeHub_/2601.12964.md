# Cross-Scale Pretraining: Enhancing Self-Supervised Learning for Low-Resolution Satellite Imagery for Semantic Segmentation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Carnegie Mellon University Africa introduced a spatial affinity component for cross-scale pretraining, integrating real high-resolution (HR) and mid-resolution (MR) satellite imagery to improve self-supervised learning. This approach consistently enhanced downstream semantic segmentation performance, achieving higher mean Intersection over Union (mIoU) on MR tasks compared to single-resolution pretraining across various benchmarks.

## Problem

- Deep learning for remote sensing tasks like semantic segmentation is hampered by the scarcity of expensive, pixel-level annotated datasets.
- Current self-supervised learning (SSL) in remote sensing primarily pretrains on readily available mid-resolution (MR) data, which limits the capture of fine spatial details crucial for many applications.
- Existing multi-resolution SSL approaches often rely on synthetic downsampling of high-resolution (HR) imagery, failing to replicate the true physical and sensor-specific characteristics of real lower-resolution images.

## Method

- Developed a pluggable ==spatial affinity component== for existing self-supervised learning (SSL) frameworks, enabling cross-scale pretraining.
- Utilizes a ==student-teacher architecture== to process real co-located high-resolution (HR) and mid-resolution (MR) image pairs simultaneously.
- Employs a ==gram loss== that computes the mean squared error between gram matrices of student and downsampled HR teacher outputs, fostering spatial alignment robust to sensor differences.

## Results

- The SA-model, incorporating cross-scale pretraining, consistently achieved higher **mIoU** in semantic segmentation across three Geo-Bench datasets compared to models pretrained exclusively on single-resolution HR or MR data.
- Pretraining with real high-resolution (HR) data led to significantly superior performance compared to using synthetically interpolated HR data, confirming the importance of actual sensor characteristics.
- Qualitative visualizations demonstrated that the SA-model learned more discriminative spatial representations for mid-resolution (MR) imagery, enabling better identification of distinct spatial features.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Real high-resolution (HR) satellite data contains critical physical and sensor-specific characteristics that are essential for learning superior mid-resolution (MR) representations, outperforming synthetically generated HR imagery.
> - Aligning the spatial structure of features using gram matrices allows effective knowledge transfer between real HR and MR imagery, tolerating sensor differences while enhancing patch-level spatial detail in MR representations.
> - Integrating a spatial affinity component into existing SSL frameworks enables robust cross-scale pretraining, demonstrating generalizability across different masked image modeling architectures like LatentMIM and I-JEPA.
