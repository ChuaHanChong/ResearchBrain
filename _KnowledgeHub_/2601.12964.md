# Cross-Scale Pretraining: Enhancing Self-Supervised Learning for Low-Resolution Satellite Imagery for Semantic Segmentation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Carnegie Mellon University Africa introduced a spatial affinity component for cross-scale pretraining, integrating real high-resolution (HR) and mid-resolution (MR) satellite imagery to improve self-supervised learning. This approach consistently enhanced downstream semantic segmentation performance, achieving higher mean Intersection over Union (mIoU) on MR tasks compared to single-resolution pretraining across various benchmarks.

## Problem

- Deep learning for remote sensing tasks like semantic segmentation is hampered by the scarcity of expensive, pixel-level annotated datasets.
- Current self-supervised learning (SSL) in remote sensing primarily pretrains on readily available mid-resolution (MR) data, which limits the capture of fine spatial details crucial for many applications.
- Existing multi-resolution SSL approaches often rely on synthetic downsampling of high-resolution (HR) imagery, failing to replicate the true physical and sensor-specific characteristics of real lower-resolution images.

## Method

- Developed a pluggable ==spatial affinity component== for existing self-supervised learning (SSL) frameworks, enabling cross-scale pretraining.
- Utilizes a ==student-teacher architecture== to process real co-located high-resolution (HR) and mid-resolution (MR) image pairs simultaneously.
- Employs a ==gram loss== that computes the mean squared error between gram matrices of student and downsampled HR teacher outputs, fostering spatial alignment robust to sensor differences.

## Results

- The SA-model, incorporating cross-scale pretraining, consistently achieved higher **mIoU** in semantic segmentation across three Geo-Bench datasets compared to models pretrained exclusively on single-resolution HR or MR data.
- Pretraining with real high-resolution (HR) data led to significantly superior performance compared to using synthetically interpolated HR data, confirming the importance of actual sensor characteristics.
- Qualitative visualizations demonstrated that the SA-model learned more discriminative spatial representations for mid-resolution (MR) imagery, enabling better identification of distinct spatial features.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Real high-resolution (HR) satellite data contains critical physical and sensor-specific characteristics that are essential for learning superior mid-resolution (MR) representations, outperforming synthetically generated HR imagery.
> - Aligning the spatial structure of features using gram matrices allows effective knowledge transfer between real HR and MR imagery, tolerating sensor differences while enhancing patch-level spatial detail in MR representations.
> - Integrating a spatial affinity component into existing SSL frameworks enables robust cross-scale pretraining, demonstrating generalizability across different masked image modeling architectures like LatentMIM and I-JEPA.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses challenges in semantic segmentation within remote sensing, a field crucial for applications such as crop cover mapping, flood extent estimation, and marine pollution detection. Deep learning methods are effective for these tasks but are constrained by the scarcity and high cost of acquiring pixel-level annotations for Earth Observation imagery.

Self-Supervised Learning (SSL) has emerged as a suitable pretraining strategy in remote sensing because it leverages abundant unannotated imagery to learn robust representations, thereby mitigating the annotation scarcity problem. While general computer vision often deals with uniform image resolutions, remote sensing involves diverse multi-sensor and multi-spatial resolution imagery. High-resolution (HR) datasets (e.g., ≤ 5m Ground Sample Distance, GSD) provide richer spatial detail and generally lead to superior performance in downstream tasks, but they are often costly or restricted. Conversely, mid-resolution (MR) datasets (e.g., ≥ 10m GSD), such as Sentinel 1/2, are widely available but yield suboptimal performance.

The majority of prior remote sensing SSL literature has focused on pretraining with MR imagery due to its accessibility. However, recent releases of large, diverse historical HR and MR image pair datasets, such as Sen2Venus, enable new pretraining strategies. A direct approach might involve pretraining solely on HR data, but this risks losing advantages associated with MR imagery, such as its higher spectral dimension, and may not transfer effectively to downstream tasks that predominantly use MR inputs.

Existing multi-resolution SSL approaches, such as Scale-MAE and Cross-Scale MAE, have attempted to bridge resolutions by mathematically downsampling HR imagery to simulate lower-resolution counterparts. However, research in super-resolution indicates that such artificial downsampling is less effective than using real low-resolution images, as it fails to replicate the physical and sensor-specific characteristics inherent in actual imagery.

This work draws inspiration from latent-space super-resolution techniques, such as Perception-GAN (PGAN), which aim to improve performance on specific tasks (e.g., object detection) by learning to represent low-resolution images with spatial details akin to higher-resolution images within the latent space. Unlike pixel-space super-resolution, which aims to generate a higher-resolution image, latent-space super-resolution focuses on improving feature representations.

The paper evaluates its proposed method within the context of contemporary masked image modeling (MIM) SSL frameworks, specifically LatentMIM and I-JEPA. These frameworks learn by reconstructing masked patch representations rather than raw pixels, a state-of-the-art approach in remote sensing SSL. By designing a component that integrates real HR/MR pairs into these existing SSL frameworks, the research aims to advance cross-scale representation learning, providing a method to capitalize on the benefits of both high-resolution detail and mid-resolution accessibility.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this research stems from the inherent characteristics and challenges of remote sensing data and its application in semantic segmentation:

1. **Annotation Scarcity in Remote Sensing:** Pixel-level annotations for Earth Observation imagery are labor-intensive and expensive to acquire, limiting the effectiveness of supervised deep learning models. Self-Supervised Learning (SSL) offers a solution by leveraging the abundant unannotated satellite imagery for pretraining, thereby improving downstream task performance in data-scarce scenarios.

2. **Disparity Between High-Resolution (HR) and Mid-Resolution (MR) Imagery:** HR datasets provide richer spatial details, leading to superior performance in tasks like semantic segmentation. However, they are often proprietary, costly, or subject to access restrictions. Conversely, MR datasets are widely available and often possess a higher spectral dimension, which can be beneficial for transfer performance. The goal is to leverage the advantages of HR imagery without sacrificing the benefits or practical applicability of MR imagery.

3. **Limitations of Existing Pretraining Strategies:**
 - **MR-only Pretraining:** While common due to data availability, pretraining solely on MR imagery results in suboptimal performance compared to what could be achieved if HR details were incorporated.
 - **HR-only Pretraining:** Pretraining exclusively on HR data, though potentially yielding rich spatial features, may not transfer effectively to downstream tasks that typically use MR imagery as input. It also disregards the higher spectral dimension often present in MR data.
 - **Synthetic Multi-resolution Data:** Previous multi-resolution SSL approaches that mathematically downsample HR images to create synthetic low-resolution counterparts are considered less effective than using real image pairs. This is because mathematical downsampling fails to capture the physical and sensor-specific characteristics of genuine lower-resolution images.

Given these motivations, the key objectives of this research are:

1. **To develop a cross-scale pretraining strategy** that effectively integrates both real high-resolution (HR) and mid-resolution (MR) satellite imagery datasets. The strategy aims to enable models to exploit the richer visual detail of HR images while retaining the benefits and practical relevance of MR images.
2. **To design a novel spatial affinity component** that can be seamlessly integrated into existing self-supervised learning (SSL) frameworks. This component's primary function is to learn richer representations of MR imagery by associating its features with the detailed spatial information present in corresponding HR images.
3. **To learn MR image patch representations** that incorporate a level of spatial detail comparable to that found in HR images. This is achieved by encouraging the MR representations to align with HR representations at a structural level.
4. **To empirically evaluate the efficacy** of the proposed cross-scale pretraining strategy by comparing its downstream semantic segmentation performance on various MR tasks against models pretrained solely on HR or MR imagery.
5. **To analyze the contribution of real HR data** versus synthetically generated HR data (via upsampling MR images) to confirm the specific value derived from authentic HR information.
6. **To conduct ablation studies** on key components of the spatial affinity module, including the loss function (Gram loss vs. MSE), HR representation downsampling methods, and patch sampling strategies, to understand their individual impact on performance.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces a cross-scale pretraining strategy centered around a novel **spatial affinity component**, designed to enhance self-supervised learning for mid-resolution (MR) satellite imagery using high-resolution (HR) counterparts. This component is designed for integration into existing SSL frameworks.

**A. Spatial Affinity Component Design:**
The spatial affinity component employs a student-teacher architecture, a common design for self-distillation in SSL to prevent model collapse.
- **Student Encoder:** This encoder processes patches from the mid-resolution image ($I_{MR}$). Its parameters are updated directly through backpropagation during the training process. This student encoder is shared with the host SSL framework (e.g., LatentMIM or I-JEPA).
- **High-Resolution Teacher Encoder:** This encoder processes patches from the corresponding high-resolution image ($I_{HR}$). Its architecture is identical to the student encoder, but its parameters are updated via an exponential moving average (EMA) of the student encoder's parameters. This EMA update mechanism helps stabilize training and prevents the teacher from collapsing to trivial solutions.

**B. Inputs and Data Handling:**
- **Image Pairs:** The pretraining is conducted using real high-resolution and mid-resolution image pairs from the **Sen2Venus dataset**. This dataset contains coincident Sentinel-2 (10-meter spatial resolution) and Venus (5-meter spatial resolution) imagery.
- **Scale Factor:** To maintain the spatial resolution difference between the MR and HR images, a scale factor `s=2` is used, such that $H_{HR}/H_{MR} = W_{HR}/W_{MR} = s$.
- **Patching:** Both HR and MR images are divided into non-overlapping patches, consistent with standard Vision Transformers (ViTs).
- **Sampling:** The input to the student encoder consists of patches sampled from the MR image. The specific sampling strategies are adopted from the host SSL frameworks: block sampling for I-JEPA and random sampling for LatentMIM. Ablation studies were conducted on block sampling for LatentMIM.
- **HR Representation Downsampling:** Due to the scale factor, each patch in $I_{MR}$ corresponds to $s^2$ (i.e., 4) patches in $I_{HR}$. After the high-resolution teacher encodes these $s^2$ HR patches into representations, these representations are downsampled to a single representation to match the dimensionality and number of representations from the student encoder. Ablations explored bilinear, bicubic interpolation, and linear projection as downsampling methods.

**C. Gram Loss:**
The core of the spatial affinity component's objective function is the **gram loss**, as defined in DINOv3. This loss function computes the mean squared error between the gram matrices of the student encoder's output and the downsampled high-resolution teacher's output.
- Let $Z_S \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times d}$ be the L2-normalized patch representations from the student encoder (MR).
- Let $Z_T \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times d}$ be the L2-normalized patch representations from the high-resolution teacher after downsampling (HR).
- The gram loss is defined as: $L = \|Z_S \cdot Z_S^\top - Z_T \cdot Z_T^\top\|_2^2$.
- **Rationale:** Using gram matrices (pairwise dot products of patch representations) instead of direct patch representations provides tolerance for sensor-specific differences that exist between real HR and MR image pairs. Simultaneously, it penalizes differences in the higher-order patch-level spatial structure, thereby encouraging the MR representations to capture the spatial detail present in the HR counterparts. This approach differs from DINOv3's "gram teacher," where parameters are drawn from an earlier iteration; here, the HR teacher is updated continuously via EMA.

**D. Implementation Details:**
- **Dataset:** A subset of 119,659 random image pairs from the Sen2Venus dataset was used for pretraining. Only the red, green, blue, and near-infrared (RGBN) bands were utilized.
- **SSL Frameworks:**
 - **I-JEPA:** Trained with a learning rate of 0.001 (cosine schedule), batch size of 64, and weight decay of 0.04 using AdamW optimizer for 300 epochs. It used a ViT-Small architecture with a patch size of 14 for encoders and teacher, and a predictor depth of 12 with an embedding size of 384.
 - **LatentMIM:** Trained with a learning rate of 0.00015, batch size of 128, and weight decay of 0.05 using AdamW optimizer for 300 epochs. It employed a ViT-Small architecture with a patch size of 16 for encoders and teacher, and a decoder depth of 3.

**E. Evaluation:**
- **Downstream Task:** Linear probing was used for semantic segmentation, a standard evaluation method for learned representations where a linear classifier is trained on frozen features.
- **Evaluation Datasets (all MR):**
 - Geo-Bench’s m-SA-Crop-Type (crop type mapping)
 - Sen1Floods11 (flood mapping)
 - PASTIS (crop mapping)
 - MADOS (marine pollutants and surface features detection)
- **Metric:** Mean Intersection over Union (mIoU) was reported, averaged over three experimental runs to ensure robustness. The input bands for evaluation were also RGBN.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents findings from a comparative analysis and several ablation studies, demonstrating the effectiveness of the proposed cross-scale pretraining approach.

**A. Semantic Segmentation Performance Comparison (Table I):**
The primary comparison involved three categories of models:
1. **MR-model:** Pretrained solely on mid-resolution (Sentinel-2) images.
2. **HR-model:** Pretrained solely on high-resolution (Venus) images.
3. **SA-model:** Pretrained using the Spatial Affinity component with both mid- and high-resolution image pairs.

The SA-model consistently demonstrated superior performance across different semantic segmentation tasks and SSL frameworks:
- **LatentMIM Framework:** The SA-model achieved the highest mIoU in all four tasks (m-SA: 23.39, Sen1Floods11: 78.82, PASTIS: 22.23, MADOS: 43.00), outperforming both the MR-model and HR-model.
- **I-JEPA Framework:** The SA-model yielded the highest mIoU in three out of four tasks (m-SA: 22.36, Sen1Floods11: 79.04, PASTIS: 15.88). For MADOS, the HR-model slightly outperformed the SA-model (42.33 vs. 31.79), and both were notably higher than the MR-model (36.85).
Overall, the SA-model generally outperformed the MR-model in all but one case and surpassed the HR-model in the majority of cases across both SSL frameworks.

**B. Impact of Real vs. False High-Resolution Imagery (Table II):**
To ascertain whether the performance gains were specifically from authentic HR data or merely from the additional spatial affinity objective, the SA-model was compared against a control model trained with 'false' HR images (generated via interpolation of MR images).
- Models utilizing **real HR data** consistently outperformed those using synthetically upsampled ('false') HR data in 7 out of 8 cases across both LatentMIM and I-JEPA frameworks. This indicates that the physical and sensor-specific information from real HR imagery is crucial for learning robust representations, surpassing the utility of interpolated data.

**C. Qualitative View (Fig. 2):**
Unsupervised hierarchical clustering of patch representations from an unseen Sentinel-2 image revealed that the SA-model's representations provided a clearer distinction of various features. The cluster maps generated from the SA-model's representations were more effective at identifying distinct features (indicated by red circles in the paper's figure) compared to those from MR-only and HR-only models, suggesting that the SA-model learns more discriminative and spatially informed representations.

**D. Ablation Studies:**
- **Gram Loss vs. MSE (Table III):** An ablation study comparing gram loss to mean squared error (MSE) for the spatial affinity component demonstrated that gram loss consistently yielded better downstream semantic segmentation performance. This supports the hypothesis that gram loss's ability to tolerate sensor-specific differences while preserving spatial structure is beneficial for cross-scale learning.
- **High-resolution Representation Downsampling (Table IV):** Three methods for downsampling the HR patch representations were evaluated: bilinear, bicubic interpolation, and linear projection. Bilinear downsampling performed comparably to or better than bicubic and linear projection across the tested tasks, despite being the most computationally efficient method.
- **Sampling Strategy (Table V):** When evaluating different patch sampling strategies for the spatial affinity component within LatentMIM, block sampling did not result in improved performance over LatentMIM's default random sampling. This suggests that for this component, random sampling is sufficient or more effective.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a notable contribution to the field of remote sensing by proposing a practical and effective strategy for leveraging multi-resolution satellite imagery in self-supervised learning. The introduction of the **spatial affinity component** and its integration into existing SSL frameworks provides a mechanism to enhance the representation learning of mid-resolution (MR) satellite imagery by incorporating the rich spatial detail from high-resolution (HR) counterparts.

The significance of this work can be understood through several points:
- **Enhanced Representation Learning:** The consistent outperformance of the SA-model over models pretrained solely on MR or HR data demonstrates that cross-scale pretraining, specifically when incorporating real multi-resolution pairs, yields more robust and semantically rich representations for MR imagery. This addresses a critical challenge in remote sensing where MR data is abundant for application but often lacks the fine detail of HR data.
- **Bridging Resolution Gaps:** The methodology provides a means to effectively bridge the information gap between different spatial resolutions. By encouraging MR representations to capture HR-level spatial detail through the gram loss and a student-teacher architecture, the approach enables models to benefit from HR data without requiring HR inputs for downstream tasks.
- **Validation of Real Data:** The ablation studies confirming the superior performance of models trained with *real* HR/MR pairs over those using artificially interpolated HR data underscore the importance of authentic multi-sensor observations. This highlights the value of datasets like Sen2Venus and encourages the development and release of similar real-world multi-resolution datasets.
- **Generalizability of the Component:** The spatial affinity component's design as an add-on to existing SSL schemes (demonstrated with LatentMIM and I-JEPA) suggests its potential generalizability across various masked image modeling architectures, allowing other researchers to readily integrate this cross-scale learning approach.
- **Practical Implications for Earth Observation:** Improved semantic segmentation performance on MR tasks directly translates to enhanced capabilities in various Earth Observation applications. This could lead to more accurate and timely maps for:
 - **Food Security:** Better crop type identification and yield prediction.
 - **Disaster Management:** More precise flood extent mapping and damage assessment.
 - **Climate Research:** Improved monitoring of environmental changes, such as marine pollution and land cover dynamics.
 Given the widespread availability and lower cost of MR satellite data, improving the performance on these inputs has a direct and broad impact on operational monitoring systems.
- **Future Research Directions:** The paper also identifies a promising direction for future work: reducing reliance on perfectly co-registered, high-quality HR/MR image pairs. This acknowledges a practical limitation and suggests avenues for developing more robust cross-scale learning methods that can operate with less stringent data requirements, potentially involving techniques for synthetic data generation or improved unsupervised alignment.

In conclusion, this research offers a validated approach to improve self-supervised pretraining for remote sensing, providing a method to effectively combine the strengths of diverse spatial resolution data. This has implications for advancing the performance and applicability of deep learning models in critical Earth Observation tasks.
