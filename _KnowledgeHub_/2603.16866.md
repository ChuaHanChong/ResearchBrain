# ManiTwin: Scaling Data-Generation-Ready Digital Object Dataset to 100K

> [!summary] Summary
> ManiTwin is an automated pipeline turning a single image into a simulation-ready 3D object twin: a generative 3D model produces the mesh, a VLM estimates physical properties and selects functional and grasp points, GraspGen proposes 6-DoF grasps, and physics simulation verifies stability. It yields ManiTwin-100K, 100K annotated assets supporting 5M verified grasps and 10M trajectories.

## Problem

- Large geometry repositories like Objaverse and ShapeNet are geometry-centric, lacking physical parameters, collision-validated meshes, and interaction semantics needed for physics-based manipulation simulation.
- Robotics-oriented asset sets with articulation or affordance labels stay small, need post-processing before simulator deployment, and rarely pair annotations with language descriptions.
- Physically faithful collections cover only tens to hundreds of objects, and automatic annotation efforts skip physics verification, so no dataset unites scale, semantics, and validated usability.

## Method

- ==Asset Generation==: an ==image-to-3D generative model== produces meshes, a VLM quality gate rejects multi-object or artifact-ridden outputs, and a VLM infers OBB size, mass, friction, plus captions.
- ==Asset Annotation==: ==farthest point sampling== picks spatially spread candidates on the surface, a VLM labels functional and grasp points with type and rationale, and ==GraspGen== synthesizes dense ==6-DoF proposals==.
- ==Verification==: spatial proximity filtering associates grasps with semantic points, then ==SAPIEN/PhysX== runs grasp-close, lift, and slide-resistance tests, with human review of sampled objects refining prompts and thresholds.

## Results

- 3D generation passes quality checks at **69.67%**, and grasp verification retains **76.13%** of candidates, cutting **81.63** proposals per object down to **62.14** verified grasps.
- Human evaluation on **500** sampled objects gives **100%** category accuracy, **99.6%** language descriptions, **92.2%** for both functional points and physical properties, and **84.8%** grasp point selection.
- The dataset spans **512** categories and object sizes from **2 cm** to **37 cm**, yielding over **5M** verified grasps and **10M** trajectories averaging **7.6 seconds** each.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Physics-simulation verification converts noisy generative and VLM outputs into trustworthy assets: roughly a quarter of proposed grasps fail stability tests and must be discarded.
> - VLM annotation accuracy degrades as judgments become more physical: naming a category is near-perfect, while choosing stable grasp locations from images is markedly harder.
> - Scaling assets, not just policies, unblocks simulation data generation, but VLM-inferred mass and friction remain uncalibrated, and rigid-only coverage excludes articulated and deformable objects.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation learning heavily relies on simulation for scalable training data. However, a significant challenge within this paradigm is the scarcity of "data-generation-ready" digital object assets that possess both geometric diversity and the necessary physical and semantic properties for interaction. Existing 3D asset datasets largely fall into two categories:

- **Geometry-Centric Datasets:** Datasets like ShapeNet, ModelNet, Objaverse, and Objaverse-XL provide vast collections of 3D meshes, often with broad category coverage and language captions. However, they are primarily designed for geometric or visual understanding tasks and typically lack critical information for robotic manipulation, such as physical parameters (mass, friction), articulation structures, collision validity, and interaction semantics. Utilizing these assets for robotics often necessitates extensive and time-consuming manual curation and annotation.
- **Robotics-Oriented Datasets:** Efforts like PartNet-Mobility, GAPartNet, PhysXNet, YCB Object Set, and RoboTwin-OD have introduced assets with articulation models, part-level semantics, affordance labels, or physical fidelity. While valuable, these datasets are generally limited in scale, sometimes require additional post-processing for simulation deployment, and may lack comprehensive language descriptions or systematic physical verification. For instance, YCB provides high physical fidelity but only covers a small number of objects, and RoboTwin-OD offers simulation-ready assets with manipulation annotations but is also limited in scale and lacks functional annotations.

The persistent gap identified by the authors is the absence of a large-scale dataset that simultaneously offers manipulation-centric objects, rich functional and grasp annotations aligned with language descriptions, and assets verified for simulation readiness and collision-freeness. ManiTwin directly addresses this gap by developing an automated pipeline to generate such assets at scale and subsequently constructing ManiTwin-100K, a dataset that integrates these features. This work aims to establish a more robust foundation for scalable simulation data synthesis and policy learning by bridging the deficiencies of current asset repositories.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind ManiTwin stems from the observation that robotic manipulation learning in simulation is constrained by the availability of appropriate digital object assets. Current asset repositories typically fall short in either scale, diversity, or the specific manipulation-centric annotations required for realistic and effective robot learning.

The key objectives of this research are:

1. **Develop an Automated and Efficient Pipeline:** To create a workflow, named ManiTwin, capable of transforming a single input image into a "data-generation-ready" digital object twin. This pipeline is designed to be efficient and scalable, enabling large-scale asset generation without extensive manual intervention.
2. **Synthesize Simulation-Ready 3D Assets with Rich Annotations:** The pipeline aims to produce 3D assets that are not merely geometric models but are fully prepared for physics-based simulation. This includes generating collision-ready meshes and automatically annotating them with essential properties such as:
 - **Physical Properties:** Oriented bounding box (OBB) dimensions, estimated mass, and surface friction coefficients.
 - **Language Descriptions:** Comprehensive semantic captions detailing object category, color, material, size, shape, and functional purpose.
 - **Functional Annotations:** Identification of regions with specific object functions (e.g., spouts, handles, buttons).
 - **Manipulation Proposals:** Densely sampled and categorized grasp points with associated intended use scenarios, complemented by learning-based 6-DoF grasp proposals.
3. **Ensure Physical Validity and Simulation Readiness:** A critical objective is to systematically verify that all generated assets and their associated manipulation annotations are physically valid and directly deployable in robotic simulation environments. This involves rigorous physics-based simulation validation for grasp stability and human review for overall quality control.
4. **Construct a Large-Scale, High-Quality Dataset:** Utilizing the developed ManiTwin pipeline, the project aims to build ManiTwin-100K, a dataset comprising 100,000 such annotated 3D assets. This dataset is intended to offer unprecedented scale combined with manipulation-centric attributes, supporting diverse applications in robotics research.
5. **Demonstrate Utility for Downstream Applications:** The research aims to show that ManiTwin and ManiTwin-100K can serve as a strong foundation for various applications, including:
 - Automated generation of manipulation training data (e.g., pick-and-place, functional manipulation, cross-embodiment data).
 - Generation of diverse, physically plausible multi-object scene layouts.
 - Synthesis of robotics-specific Visual Question Answering (VQA) data.
 - Support for 3D understanding tasks like part segmentation and affordance prediction.

Ultimately, the motivation is to mitigate the data bottleneck in robot learning by providing a scalable, diverse, and well-annotated repository of digital objects that can accelerate the development of generalizable manipulation skills in simulated and, subsequently, real-world robotic systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The ManiTwin pipeline is structured into three main stages: (I) Asset Generation, (II) Asset Annotation, and (III) Verification, followed by the creation of the ManiTwin-100K dataset.

#### 3.1. Asset Generation

This stage converts input images into simulation-ready 3D meshes and estimates fundamental physical and semantic properties.

- **3D Generation:** A state-of-the-art 3D generative model (specifically CLAY) is employed to synthesize high-fidelity 3D meshes from one or more input images or text prompts. Input images undergo preprocessing, including background removal and resolution normalization. The generated meshes are converted into formats compatible with common simulation platforms (e.g., USDZ to GLB).
- **Quality Verification:** A VLM-based (Vision-Language Model) quality gate is implemented to filter out unsuitable assets. This gate evaluates multi-view renderings against two criteria: (i) object singularity, ensuring only one coherent object is present, and (ii) visual quality, checking for severe artifacts like fragmentation, texture corruption, or geometric implausibility. Approximately 10–15% of generated content is filtered at this stage.
- **Physical Property Estimation:** For accepted assets, a VLM analyzes eight uniformly distributed renderings to infer critical physical properties: oriented bounding box (OBB) dimensions, estimated mass based on apparent material and volume, and surface friction coefficients derived from visual material cues. These estimates facilitate scale normalization to real-world dimensions.
- **Semantic Captioning:** The VLM also generates rich semantic descriptions for each object, including its category, color, material, size, shape, and functional purpose. These captions are intended for language-conditioned downstream tasks.

#### 3.2. Asset Annotation

This stage enriches the 3D assets with manipulation-relevant semantics.

- **Candidate Point Sampling:** A dense point cloud is sampled from the mesh surface. Farthest Point Sampling (FPS) is then applied to select a set of spatially distributed candidate points (e.g., 42 points), ensuring broad coverage across the object's surface for potential interaction regions. These points are visualized as numbered markers on multi-view renderings.
- **VLM-Based Point Selection:** A VLM is queried with the labeled multi-view images to identify two types of semantically meaningful points among the candidates:
 - **Functional Points:** Regions corresponding to specific object functions (e.g., "water spout," "handle," "blade"). For each, the VLM provides a function description, confidence score, and rationale.
 - **Grasp Points:** Locations suitable for stable robotic grasping, considering factors like proximity to the center of gravity, surface geometry, and safety. Each grasp point includes a grasp type (e.g., parallel-jaw, pinch, power, three-finger, enveloping) and an intended use scenario.
- **Grasp Proposal Generation:** Using GraspGen, a learning-based method, dense 6-DoF grasp proposals (position and quaternion orientation) are predicted from the object's point cloud, each with an associated confidence score.
- **Spatial Filtering and Semantic Association:** Raw grasp proposals are filtered by their spatial proximity (within a 3cm threshold) to the VLM-selected functional and grasp points. Retained grasps inherit semantic labels from their nearest associated points, allowing for task-oriented grasp selection (e.g., "grasp the handle for pouring"). A 7-DoF FPS is applied to retain a diverse set of representative grasps (e.g., 100).

#### 3.3. Verification

This final stage ensures the physical validity and simulation readiness of all annotations.

- **Simulation Verification:** Each filtered grasp proposal undergoes physics-based validation using the SAPIEN simulator with the PhysX backend. A standardized grasp sequence is executed: gripper positioning, finger closure until contact, and stability verification. Successful grasps must maintain stable contact for multiple consecutive frames. Robustness is further tested by a slide resistance protocol, where the grasped object is moved in orthogonal directions, discarding grasps with excessive displacement.
- **Human Verification:** A sampled subset of assets and their annotations are reviewed by human annotators. This process assesses mesh quality, plausibility of estimated physical properties, semantic correctness of VLM annotations, and the results of simulation verification. Feedback is used to refine VLM prompts and filtering thresholds iteratively.
- **Consolidated Output:** The final output for each digital twin includes: a simulation-ready 3D mesh (with PBR materials), estimated physical properties (dimensions, mass, friction), rich language annotations, functional and grasp point annotations with semantic labels, simulation-verified 6-DoF grasp poses, and placement annotations (position and orientation) for scene generation.

#### 3.4. ManiTwin-100K Dataset

The entire pipeline is used to construct ManiTwin-100K, a dataset of 100,000 data-generation-ready digital twins. Input images are curated from diverse sources, including e-commerce product catalogs and text-to-image generations for underrepresented categories. The dataset covers 512 manipulation-relevant object categories, with dimensions ranging from approximately 2 cm to 37 cm. Each object is equipped with 2–4 functional points, 2–3 grasp points, 10–50 simulation-verified 6-DoF grasp poses, physical properties, and rich language descriptions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents experimental results validating the quality of ManiTwin assets and annotations, as well as their utility for various downstream applications.

#### 4.1. 3D Generation Quality

The quality of generated 3D assets was evaluated using latent-based metrics to assess semantic alignment between the generated 3D assets and their input conditions (images or text).

- **Metrics:** CLIP(N-I) and CLIP(N-T) measured CLIP similarity between rendered normal maps and the input image or text, respectively, evaluating geometric alignment. CLIP(I-I) and CLIP(I-T) measured CLIP similarity between rendered RGB images and the input image or text, assessing visual fidelity. ULIP-I and ULIP-T measured 3D-to-image and 3D-to-text alignment using the ULIP latent space. All metrics were averaged across 30 rendered views.
- **Results:** The pipeline produced semantically aligned 3D assets. Image-to-3D generation achieved higher scores across all metrics (e.g., ULIP: 0.2140, CLIP(N-I): 0.6848, CLIP(I-I): 0.7769) compared to text-to-3D generation (e.g., ULIP: 0.1705, CLIP(N-T): 0.1948, CLIP(I-T): 0.2324). This difference is attributed to the richer conditioning signal provided by input images. Generation time was approximately 45 seconds per object for both methods.

#### 4.2. Annotation Quality

Annotation quality was assessed through both automated verification and human evaluation.

- **Automated Verification:**
 - The 3D generation stage achieved a 69.67% success rate, indicating a substantial filtering of low-quality outputs.
 - An average of 81.63 grasp candidates were generated per object. After physics-based simulation verification, an average of 62.14 grasps were retained per object, corresponding to a 76.13% verification success rate. This suggests that a majority of proposed grasps are physically stable.
- **Human Evaluation:** A human study on 500 sampled objects evaluated VLM-generated annotations:
 - Category classification achieved 100.0% accuracy.
 - Language descriptions reached 99.6% accuracy.
 - Functional point labels and physical property estimation both achieved 92.2% accuracy.
 - Grasp point selection had an accuracy of 84.8%, which was slightly lower than other categories, reflecting the complexity of identifying optimal grasp locations purely from visual reasoning.
 The high accuracy across various annotation types indicates the effectiveness of the VLM-based annotation approach.

#### 4.3. ManiTwin-100K Diversity

The ManiTwin-100K dataset was analyzed for semantic and object diversity.

- The dataset comprises 100,000 objects covering 512 distinct object categories.
- Object dimensions range from approximately 2 cm to 37 cm (measured by the longest OBB axis), encompassing a typical scale for household and industrial manipulation scenarios.
- Semantic attributes, including color, function, size, category, material, and shape, exhibited diversity, as illustrated by word clouds. This diversity is intended to support a broad range of tasks and manipulation data generation.

#### 4.4. Data Generation Statistics

To demonstrate scalability, statistics on large-scale grasping data generation using ManiTwin were reported.

- The dataset contains 100,000 objects.
- It includes over 5 million simulation-verified 6-DoF grasp poses.
- From these, 10 million grasp trajectories were automatically generated.
- The average length of a generated trajectory was 7.6 seconds.
This scale of automatically generated manipulation data indicates the practical utility of ManiTwin for large-scale robotic learning, facilitating the training of manipulation policies that generalize across object categories and task types without human teleoperation.

#### 4.5. Applications

The paper details several applications where ManiTwin's annotations prove useful:
- **Manipulation Data Generation:** The assets enable automated generation of pick-and-place, functional, and cross-embodiment manipulation data, reducing the need for manual task specification.
- **Scene Layout Generation:** Placement annotations and collision radii facilitate the generation of diverse, collision-free, and physically plausible multi-object scene layouts.
- **VQA Data Generation:** Rich semantic annotations allow the creation of robotics-specific VQA datasets, supporting the training of VLMs for manipulation-relevant visual concepts and actionable guidance.
- **3D Understanding Tasks:** The dataset supports tasks such as 3D part segmentation, 3D object classification, shape retrieval, cross-modal learning, and affordance prediction.
- **Empowering Real-World Manipulation:** The simulation-ready assets, when combined with pose estimation tools, can help bridge the sim-to-real gap, allowing robots to leverage digital twins for real-world action planning and execution.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ManiTwin represents a development in addressing a fundamental bottleneck in robotic manipulation learning: the lack of scalable, high-quality, and manipulation-ready digital object assets. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Bridging the Data Gap:** ManiTwin directly addresses the identified gap between large-scale, geometry-focused 3D datasets and smaller, robotics-specific datasets. It provides a dataset that simultaneously offers extensive scale (100K objects), rich semantic annotations (functional points, grasp types, language descriptions), and crucial physical properties, all verified for simulation readiness.
- **Automated and Scalable Asset Generation:** The automated pipeline for generating digital object twins from single images significantly reduces the manual effort and expertise traditionally required to create simulation-ready assets. This automation enables continuous expansion of the dataset as 3D generation technologies advance, supporting the evolving demands of robotic learning.
- **Enabling Large-Scale Robotic Learning:** By providing 100,000 objects with verified grasps and functional annotations, ManiTwin-100K establishes a robust foundation for training generalizable manipulation policies in simulation. The ability to generate millions of grasp trajectories and diverse task-oriented demonstrations at scale can lead to more robust and adaptable robot behaviors.
- **Facilitating Generalization and Transferability:** The dataset's diversity in object categories, semantic attributes, and the support for cross-embodiment data generation contributes to training policies that can generalize across different objects, task types, and robot hardware configurations.
- **Advancing Related Research Areas:** Beyond direct robotic manipulation, ManiTwin has the potential to impact various fields:
 - **Simulation Environments:** Provides a rich library for populating diverse and challenging simulation environments.
 - **Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for Robotics:** Enables the creation of specialized VQA datasets to train VLMs capable of understanding manipulation-relevant visual concepts and providing actionable guidance.
 - **3D Computer Vision:** Serves as a valuable resource for tasks like 3D object understanding, part segmentation, and affordance prediction, offering multi-modal annotations (meshes, point clouds, multi-view images, language descriptions).
- **Toward Real-World Deployment:** By providing manipulation-ready digital twins with accurate geometry, physical properties, and semantic annotations, ManiTwin contributes to bridging the simulation-to-real-world gap. Robots can leverage these twins for object reconstruction, pose estimation, and subsequent action planning in physical environments.

**Limitations and Future Work:** The authors acknowledge that ManiTwin-100K currently focuses on rigid, graspable objects and does not include articulated objects (e.g., drawers, doors) or deformable objects (e.g., cloth, rope). Additionally, physical property estimates are VLM-inferred rather than calibrated ground truth. Future work directions include extending the pipeline to articulated structures and incorporating real-world physical property calibration to enhance realism and applicability further.
