# CLA: Latent Alignment for Online Continual Self-Supervised Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> The paper introduces Continual Latent Alignment (CLA), a self-supervised learning strategy designed for online continual learning from unlabeled, non-stationary data streams. CLA consistently surpasses existing online continual self-supervised learning methods on benchmarks like Split CIFAR-100 and Split ImageNet100, and is shown to be a more efficient early-stage pretraining protocol than traditional i.i.d. training.

## Problem

- Most self-supervised learning (SSL) methods assume i.i.d. (independent and identically distributed) data and offline access to all training data, which is often unrealistic for continuous data streams.
- Existing continual self-supervised learning (CSSL) primarily focuses on "offline" scenarios with explicit task boundaries, allowing for multiple epochs of training per task.
- The challenging "Online Continual Self-Supervised Learning (OCSSL)" setting, characterized by continuous, one-pass data streams, no task boundaries, and strict computational budgets, remains largely underexplored.

## Method

- The proposed ==Continual Latent Alignment (CLA)== strategy aims to mitigate catastrophic forgetting by aligning the current model's representations with past representations, using either an ==Exponential Moving Average (EMA)== network or stored past targets from a memory buffer.
- CLA includes variants like CLA-b (base, no replay), CLA-E (EMA with ==FIFO replay buffer==), and CLA-R (stored targets with FIFO replay buffer) to adapt to different online learning conditions.
- The novel ==Cumulative Backward Passes (CBP)== metric is introduced to provide a fair and hardware-agnostic comparison of computational costs across various online continual learning methods.

## Results

- CLA variants consistently achieve state-of-the-art performance in both Final Accuracy and Average Accuracy on Split CIFAR-100 and Split ImageNet100 benchmarks across varying computational budgets.
- CLA demonstrates faster adaptation and stable convergence, sometimes surpassing the performance of conventional i.i.d. training under limited Cumulative Backward Passes (CBP) conditions.
- Models initially pretrained with CLA show superior generalization capabilities to novel downstream tasks (**SVHN**, **Stanford Cars**) when compared to models undergoing full i.i.d. pretraining.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Continuously aligning current features with a dynamically evolving 'past' representation (via EMA or stored targets) effectively mitigates catastrophic forgetting and promotes stable, fast convergence in online SSL without explicit task boundaries.
> - A First-In-First-Out (FIFO) memory buffer, which prioritizes uniform training iterations for replayed samples, proves more effective than a reservoir buffer in OCSSL when integrated with an alignment mechanism.
> - OCSSL strategies, specifically CLA, can be more efficient for early-stage pretraining compared to traditional i.i.d. training, potentially leading to better final models with reduced overall computational cost.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper tackles a highly challenging and relatively understudied area at the confluence of Self-Supervised Learning (SSL) and Continual Learning (CL), specifically **Online Continual Self-Supervised Learning (OCSSL)**.

**Self-Supervised Learning (SSL)** has emerged as a powerful paradigm for learning robust feature representations from unlabeled data, reducing reliance on expensive human annotations. However, most leading SSL methods (e.g., SimCLR, MoCo, SimSiam, BYOL, DINO) operate under the assumption of **i.i.d. (independent and identically distributed) data** and the availability of all training data simultaneously in an "offline" manner. This assumption is often unrealistic in real-world applications where data distributions drift over time.

**Continual Learning (CL)** aims to address the challenge of learning from non-stationary data streams without forgetting previously acquired knowledge (catastrophic forgetting). While CL has seen significant progress, its combination with SSL, known as **Continual Self-Supervised Learning (CSSL)**, is a more recent and active research frontier. Most existing CSSL methods, such as CaSSLe, PFR, SyCON, POCON, Osiris-D/R, and MinRed, primarily focus on "offline" CL scenarios. In these setups, data is typically divided into distinct "tasks" or "experiences" with clear boundaries, allowing models to train for multiple epochs on each task and potentially revisit data.

The paper argues that even this "offline" CSSL setting is insufficient for many practical scenarios. It identifies a crucial gap: the **Online CL (OCL)** setting. In OCL, data arrives in a continuous, one-pass stream, typically in small minibatches, with no explicit task boundaries, and under strict computational budget constraints (e.g., limited training iterations per minibatch). The authors term the intersection of SSL with OCL as **Online Continual Self-Supervised Learning (OCSSL)**. This is a significantly more demanding environment, requiring rapid adaptation, fast convergence, and robust forgetting mitigation simultaneously, all without labeled data or prior knowledge of task structure.

The research landscape for OCSSL is sparse. The paper explicitly states that **SCALE (Yu et al., 2023)** is the *only* existing dedicated OCSSL strategy. This positions CLA as a critical new contribution that addresses a significant void in the field. By proposing CLA, the authors aim to push the boundaries of what is possible in online, label-scarce, and resource-constrained continual learning. Furthermore, the paper implicitly suggests that insights gained from OCSSL can inform and improve broader deep learning practices, particularly in the computationally intensive pretraining of large foundation models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to develop an effective and efficient strategy for **Online Continual Self-Supervised Learning (OCSSL)** that can learn high-quality, generalizable representations from non-stationary, unlabeled data streams while adhering to stringent real-world constraints.

The key specific objectives and motivations are:

1. **Mitigate Catastrophic Forgetting in an Online SSL Setting:** The primary challenge in continual learning is preventing the model from forgetting previously learned knowledge when exposed to new data. In the OCSSL context, where data arrives sequentially and cannot be revisited extensively, this challenge is amplified. The paper aims to develop a mechanism that continually preserves past representations.
2. **Achieve Fast Adaptation and Quick Convergence:** Unlike offline settings, OCSSL mandates training in one pass with small minibatches and limited iterations per batch. This necessitates algorithms that can quickly adapt to new information and converge rapidly to meaningful representations. The model needs to be ready for inference at any point during the training stream.
3. **Operate Without Explicit Task Boundaries:** Real-world data streams rarely come with explicit "task boundaries." The proposed method must inherently handle the continuous, uninterrupted flow of data, making decisions about knowledge retention and plasticity without external task information.
4. **Comply with a Fixed Computational Budget:** Computational resources are often limited. The paper explicitly aims to develop a strategy that is efficient and can perform effectively under a constrained computational budget, introducing the **Cumulative Backward Passes (CBP)** metric for fair comparison. This is critical for practical deployment and sustainable AI development.
5. **Develop Meaningful Latent Representations:** The ultimate goal of SSL is to learn high-quality, generic latent features that can generalize well to various downstream tasks. The method should ensure that despite the online, continual nature of learning, the learned representations remain robust and discriminative.
6. **Explore the Potential for Efficient Pretraining:** A crucial motivation, highlighted by a surprising finding, is to investigate whether an OCSSL strategy, designed for rapid adaptation, can serve as a more efficient early-stage pretraining protocol even for conventional i.i.d. SSL, potentially reducing the computational cost of pretraining foundation models.
7. **Address the Understudied OCSSL Scenario:** The authors explicitly identify OCSSL as largely unexplored despite its high practical relevance. Their work is motivated by the need to fill this research gap and provide a robust solution for environments characterized by unlabeled, streaming, and non-stationary data, such as robotics, autonomous systems, and real-time data analytics.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose **Continual Latent Alignment (CLA)**, a novel Self-Supervised Learning (SSL) strategy specifically designed for the Online Continual Self-Supervised Learning (OCSSL) scenario. CLA's core principle is to align the representations learned by the current model with past representations, thereby mitigating catastrophic forgetting and promoting stable, fast convergence.

**Core Mechanism: Latent Alignment**
CLA builds upon the idea of knowledge distillation, inspired by previous offline CSSL methods like CaSSLe and PFR. However, it adapts this concept for the online setting by addressing two key limitations of prior work: the reliance on explicit task boundaries and the lack of integrated replay strategies for small minibatches.

1. **Alignment Target without Task Boundaries:** Instead of a frozen encoder from a previous task boundary, CLA employs an **Exponential Moving Average (EMA) network (θ')** of the current model's parameters. This EMA network provides a continuously updated "past" reference without needing explicit task information. The EMA update rule is θ' ← τ θ' + (1 - τ)θ, where τ is a scalar parameter. This ensures stability and backward transfer, as the EMA network slowly incorporates changes from the active network.
2. **Projector Head (a_ϕ):** Similar to PFR, CLA uses an additional projector head (a_ϕ) to map current features to the past feature space. This maintains plasticity for learning new features while still enforcing similarity to the target past representations.
3. **Loss Formulation:** The total loss function combines the standard SSL loss (L_SSL) from an underlying method (SimSiam or SimCLR) with a regularization term (L_reg) based on negative cosine similarity (–S_C) for alignment.
 - `L = L_SSL + ωL_reg`
 - `L_reg` measures the similarity between the projected current features and the target past features.

**Variants of CLA:**

- **CLA-b (Base Variant):** This simplest variant does not use a memory buffer. It aligns the current model's features (z) with the features extracted by the EMA network (θ') from the *same* input views (x1, x2). This enforces consistency with the evolving "average" of the model.
- **CLA-E (with EMA and Replay):** This variant introduces a memory buffer (M) for rehearsal. It concatenates replay exemplars (xr) from the buffer with the incoming streaming minibatch (x) to form an extended minibatch. Crucially, the alignment loss is applied *only* to the features derived from the replay samples (zr1, zr2), aligning them with features (ẑ1, ẑ2) from the EMA network operating on the same replay samples. This grants more plasticity for novel streaming data while preventing forgetting of past knowledge via replay and alignment.
- **CLA-R (with Replay and Stored Targets):** Similar to CLA-E, this variant uses a memory buffer for replay. However, instead of using the EMA network for alignment targets, CLA-R stores *past network output features (z\*)* alongside the original samples (x) in the memory buffer. When replay samples are drawn, their current features (zr1, zr2) are aligned directly with their corresponding stored z\*. The stored z\* is also periodically updated (z\* ← 0.5 · z\* + 0.5 · (zr1+zr2)/2), ensuring the targets remain relevant.

**Memory Buffer Strategy:**
For CLA-E and CLA-R, a **First-In-First-Out (FIFO) buffer** is chosen over a reservoir buffer. The authors argue that while a reservoir buffer provides unbiased sampling, a FIFO buffer ensures an equal expected number of training iterations for each sample in the stream. This prioritization of convergence over unbiasedness is posited as beneficial in the fast-paced OCSSL scenario, especially when combined with alignment for forgetting prevention.

**Computational Budget Metric: Cumulative Backward Passes (CBP)**
To ensure fair comparison among diverse OCSSL methods, the paper introduces a novel metric: **Cumulative Backward Passes (CBP)**.
- `CBP = n_v × n_steps × b`
 - `n_v`: number of views for the SSL method (e.g., 2 for SimSiam/SimCLR).
 - `b`: minibatch size (can include stream + replay samples).
 - `n_steps`: total number of training steps.
 - `n_steps = n_p × (N / b_s)`
 - `n_p`: number of sequential minibatch training passes per incoming streaming minibatch.
 - `N`: total number of samples in the training dataset.
 - `b_s`: streaming minibatch size.
CBP serves as a hardware-agnostic proxy for computational cost, enabling comparisons between methods with different architectural complexities or batching strategies.

**Experimental Setup:**
Experiments are conducted on Split CIFAR-100 and Split ImageNet100, common benchmarks for class-incremental CL. ResNet-18 is used as the backbone. The base SSL method is primarily SimSiam, with additional validation using SimCLR. OCSSL conditions include small streaming minibatches (b_s=10) and limited passes per minibatch (n_p=3). Two CBP budgets (Low and High) are tested. Performance is evaluated using **Final Accuracy** (after entire stream) and **Average Accuracy** (mean accuracy after each experience, reflecting performance across the stream). Various existing CSSL/OCSSL methods (MinRed, Osiris-R, SCALE, LUMP) and baselines (ER, finetuning, offline CaSSLe variants) are compared. Ablation studies examine buffer types, EMA update rate (τ), and alignment strength (ω). A unique experiment tests CLA as an early-stage pretraining protocol, followed by i.i.d. training, and finally, downstream task generalization is assessed on SVHN and Stanford Cars datasets.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yields several significant findings, demonstrating the efficacy and unique advantages of Continual Latent Alignment (CLA) in the demanding OCSSL setting.

1. **State-of-the-Art Performance in OCSSL:** CLA variants consistently outperform existing OCSSL and adapted CSSL methods across both Split CIFAR-100 and Split ImageNet100 benchmarks, under both Low and High Cumulative Backward Passes (CBP) budgets.
 - In the **High CBP setting** (Table 1), CLA-E achieves the highest Final Accuracy, while CLA-R exhibits the best Average Accuracy, indicating superior performance maintained throughout the training stream. CLA methods surpass other baselines by significant margins (e.g., over 3% on ImageNet100 Average Accuracy).
 - In the **Low CBP setting** (Table 2), where computational resources are even more constrained, CLA-E again generally leads in both metrics, with CLA-R showing comparable results. Even CLA-b, without replay, performs strongly among methods limited to small streaming minibatches.
 - CLA also performs comparably or better than offline CSSL methods (CaSSLe, CaSSLe-R) that *require* task boundaries, demonstrating that its online-compatible alignment mechanism is not a performance compromise.

2. **Faster Adaptation and Convergence:** The observed high Average Accuracy across different datasets and budgets suggests that CLA's alignment strategy facilitates faster adaptation and more stable learning during the online training process. This is attributed to the continuous regularization provided by aligning current representations with past ones, preventing drastic shifts.

3. **Surpassing i.i.d. Training at Lower CBP:** A surprising and impactful result is that CLA, especially CLA-E with SimSiam, is capable of matching or even surpassing the performance of conventional i.i.d. training (regarded as an upper bound) under certain constrained CBP conditions (Figure 1 and Table 1). The authors hypothesize this is due to the combined benefits of replay and CLA's fast adaptation properties, making it more efficient in early training stages and with limited computational budgets.

4. **Validity of the CBP Metric:** The introduction of the Cumulative Backward Passes (CBP) metric proves effective. Methods with varying configurations (e.g., `n_p=3, b=10` vs. `n_p=1, b=30`) achieve comparable accuracies when their CBP values are equivalent, validating its use as a fair measure of computational budget (Table 2). The empirical results also show that training times scale linearly with CBP (Figure 4).

5. **Effectiveness of FIFO Buffer:** An ablation study on buffer types (Table 3) reveals that a FIFO (First-In-First-Out) buffer generally yields the best performance for CLA-E and CLA-R. This supports the authors' hypothesis that prioritizing uniform training iterations (leading to better convergence) outweighs the potential bias towards recent samples, especially when alignment mechanisms are in place to mitigate forgetting.

6. **CBP Scaling Limitations for OCSSL:** Experiments varying `n_p` (Figure 5) show that while i.i.d. training performance continuously scales with increasing CBP, OCSSL methods plateau after a certain point. This suggests that simply increasing training passes per minibatch (`n_p`) beyond a threshold does not necessarily translate to proportional performance gains for online methods without also increasing minibatch size (`b`) or buffer capacity.

7. **CLA as an Efficient Early-Stage Pretraining Protocol:** One of the most significant findings is that pretraining a model with CLA for an initial phase, and then continuing with standard i.i.d. training, leads to *better* final performance than a full i.i.d. pretraining of the same total CBP (Figure 6). This strongly indicates that CLA is more efficient than i.i.d. training in the early stages of SSL, learning high-quality representations quickly and providing a superior initialization for subsequent training.

8. **Strong Generalization to Downstream Tasks:** Models pretrained with CLA exhibit excellent generalization capabilities. When evaluated on novel downstream tasks (SVHN, Stanford Cars) different from the pretraining datasets, CLA-pretrained encoders consistently outperform other methods, particularly on the more complex Stanford Cars dataset (Table 4).

9. **Consistency with SimCLR:** Repeating key experiments with SimCLR as the base SSL backbone (Appendix B) largely confirms CLA's superior performance and the general trends observed with SimSiam. SimCLR's inherently faster convergence, however, makes i.i.d. baselines harder to beat, and accentuates the importance of sufficient minibatch size for contrastive SSL methods (Table 5, 6).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "CLA: LATENT ALIGNMENT FOR ONLINE CONTINUAL SELF-SUPERVISED LEARNING" carries substantial significance and potential impact across several dimensions of deep learning research and application:

1. **Pioneering OCSSL Solutions:** The paper addresses a critical and largely understudied problem of Online Continual Self-Supervised Learning. By providing a robust and performant solution, CLA sets a new benchmark and paves the way for further research in a domain highly relevant to real-world scenarios where data is inherently streaming, unlabeled, and non-stationary (e.g., autonomous systems, robotics, real-time data processing, IoT).

2. **Paradigm Shift in Pretraining Efficiency:** The most impactful finding is CLA's ability to act as a more efficient early-stage pretraining protocol than conventional i.i.d. training. The demonstration that starting with CLA-based pretraining can lead to better final models with potentially less overall computational cost (or achieve the same performance faster) is a significant breakthrough. This has profound implications for reducing the immense computational and energy costs associated with training large foundation models, fostering more sustainable AI development.

3. **Reframing the Role of i.i.d. Baselines:** The observation that CLA can sometimes surpass i.i.d. training performance, especially under limited computational budgets, challenges the common assumption that i.i.d. represents the ultimate upper bound in all scenarios. It suggests that specific "continual" strategies, designed for rapid adaptation, can offer benefits even when data distribution is, in principle, i.i.d. or when comparing early training phases.

4. **Novel Methodological Contributions:** The introduction of Continual Latent Alignment with EMA-based targets provides a novel and effective mechanism for knowledge preservation and plasticity in online settings, circumventing the need for explicit task boundaries. The proposed Cumulative Backward Passes (CBP) metric is also a valuable contribution, offering a standardized and fair way to compare methods under constrained computational budgets, which is crucial for reproducible and resource-aware AI research.

5. **Enhanced Representation Learning:** The consistent outperformance of CLA on downstream tasks, even those unrelated to the pretraining dataset, underscores its ability to learn highly generalizable and robust feature representations. This enhances the utility of SSL in dynamic, real-world deployments.

6. **Bridging the Gap between Research and Practice:** Many real-world applications demand continuous learning from live, unlabeled data streams with limited resources. CLA directly addresses these practical constraints, making advanced self-supervised representation learning more accessible and deployable in dynamic environments where offline, batch-mode training is infeasible.

7. **Guiding Future Research:** The paper identifies open questions, such as how to design OCSSL methods that can effectively scale to even larger computational budgets without solely relying on increasing replay capacity. This provides clear directions for future research in the field of lifelong learning agents.

In conclusion, this research not only offers a powerful solution for a challenging and under-explored domain (OCSSL) but also presents findings that could fundamentally alter perspectives on pretraining efficiency and the relative merits of continual learning strategies, particularly for the development of future foundation models.
