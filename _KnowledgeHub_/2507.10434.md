# CLA: Latent Alignment for Online Continual Self-Supervised Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> The paper introduces Continual Latent Alignment (CLA), a self-supervised learning strategy designed for online continual learning from unlabeled, non-stationary data streams. CLA consistently surpasses existing online continual self-supervised learning methods on benchmarks like Split CIFAR-100 and Split ImageNet100, and is shown to be a more efficient early-stage pretraining protocol than traditional i.i.d. training.

## Problem

- Most self-supervised learning (SSL) methods assume i.i.d. (independent and identically distributed) data and offline access to all training data, which is often unrealistic for continuous data streams.
- Existing continual self-supervised learning (CSSL) primarily focuses on "offline" scenarios with explicit task boundaries, allowing for multiple epochs of training per task.
- The challenging "Online Continual Self-Supervised Learning (OCSSL)" setting, characterized by continuous, one-pass data streams, no task boundaries, and strict computational budgets, remains largely underexplored.

## Method

- The proposed ==Continual Latent Alignment (CLA)== strategy aims to mitigate catastrophic forgetting by aligning the current model's representations with past representations, using either an ==Exponential Moving Average (EMA)== network or stored past targets from a memory buffer.
- CLA includes variants like CLA-b (base, no replay), CLA-E (EMA with ==FIFO replay buffer==), and CLA-R (stored targets with FIFO replay buffer) to adapt to different online learning conditions.
- The novel ==Cumulative Backward Passes (CBP)== metric is introduced to provide a fair and hardware-agnostic comparison of computational costs across various online continual learning methods.

## Results

- CLA variants consistently achieve state-of-the-art performance in both Final Accuracy and Average Accuracy on Split CIFAR-100 and Split ImageNet100 benchmarks across varying computational budgets.
- CLA demonstrates faster adaptation and stable convergence, sometimes surpassing the performance of conventional i.i.d. training under limited Cumulative Backward Passes (CBP) conditions.
- Models initially pretrained with CLA show superior generalization capabilities to novel downstream tasks (**SVHN**, **Stanford Cars**) when compared to models undergoing full i.i.d. pretraining.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Continuously aligning current features with a dynamically evolving 'past' representation (via EMA or stored targets) effectively mitigates catastrophic forgetting and promotes stable, fast convergence in online SSL without explicit task boundaries.
> - A First-In-First-Out (FIFO) memory buffer, which prioritizes uniform training iterations for replayed samples, proves more effective than a reservoir buffer in OCSSL when integrated with an alignment mechanism.
> - OCSSL strategies, specifically CLA, can be more efficient for early-stage pretraining compared to traditional i.i.d. training, potentially leading to better final models with reduced overall computational cost.
