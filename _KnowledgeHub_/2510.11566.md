# SCOOP'D: Learning Mixed-Liquid-Solid Scooping via Sim2Real Generative Policy

> [!summary] Summary
> SCOOP'D learns robotic scooping of solids from liquid entirely in simulation. An OmniGibson algorithmic demonstrator with privileged state produces the 6,480-demo SimScoop dataset; two Diffusion Policy models handle pre-scoop ladle pose and closed-loop scooping motions, driven by a low-dimensional object-state representation from GroundingDINO, SAM2 and PointNet++. Zero-shot real deployment reaches 82.5% success across 240 trials on Level 1 objects.

## Problem

- Earlier scooping work handles a single item or one granular medium, avoiding realistic containers holding liquid mixed with several solids plus distractor objects.
- Kinesthetic-teaching methods need cumbersome physical demonstrations, and reinforcement learning for scooping is sample-inefficient and produces jerky, imprecise motions on floating targets.
- Image-based sim-to-real leaves a large visual gap for scooping, since reflections, refraction and occlusion on fluid surfaces destabilize depth sensing and segmentation.

## Method

- A ==motion-adaptive heuristic demonstrator== moves the ladle along a circular arc beneath the target using privileged simulator state, with wall-collision escape and target-motion shifting, yielding **SimScoop**.
- Two ==1D CNN Diffusion Policy== models: pre-scoop pose generator predicting arc parameters from object radius, plus a closed-loop policy outputting ladle direction, quaternion and offset.
- ==Geometry-aware localization== pipeline: **GroundingDINO** text prompts seed **SAM2** tracking, then a ==PointNet++== network trained on rendered YCB depth regresses object center and longest radius.

## Results

- Zero-shot real world: **82.5%** over **240** trials on Level 1 objects, **84.2%** large-container/standard ladle versus **80.8%** small-small; harder Level 2 items drop to **48.0%**.
- Beats all baselines on SmallCube/PingPongBall: **17/20** and **19/20** versus LAVA **7/20** and **6/20**, RGB diffusion policy **4/20** and **3/20**, fine-tuned flow VLA **2/20** and **4/20**.
- Scaling **600** to **6,480** demos under severe occlusion lifts simulation success **73.3%** to **80.0%** and real success **60.0%** to **72.5%**; removing the pre-scoop pose gives **0/20**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Compact object-centric state, poses, radius and relative motion, transfers across the visual sim-to-real gap far better than raw RGB when reflective fluid corrupts pixels.
> - Splitting a contact-rich skill into a learned initialization pose plus closed-loop refinement is decisive; without the initialization stage the closed-loop policy fails completely.
> - Generalist vision-language-action models pretrained on gripper tasks transfer poorly to unusual end-effectors like ladles, even after fine-tuning on task demonstrations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

This work is situated within the field of robotic manipulation, specifically addressing the challenging task of scooping. Scooping is a fundamental skill in various applications, including assistive feeding and environmental cleanup. The paper extends previous research by tackling more complex scenarios involving mixtures of liquids and multiple solid items, often with distractor objects.

Traditional robotic manipulation research often simplifies scooping to single items or single types of granular media. This paper contributes by moving towards more realistic and complex environments where deformable materials (liquids) interact with multiple solid objects. The complexities addressed include the infinite-dimensional configuration spaces and dynamics of deformable objects, as well as perception challenges such as occlusions, reflections, and unstable depth sensing.

The methodology leverages a Sim2Real learning paradigm, which is a common approach in robotics to overcome the difficulties and costs associated with real-world data collection. By generating demonstrations in a high-fidelity simulator (OmniGibson), the authors aim for efficient, scalable, and safe data acquisition, a strategy also employed in other robotic tasks like fabric smoothing and scissor cutting.

The learning approach is based on imitation learning, a contrast to reinforcement learning, which can suffer from low sample efficiency and result in jerky motions for precise tasks like scooping floating objects. Specifically, the paper employs generative policies via diffusion models, building upon recent advancements like Diffusion Policy, which are designed to handle multimodal and high-dimensional action distributions.

Furthermore, the work integrates advanced vision-based foundation models (e.g., GroundingDINO, SAM2) for real-time object detection, segmentation, and tracking. This integration addresses the visual Sim2Real gap and aims to provide robust object state estimation in cluttered and dynamic environments, which is a persistent challenge in perception for Sim2Real transfer.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a general and autonomous robotic policy capable of scooping items from containers that contain a mixture of liquids and multiple solid objects. This objective aims to advance robotic manipulation skills beyond simplified scenarios.

The motivations for this work stem from several key challenges and limitations in current robotic capabilities:

- **Complexity of Tool-Object Interactions:** Scooping requires intricate interactions between the tool (ladle) and various objects, which can be difficult to model and control programmatically.
- **Manipulation of Deformable Materials:** Dealing with liquids and granular media is particularly challenging due to their continuously changing shapes and dynamic behaviors, which are difficult to represent and predict. The paper specifically targets mixtures of liquids and solids, introducing additional complexity in object movement and interaction.
- **Perceptual Ambiguity:** Real-world scooping environments often present unreliable observations. Occlusions from other objects or the container itself, coupled with reflections and refractions on liquid surfaces, can lead to noisy and unstable depth sensing, making accurate scene representation and object localization difficult.
- **Realistic and Challenging Scenarios:** Prior work often simplifies the scooping task to a single item or a homogeneous material. This research seeks to address more realistic settings, including multiple target items, the presence of distractor objects, and varying container types and sizes.
- **Inefficiency of Real-World Data Collection:** Collecting large-scale, diverse real-world data for complex robotic manipulation tasks like scooping is time-consuming, expensive, and can pose safety risks. This motivates the use of simulation for data generation.
- **Limitations of Reinforcement Learning (RL):** While RL can learn complex policies, it often requires extensive training data and can result in sub-optimal, jerky movements, which are undesirable for tasks requiring smooth and precise control, such as scooping floating objects. This motivates the adoption of imitation learning.
- **Need for Robust Generalization:** A significant motivation is to develop a policy that can generalize zero-shot to diverse real-world conditions, including different item quantities, characteristics, container types, occlusion levels, and external perturbations, without requiring additional fine-tuning.
- **Leveraging Modern AI Tools:** The paper is motivated by the potential of recent advancements in simulation environments (like OmniGibson), generative models (like Diffusion Policy), and vision-language foundation models (like GroundingDINO and SAM2) to overcome these challenges and facilitate the development of more capable robotic scooping policies.

### 3. Methodology and approach

The proposed method, SCOOP’D, integrates simulation-based data collection with a two-stage generative policy for Sim2Real robotic scooping.

**Problem Statement:** The task involves a robotic manipulator equipped with a ladle to retrieve specific target items from a container filled with a mixture of water and floating solid objects. The robot receives RGBD camera input and a text prompt to identify the target(s).

**Overall Architecture (SCOOP’D):** The system consists of three main learned components: a geometry-aware network for state estimation (`gψ`), a diffusion model for pre-scoop pose generation (`fφ`), and another diffusion model for fine-grained scooping motions (`πθ`).

1. **State Estimation (`gψ`):**
 - A text prompt (e.g., "meatball") is first processed by GroundingDINO to obtain a bounding box for the target object(s).
 - This bounding box serves as a visual prompt for SAM2, which performs real-time object segmentation and tracking.
 - The segmented depth region is converted into a partial point cloud.
 - This point cloud is then fed into `gψ`, a PointNet++-based network, which robustly estimates the target object's 3D center and its longest radius, providing a scale reference. `gψ` is trained on the YCB dataset.

2. **Two-Stage Diffusion Policies:**
 - **Pre-Scoop Pose Generation (`fφ`):** This is a 1D CNN-based Diffusion model. Its purpose is to predict an effective initial "pre-scoop" ladle pose. The inputs are the estimated target radius (`r_target`) and a manually specified horizontal distance parameter (`ρ`). `fφ` predicts `h` (vertical distance) and `v` (horizontal offset), which, combined with `ρ` and the object's position, define the ladle's pre-scoop pose. The ladle moves directly to this pose, and its motion is adjusted to track any recent movement of the target object during this transition. This model is trained on a small dataset of 150 demonstrations specifically for pre-scoop poses.
 - **Fine-grained Scooping Motion Policy (`πθ`):** This is also a 1D CNN-based Diffusion Policy model that executes the actual scooping trajectory. Its input (`o_t`) is a 10D vector comprising:
 - The 3D relative position of the target object with respect to the ladle (`p_relative = p_target - p_ladle`).
 - The 1D estimated radius of the target (`r_target`).
 - A 3D parameterized representation of the pre-scoop pose (`p_pre-scoop = (ρ, h, v)`).
 - The 3D movement of the target in the previous time step (`v_target`).
 The output (`a_t`) is a 10D vector that specifies:
 - The 3D direction of the ladle's main movement (`d_ladle`).
 - The 4D ladle orientation as a unit quaternion (`q_ladle`).
 - A 3D offset movement (`d_∆`).
 The aggregate ladle motion is calculated as `d_agg = s · d_ladle + d_∆ + v_target`, where `s` is a constant step length. The policy is conditioned on the pre-scoop pose parameters.

#### 3.1. SimScoop Dataset Collection

- **Simulation Environment:** All data is collected in OmniGibson, a simulator built on NVIDIA Omniverse, which supports liquid manipulation. The setup includes various containers, objects, and ladles, with adjustable water volumes.
- **Heuristic Scooping Strategy (Algorithmic Demonstrator):** An expert demonstrator is implemented using ground-truth object state information available in simulation. This strategy defines a curved trajectory for the ladle to move underneath the target, followed by an upward lift. The trajectory considers vertical (`v`) and horizontal (`ρ`) distances from the target to the ladle and adjusts based on the target's movement. It also includes collision avoidance with the container wall.
- **Data Collection:**
 - **Small Dataset (600 demonstrations):** Used for training `πθ`. Data collection is divided into pre-scoop pose generation and subsequent scooping motion. It focuses on a "PoolBall" target with other objects as obstacles. The pre-scoop phase collects valid starting points, and the policy data includes "offset movements" to simulate collision recovery.
 - **Large Dataset (6,480 demonstrations):** This dataset contains more complex scenes with varied containers, ladles, object types/sizes, and distractors. It's collected in a single stage (pre-scoop followed by scoop) and introduces significant motion uncertainty. Each demonstration includes RGB, depth, segmentation images from three views, along with object and ladle states and motion data.
- **Sim2Real Transfer:** To mitigate the simulation-to-reality physics gap, slight random noise is added to target and ladle motions during simulation data collection. For real-world deployment, only camera calibration is performed, and the learned policy is applied directly without fine-tuning.

#### 3.2. Application to Diverse Scooping Scenarios

- **Objects in Different Regions:** For circular containers, the policy adapts to targets in various regions by applying region-based rotations (0°, 90°, 180°, 270°) to the observational data, allowing the same policy to be used across the entire container.
- **Sequential Multi-Object Scooping:** The system scoops one item at a time. After each successful scoop, the ladle lifts and repositions for the next target, while attempting to retain previously scooped items.

### 4. Main findings and results

The research presents both simulation and extensive real-world experimental results to evaluate SCOOP’D’s performance, generalization, and robustness.

#### 4.1. Simulation Results

- Under "Normal" occlusion severity and with the small dataset (600 demonstrations), SCOOP’D achieved an average success rate of 91.7% across six different objects.
- With "Severe" occlusions, the success rate for the small dataset decreased to 73.3%.
- Training with the larger dataset (6,480 demonstrations) improved performance under "Severe" occlusion to 80.0%, indicating benefits from data scaling.
- Observed failure modes included objects falling through the ladle due to simulation inaccuracies and occasional misdetection by GroundingDINO.

#### 4.2. Real-World Results (Level 1 Objects)

- SCOOP’D demonstrated an average success rate of 82.5% across 240 real-world trials involving various Level 1 objects (e.g., PingPongBall, Mushroom, SmallCube, Leaf), different containers, and ladles.
- Success rates were consistently ≥75% for each object type.
- The policy exhibited insensitivity to ladle size (standard vs. small) and action horizon (1, 2, or 3 steps), performing similarly across these variations.
- Common real-world failures included robot kinematic limits, loss of object visibility, and unintended scooping of non-target objects.
- Qualitative results (Fig. 5) illustrated the policy's ability to generate valid pre-scoop poses and execute successful scoops in dynamic, cluttered conditions.

#### 4.3. Scooping Multiple Objects

- For sequential multi-object scooping, the average number of scooped targets varied by object type and count. For PingPongBalls, scooping two targets was more successful than three, as the ladle often struggled to retain all items. For objects like SmallCubes and Mushrooms, retaining multiple items was more feasible.
- The probability of scooping non-target obstacles increased with the number of target objects.

#### 4.4. Different Liquids

- In specific experimental settings, SCOOP’D successfully scooped items (e.g., small yellow cube) from PlantOil and MilkPowder with a 5/5 success rate, suggesting some degree of "liquid generalization."

#### 4.5. Different Occlusion Severity

- Performance for Level 1 objects (SmallCube, PingPongBall) decreased from 90.0% (Normal occlusion, small dataset) to 60.0% (Severe occlusion, small dataset).
- However, using the large dataset under "Severe" occlusion improved the average success rate to 72.5%, further supporting the benefits of data scaling.

#### 4.6. Baseline Comparisons (Real World)

- SCOOP’D significantly outperformed various baselines on Level 1 objects:
 - **LAVA:** A simplified re-implementation of LAVA's wall-guided policy achieved 7/20 (SmallCube) and 6/20 (PingPongBall), demonstrating that LAVA's fixed trajectories struggled with real-world disturbances.
 - **Heuristic Method:** The heuristic method (using estimated states), with sampled pre-scoop poses, achieved 5/20 and 16/20. With SCOOP'D's generated pre-scoop pose, it improved to 9/20 and 16/20. Both heuristic versions were rigid and prone to collisions.
 - **RGB-Based Diffusion Policy:** Trained on real-world RGB data, this policy performed poorly (4/20, 3/20), indicating visual features alone were insufficient for precise underwater spatial reasoning.
 - **Real-world State Diffusion Policy:** Trained on real-world state data, this policy also had limited success (6/20, 5/20), suggesting that direct policy learning in a larger action space without proper initialization is challenging with small real-world datasets.
 - **π0 (zero-shot/fine-tune):** A state-of-the-art vision-language-action model, performed poorly (0/20 zero-shot, 2/20 and 4/20 fine-tuned), attributed to its primary training on gripper tasks rather than ladle manipulation.

#### 4.7. Ablation Studies

- **Without pre-scoop pose generation (`fφ`):** This ablation resulted in major failures (0/20 for both objects), indicating the critical role of a well-defined initial pose.
- **With sampled pre-scoop poses (instead of generated):** A slight decrease in success (15/20, 18/20) was observed, with more collisions, highlighting the value of `fφ` for reliable initializations.
- **Without PointNet++ (`gψ`):** Performance was slightly worse (14/20, 17/20), suggesting `gψ` contributes to accurate object state estimation.
- **Without relative motion in `πθ` input:** The success rate was substantially lower (7/20, 14/20), demonstrating that relative motion simplifies the learning problem for the policy.

#### 4.8. Scooping Level 2 Objects

- SCOOP’D showed robustness on challenging Level 2 objects (e.g., LargeCookie, Cream), achieving lower but notable success rates (e.g., LargeCookie 11/20, Cream 7/20) despite their complex properties like uneven mass distribution, sinking tendencies, or spreading behavior.

#### 4.9. Generalization and Robustness Tests (Appendix)

- **Viewpoint Generalization:** The policy performed consistently across different real-world camera viewpoints without finetuning.
- **Robustness to Perturbations:** The method demonstrated strong robustness under various challenging real-world disturbances, including human perturbation, human pushes on distractor objects, highly cluttered scenes, and dynamic lighting conditions (colored lights, reflections).
- **Movable Objects:** Qualitative results showed SCOOP’D's ability to scoop self-moving toy fish.

### 5. Significance and potential impact

The research presented in "SCOOP’D" contributes to the field of robotic manipulation by addressing complex, realistic scooping scenarios and demonstrating a viable Sim2Real learning strategy.

**Advancement in Robotic Scooping:** This work significantly advances robotic scooping capabilities by moving beyond simplified tasks to address the manipulation of liquid-solid mixtures, multiple target objects, and cluttered environments with distractors. This is a critical step towards developing more versatile and autonomous robotic systems for real-world applications.

**Effective Sim2Real Strategy:** The method validates the effectiveness of using algorithmic demonstrators in simulation to generate high-quality, diverse datasets for imitation learning. The successful zero-shot transfer of policies learned entirely in simulation to varied real-world conditions without fine-tuning represents a substantial achievement in bridging the Sim2Real gap, offering a scalable and cost-effective approach to acquiring complex robotic skills.

**Robustness and Generalization:** SCOOP’D exhibits notable generalization capabilities across a spectrum of variables, including different object characteristics (Level 1 and 2 difficulty), varying item quantities, diverse container types and sizes, different liquids, and different levels of occlusion severity. The demonstrated robustness to external perturbations, dynamic scenes, and varying lighting conditions highlights the policy's stability and adaptability in uncontrolled environments.

**Methodological Contributions:** The paper introduces the SimScoop dataset, a multimodal dataset of 6,480 scooping demonstrations generated in OmniGibson. Furthermore, the SCOOP’D methodology, comprising a two-stage diffusion policy (pre-scoop pose generation and fine-grained motion control) combined with perception leveraging foundation models (GroundingDINO, SAM2) and a geometry-aware network, offers a novel and effective approach to robotic manipulation. The ablation studies confirm the importance of each component, particularly the pre-scoop pose and relative motion representation, for robust performance.

**Practical Applicability:** The demonstrated capability for scooping mixed-liquid-solid items has potential practical applications in areas such as:
- **Assistive Robotics:** Enabling robots to perform tasks like assistive feeding more reliably and adaptably.
- **Environmental Cleanup:** Assisting in tasks like retrieving debris from water bodies or disaster sites.
- **Automated Kitchens/Food Handling:** Facilitating the manipulation of ingredients in fluid environments.

**Inspiration for Future Work:** This research opens avenues for future investigations, including:
- Improving performance for highly complex tasks like scooping submerged or highly deformable objects.
- Further optimizing the integration and fine-tuning of perception models (GroundingDINO, SAM2) for better task-specific performance.
- Exploring the transferability of these principles to other manipulation tasks involving fluid-solid interaction or dynamic object handling.
- Investigating hardware improvements, such as multi-camera systems or eye-on-hand cameras, to mitigate issues like losing sight of objects.
