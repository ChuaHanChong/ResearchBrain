# DOME: Taming Diffusion Model into High-Fidelity Controllable Occupancy World Model

> [!summary] Summary
> DOME predicts future 3D occupancy frames for autonomous driving using a continuous-latent VAE (Occ-VAE) plus a spatial-temporal diffusion transformer conditioned on historical occupancy and trajectory. A trajectory resampling augmentation improves controllability. Achieves state-of-the-art nuScenes results, surpassing baselines by 36.0% mIoU and 24.6% IoU on 4D forecasting.

## Problem

- Autoregressive occupancy world models rely on discrete tokenizers, causing quantization information loss that limits high-fidelity occupancy prediction.
- Autoregressive GPT-based occupancy models are hard to train and struggle to generate realistic long-duration future occupancy sequences.
- Prior diffusion-based occupancy models flatten spatial-temporal data into 1D token sequences, hindering efficient historical conditioning and fine-grained controllability.

## Method

- **Occ-VAE**: ==continuous-latent variational autoencoder== compressing 3D occupancy via ==BEV-style class embeddings==, 2D conv/attention encoder, 3D deconvolution decoder, trained with ==cross-entropy== plus ==Lovasz-softmax== and ==KL losses==.
- **DOME** spatial-temporal diffusion transformer: ==staggered spatial and temporal blocks== with conditioning mask injecting historical occupancy latents plus trajectory pose embeddings via ==adaLN== for controlled 4D forecasting.
- **Trajectory resampling**: aggregates point clouds into world frame, builds drivable-area BEV map, samples diverse smoothed ==A*== trajectories to extract augmented occupancy sequences addressing dataset motion imbalance.

## Results

- Occ-VAE achieves state-of-the-art occupancy reconstruction with **83.1%** mIoU and **77.3%** IoU at **64x** compression, beating OccWorld and OccLLaMA baselines.
- DOME-O reaches **27.10%** mIoU and **36.36%** IoU on 4D occupancy forecasting, a **36.0%** mIoU and **24.6%** IoU improvement over prior state-of-the-art.
- Ablations show trajectory conditioning raises mIoU from **18.60%** to **24.24%**, and full model generates **64** frames (**32s**), **ten times** OccWorld and **twice** OccSora duration.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Continuous latent compression preserves fine-grained occupancy detail better than discrete tokenization, a principle transferable to other 3D generative world models.
> - Data imbalance in ego-motion trajectories can silently cause world models to overfit to dominant straight-driving patterns rather than learning true controllability.
> - Simple geometric augmentation, resampling trajectories from existing point clouds, can substitute for costly additional data collection to improve conditional generation diversity.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Autonomous driving systems are increasingly reliant on sophisticated learning techniques and data processing. A significant challenge in this field involves addressing long-tail distributions and infrequent corner cases, which are difficult to capture through extensive data collection or traditional state-of-the-art methods. World models represent a promising approach to overcome these limitations by predicting the future evolution of environmental observations based on historical context and agent actions. This predictive capability allows autonomous vehicles to anticipate future states, which can enhance the evaluation of action viability and improve planning.

World models are broadly categorized into 2D video-based models and 3D representation-based models. While 2D video-based models have demonstrated success in generating realistic camera observations, they often encounter difficulties in maintaining consistency across different viewpoints and over time, which restricts their applicability in real-world driving scenarios.

In contrast, 3D representation-based models, such as those utilizing LiDAR or occupancy frameworks, offer intrinsic 3D consistency by operating directly on raw 3D data. Occupancy-based world models, specifically, process historical sequences of occupancy data to predict future occupancy observations. These models benefit from relatively accessible annotations, which can be derived from sparse LiDAR data or through self-supervision from temporal frames. Furthermore, occupancy-based models are modality-agnostic, capable of being generated from various sensor inputs, including monocular or surround cameras, and LiDAR.

Existing occupancy world models typically fall into two categories: autoregressive-based and diffusion-based. Autoregressive methods predict future occupancy using discrete tokens in a sequential manner. However, the quantization process inherent in discrete tokenizers can lead to information loss, thereby limiting the fidelity of predictions. Additionally, these methods often struggle with generating realistic long-duration occupancy sequences due to the challenges associated with training GPT-based models for extended temporal predictions.

Diffusion-based approaches, while emerging, have also presented limitations. Some prior diffusion models flatten spatial and temporal information into a single one-dimensional sequence, which can hinder their efficiency in capturing complex spatio-temporal relationships. This flattening can make it difficult to effectively integrate historical occupancy information for predictive tasks, potentially restricting their ability to generate controlled future observations. Moreover, the exploration of fine-grained control within most current occupancy world models has been insufficient, which can result in models overfitting to specific scene types and reducing their generalizability for diverse downstream tasks.

DOME addresses these identified limitations by proposing a diffusion-based world model designed for high-fidelity, long-duration, and controllable occupancy prediction, aiming to advance the capabilities of 3D world models for autonomous driving.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel diffusion-based world model, named DOME, that can accurately predict future occupancy frames based on historical observations, while overcoming specific challenges observed in previous methods. The motivation behind DOME stems from the limitations of existing occupancy world models for autonomous driving.

The key objectives and motivations are detailed as follows:

- **Achieve High-Fidelity Occupancy Prediction**: Existing autoregressive occupancy world models often rely on discrete tokenization for data compression. This process, involving quantization, can result in irreversible information loss, leading to predictions that lack fine details and fidelity. DOME aims to address this by adopting a continuous latent space representation for occupancy data, thereby preserving intricate spatial information during compression and enabling the generation of high-fidelity occupancy predictions.

- **Enable Long-Duration Occupancy Generation**: Traditional autoregressive methods face difficulties in generating realistic occupancy sequences over extended durations, partly due to the computational complexity and training challenges associated with models like GPT for long temporal sequences. DOME seeks to achieve long-duration predictions by utilizing a spatial-temporal diffusion transformer architecture, which is designed to efficiently capture spatio-temporal information and facilitate predictions over significantly longer time horizons (e.g., up to 32 seconds).

- **Enhance Controllability in Occupancy Predictions**: Many existing occupancy world models demonstrate insufficient exploration of fine-grained control, particularly regarding the influence of the ego vehicle's actions (trajectories) on future scene evolution. This limitation can lead to models overfitting to common driving patterns and scenes, restricting their adaptability to diverse or novel scenarios. DOME's objective is to introduce a robust mechanism for fine-grained controllability, allowing predictions to be precisely guided by trajectory conditions. This is motivated by the need for world models to generate varied and accurate occupancy predictions that reflect different potential actions of the autonomous agent, which is crucial for robust planning.

- **Improve Efficiency in Spatio-Temporal Information Capture**: Some prior diffusion-based approaches for occupancy prediction flatten spatial and temporal information into a one-dimensional sequence, which can lead to inefficient processing of complex spatio-temporal dependencies. This inefficiency can hinder the effective integration of historical occupancy information for making accurate future predictions. DOME aims to improve this by employing a spatial-temporal diffusion transformer that explicitly separates and processes spatial and temporal information through dedicated blocks, thereby enhancing the model's ability to efficiently learn from and integrate contextual data.

- **Address Dataset Imbalance and Lack of Trajectory Diversity**: Autonomous driving datasets, such as nuScenes, often exhibit significant imbalances in driving behavior (e.g., a majority of straight-line driving) and a lack of diverse trajectories for the same scene. This limited diversity can cause world models to overfit to observed ground truth behaviors, diminishing their capacity to generalize to a wider range of ego vehicle actions. DOME is motivated to address this through a novel trajectory resampling method, which augments the training data by generating multiple diverse and feasible occupancy sequences for each scene, thereby improving the model's generalization and controllability.

In summary, the core motivation for DOME is to develop a more capable and versatile world model for autonomous driving that produces detailed, temporally extended, and precisely controllable predictions of future 3D occupancy, moving beyond the limitations of previous tokenization, architectural, and data handling approaches.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DOME is a diffusion-based occupancy world model designed to predict future occupancy frames from historical observations. Its methodology integrates a continuous latent space for data compression, a spatial-temporal architecture for efficient prediction, and a novel trajectory resampling technique for enhanced controllability. The system consists of two main components: Occ-VAE and a spatial-temporal diffusion transformer.

#### 3.1. Occ-VAE (Occupancy Variational Autoencoder)

The Occ-VAE is a core component that serves to compress dense 3D occupancy data into a continuous latent space, addressing the information loss associated with discrete tokenization methods.

- **Occupancy Data Representation**: The input to Occ-VAE is 3D occupancy data, denoted as \(x \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times D}\), which voxelizes the ego vehicle's environment into a grid. Each voxel is assigned one of 17 semantic class labels. To process this discrete data, it is first transformed into a Bird’s Eye View (BEV) style tensor \(x_{bev} \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times D_{C_{emb}}}\) by indexing a learnable class embedding \(E_{cls} \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times C_{emb}}\). This flattens the occupancy data into a consistent feature dimension.

- **Encoder**: An encoder network, \(q_\phi(z | x)\), processes \(x_{bev}\) into a compressed representation. This representation is then split along the channel dimension into mean (\(\mu \in \mathbb{R}^{n_h \times n_w \times C}\)) and standard deviation (\(\sigma \in \mathbb{R}^{n_h \times n_w \times C}\)) vectors. A continuous latent variable \(z\) is sampled using the reparameterization trick: \(z = \mu + \sigma \odot \epsilon\), where \(\epsilon \sim N(0, I)\). The encoder incorporates 2D convolutional layers and attention blocks. The class embedding \(E_{cls}\) is initialized randomly and co-trained with Occ-VAE.

- **Decoder**: The decoder network, \(p_\theta(x | z)\), reconstructs the input occupancy from the sampled latent variable \(z\). It employs 3D deconvolution layers to upsample the latent representation. The upsampled features \(F\) are reshaped, and a logits score \(s\) is computed via a dot product with the class embedding. The final class prediction is determined by the argmax of these logits.

- **Training Loss**: Occ-VAE is trained using a loss function comprising a reconstruction loss (cross-entropy loss), a KL divergence loss (\(D_{KL}[q_\phi(z | x) \| p(z)]\)) to regularize the latent space, and an additional Lovasz-softmax loss to mitigate class imbalance issues in predictions. After training, the Occ-VAE is frozen, with its encoder used for latent feature extraction and its decoder for occupancy reconstruction.

#### 3.2. DOME: A Diffusion-Based Occupancy World Model

DOME utilizes a latent diffusion model with temporal-aware layers to predict future occupancy observations.

- **Input and Masking**: The model takes an encoded latent \(z \in \mathbb{R}^{n_f \times n_h \times n_w \times C}\) as input, where \(n_f\) is the number of frames. This latent is partially masked, allowing only \(n_c\) (context) frames to be visible. The model is trained to predict the remaining masked frames.

- **Spatial-Temporal Diffusion Transformer**: The latent representation \(z\) is partitioned into \(n_f\) frames of sequence tokens. Positional embeddings are added in both spatial and temporal dimensions. The model architecture consists of alternating spatial and temporal blocks. Spatial blocks capture information across frames at the same temporal index, while temporal blocks extract information along the temporal axis at a fixed spatial index. This staggered arrangement efficiently captures both types of dependencies.

- **Historic Occupancy Condition**: To enable future prediction, the model is conditioned on historical occupancy data. A conditioning mask \(M\) is applied, ensuring that the model's predictions are based on the available \(n_c\) context frames. During training, noised tokens are partially replaced by context latents according to this mask. A dropout mechanism (with probability \(p_{inj}\)) is used to train the model without context frames, enhancing its ability to generate unconditionally.

- **Loss Function**: The model is optimized using an extended spatial-temporal diffusion loss, which is a mean squared error (MSE) between the predicted noise and the ground truth noise. The loss for the \(n_c\) context frames is ignored due to the conditioning mask.

#### 3.3. Trajectory as Conditioning

DOME integrates trajectory information to provide enhanced motion priors and fine-grained control over predictions.

- **Trajectory Condition Injection**: The ego vehicle's pose (relative translation \(\Delta t_t\) and relative rotation \(\Delta R_t\)) is used. The [x, y] coordinates from \(\Delta t_t\) and the yaw angle \(\theta_{yaw}\) from \(\Delta R_t\) are extracted, positionally encoded, projected to the hidden size via a linear layer, and combined with time embeddings. These combined values are then passed to an adaptive layer normalization (adaLN) block to condition the diffusion model.

- **Trajectory Resampling**: This novel method addresses the issue of dataset imbalance and limited diversity in ego-vehicle actions. The procedure involves:
 1. **Multi-frame Point Cloud Aggregation**: Occupancy sequences are converted to 3D point clouds in the ego frame, then transformed to the world frame using ego poses. Dynamic objects are filtered out based on semantic labels.
 2. **Obtaining Drivable Area**: Filtered point clouds (road classes) are voxelized into a BEV map, representing the drivable area.
 3. **Generating Diverse and Feasible Trajectories**: Random start and goal points are sampled on the BEV map. A smoothed A* algorithm generates diverse, feasible trajectories connecting these points, simulating ego vehicle movements.
 4. **Extracting Resampled Occupancy**: Using the generated trajectory poses, occupancy data is resampled from the point clouds, creating diverse occupancy ground truths corresponding to varied trajectories.
 This augmentation fills gaps in trajectory distributions and enhances diversity.

#### 3.4. Applications of World Models

- **4D Occupancy Forecasting**: During inference, random noise is initialized for the prediction buffer. \(n_c\) contextual occupancy frames are encoded via Occ-VAE to obtain latents, which replace the corresponding frames in the random noise. The input is then passed through the spatial-temporal DiT for denoising. The denoised latent is subsequently passed to the Occ-VAE's decoder to generate the final occupancy prediction. The number of context frames (\(n_c\)) can be adjusted based on the requirement for precision versus controllability.

- **Rollout for Long Duration Generation**: To generate predictions beyond the \(n_f\) frames processed by the model, a rollout strategy is employed. The last predicted frame from a sequence is reused as the contextual frame for predicting the next \(n_f\) frames. This iterative process allows for long-term occupancy predictions (e.g., 32 seconds).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of DOME was evaluated through extensive experiments on the nuScenes dataset, utilizing occupancy annotations from Occ3D. The evaluation metrics included Intersection over Union (IoU) and mean IoU (mIoU) for both Occupancy Reconstruction and 4D Occupancy Prediction tasks.

#### 4.1. Occupancy Reconstruction

The Occ-VAE component of DOME was benchmarked against existing methods that use occupancy tokenizers for reconstruction accuracy.

- **Performance**: DOME achieved state-of-the-art reconstruction performance with an mIoU of 83.1% and an IoU of 77.3%.
- **Compression Rate**: The model demonstrated a relatively high compression rate of 64 times, which is four times smaller than methods like OccWorld and OccLLaMA while maintaining superior reconstruction quality. This indicates efficient data compression without substantial loss of detail.
- **Spatial Downsampling**: DOME utilized a 16x spatial compression rate, balancing compression efficiency with the need for effective contextual conditioning, unlike some methods that apply additional temporal compression.

#### 4.2. 4D Occupancy Prediction

DOME's 4D occupancy forecasting capabilities were compared with existing approaches under various input settings: using ground-truth 3D occupancy data (-O) and using predictions from off-the-shelf 3D occupancy predictors (-F), with FB-OCC as the occupancy extractor for camera input.

- **DOME-O (Ground-truth Occupancy Input)**: DOME-O achieved state-of-the-art performance, with an average mIoU of 27.10% and an average IoU of 36.36% across 1s, 2s, and 3s prediction horizons. This represents significant improvements over existing state-of-the-art methods, demonstrating the model's ability to capture the fundamental evolution of a scene over time.
- **DOME-F (Camera-based Occupancy Input)**: As an end-to-end vision-based 4D occupancy forecasting method, DOME-F (using surrounding camera captures as input) achieved competitive performance, with an average mIoU of 18.25% and an average IoU of 28.84%. This indicates the model's generalizability even when operating on predicted occupancy data from external perception modules.
- **Qualitative Results**: Visualizations demonstrated the model's ability for long-duration generation (up to 32 seconds) and its capacity to be manipulated by trajectory conditions given the same starting frame. Compared to OccWorld and OccSora, DOME showed the ability to generate predictions for the longest duration (32 seconds), ten times longer than OccWorld and twice that of OccSora.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

- **Trajectory Condition**:
 - The inclusion of trajectory information significantly improved prediction performance, providing the model with a clear direction for scenario changes.
 - The proposed trajectory resampling method (Res.) further enhanced the model's performance, leading to an mIoU of 24.24% and IoU of 34.28% compared to 18.60% mIoU and 28.09% IoU without resampling but with trajectory control.
 - Adding yaw angle embedding provided a slight additional improvement in IoU, contributing to a final mIoU of 27.10% and IoU of 36.36% when all trajectory controls are utilized.
 - Even without any pose condition, the base DOME model outperformed OccWorld.

- **Number of Contextual Frames**:
 - Providing more contextual frames generally led to better predictions, as additional frames supplied more explicit information about motion and changes within the scene and other vehicles.
 - However, increasing contextual frames alone was found to be less efficient than incorporating trajectory information, as historical frames can contain ambiguities regarding future ego-agent movements that trajectory signals clarify. For instance, with trajectory control, using 1 contextual frame yielded 22.24% mIoU, which is higher than using 4 contextual frames without trajectory control (20.07% mIoU).

These findings collectively indicate that DOME not only achieves high-fidelity and long-duration occupancy forecasting but also effectively integrates fine-grained control through its trajectory conditioning and resampling mechanism, establishing new performance benchmarks on the nuScenes dataset.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The DOME model presents several advancements in the field of autonomous driving world models, with significant implications for future research and practical applications.

- **High-Fidelity and Long-Duration Prediction**: By utilizing a continuous latent space through Occ-VAE and a spatial-temporal diffusion transformer, DOME effectively mitigates the information loss inherent in discrete tokenization, leading to more detailed and accurate occupancy predictions. The capability to generate predictions over long durations (up to 32 seconds) represents a substantial improvement over previous methods, offering autonomous systems a more extended look into future environmental states. This extended foresight is crucial for complex decision-making, especially in dynamic and unpredictable driving scenarios.

- **Enhanced Controllability for Planning**: The introduction of trajectory conditioning, coupled with a novel trajectory resampling method, provides DOME with fine-grained control over its predictions. This allows the model to generate diverse and feasible future occupancy sequences based on varying ego-vehicle actions. This capability is particularly impactful for planning modules in autonomous driving, as it enables the simulation and evaluation of multiple potential future outcomes corresponding to different actions. By reducing overfitting to specific scenes and increasing the diversity of predicted scenarios, DOME contributes to more robust and adaptable planning systems.

- **Novel Data Augmentation Strategy**: The proposed trajectory resampling method is a significant contribution, as it is presented as the first occupancy data augmentation technique specifically for world model prediction. This method addresses the common issue of dataset imbalance and limited trajectory diversity by programmatically generating new, feasible driving scenarios. Its generalizability, requiring only pose and occupancy data without relying on LiDAR or 3D bounding boxes, suggests it could be widely applied across different types of occupancy datasets (machine-annotated, LiDAR-collected, or self-supervised). This approach has the potential to improve the training efficiency and generalization of other world models in autonomous driving.

- **Advancement in 3D World Modeling**: DOME's architecture efficiently captures spatio-temporal dependencies, pushing the boundaries of 3D world modeling beyond 2D video-based approaches that struggle with cross-view consistency. Its modality-agnostic nature further broadens its potential, as it can be integrated with various perception sensor setups.

- **Potential for End-to-End Planning**: The ability of DOME to forecast future scene changes in occupancy space, combined with its controllability, makes it a promising foundation for enhancing end-to-end planning systems in autonomous driving. By providing a rich, predictive understanding of the environment and the consequences of actions, DOME can facilitate more intelligent and safer navigation.

A recognized limitation is the significant computational resources required for training the model. Future work will likely focus on developing more lightweight and computationally efficient methods, potentially through fine-tuning paradigms, to make such advanced world models more accessible for wider deployment and research. Despite this, DOME's contributions in high-fidelity, long-duration, and controllable occupancy prediction mark a notable step forward in enabling more capable and intelligent autonomous driving systems.
