# SocialGen: Modeling Multi-Human Social Interaction with Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> SocialGen is the first unified motion-language model generating and understanding interactions among an arbitrary number of people. It tokenizes multi-human motion via a new XH3D representation and aligns it with text in a shared LLM vocabulary, achieving state-of-the-art motion-language performance on the new SocialX benchmark.

## Problem

- Prior motion generation methods handle only single-person or fixed two-person interactions, relying on hard-coded models that cannot generalize to arbitrary group sizes.
- Existing multi-human motion representations like IH and IX suffer jittering and reconstruction artifacts under discrete tokenization due to high-dimensional global position encoding.
- Multi-human motion datasets such as UMPM, 3DPW, and CMU-Panoptic lack paired text descriptions, blocking language-conditioned understanding and generation research.

## Method

- ==XH3D== representation extends single-person H3D with a relative first-frame pose tuple per individual, enabling scalable low-dimensional tokenization for any number of humans.
- A ==VQVAE== motion tokenizer with ==EMA-updated codebook== discretizes each individual's motion independently, trained with ==reconstruction, embedding, and commitment losses== plus ==L1 smoothness regularization==.
- A ==T5-based== motion-aware language model unifies text, motion, and relative-pose tokens in one vocabulary, trained via ==unsupervised span-masking pre-training== then ==instruction tuning== across 25 motion tasks.

## Results

- XH3D cuts joint tokenization error versus IH/IX baselines, e.g. InterX ACCEL drops from **31.85** (IX) to **5.71** and MPJPE from **90.19** to **56.36**.
- On SocialX, the instruction-tuned model reaches FID **1.403** versus **10.163** for T2M-GPT and **2.349** for TM2T, far closer to ground truth's **0.0002**.
- Removing the participant-order shuffling augmentation raises FID from **1.403** to **1.794** and lowers Diversity from **14.213** to **13.817**, confirming its benefit.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Keeping per-person pose local (root-relative) while encoding only a compact inter-person offset tuple avoids the tokenization artifacts that plague global-coordinate multi-person representations.
> - Standard text-motion alignment metrics like R-Precision and MM-Dist, designed for one or two people, poorly capture quality in variable-group-size generation and need rethinking.
> - Order-invariance augmentation (randomizing which participant is the reference and their sequence) meaningfully improves generalization in group-interaction modeling, not just single-pair tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is positioned at the forefront of multimodal AI research, specifically addressing the challenging domain of human motion generation and understanding in social contexts. The broader landscape can be broken down into several converging fields:

- **Human Motion Generation and Understanding:** Historically, research in this area has made significant strides, particularly for single-person motion. Early works focused on realistic and diverse motion generation from various inputs like text, actions, music, or incomplete motion. More recently, diffusion-based models and vector-quantized approaches have shown promise. However, as the authors highlight, these methods primarily focus on isolated human motion or limited scenarios, struggling to capture broader motion patterns, especially in complex social interactions.
- **Human-Human Interaction Modeling:** This subfield has largely concentrated on dyadic (two-person) interactions, with tasks such as reaction synthesis (one person reacting to another) or simultaneous interaction generation. While some studies have attempted to extend to multi-person settings, they typically lack language-related capabilities and struggle to generalize to arbitrary group sizes or open-domain textual prompts. The challenge lies in modeling the intricate inter-individual and spatio-temporal dependencies inherent in group interactions.
- **Multimodal Language Models (MLLMs):** The advent of large language models (LLMs) like T5 and LLaMA has revolutionized natural language processing. Researchers have begun extending these powerful models to integrate diverse modalities such as images, videos, and audio, giving rise to MLLMs. For 3D motion specifically, models like MotionGPT have successfully framed single-human motions as a language modeling task. SocialGen builds directly upon this paradigm, aiming to extend MLLM capabilities to the far more complex realm of *multi-human social interactions*.
- **Human Motion Datasets:** Progress in motion synthesis tasks is heavily reliant on comprehensive datasets. While numerous datasets exist for single-human and two-person interactions (e.g., HumanML3D, InterGen, InterX), multi-human datasets (e.g., 4DAssociation, UMPM, 3DPW) have generally lacked high-quality, detailed textual descriptions. This absence of language annotations has been a significant bottleneck for developing multimodal models capable of understanding and generating multi-human interactions based on natural language.

SocialGen addresses a critical gap: the lack of a unified motion-language model that can robustly handle interactions among *varying numbers of individuals* while simultaneously supporting diverse language-related tasks. Previous efforts were largely confined to single or two-person interactions, often with hard-coded models and limited linguistic grounding. By introducing a novel motion representation (XH3D) and a comprehensively curated dataset (SocialX) with rich textual annotations, SocialGen pushes the boundaries of MLLMs to encompass complex social dynamics, moving beyond isolated or dyadic scenarios to a more realistic and scalable multi-human setting. This work is significant for its generalization capabilities across participant numbers and its integration of language, which promises more intuitive control and understanding of social AI systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind SocialGen stems from the increasing demand for advanced human behavior modeling in various transformative applications, including virtual reality (VR), gaming, healthcare (e.g., telemedicine, therapy), and robotics (e.g., human-robot collaboration). Current methods, largely confined to single or two-person interactions, fall short in modeling the dynamic and complex dependencies found in real-world social scenarios involving multiple individuals. This limitation restricts the realism, immersion, and social awareness of AI systems in these crucial domains.

Against this backdrop, the key objectives of the SocialGen project are:

- **Develop the first unified motion-language model for multi-human social interactions:** The primary goal is to create a single framework, SocialGen, capable of modeling interaction behaviors for *arbitrary and varying numbers of individuals*, a significant advancement over prior two-person limitations.
- **Propose a novel social motion representation (XH3D):** To achieve scalability and high-quality reconstruction, the project aims to introduce an extended motion representation that can effectively tokenize the motions of multiple individuals and encode their relative spatial relationships, moving beyond the limitations of existing single or two-human formats.
- **Align motion and language spaces:** A fundamental objective is to align the newly proposed motion representation with the language space, enabling the model to leverage vast pre-trained linguistic knowledge. This alignment facilitates a deeper understanding and reasoning about human social behaviors.
- **Curate a comprehensive multi-human interaction dataset (SocialX):** Recognizing the critical data scarcity for multi-human interactions with language annotations, a key objective is to build SocialX. This dataset is enriched with high-quality textual descriptions to support multimodal training and evaluation.
- **Establish a comprehensive benchmark:** Using SocialX, the project aims to establish the first comprehensive benchmark for evaluating models on a diverse range of multi-human interaction tasks, setting a new standard for the field.
- **Achieve state-of-the-art performance:** The ultimate objective is for SocialGen to demonstrate superior performance across various motion-language tasks, including generation, captioning, completion, and reaction generation, thus proving the effectiveness of the proposed framework, representation, and dataset.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SocialGen is designed as a unified motion-language framework that models interactions across varying group sizes by tightly aligning motion and language spaces. Its methodology is structured around two main components and a three-stage training paradigm.

#### 3.1. Motion Representation: XH3D

The paper introduces **XH3D**, a novel motion representation designed to overcome limitations of existing formats (H3D for single-human, IH, and IX for multi-human, which suffer from reconstruction artifacts due to high dimensionality). XH3D extends the H3D format by encoding each individual's motion using the same canonical representation (root angular/linear velocities, local joint positions, velocities, rotations, and foot-ground contact features). Crucially, to model multi-human interactions, XH3D introduces a tuple (s_x, s_z, s_θ) representing the *first-frame relative pose* between a randomly chosen reference human and each other individual. This design makes XH3D scalable to any number of individuals and robust in capturing inter-human spatial relationships while achieving superior tokenization performance.

#### 3.2. Motion Tokenizer

To transform continuous motion data into discrete tokens compatible with language models, SocialGen employs a **3D human motion tokenizer** based on the Vector Quantized Variational Autoencoder (VQVAE) framework.
- It consists of an encoder (E), a decoder (D), and a learnable codebook (Z).
- The encoder maps motion features into latent space, which are then quantized to discrete tokens by finding the nearest entry in the codebook.
- The decoder reconstructs the motion from these quantized tokens.
- Each individual's motion is independently tokenized using VQVAE.
- For the relative root pose (s_x, s_z, s_θ), a simple uniform binning strategy is used to discretize each component into tokens, chosen for its effectiveness and framework focus.
- Training optimizes a combination of reconstruction loss (L1), embedding loss, and commitment loss, augmented with L1 smooth loss and velocity regularization for motion quality.

#### 3.3. Motion-Language Model

The core of SocialGen is a **motion-aware language model** built upon a pre-trained Large Language Model (LLM), specifically T5.
- **Unified Vocabulary:** The discrete motion tokens (from VQVAE) and relative root pose tokens (from uniform binning) are integrated into the LLM's original text token vocabulary, creating a single, unified vocabulary.
- **Special Tokens:** Specific tokens are introduced, such as `⟨Motion_S⟩` and `⟨Motion_E⟩` for motion sequence boundaries, and triplets `⟨x_i⟩⟨z_i⟩⟨θ_i⟩` that uniquely identify each human and encode their relative pose. This structure enables scalable encoding of multi-human scenarios within a sequence.
- **Autoregressive Generation:** The model learns to represent text-motion relationships as an autoregressive sequence generation task, predicting each token conditioned on preceding ones. This allows for flexible generation of text or motion sequences of variable lengths and for varying numbers of humans.

#### 3.4. Training Paradigm

The training process unfolds in three stages:
1. **Motion Tokenizer Training:** The VQVAE-based motion tokenizer is trained using the L_VQ objective to create the motion codebook. Once trained, it is fixed.
2. **Motion-Language Alignment Pre-training:** The T5 model is further pre-trained on the SocialX dataset. This stage includes:
 - **Unsupervised Learning:** Masked token prediction on combined text and motion sequences to enhance generalization across tasks.
 - **Supervised Learning:** Training on paired text-motion data for tasks like motion-language alignment (generating motion from text or vice-versa), motion forecasting (predicting future motion), and reaction synthesis (generating missing motion for an individual).
3. **Instruction Tuning:** The pre-trained model is fine-tuned with a diverse set of prompt-based instructions. These instructions, created using GPT-4 for 25 core motion-related tasks (e.g., motion completion, captioning, human number estimation), enhance the model's instruction-following capabilities and generalization to diverse real-world scenarios.

#### 3.5. SocialX Dataset

To address data scarcity, SocialGen introduces **SocialX**, a comprehensive multi-human interaction dataset.
- **Sources:** It integrates and extends existing datasets (HumanML3D, InterHuman, InterX, HOI-M3, MuPoTS-3D, CMU Panoptic).
- **Synthesis:** To enrich scenarios, multi-human interactions are synthesized by combining motions from single-person and two-person datasets, carefully avoiding physical conflicts.
- **Caption Generation:** For datasets lacking text, GPT-4o-mini is used in a two-stage process: first generating overall scene descriptions, then refining them to focus purely on human motion and interactions.
- **Motion Postprocessing:** All motion data is standardized to SMPL parameters, normalized, and relative positions between individuals are tokenized, ensuring consistency and compatibility across diverse sources.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

SocialGen demonstrates significant advancements in modeling multi-human social interactions, evidenced through quantitative evaluations and qualitative results across various motion-language tasks.

#### 4.1. Tokenization Evaluation

The effectiveness of the proposed XH3D motion representation is a foundational finding. Compared to existing IH and IX formats on the InterHuman and InterX datasets, XH3D consistently achieves superior performance in motion reconstruction quality. It significantly reduces the Mean Per Joint Position Error (MPJPE) and Procrustes Analysis MPJPE (PA-MPJPE), indicating better spatial accuracy. Crucially, XH3D drastically outperforms other representations in the Mean Per Joint Acceleration Error (Accel), signifying a much smoother and temporally coherent reconstructed motion, which is vital for natural-looking human movements.

#### 4.2. Quantitative Comparison on SocialX

A comprehensive quantitative comparison on the newly curated SocialX test set (Table 1) showcases SocialGen's superiority over state-of-the-art baseline methods (MDM, T2M, TM2T, T2M-GPT) and its own pre-training stage.
- **Motion Quality and Diversity:** SocialGen, especially after instruction tuning, achieves the best Frechet Inception Distance (FID), indicating higher motion quality and realism. It also demonstrates superior Diversity (DIV) and Multimodality (MM), signifying its ability to generate varied and distinct motions from the same prompt, closely matching the ground truth.
- **Text-Motion Alignment:** While improvements in R-Precision and Multi-Modal Distance (MM Dist) are noted to be less pronounced, the authors attribute this to limitations in existing evaluation methodologies designed for simpler 1-2 person scenarios, rather than arbitrary group sizes. Despite this, the model's overall performance suggests strong alignment capabilities.

#### 4.3. Qualitative Results

Visual and descriptive results strongly support the quantitative findings:
- **Text-to-Motion Generation (Figure 3, 10):** SocialGen produces motions that are notably smoother, more accurate, and more precisely aligned with textual descriptions than prior approaches. It effectively captures nuanced behaviors and complex spatial relationships among multiple individuals, avoiding common issues like physical penetration seen in other methods.
- **Motion Captioning (Figure 4):** The model demonstrates a strong ability to generate meaningful and accurate textual descriptions from multi-human motion sequences, effectively capturing key actions and interactions in social scenarios.
- **Motion Completion and Forecasting (Figures 5, 6):** SocialGen successfully fills in missing motion segments or predicts future movements for varying numbers of individuals, highlighting its robust understanding of temporal dynamics and social context.
- **Reaction Generation (Figure 7):** Unlike prior methods often limited to two-person reactions, SocialGen can generate believable reactive motions for any number of individuals based on existing motions, demonstrating its advanced understanding of social causality.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Motion Tokenizer Codebook Size (Table 3):** An ablation study on the VQVAE codebook size reveals that 512 tokens strike an optimal balance between reconstruction performance and computational complexity, providing the best trade-off for learning.
- **Data Augmentation (Table 4):** Randomly shuffling the order of humans during training significantly enhances the diversity of generated outputs. This demonstrates that the augmentation strategy helps the model learn interaction dynamics invariant to participant order, improving generalization.

In summary, SocialGen not only introduces a novel and scalable framework but also empirically proves its effectiveness, setting a new standard for multi-human interaction modeling by addressing critical limitations in motion representation, data availability, and model generalization.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

SocialGen represents a significant leap forward in the field of human behavior modeling, particularly in complex social interactions, and its potential impact is multi-faceted:

- **Pioneering Unified Framework:** SocialGen is the first unified motion-language model designed to explicitly handle interactions among *any number of individuals*. This capability moves beyond the long-standing limitation of single- or two-person interaction models, opening up entirely new avenues for generating and understanding realistic group dynamics. This generalization is crucial for real-world applications where participant numbers are rarely fixed.
- **Enabling Scalable Social AI:** The novel XH3D motion representation is a key technical innovation that underpins this scalability. By effectively encoding individual motion and relative spatial relationships, XH3D allows for robust tokenization and integration with language models, paving the way for AI systems that can reason about and generate complex social behaviors in large groups.
- **Bridging Motion and Language for Social Context:** By aligning motion and language spaces within an LLM framework, SocialGen enables intuitive, natural language control over multi-human interactions. This means users can simply describe desired social scenarios, and the model can generate corresponding movements, a paradigm shift from more rigid, hard-coded interaction models. This capability is vital for creating more intelligent, user-friendly, and versatile AI applications.
- **Curating a Foundational Dataset (SocialX):** The introduction of SocialX, the first large-scale multi-human interaction dataset enriched with high-quality textual annotations, is a critical contribution. Data scarcity has been a major bottleneck, and SocialX provides an invaluable resource for the research community, facilitating the development and benchmarking of future models in this challenging domain.
- **Establishing a New Benchmark:** Alongside SocialX, SocialGen establishes the first comprehensive benchmark for multi-human interaction tasks. This provides a standardized, rigorous evaluation framework, encouraging focused research and allowing for meaningful comparisons between different approaches, which is essential for accelerating progress.
- **Diverse Application Potential:**
 - **VR/Gaming:** Developers can create far more immersive and dynamic virtual worlds with realistic multi-character interactions, from bustling crowds to intricate social encounters.
 - **Robotics:** Socially aware robots can better navigate and collaborate with multiple humans in shared spaces (e.g., factory floors, homes, healthcare settings), leading to more natural and effective human-robot interaction.
 - **Healthcare:** Understanding complex patient-physician or group therapy dynamics could be enhanced, enabling improved telemedicine, rehabilitation, and social skills training simulations.
 - **Animation and Content Creation:** Artists and animators gain powerful tools for rapidly prototyping and generating complex multi-character scenes with fine-grained linguistic control.
- **Catalyst for Future Research:** The paper identifies crucial directions for future work, such as improving evaluation metrics for multi-human interactions and extending models to open-domain scenarios with more complex, multimodal contexts. By laying a strong foundation, SocialGen is poised to stimulate significant further research in socially intelligent AI, contributing to a deeper understanding of human social behavior and more capable AI systems.
