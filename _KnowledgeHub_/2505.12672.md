# TransferTraj: A Vehicle Trajectory Learning Model for Region and Task Transferability

> [!summary] Summary
> TransferTraj introduces a vehicle GPS trajectory learning model designed for strong region and task transferability, leveraging relative spatial encoding and context-aware expert networks. The model demonstrates superior performance in trajectory prediction, recovery, and travel time estimation across diverse geographical areas without requiring task-specific fine-tuning.

## Problem

- Conventional vehicle trajectory learning models are typically trained and maintained separately for each specific region and task, leading to high computational costs and significant data requirements.
- Existing transferable trajectory models primarily learn generalized embeddings but still require additional prediction modules and fine-tuning for downstream tasks, hindering true zero-shot task transfer.
- Transferring models across different geographical regions is challenging due to unique spatial features, road network topologies, and POI distributions, which are not consistently represented by existing standardization methods.

## Method

- A ==Region-Transferable Trajectory Encoder== (RTTE) processes trajectory points using relative spatial, temporal, POI, and road network modalities, fostering region-independent feature learning.
- RTTE incorporates a Trajectory Relative Information Extraction (TRIE) block for modeling relationships based on relative spatial distances and a Spatial Context Mixture-of-Experts (SC-MoE) block for context-aware pattern learning.
- A task-transferable input-output scheme unifies diverse generative tasks through a masking and recovery paradigm, where the model is pre-trained to reconstruct masked modalities or entire trajectory points.

## Results

- TransferTraj, without fine-tuning, outperformed existing state-of-the-art baselines by an average of **20.18%** for Trajectory Prediction, **17.87%** for Trajectory Recovery, and **7.94%** for Origin-Destination Travel Time Estimation.
- In zero-shot region transfer, the model achieved an average improvement of **83.70%** for Trajectory Prediction and **10.88%** for OD Travel Time Estimation when trained on one region and directly applied to another.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the Trajectory Relative Information Extraction (TRIE) block, Spatial Context Mixture-of-Experts (SC-MoE) block, and the inclusion of POI/road network modalities are critical for the model's performance and transferability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Relative spatial encoding, combined with region-independent semantic representations for POIs and road networks, enables the model to learn movement patterns that generalize across diverse geographical areas.
> - The Spatial Context Mixture-of-Experts (SC-MoE) dynamically adapts to varying local spatial contexts, allowing the model to capture diverse movement patterns and share analogous patterns across regions.
> - A unified input-output scheme with modality and trajectory point masking allows the model to be pre-trained for comprehensive spatio-temporal understanding, enabling direct application to various generative tasks without task-specific fine-tuning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The widespread availability of vehicle GPS trajectories has spurred significant interest in developing trajectory learning models for various Intelligent Transportation Systems (ITS) applications, such as trajectory prediction, recovery, and travel time estimation. Conventionally, these tasks are addressed by training dedicated models for each specific region and task. This approach, while straightforward, suffers from substantial drawbacks: high training costs, the need for vast amounts of data for each unique scenario, and the necessity to maintain multiple specialized models.

The research community has recognized these limitations and moved towards developing "transferable" trajectory learning models. Existing efforts primarily focus on learning generalized "embedding vectors" for trajectories through pre-training techniques like auto-encoding or contrastive learning (e.g., t2vec, Trembr, START). While these methods offer some degree of task transferability by mapping trajectories into a rich embedding space, they still fall short in two critical aspects:
1. **Imperfect Task Transferability:** The learned embeddings, while informative, typically require additional "prediction modules" (e.g., simple MLP layers) that still need to be fine-tuned or trained from scratch for each downstream task. This means a truly "zero-shot" task transfer is not fully achieved, as additional training and storage of task-specific parameters are still required.
2. **Limited Region Transferability:** A more significant challenge lies in transferring models across different geographical regions. Each region possesses unique spatial features, road network topologies, Points of Interest (POIs) distribution, and socio-economic contexts that profoundly influence vehicle movement patterns. Existing standardization methods like normalization or grid discretization often fail to produce consistent spatial scales across regions, leading to models trained in one region performing poorly when directly applied to another. This prevents the direct transfer of learned movement patterns.

"TransferTraj" directly addresses these two fundamental limitations. It proposes a novel approach that aims to achieve true transferability across *both* regions and tasks *without requiring retraining* after a single pre-training phase. This ambitious goal positions TransferTraj as a significant advancement, moving beyond merely learning general embeddings to developing a truly adaptive and efficient framework for trajectory analysis in diverse and data-scarce environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop **TransferTraj**, a vehicle GPS trajectory learning model that demonstrates exceptional capability in both **region transferability** and **task transferability**.

The motivations behind pursuing this objective are multifaceted and directly address the shortcomings of existing trajectory learning paradigms:
- **Reduce Computational and Storage Overheads:** Current practices necessitate training and maintaining separate, specialized models for each distinct region and task. This leads to substantial computational costs for training and significant storage requirements for model parameters. TransferTraj aims to alleviate this by enabling a single, pre-trained model to serve multiple purposes.
- **Enhance Performance in Data-Scarce Scenarios:** Many real-world regions or newly emerging tasks may lack the extensive trajectory data required to train high-performing models from scratch. A truly transferable model can leverage knowledge gained from data-rich regions or common tasks, enabling robust performance even with limited local training data or in a zero-shot setting. This mitigates issues like overfitting and poor generalization in data-deficient environments.
- **Overcome Regional Heterogeneity Challenges:** Vehicle movement patterns are deeply intertwined with unique regional spatial contexts (e.g., road network density, POI distribution, geographical size). Existing models struggle to generalize across these varying contexts due to inherent inconsistencies in spatial feature representations. TransferTraj seeks to explicitly model and adapt to these regional differences in a generalizable manner.
- **Unify Diverse Task Requirements:** Different trajectory-related generative tasks (e.g., prediction, recovery, travel time estimation) have distinct input-output structures and focus on learning different types of correlations (sequential, temporal, etc.). This structural divergence poses a significant challenge for a single model to handle multiple tasks seamlessly. The authors aim to devise a unified input-output scheme that allows TransferTraj to adapt to these diverse task requirements without specific architectural modifications or retraining.

In essence, the motivation is to build a highly efficient, adaptable, and generalizable trajectory intelligence system that can be deployed rapidly and effectively across a broad spectrum of real-world scenarios, minimizing the need for constant re-engineering and retraining.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

TransferTraj is designed with two tightly integrated core components: the **Region-Transferable Trajectory Encoder (RTTE)** for region transferability and a **task-transferable input-output scheme** for task transferability.

#### 3.1. Region-Transferable Trajectory Encoder (RTTE)

RTTE is the primary learnable module responsible for processing trajectory points and enabling cross-region generalization. It operates in stages:

1. **Trajectory Point Modality Representation:** Each trajectory point `p_i` is enriched with information from four distinct modalities:
 - **Spatial Modality:** Instead of absolute coordinates, the model uses relative positions `(lng_i - lng_1, lat_i - lat_1)` with respect to the first point of the trajectory. This relative encoding is crucial for region transferability as it abstracts away region-specific coordinate systems and scales. A learnable linear layer and Fourier encoding further process these relative coordinates into an embedding `e_s_i`.
 - **Temporal Modality:** Timestamps `t_i` are represented by features like day of the week, hour, minute, and time difference relative to `t_1`. These are also transformed into an embedding `e_t_i` using learnable Fourier encoding.
 - **POI Modality:** For each `p_i`, the set of nearby POIs (`P_i`) within a specified distance (`φ_poi_dist`) is considered. Their textual descriptions (name, type, address) are encoded using a pre-trained text embedding model (e.g., OpenAI's embeddings), followed by mean pooling and linear transformation to obtain `e_p_i`. This semantic representation of POIs is inherently region-independent.
 - **Road Network Modality:** Similarly, nearby road segments (`R_i`) within `φ_road_dist` are semantically encoded from their textual descriptions (name, type, length) using the same pre-trained text embedding approach, resulting in `e_r_i`. This provides region-independent information about the underlying road infrastructure.

2. **Trajectory Modality Mixing:** The four modality embeddings (`e_s_i`, `e_t_i`, `e_p_i`, `e_r_i`) for each point `p_i` are fed into a Transformer. A subsequent mean pooling layer integrates the information across these modalities, producing a comprehensive embedding `e_i` for the trajectory point.

3. **Stacked Encoder Layers:** The sequence of mixed trajectory point embeddings (`⟨e_1..., e_n⟩`) is then processed by `L` identical layers. Each layer consists of two innovative blocks:
 - **Trajectory Relative Information Extraction (TRIE) Block:** Inspired by RoFormer, this block enhances the self-attention mechanism by incorporating relative spatial information. It introduces a learnable spatio-temporal rotation matrix `R_Φ(x,y)` that operates on query and key vectors. This design allows the model to naturally model relationships based on *true relative distances* `Φ(x_i-x_j, y_i-y_j)` between trajectory points. This explicit handling of relative spatial features makes the model less sensitive to absolute spatial variations across regions and more generalizable. The continuous and periodic nature of trigonometric functions further enables generalization to trajectories longer than those seen during training.
 - **Spatial Context Mixture-of-Experts (SC-MoE) Block:** Recognizing that movement patterns are heavily influenced by local spatial context (e.g., high-speed on highways vs. stop-and-go in dense POI areas), SC-MoE integrates multiple "expert" networks. A gating network, employing a noisy top-K mechanism, dynamically selects the most suitable experts based on the local spatial context (`h_i + e_i`). This allows the model to capture diverse movement patterns while simultaneously identifying and sharing similar patterns in analogous local contexts across different regions, thereby fostering strong region transferability.

4. **Trajectory Modality Predictor:** After passing through the stacked TRIE and SC-MoE layers, the latent vector `z_i` for each trajectory point is fed into linear projection layers to predict its spatial (`(hat_x_i, hat_y_i)`) and temporal (`hat_t_i`) modalities. The training objective for RTTE is to minimize the Mean Squared Error (MSE) between predicted and ground-truth modalities.

#### 3.2. Task-Transferable Input-Output Scheme

To enable seamless transfer across various generative tasks without retraining, TransferTraj unifies the input-output structure using a masking and recovery paradigm:

- **Modality Masking:** For a given trajectory point `p_i`, specific modalities (spatial or temporal) can be masked in the input. If the spatial modality is masked (`p_ms_i`), its associated POI and road network modalities are also masked, replaced by a special mask token `[m]`. If only the temporal modality is masked (`p_mt_i`), it is replaced by `[m]`.
- **Trajectory Point Masking:** Entire trajectory points can be masked and replaced by a special mask point `p_[m]`.

**Pre-training Mechanism:** TransferTraj is pre-trained by applying a combination of these masking strategies to sub-trajectories:
- A random sub-trajectory is selected, and all points within it undergo *complete trajectory point masking* in the input, requiring full recovery in the output.
- For the remaining points in the trajectory (outside the masked sub-trajectory), either their spatial or temporal modalities are *randomly masked* with equal probability, and the model is tasked with recovering these specific masked modalities.
The pre-training loss is the sum of reconstruction losses for all masked and recovered modalities. This self-supervised pre-training forces the model to learn comprehensive, context-aware representations that capture spatio-temporal dependencies and movement patterns. This holistic understanding enables TransferTraj to adapt to various downstream tasks (which can be framed as variations of modality or point recovery) directly, without requiring task-specific fine-tuning of the core model or prediction heads.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The effectiveness of TransferTraj was validated through extensive experiments on three real-world vehicle GPS trajectory datasets: **Chengdu, Xi’an, and Porto**, which collectively represent diverse spatial scales, sampling intervals, and geographic characteristics. The model's performance was evaluated across three common generative tasks: **Trajectory Prediction (TP)**, **Trajectory Recovery (TR)**, and **Origin-Destination Travel Time Estimation (OD TTE)**. A comprehensive set of state-of-the-art baselines for each task, including various trajectory representation learning methods, was used for comparison. Metrics included RMSE, MAE (for TP and TR), and RMSE, MAE, MAPE (for OD TTE), where lower values indicate better performance.

#### 4.1. Task Transferability

TransferTraj demonstrated superior task transferability, even without fine-tuning on the specific target task after pre-training (denoted as "TransferTraj (wo ft)").
- **Overall Performance:** In its pre-trained state (without fine-tuning), TransferTraj (wo ft) significantly outperformed existing SOTA baselines:
 - For TP, it achieved an average gain of **20.18%** over START (SOTA baseline).
 - For TR, it showed an average improvement of **17.87%** over MM-STGED (SOTA baseline).
 - For OD TTE, it recorded an average gain of **7.94%** over DOT (SOTA baseline).
- **Fine-tuning Impact:** When further fine-tuned on the target tasks ("TransferTraj"), performance saw additional improvements of **11.41%** (TP), **13.59%** (TR), and **8.62%** (OD TTE), showcasing the model's adaptability.
- **Comparison to Baselines' Transfer:** Crucially, some baselines that relied on freezing pre-trained encoders and only fine-tuning prediction heads (e.g., Trembr (wo ft), START (wo ft)) showed significant performance degradation. This highlights that TransferTraj's unified pre-training and input-output scheme enables true task transferability, allowing direct application with high performance, unlike previous methods that still required substantial task-specific adaptation.

#### 4.2. Region Transferability

The model's region transferability was assessed under both zero-shot and few-shot settings.
- **Zero-Shot Region Transfer:**
 - For TP, TransferTraj achieved an impressive average improvement of **83.70%** over SOTA models when trained on one region and directly applied to another.
 - For OD TTE, it showed an average gain of **10.88%**.
 - (Zero-shot TR was not conducted due to baselines' reliance on region-specific road IDs).
- **Few-Shot Region Transfer:** In scenarios where a small amount of data (5,000 trajectories) was available for fine-tuning in the target region:
 - TransferTraj achieved average improvements of **33.68%** for TP.
 - It showed **18.08%** improvement for TR.
 - And **13.07%** improvement for OD TTE.
These results underscore TransferTraj's robust ability to generalize across different geographical areas, which the authors attribute to its capacity to capture relative spatial information and extract shared movement patterns from diverse contexts.

#### 4.3. Model Analysis

- **Ablation Study:** Experiments confirmed the critical contribution of each proposed module:
 - Removing the **TRIE** component led to a significant performance decline, emphasizing the importance of modeling relative spatial information between trajectory points.
 - Omitting the **SC-MoE** module resulted in a substantial performance drop (e.g., 14.52% on OD TTE), validating its effectiveness in adapting to and capturing diverse movement patterns within varying spatial contexts.
 - Excluding the **POI and road network modalities** also degraded performance, confirming their value in providing richer and more transferable spatial context.
- **SC-MoE Activation Analysis:** An analysis of expert activation distributions across different spatial context densities (high, medium, low POI/road segment density) revealed that SC-MoE dynamically selected specialized combinations of experts. This confirms that the model intelligently adapts its internal mechanisms to effectively process movement patterns under varied environmental conditions.

#### 4.4. Efficiency Study (Appendix H)

TransferTraj is shown to be a lightweight model, comparable in size to simpler RNN-based methods and significantly smaller than other SOTA transformer-based approaches (e.g., START, LightPath). It also exhibits efficient training and competitive testing times. This efficiency, combined with its strong transferability, makes it highly practical for real-world deployment, as it reduces the need for repeated training cycles across regions and tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

TransferTraj represents a significant advancement in the field of trajectory learning by simultaneously addressing the critical challenges of region and task transferability in a comprehensive and effective manner.

**Key Contributions and Significance:**
- **Dual Transferability:** This paper is among the first to demonstrate strong performance in *both* zero-shot region transfer and zero-shot task transfer for vehicle trajectories. This capability of pre-training a single model once and deploying it across diverse geographies and applications without further retraining is a paradigm shift.
- **Novel Architectural Components:** The introduction of the **Region-Transferable Trajectory Encoder (RTTE)**, with its innovative **Trajectory Relative Information Extraction (TRIE)** and **Spatial Context Mixture-of-Experts (SC-MoE)** modules, provides a robust framework for handling spatial context variations and learning generalizable movement patterns. The use of relative spatial encoding and region-independent semantic representations for POIs and road networks is particularly noteworthy.
- **Unified Input-Output Scheme:** The task-transferable input-output scheme, based on modality and trajectory point masking and recovery, offers a truly flexible pre-training mechanism. This allows the pre-trained model to directly infer various missing spatio-temporal components needed for diverse downstream tasks, fundamentally reducing the need for task-specific prediction heads or fine-tuning.
- **Computational Efficiency:** By eliminating the need to train and maintain multiple specialized models, TransferTraj dramatically reduces computational costs and storage overhead, making advanced trajectory analytics more accessible and scalable.
- **Addressing Data Scarcity:** The ability to transfer effectively to regions or tasks with limited training data is a crucial practical benefit. It enables the deployment of high-performing models in emerging markets or for niche applications where extensive data collection is not feasible, mitigating overfitting issues.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Intelligent Transportation Systems (ITS):** TransferTraj can significantly accelerate the development and deployment of ITS applications. For instance, a traffic management system trained in one major city could be rapidly adapted to a new city with minimal local data, enhancing capabilities like real-time traffic prediction, intelligent routing, and public safety.
- **Logistics and Ride-Sharing:** Companies operating in multiple regions can use a single, unified trajectory model, streamlining operations and improving the accuracy of estimated arrival times and route optimization across their entire service area.
- **Geospatial AI:** The methodologies introduced, particularly the region-transferable encoding and context-aware expert networks, could inspire similar advancements in other domains dealing with spatio-temporal data and generalized machine learning models.

**Limitations (as stated by authors):**
The authors acknowledge that TransferTraj currently faces challenges in cross-region classification tasks, such as linking trajectories to specific users or predicting destination road segments. This is primarily because the number of users or specific road network IDs varies significantly across regions, making direct transfer for such classification tasks difficult. Future work aims to develop a more unified framework to address these challenges and expand the model's applicability to a broader range of tasks beyond generative ones.

Despite these current limitations, TransferTraj marks a substantial step forward in creating truly versatile and efficient trajectory learning models, paving the way for more adaptable and scalable geospatial AI applications.
