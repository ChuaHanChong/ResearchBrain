# Improving Neural Network Training by Decoupling the Magnitude and Direction of Weight Vectors

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at EPFL's Machine Learning and Optimization Lab developed Magnitude–Direction (MD) Decoupling, an optimizer modification that separates the learning dynamics of weight vector magnitudes and directions. This approach consistently improved performance, eliminated the need for weight decay and learning rate warmup, facilitated learning rate transfer across model sizes, and reduced compute requirements by approximately 2x for large Mixture-of-Experts models.

## Problem

- Conventional neural network optimizers couple the magnitude and direction dynamics of weight vectors, leading to unstable and unpredictable training.
- The angular change of weights becomes inversely proportional to their current magnitude, causing inconsistent directional updates across layers and training phases.
- Weight magnitudes tend to drift outwards without explicit radial gradients, necessitating auxiliary mechanisms like weight decay or QK-clip to prevent unchecked growth.

## Method

- A method called ==Magnitude==–Direction (MD) Decoupling reparameterizes each weight matrix W into a fixed-norm direction ̂W and learnable magnitude gains (e.g., diag(γ_row) and diag(γ_col)).
- The optimizer is modified to explicitly decouple updates for the direction ̂W and the magnitude gains γ, allowing them to be trained with independent learning rates.
- The direction ̂W is projected onto a hypersphere after each update to maintain a fixed norm, while gains γ are updated using a standard Adam step.

## Results

- MD Decoupling consistently improved performance over well-tuned Adam and Muon baselines across dense and sparse (Mixture-of-Experts) neural networks.
- For large Mixture-of-Experts models, the MuonMD variant achieved comparable loss to AdamW with approximately **2x** less computational budget.
- The method enabled the optimal learning rate to transfer across different model widths and depths, and eliminated the need for learning rate warmup and weight decay.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly constraining weight directions to a fixed-norm hypersphere, combined with learnable magnitude gains, allows for a more direct and stable control of learning dynamics.
> - Decoupling enables the removal of common training heuristics like weight decay and learning rate warmup, simplifying the optimization process.
> - The precise control over relative weight updates facilitates the transfer of optimal learning rates across models of different widths and depths, reducing hyperparameter tuning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Modern neural network training has seen advancements through sophisticated optimizers like Adam and more recent matrix-aware methods such as Shampoo, SOAP, and Muon. These optimizers incorporate insights into the geometry of weight space to refine updates. Alongside these optimizers, a set of heuristics and recipes, including weight decay and learning rate warmup, are commonly employed to maintain training stability and predictability, particularly at scale.

However, existing optimizers typically treat each weight matrix as a single entity, inherently coupling the dynamics of its magnitude and direction. This coupling means that the learning rate does not directly control either the angular change or the magnitude drift, leading to indirect regulation through auxiliary techniques like weight decay and warmup.

This paper positions itself within a growing body of work that explores modifications to weight parameterization or optimization to address these issues. Some recent approaches fix weights to a sphere, thereby dropping weight decay, but often do not incorporate learnable magnitudes, relying instead on normalization layers for scale control. Other concurrent efforts learn explicit scales without a sphere constraint or combine per-row magnitude and direction splitting. The present work aims to unify and extend these threads by proposing a comprehensive decoupling mechanism, investigating its practical implications for training large language models, and providing controlled experiments on various design choices (e.g., normalization axis, gain parameterization). It seeks to provide a more principled and direct way to control the learning dynamics of weight magnitude and direction, thereby improving training predictability and performance across diverse optimizers and model scales.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation for this research stems from an observed interference between the magnitude and direction of weight vectors in conventional neural network training. In a weight matrix `W`, two distinct quantities exist: its magnitude `∥W∥` and its direction `̂W = W/∥W∥`. Standard optimizers, by stepping in `W` as a whole, couple these quantities, leading to several training challenges:

1. **Directional Change Depends on Magnitude**: The angular change resulting from an optimizer update (`∆W`) is approximated by `∥∆W∥ / ∥W∥`. For normalized optimizers, the update size is set by the learning rate, implying that the directional change is inversely proportional to the current magnitude `∥W∥`. Consequently, the learning rate does not directly set the rate of directional change, which can vary undesirably across layers and over time, potentially hindering learning. This often necessitates weight decay to modulate relative updates and balance learning across layers.

2. **Magnitude Drifts Without Explicit Radial Gradient**: Updates often tend to be roughly perpendicular to the current weights. Such perpendicular updates inherently increase the magnitude of the weight vector, even in cases where the loss function is scale-invariant and provides no radial gradient to pull the weights outward. This leads to unnecessary magnitude growth that must be counteracted by mechanisms like weight decay or specific architectural fixes (e.g., QK-clip). The magnitude of weights tends to converge towards an equilibrium determined by the learning rate and weight decay, rather than a learned optimum.

These issues collectively result in standard optimizers struggling to effectively learn the magnitude of weight matrices. The objectives of this research are to:

- **Decouple Magnitude and Direction**: Introduce an optimizer modification that explicitly factorizes each weight into a fixed-norm direction on a hypersphere and learnable magnitude gains.
- **Enable Separate Control**: Allow independent control and distinct learning rates for the direction and magnitude components, preventing their dynamics from interfering.
- **Enhance Training Predictability**: Achieve more direct control over the relative weight update, leading to a more predictable and stable training process.
- **Eliminate Indirect Regulatory Mechanisms**: Remove or reduce the reliance on auxiliary techniques such as weight decay and learning rate warmup, which are currently necessary to stabilize coupled dynamics.
- **Improve Performance at Scale**: Demonstrate that this decoupling leads to improved performance on various neural network architectures, including large Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) models, and facilitates properties like learning rate transfer across model sizes.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Magnitude–Direction (MD) Decoupling, is an optimizer modification designed to address the interference between weight magnitude and direction.

**Reparameterization**:
The core idea involves factorizing each weight matrix `W` into a fixed-norm direction `̂W` (constrained to a hypersphere) and learnable magnitude gains. The general factorization used is `W = diag(γ_row) ̂W diag(γ_col)`, where `γ_row` (a vector in R^(d_out)) and `γ_col` (a vector in R^(d_in)) are learnable per-row and per-column magnitude gains, respectively. Scalar or one-sided gain variations are considered special cases. `̂W` is constrained such that its norm (e.g., Frobenius, row-wise, or column-wise) remains fixed. These magnitude gains are initialized to 1, ensuring no initial change to the model. The method is agnostic to the base optimizer used for updating the direction.

**Decoupled Optimizer Step**:
The decoupling occurs entirely within the optimizer, meaning the neural network model itself continues to operate on a single, fused weight tensor `W`. The optimizer performs the following steps (illustrated in Algorithm 2 in the appendix for per-row and per-column gains):
1. **Materialize Gains**: The raw gains (`̂γ_row`, `̂γ_col`) are transformed into positive gains (`γ_row`, `γ_col`) using a smooth, positive map (e.g., softplus).
2. **Recover Direction**: The fixed-norm direction `̂W` is recovered from the fused weight `W` by dividing out the current magnitude gains.
3. **Compute Gain Gradients**: Gradients for the raw gains (`g_̂γ_row`, `g_̂γ_col`) are computed by backpropagating through the gain map and reducing the original weight gradient `G = ∂L/∂W` over the appropriate axes.
4. **Compute Direction Gradient**: The gradient for the direction (`G_̂W`) is derived from the original `G` and the magnitude gains.
5. **Update Direction**: The direction `̂W` is updated using a base matrix optimizer (e.g., Adam, Muon) with its own learning rate (`η_W`).
6. **Project onto Sphere**: After the update, `̂W` is projected back onto the hypersphere by normalizing its norm (e.g., `̂W ← ̂W / ∥̂W∥`).
7. **Update Gains**: The raw gains (`̂γ_row`, `̂γ_col`) are updated using an AdamStep with their own learning rate (`η_γ`).
8. **Reassemble Weight**: The fused weight `W` is reassembled from the updated `γ_row`, `̂W`, and `γ_col` for the next forward pass.

**Key Properties**:
- **Optimizer Agnosticism**: The method operates as a wrapper around any base matrix optimizer.
- **Weight Decay Removal**: Explicitly constraining `̂W` to a sphere removes the need for weight decay.
- **Learning Rate Transfer**: Directly controlling the relative weight update through the sphere constraint enables the optimal learning rate to transfer across model widths without retuning.
- **Warmup Elimination**: The inherent stability of regulated relative updates from the first step mitigates the need for learning rate warmup.

**Empirical Evaluation Approach**:
The evaluation is divided into two main parts:
1. **Ablations on Dense Models**:
 - **Architecture**: GPT-style language models ranging from 181M to 1.29B parameters, using RoPE, SwiGLU MLPs, RMSNorm, GQA, QK-RMSNorm, and Sandwich Norm with a fixed block-output scale (α = 1/L). Initialized with `1/√d` for matrices and `√d` for embeddings.
 - **Dataset**: Subset of FineWeb-Edu (25B tokens, 50k steps) for extensive overtraining.
 - **Optimizers**: AdamW and Muon as base optimizers. Matrix learning rate (LR) is swept, while other parameter group LRs are fixed (e.g., embeddings at 3e-3, output layer at 1e-3). Baselines use weight decay 0.1; MD variants use none.
 - **Ablation Studies**: Investigated normalization axis (row, column, Frobenius), embedding normalization (on-sphere vs. off-sphere), magnitude gain types (scalar, per-row, per-column, row+column, rank-k) and their parameterization (direct, exponential, softplus), LR schedules (linear vs. WSD), and effects of warmup and continual training.
2. **Scaling to Large Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) Models**:
 - **Architecture**: DeepSeekMoE-style transformers (1.2B–6.7B total parameters, 270M–810M active parameters) with 64 routed experts + 1 shared expert, top-2 routing, and similar backbone conventions as dense models but without Sandwich Norms.
 - **Dataset**: Apertus 1.0 phase-5 data mixture (7.5B–44B tokens).
 - **Scaling Protocol**: Optimal LR found on a small base model (270M-active/1.2B-total), then transferred to larger models using scaling rules (Complete(d)P for baselines, `1/l^0.25` for MuonMD). Performance compared across a range of compute budgets and batch sizes.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluation of Magnitude–Direction (MD) Decoupling yielded several findings regarding its efficacy, properties, and impact on neural network training:

**Overall Performance Improvement**:
- MD Decoupling consistently improved upon well-tuned Adam and Muon baselines across dense and sparse (Mixture-of-Experts) models.
- For dense models, fixing weights onto a sphere improved the optimal loss, and the addition of learnable magnitudes (MD variant) provided a further boost (Figure 1, Left).

**Normalization Axis and Embeddings**:
- The choice of normalization axis for the fixed-norm direction (per-row, per-column, or Frobenius) had minimal impact on the final loss (Figure 4). The Frobenius constraint was adopted for its flexibility.
- Constraining each embedding vector to unit L2 norm performed at least on par with leaving its norm free, while leading to more stable embedding updates (Figure 5). This approach was adopted as a default.

**Magnitude Gains**:
- The inclusion of learnable magnitude gains significantly improved performance over spherical training without gains.
- Combined per-row and per-column gains (W = diag(γ_row) ̂W diag(γ_col)) achieved the best results among scalar, per-row, and per-column options, indicating the benefit of fine-grained scale control (Figure 6, Left).
- The specific parameterization of the gains (direct, exponential, or softplus) had minimal impact on the final loss, with softplus offering a slight, consistent edge and all methods demonstrating stable training (Figure 6, Center & Right). Dynamic analysis showed that these gains are actively learned and spread over a wide range during training (Appendix H).
- Higher-rank (k=4) gains, while more expressive, did not outperform the simpler row-and-column gains, a discrepancy potentially attributed to the parametrization and training dynamics (Figure 17).

**Learning Rate Transfer**:
- MD Decoupling enabled the optimal matrix learning rate to remain nearly constant across different model widths without requiring retuning (Figure 7, Left). This property is attributed to the precise control over relative weight updates on the sphere (Figure 8, Left & Center).
- Depth transfer was also achieved by using a fixed block-output scale (e.g., `α = 1/L` or `α = 1/√2L`), which helped maintain controlled and uniform per-layer activation RMS values across depths (Figure 7, Center; Figure 16).

**Learning Rate Schedules**:
- On the sphere, the relative weight update directly followed the nominal learning rate schedule. This implies that the shape of the learning rate schedule is more critical for on-sphere training than in standard training where implicit decay from weight norm growth occurs (Figure 9).

**Warmup-Free and Continual Training**:
- MD Decoupling removed the need for learning rate warmup. Dropping warmup improved the final loss by allowing higher effective learning rates from the initial steps (Figure 10, Left).
- Training remained stable and effective when resuming from checkpoints, even without re-warming or resetting optimizer states, indicating suitability for continual training scenarios (Figure 10, Center & Right).

**Scaling to Large Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) Models**:
- The benefits of MD Decoupling extended to large MoE models. MuonMD (Muon with MD Decoupling) outperformed well-tuned Muon and AdamW baselines (Figure 11, Left).
- On scaling law evaluations, MuonMD reached AdamW’s loss with approximately 2x less compute across a wide range of compute budgets (Figure 1, Center; Figure 11, Center).
- This advantage also held across various global batch sizes (Figure 11, Right).

**Efficiency and Comparison to nGPT**:
- The computational overhead introduced by MD Decoupling was observed to be modest, particularly minimal for MuonMD (≲2% slowdown) due to the element-wise nature of the added operations and their diminishing relative cost at larger scales or batch sizes (Table 3).
- A direct comparison with nGPT (Loshchilov et al., 2025) revealed that while nGPT's proposed architecture outperformed a baseline, applying MD Decoupling's principles (Frobenius constraint, magnitude gains) to nGPT's architecture further improved performance, especially with MuonMD (Figure 15). A significant portion of nGPT's advantage was attributed to its specific scaling of 1D learnable vectors.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Magnitude–Direction (MD) Decoupling represents a contribution to the field of neural network optimization, with several significant implications and potential impacts:

**Enhanced Training Predictability and Stability**: By explicitly decoupling the dynamics of weight magnitude and direction, MD Decoupling addresses fundamental interference issues in traditional optimizers. This leads to more predictable training behavior, as the learning rate directly controls the angular update, and magnitude drift is managed separately. This enhanced predictability can simplify the development and scaling of neural network models.

**Simplified Hyperparameter Management**: A key outcome is the reduction in the complexity of hyperparameter tuning. MD Decoupling effectively removes the need for weight decay, as weights are kept on a fixed-norm sphere, and largely eliminates the need for learning rate warmup. Furthermore, the demonstrated ability of the optimal learning rate to transfer across different model widths simplifies the scaling process, allowing hyperparameters to be tuned on smaller models and applied to larger ones without extensive re-tuning.

**Improved Compute Efficiency**: The empirical results, particularly on large Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) models, indicate that MD Decoupling can achieve comparable or superior performance with significantly less computational resource. The 2x reduction in compute to reach AdamW's loss suggests substantial efficiency gains for large-scale pretraining efforts.

**Broad Applicability**: The optimizer-agnostic nature of MD Decoupling means it can be integrated with various existing and future base optimizers (e.g., Adam, Muon, Shampoo). This broad applicability expands its potential utility across different deep learning tasks and architectures.

**Unification and Clarification of Related Concepts**: The extensive ablations and comparisons with concurrent work (such as nGPT, AdamH/MuonH) help clarify which specific components of magnitude-direction control contribute most to performance improvements. This can serve as a reference for future research in this rapidly evolving area.

**Directions for Future Research**: The work opens several avenues for further investigation:
- **Optimal On-Sphere Schedules**: The observation that the learning rate schedule's shape is more critical under MD Decoupling suggests a need for re-evaluating and designing new optimal schedules tailored for this regime.
- **Understanding Learned Magnitudes**: A deeper mechanistic understanding of how the learned per-row and per-column magnitudes contribute to model function and why fine-grained control is beneficial could yield further architectural and optimization insights.
- **Impact on Downstream Tasks**: Evaluating the effects of MD Decoupling on downstream task performance, particularly in reinforcement learning (RL) settings, and its influence on properties like loss-landscape sharpness.
- **Low-Precision Training**: Investigating how MD Decoupling interacts with and potentially benefits low-precision training schemes.

Overall, MD Decoupling offers a principled approach to improving neural network training dynamics, yielding tangible benefits in stability, efficiency, and ease of use, with implications for the design and training of next-generation large-scale models.
