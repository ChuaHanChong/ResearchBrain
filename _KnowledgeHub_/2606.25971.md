# Improving Neural Network Training by Decoupling the Magnitude and Direction of Weight Vectors

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at EPFL's Machine Learning and Optimization Lab developed Magnitude–Direction (MD) Decoupling, an optimizer modification that separates the learning dynamics of weight vector magnitudes and directions. This approach consistently improved performance, eliminated the need for weight decay and learning rate warmup, facilitated learning rate transfer across model sizes, and reduced compute requirements by approximately 2x for large Mixture-of-Experts models.

## Problem

- Conventional neural network optimizers couple the magnitude and direction dynamics of weight vectors, leading to unstable and unpredictable training.
- The angular change of weights becomes inversely proportional to their current magnitude, causing inconsistent directional updates across layers and training phases.
- Weight magnitudes tend to drift outwards without explicit radial gradients, necessitating auxiliary mechanisms like weight decay or QK-clip to prevent unchecked growth.

## Method

- A method called ==Magnitude==–Direction (MD) Decoupling reparameterizes each weight matrix W into a fixed-norm direction ̂W and learnable magnitude gains (e.g., diag(γ_row) and diag(γ_col)).
- The optimizer is modified to explicitly decouple updates for the direction ̂W and the magnitude gains γ, allowing them to be trained with independent learning rates.
- The direction ̂W is projected onto a hypersphere after each update to maintain a fixed norm, while gains γ are updated using a standard Adam step.

## Results

- MD Decoupling consistently improved performance over well-tuned Adam and Muon baselines across dense and sparse (Mixture-of-Experts) neural networks.
- For large Mixture-of-Experts models, the MuonMD variant achieved comparable loss to AdamW with approximately **2x** less computational budget.
- The method enabled the optimal learning rate to transfer across different model widths and depths, and eliminated the need for learning rate warmup and weight decay.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly constraining weight directions to a fixed-norm hypersphere, combined with learnable magnitude gains, allows for a more direct and stable control of learning dynamics.
> - Decoupling enables the removal of common training heuristics like weight decay and learning rate warmup, simplifying the optimization process.
> - The precise control over relative weight updates facilitates the transfer of optimal learning rates across models of different widths and depths, reducing hyperparameter tuning.
