# Towards Generalist Robot Learning from Internet Video: A Survey

> [!summary] Summary
> Towards Generalist Robot Learning from Internet Video surveys Learning from Videos (LfV) methods that use large-scale internet video to overcome robotics' data bottleneck, taxonomizing approaches by which RL knowledge modality, policy, dynamics model, reward, or value function, benefits from video, and reviewing video foundation models, action-representation techniques, and datasets; concludes that scalable, video-pretrained policies and dynamics models offer the most promising path toward generalist robots.

## Problem

- Robot learning is data-starved: real-world data collection is costly and dangerous, and simulation suffers physics inaccuracies, so robot policies fail to generalize like internet-scale text and image models.
- Prior surveys cover imitation learning, offline reinforcement learning, or general video foundation models narrowly, without a taxonomy organized specifically around scaling internet video toward generalist robot capabilities.
- Existing methods that leverage internet video remain fragmented and nascent, lacking systematic treatment of missing action labels, missing low-level sensing information, and distribution shift between video and robot domains.

## Method

- Introduces a taxonomy of **Learning-from-Videos (LfV)** methods organized by which RL ==knowledge modality== (policy, dynamics model, reward function, or value function) is trained or improved using internet video data.
- Categorizes ==alternative-action representations==, single-step and multi-step ==latent actions==, ==observations-as-action==, ==language-as-action==, ==visual-motion==, and ==human-embodiment cues==, that relabel action-free video for downstream policy, dynamics, or value training.
- Compiles a ==video-foundation-model== taxonomy spanning encoders, video predictors, and video-to-text models, plus a dataset and benchmark review, proposing new LfV-specific benchmark metrics and evaluation categories.

## Results

- Largest internet-scraped video dataset (InternVid, **760,000** hours, **230 million** clips) dwarfs the largest robot dataset (Open X-Embodiment, roughly **5,600** hours estimated from about **2 million** trajectories) by **two orders of magnitude**.
- Surveyed video-pretrained dynamics models, including a video-game foundation model and large diffusion-based video predictors, show reliable performance scaling with compute and model size, and particular gains on long-horizon robot tasks.
- Video-pretrained policies improve task success rate, robot data-efficiency, and out-of-distribution generalization across cited studies, though reported gains shrink markedly when moving from toy datasets to diverse, non-expert internet human video.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Scale asymmetry between internet video and robot data is the core opportunity: video supplies breadth of tasks and dynamics, robot data supplies the precision video lacks.
> - No single alternative-action representation dominates: longer-horizon, high-level actions aid long-horizon planning, while short-horizon, low-level ones decode more reliably into robot actions, a trade-off worth matching to task.
> - Reported performance gains from video pretraining are far more modest on diverse, non-curated human video than on narrow or toy datasets, a caution against overgeneralizing early positive results.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This survey paper is situated at the nexus of several rapidly evolving fields: deep reinforcement learning, foundation models, large-scale data utilization, and generalist robotics. It directly addresses a critical challenge in modern robotics: the "data bottleneck." Unlike domains such as natural language processing (NLP) and computer vision, where massive and diverse internet-scale datasets have fueled the development of highly capable "foundation models" (e.g., Large Language Models like GPT-4, and image generation models like DALL-E), robot learning has historically been constrained by the prohibitive cost, time, and safety concerns associated with collecting real-world robot data.

The paper argues that the success observed in other deep learning domains, particularly the emergence of scaling laws where performance reliably improves with increased data and compute, can potentially be replicated in robotics. It positions "Learning from Videos (LfV)"—the augmentation of traditional robot data with large-scale internet video—as a promising solution to this data scarcity. Internet video, in contrast to text or static images, inherently provides rich information about physical dynamics, human behaviors, and diverse tasks in unstructured environments, making it highly relevant for general-purpose robots.

The survey integrates the concept of "foundation models" into robotics, proposing that pre-training large, general-purpose models on diverse internet video data could imbue robots with broader world knowledge and generalizable capabilities. This aligns with the broader AI trend towards generalist agents and models capable of performing a wide array of tasks with minimal task-specific fine-tuning. By systematically reviewing LfV, the paper establishes a foundational understanding of how this emerging field can bridge the gap between human-centric internet knowledge and robot-centric control, ultimately pushing towards the grand challenge of developing generalist robots capable of operating in unstructured real-world settings. It stands as a guiding document for researchers seeking to leverage the vast, relatively untapped resource of internet video for robotic advancement.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this survey is to **systematically examine the emerging field of Learning from Videos (LfV)** as a means to overcome the data bottleneck in robot learning and accelerate progress towards general-purpose robots.

The core motivations underpinning this objective are multifaceted:
- **Addressing Data Scarcity in Robotics:** Traditional robot learning paradigms are severely limited by the high cost, time, and practical difficulties of collecting sufficiently large and diverse robot-specific datasets. This scarcity hinders the development of generalist robots capable of performing a wide array of tasks in unstructured environments.
- **Emulating Successes in Other AI Domains:** The authors draw inspiration from the remarkable breakthroughs in NLP and computer vision (e.g., video generation, natural language processing) that have been driven by scaling deep learning models on massive and diverse internet data. They posit that similar scaling laws and the development of "foundation models" could revolutionize robotics.
- **Leveraging Rich Information Content of Internet Video:** Internet video uniquely offers foundational information regarding physical dynamics, human behaviors, and tasks that are highly informative for general-purpose robots. It covers a vast range of real-world scenarios relevant to human activities, making it a valuable, yet largely untapped, resource for robot learning.
- **Catalyzing Future Research:** By providing a comprehensive review of existing methods, outlining fundamental challenges, and discussing future opportunities, the survey aims to inform and inspire further research efforts in LfV. The authors specifically emphasize the need for scalable foundation model approaches that can fully exploit available internet video to enhance the learning of robot policies and dynamics models.
- **Driving Towards Generalist Robots:** The ultimate long-term goal is to facilitate the creation of "generalist robots" capable of performing broad physical tasks, adapting to unseen scenarios, and operating in unstructured settings, thereby enabling practical applications in various domains like households and factories. The survey serves as a roadmap for how LfV can contribute to this ambitious vision.

In essence, the paper is motivated by the conviction that by systematically harnessing the vast and rich information embedded in internet videos, robot learning can overcome its data limitations and unlock unprecedented generalization capabilities, mirroring the transformative impact of large-scale data in other AI disciplines.

### 3. Methodology and Approach (of the Survey)

The authors adopt a systematic and comprehensive survey methodology to dissect the emerging field of Learning from Videos (LfV) in robotics. Their approach is structured to first define the problem space, then review existing solutions, and finally propose future directions.

The survey's methodology can be broken down into the following key steps:

1. **Establishing Foundational Concepts and Context:**
 - **Formalism:** The paper begins by formalizing the reinforcement learning (RL) setting and defining the LfV paradigm, clarifying the roles of video datasets ($D_{video}$) and robot datasets ($D_{robot}$).
 - **Assumptions:** It outlines assumptions about internet video data (primarily human-centric, broad task coverage) and generalist robots (human-like embodiment, visual reliance).
 - **Benefits and Challenges:** A dedicated section (Section 3) identifies and elaborates on the potential benefits of LfV (generalization, emergent capabilities, improved data efficiency) and the fundamental challenges (missing action labels, distribution shift, missing low-level information, high dimensionality, stochasticity, partial observability, noise, redundancy). This initial framing provides the conceptual backbone for understanding the subsequent literature review.

2. **Categorizing and Reviewing Existing Methods (Primary Taxonomy):**
 - **Knowledge Extraction from Internet Video (Section 4.1):** The survey first addresses how to extract relevant knowledge from large-scale, heterogeneous internet videos. It categorizes and reviews various "Video Foundation Models (FMs)," including:
 - **Video Encoders:** For learning rich spatio-temporal representations.
 - **Video Prediction Models:** For capturing temporal dynamics and world physics.
 - **Video-to-Text Models:** For high-level semantic understanding and reasoning.
 - This section emphasizes how these generic video FMs and their underlying techniques can be leveraged (via pretraining, finetuning, or as inspiration for robotics-specific FMs) for LfV applications.
 - **Video-to-Robot Transfer (Section 4.2):** Recognizing the inherent challenges of transferring knowledge from internet video to robots, the survey dedicates a section to methods that explicitly address two major LfV challenges:
 - **Action Representations:** Techniques for recovering or defining "alternative action representations" ($\hat{a}$) from action-free video data (e.g., single/multi-step latent actions, observations-as-action, language-as-action, visual motion, human-embodiment information). This allows video data to be "pseudo-labeled" for RL.
 - **Representations to Address Distribution Shift:** Methods that learn representations invariant to differences between human video and robot domains (e.g., human-embodiment-aware approaches, learned invariant representations, transferable abstractions).
 - **Robot Learning and Reinforcement Learning from Video (Section 4.3):** This forms the core of the literature review, taxonomizing LfV methods based on which "Reinforcement Learning Knowledge Modality (KM)" most directly benefits from video data. The KMs analyzed include:
 - **Policies:** Methods that use video to directly or indirectly aid the learning of robot policies (e.g., representation transfer, multi-modal models, alternative-action policies/decoders).
 - **Dynamics Models:** Approaches that leverage video to learn predictive models of the robot's environment.
 - **Reward Functions:** Techniques for extracting reward signals from video data.
 - **Value Functions:** Methods for pretraining value functions using video.

3. **Analyzing Data and Benchmarking (Section 5):**
 - **Datasets:** The survey discusses the desiderata for LfV video datasets (scale, diversity, content relevance, language annotations), outlines techniques for curating them (scraping, recording, manual/automated captioning), and reviews existing datasets and their limitations.
 - **Benchmarks:** It provides recommendations for designing effective LfV benchmarks, proposing metrics (in-distribution performance, data-efficiency, generalization) and categories of benchmarks (fixed evaluation environments, fixed robot datasets, fixed video datasets), and reviews existing ones.

4. **Identifying Challenges and Opportunities (Section 6):**
 - **High-Level Recommendations:** Consolidates overarching advice for future LfV research (e.g., focusing on scalable approaches, targeting policies/dynamics models, improving datasets, combining with simulation, better evaluation).
 - **Specific Directions:** Details how to improve video FMs for LfV, leverage pretrained FMs, and overcome specific LfV challenges like bridging low-level information gaps and tackling distribution shifts.
 - **Philosophical Discussion:** Concludes with a critical examination of whether "scaling is enough" for generalist robots, acknowledging potential fundamental limitations of deep learning and suggesting the need for algorithmic advances beyond pure scaling.

By meticulously structuring its analysis from problem definition to forward-looking recommendations, the survey provides a clear and actionable framework for understanding and advancing LfV research.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The survey systematically analyzes the landscape of Learning from Videos (LfV) in robot learning, yielding several key findings and insights:

1. **The Data Bottleneck as the Central Challenge in Robotics:** The survey strongly establishes that data scarcity is the primary impediment to achieving generalist robots, preventing robot learning from replicating the scaling successes seen in other deep learning domains like NLP and computer vision.

2. **Internet Video as a Promising, Underutilized Resource:** It identifies internet video as a uniquely valuable, vast, and diverse data source for robot learning. Unlike text or images, video inherently captures physical dynamics, human behaviors, and tasks, offering foundational knowledge directly relevant to embodied agents.

3. **Fundamental LfV Challenges Identified:** The paper clearly delineates the core technical hurdles in leveraging internet video for robotics:
 - **Missing Action Labels:** Raw video lacks the precise robot action data needed for direct imitation learning.
 - **Distribution Shift:** Significant differences exist between human video data and robot domains (e.g., embodiments, viewpoints, tasks).
 - **Missing Low-level Information:** Video often lacks crucial proprioceptive, tactile, or force feedback vital for skillful robot control.
 - **High Dimensionality, Noise, and Stochasticity:** Video data is inherently complex and challenging to process effectively.

4. **Foundation Models are Key for Knowledge Extraction:** The survey highlights the critical role of **Video Foundation Models (FMs)** (video encoders, video prediction models, video-to-text models) and their underlying techniques (e.g., self-supervised learning, diffusion models, transformers) as the backbone for extracting scalable, general knowledge from heterogeneous internet video. Advances in these FMs are directly correlated with progress in scalable LfV.

5. **Strategies for Addressing Missing Action Labels:** A significant finding is the proliferation of **"alternative action representations" ($\hat{a}$)**. These methods aim to infer action-analogous information from video, allowing action-free video to be used for training. Categories include latent actions, observations-as-action, language-as-action, visual motion information, and human-embodiment information. The utility and scalability of these different representations are discussed as an area for future comparative research.

6. **Approaches to Mitigate Distribution Shift:** Various methods designed to learn representations invariant to cross-domain differences (e.g., human-embodiment-aware approaches, learned invariant representations, transferable abstractions) are reviewed. While promising in narrow settings, their scalability to diverse internet video remains an open question.

7. **Taxonomy of LfV Methods by RL Knowledge Modality:** The survey's main contribution is its comprehensive categorization of LfV literature based on which RL Knowledge Modality (KM) directly benefits:
 - **Policies (Section 4.3.1):** Methods like representation transfer, multi-modal joint training, and hierarchical policies using alternative-action decoders are employed. While showing potential for improved task success, data efficiency, and generalization, gains are often modest, especially with diverse human video.
 - **Dynamics Models (Section 4.3.2):** Pretraining video prediction models on internet video to serve as robot simulators or planners is highlighted as highly promising due to their ability to learn world physics and potentially from non-expert, off-policy data.
 - **Reward Functions (Section 4.3.3):** LfV can extract visual reward functions using video-language models, video predictors, or representational similarity to a reference. This is seen as more feasible due to its visual nature, but might not reduce robot data demands as much as direct policy or dynamics learning.
 - **Value Functions (Section 4.3.4):** While less explored, methods involving TD-learning or time-contrastive learning on video are noted, facing challenges of missing labels and multimodal behavior.

8. **Importance of Improved Datasets and Benchmarks:** The survey stresses that continued efforts in curating larger-scale, higher-quality, and better-annotated internet video datasets are crucial. It also calls for the establishment of standardized LfV benchmarks that quantitatively measure key benefits like generalization and data efficiency, which are currently difficult to compare across studies.

9. **Nuanced Perspective on Scaling:** While advocating for scaling deep learning to internet video, the survey acknowledges that "scaling may be insufficient" to solve all challenges of generalist robots. Issues like safety, reliability, long-horizon tasks, memory, latency, continual learning, and true reasoning might require fundamental algorithmic advances beyond merely more data and compute.

In summary, the survey provides a structured overview of LfV, identifying its potential, outlining its inherent difficulties, and systematically mapping out the current research landscape while critically assessing the strengths and limitations of existing approaches and offering clear directions for future work.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The survey "Towards Generalist Robot Learning from Internet Video: A Survey" carries significant implications for the future trajectory of robot learning and artificial intelligence, offering both a comprehensive review and a strategic roadmap.

**1. Catalyzing Research in Learning from Videos (LfV):**
The most immediate impact of this survey is its potential to standardize terminology, categorize diverse approaches, and highlight open problems within LfV. By systematically mapping the landscape, it provides a clear entry point for new researchers and a consolidated reference for experienced ones. This structured understanding can accelerate research by fostering collaboration and directing efforts towards the most promising avenues, reducing redundant work.

**2. Accelerating Progress Towards Generalist Robots:**
The survey directly addresses the "grand challenge" of developing general-purpose robots. By proposing LfV as a viable solution to the data bottleneck, it offers a concrete path towards building robots capable of performing diverse tasks in unstructured environments. The vision is to enable robots to acquire common-sense knowledge and physical understanding from human-centric internet videos, analogous to how large language models gain world knowledge from text. This could lead to a paradigm shift from task-specific robot training to more adaptable, broadly capable systems.

**3. Informing Data Curation and Benchmark Design:**
The paper's detailed discussion on desiderata for LfV datasets (scale, diversity, content relevance, language annotations) and methodologies for their curation (scraping, recording, automated captioning) will guide future data collection efforts. Its call for improved, standardized LfV benchmarks, designed to measure crucial metrics like generalization and data efficiency, is critical for fair comparison of algorithms and for objectively tracking progress in the field. This will ensure that research efforts are aligned with the ultimate goal of general-purpose robotics.

**4. Leveraging Foundation Models for Robotics:**
A key takeaway is the emphasis on adapting and customizing powerful video foundation models (pretrained on massive internet data) for robotic applications. This signifies a move towards leveraging the vast capabilities of models like video predictors and video-language models, potentially imbuing robots with sophisticated world models, action representations, and reward functions. This cross-pollination of techniques from general AI to robotics is highly impactful, promising to bootstrap robot capabilities beyond what is achievable with robot-only data.

**5. Guiding Algorithmic Development:**
By dissecting the fundamental challenges (missing action labels, distribution shift, missing low-level info), the survey implicitly guides algorithmic innovation. It highlights the need for robust methods to infer action information, learn transferable representations across embodiments, and manage the computational demands of high-dimensional video. The recommendation to focus on learning policies and dynamics models (as opposed to just reward functions) from video data offers a strategic direction for maximizing the benefits of LfV.

**6. Fostering a Nuanced Perspective on AI Scaling:**
Crucially, the survey injects a healthy dose of realism by questioning whether "scaling is enough" for generalist robots. This discussion acknowledges the unique challenges in robotics (safety, reliability, long-horizon tasks, latency, continual learning, true reasoning) that might not be solved by brute-force data scaling alone. This nuanced perspective encourages researchers to pursue complementary algorithmic advances and hybrid approaches that combine deep learning with structured methods or classical robotics, ensuring a more holistic research agenda.

In conclusion, this survey is not merely a summary of existing work; it is a forward-looking document that critically assesses the current state of LfV, identifies its greatest opportunities and challenges, and provides a clear strategic direction for the robotics community. Its impact will be seen in the accelerated development of more capable, generalist robots, driven by an intelligent and ethical leveraging of the internet's vast visual data reserves.
