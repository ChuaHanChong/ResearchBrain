# Real-World Robot Learning with Masked Visual Pre-training

> [!summary] Summary
> MVP scales self-supervised masked autoencoder pre-training to 4.5M in-the-wild Internet and egocentric images, training vision transformers up to 307M parameters. The encoder is frozen and a small MLP policy is learned on top via behavior cloning from 20-80 demonstrations. Across 981 real-robot trials on an xArm and an Allegro hand, it beats CLIP, supervised ImageNet, and from-scratch training by large margins.

## Problem

- End-to-end visuomotor learning is too sample inefficient for real-world robot training, needing far more interaction than a handful of human demonstrations can supply.
- Supervised and language-aligned pre-training for robotics demands expert labels or paired video-text annotation, restricting pre-training to curated data rather than uncurated Internet video.
- Prior self-supervised robot representations rely on in-domain interaction data, limiting transfer beyond the training environment, and earlier masked pre-training showed no gain from larger encoders.

## Method

- ==Masked autoencoder== pre-training on a combined 4.5M-image corpus of Ego4D, ImageNet, Epic Kitchens, Something-Something, and 100 Days of Hands, with 75% patch masking and heavy-encoder light-decoder asymmetry.
- Frozen ==vision transformer== encoders at three scales (22M, 86M, 307M parameters), with a dummy classification token as the transferred feature, preventing overfitting and cutting memory and training cost.
- Per-task four-layer ==SeLU MLP== controller taking projected image features plus joint-position proprioception, trained by ==behavior cloning== on VR-teleoperated demonstrations and outputting delta joint angles.

## Results

- Beats CLIP by up to **75%**, supervised ImageNet by up to **81%**, and from-scratch training by up to **81%** across reach, push, pick, fridge, fruit, and sink tasks over 981 real trials.
- On PickFruit, the 86M model reaches **93.8%** and the 307M model **100%**, versus **31.3%** for the video-text R3M ResNet-50 and **18.8%** for CLIP.
- Matches the strongest baseline using half the demonstrations; scaling 22M to 86M on fixed small data hurts, while scaling data and model together improves monotonically.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Model scaling only pays off when the pre-training corpus scales with it; a bigger encoder on a fixed small dataset can actively degrade downstream control performance.
> - Image-only masked reconstruction can outperform language- or text-paired supervision for manipulation, since it imposes no annotation requirement and absorbs uncurated data.
> - Freezing the encoder plus wrist-mounted egocentric views matters; fine-tuning weak encoders on few demonstrations does not close the gap, and third-person views suffer scale ambiguity.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Broader Research Landscape

The field of deep learning has achieved notable advancements in various domains, including computer vision, natural language processing, and audio generation, largely attributed to the development of powerful representation learning techniques with large neural networks. Transferring these successes to robotics, particularly for real-world applications, presents distinct challenges. Robotic tasks often require high sample efficiency due to the cost and time involved in real-world data collection, and robustness to diverse visual environments.

Existing approaches to robot learning include:
- **End-to-end control:** This involves directly mapping observations to actions without intermediate explicit perception or planning steps. While promising for simplicity, these methods typically suffer from high sample inefficiency, making them impractical for real-world training with limited data.
- **Supervised pre-training for robotics:** This category involves training visual representations on pretext tasks with strong supervision (e.g., semantic segmentation, object detection, or language descriptions) and then transferring these representations. While effective, these methods rely on expert labels or cross-domain supervision, which can be expensive or limited. For instance, CLIP has been used for language-conditioned policies, and other works explore time contrastive learning with human annotations.
- **Self-supervised learning (SSL) in robotics:** This aims to improve sample efficiency by learning representations without explicit human labels. Prior SSL methods have explored learning dynamic models, visual representations from interactions, vision-based policies on self-collected trajectories, visual autoencoders, spatio-temporal representations from videos, and visual correspondence. However, many of these approaches require in-domain data collection, which can limit their generalizability beyond the specific training environment or task.

This work positions itself within the context of recent developments that facilitate representation learning in robotics:
1. **Diverse, real-world data:** Utilizing large datasets from the internet and egocentric videos.
2. **Self-supervised objectives:** Employing methods that minimize reliance on strong human-designed priors or extensive data augmentations.
3. **Scalable and high-capacity models:** Leveraging transformer architectures.
4. **Frozen visual representations:** Training control policies on top of pre-trained and fixed visual encoders.

Building upon prior work that demonstrated the effectiveness of self-supervised visual pre-training for motor control in simulation, this paper extends the framework to extensive real-world robotic tasks, addressing the challenge of transferring simulation-learned representations to physical systems and demonstrating the benefits of increased data and model scale in a real-world context. It seeks to provide a more generalized and sample-efficient approach compared to prior methods.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to investigate and demonstrate the effectiveness of self-supervised visual pre-training, specifically using Masked Autoencoders (MAE), on images derived from diverse, in-the-wild videos for a range of real-world robotic tasks and embodiments.

The motivation behind this objective stems from several key challenges and opportunities in robot learning:

- **Bridging the gap between representation learning and robotics:** Deep learning has shown success in generating powerful representations in other domains, but its transfer to real-world robotics is often hindered by sample inefficiency and the domain gap between training data and operational environments. This work aims to leverage the power of self-supervised representation learning to create robust visual features that generalize across various robotic scenarios.
- **Addressing sample inefficiency:** Traditional end-to-end robot learning requires a substantial amount of interaction data, which is time-consuming and expensive to collect in the real world. By pre-training visual representations on readily available, large-scale, unlabeled image and video data, the hypothesis is that downstream robotic policies can be learned with significantly fewer task-specific demonstrations.
- **Overcoming limitations of existing pre-training methods:**
 - **Supervised pre-training** relies on human-annotated labels, which can be costly and may not cover the full range of visual phenomena relevant to robotic manipulation.
 - **Prior self-supervised methods** in robotics often require data collected specifically within the robot's interaction environment, limiting their generalizability to novel tasks or settings. This research seeks a more generalizable approach by using diverse, large-scale "in-the-wild" data.
- **Generalizability across tasks and embodiments:** A key motivation is to develop visual representations that are universal, meaning a single pre-trained encoder can be effectively used across different robotic tasks (e.g., reaching, pushing, picking, interacting with various objects) and even different robot morphologies (e.g., a robotic arm with a gripper and a multi-finger hand) without re-training the visual encoder.
- **Investigating the benefits of scale:** The authors are motivated to explore whether scaling up the size of the visual encoder model and the diversity/volume of the pre-training dataset leads to consistent performance improvements in real-world robotic tasks, similar to trends observed in other deep learning domains. This involves training larger Vision Transformers on a massive collection of images.
- **Building upon prior simulation work:** This research builds on previous findings that established the effectiveness of self-supervised visual pre-training for motor control in simulation. The motivation here is to validate these findings in the real world, which presents additional complexities such as real-world physics, lighting variations, and sensor noise.

In summary, the work is motivated by the desire to make real-world robot learning more efficient, generalizable, and scalable by leveraging advanced self-supervised visual pre-training techniques on diverse, large-scale datasets.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a framework that leverages masked visual pre-training for real-world robotic tasks, centered on the principle of learning visual representations from diverse "in-the-wild" data and then using these frozen representations for downstream control.

#### 3.1. Masked Visual Pre-training

- **Data Collection for Pre-training:** A large-scale, diverse dataset of 4.5 million images was compiled. This dataset, referred to as "Ego," combines frames from:
 - **Ego4D:** A massive egocentric video dataset capturing daily-life activities.
 - **ImageNet:** A large-scale image database.
 - **Hand-object Interaction (HoI) data:** This consists of images from Epic Kitchens (egocentric video), YouTube 100 Days of Hands, and Something-Something (crowd-sourced video) datasets.
 - Frames were extracted at specific rates (e.g., 0.2 fps from Ego4D, 1 fps from Epic Kitchens, 0.3 fps from Something-Something). The "Ego" dataset represents a 6.5x increase in data compared to prior work utilizing only HoI data.

- **Self-supervised Objective:** The core of the representation learning is **Masked Image Modeling** via **Masked Autoencoders (MAE)**.
 - MAE operates by masking out random patches in an input image (e.g., 75% of patches) and then training a Vision Transformer (ViT) to reconstruct the missing content.
 - Key design choices for MAE include a high masking ratio and an asymmetrical encoder-decoder architecture, with a heavy encoder and a light decoder. This objective is considered simple, efficient, and does not heavily rely on dataset- or task-specific augmentations.
 - MAE models were pre-trained for 400 epochs on the combined "Ego" dataset, and 1600 epochs on smaller datasets (HoI, ImageNet) for comparative studies.

- **Architecture (Encoders):** Standard **Vision Transformer (ViT)** models were used as image encoders. The study specifically scaled model sizes:
 - ViT-Small (22M parameters)
 - ViT-Base (86M parameters)
 - ViT-Large (307M parameters)
 The ViT-Large model has approximately 15 times more parameters than the commonly adopted ResNet-50 and is noted as the largest vision model deployed for robotics in this work.

#### 3.2. Real-World Robot Learning (Downstream Tasks)

- **Control Policy Learning:** Robot tasks are learned through **Behavior Cloning (BC)**.
 - **Demonstration Collection:** Trajectories of RGB images (from a wrist-mounted camera) and the robot's joint states were collected.
 - For most tasks, an HTC Vive VR system (motion-tracked) was used to control the robot's end-effector, with inverse kinematics mapping controller poses to joint angles.
 - For tasks difficult to demonstrate via motion controller (e.g., closing a fridge door), kinesthetic teaching was employed.
 - For the Allegro multi-finger hand case study, a Meta Quest 2 device with hand tracking was used to map human hand keypoints to Allegro hand joint angles.
 - Demonstrations were typically 20-80 per task, with trajectory pruning applied.
 - **Policy Architecture:** A four-layer Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) with SeLU activations was used as the controller. The input to the controller is a joint embedding space formed by linearly projecting the image features and proprioceptive states (joint positions).
 - **Input Modalities:** The policy takes in image features (derived from the pre-trained vision encoder) and the robot's proprioceptive states (joint positions). Joint velocities were not used due to hardware limitations. Depth information from the camera was not utilized.
 - **Output:** The controller predicts delta joint angles, performing joint position control.
 - **Frozen Encoder:** A critical aspect of the methodology is that the pre-trained image encoder is **frozen** throughout the policy learning process. This prevents overfitting of large models to limited demonstration data and reduces GPU memory footprint and training time.

- **Robotic Platform:**
 - **Main Platform:** UFACTORY xArm7 (a 7-DoF robotic arm) equipped with a 1-DoF parallel jaw gripper.
 - **Camera:** A first-person wrist-mounted RealSense camera captured RGB images.
 - **Control Frequency:** 5 Hz for both demonstration collection and control execution.
 - **Secondary Platform (Case Study):** An Allegro multi-finger hand was used to evaluate generality across different robot morphologies.

- **Robotic Tasks:** A diverse set of real-world tasks was designed and evaluated:
 - **Basic Motor Control:** ReachBlock (reaching a red block), PushCube (pushing a wooden cube), PickCube (picking a yellow cube).
 - **Visually Diverse Tasks:** CloseFridge (closing a toy fridge door, varying location and angle), PickFruit (picking 8 different fruits varying in color, shape, size), PickSink (picking a detergent bottle from a cluttered sink).
 - **Multi-finger Hand Tasks (Case Study):** Visual reaching (to seen and unseen objects with index finger tip), Visual flipping (of a rubber cube in the palm).

- **Evaluation Protocol:** To ensure fair and reliable comparisons, a systematic evaluation protocol was implemented.
 - Initial object and robot positions were randomized during data collection and training.
 - For evaluation, the robot's position was fixed, and the target object was positioned at 16 pre-defined and carefully measured locations.
 - Models were evaluated sequentially at each location to maintain consistent lighting and robot conditions. This procedure aimed to control confounding factors that could affect performance, such as robot up-time or ambient lighting.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study conducted extensive evaluations involving 981 real-world experiments across various visual backbones, robotic tasks, objects, and environments.

#### 4.1. Basic Motor Control Performance

For fundamental tasks like ReachBlock, PushCube, and PickCube in visually simple environments:
- **Comparison to Vision Encoders:** The proposed approach consistently outperformed several state-of-the-art vision backbones using the ViT-Base encoder. It achieved considerably higher success rates compared to CLIP (trained on 400M text-image pairs), supervised ImageNet pre-training, and training from scratch. CLIP generally performed best among the baselines.
- **Sample Complexity:** The approach demonstrated improved sample efficiency. It achieved performance comparable to the strongest baseline (CLIP trained with 80 demonstrations) using approximately half the number of demonstrations (40).

#### 4.2. Visually Diverse Scenes and Objects Performance

When evaluated on more complex tasks involving variations in scenes and objects:
- **CloseFridge:** For the task of closing a toy fridge door with randomized locations and door angles, the proposed method significantly outperformed all baselines.
- **PickFruit:** In the task of picking eight different fruits varying in color, shape, and size (10 demonstrations per fruit), the approach achieved nearly perfect scores, while baselines struggled.
- **PickSink:** For picking a detergent bottle from a cluttered sink, a challenging task due to highly variable scene configurations, the method considerably outperformed baseline approaches using the same ViT-B encoder.
The performance gap between the proposed method and baselines was larger in these visually complex settings compared to the basic motor control tasks, suggesting that the learned representations capture more precise spatial structures beneficial for realistic contexts.

#### 4.3. Scaling Model and Data Size

The study investigated the impact of scaling both the model capacity and the size of the pre-training dataset:
- **Model Scaling Alone:** Increasing the model size from ViT-S to ViT-B while keeping the pre-training data fixed (using only the HoI image collection) did not improve performance and, in some cases, led to a decrease. This observation was consistent with previous simulation results.
- **Co-scaling Model and Data:** When both the model size and the data size were scaled (from HoI to the larger "Ego" data collection), clear performance benefits were observed.
 - Scaling from ViT-S to ViT-B with the "Ego" dataset yielded better results than ViT-S alone.
 - Further increasing the model size from ViT-B (86M parameters) to ViT-L (307M parameters) with the "Ego" dataset resulted in additional performance improvements.
- **Task-Dependent Gains:** The gains from scaling were more pronounced for visually challenging tasks, such as PickSink.
This finding indicates that both larger models and sufficiently larger, diverse datasets are necessary to achieve performance improvements, establishing the ViT-L (307M parameter) model as the largest vision model deployed on real robot tasks in this work.

#### 4.4. Comparison to Concurrent Work

A comparison to the concurrent R3M work (which uses paired video-language annotations from Ego4D) on the PickFruit task demonstrated that the proposed image-only self-supervised models significantly outperformed R3M. For instance, the medium-sized ViT-B model achieved 93.8% success, compared to 31.3% for R3M ResNet-50. Even the smallest ViT-S model (68.8% success) surpassed R3M.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies

A series of ablation studies provided insights into design choices:
- **Cameras:** A wrist-mounted camera yielded significantly better results than a third-person table camera, primarily due to better fine-grained localization and reduced scale ambiguity.
- **Modality:** Both visual and proprioceptive inputs were found to be essential; removing either led to zero success, indicating neither is a fully-observed input for the tasks.
- **Augmentations:** Adding common data augmentations (color augmentation, shift augmentation) for policy training did not provide benefits, likely because the frozen encoder already reduces overfitting on limited data.
- **From-scratch Architectures:** Smaller ResNet-18 and ResNet-50 models performed worse than a ViT-B model trained from scratch, suggesting ViT's architectural advantages.
- **CLIP Pre-training Architectures:** CLIP ViT-B performed better than CLIP ResNet-50 for robotic tasks, aligning with their performance hierarchy in computer vision.
- **Finetuning:** Finetuning the encoder on downstream tasks provided only marginal performance improvements for smaller models (ViT-S HoI, CLIP ResNet-50) and did not close the gap with superior pre-trained representations. This reinforces the benefit of a frozen, high-quality pre-trained encoder.

#### 4.6. Case Study: Multi-finger Hand

The framework demonstrated generality by successfully applying to a multi-finger Allegro hand:
- **Visual Reaching:** The hand learned to reach the top of objects with an index finger, achieving approximately 50% success on both 8 seen objects and 45 unseen objects.
- **Visual Flipping:** A success rate of 50% was observed across 30 trials for flipping a rubber cube randomized in the palm.
- **Understanding Vision Through Action:** Qualitative analysis showed that policies trained on frozen visual representations learned to identify target objects based on specific visual cues (e.g., color, shape) and could differentiate them from distractors.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of robot learning by demonstrating the efficacy of self-supervised visual pre-training on diverse, real-world data for executing robotic tasks in physical environments. The findings have several implications and potential impacts:

- **Enhanced Sample Efficiency:** The ability to achieve competitive performance with significantly fewer task-specific demonstrations (half the number compared to strong baselines) is crucial for real-world robotics. This can substantially reduce the time, cost, and human effort required for deploying new robotic skills.
- **Improved Generalizability:** By leveraging massive, in-the-wild visual data, the learned representations appear to capture fundamental visual properties that generalize across various robotic tasks, object types, and environmental contexts. This addresses a key limitation of prior methods that often required in-domain data or explicit supervision for each new task. The successful application to a multi-finger hand further highlights the morphological generality of the approach.
- **Scalability of Vision Models in Robotics:** The work provides evidence that scaling up both the model capacity (using Vision Transformers up to 307M parameters) and the diversity/volume of the pre-training data (4.5 million images) leads to consistent performance gains in real-world robot learning. This suggests a pathway for developing more powerful and robust robotic perception systems by leveraging large-scale foundation models.
- **Reduced Overfitting and Computational Burden:** The practice of freezing the pre-trained visual encoder during policy learning is significant. It mitigates overfitting to limited demonstration data, reduces GPU memory requirements, and accelerates training times for downstream tasks.
- **Foundation for Future Robotic Systems:** The development of a single, robust visual encoder capable of serving various tasks and embodiments paves the way for "foundation models" in robotics, where a universally applicable perception module could streamline the development and deployment of complex robotic behaviors.
- **Broader Accessibility of Robot Learning:** By utilizing self-supervised methods on readily available, unlabeled data (e.g., internet videos), the approach potentially democratizes access to advanced robot learning techniques, moving away from reliance on expensive, domain-specific data collection or expert annotation.
- **Insights into Robotic Perception:** The qualitative studies on what the vision system "sees" (e.g., recognizing objects by color and shape) provide insights into the underlying mechanisms of these visual representations, aiding in the development of more interpretable and controllable robotic intelligence.

While the study acknowledges limitations such as the use of toy objects in relatively clean lab environments, the demonstrated capabilities mark a step toward deploying more capable and adaptable robotic systems in increasingly complex and realistic settings.
