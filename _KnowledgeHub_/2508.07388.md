# Invert4TVG: A Temporal Video Grounding Framework with Inversion Tasks Preserving Action Understanding Ability

> [!summary] Summary
> The INVERT4TVG framework improves Temporal Video Grounding (TVG) performance by integrating novel inversion-based tasks designed to preserve action understanding within large vision-language models. This method achieved up to a 7.1% absolute improvement in R1@0.7 on Charades-STA and demonstrated superior zero-shot generalization on ActivityNet and QvHighlight, addressing the common degradation of semantic comprehension in IoU-optimized models.

## Problem

- Current Temporal Video Grounding (TVG) models, particularly those optimized for high Intersection-over-Union (IoU), often exhibit a degradation in their ability to semantically understand actions described in queries and videos.
- This degradation leads to 'wrong grounding' where models localize segments based on superficial cues rather than a comprehensive grasp of the action, exemplified by failures to distinguish similar yet semantically distinct actions.
- General action understanding tasks are not specifically tailored to the precise temporal localization demands of TVG, making their direct integration ineffective for solving this issue without compromising localization accuracy.

## Method

- INVERT4TVG employs a reinforcement learning framework to fine-tune Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) using an alternating optimization strategy.
- It introduces three 'Invert-**TVG**' tasks (Verb Completion, Action Recognition, Video Description) that reverse the standard TVG problem, mapping given video segments to query-related content, thereby focusing on semantic action understanding.
- A probabilistic sampling strategy dynamically chooses between the primary TVG task (80% probability) and an Invert-TVG task (20% probability), using simple binary rewards for stability and computational efficiency.

## Results

- Achieved up to a **7.1%** absolute improvement in R1@**0.7** on the Charades-STA dataset for the **3B** model (from 36.9 to 44.0) compared to the Time-R1 baseline.
- Demonstrated superior zero-shot performance across R1@**0.3**, R1@**0.5**, and R1@**0.7** metrics on the ActivityNet and QvHighlight datasets, indicating enhanced generalization in complex and diverse scenarios.
- Qualitative analysis confirmed improved ability to distinguish semantically similar actions, localize complex/causal actions, and handle multi-person or non-human-centric events more accurately.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly training models with 'inversion-based TVG' tasks, which infer query content from localized segments, effectively preserves and enhances semantic action understanding without increasing total training time or VRAM.
> - Combining fine-grained (Verb Completion), middle-grained (Action Recognition), and coarse-grained (Video Description) inversion tasks provides synergistic benefits, leading to optimal overall TVG performance.
> - A specific balance between primary TVG optimization and auxiliary Invert-TVG tasks (e.g., 80% TVG vs. 20% Invert-TVG) is crucial for maximizing performance, as excessive emphasis on auxiliary tasks can hinder the main objective.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Temporal Video Grounding (TVG) is a task within long-form video understanding that involves localizing a specific video segment corresponding to a given natural language query. This capability is foundational for various applications, including video-text retrieval and UAV positioning.

Existing TVG methodologies can be categorized into three main paradigms:
1. **Traditional Methods:** These approaches typically rely on hand-crafted features, sliding window techniques, and more recently, DETR-like networks to predict temporal boundaries. Their performance is often constrained by the quality and expressiveness of the extracted features.
2. **Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) with Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** Recent advancements have seen LVLMs like NumPro, TimeSuite, and VideoChat-Flash being employed. These models leverage extensive pretraining and are then fine-tuned to regress segment durations. While demonstrating strong generalization, they can still exhibit limitations in achieving precise boundary detection, particularly on challenging benchmarks.
3. **Reinforcement Learning (RL)-Finestuned LVLMs:** Approaches such as Time-R1 and VideoChat-R1 have integrated Reinforcement Learning, often utilizing rewards based on Intersection-over-Union (IoU) for temporal alignment and format rewards for structured reasoning. These methods have pushed the state-of-the-art in TVG performance by explicitly optimizing for localization metrics.

This work identifies a specific limitation within the current landscape, particularly among IoU-optimized RL-finetuned LVLMs: a degradation in their ability to understand the semantic actions described in both the video and the query. The paper posits that solely optimizing for high temporal IoU can inadvertently reduce the model's semantic action understanding capability, leading to inaccurate groundings despite high IoU scores. For example, models might localize a segment containing a "button" but fail to distinguish between "buttoning" and "unbuttoning" actions. The authors provide statistical evidence that models like Time-R1, while improving TVG metrics, degrade on auxiliary action understanding tasks.

INVERT4TVG addresses this identified gap by introducing a novel framework that integrates "inversion-based TVG" tasks as auxiliary objectives. These tasks are specifically designed to preserve and enhance the model's action understanding capability by reusing the original TVG annotations in a self-supervised manner. By tightly coupling these action understanding tasks with the primary temporal localization objective, the proposed method aims to achieve superior TVG performance grounded in deeper semantic comprehension, thereby advancing the field beyond purely temporal alignment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the accuracy and robustness of Temporal Video Grounding (TVG) by improving the model's semantic understanding of actions described in video segments and corresponding natural language queries.

The motivation for this work stems from a critical observation regarding current state-of-the-art TVG methods:
- **Degradation of Action Understanding:** Despite significant progress and high Intersection-over-Union (IoU) scores, existing TVG models frequently exhibit a diminished capacity for accurately recognizing and interpreting the underlying actions. This phenomenon is particularly prevalent in models optimized primarily for temporal IoU, where the focus on precise boundary alignment may overshadow the importance of semantic fidelity.
- **Suboptimal Grounding Results:** This degradation in action understanding leads to instances of "wrong grounding," where models might localize video segments based on superficial visual cues rather than a comprehensive grasp of the described action. An illustrative example provided is the inability of models like VideoChat-R1 and Time-R1 to differentiate between "buttoning" and "unbuttoning" actions, indicating a failure to distinguish similar yet semantically distinct events.
- **Statistical Evidence:** The authors present empirical data showing that while models like Time-R1 improve TVG metrics (e.g., R1@0.3, R1@0.5, R1@0.7), they simultaneously demonstrate a measurable decline in performance on explicit action understanding tasks (Verb Completion, Action Recognition, Video Description). This confirms the hypothesis that IoU-focused optimization can compromise semantic understanding, thereby hindering overall TVG accuracy.
- **Inadequacy of General Action Understanding Tasks:** A straightforward solution of incorporating general action recognition or detection tasks alongside TVG is considered, but dismissed. The reasoning is that such general tasks are not specifically tailored to the precise temporal localization demands of TVG, and the learned understanding might not align effectively with the core grounding objective.

Therefore, the key motivation is to devise a method that can *jointly optimize* for both precise temporal localization and robust action understanding. The proposed solution is to introduce a set of specially designed, task-specific "inversion-based TVG" tasks that directly leverage the existing TVG dataset. These inversion tasks convert the localization problem into an understanding problem by inferring query-related information from given video segments, thus ensuring that the learned semantic understanding is directly relevant and supportive of the temporal grounding objective.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The INVERT4TVG framework proposes a reinforcement learning (RL) approach to fine-tune Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) for Temporal Video Grounding (TVG) by integrating both the primary TVG task and novel "Invert-TVG" tasks. The objective is to mitigate the degradation of action understanding often observed in IoU-optimized TVG models.

#### 3.1. Base Model and RL Algorithm

The framework utilizes the Qwen2.5-VL model series as its backbone LVLM. The reinforcement learning is based on the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) algorithm, adapted from DeepSeek-R1. GRPO optimizes a policy by maximizing the sum of generation probabilities weighted by normalized rewards, incorporating a KL divergence penalty to maintain proximity to a reference policy.

#### 3.2. Invert-TVG Tasks

The core innovation lies in the introduction of three Invert-TVG tasks. Unlike the standard TVG task (which maps `(Video, Query) -> Temporal Segment`), Invert-TVG tasks reverse this by mapping `(Video Segment, Temporal Segment) -> Query-related Content`, thereby focusing on semantic action understanding. These tasks reuse existing TVG annotations. For all Invert-TVG tasks, the SpaCy NLP library (`en_core_web_sm-3.8.0`) is used to convert verbs to their root forms for comparison, treating different tenses as equivalent. Each Invert-TVG task employs a binary (0 or 1) reward function for simplicity and stability.

1. **Verb Completion (VC - Fine Granularity):**
 - **Description:** The model is given a ground-truth video segment and a modified query where a verb (action) has been masked. The task is to predict the missing verb.
 - **Example Prompt:** "Add a verb for the event 'Person [ ] the door' based on the video."
 - **Reward Function (`r_VC`):** 1 if the root form of the predicted verb matches the root form of the ground-truth verb, 0 otherwise.

2. **Action Recognition (AR - Middle Granularity):**
 - **Description:** The model is provided with a ground-truth video segment and asked to describe the event using a single verb.
 - **Example Prompt:** "Use a verb to describe the event based on the video."
 - **Reward Function (`r_AR`):** 1 if the root form of the predicted verb is present within the set of root-formed verbs extracted from the ground-truth query, 0 otherwise.

3. **Video Description (VD - Coarse Granularity):**
 - **Description:** Given a ground-truth video segment, the model is tasked with generating a comprehensive description of the actions performed.
 - **Example Prompt:** "Describe what people have done based on the video."
 - **Reward Function (`r_VD`):** 1 if the root form of the ground-truth verb (from the original query) is present within the set of root-formed verbs generated in the model's description, 0 otherwise.

#### 3.3. TVG and Format Reward Functions

For the primary TVG task, two reward functions are utilized:

1. **IoU Reward (`r_IoU`):** Standard Intersection over Union between the predicted and ground-truth temporal segments. This measures the accuracy of temporal localization.
2. **Format Reward (`r_format`):** A binary reward (0 or 1) encouraging the model to generate its predictions in a structured format, typically involving intermediate reasoning steps (e.g., `<think>***</think> <answer> ts to te </answer>`). This promotes a clear thinking process.

#### 3.4. INVERT4TVG Reinforcement Learning Framework

To overcome challenges associated with simultaneously optimizing multiple conflicting objectives (memory inefficiency, optimization conflict, instability, task bias), the framework employs an alternating optimization strategy with probabilistic sampling:

- **Reward Formulation:**
 - The reward for the TVG task is `r_TVG(o) = r_format(o) + r_IoU(o)`.
 - The reward for an Invert-TVG task is `r_Invert-TVG(o) = r_format(o) + r_inv(o)`, where `r_inv` can be `r_VC`, `r_AR`, or `r_VD`.
 - The overall reward `r(o) = α * r_TVG(o) + β * r_Invert-TVG(o)`, where `α` and `β` are binary coefficients (`α + β = 1`).

- **Probabilistic Sampling:** In each training iteration, the model dynamically chooses between executing the TVG task and an Invert-TVG task:
 - With a high probability `p` (empirically set to 0.8), the TVG task is selected (`α=1, β=0`).
 - With a low probability `1-p` (0.2), an Invert-TVG task is selected (`α=0, β=1`). When an Invert-TVG task is chosen, one of VC, AR, or VD is uniformly sampled.
 This asymmetric distribution ensures the model primarily focuses on temporal grounding while regularly reinforcing its action understanding capabilities.

- **Optimization:** The model parameters are updated via gradient ascent to maximize the reward corresponding to the sampled task.

#### 3.5. Implementation Details

- **Datasets:** Charades-STA, ActivityNet, and QvHighlight. Notably, the method is not pre-trained on additional large TVG datasets (e.g., YT-Temporal, HowTo100M) unlike some baselines.
- **Video Processing:** Frames are sampled at 2 FPS and resized to achieve approximately 2.8 million pixels per video (e.g., 100 frames of 96x96x3 for a 50-second video).
- **Optimizer:** AdamW with `β1=0.9, β2=0.999, ϵ=1e-8`, zero weight decay, and a learning rate of `5e-5`.
- **Training Environment:** 2 epochs of training, batch size 4, on a cluster with eight NVIDIA A100 GPUs (40GB each). Total training time is approximately 80 hours.
- **Computational Efficiency:** The probabilistic sampling strategy ensures that the Invert-TVG tasks do not increase the total training time or VRAM overhead, instead repurposing time that would otherwise be spent solely on the TVG task.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The INVERT4TVG framework was evaluated on three temporal video grounding datasets: Charades-STA, ActivityNet, and QvHighlight, primarily using R1@m (Recall@1 with IoU threshold m, where m ∈ {0.3, 0.5, 0.7}) and mean IoU (mIoU) metrics.

#### 4.1. Comparison with State-of-the-Art Approaches

- **On Charades-STA (Fine-tuning):**
 - **Invert4TVG (3B model)** consistently outperformed the 3B version of Time-R1 across all R1@m metrics. Specifically, it achieved R1@0.3 of 80.8 (vs. 78.7), R1@0.5 of 69.0 (vs. 64.1), and R1@0.7 of 44.0 (vs. 36.9). This represents a 7.1% absolute improvement in R1@0.7 for the 3B model.
 - **Invert4TVG (7B model)** also showed marginal improvements over the 7B version of Time-R1, with R1@0.3 of 83.0 (vs. 82.8), R1@0.5 of 72.5 (vs. 72.2), and R1@0.7 of 51.4 (vs. 50.1).
 - The framework surpassed various other VLP-based (e.g., 2D-TAN, Moment-DETR) and SFT-based (e.g., VideoChat-Flash, TimeSuite) models, demonstrating its effectiveness under comparable conditions, especially considering it was not pre-trained on additional TVG datasets like some baselines.

- **On ActivityNet and QvHighlight (Zero-shot settings):**
 - In zero-shot evaluations, both 3B and 7B variants of Invert4TVG consistently outperformed Time-R1 across R1@0.3, R1@0.5, and R1@0.7 on both ActivityNet and QvHighlight.
 - The performance gains were particularly evident on QvHighlight, a dataset known for its diverse and complex scene-action correlations, suggesting that Invert4TVG's enhanced action understanding translates to improved generalization in more intricate scenarios.

#### 4.2. Ablation Study

An ablation study on the Invert4TVG-3B model was conducted to analyze the contributions of different design choices:

- **Impact of Invert-TVG Tasks Combinations:**
 - Using any single Invert-TVG task (Only-VD, Only-AR, Only-VC) improved performance over the baseline `Only-TVG` (Time-R1, R1@0.7: 36.9). For instance, `Only-AR` achieved the highest R1@0.7 among single tasks at 43.8.
 - Combinations of two tasks (e.g., `VC+AR`, `VC+VD`) generally yielded further improvements, highlighting complementary benefits.
 - **Invert4TVG (integrating all three auxiliary tasks)** achieved the best overall results (R1@0.7: 44.0), demonstrating that the synergistic effect of fine-grained (VC), middle-grained (AR), and coarse-grained (VD) action understanding tasks is maximized when optimized together.

- **Optimal Probability for Invert-TVG Tasks:**
 - Varying the probability `(1-p)` of executing an Invert-TVG task revealed that **20% (p=0.8 for TVG)** yielded the optimal performance (highest R1@0.7 of 44.0).
 - Increasing the Invert-TVG task probability beyond 20% led to a decline in TVG performance, eventually falling below the pure TVG baseline, indicating that excessive emphasis on auxiliary tasks can detract from the primary objective. At 100% Invert-TVG probability, the model performed the worst for TVG.

- **Binary vs. Cosine Similarity Rewards:**
 - The study showed that employing a **simple binary (0 or 1) reward** for Invert-TVG tasks consistently outperformed rewards based on cosine similarity.
 - This advantage is attributed to the better controllability and stability offered by binary rewards, whereas cosine similarity was found to introduce higher variance and optimization instability due to weak semantic links between unrelated words.

- **Impact of Invert-SFT (Supervised Fine-Tuning):**
 - An initial supervised fine-tuning phase using ground-truth video clips to generate descriptions (Invert-SFT) helped stabilize the model's outputs for subsequent Invert tasks.
 - While it might cause a slight initial decrease in mIoU, both models (with and without Invert-SFT) converged to the same mIoU (57.9) after sufficient training, suggesting its role is primarily for pre-stabilization rather than ultimate performance enhancement.

#### 4.3. Qualitative Results

- **Success Cases:** Invert4TVG demonstrated enhanced action understanding in various complex scenarios:
 - **Similar Actions:** Accurately distinguished semantically similar actions (e.g., opening vs. closing a door, picking up vs. putting down).
 - **Complex Actions:** Better identified the completion state of actions, leading to more precise localization of events at their culmination.
 - **Multi-person Situations:** Exhibited robustness to distractions from secondary individuals, accurately focusing on the target person's action.
 - **Non-human Objects:** Successfully handled scenarios involving objects rather than people (e.g., throwing an eggplant into a bonfire), localizing longer, more relevant segments.
 - **Multiple/Causal Actions:** Effectively disambiguated and localized simultaneous, sequential ("and then"), and causally related actions (e.g., sneezing then taking medicine).

- **Failure Case:** The model showed limitations in understanding nuanced temporal qualifiers like "again," often failing to distinguish between multiple occurrences of the same action. This indicates an area for further improvement in deep semantic understanding of query qualifiers.

#### 4.4. Computational Efficiency

The probabilistic alternating optimization strategy proved efficient. The Invert-TVG tasks, while contributing significantly to performance, did not increase the total training time (80 hours for both TVG-only and Invert4TVG), nor did they incur additional VRAM costs compared to the baseline LVLM. Invert-TVG tasks collectively consumed 18.75% of the total training time, repurposed from the TVG task's allocation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents INVERT4TVG, a Temporal Video Grounding (TVG) framework that effectively addresses a critical limitation in current TVG models: the degradation of action understanding ability when primarily optimizing for temporal Intersection-over-Union (IoU). The contributions and potential impact are multifaceted:

- **Addressing a Core Limitation:** The paper directly tackles the observed trade-off between precise temporal localization and comprehensive semantic action understanding. By demonstrating that IoU-centric optimization can inadvertently reduce a model's grasp of actions, the work highlights a fundamental challenge in multimodal video understanding and provides a mechanism to overcome it.
- **Novel Methodology for Semantic Enhancement:** The introduction of "Invert-TVG tasks" (Verb Completion, Action Recognition, Video Description) represents a novel approach to imbue LVLMs with stronger action understanding capabilities tailored specifically for the TVG context. These self-supervised tasks are ingeniously derived from existing TVG annotations, making them data-efficient and highly relevant to the main objective, distinguishing them from generic action understanding tasks.
- **Significant Performance Improvements:** INVERT4TVG demonstrates substantial performance gains over state-of-the-art methods on multiple benchmarks. A 7.1% improvement in R1@0.7 on Charades-STA for a 3B model is a notable quantitative advancement. Furthermore, the superior zero-shot performance on challenging datasets like QvHighlight underscores the framework's enhanced generalization and ability to handle complex, diverse actions.
- **Improved Robustness and Interpretability:** The qualitative analysis reveals that the enhanced action understanding translates into more robust grounding across various challenging scenarios, including distinguishing similar actions, localizing complex or causal events, handling multi-person interactions, and processing non-human-centric events. The model's ability to "understand actions better" suggests a move towards more semantically grounded and potentially more interpretable video grounding.
- **Computational Efficiency:** The proposed probabilistic alternating optimization strategy is shown to be efficient in terms of both training time and memory footprint. By dynamically reallocating training time between the primary TVG task and auxiliary Invert-TVG tasks, the framework avoids increasing overall training costs, making it a practical solution for large-scale LVLMs.
- **Broader Impact on Video-Language Research:** By effectively bridging the gap between TVG and LVLMs, INVERT4TVG provides a stronger foundation for higher-level video understanding tasks. The principles of incorporating task-specific inversion objectives for semantic alignment could be extended to other multimodal tasks requiring fine-grained understanding alongside localization or generation, potentially influencing future research directions in video-language modeling, event detection, and video summarization.
- **Responsible AI Considerations:** The paper acknowledges the importance of responsible use and calls for ongoing efforts to mitigate potential biases in open-source datasets, aligning with ethical considerations in AI research.

In summary, INVERT4TVG offers a scientifically rigorous and empirically validated framework that advances the state of Temporal Video Grounding by strategically integrating auxiliary tasks to preserve and enhance action understanding, thereby leading to more accurate, robust, and semantically aligned localization results.
