# GTA1: GUI Test-time Scaling Agent

> [!summary] Summary
> Salesforce AI Research, in collaboration with The Australian National University and the University of Hong Kong, developed GTA1, a two-stage GUI agent that enhances robust planning through a test-time scaling strategy and achieves accurate grounding via a simplified RL-based model. This agent establishes new state-of-the-art performances on benchmarks such as ScreenSpot-Pro with 63.6% grounding accuracy and OSWorld-Verified with a 63.4% task success rate.

## Problem

- Existing GUI agents struggle with robust planning in expansive, dynamic action spaces, making them susceptible to cascading failures without computationally intensive lookahead.
- Prior grounding models, often relying on supervised fine-tuning (SFT) for exact coordinate prediction, are misaligned with the actual task of interacting with any valid point within a UI element, leading to reduced robustness.
- Recent RL-based grounding methods frequently incorporate unnecessary complexities like explicit "thinking" or auxiliary bounding box prediction rewards, potentially hindering efficiency.

## Method

- Introduces a test-time scaling strategy for planning where the agent samples K ==candidate action proposals==, and a multimodal LLM acts as a judge to select the most appropriate one, mitigating cascading failures.
- Develops a simplified Reinforcement Learning (RL) based grounding model that directly predicts pixel coordinates and uses a binary "click reward" for any prediction falling within the target element's bounding box.
- Applies a lightweight data cleaning strategy to grounding datasets to ensure that training rewards are based on visually accurate and detectable UI elements.

## Results

- Achieved new state-of-the-art grounding accuracy on ScreenSpot-Pro (**63.6%** with GTA1-**32B**), ScreenSpot-V2 (**95.2%**), and OSWorld-G (**72.2%**), outperforming much larger models and previous state-of-the-art approaches.
- Demonstrated superior agent task execution on OSWorld (**45.2%** with GTA1-**7B**) and OSWorld-Verified (**63.4%** with GTA1-**32B** and GPT-5 as planner/judge), surpassing prior methods and even some native end-to-end agents.
- The test-time scaling strategy, when increasing sampled proposals from K=1 to K=32, substantially improved success rates on OSWorld, allowing agents with shorter execution horizons to outperform baselines with longer horizons.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A simple binary "click reward" objective in RL-based grounding is highly effective and often outperforms more complex objectives like IoU or format rewards for static GUI tasks.
> - Explicit "thinking" or Chain-of-Thought reasoning is generally unnecessary for static GUI grounding and can even degrade performance, though it may offer benefits in highly dynamic, context-rich environments.
> - The test-time scaling strategy is highly generalizable and effective across various base agents and execution horizons, significantly boosting success rates and mitigating cascading failures without full sequence lookahead.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper "GTA1: GUI Test-time Scaling Agent" is positioned at the forefront of research into Graphical User Interface (GUI) agents, a field dedicated to enabling AI to autonomously interact with and complete tasks across diverse digital platforms in a manner analogous to human users. This domain is critical for realizing more general artificial intelligence and automating complex digital workflows.

The broader landscape of GUI agents is characterized by two fundamental challenges that GTA1 directly addresses:
1. **Planning:** Generating a coherent and effective sequence of actions to achieve a user-specified goal within an expansive and dynamic action space. Traditional planners often struggle with the sheer number of valid action sequences and the inability to "look ahead" due to the irreversible nature of many GUI interactions, making them vulnerable to cascading failures.
2. **Grounding:** Accurately identifying and interacting with specific visual elements (e.g., buttons, text fields) in complex, often high-resolution, and visually cluttered user interfaces. Prior grounding models, predominantly relying on supervised fine-tuning (SFT), are trained to predict the exact center of a target element. However, this approach is fundamentally misaligned with the task, as any point within an interactive element constitutes a valid click, leading to reduced robustness and generalization, especially in real-world professional interfaces.

Existing GUI agent architectures generally fall into two categories:
- **Native GUI Agents (End-to-End):** These models attempt to handle both planning and grounding within a single, unified framework (e.g., CUA). While powerful, they face challenges in long-context learning and often require extensive training data.
- **Two-Stage GUI Agents (Modular):** These decouple the problem into a planner (often a large language model like GPT-4o or Claude) responsible for generating action proposals, and a separate grounding model that precisely locates the target UI elements for interaction (e.g., Aria-UI). GTA1 adopts this two-stage approach.

Recent advancements in grounding have explored Reinforcement Learning (RL), often incorporating "Chain-of-Thought" (CoT) reasoning or auxiliary bounding box prediction rewards, inspired by models like DeepSeek-R1-Zero. GTA1 critically evaluates and simplifies these RL approaches, demonstrating that explicit reasoning or complex auxiliary rewards are often unnecessary for effective grounding in static environments.

By introducing a "test-time scaling" strategy for robust planning and a simplified, highly effective RL-based grounding model, GTA1 directly tackles the limitations of current methods. It offers a robust alternative to monolithic native agents by proving that a well-designed two-stage agent can achieve state-of-the-art performance in dynamic environments. This work not only pushes the technical boundaries of GUI agents but also offers valuable insights into the efficiency and effectiveness of different training paradigms, challenging prevailing assumptions about the necessity of complex reasoning prompts in certain contexts.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of the "GTA1: GUI Test-time Scaling Agent" paper is to develop a highly performant and robust GUI agent capable of autonomously completing diverse tasks across various digital platforms. To achieve this, the authors focus on addressing two critical and interconnected challenges that significantly hinder the reliability and efficacy of current GUI agents:

1. **Improving Planning Robustness in Expansive Action Spaces:** The digital environment presents an enormous action space, where numerous valid sequences of actions can lead to the successful completion of a task. Current planning approaches, particularly those driven by large language models, often commit to a single action proposal at each step. This makes the agent highly susceptible to "cascading failures," where an error in an early planning or grounding step can derail the entire task. Furthermore, unlike some symbolic domains, real-world GUI environments often lack a "lookahead" capability; actions can have irreversible state effects, making it impractical to roll out full action sequences in advance to evaluate plans. The key motivation here is to devise a planning strategy that can navigate this complexity more robustly, explore short-term alternatives, and recover from potential missteps without requiring computationally prohibitive full sequence lookahead.

2. **Enhancing Grounding Accuracy in Complex and High-Resolution Interfaces:** Precisely interacting with visual elements in modern GUI environments, which can feature resolutions up to 4K and intricate, hierarchical layouts, remains a significant challenge. Existing grounding models often rely on supervised fine-tuning (SFT) to predict the exact center of a target UI element. The authors argue that this introduces a fundamental misalignment with the actual task: any coordinate within the target element constitutes a successful interaction, yet SFT disproportionately penalizes deviations from the precise center. This limits the model's flexibility, reduces its robustness, and prevents it from leveraging appropriate supervision signals. While newer RL-based grounding methods, inspired by models like DeepSeek-R1-Zero, have emerged, they often incorporate explicit "thinking" (Chain-of-Thought reasoning) or auxiliary rewards for predicting bounding boxes. The authors are motivated to investigate whether these additional complexities are truly necessary or if a simpler, more direct RL approach can achieve superior performance by aligning the training objective more closely with the actual interaction goal.

In essence, the paper is driven by the need to create more intelligent and reliable GUI agents by innovating on both the strategic (planning) and tactical (grounding) levels, seeking to overcome the inherent limitations of existing methodologies through novel and efficient solutions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

GTA1 employs a two-stage GUI agent framework, modularizing the complex problem into distinct planning and grounding components. The core of its methodology lies in two complementary strategies: a test-time scaling approach for robust planning and a simplified, yet highly effective, RL-based model for accurate grounding.

#### 3.1. Test-time Scaling for Planning

To address the challenge of planning under expansive action spaces and the lack of lookahead, GTA1 introduces a novel test-time scaling strategy:
- **Action Proposal Sampling:** At each decision step, given the user instruction, the historical trajectory, and the current UI screenshot, the planner (e.g., o3 or GPT-5) does not commit to a single action. Instead, it samples *K* multiple candidate action proposals. These proposals encapsulate potential actions such as clicking a specific element or performing a keystroke.
- **Judge Model Selection:** A multimodal large language model, which can be the same model as the planner, acts as a "judge." This judge evaluates all *K* candidate proposals based on their alignment with the user's intent and the current GUI state. It then selects the single most contextually appropriate and effective action proposal. This process allows the agent to explore short-term alternatives without the prohibitive cost of rolling out entire action sequences, mitigating the risk of cascading failures.
- **Execution:** If the selected action proposal is coordinate-based (e.g., a click), it is then passed to the specialized grounding model for precise localization. Non-coordinate actions (e.g., typing, hotkeys) are executed directly.
This iterative process, involving sampling, judging, and then executing (or grounding and executing), continues until the task is complete or a termination condition is met.

#### 3.2. Grounding Model Training

GTA1's grounding model focuses on directly predicting interaction coordinates using a simplified Reinforcement Learning (RL) approach, challenging the necessity of complex reasoning in grounding.
- **Data Cleaning:** The quality of training data is paramount. The authors utilize open-source datasets (e.g., Aria-UI) but acknowledge that annotated bounding boxes can sometimes be misaligned with the actual visual rendering due to dynamic UI elements or timing issues. To rectify this, a lightweight cleaning strategy is applied:
 - For each UI screenshot, OmniParser (denoted as M(·)) is used to detect all visible UI elements and their bounding boxes {𝑏𝑖}.
 - A data sample with an annotated bounding box 𝑏_ann is discarded if its maximum Intersection over Union (IoU) with any detected bounding box 𝑏𝑖 is below a predefined threshold (τ = 0.3). This ensures that training rewards are based on visually accurate target elements.
- **RL Training with Direct Coordinate Prediction:**
 - **Framework:** The training follows the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) framework.
 - **Direct Output:** A key departure from many prior RL-based methods is that GTA1's policy multimodal large language model 𝜋(·, ·) *directly predicts* a pixel coordinate (x,y) for interaction, without first generating explicit "thinking" (Chain-of-Thought reasoning).
 - **Simple Click Reward:** A binary reward system is implemented. For each sampled prediction 𝑜_𝑛 = (𝑥_𝑛, 𝑦_𝑛), a reward 𝑟_𝑛 is given: 1 if (𝑥_𝑛, 𝑦_𝑛) falls strictly within the annotated target bounding box 𝑏_ann, and 0 otherwise. This reward function directly aligns the learning objective with the task's success criterion (i.e., successfully interacting with the target element).
 - **Advantage Calculation:** The binary rewards {𝑟_𝑛} are normalized into advantages {𝐴_𝑛} using Z-score normalization (Equation 3 in the paper), which helps stabilize training by providing a relative measure of reward.
 - **Policy Optimization:** The model is optimized using a loss function (Equation 4) that leverages these advantages to encourage the generation of high-reward (correct) predictions and suppress low-reward (incorrect) ones.
The authors highlight that while "thinking" might benefit grounding in highly dynamic, context-rich environments, it is generally unnecessary and potentially detrimental in static GUI grounding tasks, making their direct prediction approach more efficient.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

GTA1 demonstrates state-of-the-art performance across both GUI grounding and agent task execution benchmarks, backed by insightful ablation studies.

#### 4.1. Grounding Performance

GTA1 consistently achieves the best performance on challenging GUI grounding benchmarks, showcasing its robustness and accuracy.
- **ScreenSpot-Pro:** This benchmark focuses on professional, high-resolution (up to 4K) interfaces. GTA1-7B achieves 50.1% average accuracy, remarkably outperforming much larger models like UGround-72B (34.5%). The larger GTA1-32B model pushes this further to 63.6%, setting a new state-of-the-art for high-fidelity grounding.
- **ScreenSpot-V2:** Evaluating grounding across mobile, desktop, and web domains, GTA1-32B achieves 95.2% accuracy, matching the performance of proprietary models like Seed-1.5-VL. GTA1-7B also performs exceptionally well at 92.4%.
- **OSWorld-G:** Focused on Linux environments, this benchmark assesses diverse capabilities like text matching, element recognition, and fine-grained manipulation. GTA1-32B achieves a new benchmark of 72.2% grounding accuracy, surpassing all prior state-of-the-art approaches.

These results indicate that GTA1's simple, direct click-based RL training strategy for grounding is highly effective and robust across various GUI complexities and resolutions.

#### 4.2. Agent Task Execution Performance

Beyond grounding, GTA1 also excels in full agent task execution in dynamic, real-world environments.
- **OSWorld and OSWorld-Verified:** These benchmarks involve open-ended tasks in Linux environments. Using o3 as the planner and judge for its test-time scaling strategy, GTA1-7B achieves a 45.2% task success rate on OSWorld, outperforming all previous state-of-the-art methods. Notably, it surpasses its native agent counterpart, CUA o3 (42.9%), despite operating with a shorter execution horizon (100 steps vs. 200 steps for CUA o3). With GPT-5 as the planner/judge, GTA1-7B and GTA1-32B achieve even higher success rates of 61.0% and 63.4% respectively on OSWorld-Verified.
- **WindowsAgentArena:** On this Windows-based benchmark, GTA1-32B with o3 achieves 51.2% success rate, significantly outperforming competitors like Jedi-7B w/ GPT-4o (33.7%).

The strong performance of GTA1 agents challenges the assumption that end-to-end native GUI agents are inherently superior, demonstrating the power of a well-architected two-stage approach with robust planning and grounding modules.

#### 4.3. Discussion and Ablation Studies

The paper includes crucial ablation studies that provide insights into GTA1's design choices.
- **Click Reward Works the Best:** The authors compare various optimization objectives for grounding. They find that the simple binary "click reward" (rewarding any prediction within the target element's bounding box) consistently outperforms combinations with "format reward" (enforcing "thinking") or "IoU reward" (encouraging accurate bounding box predictions). For example, on ScreenSpot-Pro, pure click reward yielded 50.1% accuracy, while combining it with IoU and format rewards resulted in 44.5%. This highlights the effectiveness and efficiency of direct objective alignment.
- **"Thinking" Benefits Grounding in Dynamic Environment Only:** The ablation reveals that explicit "thinking" or Chain-of-Thought reasoning provides minimal to no benefit in static GUI grounding tasks and can even hinder performance. However, in highly dynamic environments (e.g., AndroidWorld) where rich context (task object, past trajectories, user instructions) is available, "thinking" can improve task success rates (e.g., 39% to 44%), suggesting its utility is context-dependent and primarily beneficial for complex, evolving reasoning.
- **Test-time Scaling Generalizes Well:** The test-time scaling strategy proves highly effective and generalizable. Increasing the number of sampled action proposals (*K*) from 1 to 32 significantly boosts success rates on the OSWorld benchmark across various execution horizons (15, 50, and 100 steps). For instance, with *K*=32, a 15-step horizon agent can outperform a baseline (K=1) executing for 100 steps, demonstrating substantial efficiency gains. The strategy also successfully generalizes to other base agents, such as UI-TARS-1.5-7B, showcasing its ability to mitigate cascading failures and enhance overall agent robustness.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "GTA1: GUI Test-time Scaling Agent" paper makes several significant contributions with substantial potential impact on the field of artificial intelligence and its applications.

First, **GTA1 significantly advances the state-of-the-art in GUI agents**, demonstrating unprecedented performance on a suite of challenging benchmarks for both grounding accuracy and overall agent task execution. This moves the field closer to realizing truly autonomous AI agents capable of navigating and interacting with digital environments as seamlessly as humans.

Second, the paper delivers a crucial insight into **efficient and effective GUI grounding**. By demonstrating that a simple, direct RL-based approach with a binary "click reward" outperforms more complex methods involving explicit "thinking" or auxiliary bounding box predictions, GTA1 offers a pathway to more streamlined and performant grounding models. This simplicity not only potentially reduces computational overhead during training but also aligns the learning objective directly with the ultimate goal of interacting with the target element, which is a powerful lesson for future research.

Third, the introduction of a **test-time scaling strategy for planning** provides a robust solution to a long-standing challenge in agent design: planning in expansive action spaces without full lookahead. By concurrently sampling multiple action proposals and employing a multimodal LLM as a judge, GTA1 mitigates the risk of cascading failures, where early errors derail an entire task. The demonstrated generalizability of this strategy to other agents and its ability to improve performance across various task horizons indicate its broad applicability and potential to enhance the robustness of a wide array of AI agents.

Fourth, GTA1's competitive performance in agent task execution, even surpassing some native (end-to-end) agents, **challenges the prevailing assumption that monolithic end-to-end architectures are inherently superior** for complex, dynamic GUI tasks. This finding encourages further exploration and optimization of modular, two-stage agent designs, potentially leading to more interpretable, maintainable, and adaptable AI systems.

The potential impact of GTA1 is far-reaching. In practical terms, highly capable GUI agents can revolutionize automation, from simplifying routine administrative tasks and customer service to accelerating complex professional workflows in design, development, and scientific research. By enabling AI to autonomously interact with any software or web interface, GTA1 paves the way for a new era of digital productivity and accessibility. The release of the code and models further accelerates research and application development in this critical area.

While acknowledging limitations, such as struggles with specific scenarios like image editing, the paper clearly lays a strong foundation and outlines inspiring directions for future research. GTA1 is a testament to the power of targeted innovation in addressing core AI challenges, offering both theoretical insights and practical advancements for the future of intelligent agents.
