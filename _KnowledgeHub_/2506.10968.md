# Eye, Robot: Learning to Look to Act with a BC-RL Perception-Action Loop

> [!summary] Summary
> The EyeRobot system, developed by UC Berkeley's AUTOLAB and BAIR Lab, learns to actively control a high-speed camera's gaze to facilitate robotic manipulation tasks. It employs a BC-RL perception-action loop and a foveated vision architecture, enabling robust object search and precise manipulation across large workspaces while outperforming static camera setups.

## Problem

- Traditional robotic perception is often passive, lacking the active gaze control observed in human vision for efficient task completion.
- Achieving robust manipulation across large, panoramic workspaces is challenging for robots, as fixed or wrist-mounted cameras suffer from limited field of view and resolution.
- Training active visual agents often incurs high costs and difficulties with real-world data collection, leading to a significant sim2real gap when relying solely on traditional simulators.

## Method

- Developed **EyeRobot**, a robotic system featuring a UR5e arm equipped with a high-speed, gimbal-mounted camera (mechanical eyeball) for ==active gaze control==.
- Implemented a ==Behavior Cloning-Reinforcement Learning (BC-RL) loop==, where the RL-trained eye policy is rewarded by the success of the BC-trained hand policy, eliminating the need for explicit expert gaze demonstrations.
- Introduced **EyeGym**, a ==real-to-sim environment== that generates simulated eye viewpoints by directly sampling from real 360-degree videos, thereby reducing the sim2real gap and enabling scalable training.
- Utilized a ==Foveal Robot Transformer (FoRT)== architecture for processing multi-resolution visual inputs, inspired by ==biological foveated vision==, to enhance efficiency and robustness.

## Results

- EyeRobot successfully performs complex manipulation tasks across a **210°** workspace, demonstrating an **87%** success rate in a towel search task with an average search time of **1.8 seconds**.
- The system develops emergent gaze behaviors, including automatic gaze switching between relevant objects, long-range object search, and independent tracking of dynamic targets.
- EyeRobot consistently outperforms static externally-mounted cameras and shows superiority over wrist-mounted cameras in large workspace or perturbation scenarios, achieving enhanced hand-eye coordination.
- Ablation studies confirm that the FoRT architecture improves target fixation and task performance (e.g., **4.0cm** error for E-Stop vs **5.9cm** without foveation), while Active Visual Pretraining aids in faster convergence and better grasping.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Task-driven active gaze behavior can emerge through a co-optimization framework where the success of a robot's manipulation actions (learned via BC) provides the reward signal for an eye's gaze policy (learned via RL).
> - Leveraging real-world 360-degree video data to generate simulated eye viewpoints offers a scalable and realistic approach for training active perception policies, effectively minimizing the sim2real gap.
> - A foveated vision architecture (FoRT) significantly improves the efficiency, robustness to distractors, and long-range tracking capabilities of active perception systems by intelligently allocating visual resolution.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

EyeRobot strategically positions itself at the intersection of several critical areas in robotics and AI: active vision, robot learning from demonstration (behavior cloning), and biology-inspired machine perception, while also contributing to the development of scalable simulation environments for robot learning.

**Active Vision:** The concept of active perception, where an agent actively controls its sensors to gather information, has a long history, dating back to seminal works by Bajcsy and Aloimonos. Previous active vision systems primarily focused on maximizing information gain for visual tasks like tracking, search, or 3D reconstruction. While some works coupled active sensing with manipulators for downstream task performance, they often relied on heuristics, camera action demonstrations, or ground-truth state. EyeRobot distinguishes itself by proposing to **learn optimal eye gaze policies directly in observation space using reinforcement learning, without explicit gaze demonstrations**, driven purely by the need to facilitate robotic action. This represents a significant advancement towards more autonomous and task-driven active perception for manipulation.

**Behavior Cloning (BC) and Reinforcement Learning (RL):** Behavior cloning is a well-established paradigm for teaching robots manipulation skills by imitating human demonstrations. Recent advances have expanded its capabilities to complex tasks like bimanual and language-conditioned manipulation. EyeRobot leverages these BC advances for robot arm control but innovatively integrates it with RL for gaze control. The proposed **BC-RL loop** is a novel contribution where the success of the BC-trained hand policy serves as the reward signal for the RL-trained eye policy. This tight coupling allows hand-eye coordination to emerge, addressing a fundamental challenge in multi-modal robot control and extending the utility of BC beyond simple imitation. It showcases how a behavior cloning objective can be jointly optimized with an RL objective and even serve as a reward source.

**Biology-Inspired Machine Perception:** The paper draws direct inspiration from biological vision, particularly the human foveated visual system, where peripheral vision provides broad, low-resolution coverage and foveal vision offers high-resolution over a restricted area. Many computer vision systems mimic biological principles for efficiency, adaptability, and performance (e.g., stereo cameras, event cameras, foveated systems). EyeRobot adopts this principle not through specialized hardware or hardcoded behaviors, but by mounting a standard RGB camera on a high-speed gimbal and employing a multi-resolution transformer architecture (FoRT) for foveation. The crucial difference is that the gaze policy for this foveated system is *learned* via RL, rather than being pre-programmed or evolved in synthetic environments, pushing the boundaries of biologically-plausible, learned active sensing for robotics.

**Simulation Environments and Sim-to-Real Transfer:** Robot learning often relies heavily on simulation due to the cost and complexity of real-world data collection. EyeRobot introduces **EyeGym**, a novel real-to-sim environment that generates simulated eye viewpoints by sampling directly from real 360-degree videos captured during teleoperation. This approach offers several advantages over traditional physics-based simulators (e.g., MuJoCo, Isaac Gym) or photorealistic rendering environments (e.g., Habitat, iGibson, AI2-THOR). By leveraging real-world 360-degree data, EyeGym significantly **reduces the sim2real gap**, exposing policies to authentic textures, lighting, and noise, and enabling scalable training on native 360-degree datasets. This contribution is crucial for facilitating the practical deployment of active visual perception policies on real robotic systems.

In summary, EyeRobot integrates advanced concepts from active vision, robot learning, and bio-inspired perception within a pragmatic real-to-sim framework. It moves beyond passive sensing and pre-defined gaze patterns, demonstrating how active gaze behavior can be learned end-to-end to enhance manipulation capabilities in complex, large workspaces, thereby pushing the frontier of intelligent robotic interaction with the physical world.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for EyeRobot stems from a fundamental principle observed in human perception: "We perceive in order to act and we act in order to perceive." Humans do not passively observe their surroundings; rather, their gaze is actively directed to gather information pertinent to ongoing or impending actions. This tight coupling between attention and action is crucial for efficient and effective interaction with the environment, especially given the limitations of the human visual system (e.g., foveated vision).

Translating this biological principle to robotics, the key objectives of EyeRobot are:

1. **Develop a Robotic System with Emergent Gaze Behavior:** The primary goal is to create a robotic system where the behavior of looking—how and where the camera is directed—emerges *naturally* from the robot's need to complete real-world manipulation tasks. This implies learning gaze policies without requiring explicit gaze demonstrations from human operators, contrasting with many prior active vision systems.

2. **Enable Hand-Eye Coordination through Task-Driven Learning:** The system aims to achieve seamless hand-eye coordination where the eye's movements directly facilitate the arm's actions. The motivation is that the eye should learn to look towards regions or objects that allow the robotic hand to successfully complete its task, thereby optimizing the overall task performance.

3. **Address Real-World Training Constraints with a Scalable Real-to-Sim Environment:** A significant challenge in training active visual agents is the difficulty and cost of collecting real-world experience. The paper aims to overcome this by developing **EyeGym**, a real-to-sim environment that leverages teleoperated demonstrations and 360-degree camera data to allow for scalable and realistic training of gaze policies. This motivated the need for an environment that minimizes the "sim2real gap."

4. **Implement Biologically-Inspired Foveated Vision for Efficiency and Robustness:** Motivated by the efficiency and performance of human foveated vision, another objective is to design a foveated vision architecture (FoRT). This aims to allow for high-resolution processing of task-relevant areas while maintaining broad, low-resolution contextual awareness, thereby optimizing computational budget and enhancing robustness to distractors and varying object distances.

5. **Demonstrate Robust Manipulation over Large, Panoramic Workspaces:** The ultimate objective is to validate EyeRobot's capabilities on complex manipulation tasks that extend across a large, panoramic workspace (e.g., 180-210 degrees around the robot arm). This pushes the boundaries of single-camera robotic manipulation by enabling operations on objects initially outside the camera's field of view, requiring active search and gaze control.

In essence, the motivation is to endow robots with a more intelligent and active form of perception, moving beyond passive observation to a state where the robot "learns to look to act," leading to more capable and adaptable manipulation systems in varied and challenging environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

EyeRobot employs a sophisticated methodology that integrates custom hardware, a novel real-to-sim environment, a unique training loop, and a specialized neural network architecture.

#### 3.1. EyeRobot Hardware System

The physical system consists of a **UR5e robot arm** equipped with a **mechanical eyeball** mounted rigidly to the robot's base. The eyeball itself is a high-speed, gimbal-mounted camera with two degrees of freedom for pan and tilt control. It features a 90fps, 1900x1200 global shutter RGB camera paired with a 110° FOV fisheye lens, enabling both wide peripheral view and smooth image capture during rapid movements. Direct-drive brushless DC motors allow for fast saccadic movements (20ms for 60°), crucial for reactive gaze control.

#### 3.2. EyeGym: Scalable Experience Collection Environment

To facilitate large-scale, realistic training for active gaze policies, the authors developed **EyeGym**. This environment is central to their real-to-sim approach:
- **Data Collection:** Instead of a physical eyeball, an off-the-shelf Insta360 X4 360-degree camera captures 5.7K, 30FPS equirectangular video sequences. These videos are synchronized with robot trajectories collected via a teleoperation system (GELLO).
- **Gaze Simulation:** EyeGym renders arbitrary eyeball viewpoints by sampling directly from these real equirectangular videos and images. This means policies are exposed to authentic textures, lighting, and noise, significantly reducing the sim2real gap. It also allows training on native 360-degree datasets, enhancing scalability and practical transfer to real-world camera systems. EyeGym thus avoids the overhead and potential realism issues of traditional 3D rendering engines.

#### 3.3. Learning Gaze Policies with the BC-RL Loop

The core of EyeRobot's learning mechanism is a **Behavior Cloning-Reinforcement Learning (BC-RL) loop**, designed to train gaze policies without expert gaze demonstrations.
- **Joint Optimization:** The system consists of two policies: a **BC policy** controlling the robot arm and an **RL policy** controlling the eye's gaze.
- **Information Flow:** Crucially, observations flow from the RL eye policy to the BC hand policy (i.e., the hand acts based on what the eye sees). Conversely, task success metrics flow from the BC hand policy back to the RL eye policy (i.e., the eye is rewarded if its gaze helps the hand succeed).
- **Reward Mechanism:** For the eye policy, the reward is derived from the performance of the BC agent in matching demonstrated actions. Specifically, the reward is the negative Fréchet distance between the end-effector position trajectories predicted by the BC agent and the ground-truth teleoperated trajectories. This directly incentivizes the eye to look in directions that enable the hand to accurately mimic demonstrated actions.
- **Initialization Phase:** Each episode begins with a 30-frame (1-second) pause, allowing the eye to visually search and orient itself before the BC supervision for the arm begins, promoting active exploration.

#### 3.4. Active Visual Pretraining (AVP)

To overcome challenges associated with randomly initialized networks and sparse rewards early in BC-RL training (where the target object might be out of view), EyeRobot incorporates **Active Visual Pretraining (AVP)**.
- **Pretraining Task:** Policies are pretrained in EyeGym using static 360-degree video frames with a visual object search reward. The eye is rewarded for centering the target object in its view.
- **Generalization:** During pretraining, CLIP embeddings of search targets are used as conditioning inputs, allowing the networks to learn about multiple objects. After pretraining, this input is replaced with zero tokens during the BC-RL phase. AVP thus provides a more robust initialization for both hand and eye policies, aiding BC-RL convergence.

#### 3.5. Foveal Robot Transformer (FoRT) Architecture

Inspired by biological foveated vision, EyeRobot utilizes a **Foveal Robot Transformer (FoRT)** architecture for processing visual inputs and predicting actions.
- **Multi-Resolution Foveation:** Input images are processed into an image pyramid of multiple (N) scales, all centered at the fovea (center pixel) and rescaled to a uniform 224x224 resolution. This creates a foveated input where the central region is high-resolution, and the periphery is progressively lower resolution.
- **Feature Extraction:** Patches from these multi-scale crops are independently embedded using a frozen DINOv2-ViT/S encoder.
- **Transformer Inputs:** The flattened token embeddings of these patches, along with the eye's gaze direction (as a 3D vector), current joint proprioception, and an optional target token, are fed into the transformer.
- **Outputs and Gaze Control:** Eye actions are parameterized as a categorical distribution over 8 azimuth-elevation directions and a null action (staying still). Robot actions (joint positions) are decoded similarly.
- **Positional Embeddings:** 3D Rotary Positional Embeddings (RoPE) are used to assign (time, x, y) coordinates to each token, enabling the transformer to understand spatial relationships across different scales and temporal context.
- **Efficiency:** Attention masking is employed, specifically masking image-image attention (as DINOv2 already performs this) and using sliding window attention for long rollouts (10 timesteps for the eye, 1 for the hand) to improve throughput and prevent overfitting.

This comprehensive methodology, combining specialized hardware with an innovative training framework and a biologically-inspired vision architecture, enables EyeRobot to learn complex active perception and manipulation behaviors.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

EyeRobot demonstrates compelling results across various manipulation tasks, showcasing emergent gaze behaviors and superior performance compared to conventional camera setups.

#### 4.1. Effectiveness of EyeGym for Visual Search

- **Object Search and Tracking:** Experiments confirm EyeGym's ability to train an eyeball to effectively search for and track objects. In a "towel search" task, where the towel was initially outside the camera's field of view, EyeRobot achieved an **87% success rate** with an average search time of 1.8 seconds.
- **Qualitative Tracking:** The system also demonstrated robust tracking of dynamic objects, such as a rapidly tossed stuffed bear, illustrating the learned policy's speed and accuracy.
- **Learned Search Patterns:** The gaze policy learns an oscillatory sweeping motion to explore the scene, an emergent behavior not explicitly programmed.

#### 4.2. Hand-Eye Coordination and Large Workspace Manipulation

EyeRobot was evaluated on five challenging, panoramic manipulation tasks (Eraser-on-Shelf, E-Stop Reaching, Brush Handoff, Screwdriver Servoing, Towel-in-Bucket) requiring manipulation across a **210° workspace**.
- **Consistent Performance:** EyeRobot consistently performed these tasks, which are particularly difficult due to the large workspace and the need for precision.
- **Emergent Eye Behaviors:** The learning process yielded several key emergent gaze behaviors:
 - **Gaze Switching:** In multi-step tasks (e.g., Towel-in-Bucket), the eye learned to automatically switch its gaze between relevant objects (e.g., towel to bucket) depending on the stage of the task.
 - **Long-Range Search:** The eye actively searched for target objects that were not initially visible, demonstrating true exploratory behavior.
 - **Independent Tracking:** The eye could track target locations independently of the robot arm's movement, especially when targets were perturbed mid-task, adapting its gaze to maintain visual focus.
 - **Predictive Cues:** In dynamic human-robot interaction tasks, the eye learned to attend to predictive cues from human actions (e.g., anticipating a handoff).

#### 4.3. Camera Placement Comparisons

A critical finding was EyeRobot's performance relative to conventional static camera placements:
- **Outperformance:** EyeRobot (active, foveated camera) consistently **outperformed fixed externally-mounted ("Exo") cameras** in precise manipulation tasks. Exo cameras struggled with resolution limitations, often touching but failing to grasp objects accurately due to their zoomed-out perspective of a large workspace.
- **Superiority over Wrist Cameras in Large Workspaces:** While wrist-mounted cameras showed high success when the target was within their limited field of view, their performance significantly degraded in perturbation trials or large workspaces where they could not search for targets. EyeRobot's ability to adjust its viewpoint allowed it to maintain the target (e.g., shelf) in view at all times.
- **Challenges with Multi-Camera Baselines:** A combined "Wrist+Exo" baseline sometimes performed worse than wrist-only, possibly due to the complexity of merging image tokens from multiple cameras, highlighting the benefits of a single, active, high-resolution view.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Foveation (FoRT):**
 - **Improved Fixation:** The foveated FoRT model consistently maintained the target object near the center of its field of view, unlike the non-foveated model, which only loosely kept it in view.
 - **Enhanced Performance and Speed:** Quantitatively, FoRT led to degraded performance on E-Stop (e.g., 4.0cm error vs 5.9cm for uniform resolution) and significantly slower settling times (4.2s vs 5.3s) without foveation.
 - **Robustness to Distractors and Distance:** FoRT showed significantly improved robustness to distractor objects, maintaining fixation on the target even with a human placing a yellow cup nearby, whereas the uniform resolution model split attention. It also tracked objects much farther (5x) due to its pyramidal representation.
- **Active Visual Pretraining (AVP):**
 - **Convergence Aid:** While long-range visual search *could* emerge purely from BC-RL, AVP significantly aided the convergence speed and improved grasping performance by providing better initialized network weights and ensuring correct eye fixation at the start of tasks.

In summary, the results strongly validate the EyeRobot framework, demonstrating that active, task-driven gaze control, enabled by a real-to-sim BC-RL loop and a foveated architecture, leads to robust, intelligent, and human-like hand-eye coordination for complex manipulation in large, panoramic environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The EyeRobot research represents a significant leap in robotic perception and manipulation, with far-reaching implications for the development of more intelligent and adaptable robotic systems.

**Paradigm Shift in Robotic Perception:** EyeRobot challenges the traditional paradigm of passive robotic perception by demonstrating that active, learned gaze behavior can emerge from the fundamental need to complete physical tasks. This aligns more closely with biological intelligence, where perception and action are deeply intertwined. By showing that gaze control can be learned directly from observation space with task-based RL rewards, the work opens new avenues for creating robots that "look to act," making them more efficient and capable in complex, real-world scenarios.

**Enhanced Manipulation Capabilities:** The ability to dynamically control the robot's viewpoint allows for robust manipulation in large, panoramic workspaces – a major limitation for fixed or wrist-mounted cameras. The emergent behaviors like gaze switching, long-range search, and independent tracking signify a more sophisticated level of hand-eye coordination. This directly translates to improved success rates and precision for tasks involving objects that are initially out of view, perturbed during execution, or require continuous visual attention over a wide area. This makes robots more versatile for applications in manufacturing, logistics, and assistive robotics where dynamic environments are common.

**Scalable and Realistic Robot Learning:** The introduction of EyeGym, a real-to-sim environment leveraging 360-degree videos, is a crucial contribution to scalable robot learning. By minimizing the sim2real gap, EyeGym allows policies to be trained on realistic visual data without the high computational cost and domain randomization complexities of traditional 3D rendering. This approach democratizes access to robust training data, potentially accelerating research in active vision and robot learning for systems that can readily transfer from simulation to the real world. The commitment to releasing the code and hardware instructions further enhances its impact.

**Advancements in Bio-Inspired AI and Efficiency:** The Foveal Robot Transformer (FoRT) architecture, inspired by the human retina, demonstrates that physically allocating visual tokens can lead to significant performance benefits. The observed improvements in robustness to distractors, distance invariance in tracking, and computational efficiency highlight the practical advantages of foveated vision for robotics. This work provides strong evidence that integrating biological principles with modern deep learning architectures can yield more robust and resource-efficient AI systems.

**Foundation for Future Research:** EyeRobot lays a strong foundation for several exciting future directions:
- **Mobile Manipulation:** Extending the active eyeball to mobile robots would drastically increase their operational range and autonomy, addressing navigation and manipulation challenges simultaneously.
- **Enhanced Depth Perception:** Integrating stereo vision or monocular depth estimators could overcome current limitations in depth perception, enabling more precise and delicate manipulation tasks.
- **Complex Task Hierarchies:** The BC-RL loop could be extended to more complex, long-horizon tasks requiring hierarchical planning and more abstract understanding of sub-goals.
- **Adaptive Perception for Human-Robot Interaction:** The robot's ability to attend to human cues opens up possibilities for more intuitive and cooperative human-robot collaboration.

In conclusion, EyeRobot is a pioneering work that integrates active perception into the core of robot learning, demonstrating how "learning to look" can lead to truly intelligent and capable manipulation. Its novel methodology, compelling results, and clear potential for future development position it as a significant contribution to the field of robotics and artificial intelligence.
