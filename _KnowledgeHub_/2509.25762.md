# OPPO: Accelerating PPO-based RLHF via Pipeline Overlap

> [!summary] Summary
> OPPO is a framework that accelerates PPO-based Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) training by maximizing execution overlap within its pipeline, achieving 1.8x to 2.8x speedups and 1.4x to 2.1x GPU utilization improvements while maintaining model convergence and quality.

## Problem

- PPO-based RLHF training is resource-intensive and suffers from inefficiencies due to sequential multi-model dependencies.
- Intra-step sequential execution of generation, scoring, and training stages leads to significant GPU idle time and underutilization.
- Inter-step long-tail latency from heterogeneous LLM response lengths causes "straggler" sequences to delay subsequent processing.

## Method

- **OPPO** introduces ==Intra-step Overlap== by streaming incrementally generated token chunks from the actor to downstream models for concurrent processing.
- It implements Inter-step Overlap by "overcommitting" prompts and deferring incomplete "straggler" sequences to subsequent steps, preserving partial work.
- Dynamic control mechanisms adapt chunk sizes for intra-step overlap and adjust overcommitment degrees for inter-step overlap to optimize efficiency and convergence.

## Results

- OPPO accelerated end-to-end PPO-based RLHF training by **1.8x** to **2.8x** across free-form generation, math reasoning, and code generation tasks.
- GPU utilization in the inference stage improved by **1.4x** to **2.1x**, demonstrating more efficient hardware usage.
- The framework preserved identical step-to-reward convergence and achieved comparable or slightly improved final model accuracy compared to baseline methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Significant efficiency gains in PPO-based RLHF can be achieved by maximizing execution overlap without modifying the core PPO algorithm or sacrificing convergence.
> - Bottlenecks stemming from both sequential intra-step dependencies and inter-step long-tail latency can be effectively addressed by distinct but complementary overlap strategies.
> - Dynamic adaptation of overlap parameters, such as chunk size and overcommitment degree, is essential to optimize performance and maintain training stability.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF), particularly methods based on Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), has become a standard approach for aligning large language models (LLMs) with human preferences. This process typically involves a complex pipeline with multiple interacting models: an actor (policy), a critic (value function), a reference policy for regularization, and a reward model. Despite its effectiveness and stability, PPO-based RLHF training is resource-intensive and suffers from inefficiencies due to sequential multi-model dependencies and variable generation lengths, which lead to long-tail latency.

Existing efforts to improve RLHF efficiency generally fall into two categories:
- **Algorithmic approaches**: These methods aim to reduce the complexity of the RLHF objective or components. Examples include Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) and Group-Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), which may remove components like the value or reward model. However, these often face challenges related to instability from sparse rewards, the need for extensive rollouts, or task-specific reward design complexities. Asynchronous RLHF methods reduce pipeline dependencies but can introduce staleness, potentially compromising convergence quality.
- **System-level approaches**: These focus on optimizing the underlying infrastructure and execution. Examples include RLHFuse, AReal, and Verl, which improve throughput through fine-grained parallelism, model collocation, or dynamic resource scaling. These methods often target reducing communication overhead or matching GPU resources to workload demands.

This work, OPPO, complements both algorithmic and existing system-level approaches by introducing a novel strategy: maximizing execution overlap within the PPO-based RLHF pipeline. Unlike algorithmic methods that modify the learning objective or system-level methods that primarily optimize hardware resource allocation, OPPO focuses on restructuring the execution flow to reduce idle time by concurrently running dependent stages. It addresses inefficiencies that arise from both intra-step (sequential stage dependencies) and inter-step (long-tail latency across iterations) limitations, without altering the fundamental PPO update rule or introducing staleness from evaluation using outdated models. By preserving PPO semantics while accelerating execution, OPPO aims to be broadly applicable and composable with other existing optimization strategies.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to accelerate the training efficiency of PPO-based RLHF pipelines without compromising training convergence or the quality of the aligned LLM.

The motivation for this work stems from two core inefficiencies observed in current PPO-based RLHF training designs:

1. **Inefficiency due to Intra-step Dependency**: A standard PPO-based RLHF step involves three sequential stages:
 - **Generation**: The actor model generates responses to a batch of prompts.
 - **Scoring**: The generated responses are evaluated by the critic, reference, and reward models.
 - **Training**: The actor and critic models are updated using advantage estimates and gradients derived from the scoring stage.
 These stages are tightly coupled, meaning downstream stages cannot begin until upstream stages complete. This sequential execution leads to significant idle GPU time. For instance, the reward model remains idle while the actor generates full responses. Furthermore, different models (actor vs. reward/critic) have heterogeneous computational characteristics; actor generation is memory-intensive with low GPU utilization, while scoring and training are more compute-intensive. This mismatch in resource demands across stages results in underutilized GPUs.

2. **Inefficiency due to Inter-step Dependency and Long-Tail Latency**: The generation stage is particularly susceptible to long-tail latency. Response lengths generated by LLMs are highly heterogeneous; a few exceptionally long responses within a batch can significantly delay the completion of the entire generation stage. Since subsequent scoring and training stages cannot proceed until all responses in a batch are complete, these "straggler" sequences force downstream models to wait, leading to further idle resources and poor training throughput across pipeline steps. While asynchronous training approaches exist to reduce inter-step dependencies, they can introduce staleness, potentially harming the model's convergence and final quality by using outdated evaluations.

Therefore, the key motivation is to identify and exploit opportunities for execution overlap within and across these training steps to minimize idle GPU time and accelerate overall training throughput, without introducing algorithmic compromises such as staleness or altering the PPO update.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

OPPO addresses the identified inefficiencies by introducing two complementary techniques designed to maximize pipeline execution overlap: Intra-step Overlap and Inter-step Overlap.

#### 3.1. Intra-step Overlap

This technique focuses on overlapping the actor's generation stage with the downstream models' (e.g., reward model) scoring stage within a single PPO step. The core idea is to break the strict sequential dependency where the reward model must wait for a full response.
- **Mechanism**: Instead of waiting for the actor to complete an entire response, OPPO partitions the actor's output into "right-sized chunks." As the actor generates each chunk of tokens, it streams these newly generated tokens incrementally to the downstream models. This enables the reward model (and other scoring models) to begin prefilling and processing these partial outputs concurrently with the actor's ongoing decoding. The reward model processes these chunks progressively, and only at the end of the entire sequence generation does it compute the final score based on the complete response.
- **Preservation of PPO Semantics**: The paper states that this streaming approach does not alter the generated response, policy log-probabilities, or critic/value terms. This means the gradient estimator remains point-wise equivalent to the standard PPO estimator, preserving both its expectation and variance and thus maintaining algorithmic correctness and stability.
- **Dynamic Control on Intra-step Overlap**: The choice of chunk size is critical. Small chunks might cause high overhead due to frequent context switching and resource contention, especially when models are colocated. Large chunks reduce overlap, reverting to a more sequential execution. OPPO addresses this by dynamically adjusting the chunk size. Periodically (e.g., every 50 training steps), OPPO tests a few candidate chunk sizes (e.g., 128, 256, 512 tokens) and selects the configuration that yielded the best performance for subsequent training windows. This online adaptation balances overlap efficiency against resource contention.

#### 3.2. Inter-step Overlap

This technique aims to mitigate the long-tail latency caused by heterogeneous response lengths across PPO steps without discarding partial work or introducing significant staleness.
- **Mechanism**: OPPO "overcommits" a few additional prompts per batch. If the target batch size for PPO update is B, OPPO generates responses for B + ∆ prompts, where ∆ is the overcommitment degree. During each PPO step, the first B prompts that complete their generation are immediately used for the PPO update. Any remaining ∆ sequences that are still generating (i.e., stragglers) are *not* discarded but are deferred and carried over to the next training step, where their generation resumes. This ensures that partial work is preserved and long sequences eventually complete, preventing them from perpetually delaying the pipeline.
- **Dynamic Control on Inter-step Overlap**: The overcommitment degree ∆ influences the trade-off between efficiency and convergence. A small ∆ limits tail latency mitigation, while a large ∆ can inflate per-step latency and introduce a form of staleness by delaying the inclusion of some generations into the PPO update. OPPO dynamically adjusts ∆ based on training dynamics. It uses a sliding window of `w` steps to monitor the average reward improvement (slope `s_t`). If `s_t` is positive, ∆ is gradually increased to explore more aggressive overlap, capped by `∆_max`. If `s_t` is zero or negative (indicating convergence or performance plateau), ∆ is gradually decreased, bounded by `∆_min` (which can be zero). This adaptive mechanism ensures that overcommitment is reduced as the model converges, minimizing potential staleness effects while still mitigating tail latency in earlier, more dynamic training phases.

#### 3.3. Overall Integration

Algorithm 1 outlines the combined OPPO training procedure. It involves initializing a buffer of B+∆ capacity, filling it with samples, and then performing generation with intra-step streaming. Once B sequences are finished, they are used for the PPO update, while unfinished sequences remain in the buffer for the next iteration. The dynamic ∆ adjustment is performed periodically based on reward trends. OPPO is designed as a lightweight wrapper, indicating minimal modifications to existing PPO implementations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

OPPO was evaluated across three types of tasks (free-form generation, math reasoning, code generation) using different Qwen2.5 LLM variants (3B and 7B models) on NVIDIA A100, H200, and GH200 GPUs. The baseline for comparison was the standard TRL PPO framework.

#### 4.1. End-to-End Performance Comparison

- **Training Speedup**: OPPO consistently accelerated PPO-based RLHF training by **1.8× to 2.8×** across all evaluated tasks (Figure 3).
 - On Stack-Exchange with Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, OPPO achieved a 1.9× speedup, reaching a target reward in 2,300 minutes compared to 4,300 minutes for the baseline.
 - With Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct on the same dataset, OPPO achieved a 2.5× improvement (5,200 vs. 13,000 minutes).
 - Speedups of 2.4× and 2.8× were observed on OpenCoder-SFT (Stage 2) and GSM8K, respectively.
- **Convergence Preservation**: Despite significant wall-clock speedups, OPPO demonstrated nearly identical step-to-reward convergence trajectories as the TRL baseline across all tasks (Figure 4). This indicates that OPPO achieves efficiency gains without sacrificing the quality or stability of the training process.
- **GPU Utilization**: OPPO substantially improved GPU utilization in the inference stage (Actor Generation + Reward Scoring) by **1.4× to 2.1×** (Figure 5).
 - On Stack-Exchange with Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, utilization increased from 50.6% to 71.0% (1.4×).
 - On Stack-Exchange with Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct, utilization rose from 38.7% to 73.6% (1.9×).
 - Similar improvements were seen on GSM8K (1.5×, 45.7% to 67.3%) and OpenCoder-SFT (2.1×, 35.7% to 74.1%).
- **Multi-Node Performance**: In multi-node settings (2 nodes, each with 4×A100-40GB GPUs), OPPO achieved a **4.49× reduction** in end-to-end step latency compared to TRL on Stack-Exchange-Paired with Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (Table 1).
- **Comparison with Other System Frameworks**: OPPO achieved the lowest per-step latency (99.84s) compared to state-of-the-art systems like VeRL (with various parallelism configurations) and AReaL (Table 4). OPPO outperformed VeRL w/ DP by 1.26×, suggesting it addresses orthogonal bottlenecks not typically covered by sequence-level optimizations.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies

- **Performance Breakdown by Design Components**:
 - On Stack-Exchange-Paired with Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Intra-step overlap alone provided a 1.2× speedup (4,200 to 3,500 minutes), while Inter-step overlap alone provided a 1.6× speedup (4,200 to 2,700 minutes) (Figure 6).
 - Similar trends were observed with Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct, where Intra-step overlap yielded a 1.3× speedup (13,000 to 10,000 minutes) and Inter-step overlap a 2.06× speedup (13,000 to 6,300 minutes).
 - These results demonstrate that both intra- and inter-step overlaps contribute independently to efficiency gains and target distinct bottlenecks.
- **Robustness and Staleness**: The dynamic ∆ controller ensures that most requests are processed immediately (78.48%), and nearly all deferred requests are delayed by only a single step (20.20%) (Table 2). This indicates that the inter-step overlap mechanism avoids perpetual deferral and excessive staleness.
- **Effectiveness of Inter-step Adaptation**: Dynamic ∆ adaptation consistently achieved faster convergence and more stable rewards compared to fixed ∆ values (Figure 7a). Static ∆ choices led to tradeoffs between efficiency and stability, whereas dynamic ∆ adapted to evolving rollout length distributions.
- **Impact of Streaming Degrees (Chunk Size)**: Moderate chunk sizes (around 500 tokens) yielded the fastest step speeds by optimizing the balance between overlap maximization and overhead reduction from context switching. Both very small and very large chunks resulted in slower step times (Figure 7b).
- **Final Accuracy Preservation**: OPPO-trained models demonstrated comparable or slightly improved accuracy on core benchmark tasks (ARC-Challenge, HellaSwag, TruthfulQA-MC2, GSM8K) compared to TRL-trained models (Table 3). For the 3B model, OPPO showed an average gain of 0.48 percentage points (pp). For the 7B model, the difference was minimal (average +0.02 pp), with fluctuations falling within expected statistical variance.
- **Applicability beyond PPO**: The principles of OPPO are generalized to other online preference optimization methods like DPO or GRPO that involve variable-length on-policy generations. OPPO achieved 2.35× faster convergence in a rule-based PPO setting (without a reward model) on GSM8K, demonstrating the effectiveness of the inter-step overlap even in simplified RLHF pipelines.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presents OPPO, a framework that addresses critical training inefficiencies in PPO-based RLHF by introducing pipeline overlap techniques. The significance of this work lies in its ability to substantially accelerate RLHF training and improve GPU utilization without requiring changes to the PPO algorithm's fundamental objective or compromising model convergence and final quality.

The key impacts are:
- **Enhanced Training Efficiency**: By demonstrating 1.8×–2.8× speedups and 1.4×–2.1× GPU utilization improvements, OPPO offers a concrete pathway to reduce the computational cost and time required for aligning LLMs. This is particularly relevant as LLMs and context lengths continue to grow, making RLHF increasingly expensive.
- **Two-pronged Optimization**: The introduction of both intra-step and inter-step overlap provides a comprehensive solution to distinct but related inefficiencies. Intra-step overlap directly addresses idle time within a step by streaming partial outputs, while inter-step overlap tackles the persistent problem of long-tail latency by strategically deferring stragglers without data loss or significant staleness.
- **Algorithmic Preservation and Generalizability**: OPPO's design preserves the PPO update semantics, ensuring that the benefits are purely system-level without introducing algorithmic compromises. Its lightweight wrapper approach makes it readily integrable with existing PPO implementations. Furthermore, the principles of pipeline overlap, particularly the handling of variable-length generations and stragglers, are applicable to a broader range of online preference optimization methods beyond PPO, such as DPO and GRPO, extending its potential impact.
- **Complementary to Existing Solutions**: OPPO's focus on execution overlap is orthogonal to and complements existing algorithmic optimizations (e.g., critic-free methods, RL-free methods) and system-level optimizations (e.g., fine-grained parallelism, stage fusion). This suggests that OPPO can be combined with other state-of-the-art techniques to achieve even greater overall efficiency gains in RLHF training.

The demonstrated improvements in efficiency, coupled with the preservation of training quality and its generalizability, positions OPPO as a valuable contribution to making RLHF training more accessible and scalable for future LLM alignment research and deployment.
