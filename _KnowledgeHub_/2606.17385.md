# EgoInfinity: A Web-Scale 4D Hand-Object Interaction Data Engine for Any-View Robot Retargeting and Video-to-Action Robot Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> EgoInfinity is a data engine that automatically converts web-scale RGB videos into robot-actionable 4D hand-object interaction data, including metric hand trajectories, 6-DoF object poses, and contact information. This system addresses data collection bottlenecks for generalist robots by generating physically grounded, cross-embodiment retargetable motions suitable for direct execution and policy learning on various robot platforms.

## Problem

- Robot learning for generalist robots is constrained by the limited scale, diversity, and complexity of tasks in existing laboratory-collected datasets.
- Egocentric human datasets, while providing valuable manipulation observations, typically rely on specialized hardware or extensive manual annotation, hindering large-scale open-world data acquisition.
- Vast internet videos contain human manipulation knowledge but lack the crucial metric 3D geometry, 6-DoF object states, and explicit executable actions required for direct robot learning.

## Method

- Developed a fully automated data engine, **EgoInfinity**, that converts in-the-wild RGB videos and text descriptions into agent-agnostic, metric 4D hand-object interaction representations without specialized hardware or human annotation.
- Implemented a modular pipeline with "==cross-module calibration==" to unify outputs from perception components (e.g., hand pose, object tracking, depth) into a shared metric scale, enhanced by "interaction-aware refinement" that uses grasp states to improve physical consistency.
- Proposed a novel functional cross-embodiment retargeting method that employs an SE(3)-equivariant neural network to estimate robot-specific kinematic root frames, allowing human motions to be transferred to diverse robot morphologies while preserving task-relevant actions.

## Results

- A browser-based data server and a curated subset of 106 Action100M videos were developed, demonstrating the system's ability to process, visualize, and provide diverse 4D manipulation data with identifiable objects and actions.
- The cross-embodiment retargeting achieved high IK success rates (e.g., **0.821** for Unitree G1, **0.774** for Robonaut2) with low position (e.g., **2.86** cm for G1) and orientation errors across three distinct robot embodiments.
- EgoInfinity-generated data successfully served as priors for training a grasping policy for a real dexterous hand and enabled direct execution of diverse manipulation skills (e.g., cutting, pouring) on a dual-arm Franka FR3 robot.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Robust 4D reconstruction from web videos requires a modular, metric-calibrated pipeline that can integrate and align diverse perception outputs into a unified, physically consistent coordinate system.
> - Leveraging interaction states (e.g., grasping, static contact) for refinement significantly improves the temporal stability and physical plausibility of reconstructed 6-DoF object trajectories, addressing common issues like drift and contact inconsistencies.
> - Functional cross-embodiment retargeting, enabled by an SE(3)-equivariant neural network trained in simulation, can effectively transfer human skills from arbitrary internet videos to varied robot platforms by estimating feasible robot root transformations and preserving task-relevant hand motion.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of generalist robots capable of performing diverse manipulation tasks in open-world environments necessitates access to large-scale and diverse training data. Existing robot learning datasets, whether collected in controlled laboratory settings or through teleoperation, often present limitations in terms of scale, task variety, or the inherent diversity of human interaction with objects. While egocentric datasets, such as Ego4D, EgoDex, HOT3D, and OakInk2, offer valuable human manipulation observations, they frequently rely on specialized hardware like headsets or motion capture systems, or involve labor-intensive manual annotation and controlled collection environments. These constraints restrict their scalability and applicability for training robots for broader tasks.

Conversely, the internet contains a vast and unscripted repository of human manipulation videos, capturing a wide array of everyday interactions across diverse environments and viewpoints. However, these "in-the-wild" RGB videos are not directly amenable to robot learning because they typically lack crucial robot-actionable information, such as metric 3D geometry, 6-DoF object poses, detailed contact information, and executable robot actions. Current learning-from-human-video methods often struggle with adequately grounding 2D observations into reliable 3D robot action spaces, leading to misaligned or insufficiently grounded policies.

This work positions itself at the intersection of large-scale internet video data and robot learning, addressing the bottleneck of converting arbitrary RGB footage into actionable robot training data. It builds upon recent advancements in various computer vision and robotics components, including RGB hand reconstruction, monocular metric geometry estimation, open-vocabulary segmentation and tracking, and object reconstruction. While other single-clip systems, such as Hawor and EgoGrasp, have also made progress in reconstructing world-space hand-object interactions, EgoInfinity differentiates itself by focusing on a corpus-scale data engine designed for approximately static-camera in-the-wild videos, employing a deterministic, interaction-aware refinement strategy for grasp state, and providing a robust, agent-agnostic 4D representation suitable for cross-embodiment retargeting. The paper aims to bridge the gap between the abundance of human video data and the specific data requirements for scalable robot learning, without relying on specialized collection equipment or extensive human annotation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the acknowledged challenge of scaling robot learning to encompass a wide range of manipulation tasks in unstructured environments. Training "generalist robots" demands an unprecedented volume and diversity of manipulation data, which current lab- or factory-collected datasets cannot sufficiently provide due to inherent limitations in scale, diversity of tasks, collection settings, or participant behaviors. Internet videos, with their sheer volume and naturalistic capture of human interactions with objects, represent a potentially inexhaustible resource for this data. However, the raw format of these videos—standard RGB footage—lacks the structured, robot-actionable information necessary for direct application in robot learning. Specifically, internet videos typically do not contain metric 3D geometry, precise 6-DoF object state, contact patterns, or readily executable robot actions. This absence of critical information poses a significant barrier to leveraging web-scale human manipulation knowledge for robotics.

In response to this challenge, the authors established several key objectives for EgoInfinity:

1. **Automated 4D Data Generation:** To develop a fully automated, modular data engine capable of converting arbitrary in-the-wild RGB videos into agent-agnostic, metric 4D hand-object interaction representations. This involves generating hand trajectories, 6-DoF object poses, and contact-relevant states without the need for human annotation, specialized wearables, depth sensors, or CAD models.
2. **Robust and Physically Plausible Reconstruction:** To move beyond a naive concatenation of existing perception components by integrating cross-module metric calibration and an interaction-aware refinement stage. This aims to improve the physical reliability of the reconstructed hand-object interactions, mitigating common issues such as visual drift and contact inconsistencies that can arise from purely visual reconstruction methods.
3. **Cross-Embodiment Robot Motion Retargeting:** To propose a novel functional retargeting method that can compile the recovered 3D hand motions into executable joint trajectories for a diverse range of robot morphologies. This objective is particularly important for in-the-wild videos where only partial human body information (e.g., only hands) may be visible, making exact human kinematic imitation impractical. The retargeter is designed to preserve task-relevant motion within robot constraints, enabling broad applicability across different robot types and viewpoints.
4. **Comprehensive Validation:** To validate the entire EgoInfinity pipeline through rigorous experimentation, evaluating its performance across multiple dimensions including perception fidelity, kinematic feasibility of retargeted motions, physical contact consistency, generalization across different robot embodiments, and its utility in real-robot skill acquisition (e.g., grasping, cutting, wiping, pouring).

By achieving these objectives, the research aims to establish a scalable infrastructure that bridges the gap between abundant internet video content and the actionable data requirements of modern robot learning, thereby accelerating the development of more capable and generalist robotic systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The EgoInfinity data engine is designed as a modular pipeline to convert raw internet RGB videos and their associated semantic annotations into structured 4D manipulation data. The system outputs metric hand states, object point clouds and meshes, 6-DoF object pose trajectories, and contact-relevant states, with coordinate-reframed outputs for downstream retargeting and policy learning.

#### 3.1. Modular Pipeline Architecture

The engine employs a modular design, allowing individual components to be upgraded independently as new foundation models or geometry estimation techniques emerge. It operates with a two-pass strategy for scalability:
- **Pass 1:** A lightweight temporal scan identifies segments within videos that contain hands and filters clips based on hand-motion statistics and camera-motion cues, retaining only those likely to contain useful manipulation.
- **Pass 2:** The full reconstruction stack is executed exclusively on these identified active segments.
The current implementation targets internet videos characterized by approximately static camera views.

#### 3.2. Metric-Calibrated Hand–Object Tracking

A core principle of EgoInfinity is cross-module calibration, ensuring that outputs from different modules are aligned within a shared metric scale and a unified camera frame.
- **Unified Metric Geometry:**
 - **MOGE-2** is utilized to estimate the camera's focal length and global metric scale per frame.
 - **FLOW3R** predicts dense depth maps, which are temporally aligned against a static-background template to exclude dynamic regions.
 - **GEOCALIB** estimates the 3D gravity vector from a few frames. These components establish a consistent metric camera-frame geometry for all subsequent estimations.
- **Metric 3D Hand Tracking:**
 - **WILOR** is used to estimate MANO hand parameters (pose and shape) from RGB frames, generating hand meshes, 3D keypoints, and global 6-DoF hand poses.
 - An infilling module addresses missing or unstable hand estimates.
 - The hand trajectory is grounded in the shared metric geometry, ensuring scale and frame consistency with object geometry.
- **Object Discovery, Reconstruction, and Tracking:**
 - Objects are discovered automatically using video descriptions as semantic prompts for **SAM-3** detection.
 - Detected masks are propagated through the video using **SAM-2** for tracking. These masks are then lifted with depth to produce per-frame object point clouds.
 - **SAM-3D** reconstructs the object mesh when sufficient visual evidence is available, providing a canonical mesh, orientation, and metric scale.
 - **FOUNDATIONPOSE++** tracks the object's 6-DoF pose trajectory within the same metric frame as the hands, with additional stabilization for static or weakly observed frames.
 This process yields an initial 4D manipulation state comprising hand and object meshes, keypoints, and 6-DoF poses.

#### 3.3. Interaction-Aware Refinement

To counter temporal instability, drift, and failures under occlusion inherent in pure visual object tracking, EgoInfinity incorporates an interaction-aware refinement stage:
- **Per-frame Proposal:** An initial 6-DoF pose proposal is generated from the object mask and depth, using the canonical SAM-3D orientation and translation derived from back-projected masked points.
- **State Classification:** Each frame is classified into one of three interaction states: `static`, `grasped`, or `moving`. This classification leverages **MEMFOF** optical flow and hand keypoints. A finer six-state label (e.g., `GRASPED_L`, `GRASPED_R`, `GRASPED_BOTH`) is used internally.
- **State-Dependent Pose Refinement:**
 - For `grasped` objects, the object is rigidly attached to the hand frame using a palm-aligned canonical transform derived over the grasp segment, making its motion directly follow the hand.
 - For `static` objects, the pose is locked to a robustly estimated point-cloud centroid.
 - For `moving` objects, the initial proposal is retained.
- **Sanity Checks:** Lightweight checks are applied to suppress implausible scales and spurious background detections.

#### 3.4. Output Cleanup and Coordinate Reframing

- Optional mask erosion, depth-gradient filtering, and statistical outlier removal are applied to clean object point clouds.
- An "exo-to-ego" conversion reframes the recovered 3D scene from an exocentric camera view to a consistent egocentric observation. This is achieved by placing a virtual camera at a fixed offset above a hand-derived anchor (bilateral hand midpoint), aligning its up-axis with the estimated gravity vector, and aiming it at the hand-object interaction. This rigid reframing preserves geometric consistency without generating pixel hallucination.

#### 3.5. Cross-Embodiment Robot Motion Retargeting

This component translates the agent-agnostic 3D hand motion into robot-specific, executable actions. The objective is functional retargeting, preserving task-relevant hand motion without requiring precise human body or arm kinematic imitation.
- **Equivariant Neural Estimation of Kinematic Root Frame:**
 - A neural network, Φ(·), is trained to estimate a shared kinematic root frame (e.g., a robot torso frame) in the camera frame, given reconstructed hand trajectories and optional gravity.
 - The network is designed to be **SE(3)-equivariant** using Vector Neuron (VN) layers, ensuring that rigid transformations of input trajectories result in corresponding transformations of the predicted root frame.
 - Root-frame prediction is formulated as a **flow-matching conditional generation** problem, allowing the model to capture a distribution of plausible root frames, especially under partial human body observations.
- **Robot-Specific Model Training:**
 - The network is trained entirely in **MuJoCo simulation** using procedurally generated paired hand trajectories and ground-truth root poses for each target robot.
 - Extensive **augmentations** (tracking noise, jumps, occlusion, gravity noise, gravity dropout, varied camera poses) are applied during training to enhance robustness to real-world reconstruction errors.
- **Trajectory Post-Processing and Robot Retargeting:**
 - At inference, multiple root-frame hypotheses are sampled from the flow model over overlapping temporal windows, clustered, and interpolated into smooth per-frame root trajectories.
 - Candidates are scored based on IK convergence, residual tracking error, manipulability, joint-limit margin, and smoothness, with the best candidate selected.
 - The selected joint trajectory is refined through interpolation of failed IK frames and uniform filtering to reduce high-frequency jitter.
 - For dexterous hands, finger joints are retargeted separately from MANO hand keypoints using a geometry-based, robot-specific mapping.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The validation of EgoInfinity was conducted across multiple dimensions, including data access, dataset statistics, cross-embodiment retargeting performance, and real-robot skill execution and learning.

#### 4.1. Browser-Based Data Server and Visualization

The authors developed an online, browser-based data server, providing functionality for searching, processing, visualizing, and downloading EgoInfinity-generated 4D manipulation data. This interface allows users to search the internet-video corpus using natural-language keywords, inspect retrieved clips, launch online processing, and visualize intermediate and final outputs. Visualizations include MANO hand mesh trajectories, 6-DoF object trajectories, reconstructed object geometry, point clouds, 3D bounding boxes, depth maps, optical flow, and detected grasp events. This tool facilitates failure diagnosis and interactive data curation.

#### 4.2. Curated Action100M Dataset

An extensible subset of Action100M comprising 106 processed manipulation videos was curated and hosted on the data server. Statistical analysis of this dataset indicated:
- **Clip Duration:** A median duration of 5.8 seconds per clip.
- **Object Categories:** Dominant categories included containers, tools, and food items.
- **Action Verbs:** Frequent action verbs identified were "slice," "insert," "remove," "present," "pour," "pick," and "hold."
- **Interaction State Distribution:** On average, 88% of clips and 47% of objects within the dataset involved manipulation, with a balanced distribution of left, right, and bimanual grasps across manipulated objects.

#### 4.3. Cross-Embodiment Motion Retargeting

The retargeting capabilities were evaluated on three distinct robot embodiments: Unitree G1, NASA’s Robonaut2, and a dual-Franka FR3 setup. The functional retargeting method demonstrated the ability to estimate robot-specific root transformations and solve inverse kinematics (IK) to convert 3D human hand motions into executable robot joint trajectories under the robots' kinematic constraints. Quantitative results summarized the performance:
- **IK Success Rate:** Unitree G1 achieved an IK success rate of 0.821, Robonaut2 0.774, and Dual-Franka 0.706.
- **Position Error:** Mean hand position errors were 2.86 cm for Unitree G1, 6.67 cm for Robonaut2, and 10.27 cm for Dual-Franka.
- **Orientation Error:** Mean orientation errors were 6.73° for Unitree G1, 8.25° for Robonaut2, and 12.17° for Dual-Franka.
- Other metrics such as joint-limit margin and trajectory smoothness were also reported, indicating the kinematic feasibility of the retargeted motions across different robot morphologies. The system demonstrated the transfer of motion across multiple simulated and real robot embodiments.

#### 4.4. Real-Robot Retargeting and Skill Learning

The utility of EgoInfinity-generated motions was tested in real-robot scenarios for both skill learning and direct execution:
- **Skill Learning (Grasping Policy):** Extracted hand motions were used as priors to train a grasping policy on a real LEAP dexterous hand. This policy enabled the robot to grasp diverse objects, including apples, bananas, and soup cans, demonstrating that the generated data supports the acquisition of generalizable manipulation skills.
- **Direct Execution (Functional Skills):** Directly retargeted motions were deployed on a real dual-arm Franka FR3 setup. This demonstrated the functional execution of various manipulation skills, including cutting, pouring (into a bowl and a glass), and wiping (a box and a computer). These results validate the end-to-end pipeline, from raw internet videos to 4D manipulation data, robot-specific retargeting, and successful real-world execution.

In summary, the findings indicate that EgoInfinity provides a functional framework for automatically extracting and processing web-scale human video data into robot-actionable 4D representations, enabling cross-embodiment retargeting and supporting real-robot skill acquisition.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

EgoInfinity presents a significant contribution to the field of robot learning and human-robot interaction by addressing the challenge of data scarcity and diversity. Its primary significance lies in providing a scalable and automated pipeline for converting the vast, unscripted knowledge embedded in internet videos into a structured, robot-usable format.

**Scalable Data Generation:** By introducing a universal 4D manipulation data engine, EgoInfinity offers a method for generating web-scale data that can continuously grow and adapt. This contrasts with traditional approaches that rely on constrained, lab-collected datasets, thereby circumventing a major bottleneck in providing sufficient diverse training data for open-world robot learning. The modular architecture ensures that the system can benefit from ongoing advancements in individual perception or reconstruction components, maintaining its relevance and performance over time.

**Improved Physical Grounding:** The emphasis on extracting metric 3D geometry, 6-DoF object state, and interaction-aware refinement contributes to more physically reliable and grounded data compared to methods that primarily focus on 2D pseudo-actions. This enhanced physical fidelity is crucial for tasks requiring precise contact, force control, and accurate spatial reasoning in robotics. The cross-module calibration and interaction-aware refinement stages are key to reducing drift and contact inconsistencies, improving the practical utility of the generated data.

**Diversity and Open-World Learning:** By leveraging unscripted, equipment-free internet videos, EgoInfinity enables the capture of a broader spectrum of human manipulation behaviors, including pedagogical demonstrations and natural multimodal contexts, which are difficult to replicate in controlled lab environments. This diversity of data is essential for training generalist robots that can adapt to novel objects, environments, and tasks in unstructured settings.

**Agent-Agnostic and Cross-Embodiment Transfer:** The output of EgoInfinity is an agent-agnostic 4D representation, which, coupled with its functional cross-embodiment retargeting method, facilitates transferability across diverse robot morphologies. This capability allows the same extracted human motion data to be translated into executable trajectories for different robots, even from arbitrary viewpoints or when the human body is only partially visible. This reduces the need for robot-specific data collection and enhances the versatility of robot learning from human demonstrations.

The potential impact of EgoInfinity extends to accelerating research in several areas:
- **Robot Learning:** It provides a foundational infrastructure for developing more robust and generalizable robot policies through large-scale imitation learning and reinforcement learning, leveraging human expertise from the web.
- **Multimodal Grounding:** The structured 4D data, combined with semantic annotations from video descriptions, can support advancements in multimodal grounding, linking language, vision, and action for robots.
- **Human-Robot Interaction (HRI):** The ability to extract and retarget complex human manipulation behaviors can inform more natural and intuitive human-robot collaboration and learning paradigms.

While the system currently assumes approximately static-camera videos and does not guarantee precise contact-level accuracy or provide tactile observations, its modular design indicates a pathway for future enhancements. Overall, EgoInfinity represents a step towards creating a scalable bridge from ubiquitous human video content to actionable robot intelligence, thereby advancing the capabilities of robots in open-world environments.
