# Humanoid Robot Acrobatics Utilizing Complete Articulated Rigid Body Dynamics

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces a control architecture enabling humanoid robots to perform highly dynamic acrobatic motions like jumps and flips by explicitly utilizing complete articulated rigid body dynamics throughout planning and control. The framework, which avoids model approximations, demonstrates successful execution of complex maneuvers in simulation with accurate trajectory tracking, robust constraint handling, and precise terminal state control.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots with high degrees of freedom face computational intractability when planning highly dynamic, acrobatic motions using their complete equations of motion.
- Current methods often rely on model simplifications (e.g., constant inertia, linearized dynamics) or restrict robots to quasi-static motions, leading to degraded performance in real-world dynamic tasks.
- Integrating robust constraint handling (joint limits, collision avoidance) into highly dynamic motion planning for complex robots remains challenging without sacrificing performance.

## Method

- A control architecture combining trajectory optimization and operational-space whole-body control is developed to explicitly use complete articulated rigid body dynamics.
- The '==Strangely Supported Deformable Potato==' (SSDP) model serves as a novel abstraction for trajectory optimization, simplifying the problem while retaining critical dynamic properties like adjustable rotational inertia.
- Acrobatic motions are decomposed into launch, flight, and landing phases, each optimized with a quadratic program (QP) and controlled by a multi-layered, prioritized whole-body controller.

## Results

- The simulated humanoid robot successfully executed various acrobatic maneuvers, including box jumps (up and down), a 360° front flip, and a 180° twisting jump.
- The system demonstrated accurate tracking of planned center-of-mass (COM) position, velocity, and angular momentum trajectories across all phases, maintaining contact stability and precise control of torso orientation via inertia shaping during flight.
- Robustness analysis showed precise touchdown orientation control (mean error **0.094**°, standard deviation **0.020**°) across randomly sampled targets, alongside effective avoidance of joint limits and self/external collisions during aggressive movements like the 360° front flip.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Complex acrobatic motions are achievable in high-DOF humanoids by planning and controlling directly on complete articulated rigid body dynamics, avoiding performance-degrading approximations.
> - Model abstraction, such as the SSDP, can effectively bridge the gap between high-level trajectory planning and low-level whole-body control, enabling efficient optimization for dynamic tasks.
> - Hierarchical whole-body control allows for robust simultaneous management of primary tasks (e.g., COM trajectory, angular momentum), high-priority constraints (e.g., collision avoidance, joint limits), and lower-priority posture regulation during aggressive maneuvers.
