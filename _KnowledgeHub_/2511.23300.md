# SafeHumanoid: VLM-RAG-driven Control of Upper Body Impedance for Humanoid Robot

> [!summary] Summary
> Skoltech researchers developed SafeHumanoid, a VLM-RAG-driven framework enabling a humanoid robot to dynamically adjust its upper-body impedance and speed based on real-time egocentric visual and semantic context. The system demonstrated compliant and safer human-robot interactions across various manipulation tasks on a Unitree G1 robot while maintaining task success.

## Problem

- Traditional robotic impedance control often relies on fixed or reactively adjusted gains, lacking the ability to proactively adapt to the diverse and dynamic contexts of human-shared environments.
- Existing Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models primarily focus on task feasibility and positional execution, typically neglecting the dynamic modulation of crucial physical interaction properties like stiffness, damping, and speed.
- Current robot safety measures are often "semantically blind," failing to incorporate a deeper understanding of human-robot interaction context to preemptively adjust physical compliance and prevent unsafe contacts.

## Method

- A client-server architecture is implemented where the robot's egocentric RGB frames are streamed to an offboard workstation for processing by a Molmo-7B VLM, which extracts structured semantic descriptors of the scene.
- A "Naïve ==Retrieval-Augmented Generation==" (RAG) system then uses these semantic descriptors to query a curated database of empirically validated scenarios via FAISS nearest-neighbor search.
- The RAG system retrieves a corresponding payload of 28 joint impedance parameters (Kₚ, K𝒹) and a nominal velocity, which are then applied by the robot's onboard joint-space impedance controller.

## Results

- The Unitree G1 robot consistently adapted its impedance and speed, reducing stiffness and velocity during human proximity or when handling fragile objects in tasks like surface wiping, object handover, and liquid pouring.
- The system maintained task success in all evaluated scenarios while providing perceptibly more compliant and safer human-robot interactions compared to a fixed-gain baseline.
- Generalization to out-of-dataset objects was observed (e.g., a "pin" handover), but the offboard VLM-RAG inference loop introduced a latency of up to **1.4** seconds, indicating a need for real-time performance improvements.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating high-level semantic understanding from VLMs with low-level impedance control through RAG allows for proactive, context-aware safety modulation in humanoid robots.
> - A carefully curated database of empirically validated scenarios, cross-referenced against collaborative robot safety standards (e.g., ISO/TS 15066), is essential for ensuring robust and compliant parameter retrieval.
> - The VLM-RAG approach enables a degree of generalization, allowing the robot to infer appropriate compliant behaviors for novel or out-of-database objects based on semantic task understanding.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The integration of robots into human-centric environments necessitates a paradigm shift from purely task-centric automation to safety- and comfort-aware collaboration. This paper addresses a critical gap in the existing human-robot interaction (HRI) landscape by bridging high-level semantic reasoning with low-level physical compliance control.

Historically, **impedance control** has been the cornerstone for enabling compliant physical interaction in robots, allowing them to exhibit programmed stiffness and damping. However, traditional impedance control often relies on fixed gain settings or reactive adjustments, which fall short in adapting to the vast contextual variability of human-shared spaces. While **vision-based systems** have improved robot anticipation by detecting human presence and adjusting speeds, they are often "semantically blind," lacking a deeper understanding of the interaction context (e.g., whether a human is a passive observer or an active collaborator).

Recent advancements in **Vision-Language Models (VLMs)** and **Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models** have provided robots with unprecedented common sense and reasoning capabilities, enabling them to interpret complex instructions and generate task sequences or joint trajectories. However, the paper highlights that even these advanced models typically focus on task feasibility and positional execution, often neglecting the crucial aspect of how motions should be executed safely in terms of physical interaction—i.e., modulating stiffness, damping, and speed profiles. The current VLM/VLA pipelines lack an inherent mechanism to dynamically schedule **impedance behavior** based on semantic understanding.

This work directly addresses this "missing compliance layer." It leverages **Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)**, a technique shown to improve VLM grounding by constraining model outputs with curated knowledge bases, to ensure safety and consistency. While RAG has been applied to manipulation and drone navigation in other contexts, SafeHumanoid pioneers its application to humanoid HRI, specifically linking semantic scene understanding to dynamic safety parameters like impedance gains and speed.

By combining egocentric vision, VLM semantic interpretation, and RAG-driven parameter retrieval, SafeHumanoid positions itself as a novel approach that moves beyond purely geometric or reactive safety measures. It pre-conditions robot behavior based on semantic context before contact, complementing existing safety standards (e.g., ISO/TS 15066 for power-and-force limiting, ISO 13855 for speed-and-separation monitoring) by providing context-aware *envelopes* for impedance and speed. This proactive, semantically-grounded approach aims to enhance both the safety and comfort of human-robot collaboration, making it a significant contribution to the field of intelligent and adaptive HRI.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind SafeHumanoid stems from the increasing deployment of robots in shared human environments where safety and trustworthiness are paramount, going beyond mere task completion. Current robotic systems struggle to achieve truly safe and comfortable Human–Robot Interaction (HRI) because they often lack the ability to adapt their physical interaction properties (like stiffness, damping, and speed) proactively based on the semantic context of the scene and human proximity.

The **key objectives** of the SafeHumanoid project are:

1. **Bridge Semantic Reasoning and Impedance Control:** To develop a framework that seamlessly links the high-level semantic understanding provided by Vision-Language Models (VLMs) with the low-level physical control parameters of impedance, specifically proportional (Kₚ) and derivative (K𝒹) gains, and nominal velocity (v).
2. **Enable Context-Aware Safety Modulation:** To allow a humanoid robot to dynamically adjust its upper-body impedance and speed profiles based on egocentric visual perception, scene context (e.g., object fragility, task type), and human presence.
3. **Ensure Standard-Aligned Behavior:** To ensure that the retrieved impedance and speed parameters are aligned with collaborative robot safety standards (e.g., ISO/TS 15066 and ISO 13855 guidance), thereby guaranteeing safer and more compliant interactions.
4. **Demonstrate Practical System Integration:** To implement and evaluate the proposed VLM-RAG pipeline on a real humanoid robot (Unitree G1), showcasing a practical integration of high-level AI reasoning with robust low-level motion control.
5. **Maintain Task Success while Improving Safety:** To demonstrate that adaptive impedance control based on semantic reasoning can improve safety and comfort in HRI tasks without compromising the robot's ability to successfully complete its objectives.

In essence, the project aims to equip humanoid robots with "common sense" for physical interaction, allowing them to proactively anticipate and mitigate potential risks by modulating their physical behavior in a nuanced, context-driven manner, rather than relying solely on reactive safeguards or conservative fixed settings.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The SafeHumanoid pipeline is structured as a modular client-server architecture, enabling high-level semantic reasoning to influence low-level impedance control on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot.

#### 3.1. Communication Architecture

The system employs a client-server model: the robot's onboard PC acts as the client, streaming egocentric RGB frames (at 1-2 Hz) via TCP/IP (Wi-Fi) to an offboard workstation (server). The server processes these frames and returns a "validated parameter payload" comprising 28 joint impedance parameters (Kₚ, K𝒹 for 14 upper-body joints) and a nominal velocity (v). To maintain continuity, the robot’s local control loop (50 Hz) uses the most recent valid payload and reverts to a conservative fallback if communication is lost.

#### 3.2. Perception Module (VLM)

Egocentric RGB frames captured by the robot's camera are fed to the **Molmo-7B BnB 4-bit VLM** running on the offboard server. Each frame is processed using a fixed, task-specific JSON schema prompt. This schema enforces predefined keys for extracting semantic descriptors such as:
- Task type (e.g., pick, handover)
- Main object and its fragility
- Human presence
- Obstacle count and type
- Workspace condition
- Arm posture, object location, spacing
- Overall complexity and VLM confidence score

Molmo-7B was chosen for its capability in fine-grained spatial reasoning and egocentric frame interpretation, ensuring the generation of structured, embeddable semantic descriptors.

#### 3.3. Safety Parameter Generation (RAG)

This module implements a two-stage, "Naïve Retrieval-Augmented Generation" (RAG) system, emphasizing retrieval over complex LLM-based generation.

1. **Retrieval Stage:** The structured JSON output from the VLM is embedded into a 384-dimensional vector using the `all-MiniLM-L6-v2` sentence transformer. This query embedding is then used with **FAISS** (Facebook AI Similarity Search) to perform an exact nearest-neighbor search against pre-computed embeddings of scenarios in a **curated scenario database**. This database contains empirically validated scenarios, each linked to a specific payload of joint impedance parameters (Kₚ, K𝒹) and a nominal velocity (v). The output is the index of the top candidate and a similarity score.
2. **Generation Stage:** Once the best-matching scenario is identified, its corresponding payload (28 impedance parameters, nominal joint velocity, and metadata) is returned in a standardized JSON/CSV format.

A **fallback logic** is implemented for robustness: if multiple candidates have similar scores or the best match falls below a confidence threshold, the system rejects retrieval and reverts to a conservative fallback parameter profile, preventing unsafe behaviors in ambiguous situations.

#### 3.4. Robot Control Module

Operating onboard the Unitree G1 at 50 Hz, this module integrates inverse kinematics (IK) with a joint-space impedance controller.

1. **Inverse Kinematics (IK):** An IK solver, operating on a reduced robot model (non-arm joints locked), calculates a feasible joint configuration (q_ref) and feed-forward torques (τ_ff) for gravity compensation (using the Recursive Newton–Euler Algorithm - RNEA). For this work, predefined end-effector target poses were used as IK inputs, though the architecture is designed to be agnostic to the source of joint targets.
2. **Joint-Space Impedance Control:** The G1’s actuators are commanded in position-control mode using the retrieved Kₚ and K𝒹 gains, along with the IK-generated q_ref, desired joint velocities (q̇_ref), and feed-forward torques (τ_ff). The control law is given by:
 `τ = Kₚ(q_ref - q) + K𝒹(q̇_ref - q̇) + τ_ff`
 where q and q̇ are measured joint positions and velocities. By adjusting Kₚ and K𝒹, the system effectively regulates the apparent stiffness and damping of the robot's end-effector, directly influencing interaction forces via the Jacobian (F = J(q)⁻ᵀ τ).

#### 3.5. Data Collection and Scenario Database

A curated dataset of 16 manipulation scenarios was created through pilot experiments on the Unitree G1. Tasks included cube pickup, bottle pouring, surface wiping, and object handover, performed both autonomously and in the presence of human hands. Logged data included egocentric RGB video, joint states, measured torques, and active PD gains. Successful trials, free from unsafe contact forces, were encoded as structured entries in a CSV database. Each entry linked a natural-language description (matching the VLM schema) and metadata (object fragility, human presence) to empirically validated per-joint Kₚ, K𝒹 gains, and a nominal velocity. Parameter ranges were Kₚ ∈ and K𝒹 ∈ [0.1, 2.0], with three discrete nominal speeds.

#### 3.6. Parameter Validation

Each candidate parameter set in the database underwent a rigorous two-step validation:
1. **Stability Screening:** Isolated joint tests confirmed no oscillatory or divergent behavior.
2. **Safety Compliance:** Parameter sets were cross-checked against ISO/TS 15066 and ISO 13855 guidance, ensuring maximum joint stiffness for fragile objects kept contact forces below pain thresholds, and nominal speeds for human-present scenarios were compatible with speed-and-separation monitoring. Unsafe scenarios were excluded.

This comprehensive methodology ensures that SafeHumanoid not only performs context-aware impedance modulation but does so in a stable and safety-compliant manner.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on the Unitree G1 humanoid robot demonstrated the core capabilities of the SafeHumanoid pipeline in dynamically adjusting upper-body impedance and speed based on egocentric visual context and human presence.

#### 4.1. Adaptive Impedance and Speed Modulation

Across six tabletop manipulation tasks, the system consistently adapted its control parameters:
- **Surface Wiping:** In the absence of a human, the robot operated with baseline stiffness. When a human hand entered the workspace, the pipeline immediately reduced Kₚ (stiffness), increased K𝒹 (damping), and lowered the nominal velocity (v), resulting in a perceptibly more compliant and safer wiping motion near the human.
- **Object Handover (Cube and Pin):**
 - For a cube handover (an object represented in the database), the system correctly switched to lower stiffness during human contact and reverted to baseline settings once the human hand was removed, demonstrating appropriate and transient safety modulation.
 - Remarkably, for a "pin" handover (an object not explicitly in the scenario database), the VLM-RAG module successfully generalized, retrieving an appropriate compliant profile. This indicates a degree of robustness to out-of-dataset objects, leveraging the semantic understanding of "handover" and "human presence."
- **Liquid Pouring (Soy Sauce Bottle Handover):** In a fragile object handling scenario (pouring liquid), the system dynamically adjusted gains: the nominal medium velocity was reduced to a slower profile upon detecting liquid handling, and then restored once the bottle was safely placed. This showcases adaptation to both object fragility and task-specific requirements.

#### 4.2. Task Success and Safety

In all evaluated scenarios, the robot maintained task success while simultaneously providing safer and more adaptive behavior compared to a fixed-gain baseline. The impedance modulation was consistent with the semantic context, preventing excessive forces during human interaction and promoting a sense of comfort and trust.

#### 4.3. Observed Latency

A significant limitation identified during experiments was the **inference latency** of the offboard VLM-RAG loop, which reached **up to 1.4 seconds**. This delay, attributed to large-model inference and image transmission, makes the current system unsatisfactory for highly dynamic human-robot interaction settings where real-time responsiveness is critical.

#### 4.4. Generalization and Compliance

The ability to generalize to out-of-dataset objects (like the pin) through semantic understanding, combined with the successful modulation of Kₚ, K𝒹, and v in alignment with human presence and task fragility, validates the VLM-RAG approach for achieving context-aware compliance. The system's capacity to return to baseline settings after human departure further highlights its adaptive nature.

In summary, SafeHumanoid effectively demonstrated that grounding semantic reasoning in impedance control is a viable path toward safer and standard-compliant humanoid collaboration, despite the current real-time performance challenges due to latency.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The SafeHumanoid research represents a significant advancement in the field of human-robot interaction (HRI) and intelligent robotics, offering several important implications and potential impacts:

#### 5.1. Enhancing Human-Robot Safety and Trust

The most immediate and critical impact is the improvement of safety in shared human-robot workspaces. By proactively adjusting impedance and speed based on real-time semantic understanding (e.g., human presence, object fragility), SafeHumanoid moves beyond reactive safety mechanisms. This context-aware physical compliance can drastically reduce the risk of harm during accidental contact, fostering greater trust and acceptance of robots in sensitive environments such as homes, hospitals, and collaborative workspaces.

#### 5.2. Bridging the Semantic-to-Safety Gap

The work successfully addresses a crucial "missing compliance layer" in current VLM/VLA-driven robotics. While VLMs have empowered robots with high-level reasoning and task planning, their outputs often lack the crucial physical interaction parameters needed for safe operation. SafeHumanoid demonstrates a concrete method to bridge this gap, integrating abstract semantic understanding directly into low-level impedance control, thereby enabling robots to not just *understand* tasks but also to *execute* them safely.

#### 5.3. Alignment with Safety Standards

By grounding its parameter selection in a curated database validated against ISO/TS 15066 and ISO 13855 guidelines, SafeHumanoid offers a practical framework for developing robots that inherently comply with collaborative robot safety standards. This is crucial for the regulatory approval and widespread adoption of humanoids in real-world applications, moving beyond heuristic approaches to a more systematically validated safety profile.

#### 5.4. Generalization and Robustness

The demonstration of generalization to out-of-dataset objects, such as the "pin," suggests that the VLM-RAG approach can provide robustness to novel scenarios, inferring appropriate safety profiles from semantic similarity. This is vital for real-world deployment where robots will inevitably encounter situations not explicitly programmed or pre-validated.

#### 5.5. Foundation for Future Intelligent Control

SafeHumanoid lays a strong foundation for future research in several directions:
- **Low-Latency, Edge-AI Robotics:** The identified latency limitation highlights the need for continued research into efficient VLM deployment (e.g., model distillation, edge inference), which will drive advancements in real-time perception and control for dynamic HRI.
- **Automated Knowledge Base Construction:** Future work on automating the creation and validation of scenario databases through simulation and self-labeling will enhance scalability and reduce human effort, accelerating the development of more diverse and robust safety profiles.
- **Richer Physical Reasoning:** Expanding semantic grounding to include continuous physical properties (e.g., hardness, weight, friction) rather than discrete labels will enable even more nuanced and adaptive impedance control, leading to superior robot-object and robot-human interactions.
- **Multimodal Integration:** The proposed integration of depth sensing with VLM-based object localization for distance- and context-aware compliance, as well as the use of VLA models for trajectory generation, points towards a future where robots can achieve higher levels of autonomy and adaptability in complex human environments.

In conclusion, SafeHumanoid represents a significant step towards creating more intelligent, safer, and trustworthy humanoid robots. Its VLM-RAG-driven approach to impedance control is a crucial enabler for truly collaborative HRI, paving the way for robots that can operate intuitively and safely alongside humans in a multitude of dynamic, unstructured environments.
