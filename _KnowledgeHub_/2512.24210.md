# GR-Dexter Technical Report

> [!summary] Summary
> ByteDance Seed developed GR-Dexter, a holistic framework for generalist manipulation using a bimanual robot with 21-DoF dexterous hands. It achieves strong long-horizon manipulation capabilities and improves generalization by leveraging a multi-source data pyramid and a 4-billion parameter VLA model.

## Problem

- Most existing VLA policies are deployed on robots with simple grippers, limiting dexterity for human-level manipulation in complex environments.
- Extending VLA capabilities to high-DoF dexterous hands introduces significant challenges in control, planning, and perception due to expanded action space and occlusions.
- There is a severe scarcity of high-quality, large-scale real-robot data specifically for dexterous bimanual manipulation, which is critical for training data-driven VLA models.

## Method

- A compact, 21-DoF linkage-driven anthropomorphic hand, ==ByteDexter V2==, was developed with integrated tactile sensing for bimanual manipulation on Franka FR3 arms.
- A multi-source 'data pyramid' was created, comprising teleoperated robot trajectories, web-scale vision-language data, cross-embodiment robot data, and human trajectory data.
- A 4-billion parameter **GR-Dexter** VLA model based on a ==Mixture-of-Transformer== architecture was co-trained using a ==dynamic mixing strategy== and a unified data preprocessing/retargeting pipeline.

## Results

- GR-Dexter achieved a **0.97** success rate in in-domain makeup decluttering and maintained a **0.89** success rate in out-of-distribution layouts, significantly outperforming a plain VLA baseline's **0.64**.
- The framework demonstrated strong generalization in pick-and-place tasks, achieving an **0.85** success rate for 23 unseen objects and **0.83** for unseen language instructions.
- The system successfully performed complex, long-horizon bimanual tasks in the real world, such as vacuuming and bread serving, showcasing practical dexterous and coordinated manipulation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A holistic integration of advanced hardware, intuitive teleoperation, and a multi-source data strategy is essential for realizing VLA-based generalist dexterous manipulation.
> - Leveraging cross-embodiment robot data and large-scale human trajectory data significantly overcomes the data scarcity challenge for training high-DoF dexterous robot policies.
> - Co-training with diverse vision-language and embodied data substantially enhances the generalization capabilities of VLA models to unseen objects, instructions, and environmental configurations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation is undergoing a transformative period, largely driven by the advancements in vision-language-action (VLA) models. These models, akin to large language models for robots, enable language-conditioned control and long-horizon instruction following, allowing robots to perform complex tasks based on natural language commands. However, the vast majority of existing VLA policies are deployed on robots equipped with simple, gripper-based end effectors. While effective for many pick-and-place tasks, grippers fundamentally limit the dexterity and versatility required for human-level manipulation in unstructured, human-centric environments.

GR-Dexter directly addresses this critical gap by extending VLA capabilities to **bimanual robots with high degree-of-freedom (DoF) dexterous hands**. This move represents a significant leap in complexity. Dexterous hands introduce a dramatically expanded action space (dozens of additional DoFs), leading to challenges in control, planning, and particularly, perception due to frequent occlusions between fingers and with target objects. Furthermore, VLA models are data-driven, and the scarcity of high-quality, large-scale real-robot data for dexterous bimanual manipulation is a major bottleneck.

The paper positions itself within the context of recent efforts to build more generalist robot policies, often leveraging large-scale datasets and pre-training. It builds upon ideas from prior VLA models (e.g., GR-3, RT-1/RT-2, Robocat, OpenVLA), but distinguishes itself by specifically tackling the "underexplored" domain of dexterous hands. It also relates to works exploring human-to-robot imitation (e.g., WHIRL, EgoDex) and cross-embodiment learning (e.g., GR00T N1, Octo) to mitigate the data scarcity issue, recognizing the potential of human demonstration data and diverse robot datasets. By combining novel hardware design, an intuitive teleoperation system, and a multi-source data training recipe, GR-Dexter aims to provide a practical pathway toward realizing the promise of generalist dexterous manipulation, moving beyond the limitations of simple grippers and single-arm setups.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of the GR-Dexter project is to develop a **holistic hardware-model-data framework for VLA-based generalist manipulation on a bimanual dexterous-hand robot**. This overarching goal can be broken down into several key sub-objectives, driven by specific motivations:

1. **Develop a Compact, High-DoF Dexterous Robotic Hand:** Motivated by the limitations of gripper-based systems and the desire for human-level manipulation, a key objective was to design an advanced anthropomorphic hand. This new hand, ByteDexter V2, aims to overcome the size and complexity issues of existing dexterous hands while increasing functional dexterity (e.g., more thumb DoFs, tactile sensing).
2. **Facilitate Efficient Data Collection for High-DoF Bimanual Manipulation:** Recognizing that data scarcity is a major hurdle for VLA models, especially with complex dexterous hands, a crucial objective was to create an intuitive and efficient teleoperation system. This system needed to simplify the daunting task of collecting real-robot trajectories for a 56-DoF bimanual setup (two 7-DoF arms plus two 21-DoF hands), making the process feasible for long-duration tasks.
3. **Construct a Robust VLA Model and Training Recipe for Dexterous Manipulation:** The core objective on the software side was to adapt and extend existing VLA models to the high-dimensional action space of dexterous hands. This required a training strategy capable of leveraging diverse data sources to achieve strong in-domain performance and, critically, robust generalization to unseen objects, instructions, and environmental configurations. The motivation here is to harness the power of large-scale vision-language models and cross-embodiment learning to compensate for limited on-robot dexterous data.
4. **Demonstrate Generalist Capabilities in Real-World Scenarios:** The ultimate objective was to validate the entire framework through real-world experiments. This included showing proficiency in long-horizon, everyday manipulation tasks requiring bimanual coordination and fine motor skills, as well as demonstrating generalization to novel objects and abstract instructions—a key indicator of "generalist" capability.

In essence, the project is motivated by the conviction that dexterous hands are essential for robots to operate effectively in human-centric environments, and that combining advanced hardware, intuitive control, and multi-faceted data learning is the path to overcoming the inherent challenges of high-DoF dexterous manipulation for general-purpose robotic tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

GR-Dexter adopts a holistic approach by integrating hardware design, data collection infrastructure, and a sophisticated VLA model training recipe.

#### 3.1. Hardware: The ByteDexter V2 Robotic Hand

The foundation of GR-Dexter's hardware is the **ByteDexter V2 hand**, an upgraded version of the ByteDexter V1. It is a compact, 21-DoF linkage-driven anthropomorphic hand, designed as a self-contained, modular end-effector. Key design features include:
- **High DoF:** Each of the four fingers has four DoFs (two at MCP, one at PIP, one at DIP), and the thumb has five DoFs (universal joint at CMC, additional revolute joint, and an underactuated IP joint), totaling 21 DoFs. This enhances its ability to perform human-like grasping and manipulation.
- **Compact Form Factor:** Actuators are integrated within the palm, achieving a reduced size (219 mm height, 108 mm width) compared to its predecessor.
- **Linkage-Driven Transmission:** This mechanism is chosen for its advantages in force transparency, durability, and ease of maintenance, contrasting with tendon-driven designs.
- **Underactuation:** The DIP joints of the four fingers and the IP joint of the thumb are underactuated, using a biomimetic four-bar linkage that couples the DIP to the PIP, mimicking human hand kinematics.
- **Tactile Sensing:** High-density piezoresistive tactile arrays are embedded in all five fingertips, providing fine spatial resolution for contact location and force magnitude, crucial for delicate manipulation.

The bimanual system comprises two ByteDexter V2 hands mounted on two Franka Research 3 (FR3) arms, resulting in a 56-DoF robot (7 DoF per arm + 21 DoF per hand). Four global RGB-D cameras provide comprehensive visual observations, mitigating occlusions.

#### 3.2. Data Collection and Curation

To address the challenges of collecting data for such a high-DoF system, GR-Dexter employs a multi-pronged data strategy:

1. **Bimanual Teleoperation System:** A custom teleoperation interface enables efficient real-robot data collection. It consists of:
 - **Meta Quest VR headset:** For tracking human wrist poses.
 - **Manus Metagloves:** For capturing precise human hand movements.
 - **VR Quest controllers:** Mounted on gloves to improve wrist-hand tracking reliability.
 - **Foot pedals:** To enable/disable teleoperation.
 Human motions are retargeted in real-time to robot joint position commands via a whole-body controller and a constrained optimization problem for hand motion, ensuring kinematic consistency and collision avoidance. This system was used to collect approximately 20 hours of teleoperated robot trajectories for specific tasks.

2. **Multi-Source Data Pyramid:** GR-Dexter leverages a "data pyramid" consisting of four types of data:
 - **Teleoperated Robot Trajectories:** Task-specific data collected on the ByteDexter V2 platform.
 - **Web-scale Vision-Language Data:** Reused from GR-3, covering tasks like image captioning, visual question answering, and image grounding. This data primarily trains the Vision-Language Model (VLM) backbone.
 - **Cross-Embodiment Real-Robot Data:** To overcome data scarcity, existing open-source bimanual humanoid datasets are utilized, including Fourier ActionNet, OpenLoong Baihu, and RoboMIND. These datasets encompass diverse robot embodiments and tasks.
 - **Human Trajectory Data:** Large-scale egocentric video data (over 800 hours), supplemented with additional data from Pico VR devices, providing diverse everyday dexterous behaviors.

#### 3.3. Cross-Embodiment Motion Retargeting and Transferring

A crucial part of the methodology is a unified preprocessing and retargeting pipeline to align visual perceptions and action spaces across heterogeneous data sources:
- **Visual Alignment:** All images are resized and cropped to a standardized format, ensuring consistent scale for robots, hands, and objects.
- **Cross-Embodiment Trajectory Transfer:** High-quality trajectories from other robot datasets are selected and retargeted to the ByteDexter V2 hand. This is achieved by a "fingertip-centric alignment," preserving task-relevant contact geometry while being robust to joint-level differences.
- **Human Trajectory Transfer:** Human demonstrations, which have a substantial kinematic gap and noise (ego-motion, jitter), undergo careful filtering based on hand visibility and velocity. They are then mapped into the same visual and kinematic representation as robot data.
- **Action Space Handling:** During training, unavailable or unreliable action dimensions (e.g., specific joints not present in a source embodiment) are masked out.

#### 3.4. GR-Dexter Model and Training Recipe

The **GR-Dexter model** is a VLA model built on a **Mixture-of-Transformer architecture**, similar to GR-3, with 4 billion parameters. It takes a language instruction, current observation, and robot state to generate a `k`-length action chunk. The action space is high-dimensional (88-length vector per action), encompassing:
- Arm joint actions (7 DoFs per arm)
- Arm end-effector poses (6D per arm)
- Hand joint actions (16 active DoFs per hand)
- Fingertip positions (3D per finger)

The model is co-trained on the mixture of all four data sources using a dynamic mixing strategy across mini-batches. The co-training objective is the sum of two loss functions:
- **Next-token-prediction loss:** Used for training the VLM backbone with vision-language data.
- **Flow-matching objective:** Used for training the VLM backbone and the action Diffusion Transformer (DiT) with robot trajectory data.

During policy rollout, the model generates future action chunks, which are then smoothed by a parameterized trajectory optimizer to ensure delicate grasping and smooth transitions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

GR-Dexter's capabilities were evaluated through extensive real-world experiments across two primary categories: long-horizon everyday manipulation and generalizable pick-and-place tasks.

#### 4.1. Long-Horizon Dexterous Manipulation

- **Makeup Decluttering Task:** This task involved manipulating items of diverse shapes and sizes, including articulated objects like drawers, requiring coordinated bimanual and fine-grained skills.
 - **Basic Settings (In-domain):** In scenarios with object layouts seen during training, GR-Dexter achieved a 0.97 success rate, comparable to a plain VLA baseline trained only on robot data (0.96). This indicates that co-training with vision-language data preserves strong in-domain performance.
 - **Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Settings:** When evaluated on five unseen object layouts, the plain VLA baseline's performance dropped significantly to 0.64. In contrast, GR-Dexter maintained a much higher success rate of 0.89. This finding highlights that co-training with vision-language data substantially enhances generalization to novel spatial configurations.
- **Additional Qualitative Results:** GR-Dexter successfully performed more complex long-horizon tasks involving tool use, demonstrating robust bimanual dexterity:
 - **Vacuuming:** The robot accurately grasped a tabletop vacuum, pressed its power button with a thumb, and swept confetti.
 - **Bread Serving:** The robot stably grasped food tongs to retrieve a croissant, while the other hand held a plate, then precisely placed the croissant onto the plate. These tasks showcased practical bimanual dexterity and compliant manipulation.

#### 4.2. Generalizable Pick-and-Place

This evaluation focused on the model's ability to generalize to unseen objects and instructions in a pick-and-place task, comparing three models: plain VLA, GR-Dexter without cross-embodiment data, and the full GR-Dexter.
- **Basic Settings (In-domain):** With seen objects, the plain VLA achieved 0.87 success, GR-Dexter (w/o cross-embodiment) achieved 0.85, and the full GR-Dexter achieved the best performance at 0.93. This result suggests that while vision-language data alone might not always boost in-domain performance for specific tasks, carefully processed and aligned cross-embodiment data significantly improves overall robustness and performance even in familiar settings.
- **Unseen Objects:** When tasked with picking up 23 novel objects, the plain VLA's performance dropped significantly. GR-Dexter (w/o cross-embodiment) showed improvement but still suffered from inaccurate grasping. The full GR-Dexter demonstrated strong generalization, achieving a 0.85 success rate, indicating that skills learned from cross-embodiment data were crucial for grasping unfamiliar items.
- **Unseen Instructions:** For tasks involving unseen language instructions (e.g., "pick up the darkest object," "pick up the fruit"), the plain VLA's performance again declined sharply. GR-Dexter (w/o cross-embodiment) struggled with interpretation, while the full GR-Dexter exhibited robust generalization, achieving a 0.83 success rate. This demonstrates the effectiveness of co-training with diverse vision-language data for interpreting abstract and novel commands.

Overall, the main findings clearly indicate that GR-Dexter achieves strong long-horizon manipulation capabilities with its 21-DoF ByteDexter V2 hands. Crucially, its carefully curated data pyramid—combining teleoperated robot trajectories, vision-language data, cross-embodiment demonstrations, and human trajectories—significantly improves robustness and generalization, particularly in out-of-distribution scenarios involving unseen objects, unseen instructions, and novel spatial configurations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

GR-Dexter represents a significant practical step toward **generalist dexterous-hand robotic manipulation**, addressing several long-standing challenges in the field. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Bridging the Dexterity Gap in VLA Robotics:** The most prominent impact is its successful extension of VLA models from simple grippers to complex, high-DoF bimanual dexterous hands. This demonstrates that human-like manipulation capabilities, essential for robots operating in unstructured, human-centric environments, are becoming increasingly feasible with VLA paradigms.
2. **Integrated Hardware-Software Solution:** The project's "holistic" approach—combining the advanced **ByteDexter V2 hand**, a practical **bimanual teleoperation system**, and a sophisticated **VLA training recipe**—provides a complete blueprint for tackling dexterous manipulation. The ByteDexter V2 itself is a notable contribution, offering a compact, durable, and highly articulated anthropomorphic hand with integrated tactile sensing.
3. **Scalable Data Strategy for Dexterous Manipulation:** GR-Dexter's multi-source data pyramid and robust retargeting pipeline offer a crucial pathway to overcome the severe data scarcity issue inherent in high-DoF dexterous robotics. By effectively leveraging cross-embodiment robot data and large-scale human trajectory data, it reduces the reliance on costly and time-consuming real-robot demonstrations, making VLA training for dexterous hands more scalable and accessible. This approach can inform future data collection and integration strategies for complex robotic systems.
4. **Enhanced Generalization Capabilities:** The experimental results unequivocally demonstrate that the co-training strategy significantly boosts the generalization of VLA policies to out-of-distribution settings, including unseen objects, novel instructions, and varying environmental layouts. This improved robustness is vital for deploying robots in dynamic, real-world scenarios where encountering novel situations is inevitable.
5. **Foundation for Future Generalist Robots:** By showcasing reliable performance in long-horizon, bimanual, and tool-use tasks, GR-Dexter lays a strong foundation for developing truly generalist robots capable of a wide array of everyday manipulation tasks. The work highlights the potential of combining embodied hardware design with advanced learning techniques to achieve versatile and adaptive robotic agents.

**Limitations and Future Work:** The authors acknowledge several limitations, providing clear directions for future research. Firstly, the reliance on "only a few hundred hours of human trajectories" suggests that further scaling of human data could yield even greater benefits. Secondly, the current separate control of the hand and arm might hinder optimal coordination in contact-rich tasks, pointing towards the need for more tightly integrated hand-arm control abstractions. These areas represent opportunities for further advancements, particularly in leveraging even more diverse and accessible cross-embodiment trajectories and developing embodiment-agnostic control policies.

In conclusion, GR-Dexter is a compelling demonstration of what is achievable when advanced robotic hardware is coupled with innovative data strategies and state-of-the-art vision-language-action models. It moves the needle closer to robots that can not only understand complex commands but also execute them with the finesse and versatility of human hands.
