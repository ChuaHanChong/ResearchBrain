# SPIRAL: Learning to Search and Aggregate

> [!summary] Summary
> The SPIRAL framework trains large language models to optimize the combined use of sequential, parallel, and aggregative inference compute end-to-end. This approach yields models that achieve up to 11x higher scaling efficiency in pass@k and 13.5% greater performance with recursive self-aggregation on mathematical reasoning tasks.

## Problem

- Large language models often struggle to effectively utilize scaled inference compute, leading to performance plateaus on complex reasoning tasks despite increased resources.
- Current reinforcement learning training paradigms primarily optimize LMs for sequential reasoning, neglecting test-time strategies like parallel sampling and aggregation, which rely on external, hand-designed scaffolds.
- Models frequently misallocate sequential 'thinking tokens' and generate redundant parallel traces, limiting the actual benefits of scaling compute.

## Method

- Introduced **SPIRAL**, an end-to-end ==reinforcement learning== framework, to train language models to optimally coordinate sequential, parallel, and aggregative inference compute.
- Formulated the objective using a two-level optimization, combining Set Reinforcement Learning for search trace generation with standard RL for aggregation trace generation.
- The framework decomposes the policy gradient to jointly optimize for diverse, useful search traces and effective synthesis of information from multiple attempts.

## Results

- SPIRAL achieved up to **11x** higher scaling efficiency in pass@k performance on mathematical reasoning tasks, demonstrating improved generation of diverse and useful individual search traces.
- Models trained with SPIRAL showed up to **13.5%** higher performance when evaluated with recursive self-aggregation compared to baselines.
- Recursive self-aggregation using SPIRAL-trained models scaled more favorably in performance-per-token, indicating enhanced efficiency from end-to-end learning of these primitives.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Jointly optimizing diverse search and aggregation processes through Set RL enables language models to discover more effective and autonomous reasoning strategies.
> - Training models to learn both search and aggregation significantly outperforms reliance on fixed, rule-based aggregation methods at test time.
> - Exposing language models to all three compute primitives during training allows them to internalize and efficiently utilize diverse forms of inference, bridging the training-test time gap.
