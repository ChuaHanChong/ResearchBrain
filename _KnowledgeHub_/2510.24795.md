# A Survey on Efficient Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> This survey paper provides the first comprehensive review of efficient Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, consolidating advancements in model design, training, and data collection to enable practical deployment on resource-constrained devices. It details methodologies that significantly reduce computational costs and data footprints, offering a structured framework and future roadmap for scalable embodied intelligence.

## Problem

- Foundational VLA models inherit prohibitive computational and data footprints from their LLM and VLM predecessors, hindering real-world deployment.
- High inference latency and insufficient control frequency make VLAs incompatible for real-time robotic manipulation on edge devices.
- Existing approaches require excessive pre-training costs and rely on inefficient, labor-intensive data collection, creating significant barriers to practical application.

## Method

- The authors conducted the first comprehensive survey specifically dedicated to ==efficient VLAs==, covering the entire =="model-training-data" process==.
- They introduced a novel, systematically structured taxonomy organizing core technical landscapes into three pillars: ==Efficient Model Design==, ==Efficient Training==, and ==Efficient Data Collection==.
- The survey critically distills key challenges and current limitations, outlining forward-looking research directions to guide future endeavors in scalable embodied intelligence.

## Results

- Efficient Model Design leverages innovations such as linear-time attention, Mamba-based backbones, parallel action decoding, layer pruning, and quantization to reduce model size and inference costs (e.g., SARA-RT, RoboMamba, OpenVLA).
- Efficient Training employs data-efficient pre-training (e.g., self-supervised learning on egocentric videos, mixed data co-training) and parameter-efficient post-training methods (e.g., LoRA) to minimize computational and data demands during learning.
- Efficient Data Collection shifts from labor-intensive teleoperation to scalable methods including massively parallel simulation, utilization of internet-scale and cross-domain human data, and autonomous self-exploration, often augmented by LLM-driven semantic annotations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Efficiency is a fundamental prerequisite for VLA models to transition from prototypes to practical, deployable systems on resource-constrained edge devices.
> - A holistic approach addressing model architecture, training paradigms, and data acquisition strategies concurrently is essential for achieving substantial efficiency gains in VLAs.
> - Research efforts are converging on techniques like parameter-efficient fine-tuning, leveraging massively parallel simulation, and utilizing vast internet-scale human video data to overcome the inherent resource demands of powerful VLA models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represent a critical and rapidly advancing frontier in embodied artificial intelligence. They aim to endow physical entities (like robots and autonomous vehicles) with the ability to perceive the world through vision, understand high-level instructions through language, and execute complex actions. By integrating the semantic reasoning power of Large Language Models (LLMs) and the cross-modal perception of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) with physical action, VLAs offer unprecedented dexterity and generalizability in unstructured environments. Their potential applications range from autonomous driving and industrial manufacturing to medical robotics and smart homes.

However, foundational VLA models, while exhibiting remarkable generalist capabilities, are inherited with the "prohibitive computational and data footprints" of their LLM and VLM predecessors. This poses significant bottlenecks for real-world deployment, particularly on resource-constrained edge devices. The core challenges include:
1. **Real-Time Incompatibility:** High inference latency and insufficient control frequency for responsive robotic manipulation.
2. **Excessive Computational Cost:** Prohibitively large pre-training requirements (e.g., OpenVLA consuming 21,500 A100-GPU hours).
3. **Inefficient Data Collection:** Reliance on large-scale, labor-intensive datasets (e.g., π₀ requiring over 10,000 hours of robotic trajectories).

This survey critically fits into the research landscape by addressing this glaring "efficiency gap." While several excellent surveys have covered general VLA models or broader embodied AI, a systematic review *specifically dedicated to the crucial aspect of efficiency in VLAs* has been conspicuously absent. As Figure 4 illustrates, the emergence of "Efficient VLAs" has accelerated significantly from late 2024 onwards, highlighting a growing recognition of this imperative. This work fills that critical void, providing the first comprehensive, unified framework to consolidate the disparate research efforts focused on optimizing VLAs for practical, resource-constrained deployment. It moves beyond merely describing what VLAs are capable of, to detailing *how* they can be made viable for real-world interactive systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching goal of this survey is to provide a comprehensive view of the technological advancements in efficient VLAs. The key objectives and motivations are explicitly articulated:

- **Pioneering Survey:** To present "the first comprehensive survey specifically dedicated to the realm of efficient VLAs that covers the entire 'model-training-data' process." This addresses the critical gap in existing literature, where prior surveys focused on general VLA components, architectures, or training paradigms without a dedicated emphasis on efficiency.
- **Novel Taxonomy:** To introduce "a novel and systematically structured taxonomy that organizes the core technical landscape for building efficient VLAs into three interconnected pillars: Efficient Model Design, Efficient Training, and Efficient Data Collection." This taxonomy aims to unify and consolidate fragmented research efforts, providing a clear framework for understanding and classifying advancements.
- **Future Roadmap:** To critically distill "the key challenges and current limitations plaguing the field, thereby outlining promising and forward-looking research directions to inspire and guide future endeavors in scalable embodied intelligence." This objective aims to serve as a catalyst for future research, pushing the boundaries of what is possible in practical VLA deployment.

The motivation for these objectives stems directly from the "profound inefficiencies" of foundational VLAs, which create "formidable barriers to transitioning digital intelligence into real-time actuation on edge devices." The authors highlight that efficiency is "not merely an optional optimization but a fundamental prerequisite for unlocking the transformative potential of VLAs across the broader robotics landscape." This survey, therefore, is motivated by the urgent need to bridge the gap between powerful but resource-intensive VLA prototypes and their practical, scalable deployment in diverse real-world applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors adopt a systematic and comprehensive approach to survey the field of Efficient VLAs, organizing their findings around a novel, unified taxonomy. This taxonomy is designed to cover the entire lifecycle of VLA development, from their underlying architecture to how they are trained and the data they consume.

The core methodology is structured around "the entire model-training-data pipeline" and categorizes current techniques into three interconnected pillars:

1. **Efficient Model Design:** This pillar focuses on intrinsic architectural and structural optimizations aimed at reducing computational burdens during inference and minimizing model size. It is further divided into:
 - **Efficient Architectures:** Explores innovations in attention mechanisms (e.g., linear-time attention, masking, KV-cache), alternative backbone models (e.g., Mamba), efficient action decoding (e.g., parallel, generative), lightweight components, Mixture-of-Experts (MoE), and hierarchical systems.
 - **Model Compression:** Delves into techniques that reduce representational redundancy, such as layer pruning, quantization (reducing numerical precision of weights), and token optimization (compression, pruning, caching of visual and action tokens).

2. **Efficient Training:** This pillar addresses strategies to reduce the computational and data demands during both the initial learning phase (pre-training) and subsequent specialization (post-training). It includes:
 - **Efficient Pre-training:** Focuses on instilling foundational action capabilities into pre-trained VLMs or training VLAs from scratch with minimal overhead. Sub-categories include data-efficient pre-training (self-supervised, mixed data co-training), efficient action representation (compression, innovative modeling), and other strategies (multi-stage training, RL, LoRA). The survey explicitly defines pre-training from a VLA-centric functional perspective, encompassing initial action-aware policy creation.
 - **Efficient Post-training:** Concentrates on adapting a generalist policy to excel in specific tasks, environments, or resource constraints. This involves supervised fine-tuning (e.g., parameter-efficient methods like LoRA) and RL-based methods (online and offline RL).

3. **Efficient Data Collection:** This pillar tackles the bottlenecks in acquiring and utilizing robotic data, aiming to make dataset acquisition more scalable and efficient. It covers:
 - **Human-in-the-loop Data Collection:** Optimizing human involvement through more efficient interfaces or supervisory roles.
 - **Simulation Data Collection:** Leveraging synthetic environments for large-scale, diverse data generation while addressing the sim-to-real gap.
 - **Internet-Scale and Cross-Domain Data Utilization:** Harnessing vast, unstructured online data sources (e.g., egocentric human videos, community-contributed datasets) and bridging embodiment gaps.
 - **Self-Exploration Data Collection:** Enabling agents to autonomously generate valuable training data through environment interaction.
 - **Data Augmentation:** Maximizing the utility and diversity of existing data through linguistic, semantic, trajectory, or visual manipulations.

The authors maintain a continuously updated project page (https://evla-survey.github.io/) to track developments, demonstrating a commitment to providing a living resource for the community. This structured and comprehensive approach allows for a thorough dissection of the challenges and advancements, culminating in a clear roadmap for future research.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The survey's main findings emerge from its systematic categorization and review of state-of-the-art techniques across the three core pillars of efficiency, revealing a concerted effort within the research community to overcome the limitations of foundational VLAs.

#### 4.1. Efficient Model Design

The findings in this pillar highlight a shift from scaling-centric designs to resource-conscious architectures:
- **Efficient Architectures:** Researchers are actively developing methods to mitigate the quadratic complexity of Transformer attention, such as linear-time attention (e.g., SARA-RT) and efficient masking (e.g., Long-VLA). Transformer alternatives like Mamba (e.g., RoboMamba) offer linear scaling for sequence modeling. Action decoding has moved beyond token-by-token autoregressive generation to parallel (e.g., PD-VLA, Spec-VLA) and generative methods (e.g., TinyVLA, FlowRAM) for faster, holistic action synthesis. The adoption of lightweight components (e.g., TinyVLA, MiniVLA), Mixture-of-Experts (e.g., GeRM, FedVLA) for sparse activation, and hierarchical systems (e.g., HiRT, DP-VLA) that decouple high-level planning from low-level execution are common strategies to balance capacity with efficiency.
- **Model Compression:** Significant progress has been made in reducing model size and computational cost. Layer pruning (e.g., DeeR-VLA, MoLe-VLA) dynamically excises redundant layers or skips non-essential ones. Quantization (e.g., OpenVLA, BitVLA) discretizes weights and activations to lower bit-precision, drastically reducing memory footprint and accelerating inference with minimal performance degradation. Token optimization, a crucial area, involves token compression (e.g., FAST, SmolVLA for visual tokens; VOTE for action tokens), token pruning (e.g., FlashVLA, EfficientVLA for visual tokens) to remove redundant information, and token caching (e.g., VLA-Cache, CronusVLA) to reuse static or stable representations across timesteps, thereby curbing computational overhead in iterative processes.

#### 4.2. Efficient Training

This pillar reveals strategies for making the learning process less resource-intensive and more adaptable:
- **Efficient Pre-training:** To address data scarcity, data-efficient strategies involve self-supervised training (e.g., LAPA, EgoVLA) that leverages unlabeled internet-scale egocentric human videos to learn latent action spaces or environment dynamics, reducing reliance on costly robotic labels. Mixed data co-training (e.g., GeRM, HAMSTER) combines heterogeneous datasets (expert, suboptimal, synthetic) for robust generalization. Efficient action representation focuses on compressing high-dimensional continuous actions into more succinct, semantic spaces (e.g., FAST using DCT+BPE, VQ-VAE in LAPA) or redefining actions in more optimal coordinate systems (e.g., cVLA). Other strategies include multi-stage training (decoupling modality alignment, reasoning, and action fine-tuning) and parameter-efficient methods like LoRA (e.g., TinyVLA) to adapt VLMs with minimal updates.
- **Efficient Post-training:** This phase primarily leverages existing powerful backbones and adapts them. Supervised fine-tuning benefits from parameter-efficient techniques like LoRA (e.g., OpenVLA, OpenVLA-OFT) for targeted updates on specific tasks, or novel methods like Atomic Skill Library for few-shot adaptation. RL-based methods are increasingly used to refine policies, offering both online (e.g., RIPT-VLA, SimpleVLA-RL) and offline (e.g., ConRFT, CO-RFT) paradigms to learn from environmental interaction or logged data, respectively, often improving sample efficiency and robustness against reward sparsity.

#### 4.3. Efficient Data Collection

The findings emphasize shifting away from labor-intensive human teleoperation towards scalable, automated, and diversified data acquisition:
- **Human-in-the-Loop Data Collection:** Efforts focus on optimizing human roles, such as supervisors intervening only at critical junctures (e.g., GCENT) or providing high-level natural language instructions (e.g., CLIP-RT) instead of continuous teleoperation.
- **Simulation Data Collection:** Massively parallel simulation (e.g., GraspVLA, GeRM, RoboTwin 2.0) is widely used to generate large-scale, diverse datasets at a fraction of the cost. Advanced techniques like real-to-sim-to-real pipelines (e.g., ReBot, R2R2R) are used to minimize the sim-to-real gap, ensuring the realism and transferability of synthetic data.
- **Internet-Scale and Cross-Domain Data Utilization:** This is a crucial area where researchers leverage abundant human videos (egocentric and third-person) or community-contributed robot datasets (e.g., SmolVLA, EgoVLA, MimicDreamer). The key challenge is bridging the "human-robot embodiment gap" through shared action spaces, pose estimation, and video diffusion models to align human motions with robotic capabilities.
- **Self-Exploration Data Collection:** Agents are increasingly enabled to generate their own valuable training data through interaction. This includes task-agnostic exploration (e.g., AnyPos) to map kinematic reachable spaces and online RL (e.g., SimpleVLA-RL, DiffusionRLVLA) to "learn while collecting" by generating, evaluating, and optimizing trajectories. World models (e.g., World-Env, VLA-RFT) further enhance this by providing virtual environments for safe and efficient exploration.
- **Data Augmentation:** Strategies like reformatting datasets into conversational pairs (e.g., LLaRA), using LLMs for rich semantic annotations (e.g., InstructVLA, RoboChemist), injecting failure scenarios, or manipulating visual modalities (e.g., ERMV) are employed to maximize data utility and diversity from existing datasets.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This survey holds significant implications for the field of embodied AI, providing a timely and crucial synthesis that will accelerate the development and deployment of VLA models. Its primary significance lies in:

- **Establishing a Foundational Reference:** By being the first comprehensive review dedicated to *efficient* VLAs, the survey provides a much-needed consolidation of disparate research. This serves as an indispensable reference for both new and experienced researchers, helping them navigate the complex landscape of optimization techniques and identify relevant prior art.
- **Providing a Unified Framework:** The novel "model-training-data" taxonomy offers a structured way to understand and categorize efficiency advancements. This framework is crucial for fostering a holistic perspective, enabling researchers to see how innovations in one pillar (e.g., model compression) interact with others (e.g., efficient training or data collection). This clarity can inspire more integrated research efforts.
- **Charting a Future Roadmap:** By critically distilling challenges and outlining forward-looking research directions, the survey acts as a guide for future endeavors. It highlights unresolved tensions between compactness and expressivity, scalability and stability, and data quality and accessibility. The proposed future works—such as adaptive, embodiment-agnostic architectures, scalable and resilient learning paradigms, and self-sustaining generative data ecosystems—offer concrete pathways for addressing these challenges.

The potential impact of this work is transformative, enabling the transition of VLA models from theoretical prototypes to ubiquitous, practically deployable systems across diverse domains:

- **Real-world Deployment:** The focus on efficiency directly addresses the core limitations preventing VLAs from operating effectively on edge devices. By enabling real-time compatibility, reduced computational costs, and scalable data collection, this survey paves the way for VLAs in applications such as:
 - **Intelligent Vehicles and Autonomous Driving:** Low-latency, robust models for critical real-time decision-making on mobile platforms with strict power budgets.
 - **Family Robotics and Smart Homes:** Privacy-preserving, offline operation with natural human-robot interaction, crucial for domestic settings.
 - **Industrial Manufacturing and Logistics:** Cost-effective deployment of advanced intelligence across fleets of robots, enhancing precision, throughput, and operational flexibility (e.g., CIPHER).
 - **Medical Assistive Robotics:** High-precision, low-latency control for delicate procedures, ensuring data security through on-premise processing and personalized adaptation with limited data.
- **Democratization of Robotics AI:** By reducing the prohibitive computational and data demands, efficient VLAs lower the barrier to entry for researchers and smaller institutions. This could lead to broader participation in VLA development, fostering innovation and diversity in the field.
- **Accelerated Research and Development:** The clear taxonomy and future roadmap will streamline research efforts, allowing scientists to focus on high-impact areas and build upon existing advancements more effectively. The continuously updated project page further supports this acceleration by providing a dynamic resource.

In conclusion, this survey is not just a review of past achievements but a foundational document that frames the current state and future trajectory of a critical sub-field of embodied AI. By making VLAs more efficient, it directly contributes to their practical utility and widespread adoption, bringing us closer to a future where intelligent physical agents seamlessly interact with and assist humans in various aspects of life.
