# Dream to Recall: Imagination-Guided Experience Retrieval for Memory-Persistent Vision-and-Language Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> Memoir presents an agent for memory-persistent Vision-and-Language Navigation that employs a language-conditioned world model to generate imagined future states, which then guide the selective retrieval of relevant environmental observations and navigation behavioral patterns from a hybrid memory. The agent achieved a 5.4% increase in SPL on unseen IR2R scenarios and reduced training latency by 8.3x compared to existing baselines.

## Problem

- Traditional Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) agents operate episodically, preventing progressive learning and long-term adaptation.
- Existing memory-persistent VLN methods suffer from ineffective memory access, either incorporating irrelevant information from full memory or relying on limited, fixed-horizon lookups.
- Prior memory-persistent VLN work predominantly stores environmental observations, neglecting valuable navigation behavioral patterns crucial for decision-making.

## Method

- A language-conditioned world model is used to imagine future navigation states, generating predictive queries for adaptive experience retrieval.
- A Hybrid Viewpoint-Level Memory (HVM) stores both environmental observations and navigation behavioral patterns, organized around a persistent graph.
- An experience-augmented navigation model integrates the retrieved observations and behavioral histories using specialized encoders and a dynamic fusion mechanism to guide action selection.

## Results

- Memoir achieved a 5.4% increase in SPL on unseen IR2R scenarios (73.3% vs. 67.9%) and an 11.6% increase on seen scenarios, outperforming the GR-DUET baseline.
- The approach demonstrated significant computational efficiency gains, including an 8.3x training speedup and a 74% reduction in inference memory compared to memory-intensive baselines.
- Ablation studies confirmed the effectiveness of the imagination-based retrieval, the multi-step overshooting objective in the world model, and the dedicated integration of navigation history.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Imagination of future states can serve as an adaptive, predictive query mechanism for selective memory retrieval, addressing limitations of full memory incorporation or fixed-horizon lookups.
> - Unifying and retrieving both environmental observations and navigation behavioral patterns provides a more comprehensive and strategically richer knowledge base for embodied navigation.
> - Training a world model with multi-step overshooting and pretraining improves long-horizon prediction and effectively grounds retrieval in the agent's navigation intent.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) is a fundamental task in embodied artificial intelligence, where an agent interprets natural language instructions to navigate through an environment to a specified goal. Traditional VLN tasks are episodic, meaning agents operate independently in each episode without retaining accumulated knowledge. This episodic nature restricts their ability to improve progressively or adapt to environments over time, limiting real-world applicability.

To address this, memory-persistent VLN tasks have emerged, requiring agents to accumulate and leverage experience across multiple navigation episodes. Prior work in this area has explored various memory mechanisms:
- **Episodic history stacking** (e.g., TourHAMT) involves simply concatenating past navigation sequences, which can lead to redundancy and performance degradation.
- **Augmented visual representations** (e.g., ESceme) enhance environmental observations with broader spatial contexts.
- **Multimodal topological graphs** (e.g., OVER-NAV) leverage open-vocabulary detection to strengthen keyword-observation correspondence.
- **Retained topological observation memory** (e.g., GR-DUET) has demonstrated performance gains by incorporating complete topological memory.

Despite these advancements, existing memory-persistent VLN approaches face two primary limitations. First, they often lack effective memory access mechanisms, relying either on full memory incorporation (which can lead to integrating irrelevant information and high computational overhead) or fixed-horizon spatial lookups (which may miss valuable, distant experiences). Second, these methods predominantly store only environmental observations and tend to neglect navigation behavioral patterns, which encode critical decision-making strategies. When attempts have been made to incorporate behavioral history, they have not effectively leveraged this information.

The work presented in this paper, Memoir, addresses these limitations by proposing an imagination-guided experience retrieval paradigm. It introduces a mechanism where a world model imagines future navigation states, which then serve as predictive queries to selectively retrieve relevant environmental observations and behavioral histories. This approach differentiates itself by focusing on adaptive, guided retrieval from a unified memory, rather than passive accumulation or fixed-scope lookups, and by integrating behavioral strategies alongside visual context. It thus contributes to advancing memory-persistent VLN by developing more intelligent and efficient ways for embodied agents to learn and adapt from past experiences.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a memory-persistent Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) agent that can progressively improve its navigation capabilities by effectively and efficiently leveraging accumulated experience. This overarching goal is driven by several specific motivations and aims:

1. **Overcome Ineffective Memory Access Mechanisms:** Existing memory-persistent VLN systems suffer from two main drawbacks in memory access. Some approaches incorporate the entire accumulated memory, which leads to the integration of irrelevant information, increased computational overhead, and reduced robustness. Other methods use a fixed-horizon lookup, which may cause agents to lose valuable long-range experiential knowledge. The authors are motivated by human cognitive processes, where imagination of future routes and events guides the consultation of past experiences to inform decisions. This suggests a need for an adaptive, selective retrieval mechanism that uses predictive queries to access relevant memories, avoiding both exhaustive incorporation and rigid, limited lookups.

2. **Integrate Navigation Behavioral Patterns:** Prior memory-persistent VLN research predominantly focuses on storing environmental observations (what the agent saw). However, navigation behavioral histories (how the agent made decisions and selected actions in various scenarios) contain valuable decision-making strategies. Current methods either neglect this behavioral information entirely or fail to leverage it effectively. A key objective is to design a memory system that explicitly stores and retrieves these behavioral patterns, anchoring them to specific viewpoints to enrich the agent's decision-making process.

3. **Develop a Language-Conditioned Predictive World Model:** To enable imagination-guided retrieval, a mechanism is required to generate predictive queries for future states. The paper proposes a language-conditioned world model that learns to imagine future navigation states based on instructions. This model serves a dual purpose: encoding current experiences into latent representations for storage and generating future state queries to retrieve similar past experiences. The conditioning on language ensures that the imagined states are relevant to the current instruction.

4. **Construct a Hybrid Viewpoint-Level Memory (HVM):** To support the retrieval of both environmental observations and behavioral patterns, a unified memory architecture is needed. The objective is to anchor both types of information to specific viewpoints in a persistent graph, allowing the agent to retrieve not only "what it saw" but also "how it navigated" in similar past contexts. This hybrid memory design aims to provide a more comprehensive and strategically rich knowledge base for navigation.

5. **Enhance Navigation Model with Retrieved Knowledge:** Once relevant experiences are retrieved, they must be effectively integrated into the agent's navigation policy. The motivation here is to augment existing robust navigation models (like DUET) with specialized encoders that can process and incorporate retrieved observations and behavioral histories. This integration should enable more informed decision-making, leading to improved navigation success and efficiency.

In summary, the core motivation is to move beyond passive memory accumulation towards an active, imagination-guided retrieval paradigm that selectively accesses and integrates diverse forms of past experience (both visual and behavioral) to enable more robust, efficient, and progressively improving embodied navigation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed approach, Memoir, is an agent designed for memory-persistent Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) that employs a predictive world model for adaptive experience retrieval. The framework consists of three main components: a language-conditioned world model, a Hybrid Viewpoint-Level Memory (HVM), and an experience-augmented navigation model.

#### 3.1. Language-Conditioned World Model

Memoir extends a contrastive world model by explicitly incorporating natural language instructions ($\ell$) and a reward signal ($\gamma_t$, indicating distance to the goal). The objective function is an evidence lower bound (ELBO) that includes terms for reward prediction ($J_{\text{REWARD}}$), contrastive learning ($J_{\text{NCE}}$), and dynamics regularization ($J_{\text{KL}}$). The $J_{\text{NCE}}$ term replaces pixel-level reconstruction with a contrastive objective that trains the model to distinguish correct state-observation pairs from incorrect ones, using a compatibility function $f(z_t, o_t) \propto \text{exp}(\frac{1}{\zeta} \text{sim}(\psi_s(z_t), \psi_o(x_t)))$. This function measures similarity between latent states ($z_t$) and visual observations ($x_t$).

To enhance long-horizon predictive capabilities, a multi-step overshooting objective is integrated. The final optimization objective sums the standard ELBO with d-step overshooting objectives up to a maximum distance $D$.
The world model architecture is based on the Recurrent State-Space Model (RSSM) and comprises four components:
- **Inference Model:** $q(z_t | z_{t-1}, o_t, \ell)$ infers the current latent state $z_t$ from past state, current observation, and instruction. This model encodes navigation histories.
- **Transition Model:** $p(z_t | z_{t-1})$ predicts the next latent state $z_t$ from the previous one, used for imagining future states.
- **Compatibility Model:** $p(z_t | o_t)$ (as defined by $f(z_t, o_t)$) assesses the compatibility between latent states and visual observations.
- **Reward Model:** $p(\gamma_t | z_t)$ predicts the future reward, guiding imagination termination.

#### 3.2. Hybrid Viewpoint-Level Memory (HVM)

HVM maintains two complementary memory banks organized around a persistent graph $G^{(k)} = (V^{(k)}, E^{(k)})$ that accumulates experience across episodes.
- **Observation Bank ($\mathcal{M}_o$):** Stores viewpoint features $X_o = \{x_j\}$ for recorded viewpoints $V_o = \{v_j\}$.
- **History Bank ($\mathcal{M}_h$):** Stores inferred agent states $Z_h = \{\{z_j^{(k)}\}\}$ and imagined trajectory sequences $T_h = \{\{\tau_j^{(k)}\}\}$ for viewpoints $V_h = \{v_j\}$ where navigation histories have been recorded.
At each timestep, the current viewpoint's observation feature ($x_t$), inferred state ($z_t$), and imagined trajectory ($\tau_t$) are added to their respective banks. The imagined trajectory $\tau_t = \{\hat{z}_{t+i}\}_{i=1}^{H_t}$ is generated by the transition model, with an imagination horizon $H_t$ that terminates when the predicted reward $\hat{\gamma}_{t+i}$ falls below a threshold $\epsilon$ or reaches the maximum horizon $D$.

**Environmental Observation Retrieval (Algorithm 1):**
Given an imagined trajectory $\tau_t$ at viewpoint $v_t$, observations are retrieved via topology-guided searching. For each imagined state $\hat{z}_{t+i}$ in $\tau_t$ and a stored feature $x_j$ at viewpoint $v_j$ from $\mathcal{M}_o$, a compatibility score $c_{i,j}$ is computed using the $\text{sim}(\psi_s(\hat{z}_{t+i}), \psi_o(x_j))$ function. The algorithm identifies viewpoints in the $i$-th order neighborhood $N_i(v_t)$ from $G^{(k)}$, computes their scores, filters them by a percentile ($1-\rho_o \cdot \gamma_o^{i-1}$), and then selects the top-$W$ viewpoints. Shortest paths from $v_t$ to these selected viewpoints are added to the episodic graph $G_t$.

**Navigation History Retrieval (Algorithm 2):**
This process identifies stored historical navigation patterns that have similar imagined trajectories. For a stored trajectory $\tau' = \{\hat{z}'_i\}$ at $v_t$ from $\mathcal{M}_h$, sequential similarity matching is performed with the current imagined trajectory $\tau_t$. The compatibility $c_i = \frac{1}{2}(\text{sim}(\psi_s(\hat{z}_{t+i}), \psi_s(\hat{z}'_i)) + 1)$ is computed for each step $i$, continuing until a step-dependent threshold ($\theta_h \cdot \gamma_h^{i-1}$) is met. Stored trajectories are ranked by matching length and minimum compatibility score, and the top-$P$ patterns are selected. The subsequent viewpoints and their associated inferred states and compatibility scores are then incorporated into $G_t$.

#### 3.3. Navigation Model

The navigation model extends the Dual-Scale Graph Transformer (DUET) architecture by incorporating retrieved experiential knowledge through dedicated encoders.
- **Coarse-Scale Encoder:** Processes an expanded set of viewpoint representations $X$ (including retrieved observations) from the episodic graph $G_t$. It generates global action scores $s_j^{(c)}$ for high-level planning.
- **Fine-Scale Encoder:** Processes the immediate panoramic features $r_t$ to generate local action scores $s_j^{(f)}$ for neighboring viewpoints, including a mechanism for backtracking.
- **Navigation-History Encoder:** Processes retrieved behavioral patterns. For each viewpoint $v_j$ with retrieved states $Z_j$ and compatibility scores $C_j$, a fused representation $u_j = \text{softmax}(\frac{C_j}{\zeta})^\top Z_j + x_j$ is computed. These fused states $U$ are processed to produce history-informed action scores $s_j^{(h)}$.
**Dynamic Fusion:** A learned dynamic fusion mechanism, parameterized by $\text{Softmax}(\text{FFN}([\hat{r}_0; \hat{x}_0; \hat{u}_0]))$, combines the scores from the three encoders ($\sigma_f s_j^{(f')} + \sigma_c s_j^{(c)} + \sigma_h s_j^{(h')}$) to yield the final navigation decision.

The overall navigation loop (Algorithm 3) iteratively updates the topological graphs, infers the current state, imagines future trajectories, performs observation and history retrieval, updates memory banks, and then uses the augmented navigation model to select the next action.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of Memoir involved extensive experiments across two memory-persistent VLN benchmarks: Iterative Room-to-Room (IR2R) and General Scene Adaptation (GSA-R2R), encompassing 10 distinct testing scenarios.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Analysis

- **Iterative Room-to-Room (IR2R):**
 - Memoir, when integrated into traditional VLN models like DUET and ScaleVLN, demonstrated performance improvements. For instance, on unseen IR2R scenarios, Memoir achieved an 11.1% increase in SPL for DUET-based implementations and 5.6% for ScaleVLN-based implementations, indicating that retrieved long-term memory acts as an effective prior.
 - Against memory-persistent baselines, Memoir significantly outperformed GR-DUET, achieving a 5.4% improvement in SPL on unseen scenarios (73.3% versus 67.9%) and an 11.6% improvement on seen scenarios. This suggests that Memoir's adaptive retrieval mechanism is more effective than GR-DUET's complete memory incorporation strategy, which can introduce noise.
 - The paper noted that memory-persistent variants sometimes exhibited slightly reduced performance on "seen" scenarios compared to traditional counterparts. This was attributed to shorter tour lengths in seen validation splits (limiting experience accumulation) and regularization effects from long-term memory integration, which encourages broader contextual reasoning over overfitting. Memoir substantially reduced this performance gap relative to GR-DUET, indicating a more balanced memory utilization.

- **General Scene Adaptation (GSA-R2R):**
 - Memoir consistently outperformed the GR-DUET baseline across various instruction taxonomies (user, scene, basic) and environmental characteristics (residential, non-residential). For user instructions, Memoir achieved an average 2.3% increase in Success Rate (SR) and 2.5% in SPL.
 - Across eight distinct testing scenarios with aligned experimental configurations, Memoir demonstrated an average 2.4% SR increase and 1.6% SPL improvement compared to GR-DUET. This suggests that the hybrid memory, by accessing past episodes involving similar expressions and actions, captures critical context missed by observation-only memory approaches.
 - The performance gains on GSA-R2R were observed to be slightly smaller than on IR2R, which was attributed to the higher memory density in GSA-R2R (600 episodes on average per scene), potentially enhancing GR-DUET's performance through increased topological completeness.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Analysis

A qualitative example illustrated Memoir's memory retrieval effectiveness in a challenging scenario where baseline models failed. When tasked with locating a "massage table," DUET made an incorrect approach, and GR-DUET became confused. Memoir succeeded by combining observation retrieval (identifying promising paths while controlling redundancy) and history retrieval (matching similar past objectives), guiding the agent to the correct destination.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies and Analyses

- **Computational Efficiency:** Memoir demonstrated substantial computational advantages over GR-DUET. It achieved a 55% reduction in training memory and an 88% reduction in training latency (an 8.3x speedup). During inference, memory usage decreased by 74%. The inference latency per step was mostly driven by imagination, which scaled linearly with the lookahead horizon ($D$), but retrieval latency remained low, establishing real-time feasibility.
- **Performance Scaling:** Memoir showed immediate and steep improvements in cumulative SR and SPL from the first episode, maintaining a consistent lead over baselines throughout tour progression, indicating effective long-term adaptability.
- **Memory Retrieval:** An "oracle" retrieval variant achieved 93.40% SPL, highlighting the potential benefits of ideal retrieval. Memoir's imagination-based retrieval achieved 73.46% SPL, outperforming random sampling, instruction-based retrieval (high recall but low precision due to temporal misalignment), and state-based retrieval (better precision but lower recall due to path dependency). This confirmed that querying with imagined latents effectively grounds retrieval in navigation intent.
- **World Model:** The Transformer-based world model outperformed the GRU variant. The inclusion of an overshooting objective significantly improved prediction accuracy and retrieval effectiveness (Observation Accuracy and History Recall), demonstrating the importance of grounded training for balancing exploration breadth and query precision.
- **History Integration:** Concatenating viewpoint features with state features in a dedicated Navigation-History Encoder yielded optimal performance, suggesting that state features carry crucial pattern information while benefiting from observation representation.
- **Expert Policy Strategy:** Random sampling among multiple optimal paths during training improved performance compared to deterministic SPL-based expert selection, providing better policy regularization.
- **World Model Pretraining:** Pretraining the world model components on navigation trajectories before joint training significantly improved performance (e.g., 1.91% SPL on IR2R), indicating the necessity of a well-initialized world model for effective retrieval signals.
- **Observation Completion & Neighbor Incorporation:** Completing partial observations at non-retrieved viewpoints and incorporating immediate neighbors of retrieved nodes enhanced environmental understanding and spatial context for planning, leading to further SPL improvements.
- **Parameter Study:** Analysis of hyperparameters for observation and history retrieval identified optimal configurations, such as a reduced filter rate and increased search width for observation retrieval, and balanced precision/recall settings for history retrieval.

#### 4.4. Failure Analysis

A specific failure scenario highlighted limitations:
- **Retrieval Limitations:** Inaccurate observation retrieval (semantically relevant but spatially imprecise candidates) and history retrieval (inability to distinguish between successful and failed past episodes) contributed to errors. Premature imagination termination in the world model also limited retrieval scope.
- **Exploration-Exploitation Trade-off:** The agent sometimes prioritized high-similarity but incorrect historical patterns (exploitation) over exploring novel alternatives, especially when retrieval failed to cover the true goal or distinguish task outcomes.
These failure modes indicate remaining challenges in world model accuracy, spatial relationship modeling, and the need for confidence-aware retrieval mechanisms.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces Memoir, an agent that presents a paradigm shift in memory-persistent Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) from passive memory accumulation to active, predictive, imagination-guided retrieval. This shift has several implications for the field of embodied AI.

**Novel Paradigm:** By repurposing a language-conditioned world model from a simulator to a neural search engine, Memoir establishes a novel framework where imagination is grounded in explicit, long-term memory. This "Dream to Recall" approach allows the agent to dynamically generate queries based on imagined future states, enabling selective and adaptive access to relevant past experiences. This contrasts with traditional methods that rely on full memory incorporation or fixed-horizon lookups, offering a more intelligent and efficient way for agents to leverage their knowledge.

**Hybrid Memory Architecture:** The introduction of Hybrid Viewpoint-Level Memory (HVM) is significant because it unifies the storage and retrieval of both environmental observations and navigation behavioral patterns at a fine-grained, viewpoint level. Prior work often neglected behavioral strategies or struggled to integrate them effectively. Memoir's ability to access not only "what the agent saw" but also "how it navigated" in similar past situations provides richer context for decision-making, which is crucial for complex, multi-episode navigation tasks.

**Enhanced Efficiency and Robustness:** Memoir demonstrates significant performance improvements across diverse memory-persistent VLN benchmarks, including a 5.4% SPL gain on IR2R over the best baseline. Crucially, these performance gains are accompanied by substantial efficiency improvements: an 8.3× training speedup and a 74% reduction in inference memory compared to memory-intensive baselines. This addresses a critical challenge in embodied AI, where efficient resource utilization is paramount for real-world deployment on robots or resource-constrained platforms. The approach's robustness is evidenced by its consistent outperformance across various instruction types and environmental characteristics.

**Illuminating Future Directions:** The paper's analysis, particularly the comparison between Memoir's performance (73.3% SPL) and the oracle retrieval upper bound (93.4% SPL), highlights substantial headroom for future advancements. The identified failure modes, such as limitations in the world model's ability to distinguish spatially distinct targets, premature imagination termination, and challenges in the exploration-exploitation trade-off, point to concrete avenues for future research. These include developing enhanced world modeling capabilities (e.g., through larger-scale pretraining, explicit spatial relationship modeling) and implementing confidence-aware retrieval mechanisms that can dynamically balance the reliance on past experience with the need for novel exploration.

In summary, Memoir contributes a principled framework that tightly integrates predictive simulation with explicit, hybrid memory, leading to more effective and efficient embodied navigation. Its methodological innovations and empirical successes establish a strong foundation for future research in developing progressively learning, adaptable, and resource-aware AI agents.
