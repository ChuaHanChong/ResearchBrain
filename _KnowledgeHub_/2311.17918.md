# Driving into the Future: Multiview Visual Forecasting and Planning with World Model for Autonomous Driving

> [!summary] Summary
> Drive-WM is a driving world model that generates high-fidelity, controllable multiview videos by adding temporal and multiview attention layers to a latent image diffusion model, factorizing view generation into reference and stitched views, and unifying image, text, layout, and action conditions. Coupled with a tree-based rollout and image-based rewards, it evaluates imagined futures to pick trajectories, reaching 15.8 FID, 122.7 FVD, and 94.4% multiview consistency on nuScenes.

## Problem

- End-to-end planners trained purely on expert trajectories degrade badly out-of-distribution; a mere 0.5 m lateral ego shift yields irrational trajectories.
- Prior world models operate in low-resolution pixels or vectorized state spaces, needing extra annotations and inheriting perception noise, missing fine-grained non-vectorizable events.
- Existing driving generators produce only single-view video or multiview still images, leaving consistent multiview video for full surround observation unsolved.

## Method

- ==Joint multiview-temporal diffusion==: temporal 3D conv plus attention layers and cross-view self-attention layers are added to a frozen pretrained image ==diffusion== backbone and fine-tuned on video. This backbone underlies **Drive-WM**.
- ==Factorized multiview generation== splits views into non-overlapping reference views and stitched views, generating stitched views conditioned on neighboring reference views and previous frames.
- Unified condition interface encodes context images, text, 3D boxes/HD maps/BEV segmentation, and ego actions into one d-dimensional token sequence for frame-wise cross-attention; ==tree-based rollout== scores imagined futures with map and object rewards.

## Results

- On nuScenes, generation reaches **12.99 FID** for multiview images and **15.8 FID** / **122.7 FVD** for video, versus **52.6 FID** / **452.0 FVD** for the best prior single-view video baseline.
- Factorized generation lifts keypoint-matching consistency from **45.8%** to **94.4%** with comparable quality; ablations show view layers plus layout and temporal conditioning cut FVD from **228.5** to **122.7**.
- Tree-based planning over three sampled commands improves average L2 from **1.02 m** to **0.80 m** and collision rate from **0.93%** to **0.26%**; fine-tuning on generated OOD data restores **1.59%** collisions to **0.91%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Factorizing a joint distribution into conditional pieces along a structured axis, such as neighboring camera views, buys strong geometric consistency far more cheaply than full autoregression.
> - Pixel-space imagined futures let planners be scored by generic perception models or even a multimodal LLM, capturing hazards that vectorized state representations cannot express.
> - Generated counterfactual and off-centerline rollouts serve as training data for corner cases, turning a generative world model into a robustness repair tool rather than only a simulator.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

Autonomous driving technology has made remarkable progress in recent years, yet ensuring safety and reliability in diverse, unpredictable real-world scenarios remains a significant challenge. One fundamental limitation of current end-to-end autonomous driving systems is their inability to anticipate the consequences of their actions before executing them. Humans, on the other hand, naturally simulate potential outcomes in their minds before taking action, especially in high-stakes situations like driving.

The research paper "Driving into the Future: Multiview Visual Forecasting and Planning with World Model for Autonomous Driving" introduces Drive-WM, a novel world model designed specifically for autonomous driving that enables vehicles to "imagine" potential future scenarios before making decisions. This capability represents a significant step forward in making autonomous vehicles safer and more reliable.

### 2. Background and Context

Traditional autonomous driving systems typically follow a modular approach, breaking down the driving task into perception, prediction, planning, and control. While effective in many scenarios, this approach faces challenges with component error propagation and handling complex, unusual situations.

End-to-end approaches aim to directly map sensor inputs to driving commands, potentially overcoming some of these limitations. However, they still struggle with:

1. **Out-of-distribution scenarios** - rare or unusual driving situations
2. **Safety guarantees** - ensuring the vehicle will behave safely in all circumstances
3. **Transparency** - understanding why the system makes specific decisions

World models address these challenges by learning internal representations of the environment that can predict future states based on actions. By "imagining" potential outcomes before acting, they enable:

- Evaluation of multiple possible actions
- Better handling of novel situations
- Improved safety through anticipation of risks

Despite the promising concept, prior world models have been limited to simplified environments like games or laboratory settings, lacking the fidelity required for real-world autonomous driving.

### 3. The Drive-WM Approach

Drive-WM represents a significant advance by creating a high-fidelity, controllable, and consistent multiview world model specifically designed for autonomous driving. The authors develop a model that can:

1. Generate realistic, high-quality multiview videos of driving scenarios
2. Maintain consistency across different camera views
3. Respond appropriately to control inputs (steering, acceleration)
4. Adapt to different environmental conditions (weather, lighting)
5. Integrate with end-to-end planning systems

The model achieves this through several innovative components.

### 4. Key Components

#### 4.1. Multiview Video Generation

Traditional video generation models struggle with the complexity of simultaneously generating multiple synchronized camera views. Drive-WM addresses this through:

1. **3D UNet Architecture**: Extended to handle both spatial and temporal dimensions across multiple camera views
2. **Frame-wise Cross-Attention**: Enabling temporal consistency across frames
3. **Multiview and Temporal Layers**: Specialized components to handle the complex relationships between different views and time steps

#### 4.2. Joint Factorization for Multiview Consistency

A critical innovation in Drive-WM is its approach to ensuring consistency across camera views. The authors introduce a factorization method that:

1. Divides views into reference views and stitched views
2. Generates reference views first
3. Creates stitched views conditioned on neighboring reference views
4. Uses a probabilistic graphical model to maintain global consistency

This approach is evaluated using a novel Keypoint Matching (KPM) metric that measures how well matching points are preserved across different views.

#### 4.3. Unified Conditional Generation

To create a flexible interface for incorporating different types of conditions, Drive-WM employs:

1. **Unified Cross-Attention Mechanism**: A common framework for handling diverse condition types
2. **Condition Types**:
 - Initial context images
 - Text descriptions (e.g., "Sunny day")
 - 3D object layouts (bounding boxes)
 - Action sequences (steering, acceleration)
 - HD maps

This unified approach allows the system to respond to complex, multi-modal inputs while maintaining a clean architecture.

### 5. World Model Based Planning

The core application of Drive-WM is to enhance planning in autonomous vehicles. The authors implement a tree-based planning approach that:

1. Takes current sensor inputs
2. Generates multiple potential action sequences (trajectories)
3. Uses the world model to "imagine" the outcome of each trajectory
4. Evaluates the safety and effectiveness of each imagined future
5. Selects the optimal action based on defined reward functions

#### 5.1. Reward Functions

The effectiveness of world model planning depends on meaningful evaluation of imagined futures. Drive-WM employs various reward functions:

1. **Object-based rewards**: Assessing proximity to other vehicles and obstacles
2. **Map-based rewards**: Evaluating lane-keeping and navigation
3. **Foundation model rewards**: Using GPT-4V to evaluate complex driving scenarios

The latter represents a particularly innovative approach, as it allows for non-vectorized assessments of driving safety based on visual understanding.

### 6. Evaluation and Results

The authors evaluate Drive-WM on several dimensions:

#### 6.1. Video Generation Quality

Drive-WM outperforms previous methods in terms of:
- Fréchet Inception Distance (FID)
- Fréchet Video Distance (FVD)
- Visual quality of generated videos

#### 6.2. Multiview Consistency

Using the novel Keypoint Matching (KPM) metric, Drive-WM demonstrates significantly higher consistency across views compared to baseline methods, particularly with the proposed factorization approach.

#### 6.3. Controllability

The model shows strong controllability across various dimensions:

1. **Speed Control**: Ability to generate scenarios with different vehicle speeds

2. **Turning Control**: Realistic turning maneuvers at intersections

3. **Object Placement**: Control over object position and appearance

#### 6.4. Planning Performance

When integrated with an end-to-end planner, Drive-WM improves performance in:

1. **Normal driving scenarios**: Enhanced performance on standard metrics
2. **Out-of-distribution scenarios**: Significantly improved robustness in unusual situations

### 7. Advanced Features

#### 7.1. Weather and Lighting Control

Drive-WM can generate diverse environmental conditions, allowing for testing autonomous systems in challenging scenarios like:
- Heavy rain
- Snow
- Fog
- Nighttime driving
- Glare from sunlight

#### 7.2. Action Distribution Analysis

The authors analyze the distribution of actions in the training data to ensure comprehensive coverage of driving scenarios.

### 8. Significance and Impact

Drive-WM represents a significant advancement in autonomous driving technology with several important implications:

#### 8.1. Safety Enhancement

By enabling vehicles to anticipate potential outcomes before acting, Drive-WM addresses a fundamental limitation of current systems. This "look before you leap" approach is especially valuable for:

1. Navigating complex intersections
2. Handling unexpected obstacles
3. Managing adverse weather conditions
4. Dealing with unusual road configurations

#### 8.2. Improved Generalization

The ability to generate diverse, realistic scenarios allows for better training and testing of autonomous systems, improving their ability to handle novel situations. This addresses one of the major challenges in deploying autonomous vehicles in the real world.

#### 8.3. Neural Simulation

Drive-WM effectively creates a neural simulator that can generate realistic driving scenarios without the computational overhead of traditional physics-based simulators. This opens up new possibilities for:

1. Accelerated testing and validation
2. Data augmentation for training
3. Scenario generation for edge cases

#### 8.4. Integration with Existing Systems

A key strength of Drive-WM is its ability to enhance existing end-to-end planners without requiring a complete redesign. This incremental approach makes it more practical for adoption in current autonomous driving platforms.

### 9. Conclusion

Drive-WM represents a significant step forward in addressing the safety and reliability challenges of autonomous driving. By creating a high-fidelity world model that enables vehicles to anticipate the consequences of their actions, the research pushes autonomous driving technology closer to human-like reasoning and planning.

The ability to generate consistent multiview videos that respond appropriately to control inputs creates new possibilities for training, testing, and deploying autonomous vehicles. Furthermore, the integration with end-to-end planning systems provides a practical path to implementing these advances in real-world autonomous vehicles.

As autonomous driving technology continues to evolve, approaches like Drive-WM that combine the strengths of world models with end-to-end learning are likely to play an increasingly important role in creating safer, more reliable autonomous vehicles capable of handling the full complexity of real-world driving scenarios.
