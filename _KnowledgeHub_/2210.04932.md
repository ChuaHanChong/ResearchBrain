# NeRF2Real: Sim2real Transfer of Vision-guided Bipedal Motion Skills using Neural Radiance Fields

> [!summary] Summary
> DeepMind developed NeRF2Real, a system that uses Neural Radiance Fields trained from phone video to create high-fidelity simulation environments for robotics. This approach enabled the zero-shot sim-to-real transfer of complex, vision-guided bipedal locomotion policies, achieving an 87% success rate for navigation and obstacle avoidance on a real robot.

## Problem

- Bridging the sim-to-real gap for vision-guided robots, particularly for complex, unstructured "in the wild" scenes, due to difficulties in accurately modeling visual appearance and geometry.
- Lack of scalable and accessible methods for creating high-fidelity, photorealistic simulation environments that precisely reflect real-world visual and physical properties from simple data capture.
- Training robust, vision-based control policies for complex robotic platforms like bipedal humanoids is challenging due to their high degrees of freedom, unstable dynamics, and reliance on accurate visual input.

## Method

- A semi-automated pipeline that uses ==Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs)==, trained from consumer-grade video footage, to generate photorealistic scene renderings for the robot's camera within simulation.
- The implicit geometry from the trained NeRF is extracted into a collision mesh using ==voxelization and marching cubes==, which is then integrated into a **MuJoCo** physics simulator.
- Robot policies are trained using deep reinforcement learning (==DMPO==) within this NeRF-powered simulation, combining recurrent image encoders for visual input with proprioceptive observations, and then transferred to a real bipedal robot.

## Results

- Policies for a bipedal humanoid achieved **87%** success rate in zero-shot transfer for visual navigation and obstacle avoidance on a real robot, closely matching the **86%** simulation success.
- The policies demonstrated effective visual self-localization with a median error of approximately **0.25m** in real-world deployments.
- Complex behaviors like active ball searching and pushing emerged and successfully transferred to the real robot, though contact-rich tasks showed a larger sim-to-real gap (e.g., **43%** success when starting near a wall for ball pushing).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs) can effectively bridge the visual and geometric sim-to-real gap for vision-guided robots by generating photorealistic and geometrically accurate simulation environments from commodity video data.
> - The developed NeRF2Real pipeline enables zero-shot sim-to-real transfer of complex, whole-body, vision-guided locomotion policies for highly articulated bipedal robots using only an RGB camera.
> - Extracting collision meshes from NeRFs and combining them with traditional physics simulation (MuJoCo) allows for training policies that interact physically with the environment.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research sits at the critical intersection of **reinforcement learning (RL), robotics, computer vision, and neural scene representations**. It primarily addresses a long-standing challenge in robotics: the **sim-to-real gap**.

**Sim-to-Real Transfer:** Training robot policies directly on hardware is often impractical due to safety concerns, high data requirements, and the wear and tear on physical robots. Sim-to-real transfer, where policies are trained in simulation and then deployed on real robots, offers an appealing alternative. However, the performance of transferred policies heavily relies on the fidelity of the simulation. Discrepancies between simulation and reality, known as the sim-to-real gap, arise from inaccurate modeling of robot dynamics, sensor characteristics, and most notably, the visual and physical properties of the environment. Existing methods to reduce this gap include manual tuning, system identification, learning neural network models of system components, and techniques like Domain Randomization and Domain Adaptation. While successful for many tasks, modeling complex, unstructured "in the wild" scenes, especially for vision-guided robots using RGB cameras, remains a significant hurdle. Depth sensors or LiDARs are easier to simulate, but their use restricts the range of tasks and sensor modalities, making RGB camera fidelity crucial for general-purpose visual navigation.

**Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs):** A key innovation enabling this work is the advent of Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs). NeRFs are a rapidly evolving class of implicit neural scene representations capable of synthesizing novel, photorealistic views from a sparse set of input images. Unlike traditional 3D reconstruction methods (e.g., those used for Matterport3D, Gibson, Replica datasets) that often require specialized scanning equipment and struggle with outdoor scenes, NeRFs can be learned from ordinary video footage captured with commodity mobile cameras. Crucially for this work, NeRFs not only offer superior photorealistic rendering but also provide access to the underlying scene geometry (via volume density). Recent advancements in NeRFs, such as faster training/rendering (e.g., Instant NGP), support for large unbounded scenes (mip-NeRF 360), improved geometry reconstruction (Ref-NeRF), and potential for dynamic scenes, have made them increasingly attractive for robotics applications.

**NeRF in Robotics:** While NeRFs have seen applications in robotics for pose estimation, representation learning, grasping, and dynamics modeling, their direct integration into physics simulations for training generalizable, vision-guided locomotion policies has been limited. Prior work has explored obstacle avoidance in NeRF environments, but often without real-world transfer demonstrations, or for dynamically simpler platforms (e.g., wheeled robots).

**Visual Navigation/Embodied AI:** The paper also contributes to the field of visual navigation and embodied AI. While many simulation suites (e.g., Habitat, iGibson, AI2/ROBO-THOR) exist for training visual navigation policies, they primarily focus on wheeled robots or quadrupeds with depth/LiDAR sensors, assuming localization or relying on low-level controllers. This work distinguishes itself by tackling vision-based, whole-body control for a bipedal humanoid robot using only an RGB camera, a significantly more challenging setup due to the higher sim-to-real gap associated with visual fidelity and complex bipedal dynamics.

In essence, "NeRF2Real" proposes a powerful paradigm shift: instead of meticulously hand-crafting or laboriously scanning complex environments for simulation, it leverages the emergent capabilities of NeRFs to automatically generate visually and geometrically accurate representations of real-world scenes directly from simple video data. This integration with a physics simulator allows for the training of advanced, vision-guided policies that can robustly transfer to the real world, pushing the frontier of accessible and high-fidelity sim-to-real robotics.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **develop and demonstrate a system for applying sim-to-real approaches to "in the wild" scenes with realistic visuals, specifically for policies that rely on active perception using RGB cameras.**

The motivations behind this objective are multifaceted:

1. **Bridging the Sim-to-Real Visual Gap:** A major bottleneck in sim-to-real transfer is accurately modeling the visual appearance of unstructured real-world scenes as perceived by RGB cameras. Traditional simulation environments often lack the photorealistic detail and geometric fidelity required for policies heavily reliant on visual input, leading to a significant sim-to-real gap. The authors aim to overcome this by creating simulations that closely mimic real-world visual complexity.
2. **Addressing Geometry Challenges:** Beyond visuals, accurate contact geometry is crucial for physically realistic interaction in simulation. Reconstructing this geometry from complex, real-world scenes in a scalable manner has been difficult. The paper seeks to leverage NeRF's ability to implicitly represent and extract scene geometry.
3. **Scalability and Accessibility:** Current methods for photorealistic scene reconstruction often rely on specialized 3D scanning setups or extensive manual effort, limiting their widespread applicability. The authors are motivated to create a more accessible pipeline that uses commodity hardware (a generic phone) and minimizes manual intervention for scene capture.
4. **Leveraging NeRF Advancements:** Recent rapid progress in NeRF technology, particularly its ability to synthesize photorealistic views and implicitly encode geometry from simple video data, provides a strong motivation for its integration into sim-to-real pipelines. The authors aim to demonstrate NeRF's utility in creating high-fidelity robotic simulation environments.
5. **Enabling Complex Vision-Guided Behaviors:** The ultimate goal is to enable the training and zero-shot transfer of complex, vision-based control policies for advanced robotic platforms, specifically bipedal humanoids with actuated head-mounted cameras. Such robots present unique challenges due to their high degrees of freedom, complex dynamics, and reliance on visual cues for navigation and interaction. The paper aims to prove that the NeRF2Real pipeline can support learning these challenging skills.
6. **Unlocking New Research Avenues:** By simplifying the process of creating realistic simulation environments, the work aims to unlock new research possibilities in embodied AI, allowing researchers to explore a wider range of tasks and real-world scenarios without the prohibitive costs and time associated with traditional simulation setup.

In summary, the core motivation is to make sim-to-real transfer more practical and effective for vision-guided robots operating in complex, unstructured real-world environments by automating the generation of high-fidelity simulations using cutting-edge neural scene representations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The NeRF2Real system presents a semi-automated pipeline for creating high-fidelity simulation environments from real-world scenes, which are then used to train vision-guided robotic policies. The approach consists of six main steps:

**A. Capturing a Video of the Real-World Scene:**
A short video (5-6 minutes) of the static scene is captured using an off-the-shelf mobile phone camera (e.g., Google Pixel 6). The human operator ensures slow, even camera movement to minimize motion blur and drastic viewpoint changes. Consistent lighting is maintained by fixing white balance and brightness, and high-resolution videos (≥1080p) are preferred for better localization and NeRF results.

**B. Localization:**
Approximately 1000 keyframes are extracted from the video, selected based on a heuristic to minimize motion blur (using Laplacian variance). COLMAP, an open-source Structure-from-Motion (SfM) package, is then used to estimate the intrinsic parameters of the camera and the extrinsic pose for each keyframe.

**C. NeRF Training:**
Using the extracted images and their corresponding camera poses, a Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) model is trained. The authors leverage several advanced NeRF extensions to improve reconstruction quality, geometry, and rendering speed:
- **Anti-aliasing:** Sampling the average of the volume over a normal distribution to avoid artifacts at low resolutions (mip-NeRF style).
- **Large Scenes:** Using a space squashing formulation for unbounded scenes (mip-NeRF 360).
- **Improved Geometry:** Optimizing separate specular and diffuse colors to enhance reconstructed geometry (Ref-NeRF).
- **Speedup:** Implementing a multi-scale spatial hash grid approach (Instant NGP) for significantly faster training and rendering (e.g., 6ms per frame on a V100 GPU).
- **Robustness:** Applying Gaussian blur with random variance to training samples and providing the diagonalized variance as an extra input to the MLP, which helps the network interpolate in scale-space and improves low-resolution reconstruction.

**D. Rendering in Simulation:**
The trained NeRF serves as the rendering engine for the static scene within the simulation. Critically, the NeRF is queried with the calibrated intrinsics of the robot's head-mounted camera (Logitech C920) to ensure visual consistency between the simulation and the real robot's perception.

**E. Collision Mesh Extraction:**
The implicit scene geometry learned by the NeRF (its occupancy volume density) is extracted to create a collision mesh for the physics simulator. This involves:
- **Voxelization:** Discretizing the predicted occupancy into a voxel grid.
- **Marching Cubes:** Applying the marching cubes algorithm to generate a 3D mesh from the voxelized occupancy.
- **Post-processing:** Manually aligning the mesh to the simulator's world frame by solving a least-squares optimization to align the dominant floor plane with the z-axis. The mesh is rotated, scaled (by comparing an object's size), and cropped to the simulation's required extent. Crucially, the potentially artifact-laden floor in the mesh is replaced with a flat primitive for better collision fidelity. For MuJoCo, the mesh is also decomposed into convex sub-components.

**F. Physics Simulation:**
The core physics simulation is built using MuJoCo. The extracted and processed NeRF mesh represents the static environment and is fixed to the world frame. Dynamic objects, such as the robot itself and other interactive elements (e.g., a ball), are modeled with their known physical and visual properties.
- **Composite Rendering:** A straightforward approach is used where the dynamic objects (rendered by MuJoCo's built-in renderer) are overlaid on top of the NeRF rendering of the static scene. This assumes dynamic objects are always in the foreground, simplifying occlusion handling.

**Sim2Real Training Setup:**
- **Robot Platform:** A 20-DoF Robotis OP3 humanoid robot (35 cm tall, 3.5 kg) is used. Policies run at 40Hz on the robot's on-board computer, using joint encoders, gyroscope, accelerometer, and the head-mounted Logitech C920 camera.
- **Reducing Dynamics Sim2Real Gap:** The simulation accurately models sensors (low-pass filtering of IMU data). Limited domain randomization is applied, including random pushes, constant/jittered sensor delays, random mass/IMU position variations on the robot, and variations in ball mass/radius for the pushing task.
- **Policy Training:** Policies are trained using DMPO (DeepMind's Maximum a Posteriori Policy Optimization), a state-of-the-art distributional deep reinforcement learning algorithm. The policy network employs a recurrent image encoder (ResNet + LSTM) for visual input (30x40 or 60x80 resolution), combined with proprioceptive observations (gyroscope, accelerometer, gravity direction, joint positions, previous control signal). An asymmetric actor-critic setup is used, where the critic receives privileged ground-truth state information from the simulator to facilitate efficient learning.
- **Image Augmentations:** To mitigate mismatches in image intensity properties between NeRF renderings and the real camera (which cannot be easily modulated), augmentations like random brightness, saturation, hue, contrast, and translation are applied during training.
- **Reward Shaping:** A combination of regularization rewards (penalizing high yaw speed, L2 regularization on joint angles towards a standing pose, encouraging forward walking speed) and task-specific rewards (sparse goal bonus, movement towards goal, ball interaction rewards) are used to encourage robust and transferable behaviors.
- **Tasks:** Two main tasks are demonstrated:
 - **Navigation and Obstacle Avoidance:** Point-to-point visual navigation within a 5m x 4m scene with obstacles (plant, chair, walls). The robot must reach randomly placed goals without falling or colliding.
 - **Ball Pushing:** Moving a basketball to a target corner within a 3m x 3m workspace, demonstrating interaction with dynamic objects.

This comprehensive methodology, integrating advanced neural scene representation with robust physics simulation and reinforcement learning techniques, allows for an end-to-end pipeline from real-world video capture to deployable robotic policies.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper successfully demonstrates the feasibility and effectiveness of the NeRF2Real pipeline for sim-to-real transfer of vision-guided bipedal locomotion policies.

**A. Training Time:**
The pipeline, while semi-automated, involves several computationally intensive steps:
- COLMAP localization (from ~1000 frames): 3-4 hours.
- NeRF training: ~20 minutes on a cluster of 8 V100 GPUs (with potential for further speedup).
- Mesh extraction and post-processing: A few hours.
- Policy training (RL): ~24 hours for navigation (8M gradient updates, 128M environment steps), and twice as long for ball pushing.
The total time from video capture to a trained policy is typically 1-2 days.

**B. Navigation and Obstacle Avoidance Results:**
The navigation task involved the Robotis OP3 humanoid reaching multiple target locations in a 5m x 4m scene with obstacles. The evaluation compared policies trained in simulation to their zero-shot transfer performance on the real robot across 54 real-world episodes.

- **Zero-Shot Transfer Success:** The policies exhibited a remarkably small gap between simulation and real-world performance. The 60x80 resolution policy achieved an 87% success rate (47/54 episodes) on the real robot, which was nearly identical to its 86% success rate in simulation. The 30x40 resolution policy performed slightly worse but still strong, with 69% success in real vs. 73% in sim. This demonstrates highly effective transfer of complex vision-guided locomotion.
- **Dynamical Property Transfer:** The time taken to reach the goal was also very similar in simulation and real-world (e.g., 11.2 seconds real vs. 10.8 seconds sim for 60x80 policy). This indicates that the simulated dynamics, including gait velocity, align well with reality.
- **Localization Accuracy:** The policies learned effective visual representations for self-localization. The median localization error (difference between policy's belief and ground truth from motion capture) across all control steps and trials was approximately 0.25m. Failures, especially for the lower-resolution policy, were sometimes correlated with higher localization errors, indicating the importance of robust visual processing. The agent showed the ability to quickly localize itself accurately within ~2 seconds of data and generally maintain tracking.

**C. Ball Pushing Results:**
This task required the robot to move a basketball to a target corner in a 3m x 3m workspace, demonstrating interaction with dynamic objects.
- **Complex Behavior Emergence:** The policies learned to use the robot's hands to push the ball and exhibited active perception behaviors (searching and tracking the ball) without explicit encouragement in the reward function. These complex behaviors successfully transferred from simulation to the real world.
- **Sim2Real Gap in Contact-Rich Tasks:** While impressive, the sim-to-real gap was larger for this contact-rich task compared to navigation. For example, the 30x40 policy achieved 78% success when the ball started in the center but only 43% when starting near a wall. This was attributed to the ball sometimes getting stuck near walls in reality, a common challenge in physics simulation of intricate contact dynamics.

**D. Qualitative Observations:**
The authors also performed qualitative evaluations in perturbed real-world settings (e.g., moving fixed objects, humans walking around). Surprisingly, the "low-level" locomotion (stable gait) appeared to be disentangled from the "high-level" scene understanding. For instance, if the scene changed, the robot might head in the wrong direction but maintain a stable walk. Similarly, blocking the robot's camera caused it to stop without falling, resuming when its view was restored. This suggests a degree of robustness and modularity in the learned policies.

In summary, the main findings confirm that the NeRF2Real pipeline can generate simulation environments realistic enough to enable robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer of complex, vision-guided bipedal locomotion skills, including navigation and object interaction, using only an RGB camera. While contact-rich tasks still pose a greater challenge, the overall success marks a significant step forward in sim-to-real robotics.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "NeRF2Real" work holds significant implications for the field of robotics and embodied AI, particularly in pushing the boundaries of sim-to-real transfer.

**1. Enabling High-Fidelity Simulators from Commodity Data:**
The most profound significance is the demonstration of a pipeline that can generate highly realistic and geometrically accurate simulation environments directly from easily collected commodity video data (e.g., a phone camera). This bypasses the need for expensive, specialized 3D scanning equipment or laborious manual reconstruction, making high-fidelity simulation setup far more accessible to a broader range of researchers and applications.

**2. Addressing the Visual Sim2Real Gap for RGB Cameras:**
For vision-guided robots, especially those relying solely on RGB cameras, the visual fidelity of the simulation is paramount. By leveraging NeRFs' exceptional photorealistic rendering capabilities, the paper significantly reduces the visual sim-to-real gap, a long-standing challenge that has limited the effectiveness of vision-based policies in real-world deployment. This opens up new possibilities for tasks that require fine-grained visual perception.

**3. Advancing Bipedal Locomotion and Whole-Body Control:**
Successfully demonstrating zero-shot transfer of whole-body, vision-guided policies for a 20-DoF bipedal humanoid robot is a substantial achievement. Bipedal robots are inherently unstable and complex to control. Learning robust locomotion and navigation skills for such platforms, especially when guided by potentially noisy or ambiguous visual inputs, is extremely difficult. This work provides a scalable method for training these skills in a safe and data-efficient manner.

**4. Paving the Way for More Generalizable Robotic Skills:**
The ability to quickly generate accurate simulations of diverse "in the wild" scenes allows for training policies across a much wider distribution of environments. This can lead to more generalizable and robust robotic skills that are less prone to breaking when deployed in novel real-world settings.

**5. Future of Robotics Research:**
The paper's discussion on limitations and future work hints at exciting potential impacts:
- **Increased Automation:** Further automation of mesh post-processing using advanced NeRF models could lead to a fully automated scene generation pipeline.
- **More Complex Interactions:** Evaluating the approach on more contact-rich tasks like climbing would stress-test current limitations and guide future developments in NeRF and scene modeling for physics interaction.
- **Dynamic Scenes:** Leveraging advancements in dynamic NeRFs (which can represent changing scenes or moving objects) would allow the pipeline to extend beyond static environments and simple foreground overlays to much more complex, dynamic real-world scenarios.
- **Real-Time Deployment:** As NeRF training and rendering become faster, combined with faster RL training, it could enable near real-time scene modeling and policy adaptation, potentially leading to online, adaptive robot learning in situ.

In essence, "NeRF2Real" represents a significant leap towards more practical and scalable robot learning in the wild. By providing an easier way to create high-fidelity simulations that accurately reflect real-world visual and geometric properties, it empowers researchers to develop and deploy more capable and intelligent robotic systems for a wide array of applications, from complex manipulation to autonomous navigation in human-centric environments. The authors' impact statement acknowledges the potential for beneficial applications while reiterating their opposition to any uses designed to cause harm.
