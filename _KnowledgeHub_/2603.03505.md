# PhyPrompt: RL-based Prompt Refinement for Physically Plausible Text-to-Video Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> PhyPrompt introduces an RL-based prompt refinement framework to enhance the physical plausibility of text-to-video generation by automatically transforming generic user prompts into physics-aware descriptions. The system achieves a 40.8% joint success rate (semantic adherence and physical commonsense scores of 4 or higher) on the CogVideoX-2B generator, an 8.6 percentage point improvement over baseline prompts, and demonstrates zero-shot transferability across various T2V models.

## Problem

- State-of-the-art text-to-video (T2V) generation models frequently produce videos that violate fundamental physical laws and commonsense, despite high visual quality.
- Manually engineering detailed, physics-aware prompts to guide T2V models is time-consuming, requires specialized expertise, and does not scale efficiently across diverse scenarios.
- Existing automated prompt enhancement techniques often struggle to balance semantic fidelity with physical realism, or are not specifically optimized for physical plausibility in video generation.

## Method

- A two-stage ==reinforcement learning== (==RL==) framework trains a lightweight, model-agnostic language model (LLM) as a prompt rewriter, sitting between the user and a frozen T2V generator.
- The first stage involves supervised ==fine-tuning== (SFT) of the LLM using a specialized Physics-Focused ==Chain-of-Thought== (==CoT==) dataset to teach physics-aware prompt enhancement.
- The second stage refines the SFT-trained LLM's policy using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with a dynamic multi-objective reward, which balances semantic adherence and physical commonsense via a ==curriculum learning== approach.

## Results

- PhyPrompt-**7B** achieved 40.8% joint success (SA 

4 and PC 

4) on CogVideoX-**2B**, representing an **8.6** percentage point absolute improvement over original prompts and outperforming Promptist (30.2%) and PhyT2V (36.2%).
- The prompt rewriter demonstrated zero-shot transferability, significantly improving joint success across diverse T2V generators, including Lavie (+8.2%), VideoCrafter2 (+16.8%), and CogVideoX-**5B** (+6.6%).
- The dynamic curriculum reward system achieved superior performance, reaching 47.8% SA and 66.8% PC, surpassing the upper bounds of both SA-only and PC-only training, demonstrating synergistic optimization of the conflicting objectives.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Physical implausibility in T2V often stems from insufficient physics-aware textual prompts, rather than inherent limitations of the T2V generators themselves, as detailed prompts can elicit plausible content.
> - A dynamic curriculum-based reward mechanism can synergistically optimize conflicting objectives like semantic adherence and physical commonsense, leading to compositional learning that surpasses static or single-objective optimization.
> - A lightweight, domain-specialized prompt rewriter trained with task-specific RL feedback can be more effective and parameter-efficient for physics-aware video generation than scaling up general-purpose large language models or fine-tuning large video generators.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of text-to-video (T2V) generation has advanced significantly, with large-scale models capable of producing videos that exhibit high visual quality. However, a persistent challenge in these state-of-the-art T2V systems is their frequent violation of fundamental physical laws and commonsense, even when the visual fidelity is high. Objects in generated videos may exhibit unrealistic behaviors such as teleportation, disregard for gravity, or interpenetration. Recent evaluations, such as VideoPhy and PhyGenBench, have quantified these inconsistencies, indicating a gap between visual realism and physical plausibility that restricts T2V models' applicability in domains requiring accurate real-world dynamics, like robotics or scientific simulation.

This work identifies a primary cause for this issue: a deficiency in physical constraints within the textual prompts provided to T2V models, rather than inherent limitations of the models themselves. The paper demonstrates that T2V generators can produce physically plausible content when supplied with prompts that explicitly detail physical phenomena, such as object motion and force interactions. However, manually engineering such physics-aware prompts demands specialized expertise, is time-consuming, and does not scale effectively across diverse scenarios.

Existing automated prompt enhancement techniques partially address prompt quality but have limitations regarding physical plausibility. Promptist, for instance, refines text-to-image prompts using reinforcement learning (RL) guided by aesthetic rewards, but it does not target physical realism. Early T2V approaches utilized large language models (LLMs) like GPT-4o for prompt enhancement through in-context learning, showing some improvement in physics adherence but sometimes at the cost of semantic fidelity and without systematic optimization for video-level physical commonsense. VPO employs preference-based RL for prompt refinement but does not explicitly focus on physical plausibility. PhyT2V, a recent method, uses an LLM-guided iterative self-refinement loop to improve physics adherence, but its reliance on multiple prompting rounds and complex "step-back" mechanisms can limit efficiency.

PhyPrompt positions itself within this landscape by offering a novel framework that directly addresses the automated generation of physics-aware prompts for T2V, aiming to match the quality of expert-engineered prompts efficiently. It distinguishes itself by employing a two-stage, RL-based approach with a specific curriculum designed to balance semantic fidelity and physical commonsense, training a lightweight, model-agnostic rewriter that transfers zero-shot across different T2V architectures. This approach targets the prompt as the bottleneck, offering a solution that is both effective and computationally efficient compared to fine-tuning large video generators or relying on general-purpose LLMs without specialized optimization.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind PhyPrompt stems from the observation that current state-of-the-art text-to-video (T2V) generation models, despite their advanced visual quality, frequently produce videos that violate basic physical laws. This deficiency, identified through recent evaluations, significantly limits their practical utility in applications where adherence to real-world physics is crucial, such as robotics, engineering simulations, or educational content.

The authors postulate that this issue is not primarily due to fundamental architectural limitations of the T2V generators themselves, but rather an inadequacy in the input prompts. They provide an example where a simple prompt like "A wine is poured from a bottle into a glass" might yield a video where the liquid level in the glass remains static. Conversely, manually augmenting the prompt with explicit physical details, such as "The level in the glass rises steadily," reliably results in a physically plausible video where the liquid visibly accumulates. This demonstrates that T2V generators possess the underlying capability to synthesize physically realistic content, provided they receive sufficiently detailed, physics-aware prompts.

Given this diagnosis, the key objectives of the PhyPrompt research are:

1. **Automated Prompt Refinement:** To develop a system that can automatically transform generic user prompts into enriched, physics-aware descriptions. This aims to overcome the need for manual prompt engineering, which requires specialized domain expertise, is time-consuming, and does not scale efficiently across diverse scenarios.
2. **Enhance Physical Plausibility:** To significantly improve the physical commonsense and realism of generated videos, ensuring that objects and their interactions adhere to fundamental physical laws (e.g., gravity, conservation of mass and momentum, fluid dynamics).
3. **Preserve Semantic Fidelity:** While enhancing physical realism, to simultaneously ensure that the refined prompts accurately preserve the user's original intent and the semantic content of the initial prompt. This addresses a common challenge where physics enhancements might unintentionally alter or degrade the desired scene description.
4. **Achieve Synergistic Optimization:** To address the inherent conflict between optimizing for semantic adherence (SA) and physical commonsense (PC). The goal is to develop a learning mechanism that allows for synergistic improvements in both metrics, rather than a trade-off where gains in one objective lead to losses in the other.
5. **Develop an Efficient and Scalable Solution:** To create a lightweight, model-agnostic prompt rewriter that can be easily integrated with various existing T2V generators without requiring their costly fine-tuning. This promotes efficient deployment and broader applicability across different T2V back-ends.

By achieving these objectives, PhyPrompt aims to bridge the gap between user intent and physically consistent video generation, making T2V technologies more reliable and versatile for real-world applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PhyPrompt introduces a two-stage reinforcement learning (RL) framework designed to automatically refine textual prompts for physically plausible text-to-video (T2V) generation. The core idea is to train a lightweight, model-agnostic language model (LLM) as a prompt rewriter that sits between the user and a frozen T2V generator.

#### 3.1. Physics-Focused Chain-of-Thought Dataset

The first stage of the methodology involves supervised fine-tuning (SFT) of a large language model. For this, a specialized Chain-of-Thought (CoT) dataset was constructed to teach the LLM physics-aware prompt enhancement.
- **Data Source:** The dataset started with 160 examples from PhyGenBench, each comprising an original prompt, a relevant physical law, and an enhanced prompt generated by GPT-4o.
- **Reasoning Chain Generation:** To imbue the LLM with reasoning capabilities, GPT-4o-preview was utilized to generate step-by-step reasoning chains. These chains explain how the original prompt is transformed into the physics-aware version, referencing the physical law.
- **Dataset Structure:** The final dataset consists of triplets: (original prompt *x*, reasoning chain *r*, physics-aware prompt *y_CoT*).
- **SFT Target:** This dataset is used to fine-tune a Qwen2.5-Instruct LLM, enabling it to reason about physical phenomena and translate these considerations into descriptive text suitable for video generation while preserving user intent. The SFT objective minimizes the cross-entropy loss between the model's generated prompt and the *y_CoT* target.

#### 3.2. Reinforcement Learning via Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)

The second stage refines the SFT-trained LLM's prompt generation policy using reinforcement learning.
- **Pipeline Overview:** For a given user prompt, the LLM generates multiple candidate enhanced prompts. Each enhanced prompt is then fed into a *frozen* T2V generator (e.g., CogVideoX-2B) to produce a video. This generated video is subsequently evaluated by an automated video evaluator (VideoPhy2-AutoEval) to provide a reward signal, which drives the RL process to update the LLM's policy.
- **GRPO Algorithm:** Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) is employed due to its efficiency in exploring prompt space and not requiring a separate value network.
 - For each prompt *x* in a batch, the LLM (policy πθ) samples *G* (e.g., G=4) candidate rewrites *y_i*.
 - Each rewritten prompt *y_i* is used by the T2V generator *G* to produce a video *v_i = G(y_i)*.
 - The video *v_i* is scored by VideoPhy2-AutoEval to yield a reward *r_i*.
 - The algorithm calculates a group mean reward *r̄* for the candidates and an advantage *A_i = r_i - r̄*.
 - The LLM policy πθ is updated using a clipped GRPO loss function, incorporating an importance ratio and a KL divergence regularization term to prevent large policy shifts from the SFT initialization.

#### 3.3. Dynamic Multi-Objective Reward Design

A key innovation in PhyPrompt is its dynamic reward mechanism, inspired by curriculum training, to address the inherent conflict between semantic adherence (SA) and physical commonsense (PC).
- **Reward Components:** VideoPhy2-AutoEval provides two scores on a 1-5 Likert scale:
 - *r_sa*: Measures how well the video aligns with the prompt's semantic content.
 - *r_pc*: Measures the physical plausibility of the video.
- **Dynamic Curriculum:** The composite reward R(t) is defined as a time-dependent weighted sum:
 - R(t) = *w_sa*(t) ⋅ *r_sa* + *w_pc*(t) ⋅ *r_pc*
 - The weights evolve over training steps (*t*) using an exponential decay function:
 - *w_sa*(t) = exp(-α * t/T*)
 - *w_pc*(t) = 1 - *w_sa*(t)
 - Where *T* is the total training steps and *α* controls the decay rate.
- **Compositional Learning Mechanism:** This curriculum design prioritizes semantic alignment in the early stages of training (*w_sa* ≈ 1). This allows the LLM to first establish "semantic scaffolding" – learning object identities, relationships, and scene structure. As training progresses and the LLM's policy matures, the emphasis gradually shifts towards physical commonsense (*w_pc* ≈ 1), refining this scaffolding with physical specificity (forces, dynamics, causal interactions). This staged approach aims to discover novel compositional prompt structures that are difficult to achieve with static multi-objective optimization, leading to synergistic improvements in both SA and PC.

This two-stage approach, combined with the dynamic curriculum, allows PhyPrompt to generate prompts that elicit physically realistic videos without compromising the user's original semantic intent, all while keeping the underlying T2V generators fixed.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of PhyPrompt focused on its effectiveness in improving semantic adherence (SA) and physical commonsense (PC), its transferability across different T2V generators, and the contribution of its key components through ablation studies.

#### 4.1. Quality Assessment of Generated Videos (with CogVideoX-2B)

- **Effectiveness of PhyPrompt:** PhyPrompt variants consistently outperformed baseline methods and original prompts on the CogVideoX-2B generator.
 - The baseline (original prompts) achieved 43.4% SA, 55.8% PC, and 32.2% joint success (SA ≥ 4 and PC ≥ 4).
 - Promptist, a generic text-to-image adapter, degraded performance, reducing joint success to 30.2%.
 - PhyT2V, an iterative LLM feedback approach, showed improvements (36.2% joint) but was less effective than PhyPrompt.
 - PhyPrompt-7B achieved the highest scores: 47.8% SA, 66.8% PC, and 40.8% joint success. This represents an absolute improvement of 8.6 percentage points (pp) in joint success over the baseline and 10.6 pp over Promptist.
 - Even the smaller PhyPrompt-1.5B variant surpassed Promptist, achieving 37.4% joint success using significantly fewer parameters than general-purpose LLMs.
- **Advantages over General-Purpose LLMs:** PhyPrompt demonstrated that domain-specialized training can outperform larger, general-purpose LLMs for physics-aware video generation.
 - PhyPrompt-7B achieved comparable SA (47.8%) to GPT-4o (47.0%) but delivered substantially higher PC (66.8% vs. 60.0%) and joint performance (40.8% vs. 37.0%). This 6.8 pp PC advantage is attributed to end-to-end RL optimization on video quality metrics, physics-focused CoT pretraining, and the dynamic reward curriculum.
 - DeepSeek-V3, despite having 100 times more parameters (671B), trailed PhyPrompt-7B in PC (64.6% vs. 66.8%) and joint performance (38.6% vs. 40.8%), indicating that task-specific feedback is more effective than raw parameter scaling for this domain.

#### 4.2. Zero-Shot Transfer Across Generators

- PhyPrompt-7B, trained solely on CogVideoX-2B outputs, demonstrated zero-shot transferability across architecturally diverse diffusion models: Lavie (860M), VideoCrafter2 (1.2B), and CogVideoX-5B (5B).
- **Joint-Metric Improvement:** PhyPrompt substantially surpassed baselines on every generator:
 - Lavie: 31.6% joint success (+8.2% over original).
 - VideoCrafter2: 34.8% joint success (+16.8% over original).
 - CogVideoX-5B: 42.0% joint success (+6.6% over original).
- Smaller PhyPrompt variants (1.5B and 3B) also showed consistent improvements.
- This confirms that PhyPrompt learns model-agnostic physical priors rather than generator-specific biases, enabling deployment without per-generator fine-tuning.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study: Reward Design

- **Negative Transfer:** Experiments confirmed that semantic adherence (SA) and physical commonsense (PC) are conflicting objectives with single-objective optimization.
 - SA-only training improved SA but degraded PC, resulting in worse joint performance (31.6% vs. 32.2% baseline).
 - PC-only training improved PC but degraded SA, showing only marginal joint improvement (33.4%).
 - Static balanced weighting achieved 35.4% joint success, better than single-objective but still 5.4% below the dynamic curriculum.
- **Dynamic Curriculum Exceeds Single-Objective Upper Bounds:** The dynamic curriculum achieved 47.8% SA and 66.8% PC. Notably, this performance surpassed SA-only training on semantic adherence (47.8% vs. 45.6%) and simultaneously surpassed PC-only training on physical commonsense (66.8% vs. 65.2%). This indicates that the curriculum-based approach enables synergistic optimization, leading to compositional learning that transcends the limitations of single-objective methods.
- **Qualitative Example:** A case study with the "hammer and nail" scenario illustrated that prompts generated by the static reward failed semantically (nail absent), while the dynamic curriculum generated prompts leading to physically coherent videos with the nail progressively driven into the wood.

The ablation studies confirmed that both the curriculum reward and the two-stage training procedure contribute to the observed performance gains. The dynamic curriculum is crucial for resolving the SA-PC conflict and achieving superior, synergistic improvements.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

PhyPrompt represents a significant step in enhancing the practical utility and trustworthiness of text-to-video (T2V) generation systems. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Bridging the Physical Plausibility Gap:** The work directly addresses a major shortcoming of current T2V models: their inability to consistently generate physically plausible videos despite high visual fidelity. By enabling automatic generation of physics-aware prompts, PhyPrompt moves T2V systems closer to producing content that accurately reflects real-world dynamics.
2. **Novel Multi-Objective Optimization:** The introduction of a dynamic, curriculum-based reward mechanism for reinforcement learning is a key methodological contribution. It demonstrates a method for synergistically optimizing conflicting objectives (semantic adherence and physical commonsense) where static weighting or single-objective training typically lead to trade-offs or negative transfer. This "superadditive" performance, where the curriculum exceeds the upper bounds of either single-objective approach, has broader implications for multi-objective optimization challenges in generative AI.
3. **Efficiency and Scalability:** PhyPrompt's design as a lightweight, model-agnostic prompt rewriter is highly practical. It avoids the computationally expensive process of fine-tuning large T2V generators and exhibits zero-shot transfer across diverse architectures (Lavie, VideoCrafter2, CogVideoX). This efficiency makes it readily deployable and adaptable to various existing and future T2V pipelines, significantly reducing computational overhead for achieving physics-aware generation.
4. **Implications for AI Scaling Laws:** The findings underscore that domain-specialized training with direct task-specific feedback and thoughtful curriculum design can be more effective and parameter-efficient than simply scaling up general-purpose models. PhyPrompt-7B's superior performance compared to much larger LLMs like GPT-4o and DeepSeek-V3 for this specific task suggests a valuable alternative path for developing capabilities in complex, niche domains.
5. **Enabling New Applications:** By ensuring physically grounded video synthesis, PhyPrompt expands the potential applications of T2V technology. This includes:
 - **Robotics and Simulation:** Generating realistic scenarios for training and testing robotic systems, where physical accuracy is paramount.
 - **Scientific Visualization:** Creating accurate visual representations of complex physical phenomena for research and analysis.
 - **Education:** Producing engaging and physically correct educational content that aids understanding of scientific principles.
 - **Content Creation:** Empowering creators to generate more believable and immersive video content without needing expert knowledge of physics in prompt engineering.

In conclusion, PhyPrompt offers a practical, robust, and theoretically insightful solution for improving physical plausibility in T2V. Its innovative curriculum-based RL framework and demonstrated transferability pave the way for more reliable and impactful applications of generative video AI.
