# DeiT III: Revenge of the ViT

> [!summary] Summary
> This paper presents DeiT III, a comprehensive revision of supervised training procedures for Vision Transformers, demonstrating that carefully optimized training allows vanilla ViTs to achieve state-of-the-art performance without architectural modifications or self-supervised pre-training. The work achieved up to 85.2% top-1 accuracy on ImageNet-1k and significantly reduced computational resource requirements for training large models.

## Problem

- A prevailing belief suggested that Vision Transformers (ViTs) required either massive datasets or complex self-supervised learning methods to reach high performance.
- Supervised training baselines for vanilla ViTs were not fully optimized, leading to potentially unfair comparisons with novel architectures or self-supervised approaches.
- The sources of performance gains in ViTs were unclear, with a debate on whether they stemmed from self-supervised mechanisms or underlying training ingredients.

## Method

- The authors developed **DeiT III**, a systematic and simplified supervised training procedure for Vision Transformers, building on established practices while optimizing key components.
- They introduced ==3-Augment==, a simple data augmentation strategy that randomly applies grayscale, solarization, or Gaussian blur.
- They proposed ==Simple Random Crop (SRC)== as a superior cropping method for large-scale pre-training on datasets like ImageNet-21k, and utilized ==Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE) loss== combined with ==Mixup/CutMix== for larger ViTs.

## Results

- A ViT-H model trained from scratch achieved **85.2%** top-1 accuracy on ImageNet-1k, representing a **+5.1%** improvement over previous supervised ViT-H results.
- ViT-L showed a **+3.0%** improvement in accuracy compared to prior approaches when pre-trained on ImageNet-21k.
- The billion-parameter ViT-H achieved **84.9%** accuracy while requiring more than **2x fewer** GPUs and training time compared to standard approaches.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Vanilla Vision Transformers (ViTs) can achieve competitive performance solely through optimized supervised training, challenging the need for extensive self-supervised pre-training or architectural modifications.
> - Simple data augmentation strategies, like '3-Augment' (grayscale, solarization, Gaussian blur), can outperform more complex policies for ViTs.
> - The optimal cropping strategy for ViTs depends on dataset size: Simple Random Crop (SRC) is better for large datasets like ImageNet-21k, while Random Resized Crop (RRC) suits smaller ones.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

#### 1.1. Introduction

Vision Transformers (ViTs) have fundamentally changed computer vision since their introduction, demonstrating that the transformer architecture can achieve excellent performance on image recognition tasks. However, a persistent belief has emerged that ViTs require either massive datasets for pre-training or sophisticated self-supervised learning methods to reach their full potential. "DeiT III: Revenge of the ViT" challenges this assumption by showing that carefully optimized supervised training can unlock remarkable performance from vanilla ViTs.

The paper, authored by researchers from Meta AI and Sorbonne University, presents a comprehensive revision of supervised training procedures for Vision Transformers. Building on their previous work with the original DeiT (Data-efficient Image Transformers), the authors demonstrate that vanilla ViTs can achieve state-of-the-art performance through refined training techniques alone, without architectural modifications or self-supervised pre-training.

#### 1.2. Background: The Debate Over Performance Gains in ViTs

The computer vision landscape has been shaped by an ongoing debate about the sources of performance gains in Vision Transformers. Since the introduction of ViTs, researchers have pursued three main directions: architectural innovations (like Swin Transformers and ConvNeXt), self-supervised learning approaches (such as MAE and BeiT), and supervised training optimizations. While the first two have received extensive attention, supervised training of vanilla ViTs has been relatively underexplored.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

#### 2.1. Identifying the Optimization Gap

The authors identify a critical gap in the literature: the supervised training baselines for ViTs have not been optimized to their full potential. This creates an unfair comparison when evaluating new architectures or self-supervised methods, as they may appear superior simply because the supervised baseline was suboptimal. The paper aims to "feed the debate" about whether performance gains from self-supervised approaches stem from their core mechanisms or from training ingredients that could equally benefit supervised learning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

#### 3.1. Core Training Methodology

The DeiT III methodology represents a systematic approach to optimizing supervised ViT training. The authors build upon established practices while introducing novel simplifications and optimizations. Their approach emphasizes three core principles: simplification over complexity, resource efficiency for large models, and focus on generalization.

The training procedure starts from a foundation inspired by successful ResNet training recipes, adapting them for the transformer architecture. The authors use the LAMB optimizer (a derivative of AdamW) chosen for its effectiveness with large batch sizes typical in ViT training. Standard hyperparameters include cosine learning rate decay, weight decay of 0.02, and 5 warm-up epochs.

Several key regularization techniques are employed. Stochastic depth randomly drops layers during training, with drop rates adapted based on model size. LayerScale, introduced in the authors' previous work, helps stabilize training of very deep transformers by scaling down residual connections. Importantly, the paper introduces the use of Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE) loss instead of the more common Cross-Entropy loss for ImageNet-1k training, showing significant improvements for larger ViTs when combined with Mixup and CutMix.

#### 3.2. 3-Augment: Simplified Data Augmentation

One of the paper's most significant contributions is the introduction of "3-Augment," a remarkably simple data augmentation strategy. Instead of complex learned policies like RandAugment, 3-Augment randomly selects one of three transformations with uniform probability:

- **Grayscale**: Encourages color invariance and focus on shape features
- **Solarization**: Introduces strong color noise for robustness to intensity variations
- **Gaussian Blur**: Slightly alters image details

These are combined with standard ColorJitter and horizontal flip operations. Ablation studies confirm that this simple approach outperforms more complex learned augmentation policies for ViTs, suggesting that transformers may benefit from different types of regularization than convolutional networks.

#### 3.3. Simple Random Crop (SRC) vs Random Resized Crop (RRC)

The paper introduces an important distinction in cropping strategies based on dataset size. For pre-training on large datasets like ImageNet-21k, Simple Random Crop (SRC) proves superior to the widely used Random Resized Crop (RRC).

SRC involves resizing the image so the smallest side matches training resolution, applying small padding, and taking a square crop. This approach preserves aspect ratios and covers a larger fraction of the original image, reducing instances where the labeled object is cropped out. While RRC provides stronger regularization beneficial for smaller datasets like ImageNet-1k, SRC's ability to maintain object presence makes it more suitable for large-scale pre-training.

#### 3.4. Lower Training Resolution Strategy

The paper leverages the "FixRes" effect, where training at lower resolution followed by evaluation at higher resolution can improve performance. For very large models like ViT-H, training at 126×126 resolution instead of 224×224 processes 70% fewer tokens, significantly reducing memory requirements and training time while acting as a regularizer to prevent overfitting.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. Experimental Results

The experimental validation demonstrates unprecedented performance improvements across multiple benchmarks. On ImageNet-1k trained from scratch, a ViT-H model achieves 85.2% top-1 accuracy, representing a remarkable +5.1% improvement over previous supervised ViT-H results. For ImageNet-21k pre-training, ViT-L shows a +3.0% improvement compared to prior approaches.

The training strategies scale effectively to the largest models without hyperparameter adaptation. The billion-parameter ViT-H (52 layers) achieves 84.9% accuracy while requiring more than 2× fewer GPUs and training time compared to standard approaches.

Performance on challenging datasets further validates the approach. Models trained with DeiT III show superior generalization to ImageNet-V2, indicating reduced overfitting to the training distribution. Transfer learning experiments across six fine-grained classification datasets demonstrate competitive results, often with better FLOPs-accuracy trade-offs than architectural variants like Swin Transformers.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

#### 5.1. Impact and Significance

The significance of DeiT III extends far beyond incremental performance improvements. The work fundamentally challenges prevailing assumptions about what's necessary for competitive ViT performance. By demonstrating that vanilla ViTs can match or exceed more complex architectures and self-supervised methods through optimized supervised training alone, the paper reshapes the research landscape.

The resource efficiency gains are particularly impactful for democratizing large model training. The ability to train billion-parameter models with halved GPU requirements makes high-performance ViTs accessible to a broader research community, potentially accelerating innovation in the field.

The emphasis on simplicity over complexity provides valuable lessons for the broader machine learning community. The success of 3-Augment and Simple Random Crop demonstrates that understanding model and data characteristics often yields better results than brute-force complexity or automated search methods.

#### 5.2. Conclusion

"DeiT III: Revenge of the ViT" represents a paradigm shift in Vision Transformer research. By demonstrating that meticulous optimization of supervised training can unlock significant performance gains in vanilla ViTs, the work compels researchers to reconsider the true sources of performance improvements in vision models.

The paper's contributions extend beyond numerical improvements to provide practical methodologies that make large-scale ViT training more accessible and efficient. The simplified yet highly effective training ingredients challenge the notion that complexity is necessary for state-of-the-art performance, instead advocating for principled understanding of model requirements.

As the field continues to evolve toward foundation models and large-scale vision systems, DeiT III provides crucial insights into effective supervised training strategies. The work establishes stronger baselines for future research while offering clear alternatives to computationally intensive self-supervised approaches, ultimately advancing the democratization of high-performance computer vision models.
