# From Spatial to Actions: Grounding Vision-Language-Action Model in Spatial Foundation Priors

> [!summary] Summary
> FALCON, a Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model from ByteDance Seed, NUS, NTU, THU, and SMU, enhances generalist robot policies by integrating robust 3D spatial understanding without compromising pre-trained vision-language alignment. It leverages an Embodied Spatial Model and a Spatial-Enhanced Action Head, achieving state-of-the-art performance across simulation benchmarks and real-world manipulation tasks, particularly in cluttered scenes and those requiring precise spatial reasoning.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, built on 2D Vision-Language Models (VLMs), exhibit a "spatial reasoning gap" when operating in the 3D physical world, limiting generalization.
- Prior methods for 3D integration either rely on specialized 3D sensors with poor "modality transferability" or inject weak 3D cues insufficient for robust geometric understanding.
- Directly concatenating spatial embeddings with text tokens into the VLM's backbone disrupts pre-trained vision-language alignment, degrading zero-shot generalization capabilities.

## Method

- FALCON introduces an ==Embodied Spatial Model== (ESM) that extracts robust global 3D geometric priors from diverse input modalities (RGB, optional depth, and camera poses).
- It employs a Spatial-Enhanced Action Head that integrates these geometric tokens from the ESM with semantic action representations from a 2D VLM, preserving the VLM's pre-trained alignment.
- A two-stage post-training pipeline is used to align spatial features and then refine VLM semantic features to incorporate spatial cues for precise action prediction.

## Results

- FALCON achieved state-of-the-art performance on the CALVIN benchmark (improving Avg. Len. by 1.05 over 3D Diffuser Actor) and consistently outperformed baselines in SimplerEnv tasks (e.g., **62.9%** overall success on Google Robot tasks vs. **3.7%** for RT-2-X in a complex drawer task).
- In real-world experiments, the model achieved a **70.0%** average success rate across nine cluttered task suites, outperforming SpatialVLA by **25.6%**, and demonstrated superior few-shot adaptation capabilities (e.g., **80%** success for unseen objects).
- Evaluations confirmed FALCON's enhanced spatial understanding, showing robustness to object scale variations and precise adherence to abstract spatial instructions, leveraging additional 3D inputs to boost success rates in height-sensitive tasks from **60%** to **80%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating 3D spatial priors via a dedicated action head, rather than the VLM's backbone, effectively avoids "embedding drift" and preserves the VLM's zero-shot generalization.
> - A stochastically conditioned Embodied Spatial Model can extract strong geometric priors from RGB-only inputs and seamlessly leverage additional 3D modalities (depth, pose) when available, ensuring robust modality transferability.
> - Simple element-wise addition effectively fuses compressed spatial features with semantic action tokens, achieving superior performance compared to more complex fusion strategies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics is actively pursuing generalist robot policies that enable machines to interpret natural language instructions and execute complex action sequences in the physical world. A significant area of advancement in this pursuit is Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which leverage large-scale pre-trained Vision Language Models (VLMs). These VLMs, such as Kosmos-2, Flamingo, or RT-2, are primarily designed to align 2D images with text, excelling at semantic understanding and supporting manipulation tasks conditioned on language and camera inputs.

However, a fundamental challenge arises from the inherent discrepancy between the 2D nature of VLM processing and the 3D requirements of real-world robot interaction. This "spatial reasoning gap" limits the generalization and adaptability of current VLAs. Robots often struggle with tasks requiring precise geometric reasoning, depth perception, or understanding of spatial relations, leading to poor transferability to novel scenes or variations in object properties like height or scale.

Prior attempts to address this spatial gap typically fall into two categories:
1. **Direct 3D Input Integration:** Methods like PointVLA and GeoVLA consume explicit 3D representations (e.g., point clouds, depth maps). While these approaches enhance geometric awareness, they depend on specialized sensors and specific input modalities, which are often expensive, difficult to deploy, and not readily available in large-scale datasets. This limits their "modality transferability"—the ability to function with different sensor setups without retraining.
2. **Weak 3D Cue Injection:** Alternative methods introduce weak 3D cues, such as pseudo-depth estimates or learnable spatial embeddings, directly into the VLM's input space (e.g., 3D-VLA, SpatialVLA, Evo-0). These approaches face limitations in spatial representation, often failing to capture robust 3D priors for fine-grained tasks. Furthermore, concatenating spatial embeddings with text tokens can disrupt the VLM's pre-trained vision-language alignment, leading to "embedding drift" and degradation of zero-shot generalization.

This work, FALCON, positions itself to address these persistent limitations by proposing a novel paradigm for integrating richer 3D spatial information. It aims to deliver robust 3D spatial understanding, strong modality transferability, and principled integration of 3D priors into VLAs, moving beyond the limitations of existing 2D-centric models and the challenges of prior 3D integration techniques.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model capable of robust 3D spatial understanding to enhance generalist robot policies. This objective is motivated by several critical limitations identified in the current generation of VLA models:

1. **Bridging the 2D-3D Spatial Reasoning Gap:** Existing VLA models largely rely on 2D vision-language encoders, which lack explicit mechanisms for 3D geometric perception. This creates a fundamental "spatial reasoning gap" because robots must operate in a 3D physical world. The motivation is to overcome this gap, enabling VLAs to perform reliably in scenarios demanding reasoning about geometry, depth, or spatial relations, which is crucial for tasks like precise grasping, manipulation in cluttered environments, or dealing with objects of varying scales and heights. Without robust 3D awareness, VLAs exhibit limited generalization to novel scenes and struggle with adaptability to environmental changes.

2. **Enhancing Modality Transferability:** Previous attempts to incorporate 3D information often depend on specialized sensors (e.g., RGB-D cameras, LiDAR) or specific 3D input formats (e.g., point clouds). This leads to poor modality transferability, meaning these models cannot function effectively or leverage different available sensor inputs (e.g., RGB-only, RGB-D, camera pose) without significant retraining or architectural modifications. A key motivation is to design a VLA that can flexibly utilize various 3D modalities when available, yet remain robust and effective with only RGB input, thereby reducing dependency on specialized sensor setups and improving scalability, especially given the scarcity of large-scale 3D-annotated robotic datasets.

3. **Improving Spatial Representation Beyond Weak Cues:** Some methods attempt to inject weak 3D cues (e.g., pseudo-depth or learnable spatial embeddings) into VLAs. However, these cues often provide insufficient geometric signals, failing to capture the robust 3D priors necessary for complex manipulation tasks requiring detailed spatial understanding (e.g., relative heights, object sizes). The motivation here is to inject richer, more representative 3D spatial tokens that provide comprehensive geometric information, ideally derivable from RGB alone, but augmentable with higher-fidelity 3D inputs when present.

4. **Preserving Vision-Language Alignment:** Directly concatenating spatial embeddings with text tokens within the vision-language backbone of VLAs risks disrupting their pre-trained vision-language alignment. This misalignment can degrade zero-shot generalization, particularly in tasks requiring high-level language reasoning, such as abstract spatial prompts. The motivation is to integrate spatial information in a principled manner that maintains the integrity and semantic understanding capabilities of the pre-trained VLM while still enabling precise action generation.

In summary, FALCON aims to provide (i) robust spatial reasoning by leveraging spatial foundation models, (ii) strong modality transferability through a flexible Embodied Spatial Model, and (iii) principled integration of 3D priors via a Spatial-Enhanced Action Head that avoids compromising the VLM's language understanding.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

FALCON (From Spatial to Action) is an end-to-end VLA model designed to integrate robust 3D spatial understanding into generalist robot policies. The approach is structured around a problem definition, an overall architecture, a specific training objective, and two core components: an Embodied Spatial Model (ESM) and a Spatial-Enhanced Action Head.

#### 3.1. Problem Definition

The system addresses the problem of task-oriented, language-guided robot control. At each timestep *t*, the robot receives visual observations $O_t = \{I_1^t, \dots, I_n^t\}$ (e.g., side camera $I_{3rd}^t$, wrist-mounted camera $I_{hand}^t$) and a natural language instruction $L$. The goal is to generate an action sequence $A_t = [a_t, \dots, a_{t+C-1}]$, where each action $a_i$ is a 7D vector encoding a 6-DoF gripper pose (Euler angles) and a binary open/close state. Optional inputs, such as depth maps $D_t \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W}$ and camera poses $P \in \mathbb{R}^7$, can enhance spatial grounding but are not strictly required, ensuring adaptability across different sensing setups. The function $F$ maps these inputs to actions: $A_t = F(O_t, L, D_t, P)$, where $D_t$ and $P$ are optional.

#### 3.2. Overall Architecture

FALCON consists of three main components:
1. **2D Vision-Language Model (VLM):** A pre-trained VLM (e.g., Kosmos-2) processes visual observations $O_t$ and language instruction $L$. It tokenizes these inputs into a unified multi-modal sequence, appending a learnable action token $t_{act}$. The output hidden state corresponding to $t_{act}$, denoted $\hat{t}_{act}$, serves as the semantic action representation.
2. **Embodied Spatial Model (ESM):** This component processes a third-view image $I_{3rd}^t$ along with optional geometric inputs (depth $D_t$ and camera poses $P$). Through a spatial encoder $E_{spl}(\cdot)$, it extracts a set of spatial tokens $T_{spl}$, which encapsulate global 3D geometric priors.
3. **Spatial-Enhanced Action Head:** This head integrates the semantic action token $\hat{t}_{act}$ from the VLM and the spatial tokens $T_{spl}$ from the ESM to generate precise robot actions.

#### 3.3. Training Objective

The training process employs a two-stage post-training pipeline:
1. **Stage 1: Feature Space Alignment:** All pre-trained components (VLM, action predictor, ESM) are frozen. Only a lightweight adapter (D) is optimized to establish an initial alignment between spatial tokens and the VLA's feature space. The adapter's final linear layer is zero-initialized to ensure spatial tokens initially contribute minimally, preserving the pre-trained feature space.
2. **Stage 2: Joint Feature Refinement:** The VLM and the adapter are unfrozen, while the action predictor and ESM remain frozen. This stage allows the VLM to adapt its semantic features to incorporate spatial cues, which then inform action prediction.
The overall loss function for action sequence generation combines Mean Squared Error (MSE) for the 6-DoF gripper pose and Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE) for the binary open/close state, with a weighting factor $\lambda$ balancing their contributions.

#### 3.4. Embodied Spatial Model (ESM)

The ESM is designed to leverage spatial foundation models while also exploiting additional 3D modalities.
1. **Spatial Token Encoding:** An input image $I_t$ is encoded into visual tokens $T_{vis}$ via DINO, which are then concatenated with a learnable camera token $t_{cam}$ and fed into a Spatial Encoder $E_{spl}(\cdot)$. This encoder, comprising cross-attention and self-attention blocks (following VGGT), outputs spatial tokens $T_{spl}$ and refined camera tokens $\hat{t}_{cam}$. These tokens are subsequently used by reconstruction heads for depth and camera prediction.
2. **3D Conditions Encoding:**
 - **Camera Pose:** An MLP-based camera encoder $E_{cam}(\cdot)$ encodes intrinsic and normalized extrinsic parameters of the side camera into a GT camera token $t_{gt-cam}$.
 - **Depth Map:** A depth encoder $E_{dpt}(\cdot)$, consisting of convolutional layers, processes the normalized depth map $D'_t$ concatenated with its validity mask $M_{dpt}$ to produce depth tokens $T_{dpt}$.
3. **3D Conditions Injection and Training Strategy:** To enhance geometric grounding of $T_{spl}$, the learnable $t_{cam}$ is replaced with $t_{gt-cam}$, and $T_{dpt}$ is fused with $T_{vis}$ via element-wise addition. A stochastic conditioning strategy (using Bernoulli probabilities $b_d, b_p$) is employed during training, randomly deciding whether to inject depth and/or pose. This ensures the model can utilize 3D cues when available but retains strong image-only spatial reasoning when they are absent. Multi-task supervision, including depth, point map, and pose losses, is used for ESM training.

#### 3.5. Spatial-Enhanced Action Head

This component integrates the geometric $T_{spl}$ from ESM with the semantic $\hat{t}_{act}$ from the VLM.
1. **Modality Fusion Strategies:** The spatial tokens $T_{spl}$ are first compressed into a single vector $t_{spl}$ via max-pooling. This $t_{spl}$ is then projected into the VLM's feature space using a lightweight MLP adapter $D$ to obtain $e_{t_{spl}}$. The aligned spatial feature $e_{t_{spl}}$ is then fused with the semantic action token $\hat{t}_{act}$ using element-wise addition. (Other fusion strategies like cross-attention and FiLM-gated modulation were also investigated and compared).
2. **Action Predictor:** The fused feature vector $f_{fused}$ is fed into an action predictor $\pi$. Two architectures are explored: an MLP-based predictor for direct mapping ($A_t = \pi(f_{fused}^t)$) or an LSTM-based predictor for long-horizon tasks, which processes a history of fused feature representations ($f_{fused}^{t-H+1}, \dots, f_{fused}^t$) before an MLP generates the action chunk.

This methodological design aims to provide a robust, flexible, and well-aligned integration of 3D spatial information into VLA models for complex robotic manipulation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

FALCON was evaluated comprehensively across three simulation benchmarks (CALVIN, SimplerEnv) and eleven real-world tasks, demonstrating its performance and robustness.

#### 4.1. Simulation Experiments

- **CALVIN Benchmark:** FALCON achieved state-of-the-art (SOTA) performance in both the zero-shot ABC→D and the in-distribution ABCD→D settings. In ABC→D, FALCON surpassed previous methods, including those relying on ground-truth point clouds, by improving the average length of completed tasks (Avg. Len.) by 1.05 compared to the 3D Diffuser Actor. This indicates effective implicit spatial information integration.
- **SimplerEnv Evaluations:**
 - **WidowX Robot tasks:** FALCON consistently outperformed all baselines, achieving the best performance in tasks like "Put Spoon on Towel" (62.5% success vs. 16.7% for SpatialVLA) and "Put Carrot on Plate" (41.7% vs. 25.0%). This demonstrates superior adaptability.
 - **Google Robot tasks:** FALCON achieved an overall success rate of 62.9%, surpassing all baseline methods. Notably, in the challenging "Open Top Drawer and Place Apple" task, FALCON achieved 41.7% success, significantly outperforming large-scale models like RT-2-X (3.7%) and other baselines, which often showed near-zero success rates. This highlights FALCON's generalization and spatial perception capabilities.

#### 4.2. Real-World Experiments

Real-world evaluations were conducted across Base Tasks, Few-shot Adaptation, and Spatial Understanding Capability Evaluations, all pre-trained on the Open X-Embodiment dataset and fine-tuned on real-robot data.

- **Base Tasks (Cluttered Scenes):** FALCON achieved the highest average success rate of 70.0% across nine task suites, outperforming the advanced SpatialVLA (44.4%) by 25.6%. In specific tasks like "pick banana and place on red plate," FALCON demonstrated precise instruction following and superior scene understanding, consistently placing the object on the correct target, unlike some baselines that misidentified targets.
- **Few-shot Adaptation:** FALCON exhibited the highest performance across various few-shot settings, outperforming the second-best model by 27.5% in "Simple" scenarios and 27% in "Unseen Average" scenarios. In the "Unseen Object" variation of "open drawer and place bread," FALCON achieved an 80% success rate, whereas other models showed near-zero success.
- **Spatial Understanding Capability Evaluations:** FALCON demonstrated superior spatial understanding compared to baselines. For instance, it showed strong robustness to object scale variations (e.g., in "stack blue block on red block" with larger or smaller blocks), avoiding common failures like collisions or premature releases observed in methods like RoboVLM. It consistently achieved the highest success rates in tasks requiring reasoning about varying object sizes, heights, and abstract spatial instructions.

#### 4.3. In-Depth Analysis (Ablation Studies)

- **Spatial Token Injection:** Directly injecting spatial tokens into the action head (FALCON's design) significantly outperformed injecting them into the VLM's vision-language backbone (FALCON$_{VLM-tokens}$). The latter caused a notable performance degradation (e.g., Avg. Len. dropped from 3.91 to 3.79 in ABC→D), confirming that preserving the VLM's pre-trained semantic representation space is beneficial.
- **Modality Fusion Strategies:** Element-wise addition, FALCON's standard approach, consistently yielded the best performance compared to cross-attention and FiLM-gated modulation. This indicates that a simple, parameter-free fusion strategy is effective and computationally efficient for combining spatial and semantic representations.
- **Modality Transferability:**
 - FALCON (RGB-only) achieved competitive performance compared to Kosmos-VLA (RGB-D) and surpassed Kosmos-VLA (RGB-only) by 0.43 in Avg. Len. in ABC→D.
 - The model demonstrated robust performance when trained only on RGB but evaluated with additional depth (test w/ d), showing a slight improvement in Avg. Len. Conversely, when trained with RGB-D but evaluated without depth (test w/o d), performance remained robust.
 - In real-world tasks, incorporating depth and camera poses increased success rates from 60% to 80% in scenarios involving objects of varying heights, validating the effective utilization of additional geometric information.
- **Embodied Spatial Model (ESM):** The ESM achieved monocular depth estimation performance comparable to VGGT with RGB-only input, and significantly improved when additional depth or camera pose information was provided. This confirms ESM's ability to seamlessly leverage diverse 3D modality inputs, contributing to FALCON's modality transferability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in this paper introduces FALCON, a VLA model that addresses several critical limitations in the field of generalist robotics, particularly concerning 3D spatial understanding. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows.

1. **Bridging the 2D-3D Gap:** FALCON directly tackles the spatial reasoning gap inherent in existing VLAs that are built on 2D vision-language models. By effectively integrating rich 3D spatial tokens into the action head, it enables more robust reasoning about geometry, depth, and spatial relations in the physical world.
2. **Enhanced Modality Transferability:** The Embodied Spatial Model (ESM) allows FALCON to extract strong geometric priors from RGB images alone while simultaneously being capable of fusing additional 3D modalities (e.g., depth, camera poses) for higher fidelity when available. This flexibility in handling diverse sensory inputs without requiring architectural changes or retraining represents a significant advance in practical deployability.
3. **Principled Integration of Spatial Priors:** The design choice to inject spatial tokens into a Spatial-Enhanced Action Head, rather than concatenating them into the vision-language backbone, prevents disruption of the VLM's pre-trained semantic understanding and vision-language alignment. This maintains the VLM's high-level reasoning capabilities while providing the action head with fine-grained spatial cues necessary for precise control.
4. **State-of-the-Art Performance:** Through comprehensive evaluations across both simulation and real-world tasks, FALCON consistently outperforms existing VLA methods, demonstrating superior robustness and generalization in spatially demanding scenarios, including cluttered scenes, varying object scales and heights, and spatial-prompt conditioning.

**Potential Impact:**
1. **Advancement of Generalist Robotics:** FALCON contributes to the development of more capable and reliable generalist robot policies. By improving 3D spatial understanding, robots can tackle a wider range of complex manipulation tasks in unstructured and dynamic environments, moving closer to human-like dexterity and adaptability.
2. **Increased Practical Deployability:** The modality transferability of FALCON is crucial for real-world applications. Robots can be deployed with simpler RGB cameras when 3D sensors are unavailable or cost-prohibitive, yet still benefit from high-quality 3D data if it becomes accessible. This reduces dependency on specialized hardware, lowering barriers to adoption in various industries.
3. **Foundation for Future Research:** The paradigm introduced by FALCON—leveraging spatial foundation models and a specialized action head for spatial integration—opens new avenues for research into robot perception and control. Future work could explore integrating different types of spatial foundation models, more sophisticated multi-view camera systems, and advanced fusion mechanisms, building upon the demonstrated success of separating semantic and spatial processing.
4. **Improved Robustness and Generalization:** The enhanced spatial reasoning capabilities lead to better generalization to unseen objects, backgrounds, and task descriptions. This robustness is critical for reliable long-term autonomous operation in real-world settings where variability is high.

In conclusion, FALCON represents a step in addressing the foundational challenges of spatial awareness in vision-language-action models, contributing to the development of more intelligent and adaptable robotic systems.
