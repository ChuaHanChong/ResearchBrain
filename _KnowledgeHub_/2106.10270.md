# How to train your ViT? Data, Augmentation, and Regularization in Vision Transformers

> [!summary] Summary
> The paper empirically investigates the interplay of data size, augmentation, regularization, and model capacity in Vision Transformer training. It provides quantitative guidelines showing that well-tuned augmentation and regularization can act as a data multiplier, matching performance of larger datasets, and confirms the superior transferability of models pre-trained on more extensive data.

## Problem

- Lack of a comprehensive, systematic study on the interplay between data size, augmentation, regularization, model capacity, and computational budget in ViT training.
- ViTs' weaker inductive biases often necessitated extensive data or careful augmentation and regularization (AugReg), but their optimal balance and quantitative effects were largely unquantified in a controlled setting.
- Inconsistencies and noise from varying implementations and hyper-parameters in previous works hindered robust comparison and practical guidance for ViT training.

## Method

- Conducted a systematic and large-scale empirical study across various data sizes, model configurations (==ViT-Ti== to ==ViT-L==), and 28 distinct **Augmentation and Regularization (AugReg)** strategies.
- Utilized a unified ==JAX/Flax== codebase on Google's TPU infrastructure to ensure consistent optimization and hardware performance, minimizing confounding factors.
- Evaluated models on ImageNet-1k and a diverse subset of the ==Visual Task Adaptation Benchmark (VTAB)== to assess transfer learning capabilities, with extensive release of trained models and code.

## Results

- Well-tuned AugReg on ImageNet-1k achieved performance comparable to pre-training on the **10x** larger ImageNet-21k without AugReg.
- Transferring publicly available pre-trained ViT models was overwhelmingly more effective and cost-efficient for small to mid-sized downstream datasets compared to training from scratch.
- While both AugReg types can be beneficial, augmentation techniques were generally preferred over explicit model-based regularization (Dropout, Stochastic Depth) on larger datasets like ImageNet-21k, where regularization often hurt performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Judicious Augmentation and Regularization (AugReg) can effectively act as a 'data multiplier,' compensating for a lack of training data and matching performance from significantly larger datasets.
> - Pre-training Vision Transformers on larger datasets consistently generates more generic and transferable representations, leading to better performance across diverse downstream tasks.
> - For similar inference throughput, increasing patch size (e.g., ViT-B/32) generally yields significantly better accuracy than thinning the model (e.g., ViT-Ti/16), indicating the importance of token sequence length.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The emergence of Vision Transformers (ViTs) marked a significant shift in computer vision, challenging the long-held dominance of Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs). Introduced by Dosovitskiy et al. (2020), ViTs leverage the self-attention mechanism, originally popularized in natural language processing, to process images as sequences of patches. While ViTs demonstrated competitive, and often superior, performance, particularly on large-scale datasets like JFT-300M, they were initially observed to have a "weaker inductive bias" compared to CNNs. This means ViTs do not inherently possess properties like translational equivariance, which are crucial for generalization in visual tasks. Consequently, early studies showed that ViTs performed best when trained on massive amounts of data or required strong regularization and data augmentation ("AugReg") schemes to prevent overfitting, especially on smaller datasets like ImageNet-1k.

This paper directly addresses a critical knowledge gap in this evolving landscape: the lack of a comprehensive, systematic study on the interplay between data size, augmentation, regularization, model capacity, and computational budget in ViT training. Previous works, such as the original ViT paper, focused on performance at scale, while others, like DeiT (Touvron et al., 2020), emphasized data efficiency through strong AugReg on smaller datasets. However, no study had systematically varied these factors across a wide range of settings and model sizes under a consistent experimental framework.

The authors position their work as a foundational empirical study, providing quantitative insights that can guide practitioners and researchers. It bridges the gap between the theoretical promise of ViTs and their practical deployment, particularly for those with limited access to extremely large, proprietary datasets. By analyzing various trade-offs, the paper informs the optimal strategies for training ViTs efficiently and effectively, aligning with a broader trend in machine learning research that emphasizes understanding the scaling laws and practical considerations for large models. The study also implicitly compares with previous findings on CNNs regarding data efficiency and transfer learning (e.g., Kornblith et al., 2019; Kolesnikov et al., 2020), extending these insights to the ViT architecture.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to conduct a systematic and comprehensive empirical study to understand the intricate relationships between various factors influencing the training of Vision Transformers (ViTs). Specifically, the authors aim to elucidate the interplay between:
1. **Amount of training data:** Investigating how performance scales with different dataset sizes (e.g., ImageNet-1k vs. ImageNet-21k).
2. **Augmentation and Regularization (AugReg):** Analyzing the impact of different AugReg strategies and their strengths.
3. **Model size and architecture:** Studying how different ViT configurations (Tiny to Large, and hybrid models) respond to varying data and AugReg.
4. **Compute budget:** Examining the computational cost associated with achieving specific performance levels across different training settings.

A key motivation for this study stems from the observation that ViTs' weaker inductive biases often necessitate extensive data or careful AugReg, yet the optimal balance and the quantitative effects of these factors remained largely unquantified in a controlled setting. The authors explicitly state their focus on insights useful for "a practitioner with limited compute and data budgets," implying a practical, rather than purely theoretical, orientation. They highlight the issue of inconsistencies and noise in comparing results from different works due to varying implementations and hyper-parameters. Therefore, a crucial underlying motivation is to provide a "homogeneous study," training and evaluating all models in a "strictly controlled setting" using a unified codebase, thereby minimizing confounding factors and enabling robust conclusions. This rigorous approach seeks to replace anecdotal evidence and scattered observations with systematic, quantifiable findings that can serve as reliable guidelines for the community.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The study's methodology is characterized by its systematic, large-scale, and controlled empirical design. The authors employ a unified experimental setup to ensure consistency and comparability across a vast number of experiments.

**Unified Experimental Setup:**
- All models were trained using a single JAX/Flax codebase on Google's TPU infrastructure, ensuring consistent optimization and hardware performance.
- Datasets were accessed via the TensorFlow Datasets library, standardizing data loading and preprocessing.
- Inference speed measurements were obtained on V100 GPUs using the `timm` PyTorch library, covering both training and deployment aspects.

**Datasets:**
- **Pre-training:** ImageNet-1k (1.3M images, 1k categories) and ImageNet-21k (14M images, 21k categories). ImageNet-21k images were de-duplicated against downstream task test sets.
- **Evaluation (Transfer Learning):** A subset of the Visual Task Adaptation Benchmark (VTAB) was used, including CIFAR-100, Oxford IIIT Pets (Pets37), Resisc45 (satellite imagery), and Kitti-distance (geometric task). The full 19-dataset VTAB benchmark was used for robustness checks.
- **Metrics:** Top-1 classification accuracy, with hyper-parameters selected on validation splits and final numbers reported on test splits. A "minival" split (1% of training data) was used for ImageNet-1k model selection, but for ImageNet-21k pre-trained models fine-tuned on ImageNet-1k, ImageNetV2 was used as a validation set to mitigate data overlap bias.

**Models:**
- **Vision Transformers (ViT):** Four configurations from (Dosovitskiy et al., 2020; Touvron et al., 2020): ViT-Ti (Tiny), ViT-S (Small), ViT-B (Base), and ViT-L (Large), spanning 5.8M to 307M parameters. Patch sizes of 16x16 and 32x32 were explored. A minor modification from the original ViT involved dropping the hidden layer in the head for most ImageNet-21k pre-training, as it did not improve accuracy and could cause instability.
- **Hybrid Models:** Combined a ResNet backbone (ResNet stem + bottleneck blocks) with a ViT, feeding the spatial output of the ResNet to the ViT. Examples include R+Ti/16 and R50+L/32.

**Regularization and Data Augmentation (AugReg):**
- **Regularization:** Dropout (on intermediate ViT activations) and Stochastic Depth (linearly increasing layer dropping probability).
- **Data Augmentation:** Mixup (varying `alpha` parameter) and RandAugment (varying magnitude `m` and layers `l`).
- **Weight Decay:** Two values (0.1 or 0.03) were explored, with decoupled weight decay.
- **Systematic Sweep:** A total of 28 AugReg configurations were tested (2 regularization settings × 7 augmentation setups × 2 weight decay values).

**Pre-training:**
- **Optimizer:** Adam with $\beta_1=0.9, \beta_2=0.999$.
- **Batch Size:** 4096.
- **Learning Rate Schedule:** Cosine decay with linear warmup (10k steps).
- **Gradient Clipping:** At global norm 1.
- **Image Preprocessing:** Inception-style cropping and random horizontal flipping.
- **Training Durations:** ImageNet-1k for 300 epochs; ImageNet-21k for 30 and 300 epochs, allowing for compute-constant comparisons between 1k (300ep) and 21k (30ep).

**Fine-tuning:**
- **Optimizer:** SGD with momentum 0.9.
- **Batch Size:** 512.
- **Learning Rate Schedule:** Cosine decay with linear warmup.
- **Gradient Clipping:** At global norm 1.
- **Hyper-parameter Sweep:** 2-3 learning rates and 1-2 training durations per dataset.
- **Resolutions:** Both original (224px) and higher (384px) resolutions were used.

This rigorous and comprehensive experimental design, coupled with the release of models and code, forms the backbone of the study, enabling the derivation of robust and practically actionable insights.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive empirical study yielded several key findings, shedding light on effective strategies for training ViTs:

1. **Augmentation and Regularization (AugReg) as a Data Multiplier:** The study found that a judicious combination of AugReg techniques can effectively compensate for a lack of training data. Specifically, well-tuned AugReg on ImageNet-1k achieved performance comparable to pre-training on the 10x larger ImageNet-21k without AugReg. Similarly, AugReg applied to ImageNet-21k, combined with increased compute, could match or even surpass results from models trained on the 25x larger, proprietary JFT-300M dataset. This suggests AugReg can effectively act as a "data multiplier," but note that the compute required to achieve similar performance remains roughly the same, regardless of whether it's achieved through more data or better AugReg.

2. **Transfer Learning Is Superior to Training from Scratch:** For practitioners dealing with small to mid-sized downstream datasets, transferring a publicly available pre-trained ViT model is overwhelmingly more effective and cost-efficient than training a ViT from scratch, even with aggressive AugReg. On small datasets like Pets37 (3,000 images), training from scratch could not approach the accuracy of transferred models. For larger, more diverse datasets like Resisc45 (30,000 satellite images), training from scratch required orders of magnitude more compute and still struggled to match transfer performance, highlighting the immense value of pre-trained models.

3. **More Data Yields More Generic Models:** Pre-training on larger datasets consistently produced more generic and transferable representations. Models pre-trained on ImageNet-21k (13M images) significantly outperformed those trained on ImageNet-1k (1.3M images) when evaluated across the diverse tasks of the VTAB benchmark, even when the total pre-training compute was kept constant (ImageNet-1k for 300 epochs vs. ImageNet-21k for 30 epochs). Longer training on ImageNet-21k further improved transferability, reinforcing the notion that greater data diversity and volume lead to more broadly applicable models.

4. **Prefer Augmentation to Regularization:** While both augmentations (Mixup, RandAugment) and regularization (Dropout, Stochastic Depth) can be beneficial, the study indicates a general preference for augmentation. For medium-sized datasets like ImageNet-1k, most forms of AugReg were helpful. However, on the larger ImageNet-21k dataset, model-based regularization techniques (Dropout, Stochastic Depth) frequently hurt performance, especially for smaller models or shorter training schedules. Augmentation, conversely, tended to be more broadly beneficial or less detrimental.

5. **Effective Model Selection for Transfer Learning:** A practical finding for practitioners is that selecting a pre-trained model based on its upstream validation accuracy (e.g., on ImageNet-21k) is generally a cost-effective and optimal strategy for transfer learning. While fine-tuning all available pre-trained checkpoints and then selecting the best can yield marginal gains in some cases, the simpler strategy works well in most scenarios. A crucial caveat was identified for ImageNet-1k evaluation: when pre-training on ImageNet-21k, the standard "minival" split of ImageNet-1k can be biased due to data overlap. The authors recommend using the independently collected ImageNetV2 dataset for validation in such cases to ensure fair hyper-parameter selection.

6. **Patch Size over Model Thinning for Speed-Accuracy Trade-offs:** For models with similar inference throughput, the study found that increasing the patch size (e.g., ViT-B/32, which uses larger patches) generally resulted in significantly better accuracy than making the model "thinner" (e.g., ViT-Ti/16, which has fewer parameters but smaller patches). This highlights that parameter count alone is not always a reliable indicator of model capacity or speed-accuracy trade-offs; the token sequence length, influenced by patch size, plays a crucial role.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The paper makes highly significant contributions by providing a meticulously controlled, large-scale empirical analysis of Vision Transformer training, moving beyond anecdotal observations to offer quantitative insights. Its potential impact is multi-faceted:

1. **Guidance for Practitioners:** The study provides concrete, actionable recommendations for researchers and developers working with ViTs. The advice to leverage pre-trained models, judiciously apply data augmentation (often preferring it over explicit regularization), and prioritize larger pre-training datasets is invaluable, especially for those with limited compute resources or access to proprietary data. The released checkpoints and code further democratize access to these optimal training settings.

2. **Foundation for Future Research:** By systematically charting the complex landscape of ViT training, the paper establishes a strong empirical foundation. It clarifies which factors are most critical and under what conditions, thereby guiding future architectural designs, training strategies, and hyper-parameter optimization efforts for ViT variants. It identifies areas for further exploration, such as the systematic comparison of self-supervised and supervised pre-training.

3. **Understanding ViT Behavior:** The findings deepen the community's understanding of ViTs' strengths and weaknesses. The "data multiplier" effect of AugReg, the superior transferability from larger datasets, and the subtle interplay between model size, patch size, and regularization strength provide critical insights into how ViTs learn and generalize.

4. **Addressing Reproducibility and Comparability:** By conducting a homogeneous study with a unified codebase and releasing trained models, the authors directly address the pervasive challenges of reproducibility and fair comparison in deep learning research. This commitment to open science sets a high standard and offers a valuable public resource.

5. **Compute Efficiency:** While the study itself consumed significant compute, its ultimate goal is to enable more efficient research and deployment. By providing optimal training recipes and recommended checkpoints, it aims to reduce the need for extensive hyper-parameter searches for individual practitioners, thus lowering the overall carbon footprint and computational cost associated with developing new ViT-based applications.

In conclusion, "How to train your ViT?" is a landmark paper that provides clear, empirically-backed answers to fundamental questions about training Vision Transformers. Its practical recommendations and comprehensive analysis are set to significantly influence the way ViTs are developed and applied across various computer vision tasks, solidifying its place as an essential reference in the field.
