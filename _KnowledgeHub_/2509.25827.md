# Overthinking Reduction with Decoupled Rewards and Curriculum Data Scheduling

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Fudan University, Shanghai Jiao Tong University, and Shanghai Artificial Intelligence Laboratory developed DECS, a framework that reduces "overthinking" in large reasoning models by over 50% while maintaining or improving reasoning performance across various benchmarks. This method introduces decoupled token-level rewards and a curriculum data scheduling strategy to surgically address redundancy without compromising essential exploratory behaviors.

## Problem

- Large reasoning models (LRMs) trained with critic-free reinforcement learning often exhibit "overthinking," generating unnecessarily long reasoning paths without a commensurate improvement in performance.
- Existing length-based regularization methods, intended to reduce verbosity, frequently degrade reasoning performance, suggesting a fundamental misalignment between sequence-level penalties and token-level policy optimization.
- Specifically, current length-reward mechanisms erroneously penalize essential high-entropy exploratory tokens and inadvertently reward partially redundant steps, hindering true efficiency gains.

## Method

- The **DECS** (==Decoupled token-level== rewards and Curriculum data Scheduling) framework is introduced, comprising a Necessary Reasoning Prefix (NRP) detector, a decoupled token-level reward assignment, and a curriculum prompt scheduling strategy.
- A lightweight, highly accurate (over 99%) generator model, M_judge, identifies the NRP, defined as the shortest prefix of a reasoning trajectory that justifies the correct answer, enabling fine-grained intervention.
- A decoupled token-level reward system assigns a high positive reward to NRP tokens and monotonically decreasing, length-scaled negative advantages to redundant post-NRP tokens, ensuring consistent penalization of only unnecessary generations. Concurrently, a curriculum prompt schedule adaptively adjusts batch composition to protect high-entropy exploratory tokens from undue suppression.

## Results

- DECS achieved an average reasoning token reduction of **57.17%** on the **1.5B** model and **49.50%** on the **7B** model, while simultaneously improving Pass@1 accuracy by +2.48 points and +**0.8** points, respectively.
- The framework consistently outperformed all baselines in Average Efficiency Score (AES), demonstrating a superior balance between brevity and accuracy, with improvements of **0.12** (**1.5B**) and **0.14** (**7B**).
- DECS showed robust generalizability, reducing tokens by **56.33%** on GPQA-D and **33.52%** on LCB (out-of-domain tasks), and improving performance on Qwen3-**4B** with a **54.80%** token reduction and a 1.32 Pass@1 improvement.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Existing length-reward mechanisms in RL for LRMs suffer from two specific flaws: erroneously penalizing essential exploratory tokens (Theorem 1) and inadvertently rewarding partial redundancy (Theorem 2).
> - Fine-grained identification of the Necessary Reasoning Prefix (NRP) is crucial for differentiating between essential reasoning steps and redundant tokens, allowing for precise reward assignment.
> - Adaptive curriculum data scheduling is necessary to prevent the over-penalization of high-entropy tokens, which are vital for exploratory reasoning but can be suppressed by uniform length penalties in 'easy' training batches.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a challenge within the field of large reasoning models (LRMs) that are trained using critic-free reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR). Recent advancements in LRMs, particularly those leveraging algorithms like GRPO, DAPO, and REINFORCE++, have demonstrated enhanced reasoning capabilities by generating high-entropy tokens that facilitate logical transitions and exploration. However, a significant practical limitation, termed "overthinking," has emerged. Overthinking refers to the phenomenon where models generate excessively long reasoning paths or verbose outputs without a commensurate improvement in performance.

Prior attempts to mitigate overthinking have largely focused on incorporating length penalties into the reward function, aiming to incentivize shorter, yet correct, responses. Such methods, including ThinkPrune, TLMRE, AdaptThink, and LC-R1, have achieved some success in reducing output length. However, the authors identify a recurring issue: these existing length-based regularization techniques often lead to degraded reasoning performance. This degradation suggests a fundamental misalignment between the trajectory-level rewards (which penalize total sequence length) and the token-level policy optimization (which updates individual token probabilities). This paper posits that to advance the utility of state-of-the-art LRMs, it is necessary to develop a more nuanced approach to efficiency optimization that resolves this misalignment, thereby enabling substantial reductions in reasoning verbosity without compromising the model's inherent reasoning power.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel framework that effectively reduces "overthinking" in large reasoning models (LRMs) without sacrificing their reasoning performance. The authors are motivated by the observation that while current LRMs trained with critic-free reinforcement learning and verifiable rewards (RLVR) exhibit advanced reasoning capabilities, they tend to generate unnecessarily long and verbose reasoning paths—a phenomenon termed "overthinking." Existing solutions, which typically employ length penalties, frequently lead to a degradation in performance, indicating a critical gap in current methodologies.

The motivation for this work stems from a theoretical analysis identifying two specific, previously unaddressed flaws in existing length-reward mechanisms:

1. **Erroneous Penalization of Essential Exploratory Tokens:** The current sequence-level length penalties uniformly assign negative advantages to all tokens within an overlong, yet correct, trajectory. This indiscriminate penalization extends to "high-entropy tokens" (e.g., "wait," "however," "alternatively") that are crucial for initiating exploratory reasoning paths and making logical transitions. When easy prompts dominate a training batch and response lengths vary, longer correct trajectories receive negative advantage signals, leading to a reduction in the logits of these high-entropy tokens. This can suppress valuable exploratory behavior, causing premature convergence and a sub-optimal trade-off between efficiency and efficacy. Theorem 1 formally establishes the conditions under which this misaligned penalization leads to a decline in the probability of generating correct high-entropy tokens, potentially degrading overall reasoning performance.

2. **Inadvertent Rewarding of Partial Redundancy:** Conventional length-based rewards operate at the sequence level and do not explicitly distinguish between the "Necessary Reasoning Prefix (NRP)"—defined as the minimal sequence of tokens required to justify a correct answer—and subsequent, redundant generations. Consequently, tokens generated *after* the NRP in *shorter* correct trajectories can still receive positive advantages. This occurs because, in a group of correct trajectories where length is the sole differentiator for reward, shorter sequences (even if they contain post-NRP redundancy) may be favored over longer ones. Such spurious positive reinforcement for redundant steps distorts the learning signal, impeding the model's ability to truly minimize unnecessary generation and slowing down the convergence towards optimal efficiency. Theorem 2 formally demonstrates that, under existing length-reward schemes, the expected policy gradient signal for the first redundant token beyond the NRP remains positive, preventing full reduction of overthinking.

To address these fundamental limitations, the authors propose to develop a framework that (i) surgically distinguishes and penalizes redundant tokens at a fine-grained level and (ii) adaptively manages the training data distribution to protect essential exploratory behaviors. The overarching objective is to achieve a substantial reduction in reasoning tokens while concurrently maintaining or improving the model's reasoning performance, thereby establishing a more effective efficiency-efficacy equilibrium.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors introduce DECS (Decoupled token-level rewards and Curriculum data Scheduling), a framework designed to overcome the identified limitations of existing length-guided reasoning optimization. DECS incorporates three main components: a Necessary Reasoning Prefix (NRP) detector, a decoupled token-level reward assignment mechanism, and a curriculum prompt scheduling strategy.

#### 3.1. Detection of Necessary Reasoning Prefix (NRP)

To enable fine-grained intervention, DECS first identifies the Necessary Reasoning Prefix (NRP) within a generated response. The NRP is formally defined as the shortest prefix of a reasoning trajectory that suffices to justify the final correct answer.
Instead of a token-level classification model that requires a specific tokenizer, the authors implement the NRP detector as a lightweight generator model, M_judge. This model determines whether a reasoning chunk, segmented from a correct rollout, contains the correct answer to a given problem.
- **Process:** For a correct rollout, reasoning tokens (from start to `</think>`) are segmented into chunks using predefined discourse markers (e.g., "Wait," "However"). M_judge is then prompted with the problem, ground truth answer, and each reasoning chunk to output "yes" or "no" for correctness entailment.
- **NRP Identification:** The NRP is the concatenation of all reasoning chunks from the start up to and including the *first* chunk whose judgment is "yes."
- **Training and Efficiency:** M_judge is a fine-tuned Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct model. It demonstrates high accuracy (>99%) and incurs a low training overhead (approximately 5.1% of total training time), making it a practical and reliable component. Its generalization capabilities are also demonstrated on out-of-domain tasks (science and coding) with high accuracy.

#### 3.2. Decoupled Reward Assignment

Building on the NRP detection, DECS employs a token-level reward function designed to consistently penalize redundancy while protecting necessary reasoning steps.
- **Reward Structure (Eq. 9):**
 - **NRP Tokens:** Tokens belonging to the NRP, as well as answer conclusion tokens (not part of reasoning but providing the final answer), receive a fixed high reward (r+ = 1.1). This ensures that essential reasoning components are always positively reinforced.
 - **Redundant Tokens:** Tokens generated *after* the NRP within the reasoning part receive a monotonically decreasing, length-scaled reward (r0 - (r+ - r0)L_i/L_max, where r0 = 1.0). This design ensures that redundant tokens almost always receive negative advantages, regardless of sequence length.
- **Advantage Calculation (Eq. 10):** The token-level advantage (A_DECS) is computed similarly to GRPO, but using these decoupled rewards: each token's reward is compared against the mean and standard deviation of rewards at that specific token position across all rollouts in the group.
- **Impact:** This decoupled reward system ensures that only redundant thinking tokens are subject to penalization, preventing detrimental impacts on essential reasoning and exploration. It addresses the issue of inadvertent rewarding of partial redundancy by ensuring negative advantages for post-NRP tokens, promoting conciseness through the autoregressive nature of LRMs.

#### 3.3. Curriculum Prompt Schedule

To prevent the over-penalization of high-entropy tokens, which are crucial for exploration but might be associated with longer, overthinking trajectories, DECS introduces a curriculum batch scheduling strategy.
- **Goal:** Adaptively balance the proportion of "easy" prompts in a training batch. Easy prompts are those where all generated rollouts are correct, making length the primary differentiator for advantages. In such cases, longer (even if correct) responses receive negative advantages, potentially suppressing high-entropy tokens as identified in Theorem 1.
- **Mechanism (Eq. 11):** The ratio of easy prompts (κ_m) in a batch is dynamically adjusted based on the average NRP ratio (R_m) observed in correct sequences within the current batch.
 - κ_m = clip(κ_{m-1} + β(R_m - R_{m-1}), 0, κ_0_m).
 - R_m reflects the current level of redundancy; a higher R_m means more tokens are classified as NRP, implying less overthinking.
- **Benefit:** This strategy ensures that the training process maintains an unbiased learning signal for high-entropy tokens, preventing their undue suppression. By controlling the proportion of easy prompts, DECS ensures that the condition identified in Theorem 1 (where κσ_L becomes too large, leading to performance degradation) is consistently avoided. This results in stable convergence without performance degradation.

The overall algorithm combines these components: outputs are generated, the NRP detector identifies boundaries, decoupled rewards are assigned, group-relative advantages are computed, and the policy is updated using a PPO-like surrogate loss, all while the curriculum scheduler adapts the prompt distribution.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of DECS involved two base models (DeepSeek-R1-Distill-1.5B and 7B) and seven benchmarks covering diverse tasks (math, coding, science), including in-domain (MATH500, AMC23, OlympiadBench, AIME2024, AIME2025) and held-out (GPQA-Diamond, LiveCodeBench-v6) testbeds. Baselines included ThinkPrune, TLMRE, AdaptThink, LC-R1, and the base GRPO model.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Performance and Efficiency

- **Significant Token Reduction:** On the 1.5B model, DECS achieved an average reasoning token reduction of 57.17%. For the 7B model, the reduction was 49.50%. This demonstrates a substantial improvement in reasoning efficiency.
- **Performance Maintenance/Improvement:** Simultaneously, DECS maintained or improved performance, showing a +2.48 Pass@1 accuracy gain on the 1.5B model and a +0.8 point gain on the 7B model.
- **Superior Efficiency-Performance Trade-off:** DECS consistently outperformed all baselines in terms of the Average Efficiency Score (AES), with improvements of 0.12 and 0.14 on the 1.5B and 7B backbones, respectively. This indicates a more balanced approach to achieving both brevity and accuracy.
- **Generalizability:** The framework showed robust transferability to out-of-domain tasks, reducing tokens by 56.33% on GPQA-D and 33.52% on LCB, while maintaining or improving performance. Further generalization to Qwen3-4B also yielded a 54.80% token reduction and a 1.32 Pass@1 improvement.

#### 4.2. Ablation Study

An ablation study on the DS-1.5B model confirmed the complementary relationship and necessity of both main components:
- **Without Curriculum Scheduling (w/o CS):** A noticeable performance drop occurred, verifying the theoretical impact of misaligned penalization on high-entropy tokens as described in Theorem 1.
- **Without Decoupled Rewards (w/o DR):** The policy retained approximately 25% of overthinking tokens, supporting the theoretical finding that sequence-level length rewards are insufficient to fully reduce redundancy (Theorem 2).

#### 4.3. Detailed Analysis

The paper further analyzed specific aspects of DECS's behavior:
- **Reduction of Non-NRP Tokens:** DECS achieved a high Proportion of NRP (PNRP) score (Fig. 3b), indicating that the token reduction primarily targeted non-NRP (redundant) tokens, preserving necessary reasoning steps. This was more effective than baselines which sometimes reduced necessary tokens, leading to performance drops.
- **Exploration Potential:** Pass@K scores across challenging benchmarks (AIME2024, AIME2025, AMC23) showed that DECS maintained exploration potentials similar to the base model (Fig. 3c), suggesting that preventing negative gradients to high-entropy tokens preserved creative problem-solving abilities.
- **Variable Token Budgets:** DECS consistently improved token efficiency across a wide range of maximum response lengths (from 2,048 to 32,768 tokens) without compromising performance, demonstrating its utility in token-constrained scenarios and its ability to achieve efficiency without sacrificing capability boundaries (Fig. 4a).
- **High-Entropy Token Distribution:** Analysis of representative high-entropy tokens (Fig. 4b) revealed that DECS significantly reduced the frequency of "Self-Correction & Verification" and "Conclusion Drawing" tokens, which are often sources of overthinking. In contrast, the frequency of "Exploration & Alternatives" and "Context Setting" tokens remained largely unchanged, further supporting the preservation of creative thinking.
- **Consistency Across Difficulty Levels:** DECS consistently achieved high PNRP scores (>90%) across various difficulty levels (Fig. 4c), indicating uniform effectiveness in compressing non-NRP tokens regardless of input complexity.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in this paper carries several significant implications for the development and application of large reasoning models (LRMs).

**Theoretical Contribution:**
The most fundamental contribution is the theoretical identification and formalization of two critical flaws in existing length-reward-based reinforcement learning (RL) methods for reducing LRM overthinking. By demonstrating the "erroneous penalization of essential exploratory tokens" (Theorem 1) and the "inadvertent rewarding of partial redundancy" (Theorem 2), the authors provide a new understanding of why previous attempts to enhance efficiency have often resulted in performance degradation. This theoretical underpinning establishes a robust basis for the proposed solutions.

**Methodological Innovation:**
The introduction of DECS, with its decoupled token-level reward mechanism and curriculum data scheduling, represents a novel methodological advancement. The use of a lightweight NRP detector for fine-grained identification of necessary reasoning steps, combined with a reward function that differentially treats NRP and non-NRP tokens, addresses the credit assignment problem at a token level. Furthermore, the adaptive curriculum scheduling strategy mitigates the suppression of exploratory behaviors, which is a key challenge highlighted by their theoretical analysis. This integrated approach offers a more sophisticated and effective way to manage the efficiency-efficacy trade-off.

**Practical Utility and Impact:**
The experimental results demonstrate a substantial and practical impact. DECS achieves a dramatic reduction in reasoning tokens (over 50%) across various benchmarks and model scales, while simultaneously maintaining or improving reasoning performance. This efficiency gain is crucial for deploying LRMs in real-world scenarios, where computational costs, latency, and user experience are critical factors. By producing more concise and relevant outputs, DECS can make LRMs more usable and cost-effective. The demonstrated generalizability to different model backbones (DeepSeek-R1 and Qwen3) and out-of-domain tasks (science, coding) suggests broad applicability, extending its potential impact beyond specific math reasoning problems.

**Broader Implications for LRM Development:**
This research conclusively demonstrates that substantial gains in reasoning efficiency can be achieved without compromising a model’s underlying reasoning power or its exploratory capabilities. This finding challenges the notion that verbosity is an unavoidable side effect of robust reasoning in LRMs. By providing a framework that enables more efficient reasoning, this work opens avenues for future research into optimizing other aspects of LRM behavior, such as computational resource allocation, user interaction design, and the development of even more capable yet streamlined AI systems. The principles of fine-grained reward design and adaptive curriculum learning could potentially inspire similar solutions for other challenges in complex AI model optimization.
