# DreamX-World 1.0: A General-Purpose Interactive World Model

> [!summary] Summary
> The DreamX Team developed DreamX-World 1.0, a general-purpose interactive world model that transitions video generation from passive synthesis to dynamic, user-controlled simulation. This model achieved the highest overall scores in basic (84.76) and long-horizon (70.41) evaluations against larger competitors, and was preferred by humans in 57.5-61.9% of cases, while also demonstrating enhanced scene persistence and real-time streaming at up to 16 FPS.

## Problem

- Existing video generation models struggle to extend to interactive simulation (world models), lacking response to user controls, long-term scene state maintenance, and consistency upon revisiting regions.
- Operating across diverse visual domains (photorealistic, game-style, stylized) within a single interactive framework presents a significant challenge.
- Key limitations include maintaining scene consistency under user camera control, managing long-horizon persistence with limited context windows, and enabling composable event interaction with low latency for real-time use.

## Method

- A multi-domain data engine was developed, integrating Unreal Engine (UE) generated data with real-world and game videos, featuring extensive annotations for camera poses, agent actions, and structured event descriptions.
- A progressive training pipeline, initialized from Wan2.2-TI2V, incorporated ==Efficient Projective Positional Encoding (E-PRoPE)== for camera control, memory-conditioned scene persistence, event instruction tuning, autoregressive distillation, and reinforcement learning alignment.
- An optimized inference pipeline was designed using mixed-precision DiT denoising, residual reuse (TeaCache), a pruned VAE, and asynchronous pipeline parallelism to achieve real-time streaming capabilities.

## Results

- DreamX-World **1.0** achieved the highest overall quantitative scores in both 5-second basic evaluation (**84.76**) and 30-second long-horizon evaluation (**70.41**), outperforming larger competitor models.
- The model demonstrated superior memory consistency, achieving the highest gains in pixel-level, perceptual, semantic, and place-recognition metrics during revisit consistency tests.
- A human preference study showed DreamX-World **1.0** was preferred in overall preference (winning **57.5%** vs. HY-WorldPlay **1.5**, **61.9%** vs. LingBot-World), visual quality, and artifact detection, while maintaining competitive camera controllability.
- Inference optimizations enabled real-time streaming performance at up to 16 FPS on eight RTX 5090 GPUs.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - E-PRoPE enables efficient and accurate camera control by applying projective conditioning to spatially reduced tokens, reducing inference latency by approximately 30% while retaining performance.
> - Integrating geometry-guided memory retrieval and error injection during memory conditioning significantly improves long-horizon scene persistence and reduces temporal drift upon revisiting locations.
> - A progressive training strategy, combining data curation, architectural enhancements, multi-stage distillation, and RL alignment, is effective for building a robust, general-purpose interactive world model capable of operating across diverse visual domains and supporting composable events.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Contemporary research in video generation has advanced significantly with diffusion models capable of producing high-quality short clips. However, extending these capabilities to interactive simulation, commonly referred to as world models, presents several challenges. Existing video generation models often lack the ability to respond to user controls, maintain scene state over long temporal horizons, and ensure consistency when revisiting previously observed regions. Furthermore, operating across diverse visual domains, such as photorealistic, game-style, and stylized content, within a single interactive framework remains an area of active development.

The broader landscape includes various efforts to address these challenges. Models like Wan2.2, LingBot-World, and HY-WorldPlay 1.5 have explored aspects of interactive video generation, often focusing on camera navigation, streaming capabilities, or limited object interaction. Previous work on camera control, such as MotionCtrl, CameraCtrl, AC3D, and PRoPE, has introduced methods for injecting camera trajectories or geometric information into video diffusion models. For long-horizon generation, techniques like Self-Forcing, Stable Video Infinity, LongLive, Infinity-RoPE, and Causal Forcing aim to mitigate issues like exposure bias and error accumulation inherent in autoregressive generation. Memory-based methods, including those in Matrix-Game 3.0, address scene persistence during revisits by retrieving earlier observations. Efficient sampling and inference optimizations, using techniques like distribution matching distillation (DMD), KV caching, low-precision attention (SageAttention), residual reuse (TeaCache), and parallel VAE decoding (ParaVAE), are also crucial for real-time interaction. Finally, reinforcement learning (RL) methods like DDPO, Flow-GRPO, DiffusionNFT, WorldCompass, and Astrolabe are being explored to optimize diffusion models for non-differentiable objectives such as perceptual quality and camera alignment.

DreamX-World 1.0 positions itself within this landscape by integrating advancements across data curation, model architecture, training methodologies, and inference optimization to create a general-purpose interactive world model. It specifically aims to address the multi-domain data problem, efficient and accurate camera control, long-horizon scene persistence with memory, composable event interaction, and the latency requirements for real-time streaming, which are often tackled individually or partially by other systems. The work introduces new components like E-PRoPE for efficient camera control and a comprehensive training pipeline that progressively builds these capabilities, culminating in an integrated system that aims to surpass the performance of existing models in key interactive metrics.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a general-purpose interactive world model that extends video generation from passive visual synthesis to dynamic simulation capable of responding to user controls and preserving scene state over extended periods. This model is intended to operate across diverse visual domains, including photorealistic, game-style, and stylized content.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges identified in the current state of video generation and interactive systems:

1. **Scene Consistency under User Control:** Modern video diffusion models can generate high-quality short clips. However, interactive systems demand that changing a camera trajectory reveals a consistent scene, revisiting a location preserves its layout and identity, and prompted events modify the existing world state rather than generating unrelated plausible views. This consistency is particularly difficult to maintain across varied visual domains.
2. **Data Availability and Annotation:** Building such a comprehensive model requires extensive video data with precise annotations for camera parameters, agent actions, and event descriptions. No single data source currently provides this coverage at a sufficient scale across different visual domains (synthetic, game, and real-world).
3. **Efficient and Accurate Camera Control:** Translating a prescribed camera trajectory into consistent viewpoint changes across scenes with different scales and motion distributions is a technical challenge. The conditioning mechanism must provide sufficient geometric accuracy without substantially increasing the computational cost of the video backbone, which is a common issue with direct application of detailed geometric encodings.
4. **Long-Horizon Scene Persistence:** Interactive generation necessitates preserving scene content beyond the immediate local context window. When an earlier view leaves the context, models often fail to maintain consistency, rendering different scenes upon revisitation. Autoregressive generation exacerbates this problem, leading to an accumulation of small prediction errors, resulting in appearance, style, and color drift over time.
5. **Real-Time Interaction and Low Latency:** Continuous user interaction imposes a latency requirement that is less critical for offline video generation. While reducing the number of diffusion steps can improve throughput, aggressive distillation methods often degrade visual quality, camera control, and rollout stability. A practical system must therefore optimize both sampling and decoding costs while retaining the capabilities of a high-quality bidirectional model.

To address these motivations, DreamX-World 1.0 aims to deliver efficient and controllable world generation, long-horizon generation with scene persistence, and real-time streaming deployment, integrating these capabilities into a single, comprehensive framework.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DreamX-World 1.0 employs a multi-faceted methodology that encompasses data curation, a progressive training pipeline, and inference acceleration strategies.

#### 3.1. Data Engine

The system utilizes a data engine that combines Unreal Engine (UE)-generated data, real-world videos, and game data to achieve diverse visual domains and rich annotations.

- **UE-generated Data:** Synthesized in UE5, this data provides controllable camera and agent motion with ground-truth annotations, including discrete action vectors and 6-DoF camera poses. It comprises first-person (free camera exploration), third-person (character-following camera), and event subsets (object interactions). A two-stage pipeline is used: first, scene exploration and trajectory validation (collision checking, duration/path length constraints, stuck detection), then offline rendering with UE’s Movie Render Queue.
- **Real-world and Game Data:** Real-world videos are sourced from SpatialVID, RealEstate10K, Sekai, and DL3DV, with sparse camera poses estimated by MegaSaM and interpolated. Game data comes from Sekai-Game and OmniWorld-Game, with engine-exported poses converted to a unified coordinate system.
- **Data Annotation and Filtering:** A three-stage quality control process is applied:
 - **Basic Filtering:** Removal of clips with insufficient duration, low frame rate, overlaid text, black borders, or minimal visual change.
 - **Geometric Camera-pose Cleaning:** For real-world videos, sparse poses are densified using SLERP (rotation) and linear interpolation (translation), then normalized and checked for inconsistencies (spikes, rapid rotations, jitter).
 - **Video Captioning and Attribute Tagging:** Clips receive global captions describing scene, subjects, actions, and temporal changes. Tags include aesthetic quality, motion intensity, scene category, visual style, subject type, and motion category (3D for camera motion, 4D for camera and object motion).
- **Event Instruction Data:** High-quality clips with visible state changes are selected and annotated with structured event descriptions, combining holistic global descriptions with dense, time-aligned entity-level event records, supporting both single-object and composable events.

#### 3.2. Progressive Training Pipeline

DreamX-World 1.0 is initialized from Wan2.2-TI2V and progressively adapted through several training stages:

- **Camera-Aware Training with E-PRoPE:**
 - Introduces Efficient Projective Positional Encoding (E-PRoPE), a lightweight variant of PRoPE.
 - Unlike PRoPE which applies to full token sets, E-PRoPE computes projective attention on spatially reduced tokens (e.g., 4.5x downsampling from 18480 to 4096 tokens for a 5-second 720P video).
 - It only uses the projective submatrix (D_Proj) and omits the standard rotary position embeddings (D_RoPE), leveraging the DiT backbone for fine-grained semantic modeling.
 - Outputs are upsampled and added to the original DiT attention output.
 - During training, the DiT backbone is frozen, and only E-PRoPE parameters are updated, resulting in approximately 30% lower inference latency while retaining comparable performance.
- **Memory-Conditioned Scene Persistence:**
 - Addresses inconsistency in scene content when previously observed regions leave the local context.
 - The Diffusion Transformer (DiT) is trained to use both recent history frames (latest denoised latents) and retrieved memory frames (predicted clean latents from earlier history).
 - Input sequence `z_pack = [z_M | z_H | z_τ_C]` is formed, where `z_M` are memory latents, `z_H` are history latents, and `z_τ_C` are noisy target latents.
 - Loss is computed only on target latent frames using a rectified flow objective.
 - **Memory Retrieval:** History frames relevant to the target view are selected using camera pose and view overlap. RoPE embeddings are applied to memory frames to indicate their original temporal location. Long-range positional encoding methods (e.g., NTK-aware RoPE scaling) are used for large time gaps.
 - **Exposure Bias Mitigation:** The training-inference gap, where inference uses model-generated (imperfect) conditioning frames while training uses clean data, is mitigated by adopting an error injection approach (from Stable Video Infinity). Only conditioning tokens are perturbed, forcing the model to rely on its prior when memory frames contain errors.
- **Event Instruction Tuning for Composable Events:**
 - Adds structured multi-entity event control via the text-conditioning interface.
 - Fine-tunes the full DiT model (architecture unchanged) using event instruction data.
 - Structured event instructions are rendered as natural-language prompts.
 - The tuning mixture combines event-instruction samples with non-event training clips to preserve general generation capability.
 - Conservative updates and strict gradient clipping are applied to maintain pretrained priors.
- **Autoregressive Long Video Generation and Distillation:**
 - Converts the bidirectional video model into a few-step autoregressive generator for streaming.
 - Employs causal forcing, DMD-style distillation, and long-rollout training (using Infinity-RoPE) on self-generated long-horizon contexts to reduce style and color drift.
 - An E-PRoPE branch is integrated into the long-video T2V student.
 - DMD-forcing pipeline matches camera-controlled student rollouts to a bidirectional E-PRoPE teacher over local temporal windows from long videos, enhancing motion smoothness and camera controllability.
 - I2V DMD distillation uses the bidirectional E-PRoPE model as a teacher, with the first latent frame of each DMD window decoded by VAE as the image condition.
- **Reinforcement Learning (RL) Alignment:**
 - A post-distillation stage to recover visual quality and camera control that might degrade after aggressive DMD distillation.
 - Uses a gradual update strategy to maintain stability.
 - Long-horizon rollouts are generated, and short clips sampled from these are evaluated by two reward models: one for camera control accuracy (horizontal translation and rotation) and another for visual quality.
 - The fused reward guides a moderated DiffusionNFT soft update, with KL regularization to keep the model close to the DMD-distilled prior.

#### 3.3. Inference Acceleration

The inference pipeline is optimized for real-time streaming:

- **Autoregressive Streaming Inference:**
 - Video is generated chunk by chunk, with each chunk starting from noise, denoised by the distilled sampler under text prompt, chunk-relative camera trajectory, and a rolling KV cache.
 - For I2V, the first chunk’s first frame is replaced by the input image.
 - Camera controls are represented in a chunk-relative form using E-PRoPE relative camera conditioning.
- **Inference Optimizations:**
 - **DiT Denoising:** Mixed-precision (INT8 SageAttention for attention, FP8 AngelSlim for FFN), sequence parallelism for sharding long token sequences, fused Triton kernels, and residual reuse (TeaCache) to skip Transformer-block forward passes in stable timestep regions.
 - **VAE Decoding:** Employs a 75%-pruned Matrix-Game 3.0 VAE for faster decoding (approx. 0.25 seconds per chunk). Torch.compile further reduces latency. ParaVAE is used to distribute latent video decoding across GPUs.
 - **Serving:** Asynchronous pipeline parallelism overlaps VAE decoding of chunk *k* with control reception, KV-cache update, and DiT denoising of chunk *k + 1*, hiding VAE latency.
- This optimized pipeline achieves up to 16 FPS on eight RTX 5090 GPUs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of DreamX-World 1.0 includes qualitative results, quantitative comparisons on basic and long-horizon metrics, memory consistency assessment, and a human preference study.

#### 4.1. Qualitative Observations

Qualitative results demonstrate the model's ability to generate interactive videos with smooth, temporally coherent transitions and high visual fidelity across diverse scene types, camera trajectories, and visual styles (Figure 10). The project page provides additional samples and interactive demos.

#### 4.2. Quantitative Evaluation

DreamX-World 1.0 (5B parameters) was compared against HY-WorldPlay 1.5 (8B) and LingBot-World (14B).

- **Basic Evaluation (5-second clips):**
 - **Metrics:** Camera Control (MegaSaM-based pose recovery, enriched trajectories, error `e_camera`), Visual Quality (imaging quality, temporal flickering, motion smoothness, dynamic degree, transition detection from Omni-WorldBench), and Artifact Detection (multimodal LLM Gemini-3.1-Pro). All scores normalized to.
 - **Results (Table 3):** DreamX-World 1.0 achieved the highest camera control score (73.75) and the highest overall score (84.76), outperforming HY-WorldPlay 1.5 (80.79) and LingBot-World (80.45). It maintained competitive visual quality and generated dynamics described as richer and more physically plausible.
- **Long-Horizon Evaluation (approximately 30-second rollouts):**
 - **Metrics:** The same set of metrics as the basic evaluation was used.
 - **Results (Table 4):** DreamX-World 1.0 achieved the highest overall score (70.41), surpassing HY-WorldPlay 1.5 (68.85) and LingBot-World (67.43). The model demonstrated the best imaging quality and artifact detection scores, indicating sustained visual fidelity over longer durations compared to larger competing models.
- **Memory Evaluation via Revisit Consistency (10-second videos):**
 - **Methodology:** A novel evaluation suite assessed long-horizon spatial memory using three trajectory templates: out-and-back, closed-loop, and translation-rotation (Figure 11). Revisit pairs were detected based on camera extrinsics and a minimum temporal gap.
 - **Metrics:** Pixel-level fidelity (∆PSNR, ∆SSIM), perceptual consistency (∆LPIPS), semantic identity (∆DINO-Sim), place recognition (∆VPR-Sim), and geometric structure (∆SP-Match). Temporal smoothness was measured by CLIP-Video. Metrics were reported as gains over non-revisit baselines (except CLIP-Video).
 - **Results (Table 5):** DreamX-World 1.0 achieved the highest gains in pixel-level, perceptual, semantic, and place-recognition metrics, demonstrating stronger memory at various levels of abstraction. HY-WorldPlay 1.5 led in SP-Match and CLIP-Video. LingBot-World showed lower gains across all revisit metrics.

#### 4.3. Human Preference Study

- **Methodology:** A blind side-by-side comparison study was conducted against HY-WorldPlay 1.5 and LingBot-World under identical conditions. Assessors judged overall preference, camera control, visual quality, and artifact detection.
- **Results (Figure 12):** DreamX-World 1.0 was preferred in overall preference (winning 57.5% against HY-WorldPlay 1.5 and 61.9% against LingBot-World). It also showed higher win rates in visual quality (57.5%, 61.3%) and artifact detection (59.4%, 56.2%). Camera-control judgments were closer, with higher tie rates, suggesting comparable perceived controllability among the systems. The human study corroborated the automatic evaluation, supporting improved perceptual quality and artifact robustness while maintaining competitive camera controllability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

DreamX-World 1.0 represents an integrated effort to advance the capabilities of general-purpose interactive world models. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Addresses Key Challenges:** The work directly addresses fundamental limitations in current interactive video generation, including the need for diverse and annotated multi-domain data, efficient and geometrically accurate camera control, long-horizon scene persistence, composable event interaction, and real-time streaming performance.
- **Efficient Camera Control:** The introduction of E-PRoPE provides an efficient mechanism for camera-controlled generation. By applying projective conditioning to spatially reduced tokens, it maintains comparable trajectory-following performance while significantly reducing inference latency (approximately 30% reduction).
- **Enhanced Long-Horizon Consistency and Memory:** Through a combination of geometry-guided memory retrieval, error-recycled memory conditioning, autoregressive distillation with long-rollout training, and reinforcement learning alignment, DreamX-World 1.0 demonstrates improved scene persistence and reduced drift over extended generations and during revisits. This is crucial for creating believable and interactive virtual environments.
- **Composable Event Control:** The Event Instruction Tuning stage enables structured multi-entity event control, allowing users to specify complex interactions involving multiple objects within a single generation. This capability enhances the realism and interactivity of simulated worlds.
- **Real-time Streaming Deployment:** The comprehensive set of inference optimizations—including mixed-precision DiT execution, residual reuse, pruned VAE decoding, and asynchronous pipeline parallelism—allows the model to achieve up to 16 FPS on eight RTX 5090 GPUs, making real-time interactive streaming a practical reality.
- **Competitive Performance with Efficiency:** DreamX-World 1.0 achieves superior overall quantitative scores in both basic and long-horizon evaluations, as well as favorable human preferences, often with a smaller model size (5B parameters) compared to larger baselines (8B and 14B). This suggests an efficient design and effective training.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The "full-stack" approach, integrating advancements across data, training, evaluation, and inference, establishes a promising foundation for the next generation of interactive world models. The identified future directions—character-centric world models and native audio-visual world models—point towards more embodied, expressive, and immersive simulations.

The combined advancements in data utilization, model architecture, training stability, and deployment efficiency contribute to a system that pushes the boundaries of interactive world modeling, paving the way for more sophisticated virtual experiences and research into intelligent agents operating within simulated environments.
