# Compositional Visual Generation with Composable Diffusion Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from MIT and University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign reinterpret denoising diffusion models as Energy-Based Models to enable explicit compositionality in visual generation. This allows for the synthesis of images by combining multiple concepts, such as language descriptions, object attributes, and spatial relationships, during inference without requiring additional training.

## Problem

- Large text-guided diffusion models often struggle with complex compositional requests, such as accurately binding attributes to specific objects or interpreting intricate relationships between multiple scene elements.
- Encoding complex textual descriptions into fixed-size latent vectors creates an information bottleneck, limiting the capacity for arbitrarily complex inputs and leading to generation errors.
- Previous attempts at compositional generation using Energy-Based Models (EBMs) faced challenges with training stability and scalability, despite their inherent probabilistic composition capabilities.

## Method

- ==Denoising diffusion models== are reinterpreted as implicitly parameterized ==Energy-Based Models (EBMs)==, leveraging the equivalence between the diffusion model's denoising network and an EBM's ==score function==.
- Multiple diffusion models are composed during inference by ==summing their individual score functions==, which directly translates the compositional properties of EBMs to the stable generation process of diffusion models.
- Two ==compositional operators==, ==Conjunction (AND)== and ==Negation (NOT)==, are introduced to combine concepts and specify their absence without requiring additional training for novel combinations.

## Results

- On the CLEVR dataset, the method achieved **31.36%** accuracy for 3-component object composition, a **24.02%** improvement over the best baseline (EBM at **7.34%**), while maintaining higher image quality (lower FID).
- For composing language descriptions, the 'Composed GLIDE' model qualitatively demonstrated superior ability to capture intricate textual details (e.g., 'overwater bungalows,' 'a polar bear') compared to the original GLIDE.
- The approach successfully generated high-fidelity human faces on FFHQ, correctly incorporating multiple facial attributes using conjunction and negation, achieving **68.86%** accuracy for 3 components with low FID (**16.95**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The functional similarity between DDPM sampling and EBM Langevin dynamics allows diffusion models' denoising networks to be viewed as implicit score functions of EBMs, enabling EBM-style composition.
> - Summing the score functions of different diffusion models provides a robust mechanism to combine diverse concepts, generalizing the principle of classifier-free guidance to multiple conditions.
> - The proposed compositional operators facilitate zero-shot generalization to novel and more complex combinations of concepts at inference time, overcoming the need to train on every possible combination.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of generative modeling, particularly visual generation, has experienced advancements with the introduction of large text-guided diffusion models like DALL-E 2. These models are capable of producing photorealistic images from natural language descriptions. However, a limitation of these models lies in their ability to correctly interpret and compose complex concepts. Existing systems often struggle with binding attributes to specific objects or understanding intricate relationships between multiple objects within a scene. This difficulty is posited to stem from encoding complex textual descriptions into fixed-size latent vectors, which limits the information capacity for arbitrarily complex inputs.

Prior research has explored explicit forms of compositionality, particularly within the framework of Energy-Based Models (EBMs). EBMs offer a mechanism to combine different learned factors describing a scene through their probabilistic formulation. However, direct training of EBMs has historically presented challenges related to stability and scalability.

This work positions itself as an advancement in compositional visual generation by integrating the strengths of diffusion models—known for their generation stability and image quality—with the compositional capabilities of EBMs. By interpreting diffusion models as implicitly parameterized EBMs, the authors aim to overcome the stability issues of directly trained EBMs while enabling robust compositional generation. The research addresses a key challenge in generative AI: enabling models to exhibit structured generalization by composing concepts at inference time, rather than requiring exposure to every possible combination during training. This allows for the generation of scenes with complexity that exceeds that seen in the training data.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a structured approach for compositional visual generation using diffusion models. This involves enabling diffusion models to synthesize images by combining multiple distinct concepts, such as language descriptions, object attributes, and spatial relationships, without requiring additional training for novel combinations.

The motivation for this objective arises from several observations:

1. **Limitations of existing large text-guided diffusion models:** While powerful, current models like DALL-E 2 frequently exhibit challenges in understanding complex compositions. These difficulties manifest as confusion of attributes between different objects or misinterpretation of relationships between objects when presented with elaborate descriptions. This suggests an inherent limitation in their ability to fully disentangle and recombine learned concepts.
2. **The compositional nature of human cognition:** Human understanding of the world is inherently compositional, allowing for the rapid comprehension of new objects from their components and the formation of complex sentences from individual words. This ability, often described as "infinite use of finite means," is a desirable capability for advanced machine learning systems.
3. **Scalability of information encoding:** Encoding increasingly complex textual descriptions into fixed-size latent vectors presents an information bottleneck. As descriptions become more elaborate, a fixed-size vector may be insufficient to capture all the necessary details for accurate compositional generation, leading to errors.
4. **Leveraging EBM advantages without their drawbacks:** Energy-Based Models have previously demonstrated potential for compositional generation due to their explicit probabilistic composition mechanisms. However, training EBMs directly can be unstable and difficult to scale. The authors are motivated to harness the compositional benefits of EBMs by reinterpreting diffusion models through an EBM lens, thereby gaining the stability and image quality benefits of diffusion models.

In summary, the research aims to factorize the compositional generation problem, allowing different diffusion models to capture specific components of a specification. These models are then explicitly combined during inference, enabling zero-shot generalization to more complex and novel concept combinations, fostering structured generalization in visual synthesis.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of this work involves interpreting denoising diffusion models as implicitly parameterized Energy-Based Models (EBMs) to enable explicit compositionality during image generation.

#### 3.1. Background on Denoising Diffusion Models and Energy-Based Models

- **Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPMs):** DDPMs model image generation as an iterative denoising process. Starting from random Gaussian noise (x_T), a series of denoising steps (x_T, x_T-1..., x_0) are performed until a clear image (x_0) is produced. This process is governed by a reverse Markov chain, where each step p_θ(x_t-1|x_t) is approximated by a Gaussian distribution with a learned mean μ_θ(x_t, t) and fixed covariance σ^2_t I. The mean is parameterized by a learned denoising network ϵ_θ(x_t, t), which predicts the noise to be removed from x_t.
- **Energy-Based Models (EBMs):** EBMs define a data distribution p_θ(x) ∝ e^(-E_θ(x)), where E_θ(x) is a learnable energy function. Sampling from EBMs is typically performed using gradient-based MCMC procedures, such as Langevin dynamics. This process iteratively refines an image x by moving it in the direction opposite to the gradient of the energy function, ∇_x E_θ(x), while adding a small amount of noise.

#### 3.2. Diffusion Models as Energy-Based Models

The methodology establishes a functional similarity between the sampling procedures of DDPMs and EBMs. The denoising network ϵ_θ(x_t, t) in diffusion models is trained to predict the underlying "score" (gradient of the logarithm of the data distribution, ∇_x log p(x_t)) of the data distribution. Similarly, in EBMs, the gradient of the energy function, ∇_x E_θ(x_t), also corresponds to the negative score of the data distribution (∇_x log p_θ(x_t) = -∇_x E_θ(x_t)). This equivalence implies that a trained diffusion model's ϵ_θ(x_t, t) can be viewed as an implicitly parameterized EBM's score function. This interpretation allows the application of EBM composition techniques to diffusion models.

#### 3.3. Composing Diffusion Models

Leveraging the EBM interpretation, the authors propose a method to compose multiple diffusion models. In EBMs, n independent EBMs (E^i_θ(x)) can be combined by summing their energy functions, resulting in a composed distribution p_compose(x) ∝ e^(-Σ E^i_θ(x)). This leads to a composed Langevin dynamics update rule where the gradient is the sum of individual EBM gradients.

Applying this principle to diffusion models, the generative process of composing multiple diffusion models involves summing their individual score functions. The modified score function for a composed diffusion model is then used in the iterative denoising process.

#### 3.4. Compositional Operators

The research introduces two compositional operators—Conjunction (AND) and Negation (NOT)—inspired by EBMs, to combine concepts during inference without additional training:

1. **Concept Conjunction (AND):** To generate images conditioned on a set of concepts {c1, c2..., cn}, the conditional probability p(x|c1..., cn) is factorized. Assuming conditional independence of concepts given the image x, the joint probability p(x, c1..., cn) is expressed as p(x) * Π p(ci|x). The term p(ci|x) is then rewritten using the ratio of conditional and unconditional distributions: p(x|ci) / p(x). Taking the gradient of the logarithm of this composed probability leads to a new composed score function ˆϵ(x_t, t):
 ˆϵ(x_t, t) = ϵ_θ(x_t, t) + Σ_i w_i * (ϵ_θ(x_t, t|c_i) - ϵ_θ(x_t, t))
 Here, ϵ_θ(x_t, t|c_i) is the score conditioned on concept c_i, ϵ_θ(x_t, t) is the unconditional score, and w_i are hyperparameters controlling the influence of each concept. This formulation generalizes classifier-free guidance when only one concept is present.

2. **Concept Negation (NOT):** To generate images with the absence of a specific concept (˜cj) while incorporating another (ci), the compositional probability p(x|not ˜cj, ci) is formulated. This is factorized as p(x) * (p(ci|x) / p(˜cj|x)). Similar to conjunction, substituting the p(c|x) term and taking the gradient of the logarithm yields the composed score function:
 ˆϵ(x_t, t) = ϵ_θ(x_t, t) + w * (ϵ_θ(x_t, t|c_i) - ϵ_θ(x_t, t|˜c_j))
 Here, w is a hyperparameter determining the strength of the negation.

The inference process, summarized in Algorithm 1, involves iteratively applying these modified score functions to denoise a noisy image, starting from Gaussian noise, until a generated image is obtained. This approach allows for zero-shot compositional generation by combining pre-trained conditional and unconditional diffusion models.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents findings across various domains, evaluating the proposed method's ability to perform compositional visual generation compared to several baselines.

#### 4.1. Experimental Setup

- **Datasets:**
 - **CLEVR:** A synthetic dataset of objects with varying shapes, colors, and sizes, with 2D coordinate labels for object positions. Used for composing objects.
 - **Relational CLEVR:** An extension of CLEVR with relational descriptions between objects (e.g., "to the left of"). Used for composing object relations.
 - **FFHQ (Flickr-Faces-HQ):** A real-world dataset of human faces, annotated with binary attributes (smile, gender, glasses). Used for composing facial attributes.
- **Evaluation Metrics:**
 - **Binary Classification Accuracy:** Measures the percentage of generated images that correctly capture *all* specified concepts. Classifiers are trained on real data to predict the presence of concepts in generated images. This metric assesses adherence to the compositional prompt.
 - **Fréchet Inception Distance (FID):** A common metric for image quality, measuring the similarity between the feature distributions of generated and real images. Lower FID indicates higher quality and realism.
- **Test Settings:** Experiments include 1 Component (matching training distribution), 2 Components, and 3 Components (evaluating zero-shot generalization to more complex combinations).
- **Baselines:** StyleGAN2-ADA, StyleGAN2, LACE, GLIDE (original), and Energy-based models (EBM).

#### 4.2. Results by Domain

- **Composing Language Descriptions:**
 - **Qualitative Results (Figure 3):** The "Composed GLIDE" (our method applied to GLIDE) effectively combines multiple short textual descriptions (e.g., "A pink sky in the horizon" AND "A sailboat at the sea" AND "Overwater bungalows"). It demonstrates a superior ability to accurately capture intricate text details (e.g., "overwater bungalows," "a polar bear") compared to the original GLIDE model, which processes a single long sentence.
 - **3D Asset Generation (Appendix A.1, Figure 7):** The method extends to composing language descriptions for 3D point cloud generation using Point-E, demonstrating the generation of novel 3D hybrid objects (e.g., "A green avocado" AND "A chair").

- **Composing Objects (CLEVR Dataset):**
 - **Qualitative Results (Figure 4, Figure 14):** The proposed method reliably generates images with objects placed at specified 2D coordinates, even for combinations with more objects than typically seen during training (out-of-distribution). Baseline methods like StyleGAN2 and LACE frequently miss objects or place them incorrectly.
 - **Quantitative Results (Table 1):** Our method achieved a binary classification accuracy of 86.42% for 1 component, 59.20% for 2 components, and 31.36% for 3 components. This represents a significant improvement, with 31.36% accuracy for 3 components being 24.02% higher than the best baseline (EBM at 7.34%). Additionally, our method consistently yielded lower FID scores (e.g., 10.51 for 3 components), indicating higher image quality and realism compared to baselines.

- **Composing Object Relations (Relational CLEVR Dataset):**
 - **Qualitative Results (Figure 5):** The method successfully composes multiple object relational descriptions (e.g., "A large blue metal cube to the left of a small yellow metal sphere" AND "A large blue metal cube in front of a large cyan metal cylinder"). It captures these relations more accurately than LACE and StyleGAN2, and generates higher quality images with sharper object boundaries than EBMs. DALL-E 2 and GLIDE, while generating high-quality images, sometimes failed to correctly understand the specified object relations.
 - **Quantitative Results (Table 2):** In terms of accuracy, our method performed comparably or better than most baselines, achieving 60.40% for 1 component, 21.84% for 2 components, and 2.80% for 3 components. While EBM showed higher accuracy for 1 and 2 components, its FID scores were significantly worse (e.g., 55.89 vs. 29.82 for 2 components), indicating unrealistic image generation. Our method maintained competitive accuracy while producing more realistic images (lower FID).

- **Composing Human Facial Attributes (FFHQ Dataset):**
 - **Qualitative Results (Figure 8, Figure 15):** The method generated high-fidelity human faces that correctly incorporated multiple facial attributes using both conjunction and negation operators (e.g., "(NOT Smiling) AND Glasses AND (NOT Male)"). Baselines like StyleGAN2 and LACE often failed to incorporate all attributes or generated incorrect ones, while EBM produced low-quality images.
 - **Quantitative Results (Table 3):** Our method achieved an accuracy of 99.26% for 1 component, 92.68% for 2 components, and 68.86% for 3 components, along with low FID scores (e.g., 16.95 for 3 components). These results are comparable to the best-performing baselines while maintaining high image quality.

#### 4.3. Results Analysis and Failure Cases

The results indicate that the proposed method facilitates effective zero-shot compositional generalization, consistently outperforming baselines in accuracy and/or image quality across various compositional tasks and complexity levels. It demonstrates the ability to generate images that integrate multiple concepts accurately, even when the combinations are complex or novel.

However, certain failure cases were identified (Figure 6):
1. **Pre-trained model concept misunderstanding:** In some instances, the underlying pre-trained diffusion model (e.g., GLIDE) did not understand specific concepts, such as "person," due to training biases (e.g., GLIDE's avoidance of human image generation).
2. **Object attribute confusion:** The diffusion models sometimes confused attributes between objects, for example, generating "a red bear" when the input was "a bear in a red forest."
3. **Composition failure:** In certain complex scenarios, especially when objects were centrally located, the compositional operators failed to integrate concepts correctly (e.g., generating a "bird-shape and flower-color object" instead of a bird and a flower).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces a method for compositional visual generation using diffusion models by reinterpreting them as Energy-Based Models. This approach enables explicit combination of concepts during inference, addressing a significant limitation of large, monolithic generative models that struggle with complex, multi-concept descriptions.

The significance and potential impact of this work are manifold:

1. **Enhanced Compositional Generalization:** The method allows for zero-shot compositional generalization, meaning it can generate images incorporating complex combinations of concepts (e.g., multiple objects, relations, or attributes) that were rarely or never seen together during the model's training. This capability is crucial for building more flexible and versatile generative AI systems that can adapt to novel instructions.
2. **Improved Control in Visual Generation:** By providing explicit operators (Conjunction and Negation) for combining concepts, the approach offers finer-grained control over the generated image content. Users can specify not only what should be present but also what should be absent, leading to more precise and controllable image synthesis.
3. **Leveraging Diffusion Model Strengths:** The work successfully integrates the compositional benefits of EBMs with the known stability and high image quality of diffusion models. This overcomes prior challenges associated with the instability of directly training EBMs for complex visual tasks.
4. **Applicability Across Diverse Domains:** The demonstrated effectiveness across various domains—including natural language descriptions (for both 2D images and 3D assets), object composition, object relations, and human facial attributes—suggests broad applicability for this compositional framework. The ability to apply it to pre-trained models like GLIDE and Point-E further enhances its practical utility.
5. **Foundation for Structured Reasoning:** The method represents a step towards equipping generative models with structured generalization capabilities, moving beyond mere pattern recognition to a more conceptual understanding of how elements compose. This aligns with human-like compositional intelligence and could inform the development of AI systems capable of more advanced visual reasoning and scene understanding.

A recognized limitation is the challenge in composing diffusion models trained on *different* datasets, suggesting that current compositional successes are primarily with instances of the same model. Future work, potentially by incorporating structures from EBMs like conservative score fields into diffusion models, could address this to enable more flexible composition of independently trained models, further broadening the impact of this research.
