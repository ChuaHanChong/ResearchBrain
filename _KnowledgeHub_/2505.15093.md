# Steering Generative Models with Experimental Data for Protein Fitness Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> The study from California Institute of Technology and Microsoft Corporation explores optimal strategies for Steered Generation for Protein Optimization (SGPO), demonstrating that plug-and-play guidance with discrete diffusion models effectively directs modern generative models towards higher protein fitness using limited experimental data. An adaptive optimization framework leveraging ensemble classifiers further boosted the discovery of maximum fitness variants, outperforming several baseline methods in low-data protein engineering scenarios.

## Problem

- The vast combinatorial protein design space and low-throughput experimental validation limit the amount of available labeled sequence-fitness data (typically hundreds of pairs).
- Prior Steered Generation for Protein Optimization (SGPO) research often relied on computational surrogates or larger datasets, lacking a comprehensive evaluation of generative priors and steering strategies with real, small-scale experimental data.
- The integration of adaptive optimization principles, such as uncertainty-aware exploration, into SGPO for guiding generative models remained underexplored.

## Method

- The authors developed and evaluated various **SGPO** methods, combining pretrained generative models (e.g., discrete diffusion models, autoregressive language models) with different plug-and-play and finetuning steering strategies.
- They specifically tested Classifier Guidance (CG) and Decoupled Annealing Posterior Sampling (DAPS) for guidance, and Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) for finetuning, using only 200 labeled sequence-fitness pairs.
- An adaptive optimization framework, mimicking batch Bayesian optimization with an ensemble of neural network regressors and a Thompson sampling-like approach, was introduced to enable uncertainty-aware exploration over iterative rounds.

## Results

- Steering with modest amounts of labeled data (**200** pairs) consistently improved fitness, with Classifier Guidance (CG) and Decoupled Annealing Posterior Sampling (DAPS) showing the strongest steerability when applied to discrete diffusion models.
- Adaptive SGPO methods, especially those using ensemble classifiers, consistently achieved higher maximum fitness compared to baselines like DPO with ARLMs and latent space Bayesian optimization (APEXGo) in iterative optimization campaigns.
- Most guided models produced entirely novel protein sequences distinct from the training data, demonstrating that the steering mechanisms enabled effective exploration beyond known solutions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Pretrained generative priors are effective at capturing natural protein sequence distributions and sampling sequences with inherently higher mean fitness than random sequences.
> - Plug-and-play guidance strategies, particularly those based on discrete diffusion models, generally outperform finetuning protein language models in low-data regimes due to simpler tuning and lower computational cost.
> - An ensemble-based adaptive optimization approach, leveraging predictive uncertainty via Thompson sampling, enhances exploration and leads to the discovery of higher-fitness variants compared to guidance with a single value function.
