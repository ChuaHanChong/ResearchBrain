# Real-World Deployment of a 5G-Connected Edge-Controlled Aerial Robot in Industrial Subterranean Mines

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Luleå University of Technology achieved the first real-world autonomous flight of a 5G-connected aerial robot in an active subterranean mine, successfully offloading its high-level controller to a Kubernetes-based edge cluster. The system demonstrated stable, time-critical control over a 5G NR SA network, with average drift-corrected communication delays of 9.5 ms for uplink and 6.5 ms for downlink.

## Problem

- Most 5G-connected and edge-controlled robotic systems are limited to laboratory or simulated environments, lacking real-world validation in complex industrial settings.
- Resource-constrained onboard processors of aerial robots struggle to execute computationally intensive advanced control algorithms (e.g., AI, MPC), limiting their autonomy and capabilities.
- Hazardous industrial environments like mining require robust, real-time autonomous systems, but widespread robot adoption is hampered by limitations in current automation capabilities and infrastructure.

## Method

- Deployed a custom-built 5G-enabled quadcopter, an Ericsson 5G NR SA network, and a Kubernetes edge computing cluster within an active industrial subterranean mine.
- Offloaded the Nonlinear ==Model Predictive Control== (NMPC) algorithm, acting as the high-level controller, to the edge cluster as a containerized application, communicating with the robot over UDP via 5G.
- Implemented onboard LiDAR processing for localization and transmitted only minimal telemetry and control commands to minimize network load and meet real-time constraints.

## Results

- The aerial robot achieved autonomous flight, accurately following human-selected waypoints in the subterranean mine, demonstrating effective navigation and control with the edge-offloaded system.
- The 5G NR SA network provided stable, low-latency communication, with average drift-corrected delays of **9.5** ms (UL) and **6.5** ms (DL), and jitter ranging from 0 ms to 11 ms, confirming stability for the NMPC controller.
- Robust radio performance was maintained in the mining environment, with RSRP ranging from -100 dBm to -83 dBm and SINR from **12.5** dB to **27.0** dB, ensuring reliable connectivity.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The successful real-world deployment validates that current 5G NR SA networks and edge computing can reliably support time-critical, closed-loop robotic control in demanding, uncontrolled industrial environments.
> - Offloading computationally intensive control modules to the edge significantly reduces onboard processing demands, enabling lighter, more cost-effective, and easier-to-maintain robotic platforms.
> - The architecture provides a practical blueprint for future edge-controlled robotic systems, paving the way for advanced data processing offloading and network-aware mission planning in industrial applications.
