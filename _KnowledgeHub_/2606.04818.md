# Real-World Deployment of a 5G-Connected Edge-Controlled Aerial Robot in Industrial Subterranean Mines

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Luleå University of Technology achieved the first real-world autonomous flight of a 5G-connected aerial robot in an active subterranean mine, successfully offloading its high-level controller to a Kubernetes-based edge cluster. The system demonstrated stable, time-critical control over a 5G NR SA network, with average drift-corrected communication delays of 9.5 ms for uplink and 6.5 ms for downlink.

## Problem

- Most 5G-connected and edge-controlled robotic systems are limited to laboratory or simulated environments, lacking real-world validation in complex industrial settings.
- Resource-constrained onboard processors of aerial robots struggle to execute computationally intensive advanced control algorithms (e.g., AI, MPC), limiting their autonomy and capabilities.
- Hazardous industrial environments like mining require robust, real-time autonomous systems, but widespread robot adoption is hampered by limitations in current automation capabilities and infrastructure.

## Method

- Deployed a custom-built 5G-enabled quadcopter, an Ericsson 5G NR SA network, and a Kubernetes edge computing cluster within an active industrial subterranean mine.
- Offloaded the Nonlinear ==Model Predictive Control== (NMPC) algorithm, acting as the high-level controller, to the edge cluster as a containerized application, communicating with the robot over UDP via 5G.
- Implemented onboard LiDAR processing for localization and transmitted only minimal telemetry and control commands to minimize network load and meet real-time constraints.

## Results

- The aerial robot achieved autonomous flight, accurately following human-selected waypoints in the subterranean mine, demonstrating effective navigation and control with the edge-offloaded system.
- The 5G NR SA network provided stable, low-latency communication, with average drift-corrected delays of **9.5** ms (UL) and **6.5** ms (DL), and jitter ranging from 0 ms to 11 ms, confirming stability for the NMPC controller.
- Robust radio performance was maintained in the mining environment, with RSRP ranging from -100 dBm to -83 dBm and SINR from **12.5** dB to **27.0** dB, ensuring reliable connectivity.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The successful real-world deployment validates that current 5G NR SA networks and edge computing can reliably support time-critical, closed-loop robotic control in demanding, uncontrolled industrial environments.
> - Offloading computationally intensive control modules to the edge significantly reduces onboard processing demands, enabling lighter, more cost-effective, and easier-to-maintain robotic platforms.
> - The architecture provides a practical blueprint for future edge-controlled robotic systems, paving the way for advanced data processing offloading and network-aware mission planning in industrial applications.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The integration of 5G New Radio (NR) Standalone (SA) networks and edge computing technologies with robotic systems has been an area of increasing interest within robotics and telecommunications research. Existing literature often investigates the potential of these technologies, including the offloading of computationally intensive control modules to edge infrastructure, to enhance robotic capabilities by leveraging increased computational resources and reduced latency compared to onboard or cloud-based solutions. Studies have explored concepts such as cloud-based whole-body control over 5G networks in simulated environments and Unmanned Aerial Vehicle (UAV) trajectory control offloaded to the edge via 5G in laboratory settings. Various research efforts have also proposed 5G-edge-cloud architectures for service robots, explored 5G networks for offloading high-level control modules for UAVs, and introduced frameworks for dynamic 5G Quality of Service (QoS) selection for UAVs. Other investigations have focused on offloading autonomous driving services or developing edge orchestration approaches for 5G-connected robots.

However, the authors identify a significant gap in the existing research: the limited number of real-world implementations of such systems outside of controlled laboratory or simulated environments. Most prior work has demonstrated the feasibility of 5G-connected and edge-controlled robotics in idealized conditions. This paper addresses this gap by presenting a real-world deployment of a 5G-connected, edge-controlled aerial robot operating autonomously in an active industrial subterranean mine. It extends the concept of edge-offloaded control from theoretical and laboratory settings to a complex, uncontrolled industrial environment, thereby aiming to bridge the divide between academic research and practical industrial application. The work positions itself as the first real-world validation of a time-critical, 5G-connected, edge-controlled aerial robotic platform in a non-laboratory, operational industrial setting.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The motivation for this research stems from several identified needs and challenges in the fields of robotics, telecommunications, and industrial automation:

- **Lack of Real-World Validation:** Despite growing interest and theoretical advancements in integrating 5G NR SA networks and edge computing with robotic systems, real-world deployments and validations remain limited. Most studies have been conducted in controlled laboratory environments or simulations, creating a gap between theoretical potential and practical application.
- **Computational Limitations of Robotic Platforms:** Advanced robotic algorithms, particularly those leveraging Artificial Intelligence (AI) and Model Predictive Control (MPC), often require substantial computational resources. Resource-constrained onboard processors of robotic platforms, especially aerial robots, may struggle to execute these algorithms efficiently, limiting their autonomy and capabilities.
- **Demands of Industrial Environments:** Industries such as mining present hazardous and challenging conditions, making them suitable candidates for autonomous robotic systems to enhance safety and efficiency. However, the unique complexities of these environments, including difficult terrain and the need for robust, real-time control, impose stringent requirements on robotic platforms and their supporting infrastructure. Widespread adoption of robots in such industries has been constrained by limitations in automation capabilities.
- **Need for Low-Latency, High-Throughput Communication:** Offloading computationally intensive tasks to edge infrastructure requires reliable, low-latency, and high-throughput communication to ensure real-time control and system stability. Traditional network architectures or limited cellular capabilities may not meet these time-critical requirements.
- **Bridging Research and Application:** The authors emphasize the motivation to bridge the gap between academic research advancements and their practical adoption in industrial settings, aiming to deliver tangible, real-world solutions.

Based on these motivations, the key objectives of this work are:

- To achieve and present the first real-world autonomous flight of a 5G-connected aerial robot where its high-level controller is offloaded to a Kubernetes-based edge cluster located nearby.
- To validate the feasibility of leveraging current 5G NR SA network and edge computing technologies to automate specific mining operations, thereby demonstrating their practical utility in active industrial subterranean environments.
- To develop and deploy a time-critical closed-loop edge-based framework that enables the offloading of computationally intensive control modules for robotic platforms in industrial settings.
- To integrate 5G NR SA networks to meet the real-time control requirements essential for stable and safe robotic operation.
- To rigorously evaluate and validate the functionality and performance of such a system in an operational industrial environment, specifically an active subterranean mine, demonstrating its potential for safe and efficient future deployments.
- To contribute to the broader realization of edge robotics via 5G by providing practical, real-world evidence and a blueprint for future industrial and societal applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employed a holistic approach involving the design, deployment, and evaluation of a complete 5G-connected, edge-controlled robotic system within a real industrial subterranean mine. The methodology encompassed three primary components: a 5G NR SA network, edge computing resources, and a 5G-enabled robotic platform.

**System Architecture:** The overall system architecture comprised a quadcopter drone as the robotic platform, which communicated via a 5G NR SA network with an on-premises edge computing cluster. The high-level controller for the drone was offloaded to this edge cluster.

**5G-Connected Edge-Controlled Robotic Platform:**
- **Hardware:** A custom-made quadcopter served as the aerial robot. It was equipped with an onboard Intel NUC companion computer running Ubuntu 20.04 and ROS 1, managing all robotic operations. An Ouster LiDAR sensor was mounted to provide real-time environmental point cloud data for both positioning and collision avoidance.
- **5G Connectivity:** To enable communication with the edge cluster, the drone featured a lightweight 5G communication setup. This included a Sierra Wireless 5G modem (EM9293), an M.2 key B to USB-C adapter, and four antennas strategically positioned 90 degrees apart to ensure comprehensive spatial coverage. Cellular modem connectivity and management were handled by the ModemManager and NetworkManager packages in Linux.
- **Data Transmission:** To minimize network load and respect the real-time constraints, only telemetry and command actions, typically in kilobytes of information, were transmitted between the robot and the edge. Computationally heavy sensor data, such as full LiDAR point clouds, were processed onboard for localization to avoid high-throughput transmissions.

**5G Data Transmission Infrastructure:**
- **Network Deployment:** An Ericsson 5G NR SA network was deployed at the industrial mining site. Base stations were adapted for the harsh mining conditions. Outdoor coverage was provided by a multi-sector macro base station operating in the 3.5 GHz spectrum (band N78). For indoor tunnel environments, customized two-sector base stations with highly directive antennas were used to ensure longitudinal coverage and effective radio overlap. Wider open areas utilized more omnidirectional radio equipment.
- **Backbone:** All radio equipment was connected via optical fiber to underground Remote Radio Units (RRUs), which in turn were linked to a centralized Base Band Unit (BBU), also located underground.
- **Core and Edge Location:** The 5G network core and the edge computing infrastructure were situated above ground in a dedicated enclosure.
- **Network Slicing:** The network included multiple 5G slices, with one specifically dedicated to the experimental testbed and optimized for low-latency applications, while another was configured for higher-latency use cases (e.g., worker mobile devices).

**Edge-Offloaded Control:**
- **Edge Cluster:** The edge computing resources were organized as a Kubernetes (k8s) cluster, comprising four active nodes. Each node featured an Intel multi-core Central Processing Unit (CPU), 590 GiB of Random Access Memory (RAM), and NVIDIA Graphics Processing Unit (GPU) capabilities (NVIDIA GTX 2080 Ti), intended for advanced Machine Learning (ML) and AI tasks. The cluster was managed using Rancher Kubernetes Engine (RKE) and ran k8s version 1.28.15, consisting of a mix of control plane and worker nodes.
- **Resource Management:** Access to resources was managed through containerized environments and Virtual Machines (VMs) running on a virtualization layer atop bare-metal hardware. This setup allowed for fine-grained resource allocation, aligning with application demands and network slicing constraints, critical for real-time and compute-intensive workloads.
- **Control Algorithm:** Nonlinear Model Predictive Control (NMPC) was selected as the control algorithm due to its predictive capabilities and high performance, particularly for UAV trajectory control and collision avoidance.
- **Controller Deployment:** The NMPC controller was deployed as a containerized application within a dedicated k8s pod on the edge cluster. NodePort services were configured to facilitate seamless bidirectional communication over User Datagram Protocol (UDP). This enabled the efficient transmission of telemetry and sensor data from the robot to the edge and control commands from the NMPC back to the robot. The NMPC container was managed under the `drone-controller` namespace with specific port mappings (e.g., 30200–30203).
- **Closed-Loop Operation:** A human operator selected waypoints, which were sent to the edge-offloaded NMPC. The robot transmitted its state (`x(t)`) to the edge, where the NMPC received the delayed state (`x(t - t_u)`). The NMPC then computed control actions (`u(t)`) and sent them back to the robot, which received the delayed command (`u(t - t_d)`). The round-trip time (RTT), defined as `t_rtt(t) = t_u(t) + t_d(t) + t_p(t)` (where `t_p` is processing time), was a critical performance metric. The MPC model explicitly incorporated measured uplink (UL) and downlink (DL) delays. Localization was estimated onboard to reduce data transmission.

**Experimental Validation:**
- **Test Environment:** Experiments were conducted at an active mining site in northern Sweden, leveraging the installed 5G and edge computing infrastructure.
- **Scenario:** A human operator connected to the edge cluster to select waypoints for the aerial robot. The edge-offloaded controller processed this input along with sensor data from the robot to generate control actions, guiding the robot to autonomously follow the designated path.
- **Performance Metrics:** System performance was evaluated by tracking the robot's actual trajectory against the reference waypoints. Network performance was assessed through 5G radio specifications and measurements (e.g., RSRP, SINR, TX Power, RSSI) and, critically, end-to-end communication delay and jitter. Qosium, a real-time passive network monitoring tool, was used to measure maximum delay, drift-corrected delay, and network jitter for both UL and DL channels. The stability of the closed-loop system was evaluated by confirming that the measured RTT remained below the maximum allowable delay (`τ_max`) for stable NMPC operation, as established in prior research.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental validation in an active industrial subterranean mine yielded several findings regarding the operational capability and performance of the 5G-connected, edge-controlled aerial robot system.

**1. System Operationality and Trajectory Following:**
- The aerial robot successfully performed autonomous flight, accurately following human-selected waypoints within the complex subterranean mining environment. This demonstrated the system's capability to execute time-critical control tasks with its high-level controller offloaded to the edge (Fig. 5). The robot's actual trajectory closely aligned with the reference waypoints, indicating effective navigation and control.

**2. 5G Network Radio Performance:**
- Measurements of the 5G NR SA network provided insights into its performance in the mining environment (Table I):
 - **Operating Band:** n78 (3.5 GHz)
 - **Bandwidth:** 100 MHz
 - **Reference Signal Received Power (RSRP):** Ranged from -100 dBm to -83 dBm.
 - **Signal-to-Interference-plus-Noise Ratio (SINR):** Ranged from 12.5 dB to 27.0 dB.
 - **EU Transmit Power (TX Power):** Ranged from 13.0 dBm to 23.0 dBm.
 - **Received Signal Strength Indication (RSSI) per Antenna:** Values ranged from -73.8 dBm to -43.3 dBm across the four antennas.
- These radio parameters indicate a robust signal quality and coverage within the operational area of the mine.

**3. End-to-End Communication Delay and Jitter:**
- **Maximum Network Delay (Fig. 6):**
 - Uplink (UL) delay ranged between 5 ms and 34 ms.
 - Downlink (DL) delay varied from 3 ms to 27 ms.
- **Drift-Corrected Delay (Fig. 7):** This metric accounted for potential clock drift, providing a refined estimate of actual communication latency.
 - UL drift-corrected delay ranged from 4 ms to 13 ms, with an average of 9.5 ms.
 - DL drift-corrected delay ranged from 2 ms to 15 ms, with an average of 6.5 ms.
- **Network Jitter (Fig. 8):**
 - UL jitter values ranged from 0 ms to 5 ms.
 - DL jitter values ranged from 0 ms to 11 ms.
- **Traffic:** The average UL traffic was measured at 2.1 Mbps, while the average DL traffic was 0.1 Mbps.
- **System Stability:** The measured drift-corrected delay values, specifically the average UL of 9.5 ms and DL of 6.5 ms, were within the stable operating regime for the offloaded NMPC control architecture. According to prior research, system stability is maintained as long as the Round-Trip Time (RTT) remains below a specific maximum allowable delay (`τ_max`). The observed delays confirmed that the 5G NR SA network provided communication latency sufficient to maintain the stability of the closed-loop control system.

These results collectively demonstrate that the deployed 5G network and edge computing infrastructure can support real-time, time-critical robotic operations in a demanding industrial environment, enabling the stable execution of an offloaded NMPC controller.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a notable advancement in the field of robotics and industrial automation by providing empirical validation of a 5G-connected, edge-controlled aerial robot in an active industrial subterranean mine. The significance and potential impact of this work are multifaceted:

**1. Real-World Validation of Edge Robotics:** The primary contribution is the demonstration of real-world functionality of a time-critical, 5G-connected, edge-controlled aerial robotic platform in an uncontrolled, non-laboratory industrial setting. This addresses a critical gap in existing research, moving beyond simulations and academic laboratory experiments. It provides concrete evidence that these advanced technological integrations are viable for practical deployment.

**2. Enabling Advanced Industrial Automation:** The successful deployment validates the potential of 5G NR SA networks and edge computing to facilitate industrial automation in harsh and hazardous environments. Industries such as mining, which pose significant safety risks to human workers and are often challenging for conventional automation, can benefit from autonomous robotic systems for tasks like inspection, data collection, and search and rescue. By demonstrating the feasibility of edge-controlled robots, this work opens pathways for wider adoption of automation in these domains, potentially enhancing both safety and operational efficiency.

**3. Optimization of Robotic Platforms:** Offloading computationally intensive control modules, such as Nonlinear Model Predictive Control (NMPC), to edge infrastructure reduces the processing demands on onboard robotic hardware. This has several implications:
 - **Cost Reduction:** Robotic platforms can be designed with less powerful and therefore more affordable onboard computers.
 - **Weight Reduction:** For aerial robots, reducing onboard processing hardware directly translates to lighter platforms, which can lead to increased flight times and payload capacities.
 - **Simplified Maintenance and Upgradability:** Maintenance and software upgrades for complex control algorithms can be managed centrally at the edge, reducing the need for direct access to individual robots and streamlining updates.

**4. Confirmation of 5G Capabilities for Time-Critical Applications:** The experimental results provide quantitative data on 5G network performance (radio parameters, delays, and jitter) in a challenging industrial environment. The achieved low-latency communication (average drift-corrected UL delay of 9.5 ms and DL delay of 6.5 ms) confirms that 5G NR SA networks can meet the stringent real-time requirements necessary for stable and safe closed-loop control of robotic systems.

**5. Foundation for Future Robotic Architectures:** This work establishes a practical blueprint for the design and deployment of future edge-controlled robotic systems. It suggests directions for further architectural developments, including:
 - **Advanced Data Processing Offloading:** Exploring the offloading of even more sophisticated data processing tasks to the edge.
 - **UL-Oriented 5G Networks:** Investigating the optimization of 5G networks for uplink-heavy scenarios, which are often characteristic of sensor-rich robotic applications.
 - **Network-Aware Mission Planning:** Integrating network link quality parameters into autonomous robot mission planning. This could allow robots to dynamically adapt their operational strategies (e.g., adjusting data offloading rates, modifying operational distances) based on real-time network conditions, thereby enhancing robustness and safety in unpredictable environments.

In summary, this research advances the state of the art by demonstrating the practical application and benefits of 5G-enabled edge robotics in a demanding industrial context. It provides a foundation for the widespread integration of advanced autonomous systems, particularly in environments where human presence is hazardous or inefficient.
