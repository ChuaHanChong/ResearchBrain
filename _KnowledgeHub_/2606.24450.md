# NoContactNoWorries: Estimating Contact through Vision and Proprioception for In-Hand Dexterous Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> The `NoContactNoWorries` framework estimates binary fingertip contact in real-time using ego-centric RGB-D vision and robot proprioception, eliminating the need for physical tactile sensors in dexterous manipulation. This approach achieved high F1 scores for contact prediction and enabled in-hand object reorientation with performance comparable to ground-truth tactile input.

## Problem

- Physical tactile sensors for dexterous manipulation are costly, fragile, offer limited coverage, and are complex to integrate into robotic systems.
- Vision-only methods for contact inference often struggle with self-occlusion during close-range interactions and lack direct supervision for contact-dependent phenomena.
- There is a need for a real-time, deployable method to infer detailed contact information without relying on specialized tactile hardware.

## Method

- A transformer-based multimodal deep learning framework combines ego-centric RGB-D visual features with current and commanded robot proprioception to predict binary fingertip contact states.
- A ==pose-conditioned cross-attention== mechanism fuses proprioceptive data with visual tokens, explicitly distinguishing between reactive (current pose) and anticipatory (commanded pose) sensing.
- A causal Transformer encoder processes a sequence of fused representations over a temporal window to capture dynamic contact cues, outputting binary contact predictions for fixed fingertip locations.

## Results

- The model achieved F1 scores up to **0.9** for contact prediction in simulation and robustly transferred to real-world scenarios with F1 scores around **0.82**-**0.84** for convex objects and **0.71**-**0.74** for non-convex objects.
- In a real-world in-hand object reorientation task, policies leveraging predicted contacts performed comparably to those using oracle ground-truth contacts, sometimes outperforming physical FSRs due to better alignment with simulator-trained policies.
- The framework demonstrated an average inference latency of 8 ms, ensuring its suitability for real-time closed-loop control in robotic manipulation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Multimodal fusion of vision and proprioception is crucial for robust contact inference, particularly in overcoming visual occlusions and ambiguities inherent in in-hand manipulation.
> - Temporal modeling significantly enhances contact prediction accuracy by enabling the system to disambiguate contact events from mere proximity or transient visual changes.
> - The proposed "virtual sensor" can effectively substitute for ground-truth tactile feedback in downstream control tasks, demonstrating its practical utility and transferability to real-world robotics without policy retraining.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Dexterous manipulation in robotics necessitates precise control of physical contact between robot end-effectors and objects. This control typically relies on rich contact feedback, such as force distribution, slip detection, and localized stability information. While vision and proprioception provide global scene context and the robot's internal state, tactile sensing is often considered crucial for discerning localized contact dynamics that are ambiguous or occluded in visual data, particularly during in-hand manipulation tasks.

Historically, tactile sensing has faced challenges in scalability, encompassing high costs, fragility, limited coverage, and complex integration requirements. In contrast, cameras are widely available, though ego-centric vision (e.g., wrist-mounted RGB-D) can suffer from self-occlusion during close-range interactions. Existing vision-based methods have achieved success in visuomotor control and object reorientation but often lack direct supervision over contact-dependent phenomena. Conversely, approaches leveraging tactile feedback have demonstrated improved performance in contact-rich skills, including re-grasping and fine motor control. Many advanced systems integrate vision and touch, but these typically assume the availability of physical tactile sensors during deployment or require joint retraining of downstream policies.

This research addresses a specific gap in this landscape: the inference of contact *without* dedicated physical tactile sensors, relying solely on readily available ego-centric vision and proprioception. This approach is motivated by biological observations that humans integrate multimodal sensory cues to infer contact properties. Prior work has explored using predicted contact models for training rewards, but these predictions were not typically supplied as real-time observations to downstream controllers. The current work proposes a "virtual sensor" paradigm, where inferred contact signals are provided directly to the robot as real-time observations, enabling active reaction to contacts without requiring policy retraining or specialized hardware. This positions the work as an alternative solution for acquiring contact information in scenarios where deploying physical tactile sensors is impractical.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the practical limitations of physical tactile sensors in robotic manipulation. While tactile sensing provides critical localized contact information for dexterous tasks, it is characterized by high cost, fragility, limited coverage over complex hand geometries, and significant integration overhead (e.g., wiring, calibration). These factors hinder the widespread and scalable deployment of high-fidelity tactile hardware on robotic systems.

Complementing this, the authors draw inspiration from biological systems. Humans demonstrate an ability to infer physical contact by synthesizing visual information with proprioceptive feedback, such as their body's pose and movement. Furthermore, the primary somatosensory cortex (S1) in the brain receives convergent inputs from both visual and proprioceptive pathways, suggesting a neural basis for such multimodal integration. This biological precedent forms a hypothesis that robots could similarly infer contact without direct tactile measurement.

Given these motivations, the key objectives of the study are:

1. **Develop a pseudo-tactile sensing framework:** To design and implement a multimodal deep learning framework capable of predicting binary fingertip contact states from ego-centric RGB-D vision and robot proprioception. The framework aims to serve as a "virtual sensor," providing contact information when physical tactile sensing is unavailable or impractical.
2. **Validate predictive accuracy:** To evaluate the accuracy of the proposed model in estimating binary contact signals across various object geometries, including those not seen during training, and demonstrate its performance in both simulated and real-world environments.
3. **Assess architectural component contributions:** To conduct ablation studies to determine the individual and combined contributions of different sensory modalities (RGB-D vision, current proprioception, commanded proprioception) and architectural components (pose-conditioned cross-attention, temporal modeling) to the overall contact prediction performance.
4. **Demonstrate utility for downstream control:** To show that the predicted pseudo-tactile signals can effectively substitute for ground-truth or physical tactile input in a closed-loop dexterous manipulation task, specifically in-hand object reorientation, without requiring retraining of the manipulation policy. This includes evaluating performance on both seen and novel objects in real-world hardware.
5. **Ensure real-time applicability:** To confirm that the inference latency of the proposed model is sufficiently low to support real-time closed-loop control in robotic manipulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, named `NoContactNoWorries`, is a transformer-based multimodal framework designed to infer binary contact signals at fixed fingertip locations using RGB-D vision and proprioceptive sensing. The approach integrates these complementary signals over short temporal windows to capture dynamic correlations.

**Problem Formulation:**
At each timestep `t`, the robot receives an RGB image `I_t`, a depth map `D_t`, its current joint configuration `q_t`, and a commanded joint configuration `q_com_t`. The objective is to predict a binary contact vector `ˆc_t ∈ {0, 1}^K`, where `K` denotes the number of tactile sensing sites (fingertips).

**A. RGB-D Feature Extraction:**
The framework utilizes a frozen RGB-D encoder `Φ` to map raw RGB-D inputs into a shared spatial feature space `f_t`. This encoder is adapted from an existing semantic segmentation backbone, which processes RGB via a convolutional network and depth via a transformer-based module, leveraging local spatial attention and cross-modal fusion. This pre-trained, frozen encoder aims to provide robust visual representations without requiring large-scale, modality-specific visual supervision. A lightweight, trainable convolutional module `Ψ_conv` then processes `f_t` further, reducing spatial resolution and projecting features into a fixed embedding dimension `D`, yielding a set of compact spatial visual tokens `v_t ∈ R^{N×D}`.

**B. Pose-Conditioned Spatial Fusion:**
To incorporate proprioceptive information and address visual ambiguities, a cross-attention mechanism fuses proprioceptive data with the visual tokens.
1. **Proprioceptive Embeddings:** The current joint configuration `q_t` and the commanded joint configuration `q_com_t` are embedded into vectors `h_t` and `h_com_t`, respectively, using learned linear projections (`ψ` and `ψ_com`). Both `h_t` and `h_com_t` are of dimension `D`.
2. **Cross-Attention:** These embedded proprioceptive vectors serve as distinct queries in a shared multi-head cross-attention mechanism over the visual tokens `v_t`. This generates two attended visual representations: `a_t` (conditioned on current pose) and `a_com_t` (conditioned on commanded pose).
3. **Fused Representation:** The resulting `a_t` and `a_com_t` are concatenated and passed through a learned multilayer perceptron (MLP) to produce a single, fused, proprioception-aware visual embedding `z_t ∈ R^D`. This design distinguishes between reactive sensing (from `q_t`) and anticipatory sensing (from `q_com_t`), reflecting their complementary roles in sensorimotor control.

**C. Temporal Modeling for Contact Estimation:**
Contact events often manifest through dynamic cues rather than instantaneous observations. To capture these, the model processes a sequence of the fused representations `{z_{t-T+1}..., z_t}` over a fixed temporal window `T` using a causal Transformer encoder. This encoder produces a contextualized representation `˜z_t` at the current timestep. Finally, a linear contact head with a sigmoid activation function processes `˜z_t` to output the binary contact vector `ˆc_t ∈^K`.

**System Setup and Training Protocol:**
- **Platform:** The LEAP Hand robotic platform is used, simulated in NVIDIA Isaac Gym. A wrist-mounted Intel RealSense D455 camera provides RGB-D feedback.
- **Data Collection:** Demonstrations are collected in simulation using a Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU)-based policy trained with Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) for in-hand object rotation. The policy during its training phase in simulation is provided with ground-truth contacts from the physics engine.
- **Ground-Truth Labels:** In simulation, binary contact labels for four fingertip locations (distal phalanges of active fingers) are obtained from the PhysX engine. For real-world evaluation, custom low-profile force-sensitive resistors (FSRs) are attached to the fingertips, and their signals are binarized using a 0.1 N threshold. These physical sensors are *only* for evaluation metrics and are disconnected during model inference and policy deployment.
- **Domain Randomization:** To facilitate sim-to-real transfer, various domain randomization techniques are applied, including physics parameters (object mass, friction, PD gains, scale) and perceptual variations (visual distractors, camera viewpoints, Gaussian sensor noise, object perturbation forces).
- **Dataset and Training:** The dataset comprises 50 15-second trajectories (10 per object) generated using five training object geometries. The contact prediction model (excluding the frozen RGB-D encoder) is trained using supervised learning with a binary cross-entropy loss over all timesteps. AdamW is used as the optimizer with a learning rate of 1 × 10⁻⁴. The causal transformer and cross-attention modules each use one attention layer with one head and an embedding dimension of 256, operating over a temporal window `T = 8`.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluated `NoContactNoWorries` in terms of contact prediction accuracy and its utility for downstream in-hand manipulation tasks, across both simulation and real-world scenarios, for seen and novel objects.

**A. Contact Prediction Performance (F1 Scores):**
The full `NoContactNoWorries` model consistently achieved the highest F1 scores across all evaluation settings (Table I).
- **Overall Accuracy:** In simulation, the model achieved F1 scores exceeding 0.9 for convex objects (e.g., Cuboid, Dodecahedron) and strong performance for challenging non-convex geometries (e.g., Star, Stairs). In the real world, performance saw a degradation but remained robust, with F1 scores around 0.82-0.84 for convex objects and 0.71-0.74 for non-convex objects (Star, Letter R), indicating effective sim-to-real transfer.
- **Visibility and Geometric Ambiguity:** A controlled analysis (Table II) showed that vision-only variants degraded significantly under visual occlusion (F1 0.79 visible vs. 0.51 occluded for Hexagonal Prism), highlighting the incompleteness of visual information alone. A kinematic depth baseline, despite having access to calibrated geometry, performed similarly and also degraded under occlusion (0.81 visible vs. 0.54 occluded). In contrast, the full multimodal model maintained robustness across visibility regimes (0.93 visible vs. 0.85 occluded), demonstrating the benefit of leveraging proprioceptive cues.
- **Role of Proprioception and Temporal Modeling:**
 - **"Pose-only" variants** (without visual input) demonstrated that proprioception alone could achieve reasonable performance, attributed to the closed-loop nature of the policies where discrepancies between commanded and realized motion correlate with contact events (Figure 6). The query-asymmetric design for current vs. commanded pose performed better than symmetric or MLP-based fusions, indicating the importance of this architectural distinction.
 - **"Vision-only"** performance was consistently lower than the full model, emphasizing the limitations of vision under self-occlusion.
 - **"No temporal modeling"** consistently resulted in lower F1 scores, confirming that temporal reasoning is crucial for disambiguating contact from mere proximity or transient occlusions.
- **Mismatched Command Stress Test:** The model's F1 scores decreased only slightly when supplied with a mismatched `q_com_t` (Table I), suggesting that the model does not over-rely on motion intent as a shortcut but grounds its inference in multimodal cues and their temporal consistency.

**B. Downstream Performance (Cumulative Rotation Reward (CRR) and Cumulative Rotation Angle (CRA)):**
The predicted contact signals effectively supported closed-loop object rotation, demonstrating their utility as a substitute for true tactile sensing (Table III).
- **Overall Policy Performance:** Policies using `NoContactNoWorries` predicted contacts achieved performance comparable to policies using oracle ground-truth contacts, especially on convex objects.
- **Sim-to-Real Gap and "Oracle" Comparison:** A notable finding was that on convex objects in the real world, the `NoContactNoWorries` model yielded higher returns than the hardware Oracle (using real FSRs). This was attributed to the policy being trained with idealized simulator dynamics, meaning that the real FSRs introduced unmodeled noise that degraded performance. The predicted simulated labels, by aligning with the simulator's expectations, effectively buffered against real-world hardware noise when visual occlusion was minimal. Conversely, for highly occluded, non-convex geometries, the Oracle's physical signals regained an advantage, indicating visual ambiguity dominated.
- **Ablation Trends:** Similar to contact prediction, the trends from ablation studies persisted: multimodal fusion, pose-conditioned cross-attention, and causal temporal reasoning were critical for robust downstream control. Vision-only control proved to be the least reliable.
- **Real-time Capability:** The model demonstrated an average inference latency of 8 ms, which is well within the 50 ms budget for a 20 Hz control loop, confirming its suitability for real-time applications.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework that demonstrates the feasibility of inferring binary fingertip contact signals from readily available RGB-D vision and robot proprioception, thus offering a practical surrogate for dedicated tactile sensing in dexterous manipulation. This represents a significant contribution by addressing the known challenges of cost, fragility, limited coverage, and integration complexity associated with physical tactile hardware.

The primary significance lies in enabling contact-aware control for in-hand manipulation in scenarios where tactile instrumentation is unavailable, unreliable, or impractical. By providing a "virtual sensor" that generates real-time pseudo-tactile signals, the method allows manipulation policies to retain many benefits of tactile feedback. The successful generalization to novel objects and robust performance in real-world experiments without requiring policy retraining highlight the practical utility and transferability of the learned contact inference model. The model's ability to sometimes outperform real-world FSRs for policies trained in simulation, due to better alignment with simulated dynamics, further underscores its potential as a buffer against real-world sensor noise.

Potential impacts include:
- **Democratization of Dexterous Manipulation:** Lowering the barrier to entry for complex manipulation tasks by reducing reliance on expensive and specialized hardware.
- **Enhanced Robustness:** Enabling robots to adapt to diverse objects and unstructured environments where tactile sensors might fail or be impractical to deploy.
- **Scalability:** Facilitating the development of more scalable robotic systems by using ubiquitous and less complex sensing modalities.
- **Foundational Research:** The work lays groundwork for developing more sophisticated pseudo-tactile sensing, potentially inspiring future research into foundational models for generalized hand-object interactions that can predict richer tactile quantities like force, shear, or torque.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The authors acknowledge several limitations. The current study focuses on binary fingertip contacts at a fixed set of sensing sites; future work could extend this to denser contact maps across the palm and other hand regions. The model is specifically trained for in-hand dexterous manipulation, implying it may not serve as a general-purpose contact detector for vastly different tasks like tool use or assembly. A promising future direction involves pre-training a foundational pseudo-tactile model on diverse hand-object interaction datasets. Additionally, while the frozen RGB-D encoder was effective, benchmarking alternative vision backbones could be beneficial. Finally, the method currently predicts only binary contact states, not richer tactile quantities like force magnitudes or shear, which are crucial for tasks involving deformable objects or precise force control. Extending pseudo-tactile sensing to these richer modalities is identified as an exciting avenue for future research.
