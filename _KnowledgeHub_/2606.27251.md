# Advancing Omnimodal Embodied Agents from Isolated Skills to Everyday Physical Autonomy

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers introduced OmniAct, a framework for persistent embodied agents that integrates multimodal perception, unified cyber-physical actuation, and robust autonomous recovery for reliable operation in dynamic real-world environments. It achieved up to 50.0% end-to-end success on complex manipulation tasks and 54.2% on navigation, while demonstrating effective context management for long-horizon scalability.

## Problem

- Existing embodied systems lacked a unified action space, often separating cyber (APIs, IoT) and physical (robot manipulation, navigation) domains, leading to brittle interfaces for interleaved instructions.
- Current agent frameworks suffered from linear context growth, causing context overflow, diminished temporal coherence, and semantic conflicts over long operational periods.
- Many Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies were open-loop, unable to detect or recover from physical execution failures, which could cascade into irreversible system faults.

## Method

- Developed **OmniAct**, a hierarchical asynchronous architecture that explicitly separates high-level planning, long-horizon state tracking, and real-time physical verification into specialized modules.
- Introduced a ==Multimodal Semantic Planner== to unify cyber tools and physical control within a single skill-routing space, enabling the orchestration of diverse heterogeneous tools.
- Implemented a Closed-Loop Execution Engine with asynchronous visual preemption, utilizing a VLM to semantically assess physical execution, trigger replanning upon anomalies, and generate failure reflections.

## Results

- Achieved higher task success rates on complex cyber-physical orchestration, with **50.0%** End-to-End success on L3 manipulation tasks and **54.2%** on navigation, outperforming baselines.
- The adaptive hierarchical memory system maintained per-call token usage within a bounded 4k–8k range over 100k+ token interaction traces, demonstrating superior long-horizon scalability.
- Consistently improved the performance of six different foundation models, with mid-tier models (e.g., Qwen3-VL-30B-A3B) showing substantial gains, reaching **80.0%** constraint satisfaction from **50.0%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An explicit separation of planning, memory, and verification responsibilities within a hierarchical asynchronous architecture is crucial for achieving persistent and resilient embodied autonomy.
> - Unifying cyber and physical action spaces through a single planning system allows for more natural and complex human-robot interactions requiring interleaved cross-domain actions.
> - Real-time semantic-level physical execution monitoring coupled with cumulative learning from past failures is essential for robust autonomous recovery and long-term adaptation in dynamic environments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research contributes to the field of embodied artificial intelligence, specifically focusing on the development of persistent agents capable of operating reliably in unstructured real-world environments over extended periods. The paper identifies limitations within existing embodied systems, including end-to-end Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models and Large Language Model (LLM)-based planning frameworks.

The authors observe that current VLM-based planners often lack a unified action space that encompasses both cyber (e.g., APIs, IoT devices) and physical (e.g., robot manipulation, navigation) domains. This separation necessitates brittle, hand-crafted interfaces between domains, restricting the system's ability to handle complex, interleaved instructions. Furthermore, contemporary agent frameworks typically accumulate interaction history in a linear fashion, leading to context growth that can exceed model capacity, diminish temporal coherence, and introduce semantic conflicts between long-term preferences and recent states. A third identified limitation is the open-loop nature of many VLA policies, which often lack mechanisms to detect or recover from physical execution failures. Such undetected failures can cascade into irreversible system faults.

Against this background, the presented work, OmniAct, proposes a departure from monolithic end-to-end models. Instead, it advocates for a hierarchical asynchronous architecture that explicitly separates high-level planning, long-horizon state tracking, and real-time physical verification into specialized, cooperating modules. This approach aims to unify cyber tools and physical control within a single skill-routing space, manage context growth through event-boundary-driven hierarchical memory, and implement closed-loop verification via asynchronous visual preemption. In doing so, OmniAct positions itself as a framework that incorporates VLA models as low-level executors while addressing their inherent limitations in reasoning, cross-embodiment transfer, multi-task versatility, and autonomous failure handling through an overarching orchestration layer.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The fundamental objective of this research is to enable the construction of persistent physical agents that can operate reliably and autonomously in dynamic, unstructured real-world environments over prolonged temporal horizons. Achieving this goal requires the integration of several capabilities, including multimodal perception (speech, vision, and language), unified actuation across diverse tools (such as IoT devices, web APIs, robotic manipulators, and mobile platforms), and robust autonomous recovery from unexpected physical failures.

The motivation for OmniAct stems from specific shortcomings observed in current embodied AI systems, which are broadly categorized into two main challenges:

1. **Cyber-Physical Coordination:** Existing methodologies tend to compartmentalize tool invocation (e.g., interacting with web APIs or IoT devices) and physical robotic manipulation. This segregation necessitates the development of distinct pipelines and interfaces for each domain. However, realistic user commands, often delivered through natural speech, frequently require the seamless coordination of multiple heterogeneous tools within a single, coherent task flow. The absence of a unified action space that spans both cyber and physical domains limits the system's ability to interpret and execute such interleaved instructions efficiently, often relying on fragile, custom-built domain bridges.

2. **Resilient Long-Horizon Execution:** The deployment of embodied agents in real-world settings over extended periods introduces challenges related to state management and failure recovery.
 - **Context Accumulation:** Agent frameworks that maintain a linear history of interactions (e.g., by concatenating past turns) experience rapid context growth. This leads to increased memory overhead, potential dilution of critical historical information, and the emergence of semantic conflicts between long-term user preferences and immediate environmental states. Managing this context growth effectively is crucial for sustained operation.
 - **Failure Recovery:** Current end-to-end VLA policies, while demonstrating progress in direct control, are largely open-loop in nature. Once an action is executed, these policies may not inherently perceive whether the physical world has deviated from expectations. This lack of self-correction means that undetected physical failures can cascade into severe, unrecoverable system faults, hindering persistent autonomy.

To address these dual challenges, the research proposes OmniAct, a framework designed around a hierarchical asynchronous architecture that explicitly decouples high-level planning, long-horizon state tracking, and real-time physical verification into specialized, collaborating modules. This architectural design is motivated by the hypothesis that such separation of concerns is necessary for true persistent autonomy, rather than relying on a monolithic model.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

OmniAct is structured as a hierarchical asynchronous architecture comprising three integrated modules: a Multimodal Semantic Planner, a Closed-Loop Execution Engine with Asynchronous Visual Preemption, and an Adaptive Hierarchical Memory. The framework operates on a problem formulation where the agent makes decisions over a unified cyber-physical action space. At each time step `t`, the system receives a multimodal observation (`o_t` comprising visual frame `v_t` and discrete state `s_t`) and current user intent. The high-level planning policy (`a_t ~ π(· | c_t, H_t, T)`) selects an action `a_t` which is a tuple (`τ_t`, `g_t`) indicating the target skill and skill-specific parameters, conditioned on the multimodal context `c_t`, historical trajectory `H_t` from memory, and the available skill inventory `T`.

#### 3.1. Multimodal Semantic Planner

This module is responsible for the high-level decision-making process. It performs three primary functions:
1. **Intent Interpretation:** It grounds user intent, derived from speech and visual observations, against the current state of available tools and the environment, even for ambiguous commands.
2. **Task Decomposition and Skill Routing:** It breaks down long-horizon instructions into a sequence of executable sub-goals. Based on task requirements and environmental constraints, it selects appropriate skills (`τ_t`) from the inventory `T`.
3. **Experience Integration:** It utilizes historical experience retrieved from the hierarchical memory (`H_t`) to inform planning, aiming to avoid previously encountered failures and adhere to long-term user preferences.
The planner outputs a structured JSON specification, designed to support the parallel dispatch of multiple skill calls within a single planning step.

#### 3.2. Closed-Loop Execution with Asynchronous Visual Preemption

This component addresses the limitations of open-loop VLA policies by introducing a feedback mechanism. When the execution engine dispatches an action:
- **Cyber Commands:** Discrete cyber commands (e.g., IoT control, API calls) are verified via deterministic return codes.
- **Physical Commands:** Continuous physical commands, which typically lack intrinsic verification signals, are handled by an asynchronous visual monitor. This monitor periodically captures visual frames during execution. These frames are then submitted to a Vision-Language Model (VLM) for a semantic-level assessment of execution progress and physical safety.
- **Anomaly Detection and Replanning:** If the VLM detects an anomaly (e.g., an object not grasped as expected, the robot deviating from its goal, or other high-level task semantic failures), the system immediately halts the VLA executor. The context of the failure is packaged and control is returned to the planner for autonomous replanning. This mechanism differs from low-level geometric constraints by reasoning about higher-level task semantics, capturing failure modes that might be invisible to basic controllers.
- **Failure Reflection:** Upon preemption or failure from the cyber executor, the system initiates a reflection process. This process generates a structured record of the root cause of the failure and potential corrective strategies, which are then stored in the long-term memory to prevent recurrence.

#### 3.3. Adaptive Hierarchical Memory

To manage context growth effectively and enable long-term operational awareness, OmniAct employs a three-level hierarchical memory system with event-boundary-driven compression:
1. **Sensory Memory:** A lightweight First-In, First-Out (FIFO) queue stores recent visual frames. This memory is primarily used by the control policy and visual monitor and is decoupled from the main planning context to avoid unnecessary data accumulation.
2. **Episodic Memory:** This level manages the interaction history. It employs event-boundary-driven compression, where raw observation sequences are summarized into compact keyframe records upon the detection of semantic boundaries (e.g., completion of a sub-task, significant state change). Each record includes semantic content, state transitions, and action outcomes, while redundant procedural information is discarded. This approach ensures that context growth scales sub-linearly with distinct semantic events rather than raw interaction turns.
3. **Reflective Memory:** This highest level stores both static knowledge (e.g., tool schemas, skill inventory) and dynamically accumulated operational experience. Specifically, when an execution failure or visual preemption occurs, the system generates a structured record detailing the root cause and a corrective strategy. During subsequent planning, relevant reflections are retrieved and injected into the planning context. This mechanism facilitates cumulative adaptation, allowing the agent to learn from past failures and adapt to dynamic environmental changes over its operational lifetime.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of OmniAct was structured around three key axes: cross-domain orchestration across heterogeneous hardware, memory effectiveness and scalability under long-horizon deployment, and generalizability across various foundation models.

#### 4.1. Long-Horizon Cyber-Physical Orchestration

Experiments were conducted on 40 real-world long-horizon tasks across two distinct robotic platforms: a UR5e 6-DoF manipulator and a Keenon wheeled mobile robot. The tasks involved coordinating with four household IoT devices and twelve virtual tool APIs. Tasks were categorized into three complexity levels (L1: Physical Only, L2: Cyber-Physical, L3: Full Multimodal).
- **Success Rates:** OmniAct demonstrated a more graceful degradation in performance as task complexity increased from L1 to L3, compared to baseline methods (Direct Policy, SayCan, Code as Policy, RoboBrain2).
 - For the most complex L3 tasks, OmniAct achieved 50.0% End-to-End (E2E) success on manipulation tasks and 54.2% on navigation tasks. These figures exceeded the best baseline performance (SayCan: 43.8% on manipulation; Code as Policy: 37.5% on navigation).
 - OmniAct also achieved the highest Sub-task (Sub) success rates across all complexity levels, with L3 Sub reaching 80.5% on manipulation and 80.6% on navigation, surpassing best baseline scores of 60.9% and 57.9%, respectively.
- **Failure Recovery:** The consistent difference between Sub and E2E success rates across baselines, and OmniAct's comparatively smaller gap, was attributed to the asynchronous visual preemption and reflective memory. These mechanisms allowed OmniAct to detect execution failures (e.g., grasp slippage) and trigger replanning, converting potentially catastrophic failures into recoverable delays.
- **Cross-Modal Coordination:** The RoboBrain2 baseline, despite its capabilities on planning benchmarks, showed low scores in tasks requiring interleaved cyber-physical actions, indicating that cross-modal tool coordination demands an explicit orchestration layer not inherently present in such models.
- **Platform Generalizability:** OmniAct showed comparable relative improvements on both the UR5e manipulator and the Keenon mobile robot, without platform-specific architectural modifications or additional tuning. This suggests that the framework’s core contributions operate at an abstraction level independent of specific embodiment.

#### 4.2. Adaptive Hierarchical Memory

The memory system was evaluated for temporal conflict resolution and long-horizon scalability.
- **Temporal Conflict Resolution:** In scenarios with 10 samples exceeding 10 hours of interaction (40k+ accumulated tokens) and embedded temporal preference conflicts, OmniAct (Full) achieved the highest constraint satisfaction rates: 86.67% (Tool Use), 66.67% (Home Assistant), and 80.0% (Manipulation). This was a 13–20 percentage point improvement over the best ablation variant (Episodic Only or Turn Summary) and significantly higher than Raw History (46.67%–66.67%) or Sliding Window (near-zero). The reflective memory was found to be critical for explicitly surfacing long-term constraints and failure-derived corrective strategies.
- **Long-Horizon Scalability:** Over extended interaction traces of 100k+ tokens (approximately 270 rounds), raw history replay exhibited linear growth in per-call input token consumption, exceeding 120k tokens by round 260. In contrast, OmniAct's event-boundary-driven compression maintained per-call token usage within a bounded 4k–8k range, which plateaued early and fluctuated only with local episode complexity. This near-flat scaling profile indicates that OmniAct can enable persistent operation over arbitrarily long horizons without context overflow or progressive quality degradation.

#### 4.3. Cross-Model Generalizability

To assess if the gains were architectural rather than backbone-dependent, OmniAct was tested with six different foundation models (Gemini-3.1-Pro, Gemini-3-Flash, Qwen3.6-Plus, Qwen3.6-Flash, Qwen3-VL-30B-A3B, Qwen3-VL-8B).
- **Performance Improvements:** OmniAct consistently improved the performance of all backbones with sufficient baseline capabilities. Mid-tier models, such as Qwen3-VL-30B-A3B, showed the most substantial gains, improving from 50.0% to 80.0% (+30.0) in constraint satisfaction. This allowed it to match the vanilla performance of the larger Gemini-3.1-Pro.
- **Model Scale Impact:** Even the proprietary Gemini-3.1-Pro model showed a benefit (+3.3, from 76.7% to 80.0%), indicating that OmniAct's architectural solutions address systematic failure modes regardless of model scale. The smallest model, Qwen3-VL-8B, showed only marginal improvement (+6.7), suggesting a lower bound on the required backbone capacity for effective integration. These results support the conclusion that architectural clarity and structured orchestration complement model capacity.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of OmniAct marks a contribution toward the realization of persistent, autonomous embodied agents capable of operating in complex, unstructured real-world environments. The framework’s key significance lies in its proposed hierarchical asynchronous architecture, which addresses several fundamental limitations of existing embodied AI systems.

One significant impact is the **unification of cyber and physical action spaces**. By enabling a single planning system to orchestrate both discrete cyber tools (like APIs and IoT devices) and continuous physical control (like robotic manipulation and navigation), OmniAct facilitates more natural and complex human-robot interactions. This integration is crucial for tasks requiring interleaved actions across domains, such as a robot needing to query a web API, activate an IoT device, and then perform a physical manipulation, all within a single user instruction.

Another area of impact is **enhanced resilience through closed-loop execution and autonomous failure recovery**. The asynchronous visual preemption engine allows the system to detect and respond to physical execution failures in real-time. This capability reduces the occurrence of cascading faults that often plague open-loop systems, thereby improving task completion rates and reducing the need for human intervention in failure-prone scenarios. The reflective memory further reinforces this by enabling cumulative adaptation, where the agent learns from past failures and integrates corrective strategies into future planning.

The **adaptive hierarchical memory system** offers a solution to the challenge of managing context growth in long-horizon interactions. By compressing interaction history based on semantic event boundaries, OmniAct maintains sub-linear growth in token consumption. This ensures that the system can operate over extended durations without exceeding model context windows or incurring escalating inference costs, thus enabling practical, long-term deployment of embodied agents.

Furthermore, OmniAct demonstrates **cross-model generalizability**, notably elevating the performance of mid-scale open-weight models to a level comparable with proprietary systems. This suggests that the architectural design and structured orchestration provided by OmniAct can act as a force multiplier for diverse foundation models, potentially democratizing access to advanced embodied AI capabilities by reducing reliance on the largest, most resource-intensive proprietary models.

Overall, the research suggests that an explicit separation of planning, memory, and verification responsibilities, as implemented in OmniAct, offers a viable and potentially more resource-efficient pathway toward achieving persistent embodied autonomy in real-world settings. This has implications for applications ranging from domestic service robots and industrial automation to assistive robotics, where reliability, adaptability, and long-term operational capacity are paramount.
