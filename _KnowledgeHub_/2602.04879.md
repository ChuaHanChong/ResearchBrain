# Rethinking the Trust Region in LLM Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Sea AI Lab and the National University of Singapore developed Divergence Proximal Policy Optimization (DPPO), a framework for LLM fine-tuning that replaces PPO's heuristic ratio clipping with a principled, divergence-based trust region. This approach delivers improved training stability and efficiency across various LLM architectures and tasks, often reducing the need for external stabilization techniques.

## Problem

- Proximal Policy Optimization's (PPO) heuristic ratio-clipping mechanism is structurally ill-suited for the vast, long-tailed vocabularies inherent to Large Language Models (LLMs), leading to suboptimal and unstable learning.
- LLM fine-tuning with Reinforcement Learning (RL) frequently suffers from training instability, often linked to 'bad updates' that cause catastrophic performance degradation, and inefficiency due to over-constraining critical token updates.
- Principled trust region methods, such as TRPO, are computationally prohibitive for LLMs because they require calculating full vocabulary probability distributions, leading to high memory and computational overhead.

## Method

- Derived new ==policy improvement bounds== tailored for the ==finite-horizon, undiscounted setting== of LLM generation, providing a rigorous theoretical basis for trust-region methods in this context.
- Introduced **DPPO** (==Divergence Proximal Policy Optimization==), which replaces PPO's ratio clipping with a ==dynamic mask applied to the policy gradient objective==, based on a direct measure of ==policy divergence== (e.g., ==Total Variation== or ==KL divergence==).
- Developed ==memory-efficient approximations== for policy divergence calculation (==Binary== and ==Top-K Approximations==) to ensure the method's scalability and negligible overhead for LLMs with massive vocabularies.

## Results

- DPPO consistently demonstrated superior training stability and efficiency, achieving faster reward optimization and better converged performance compared to baselines like GRPO-ClipHigher and CISPO across various LLM architectures.
- DPPO maintained stable training where baselines frequently exhibited instability or catastrophic collapse, and its inherent stability significantly reduced the reliance on complex external stabilization techniques like Rollout Router Replay (R3).
- The simpler 'binary approximation' of TV/KL divergence performed comparably to the richer 'Top-K approximation,' proving to be a sufficient and computationally efficient choice for scalable RL in LLMs across diverse reasoning tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A trust region is essential for stable LLM training even at low learning rates, and it must be correctly anchored to the original behavior policy (μ) rather than a recomputed policy distribution (π).
> - Training instability in LLM RL is primarily caused by a small fraction (≤ 0.5%) of 'bad updates' on negative samples that aggressively decrease the probability of a sampled token far outside the trust region.
> - PPO's ratio clipping mechanism 'over-constrains' updates to low-probability, high-entropy tokens, hindering exploration and efficiency, and Truncated Importance Sampling (TIS) can degrade stability rather than improve it.
