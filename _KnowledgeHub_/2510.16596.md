# SHIELD: Suppressing Hallucinations In LVLM Encoders via Bias and Vulnerability Defense

> [!summary] Summary
> SHIELD introduces a training-free framework that effectively mitigates object hallucinations in Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) by targeting specific issues within their visual encoders, including statistical bias, inherent bias, and vulnerability. The method achieves substantial reductions in hallucination metrics, such as an 18% improvement over previous training-free approaches on CHAIR benchmarks, while also enhancing general perception capabilities across various LVLM architectures.

## Problem

- Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) frequently generate object hallucinations, producing plausible but factually inaccurate descriptions of objects, which compromises their reliability and safety in critical applications.
- Current hallucination mitigation strategies primarily focus on the Large Language Model (LLM) component of LVLMs or require computationally expensive retraining/fine-tuning.
- The direct contributions of the visual encoder—the component responsible for processing image information—to the problem of object hallucination in LVLMs remained largely unaddressed.

## Method

- ==SHIELD== proposes a training-free framework that intervenes on visual tokens extracted by the encoder and adjusts the decoding process to suppress hallucinations.
- It implements ==Token Re-weighting== to mitigate statistical bias by redistributing attention across object-relevant visual tokens, leveraging a similarity matrix derived from a naive image caption.
- The framework incorporates ==Token Subtraction== to reduce inherent bias by removing noise-derived erroneous features, and utilizes ==Contrastive Decoding== with adversarial attacks and an adaptive plausibility constraint to address visual encoder vulnerability and suppress hallucination-prone tokens.

## Results

- SHIELD significantly outperformed existing training-free methods, achieving an **18%** improvement over the second-best method (OPERA) in reducing sentence-level hallucinations on CHAIR benchmarks for LLaVA-1.5.
- Consistent and substantial reductions in object, attribute, and relational hallucinations were observed across multiple LVLM architectures (LLaVA-1.5, InstructBLIP, Qwen-VL) and various benchmarks, including GPT4o-aided LLaVA-Bench and POPE.
- The framework also improved general perception capabilities, showing gains in subtasks like OCR and Posters on the MME benchmark, indicating that addressing visual encoder issues enhances overall visual understanding beyond just hallucination suppression.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Object hallucinations in LVLMs are directly linked to specific issues within their visual encoders: statistical bias (overemphasis on frequent patterns), inherent bias (generating dominant objects regardless of input), and vulnerability (sensitivity to minor input perturbations).
> - Targeting these visual encoder-specific biases and vulnerabilities at the feature processing level can effectively reduce hallucinations without requiring modifications to the core model parameters.
> - Interventions like re-weighting visual tokens based on textual context and subtracting bias-induced features, combined with contrastive decoding, improve factual consistency and overall visual understanding in LVLMs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) have demonstrated significant advancements in integrating visual and textual information for diverse cross-modal tasks. However, a persistent challenge in these models is object hallucination, where they generate plausible yet factually inaccurate descriptions of objects within an image. This issue can manifest as misidentified attributes (e.g., color, quantity, position) or the assertion of non-existent objects, posing substantial reliability and safety concerns in critical applications such as healthcare, autonomous systems, and robotics.

Prior research on mitigating object hallucinations has generally fallen into two categories. Earlier methods, designed for smaller-scale Vision-Language Models (VLMs), focused on fine-grained modality alignment or data augmentation to reduce co-occurrence biases. However, these approaches often do not generalize effectively to the larger, more complex LVLMs. More recent efforts have been broadly classified as either training-required or training-free. Training-required methods, including preference optimization, post-hoc revisers, and reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF), typically involve modifying model parameters or training auxiliary modules. While effective, these methods demand considerable human and computational resources. Conversely, training-free methods, such as contrasting outputs from distorted inputs or applying over-trust penalties during decoding, offer more efficient solutions by improving decoding strategies without model re-training.

A notable gap in the existing literature, which this paper addresses, is the underexplored role of the visual encoder component in contributing to LVLM hallucinations. Most prior training-free and training-required approaches have primarily concentrated on the Large Language Model (LLM) component of LVLMs. This work positions itself as an initial investigation to systematically trace LVLM hallucinations to the visual encoders, thereby filling this identified research gap. By identifying specific issues within these encoders—statistical bias, inherent bias, and vulnerability—the paper proposes a novel training-free framework, SHIELD, that directly targets these encoder-centric problems, offering a distinct contribution to the field of multimodal hallucination mitigation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to investigate and mitigate object hallucinations in Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) by addressing issues within their visual encoder components. The motivation stems from the recognition that despite the advanced capabilities of LVLMs in various cross-modal tasks, object hallucination remains a significant problem. These hallucinations, characterized by the generation of plausible but factually incorrect object descriptions or the assertion of non-existent objects, undermine the reliability and trustworthiness of LVLMs. This unreliability is particularly critical in high-stakes domains like healthcare, autonomous driving, and robotics, where erroneous outputs can have severe consequences.

The authors motivate their approach by identifying three specific, previously underexplored issues within visual encoders that contribute to object hallucinations:
1. **Statistical Bias**: Visual encoders, often pre-trained on large-scale datasets (e.g., CLIP), are influenced by the imbalanced distribution of visual concepts. This leads to an over-reliance on frequent visual patterns, causing certain visual tokens to be overemphasized (exhibiting disproportionately high L2 activation values). This overemphasis can distort the downstream LLM's perception of fine-grained details, leading to hallucinations. Experimental analysis demonstrated a correlation between higher token overemphasis (measured by peak-to-average L2 norm ratio) and increased hallucination rates in LLaVA-1.5.
2. **Inherent Bias**: Beyond statistical overemphasis, the visual encoder exhibits an inherent tendency to generate erroneous representations of dominant objects found in its pretraining data, even when presented with meaningless inputs (e.g., random noise). This suggests a strong inductive bias that leads to falsely perceiving the presence of frequently encountered objects. Analysis showed that dominant objects like "car," "chair," and "table" were frequently hallucinated by LLaVA-1.5 when given noise images.
3. **Vulnerability**: Visual encoders demonstrate limited robustness to subtle perturbations and noise. This vulnerability means that minor alterations to the input image can lead to the construction of inaccurate visual representations, which subsequently contribute to hallucinations. The paper illustrates this with a significant drop in LLaVA-1.5's performance (F1 score) on a POPE COCO subset after only a few adversarial attack steps.

The key objective is to develop a training-free framework, SHIELD, that directly counters these three identified issues within the visual encoder. By addressing these root causes, the researchers aim to effectively suppress object hallucinations, thereby enhancing the factual consistency and reliability of LVLM outputs without the need for extensive retraining.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, SHIELD, is a training-free method designed to mitigate object hallucinations by addressing statistical bias, inherent bias, and vulnerability within LVLM visual encoders. The approach integrates three distinct strategies: token re-weighting, token subtraction, and contrastive decoding.

The inference process of an LVLM is conceptualized in three stages:
1. **Visual Encoding**: The visual encoder `E(·)` extracts `N` visual tokens `x_v = {x_0..., x_{N-1}}` from an input image `v`.
2. **Logit Computation**: Given `x_v` and a query text `t`, the LLM computes output logits `logit(y_i | x_v, t, y_<i)` for a token `y_i` conditioned on preceding tokens `y_<i`.
3. **Token Selection**: Logits are converted to a probability distribution `p(y_i | x_v, t, y_<i)` via softmax, and the next token is selected based on a decoding strategy.

SHIELD modifies these stages as follows:

#### 3.1. Mitigating Statistical Bias (Token Re-weighting)

To address the visual encoder's overemphasis on tokens corresponding to frequent visual patterns, token re-weighting is applied. This aims to redistribute attention towards tokens more relevant to ground-truth objects.
1. **Naive Caption Generation**: A vanilla LVLM generates a descriptive "naive caption" `c_naive` for the input image `v` using a general prompt like "Please describe this image."
2. **Text Encoding**: A CLIP text encoder `E_t(·)`, which is pre-trained to align with the visual encoder, encodes `c_naive` into `P` text tokens `c = {c_0..., c_{P-1}}`.
3. **Similarity Matrix Computation**: A similarity matrix `M` of size `N × P` is computed between the visual tokens `x_v` and the text tokens `c`. This matrix captures the cross-modal relevance.
4. **Weight Derivation**: For each visual token, a weight `W_v` is obtained by taking the maximum similarity across all caption tokens (`max_j M_{i,j}`) and normalizing these values to the range.
5. **Residual Re-weighting**: The derived weights `W_v` are applied to the original visual tokens `x_v` via residual addition: `x_v' = x_v + x_v ⊙ W_v`. This process emphasizes visual tokens that are strongly aligned with the objects mentioned in the naive caption, without being significantly affected by potential hallucinations in the caption itself, as hallucinated objects typically lack strong visual grounding.

#### 3.2. Reducing Inherent Bias (Token Subtraction)

To counter the visual encoder's tendency to produce erroneous representations of dominant objects regardless of input, token subtraction is employed.
1. **Erroneous Representation Estimation**: `K` random noise inputs `n_i` (of the same size as the image) are passed through the visual encoder `E(·)`. The resulting visual tokens from these noise inputs are averaged: `(1/K) Σ E(n_i)`. This average serves as an estimate of the "erroneous representations"—the baseline activations the encoder produces for dominant objects even without true visual cues. This estimation can be pre-calculated for each model to improve efficiency.
2. **Feature Subtraction**: This estimated erroneous representation is then subtracted from the statistical-bias-corrected tokens `x_v'`: `x_v'' = x_v' - (1/K) Σ E(n_i)`. This yields bias-reduced tokens `x_v''`, which are subsequently fed into the LLM to produce bias-reduced logits.

#### 3.3. Addressing Vulnerability (Contrastive Decoding)

To enhance the visual encoder's robustness to perturbations and suppress hallucinations induced by subtle noise, a two-step strategy involving adversarial attacks and contrastive decoding is utilized.
1. **Adversarial Attack**:
 - A learnable perturbation `δ` is added to the input image `v`.
 - This `δ` is optimized by minimizing an adversarial loss `l_adv`, defined as the cosine similarity between the global representation of the perturbed image `E(v + δ)` and the global representation of the naive caption `E_t(c_naive)`. The goal is to make the perturbed image's visual features less aligned with the true content, thus exposing vulnerabilities.
 - The optimized `δ*` is then applied to `v` to create a perturbed image `v + δ*`, which is passed through the visual encoder to produce "inaccurate visual representations" `x_v`. These representations are used to generate adversarial logits by the LLM.
2. **Contrastive Decoding**:
 - During autoregressive decoding, SHIELD contrasts the bias-reduced logits (derived from `x_v''`) with the adversarial logits (derived from `x_v`).
 - The final output probability distribution `p_shield(y_i)` for the next token `y_i` is adjusted using the formula: `p_shield(y_i) = softmax((1 + α) logit(y_i | x_v'', t, y_<i) - α logit(y_i | x_v, t, y_<i))`, where `α` controls the strength of the contrastive decoding.
 - An adaptive plausibility constraint is also applied, retaining only tokens whose probabilities are at least a fraction `β` of the maximum probability, effectively filtering out low-probability, potentially implausible outputs.

**Implementation Details**: Experiments were conducted using LLaVA-1.5, InstructBLIP, and Qwen-VL, compared against vanilla models, VCD, and OPERA. Hyperparameters were set to α=2, β=0.35, K=32, and learning rate `l`=0.02, unless specified otherwise. All experiments were performed on a single RTX A6000 GPU.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted to evaluate SHIELD's effectiveness demonstrated consistent improvements across various benchmarks and LVLM architectures.

#### 4.1. Object Hallucination Evaluation

- **CHAIR Evaluation**: SHIELD significantly reduced object hallucinations in image captioning tasks. On the COCO 2014 validation set, SHIELD consistently outperformed state-of-the-art training-free methods (VCD, OPERA) across LLaVA-1.5, InstructBLIP, and Qwen-VL. For instance, on LLaVA-1.5, SHIELD achieved a C_S (sentence-level hallucination) score of 36.6 and C_I (instance-level hallucination) score of 10.3, representing an improvement of up to 18% over the second-best method.
- **GPT-4o Assisted Evaluation**: Using GPT-4o to assess responses on the LLaVA-Bench dataset for correctness and detailedness, SHIELD achieved notable gains in correctness across all tested LVLMs (LLaVA-1.5, InstructBLIP, Qwen-VL). For example, on LLaVA-1.5, SHIELD's correctness score improved from 4.9 (Vanilla) to 6.2, confirming its efficacy in mitigating hallucinations, though detailedness improvements were modest.
- **POPE Evaluation**: SHIELD exhibited superior performance on the POPE benchmark for existence-level hallucinations (VQA format). Across random, popular, and adversarial splits of the COCO subset, SHIELD generally outperformed VCD and OPERA. Its performance on the challenging "Adversarial" split, where objects are semantically related to those in the image, was particularly strong, indicating that encoder biases are significant contributors to hallucinations in complex scenarios. For InstructBLIP, improvements were observed but were comparatively limited, which the authors attributed to constraints imposed by its Q-Former module. Additional evaluations on A-OKVQA and GQA datasets also confirmed SHIELD's effectiveness, particularly in adversarial settings.
- **MME Hallucination Subset Evaluation**: For a comprehensive assessment of object-level (existence, count) and attribute-level (position, color) hallucinations, SHIELD showed consistent improvements across all categories and models, leading to higher total scores. This suggests that correcting statistical bias in visual encoders effectively enhances fine-grained perception and mitigates attribute-level errors.
- **AMBER Hallucination Evaluation**: On the AMBER benchmark, designed for attribute and relation-level hallucinations across generative and discriminative tasks, SHIELD achieved the highest AMBER Score (88.0) and outperformed baselines on most metrics using LLaVA-1.5 7B, further demonstrating its broad mitigation capabilities.

#### 4.2. LVLM General Evaluation

- **MME Full Set Evaluation**: Beyond hallucination mitigation, SHIELD demonstrated benefits in general perception tasks on the full MME benchmark (using LLaVA-1.5 7B). It improved scores in perception-related subtasks such as OCR and Posters, contributing to an overall enhanced model capability. However, improvements were limited for tasks requiring external knowledge (e.g., Celebrity, Scene, Landmark, Artwork) or simpler visual scenes, as contrastive decoding might prioritize visual input over latent knowledge in the LLM.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Module Ablation on CHAIR**: Each component of SHIELD (mitigating statistical bias, reducing inherent bias, addressing vulnerability) individually contributed to hallucination reduction. The full combination yielded the most significant improvement. The adaptive plausibility constraint alone, while integral to contrastive decoding, showed limited independent impact. Notably, addressing statistical bias provided a substantial reduction in hallucinations, particularly for longer descriptions, after vulnerability was already addressed.
- **Hyper-parameter Ablations**: Studies on α (contrastive decoding strength), β (plausibility constraint threshold), K (number of noise inputs for inherent bias), and `l` (learning rate for adversarial attack) indicated optimal ranges for these parameters, demonstrating their impact on performance.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Results and Visualizations

- Visualizations confirmed SHIELD's mechanisms: token re-weighting highlighted more object-relevant tokens; token subtraction effectively reduced hallucinations of dominant objects, in some cases eliminating them entirely for certain categories; and contrastive decoding improved robustness against perturbations, reducing performance degradation.
- Case studies on LLaVA-Bench provided concrete examples where SHIELD corrected hallucinations such as misidentified objects (e.g., "fried fish" corrected to "fried chicken") and incorrect attributes (e.g., "blue ironing board" where the shirt was blue).

#### 4.5. Efficiency Analysis

- SHIELD achieved the lowest hallucination ratio on CHAIR while maintaining acceptable inference overhead compared to other training-free methods. It was notably faster than OPERA and presented a more favorable trade-off between hallucination reduction and computational cost than VCD.
- Module-wise analysis indicated that most overhead stemmed from the "Address Vulnerability" module (due to adversarial tensor computation) and the "Mitigate Statistical Bias" module (due to naive caption generation). The "Reduce Inherent Bias" module introduced minimal overhead. The overall cost can be adjusted by tuning parameters like caption length or adversarial optimization steps.

#### 4.6. Extended Comparisons

- SHIELD was found to outperform other training-free baselines and remain competitive with training-based methods on the CHAIR benchmark, showcasing its strong performance without the need for VLM fine-tuning.
- Further evaluation on recent Qwen-VL variants (Qwen2-VL, Qwen2.5-VL, Qwen3-VL) demonstrated SHIELD's consistent effectiveness and generalization across more capable and recently released LVLMs.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work presents a significant contribution to the field of Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) by shifting the focus of hallucination mitigation from solely the LLM component to the visual encoder. The paper's primary significance lies in its novel identification and systematic addressal of three distinct issues within visual encoders—statistical bias, inherent bias, and vulnerability—as fundamental causes of object hallucinations. This provides a new understanding of the origins of these errors and opens new avenues for research.

The potential impact of SHIELD is multi-faceted:
- **Enhanced Reliability and Safety**: By demonstrably reducing object hallucinations across various benchmarks and LVLM architectures, SHIELD contributes to making LVLMs more factually consistent and reliable. This has direct implications for safety-critical applications in domains such as healthcare (reducing misdiagnosis risks), autonomous systems (improving object recognition accuracy), and robotics (ensuring correct environmental understanding).
- **Improved LVLM Performance**: The framework not only mitigates hallucinations but also shows improvements in general perception tasks on benchmarks like MME. This suggests that by addressing fundamental biases and vulnerabilities in the visual encoder, SHIELD enhances the foundational visual understanding capabilities of LVLMs, leading to a more robust and accurate overall performance.
- **Efficiency and Accessibility**: As a training-free method, SHIELD offers a computationally efficient alternative to resource-intensive training-required approaches. This makes hallucination mitigation more accessible for practitioners and researchers, as it does not require extensive fine-tuning or specialized datasets, allowing for easier integration into existing LVLM workflows.
- **Guidance for Future Research**: The paper's findings highlight the critical role of visual encoders in LVLM performance and hallucination generation. This can inspire future research efforts aimed at designing more robust visual encoders from the outset, developing more sophisticated pre-training strategies that account for distribution biases, and optimizing adversarial attack mechanisms for real-time applications to further reduce computational overhead.
- **Broader Applicability**: SHIELD's consistent effectiveness across diverse LVLM families (LLaVA, InstructBLIP, Qwen-VL) and recent variants underscores its broad applicability and generalizability, suggesting it could be a valuable tool for enhancing a wide range of multimodal models.

In conclusion, SHIELD provides a systematic and effective approach to tackling a critical problem in LVLMs, offering both theoretical insights into the root causes of hallucinations and a practical, efficient solution that can lead to more trustworthy and capable multimodal AI systems.
