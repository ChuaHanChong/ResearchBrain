# PhysWorld: From Real Videos to World Models of Deformable Objects via Physics-Aware Demonstration Synthesis

> [!summary] Summary
> PhysWorld presents a framework for constructing accurate and fast world models of deformable objects from limited real-world video observations by synthesizing physically plausible and diverse training data using a high-fidelity Material Point Method simulator. This approach achieves real-time inference at 799 FPS, significantly outperforming prior state-of-the-art methods in prediction accuracy and generalization to novel interactions.

## Problem

- Acquiring sufficient real-world interaction data with ground-truth physical properties for deformable objects is extremely difficult and costly for learning-based models.
- Existing dynamics modeling methods face a trade-off: physics-based simulators offer high fidelity but are computationally expensive, while learning-based models are fast but often lack physical consistency and generalization, especially for spatially-varying properties.
- Models struggle to generalize robustly to novel interactions and accurately capture heterogeneous physical properties within deformable objects.

## Method

- A three-stage framework that first builds a physics-consistent digital twin from short real-world videos, employing VLM-assisted constitutive model selection and a global-to-local physical property optimization.
- Synthesizes a vast and diverse set of augmented interaction demonstrations using Various Motion Pattern Generation (VMP-Gen) for diverse motions and Part-aware Physical Property Perturbation (P³-Pert) for realistic property variations.
- Trains an efficient Graph Neural Network (GNN) on the synthetic data, which is then fine-tuned with real-world video data to reduce the sim-to-real gap and enable action-conditioned video prediction.

## Results

- Achieved an inference speed of 799 FPS, which is 47 times faster than PhysTwin (17 FPS), while maintaining superior prediction accuracy with a Chamfer Distance of 0.010.
- Demonstrated superior generalization to unseen interaction scenarios, generating physically plausible predictions without artifacts and outperforming baselines in aesthetic quality, motion smoothness, and subject consistency.
- Enabled model-based planning in robotic manipulation, successfully guiding deformable objects to target configurations using Model-Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) control.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - High-fidelity physics simulators can effectively act as 'data factories' to generate diverse and physically plausible training data, thereby overcoming the critical challenge of real-world data scarcity for learning-based models.
> - A global-to-local optimization strategy for physical properties is crucial for robustly constructing digital twins that accurately capture both homogeneous and heterogeneous material characteristics of deformable objects.
> - Generating varied motion patterns and part-aware physical property perturbations in synthetic data significantly enhances the robustness and generalization capabilities of learned dynamics models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of "world models" capable of predicting object dynamics is a critical challenge and a rapidly evolving area in artificial intelligence, fundamental for applications in robotics, virtual reality (VR), and augmented reality (AR). This work addresses a specific, yet crucial, sub-problem within this landscape: learning physics-consistent dynamics models for **deformable objects**, especially when faced with **limited real-world video data** and the need to model **spatially-varying physical properties**.

Current approaches to dynamics modeling generally fall into two categories:

- **Physics-based simulation methods:** These leverage established simulators like the Material Point Method (MPM) or Mass-Spring Systems (MSS). They benefit from inherent physical priors, leading to high realism and accuracy. Recent advancements, such as PhysTwin (Jiang et al., 2025), have improved their ability to construct "digital twins" from sparse videos. However, their primary limitation is computational cost, which often prohibits real-time inference, making them unsuitable for time-sensitive applications like robotic planning. Furthermore, a persistent "domain gap" exists between simplified simulator dynamics and complex real-world phenomena.

- **Learning-based methods:** These employ neural networks, often Graph Neural Networks (GNNs), to learn dynamics directly from data. They are efficient and can achieve real-time inference. However, they are notoriously data-hungry, requiring vast amounts of training data, which is labor-intensive and expensive to acquire for real-world deformable objects. While simulated data can augment this, it often lacks physical consistency with real-world observations. Some approaches, like AdaptiGraph (Zhang et al., 2024a), condition GNNs on physical properties, but their data synthesis may be physically inconsistent or limited to global parameters, restricting applicability to objects with heterogeneous properties.

PhysWorld occupies a crucial intersection between these two paradigms. It aims to bridge the gap by leveraging the strengths of high-fidelity physics simulators (for physical consistency and diverse data generation) to train lightweight, efficient learning-based models (for real-time inference). The core innovation lies in its strategy to **synthesize physically plausible and diverse demonstrations** from limited real-world data, thereby overcoming the data scarcity challenge for learning-based models without sacrificing physical accuracy or real-time performance. This positions PhysWorld as a significant advancement towards building practical and robust world models for deformable objects in real-world scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of PhysWorld is to construct **accurate and fast world models for deformable objects** from **short real-world video observations**. The authors are motivated by the inherent challenges in current dynamics modeling approaches:

- **Data Scarcity for Deformable Objects:** Acquiring sufficient real-world interaction data for deformable objects, especially with ground-truth physical properties, is extremely difficult and costly. Learning-based models heavily rely on such data.
- **Trade-off between Accuracy and Efficiency:** Physics-based simulators offer high fidelity but are computationally expensive, hindering real-time applications. Learning-based models are fast but often lack the physical consistency and generalization capabilities of simulators, particularly when data is limited.
- **Modeling Spatially-Varying Physical Properties:** Real-world deformable objects often have heterogeneous physical properties (e.g., varying stiffness or density across different parts). Current learning-based methods often struggle to capture these localized variations effectively.
- **Generalization to Novel Interactions:** A robust world model should be able to predict the behavior of objects under unseen or novel interactions, which is difficult for models trained on limited, specific trajectories.

To address these challenges, PhysWorld aims to:
1. **Leverage high-fidelity simulators:** Use a Material Point Method (MPM) simulator as a "data factory" to generate extensive and diverse training data.
2. **Ensure physical plausibility and diversity:** Develop methods (VMP-Gen and P³-Pert) to guarantee that the synthesized data is both physically consistent with real-world observations and rich enough to train robust models.
3. **Train lightweight models:** Utilize GNNs to achieve real-time inference speeds.
4. **Refine with real data:** Incorporate real-world video data for fine-tuning to further mitigate the sim-to-real gap and enhance alignment.
5. **Enable downstream applications:** Produce world models capable of supporting applications like model-based planning and robotic manipulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PhysWorld operates through a novel three-stage framework:

#### 3.1. Physics-Consistent Digital Twin Construction

This stage aims to create a high-fidelity digital representation of the deformable object, grounded in physics, from short real-world interaction videos.
- **Initial Digital Twin Construction:** After extracting the object's point cloud from video (e.g., using methods like Jiang et al., 2025), a volumetric representation is created via tetrahedral mesh refinement. Robotic manipulation primitives (Grasping, Pushing) are implemented within the MPM framework to re-simulate observed interactions. A crucial step is the **VLM-assisted Constitutive Model Selection**: a Vision-Language Model (VLM) like Qwen3 analyzes the deformation behaviors in the video to automatically select the most suitable material constitutive models (e.g., Neo-Hookean, Anisotropic Hyperelastic) from a predefined physics library, ensuring the simulation adheres to the correct continuum mechanics principles.
- **Physical Property Optimization:** To align the digital twin with real-world observations, the physical properties (e.g., friction, density, Young’s modulus) of the MPM particles are optimized. This is done by driving the differentiable MPM simulator with the extracted hand motion trajectory and minimizing the Chamfer Distance and L1 loss between simulated and ground-truth point cloud movements. A **global-to-local optimization strategy** is employed: first, homogeneous properties are optimized globally across all particles, providing a robust initialization. Then, per-particle heterogeneous properties are refined locally, allowing for spatially-varying material characteristics.

#### 3.2. Augmented Interaction Demonstration Synthesis

Once the physics-consistent digital twin is established, this stage focuses on generating a vast and diverse set of synthetic interaction demonstrations, crucial for training a robust GNN.
- **Various Motion Pattern Generation (VMP-Gen):** To overcome the limitation of a single real-world trajectory, the method synthesizes diverse control point trajectories for interaction. This is achieved using curvature-constrained B´ezier curves, whose temporal evolution is governed by normalized arc-length parameterization and a three-phase (acceleration, uniform, deceleration) velocity profile with C1 smooth transitions. This ensures physically consistent and varied motion patterns.
- **Part-aware Physical Property Perturbation (P³-Pert):** To address potential inaccuracies in properties optimized from limited real videos and to inject controlled diversity, semantic-partition-guided stochastic perturbations are applied to the digital twin's physical properties. Part-semantic features (e.g., from PartField) are used to compute feature similarity between particles. A covariance matrix is constructed based on these similarities (approximated via Nyström for efficiency) to sample stochastic perturbations. These perturbations are applied to properties like Young's modulus and density, ensuring that changes are "part-aware" and maintain physical continuity, leading to diverse but plausible material property distributions. The output of this stage is a collection of 4D demonstrations {Xt, Φ, at} comprising object point clouds, per-particle physical properties, and control point velocities.

#### 3.3. GNN-based World Models

This stage trains a lightweight and efficient GNN-based world model using the synthetic demonstrations.
- **Model Architecture:** The GNN predicts future object states X(t+1) given historical states X(t-h:t), action a(t), and time-invariant physical properties Φ. It uses farthest point sampling (FPS) to select a manageable number of control particles and their corresponding physical properties. Control points are added as graph vertices, and bidirectional edges are established. The architecture comprises vertex/edge encoders (processing type indicators, velocities, physical properties, and historical distances), a multi-step message propagator (MLP blocks), and a motion prediction decoder. The design ensures translation equivariance.
- **Model Training:** The GNN is trained using the synthesized demonstrations, minimizing the mean squared error (MSE) between predicted and ground-truth particle positions over a multi-step prediction window. To enhance robustness and reduce error accumulation during long rollouts, noise is injected into historical vertex positions and the GNN's physical properties Φ during training.
- **Physical Property Fine-Tuning:** While the GNN is trained on synthetic data, its physical properties (Φ) are further fine-tuned using the original real-world video data. This step, performed while freezing GNN network parameters, leverages the GNN's stability against gradient issues (unlike MPM) to precisely align the learned physical properties with real objects, thus narrowing the sim-to-real domain gap. Linear Blend Skinning (LBS) is used to interpolate positions for target track points from GNN vertex outputs, and a composite loss combining Chamfer distance and MSE is used for optimization.
- **Action-Conditioned Video Prediction:** For visual output, the object's appearance is modeled using 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) at t=0. For subsequent timesteps, 3DGS kernels are updated via LBS using the GNN's predicted vertex motion field, enabling action-conditioned video synthesis.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

PhysWorld demonstrates significant advancements, outperforming existing state-of-the-art methods across several key metrics:

- **Action-Conditioned Future Prediction:**
 - **Accuracy:** PhysWorld (Our GNN w/ Finetuning) achieves the lowest Chamfer Distance (CD=0.010) and tracking error (Track=0.021), competitive with "Our MPM" (CD=0.011, Track=0.021) which represents an upper bound for physics-based accuracy. It significantly outperforms PhysTwin (CD=0.012, Track=0.022), Spring-Gaus (CD=0.062), and GS-Dynamics (CD=0.041).
 - **Visual Quality (2D Metrics):** PhysWorld achieves competitive image reconstruction quality with high IoU (73.3), PSNR (25.940), SSIM (0.941), and low LPIPS (0.055), again demonstrating superiority over PhysTwin across most metrics.
 - **Inference Speed:** A critical finding is PhysWorld's remarkable efficiency. It achieves an inference speed of 799 FPS, which is **47 times faster than PhysTwin (17 FPS)** and approximately 266 times faster than "Our MPM" (3 FPS). This highlights its practical applicability for real-time systems.

- **Generalization to Unseen Interactions:** PhysWorld demonstrates superior generalization capabilities to novel and unseen interaction scenarios (e.g., lifting a pushed rope, rotating a lifted sloth). Visual results (Fig. 3) show it generates physically plausible predictions without artifacts like fracture-like distortions or unnatural deformations, which affect PhysTwin. Quantitative metrics (Table 2) further support this, with PhysWorld achieving higher aesthetic quality, motion smoothness, and subject consistency scores.

- **Model-Based Planning:** The real-time inference capability of PhysWorld enables its integration into model-based planning frameworks, such as Model-Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) control. Case studies (Fig. 4) demonstrate its ability to successfully generate control trajectories to guide deformable objects (e.g., rope, zebra doll) from initial to target configurations, showcasing its potential for robotic manipulation.

- **Ablation Studies:**
 - **Effect of Global-to-Local Optimization:** The global-to-local strategy for physical property optimization in MPM significantly outperforms global-only and local-only optimizations (Table 3), demonstrating its importance for stability and accuracy.
 - **Effect of VMP-Gen:** The Various Motion Pattern Generation (VMP-Gen) module substantially improves GNN prediction accuracy and robustness (Table 4) by increasing motion diversity beyond simple linear patterns.
 - **Effect of P³-Pert:** The Part-aware Physical Property Perturbation (P³-Pert) module is crucial for generating physically realistic and diverse property variations, leading to better overall performance compared to random or uniform perturbation strategies (Table 5).
 - **Training GNN Directly on Real Data:** Directly training a GNN on limited real-world data leads to severe overfitting and significantly compromised performance (Table 6), reinforcing the critical role of PhysWorld's synthetic data generation strategy.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

PhysWorld represents a significant step forward in the field of deformable object dynamics modeling, with far-reaching potential impacts:

- **Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap and Data Scarcity:** The most impactful contribution is the effective strategy to overcome the perennial challenges of data scarcity in the real world and the sim-to-real gap. By intelligently leveraging high-fidelity physics simulators to generate physically plausible and diverse training data, PhysWorld allows lightweight learning models to achieve high accuracy without extensive real-world data collection, making the development of world models more scalable and practical.
- **Enabling Real-time Robotic Manipulation:** The achievement of real-time inference speeds (47x faster than PhysTwin) while maintaining high accuracy is a game-changer for robotic manipulation of deformable objects. Tasks like cloth folding, cable routing, or manipulating soft tissues in surgical contexts, which require continuous, fast, and accurate predictions of object deformation, can now be addressed more effectively. The demonstrated model-based planning capabilities are a direct testament to this impact.
- **Advancing Interactive VR/AR Experiences:** In VR and AR, realistic and interactive deformable objects are crucial for immersive experiences. PhysWorld's ability to create accurate, real-time, and generalizable world models will allow for more compelling and believable virtual environments where users can interact naturally with soft objects.
- **Robustness to Novel Interactions:** The enhanced generalization to unseen interactions is vital for deploying intelligent agents in unstructured and dynamic environments. This improves the robustness and adaptability of systems that rely on dynamics predictions.
- **Hybrid Physics-Learning Paradigms:** PhysWorld exemplifies a powerful hybrid approach that combines the strengths of classical physics-based simulation with modern data-driven learning. This paradigm, where simulators generate intelligent training data for neural networks, is likely to inspire further research in other complex physical systems.
- **Accessible Digital Twin Creation:** The VLM-assisted constitutive model selection simplifies the process of creating digital twins, reducing the need for manual expertise in material science and physics. This makes digital twin creation more accessible and scalable.

In conclusion, PhysWorld delivers a powerful framework for constructing world models of deformable objects that are both highly accurate and computationally efficient. Its novel approach to physics-aware demonstration synthesis addresses long-standing challenges in the field, paving the way for more sophisticated and practical applications in robotics, virtual reality, and beyond.
