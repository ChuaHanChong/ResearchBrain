# GraspMolmo: Generalizable Task-Oriented Grasping via Large-Scale Synthetic Data Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> GraspMolmo introduces a generalizable open-vocabulary task-oriented grasping model that predicts semantically appropriate and stable grasps from a single RGB-D frame and natural language instruction. The model, fine-tuned on a new large-scale synthetic dataset called PRISM, achieves a 70.4% prediction success and 61.1% overall success rate in real-world robotic manipulation tasks, outperforming prior state-of-the-art methods.

## Problem

- Most robotic grasping systems prioritize grasp stability for an object but remain task-agnostic, leading to inappropriate or unsafe grasps for specific manipulation tasks.
- Existing datasets for Task-Oriented Grasping (TOG) are limited in scale, object diversity, scene complexity, and rely on templated language, preventing generalization to real-world scenarios.
- Previous TOG models often required restrictive inputs like pre-segmented point clouds or multi-view observations, which are impractical for real-time robotic deployment.

## Method

- Fine-tuned Molmo, a state-of-the-art vision-language model, to predict semantically appropriate and stable grasp points conditioned on natural language instructions and a single RGB-D frame.
- Introduced PRISM, a large-scale synthetic dataset (379,000 samples) featuring complex scenes, diverse objects, and natural language task descriptions, generated using LLMs and human verification.
- Implemented a point-to-grasp matching mechanism that uses GraspMolmo's predicted 2D pixel points to select optimal 6-DoF grasps from a stable grasp proposal network.

## Results

- Achieved a 76.7% Top-1 accuracy on the TaskGrasp-Image benchmark, outperforming Molmo (75.6%) and GraspGPT (72.3%).
- Demonstrated superior generalization on the challenging PRISM-Test dataset, achieving a 62.5% success rate compared to Molmo's 49.8% and GraspGPT's 40.0%.
- Achieved a 70.4% prediction success rate and 61.1% overall success rate in real-world robotic manipulation tasks, significantly more than double the closest baseline.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large-scale synthetic data, like PRISM, designed with complex scenes and diverse task-oriented language, enables effective training of vision-language models for robust sim-to-real transfer in task-oriented grasping.
> - A decomposition strategy leveraging LLMs and human validation for generating grasp descriptions and matching them to tasks significantly reduces the annotation effort for large-scale TOG datasets.
> - Co-training a vision-language model on a mixture of synthetic and real-world image datasets is crucial for maintaining generalization and improving real-world transfer performance in robotic grasping.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work on GraspMolmo addresses limitations in the field of robotic grasping, specifically focusing on Task-Oriented Grasping (TOG). Traditional robotic grasping methods often prioritize grasp stability for a given object, operating in an object-centric and task-agnostic manner. This approach can be insufficient in real-world scenarios where the appropriate grasp for an object depends on the intended manipulation task. For instance, grasping a knife blade might be stable for handing, but unsuitable for chopping vegetables. The need for TOG, where grasp selection is informed by the task, has been identified in prior research.

Previous attempts at TOG have been constrained by several factors. Existing datasets, such as TaskGrasp, often feature simplistic scenes, a limited diversity of objects, and templated language instructions (e.g., "grasp the mug to pour"). These limitations hinder models from generalizing to the complexity and variability of real-world environments and natural language commands like "mince some garlic" or "do the dishes." Furthermore, many prior TOG models impose restrictive input requirements, such as pre-segmented point clouds or multi-view observations, which can complicate real-time deployment. Specific prior works, including GraspGPT and FoundationGrasp, have relied on such limited datasets. Other methods like LERF-TOGO incurred high computational costs, while CROG and GraspCLIP were restricted to 4-Degrees of Freedom (DoF) grasps and specific camera views.

This research integrates with the broader trend of leveraging large foundation vision-language models (VLMs) in robotics. VLMs have demonstrated potential for improving generalization across various robotic tasks, including action prediction, physics understanding, navigation, and spatial reasoning. GraspMolmo aligns with approaches that fine-tune these powerful VLMs for specific robotic capabilities, differentiating itself from direct VLM applications or vision-language-action (VLA) models that predict low-level robot actions. By directly outputting grasps, GraspMolmo aims to avoid embodiment-specific constraints and redundant learning of free-space motion. While other VLM-based works predict points for navigation or high-level plans, GraspMolmo specifically grounds its point predictions in object affordances tailored for grasping.

The study also contributes to the understanding and application of object affordances in robotics, which describe how objects can be manipulated. While affordance prediction has influenced 6-DoF grasping and stable object placement, many existing VLM-based approaches utilize pre-trained VLMs for visual grounding without incorporating deeper semantic reasoning for accurate point detection. GraspMolmo's approach involves fine-tuning Molmo, a VLM designed for semantic understanding, to generate task-aware and stable grasp points through contextualized spatial reasoning.

Finally, the work positions itself within the context of synthetic data generation for robotics. Simulation is recognized as a scalable and promising source for generating large-scale data that can facilitate generalization to the real world. GraspMolmo's use of a novel, large-scale synthetic dataset for complex task-dependent object manipulation aims to overcome the data scarcity and limitations of real-world data collection in TOG.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop GraspMolmo, a generalizable open-vocabulary task-oriented grasping (TOG) model. This model is designed to predict semantically appropriate and stable grasps, conditioned on a natural language instruction and a single RGB-D frame. The authors explicitly outline several detailed objectives and motivations for this work.

#### 2.1. Key Objectives

1. **Generalizable Open-Vocabulary Task-Oriented Grasping:** The central objective is to create a model that can understand and execute grasping actions for a wide range of objects and tasks described in natural language, including those not encountered during training. This moves beyond object-centric or task-agnostic grasping.
2. **Robust Grasp Prediction:** The model should predict grasps that are not only stable but also semantically appropriate for the given task. For example, it should differentiate between grasping a teapot handle for pouring versus its body for lifting.
3. **Single-Frame Input:** GraspMolmo is designed to operate from a single RGB-D frame, aiming for efficiency and practicality in real-time robotic deployment, unlike systems requiring multi-view observations or pre-segmented point clouds.
4. **Leverage Synthetic Data for Scalability and Diversity:** A core objective is to overcome the limitations of small, simplistic, and manually annotated datasets by developing a novel, large-scale synthetic dataset (PRISM) that features complex environments and diverse, realistic task descriptions. This dataset is intended to enable training of models that generalize effectively.
5. **Fine-tune Vision-Language Models:** The research aims to fine-tune an existing state-of-the-art vision-language model (Molmo) on the new synthetic data to imbue it with task-semantic grasping capabilities, thus enabling generalization to novel instructions and objects.
6. **Demonstrate Real-World Transfer:** A critical objective is to validate the effectiveness of the synthetically trained model through robust evaluation in challenging real-world scenarios, including objects and tasks entirely unseen during training.
7. **Explore Zero-Shot Bimanual Grasping:** The work also seeks to qualitatively demonstrate the model's ability to extend to more complex bimanual grasping tasks in a zero-shot manner, leveraging its task-semantic understanding.

#### 2.2. Motivation

1. **Addressing Task-Agnostic Grasping Limitations:** The fundamental motivation stems from the recognition that most existing robotic grasping systems are task-agnostic, predicting only stable grasps without considering the downstream task. This leads to ineffective or unsafe manipulation in many real-world contexts, as exemplified by grasping a knife blade for chopping.
2. **Overcoming Dataset Deficiencies:** Current TOG datasets are identified as a major bottleneck due to their limited scale, lack of realism, and simplistic task definitions. These deficiencies prevent models from learning the complex reasoning required for diverse real-world tasks and instructions. The paper highlights the inadequacy of templated language (e.g., "grasp the mug to pour") in capturing human-like task specifications.
3. **Reducing Input Constraints:** Many prior TOG models necessitate auxiliary information like pre-segmented point clouds or specific camera views, which can be computationally intensive or impractical for real-time robotic systems. The motivation is to develop a model that can directly process raw sensor data and freeform natural language.
4. **Enabling Generalization:** A key driver is the desire for robotic systems that can generalize to novel objects and tasks without extensive re-training or explicit programming. This requires models to learn underlying task-grasp relationships rather than memorizing specific object-grasp pairings.
5. **Advancing Robotic Manipulation Capabilities:** The overarching motivation is to advance robotic manipulation beyond basic pick-and-place operations towards systems that understand "how an object should be manipulated for specific intended uses," thereby enabling more intelligent and versatile robotic agents in unstructured environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The GraspMolmo methodology is structured around a multi-stage approach involving large-scale synthetic data generation, vision-language model fine-tuning, and a point-to-grasp matching mechanism for inference.

#### 3.1. Overall Method Overview

The process begins with the generation of PRISM (Purpose-driven Robotic Interaction in Scene Manipulation), a large synthetic dataset annotated with task-semantic information. This dataset is then used to fine-tune Molmo, an existing vision-language model (VLM), enabling it to predict grasp points on an image. During inference, these predicted points are matched to candidate grasps provided by a stable grasp proposal network.

#### 3.2. Generation of PRISM-Train and PRISM-Test

PRISM is a novel dataset designed to overcome the limitations of existing TOG datasets.

- **Scene Generation:**
 - Assets from ShapeNet-Sem, with grasps derived from ACRONYM, form the basis of the scenes.
 - SceneSynthesizer procedurally composes 10,000 scenes featuring 2,356 unique object instances across 91 object classes (Table 3).
 - To enhance visual diversity and realism, scenes incorporate variations in lighting, camera randomization, and multiple objects per task.
 - Each scene is rendered from 10 distinct camera viewpoints to capture objects from various perspectives, tracking grasp visibility for data quality.
 - PRISM-Test is created as a synthetic evaluation set, holding out 10% of object instances from each class and 4 entire object classes (TeaCup, Fork, DSLRCamera, PillBottle) to assess generalization to novel objects and scenes.

- **Grasp Descriptions as a Bridge:**
 - To scale task-grasp annotations, the authors introduce a decomposition strategy. Instead of directly annotating every task-object-grasp triple (O(T*O*G)), they annotate object-grasp pairs with natural language descriptions of how the grasp grips the object. Separately, for each task, descriptions are generated for how an object should be grasped to complete that task.
 - Tasks are then matched to object-grasp pairs using text matching, significantly reducing annotation effort to O(T + OG). Common-sense reasoning from Large Language Models (LLMs) aids this process.

- **Grasp Description Generation:**
 - GPT-4o is initially used to generate synthetic grasp descriptions from multiple viewpoints of an object being grasped.
 - Human workers on the Prolific platform verify and correct these LLM-generated descriptions. Approximately 45% of the LLM-generated descriptions were deemed accurate, with the remainder requiring manual editing, emphasizing the role of human validation.

- **Task Generation:**
 - GPT-4.1 is prompted to generate two maximally distinct yet plausible grasp descriptions for each object class.
 - For each of these grasp descriptions, the LLM generates four valid semantic tasks, minimizing compatibility with the alternative grasp.
 - In total, 728 unique tasks are generated, corresponding to two grasps for each of the 91 object classes.

- **Matching Tasks to Grasps:**
 - GPT-4o is again utilized to match the generated task grasp descriptions with the annotated object-grasp descriptions for all visible grasps in a scene.
 - If a match is determined, a task-object-grasp triple is created and added to the dataset.

- **Assembling PRISM-Train and PRISM-Test:**
 - PRISM-Train comprises 9,424 unique object-centric grasp instances across 2,356 unique objects, scaled combinatorially.
 - The final dataset contains 378,844 samples, each including a scene render, a natural language task, a ground-truth semantically appropriate and stable grasp (in calibrated camera coordinates), an object-centric spatial description of that grasp, and a pixel location corresponding to the correct grasp point.

#### 3.3. TaskGrasp-Image Dataset

To address limitations of the existing TaskGrasp dataset (point clouds, simple tasks), the authors construct TaskGrasp-Image. This dataset uses the original RGB-D images from TaskGrasp, preserving ground-truth grasp annotations within the context of unprocessed real imagery. The transformation between the original fused point clouds and RGB-D views is recovered using point cloud registration techniques (GroundingDINO, SAM2, DeepGMR, and ICP). This results in 299 successfully registered views, where annotated grasps are transformed into the camera frame of each viewpoint.

#### 3.4. GraspMolmo Model and Training

- **Base Model:** GraspMolmo is created by fine-tuning Molmo, a state-of-the-art VLM known for pointing and spatial reasoning.
- **Co-training Strategy:** The model is co-trained on PRISM-Train, TaskGrasp-Image (split 0), and other existing Molmo training tasks (PixMo, VQA datasets) to maintain generalization capabilities while adapting to grasping.
- **Chain-of-Thought Processing:** The natural language object-centric grasp description from the training data is used as a chain-of-thought step, requiring the model to output both the pixel location for the grasp and an object-centric description of that grasp.
- **Data Mixture:** The training data mixture allocates 45% to PRISM-Train, 10% to TaskGrasp-Image, and proportionally downweights other data sources (Table 4).
- **Training Details:** The Molmo-7B-D-0924 model is fine-tuned for 10,000 steps with a batch size of 8 per device, utilizing 64 Nvidia H100 GPUs, taking approximately 9 hours.
- **Grasp Point Supervision:** For each grasp in PRISM, a "grasp point" on the object mesh is determined by raycasting from the grasp TCP along the grasp direction onto the object. This 2D pixel projection of this point supervises GraspMolmo's prediction.

#### 3.5. Mapping Output Points to Grasps

- GraspMolmo outputs a 2D pixel point `p` on the image plane.
- A stable grasp generator (M2T2) provides a set of candidate 6-DoF grasps `G`.
- Each candidate grasp `g` from `G` is mapped to a pixel coordinate `f(g)` on the image plane. This is achieved by finding points within the gripper fingers, selecting the one closest to the gripper origin, and projecting it to the image.
- The grasp `g_hat` from the candidates is selected by finding the one whose corresponding pixel point `f(g)` is closest to Molmo’s predicted pixel point `p` (i.e., `arg min_g∈G ||f(g) - p||`). This method is chosen over naive 3D back-projection to mitigate issues with incomplete depth information and pixel-to-3D error propagation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of GraspMolmo and baselines was conducted across three distinct benchmark settings: a literature benchmark, a synthetic held-out dataset, and real-world transfer scenarios. The results indicate GraspMolmo's ability to achieve robust performance and generalize across various levels of complexity.

#### 4.1. Performance on TaskGrasp-Image

On the TaskGrasp-Image benchmark, which uses single-view RGB-D observations and split 0 of the "task" split type from TaskGrasp, GraspMolmo demonstrated a higher prediction accuracy compared to baselines.
- **GraspMolmo:** 76.7% Top-1 accuracy.
- **Molmo (base VLM):** 75.6% Top-1 accuracy.
- **GraspGPT (State-of-the-art alternative):** 72.3% Top-1 accuracy.
- **Random Baseline:** 54.5% Top-1 accuracy.
This indicates an improvement over existing methods even on a benchmark derived from simpler scenes, which has been normalized across tasks for evaluation.

#### 4.2. Performance on PRISM-Test

PRISM-Test, a synthetic evaluation set built with held-out object instances and entirely novel object classes, was designed to test generalization capabilities.
- **GraspMolmo:** 62.5% success rate.
- **Molmo:** 49.8% success rate.
- **GraspGPT:** 40.0% success rate.
- **Random Baseline:** 29.3% success rate.
In these more challenging scenarios involving unseen objects and scenes, GraspMolmo maintained a higher success rate, while the performance of baselines decreased below 50%. This widening performance gap suggests GraspMolmo's enhanced ability to generalize to novel task-grasp relationships rather than relying on memorization of specific object-grasp pairings. The paper notes that PRISM-Test's complex scenes, diverse tasks, and aggressive randomization contribute to its challenging nature.

#### 4.3. Real-World Transfer with PRISM-Real

To evaluate real-world transfer, models were tested on a 7-DoF Franka FR3 arm in three realistic household scenes, featuring a total of 9 distinct household objects, each with 2 associated tasks. A stable grasp generator, M2T2, was used for grasp execution. A total of 54 trials were performed per method.
- **Prediction Success Rate (GraspMolmo):** 70.4% (correctness of predicted grasp).
- **Overall Success Rate (GraspMolmo):** 61.1% (predicted grasp correct AND robot successfully grasped).
- **Prediction Success Rate (Molmo/GraspGPT):** 35.2%.
- **Overall Success Rate (Molmo):** 33.3%.
- **Overall Success Rate (GraspGPT):** 24.1%.
GraspMolmo considerably outperformed baselines, achieving more than double the prediction accuracy and overall success rate of the closest alternatives. A paired t-test confirmed that GraspMolmo's improvements over Molmo and GraspGPT were statistically significant at p < 0.05. The authors attribute GraspMolmo's strong sim-to-real transfer to co-training with real images (comprising 55% of the total training data) from PixMo and TaskGrasp-Image, as well as the adversarial nature of PRISM-Test's randomization.

#### 4.4. Correlation between Synthetic and Real-World Performance

The study observed that performance on PRISM-Test correlated more strongly with real-world performance (R² = 0.96) than performance on TaskGrasp-Image (R² = 0.72). This indicates the utility of PRISM-Test as a more indicative benchmark for real-world scenarios.

#### 4.5. Extension to Bimanual Grasping

Qualitative preliminary experiments demonstrated that GraspMolmo can support semantically-appropriate bimanual grasping in a zero-shot manner, by modifying prompts. An example illustrated the model's ability to identify grasps for "hold the water bottle" and "open the water bottle" for a bimanual task, suggesting potential for more complex, coordinated robotic actions.

#### 4.6. Failure Analysis (PRISM-Real)

The authors noted common failure modes for GraspMolmo on PRISM-Real:
- Pointing to the wrong part of the correct object (62% of failures).
- Pointing to the wrong object (24% of failures).
- Pointing to the correct location but matching to an incorrect grasp candidate (5% of failures).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of GraspMolmo represents a notable advancement in the field of robotic manipulation, particularly for task-oriented grasping (TOG). Its significance and potential impact can be summarized in several key areas:

#### 5.1. Advancement in Generalizable Task-Oriented Grasping

GraspMolmo moves beyond traditional object-centric grasping by predicting semantically appropriate grasps conditioned on natural language instructions. This capability is crucial for robots operating in complex, unstructured environments where the function dictates the form of interaction. The model's ability to handle open-vocabulary instructions, such as "mince some garlic" rather than constrained "grasp [noun] to [verb]" templates, suggests a step towards more intuitive human-robot interaction.

#### 5.2. Overcoming Data Scarcity and Diversity Challenges

The introduction of PRISM, a large-scale synthetic dataset of 379,000 samples, addresses a long-standing challenge in TOG research: the scarcity and limited diversity of real-world annotated data. The novel methodology for generating task-grasp annotations via grasp descriptions and LLM-based matching significantly scales data creation. This synthetic data generation pipeline, designed with complex scenes, diverse objects, and varied language, provides a robust training ground that can accelerate the development of more capable robotic systems.

#### 5.3. Enhanced Generalization and Real-World Robustness

The results demonstrate GraspMolmo's superior generalization to novel objects, scenes, and complex instructions compared to previous state-of-the-art methods. Its strong performance in challenging real-world evaluations, with a 70% prediction success and 61.1% overall success rate on complex tasks, validates the effectiveness of its synthetic training methodology for sim-to-real transfer. This indicates a potential for deploying more reliable robotic manipulation systems in real-world settings without extensive real-data collection or task-specific re-training.

#### 5.4. Foundation for Advanced Robotic Manipulation

By enabling robots to understand not just what an object is, but how it should be manipulated for specific intended uses, GraspMolmo contributes to laying a foundation for more sophisticated robotic capabilities. This work facilitates the progression of robotic manipulation beyond simple pick-and-place tasks toward more nuanced, task-semantic operations, opening avenues for automation in complex household or industrial environments.

#### 5.5. Acceleration of Future Research

The commitment to release the PRISM synthetic dataset, GraspMolmo model, code, and benchmarks is a significant contribution to the research community. This open-sourcing initiative is expected to lower barriers for entry, enable reproducibility, and accelerate further research and development in task-semantic robotic manipulation and generalizable grasping.

#### 5.6. Potential for Bimanual Tasks

The preliminary demonstration of zero-shot bimanual grasping capability suggests a promising direction for future research. Extending task-semantic understanding to coordinated multi-arm manipulation could enable robots to perform more dexterous and complex tasks that require opposing forces, such as unscrewing bottle caps or folding clothes, which are challenging for single-arm systems.

#### 5.7. Identified Limitations for Future Work

Despite the advancements, the paper acknowledges several limitations, including GraspMolmo's dependency on a separate grasp proposer and the need for integration with motion planning algorithms. The point-based grasp representation is also identified as a potential constraint for scenarios requiring fine-resolution rotational adjustments. These limitations indicate clear directions for future research, particularly towards models capable of directly generating semantically-appropriate 6-DoF stable grasps without intermediate representations.

In summary, GraspMolmo represents a step forward in making robotic grasping more intelligent and adaptable, driven by scalable synthetic data and VLM fine-tuning. Its demonstrated generalization and real-world performance, alongside open-source contributions, position it as a significant influence on the trajectory of task-semantic robotic manipulation research.
