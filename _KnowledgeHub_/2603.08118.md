# Model-based Offline RL via Robust Value-Aware Model Learning with Implicitly Differentiable Adaptive Weighting

> [!summary] Summary
> ROMI introduces a model-based offline reinforcement learning approach that uses robust value-aware model learning and implicitly differentiable adaptive weighting to achieve controllable conservatism and stable training. The method demonstrates superior performance over RAMBO by 18.6% on D4RL MuJoCo datasets and achieves top results on NeoRL and D4RL Antmaze benchmarks, while maintaining training stability.

## Problem

- Model exploitation in model-based offline RL, where policies exploit inaccuracies in learned dynamics, particularly in Out-Of-Distribution (OOD) regions.
- Existing adversarial model learning methods, like RAMBO, exhibit hyperparameter sensitivity, training instability (e.g., gradient explosion), and over-conservatism with increased adversarial influence.
- Difficulty in precisely controlling the degree of conservatism, as fixed hyperparameters often lead to either minimal adversarial impact or severe Q-value underestimation and training collapse.

## Method

- The ==Robust Value-aware== Model Learning (**RVL**) component redefines adversarial model learning to characterize one-step value estimation error, ensuring value predictions are consistent with the minimum value over a dynamics uncertainty set. This set's scale (ξ) directly controls conservatism.
- Implicitly Differentiable Adaptive Weighting (IDAW) employs a bi-level optimization framework, using an adaptive weighting network to prioritize transitions for dynamics model updates while minimizing the robust value-aware loss.
- The overall ROMI algorithm combines the learned robust dynamics model from the bi-level optimization with Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) for policy learning, sampling from both the offline dataset and a model buffer.

## Results

- ROMI outperformed RAMBO on 11 out of 12 D4RL MuJoCo datasets, achieving a total normalized score **18.6%** higher, and showed competitive or superior performance against other state-of-the-art methods.
- The method surpassed all baselines on 6 out of 9 NeoRL datasets, achieving the highest total normalized score and consistently outperforming RAMBO across all NeoRL tasks.
- Ablation studies confirmed that adaptive weighting improved performance and reduced model prediction error during multi-step rollouts, while the uncertainty set scale (ξ) provided stable and controllable conservatism without causing training collapse.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Minimizing the expected value over a dynamics uncertainty set defined by Wasserstein distance is theoretically equivalent to minimizing over a scale-adjustable state uncertainty set, allowing direct control of conservatism via the scale parameter ξ.
> - The implicitly differentiable adaptive weighting within a bi-level optimization effectively balances both dynamics awareness (through weighted supervised learning) and value awareness (through the robust value-aware loss) for improved OOD generalization.
> - Unlike previous methods, ROMI's design ensures controllable conservatism and stable model updates by moving away from problematic direct model gradient approaches, preventing Q-value underestimation and training instability.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated within the field of Model-based Offline Reinforcement Learning (RL). Offline RL focuses on training agents using pre-collected datasets without further interaction with the environment. Within this paradigm, model-based approaches aim to improve data efficiency and generalization capabilities by learning an environmental dynamics model from the offline data, which can then be used for policy exploration.

A significant challenge in model-based offline RL is "model exploitation." This phenomenon occurs when a policy exploits inaccuracies in the learned dynamics model, particularly in Out-Of-Distribution (OOD) regions where the model's predictions may be unreliable. Such exploitation can lead to a degradation in the algorithm's performance. To address this, conservatism (also known as pessimism) is often incorporated into the learning process.

Existing strategies for incorporating conservatism primarily fall into two categories:
1. **Uncertainty Estimation:** Methods like MOPO, MoReL, SUMO, Count-MORL, and MOBILE estimate model uncertainty and use it to penalize rewards or value functions. However, the reliability of uncertainty estimation for neural networks can be a concern.
2. **Adversarial Model Learning:** This approach, exemplified by RAMBO, COMBO, and others, frames the problem as a maximin optimization, where the policy optimizes against an adversarial dynamics model within an uncertainty set. RAMBO, in particular, has emerged as a representative and widely adopted method within this framework, using model gradients to reduce the value function in OOD regions.

This paper builds upon the adversarial model learning paradigm, specifically identifying and addressing empirical limitations of RAMBO. It aims to develop a more stable and controllable approach to adversarial model learning for offline RL, thereby contributing to the development of more robust and reliable model-based offline RL algorithms.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to overcome the identified limitations of existing adversarial model-based offline RL methods, particularly RAMBO, by proposing a novel approach that ensures controllable conservatism and stable training while enhancing out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization.

The motivation for this research stems from an empirical analysis of RAMBO, a widely recognized method in adversarial model learning for offline RL. The authors' investigation revealed several issues:
- **Hyperparameter Sensitivity and Minimal Adversarial Impact:** RAMBO introduces a tradeoff coefficient, λ, to balance adversarial training with maximum likelihood estimation. The authors observed that in its original implementation, λ is set to a very small value (e.g., 3e-4), which minimizes the adversarial term's influence. This suggests that the adversarial component might not be effectively contributing to conservatism under typical settings.
- **Training Instability and Over-conservatism:** Further empirical testing demonstrated that even a slight increase in λ (e.g., to 0.05 or 0.1) could lead to severe Q-value underestimation and, in some cases, gradient explosion, resulting in training collapse. This behavior indicates that RAMBO tends to be overly conservative when the adversarial term is more prominent and struggles with stable model updates. The fixed nature of λ as a hand-tuned Lagrange multiplier contributes to the difficulty in precisely controlling the degree of conservatism.
- **Model Gradient Issues:** The authors suggest that updating the dynamics model directly with a model gradient, as done in RAMBO, might encourage the model to explore regions where values sharply decrease, which can lead to the observed Q-value underestimation and gradient instability.

Based on these observations, the key objectives for the proposed method, ROMI, are:
1. To develop a robust value-aware model learning approach that provides **flexible and controllable conservatism adjustment** without suffering from the instability and over-conservatism observed in RAMBO's model gradient method. This involves designing a mechanism where the degree of conservatism can be explicitly quantified and adjusted.
2. To enhance **OOD generalization** during multi-step policy rollouts. While focusing on value-aware conservatism, the method also aims to incorporate dynamics awareness, which is crucial for preventing compounding prediction errors in unobserved state-action regions. This is motivated by the understanding that a solely value-aware approach might neglect the accuracy of dynamics in OOD regions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, RObust value-aware Model learning with Implicitly differentiable adaptive weighting (ROMI), aims to address the limitations of RAMBO by introducing a novel model learning approach within a bi-level optimization framework.

#### 3.1. Robust Value-Aware Model Learning (RVL)

ROMI re-conceptualizes the adversarial model learning problem by focusing on explicitly characterizing the one-step value estimation error. Instead of RAMBO's direct adversarial loss, ROMI aims to learn a dynamics model (`bT_m`) such that its value predictions are consistent with the minimum value over a dynamics uncertainty set (`Mξ`). To avoid arbitrarily low Q-value estimates in OOD regions, this value-aware error is evaluated based on states and actions from the offline dataset and a target value function during training.

A key challenge is the practical handling of the dynamics uncertainty set `Mξ`. ROMI addresses this by defining `Mξ` using the Wasserstein distance (Definition 4.1). A theoretical result (Proposition 4.1) demonstrates that, under the Wasserstein distance, minimizing the expected value over the dynamics uncertainty set (`min bT∈Mξ E_s'~bT(·|s,a) bV(s')`) is equivalent to `E_s'~bT_MLE(·|s,a) [min_ŝ∈Uξ(s') bV(ŝ)]`, where `Uξ(s')` is a "state uncertainty set" defined as `Uξ(s') = {ŝ | d(ŝ, s') ≤ ξ}`. Here, `ξ` represents the scale of this uncertainty set. This transformation is significant because it allows the level of conservatism to be directly controlled by adjusting the scale `ξ`.

Based on this, ROMI formulates a Robust Value-aware model Learning (RVL) loss (Equation 6):
`L_RVL(ψ) := E_(s,a,s')∈D [E_ŝ'~bTψ(·|s,a) bV(ŝ') - min_{s̃'i}N_i=1∈Uξ(s') bV(s̃'i)]^2`.
This loss function requires the learned dynamics model (`bTψ`) to predict future states whose values are close to the minimum Q-value within the scale-adjustable state uncertainty set `Uξ(s')`. This design offers controllable conservatism and is intended to stabilize model updates by moving away from the problematic model gradient of RAMBO. Furthermore, Proposition 4.2 guarantees that the Q-function learned by minimizing this loss maintains bounded values during policy rollouts, assuming generalization errors are bounded.

#### 3.2. Implicitly Differentiable Adaptive Weighting (IDAW)

While RVL ensures value awareness and conservatism, it might overlook dynamics awareness, which is critical for OOD generalization during multi-step rollouts. Relying solely on value awareness can lead to compounding prediction errors as the policy explores OOD regions. To integrate both dynamics and value awareness, ROMI introduces implicitly differentiable adaptive weighting within a bi-level optimization framework (Equation 8).

The framework consists of:
- **Adaptive Weighting Network (`w_ν(s, a, s')`):** A neural network is introduced to assign an individual weight to each transition in the offline dataset.
- **Inner Level Optimization:** The dynamics model (`ψ`) is updated by minimizing a Weighted Supervised Learning (WSL) loss: `L_WSL(ψ, ν) := E_(s,a,s')∈D [w_ν(s, a, s') log bTψ(s'|s, a)]`. This enforces dynamics awareness, with the weighting network (`w_ν`) dynamically prioritizing samples. The inner loop performs gradient descent for `ψ`.
- **Outer Level Optimization:** The weighting network (`ν`) is updated by minimizing the robust value-aware model loss `L_RVL(ψ(ν))` using implicit differentiation. This ensures value awareness, as `w_ν` learns to assign weights that lead to a dynamics model minimizing `L_RVL`. The gradient calculation for `ν` involves the gradient of `L_RVL` with respect to `ψ` and the implicit derivative of `ψ` with respect to `ν`.

This hierarchical optimization adaptively balances the two objectives. Corollary 4.1 states that under assumptions of Lipschitz continuity and bounded prediction error, this framework can bound the generalization error (ϵ1), which is crucial for multi-step rollouts. Proposition 4.3 provides a convergence rate analysis for this bi-level optimization, showing that both inner and outer losses can achieve a convergence rate of O(1/√K).

#### 3.3. ROMI Algorithm

The complete ROMI algorithm combines the learned dynamics model from the bi-level optimization with policy learning. The policy is learned using Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) by sampling data from both the original offline dataset (D) and a model buffer (D_model) generated through rollouts with the learned dynamics model. The core distinction of ROMI from RAMBO lies in its strategy for training the dynamics model.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluation of ROMI was conducted on D4RL MuJoCo, D4RL Antmaze, and NeoRL datasets, comparing its performance against several model-free and model-based offline RL baselines, including RAMBO, MOBILE, and Count-MORL.

#### 4.1. Benchmark Results

- **D4RL MuJoCo Datasets (Table 1):**
 - ROMI demonstrated superior performance compared to RAMBO, outperforming it on 11 out of 12 datasets and achieving a total normalized score 18.6% higher.
 - ROMI also exhibited competitive or superior performance against other state-of-the-art methods like MOBILE and Count-MORL, achieving (one of) the best results on 11 out of 12 datasets.
- **NeoRL Datasets (Table 2):**
 - ROMI surpassed all baselines on 6 out of 9 datasets and achieved the highest total normalized score.
 - It consistently outperformed RAMBO across all 9 NeoRL tasks.
 - Notably, ROMI also showed stronger performance than most model-free baselines, which often outperform model-based methods on these benchmarks.
- **D4RL Antmaze Datasets (Table 6 in Appendix F.1):**
 - On these challenging sparse-reward tasks, ROMI achieved the highest total success rate (186.5) among all compared methods, including MOBILE (173.4) and RAMBO (94.6).
 - It outperformed RAMBO on five out of six tasks and showed stronger performance than MOBILE on `umaze-diverse` and `medium-diverse`.

#### 4.2. Study on the Effect of Dynamics Awareness (Adaptive Weighting)

An ablation study was conducted to evaluate the contribution of the implicitly differentiable adaptive weighting component. The results (Figure 2) indicated that including adaptive weighting:
- **Improved Performance:** The learning curves showed higher normalized scores with adaptive weighting.
- **Reduced Prediction Error:** The model prediction error during multi-step rollouts was lower across various rollout steps when adaptive weighting was employed.
This suggests that integrating dynamics awareness through adaptive weighting is crucial for enhancing OOD generalization and achieving robust performance.

#### 4.3. Study on the Effect of the Uncertainty Set Scale (ξ)

An investigation into the impact of the key hyperparameter, `ξ` (uncertainty set scale), was performed on the `halfcheetah-medium-expert-v2` (Figure 3) and `walker2d-medium-expert-v2` (Figure 9 in Appendix F.3) datasets. The findings contrast with the observed instability in RAMBO:
- **Stable Training:** ROMI did not exhibit severe Q-value underestimation or training collapse, even with a large `ξ` value (e.g., 10). The gradient norms for both inner and outer losses remained small and stable throughout training.
- **Controllable Conservatism:** The estimated Q-values clearly differentiated across various `ξ` values, with larger `ξ` corresponding to lower Q-values. This indicates that `ξ` provides a controllable mechanism to adjust the level of conservatism.
These results confirm that ROMI offers a more controllable degree of conservatism while maintaining training stability, addressing a core limitation of RAMBO.

An additional study on the number of samples `N` from the uncertainty set (Table 7 in Appendix F.3) showed that increasing `N` beyond 10 did not yield significant performance improvements, suggesting that a modest `N` is sufficient for the tasks evaluated.

#### 4.4. Acknowledged Limitations

The authors acknowledge two main limitations of ROMI:
- It incurs additional computational cost due to the bi-level optimization framework.
- The conservatism degree (`ξ`) must be specified prior to training, lacking runtime adjustability as seen in some other methods.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents ROMI as a method that advances model-based offline reinforcement learning by directly addressing critical limitations identified in prior work, particularly RAMBO. The significance and potential impact of this work include:

- **Enhanced Robustness and Stability:** By replacing RAMBO's unstable model gradient-based adversarial learning with a robust value-aware model learning scheme, ROMI provides a more stable training process. The empirical demonstration of consistent performance across a wide range of conservatism parameters (`ξ`) without training collapse represents a notable improvement in reliability for model-based offline RL.
- **Controllable Conservatism:** The direct mapping between the uncertainty set scale (`ξ`) and the degree of conservatism offers practitioners a transparent and effective way to manage the trade-off between exploration and exploitation in offline settings. This explicit control is a crucial practical advantage.
- **Improved OOD Generalization:** The introduction of implicitly differentiable adaptive weighting to balance dynamics and value awareness is a novel mechanism for mitigating compounding errors during multi-step rollouts into OOD regions. This enhancement in generalization capability is vital for the reliable deployment of offline RL policies in real-world scenarios where precise model accuracy across all state-action spaces cannot be guaranteed.
- **Competitive Performance:** ROMI's ability to outperform RAMBO and achieve competitive or superior results against other state-of-the-art model-free and model-based offline RL algorithms across diverse and challenging benchmarks (D4RL, NeoRL) indicates its effectiveness. This performance suggests it could be a valuable tool for learning policies from static datasets.
- **Theoretical Contributions:** The theoretical grounding for the robust value-aware loss through the Wasserstein distance and the convergence analysis of the bi-level optimization framework provide a deeper understanding of the method's properties and contribute to the theoretical foundations of adversarial model learning in RL.

The work's impact could be seen in fostering more reliable and adaptable model-based offline RL systems, particularly in applications where data scarcity necessitates robust learning from fixed datasets and where model errors need to be carefully managed. The identified limitations also open avenues for future research into more computationally efficient or adaptively parameterizable robust model learning approaches.
