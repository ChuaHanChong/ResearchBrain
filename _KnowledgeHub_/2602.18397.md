# How Fast Can I Run My VLA? Demystifying VLA Inference Performance with VLA-Perf

> [!summary] Summary
> NVIDIA Research developed VLA-Perf, an analytical, roofline-based performance model, to systematically study the inference performance of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models across various architectures and deployment systems. This work provides 15 key takeaways, guiding the design of future VLA models and systems to meet real-time robotics requirements, achieving up to 314 Hz for small models on datacenter GPUs and demonstrating significant performance advantages for server-side diffusion VLAs.

## Problem

- Deploying sophisticated Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models on real-world robots is challenging due to stringent real-time inference constraints (e.g., 10-100 Hz).
- A comprehensive understanding of VLA inference performance is lacking, given the vast combinatorial space of model architectures, scaling factors, and inference system configurations.
- Prior work on VLA inference efficiency often targets specific model-system pairings, leading to a fragmented view without systematic guidance for design and deployment.

## Method

- Introduced **VLA-Perf**, an open-source, analytical, ==roofline-based performance model==, to efficiently analyze VLA inference latency across a wide range of model and system parameters.
- Modeled VLA inference as a sequence of components (==Vision Encoder==, ==VLM Backbone==, ==Action Expert==) and data transfers, calculating operator latencies based on ==compute or memory bounds==.
- Validated VLA-Perf against a ==Triton-based== implementation of the **π0** model, demonstrating **73.3% to 82.6%** fidelity to real-world performance for coarse-grained analysis.

## Results

- Datacenter GPUs (A100, H100, B100) achieve **61-314 Hz** inference for models like π0, exceeding typical camera frame rates, while edge GPUs (Jetson Thor) are limited to **19 Hz**.
- Datacenter GPUs (B100) can support real-time inference (**9.6 Hz**) for VLA models an order of magnitude larger (e.g., **81B parameters**) and long-context VLAs with up to **1,000** past timesteps (**11.7 Hz**).
- Diffusion-based VLA inference is **1-2 orders of magnitude** faster (e.g., **102x** for default π0) than vanilla autoregressive VLA inference when action chunking is used, while action chunk size itself has a negligible impact on performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Action prediction in VLAs is primarily memory-bound, while vision and VLM inference are often compute-bound on powerful GPUs, highlighting distinct bottlenecks.
> - Server-side inference, even with consumer GPUs and wireless networks, generally outperforms on-device solutions, underscoring the importance of powerful external compute.
> - Diffusion-based VLA inference is significantly faster than vanilla autoregressive methods for action chunking, and asynchronous inference can substantially boost effective throughput for server-side deployments.
