# Segmenting Visuals With Querying Words: Language Anchors For Semi-Supervised Image Segmentation

> [!summary] Summary
> HVL, a framework developed by researchers from the University of Regina, University of Western Australia, and University Canada West, enhances semi-supervised semantic segmentation by integrating hierarchical vision-language synergy. It leverages VLM text embeddings as dynamic, context-aware object queries, achieving state-of-the-art performance with less than 1% labeled data across major benchmarks.

## Problem

- Acquiring pixel-level annotations for semantic segmentation is prohibitively expensive and time-consuming, necessitating label-efficient methods.
- Existing semi-supervised semantic segmentation (SSS) methods, relying primarily on visual features, often struggle with semantic ambiguities (e.g., distinguishing visually similar classes) and are vulnerable to domain shifts.
- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) offer robust, domain-invariant semantic understanding but lack the fine-grained spatial precision required for pixel-level dense prediction tasks like segmentation.

## Method

- HVL introduces a transformer-based framework that integrates ==domain-invariant text embeddings== from VLMs as hierarchical object queries for semi-supervised semantic segmentation.
- A ==Hierarchical Textual Query Generator (HTQG)== creates multi-level, image-relevant, and semantically rich class queries by filtering classes present in an image and capturing intra-class variations.
- A ==Pixel-Text Refinement Module (PTRM)== mutually refines visual pixel features and textual queries using bidirectional cross-attention, while a ==Dual-Query Transformer Decoder (DQTD)== combines these class-level textual queries with instance-level visual queries, supported by regularization losses to maintain vision-language alignment.

## Results

- HVL achieved new state-of-the-art mean Intersection over Union (mIoU) across four major benchmarks, including a **+**9.3%** mIoU** gain on COCO with **232** labeled images and **+**13.5%** mIoU** on ADE20K with **158** labeled images.
- Qualitative results demonstrate enhanced class discrimination for visually similar objects, reduced over-segmentation, sharper object boundaries, and more accurate instance recognition compared to prior methods.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the Hierarchical Textual Query Generator and Pixel-Text Refinement Module contribute the most significant performance gains, particularly in low-label settings, with all regularization losses collectively boosting performance by **2.4 mIoU** on Pascal VOC.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Directly leveraging VLM text embeddings as hierarchical, image-context-aware object queries significantly enhances semantic grounding and generalization in low-supervision segmentation settings.
> - The explicit mutual refinement between global semantic textual queries and local spatial visual features (PTRM) effectively bridges the modality gap, improving both semantic clarity and spatial precision.
> - Combining domain-invariant class-level textual queries with instance-level visual queries in a dual-query decoder effectively captures both high-level semantic understanding and fine-grained intra-class variations for robust segmentation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of semantic segmentation, which involves assigning a class label to each pixel in an image, is critical for various applications such as autonomous driving, medical imaging, and agriculture. A significant challenge in this field is the high cost and time required for acquiring dense pixel-level annotations. Semi-supervised Semantic Segmentation (SSS) aims to mitigate this by leveraging a small set of labeled images alongside a larger pool of unlabeled data.

Traditional SSS methods often employ strategies like adversarial training, self-training, entropy minimization, and consistency regularization to extract supervisory signals from unlabeled data. Despite advancements, these methods frequently encounter issues such as misclassification, particularly with significant intra-class variation, visually similar classes, and rare categories. The limited labeled data in SSS hinders robust feature learning and comprehensive semantic understanding, unlike fully supervised or domain adaptation scenarios.

Recently, Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have emerged as a promising direction. VLMs, pre-trained on large-scale image-text pairs, can encode domain-invariant semantic representations, aligning visual and textual modalities in a shared embedding space. This capability suggests potential for guiding pixel-level predictions in segmentation tasks. However, the integration of VLMs into SSS remains underexplored. Existing VLM-based segmentation methods typically either operate without dense annotations, leading to limited precision, or rely on extensive labeled datasets, which contradicts the core motivation of SSS. Approaches like SemiVL have partially addressed this by adapting VLM text embeddings, but they primarily treat textual cues as single-level, domain-invariant features. Other methods, such as Pseudo-SD, leverage pseudo-labels and text embeddings to fine-tune generative models for synthetic data generation.

This work positions itself within this landscape by addressing two key limitations of current VLM integration in SSS:
1. **Lack of Domain Awareness**: Pre-trained VLM embeddings, being generic, often lack adaptation to dataset- and image-specific contexts. This limits their effectiveness in low-data regimes, where fine contextual cues are crucial for distinguishing similar classes (e.g., "chair" vs. "sofa" in specific datasets) and establishing stable representations for under-represented categories.
2. **Weak Vision-Language Alignment**: Current methods often relegate the language component to a weak auxiliary cue, primarily optimized through pixel-level supervision. This results in shallow vision-language alignment, which hinders contextual reasoning and limits the full extraction of semantic knowledge from pre-trained VLMs.

The proposed Hierarchical Vision-Language Transformer (HVLFormer) seeks to advance SSS by developing a VLM-driven framework that achieves domain-aware and domain-robust alignment between visual and textual representations, explicitly leveraging language as an active semantic guide within a query-driven mask-transformer architecture.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the persistent challenges in Semi-supervised Semantic Segmentation (SSS), specifically the difficulties in achieving robust semantic understanding and accurate segmentation with limited labeled data. The authors identify that current SSS models, even those incorporating Vision-Language Models (VLMs), struggle with:
- **Contextual reasoning**: Misclassifications often occur in scenarios with high intra-class variation or visually similar classes due to insufficient contextual understanding derived from limited annotations.
- **Weak intra-class discrimination**: Models fail to adequately distinguish subtle differences within a class (e.g., different types of "chairs") or between highly correlated classes (e.g., "sofa" and "chair").
- **Confusion of similar classes**: The scarcity of labeled examples, particularly for rare categories, leads to blurred decision boundaries and misidentification.
- **Semantic misalignment**: Current VLM integration often uses domain-invariant text embeddings without adapting them to specific dataset and image contexts, leading to a gap between global VLM semantics and local visual scene structures.

To address these challenges, the key objectives of this work are to:

1. **Establish Domain-Aware Vision-Language Alignment**: Develop a mechanism to transform generic, domain-invariant text embeddings from pre-trained VLMs into multi-scale, dataset-aware textual object queries. These queries should capture class semantics from coarse to fine granularity and adapt to dataset- and image-specific contexts, thereby enhancing the model’s semantic understanding in low-data regimes.
2. **Refine Textual Queries with Image-Specific Context**: Enhance the discriminative power of textual queries by injecting image-specific visual context. This aims to align global textual semantics with local scene structures, enabling better class discrimination and adaptation to complex visual distributions.
3. **Achieve Domain-Robustness through Consistency Regularization**: Introduce a mechanism to ensure that language queries remain robust to intra-class variations and diverse visual scenes, preventing overfitting to the small labeled set. This involves maintaining stable vision-language alignment under perturbations during the decoding process.
4. **Develop a Unified Query-Driven Mask-Transformer Architecture**: Integrate these objectives into a coherent framework where language queries actively guide the prediction of class-specific segmentation masks under limited supervision.

By achieving these objectives, the research aims to improve label efficiency, enhance contextual reasoning, and strengthen class discrimination in SSS, ultimately leading to more precise and reliable semantic segmentation with minimal reliance on dense annotations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

HVLFormer is a unified mask-transformer-based framework designed for semi-supervised semantic segmentation, leveraging Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for language-guided segmentation. The architecture builds upon a mask-classification backbone and comprises several interconnected modules.

**Overall Architecture:**
The model uses a pre-trained VLM, where the image encoder ($E_I$) is fine-tuned on labeled images with standard cross-entropy loss, and the text encoder ($E_T$) is frozen to preserve the VLM's semantic space. For an input image, $E_I$ generates multi-scale feature maps which are processed by the HVLFormer pixel decoder to produce multi-scale pixel features ($z$) and dense per-pixel embeddings ($Z_{pixel}$). Textual queries, derived from text embeddings, along with pixel features, are fed into a transformer decoder to generate segmentation masks. The core methodology integrates vision-language interaction modules, hierarchical query generation, pixel-text refinement, and cross-view consistency regularization.

#### 3.1. Hierarchical Textual Query Generation (HTQG)

This module aims to create domain-aware and multi-scale textual queries from VLM embeddings.

- **Dataset-Aware Prompting**: A learnable dataset-specific prompt $p_k$ is employed for each semantic class $k$. This prompt incorporates dataset attributes along with the class name, allowing the text encoder $E_T$ to generate textual embeddings ($t_k = E_T(p_k)$) that are contextually relevant to the specific dataset (e.g., urban driving scenes in Cityscapes vs. object-centric images in Pascal VOC). This addresses the issue of generic VLM embeddings.
- **Hierarchical Query Generation (HQG)**: To capture intra-class variations and fine-grained semantics, each class embedding $t_k$ is projected into multiple abstraction levels. $E$ distinct MLP heads transform $t_k$ into queries $\{q^e_k = MLP_e(t_k)\}^{K,E}_{k=1,e=1}$, where $E$ is the number of abstraction levels (set to 3). These queries are aligned with corresponding pixel feature maps from the pixel decoder, with coarse queries encoding global structure and finer queries capturing texture and boundary details. A diversity regularization term ($L_{div}$) encourages distinct semantics across these scales.
- **Semantic Relevance Estimation (SRE)**: To mitigate noise from queries corresponding to classes absent in an image, SRE estimates the likelihood of each class being present. A relevance score $s_k$ is computed by projecting fused multi-scale pixel features and the class text embedding into a shared space via a lightweight adapter and sigmoid activation. The final hierarchical queries $\tilde{Q} = \{\tilde{q}^e_k = s_k \cdot q^e_k\}^{K,E}_{k=1,e=1}$ are obtained by soft-weighing the generated queries with these relevance scores, suppressing irrelevant class semantics.

#### 3.2. Pixel-Text Refinement Module (PTRM)

PTRM integrates image-specific context into the hierarchical textual queries and injects class-level semantics into pixel features, aiming for bidirectional adaptation and improved alignment.

- **Shared Latent Space Projection**: At each pixel-feature level $e$, textual queries $\tilde{Q}^e$ and pixel feature map $z^e$ are projected into a shared latent space, yielding text ($T^e$) and visual ($V^e$) representations.
- **Shallow Fusion**: $F^e = T^e + V^e$ creates an initial alignment between class semantics and image-spatial structure.
- **Spatially Guided Conditioning**: Average-pooled representations across feature maps are computed ($X^e = [\varsigma(T^e), \varsigma(V^e), \varsigma(F^e)]$). Two convolutional layers then generate spatial attention weights $W^e$, decomposed into text-guided ($W^e_T$), pixel-guided ($W^e_V$), and fused ($W^e_F$) attention maps.
- **Bidirectional Adaptation**: These attention maps regulate the flow of information:
 - $T'^e = T^e \odot W^e_V \odot W^e_F$: Local visual cues enrich textual features.
 - $V'^e = V^e \odot W^e_T \odot W^e_F$: Class-level priors from text enhance pixel features.
 - $W^e_F$ ensures updates occur in regions of mutual confidence.
- **Output**: The module outputs enhanced pixel features ($z^e_{out}$) and refined, context-aware textual queries ($Q^e$), which are then passed to the transformer decoder.

#### 3.3. Transformer Decoder

Following Mask2Former, the transformer decoder iteratively refines the aligned textual queries using the enhanced pixel features through $N$ masked attention layers. This process outputs refined queries $Q^{(N)}$. These queries are then projected into mask embeddings ($M_E$) to generate pixel-level masks with $Z_{pixel}$, and separately classified via a linear projection to produce class logits ($\hat{C}$). The final segmentation prediction ($\hat{Y}$) is obtained by combining the pixel-level mask and class scores.

#### 3.4. Cross-View and Modal Consistency Regularization (CMCR)

CMCR addresses overfitting to limited labeled data and amplifies misalignments in the transformer decoder by enforcing consistency.

- **Three-View Augmentation**: An unlabeled image $I$ is augmented into a weakly augmented version $I_w$ (mild photometric changes) and a strongly augmented version $I_s$ (color jitter, CutMix, geometric distortions).
- **Consistency Enforcement**: CMCR enforces consistency across all three views (original-weak and original-strong pairs) at every decoder layer $n$. This includes:
 - $\mathcal{L}_{mask}$: Global segmentation consistency for mask predictions ($\hat{Y}^n, \hat{Y}^n_w, \hat{Y}^n_s$).
 - $\mathcal{L}_{class}$: Query-specific class prediction consistency ($\hat{C}^n_i, \hat{C}^n_{w,i}, \hat{C}^n_{s,i}$).
 - $\mathcal{L}_{align}$: Pixel-text attention map consistency ($A^n_i, A^n_{w,i}, A^n_{s,i}$).
This regularization helps textual queries adapt to diverse image contexts and stabilizes vision-language alignment.

#### 3.5. Overall Training Objective

The total loss function combines standard segmentation loss ($\mathcal{L}_{seg}$), regularization loss ($\mathcal{L}_{reg}$) from the TQDM baseline, the diversity regularization ($\mathcal{L}_{div}$), and the CMCR regularization ($\mathcal{L}_{cmcr}$):
$\mathcal{L}_{total} = \mathcal{L}_{seg} + \lambda_{reg}\mathcal{L}_{reg} + \lambda_{div}\mathcal{L}_{div} + \lambda_{cmcr}\mathcal{L}_{cmcr}$. Weighting factors ($\lambda$) control the contribution of each term.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of HVLFormer demonstrated performance advancements across multiple semi-supervised semantic segmentation benchmarks.

**Overall Performance:**
HVLFormer consistently achieved state-of-the-art (SOTA) performance across Pascal VOC, COCO, ADE20K, and Cityscapes datasets. This was observed even when utilizing less labeled data and with shallower backbones, indicating enhanced semantic understanding and improved label efficiency under low-label conditions. The model's ability to produce precise segmentations, disambiguate visually similar classes (e.g., sofa-chair), and avoid misclassifications in cluttered regions (e.g., racket nets) was noted.

**Comparison on Pascal VOC:**
On Pascal VOC, with varying labeled image splits (from 92 to 1,464 images), HVLFormer consistently outperformed prior VLM-based methods, including Pseudo-SD and SemiVL. At the lowest labeled split (92 images), HVLFormer achieved gains of up to +5.4 mIoU over previous SOTA VLM methods. Compared to its baseline TQDM, improvements ranged from +13.3 mIoU (lowest split) to +3.2 mIoU (highest split). With 14% labeled data, HVLFormer achieved a new SOTA of 91.8 mIoU, surpassing UniMatch V2's 90.8%.

**Comparison on COCO:**
For the COCO dataset, characterized by higher category diversity and fine-grained inter-class overlap, HVLFormer demonstrated SOTA performance. With only 232 labeled images, it achieved a +9.3 mIoU gain over the previous SOTA and up to a +19.2 mIoU improvement over its baseline. These consistent gains across splits indicated reduced ambiguity in segmenting similar and rare classes, along with improved modeling of intra-class variation.

**Comparison on ADE20K:**
On the ADE20K scene-parsing challenge, HVLFormer yielded a +13.5 mIoU gain over its baselines with only 158 labels and a +4.8 mIoU improvement over existing SOTA with 316 training labels, further validating its effectiveness across numerous classes and challenging datasets.

**Comparison on Cityscapes:**
In the Cityscapes dataset, HVLFormer outperformed SemiVL by 3.4% with 100 labels and surpassed UniMatch V2 by +0.6 mIoU with 186 labels. The authors noted that the smaller overall gains on Cityscapes might be attributed to the presence of many small and distant object classes (e.g., poles, traffic signs) that may not be well-represented in VLM pre-training captions.

**Ablation Studies:**
The ablation studies conducted on Pascal VOC and COCO demonstrated the progressive contribution of each HVLFormer component. Larger gains were observed in low-label splits, suggesting that the modules designed to enhance semantic understanding through domain-robust and aware textual queries provide greater benefits when supervision is limited.
- **HTQG (Dataset Attr)**: Introducing dataset-aware language priors increased Pascal mIoU by +0.8 and COCO mIoU by +1.7.
- **HTQG (HQG)**: Adding hierarchical query generation provided further gains, particularly on COCO, attributed to its higher class diversity.
- **SRE**: The Semantic Relevance Estimator, which filters out absent class queries, improved results by mitigating semantic drift. The soft-weighing strategy outperformed fixed thresholding or no SRE.
- **PTRM**: The Pixel-Text Refinement Module contributed substantial gains, confirming that image-conditioned query refinement enhances cross-modal alignment and semantic understanding.
- **CMCR**: Incorporating the Cross-View and Modal Consistency Regularization terms ($L_{mask}$, $L_{class}$, $L_{align}$) produced further improvements, indicating that enforcing cross-view consistency and pixel-text regularization strengthens query domain robustness and stabilizes vision-language interactions.

**Impact of Prompting Strategies:**
A comparison of prompting strategies for HTQG revealed that learnable prompts incorporating class names and dataset attributes achieved the best performance. Fixed prompts with only class names performed the weakest, while the inclusion of dataset attributes significantly improved results by providing crucial contextual cues to the VLM, leading to richer, context-sensitive language embeddings.

**Analysis of HQG Levels and Query Enhancement:**
Increasing the hierarchical levels (E) for HQG consistently improved performance, with more pronounced effects on datasets with higher class diversity like COCO. This demonstrated the benefit of multi-level queries in capturing semantics from coarse to fine granularity, particularly in resolving fine-grained distinctions and intra-class variability. PTRM, which incorporates pixel-level positional cues and local feature distributions into textual queries, was found to be the most effective query enhancement method, outperforming other cross-attention variants.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework that addresses fundamental limitations in semi-supervised semantic segmentation (SSS), particularly concerning the effective utilization of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) under data scarcity. The significance of HVLFormer lies in its systematic approach to integrate domain-aware semantics and robust vision-language alignment into a query-driven mask-transformer architecture.

The introduction of **Hierarchical Textual Query Generation (HTQG)**, comprising dataset-aware prompting, multi-scale query generation (HQG), and Semantic Relevance Estimation (SRE), marks a shift from generic VLM embeddings to contextually rich, scale-adaptive language anchors. This is significant because it allows the model to overcome the inherent domain-invariance of pre-trained VLMs, enabling finer discrimination between similar or rare classes and better adaptation to dataset-specific visual characteristics.

The **Pixel-Text Refinement Module (PTRM)** further enhances this alignment by facilitating bidirectional information flow between image-specific visual cues and class-level semantics. This module injects local scene structures into textual queries, making them more discriminative and spatially aware, which is crucial for precise segmentation in complex visual distributions.

Perhaps one of the most notable contributions is the **Cross-View and Modal Consistency Regularization (CMCR)**. By enforcing prediction consistency across augmented views and regularizing pixel-text interactions at every decoder layer, CMCR ensures domain robustness. This prevents overfitting to limited labeled data and stabilizes the vision-language alignment during iterative decoding, which is a critical challenge in low-annotation regimes where early misalignments can lead to cumulative errors. The authors state this as the first consistency-driven refinement within a mask-transformer framework for SSS.

The demonstrated state-of-the-art performance across diverse and challenging benchmarks (Pascal VOC, COCO, ADE20K, and Cityscapes) with significantly less labeled data underscores the practical impact of HVLFormer. The model's ability to achieve substantial performance gains, especially in scenarios with minimal supervision, suggests an improved label efficiency for SSS.

Potential impacts include:
- **Reduced Annotation Burden**: By achieving high performance with less than 1% of labeled data, HVLFormer can significantly reduce the extensive and costly annotation efforts typically required for dense semantic segmentation, making it more feasible for real-world applications.
- **Enhanced Semantic Understanding in Data-Scarce Domains**: The framework's capacity for robust class discrimination and contextual reasoning under limited supervision is particularly beneficial for domains such as medical imaging, agriculture, and autonomous driving, where acquiring large, densely annotated datasets is challenging.
- **Advancement in VLM Integration for Dense Prediction**: This work contributes to the broader understanding of how to effectively leverage the rich semantic priors from VLMs for dense prediction tasks like segmentation, moving beyond simple auxiliary roles to active, query-driven guidance.
- **Foundation for Future Research**: The principles of domain-aware query generation, bidirectional pixel-text refinement, and multi-modal consistency regularization within a transformer architecture could serve as a foundation for developing more advanced semi-supervised and even weakly-supervised vision-language models for a wider array of tasks.

In summary, HVLFormer represents a significant step towards more effective and label-efficient semantic segmentation by addressing core issues of semantic misalignment and domain robustness in VLM-integrated SSS frameworks.
