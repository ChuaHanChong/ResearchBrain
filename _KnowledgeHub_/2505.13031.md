# MindOmni: Unleashing Reasoning Generation in Vision Language Models with RGPO

> [!summary] Summary
> MindOmni, a unified multimodal large language model, enhances reasoning generation in vision-language tasks by integrating Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning with a novel Reinforcement Learning (RGPO) algorithm. The model produces explicit logical steps before image generation and achieves a 0.71 score on the WISE benchmark for reasoning-aware image generation, while maintaining strong performance in general image generation and vision understanding.

## Problem

- Current Text-to-Image (T2I) systems lack multimodal input capabilities and explicit reasoning, failing on complex prompts requiring world knowledge or logic.
- Existing unified Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) primarily function as semantic feature extractors, often underperforming on tasks requiring deep reasoning.
- Prior Chain-of-Thought (CoT) approaches for image synthesis are often constrained to specific domains, rely on inflexible templates, or lack generalizable complex reasoning.

## Method

- MindOmni employs a unified architecture based on ==Qwen2.5-VL== and an ==OmniGen diffusion decoder==, connected by a lightweight two-layer connector.
- A three-phase training strategy includes supervised pre-training for unified capabilities, supervised fine-tuning with coarse-to-fine Chain-of-Thought instruction data, and reinforcement learning.
- A novel ==Reasoning Generation Policy Optimization (RGPO)== algorithm enhances explicit reasoning generation using format and consistency rewards, alongside dual KL regularizers (text and image) for training stability.

## Results

- MindOmni achieved an overall score of **0.71** on the WISE benchmark for reasoning-aware image generation, outperforming all other methods, including leading generative-only and unified models.
- The model achieved an **83%** overall score on GenEval, representing state-of-the-art performance for unified MLLMs in general text-to-image generation.
- MindOmni maintained strong vision understanding performance, showing a negligible **0.1%** average difference from its Qwen2.5-VL backbone and improving **10.6%** on MMMU over Janus-Pro.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reinforcement learning, specifically the RGPO algorithm, can successfully foster explicit, step-by-step reasoning (Chain-of-Thought) in generative multimodal models.
> - Integrating explicit reasoning generation improves the accuracy and contextual appropriateness of visual outputs for complex, logically demanding prompts.
> - A multi-stage training strategy, including supervised fine-tuning with Chain-of-Thought instruction data and reinforcement learning, is effective for achieving superior performance in reasoning-aware image generation while maintaining strong general capabilities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of artificial intelligence has seen rapid advancements in large language models (LLMs) and diffusion-based text-to-image (T2I) systems. However, a significant challenge remains: bridging the gap between powerful language understanding and high-fidelity image generation, especially when complex reasoning is required.

**Current T2I systems** like SD-XL, FLUX, and Sana excel at generating images from textual descriptions but suffer from two main limitations:
1. **Limited Multimodal Input:** They typically rely on fixed text encoders, restricting their ability to incorporate other modalities like images or audio as direct input.
2. **Lack of Explicit Reasoning:** They struggle with instructions that demand deeper understanding of world knowledge, mathematical logic, or complex spatiotemporal relationships. They cannot produce explicit inferences or "think" through a problem before generating.

To address the multimodal input challenge, **unified Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs)** have emerged (e.g., DreamLLM, Emu-series, Chameleon, Janus, TokenFlow). These models integrate vision and language, enabling them to process both types of inputs. However, the authors argue that most existing unified MLLMs primarily function as semantic feature extractors and still "fall short of the performance of task-specific architectures" when it comes to complex reasoning tasks.

Concurrently, **Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning** has revolutionized LLMs by enabling them to articulate step-by-step reasoning, leading to improved performance on complex tasks. While CoT has been explored in VLMs and applied to image synthesis (e.g., RPG-DiffusionMaster, GoT), these approaches are often constrained to specific domains like spatial relations, employ inflexible templates, or rely on intricate separation processes. They often guide image generation through textual *layout plans* rather than deep, generalizable reasoning.

A pivotal development in LLMs has been the successful application of **Reinforcement Learning (RL)**, particularly algorithms like Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), to enhance reasoning capabilities. DeepSeek-R1, for instance, demonstrated significant improvements in mathematical and coding tasks by using RL to generate accurate and diverse CoT trajectories. This success has prompted the core question driving this paper: "Can reinforcement learning be leveraged to unleash the reasoning generation capabilities of unified VLMs?"

MindOmni directly addresses this gap by aiming to integrate the strengths of unified MLLMs (multimodal input and understanding) with the advanced reasoning capabilities fostered by CoT and RL, specifically for the purpose of *reasoning-aware image generation*. Unlike previous works that focus on "strengthening the consistency between images and instructions," MindOmni leverages the MLLM's "powerful logical reasoning capabilities for genuine reasoning generation," making its outputs more interpretable and aligned with complex human instructions. This positions MindOmni at the forefront of developing truly intelligent and versatile multimodal AI systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel unified multimodal large language model, named **MindOmni**, that significantly advances the capability of AI systems to perform **reasoning generation** in the context of vision-language tasks. This involves not only understanding complex multimodal inputs but also explicitly generating logical chains of thought that guide the image generation process.

The motivation behind MindOmni stems from several critical limitations observed in existing state-of-the-art models:

1. **Limitations of Current Text-to-Image (T2I) Systems:** While highly capable of generating photorealistic images from text, these systems are fundamentally restricted. They cannot process multimodal inputs (e.g., an image combined with a text query) and critically lack the ability to perform explicit reasoning based on world knowledge, mathematical logic, or complex spatiotemporal concepts. This leads to failures when prompts demand inferential understanding beyond simple descriptive mapping.
2. **Incompleteness of Existing Unified MLLMs:** Although models like Janus-Pro and MetaMorph integrate vision and language for multimodal understanding and generation, they often treat the vision language model primarily as a semantic feature extractor. This approach, while enabling multimodal input, largely overlooks the inherent "powerful logical reasoning capabilities of LLMs." As a result, they still underperform on tasks requiring deep reasoning.
3. **Constraints of Prior Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Approaches in Vision-Language Tasks:** While CoT has been explored to guide image synthesis (e.g., GoT), these methods are often limited to specific applications like spatial relations, relying on inflexible templates or intricate, task-specific separation processes. They don't offer a generalizable framework for complex reasoning across diverse domains.
4. **Leveraging Reinforcement Learning for Reasoning:** Inspired by the success of reinforcement learning (RL) algorithms, particularly GRPO, in enhancing the reasoning abilities of LLMs (e.g., DeepSeek-R1 for mathematics and coding), the authors hypothesized that a similar RL approach could unlock advanced reasoning generation capabilities in unified VLMs. The core motivation is to move beyond implicit understanding to explicit, step-by-step reasoning outputs that are both interpretable and controllable.

In essence, MindOmni aims to create a more intelligent, general-purpose multimodal AI by enabling it to "think" (generate a Chain-of-Thought) before it "acts" (generates or edits an image), thus producing more accurate, contextually appropriate, and semantically aligned visual outputs, especially for tasks requiring complex logical inferences.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MindOmni is built as a unified multimodal large language model designed for both vision understanding and generation. Its core innovation lies in a three-phase training strategy that integrates advanced VLM components with a diffusion module, crucially leveraging a novel reinforcement learning algorithm for reasoning generation.

#### 3.1. Model Architecture (MindOmni)

MindOmni's architecture integrates state-of-the-art components:
- **Vision Language Model (VLM):** It is based on **Qwen2.5-VL**, a powerful pre-trained VLM, which provides robust world knowledge and cross-modal reasoning skills for understanding. It uses a ViT encoder to extract continuous vision features.
- **Diffusion Decoder:** For image generation, MindOmni incorporates a **decoder-only diffusion module** from **OmniGen**. OmniGen is praised for its streamlined, LLM-style network design and promising X2Image performance.
- **Lightweight Connector:** A crucial component bridging the VLM and the diffusion decoder is a **lightweight connector** comprising two standard LLM decoder layers. This connector aligns the VLM's hidden states with the diffusion decoder's input dimension, enabling seamless feature flow between understanding and generation modules.
- **Input Processing:** For vision understanding, MindOmni takes an image (I) and text (T). For generation and editing, it processes both vision features (ViT features for semantics, VAE features for low-level details like texture/layout preservation) and text tokens. A special token triggers the connector to align features, which are then concatenated with time tokens and noise latent tokens for the diffusion process.
- **Reasoning Generation:** A key aspect is the explicit Chain-of-Thought generation guided by a system prompt, where the model autoregressively produces reasoning before the final answer and image generation.

#### 3.2. Three-Phase Training Strategy

The development of MindOmni involves a meticulous three-stage training pipeline:

**Stage 1: Supervised Pre-training for Unified Capabilities**
- **Objective:** To enable the model to initially achieve basic text-to-image generation and editing capabilities. This stage establishes the fundamental connection between the VLM and the diffusion decoder.
- **Data:** Utilizes large-scale open-sourced image-caption pairs (Laion COCO, JourneyDB) and in-house data, along with X2I data pairs (from OmniGen) which include computer vision tasks, in-context learning, multimodal instructions, and subject-driven generation.
- **Training:** Only the **connector** is trained in this stage, ensuring it can effectively translate MLLM semantic representations for the diffusion decoder.
- **Objective Functions:**
 - **Diffusion Loss (L_diffusion):** Based on rectified flow, it optimizes the model to denoise latent noise into real images, minimizing the difference between the model's predicted noise and the actual noise.
 - **KL Distillation Loss (L_distillation):** This loss enforces consistency with a teacher model (μ, likely OmniGen itself) during the denoising process by minimizing the KL divergence between the feature distributions of the student (MindOmni) and the teacher model. This helps mitigate distribution shift and maintain generation quality.

**Stage 2: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) with Chain-of-Thought Instruction Data**
- **Objective:** To prepare the model for reinforcement learning by providing a solid foundation in generating Chain-of-Thought (CoT) content. This stage is crucial for initiating the RL algorithm effectively.
- **Data:** A curated dataset of "coarse-to-fine CoT instruction data" is constructed. This involves using coarse-grained image descriptions as the "answer" content and fine-grained descriptions as the "reasoning" content. High-quality images are retrieved for these pairs using leading text-to-image models (FLUX, Sana). Qwen2.5-VL is used to generate this CoT instruction data.
- **Training:** The **connector and diffusion decoder** are trained again, but this time exclusively with the diffusion loss, focusing on generating images guided by the CoT instructions.

**Stage 3: Reasoning Generation Policy Optimization (RGPO - Reinforcement Learning)**
- **Objective:** To significantly enhance the model's *explicit reasoning generation* capabilities, enabling it to produce precise and logical chains of thought. This is the most innovative part of the methodology.
- **Inspiration:** DeepSeek-R1 and GRPO, adapted for multimodal context.
- **Training Data:** A plain-text training dataset comprising user instructions, ground-truth prompts, and corresponding explanations. A "reasoning generation-oriented system prompt" is designed to guide the model.
- **Rollout Process:** For each user request, the current policy model (π_θ) samples multiple groups (`G`) of results. Each group consists of a reasoning chain (`o_T_i`) and its corresponding generated image (`o_I_i`).
- **Reward Functions:** Two types of rewards are used to evaluate the sampled outputs:
 1. **Format Reward:** A binary reward (1 or 0) that checks if the generated reasoning chain adheres to the expected structure (e.g., enclosed within `<think>` and `<answer>` tags). This encourages well-structured CoT outputs.
 2. **Consistency Reward:** Measures the semantic alignment between the generated image (`o_I_i`) and the ground-truth prompt using cosine similarity derived from CLIP image and text encoders. This ensures the visual output accurately reflects the intent of the prompt.
- **Advantage Calculation:** The advantage (`A_i`) for each sampled output is computed by normalizing its reward against the mean and variance of all rewards in the group, `A_i = (r_i - mean(r)) / Var(r)`.
- **KL Regularizers:** To stabilize training and prevent "catastrophic forgetting" of previously learned knowledge during RL, two independent Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence regularizers are introduced:
 1. **D_T_KL (Text KL):** Penalizes large deviations in the probability distribution of generated text tokens between the current policy and a reference model (π_ref, usually an older version of the policy).
 2. **D_I_KL (Image KL):** Penalizes deviations in the generated image feature distribution (using a feature mapping ϕ from, e.g., CLIP) between the current policy and the reference model.
- **Objective Function (L_GRPO):** The model is optimized by minimizing a PPO-like objective function that includes the calculated advantages, a clipped ratio term (to prevent overly large policy updates), and the two KL regularizers (with hyperparameters β_T and β_I controlling their influence). This ensures stable and effective policy updates towards better reasoning generation.

By combining a robust unified architecture with this multi-stage training strategy, especially the innovative RGPO algorithm, MindOmni aims to overcome the limitations of previous models and achieve superior performance in reasoning-aware multimodal generation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

MindOmni demonstrates impressive performance across various benchmarks, outperforming existing models in both vision understanding and generation, with a particular focus on its novel reasoning generation capabilities.

#### 4.1. Vision Understanding and General Image Generation

- **Vision Understanding (Table 4):** MindOmni was evaluated on MMMU, MMBench, and RealworldQA. Critically, the integration of the text KL regularization term in the RL phase ensured that the model's understanding performance remained nearly identical to its original VLM backbone (Qwen2.5-VL), with only a negligible 0.1% average difference. MindOmni significantly boosted performance over previous unified counterparts, achieving a **10.6% improvement on MMMU over Janus-Pro** and a **9.8% improvement on MMBench over MetaMorph**. This validates its ability to maintain strong comprehension alongside generative capabilities.
- **General Text-to-Image Generation (Table 2):** On standard text-to-image generation benchmarks like GenEval and DPG-Bench, MindOmni achieves state-of-the-art performance for unified MLLMs. It obtained an **83% overall score on GenEval**, surpassing its base OmniGen model and other strong competitors like MetaQuery-XL. On DPG-Bench, it outperformed MetaQuery by 0.4%, further solidifying its general generative prowess. The "MindOmni*" variant, trained with higher-quality data, pushed the GenEval score even higher to 83%.

#### 4.2. Reasoning Generation (WISE Benchmark)

This is where MindOmni showcases its core strength. Evaluated on the WISE benchmark, which assesses reasoning-aware generation across cultural knowledge, spatio-temporal reasoning, and natural science domains, MindOmni demonstrated remarkable superiority (Table 3).
- **Overall SOTA:** MindOmni (with thinking mode) achieved an overall score of **0.71**, significantly outperforming all other methods, including leading generative-only models (e.g., FLUX 0.50, PixArt 0.47) and unified models (e.g., MetaQuery-XL 0.55, BLIP-3o 0.52). The "MindOmni*" variant further improved this to 0.71.
- **Domain-Specific Excellence:** It showed notable margins in specific subcategories, surpassing MetaQuery-XL in both time reasoning and physics reasoning. This consistent advantage across categories reflects the benefit of its joint understanding-generation training and, crucially, its CoT-guided reinforcement learning approach.
- **RL Process Metrics (Fig. 4 & 5):** The reinforcement learning process showed steady increases in both the consistency reward (reflecting better image-text alignment) and completion length (of the generated reasoning). However, the authors wisely note that merely increasing the output thinking content length does not guarantee better end results, highlighting the importance of *quality* and *relevance* of reasoning.

#### 4.3. Qualitative Results

- **Superior Reasoning-Aware Image Generation (Fig. 6 & 7):** Qualitative comparisons against advanced T2I methods (Flux, Sana, SD3.5) and unified systems (Janus-Pro, GPT-4o, Gemini-2.5 Flash) vividly illustrate MindOmni's advantage. For complex prompts involving mathematical logic ("animal with (3+6) lives"), physical perception ("elephant and rabbit on a seesaw"), or world knowledge ("Sydney Opera House when New York is at noon"), MindOmni generated images that were remarkably more accurate and contextually appropriate. For instance, GPT-4o often failed to perform reasoning generation without an explicit "thinking" mode for mathematical and physical reasoning tasks. MindOmni effectively interprets implicit calculations and complex scenarios.
- **Multimodal Input Reasoning:** MindOmni also demonstrates strong reasoning generation with multimodal inputs (e.g., "Show a girl holding this plant in the autumn" given an image of a plant), indicating its seamless integration of visual context into the reasoning process.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Effectiveness of Training Stages (Table 5):**
 - Stage 1 (pre-training) provides foundational generation capabilities.
 - Stage 2 (SFT with CoT instruction data) is critical, providing a significant boost to reasoning generation (WISE score +0.12). Skipping this stage and directly applying RGPO leads to poorer performance, underscoring the necessity of CoT instruction SFT as a strong starting point for RL.
 - Stage 3 (RGPO) provides a further significant boost to WISE, confirming the efficacy of the RL approach for unleashing reasoning generation.
- **Impact of Different Connectors (Table 6):** Using a decoder-based projector (2 layers) for the connector proved more effective than a simple MLP, indicating that a more sophisticated architectural bridge helps convey semantic information from the VLM to the diffusion decoder.
- **RGPO Components (Table 7, 8, 9):**
 - **Consistency Reward:** Crucial for steering reasoning generation effectively, as its removal significantly degrades WISE scores.
 - **KL Divergence:** The inclusion of visual KL divergence (β_I) helps maintain the model's fundamental text-to-image generation abilities while improving reasoning. Optimal values for KL coefficients (β_T, β_I) and group numbers were identified.

In summary, MindOmni not only achieves competitive, often state-of-the-art, performance on general vision understanding and image generation tasks but also sets a new benchmark for reasoning-aware image generation, validating the effectiveness of its novel RGPO reinforcement learning approach guided by Chain-of-Thought principles.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

MindOmni represents a significant step forward in the development of truly intelligent and versatile multimodal AI systems. Its contributions and potential impact are multi-faceted:

1. **Bridging Understanding and Generation with Deep Reasoning:** The most critical significance of MindOmni lies in its ability to effectively bridge the gap between advanced multimodal understanding and high-fidelity image generation, particularly by integrating complex, explicit reasoning. While previous unified models enabled multimodal input, MindOmni pushes the frontier by allowing the model to "think" through a problem, demonstrating mathematical, spatio-temporal, and world-knowledge reasoning before generating visual outputs. This makes the system more intelligent and capable of handling nuanced and abstract user prompts.

2. **Novel Reinforcement Learning for Reasoning Generation:** The introduction of the Reasoning Generation Policy Optimization (RGPO) algorithm is a key methodological innovation. By incorporating multimodal feedback signals (from both image and text features) and dual KL divergence regularizers, RGPO effectively guides the model's policy updates. This demonstrates that RL can be successfully applied to foster explicit, step-by-step reasoning (Chain-of-Thought) in generative multimodal models, going beyond traditional text-only RL applications. This approach makes the reasoning process more interpretable and potentially more robust.

3. **Enhanced Reliability and Interpretability:** By explicitly generating a Chain-of-Thought, MindOmni's outputs become more interpretable. Users can see the logical steps the model took to arrive at a particular image, fostering greater trust and enabling easier debugging or refinement of prompts. This moves towards more "responsible AI" outputs by making the internal reasoning process transparent.

4. **Advancement Across Multiple AI Benchmarks:** MindOmni's superior performance across a wide array of benchmarks – including vision understanding (MMMU, MMBench), general text-to-image generation (GenEval, DPG-Bench), and crucially, reasoning generation (WISE) – highlights its generalizability and robustness. This indicates that the proposed unified architecture and training strategy are highly effective. The ability to excel in both understanding and generation tasks within a single framework is a testament to its holistic design.

5. **Paving the Way for Advanced Multimodal AI Applications:** The capabilities showcased by MindOmni have profound implications for future AI applications. It could power:
 - **Next-generation creative AI tools:** Enabling artists and designers to generate complex scenes or scenarios with highly specific logical or conceptual constraints.
 - **Intelligent assistants:** That can not only understand complex verbal and visual cues but also generate visual responses that reflect deep comprehension and inference.
 - **Educational platforms:** Where AI can visually explain complex concepts (e.g., physics, biology, mathematics) by generating illustrative images based on reasoning.
 - **Interactive AI systems:** Such as those capable of playing complex strategy games like chess or tic-tac-toe by visually responding to moves, opening new avenues for embodied AI.

6. **Foundation for Future Research:** The paper identifies limitations, such as the current reliance on plain-text CoT, suggesting future work on "multimodal CoT processes." This indicates a clear path for extending the reasoning capabilities to involve visual elements directly within the reasoning chain itself. The open-sourcing of the code ("All codes will be made public") will undoubtedly accelerate further research and development in this exciting area, allowing the broader AI community to build upon MindOmni's foundational advancements.

In essence, MindOmni pushes the boundaries of unified multimodal AI by endowing models with explicit and robust reasoning capabilities, moving closer to truly intelligent systems that can understand, reason, and generate in a human-like manner.
