# Fast Flow-based Visuomotor Policies via Conditional Optimal Transport Couplings

> [!summary] Summary
> Sochopoulos et al. present COT Policy, a Flow Matching methodology that trains visuomotor policies capable of high-quality, multimodal action generation with 1-2 inference steps. This approach reduces inference latency by approximately 10x compared to Diffusion Policy while maintaining training efficiency, enabling real-time robot control.

## Problem

- Generative models like Diffusion Models and Flow Matching, while effective for visuomotor robot policies, suffer from high inference latency due to the many integration steps required, limiting real-time application.
- Existing acceleration techniques for generative models often introduce significant training overhead or compromise the ability to capture diverse action trajectories.
- Directly applying unconditional Optimal Transport (OT) to conditional generative tasks results in biased action generation because it fails to account for the observation-dependent nature of robot actions.

## Method

- ==Proposes Conditional Optimal Transport (COT)== couplings within Flow Matching, where the Optimal Transport cost function explicitly incorporates condition variables to pair noise and target samples based on both their values and their conditional context.
- Employs an encoder for high-dimensional robot observations, followed by K-means clustering to discretize these features, enabling practical computation of the conditional Optimal Transport plan.
- Trains the conditional vector field v_θ using raw observations, while the discretized conditions are exclusively used to compute the unbiased optimal transport coupling, maintaining full information for policy execution.

## Results

- Achieved an **81.8%** average success rate on simulated robot manipulation tasks with just 2 inference steps, outperforming Diffusion Policy (DP) at 20 Network Function Evaluations (NFE), indicating a roughly **10x** speed-up.
- Demonstrated superior preservation of action multimodality, generating diverse and successful trajectories in both synthetic and robotic tasks, even with 1-2 inference steps.
- In real-world robot experiments with a KUKA IIWA 14, COT Policy with 1-2 steps showed higher success rates and smoother, real-time motions compared to CFM, successfully reproducing multimodal task solutions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating conditional information into Optimal Transport couplings via a modified cost function is essential for learning unbiased and accurate flow paths in conditional generative models.
> - Discretizing high-dimensional continuous observations through clustering provides a practical and robust method for computing conditional Optimal Transport plans within mini-batches.
> - The COT Policy methodology achieves highly straightened integration paths, allowing for effective 1-2 step inference that preserves action multimodality without increasing training complexity.
