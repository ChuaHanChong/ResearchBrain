# Fast Flow-based Visuomotor Policies via Conditional Optimal Transport Couplings

> [!summary] Summary
> Sochopoulos et al. present COT Policy, a Flow Matching methodology that trains visuomotor policies capable of high-quality, multimodal action generation with 1-2 inference steps. This approach reduces inference latency by approximately 10x compared to Diffusion Policy while maintaining training efficiency, enabling real-time robot control.

## Problem

- Generative models like Diffusion Models and Flow Matching, while effective for visuomotor robot policies, suffer from high inference latency due to the many integration steps required, limiting real-time application.
- Existing acceleration techniques for generative models often introduce significant training overhead or compromise the ability to capture diverse action trajectories.
- Directly applying unconditional Optimal Transport (OT) to conditional generative tasks results in biased action generation because it fails to account for the observation-dependent nature of robot actions.

## Method

- ==Proposes Conditional Optimal Transport (COT)== couplings within Flow Matching, where the Optimal Transport cost function explicitly incorporates condition variables to pair noise and target samples based on both their values and their conditional context.
- Employs an encoder for high-dimensional robot observations, followed by K-means clustering to discretize these features, enabling practical computation of the conditional Optimal Transport plan.
- Trains the conditional vector field v_θ using raw observations, while the discretized conditions are exclusively used to compute the unbiased optimal transport coupling, maintaining full information for policy execution.

## Results

- Achieved an **81.8%** average success rate on simulated robot manipulation tasks with just 2 inference steps, outperforming Diffusion Policy (DP) at 20 Network Function Evaluations (NFE), indicating a roughly **10x** speed-up.
- Demonstrated superior preservation of action multimodality, generating diverse and successful trajectories in both synthetic and robotic tasks, even with 1-2 inference steps.
- In real-world robot experiments with a KUKA IIWA 14, COT Policy with 1-2 steps showed higher success rates and smoother, real-time motions compared to CFM, successfully reproducing multimodal task solutions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating conditional information into Optimal Transport couplings via a modified cost function is essential for learning unbiased and accurate flow paths in conditional generative models.
> - Discretizing high-dimensional continuous observations through clustering provides a practical and robust method for computing conditional Optimal Transport plans within mini-batches.
> - The COT Policy methodology achieves highly straightened integration paths, allowing for effective 1-2 step inference that preserves action multimodality without increasing training complexity.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of generative artificial intelligence, imitation learning, and real-time robotic control.

**Generative Models in Robotics:** Over the past few years, continuous-time generative models, such as Diffusion Models (DMs) and Flow Matching (FM) policies, have demonstrated remarkable success in imitation learning for visuomotor robot policies. These models excel at capturing complex, high-dimensional, and often multimodal distributions of robot actions conditioned on observations. This capability is particularly advantageous in scenarios where a task can be achieved in multiple ways, or where human demonstrations naturally exhibit variability. This work builds directly on the success of these models, particularly Flow Matching (CFM), as an alternative to traditional explicit policy learning methods like Behavior Cloning (BC) that often struggle with multimodality.

**The Challenge of Real-time Inference:** Despite their prowess in modeling complex distributions, the primary limitation of DMs and FM policies, especially for real-time robotic applications, is their computational cost during inference. Generating an action sequence requires numerically integrating an Ordinary Differential Equation (ODE) or Stochastic Differential Equation (SDE) over many discretization steps. This leads to high latency, which can result in intermittent robot motions or an inability to perform tasks in dynamic environments. This paper directly tackles this critical bottleneck.

**Existing Acceleration Techniques and Their Limitations:** The broader machine learning community, especially in image generation, has seen significant efforts to accelerate inference in continuous-time generative models through methods like alternative samplers (e.g., DDIM for DMs) and distillation techniques (e.g., Consistency Models). While these methods reduce the number of inference steps, they often come with trade-offs. Distillation, in particular, often requires a trained "teacher" model and involves complex, multi-phase training procedures or significantly increased training steps, making it computationally expensive in its own right and sometimes compromising the ability to capture diverse action trajectories.

**Optimal Transport in Flow Matching:** A related line of work explores the use of Optimal Transport (OT) couplings within Flow Matching to "straighten" the learned integration paths. By finding an optimal mapping between a noise distribution and the target data distribution, OT can encourage the ODE solver to follow more direct trajectories, thereby potentially reducing the number of integration steps required for high-quality samples. Previous work, like Tong et al., explored this for unconditional Flow Matching (OT-CFM).

**The Novelty: Conditional Optimal Transport for Robotics:** The critical gap this paper addresses is the effective application of OT couplings in *conditional* generative modeling, especially when conditions are high-dimensional and continuous (like robot observations). Naive application of unconditional OT in conditional settings leads to biased flows because the coupling process ignores the conditioning variables, resulting in incorrect sample generation for specific conditions. This paper introduces Conditional Optimal Transport (COT) couplings, specifically adapted for visuomotor policies, to overcome this challenge. It provides a method that aims to achieve fast, high-quality, and multimodal action generation *without* the increased training complexity of distillation methods, making it a significant advancement for real-time robotic control.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The paper's key objectives and motivations stem from the inherent tension between the high-performance capabilities of continuous-time generative models for robotic policy learning and their computational limitations for real-time deployment.

**Primary Objective:**
To introduce a novel Flow Matching (FM) methodology, termed COT Policy, that enables the training of high-quality, *few-step* visuomotor policies while preserving action multimodality and maintaining training efficiency comparable to existing FM techniques.

**Core Motivations:**

1. **Addressing High Inference Latency of Generative Policies:** State-of-the-art generative models like Diffusion Policy (DP) and vanilla Flow Matching (CFM) have shown remarkable ability to capture complex, multimodal distributions of robot actions. However, their inference process, which involves numerically integrating an ODE or SDE, is computationally expensive. This often requires many Network Function Evaluations (NFE) (e.g., 20+ steps), leading to high latency. This latency directly impacts real-world applicability, causing issues like intermittent robot motions or an inability to operate in dynamic or time-sensitive environments (e.g., manipulating deformable objects). The primary motivation is to drastically reduce this inference cost to enable real-time robot control.

2. **Overcoming Drawbacks of Existing Acceleration Methods:** While methods like distillation exist to accelerate generative models (achieving one-step inference), they typically incur significant training overhead. This often involves multiple training phases or a substantially increased number of training steps, which can be prohibitive for large policies and rich, high-dimensional datasets common in robotics. The authors are motivated to find an acceleration method that does *not* demand this increased training complexity.

3. **Correctly Applying Optimal Transport in Conditional Settings:** Previous work demonstrated that Optimal Transport (OT) couplings can "straighten" the learned paths in Flow Matching, improving few-step inference for *unconditional* generative tasks. However, the authors identify a critical issue: naively applying unconditional OT to *conditional* generative tasks (where robot actions depend on observations) leads to biased flows and degraded performance. The motivation is to extend OT principles to properly account for conditional distributions, thereby allowing for straightened, accurate, and unbiased conditional action generation.

4. **Preserving Action Multimodality:** A key advantage of generative models is their ability to capture and reproduce the diverse ways an expert might solve a task. This multimodality is crucial for robust and flexible robot behavior. The paper aims to ensure that its acceleration strategy does not compromise this ability, unlike some simplified single-step models that might only capture the conditional mean, losing critical behavioral diversity.

In essence, the paper seeks to achieve the "holy grail" of fast, high-quality, and diverse robot action generation with a low-cost training paradigm, by innovatively adapting Optimal Transport for conditional visuomotor policy learning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the proposed methodology, named COT Policy, lies in extending Flow Matching (FM) by incorporating Conditional Optimal Transport (COT) couplings to ensure straighter and more accurate integration paths, especially in conditional generative tasks prevalent in robotics.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries: Conditional Flow Matching (CFM)

The authors first set the stage by reviewing Conditional Flow Matching. In CFM, a neural ODE `dx = v_θ(t, x) dt` is trained to transform an initial noise distribution `p_0` into a target data distribution `p_1` (which, in a conditional setting, is `p_1(· | c)` where `c` is the condition). The training involves minimizing a stochastic regression objective (`L_CFM`) that measures the difference between the learned vector field `v_θ` and a target vector field `x_1 - x_0` derived from a linear interpolant `x(t) = tx_1 + (1-t)x_0`. A key aspect of CFM is the "coupling distribution" `q(x_0, x_1)` which pairs samples from `p_0` and `p_1`.

#### 3.2. The Role of Optimal Transport (OT) Couplings (and their limitations)

- **Unconditional OT-CFM:** For unconditional tasks, replacing the independent coupling `q(x_0, x_1) = q(x_0)q(x_1)` with an Optimal Transport (OT) plan (specifically, the 2-Wasserstein OT plan for squared Euclidean cost) between `p_0` and `p_1` has been shown to reduce objective variance and, critically, induce straighter integration curves. This allows for accurate ODE integration with fewer steps.
- **Challenge in Conditional Tasks:** The paper highlights that this naive application of OT-CFM fails in *conditional* generative modeling (i.e., learning `p_1(· | c)`). If the OT plan is computed between the marginals of `p_0` and `p_1` (ignoring `c`), it can lead to biased generation. For example, if a noise sample `x_0` is paired with a target `x_1` based purely on spatial proximity, but they correspond to different conditions `c_0` and `c_1`, the learned flow will incorrectly guide `x_0` towards `x_1` for condition `c_1`, even if `x_0` should be mapped to a different `x_1'` for its *true* condition `c_0`. This is visually demonstrated in Fig. 1 (Two Moons example), where OT-CFM produces biased samples.

#### 3.3. Proposed Solution: Conditional Optimal Transport (COT) Couplings

To address the conditional task challenge, the authors propose a novel approach for constructing the coupling distribution `q(x_0, x_1)`:

1. **Enriching the Coupling with Conditions:** Instead of coupling `x_0` and `x_1` directly, they define a cost function for Optimal Transport that explicitly includes the condition variables `c`:
 `c((x_0, c_0), (x_1, c_1)) = ||x_0 - x_1||^2 + ||γ(c_0 - c_1)||^2`
 Here, `(x_0, c_0)` are noise-condition pairs, and `(x_1, c_1)` are target-condition pairs. The hyperparameter `γ` (gamma) controls the importance of matching conditions. A large `γ` prioritizes pairing `x_0` with `x_1` that have similar conditions `c_0` and `c_1`. This effectively computes a "conditional OT plan" where samples are paired if they are both spatially close *and* belong to similar conditional contexts. The `c_0` (noise conditions) are generated by a random permutation of the observed target conditions `c_1`, ensuring matched marginal distributions.

2. **Handling Continuous and High-Dimensional Conditions (Robotics Specific):**
 - **Encoder (E):** For raw robot observations `o` (e.g., RGB images, proprioception), a vision encoder `E` is used to extract relevant features `e = E(o)`. In their implementation, `E` is a PCA transformation for images (learned offline) and a direct pass-through for proprioceptive data.
 - **Discretizer (Q):** Since OT computation can be challenging with arbitrary continuous conditions (especially for minibatch OT where matching identical conditions is difficult), the encoded features `e` are discretized into a finite set of clusters `c = Q(e)`. K-means clustering is employed for this purpose, with `K` being the number of clusters. The centroids of these clusters serve as the discretized condition values.
 - **Training Pipeline (Algorithm 1):**
 - For each training batch, target actions `a` and observations `o` are sampled.
 - Noise samples `x_0` are drawn from `p_0`.
 - Observations `o` are processed through `E` and `Q` to get discretized conditions `c_a`.
 - Noise conditions `c_x0` are generated by randomly permuting `c_a`.
 - The minibatch OT plan `π` is computed between `(x_0, c_x0)` and `(a, c_a)` using the modified cost function.
 - The actual `x_0` used in the CFM loss is then selected based on this `π`, paired with `a`.
 - Crucially, the learned conditional vector field `v_θ` itself is conditioned on the *unprocessed, raw observations* `o`, maintaining full information, while the discretized `c` is used *only for the OT coupling calculation*.

#### 3.4. Training Complexity

A key advantage is that COT Policy maintains the same training complexity as vanilla CFM and Diffusion Policy. The added steps of dimensionality reduction (PCA) and clustering (K-means) are performed on batches and are designed to be GPU-accelerated, incurring negligible computational overhead compared to the forward and backward passes of the large neural network (`v_θ`). This distinguishes it from distillation methods that typically require more intensive training regimes.

In summary, COT Policy smartly integrates conditional information into the optimal transport coupling process, allowing Flow Matching to learn much straighter paths that are accurate for specific conditions, even with very few inference steps, all while maintaining efficient training.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate that COT Policy significantly outperforms existing flow-based and diffusion-based methods in terms of inference speed, success rate, and multimodality preservation, across both synthetic and complex robotic tasks.

#### 4.1. Low-Dimensional Distribution Learning (Synthetic Tasks - Appendix D.1)

- **Quantitative Performance:** On tasks like generating a 2D "two moons" distribution or a 1D "fork" distribution from noise, COT-CFM achieves substantially lower 2-Wasserstein distances (a measure of distribution similarity) with only 1-2 Euler integration steps compared to CFM and OT-CFM. Remarkably, 2-step COT-CFM with an Euler solver even outperforms CFM and OT-CFM when they use more than 60 steps with an adaptive, higher-order Dormand-Prince solver, highlighting its superior path straightness.
- **Qualitative Visualization (Fig. 1 & 8):** COT-CFM is the only method that accurately reconstructs the multimodal distributions with NFE=1. CFM, with few steps, tends to generate samples around the conditional mean, losing multimodality. OT-CFM yields biased samples due to its inability to correctly handle conditions in the coupling. This confirms the theoretical claim that COT-CFM effectively balances straight paths with unbiased conditional generation.

#### 4.2. Simulated Robot Manipulation Tasks (Table 1, Fig. 2)

- **Superior Success Rate with Few Steps:** On a diverse set of six simulated robot manipulation tasks (MimicGen, Push-T, Ball-in-Cup), 2-step COT Policy consistently achieves the highest average success rate (81.8%). This outperforms CFM and OT-CFM (even with NFE=4), and crucially, it surpasses Diffusion Policy (DP) even when DP uses 20 Network Function Evaluations (NFE), demonstrating a significant speed-up (approximately 10x faster than DP at 20 NFE for comparable or better performance).
- **OT-CFM's Deficiency:** As observed in synthetic tasks, OT-CFM consistently underperforms other methods in robot tasks, confirming that ignoring observation conditions during coupling leads to biased and ineffective policies.
- **Adaflow Comparison:** Adaflow, an adaptive CFM sampler, performs worse than 2-step CFM and COT Policy, despite its adaptive nature, possibly due to its maximum 2-step constraint not aligning with its internal step count.

#### 4.3. Evaluation of Multimodality (Trajectory Variance - Fig. 3 & D.2)

- **Diverse Action Generation:** COT Policy consistently exhibits higher Trajectory Variance (TV) across tasks compared to CFM, especially at low NFE (1-2 steps). TV, defined using Dynamic Time Warping distance to the barycenter of trajectories, measures the diversity of generated action plans. This finding confirms that COT Policy successfully preserves the multimodality of action distributions even with highly accelerated inference, which is vital for robust and flexible robot control. The visualizations further support this, showing COT Policy generating distinct, successful strategies for maze navigation.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies (Fig. 4, 5, Table 6)

- **Effect of Cluster Number (K):** The number of clusters `K` is a critical hyperparameter. Too few clusters (e.g., K=8) leads to performance degradation similar to OT-CFM, as observations are not sufficiently differentiated. Optimal performance is observed when `K` is close to the batch size (e.g., K=64 for batch size 64), which allows for sufficient diversity of conditions within a batch while ensuring reasonable pairing opportunities. As `K` approaches the dataset size, COT Policy's behavior asymptotically resembles CFM.
- **Importance of Clustering (Table 6):** Clustering significantly improves success rates compared to a variant of COT Policy without clustering. This highlights that discretization is crucial for making the conditional OT formulation practical and robust, avoiding the need for extensive tuning of the `γ` hyperparameter.
- **NFE and Solver Type:** While higher NFE generally improves performance for all methods, COT Policy's gains with increasing NFE are more subtle, reinforcing its inherent efficiency at low NFE. The midpoint solver consistently outperforms the Euler solver for the same NFE, indicating the choice of ODE solver is still important for achieving the best results with few steps.
- **Training Complexity:** Training convergence plots (Fig. 5) show that COT Policy converges at approximately the same rate as CFM and DP, validating the claim of negligible computational overhead during training.

#### 4.5. Real-World Robot Experiments (Table 2, Fig. 6 & 7)

- **Real-time Performance and Robustness:** On three real-world manipulation tasks (push-T, cup-stacking, cup-in-drawer) using a KUKA IIWA 14 robot, COT Policy with 1-2 inference steps consistently outperforms CFM policies in both success rate (SR) and Time To Completion (TTC).
- **Smoother Motions:** CFM policies often exhibited intermittent or jerky motions due to the longer inference times and insufficient action generation quality at low NFE. In contrast, COT Policy enabled smoother, more continuous robot operation.
- **Multimodal Real-world Solutions:** COT Policy successfully reproduces distinct modes of solving real tasks, such as pushing a T-block both clockwise and anti-clockwise (Fig. 7), even with 1-step Euler inference, demonstrating its ability to leverage multimodal human demonstrations effectively in physical systems.

In conclusion, the findings robustly support the paper's claims: COT Policy achieves high-quality, multimodal action generation with significantly faster inference (1-2 steps) than leading generative models, all without increasing training complexity, making it highly suitable for real-time robot control.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of COT Policy represents a significant step forward in making advanced generative models practical for real-time robotic control, with broad potential impacts across research and application domains.

1. **Enabling Real-time Deployment of Generative Policies:** The most immediate and critical impact is the reduction of inference latency for diffusion and flow-based policies to just 1-2 network evaluations. This addresses a major bottleneck that previously limited the applicability of these powerful models in dynamic, real-time robotic systems. Robots can now execute policies with smoother, more continuous motions, responding swiftly to environmental changes without the "stop-and-go" behavior often associated with high-latency controllers. This is crucial for complex manipulation tasks, human-robot interaction, and any scenario requiring fluid, responsive behavior.

2. **Efficient Acceleration Without Training Overhead:** Unlike distillation techniques, which often require extensive multi-phase training or significantly longer training times, COT Policy achieves its acceleration with negligible additional computational overhead during training. This makes it a more accessible and resource-efficient solution for researchers and practitioners, especially when dealing with large datasets and complex neural network architectures. It allows for the benefits of fast inference without the high cost of model compression or knowledge transfer.

3. **Preservation of Multimodality for Robust Control:** A key strength of generative models is their ability to capture and reproduce diverse action trajectories, reflecting multiple valid ways to achieve a goal. COT Policy successfully retains this multimodality even at very low inference steps. This is vital for robot robustness, allowing policies to adapt to varying initial conditions, unexpected perturbations, or simply exhibiting more natural, human-like behavior. In tasks with inherent ambiguities or multiple solutions, this capability enhances the robot's flexibility and success rate.

4. **Advancing Conditional Generative Modeling:** The methodology for integrating conditional information into Optimal Transport couplings via dimensionality reduction and clustering is a novel contribution that extends the theoretical and practical reach of Flow Matching. This approach provides a robust way to learn conditional distributions effectively, even when conditions are high-dimensional and continuous, which is common in sensor-rich robotic environments. This framework could inspire similar advancements in other conditional generative tasks beyond robotics.

5. **Broad Applicability in Robotics:** The demonstrated success across diverse simulated manipulation tasks, maze navigation, and real-world robot experiments suggests that COT Policy is a generalizable approach. It can likely be applied to a wide array of visuomotor control problems, from industrial automation to service robotics, where responsive and adaptive behavior is paramount. The modular design, using a vision encoder and a clustering module, also makes it adaptable to different sensor modalities (e.g., point clouds, force-torque data).

**Potential Future Directions and Impact:**
The work also opens several promising avenues for future research. Exploring more sophisticated and adaptive encoders/discretizers for conditions could further enhance performance and generalization. Combining COT couplings with single-phase distillation techniques might lead to even better one-step performance while minimizing training cost. Furthermore, integrating pre-trained visual representations (PVRs) within this framework could improve policy generalization to novel environments and objects.

In conclusion, COT Policy provides a practical, efficient, and high-performing solution for deploying advanced generative visuomotor policies in real-time robotic systems. By bridging the gap between sophisticated generative modeling and the stringent demands of real-world robot control, this work significantly accelerates the transition of cutting-edge AI research into tangible robotic capabilities.
