# PhysInOne: Visual Physics Learning and Reasoning in One Suite

> [!summary] Summary
> PhysInOne introduces a large-scale synthetic dataset of 2 million dynamic videos across 153,810 3D scenes, encompassing 71 diverse physical phenomena with comprehensive ground-truth annotations. The dataset serves to advance visual physics learning and reasoning in AI models, demonstrating that fine-tuning can improve physical plausibility in generated content while exposing current models' limitations in generalizing 3D physical motion and inferring complex physical properties.

## Problem

- There is a scarcity of large-scale, high-quality, physically-grounded datasets for training AI models in visual physics learning and reasoning.
- Current AI models, including generative AI, often produce physically implausible outputs due to a lack of understanding of real-world physics and limited training data.
- Existing datasets are typically narrow in scope, focusing on single physical phenomena or simplified scenarios, thus hindering the development of general-purpose physics-aware AI systems.

## Method

- Developed **PhysInOne**, a ==synthetic dataset== featuring 2 million dynamic videos from 153,810 unique 3D scenes, systematically covering 71 basic physical phenomena across mechanics, optics, fluid dynamics, and magnetism.
- Employed a multi-stage generation pipeline involving extensive 3D asset collection, creating multi-physics and multi-object scenes, and utilizing multi-engine physical dynamics simulation (e.g., Unreal Engine's Chaos Physics, SPH, MPM) to ensure physical accuracy.
- Provided comprehensive ground-truth annotations for each scene, including 3D geometry, semantic object masks, 3D motion trajectories, detailed physical properties (e.g., friction, density, viscosity), and manually curated text descriptions.

## Results

- Fine-tuning on PhysInOne led to improvements in physical plausibility, with video generation models like SVD and Wan2.2 showing increased PMF scores, particularly for magnetism and fluid dynamics.
- Future frame prediction tasks revealed a substantial performance drop for predicting from novel viewpoints compared to seen viewpoints, highlighting difficulties in generalizing 3D physical motion modeling.
- Physical property estimation methods were able to infer plausible properties but showed considerable error ranges and lacked precision in accurately reproducing ground-truth dynamics during resimulation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Fine-tuning existing video generation models on PhysInOne generally improves their Physical Motion Fidelity (PMF), with SVD's PMF increasing from 2.753 to 3.147 with Supervised Fine-Tuning, indicating enhanced acquisition of physics knowledge.
> - Current models exhibit significant challenges in generalizing 3D physical motion modeling to novel viewpoints (e.g., DefGS PMF dropped from 3.980 to 3.347 for seen vs. novel views) and accurately inferring physical properties for complex materials.
> - While motion transfer methods maintain visual fidelity, they largely fail to accurately replicate intricate, multi-object physical dynamics, suggesting a fundamental limitation in capturing and propagating complex physical behaviors.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of advanced artificial intelligence (AI) systems necessitates large-scale, high-quality datasets for training and evaluation. While domains such as natural language processing and 2D computer vision have benefited significantly from massive datasets like Common Crawl, ImageNet, and LAION-5B, the field of visual physics learning and reasoning has experienced a comparative scarcity of suitable data. Existing datasets relevant to physical dynamics, properties, and understanding are often limited in scale, diversity of physical phenomena, object complexity, or background realism. Many focus on a narrow set of physical interactions, use oversimplified object shapes, or employ clean, uniform backgrounds.

This limitation contributes to current AI models, including large language models (LLMs), vision-language models (VLMs), and generative AI, exhibiting deficiencies in comprehending and simulating the physical world. For instance, AI-generated videos frequently display physical implausibilities, such as objects moving unnaturally or violating basic physical laws.

PhysInOne positions itself to address this gap by introducing a substantially larger and more diverse synthetic dataset designed specifically for visual physics learning. By systematically encompassing a broader range of physical phenomena, object types, and environmental complexities than previous works, PhysInOne aims to serve as a foundational resource. It seeks to enable the development and rigorous evaluation of next-generation AI models capable of exhibiting more physically plausible behaviors and a deeper understanding of real-world physics, thereby contributing to advancements in areas such as physics-aware video generation, future frame prediction, physical property estimation, and embodied AI.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop and introduce PhysInOne, a large-scale synthetic dataset designed to overcome the current data scarcity in visual physics learning for AI systems. The motivation for this objective stems from several identified limitations in the current state of AI and existing datasets:

- **Lack of Physically-Grounded Training Data:** Despite the success of large-scale datasets in other AI domains, there is a recognized deficiency of high-quality, physically-grounded data for training AI models to understand and simulate the physical world. Existing datasets are typically limited to dozens, hundreds, or thousands of examples, restricting models to a narrow range of physical phenomena and often simplifying real-world complexity.
- **Physical Implausibility in Generated Content:** State-of-the-art generative AI models, particularly in video generation, frequently produce outputs that, while visually appealing, violate fundamental physical laws. This indicates a gap in their ability to comprehend the underlying physics governing object interactions and environmental dynamics. Addressing this requires training data that explicitly models and enforces physical correctness.
- **Limited Scope of Existing Datasets:** Current datasets for visual physics often focus on a single physical phenomenon (e.g., object stability, fluid flow) or simplified scenarios with basic object shapes and uniform backgrounds. They frequently lack comprehensive ground-truth annotations critical for quantitative analysis of physical reasoning. This narrow scope hinders the development of general-purpose AI systems that can reason about diverse, multi-object, multi-physics interactions in complex environments.
- **Need for Robust Benchmarking:** The absence of a large-scale, diverse dataset also limits the ability to rigorously benchmark AI models' understanding of physics. Existing benchmarks often rely on qualitative assessments or focus on question-answering tasks in constrained scenarios, rather than providing the detailed visual and physical annotations necessary for quantitative evaluation of physics-grounded learning.

Therefore, the key motivation is to provide a comprehensive, richly annotated dataset that:
1. **Increases Scale and Diversity:** Offers a significantly larger number of dynamic 3D scenes and videos, covering a broad spectrum of everyday physical phenomena and multi-object interactions.
2. **Enhances Realism and Complexity:** Features complex objects and backgrounds, moving beyond simplified shapes and clean environments to better reflect real-world scenarios.
3. **Provides Comprehensive Ground-Truth Annotations:** Includes detailed 3D geometry, semantics, dynamic motion, physical properties, and textual descriptions to support various learning tasks and quantitative evaluation.
4. **Enables Physics-Aware AI Development:** Facilitates the training and fine-tuning of AI models to improve their physical plausibility in generative tasks, accuracy in future state prediction, and capability in inferring physical properties.
5. **Establishes a New Benchmark:** Serves as a challenging testbed to expose current limitations in AI models' physical reasoning abilities and drive future research towards more general-purpose world models and embodied AI.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The construction of the PhysInOne dataset follows a multi-stage pipeline designed to generate a large volume of richly annotated, physically-grounded dynamic 3D scenes and videos.

#### 3.1. Physical Phenomena and Laws Identification

The authors identified 71 basic physical phenomena from four core areas of everyday physics: mechanics, optics, fluid dynamics, and magnetism. These phenomena are governed by fundamental physical laws such as Newton’s Laws, Conservation of Momentum, Hooke’s Law, and Bernoulli’s Principle. Thermodynamics and acoustics were excluded due to their often invisible nature or requirement for non-visual sensory data. Each identified phenomenon represents a conceptual occurrence rather than a specific 3D scene at this stage.

#### 3.2. 3D Asset Collection

To populate the scenes, a comprehensive set of 3D assets was collected from platforms like Sketchfab, FAB, ShareTextures, and BlenderKit, ensuring compatible licensing. This collection includes:
- **3D Objects:** 2,231 common objects across approximately 163 categories, relevant to physical interactions. These include solid objects, interactable objects (with movable parts implemented as Blueprints in Unreal Engine), destructible objects (breakable glasses implemented as Geometry Collections), deformable objects (simulated using MPM), granular objects (simulated using MPM), and liquids (simulated using SPH).
- **Materials:** 623 materials across five categories (plastic, metal, wood, stone, fabric), each with adjustable physical properties (e.g., friction coefficients, density, restitution) to enable scene variation.
- **3D Backgrounds:** 528 distinct 3D backgrounds covering various indoor and outdoor scenarios to provide realistic environments.

#### 3.3. Multiphysics Multiobject 3D Scene Creation

The identified phenomena and collected assets are combined to create 153,810 unique dynamic 3D scenes through a four-step process:
- **Step 1: Emulating Multiphysics:** 3,284 distinct physical activities are defined by combining the 71 basic phenomena. These include single-physics activities (71), double-physics activities (943 carefully selected combinations), and triple-physics activities (2,270 selected combinations). The average number of objects per scene increases with activity complexity (3.9 for single, 6.3 for double, 7.8 for triple-physics activities).
- **Step 2: Setting Up Backgrounds:** Physically plausible backgrounds are selected and set up for each target activity.
- **Step 3: Placing Multiobjects:** Multiple 3D assets are selected and placed into the background for each activity. For scenes involving liquids or special materials, specific configurations (e.g., containers, initial object shapes, boundary objects) are designed.
- **Step 4: Varying Materials:** Materials of the objects within each scene are altered, and their physical properties are varied to create numerous scene variations.

#### 3.4. Physical Dynamics Simulation

To ensure physical accuracy, multiple simulation algorithms and engines are employed:
- **Chaos Physics (Unreal Engine 5):** Handles rigid body dynamics, fracture and destruction, wind forces (modeled as decaying force fields), laser interactions (reflection based on surface normals and material properties), and magnetic forces (modeled using dipole field formulations).
- **Smoothed Particle Hydrodynamics (SPH) (Doriflow):** Utilized for liquid simulations (Newtonian and non-Newtonian fluids). It computes pressure and viscous forces between particles and handles fluid-object interactions.
- **Material Point Method (MPM) (Taichi Lang):** Employed for simulating deformable objects (e.g., plasticine) and granular substances (e.g., sand). It implements various constitutive laws and plasticity projections for elastic solids, plasticine, Newtonian/non-Newtonian fluids, and granular materials, using a full MPM pipeline with Taichi and integrated into Blender.

The simulators’ errors are acknowledged and stated as controlled.

#### 3.5. Camera Sampling and Video Rendering

For each dynamic 3D scene, a total of 13 videos are rendered:
- **Static Cameras:** 12 cameras are uniformly placed across the upper hemisphere at elevation angles of 30° to 60°, with positions varying across scenes to ensure comprehensive, unobstructed multiview coverage.
- **Monocular Camera:** A single moving camera records a challenging monocular video. Three trajectory sampling strategies (Linear Drift, Sinusoidal Interpolation, Circular Loop) are used to generate diverse, smooth camera paths.
All videos are rendered at 1120×1120 resolution at 30 FPS (60 FPS for laser activities), with an average duration of 5.2 seconds, resulting in a total of 2 million dynamic videos. Approximately 75% of videos are longer than 5 seconds.

#### 3.6. Annotation and Dataset Splitting

Comprehensive ground-truth annotations are provided for each scene:
- **Geometry:** Depth images (1120x1120 resolution) and 3D meshes of all assets.
- **Semantics:** Per-frame object masks (1120x1120 resolution) with unique IDs.
- **Motion:** 3D trajectories (position and rotation) of dynamic objects at every time step.
- **Physical Properties:** Key material parameters (dynamic/static friction coefficient, density, restitution coefficient) stored in JSON format, linked to object IDs. For special materials, parameters like Young's modulus, Poisson's ratio, viscosity, yield stress, and friction angle are provided.
- **Text Descriptions:** Manually curated paragraphs (average 64 words) describing visual elements and physical activities, refined using Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B-Thinking to ensure grammatical correctness, clarity, and physical informativeness.

The dataset is partitioned into train, validation, and held-out test sets using an 8:1:1 ratio. Care is taken to ensure that all 3D assets appear exclusively in one partition to prevent data leakage and that the proportion of each physical phenomenon is preserved across splits. Dataset quality is maintained through a structured workflow involving standardized development and independent validation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates PhysInOne's utility across four application areas, demonstrating its effectiveness and highlighting current limitations in AI models' physical reasoning capabilities.

#### 4.1. Physics-aware Video Generation

- **Objective:** To improve the physical plausibility of videos generated by foundation models.
- **Methodology:** Three representative video generation models (SVD-XT, CogVideoX-1.5-5B, Wan2.2-5B) were fine-tuned using a subset of 83,650 text-video pairs from PhysInOne. Three fine-tuning techniques were explored: Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA), Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), and Final Layer Tuning (FLT). Models were evaluated on 772 test text-video pairs.
- **Metrics:** A new metric, Physical Motion Fidelity (PMF), was introduced. PMF quantifies kinematic discrepancies by comparing the normalized energy spectra of the generated and reference videos' Discrete Fourier Transforms (DFT), making it invariant to spatiotemporal shifts and brightness changes. Higher PMF indicates greater physical motion fidelity. Traditional FVD and a human user study (ranking physical plausibility) were also conducted.
- **Results:** Fine-tuning on PhysInOne generally led to improvements in PMF scores across SVD and Wan2.2 models, particularly with SFT, suggesting enhanced physical knowledge acquisition. For instance, SVD's PMF increased from 2.753 to 3.147 with SFT, and Wan2.2's increased from 2.041 to 2.978 with SFT. FVD scores generally decreased (improved) after fine-tuning. Human ratings of physical plausibility showed correlation with the PMF metric. The right part of Table 2 indicated that models exhibited higher PMF in magnetism and fluid dynamics, but lower scores in mechanics and optics, suggesting that these areas present greater challenges for current models.

#### 4.2. Future Frame Prediction

- **Objective:** To assess models' ability to predict future video frames, requiring comprehension of underlying physical dynamics. Two tasks were defined: long-term and continuous short-term prediction.
- **Methodology:**
 - **Long-term Prediction:** Models (TiNeuVox, DefGS, FreeGave, TRACE as 4D modeling methods; ExtDM, MAGI-1 as video prediction methods) were tasked with predicting the second half of a 3D scene (∼2.6 seconds, ∼78 frames) from the first half. Evaluation was performed on 103 test scenes, including both seen and novel viewpoints.
 - **Continuous Short-term Prediction:** Models (DefGS, FreeGave, ExtDM, MAGI-1) continuously predicted the next 10 frames given a stream of observed frames, mimicking robotic manipulation scenarios. Evaluation was on the same 103 test scenes.
- **Metrics:** PMF, PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS.
- **Results:**
 - **Long-term:** Current methods showed reasonable performance for trained viewpoints (e.g., MAGI-1 achieved PMF 4.086), but a significant drop in quality was observed when predicting from novel viewpoints (e.g., DefGS PMF 3.980 for seen vs. 3.347 for novel). This indicates a challenge in generalizing 3D physical motion modeling to unseen perspectives.
 - **Short-term:** Similar trends were observed, with models like FreeGave achieving a PMF of 4.742 for seen views, but a decrease to 3.706 for novel views. The results highlight that modeling complex physical motions in 3D space, especially across novel views, remains a substantial challenge.

#### 4.3. Physical Properties Estimation

- **Objective:** To infer physical properties of objects from visual observations.
- **Methodology:** Two scene-specific methods (PAC-NeRF, GIC) were evaluated on 20 test scenes, representing five material categories: elastic solids, plasticine, Newtonian fluids, non-Newtonian fluids, and granular substances. Each category involved distinct material properties (e.g., Young’s modulus, Poisson’s ratio, viscosity, yield stress, friction angle).
- **Metrics:** Accuracy of estimated physical properties compared to ground truth, and PMF, PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS for resimulation under novel initial conditions.
- **Results:** Both PAC-NeRF and GIC were able to infer physically plausible properties to some extent. However, the quantitative results (Table 5) showed considerable error ranges for the estimated parameters, particularly for complex materials like non-Newtonian fluids. When resimulating scenes using these estimated properties (Table 6), the models still fell short of accurately reproducing ground-truth dynamics, demonstrating that current methods struggle with the complexity of objects and backgrounds in the dataset. For instance, GIC achieved a PMF of 5.938 for resimulation, while PAC-NeRF achieved 5.617, indicating that while they maintain some physical plausibility, precision is lacking for complex cases.

#### 4.4. Motion Transfer

- **Objective:** To transfer motion dynamics from a source video to a target image, creating a new video with the target's visual attributes and source's motion patterns.
- **Methodology:** Recent methods (MotionPro, GoWithTheFlow) were evaluated using a subset of 273 dynamic 3D scenes from the validation set. Paired target scenes were created by replacing source objects with alternative shapes and materials while preserving identical physics activities for quantitative assessment.
- **Metrics:** PMF, PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS.
- **Results:** Both MotionPro and GoWithTheFlow maintained high visual fidelity in the generated videos. However, they largely failed to accurately transfer intricate physical motions (e.g., complex collisions or deformations), as indicated by PMF scores (GoWithTheFlow: 3.309, MotionPro: 3.484). This suggests a fundamental limitation in current methods' ability to capture and propagate complex, multi-object physical dynamics.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

PhysInOne represents a contribution to the field of AI research by addressing the recognized scarcity of large-scale, physically-grounded training data. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key aspects:

- **Enabling Physics-Aware AI Development:** By providing 2 million videos across 153,810 dynamic 3D scenes covering 71 diverse physical phenomena, PhysInOne offers a resource for training and fine-tuning AI models to better understand and simulate the physical world. This is particularly relevant for improving the physical plausibility of generative AI outputs and the predictive capabilities of world models.
- **Advancing World Models and Embodied AI:** The comprehensive nature of the dataset, including multi-object interactions, multi-physics activities, complex objects, and detailed ground-truth annotations (geometry, semantics, motion, physical properties, text), is intended to accelerate progress in building robust world models. These models are crucial for applications in embodied AI, robotics, and autonomous systems, where an accurate understanding of physics is essential for planning and interaction.
- **Rigorous Benchmarking and Gap Identification:** PhysInOne serves as a challenging benchmark that exposes current limitations in AI models' physical reasoning abilities. The experiments across tasks like video generation, future frame prediction, physical property estimation, and motion transfer demonstrate that while fine-tuning on PhysInOne can enhance physical plausibility, significant challenges remain, particularly in modeling complex multi-object dynamics, generalizing to novel viewpoints, and accurately inferring intrinsic physical properties. This identification of specific gaps (e.g., in mechanics, optics, and complex motion transfer) provides clear directions for future research.
- **Standardization and Reproducibility:** The systematic approach to dataset construction, including the detailed identification of physical phenomena, standardized asset collection, and multi-engine simulation, offers a framework for creating large-scale synthetic data. The public availability of the dataset can foster standardized evaluation and reproducible research in visual physics learning.
- **Interdisciplinary Contribution:** The dataset's focus on fundamental physics principles (mechanics, optics, fluid dynamics, magnetism) bridges concepts from physics education with advanced AI research, potentially fostering interdisciplinary collaborations and new approaches to AI development inspired by human physical cognition.

In summary, PhysInOne is positioned as a foundational dataset that can drive the next generation of AI systems towards a more nuanced and accurate comprehension of the physical world, moving beyond superficial visual fidelity to physically-grounded intelligence.
