# CORE: Concept-Oriented Reinforcement for Bridging the Definition-Application Gap in Mathematical Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> The CORE framework enhances large language models' mathematical reasoning by explicitly turning mathematical concepts into fine-grained reinforcement learning signals, bridging the gap between concept definition and application. This approach yielded up to a 9.6% accuracy improvement on benchmarks like TheoremQA and demonstrated improved robustness against conceptual distractors across various LLMs.

## Problem

- Large language models (LLMs) often exhibit a 'definition–application gap' in mathematical reasoning, capable of reciting definitions but failing to apply them correctly in varied contexts, suggesting reliance on pattern matching.
- Existing Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) pipelines provide only coarse, terminal scalar rewards, which are insufficient to offer fine-grained conceptual guidance and can lead to models improving via pattern reuse rather than genuine conceptual understanding.
- Quantitative diagnostics revealed that LLMs, such as GPT-4o, can accurately restate mathematical concept definitions but frequently fail corresponding application-based quizzes, especially under robustness tests with permuted options.

## Method

- CORE (==Concept-Oriented== REinforcement) is an RL-based framework that injects explicit mathematical concepts as controllable supervision signals during training, compatible with policy gradient algorithms like GRPO.
- It utilizes a curated dataset from a Chinese textbook, including concept definitions and aligned exercises, along with synthetically generated Concept Probes and robustness evaluations to diagnose the definition-application gap.
- Two main reinforcement recipes are introduced: CORE-CR (Concept-Guided Trajectory Replacement) which substitutes incorrect trajectories with concept-guided ones, and CORE-KL (Concept-Guided KL-Regularization) which aligns the model's standard reasoning process with its concept-primed process using a KL-divergence objective.

## Results

- CORE-KL achieved a **9.3%** accuracy gain (**55.7%**) over the Vanilla baseline (**46.4%**) on in-domain textbook exercises for Qwen2-Math-**7B**, and CORE-CR showed a **9.6%** gain (**44.2%**) on TheoremQA.
- The framework demonstrated cross-model generalization, improving average performance across various LLMs (e.g., Llama-3-**8B**-Instruct saw an overall +**0.8%** average improvement on diverse benchmarks).
- CORE-trained models exhibited improved robustness to irrelevant concept perturbations, showing consistently smaller accuracy drops compared to baselines when distractors were introduced.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LLMs' proficiency in mathematical reasoning often stems from exploiting superficial patterns or mimicking solution templates rather than a deep, flexible conceptual understanding.
> - Providing fine-grained, concept-specific supervision signals during RL training is crucial for moving LLMs beyond heuristic reliance towards genuinely applying mathematical concepts.
> - The efficacy of CORE is robust to the quality of concept probes (even self-generated ones) and does not strictly depend on a superior expert teacher model or external verifiers, indicating intrinsic intervention logic.
