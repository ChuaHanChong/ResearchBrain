# CORE: Concept-Oriented Reinforcement for Bridging the Definition-Application Gap in Mathematical Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> The CORE framework enhances large language models' mathematical reasoning by explicitly turning mathematical concepts into fine-grained reinforcement learning signals, bridging the gap between concept definition and application. This approach yielded up to a 9.6% accuracy improvement on benchmarks like TheoremQA and demonstrated improved robustness against conceptual distractors across various LLMs.

## Problem

- Large language models (LLMs) often exhibit a 'definition–application gap' in mathematical reasoning, capable of reciting definitions but failing to apply them correctly in varied contexts, suggesting reliance on pattern matching.
- Existing Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) pipelines provide only coarse, terminal scalar rewards, which are insufficient to offer fine-grained conceptual guidance and can lead to models improving via pattern reuse rather than genuine conceptual understanding.
- Quantitative diagnostics revealed that LLMs, such as GPT-4o, can accurately restate mathematical concept definitions but frequently fail corresponding application-based quizzes, especially under robustness tests with permuted options.

## Method

- CORE (==Concept-Oriented== REinforcement) is an RL-based framework that injects explicit mathematical concepts as controllable supervision signals during training, compatible with policy gradient algorithms like GRPO.
- It utilizes a curated dataset from a Chinese textbook, including concept definitions and aligned exercises, along with synthetically generated Concept Probes and robustness evaluations to diagnose the definition-application gap.
- Two main reinforcement recipes are introduced: CORE-CR (Concept-Guided Trajectory Replacement) which substitutes incorrect trajectories with concept-guided ones, and CORE-KL (Concept-Guided KL-Regularization) which aligns the model's standard reasoning process with its concept-primed process using a KL-divergence objective.

## Results

- CORE-KL achieved a **9.3%** accuracy gain (**55.7%**) over the Vanilla baseline (**46.4%**) on in-domain textbook exercises for Qwen2-Math-**7B**, and CORE-CR showed a **9.6%** gain (**44.2%**) on TheoremQA.
- The framework demonstrated cross-model generalization, improving average performance across various LLMs (e.g., Llama-3-**8B**-Instruct saw an overall +**0.8%** average improvement on diverse benchmarks).
- CORE-trained models exhibited improved robustness to irrelevant concept perturbations, showing consistently smaller accuracy drops compared to baselines when distractors were introduced.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LLMs' proficiency in mathematical reasoning often stems from exploiting superficial patterns or mimicking solution templates rather than a deep, flexible conceptual understanding.
> - Providing fine-grained, concept-specific supervision signals during RL training is crucial for moving LLMs beyond heuristic reliance towards genuinely applying mathematical concepts.
> - The efficacy of CORE is robust to the quality of concept probes (even self-generated ones) and does not strictly depend on a superior expert teacher model or external verifiers, indicating intrinsic intervention logic.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of large language models (LLMs) has led to notable advancements in various domains, including mathematical reasoning. Recent LLMs are increasingly capable of addressing complex, competition-level mathematical problems. However, a recurring observation is that their performance often stems from pattern matching, execution of numerical calculations, or mimicking solution templates, rather than a deep, conceptual understanding of the underlying mathematics. This phenomenon is referred to as the "definition-application gap," where models can recite definitions but fail to apply them correctly in varied contexts.

Existing approaches to enhance mathematical reasoning in LLMs, such as those employing Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) pipelines (e.g., Schulman et al., 2017; Shao et al., 2024), typically reinforce final answers. While this can improve search heuristics, the scalar reward signal is often too coarse to provide fine-grained conceptual guidance. This limitation can lead models to improve at pattern reuse rather than genuine conceptual application, as evidenced by perturbation-based tests (Patel et al., 2021a; Yu et al., 2024; Mirzadeh et al., 2025). Other specialized training methods, such as those leveraging Evol-Instruct feedback (Luo et al., 2025), hybrid instruction tuning (Yue et al., 2024), or extensive pre-training on mathematical corpora (Azerbayev et al., 2024; Shao et al., 2024; Yang et al., 2024b), have also been explored. Despite these advancements, many still rely on data scale or final answer rewards, which may leave concept selection and application undertaught.

This work contributes to the landscape by specifically addressing the conceptual reasoning gap. It proposes a framework, CORE, that differentiates itself by explicitly turning mathematical concepts into a controllable supervision signal during RL training. This approach moves beyond terminal scalar rewards by injecting fine-grained conceptual signals into rollouts and regularizing outcomes, aiming to foster an intrinsic understanding rather than reliance on surface regularities or memorized solution paths. By focusing on how models select and apply concepts, rather than just achieving correct answers, CORE aligns with a growing body of research that questions the sufficiency of end-point scores for assessing true conceptual understanding (e.g., Chen et al., 2023; Mirzadeh et al., 2025; Li et al., 2025).

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to bridge the "definition–application gap" observed in large language models' mathematical reasoning capabilities. This gap manifests as LLMs' ability to solve challenging math exercises while concurrently failing to apply fundamental concepts correctly when genuine understanding is required.

The motivation for addressing this gap stems from several observations and limitations within current LLM training paradigms:

1. **Reliance on Surface Regularities:** Many large collections of exercise-style problems, often used for training LLMs, can be solved by exploiting superficial patterns such as formats, keywords, or step sequences. This often bypasses the need for engaging with the intended mathematical concepts. Consequently, models may mimic solution templates and chain together routine algebraic steps without truly understanding the underlying principles, leading to failures when novel applications of concepts are required.
2. **Limitations of Coarse Rewards in RLVR Pipelines:** Widely adopted Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) pipelines typically optimize for a terminal scalar reward based on the correctness of the final answer. While beneficial for improving search heuristics, this signal is considered too coarse to provide specific guidance on *which* concept should be invoked, *where* it should be applied in the argument, or *how* it supports subsequent reasoning steps. This lack of fine-grained conceptual feedback contributes to models improving via pattern reuse rather than developing a deeper conceptual grasp.
3. **Difficulty in Assessing Conceptual Understanding:** Directly assessing whether a model genuinely understands and applies a concept, rather than merely recalling its definition, presents a challenge. The authors operationalize this by pairing concise concept definitions with aligned quiz questions that inherently necessitate the application of the concept. Through preliminary diagnostics, the authors observed that models can readily restate concept definitions, yet frequently fail the corresponding quizzes, quantitatively demonstrating a pronounced definition-application gap. For example, GPT-4o could correctly restate the Rational Root Theorem but failed to apply it when the numerator and denominator were swapped in a problem, indicating a rigid, pattern-matching heuristic rather than flexible conceptual engagement.
4. **Need for Fine-Grained Conceptual Supervision:** To mitigate the identified gap, the authors argue for the necessity of a training paradigm that explicitly forces the model to ground its reasoning in concepts. This involves transforming explicit mathematical concepts into concept-driven training signals during the model sampling and optimization process.

In summary, the core motivation is to move LLM mathematical reasoning beyond superficial pattern matching and procedural execution towards a state of genuine conceptual competence, where models can reliably identify and apply mathematical concepts under varied problem conditions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, CORE (Concept-Oriented REinforcement), is an RL-based training framework designed to inject explicit mathematical concepts as controllable supervision signals. The methodology proceeds through three main stages: dataset curation, gap diagnostics, and concept reinforcement recipes.

#### 3.1. Dataset Curation

To obtain rigorous conceptual reasoning signals and minimize data contamination, a high-quality corpus was curated from a canonical Chinese textbook, "Advanced Algebra (3rd Edition)" (Yao & Xie, 2015). This textbook was selected due to its structured curriculum, logical progression of topics, and clear association between concepts and exercises. Each of its ten chapters contains core concept definitions (C), illustrative examples, and concept-aligned exercises (E). The Chinese origin, followed by manual translation into English, was intended to mitigate contamination risks often present in English-language corpora.

The extraction process yielded:
- 236 concept texts.
- 703 examples.
- 140 multiple-choice questions from the exercise sections, which serve as the in-domain test set.

#### 3.2. Gap Diagnostics

Before developing the CORE reinforcement recipes, the authors conducted diagnostic experiments to confirm and quantify the definition-application gap.

#### 3.2.1. Probing the Gap Between Knowledge Recitation and Application

A qualitative analysis was performed on conceptual errors made by GPT-4o on the curated multiple-choice exercises. When GPT-4o failed an exercise, it was prompted to describe the core mathematical concept related to the problem. Observations indicated that in most cases, the model could accurately recite the definition and properties of the concept, suggesting parametric knowledge encoding. However, the model often failed to apply this knowledge correctly in the problem, demonstrating a "rigid, pattern-matching heuristic" rather than flexible conceptual application.

#### 3.2.2. Synthetic Concept Probes and Robustness Evaluation

To quantitatively measure conceptual understanding, the authors introduced "Concept Probes"—targeted quizzes generated directly from the textbook’s concept definitions.
- **Generation:** Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct was used to create 5–8 multiple-choice quizzes for each of the 236 concept texts, resulting in 1,200 candidate quizzes.
- **Validation:** A separate model, GPT-4o, was used as an assessor to filter these quizzes. GPT-4o evaluated six dimensions (e.g., clarity, correctness) and provided an overall rating. 90 quizzes rated "Fair" or "Poor" were discarded, leaving 1,110 high-quality Concept Probes.
- **Robustness Evaluation Protocol:** To test reliance on superficial heuristics, each of the 1,110 quizzes was subjected to a robustness test. Three variants were generated by randomly permuting the multiple-choice options. A model was considered to have robustly solved a problem only if it correctly answered the original question and all three permuted variants. This experiment, applied to Qwen2-Math-7B, OLMo-7B-Instruct, and Llama-3-8B, showed significant performance degradation under robust evaluation (e.g., OLMo-2-7B dropped from >70% to <50%), supporting the hypothesis of reliance on shallow heuristics.

#### 3.3. Concept Reinforcement Recipe

CORE is presented as an RL-based framework compatible with any policy gradient algorithm; in this paper, Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) serves as the backbone. CORE does not involve architectural changes to the LLM but intervenes during training with concept-guided instruction when failures occur. Three variants were explored:

#### 3.3.1. CORE-Base: Standard RL on Conceptual Quizzes

This foundational approach involves training the policy directly on the curated set of 1,110 conceptual quizzes using the standard GRPO algorithm. It assesses the model's ability to implicitly learn concepts from question-answer pairs without explicit conceptual guidance during training.

#### 3.3.2. CORE-CR: Concept-Guided Trajectory Replacement

This variant introduces a conditional intervention.
- **Trigger:** Activated when all `N` responses generated by the model for a given problem within a GRPO group are incorrect.
- **Intervention:** A concept-guided prompt `p_c` is formed by concatenating the original problem `q` with its associated ground-truth concept text `c_q` (`p_c = c_q ⊕ q`).
- **Generation and Replacement:** `K` new trajectories are generated from the concept-guided policy using `p_c`. `K` trajectories (where `1 ≤ K < N`) from the original failed group are then randomly replaced with these new concept-guided trajectories.
- **Reward Augmentation:** The new concept-guided trajectories are assigned an augmented reward `R'(τ_c,j) = R(τ_c,j) + r_bonus`, where `r_bonus` is a positive hyperparameter (set to 0.4). The GRPO update is then performed on this partially replaced, concept-guided batch.

#### 3.3.3. CORE-KL: Concept-Guided KL-Regularization

This method provides a fine-grained regularization signal, also triggered by a conceptual failure event.
- **Objective:** It encourages the model's standard step-by-step predictive process (π_θ(· | q, y_<t)) to align with the process it exhibits when primed with a concept (π_θ(· | p_c, y_<t)).
- **Formulation:** A forward KL-divergence objective is used. Upon a conceptual failure, a high-quality reference trajectory `Y*` is sampled from the current concept-guided policy (Y* ~ π_θ(· | p_c)). The objective minimizes the KL-divergence between the next-token predictive distributions of the guided and un-guided policies at each timestep `t`, conditioned on the prefix of `Y*`.
 The loss term added to the base RL objective is:
 `L_total = L_GRPO + λ_KL · E_Y*~π_θ(·|pc) [∑_{t=1}^{|Y*|} D_KL (π_θ(· | p_c, y*_<t) || π_θ(· | q, y*_<t))]`
 A dynamic KL coefficient `λ_KL` is applied (0.03 if the reference trajectory is correct, 0.005 if incorrect).

#### 3.4. Training and Evaluation Settings

- **Training:** Models were trained for 3 epochs on four H100 GPUs using the verl framework for GRPO. Adam optimizer was used with an actor learning rate of 1 × 10⁻⁶. A standard KL-divergence penalty with a fixed coefficient of 0.001 was applied. Sampling temperature was 0.7, batch size 128, mini-batch size 32. Four responses were generated per prompt for GRPO updates. Rewards were binary (1 for correct, 0 for incorrect). Maximum prompt length was 1024, response length varied by model (1024 to 6000).
- **Evaluation:**
 - **In-domain:** 140 multiple-choice exercises from the curated textbook ("Textbook").
 - **Out-of-domain:** A diverse suite of benchmarks including GSM8K, ASDiv, MAWPS, TabMWP, MATH, MMLU-STEM, Gaokao 2023 (EN), Gaokao-Math-QA, CMATH, Minerva Math, SVAMP, CounterMath, TheoremQA, and OlympiadBench.
 - **Metrics:** Self-consistency (SC@21) was used, involving sampling 21 distinct reasoning paths with a temperature of 0.7 and determining the final prediction by majority voting.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate that the CORE framework consistently improves LLMs' mathematical reasoning abilities across various models and benchmarks.

#### 4.1. Consistent Gains on Qwen2-Math-7B

For the primary evaluation model, Qwen2-Math-7B, CORE variants showed consistent and notable performance improvements:
- **In-domain (Textbook):** CORE-KL achieved an accuracy of 55.7%, a gain of 9.3% over the Vanilla baseline (46.4%). CORE-CR also improved to 52.1%.
- **Out-of-domain:**
 - **TheoremQA:** CORE-KL matched CORE-CR at 44.2%, a 9.6% gain over Vanilla (34.6%).
 - **OlympiadBench:** CORE-CR achieved 34.5%, a 5.8% gain over Vanilla (28.7%).
 - **TabMWP:** CORE-CR reached 93.6%, a 3.4% gain over Vanilla (90.2%).
 - **MATH:** CORE-CR showed 71.4%, a 2.3% gain over Vanilla (69.1%).
 - Consistent gains were observed on GSM8K, ASDiv, MAWPS, and Gaokao 2023 (EN).

#### 4.2. Cross-Model Generalization

CORE-CR was evaluated on DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B, Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B, and Llama-3-8B-Instruct, demonstrating its robustness and applicability across different model architectures and scales:
- **DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B:** Achieved improvements on MMLU-STEM (+1.3%), SVAMP (+1.2%), and Gaokao 2023 (EN) (+1.0%). The overall average improvement was +0.4%.
- **Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B:** Demonstrated strong enhancements on Minerva Math (+3.3%), TabMWP (+1.9%), and MATH (+1.3%). The overall average improvement was +0.3%.
- **Llama-3-8B-Instruct:** Showed notable gains on TabMWP (+3.3%), MAWPS (+1.6%), SVAMP (+1.6%), and MMLU-STEM (+1.4%). The overall average improvement was +0.8%.
These results suggest that CORE's benefits are not restricted to a specific model.

#### 4.3. Enhanced Concept Selection and Application

An analysis was conducted on a diagnostic subset of 19 problems (W) that were solved by both CORE-CR and CORE-KL but missed by Vanilla or CORE-Base.
- **Classification:** 10 out of 19 (52.6%) cases were categorized as "Concept-Selection," meaning both CORE variants explicitly invoked the target concept and did not show heuristic cues. 9 cases (47.4%) were "Mixed," and 0 cases were "Heuristic-selection."
- **Interpretation:** This analysis indicates that the accuracy gains are attributable to an improved mechanism of concept selection and application, rather than superficial shortcutting or mere prompt engineering at test time.

#### 4.4. Improved Robustness to Irrelevant Concept Perturbations

Models were tested for robustness by prepending K (1, 2, or 3) irrelevant concepts to 140 textbook exercises. The Retention Under Distractors (RUD_K) metric measured accuracy on items a model originally solved without perturbation.
- **Observation:** As the number of distractors (K) increased, CORE-trained models, particularly CORE-CR, exhibited consistently smaller accuracy drops compared to Vanilla and CORE-Base.
- **Interpretation:** This indicates that CORE not only improves baseline accuracy but also enhances the robustness of reasoning, making predictions more stable against irrelevant conceptual noise.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies: CORE vs. GRPO Artifacts

To determine if gains originated from CORE's curriculum or GRPO artifacts, controlled ablations were performed on Qwen2-Math-7B:
- **Random-Reward GRPO:** Assigning random binary rewards yielded no meaningful gains, indicating that stochastic reward noise alone does not produce the observed improvements.
- **More-Candidates Top-k Control (Top 4 of 6 GRPO):** Generating more candidates (6) and selecting the top 4 for training resulted in small and often regressive changes.
- **Conclusion:** These ablations suggest that CORE, rather than general GRPO mechanics or increased rollout counts, is the primary source of the observed performance improvements.

#### 4.6. Intrinsic Conceptual Reinforcement vs. Knowledge Distillation

To investigate whether CORE's gains were due to knowledge distillation from a superior teacher model, a "Self-Supervised" experiment was conducted using only the Qwen2-Math-7B family (Qwen2-Math-7B-Instruct for generation, Qwen2-Math-7B as learner). A self-verification protocol retained only quizzes the model could solve via self-consistency.
- **Results:** CORE-CR delivered consistent improvements (e.g., GSM8K +1.6%, TabMWP +2.3%, OlympiadBench +4.2%) even with self-generated, potentially noisy concept probes.
- **Comparison with Verifier-Guided RL:** A CORE-Base + Verifier variant, which used a verifier to assign intrinsic process rewards for correct concept invocation, yielded modest gains but consistently underperformed CORE-CR.
- **Conclusion:** This indicates that CORE's efficacy is robust and relies on its intrinsic intervention logic, effectively bridging the reasoning gap through a closed-loop, self-improving process, rather than depending on a perfectly clean teacher or a superior expert model.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The CORE framework represents a development in enhancing the mathematical reasoning capabilities of large language models. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Addressing a Fundamental LLM Limitation:** CORE directly addresses the "definition–application gap," a recognized limitation where LLMs can exhibit high problem-solving competence through pattern matching but lack genuine conceptual understanding. This work provides a method to move beyond superficial heuristics towards a more principled reasoning ability.
2. **Novel Conceptual Supervision:** The framework introduces a novel approach to injecting fine-grained conceptual supervision during reinforcement learning. By transforming explicit mathematical concepts into training signals and incorporating them via concept-aligned quizzes, trajectory replacement, and KL-divergence regularization, CORE provides more targeted guidance than traditional scalar rewards.
3. **Demonstrated Generalizability and Robustness:** The consistent performance gains observed across multiple LLMs (including base and instruction-tuned variants like Qwen2-Math-7B, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B, Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B, and Llama-3-8B-Instruct) and a diverse suite of in-domain and out-of-domain benchmarks suggest that CORE is a generalizable and model-agnostic solution. Its ability to improve robustness against irrelevant conceptual perturbations further indicates a deeper shift in reasoning rather than mere overfitting.
4. **Algorithmic and Verifier Agnosticism:** CORE is designed to be compatible with standard policy-gradient methods (demonstrated with GRPO and PPO) and does not require architectural modifications to the LLM. Furthermore, the "Self-Supervised" experiments indicate that its efficacy does not strictly depend on a superior expert teacher model or external verifiers, suggesting its potential for broader application in resource-constrained settings or for self-improvement loops.
5. **Foundation for Enhanced Trustworthiness:** By fostering genuine conceptual understanding, CORE has the potential to enhance the trustworthiness of LLM outputs in critical domains. Models that understand *why* a concept applies, rather than just *that* it applies, may produce more reliable and explainable reasoning steps.
6. **Broader Applicability:** While demonstrated in mathematical reasoning, the principles of concept-oriented reinforcement could be extended to other domains that demand principled reasoning and where a clear definition-application gap might exist, such as physics, logic, or even complex legal and medical decision-making. This could motivate further research into concept-centered training signals across various AI applications.
