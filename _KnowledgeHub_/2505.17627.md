# H2-COMPACT: Human-Humanoid Co-Manipulation via Adaptive Contact Trajectory Policies

> [!summary] Summary
> The H²-COMPACT framework introduces a hierarchical approach for human–humanoid co-manipulation, enabling a Unitree G1 robot to carry extended loads with a human partner guided solely by haptic cues. This system achieved stable, load-adaptive legged locomotion with improved kinematic synchrony and reduced leader effort compared to human-human cooperative tasks.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots lack the ability to effectively perform cooperative physical tasks like carrying awkward loads, which requires intuitive inference of human intent through haptic cues.
- Existing legged robot controllers typically ignore external forces as meaningful communication signals or treat them merely as disturbances, hindering fluid physical human-robot interaction.
- Prior research in human-robot co-manipulation mainly focused on fixed-base manipulators or wheeled platforms, lacking solutions for dynamic whole-body locomotion on general-purpose humanoids under variable payloads.

## Method

- A two-tier hierarchical policy learning framework was developed, separating haptic intent inference from robust legged locomotion control.
- An upper-tier conditional diffusion policy, trained on human-human collaboration data, interprets six-axis force/torque inputs to generate high-level whole-body linear and angular velocities.
- A lower-tier reinforcement learning policy, trained in ==Isaac Gym== with randomized external forces (0-3 kg payload), translates these high-level commands into stable, load-adaptive joint trajectories for the humanoid.

## Results

- The human-humanoid system demonstrated improved kinematic synchrony, with an average speed mismatch (Δ_v) of **0.143** m/s, lower than human-human dyads (**0.165** m/s).
- Human leaders exerted less haptic effort when collaborating with the humanoid, as indicated by a reduced average follower force (F̄) of **16.23** N compared to **17.36** N for human followers.
- The load-adaptive RL policy exhibited robust stability and precise velocity tracking under a 30N payload in simulation, a significant improvement over baseline controllers which experienced instability and failure.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Haptic feedback alone can serve as an effective primary modality for guiding full-body humanoid movements during co-manipulation, even in scenarios where visual cues are occluded.
> - Decoupling the interpretation of human haptic intent (force-to-velocity mapping) from the low-level legged locomotion control (velocity-to-joint trajectories) simplifies policy learning and improves overall system robustness.
> - Explicitly training a locomotion policy with randomized external forces in simulation significantly enhances its ability to adapt to variable payloads and dynamic interactions in real-world human-robot collaborative tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper "H²-COMPACT" addresses a critical gap in the field of human-robot collaboration, particularly concerning legged humanoid robots. While advancements in reinforcement learning (RL) and imitation learning (IL) have enabled humanoid robots to perform impressive agile and dynamic actions across diverse terrains, these capabilities have largely not extended to practical human assistance tasks, especially those requiring close physical cooperation. Human teams naturally excel at cooperative tasks like carrying awkward payloads, relying on intuitive sensing and adaptation to each other’s applied forces. However, replicating this fluidity with humanoids remains a significant challenge.

The existing research landscape for human-robot co-manipulation has predominantly focused on fixed-base manipulators or wheeled platforms, employing control schemes like impedance or admittance control to respond to human force inputs. Examples include, and, which explore various methods for disambiguating intent during object carrying, often relying on state-machine heuristics or probabilistic estimators. More advanced approaches have been demonstrated on humanoid arms, such as proactive haptic transport or EMG/motion-capture augmented compliance, but these typically involve predefined trajectories or robots with limited degrees of freedom (DoF). Critically, none of these prior works combine learned haptic inference with dynamic whole-body locomotion on a general-purpose humanoid, especially when carrying extended objects where visual cues for the lower body may be occluded, making haptic feedback the primary and most natural communication modality.

Furthermore, current humanoid perception predominantly relies on visual input to understand the environment. Haptic perception, crucial for physical interaction, has received comparatively limited attention. Most legged-robot controllers either neglect external forces entirely or treat them merely as disturbances, rather than recognizing them as meaningful channels of communication. This paper directly challenges this paradigm by asserting that haptic feedback can effectively guide humanoid movements during co-manipulation.

In parallel, RL has seen remarkable success in enabling stable locomotion in quadruped- and humanoid robots-. These RL-trained policies offer advantages over classical optimal-control-problem (OCP) based controllers by learning end-to-end control mappings offline and executing them in constant time, thus avoiding the latency and sensitivity to modeling errors inherent in online trajectory optimization. While some RL research has explored forceful interactions, such as dribbling a ball, door-opening, or controlling force at the end effector of a legged manipulator, these works do not integrate haptic intent inference with full-body legged locomotion for cooperative carrying tasks.

Therefore, H²-COMPACT distinguishes itself by being the *first* to demonstrate learned haptic guidance fused with full-body legged control for fluid human–humanoid co-manipulation. It bridges the gap between sophisticated legged locomotion and intuitive human-robot physical interaction, positioning itself as a foundational step towards enabling humanoids to genuinely assist humans in everyday physical tasks requiring close collaboration.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind H²-COMPACT stems from the current limitations of humanoid robots in practical human assistance, particularly in cooperative physical tasks. While human teams effortlessly engage in co-manipulation, such as carrying extended or awkward loads, by intuitively inferring intent through haptic cues, humanoid robots largely lack this capability. Their reliance on visual perception often overlooks the richness of haptic communication, and their locomotion controllers typically fail to treat external forces as meaningful signals, let alone adapt robustly to variable payloads.

The overarching objective of this work is to enable a legged humanoid robot to cooperatively carry extended loads with a human partner, relying *solely on haptic cues* for inferring the human's intent. To achieve this, the paper sets forth several key objectives:

1. **Develop a Hierarchical Policy Learning Framework:** The core objective is to propose a novel two-tier architecture that effectively separates and addresses the distinct challenges of human intent inference and robust legged locomotion under load.
2. **Integrate Haptic-Intent Inference:** A crucial goal is to create a compact and effective haptic inference model that can accurately interpret human movement intent from six-axis force/torque data, translating these signals into high-level whole-body velocity commands.
3. **Achieve Payload-Adaptive Locomotion:** The framework aims to incorporate a lower-level locomotion policy capable of translating these high-level velocity commands into stable, under-load joint trajectories, maintaining balance and mobility across a range of variable payloads (0-3 kg) and friction conditions.
4. **Decouple Intent Interpretation from Locomotion Control:** By separating the "force to high-level velocity" mapping from the "high-level velocity to low-level joint angles" control, the objective is to combine intuitive responsiveness to human inputs with robust, load-adaptive walking, overcoming the complexities of an end-to-end approach for such a dynamic task.
5. **Enable Affordable and Widely Deployable Data Collection:** The research seeks to streamline the data collection process by using only synchronized RGB video and force/torque sensor readings, avoiding the need for expensive motion-capture systems or specialized markers. This includes developing a robust vision-only preprocessing pipeline for human pose and velocity estimation.
6. **Demonstrate Real-World Performance:** The ultimate objective is to validate the proposed framework through extensive real-world trials on a physical humanoid robot (Unitree G1), benchmarking its cooperative carry-and-move performance against a blindfolded human-follower baseline using quantitative metrics like completion time, trajectory deviation, velocity synchrony, and follower-applied force.

By achieving these objectives, H²-COMPACT aims to pave the way for more fluid, natural, and effective physical collaboration between humans and advanced legged robots in practical applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

H²-COMPACT employs a hierarchical policy learning framework, designed to tackle the complexities of human-humanoid co-manipulation by decoupling intent inference from locomotion control.

**A. Hierarchical Policy Learning Structure:**

1. **Upper Tier: Haptic Inference Model:**
 - **Purpose:** To interpret human movement intent from haptic cues.
 - **Inputs:** Six-axis force and torque data streams from dual ATI Mini-40/45 sensors mounted on the object handles, collected over a past time window (T force frames).
 - **Output:** High-level whole-body planar linear velocities (v_x, v_y) and yaw rate (ω_z) for a future time window (H frames).
 - **Architecture:** A conditional diffusion policy implemented with a block-wise cross-attention Transformer.
 - **Encoding:** Force-torque signals are first processed using a multi-resolution stationary wavelet transform, which decomposes them into different frequency bands. This helps preserve essential low-frequency features and suppress high-frequency noise.
 - **Training:** This model is trained offline using behavioral cloning on demonstration data collected from human-human collaborations.
 - **Inference:** Deterministic DDIM sampling is used to generate the high-level actions (velocities).

2. **Lower Tier: RL-Based Payload-Adaptive Locomotion Policy:**
 - **Purpose:** To translate high-level whole-body velocities into stable, load-adaptive joint trajectories.
 - **Inputs:** High-level velocities (v_x, v_y, ω_z) from the upper tier as reference states, combined with robot proprioceptive observations (body angular velocity, projected gravity vector, joint positions and velocities for legs, previous action, and normalized gait phase).
 - **Output:** Desired joint angles for the robot's legs. These are then converted to torques using a PD controller (Eq. 17).
 - **Architecture:** A feed-forward policy (three-layer MLPs for actor and critic) trained using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO).
 - **Training Environment:** Isaac Gym is used for high-speed, GPU-based parallel training.
 - **Load Adaptation:** The policy is explicitly trained to handle dynamic payloads by applying randomized external forces (sampled from U([-15N, 15N])) to the robot's wrist-roll links during each episode. This emulates variable payload masses (0-3 kg) and distributions, ensuring robustness to both resisting and assisting forces.
 - **Robustness:** Domain randomization (perturbing ground contact properties, robot base mass, applying external pushes) is applied during training to enhance sim-to-real transferability and robustness to real-world variability.

**B. Data Preprocessing for Intent Inference:**

To enable affordable and widely deployable data collection without specialized motion capture hardware, a streamlined vision-only pipeline is employed:
1. **Data Collection:** Synchronized RGB video stream and dual force-torque sensor channels are recorded.
2. **Image Cleaning:** The Segment Anything Model v2 (SAM2) is used to remove background clutter and non-human objects from RGB frames, inpainting the masked regions to create clean, human-only imagery.
3. **Human Pose and Velocity Estimation:** Cleaned frames are fed into the WHAM 3D human pose-and-velocity network, which estimates 3D joint positions in a world frame. Linear and angular velocities are then computed by finite-difference of the torso joint over consecutive frames.
4. **Data Fusion:** The estimated human velocities are paired with synchronized six-axis force/torque readings from both handles, forming the training dataset for the haptic inference model.

**C. Hardware for Data Collection and Deployment:**

- **Data Collection Rig:** A modified box with 3D-printed handles, each mounting an ATI Mini45 six-axis force/torque sensor, is used. F/T data (1 kHz) and RGB video are time-synchronized via ROS. Blindfolded human-human leader-follower trials are conducted using eight motion primitives and four payloads (0, 1, 3, 4 kg).
- **Robot Deployment:** An Unitree G1 humanoid robot is equipped with custom 3D-printed mounts for ATI Mini45 sensors on its wrists. The sensors draw power from the robot's internal bus, and data is transmitted via EtherCAT to the onboard computer.

**D. Evaluation Metrics:**

Performance in real-world human-humanoid co-manipulation is quantitatively compared against a human-human baseline using four metrics:
1. **Completion Time (T_c):** Total duration of the carry-and-move task.
2. **Mean Trajectory Deviation (Δ_traj):** Average Euclidean distance between human and robot end-effector positions.
3. **Mean Velocity Difference (Δ_v):** Average absolute speed mismatch between human and robot.
4. **Average Follower Force (F̄):** Mean total magnitude of force exerted by the follower (human or robot) on the object handles, indicating responsiveness.

The policy undergoes sim-to-sim validation in MuJoCo before zero-shot sim-to-real deployment on the Unitree G1 in both open-area and Vicon-tracked obstacle courses.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments presented in H²-COMPACT validate the effectiveness of the proposed hierarchical policy learning framework for fluid human-humanoid co-manipulation, demonstrating performance comparable to, and in some aspects exceeding, human-human dyads.

**A. Real-world Human-Humanoid Co-manipulation:**

The core evaluation involved comparing the Unitree G1 humanoid, guided by the H²-COMPACT framework, with a blindfolded human follower, both co-manipulating a box with a human leader.
- **Completion Time (T_c):** The human-humanoid pairs exhibited a longer completion time (51.47 seconds) compared to human-human pairs (23.78 seconds). This difference was primarily attributed to the Unitree G1's hardware speed cap of 0.8 m/s, rather than a lack of cooperative ability. Despite the slower pace, the robot maintained a smooth, human-like trajectory, indicating effective haptic following within its physical limits.
- **Trajectory Deviation (Δ_traj):** The mean trajectory deviation increased only slightly from 0.1109 m for human-human dyads to 0.1294 m for human-humanoid dyads. This marginal increase signifies that the robot's path remained tightly coupled to the human leader's intent, demonstrating accurate spatial agreement and intuitive movement alongside the human.
- **Velocity Difference (Δ_v):** Remarkably, the average speed mismatch actually *decreased* from 0.165 m/s in human-human interactions to 0.143 m/s in human-humanoid interactions. This indicates an *improved kinematic synchrony* for the humanoid follower, suggesting that the robot's responses were smoother and more consistent than a blindfolded human's, preventing jerky or lagging movements.
- **Average Follower Force (F̄):** The mean follower force significantly *decreased* from 17.36 N for human followers to 16.23 N for the humanoid robot. This is a crucial finding, as it implies that the human leader needed to exert less haptic effort to guide the robot. A lower average follower force indicates that the humanoid was highly responsive and effectively interpreted the leader's intent with minimal resistance, showcasing superior haptic coupling.

Overall, these real-world results confirm that the load-adaptive Unitree G1 policy enables cooperative and stable co-manipulation that is qualitatively on par with, and quantitatively superior in terms of velocity synchrony and reduced leader effort, to purely human dyads, even with the robot's inherent speed limitations.

**B. Load-Adaptive RL Policy (Sim2Sim Transfer):**

To validate the robustness of the load-adaptive locomotion policy, a sim-to-sim transfer experiment was conducted in MuJoCo, comparing it against a baseline controller under a constant 30 N payload.
- The **baseline controller** exhibited significant instability: large overshoots, oscillations, and even reversals in both linear (x and y) velocity channels, ultimately leading to the robot tipping and falling.
- In stark contrast, the **load-adaptive policy** tracked the commanded velocities (x-velocity, y-velocity, and yaw rate) almost perfectly across all three channels. It demonstrated minimal transient error and smooth convergence to the target velocities, consistently maintaining stability while carrying the load.

These findings clearly demonstrate the substantial improvement in stability and payload robustness achieved by the RL-trained load-adaptive locomotion policy, proving its effectiveness in handling external forces as intended.

**C. Sim2Real Deployment:**

The trained policies were successfully deployed for zero-shot sim2real transfer on the physical Unitree G1 robot. This was tested in both an open-area course with obstacles and a Vicon motion-capture arena. The robust performance in these diverse real-world settings, under varying payloads, validates the effectiveness of the domain randomization techniques and the overall simulation-to-real transfer capabilities of the framework.

In summary, the main findings highlight H²-COMPACT's ability to achieve intuitive and stable human-humanoid co-manipulation by effectively integrating haptic intent inference with robust, load-adaptive legged locomotion.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The H²-COMPACT framework represents a significant advancement in the field of human-robot interaction and legged robotics, with substantial potential impact across various domains.

**A. Technical Significance:**

- **Novelty in Humanoid Co-manipulation:** The paper presents the *first* demonstrated instance of learned haptic guidance fused with full-body legged control for fluid human-humanoid co-manipulation. This addresses a critical gap where prior work primarily focused on fixed-base manipulators or limited-DoF humanoid arms, or treated external forces on legged robots as mere disturbances.
- **Hierarchical Design for Complexity Management:** The hierarchical policy learning framework, by decoupling haptic intent interpretation (force to velocity) from legged locomotion (velocity to joint trajectories), effectively manages the inherent complexity of the task. This separation allows for intuitive responsiveness to human inputs while maintaining the robust, load-adaptive stability required for dynamic legged movement.
- **Haptic Perception as a Primary Modality:** By demonstrating that humanoids can effectively infer intent solely from haptic cues, the work elevates the role of haptic perception in humanoid control, challenging the traditional visual-centric approach. This is particularly relevant in scenarios like carrying extended objects where the lower body might be occluded.
- **Robustness and Generalization:** The training methodology, incorporating randomized payloads and friction conditions in simulation (Isaac Gym) coupled with domain randomization, ensures the learned policy is highly robust. The successful zero-shot sim2real transfer to a physical Unitree G1 robot, validated in diverse real-world environments and MuJoCo, underscores the policy's generalization capabilities.
- **Affordable Data Collection:** The innovative vision-only data preprocessing pipeline, leveraging SAM2 for image cleaning and WHAM for 3D human pose/velocity estimation, eliminates the need for expensive and complex motion-capture systems. This significantly lowers the barrier to entry for collecting large-scale, high-quality human-robot interaction data, making the approach more accessible and scalable for wider research and development.

**B. Potential Impact:**

- **Enabling Practical Humanoid Assistance:** The most immediate impact is the potential to enable humanoid robots to genuinely assist humans in a wide range of practical daily and workplace activities that require physical collaboration. This includes tasks such as jointly carrying heavy or awkward objects in logistics, construction, manufacturing, or even in domestic settings.
- **Enhanced Human-Robot Trust and Collaboration:** By responding intuitively and robustly to haptic cues, the humanoid can become a more natural and trustworthy partner. The reduced haptic effort required from the human leader, as demonstrated in the results, signifies improved comfort and fluidity in interaction, fostering greater acceptance and integration of robots into human environments.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** This work opens several avenues for future research. It can serve as a foundation for integrating other modalities (e.g., vision, speech) with haptic feedback for even richer intent inference. It could also inspire the development of more sophisticated co-manipulation behaviors, such as proactive assistance, shared autonomy, or dynamic task planning in collaboration with humans on legged platforms.
- **Advancement in Embodied AI:** The robust control policies and efficient learning paradigms developed contribute broadly to the field of embodied AI, pushing the boundaries of what autonomous systems can achieve in complex physical interactions.

In conclusion, H²-COMPACT significantly advances the state-of-the-art in human-humanoid co-manipulation. By providing a robust, intuitive, and accessible framework for haptically guided, load-adaptive locomotion, it paves the way for a new generation of humanoids capable of seamless and effective physical collaboration with humans, addressing real-world needs and enhancing productivity and assistance in various sectors.
