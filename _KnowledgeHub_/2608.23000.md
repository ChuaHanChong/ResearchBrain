# Free-Energy-Gated Plasticity for Real-Time Online Motor Learning in Physical Human-Robot Interaction

> [!summary] Summary
> FEGP (Free-Energy-Gated Plasticity) extends PV-RNN so synaptic weights adapt at every sensory sample during physical human-robot interaction. A sigmoid gate on log window-averaged variational free energy, with hysteretic thresholds, raises the effective learning rate under model-environment mismatch and suppresses it otherwise. From random initialization a Torobo humanoid acquired three cyclic arm patterns online, lifting repertoire coverage from 18.9% to 45.9% and retention from 0.0% to 23.1%.

## Problem

- Regularization-based continual learning such as Elastic Weight Consolidation and Synaptic Intelligence assumes task labels or consolidation events, unavailable when weights update every sample without transition signals.
- Replay buffers and meta-learning rely on stored past experience or offline meta-training over a predefined task distribution, neither available inside an uninterrupted real-time sensorimotor loop.
- PV-RNN and learning-from-demonstration pipelines freeze weights after offline training, so recurrent dynamics cannot be acquired online, and window-limited updates bias the model toward recent behavior.

## Method

- Fully online ==PV-RNN==: a randomly initialized network minimizes ==variational free energy== over a 500-step sliding window, updating posterior latents then synaptic weights by ==gated backpropagation through time== at 20 Hz.
- **Free-Energy-Gated Plasticity** scales the Adam step for weights by a ==sigmoid gain== on the log window-averaged free energy (prediction divergence plus ==meta-prior-weighted KL==), coupling adaptation to mismatch.
- **Hysteretic regime extension** replaces the single threshold with separate opening and closing thresholds and stochastic direction-checked transitions; a ==random-forest segmenter== plus ==banded DTW classifier== scores rollouts post hoc.

## Results

- Across **ten** teaching streams and **five** initializations, FEGP beat constant plasticity on coverage (**45.9%** versus **18.9%**) and retention (**23.1%** versus **0.0%**); a matched-average-rate control reached only **12.5%** and **0.2%**.
- Replaying identical gain values permuted, reversed, or shifted gave **0.5-2.2%** retention; Synaptic Intelligence peaked at **28.8%** coverage with **0.4%** retention and collapsed for coefficients at or above **0.001**.
- **Four** live **5-minute** sessions yielded all **three** patterns in autonomous rollouts with near-zero unidentified segments; hysteresis cut gate transitions from **718** to **54** and intermediate-gain updates from **19.3%** to **4.7%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - When plasticity is applied matters more than how much: the same gain values in scrambled temporal order lose almost the entire retention benefit.
> - A mismatch signal already computed by inference can gate learning, removing any need for task labels, replay buffers, or offline consolidation events.
> - Expect a trade-off: gating broadened the retained repertoire but slightly degraded single-cycle shape accuracy, 40 mm versus 36 mm hand-position error.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research addresses a fundamental challenge in artificial intelligence and robotics: enabling an agent to continuously learn new behaviors while retaining previously acquired knowledge, particularly in an embodied, real-time, and boundary-free setting.

**Relationship to Continual Learning (CL):**
The work is situated within the field of continual learning, specifically tackling the stability-plasticity dilemma, which manifests as catastrophic forgetting in neural networks. Traditional CL approaches often fall into categories such as regularization-based methods (e.g., Elastic Weight Consolidation, Synaptic Intelligence) or replay-based methods (e.g., Gradient Episodic Memory, Deep Generative Replay). While these methods have demonstrated success in mitigating forgetting, they frequently assume explicit task labels, task boundaries, or rely on stored/regenerated past experiences. This paper distinguishes itself by focusing on a "fully online, real-time, boundary-free" setting where such assumptions are relaxed, and learning occurs continuously during ongoing interaction without explicit task signals or offline consolidation.

**Relationship to Embodied Learning and Robotics:**
In robotics, motor learning is frequently approached through Learning from Demonstration (LfD) frameworks, where demonstrations are collected prior to policy or model optimization (e.g., Dynamic Movement Primitives). More recent continual robot learning efforts have explored task-structured benchmarks and skill libraries to preserve dynamics. This research diverges from these paradigms by demanding that state inference, motor generation, physical interaction, and synaptic adaptation unfold concurrently within a single, uninterrupted sensorimotor loop. The paper highlights that current methods often separate learning and generation, or perform weight updates offline or episodically, contrasting with the continuous adaptation required for embodied agents.

**Relationship to Predictive Coding and the Free-Energy Principle:**
The proposed model builds upon the Predictive-Coding-inspired Variational Recurrent Neural Network (PV-RNN) framework, which itself is rooted in predictive coding and the free-energy principle. These principles formulate inference and learning as variational free-energy minimization, providing a mechanism for continuous comparison of predictions with sensory observations. While PV-RNN has been applied to online state inference and human-robot interaction, its synaptic weights typically remain fixed after an initial offline training phase. This work extends the PV-RNN by enabling continuous online adaptation of synaptic weights, directly addressing the limitation of fixed recurrent dynamics during ongoing interaction. The core innovation of regulating plasticity using an internally generated mismatch signal (variational free energy) aligns with biological and computational theories suggesting that the rate of synaptic change can be modulated by signals like prediction error, surprise, or uncertainty.

In summary, this work seeks to bridge the gap between theoretical models of continual learning and the practical demands of real-time, embodied robotic agents, by introducing a mechanism for online plasticity regulation derived from internal model-environment mismatch signals within a predictive coding framework.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable a robotic agent to continuously acquire new motor behaviors in a "fully online, real-time, and boundary-free" setting, while simultaneously preserving previously learned dynamics. This involves addressing the stability-plasticity dilemma directly within an ongoing physical human-robot interaction (pHRI) scenario.

The detailed motivations for this objective are multifaceted:

- **Mimicking Biological Learning:** Human and animal motor skill acquisition occurs through continuous interaction, without distinct phases for inference and learning. Perception, prediction, action, and synaptic modification are intertwined. Artificial systems often separate these processes, with learning occurring offline or episodically. The authors aim for a more biologically inspired, seamless learning process.

- **Overcoming Limitations of Existing Continual Learning (CL) Approaches:**
 - Many CL methods (e.g., regularization-based, replay-based) are designed for settings where task boundaries, task identities, or explicit task transition signals are available. The authors aim to remove these requirements, focusing on a truly boundary-free scenario.
 - Replay-based methods rely on storing or regenerating past experiences, which may not be practical or desirable for embodied agents due to memory constraints or the computational cost of regeneration. This work seeks a mechanism that operates purely on signals available during ongoing interaction.
 - Meta-learning approaches often require offline meta-training over a predefined task distribution, which contrasts with the desired "learning from scratch" and "on-the-fly adaptation" capabilities.

- **Addressing Challenges in Robot Motor Learning (LfD):**
 - Traditional Learning from Demonstration (LfD) often involves collecting demonstrations prior to policy or model optimization. This creates a separation between demonstration and learning phases.
 - Current continual robot learning often utilizes task-structured benchmarks or skill libraries. This paper aims for a more open-ended acquisition of recurrent motor dynamics from initial random states.

- **Extending the Predictive-Coding-inspired Variational Recurrent Neural Network (PV-RNN):**
 - While PV-RNN excels at online *state inference*—adapting its latent variables to recent observations—its synaptic weights typically remain fixed after offline training. This prevents the recurrent dynamics themselves from adapting to new behaviors during ongoing interaction.
 - Simply allowing naive online synaptic adaptation (constant learning rate) would lead to catastrophic forgetting, as updates are driven primarily by recent context, interfering with earlier learned dynamics.

- **Identifying an Internal Signal for Plasticity Regulation:**
 - The core motivation is to find a mechanism that regulates "when and how strongly synaptic adaptation occurs" using signals intrinsically available within the agent's ongoing inference process.
 - Prediction error, surprise, or uncertainty have been linked to adaptive updates and plasticity regulation in biological and computational neuroscience. Variational free energy, already computed within the PV-RNN framework for online inference, is identified as a natural and available candidate for such a signal. It reflects the model-environment mismatch.

The key objective is therefore to propose and evaluate **Free-Energy-Gated Plasticity (FEGP)**, a mechanism that scales synaptic updates as a function of variational free energy. The hypothesis is that increasing plasticity under substantial mismatch (when the model is "surprised" or poorly explains observations) and suppressing it when the model adequately explains the interaction will allow the agent to acquire new motor patterns without extensively interfering with previously learned ones.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research centers on extending the Predictive-Coding-inspired Variational Recurrent Neural Network (PV-RNN) to incorporate continuous online synaptic adaptation, regulated by a novel mechanism called Free-Energy-Gated Plasticity (FEGP).

**Base Model and Online Learning Formulation:**
The foundation is the PV-RNN architecture, specifically its generative model and physical interaction scheme, as described in previous work. The key modification is the removal of the traditional phase separation between offline synaptic learning and online interaction. In this "fully online" setting, the network begins with random weights, without any offline pretraining. During continuous interaction, posterior-state inference, synaptic adaptation, and future trajectory generation are all performed repeatedly and concurrently.

Learning and inference are framed as the minimization of variational free energy ($F$), composed of a Kullback-Leibler divergence term (penalizing divergence between posterior and prior latent distributions) and an accuracy term (penalizing inaccurate sensory predictions). This objective is continuously evaluated over a **sliding window** of the $N=500$ most recent sensory observations. This window provides the necessary finite context for online inference and synaptic adaptation, but it is explicitly not an episodic replay buffer.

**Naive Online Weight Adaptation (Baseline):**
As a baseline, the authors consider "naive online weight adaptation," where synaptic parameters ($\theta$) are updated at every model update ($t$) using a constant base learning rate ($\alpha$) applied to the gradient of the free energy ($\nabla_\theta F_t$). This approach, driven purely by recent prediction errors within the sliding window, is hypothesized to lead to interference with previously learned dynamics.

**Free-Energy-Gated Plasticity (FEGP):**
FEGP is introduced to dynamically regulate the effective learning rate. The effective learning rate ($\alpha_{eff}(t)$) is defined as the base learning rate ($\alpha$) multiplied by a **plasticity gain** ($g(s_t)$), where $g(s_t) \in (0, 1)$.

- **Gate Signal ($s_t$):** This signal quantifies the model-environment mismatch. It is defined as the natural logarithm of the window-averaged variational free energy ($\bar{F}_t$). The free energy includes both an accuracy term (mean prediction divergence over the window) and a complexity term (meta-prior-weighted posterior-prior divergence for each layer). Averaging over the window ensures consistency as the window grows.
- **Plasticity Gain ($g(s_t)$):** The gain is a sigmoid function of the gate signal: $g(s_t) = \sigma((s_t - \lambda)/T)$. Here, $\lambda$ is a threshold that determines the gate's midpoint, and $T$ controls the sharpness of the transition. When $s_t$ is high (large mismatch), $g(s_t)$ approaches 1, allowing full plasticity. When $s_t$ is low (good prediction), $g(s_t)$ approaches 0, suppressing synaptic adaptation.
- **Application to Optimizer:** The Adam optimizer is used. In each model update, $K_{tot}=10$ optimization iterations are performed. The first $K_{ER}=5$ iterations update only the posterior latent variables (ungated). The subsequent $K_W=5$ iterations update both posterior variables and synaptic parameters, with the synaptic updates gated by $\alpha g(s_t)$.
- **Hysteretic Extension:** To prevent rapid fluctuations of the gain when $s_t$ hovers near the threshold $\lambda$, a hysteretic mechanism is introduced. This uses a binary regime state ($r_t \in \{0, 1\}$ for stable/adaptive) with separate thresholds: $\lambda_{high}$ to enter the adaptive regime and $\lambda_{low}$ (where $\lambda_{low} < \lambda_{high}$) to exit it. Stochastic transition rules (Eqs. 9, 10) further smooth the regime switching, adding temporal persistence.

**Robotic Implementation:**
Experiments were conducted on a dual-arm humanoid robot (Torobo). The PV-RNN operates in end-effector space (14-dimensional pose vector: 3D position and 4D unit quaternion for each arm). Predicted poses are converted to joint-angle targets via inverse kinematics and executed by a compliant low-level controller. Sensory observations (robot's current end-effector poses) are derived from measured joint angles via forward kinematics.

**Experimental Design:**
Two main experiments were performed:

- **Experiment 1: Controlled Analysis of FEGP (Prerecorded Data):**
 - **Teaching Data:** Ten teaching streams, each 6,000 model updates long, were constructed by concatenating prerecorded kinesthetic demonstrations of three cyclic motor patterns (A, B, C). Patterns were switched probabilistically, ensuring patterns left the observation window.
 - **Objectives:** Identify optimal FEGP parameters ($\lambda_{low}, \lambda_{high}, T$), compare FEGP against various control conditions, and analyze retention.
 - **Control Conditions:**
 - **Constant-plasticity baseline:** $g(s_t) = 1$.
 - **Time-averaged learning-rate control:** A constant learning rate matched to the *average* effective rate produced by FEGP. This isolates the effect of varying plasticity from simply a reduced average rate.
 - **Replayed-gain control:** The gain sequence generated by FEGP was replayed in permuted, time-reversed, or shifted order. This preserves the *distribution* of gain values but disrupts their *temporal alignment* with model-environment mismatch.
 - **Synaptic Intelligence (SI) comparison:** A continuously accumulating variant of SI [S2] was used with varying regularization coefficients ($c$) to compare against a representative continual learning regularizer.

- **Experiment 2: Online Acquisition During Physical Interaction (Live Data):**
 - **Teaching Data:** Four independent 5-minute sessions (6,000 model updates) of live kinesthetic teaching by a human experimenter, following a cyclic order of patterns A, B, C.
 - **Objective:** Demonstrate the complete system's ability to acquire multiple motor behaviors from scratch in real-time pHRI using the optimized FEGP configuration.
 - **Evaluation:** Analysis of online rollouts and autonomous generations from saved model checkpoints.

**Evaluation Pipeline (Offline):**
A fixed, post hoc evaluation pipeline was developed to quantify learning outcomes, separated from the online learning loop.
- **Segment-boundary detection:** A random forest classifier detected cycle boundaries in generated trajectories.
- **Segment classification:** Multivariate Dynamic Time Warping (DTW) classified detected segments as pattern A, B, C, or "Unknown" based on distance to reference trajectories and duration criteria.
- **Metrics:** Repertoire coverage (fraction of rollouts with all three patterns), retention (fraction of rollouts with a pattern absent from the observation window), shape distance (median DTW distance to assigned class reference), and unidentified-segment ratio. The pipeline was rigorously validated against ground truth and held-out demonstrations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments yielded several findings regarding the efficacy and mechanisms of Free-Energy-Gated Plasticity (FEGP) in fully online, real-time motor learning.

**1. Real-Time Online Acquisition of Motor Patterns in pHRI:**
- Experiment 2 demonstrated that a randomly initialized PV-RNN, when equipped with FEGP, could acquire three distinct cyclic motor patterns (A, B, C) from scratch during continuous 5-minute physical human-robot interaction sessions.
- As learning progressed, the number of identified movement types in the robot's autonomous rollouts increased, and the proportion of unidentified segments decreased to near zero. All three instructed patterns eventually emerged in autonomous generation across the four independent sessions.
- This qualitatively supported the retention observed in controlled experiments, showing that patterns could be generated even when not recently observed.

**2. Improved Repertoire Coverage and Retention with FEGP:**
- In Experiment 1, FEGP substantially outperformed naive online weight adaptation (constant plasticity). Repertoire coverage (all three patterns present in rollout) increased from 18.9% to 45.9%.
- Crucially, retention of previously acquired patterns, particularly after they left the recent observation window, improved from 0.0% under constant plasticity to 23.1% under FEGP. This indicates that unregulated online adaptation provided minimal preservation of learned behaviors.

**3. Temporal Allocation of Plasticity is Critical:**
- The improvements under FEGP were not attributable to a mere reduction in the average effective learning rate or to the overall distribution of gain values.
 - The **time-averaged learning-rate control**, which used a constant learning rate equal to FEGP's average effective rate, achieved only 12.5% coverage and 0.2% retention. This demonstrated that simply reducing the average plasticity was insufficient; its temporal modulation was key.
 - The **replayed-gain controls** (permuted, reversed, or shifted gain sequences derived from FEGP) also showed substantially lower performance (11.4-21.3% coverage, 0.5-2.2% retention). This indicated that preserving the *values* of the plasticity gain was not enough; their *temporal alignment* with the model-environment mismatch was essential for FEGP's benefits.

**4. FEGP Primarily Acts on Retention of Absent Patterns:**
- The effect of FEGP was most pronounced after a learned pattern was no longer present in the 500-update observation window.
 - While a pattern was still in the window, FEGP produced it in 66.6% of rollouts, comparable to 60.6% for constant plasticity.
 - However, once a pattern left the window, constant plasticity and the matched-rate control rapidly lost retention (dropping to 0.0-0.8%). In contrast, under FEGP, retention remained substantial (23.8% to 32.7%) even after several hundred model updates of absence.
- This suggests FEGP primarily extends the duration over which previously learned movements remain available without continuous rehearsal.

**5. Trade-off Between Shape Accuracy and Repertoire Maintenance:**
- FEGP did not improve the shape accuracy of individual generated movements. The median DTW shape distance was slightly higher under FEGP (0.475) compared to constant plasticity (0.393), though the difference in physical units (40mm vs 36mm hand position error) was minor relative to demonstration variability.
- This indicates a trade-off where constant plasticity produced slightly more accurate individual movements, while FEGP prioritized maintaining a broader and more diverse behavioral repertoire.

**6. Hysteretic Gate Dynamics:**
- The hysteretic extension to FEGP significantly stabilized the plasticity mechanism. It reduced rapid switching between low- and high-plasticity regimes, decreasing the average number of threshold crossings from 718 to 54 and the proportion of updates with intermediate gain from 19.3% to 4.7%.

**7. Comparison with Synaptic Intelligence (SI):**
- A continuously accumulating variant of Synaptic Intelligence showed a narrow operating range.
- Weak SI regularization allowed repertoire acquisition comparable to the unregularized baseline (28.8% coverage at $c=10^{-5}$) but did not yield retention benefits (0.4%).
- Stronger regularization prevented stable acquisition altogether. This suggested SI, in its tested continuous accumulation form, did not provide a beneficial acquisition-retention trade-off in this boundary-free setting.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the fields of artificial intelligence, robotics, and computational neuroscience by demonstrating a viable approach to fully online, real-time, and boundary-free motor learning in embodied systems.

**1. Advancement in Continual Learning for Embodied Systems:**
The work offers a method for addressing the stability-plasticity dilemma in a context where traditional continual learning methods often fall short: continuous, unsegmented interaction with a physical environment. By demonstrating learning from scratch without offline pretraining, replay buffers, or explicit task boundaries, it pushes the frontier of embodied continual learning towards more biologically plausible and autonomous systems.

**2. Practical Implications for Physical Human-Robot Interaction (pHRI):**
The ability of a robot to continuously acquire and retain multiple motor skills during ongoing kinesthetic interaction without external supervision or task signals represents a significant step for pHRI. This could enable robots to adapt to evolving user preferences or environmental changes over extended periods, making them more intuitive, flexible, and robust partners in dynamic human environments. For instance, a robot could incrementally learn new manipulation tasks or interaction patterns as a human guides it, rather than requiring re-programming or re-training for each new skill.

**3. Validation of Free-Energy Principle and Predictive Coding:**
By leveraging variational free energy as an intrinsic signal for plasticity regulation, the research further validates the utility of predictive coding and the free-energy principle for designing adaptive agents. It demonstrates how signals already computed for inference can be repurposed to govern learning, suggesting a parsimonious computational architecture consistent with certain theories of brain function.

**4. Enhanced Adaptive Capabilities of PV-RNNs:**
The extension of PV-RNNs from online state inference to online synaptic learning significantly enhances their adaptive capabilities. This allows the recurrent dynamics of the model itself to evolve and adapt to new motor primitives, rather than merely adjusting latent states within fixed dynamics. This could broaden the applicability of PV-RNNs to a wider range of open-ended learning tasks.

**5. Foundation for Future Research:**
This work opens several avenues for future investigation:
- **Scaling and Generalization:** Exploring the scalability of FEGP to larger and more diverse repertoires of motor behaviors, more complex sensorimotor spaces, and longer interaction durations.
- **Autonomous Parameter Adaptation:** Investigating methods for automatic adaptation of FEGP's gating parameters (e.g., $\lambda$, $T$) rather than empirical selection, to make the system more autonomous.
- **Long-Term Memory and Consolidation:** While FEGP improves retention, further research into mechanisms for long-term memory consolidation and more robust prevention of forgetting over very extended periods could be integrated.
- **Richer Interaction Paradigms:** Applying FEGP to more complex and nuanced physical and social interactions, beyond kinesthetic teaching, to assess its generality.

In conclusion, this research provides a computational mechanism that allows an embodied agent to learn multiple motor skills in an online, continuous, and boundary-free manner, regulated by its own internal assessment of model-environment mismatch. This approach holds potential for developing more adaptive, autonomous, and biologically-inspired robotic systems capable of lifelong learning in dynamic environments.
