# H-Flow: Self-supervised Human Scene Flow via Physics-inspired Joint Multi-modal Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> H-Flow introduces a self-supervised method for dense human scene flow that integrates skeletal kinematics and non-rigid surface deformations from monocular video using physics-inspired joint multi-modal learning. The system achieves state-of-the-art dense flow estimation, reducing 3D End-Point-Error by 37% on Fit3D and 33% on DynAct4D compared to leading baselines, while also improving companion modalities like pose and depth.

## Problem

- Parametric human models (e.g., SMPL) cannot fully represent non-skeletal motion, such as clothing dynamics or soft tissue deformation, due to their reliance on linear blend skinning (LBS).
- Generic 3D scene flow methods, which often rely on local rigid-body assumptions, produce inaccurate motion estimates when applied to highly articulated and deformable human bodies.
- The absence of dense 3D human flow ground truth data makes it infeasible to train supervised models for high-fidelity non-rigid clothed human motion estimation.

## Method

- **H-Flow** employs a unified multi-head transformer architecture to jointly estimate dense depth, 3D scene flow, a soft human mask, 3D joint positions, and camera parameters from monocular video.
- A ==self-supervised learning== paradigm leverages physics-inspired ==cross-modal== training objectives, including geometric silhouette alignment, skeletal-surface coupling, center-of-mass support, and minimum effort path.
- Auxiliary terms, such as a margin-based distillation anchor and camera consistency constraints, provide absolute metric scale and prevent degenerate solutions during ==self-supervised== training.

## Results

- H-Flow achieved a 3D End-Point-Error (EPE) of 24.2 mm on Fit3D, a **37%** reduction compared to the next best method, and 28.2 mm on the DynAct4D benchmark under zero-shot conditions, outperforming baselines by **33%**.
- The system demonstrated superior pose estimation, achieving the lowest Mean Per-Joint Position Error (MPJPE) on 3DPW (91.5 mm) and Fit3D (112.8 mm), surpassing dedicated pose estimators.
- H-Flow significantly reduced depth estimation errors within the SMPL-X body region, with a **47%** decrease in Mean Absolute Error (MAE) on Fit3D compared to generic monocular depth predictors.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Physics-inspired priors, encoded as differentiable cross-modal constraints, enable effective self-supervised learning of dense human scene flow without requiring direct flow ground truth.
> - Joint estimation of multiple related modalities (flow, depth, pose, mask) within a single architecture leads to mutual performance benefits, where each output's accuracy improves by leveraging consistency with the others.
> - The distillation anchor is crucial for injecting global metric scale and anthropometric priors into the network, enabling robust zero-shot generalization to in-the-wild videos without runtime calibration.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of human motion analysis and reconstruction aims to capture the complex dynamics of a person's movement. This objective involves understanding both the global skeletal kinematics and the intricate surface deformations caused by clothing and soft tissue. Current methodologies typically fall into two main categories: parametric human models and generic 3D scene flow methods.

Parametric human models, such as SMPL and SMPL-X, have advanced robust 3D pose and shape recovery from monocular inputs. These models excel at capturing global skeletal structure and pose, providing strong geometric priors. However, their reliance on linear blend skinning (LBS) limits their ability to represent motions independent of the skeletal topology, such as the dynamic behavior of loose clothing or the nuanced deformation of soft tissue. This inherent limitation means critical details for realistic modeling and behavior understanding remain uncaptured by these skeleton-bound approaches.

In parallel, generic 3D scene flow methods estimate a dense motion vector for every visible point in a scene, offering the potential to capture fine-grained surface dynamics. However, these methods primarily rely on local rigid-body assumptions for regularization, which are fundamentally incompatible with highly articulated and non-rigid human motion. When applied to humans, these rigidity priors often result in degraded predictions, such as smeared motion vectors at limb extremities or garment edges, leading to reduced accuracy in human-specific regions compared to generic scenes.

The challenge is further compounded by the absence of dense 3D human flow ground truth data. Acquiring such pixel-level supervision for non-rigid clothed human bodies in real-world scenarios is largely intractable. Previous self-supervised scene flow methods attempt to circumvent this by using rigidity priors or pseudo-label generation, but these approaches often lead to unnatural stiffening or feature tearing on deformable surfaces. Human-centric benchmarks have focused on 3D kinematics or parametric body recovery, lacking dense surface flow annotations.

H-Flow addresses this gap by developing a dense human scene flow representation that integrates the skeletal accuracy characteristic of parametric models with the per-point density offered by generic scene flow. It seeks to capture both articulated motion and non-rigid surface deformation within a unified framework, specifically designed to operate without direct flow supervision by leveraging a physics-inspired self-supervised learning paradigm. The introduction of the DynAct4D benchmark also contributes to the landscape by providing a dedicated resource for evaluating dense human scene flow, a critical need given the limitations of existing datasets.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop H-Flow, a dense human scene flow method capable of capturing both the underlying skeletal kinematics and the complex, non-rigid surface deformations of clothed human bodies from monocular video. This encompasses the goal of providing a per-point 3D motion vector for every visible human point, including garments and soft tissue, while maintaining the structural accuracy typically associated with parametric models.

The motivation for this work arises from several limitations in existing human motion perception paradigms:

1. **Incompleteness of Parametric Models:** Current parametric human models, while effective for global pose and shape, cannot accurately represent non-skeletal motion. The linear blend skinning (LBS) approach inherently restricts surface deformation to bone transformations, failing to capture phenomena like a flowing skirt or the jiggle of soft tissue. These details are crucial for realistic animation, virtual try-on, medical diagnostics, and detailed behavior analysis.

2. **Limitations of Generic Scene Flow on Articulated Bodies:** Generic 3D scene flow methods, designed for dense motion estimation, typically rely on local rigid-body assumptions. These assumptions break down when applied to highly articulated and deformable human bodies, leading to inaccurate motion estimates, particularly at limb tips and garment boundaries. This structural incompatibility results in a fundamental barrier for their effective application to human subjects.

3. **Absence of Dense Human Flow Ground Truth:** A significant hurdle for developing dense human scene flow methods is the practical impossibility of acquiring dense, per-pixel 3D motion ground truth for non-rigid, clothed human bodies in real-world settings. This lack of supervision prevents the direct application of supervised learning techniques, necessitating a novel self-supervised approach.

To address these motivations, H-Flow sets forth the following specific objectives:

- **Self-supervised Learning Paradigm:** To design a self-supervised learning framework that circumvents the need for dense flow ground truth. This involves jointly estimating multiple modalities (pose, depth, mask, camera, and flow) within a single architecture and imposing physical consistency constraints between them.
- **Physics-inspired Priors:** To encode geometric, structural, and biomechanical priors of human motion as cross-modal training objectives. This approach anchors the network in the physical realities of human movement, enabling learning from monocular video alone.
- **Mutual Benefit across Modalities:** To demonstrate that by explicitly coupling these modalities and priors, the system achieves mutual gains, where each estimated output (pose, depth, flow) benefits from the others, leading to improved accuracy beyond individual, specialized estimators.
- **High-fidelity Synthetic Benchmark (DynAct4D):** To create a new, rigorously annotated synthetic dataset, DynAct4D, that provides exact dense 3D flow ground truth for diverse clothed human characters performing a wide range of actions. This benchmark is crucial for quantitative evaluation, especially for zero-shot generalization.
- **Zero-shot Generalization:** To demonstrate that the learned physics-inspired priors enable H-Flow to generalize effectively to in-the-wild videos without requiring further fine-tuning or runtime calibration.

In summary, the research is motivated by the need for a comprehensive human motion representation that overcomes the limitations of existing methods, particularly the challenge of acquiring supervision, by leveraging a unified, physics-informed self-supervised learning approach.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

H-Flow employs a multi-modal, self-supervised learning paradigm to estimate dense human scene flow from monocular video. The approach is structured around a unified transformer architecture and a set of physics-inspired cross-modal training objectives.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation and Architecture

The system is formulated to jointly estimate five outputs for each video frame: dense depth ($D_i$), forward 3D scene flow ($F_i$), a soft human mask ($M_i$), 3D joint positions ($P_i$), and camera parameters ($C_i$). These outputs are considered projections of a single underlying physical system.

The core architecture, denoted as $G_\theta$, is a unified multi-head transformer. It utilizes a frozen DINOv3 ViT-L/16 backbone, pre-trained on a large visual dataset, to extract visual features. A lightweight projector module adapts these features for downstream geometric tasks. The main trunk consists of 16 joint query-patch attention layers, where patch features and learnable queries for pose and camera interact at every layer. This shared processing within the transformer ensures "representation-level coupling" among the different modalities. Three independent DPT-style multi-scale fusion modules serve as dense decoder heads for depth, flow, and mask, while MLPs convert final query states into pose and camera outputs. The camera parameters include intrinsics, rotation, and translation, with rotation parameterized by a continuous 6D representation.

#### 3.2. Self-supervised Constraints from Physical Priors

Direct supervision for dense human flow is unavailable. Instead, $G_\theta$ is optimized against a set of differentiable constraints, each encoding a physical prior of human movement. This constitutes the "supervision-level coupling."

- **Geometric: Silhouette Edge Alignment ($C_{silh}$):** This constraint ensures that discontinuities in estimated depth and flow fields align with the visible human boundary defined by the mask ($M$). It penalizes the gradient norms of depth and flow, weighted by the signed distance field to the mask boundary. This symmetric penalty pulls interior spurious edges outwards and exterior leakage inwards, coupling depth ($D$), flow ($F$), and mask ($M$).

- **Structural: Skeletal-Surface Coupling ($C_{skel}$):** This prior anchors surface motion to the underlying skeletal kinematics. Each foreground pixel is lifted to a 3D surface point using predicted depth, assigned to its nearest bone segment, and its predicted flow is compared against the motion of that bone. A tolerance margin, $\rho(u)$, is applied, which increases with the 3D distance from the surface point to its nearest bone. This allows for clothing dynamics (wider margin for loose garments) while enforcing skeletal alignment for points closer to the body. This constraint couples flow ($F$), depth ($D$), and pose ($P$).

- **Structural: Center-of-Mass Support ($C_{CoM}$):** This constraint enforces biomechanical stability by requiring the projected center of mass ($\mu_{xy}$), derived from weighted joint positions, to lie within the support polygon. The support polygon is formed by ground-contact end-effectors, identified using pose and depth, and its convex hull is computed on a fitted ground plane. The constraint penalizes the signed distance by which the mass center falls outside this polygon, coupling pose ($P$) and depth ($D$).

- **Biomechanical: Minimum Effort Path ($C_{eff}$):** This constraint regularizes multi-frame pose trajectories. For a given temporal window, the network's predicted endpoint poses are used to derive a "minimum-jerk" trajectory in joint-angle space. The intermediate predicted poses are then penalized for deviating from this energy-optimal reference path beyond a tolerance margin. Stop-gradient is applied to the endpoint poses to anchor regularization on intermediate dynamics. While directly acting on pose ($P$), its gradient propagates through the shared backbone to other modalities.

#### 3.3. Anchoring and Consistency

To prevent degenerate minima inherent in purely self-supervised training and to inject absolute metric scale, two auxiliary terms are used:

- **Distillation Anchor ($C_{dist}$):** A margin-based distillation loss anchors the estimated depth ($D$) and pose ($P$) against predictions from off-the-shelf teachers (Depth Pro for depth, SAM 3D Body for pose). This term activates only when H-Flow's predictions deviate significantly from the teacher, providing a noisy but metrically scaled reference without strictly mimicking the teacher.

- **Camera Consistency ($C_{cam}$):** To ensure consistent camera intrinsics across frames from a single video clip, a consistency term softly drives the per-frame intrinsic parameters to agree.

#### 3.4. DynAct4D Benchmark Construction

A key part of the methodology is the creation of DynAct4D, a high-fidelity synthetic benchmark for dense human scene flow.
- **Generation:** It is built using the MetaHuman framework within Unreal Engine, allowing for photorealistic and anatomically diverse human characters with physics-simulated clothing.
- **Ground Truth:** DynAct4D provides exact per-pixel 3D flow ground truth by directly tracking world-space positions of skinned mesh vertices and propagating this motion to pixels via a custom G-buffer pass. This bypasses the limitations of composite 2D flow or sparse vertex data.
- **Diversity:** The dataset includes 10 characters spanning genders, ethnicities, and body types, 10 garment configurations (tight to loose), 10 action categories (from Mixamo), 8 synchronized viewpoints, and 3 environments. This results in 800 test sequences totaling ~720K frames at 1920x1080 resolution.
- **Purpose:** DynAct4D serves as an out-of-domain test set, evaluating zero-shot transfer performance of methods trained on external data.

#### 3.5. Training and Inference

H-Flow is pretrained on Human3.6M, followed by fine-tuning on Fit3D and 3DPW train splits. Training uses RGB video only, with no modality ground truth from these datasets. Inference supports arbitrary video lengths, processed via a sliding window for longer clips, and produces all outputs in a single forward pass without test-time optimization.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of H-Flow was conducted across multiple datasets, including Fit3D (in-domain), 3DPW (for companion modalities), and the newly introduced DynAct4D (out-of-domain, zero-shot).

#### 4.1. Scene Flow Estimation

H-Flow demonstrates improved performance in dense scene flow estimation compared to various baseline methods.
- **Quantitative Performance:** On Fit3D, H-Flow achieved a 3D End-Point-Error (EPE) of 24.2 mm, a 37% reduction compared to the next closest competitor, the Depth Pro + H-MoRe pipeline (38.3 mm). On DynAct4D, under zero-shot transfer, H-Flow maintained superior performance with an EPE of 28.2 mm, outperforming Depth Pro + H-MoRe (42.2 mm) by 33%. Generic scene flow methods like ZeroMSF and Self-Mono-SF showed significantly higher EPEs (above 50 mm on Fit3D and 68 mm on DynAct4D), indicating their limitations with articulated human motion.
- **Qualitative Performance:** Visualizations indicate that H-Flow recovers fine-grained surface flow that aligns with both skeletal kinematics and clothing deformation. In contrast, generic scene flow baselines exhibited smearing at limb tips and clothing edges, optical flow lifting pipelines accumulated edge artifacts, and mesh residual methods, while preserving global pose, flattened secondary surface dynamics (e.g., garment swirl and compression). H-Flow's ability to maintain coherent volumetric structure across different viewpoints, even for complex elements like a ballet tutu, further highlights its robustness.

#### 4.2. Companion Modality Estimation

The joint multi-modal estimation framework of H-Flow also yields competitive or superior results for companion modalities (pose and depth).
- **Pose Estimation:** H-Flow achieved the lowest Mean Per-Joint Position Error (MPJPE) on both 3DPW (91.5 mm) and Fit3D (112.8 mm), surpassing dedicated pose estimators like SAM 3D Body, CameraHMR, and PromptHMR. This is attributed to the metric depth awareness provided by the joint estimation architecture. On Procrustes-aligned MPJPE (PA-MPJPE), H-Flow remained competitive with specialized 2D-to-3D lifting methods like HiPART and MotionAGFormer.
- **Depth Estimation:** Within the SMPL-X body region, H-Flow significantly reduced the Mean Absolute Error (MAE) and Scale-Invariant Log Error (SiLog) compared to generic monocular metric depth predictors. For instance, on Fit3D, MAE dropped from 234.3 mm (Depth Pro) to 124.5 mm (H-Flow), a 47% reduction. This indicates that H-Flow's design concentrates model capacity on the human surface, relevant for downstream scene flow, rather than uniformly across the entire scene.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies confirmed the contribution of each physical prior and the distillation anchor to H-Flow's overall performance.
- **Cross-modal Effects:** Removing the Skeletal-Surface Coupling ($C_{skel}$) resulted in the most significant degradation in scene flow EPE, doubling it on Fit3D and more than doubling it on DynAct4D. The Silhouette Edge Alignment ($C_{silh}$) primarily sharpened depth and indirectly improved scene flow at edge regions. The Center-of-Mass Support ($C_{CoM}$) had the largest impact on pose accuracy, improving physical plausibility. The Minimum Effort Path ($C_{eff}$) influenced pose trajectories, and its removal also led to a substantial increase in Fit3D scene flow EPE, indicating the interconnectedness of modalities through the shared backbone. These results confirm the "mutual gains across all three modalities" principle.
- **Distillation as Anthropometric Anchor:** Removing the distillation anchor caused absolute errors (EPE, MPJPE, MAE) to double or triple, while scale-invariant metrics (PA-MPJPE, SiLog) remained largely unaffected. This demonstrates that the four physical priors are scale-invariant, and the distillation anchor is crucial for injecting global metric scale into the network, internalizing an anthropometric prior that enables zero-shot transfer without runtime calibration.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The H-Flow research presents several significant advancements and potential impacts within the domains of computer vision, human motion analysis, and related applications.

1. **Comprehensive Human Motion Representation:** H-Flow offers a novel solution for capturing dense human scene flow that integrates both skeletal kinematics and non-rigid surface deformation. This unified approach addresses a critical limitation of previous methods, which either focused on rigid skeletal motion (parametric models) or struggled with articulated, deformable bodies (generic scene flow). This capability is crucial for generating more realistic human animations, virtual try-on systems, and detailed biomechanical analyses.

2. **Pioneering Self-supervised Learning without Flow Ground Truth:** The introduction of a physics-inspired self-supervised learning paradigm is a substantial contribution. By leveraging geometric, structural, and biomechanical priors as cross-modal training objectives, H-Flow circumvents the intractable challenge of acquiring dense 3D human flow ground truth. This approach demonstrates a viable pathway for learning complex, high-dimensional motion in scenarios where direct supervision is impractical or impossible.

3. **Enhanced Modality Interdependence and Mutual Benefit:** The joint estimation of pose, depth, mask, camera, and flow within a single architecture, coupled with physics-inspired constraints, showcases a powerful example of cross-modal mutual benefit. The findings indicate that optimizing these interdependencies leads to improved performance across all modalities, where each output benefits from the context and regularization provided by the others. This paradigm could influence future multi-modal learning system designs.

4. **Robustness and Zero-shot Generalization:** H-Flow's ability to achieve state-of-the-art performance on in-domain datasets (Fit3D) and, critically, to generalize zero-shot to entirely new, out-of-domain videos (DynAct4D and in-the-wild ballet) is a strong indicator of its robustness and the transferability of its learned physical priors. This generalization capability is vital for real-world applications, reducing the need for extensive dataset-specific fine-tuning.

5. **Introduction of a Rigorous Benchmark:** The creation of DynAct4D fills a notable gap in human motion datasets. By providing high-fidelity, per-pixel 3D flow ground truth for diverse clothed human characters and actions, DynAct4D establishes a much-needed benchmark for the rigorous evaluation of dense human scene flow methods, particularly for assessing performance on non-rigid clothing deformation and out-of-distribution scenarios. This resource will be instrumental for guiding future research in the field.

6. **Foundation for Future Research:** The work opens several avenues for further exploration. Acknowledging that the current approach relies on a distillation anchor for global metric scale, a fully self-supervised method for anthropometric grounding remains an open challenge. Furthermore, the architecture and principles could be extended to more complex scenarios, such as human-object interaction, by incorporating additional physical priors related to contact, grasp, and release dynamics, thereby expanding its applicability beyond isolated human motion.
