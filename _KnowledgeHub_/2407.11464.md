# Crowd-SAM: SAM as a Smart Annotator for Object Detection in Crowded Scenes

> [!summary] Summary
> Crowd-SAM introduces a framework that adapts the Segment Anything Model (SAM) and DINOv2 to serve as a smart annotator for object detection in crowded scenes, achieving performance comparable to fully-supervised methods using as few as 10 labeled images. This approach effectively resolves geometric ambiguities and efficiently generates high-quality object masks by integrating lightweight, learnable modules.

## Problem

- High annotation costs for object detection, particularly in 'crowded scenes' with numerous, often occluded objects, where manual labeling is time-consuming and expensive.
- Existing object detectors and even foundation models like SAM struggle with accuracy and efficiency in highly occluded and dense environments, often producing part-level segments or false positives.
- Traditional label-efficient methods often introduce complexity or do not specifically address the unique challenges of crowded scenes while requiring significant labeled data.

## Method

- ==Crowd-SAM== integrates frozen ==SAM== and ==DINOv2== foundation models with specialized, lightweight, and learnable modules to act as a smart annotator.
- It generates class-specific prompts using a DINOv2-based ==semantic heatmap==, trained with refined pseudo-labels derived from SAM with ground truth bounding box centers.
- An ==Efficient Prompt Sampler (EPS)== prunes redundant prompts for computational efficiency, while a ==Part-Whole Discrimination Network (PWD-Net)== selects the best mask and distinguishes whole objects from parts or background noise in occluded scenarios.

## Results

- Crowd-SAM achieves **78.4%** AP on the CrowdHuman dataset with only **10 labeled images**, demonstrating performance comparable to state-of-the-art fully-supervised detectors.
- On the OccHuman dataset for occluded pedestrian detection, it obtains **31.4%** AP, outperforming the fully-supervised Pose2Seg by **9.2%** AP.
- Ablation studies confirm that the Part-Whole Discrimination Network is critical, contributing a **40%** AP increase, and the DINOv2-based foreground location module improves AP by **7.4%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Powerful pre-trained foundation models like SAM and DINOv2 can be effectively adapted for challenging downstream tasks with minimal task-specific training and few learnable parameters.
> - A dedicated discrimination network (PWD-Net) is indispensable for accurate mask selection in crowded scenes, crucial for addressing occlusion and part-whole ambiguity.
> - Efficient prompt sampling and semantic-guided prompt generation are essential for managing computational costs and improving recall in dense object environments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Object detection is a cornerstone task in computer vision, with wide-ranging applications from autonomous vehicles to video surveillance. While significant advancements have been made, particularly with two-stage, one-stage, and query-based detectors, these methods predominantly operate in a supervised manner. This reliance on extensive labeled datasets incurs prohibitive annotation costs, a problem exacerbated in "crowded scenes" characterized by numerous, often occluded, objects. The paper notes that annotating a single object can take approximately 42.4 seconds, with crowded scenes like CrowdHuman averaging 22 objects per image, significantly multiplying this burden.

To mitigate the annotation bottleneck, the research community has explored various label-efficient paradigms, including few-shot learning, weakly supervised learning, semi-supervised learning (SSL), and unsupervised learning. Semi-supervised object detection (SSOD) has shown promise by utilizing both labeled and unlabeled data but often introduces additional complexity through sophisticated augmentations and online pseudo-labeling schemes.

A recent paradigm shift in computer vision has been the emergence of "foundation models," pre-trained on massive datasets and demonstrating strong generalization capabilities. The Segment Anything Model (SAM) is a prime example, offering powerful zero-shot instance segmentation based on flexible prompts (points, boxes, masks, text). SAM's success has inspired numerous adaptations for diverse tasks, including medical imaging, remote sensing, and industrial inspection. However, despite its general prowess, SAM and its variants face limitations when confronted with the unique challenges of *crowded scenes*, such as degraded accuracy and efficiency due to frequent occlusions and the inherent ambiguity of class-agnostic segmentation in dense environments. Many existing SAM adaptations still require considerable labeled data or do not specifically address the issues of occlusion and object discrimination in highly clustered settings.

Crowd-SAM positions itself precisely in this intersection. It aims to bridge the gap between SAM's powerful general segmentation abilities and the specific, demanding requirements of object detection in crowded scenes, all while maintaining high label efficiency. By proposing a framework that adapts SAM with "few learnable parameters and minimal labeled images," this work directly addresses the annotation cost problem while leveraging the robust visual knowledge embedded in foundational models like SAM and DINOv2. It seeks to convert SAM from a general segmenter into a "smart annotator" tailored for complex, real-world crowded scenarios, effectively extending the utility of large pre-trained models to a highly challenging and practical domain. This moves the field towards more practical and deployable computer vision systems that reduce human annotation effort.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop **Crowd-SAM**, a framework that enhances the performance of the Segment Anything Model (SAM) for object detection in crowded scenes, specifically designed as a "smart annotator."

The motivations underpinning this objective are twofold:

1. **Leveraging Pre-trained Knowledge:** SAM has been pre-trained on an enormous dataset (SA-1B), encompassing a vast array of common objects. This implies that SAM already possesses substantial knowledge about object shapes and boundaries. The authors aim to effectively utilize this embedded knowledge to facilitate the labeling of massive datasets and the training of new detectors, thereby reducing the need for extensive manual annotation.
2. **Robust Segmentation in Complex Scenes:** SAM has demonstrated a remarkable ability to segment objects even in complicated scenes characterized by clustered objects. This robustness is particularly valuable for crowded scenarios where occlusions and dense packing make traditional object detection challenging for models trained from scratch. Crowd-SAM seeks to harness this inherent capability and adapt it specifically for accurate detection and mask selection in such difficult environments.

More specifically, the paper outlines several key goals for Crowd-SAM:
- To create a **self-prompted segmentation method** that can accurately label images containing clustered objects with only a few examples.
- To overcome the limitations of SAM in crowded scenes, where accuracy and efficiency are often compromised due to occlusions.
- To achieve performance comparable to state-of-the-art fully-supervised object detection methods, despite requiring significantly fewer labeled images.
- To introduce novel components that specifically address the challenges of dense prompts and accurate mask selection in occluded scenarios.

Ultimately, the overarching motivation is to significantly reduce the labor-intensive and costly annotation burden associated with object detection datasets, especially in complex crowded environments, by transforming a powerful foundation model like SAM into an intelligent and efficient annotation tool.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Crowd-SAM proposes a lightweight, SAM-based framework designed to act as a "smart annotator" for object detection in crowded scenes, minimizing learnable parameters and labeled data. The core idea is to leverage the robust capabilities of pre-trained foundation models (SAM and DINOv2) and introduce specialized modules to handle the unique challenges of density and occlusion.

The framework comprises several key components and a sequential process:

1. **Preliminaries (Frozen Foundation Models):**
 - **SAM (ViT-L):** Utilized for its powerful zero-shot segmentation capabilities. Its image encoder, prompt encoder, and mask decoder components are frozen during Crowd-SAM's training to preserve its vast pre-trained knowledge.
 - **DINOv2 (ViT-L):** Employed for its strong self-supervised learned visual representations, which are semantically rich and crucial for distinguishing foreground objects. Its parameters are also frozen.

2. **Class-specific Prompt Generation (Semantic Heatmap):**
 - **Motivation:** Generating individual point prompts for every object in a crowded scene is difficult. The authors adopt a heatmap-based approach to identify potential object regions.
 - **Process:** DINOv2's image encoder extracts semantic features (F_DINO). A lightweight MLP block and a binary segmentation head (Head_CLS) are appended to DINOv2 to classify these features pixel-by-pixel, generating a semantic heatmap (H_hat).
 - **Supervision:** To train Head_CLS with minimal data, the framework uses high-quality *pseudo masks*. These are initially generated by SAM itself, prompted by ground truth bounding box centers from a few labeled images. The pseudo masks are merged into a single foreground mask (H).
 - **Loss:** A Dice loss (L_fg) is used to optimize Head_CLS with these pseudo masks.
 - **Prompt Extraction:** During inference, the binarized heatmap (after thresholding) identifies positive regions, and point prompts (P_G) are extracted from these regions.

3. **Semantic-guided Mask Prediction:**
 - **Challenge:** Dense point prompts (necessary for high recall in crowded scenes) lead to computational inefficiency and numerous false positives (FPs) from SAM.
 - **Efficient Prompt Sampler (EPS):**
 - **Purpose:** Dynamically prunes redundant prompts to reduce computational cost and FPs.
 - **Mechanism:** EPS iteratively samples batches of prompts from P_G. SAM then generates masks for these batches. The generated masks are evaluated by the **PWD-Net** (described next) to assign confidence scores. Masks with scores above a certain threshold are considered "valid." Any prompt in P_G that falls within a valid mask is removed from P_G. The process continues until P_G is empty or a maximum number of samples (K) is reached. This ensures coverage while avoiding redundant computations and filtering out prompts for already detected objects.

 - **Part-Whole Discrimination Network (PWD-Net):**
 - **Purpose:** Selects the best-fitting mask among SAM's multiple outputs and discriminates between correct object masks, background, and part-level segmentations (a common issue with class-agnostic SAM in occluded scenes).
 - **Inputs:** It leverages three types of tokens: SAM's Mask Tokens (M) and IoU Tokens (U) (from its mask decoder), and DINOv2's semantic features (F_DINO).
 - **Refined IoU Score (S_iou):** A parallel IoU head (Head_par, an MLP) is trained to refine the IoU score, taking concatenated and repeated Mask and IoU Tokens as input, and adding it to the original (frozen) SAM IoU Head's output. This provides shape-aware quality assessment.
 - **Discriminant Score (S_cls):** This score predicts whether a mask belongs to the foreground or background. It's calculated by pooling DINOv2's semantic features (F_DINO) weighted by the downscaled, softmaxed SAM masks (M_hat), then passed through a binary classifier (Head_CLS, sharing parameters with the one used for prompt generation).
 - **Joint Score (S):** The final confidence score for a mask is a product of the refined IoU score and the discriminant score: `S = S_iou * S_cls`.
 - **Loss:** An MSE loss (L_iou) is applied, where the target score (S_target) is the true IoU for foreground prompts and 0 for background prompts (determined from pseudo masks generated by SAM with GT boxes).

4. **Training and Inference:**
 - **Total Loss:** The entire framework is optimized using a combined loss: `L = L_fg + L_iou`.
 - **Inference:** During inference, PWD-Net selects the mask with the highest confidence score among SAM's four outputs. A multi-cropping strategy is applied to images (especially beneficial for small objects), and Non-Maximum Suppression (NMS) is used to merge results and remove duplicate proposals.

By combining the powerful pre-trained knowledge of SAM and DINOv2 with learnable, lightweight modules like EPS and PWD-Net, Crowd-SAM efficiently generates class-specific prompts, prunes redundant segmentations, and accurately selects high-quality object masks, specifically tailored for challenging crowded environments.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

Crowd-SAM demonstrates significant performance improvements, particularly in label-efficient object detection within crowded and occluded scenes. The experimental results, evaluated on several challenging benchmarks, highlight the effectiveness of the proposed framework:

1. **Performance on CrowdHuman (Pedestrian Detection):**
 - With only **10 labeled images**, Crowd-SAM achieves an AP of 71.4% (without multi-crop) and **78.4%** (with multi-crop).
 - This performance is **comparable to state-of-the-art fully-supervised object detectors** that are trained on entire datasets. For example, it outperforms FCOS (76.3% AP) and approaches DINO (86.7% AP), both fully-supervised methods using ResNet-50.
 - Crowd-SAM significantly **outperforms existing few-shot object detectors**, such as De-FRCN (46.4% AP with ResNet-101), by a substantial margin of over 32% AP, demonstrating its superior label efficiency.
 - Compared to a direct SAM baseline (zero-shot), Crowd-SAM shows remarkable gains in Recall (e.g., 79.6% for SAM* vs. 85.6% for Crowd-SAM*), underscoring the value of its adaptation modules.

2. **Performance on OccHuman (Occluded Pedestrian Detection):**
 - On the dataset specifically designed for occluded persons, Crowd-SAM achieves **31.4% AP**, outperforming the fully-supervised Pose2Seg (22.2% AP) by 9.2% AP. This strong result validates Crowd-SAM's robustness and effectiveness in highly occluded scenarios.

3. **Performance on CityPersons (Urban Pedestrian Detection):**
 - Even on less crowded but realistic urban scenes, Crowd-SAM remains competitive. With 50 shots, it achieves **33.3% AP**, surpassing leading few-shot object detectors like TFA (30.6% AP), FSCE (32.2% AP), and De-FRCN (25.5% AP). This demonstrates the generalizability of the method beyond extremely crowded scenarios.

4. **Multi-class Object Detection (COCO and COCO-OCC):**
 - A multi-class version of Crowd-SAM, with a multi-class classifier replacing the binary one, shows promising results on COCO (22.0% AP) and COCO-OCC (20.6% AP). While not reaching the performance of fully-supervised methods like Faster R-CNN (33.5% AP), the minor drop in AP between COCO and COCO-OCC (1.4% AP) indicates good robustness to occlusion in a multi-class setting, further supporting its applicability in diverse scenarios.

5. **Ablation Studies (Module Effectiveness):**
 - **Foreground (FG) Location:** Removing the DINOv2-based foreground location module significantly drops AP by 7.4% and Recall by 8.5%, proving its criticality in restricting prompts to relevant areas.
 - **Efficient Prompt Sampler (EPS):** EPS contributes 0.6% AP, but its main value lies in handling dense grids without out-of-memory errors (OOM) at large grid sizes (e.g., 128x128 or higher) and improving recall, indicating its efficiency and ability to focus on challenging regions.
 - **Part-Whole Discrimination Network (PWD-Net):** This module is indispensable; removing it leads to a catastrophic drop of 40% AP (to 17.0%). This highlights its central role in accurately selecting and refining masks.
 - Within PWD-Net, the Mask Token (M) is particularly crucial, as its removal causes a 40.0% AP drop, emphasizing its shape-aware information.
 - Freezing SAM's original IoU Head and training a parallel one (Head_par) is more effective than tuning the original head (-2.8% AP when tuning), preventing overfitting in the few-shot setting.
 - **Multi-Crop Trick:** Applying a multi-cropping strategy during inference provides a substantial boost of 7.0% AP, demonstrating its effectiveness for small object detection.

**Qualitative Analysis:** Figure 4 visually demonstrates that Crowd-SAM generates more accurate predictions, especially around object boundaries, compared to De-FRCN. It also shows the high quality of the generated pseudo masks and the effective pruning of prompts by EPS.

In summary, the main findings establish Crowd-SAM as a highly effective and label-efficient framework for object detection in crowded and occluded scenes. It successfully adapts powerful foundation models to a challenging downstream task, achieving performance competitive with fully-supervised methods while drastically reducing annotation requirements.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Crowd-SAM represents a significant advancement in the field of object detection, particularly for challenging crowded scenes, by effectively harnessing the power of modern foundation models. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Revolutionizing Annotation Efficiency:** The most immediate and impactful contribution is its role as a "smart annotator." By achieving highly competitive performance with only a handful of labeled images (e.g., 10-shot on CrowdHuman), Crowd-SAM drastically reduces the labor and cost associated with creating large-scale datasets for crowded scenes. This can accelerate research and development in areas like autonomous driving, smart cities, and video surveillance, where data annotation is a major bottleneck.

2. **Bridging Foundation Models and Downstream Tasks:** The paper effectively demonstrates a paradigm for adapting powerful, general-purpose foundation models (SAM for segmentation, DINOv2 for semantic features) to specialized, complex downstream tasks (object detection in crowded scenes) with minimal task-specific training. This approach moves beyond traditional fine-tuning, showcasing how freezing large backbones and integrating lightweight, learnable modules can yield highly efficient and effective solutions. This methodology could inspire similar adaptations for other niche computer vision problems.

3. **Robustness to Crowded and Occluded Scenes:** Crowd-SAM's exceptional performance on datasets like CrowdHuman, OccHuman, and CityPersons, especially its strong showing against fully-supervised methods in occluded scenarios, highlights its practical utility. Real-world applications often involve dense and partially obscured objects, making such robustness critical for reliable deployment. The framework's ability to discriminate between entire objects and their parts, and filter out background noise, is a key enabler for this robustness.

4. **Advancement in Few-Shot Object Detection:** By significantly outperforming existing few-shot object detection methods, Crowd-SAM sets a new benchmark for label-efficient learning in this domain. This contributes to the broader goal of developing AI systems that can learn effectively from limited data, reducing reliance on massive annotated datasets.

5. **Inspiration for Future Research:**
 - The success of EPS and PWD-Net in addressing dense prompts and mask ambiguity could lead to further innovations in prompt engineering and mask selection mechanisms for foundation models.
 - The demonstrated effectiveness of combining semantic features from DINOv2 with segmentation tokens from SAM provides a blueprint for integrating multi-modal or multi-representational knowledge within adaptive frameworks.
 - The framework's extensibility to multi-class detection on COCO, even with its modest few-shot performance, suggests avenues for scaling such label-efficient approaches to broader object categories.

In conclusion, Crowd-SAM is a pioneering work that not only pushes the boundaries of object detection in crowded scenes but also offers a practical and scalable solution for data annotation challenges. Its impact lies in accelerating the development of robust AI systems for complex real-world environments while significantly reducing the human effort currently demanded by data labeling.
