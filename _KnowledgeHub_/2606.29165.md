# Multi-Contact Force Estimation for Continuum Robots via Gaussian-Parameterized Factor Graphs

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Georgia Institute of Technology developed a unified framework that combines a Gaussian-parameterized force model with a probabilistic Cosserat rod model within a factor graph, enabling simultaneous estimation of continuum robot shape and multi-contact forces. This approach improved force localization accuracy by mitigating ill-conditioning and probabilistically fusing sensor data, demonstrating robust performance even with sensor noise.

## Problem

- Accurate estimation of external contact forces along a continuum robot's body is essential for safe and effective operation in unstructured environments.
- The multi-contact force estimation problem is inherently ill-conditioned, especially when contact locations are unknown, as various force distributions can cause similar deformations.
- Existing methods, such as tip-force estimation or kinematics-based approaches, struggled with scalability for multiple contacts, reliability in proximal segments, and robustness to sensor noise.

## Method

- The framework integrates a discretized ==probabilistic Cosserat rod model== to describe robot kinematics and statics.
- External forces are parameterized using a ==Gaussian mixture model==, which reduces the dimensionality of the estimation problem compared to node-wise force estimation.
- A ==factor graph== formulation probabilistically fuses multi-modal sensor data (strain, tendon tension, pose) to simultaneously estimate robot shape and force parameters, explicitly accounting for uncertainties.

## Results

- Under noisy conditions, the proposed method achieved a mean single-contact location error of **8.74 mm**, outperforming baseline methods (**20.1 mm** and **36.68 mm**).
- In two-contact scenarios with noise, the proposed estimator bounded the maximum location error for the second contact at **48.32 mm**, a substantial improvement over a baseline's **82.47 mm** error.
- The progressive multi-contact estimation algorithm successfully detected, localized, and tracked sequential contacts during simulated confined navigation, dynamically adding force basis functions as new obstacles were encountered.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Parameterizing distributed external forces with Gaussian mixture models significantly reduces the dimensionality and ill-conditioning of the multi-contact force estimation problem.
> - Probabilistic fusion within a factor graph architecture allows the system to robustly handle sensor noise and modeling uncertainties, leading to more accurate force localization and magnitude estimates.
> - A progressive estimation variant can dynamically detect and localize sequential contacts, introducing new Gaussian basis functions as new interactions occur, enabling adaptive operation in changing environments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Continuum robots are recognized for their inherent flexibility and compliance, offering advantages in navigating unstructured and confined environments. Their continuous deformability and structural compliance facilitate safe interaction with surroundings, making them suitable for applications such as minimally invasive surgery, post-disaster search and rescue, and complex machinery inspection.

A critical challenge for the safe and effective operation of these robots in unknown or uncertain environments is the accurate estimation of external contact forces along their bodies. This estimation problem is inherently ill-conditioned, particularly when multiple contacts occur at unknown locations, as multiple force distributions can result in similar deformations, and some external forces may have limited observability.

Prior research has addressed force estimation in continuum robots with various approaches:
- **Tip-force estimation:** Many studies have focused on estimating forces at the robot's tip, which is effective for specific tasks where the body remains in free space. However, this assumption is often invalid in complex environments where body collisions are common.
- **Kinematics-based methods:** Methods based on kinematics have been proposed for contact detection and localization. Early work primarily addressed single contacts, with extensions to multiple contacts using particle filters and kinetostatic models. However, these methods showed limitations in reliability for proximal segments and poor scalability with an increasing number of contacts.
- **Statics models with force parameterization:** Approaches utilizing statics models to jointly localize and estimate contact forces have identified the ill-posed nature of the problem. Gaussian functions have been employed to parameterize external loads, enabling approximation of both point and distributed loads. A limitation of such deterministic models is their vulnerability to sensor noise and reliance on known pose measurements.
- **Proprioceptive measurements:** Research has demonstrated the utility of proprioceptive measurements, specifically strain and tendon tension, for force estimation. Examples include using embedded fiber Bragg grating (FBG) fibers, stretch sensors, and tension profiles to recover shape and 3D forces. These studies suggest a sensor-efficient approach to force estimation.
- **Unified shape and force estimation frameworks:** Efforts have been made to develop unified frameworks. One approach utilized Gaussian process regression, but its modeling of distributed forces via Brownian motion was noted as potentially ill-suited for localized point forces typical of obstacle collisions. Another line of work introduced a discretized stochastic Cosserat model that accounts for uncertainty in sensing, tendons, and mechanics, allowing for a wrench at every node. However, estimating point forces at every node remains an ill-conditioned problem due to the high dimensionality.

This work builds upon the foundation of multimodal sensing strategies and probabilistic Cosserat rod models. It seeks to overcome the limitations of previous methods by integrating a Gaussian mixture parameterization of external forces into a discretized probabilistic Cosserat rod model within a factor graph framework. This aims to reduce the dimensionality of the force estimation problem, mitigate ill-conditioning, and probabilistically fuse various sensor measurements to achieve robust, simultaneous shape and multi-contact force estimation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the necessity for continuum robots to operate safely and effectively in unstructured, unknown, or uncertain environments. Such operation requires accurate estimation of external contact forces acting at any point along the robot's body, which is critical for tasks like regulating contact force during surgical procedures or enabling adaptive stiffness and shape adjustments during confined path navigation.

The key objectives of this work are:

- **Develop a unified shape and force estimation framework:** The research aims to create a single framework capable of simultaneously estimating both the robot's deformed shape and the external forces applied along its length.
- **Address ill-conditioned multi-contact force estimation:** A central objective is to mitigate the inherent ill-conditioning of estimating external forces at unknown locations, particularly when multiple contact points are present. This is a significant challenge for existing node-wise or overly-parameterized methods.
- **Reduce dimensionality of external force representation:** By parameterizing external forces using a Gaussian mixture model, the work seeks to reduce the number of unknown variables compared to estimating forces at every discrete node. This reduction is intended to improve the tractability and robustness of the estimation problem.
- **Incorporate probabilistic fusion of multi-sensory data:** The framework is designed to probabilistically fuse diverse sensor measurements, including strain, tendon tension, and pose data. This approach aims to account for modeling and sensor uncertainties, enhancing the robustness of the estimates under realistic operating conditions.
- **Demonstrate improved performance over existing methods:** A comparative objective is to numerically demonstrate that the proposed method outperforms existing techniques in terms of force location and magnitude estimation, for both single and multiple contact scenarios, especially in the presence of sensor noise.
- **Enable progressive estimation of sequential contacts:** For dynamic navigation tasks, the work aims to develop a progressive variant of the estimation framework that can dynamically introduce basis functions as new contacts are encountered, allowing for sequential estimation of multiple contacts.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology integrates a discretized probabilistic Cosserat rod model with a Gaussian mixture force parameterization within a factor graph framework.

**A. Cosserat Rod Model Discretization and Force Parameterization:**
- **Kinematics and Constitutive Equations:** The robot's pose is described by SE(3) elements, with kinematics relating the change in pose along the arc length to a strain vector. A constitutive equation relates the internal wrench (moment and force) to the strain, incorporating a generalized stiffness matrix and modeling error.
- **Statics Equation:** The statics of the Cosserat rod in the world frame relates the change in internal wrench to external and tendon actuation loads.
- **Discretization:** The robot's arc length is discretized into `K` nodes. Kinematics are approximated using an exponential map, and strain is calculated using a midpoint approximation of internal wrenches. The statics equation is integrated over each interval, transforming world-frame wrenches to the body frame.
- **Gaussian Mixture Force Parameterization:** To address the ill-conditioned nature of estimating point forces at every node, the distributed external force `f_e(s)` is parameterized as a sum of `M` normalized Gaussian basis functions. Each basis function is defined by a 3D amplitude vector (`α_i`) and a center (`μ_i`) with a fixed standard deviation (`σ_i`, chosen as 12 mm for localized forces in simulations). The basis centers `μ_i` are bounded within the robot's length using a `tanh` function. This parameterization reduces the dimensionality of the force estimation problem to `4M` parameters (`γ`).
- **Tendon Actuation Model:** Tendons interact with the backbone at discrete routing discs. The distributed actuation load is modeled as concentrated wrenches at these disc locations, dependent on tendon routing geometry and tension.
- **Wrench Balance Equation:** The discretized statics equation is updated to incorporate the integrated external Gaussian-parameterized force and the discrete tendon actuation wrenches, including process noise terms.

**B. Factor Graph Formulation:**
- **State Variables:** The unknown state vector `Θ` includes node-wise poses (`T_k`), internal wrenches (`Λ_k`), disc-specific concentrated tendon wrenches (`D_d`), the tendon tension vector (`Q`), and the external force parameter vector (`γ`).
- **Factor Graph Architecture:** A factor graph is constructed, representing the Maximum a Posteriori (MAP) estimation problem as a nonlinear least-squares optimization. Variable nodes represent the states, and factor nodes represent physical or measurement constraints.
- **Factor Categories:**
 - **Mechanics Factors:**
 - **Kinematics Factor:** Enforces consistency between strain and pose, linking `T_k`, `T_{k+1}`, `Λ_k`, `Λ_{k+1}`.
 - **Wrench Balance Factor:** Enforces the discretized body-frame static balance equation, linking `T_k`, `T_{k+1}`, `Λ_k`, `Λ_{k+1}`, `γ`, and `D_{k+1}` (if applicable).
 - **Tendon Actuation Factor:** Relates the concentrated tendon wrench `D_d` to the individual tendon wrench contributions and the tension `Q`.
 - **Measurement Factors:**
 - **Tendon Tension Measurement:** Incorporates measured tendon tension `Q`.
 - **Pose Measurements:** Incorporates measured poses `T_k` at specific nodes.
 - **Strain Measurements:** Incorporates measured strains `ξ_k` at specific nodes.
 - **Prior and Boundary Condition Factors:**
 - **Base Boundary Condition:** Constrains the robot's base pose `T_1`.
 - **Tip Boundary Condition:** Constrains the internal wrench `Λ_K` at the tip (e.g., to zero for free space or a known value).
 - **Force Parameter Prior:** A zero-mean prior is placed on the Gaussian force amplitudes (`α_i`) to regularize the estimation.
- **Optimization:** The MAP estimate is obtained by minimizing a cost function `J(Θ)` comprising the sum of squared residuals from all factors. The optimization is implemented using the GTSAM library, leveraging analytical Jacobians with respect to force parameters and Powell’s Dog-Leg algorithm.

**C. Progressive Multi-Contact Estimation:**
- **Dynamic Basis Function Addition:** For scenarios with an unknown number of contacts, a progressive algorithm is proposed. It starts with a single Gaussian basis function and dynamically adds new basis functions (e.g., at the distal region) if the estimated force magnitude of an active basis exceeds a predefined threshold.
- **Numerical Stability Factors:**
 - **Axial Force Factor:** A quadratic penalty is applied to the tangent-direction projection of each basis amplitude to regularize unobservable axial components, ensuring numerical stability without affecting shape.
 - **Spatial Separation Penalty:** A non-Gaussian pairwise repulsion penalty is introduced on normalized center coordinates (`μ_i`) to prevent multiple basis functions from collapsing onto the same arc-length location, promoting distinct contact representations.
- **Initialization:** For continuous operation, states are initialized from the previous step's posterior. New basis functions are initialized in regions where new contacts are likely (e.g., distal half).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The proposed framework was evaluated through numerical simulations using SoroSim, comparing its performance against two baselines: a discrete node-wise estimator (, Baseline 1) and a deterministic Gaussian force parameterization method (, Baseline 2).

**A. Single Force Estimation (2,000 randomized samples):**
- **Noise-Free Conditions:** The proposed method achieved a mean location error of 3.47 mm, which was comparable to Baseline 2 (3.5 mm) and notably superior to Baseline 1 (9 mm).
- **With Measurement Noise:** Under noisy conditions (1 mm tip position noise, 0.1 rad/m angular strain noise), the proposed method maintained robustness with a mean location error of 8.74 mm. This significantly outperformed Baseline 2 (20.1 mm) and Baseline 1 (36.68 mm), highlighting the benefit of the probabilistic factor graph formulation in handling sensor uncertainty.
- **Force Spread:** Baseline 1 exhibited a large force spread (50.1 mm noise-free, 122.59 mm noisy), indicating its tendency to distribute the load broadly due to ill-conditioning, potentially fitting sensor noise. The proposed method, with its parameterized Gaussian basis, maintained a localized estimate (12 mm spread).
- **Shape Error vs. Force Accuracy:** Baseline 1 occasionally yielded a smaller shape error, but this came at the cost of a less localized and accurate force estimate, as it could fit noise by spreading forces. The proposed method, by localizing forces, might have a slightly higher shape error residual but provides more accurate force localization.
- **Force Magnitude Error:** The proposed method demonstrated superior force magnitude estimation compared to Baseline 2 when noise was present.
- **Computational Performance:** The proposed method generally required more iterations to converge than Baseline 1, resulting in a slightly higher mean runtime (132.30 ms vs. 122.20 ms with noise). However, the reduction in unknown variables led to a shorter per-step time despite some loss of sparsity.

**B. Two Force Estimation (4,000 randomized samples):**
- **Average Minimum Location Error:** Both the proposed method and Baseline 1 showed comparable minimum location errors to their single-force performance, suggesting effective isolation of at least one contact.
- **Average Maximum Location Error:** For the second contact, the proposed estimator significantly outperformed Baseline 1. Under noisy conditions, the maximum location error for the proposed method was bounded at 48.32 mm, while Baseline 1's error spiked to 82.47 mm.
- **Force Profile Visual Comparison:** Visual analysis (Figure 3) demonstrated that Baseline 1 often produced smeared force profiles with spurious peaks, indicating incorrect localization. In contrast, the proposed method successfully resolved two distinct Gaussian peaks at the correct locations.
- **Operational Boundaries:** Analysis of "detectability" (activation ratio of basis function magnitudes) revealed regions where high mean force location errors occurred, primarily when two forces were applied in close proximity, particularly near the robot's tip. In these "highly coupled" regions, one basis function might absorb the aggregate effect of both forces, reducing the detectability of the second basis function and artificially inflating the mean location error for that configuration.

**C. Progressive Multi-Contact Estimation during Confined Navigation:**
- The progressive algorithm successfully demonstrated dynamic estimation of contacts during a simulated confined navigation task.
- **Initial Free Space:** In the absence of contact, estimated force magnitude remained near zero, and location fluctuated widely, consistent with low observability of location parameters at low force amplitudes.
- **First Contact:** As the robot contacted the first corner, the estimated force location converged to the contact point, and the magnitude increased, eventually exceeding a predefined threshold (`τ`). This triggered the dynamic addition of a second Gaussian basis function.
- **Second Contact:** The newly spawned basis function converged towards the tip and became active as the robot engaged a second wall, effectively capturing the dual-contact state.
- The introduced axial force and spatial separation penalties contributed to numerical stability by preventing degenerate solutions and ensuring distinct contact representations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a notable advancement in the field of continuum robot state estimation, specifically addressing the challenging problem of multi-contact force estimation.

The **significance** of this work lies in:
- **Addressing Ill-Conditioning:** By integrating a Gaussian mixture force parameterization into a discretized Cosserat rod model, the framework effectively mitigates the ill-conditioned nature of estimating external forces at unknown locations, a long-standing challenge in the field.
- **Robustness to Uncertainty:** The use of a factor graph architecture allows for the probabilistic fusion of multi-modal sensor data (strain, tendon tension, pose) while explicitly accounting for modeling and sensor uncertainties. This leads to significantly more robust and accurate force estimates, particularly under realistic noisy conditions, outperforming methods that rely on deterministic models or over-parameterized node-wise approaches.
- **Unified Framework:** The approach provides a unified framework for simultaneously estimating both the robot's shape and multiple localized contact forces, which is crucial for comprehensive environmental interaction.
- **Scalability for Multiple Contacts:** The parameterized approach allows for estimation of multiple distinct contact points, demonstrating superior performance in localizing secondary contacts compared to node-wise estimation.
- **Dynamic Contact Detection:** The proposed progressive estimation strategy provides a mechanism for dynamically detecting and localizing sequential body collisions, which is essential for autonomous operation in unknown and changing environments.

The **potential impact** of this work is substantial for the deployment and capabilities of continuum robots:
- **Enhanced Safety:** Accurate force estimation enables robots to operate more safely by detecting and localizing environmental interactions, preventing damage to the robot or its surroundings.
- **Improved Autonomy:** Knowledge of contact locations and magnitudes is critical for autonomous navigation, path planning, and reactive obstacle avoidance in confined and unstructured spaces, such as during minimally invasive surgeries or search and rescue missions.
- **Advanced Control Strategies:** The estimated force and shape information can be directly integrated into closed-loop robotic systems for advanced force control and adaptive stiffness adjustment, allowing for more dexterous and compliant interaction with the environment.
- **Broader Applicability:** By improving the reliability and accuracy of contact sensing, this framework expands the range of applications where continuum robots can be effectively and safely utilized.

Future work outlined by the authors includes integrating this estimation framework into closed-loop robotic systems for practical applications and extending the quasi-static model to full continuum dynamics for estimation during dynamic maneuvers, further broadening its potential impact.
