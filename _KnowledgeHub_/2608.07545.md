# DarwinX: Evolving Agent Harnesses Through Natural Selection

> [!summary] Summary
> DarwinX evolves an LLM agent's harness (prompts, skills, tools, control flow) while the base model stays frozen, treating self-improvement as selection over a population of variants. A preserve-and-extend contract admits only children that gain on some task without regressing others, an archive retains lineages for recombination, and failure-, teacher-, and self-derived evidence feed one edit interface. Gains average about 17 points across four benchmarks.

## Problem

- Single-lineage self-editing agents are path-dependent: early edits bias the search, lineages plateau, and complementary specialists found in separate branches are never recombined.
- Cross-task interference means an edit fixing one task family silently regresses another, so prior systems accumulate sub-threshold regressions and stagnate on mixed task distributions.
- Prior optimizers restrict edits to one artifact such as prompts, workflows, or skill documents, leaving tools, control flow, and implementation outside the search under noisy fitness.

## Method

- ==Preserve-and-extend contract== scores each variant by per-task avg@k, admitting a child only when net gain is positive and summed regression stays under a tolerance, then re-testing promoted children with a ==preservation probe==.
- ==Archive==-based population with a cross-lineage ==merge operator==: additive code, skill, prompt, and tool deltas from complementary specialists are combined, and the merged child is kept only if it covers the union of its parents' solves.
- ==Modular learning-signal interface== routes failure-derived diagnosis to ordinary mutations, teacher demonstrations to walls with no successful rollout, and self-derived pass/fail contrast to variance-band tasks, plus shared memory of failure themes.

## Results

- Terminal-Bench 2.1 avg@5 rises from **75.5%** to **83.2%** on a frozen GPT-5.5 base and to **84.7%** on GPT-5.6 Sol, above Terminus-2 at **78.0%** and Codex at **83.1%**.
- WebArena-Infinity audit-clean pass@1 moves from **43.5%** to **93.0%** on 1,260 unseen real tasks after evolving only on synthetic intents, with invalid trajectories falling from **293** to **17**.
- TerminalWorld held-out pass@1 reaches **68.3%** (28 of 41) versus **65.9%** for Claude Code; four specialists solve 24-27 each, and the terminal harness transfers unchanged to **84.2%** on SWE-bench Verified.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A frozen model is not a fixed agent: selection over the harness converts evaluation compute into durable capability with no weight updates.
> - Keeping a diverse archive beats greedy hill-climbing: the in-loop proxy saturated at 1.000 while held-out stayed 68.3%, and merged specialists generalized past every individual one.
> - Gains traced to verification-before-finalization skills rather than domain knowledge; separating exploration from confirmation curbs lucky rollouts, but per-operator causal attribution stays unproven.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses the evolving capabilities of Large Language Model (LLM) agents, which are increasingly dependent on their "harnesses"—the prompts, tools, memory, and control flow that mediate the underlying model—rather than solely on the model's weights. A growing body of research focuses on self-improving harnesses, with existing systems varying in what they edit (e.g., prompts, skills, workflows, agent source code) and how they search for improvements (e.g., single-lineage vs. population/archive).

Most contemporary self-improving agent systems tend to converge on a single-lineage inner optimization loop: agents perform rollouts, reflect on outcomes, propose bounded edits, and gate these changes against a regression signal. This approach, while effective for local optimization, frequently encounters two limitations:
1. **Path dependence**: Single-lineage search can be biased by early edits, leading to plateaus in improvement.
2. **Cross-task interference**: Edits that benefit one set of tasks may inadvertently degrade performance on others, causing stagnation when attempting to evolve over diverse task distributions. Previous attempts to mitigate this, such as isolating variants, resulted in specialized agents that remained separate.

DarwinX positions itself within this landscape by building on the concept of self-modifying agents and archives (inherited from open-ended and quality-diversity search, notably exemplified by the Darwin Gödel Machine, DGM). However, DarwinX specifically contributes a novel selection process for evolving *harnesses* within a population-based archive, crucially while keeping the underlying LLM *frozen*. Unlike DGM, which mutates one parent and scores the child against it (potentially losing complementary tasks), DarwinX introduces an explicit "preserve-and-extend contract" for admitting new variants, allowing for recombination of complementary specialists across lineages. Its selection is driven by measured fitness (avg@k solve rate under the benchmark's own verifier), rather than gold solutions or hand-picked winners, aiming to evolve general agent competence.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of DarwinX is to enable the self-evolution of an LLM agent's harness through a process of natural selection, while the underlying model weights remain frozen. This approach aims to durably enhance agent capabilities by optimizing the mediating components that guide the model's interaction with environments and tasks.

The motivation for this specific approach stems from several detailed considerations regarding the limitations of existing self-improvement paradigms:

1. **Harness as a Critical Factor**: The performance of modern LLM agents is significantly influenced by their harnesses (prompts, tools, memory, control flow). Improving these components offers a pathway to increased capability even when the base LLM is fixed.
2. **Addressing Path Dependence**: Single-lineage self-improvement systems are prone to path dependence. Early edits, even if locally optimal, can lock an agent into a suboptimal evolutionary trajectory, preventing the discovery of more effective strategies that might involve temporary performance dips or exploration of alternative solutions. DarwinX mitigates this by maintaining a population of harnesses and an archive of alternative lineages.
3. **Mitigating Cross-Task Interference**: Edits intended to fix one family of tasks often silently degrade performance on others. This "cross-task interference" becomes more severe with broader task distributions, leading to stagnation when agents attempt to evolve across a mixed set of objectives. DarwinX addresses this by explicitly introducing a "preserve-and-extend contract" which only admits variants that extend coverage without regressing existing capabilities, and by enabling the recombination of complementary specialists across different lineages.
4. **Accumulation of Capabilities**: Traditional "keep-best" strategies can lead to a trade-off where a local win on one task might come at the expense of another. DarwinX aims for additive capability accumulation, where improvements discovered in different lineages can be merged without discarding valuable partial solutions.
5. **Robust and Generalizable Improvements**: The system seeks to evolve general agent competence that is robust to changes in task, verifier, and base model, rather than benchmark-specific patches or reward-hacking behaviors. This is achieved by relying on measured fitness from benchmark verifiers (without gold solutions) and by emphasizing an audit-clean approach to evaluation.
6. **Modular Learning Signals**: To drive evolution efficiently, DarwinX is motivated to unify various feedback mechanisms (failure-derived diagnosis, teacher-derived demonstrations, self-derived rollout contrast) behind a single, harness-level edit interface, ensuring that the most informative evidence is used for improvement without altering model weights.

In essence, DarwinX is motivated by the hypothesis that a carefully designed selection process over diverse harness variants can overcome the inherent challenges of self-improvement loops, transforming evaluation compute into durable and transferable agent capabilities, even with a static underlying LLM.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DarwinX conceptualizes agent improvement as a selection problem, not a training problem, maintaining a frozen base LLM and focusing exclusively on evolving its harness. The harness comprises two editable layers: skill (prompts, memory, distilled knowledge) and code (tools, control flow, agent loop). The method consists of three interconnected components: a selection loop driven by a preserve-and-extend contract, a population-level inheritance and recombination mechanism via an archive, and a modular learning signal interface.

#### 3.1. Fitness and the Preserve-and-Extend Contract

A core principle is that a child harness must earn its place. It must measurably improve on at least one task (`g(c) > 0`) without regressing beyond a small tolerance (`R(c) ≤ δ`) on tasks its parent solved.
- **Fitness Metric**: Each variant is scored by its per-task solve rate (`avg@k`) using the benchmark's own verifier, without reliance on gold solutions.
- **Adjudication**: A reasoned verifier agent adjudicates promotion in two stages:
 1. **Promotion**: A candidate can enter the archive if it shows a promising, bounded-downside gain.
 2. **Preservation Probe**: A stricter re-test at higher fidelity confirms the promoted child's capabilities and ensures it preserves inherited capabilities before it can steer future search. This two-speed design balances rapid exploration with rigorous confirmation.
- **Cumulative Lineage Gain**: Each node in the archive carries a cumulative lineage gain (`G(c) = G(p) + g(c)`), used for parent selection to compound improvements.

#### 3.2. Branch Evolution

This loop repeatedly picks a promising harness from the archive, proposes an edit, and evaluates its survival.
- **Parent Selection**: Parents are sampled based on cumulative lineage gain, prioritizing exploitation of high-gain nodes (`(1-β)δ_argmax G(v)`) while also broadening search across the wider population (`β Broaden(P)`).
- **Proposer**: Produces additive harness edits (modifying prompts, skills, tools, control flow, or source code) aimed at fragile or failing tasks, while attempting to preserve already solved tasks.
- **Additive Edits**: Edits are designed to be additive, allowing capabilities to accumulate rather than being traded off, making long lineages productive.

#### 3.3. Population and Recombination

DarwinX retains all scored variants in an archive, organized as a tree, to enable population-level search and recombination.
- **Variant Classification**: Children are classified based on their solved task set `S(c)` relative to their parent `S(p)`:
 - **Improvers** (`S(c) ⊋ S(p)`) and **Neutral Children** (`S(c) = S(p)`) are eligible for inheritance.
 - **Stepping Stones** (`S(c) ⊊ S(p)`) and **Archived Nodes** (trading some solves for others) contribute distilled lessons but are not eligible for direct inheritance.
- **Specialists**: Any variant that solves a task no sibling does is retained as potential genetic material.
- **Merge Operator**: When complementary variants (`v1, ..., vn`) solve different tasks, their additive edits (`∆`) from a common ancestor (`H0`) are merged to create an inherited child harness (`H = H0 ⊕ ∆`). The child is kept only if it covers the union of its parents’ wins (`S(child) ⊇ ⋃ S(vi)`). This mechanism prevents useful edits from being trapped in separate lineages.

#### 3.4. Learning Signals

DarwinX employs a modular signal interface, allowing different evidence sources to drive harness edits without touching model weights.
- **Failure-Derived Signals (∇)**: Default for ordinary mutations, summarizing failed trajectories and localizing missing capabilities.
- **Teacher-Derived Signals (π*)**: Distilling successful trajectories from a reference solver, used for "wall" tasks where the agent consistently fails.
- **Self-Derived Signals (A)**: Contrasting the agent’s own passing and failing rollouts to identify conditions for reliable success, used for "variance-band" tasks.
This dynamic partitioning ensures the proposer always receives informative evidence.

#### 3.5. Measurement and Confirmation

- **Avg@k**: Binary avg@k is used for scoring. Agent timeouts are counted as failures.
- **Preservation Probe**: Before a promoted child becomes an ancestor, a preservation probe re-samples the lineage’s known solved set to confirm its robustness.

#### 3.6. Cross-Task Themes and Shared Memory

A failure-mode classifier labels trials and aggregates dominant themes (e.g., "timeout-setup"), which are added to a shared memory (`Kg`). This memory informs the proposer and verifier, guiding the search towards developing global capabilities for systemic bottlenecks rather than just per-task patches.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

DarwinX was evaluated across four benchmarks, ordered by increasing separation between the evolution signal and the test, and an ablation study to explain the gains. The base model was frozen in all matched comparisons, attributing all gains to the evolved harness.

#### 4.1. RQ1: In-Domain Test-Time Evolution (Terminal-Bench 2.1)

- **Performance**: On a frozen GPT-5.5 base, DarwinX improved Monet from 75.5% to 83.2% avg@5 (+7.7 points). On GPT-5.6 Sol, it achieved 84.7%, matching or exceeding the verified leaderboard frontier.
- **Source of Gains**: Improvements were concentrated in areas requiring procedural rather than knowledge-bound solutions: ML & scientific-computing tasks (60.1% to 74.9%, +14.8 points) and data/database tasks (83.9% to 97.8%, +13.8 points).
- **Preservation**: Out of 88 tasks, 36 improved, 43 were unchanged, and 9 regressed (within small tolerance), demonstrating the effectiveness of the preserve-and-extend contract.
- **Compute Allocation**: The evolved harness did not uniformly increase compute; it roughly doubled turns and quadrupled tokens (from 89K to 380K) on the six tasks it newly solved, while compute on already-solved tasks remained largely stable. This indicates targeted, productive compute.
- **Audit**: An audit found no harness-level cheating or reward hacking. The gains were attributed to durable capability rather than exploitation of verifier weaknesses.

#### 4.2. RQ2: Held-Out Task Generalization (TerminalWorld)

- **Performance**: On a frozen Opus 4.8 base, the DarwinX-evolved harness resolved 28 out of 41 held-out tasks (68.3%), which was the best result on the split and an improvement of +7.3 points over the unevolved base (25/41). The matched GPT-5.5 pair saw a 3-task improvement (20/41 to 23/41).
- **Archive Diversity**: While the in-loop training score overfit, the held-out gain stemmed from retaining a diverse population of specialists. Four high-scoring specialists solved 24, 25, 26, and 27 tasks individually, but the merged DarwinX harness surpassed all of them by solving 28 tasks. This provides direct evidence for the value of the archive and recombination.

#### 4.3. RQ3: Synthetic-to-Real Generalization (WebArena-Infinity)

- **Performance**: Evolving on 300 synthetic intents with an LLM judge, the frozen harness achieved an audit-clean pass@1 of 93.0% on 1,260 real tasks with deterministic verifiers. This represented a substantial increase of +49.5 points over base Monet (43.5%).
- **Action Validity and Anti-Cheating**: A two-stage audit revealed that invalid trajectories (e.g., privileged knowledge access, exploits) decreased dramatically from 293 to 17 (23.5% to 1.4%). Exploit-style mechanisms disappeared entirely. This indicates that capability and compliance improved concurrently.
- **Harness Changes**: The evolved browser harness incorporated four new contract-oriented browser skills and a modified system prompt, emphasizing explicit acceptance contracts, state inspection, app-owned semantic operations, and verification of rendered state and persistence.

#### 4.4. RQ4: Cross-Benchmark Transfer (Terminal-Bench 2.1 → SWE-bench Verified)

- **Performance**: The Terminal-Bench 2.1-specialized harness, run unchanged on all 500 SWE-bench Verified issues with a frozen Opus 4.8 base, achieved an 84.2% pass@1. This was +3.4 points over a strong fix-skill reference (80.8%). No in-domain SWE-V feedback was used during evolution.
- **Implication**: This suggests that the verification and contract skills evolved on Terminal-Bench 2.1 are generalizable to repository-level software engineering tasks.

#### 4.5. RQ5: Ablation and Mechanism (Terminal-Bench 2.1)

- **Evolved Skill Bundle**: The evolved harness added seven skills, all belonging to a "verification / artifact-contract" family. These skills focus on deriving acceptance contracts, verifying graded artifacts, and grounding outputs in real tool execution.
- **Correlation with Gains**: These skills align with the observed gains in procedural tasks like ML & scientific-computing and data/database, suggesting a mechanism of establishing and checking explicit acceptance contracts to unblock multi-step work.
- **Cross-Benchmark Consistency**: Similar contract-oriented skills and compliance improvements were observed in WebArena-Infinity. TerminalWorld highlighted the value of diverse specialists from the archive for held-out generalization.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The DarwinX research demonstrates that significant and generalizable improvements in LLM agent capabilities can be achieved through the evolutionary selection of their harnesses, even when the underlying language model is kept frozen. This finding challenges the assumption that substantial agent progress necessitates continuous model re-training or fine-tuning.

**Key aspects of significance and potential impact include:**

1. **Harness-Centric Advancement**: The work underscores the critical role of the agent harness (prompts, tools, memory, control flow) as a distinct and optimizable layer of capability. By isolating harness evolution from model training, DarwinX provides a clear attribution that observed performance gains originate from enhanced agent procedures and strategies rather than increased model intelligence.
2. **Robust and Generalizable Competence**: DarwinX successfully addresses long-standing challenges in self-improving agents, such as path dependence and cross-task interference. The "preserve-and-extend contract" ensures that new capabilities accumulate without regressing existing ones, while the population-based archive with recombination fosters diverse solutions and facilitates the merging of complementary specialist behaviors. This is evidenced by consistent gains across diverse benchmarks—in-domain terminal tasks, held-out tasks, synthetic-to-real browser tasks, and cross-benchmark transfer to software engineering. The average gain of approximately 17 points across these varied regimes, with a frozen model, highlights the durability and transferability of the evolved competence.
3. **Enhanced Reliability and Compliance**: A notable impact is DarwinX's ability to improve both agent capability and compliance simultaneously. The WebArena-Infinity results, where audit-clean pass@1 surged from 43.5% to 93.0% while invalid trajectories plummeted from 293 to 17, indicate that the evolutionary process can guide agents away from "reward hacking" or exploitative behaviors towards legitimate, contract-aware, and verifiable actions. This has implications for deploying more trustworthy and secure agents.
4. **Efficient Use of Evaluation Compute**: DarwinX demonstrates that evaluation compute can be productively converted into durable capabilities. The system intelligently allocates additional compute (turns and tokens) to tasks that are newly being solved, rather than spending uniformly more across all tasks. This targeted resource utilization contributes to the efficiency of the evolutionary process.
5. **New Research Directions**: The success of DarwinX opens avenues for future research, including investigating the co-evolution of models and harnesses, treating harnesses as long-lived assets across different base model generations, leveraging the preservation probe for rigorous safety and compliance policy enforcement, and developing methods for continual, deployed evolution in real-world settings.
6. **Auditability**: The fact that harness edits are human-readable (unlike weight updates) means that changes and their justifications are auditable. This transparency is crucial for understanding agent behavior and for building deployable systems.

In summary, DarwinX signifies that the operational "surface" of an LLM agent—its harness—is a fertile ground for evolutionary optimization, yielding substantial, robust, and ethical improvements in agent performance without requiring modifications to the core language model itself. This provides a pragmatic and powerful paradigm for advancing agent capabilities.
