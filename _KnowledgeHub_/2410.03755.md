# Denoising with a Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture

> [!summary] Summary
> Denoising with a Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (D-JEPA) integrates self-supervised representation learning from JEPAs with diffusion models, achieving state-of-the-art image generation performance on ImageNet with an FID of 1.54. This framework also demonstrates remarkable sampling efficiency and versatility across video, audio, and text-to-image modalities.

## Problem

- Generative models, such as autoregressive and diffusion models, often lack explicit mechanisms for learning robust, high-level semantic representations.
- Self-supervised representation learning methods, particularly Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs), excel at learning features but typically do not directly support high-quality content generation.
- The field lacked a unified approach to synergistically leverage advancements in both representation learning and generative modeling, especially for continuous data.

## Method

- **D-JEPA** introduces a framework that combines Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) with diffusion (or flow matching) loss for ==denoising==, within a generalized next-token prediction strategy.
- It employs a visual transformer-based architecture with a context encoder, an EMA-updated target encoder, and a feature predictor for masked tokens.
- The model utilizes a dual loss function, integrating a prediction loss (LpL_pLp) for representation learning and a denoising loss (LdL_dLd) for generative capabilities.

## Results

- Achieved state-of-the-art conditional image generation on ImageNet 256x256, with D-JEPA-H reaching an FID of 1.54 and IS of 341.0 (with classifier-free guidance).
- Demonstrated high sampling efficiency, generating high-quality 256x256 images (FID ~2.0) in approximately 120 milliseconds on an H800 GPU.
- Learned robust visual representations, achieving competitive linear probing accuracy on ImageNet-**1K**, comparable to dedicated self-supervised models, and showcased multi-modal extensibility to text-to-audio, text-to-image, and class-conditional video generation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The integration of JEPA-style masked image modeling with diffusion/flow matching loss can directly unlock state-of-the-art generative performance while simultaneously learning strong representations.
> - The dual loss structure effectively mitigates representation collapse in JEPAs, allowing for simpler encoder designs and efficient training.
> - D-JEPA exhibits a 'larger is faster' scaling law, where increased model size leads to faster convergence and fewer auto-regressive steps for optimal sampling.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The D-JEPA paper situates itself at the intersection of two rapidly advancing fields in artificial intelligence: **generative modeling** and **self-supervised representation learning**.

**Generative Modeling Landscape:**
The paper acknowledges the transformative success of generative language models, particularly in "next-token prediction" for discrete data. This success has motivated the adaptation of autoregressive models for other domains, especially image generation. However, a significant challenge in adapting these models to vision has been the necessity to discretize visual data into token sequences, which can often degrade image quality.
More recently, **diffusion models** have emerged as the dominant paradigm in generative modeling, surpassing Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) in generating high-fidelity images. Diffusion models excel at modeling complex probability distributions by iteratively denoising a noisy signal.
Within this landscape, recent efforts like MAR (Li et al., 2024) and Transfusion (Zhou et al., 2024) have sought to integrate diffusion models within an autoregressive framework. However, the authors argue that these models primarily focus on enhancing generative performance from a *generation-centric view*, often overlooking the synergistic potential of advancements in representation learning.

**Self-Supervised Representation Learning Landscape:**
Concurrently, self-supervised learning has seen remarkable progress, particularly through **masked image modeling (MIM)** (e.g., MAE, BEiT) and **invariance-based techniques** (e.g., contrastive learning, BYOL). A prominent method in this space is **Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs)**, championed by researchers like Yann LeCun. JEPAs are designed to predict embeddings of a target signal using a context signal, proving highly effective for learning robust visual representations for downstream tasks.
However, the paper points out that researchers in representation learning typically emphasize model performance on downstream tasks, neglecting the potential for these models to excel directly in generative modeling. Traditional JEPAs, for instance, predict feature embeddings, which are not directly usable for high-quality image generation or even outpainting tasks.

**D-JEPA's Bridging Role:**
D-JEPA specifically addresses this identified gap. It aims to bridge the divide between these two powerful paradigms. The core insight is that while existing generative models often lack strong representation learning capabilities and existing representation learning models struggle with direct generation, their strengths are complementary. D-JEPA proposes a novel framework that effectively leverages next-token prediction from a representation learning perspective (specifically, JEPA's masked image modeling approach) to enhance generative modeling. By doing so, it attempts to create a generative model that not only produces high-quality content but also learns semantically rich, high-level representations. This positions D-JEPA as a significant step towards more unified and versatile AI models capable of both understanding and generating diverse data types.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to **pioneer the integration of Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) within the realm of generative modeling**, specifically by developing "Denoising with a Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture" (D-JEPA).

The motivations underpinning this objective are multi-faceted:

- **Unlocking Generative Potential of Representation Learning:** The authors observe a significant disconnect: while self-supervised representation learning (especially JEPAs) has made substantial progress in learning high-level semantic features, its direct application to generative modeling remains underexplored. D-JEPA aims to harness these powerful learned representations to drive superior data generation, thereby demonstrating that advancements in representation learning can directly translate to state-of-the-art generative capabilities.
- **Addressing Limitations of Existing Generative Models:** Current autoregressive models, despite their success in language, often struggle with discretizing continuous visual data, leading to quality degradation. Diffusion models, while excellent, often operate without direct benefit from explicit representation learning frameworks like JEPAs. D-JEPA seeks to overcome these limitations by combining the strengths of both, offering a new perspective on how to achieve high-fidelity generation.
- **Reinterpreting Masked Image Modeling for Generation:** A key conceptual motivation is to reinterpret JEPA's masked image modeling (MIM) approach as a "generalized next-token prediction strategy." This reinterpretation facilitates autoregressive data generation, moving beyond mere feature prediction to actual content synthesis.
- **Enabling Continuous Data Generation:** D-JEPA is motivated to enable data generation in a continuous space, rather than relying solely on discrete tokens. This is achieved by incorporating diffusion loss (or flow matching loss) to model the per-token probability distribution, allowing for fine-grained control and higher quality in continuous data domains.
- **Enhancing Scalability and Efficiency:** The researchers are motivated to develop a generative model that exhibits good scalability, achieving better performance with increased model size and fewer training epochs. Furthermore, optimizing sampling efficiency by intelligently adjusting auto-regressive steps for different model scales is a practical motivation.
- **Laying Groundwork for Unified Multi-modal Models:** A long-term motivation is to provide theoretical support and empirical validation for constructing unified multi-modal generation models. By demonstrating D-JEPA's versatility across images, video, and audio, the authors aim to pave the way for architectures that can seamlessly generate diverse types of continuous data within a single framework.

In essence, D-JEPA seeks to create a more powerful, flexible, and scalable generative AI, informed by the rich representational understanding fostered by self-supervised learning, and capable of operating across a spectrum of continuous data modalities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

D-JEPA introduces a novel framework that marries the principles of Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) with the efficacy of diffusion models, all framed within a generalized next-token prediction strategy.

#### 3.1. Tokenization and Masking

- **Input Processing:** Training data (images) is first processed into non-overlapping semantic tokens, `U`. While D-JEPA can operate on raw pixels, the authors primarily adopt a latent space modeling strategy, encoding images into a latent space using a Variational Autoencoder (VAE) before patchification. This is done to reduce training cost and accelerate sampling, though performance in representation tasks might be slightly degraded compared to raw pixel training.
- **Masking Strategy:** A significant portion of these tokens (more than 70%, with masking ratios `r_mask` sampled from a truncated normal distribution with a mean of 1.0 and std of 0.25) are randomly masked. This creates a set of unmasked "context tokens" (`Y`) and masked tokens to be predicted. High masking ratios are employed to reduce pre-training time, memory usage, and improve performance, consistent with prior Masked Autoencoder (MAE) research.

#### 3.2. Architecture

D-JEPA comprises three key components, all implemented as identical visual transformer backbones:
- **Context Encoder ($\phi$):** Processes the unmasked tokens to extract contextual information.
- **Target Encoder ($\bar{\phi}$):** Processes *all* original tokens (`U`) to generate ground-truth target embeddings for the masked regions. Its parameters are updated via an Exponential Moving Average (EMA) of the context encoder's parameters (`$\bar{\phi}$ ← α $\bar{\phi}$ + (1 − α)$\phi$`), with `α = 0.9999`. A `stop-gradient` operation is applied to the output of `$\bar{\phi}$` to prevent backpropagation through it, ensuring its parameters only update via EMA.
- **Feature Predictor ($\gamma$):** Takes the context features from `$\phi$` and predicts the embeddings for the masked tokens.

#### 3.3. Dual Loss Functions

D-JEPA leverages two complementary loss functions:

- **Prediction Loss ($L_p$ - JEPA Component):**
 - This loss drives the representation learning aspect. The feature predictor `$\gamma$` predicts target embeddings (`$z_i$`) for the masked tokens. The ground truth (`$g_i$`) for these embeddings is provided by the target encoder `$\bar{\phi}$` (with `stop-gradient`).
 - The loss objective is `$L_p = E_{z_i=\psi(c_i),g_i} [D(u_\theta(z_i), g_i)]$`, where `D` is a distance measure (smoothed L1 function in D-JEPA) and `$u_\theta$` is a two-layer MLP projecting `$z_i$`.
 - Crucially, this loss is applied *only* to masked tokens.
 - A key advantage: the diffusion loss inherently mitigates the "representation collapse" issue commonly faced by JEPAs, allowing for simpler architectural designs (e.g., identical encoders) and EMA update rules.

- **Denoising Loss ($L_d$ - Diffusion Component):**
 - This loss enables the generative capabilities, modeling the conditional probability distribution `$p(x_i | z_i)$` for *each individual token* `$x_i$`, given its corresponding predicted embedding `$z_i$` from the feature predictor.
 - The loss is formulated as `$L_d = E_{\epsilon,t} [\parallel \epsilon - \epsilon_\theta(x_t^i | t, z_i) \parallel^2]$`, where `$\epsilon_\theta$` is a compact Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) acting as a noise estimator. This small MLP is conditioned on time `t` and the predicted embedding `$z_i$`.
 - **Flow Matching Loss Alternative:** D-JEPA also explores and adopts flow matching loss (Eq. 7) as an alternative to the denoising loss. Flow matching directly trains a velocity field to transform noise into data, offering potentially faster convergence and improved texture quality, particularly for more complex or multimodal tasks.

- **Combined Loss:** The overall loss function is simply the sum: `$L = L_d + L_p$`. The authors argue these losses are not conflicting; `$L_d$` helps prevent collapse for `$L_p$`, while `$L_p$` provides high-level semantic information (`$z_i$`) to `$L_d$`.

#### 3.4. Sampling Strategy (Generalized Next Set-of-Tokens Prediction)

- D-JEPA employs an iterative sampling strategy akin to MaskGIT, gradually reducing the masking ratio from 1.0 to 0.0 following a cosine schedule.
- In each auto-regressive step, the model predicts and denoises a *set* of tokens based on the current context (already sampled tokens), rather than just a single next token.
- The number of auto-regressive steps (`T`) can be varied (e.g., 64 steps for optimal balance).
- **Randomized Ordering:** D-JEPA uses fully randomized orderings for predicting the next set of tokens, enhancing diversity.
- **Controlled Generation:** Classifier-free guidance (CFG) and a temperature parameter (`τ`) are used to control the trade-off between image quality and diversity during sampling.

In essence, D-JEPA innovatively merges JEPA's strength in learning abstract, predictive representations with diffusion models' prowess in generating high-fidelity continuous data, all structured under a flexible autoregressive prediction paradigm.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

D-JEPA demonstrates significant empirical success across several generative tasks, particularly image generation, and showcases remarkable scalability and versatility.

#### 4.1. State-of-the-Art Image Generation Performance

- **ImageNet 256x256 Conditional Generation (without Classifier-Free Guidance - CFG):**
 - D-JEPA consistently achieves state-of-the-art Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) and Inception Score (IS) across all model scales (Base, Large, Huge).
 - D-JEPA-L (687M parameters) outperforms all previous generative models, including larger ones like MAR-H (943M parameters), achieving an FID of 2.32 (vs. MAR-H's 2.35) and an IS of 233.5.
 - D-JEPA-H (1.4B parameters) further sets a new benchmark with an FID of 2.04 and IS of 239.3, demonstrating superior performance even against models like VDM++ (2.0B parameters).
- **ImageNet 256x256 Conditional Generation (with CFG):**
 - With CFG, D-JEPA-L achieves an FID of 1.58, and D-JEPA-H reaches an FID of 1.54, which are very close to the theoretical upper limit of the underlying VAE (FID = 1.199). While MAR-H achieves a comparable FID (1.55), D-JEPA-L and D-JEPA-H exhibit significantly higher IS (327.2 and 341.0 vs. MAR-H's 303.7), indicating better diversity and quality.
- **ImageNet 512x512 Generation:** D-JEPA-L achieves an FID of 1.89 (without CFG) and 1.62 (with CFG), surpassing previous systems at this higher resolution.
- **Qualitative Results:** Generated images are highly realistic, exhibiting rich details and high fidelity. D-JEPA-H even demonstrates the capability to generate high-quality portraits, a feature not commonly seen in previous generative models.

#### 4.2. Scalability and Training Efficiency

- **Fewer Training Epochs:** As the model scale increases, D-JEPA requires significantly fewer training epochs to achieve state-of-the-art performance. For example, D-JEPA-L achieves SOTA results in 480 epochs, while D-JEPA-H achieves its best in 320 epochs, contrasting with other models where epochs either increase or remain constant with scale.
- **Scaling Law:** A surprising finding is that with increasing model size, the *number of auto-regressive steps required for optimal sampling decreases*. D-JEPA-H achieves its best results with just 64 sampling steps, making it highly efficient.
- **Sampling Efficiency:** D-JEPA can generate high-quality images very rapidly, achieving an FID of approximately 4.0 in just 43 milliseconds and an FID of approximately 2.0 within 120 milliseconds for 256x256 images on an H800 GPU. This represents a significant speedup over many existing diffusion and autoregressive models.

#### 4.3. Representation Learning Capabilities

- When trained directly on raw pixels, D-JEPA (specifically D-JEPA-B and D-JEPA-L) achieves state-of-the-art linear probing accuracy among generative models on ImageNet-1K and remains highly competitive with dedicated self-supervised representation learning models (e.g., I-JEPA, MAE). This confirms its ability to learn robust and transferable visual features.
- For fine-tuning, D-JEPA also demonstrates strong performance improvements over training from scratch, further validating its learned representations.

#### 4.4. Flexibility and Extensibility

- **Flow Matching Loss:** Experiments show that substituting diffusion loss with flow matching loss leads to faster convergence and, in many cases, superior performance, especially for more challenging tasks.
- **Inpainting and Outpainting:** D-JEPA exhibits excellent capabilities in image restoration, inpainting, and outpainting, even with minimal contextual information, demonstrating its strong understanding of visual content.
- **Multi-modal Generation:** D-JEPA proves to be highly extensible beyond image generation:
 - **Text-to-Audio Generation:** Successfully adapted for speech synthesis on the LJSpeech dataset, producing clear and natural-sounding audio by incorporating phoneme and pitch encoders and multimodal attention.
 - **Text-to-Image Generation:** Demonstrates progressive understanding of objects and spatial relationships during training on an internal dataset, generating high-quality images from text prompts.
 - **Class-Conditional Video Generation:** Achieves competitive FVD scores on the UCF101 dataset, generating coherent video clips by directly training on video tokens and treating them holistically.
 - **Unified Multimodal Model:** Preliminary experiments showcase D-JEPA's potential to serve as a backbone for unified multimodal generation models, capable of simultaneously generating text and images by intelligently switching between next-token prediction for discrete text and next-set-of-tokens prediction for continuous visual data.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The D-JEPA framework represents a significant advancement in generative artificial intelligence, with far-reaching implications and potential impacts across several domains:

#### 5.1. Bridging Representation Learning and Generative Modeling

- **Key Contribution:** The most profound significance of D-JEPA lies in its successful integration of Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) – a cornerstone of self-supervised representation learning – into generative modeling. This validates the hypothesis that learning high-level semantic representations can directly enhance and drive state-of-the-art generative performance.
- **Unified AI Goal:** This convergence pushes the field closer to the goal of "unified AI," where a single model can both understand (learn representations) and create (generate data), potentially leading to more powerful and versatile general-purpose AI systems.

#### 5.2. A Generalizable Generative Architecture for Continuous Data

- **Modality Agnostic:** D-JEPA is not limited to images; its core design allows it to handle various continuous data types, including video and audio. This is a crucial step towards developing truly modality-agnostic generative models, where the underlying mechanism for generating different data forms remains consistent.
- **Flexibility with Losses:** The ability to seamlessly switch between diffusion loss and flow matching loss provides significant flexibility, allowing researchers to choose the optimal approach based on task complexity and desired performance characteristics (e.g., faster convergence, better texture).

#### 5.3. Superior Scalability and Efficiency

- **Enhanced Performance with Scale:** D-JEPA demonstrates a robust scaling law, where larger models not only achieve better performance but also converge faster (fewer training epochs) and generate samples more efficiently (fewer auto-regressive steps). This "larger is faster" property is highly desirable for deploying models in real-world applications and generating high-resolution, complex content.
- **Faster Inference:** The remarkable sampling efficiency, achieving high-fidelity images in milliseconds, positions D-JEPA as a strong candidate for applications requiring real-time or near real-time content generation.

#### 5.4. Novel Conceptual Framework

- **Generalized Next-Token Prediction:** Reinterpreting masked image modeling as a generalized next-token prediction strategy provides a fresh conceptual framework for autoregressive generation. The finding that fewer auto-regressive steps can be optimal for larger models challenges conventional wisdom and opens new avenues for optimizing generation processes.

#### 5.5. Foundation for Unified Multimodal Models

- **Sora-like Potential:** By demonstrating its extensibility to text-to-image, text-to-audio, and class-conditional video generation, and even exploring preliminary multimodal generation, D-JEPA lays a strong theoretical and empirical foundation for constructing advanced unified multimodal models. This research provides insights crucial for developing systems capable of handling and generating diverse data types in a coherent and interconnected manner, akin to recent groundbreaking models like OpenAI's Sora.
- **Impact on AGI Research:** This work contributes to the broader quest for Artificial General Intelligence (AGI) by proposing an architecture that can learn universal representations and apply them across multiple sensory modalities, potentially leading to more holistic and context-aware generative capabilities.

In summary, D-JEPA is a significant contribution that redefines the landscape of generative AI by harmonizing representation learning with diffusion-based generation. Its demonstrated scalability, efficiency, and multi-modal versatility promise to accelerate the development of more intelligent and capable AI systems in the future.
