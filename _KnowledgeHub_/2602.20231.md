# UniLACT: Depth-Aware RGB Latent Action Learning for Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> UNILACT is a transformer VLA pretrained on depth-aware latent action tokens learned by UNILARN, a shared RGB-depth vector-quantization framework using inverse/forward dynamics. Depth is used only during training; inference is RGB-only. Beats RGB-only latent baseline Moto by 29.2% relative average sequence length on CALVIN out-of-domain pretraining, plus real-world gains.

## Problem

- Latent action pretraining from unlabeled video captures only appearance-driven dynamics from RGB, lacking 3D geometric structure needed for contact-rich manipulation.
- Prior depth-augmented VLAs treat depth as pixel or feature-level policy input, requiring substantial robot-annotated trajectories rather than improving unsupervised latent pretraining.
- It remains unexplored whether geometric structure can be embedded directly into RGB latent action spaces so downstream policies inherit spatial priors without extra labeled data.

## Method

- **UNILARN**: two-stage ==vector-quantization== pipeline where modality-specific IDMs produce RGB and depth latents through a shared codebook, then fuse and re-quantize into a unified latent via a second codebook.
- Modality-specific ==forward dynamics models== condition on the unified latent to reconstruct future RGB and depth observations, forcing the unified code to retain complementary cross-modal dynamics.
- **UNILACT**: GPT-2-based ==autoregressive transformer== pretrained on cross-modal latent-token prediction (RGB, depth, unified), then fine-tuned with a convex combination of unified-latent and 7-DoF action-regression losses.

## Results

- On CALVIN ABC to D with OXE out-of-domain pretraining, UNILACT reaches **3.10** average sequence length versus Moto's **2.40**, a **29.2%** relative improvement.
- Real-world xArm7 trials across four tasks show UNILACT at **62.5%** average success versus Moto's **52.5%**, with gains up to **+20** points on seen tasks like carrot placement.
- Ablations show unified plus modality-specific latents (**2.859**) beat RGB-only (**2.601**), depth-only (**2.402**), and naive RGB+depth concatenation (**2.080**), at equal **89.8M** parameters and **27ms** latency as Moto.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Depth need not persist at inference; embedding geometric structure only into the pretraining supervisory signal still transfers spatial priors to an RGB-only runtime policy.
> - Learning disentangled modality-specific latents alongside a unified latent outperforms collapsing modalities into one joint space, preserving complementary information a single space discards.
> - Cross-modal supervision helps most during pretraining, but narrowing the fine-tuning target to a single unified latent avoids task interference from jointly optimizing multiple latent objectives.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represent a paradigm in robotics research aimed at enabling robots to generalize across new tasks, unfamiliar objects, and varying environmental conditions. Current VLA models frequently rely on extensive robotic datasets, often collected through human teleoperation, which makes the pretraining process costly and challenging to scale.

To mitigate the reliance on such expensive labeled data, a recent line of research has focused on unsupervised pretraining using widely available, unlabeled internet-scale videos. This approach leverages latent action representations, which abstract control dynamics into a discrete latent space, subsequently transferring these learned priors to downstream robot tasks with limited explicit supervision. Existing latent action methods, such as Moto and UniVLA, predominantly derive these representations from RGB observations alone. This focus on RGB data means the learned latents primarily capture appearance-driven dynamics, often lacking explicit 3D geometric structure. For precise and contact-rich manipulation tasks, such as grasping, placement, and collision avoidance, 3D geometric understanding is considered essential.

Prior work has explored the integration of depth information into VLA policies to enhance spatial reasoning. These methods, including 3D-CaVLA, DepthVLA, and SpatialVLA, typically incorporate depth as a pixel or feature-level input to the policy network. However, these approaches often still necessitate substantial robot-annotated trajectory data for training. The question of whether depth can enhance RGB-based latent action representations themselves, embedding geometric structure during unsupervised pretraining to provide stronger spatial priors without requiring additional labeled data, remained an underexplored area.

This paper addresses this gap by proposing a method to incorporate depth information into the latent action learning process, thereby enriching the latent space with 3D geometric understanding during unsupervised pretraining. This approach aims to improve the scalability and robustness of VLA models for robotic manipulation by leveraging a more comprehensive understanding of the environment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce UNILACT, a Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model that incorporates explicit 3D geometric structure into its latent action representations during unsupervised pretraining. This is achieved by leveraging depth information alongside RGB observations.

The motivations for pursuing this objective are multi-faceted:

- **Addressing the limitations of RGB-only latent actions:** Current latent action representations, learned solely from RGB observations, primarily encode appearance-driven dynamics. This leads to a lack of explicit 3D geometric structure, which is a critical component for robots performing precise, contact-rich manipulation tasks such as accurate grasping, careful object placement, and effective collision avoidance. A robot policy without embedded depth reasoning may struggle to assess object reachability or potential collision events.
- **Enhancing spatial priors in VLA models:** While prior work has shown that integrating depth into VLA policies can improve spatial reasoning, these methods typically treat depth as a direct input to the policy or as auxiliary supervision, still requiring significant amounts of robot-annotated trajectory data for training. This research is motivated by the unexplored potential of enhancing the RGB-based latent action representations themselves with geometric structure during unsupervised pretraining. The goal is for downstream policies to inherit stronger spatial priors without incurring the cost of additional labeled data.
- **Developing a scalable pretraining paradigm:** The high cost and difficulty of collecting large-scale, robot-annotated data for VLA pretraining necessitate alternative, more scalable approaches. By leveraging unlabeled video data (RGB-D) for learning depth-aware latent actions, the proposed method aims to reduce this dependency and provide a more efficient pretraining mechanism.
- **Improving generalization and precision:** The hypothesis is that a unified latent action space, which captures both visual semantics from RGB and 3D geometric structure from depth, will enable VLA models to exhibit improved generalization capabilities across novel tasks and environments, along with enhanced precision in manipulation. This is particularly relevant for tasks requiring fine motor control and accurate spatial reasoning in cluttered or dynamic environments.
- **Efficient inference:** An additional motivation is to ensure that while depth information is crucial during the training phase, the deployed model (at inference time) can operate effectively using only RGB observations and task instructions, thereby maintaining computational efficiency and reducing sensor requirements during operation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed UNILACT framework is a transformer-based Vision-Language-Action model designed to leverage depth-aware latent action representations. Its training involves three distinct stages: Unified Latent Action Learning (UNILARN), Unified Latent Pretraining, and Action Fine-Tuning.

**Stage 1: UNILARN (Unified Latent Action Learning)**

UNILARN aims to learn both modality-specific (RGB and depth) and a unified latent action representation within a shared embedding space, while explicitly modeling their cross-modal interactions. This is achieved through a two-stage vector-quantization (VQ) pipeline:

- **Modality-Specific Latent Learning:** For each modality `m` (RGB `r` or depth `d`), an Inverse Dynamics Model (IDM) `I_m` takes paired observations `(o_m_t, o_m_(t+H))` (current and future frames with temporal offset `H`) and maps them to a continuous latent embedding `~z_m_t`. This embedding is then discretized using a vector-quantization operator `VQ` with respect to a shared codebook `C^(s)`, resulting in modality-specific discrete latent action representations `z_m_t`. Corresponding codebook embeddings `e_m_t` are then retrieved.
- **Unified Latent Learning:** The modality-specific codebook embeddings (`e_r_t` and `e_d_t`) are concatenated and linearly projected into a continuous unified latent space, producing `h_t`. This projected representation is then discretized by a second `VQ` operator using a distinct codebook `C^(u)`, yielding the unified latent action representation `z_u_t`.
- **Forward Dynamics Modeling:** The unified latent action `z_u_t` is used to condition modality-specific Forward Dynamics Models (FDMs) `F_m`. Each FDM `F_m` takes the current observation `o_m_t` and `z_u_t` to reconstruct the future observation `^o_m_(t+H)`. This disentangled reconstruction objective ensures `z_u_t` captures complementary dynamics across both RGB and depth modalities.

The IDMs consist of a frozen ViT-L encoder (pretrained with MAE) followed by a spatio-temporal transformer. Learnable query embeddings are appended to patch features to capture spatio-temporal changes. FDMs are implemented as ViT-B style decoders trained with an MSE reconstruction loss.

**Stage 2: Unified Latent Pretraining**

In this stage, the pretrained UNILARN encoder is used to extract modality-specific (`z_r_t`, `z_d_t`) and unified (`z_u_t`) latent action tokens from RGB-D videos. These tokens serve as action-free supervisory signals for pretraining UNILACT.

- **Model Architecture:** UNILACT is built upon a GPT-2 causal transformer backbone. Task instructions `l` are encoded using a frozen T5 encoder, and visual inputs `o_t` are processed by a ViT-L vision encoder.
- **Pretraining Objective:** UNILACT is trained as an autoregressive transformer to predict a target latent token sequence `z_m_1:N`, where `m` can indicate RGB, depth, or unified latents. The model factorizes the conditional distribution `p_θ(z_m_1:N | o_t, l, z_u_t)`.
- **Cross-Modal Learning:** To encourage the internalization of complementary semantic and geometric cues, UNILACT conditions its predictions on the unified latent representation `z_u_t` and is trained to predict one of the latent types (RGB, depth, or unified). This cross-modal latent prediction setup is designed to promote alignment between modality-specific and unified latent spaces while preserving modality-specific structure. The pretraining utilizes a standard autoregressive next-token prediction objective `L_m_latent`.

**Stage 3: Action Fine-Tuning**

The pretrained UNILACT predicts discrete latent action tokens, which are not directly executable by a robot. Therefore, a fine-tuning stage is introduced to map these predicted latent actions to continuous robot control commands.

- **Robot Control:** Action query tokens are appended to the input sequence (alongside visual, language, and unified latent tokens). The transformer outputs corresponding to these action query tokens are then fed into a lightweight action decoder.
- **Action Decoder:** The action decoder is a 2-layer MLP followed by separate linear layers. It outputs 7-DoF end-effector deltas, comprising positional offsets `Δp ∈ R^3`, rotational offsets `Δr ∈ R^3`, and a binary gripper command `g ∈ {0, 1}`.
- **Fine-tuning Loss:** Continuous components are trained using an L1 regression loss, while the gripper command is optimized with a binary cross-entropy loss, collectively forming `L_action`. To preserve the pretrained latent structure, fine-tuning uses a convex combination of the unified latent action prediction loss (`L_u_latent`) and `L_action`.
- **Inference:** During inference, UNILACT operates solely on RGB observations and task instructions. The depth modality is utilized exclusively during the training phases (UNILARN and Unified Latent Pretraining) to learn the depth-aware unified latent action representations.

**Data Handling:** For real-world experiments where depth observations might be missing in datasets like OXE, depth maps are generated from RGB images using a foundational model, Depth-Anything-V2. All experiments are conducted using PyTorch on 4 NVIDIA H200 GPUs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The effectiveness of UNILACT was evaluated through extensive experiments in both simulated (CALVIN benchmark) and real-world settings, supported by computational analysis and ablation studies.

**CALVIN Benchmark (Simulation: ABC→D Setting)**

- **Performance Comparison:** UNILACT consistently outperformed RGB-based latent action baselines, such as Moto, in the challenging ABC→D setting (training on environments A, B, C; evaluating on unseen environment D).
 - **In-domain pretraining:** UNILACT achieved an average sequence length of 2.86, surpassing Moto's 2.60. This indicated an immediate benefit of incorporating depth into latent action representation under comparable settings.
 - **Out-of-domain pretraining (Open X-Embodiment - OXE):** UNILACT demonstrated a 29.2% relative improvement in average sequence length over Moto (3.10 vs. 2.40). This result suggests that the unified latent action representation, learned jointly from RGB and depth, enhances generalization and robustness when trained on diverse pretraining data.
- **Task-Level Analysis:** A task-wise comparison on CALVIN revealed that while RGB-based latent representations performed competitively on appearance-driven tasks (e.g., stack blocks, rotate blue block), unified (RGB+depth) latents yielded larger gains on geometry-centric tasks (e.g., move slider, turn on light bulb). This supports the premise that incorporating depth enriches the latent action space with stronger geometric priors, which are critical for precise and contact-rich manipulation.

**Real-World Experiments (7-DoF xArm7 Manipulator)**

- **Overall Improvement:** UNILACT consistently outperformed the RGB-only Moto baseline, achieving a 10% overall improvement in average success rate across four tabletop manipulation tasks (two seen, two unseen).
- **Task-Specific Performance:**
 - T1 (Pick up carrot, place in bowl): UNILACT achieved 90.0% success (+20% over Moto). Qualitative results showed Moto failing due to inaccurate depth prediction and collision, while UNILACT accurately positioned the carrot.
 - T2 (Move <object1> near <object2>): UNILACT achieved 60.0% success (+20% over Moto). Qualitatively, Moto struggled to grasp and collided with the workspace, whereas UNILACT executed a precise grasp.
 - T3 (Put eggplant into plate): UNILACT achieved 60.0% success (-10% compared to Moto).
 - T4 (Lift block): UNILACT achieved 40.0% success (+10% over Moto).
- **Qualitative Observations:** The real-world results indicated UNILACT's enhanced depth awareness in its latent action representations, enabling more precise manipulation in contact-rich scenarios and better collision avoidance compared to appearance-only methods.

**Computational Analysis**

- **Efficiency:** Despite incorporating both RGB and depth information during training, UNILACT maintained the same model size (89.8 million parameters) and per-step inference latency (27 ms) as the RGB-based Moto model. This is attributed to UNILACT's design, where the depth modality is exclusively used during training and not required at inference time.

**Ablation Studies (CALVIN ABC→D Benchmark)**

- **Impact of Visual Modality:**
 - Both RGB and depth-based latent actions significantly improved performance over a no-latent baseline, confirming the value of pretraining with motion priors.
 - A naive multimodal approach, where RGB and depth latent actions were learned independently with alternating supervision, resulted in performance degradation compared to individual modalities, suggesting representation inconsistency.
 - A UniSkill-style variant (learning only a joint unified latent space without modality-specific latents) showed improvement over the naive RGB+Depth approach.
 - UNILACT (Unified + Modality-specific), which jointly aligns RGB and depth in a shared latent space while retaining modality-specific latents, achieved the best results, validating its design.
- **Impact of Output Modalities:** Pretraining UNILACT with cross-modal targets (predicting unified, RGB, and depth latents) and then fine-tuning exclusively on the unified latent representation yielded the best performance. This suggests that comprehensive cross-modal supervision during pretraining enriches the latent space, while a focused, compact unified target during fine-tuning promotes effective action prediction.
- **Impact of Training Objectives:** A single-task latent prediction setting (predicting one modality at a time, specifically the unified latent) achieved higher performance than a multi-task setting (jointly predicting RGB, depth, and unified latents). This suggested that jointly optimizing multiple latent objectives might introduce task interference, leading to less effective optimization in this context.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research demonstrates the effectiveness of incorporating depth information directly into latent action representations for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. By introducing UNILARN and UNILACT, the authors present a framework that addresses a notable limitation in existing RGB-only latent action methods: the lack of explicit 3D geometric understanding critical for robotic manipulation.

The significance of this work lies in several areas:

- **Enhanced Spatial Understanding:** The integration of depth during unsupervised pretraining allows UNILACT to develop stronger spatial priors, leading to improved 3D geometric reasoning. This is particularly impactful for contact-rich manipulation tasks where precise grasping, placement, and collision avoidance are paramount.
- **Scalable Pretraining Paradigm:** The proposed approach leverages widely available unlabeled RGB-D video data for learning depth-aware latent actions. This reduces the dependency on costly, human-teleoperated, robot-annotated datasets, offering a more scalable and cost-effective pretraining solution for VLA models.
- **Improved Generalization and Robustness:** Experimental results in both simulation and real-world settings indicate that UNILACT consistently outperforms RGB-based baselines. The significant gains observed under out-of-domain pretraining highlight the enhanced generalization and robustness afforded by depth-aware latent representations.
- **Practical Applicability:** A key design feature is that while depth is utilized during training to enrich the latent space, UNILACT operates solely on RGB observations and task instructions at inference time. This ensures that the benefits of depth awareness are realized without imposing additional sensor requirements or computational overhead during deployment, making the approach practical for real-world robotic systems.
- **Contribution to Latent Action Learning:** The UNILARN framework contributes to the field of latent action learning by demonstrating a method to jointly learn modality-specific and unified latent representations, fostering alignment and complementary dynamics between visual semantics and geometric structure.

The potential impact of this research includes the acceleration of VLA model development for robotic control. By providing a method to imbue VLA models with a more comprehensive understanding of their 3D environment during unsupervised pretraining, it could lead to the creation of more capable, reliable, and versatile robot policies for a wider array of complex manipulation tasks in diverse, unstructured environments. This work lays a foundation for future research exploring multimodal latent spaces for embodied AI.
