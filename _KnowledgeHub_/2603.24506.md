# Toward Physically Consistent Driving Video World Models under Challenging Trajectories

> [!summary] Summary
> PhyGenesis introduces a driving world model that generates high-fidelity, multi-view driving videos with strong physical consistency, even when conditioned on challenging or counterfactual trajectories. The system rectifies physically inconsistent input trajectories into plausible 6-DoF motions and leverages a heterogeneous dataset, outperforming prior methods in visual quality and physical plausibility across various driving scenarios.

## Problem

- Existing driving world models lack physical awareness of trajectory feasibility, often producing rendering artifacts and structural failures when given physically impossible or inconsistent input trajectories.
- Current models struggle with physics-consistent generation for rare or extreme (but feasible) scenarios, as they are trained primarily on real-world datasets biased towards nominal, safe driving behaviors.
- Prior high-risk scenario generation efforts (e.g., AVD2, Ctrl-Crash, SafeMVDrive) were limited to single-view or low-quality outputs, restricting their utility for realistic accident and safety-critical scenario synthesis.

## Method

- A ==Physical Condition Generator== rectifies potentially physics-violating 2D trajectories into physically plausible 6-DoF vehicle motions, using an attention-based architecture with a Temporal Convolutional Network output head.
- A ==Physics-Enhanced Multi-view Video Generator== (**PE-MVGen**), built on a ==diffusion transformer==, is co-trained on a heterogeneous dataset to synthesize high-fidelity and physically consistent multi-view video sequences.
- A novel ==heterogeneous multi-view dataset== combines real-world data (==nuScenes==) with carefully curated, physically challenging simulated scenarios (CARLA) to imbue the model with robust physical understanding.

## Results

- **PhyGenesis** consistently achieved superior visual quality (**lower FID/FVD**), higher physical consistency (**PHY scores**), and better human preference across nuScenes and challenging CARLA datasets compared to baseline methods.
- The Physical Condition Generator effectively reduced **6-DoF L2 distance errors**, demonstrating its ability to rectify physics-violating trajectories and prevent artifacts like vehicle penetration, particularly in simulated challenging scenarios.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both the Physical Condition Generator and the heterogeneous co-training strategy were critical for improving overall performance, visual quality, and physical consistency under challenging driving conditions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly addressing trajectory feasibility with a dedicated condition generator significantly improves the physical consistency and visual fidelity of generated driving videos, especially for challenging inputs.
> - Co-training a video world model on a balanced heterogeneous dataset, encompassing both nominal real-world data and simulated extreme physical interactions, is crucial for learning robust physical priors.
> - The use of 6-DoF trajectories and a TCN-based condition generator enables the capture and prediction of abrupt physical changes and finer motion details during safety-critical events.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of autonomous driving research has increasingly focused on video world models as a scalable alternative to costly real-world data collection and high-fidelity physical simulators. These models aim to synthesize future scenes and behaviors, often conditioned on structured inputs like vehicle trajectories, enabling applications such as closed-loop evaluation, high-risk scenario generation, and integration with end-to-end planners.

Existing driving world models, such as BEVGen, BEVControl, MagicDrive, DriveDreamer, DiST-4D, WorldSplat, Genesis, and UniScene, have demonstrated capabilities in generating high-fidelity multi-view future scenes. However, these methods are primarily trained on real-world driving datasets that predominantly feature nominal and safe driving scenarios. Consequently, a limitation arises when these models encounter challenging or counterfactual trajectories, which are common outputs from trajectory simulators, planning systems, or human interaction. In such situations, existing models often produce videos with physical inconsistencies, rendering artifacts, and structural failures.

Research into high-risk driving video generation, exemplified by efforts like AVD2, DrivingGen, Ctrl-Crash, SafeMVDrive, and Challenger, has attempted to address the synthesis of accident scenarios. However, earlier methods were limited by single-view or low-quality data, while more recent approaches combine trajectory simulators with video generators, but their video generators are typically still trained on nominal data, leading to restricted quality and limited depiction of physical interactions such as collisions.

This work, PhyGenesis, positions itself by addressing the gap between nominal driving world models and high-risk scenario generation. It aims to develop a world model capable of both handling nominal scenarios and robustly synthesizing high-risk events. The primary distinction lies in its explicit handling of both trajectory feasibility and physics-consistent video generation, supported by a heterogeneous dataset that combines real-world data with simulation-derived extreme scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a driving world model, termed PhyGenesis, that can generate driving videos exhibiting high visual fidelity and strong physical consistency, particularly when conditioned on challenging or counterfactual trajectories.

The motivation for this work stems from two identified fundamental limitations of existing driving world models:

1. **Lack of Physical Awareness of Trajectory Feasibility**: Current models typically act as "condition-to-pixel translators." They are designed to render visual scenes based on input trajectories but lack an intrinsic understanding of physical constraints. When trajectory conditions generated by simulators, planners, or user inputs violate fundamental physical laws (e.g., overlapping paths indicating object penetration or abrupt impossible maneuvers), existing models struggle. This often results in the generation of videos with severe rendering artifacts, geometric distortions, and structural failures, as they are forced to produce visuals from physically impossible inputs. The paper highlights that these models fail to perform explicit physical reasoning about trajectory validity.

2. **Lack of Physics-Consistent Generation Capability**: Most existing world models are predominantly trained on real-world driving datasets. These datasets are inherently biased towards safe, nominal, and routine driving behaviors, making complex physical interactions (e.g., collisions, off-road departures, rapid accelerations/decelerations) scarce. Consequently, when presented with physically feasible but rare or extreme trajectories, even if valid, these models often fail to generate realistic dynamics. They produce videos that may contain unnatural object behaviors, disappearances, or deformations, indicating a deficit in their learned physical priors for non-nominal scenarios. The qualitative examples in Figure 1 illustrate how prior methods can produce deformed or inconsistent scenes under challenging conditions, even when the underlying trajectory might be physically feasible.

To address these limitations, PhyGenesis aims to:
- Develop a mechanism to transform potentially invalid trajectory inputs into physically plausible conditions.
- Create a video generator capable of synthesizing high-fidelity, multi-view driving videos that are physically consistent under these rectified or inherently challenging conditions.
- Construct a training dataset that is rich in diverse physical interactions, including extreme and safety-critical scenarios, to imbue the model with robust physical understanding.

The overarching motivation is to create a more robust and reliable world model that can serve as a foundation for evaluating and testing autonomous driving systems in a wider range of scenarios, particularly those involving safety-critical events that are difficult or dangerous to collect in the real world.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The PhyGenesis framework is designed to generate physically consistent and high-fidelity multi-view driving videos, especially under challenging trajectory conditions. It integrates a novel approach to handle trajectory feasibility and physics-enhanced video generation.

#### 3.1. Overview of PhyGenesis

The system takes initial multi-view images ($I_0$), a static map ($M$), and future 2D trajectories ($T_{orig}$) for multiple agents as input. These 2D trajectories can be physically inconsistent. The framework aims to synthesize a high-fidelity, multi-view video sequence ($V_{1:T}$) that reflects intended behaviors while adhering to real-world physical constraints. The framework comprises two main components:
1. **Physical Condition Generator**: Rectifies potentially invalid 2D trajectories into physically plausible 6-DoF vehicle motions.
2. **Physics-Enhanced Multi-view Video Generator (PE-MVGen)**: Synthesizes high-fidelity and physically consistent multi-view video sequences based on the rectified conditions.
These components are trained on a heterogeneous dataset combining real-world data with simulated challenging scenarios.

#### 3.2. Heterogeneous Multi-view Data

To imbue the world model with robust physical understanding, a diverse training dataset is constructed:

- **Real-World Multi-view Data**: The nuScenes dataset is utilized, providing a foundation of nominal driving behaviors in urban environments. However, this data is acknowledged to be biased towards safe driving and lacks complex physical interactions.

- **Simulated Physically-challenging Multi-view Data**: To address the scarcity of safety-critical data, the CARLA simulator is used. This simulator offers a high-fidelity physics engine and controllable environmental variations.
 - **Data Collection Strategy**: The Bench2Drive routing setup is followed to ensure diversity in scenes, weather, and traffic. Two subsets are curated:
 - **CARLA Ego**: Focuses on interactions involving the ego vehicle (e.g., collisions, off-road departures).
 - **CARLA Adv**: Focuses on interactions centered around a nearby non-ego adversarial agent.
 - **Perturbation**: Routes and target speeds of the ego or adversarial vehicle are perturbed to induce aggressive dynamics, including collisions, off-road events, and abrupt maneuvers, as evidenced by a shift towards higher ego-acceleration distributions compared to nuScenes.
 - **Sensor Suite**: A sensor suite mirroring nuScenes (LiDAR, 6 surround-view cameras, radars, IMU/GNSS) is used. Crucially, a collision sensor and HD map metadata are integrated to precisely record physical interaction timestamps. Data is recorded at 12Hz.
 - **Annotation**: Data is meticulously annotated and organized into a nuScenes-compatible format.
 - **Heterogeneous Dataset Construction**: Approximately 31 hours of simulated driving data (15.5 hours CARLA-Adv, 15.2 hours CARLA-Ego) are collected. Using collision sensors and map metadata, 9.7 hours of highly physically-challenging video clips are extracted. This is combined with 4.6 hours of real-world nuScenes data to form the final heterogeneous dataset.

#### 3.3. Physical Condition Generator

This component dynamically rectifies potentially physics-violating 2D trajectories ($T_{orig}$) into physically plausible 6-DoF trajectories ($\hat{T}_{6dof}$), including (x, y, z, pitch, yaw, roll). The 6-DoF representation is critical for capturing vertical and rotational variations during extreme interactions.

- **Architecture**:
 - Input 2D trajectories are encoded via sine-cosine positional encoding and an MLP into agent tokens ($q$).
 - **Spatial Cross-Attention**: Interacts with multi-view Perspective View (PV) features ($F_{pv}$) based on trajectory coordinates to ground queries spatially ($q_s$).
 - **Agent-Agent Self-Attention**: Enables tokens to perceive kinematic states of surrounding vehicles, addressing overlapping/penetration conflicts ($q_a$).
 - **Map Cross-Attention**: Integrates vectorized map embeddings ($E_{map}$) for off-road awareness ($q_m$).
 - **Feed-Forward Network (FFN)**: Refines aggregated features ($q_f$).
 - **Time-Wise Output Head**: A Temporal Convolutional Network (TCN) processes step-specific learnable temporal embeddings concatenated with refined agent tokens, followed by an MLP. This design is specifically chosen to capture abrupt physical changes (e.g., instantaneous velocity drops during collisions), unlike traditional MLPs that tend to smooth outputs.

- **Training Pair Construction**: To learn rectification, counterfactual trajectory corruption is applied to collision clips in the simulated dataset. Pre-collision trajectories are kept, but post-collision trajectories are corrupted by extending agents' paths with their pre-collision velocity, simulating penetration. Ground-truth simulation logs provide the correction target. Real-world nominal nuScenes trajectory-condition pairs are also included without corruption.

- **Optimization**: A weighted distance (L1) loss is used between predicted 6-DoF trajectories and ground truth. Weights ($W_{i,t}$) are defined by an `λ_event` (temporal window around event timesteps) and `λ_agent` (for agents involved in the interaction) to focus on critical physical moments.

#### 3.4. Physics-Enhanced Multi-view Video Generator (PE-MVGen)

This is the second stage, building upon Wan2.1, a diffusion transformer (DiT) base model. It is adapted for controllable, multi-view generation in autonomous driving with deep physical awareness.

- **Multi-View & Layout Conditioning**:
 - Input multi-view clips are encoded into latents ($z$) using a pre-trained 3D VAE.
 - Multi-view modeling is achieved by concatenating the view dimension into the spatial axis, allowing self-attention to capture cross-view dependencies.
 - Future T-frame 3D agent boxes and map polylines are projected onto each camera view using intrinsics and extrinsics to create view-specific control images ($M_v$). These are encoded by the VAE and concatenated with noisy latent input ($z_t$) as conditions.

- **Data-Driven Physical Enhancement**: PE-MVGen is co-trained on the heterogeneous dataset, maintaining a balanced 1:1 ratio of nominal real-world logs and simulated physically challenging data. Importantly, it is supervised with *ground-truth* physical trajectories at this stage, decoupling physical correction from rendering. This co-training improves the model's physical understanding and generalization to challenging scenarios.

- **Training Objective**: Optimized via Rectified Flows, which uses a flow-matching objective (Mean Squared Error) to predict the velocity vector, conditioned on an initial context frame ($c_{init}$), scene caption ($c_{text}$), and future multi-view layout images ($c_{layout}$).

- **Curriculum Co-Training Strategy**: A two-stage curriculum is employed: lower resolution pre-training (224 × 400) for faster learning of multi-view geometry and challenging layout mappings, followed by high-resolution fine-tuning (448 × 800) for visual fidelity.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of PhyGenesis encompassed visual quality, physical plausibility, and controllability, comparing it against UniMLVG, MagicDrive-V2, and DiST-4D.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance

PhyGenesis consistently outperformed baseline methods across all evaluation metrics and datasets (nuScenes, CARLA Ego, CARLA ADV).
- **Visual Quality (FID/FVD)**: PhyGenesis achieved lower FID and FVD scores, indicating higher realism and fidelity. For instance, on nuScenes, it obtained FID of 10.24 and FVD of 40.41, compared to DiST-4D's 10.49 and 46.95. The improvements were notably larger on the physically challenging CARLA Ego and CARLA ADV datasets.
- **Physical Consistency (PHY)**: PhyGenesis demonstrated superior physical consistency, with PHY scores of 0.97 on nuScenes, 0.71 on CARLA Ego, and 0.87 on CARLA ADV, significantly higher than baselines (e.g., DiST-4D's 0.39 on CARLA Ego).
- **Human Preference (Pref.)**: Human annotators consistently preferred PhyGenesis-generated videos, particularly for challenging trajectories, with preference rates of 0.67 on nuScenes, 0.71 on CARLA Ego, and 0.66 on CARLA ADV.
- **Qualitative Observations (Figure 1, Figure 5)**: Baselines often exhibited artifacts, geometric distortions, object deformation, or disappearance under physically challenging trajectories, while PhyGenesis maintained physical consistency and visual fidelity.

#### 4.2. nuScenes Stress Test

To evaluate performance under out-of-distribution, physics-violating trajectories in real-world scenes, a stress test was conducted on nuScenes by scaling ego vehicle speed and isolating collision cases.
- PhyGenesis showed greater physical consistency under these corrupted conditions compared to baselines, as indicated by both quantitative metrics (Figure 7) and qualitative comparisons (Figure 6).

#### 4.3. Performance of Physics-Enhanced Multi-view Video Generator (PE-MVGen)

When all methods were conditioned on *ground-truth* trajectories (i.e., no counterfactual inputs), the PE-MVGen component of PhyGenesis still demonstrated superior performance (Table 2).
- It achieved better visual realism (FID/FVD), physical consistency (PHY), and lower controllability error (CtrlErr) across nuScenes, CARLA Ego, and CARLA ADV.
- This indicates that co-training with physics-rich data enables the generator to better capture and render complex interactions, even when input trajectories are physically feasible, leading to higher quality and better adherence to conditions.

#### 4.4. Performance of Physical Condition Generator

The Physical Condition Generator's ability to rectify physics-violating 2D trajectories into physically plausible 6-DoF trajectories was evaluated (Table 3, Figure 8, Figure 9).
- It consistently reduced the 6-DoF L2 distance error to ground truth across all datasets.
- The most substantial error reductions were observed for physics-violating trajectories in CARLA Ego and CARLA ADV, confirming its effectiveness in correcting invalid inputs (e.g., preventing vehicle penetration into guardrails).
- On nuScenes, where inputs are 2D, the model primarily recovered the missing 4-DoF (z, pitch, yaw, roll).

#### 4.5. Ablation Study

An ablation study investigated the contribution of the two key components: the Physical Condition Generator ("Phy-Model") and heterogeneous co-training with CARLA data ("Mixed Data") (Table 4, Figure 9, Figure 10).
- **Effect of Physical Condition Generator**: Its inclusion improved both visual quality and physical consistency, particularly on CARLA Ego and CARLA ADV. This was observed through a reduction in FVD (e.g., from 116.51 to 72.48 on CARLA Ego) and improved PHY scores, along with qualitative reductions in penetration and deformation artifacts.
- **Effect of Heterogeneous Co-training**: Incorporating physics-rich CARLA data consistently improved performance on physically challenging data. For instance, on CARLA ADV, it reduced FVD from 89.83 to 77.83 and significantly increased human preference. Qualitatively, training with nominal nuScenes data alone often resulted in deformed vehicles during challenging interactions, whereas heterogeneous co-training produced sharper, more physically coherent dynamics.
- **Weighting Design (λ_event and λ_agent)**: An additional ablation (Tables 5 and 6 in supplementary material) indicated that the model's performance was largely robust to different values of `λ_event` and `λ_agent`, suggesting the weighting mechanism effectively focuses supervision without extreme sensitivity to hyperparameter choices.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

PhyGenesis addresses critical limitations in existing driving world models by specifically tackling their inability to handle challenging or counterfactual trajectories and generate physically consistent videos under such conditions. This work has several significant implications and potential impacts:

1. **Enhanced Simulation Reliability**: By explicitly handling trajectory feasibility and ensuring physics-enhanced video generation, PhyGenesis enables more reliable simulation of safety-critical events such as collisions and off-road behaviors. These scenarios are inherently rare and dangerous to collect in real-world data, making high-fidelity simulation crucial for robust autonomous driving development.

2. **Improved Autonomous Driving Evaluation and Testing**: The ability to generate physically consistent videos from potentially flawed or extreme trajectory inputs provides a practical building block for simulation-driven evaluation and safety testing. This facilitates the assessment of autonomous driving planners and systems in a wider, more diverse range of challenging situations than currently possible with models trained solely on nominal data.

3. **Foundation for Robust Planning and Decision Making**: A world model that can accurately predict and visualize the physical consequences of various trajectories, including unsafe ones, could provide critical feedback to end-to-end planners and decision-making systems, potentially leading to the development of more robust and safer autonomous driving policies.

4. **Advancement in Generative AI for Embodied Agents**: The framework's ability to learn complex object-environment interactions and generate coherent physical dynamics from a heterogeneous dataset represents an advancement in video generation for embodied AI agents, moving towards more intelligent and physics-aware world models.

5. **Hybrid Data Utilization**: The methodology of combining real-world and carefully curated synthetic data, with specific strategies for counterfactual trajectory rectification and physics-enhanced generation, offers a blueprint for leveraging the strengths of both data sources to overcome their individual limitations.

In conclusion, PhyGenesis contributes to the safety and robustness of autonomous driving systems by bridging the gap between existing high-fidelity video generation and the need for physical consistency in challenging, safety-critical scenarios.
