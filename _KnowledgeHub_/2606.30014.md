# Shell-Supervised Gaussian Splatting for Urban Real-to-Sim Reconstruction

> [!summary] Summary
> Shell-supervised Gaussian Splatting improves urban real-to-sim reconstruction by aligning a lightweight exterior facade shell to a video-driven 3DGS reconstruction, rendering per-view shell depth, camera-space normal, and valid-mask maps, then applying mask-gated depth-NCC and normal losses only on shell-supported pixels. On close-range facade scenes it cuts mean normal error from 55.21 degrees to 17.42 degrees and Chamfer distance from 0.069 m to 0.052 m, with comparable rendering quality.

## Problem

- Photorealistic Gaussian rendering does not guarantee reliable geometry; close-range facades with glass, reflections, repeated windows, and weak texture yield plausible images over unstable surfaces.
- Monocular depth and normal priors are still inferred from the same ambiguous RGB evidence, so they fail to correct facade orientation in reflective, repetitive regions.
- Surface-oriented primitives such as 2D Gaussian Splatting still miss facade orientation, while detailed semantic building priors are sensitive, proprietary, and often impossible to release.

## Method

- **Shell-Supervised Gaussian Splatting**'s ==facade structural shell==: an exterior-only geometric prior excluding interiors, MEP systems, and project metadata, aligned once to the video reconstruction frame and kept fixed throughout training.
- ==Shell-to-camera rendering== via ==Open3D ray casting== produces per-view shell depth, camera-space normal, and a binary valid mask marking pixels where camera rays first hit the shell.
- ==Mask-gated objective== adds a local patch ==inverse-depth NCC== term, a ==normal dot-product term==, and an auxiliary geometry-consistency regularizer, evaluated only on valid shell-supported pixels beside the RGB loss.

## Results

- On the primary scene, mean unsigned normal error drops from **55.21** degrees photo-only, **53.57** MONO, and **57.46** 2DGS to **17.42**; pixels under 20 degrees rise from **7.71%** to **73.12%**.
- Visible-surface point clouds improve: Chamfer distance **0.069 m** to **0.052 m** and F-score at 5 cm **0.546** to **0.710** versus photo-only 3DGS, while 2DGS reaches only **0.066 m** and **0.563**.
- Ablation shows normal-only supervision carries the gain at **17.74** degrees, depth-only improves the scale-aligned depth diagnostic from **0.236** to **0.113** but not orientation; PSNR falls slightly, **23.67** to **23.06 dB**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Photometric quality is a poor proxy for geometry; evaluate reconstructions with orientation and surface metrics before treating them as simulation assets.
> - Coarse external priors can beat learned monocular cues: supervision geometrically independent of RGB resolves ambiguity that image-derived cues structurally cannot.
> - Mask-gating makes a partial prior safe to use, but supervision does not extrapolate to unsupported regions, and transparency and reflection stay unmodeled.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated within the intersection of neural rendering, 3D reconstruction, and embodied artificial intelligence (AI) for simulation environments.

**Neural Rendering and 3D Gaussian Splatting:** The field of neural rendering has progressed from volumetric radiance fields (e.g., NeRF) to more explicit and efficient representations like 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS), which facilitates real-time photorealistic view synthesis from posed images. While 3DGS offers high visual fidelity, a known limitation is its tendency to produce geometrically unstable or inconsistent surface reconstructions, particularly in challenging urban environments. Subsequent Gaussian methods have aimed to improve robustness and structure through various techniques such as anti-aliasing, density control, and surface-aligned representations (e.g., 2DGS). This work utilizes a 3DGS backbone but introduces an external mechanism to address its geometric inconsistencies.

**Geometric Priors for Neural Reconstruction:** Photometric supervision alone often results in under-constrained geometry. Previous research has explored incorporating geometric priors to regularize neural reconstructions. These priors include learned video geometry, implicit surface formulations, sparse depth, monocular depth/normal cues, and structural priors for weakly textured or planar regions. For instance, DN-Splatter demonstrated the use of depth and normal priors to regularize Gaussian Splatting geometry. This paper distinguishes itself by deriving its geometric priors not from image-based inference or sensor data directly, but from an external, aligned structural shell.

**Real-to-Sim and Embodied Simulation:** Embodied AI relies heavily on simulation platforms for scalable agent training and evaluation. Recent advancements in "real-to-sim" systems aim to convert real-world captures (e.g., videos) directly into interactive simulation assets, exemplified by systems like Vid2Sim. In this context, the fidelity of the reconstructed geometry is critical for collision reasoning, physical interaction, and navigation-relevant observations, extending beyond mere photorealistic rendering. This work specifically targets the reconstruction stage of such pipelines, aiming to improve the geometric quality of the assets before they are used in interactive simulations.

**Building Priors and Facade Geometry:** The use of external structural information to enhance reconstruction in built environments has precedents. Methods leveraging scan-based building models or architectural priors have been employed for tasks like LiDAR-camera pose refinement. GS4Buildings, for example, guides Gaussian reconstruction using semantic 3D building models. This paper differs by not requiring a complete semantic building model, instead employing a "lightweight exterior structural shell." The shell acts as mask-gated, per-view supervision rather than a direct initialization or replacement for the Gaussian representation, focusing on close-range handheld facade captures where ambiguities like glass, reflections, and repeated textures pose significant challenges to purely image-based methods.

In summary, this research addresses a recognized weakness in modern neural rendering techniques (geometric instability) specifically within the demanding context of urban facade reconstruction for embodied AI. It proposes a novel approach of integrating a lightweight external structural prior—the facade shell—to enhance geometric consistency while retaining the appearance-driven qualities of Gaussian Splatting, distinguishing itself from other geometric prior methods by the nature and application of its external guidance.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the geometric consistency of 3D reconstructions of urban facades, particularly for applications in real-to-sim pipelines used by embodied AI. This enhancement is sought without compromising the photorealistic rendering capabilities of modern neural rendering techniques.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges and requirements:

1. **Requirement for Usable Geometry in Embodied AI:** Real-to-sim reconstruction for embodied AI necessitates geometric models that are not only visually plausible but also functionally useful for tasks such as collision reasoning, physical interaction, and agent navigation. Current photorealistic novel-view synthesis models, while visually compelling, do not inherently guarantee reliable or consistent underlying geometry, which can render the reconstructed scenes unsuitable for robust simulation.
2. **Challenges in Urban Facade Reconstruction:** Close-range urban facades present specific difficulties for video-to-3D reconstruction systems. These challenges include:
 - **Reflective and Transparent Surfaces:** Glass and reflective elements common in modern facades are known to be problematic for many 3D reconstruction algorithms, often leading to unstable surface estimations.
 - **Repeated Patterns and Weak Texture:** Repetitive window designs and weakly textured surfaces can provide ambiguous visual cues, allowing multiple geometric interpretations that appear plausible in RGB renderings but are geometrically inconsistent.
 - **Limited Viewpoints:** Handheld video captures, common for close-range facades, might offer limited viewpoint diversity, further exacerbating geometric ambiguities.
 - These factors can result in visually acceptable renderings but with inaccurate or inconsistent surface orientations and depths, negatively impacting their utility for simulation.
3. **Limitations of Existing Geometry-Aware Methods:**
 - **Monocular Cues:** Methods relying on monocular depth or normal cues inferred from RGB images are still subject to the inherent ambiguities of those images.
 - **Detailed Building Priors:** Existing approaches that use complete semantic building models can be overly complex, sensitive to detailed information, or difficult to obtain and release due to proprietary or privacy concerns.
4. **Preserving Appearance while Improving Geometry:** The goal is to improve geometry without sacrificing the photorealistic appearance achieved by methods like 3D Gaussian Splatting. The approach should regularize geometry specifically in problematic facade regions while allowing the video-driven appearance to be maintained elsewhere.

To address these motivations, the key objective is to formulate and implement a reconstruction-stage framework that leverages a "lightweight exterior structural shell" as geometric supervision. This shell is intended to guide the Gaussian reconstruction process, specifically improving facade orientation and visible-surface consistency in shell-supported regions, thereby producing more reliable geometric assets for urban real-to-sim pipelines.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, "shell-supervised Gaussian Splatting," is a reconstruction-stage framework designed to improve the geometry of close-range urban facades within a video-to-3D pipeline for embodied AI. It operates in three main stages: shell preparation and alignment, shell-to-camera geometry rendering, and shell-supervised Gaussian optimization.

#### 3.1. Vid2Sim-style Gaussian Reconstruction Backbone

The method uses a standard 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) framework as its video-driven reconstruction backbone. A scene is represented by a set of anisotropic Gaussian primitives, each defined by its center (μ), covariance (Σ), opacity (α), and view-dependent color parameters (c). During training, these Gaussians are optimized to render an RGB image (ˆIᵢ) for each camera view (i) that matches the input RGB frame (Iᵢ). The baseline photo-only reconstruction optimizes the Gaussian scene using a combination of ℓ₁ and D-SSIM photometric terms. This approach prioritizes visual fidelity but often results in unstable surface geometry, especially for challenging facade elements like glass or repeated windows, consistent with observations about Gaussian reconstruction's sensitivity to SfM initialization quality. The MONO baseline, a component of the Vid2Sim-style pipeline, incorporates image-derived monocular depth and normal cues, but these are still inferred from potentially ambiguous RGB data. The proposed method aims to augment this backbone with stronger, external geometric supervision.

#### 3.2. Facade Structural Shell Preparation and Alignment

A crucial component is the "facade structural shell" (S), which provides exterior structural geometry. This shell is described as a lightweight geometric prior, differing from a complete semantic building model by excluding internal or sensitive information. It can be derived from various sources, including scan-based modeling (e.g., laser scanning), mobile mapping, or photogrammetric reconstruction. Before training, the facade shell is aligned to the video reconstruction's coordinate frame. This alignment is treated as a fixed and validated geometric prior, not subject to further optimization, ensuring its consistency with the video data.

#### 3.3. Shell-to-Camera Geometry Rendering

Once aligned, the facade shell is rendered into each training camera view. For every pixel (p) in each view (i), a camera ray is cast through the camera's intrinsics (Kᵢ) and pose (Rᵢ, tᵢ) to query its first intersection with the aligned shell (S_c). This ray-casting process utilizes a tool like Open3D RaycastingScene to efficiently identify first-hit distances and primitive normals. This step generates three per-view maps:
- **Shell Depth (Zˢᵢ):** The forward depth from the camera to the first intersection point with the shell.
- **Camera-space Shell Normal (Nˢᵢ):** The normal vector of the shell surface at the intersection point, expressed in the camera's coordinate system.
- **Valid Mask (Mˢᵢ):** A binary mask indicating pixels where a ray successfully intersected the shell (i.e., shell-supported pixels). These maps serve as the direct supervision signals for the subsequent Gaussian optimization.

#### 3.4. Shell-supervised Gaussian Optimization

During optimization, the 3D Gaussian scene not only renders RGB images (ˆIᵢ) but also predicts inverse depth (ˆDᵢ) and normals (ˆNᵢ) for each view. The training objective (L) combines the standard RGB photometric loss (R) with mask-gated shell losses for depth (D) and normals (N), plus an auxiliary geometry-consistency regularizer (C) inherited from the Vid2Sim pipeline:
`L = R + α(t)D + β(t)N + γ(t)C`
- **RGB Photometric Loss (R):** This term, identical to the photo-only baseline, ensures the preservation of video-driven appearance.
- **Shell Depth Loss (D):** This operates on inverse depth (Bˢᵢ = 1/Zˢᵢ). A local patch-normalized cross-correlation (NCC) objective is applied between the rendered inverse depth (ˆDᵢ) and the shell inverse depth (Bˢᵢ). This NCC term is evaluated only on valid shell-supported pixels (Ωˢᵢ), encouraging local structural depth agreement rather than an absolute metric L1 depth objective.
- **Shell Normal Loss (N):** This term encourages the rendered Gaussian normals (ˆNᵢ) to align with the camera-space shell normals (Nˢᵢ). It minimizes `1 - dot_product(ˆNᵢ(p), Nˢᵢ(p))` for valid shell-supported pixels (p ∈ Ωˢᵢ). Both normal vectors are unit-normalized. This loss is critical for correcting facade orientation in challenging regions.
- **Auxiliary Geometry-Consistency Regularizer (C):** This term is derived from the Vid2Sim-style reconstruction backbone and is also applied with the same valid-mask gating to focus regularization on shell-supported regions.
- **Mask-Gated Application:** All shell-related losses (D, N, C) are strictly applied only to pixels within the valid mask (Ωˢᵢ). This design prevents the shell prior from imposing constraints on sky, dynamic objects, occluded areas, or regions not covered by the available facade shell.
- **Scalar Weights (α(t), β(t), γ(t)):** These weights dynamically adjust with training iteration (t). The inverse-depth NCC weight (α(t)) is exponentially decayed, while normal (β(t)) and auxiliary (γ(t)) weights are fixed.

#### 3.5. Optimization Procedure

The shell-supervised optimization procedure initializes the Gaussian scene using a video-to-3D process. The aligned shell is pre-rendered into depth, normal, and valid-mask maps for all training views. During iterative training, a view is sampled, and the Gaussian scene renders its outputs. The loss function, including mask-gated shell losses, is computed, and the Gaussian parameters are updated using gradients. Densification and pruning of Gaussians occur during the initial phase of training, following standard 3DGS practices. The shell acts purely as supervision, not as initialization or a replacement for the video-derived reconstruction, enabling the method to preserve appearance while correcting geometry.

#### 3.6. Experimental Details

Experiments were conducted on two anonymized close-range urban facade sequences (primary and secondary scenes). All methods used consistent RGB frames, SfM camera parameters, train/test splits, image resolutions, and sparse initializations. Training spanned 30k iterations. For shell-guided models, the shell was aligned once and kept fixed; rendered supervision maps were used directly without denoising or manual editing. Ablation studies varied the included shell-guided loss terms to assess their individual contributions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluated the proposed shell-supervised Gaussian Splatting against appearance-driven and geometry-aware baselines across appearance and geometry metrics, primarily focusing on shell-supported regions.

#### 4.1. Appearance Metrics (Held-out Test Views)

- **Photometric Quality:** The photo-only 3DGS baseline achieved the highest PSNR (23.67 dB), as expected, given its sole optimization objective.
- **Comparison:** The shell-guided model (Ours) produced a slightly lower PSNR (23.06 dB) compared to photo-only 3DGS but maintained comparable SSIM and a lower LPIPS than 2DGS. This indicates that while the shell losses introduce geometric regularization, they generally preserve the video-driven appearance quality to a comparable level.

#### 4.2. Image-Space Geometry Evaluation (Shell-supported Test Views)

- **Facade Orientation (Normal Error):** This metric showed the most substantial improvement.
 - Photo-only 3DGS, MONO, and 2DGS exhibited high mean unsigned normal errors (above 53 degrees), indicating inconsistent facade orientations.
 - The proposed shell-guided model significantly reduced the mean normal error to 17.42 degrees. It also dramatically increased the percentage of pixels with normal errors below 20 degrees (from 7.71% for photo-only 3DGS and 14.65% for MONO to 73.12% for Ours).
- **Scale-aligned Depth Error (D-MAE):** D-MAE measures local structural depth agreement after per-view median scale alignment.
 - 2DGS achieved the lowest D-MAE (0.201 m), even with high normal error.
 - Our method had a larger D-MAE (0.273 m) but notably superior facade orientation. This discrepancy is attributed to D-MAE being a local, scale-normalized diagnostic, whereas the inverse-depth NCC training objective encourages structural agreement rather than absolute metric L1 depth accuracy.

#### 4.3. Visible-Surface Point-Cloud Evaluation (Shell-supported Test Views)

This evaluation used raw rendered depth for 3D back-projection without per-view rescaling.
- **Chamfer Distance (CD):** Our method improved visible-surface geometry.
 - Our method reduced the symmetric Chamfer distance from 0.069 m (photo-only 3DGS) to 0.052 m.
 - MONO produced similar point-cloud accuracy to photo-only reconstruction.
 - 2DGS showed a modest improvement in CD (0.066 m) over photo-only 3DGS but was still inferior to the shell-guided model.
- **F-score:** Measures precision and recall at various distance thresholds.
 - Our method improved F₅ (5 cm threshold) from 0.546 (photo-only 3DGS) to 0.710.
 - Compared to 2DGS, Ours improved F₅ from 0.563 to 0.710 and F₁₀ (10 cm threshold) from 0.788 to 0.865. Both methods were comparable at the looser F₂₀ (20 cm threshold). This indicates that while 2DGS improves coarse consistency, shell supervision is more effective for accurate shell-supported facade geometry.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study of Shell Supervision (Primary Scene)

- **Depth-only:** Substantially improved the scale-aligned depth diagnostic but did not significantly improve facade orientation.
- **Normal-only:** Demonstrated that shell-rendered normal supervision is the dominant cue for orientation consistency, reducing mean normal error from 55.21 degrees (photo-only) to 17.74 degrees.
- **Depth+Normal:** Achieved the best image-space orientation metrics, suggesting complementary benefits from both shell depth and normal cues.
- **Full Objective:** Obtained comparable orientation performance to Depth+Normal while retaining the auxiliary geometry-consistency regularizer. The results showed that the primary improvement originated from shell-rendered depth and normal supervision, not the auxiliary regularizer alone.

#### 4.5. Qualitative Analysis and Cross-scene Validation

- **Qualitative Observations (Primary Scene):** On held-out views, photo-only, MONO, and 2DGS reconstructions produced fragmented normal fields, particularly on glass curtain walls and repeated-window regions, where the underlying facade was planar. The shell-guided model generated more coherent facade orientations that aligned with the shell-rendered reference, while preserving fine structures like columns.
- **Cross-scene Validation (Secondary Scene):** The method was validated on a secondary scene with fewer frames and noisier shell-supported regions. The trend observed in the primary scene was consistent: shell-guided supervision improved facade-orientation consistency on valid shell-supported pixels while maintaining comparable rendering quality.
- **Spatial-region Holdout Stress Test (Supplementary):** An additional test where shell supervision was provided only on alternating vertical bands showed that improvements were strongest on directly supervised shell-supported regions, with limited extrapolation to disjoint unsupervised facade bands.

In conclusion, the research demonstrates that lightweight exterior facade shells, when used as mask-gated per-view supervision during Gaussian optimization, significantly improve visible facade orientation and visible-surface point-cloud consistency. This is achieved while preserving competitive RGB rendering quality compared to methods relying solely on photometric or image-derived monocular cues.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework that offers a practical advancement in 3D reconstruction for urban environments, particularly within the context of real-to-sim pipelines. The significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Improved Geometric Reliability for Embodied AI:** The primary significance lies in providing more geometrically reliable 3D assets for embodied AI agents. By enhancing facade orientation and visible-surface consistency, the reconstructed environments become more suitable for critical simulation tasks such as collision detection, physical interaction, and navigation-relevant observations. This moves beyond mere photorealistic rendering, addressing a key requirement for robust agent training and evaluation in simulated real-world scenarios.
2. **Addressing Urban Reconstruction Challenges:** The method effectively tackles specific challenges posed by urban facades, including glass, reflective surfaces, repeated patterns, and weak textures—conditions where purely photometric or monocular cue-based methods often fail to produce stable geometry. This makes real-to-sim reconstruction more robust in complex urban settings.
3. **Leveraging Lightweight Structural Priors:** The introduction of a "lightweight exterior structural shell" as supervision is a key contribution. Unlike approaches requiring full semantic building models, this method demonstrates that a simpler geometric prior can yield substantial improvements. This potentially broadens the applicability of such techniques by reducing the data acquisition and modeling overhead, as these shells can be obtained from various existing sources (e.g., laser scans, aerial mapping, photogrammetry) without needing detailed internal or sensitive information.
4. **Preservation of Appearance Quality:** The mask-gated supervision design ensures that geometric regularization is applied only to relevant shell-supported regions. This allows the video-driven appearance, a strength of Gaussian Splatting, to be preserved in other areas and across the scene, providing a balance between geometric accuracy and visual fidelity.
5. **Modular and Extensible Framework:** The proposed method acts as a reconstruction-stage framework that can be integrated with existing video-to-3D pipelines. By using rendered depth, normal, and mask cues, it provides a flexible mechanism to inject external geometric knowledge into neural rendering systems like 3D Gaussian Splatting.
6. **Diagnostic Insights into Gaussian Reconstruction:** The ablation studies provide valuable insights into the contributions of different geometric cues. They highlight that shell-rendered normal supervision is particularly effective for facade orientation, while shell depth offers complementary structural guidance. This understanding can inform future developments in geometry-aware neural reconstruction.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
The discussion acknowledges several limitations, which also implicitly suggest avenues for future work:
- **Dependency on Shell Preparation and Alignment:** The method assumes the availability and accurate alignment of an exterior shell, which remains a prerequisite.
- **Scope of Supervision:** Supervision is limited to shell-supported regions, meaning areas like sky, vegetation, dynamic objects, or interiors are not directly constrained by the shell. The spatial-region holdout test indicates limited extrapolation to unsupervised facade bands.
- **Glass and Material Modeling:** The current formulation does not explicitly model complex optical phenomena like transparency, reflection, or refraction of glass, suggesting room for integrating more sophisticated material representations.
- **Asset vs. Simulation Validation:** The improvements are validated as asset-level geometry enhancements, not through downstream interactive simulation or reinforcement learning results. Future work could integrate these improved assets into full simulation environments to demonstrate tangible benefits for agent performance.

Despite these limitations, the work establishes exterior facade shells as a practical and effective structural prior for improving the critical geometric fidelity needed for urban real-to-sim reconstruction. Its impact is likely to be seen in the development of more reliable and usable virtual environments for training and testing embodied AI systems.
