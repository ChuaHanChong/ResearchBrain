# MoMaGen: Generating Demonstrations under Soft and Hard Constraints for Multi-Step Bimanual Mobile Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> MOMAGEN is a framework developed by researchers at Stanford University, The University of Texas at Austin, and Amazon that automates the generation of diverse, high-quality synthetic demonstrations for multi-step bimanual mobile manipulation. It formulates data generation as a constrained optimization problem, explicitly incorporating reachability and object visibility to produce data that improves imitation learning policy performance and facilitates sim-to-real transfer from a single human demonstration.

## Problem

- The high cost and time investment required to collect diverse human demonstration data for complex, high-degrees-of-freedom multi-step bimanual mobile manipulation tasks.
- Previous automated data generation methods (e.g., X-Gen) struggled with mobile manipulation, failing to ensure target objects remained within the robot's arm workspace (reachability) or visible to onboard cameras (visibility).
- Such tasks require intricate coordination of navigation, bimanual arm movements, and contact-rich interactions, posing challenges for existing data generation approaches.

## Method

- MOMAGEN formulates automated demonstration generation as a constrained optimization problem, integrating hard constraints (e.g., task success, reachability, object visibility during manipulation) and soft constraints (e.g., object visibility during navigation, efficient robot retraction).
- It leverages a single human demonstration, segments it into subtasks, and iteratively generates new variations by sampling robot base and head camera poses while ensuring satisfaction of reachability and visibility constraints.
- The system uses inverse kinematics checks for preemptive filtering and employs a motion planner (cuRobo) to generate base, torso, arm, and head camera trajectories under defined constraints.

## Results

- MOMAGEN generated significantly more diverse datasets, covering a wider range of object and robot poses, and achieved an average data generation success rate of 63% on D0 randomization and successful data generation across all tasks up to D2 randomization levels.
- The generated demonstrations exhibited substantially higher task-relevant object visibility (often double the visibility ratio) compared to baselines, with ablation studies confirming the critical role of both hard and soft visibility constraints.
- Policies trained on MOMAGEN data consistently outperformed baselines (e.g., enabling success on tasks where baselines failed) and showed improved sim-to-real transfer, with π0 models achieving a 60% real-world success rate on Pick Cup after pretraining on synthetic data and fine-tuning on 40 real demonstrations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Framing data generation as a constrained optimization problem, explicitly modeling hard and soft constraints, provides a robust and generalizable approach for complex robotic tasks, extending prior X-Gen methods.
> - Explicitly enforcing reachability and object visibility as constraints during data generation is essential for creating behaviorally valid and perceptually relevant demonstrations for mobile manipulators, overcoming fundamental limitations of prior methods.
> - High-quality, diverse synthetic data generated from minimal human input (a single demonstration) can effectively train imitation learning policies, leading to improved performance, enhanced generalizability, and more efficient sim-to-real transfer.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper is situated within the field of robot learning, specifically focusing on imitation learning (IL) from human demonstrations. This paradigm has been recognized for its effectiveness in teaching robots complex manipulation skills. A primary challenge in scaling IL is the high cost and time required for collecting diverse, large-scale human demonstration data, particularly for tasks involving high-degrees-of-freedom robotic systems.

Previous research efforts to address this data scarcity can be broadly categorized. Offline data augmentation techniques, such as image augmentation, boost data quantity but may not generate behaviorally diverse or physically executable data. Fully automated approaches, like trial-and-error or scripted experts, have not consistently proven effective for complex, multi-step tasks involving contact-rich interactions.

A promising hybrid approach, exemplified by the "X-Gen" family of techniques (e.g., MimicGen, SkillMimicGen, DexMimicGen), involves using a small set of human demonstrations as seeds to synthesize numerous novel variations in simulation. This method aims to expand the support and convergence region of policies by generating validated, behaviorally diverse data. While X-Gen methods have demonstrated success in static table-top manipulation, they encounter significant limitations when applied to more complex robotic embodiments and tasks, specifically multi-step bimanual mobile manipulation.

This paper identifies two key challenges that prior X-Gen methods do not adequately address for mobile manipulation:
1. **Reachability:** A mobile base introduces the necessity of appropriately positioning the robot to enable subsequent manipulation. Naive replay of navigation segments from human demonstrations often results in robot configurations where target objects are outside the manipulators' workspace.
2. **Visibility:** An active camera on a mobile base creates problems of partial observability. For visuomotor policies, task-relevant objects may move out of the camera's field of view during navigation or manipulation, hindering optimal decision-making based on onboard sensor data.

MOMAGEN directly addresses these shortcomings by proposing a data generation framework that explicitly models and enforces reachability and visibility as constraints. By framing data generation as a constrained optimization problem, this work aims to extend the benefits of automated data generation to the challenging domain of bimanual mobile manipulation, which requires coordinating navigation, bimanual arm movements, and contact-rich interactions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind MOMAGEN is to mitigate the substantial cost and time associated with collecting high-quality human teleoperation data for multi-step bimanual mobile manipulation tasks. These tasks often require whole-body control, involving the simultaneous coordination of a mobile base and two high-degrees-of-freedom arms, which poses a significant control burden on human operators. The expense and complexity of such data collection hinder the scaling of imitation learning approaches for real-world mobile manipulation.

The key objectives of this research are:

1. **Formulate Data Generation as a Constrained Optimization Problem:** Develop a principled framework that treats automated demonstration generation as an optimization problem. This framework is designed to satisfy critical "hard" constraints (e.g., ensuring task success, kinematic feasibility, collision avoidance, and object visibility during manipulation) while balancing "soft" constraints (e.g., promoting object visibility during navigation or efficient robot retraction). This formulation aims to generalize and unify existing automated data generation approaches, providing a foundation for future methods.
2. **Address Reachability for Mobile Manipulation:** Introduce and enforce reachability as a hard constraint within the data generation process. This ensures that when new object arrangements are introduced, the generated robot base poses facilitate, rather than hinder, subsequent manipulation by keeping target objects within the robot's arm workspace.
3. **Ensure Object Visibility for Visuomotor Policies:** Integrate object visibility as both a hard and soft constraint. A hard constraint ensures task-relevant objects remain in the camera's view during manipulation, while a soft constraint biases the head camera towards the target object during navigation. This is crucial for training visuomotor policies that rely on onboard camera inputs.
4. **Generate Diverse and High-Quality Datasets:** Produce demonstration datasets that exhibit greater behavioral diversity (in terms of robot base poses, end-effector trajectories, and joint configurations) and higher object visibility compared to datasets generated by previous methods. This diversity is expected to enhance the generalization capabilities of learned policies.
5. **Enable Effective Imitation Learning from Synthetic Data:** Demonstrate that the data generated by MOMAGEN can be used to train successful imitation learning policies from a minimal initial investment, specifically a single human-collected demonstration.
6. **Facilitate Sim-to-Real Transfer:** Show that policies trained on MOMAGEN-generated data can be fine-tuned with a limited amount of real-world data to achieve successful deployment on physical robotic hardware, thereby bridging the simulation-to-real gap.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MOMAGEN formulates the automated generation of robot demonstrations as a constrained optimization problem. This approach positions the system as a generalization of previous X-Gen methods, which can be interpreted as using different sets of hard and soft constraints.

**Problem Formulation:**
The objective is to generate a new set of successful demonstrations `D` given source demonstrations `D_src` and a set of constraints `G_i`. The generation process is an optimization problem: `arg min_a L(.)` subject to several constraints.
- `L(.)`: Represents user-specified soft constraints (e.g., shorter trajectories, reduced jerkiness).
- `f(s_t, a_t)`: Denotes the system dynamics.
- `G_kin(s_t, a_t) <= 0`: Ensures kinematic feasibility (e.g., joint limits).
- `G_coll(s_t, a_t) >= 0`: Ensures collision avoidance.
- `G_vis(s_t, a_t, o_i(t)) <= 0`: Enforces visibility of task-relevant objects.
- `T_E^k_W = T_o^i_W (T_o^{i,src}_W)^-1 T_E^k_W`: Preserves relative end-effector to object poses for contact-rich subtasks.
- `s_t ∈ D_success`: Guarantees task success.

**Decomposition of Demonstrations:**
Each source demonstration is segmented into `N` subtasks. These subtasks can be single-arm or bimanual motions. Each subtask includes an object trajectory and an end-effector trajectory. Subtasks are classified as either free-space (e.g., moving to a pre-grasp pose) or contact-rich (e.g., grasping, wiping). For contact-rich subtasks, the relative poses between the end-effector and the target object are maintained from the source to the generated demonstrations.

**Key Constraints for Bimanual Mobile Manipulation (Innovations):**
MOMAGEN introduces specific constraints to address the challenges of mobile manipulation:
- **Reachability (Hard Constraint):** When generating demonstrations for novel object arrangements, the robot base pose is sampled to ensure that all required end-effector trajectories remain within the robot's arm workspace. This prevents situations where the robot is unable to manipulate a target object due to its base positioning.
- **Object Visibility during Manipulation (Hard Constraint):** A sampled base and head camera pose must ensure that task-relevant objects are within the head camera's field of view and not occluded, which is essential for visuomotor policies.
- **Object Visibility during Navigation (Soft Constraint):** During base movement, a soft constraint (cost) biases the head camera to track the task-relevant object. This is desirable for policy learning but not strictly enforced to allow for flexible navigation.
- **Retraction (Soft Constraint):** After manipulation, the robot is encouraged to retract its torso and arms to a compact configuration, reducing its footprint and increasing safety during subsequent navigation.

**Automated Demonstration Generation (Algorithm 1):**
MOMAGEN's generation process leverages a single human-collected demonstration and its annotations:
1. **Source Demonstration Annotation:** The single source demonstration is segmented into subtasks, with annotations for target objects, held objects, specific timesteps (e.g., pre-grasp, end), and retraction types.
2. **Iterative Subtask Generation:** For each subtask:
 - The system first checks if the robot holds any required object (for continuity).
 - It calculates the required end-effector poses for contact-rich motions based on the new, randomized object configurations.
 - **Constraint Checking:** The current robot base and head camera configuration are checked for reachability and visibility. If these hard constraints are met, the robot proceeds to manipulate.
 - **Sampling New Poses:** If constraints are not met, the system iteratively samples new robot base poses and head camera configurations. This sampling incorporates heuristics and inverse kinematics to quickly filter for valid (reachable and visible) configurations.
 - **Motion Planning:** Once a valid base/camera pose is determined, a motion planner (cuRobo is used) generates:
 - A base and torso trajectory to move the robot to the sampled base pose, while also planning the head camera motion under the soft visibility constraint.
 - An arm trajectory to reach the pre-grasp pose.
 - **Execution:** The contact-rich segment is then replayed in task space using task-space control.
 - **Retraction:** The robot attempts to retract its arms and torso to a canonical or prior joint configuration.
This process continues for all subtasks.

**Key Novelties in MOMAGEN:**
1. **Full-body Motion:** Jointly considers and plans for end-effector, head camera, and base poses, unlike prior works that primarily focused on end-effector.
2. **Visibility Guarantee:** Explicitly enforces object visibility through hard and soft constraints.
3. **Expanded Workspace:** Generates diverse base poses near target objects and plans cross-room base motions, fully exploiting the mobile manipulator's capabilities.
4. **Efficient Generation:** Uses fast Inverse Kinematics (IK) checks for preemptive filtering and decomposes the robot's configuration into torso/arm subspaces for conditional sampling, akin to task-motion planning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of MOMAGEN was conducted on four multi-step bimanual mobile manipulation tasks: Pick Cup, Tidy Table, Put Dishes Away, and Clean Frying Pan, implemented in the OmniGibson simulation environment. Three levels of domain randomization (D0, D1, D2) were used, ranging from minor object pose variations to aggressive randomization with added obstacles. MOMAGEN was compared against SkillMimicGen and DexMimicGen, both augmented with base trajectory replay.

**Data Diversity:**
- MOMAGEN demonstrated the ability to generate significantly more diverse datasets than baseline methods. For instance, in the Tidy Table task, MOMAGEN with D1 randomization covered a much wider range of object poses (e.g., across an entire counter) compared to baselines, which were limited to D0 and a smaller area due to their inability to generate novel base motions.
- Even under identical D0 randomization, MOMAGEN produced substantially broader coverage of base and end-effector poses and joint positions due to its flexible base placement.
- The method successfully generated demonstrations in cluttered environments (D2), further enhancing diversity in robot actions and visual observations.

**Data Generation Success Rates:**
- MOMAGEN achieved an average data generation success rate of 63% for D0 and successfully generated data for all tasks across all randomization levels (D0, D1, D2), although success rates decreased with increasing difficulty.
- Baseline methods performed adequately on simpler tasks (e.g., Pick Cup D0) but exhibited lower success rates on more complex tasks (e.g., Clean Frying Pan D0) and completely failed to generate data for D1 or D2 randomization levels, as objects often lay beyond the reachability afforded by replayed base poses.
- Ablation studies indicated that enforcing hard visibility constraints generally improved success rates, particularly for more complex tasks, by enabling the robot's torso to be positioned suitably for downstream manipulation.

**Object Visibility during Demonstration Generation:**
- MOMAGEN generated demonstrations with substantially higher task-relevant object visibility compared to baselines and ablations, often achieving double the visibility ratio, particularly in multi-step tasks.
- Analysis confirmed that both the hard and soft visibility constraints were critical in maintaining high object visibility during navigation and manipulation segments of the generated trajectories.

**Cross-Embodiment Data Generation:**
- MOMAGEN successfully transferred a single source demonstration collected on a Galexea R1 robot to generate Pick Cup demonstrations for a TIAGo robot. This was achieved by planning and replaying dense end-effector trajectories in task space, which is largely agnostic to specific robot kinematics.
- Limitations included potential failures due to differences in gripper size in confined spaces or self-collisions with bulkier arms.

**Policy Learning with Generated Demonstrations:**
- **Impact on Policy Performance:** Policies trained on MOMAGEN-generated data consistently outperformed those trained on data from other generation methods. For the Tidy Table (D0) task, MOMAGEN data led to significantly higher success rates, while for Pick Cup (D1), only MOMAGEN enabled the policy to achieve any success. The diverse base motions generated by MOMAGEN were identified as contributing to improved performance.
- **Generalizability Across IL Methods:** MOMAGEN-generated data proved beneficial for different imitation learning approaches, including training WB-VIMA from scratch and fine-tuning a pre-trained π0 model. Fine-tuned π0 models achieved success rates comparable to WB-VIMA across various tasks.
- **Importance of Object Visibility:** Ablation studies demonstrated that enforcing visibility constraints during data generation substantially improved policy performance. Policies trained without these constraints showed considerably lower success rates, particularly for tasks where visual feedback is critical for execution.
- **Data Scaling:** Experiments with π0 showed promising scaling trends, where increasing the number of MOMAGEN-generated demonstrations (from 500 to 2000) led to performance gains, especially under aggressive D1 randomization.
- **Sim-to-Real Deployment:** For the Pick Cup task on a real Galexea R1 robot, pretraining on 1,000 synthetic MOMAGEN demonstrations followed by fine-tuning on 40 real-world demonstrations improved real-world success rates (e.g., 10% for WB-VIMA vs. 0% for no pretraining; 60% for π0 vs. 0% for no pretraining). This indicated that diverse synthetic data provide a valuable prior for efficient policy learning in low-data real-world scenarios.

**Failure and Computational Cost Analysis:**
- **Failure Analysis:** A significant portion of failures (35%) was attributed to simulation instabilities. Among planner-related failures, arm-level motion planning contributed the largest share (40% on average), exceeding base-level planning (26% on average). For D2 randomization, navigation-related failures increased due to scene complexity.
- **Computational Cost:** Simulation execution time dominated total computation, significantly surpassing motion planning time. Base sampling also exhibited high variance, taking longer in complex scenes with fewer feasible base poses. Generating a single successful demonstration required 0.1 to 1.3 GPU hours on an NVIDIA TITAN RTX.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

MOMAGEN represents an advancement in automated data generation for robotic systems, specifically addressing the complexities of multi-step bimanual mobile manipulation. Its significance lies in several key areas:

1. **Addressing a Critical Data Collection Challenge:** The work directly tackles the high cost and labor intensity of acquiring high-quality human demonstrations for complex mobile manipulation tasks. By enabling the generation of diverse and validated synthetic data from a single human-collected demonstration, MOMAGEN offers a path to scale up data collection for previously intractable domains.
2. **Principled Framework for Data Generation:** The formulation of data generation as a constrained optimization problem provides a robust and generalizable framework. This framework unifies existing X-Gen approaches and offers a systematic method for integrating task-specific constraints.
3. **Novel Constraints for Mobile Manipulation:** The introduction of explicit hard and soft constraints for reachability and object visibility is a key technical contribution. These constraints are crucial for ensuring the behavioral validity and perceptual relevance of generated demonstrations for mobile manipulators with active cameras, overcoming fundamental limitations of prior methods.
4. **Enhanced Data Diversity and Quality:** MOMAGEN's ability to generate significantly more diverse datasets, encompassing a wider range of robot base and end-effector configurations, contributes to the robustness and generalization capabilities of trained policies. The emphasis on maintaining high object visibility during generation is particularly impactful for visuomotor policy learning.
5. **Improved Imitation Learning Performance:** The experimental results demonstrate that policies trained on MOMAGEN-generated data achieve higher success rates and better generalization compared to baselines. This holds true for both policies trained from scratch and those fine-tuned from large pre-trained models, indicating broad applicability across different imitation learning algorithms.
6. **Facilitating Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The empirical evidence suggesting that synthetic data from MOMAGEN can serve as a valuable prior for fine-tuning policies in real-world, low-data regimes is a practical contribution. This helps to bridge the simulation-to-real gap, accelerating the deployment of mobile manipulation robots in physical environments.

**Potential Impact:**
The methodologies introduced by MOMAGEN have the potential to:
- Accelerate the development and deployment of mobile manipulation robots for a wide array of real-world applications, including household assistance and logistics, by significantly reducing reliance on expensive human teleoperation.
- Advance the state of imitation learning by providing a framework for creating high-quality, diverse datasets that are tailored to the unique challenges of complex robot embodiments.
- Inspire future research in automated data generation, particularly in developing more sophisticated constraint satisfaction techniques and informed sampling strategies to improve efficiency and broaden applicability to even more complex, unstructured environments and whole-body manipulation tasks.

**Limitations:**
Despite its contributions, the work acknowledges several limitations:
- The current approach assumes full scene knowledge in simulation, which poses a challenge for real-world application without robust object pose estimation from vision models.
- The framework primarily focuses on tasks with alternating phases of navigation and manipulation; its extension to continuous whole-body manipulation is left for future work.
- The data generation process is computationally intensive, requiring substantial GPU resources, which indicates a need for further optimization in efficiency, particularly in base sampling and motion planning.
