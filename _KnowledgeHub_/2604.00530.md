# AceTone: Bridging Words and Colors for Conditional Image Grading

> [!summary] Summary
> AceTone introduces a multimodal framework that generates 3D Look-Up Tables (3D-LUTs) directly from text prompts or reference images for conditional image grading. This approach unifies photorealistic style transfer and instruction-guided grading, achieving improved performance on objective metrics and demonstrating higher user preference compared to existing methods.

## Problem

- Existing automated color grading methods often lack expressiveness, struggle to generalize across diverse creative intents, and produce outputs inconsistent with human aesthetic preferences.
- Current workflows are inefficient and fragmented, with photorealistic style transfer (PST) and instruction-guided grading (IGG) tasks typically requiring separate and incompatible model designs.
- Adversarial training paradigms commonly used for color grading models suffer from instability, mode collapse, and scalability issues, hindering consistent performance improvement.

## Method

- A 3D Vector Quantized Variational Autoencoder (==VQ-VAE==) tokenizes continuous 3D-LUTs into compact, perceptually accurate discrete representations (tokens).
- A Vision-Language Model (VLM) is pre-trained to autoregressively generate LUT tokens conditioned on multimodal inputs (text prompts or reference images).
- The model undergoes supervised fine-tuning for PST and IGG tasks, followed by reinforcement learning (Group Relative Policy Optimization - GRPO) using color similarity and aesthetic quality rewards to align outputs with human preferences.

## Results

- The 3D VQ-VAE tokenizer reconstructs LUTs with an average CIEDE2000 0E of 1.38, indicating perceptually minimal differences, while achieving a **99.98%** compression ratio.
- AceTone reduced LPIPS by approximately **40%** on the PST-50 benchmark compared to Neural Preset (**0.09** vs. **0.15**) and achieved higher aesthetic scores (3.29 vs. 3.03).
- On AceTone-Bench[Instruct], AceTone achieved a higher aesthetic score (3.54 vs. 3.19 for Qwen-Image-Edit) and a lower LPIPS (**0.22** vs. **0.33**), with user studies showing preference for AceTone's results in both PST (**41.4%**) and IGG (**46.9%**) tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Directly tokenizing 3D-LUTs enables a high-fidelity, compact discrete representation of color transformations, facilitating their integration and generation by Vision-Language Models.
> - A unified VLM architecture can effectively handle both reference-image and text-instruction guided color grading, streamlining previously disparate creative workflows.
> - Reinforcement learning, specifically GRPO, provides a stable and scalable mechanism to refine color grading outputs, effectively aligning them with aesthetic and perceptual preferences beyond what supervised fine-tuning alone can achieve.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Color grading, also known as toning, is a fundamental aspect of image post-production, significantly influencing the aesthetic and emotional impact of visual content. Existing automated color grading methods typically rely on patch-wise recoloring using convolutional neural networks (CNNs) or fixed/pre-defined filter banks. While these approaches can be effective in certain contexts, they often encounter limitations in terms of expressiveness, generalization across diverse creative intents, and alignment with human aesthetic preferences. Furthermore, tasks such as photorealistic style transfer (PST) and instruction-guided grading (IGG) have traditionally been addressed by separate and incompatible model designs, hindering the integration of a unified creative workflow.

A significant challenge in developing color grading models lies in their optimization objectives. Many existing methods employ adversarial designs to assess similarity between predicted and ground-truth results. However, this min-max adversarial paradigm can be sensitive to hyperparameters, prone to mode collapse, and suffer from unstable convergence, making it difficult to scale training for consistent improvement.

The work presented in this paper positions itself within the broader context of generative models and the recent advancements in aligning these models with human preferences through reinforcement learning (RL). By reformulating color grading as a generative color transformation task and directly producing 3D Look-Up Tables (3D-LUTs) conditioned on multimodal inputs (text or reference images), AceTone aims to address the limitations of prior methods. It seeks to provide a unified framework that enhances expressiveness, improves efficiency, and ensures better alignment with human aesthetic judgments through a stable, scalable learning strategy that incorporates perceptual and aesthetic preferences. This approach diverges from previous methods by postponing aesthetic alignment to a post-training phase, enabling a more robust generative process.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop AceTone, a multimodal conditioned color grading approach that operates within a unified framework. This framework aims to generate 3D-LUTs directly from text prompts or reference images, formulating color grading as a generative color transformation task.

The motivation for this work stems from several limitations observed in previous color grading methodologies:
- **Lack of Expressiveness and Generalization:** Existing methods, often based on patch-wise recoloring or fixed filter banks, frequently struggle to capture the full spectrum of creative intents or generalize effectively across varied aesthetic styles. They may produce outputs that are inconsistent with human aesthetic preferences.
- **Inefficiency and Disjoint Workflows:** Many traditional approaches can be computationally intensive, particularly for high-resolution images. Furthermore, the segregation of tasks like photorealistic style transfer and instruction-guided grading into distinct models leads to incompatible solutions and a fragmented creative workflow, preventing a holistic approach to image manipulation.
- **Challenges in Optimization:** The prevalent use of adversarial training paradigms in existing models for assessing output similarity has inherent instability issues, including sensitivity to hyperparameters, mode collapse, and unstable convergence. These issues impede the scalability of training and the consistent improvement of model performance.
- **Desire for Unified, Controllable, and Perceptually Aligned Grading:** The authors are motivated to create a system that can interpret diverse user intentions, whether expressed through natural language or visual references, and translate them into precise, interpretable, and non-destructive color transformations (3D-LUTs). This system should produce visually pleasing and stylistically coherent results that align with human perception and aesthetic standards.

To address these motivations, the research establishes specific technical objectives:
- **High-Fidelity LUT Tokenization:** To enable efficient processing and integration with vision-language models (VLMs), a method for compressing continuous 3D-LUTs into a discrete token representation with high perceptual fidelity is required.
- **Multimodal Conditional Generation:** The system must be capable of generating these LUT tokens conditioned on both visual (reference image) and textual (instructional prompt) inputs.
- **Large-Scale Dataset Development:** To support the training of a VLM for semantic-aware color transformations, a comprehensive dataset comprising diverse LUTs, expert edits, and curated instruction annotations is necessary.
- **Stable and Scalable Aesthetic Alignment:** To overcome the instability of adversarial training, a robust learning strategy is needed that can align model outputs with aesthetic and perceptual preferences in a controllable and scalable manner, ideally through a phased approach that includes pre-training and post-training with reinforcement learning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

AceTone performs color grading by directly generating discrete 3D-LUT tokens that represent color transformations. The framework is designed for compatibility and efficiency with Vision-Language Models (VLMs), supporting multimodal conditioning from both text prompts and reference images. The overall approach involves three main stages: LUT tokenization, next LUT token prediction (generative pretraining), and post-training (supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning).

#### 3.1. LUT Tokenization

To enable a compact and discrete representation of 3D-LUTs, the authors designed a 3D Vector Quantized Variational Autoencoder (VQ-VAE).
- **Compression:** A continuous 3x32x32x32 LUT (L) is processed by a cascaded learnable 3D convolutional encoder. This encoder progressively downsamples the LUT to a latent tensor.
- **Discretization:** The latent features are then discretized using a vector-quantization layer, yielding a sequence of 64 discrete tokens (z_L) for each LUT. The codebook for quantization consists of K=256 entries.
- **Reconstruction:** During decoding, these quantized indices are embedded back into continuous vectors and upsampled through three stages of learnable 3D convolutions to reconstruct the LUT (ˆL).
- **Training Objective:** The tokenizer is optimized using a combination of a voxel-wise mean squared error (MSE) reconstruction loss (L_rec = ||L - ˆL||²) and a vector-quantization commitment loss (L_commit). The commitment loss constrains encoder outputs to remain close to their quantized counterparts, with codebook entries updated via exponential moving averages. Data augmentations, including random gamma correction, contrast, exposure jitter, and smoothed Gaussian noise, are applied to LUT vectors to enhance robustness.

#### 3.2. Next LUT Token Prediction (Generative Pretraining)

This phase adapts a VLM to generate valid LUT representations autoregressively.
- **Data:** The model is exposed to a large number of image-LUT pairs, representing color-transfer tasks. These pairs consist of an original image (I), its graded counterpart (L(I)), and a generic text prompt (c). The LUT (L) is quantized into a sequence of discrete tokens (z_L).
- **Objective:** The VLM learns to predict the LUT tokens (z_t) sequentially, conditioned on the preceding tokens (z_<t), the original image (I), the toned image (L(I)), and the text prompt (c). This objective aligns the VLM's generative capacity with the structured color transformation space. The VLM is initialized from Qwen2.5-VL-3B, with its vocabulary extended to include LUT token entries. The visual encoder is frozen, while the MLP connector and language model are fine-tuned.

#### 3.3. Post-training

This stage refines the VLM's capabilities and aligns its outputs with human preferences.

- **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** The model is fine-tuned for specific realistic use cases:
 - **Photorealistic Style Transfer (PST):** Paired data (I1, I2, L, ϕ, c) are constructed, where I1 and I2 are sampled images, L is a sampled LUT, and ϕ is a low-intensity color perturbation for augmentation. The model predicts z_L based on a reference image (L(ϕ(I2))) and a perturbed query image (ϕ(I1)).
 - **Instruction-Guided Grading (IGG):** An instruction dataset (I, L, c_L) is used, where c_L is a textual instruction describing the desired color adjustment. These instructions are generated using the Qwen2.5-VL-32B model by comparing ungraded and graded images. The model predicts z_L conditioned on the query image (I) and the textual instruction (c_L).
 - The model is jointly optimized using the objectives for PST and IGG.

- **Reinforcing AceTone with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO):** To ensure that generated results are aesthetically pleasing and faithful to human intent, a reinforcement learning (RL) stage is introduced.
 - **Reward Functions:**
 - **Color similarity (r_color):** Measures the perceptual color consistency between the predicted graded image (L_pred(I)) and the ground-truth image (I_gt) using the CIEDE2000 ∆E metric. The reward is inversely proportional to ∆E, peaking when ∆E is below a threshold.
 - **Aesthetic quality (r_aes):** Evaluates the visual appeal of the graded image using a pretrained aesthetic assessment model (DeQA), providing a continuous score scaled to.
 - **GRPO Algorithm:** GRPO is employed to optimize the policy. It samples a group of G responses (o_i) from the current policy, computes composite rewards (r_i = r_color + r_aes) for each, and then calculates an advantage (A_i) based on the deviation of each reward from the group mean. The policy is updated to maximize a reward component (R(θ)) while regularizing against the reference policy (π_ref) using a KL divergence term.

#### 3.4. Dataset

The AceTone-800K dataset was constructed to support large-scale training.
- **LUT Libraries:**
 - **Filter Library:** Approximately 10,000 licensed.cube LUT filters, resampled to 3x32x32x32, used for general LUT representation learning.
 - **Expert Library:** Around 34,000 LUTs derived from professional Adobe Lightroom templates in the PPR-10K dataset, used for learning subtle artistic adjustments.
 - **Fuse Library:** A compact set of 8,192 clustered LUTs, formed by applying PCA and K-means to the combined Filter and Expert libraries, serving as the basis for instruction annotation and evaluation.
- **Image Datasets:** MS-COCO, Adobe-5K, and PPR-10K are used as primary image sources.
- **Dataset Curation for Stages:**
 - **Tokenizer Training:** Exclusively on the filter library.
 - **Generative Pretraining:** MS-COCO images randomly paired with 64 LUTs from the filter library.
 - **Supervised Fine-tuning:** Adobe-5K and PPR-10K images used. For PST, LUTs are sampled from the fuse library. For IGG, 300K image-LUT pairs have instructions generated by Qwen2.5-VL-32B, resulting in ~800K annotated tuples after quality filtering.
 - **Benchmarks:** AceTone-Bench[Transfer] (1024 samples) and AceTone-Bench[Instruct] (128 high-quality human-checked examples) are created for evaluation.
 - **Reinforcement Learning:** 30K (c, I, I_gt) pairs are sampled from SFT data for PST and IGG tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of AceTone involved quantitative comparisons, qualitative visualizations, and ablation studies, demonstrating its performance across both text-guided and reference-guided grading tasks.

#### 4.1. Tokenizer Evaluation

The 3D VQ-VAE tokenizer achieved an average PSNR of 37.5 dB on a held-out set of 1024 test LUTs. When original and reconstructed LUTs were applied to natural images, the resulting CIEDE2000 ∆E was 1.38, indicating a reconstruction difference that is perceptually minimal. The tokenizer achieved a compression ratio of 99.98% while maintaining a low color error, outperforming a continuous alternative which had a ∆E of 3.11 with a 99.87% compression ratio. The tokenizer is compact, comprising approximately 4 million parameters.

#### 4.2. Quantitative Comparison

- **Photorealistic Style Transfer (PST):** AceTone demonstrated performance improvements over existing methods (Neural Preset, WCT2, ModFlow, SA-LUT) on both the established PST-50 benchmark and the newly proposed AceTone-Bench[Transfer] dataset (Table 2). For instance, on PST-50, AceTone achieved an LPIPS of 0.09, which is approximately a 40% reduction compared to Neural Preset's 0.15. It also showed higher aesthetic scores (3.29 vs. Neural Preset's 3.03) and PSNR, alongside lower ∆E. The evaluation on PST-50 was conducted in a zero-shot manner.
- **Instruction-Guided Grading (IGG):** On the AceTone-Bench[Instruct] dataset, AceTone outperformed diffusion-based models (InstructPix2Pix, Qwen-Image-Edit) and agent-based models (JarvisArt) (Table 3). It achieved a higher aesthetic score (3.54 vs. Qwen-Image-Edit's 3.19), a lower LPIPS (0.22 vs. Qwen-Image-Edit's 0.33), and a better average rank (1.43 vs. Qwen-Image-Edit's 2.37), as assessed by the Gemini-2.5-Pro preference evaluator. Inference latency for AceTone was approximately 1 second per request, which can be further reduced to under 300 ms with vLLM integration.

#### 4.3. Qualitative Results

Qualitative examples (Figure 4) illustrated AceTone's ability to capture color styles from reference images in PST tasks while avoiding common artifacts such as color banding, over-saturation, and unnatural hue shifts. In IGG tasks, AceTone consistently adhered to user-specified textual instructions, producing visually coherent and aesthetically balanced color adjustments while preserving fine structural details and tonal consistency.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Reinforcement Learning Effectiveness:** Ablation studies confirmed that the reinforcement learning (RL) stage, specifically using GRPO, led to consistent performance improvements (e.g., further reduction in ∆E) even after the supervised fine-tuning (SFT) stage had reached saturation (Figure 5). This indicated the effectiveness of reward-driven optimization in refining the model's behavior. Furthermore, training with a more diverse LUT pool (8,000 LUTs) for RL resulted in better generalization compared to a smaller pool (2,000 LUTs).
- **Reward Design:** The combination of color similarity (∆E) and aesthetic quality rewards was found to be sufficient for RL optimization (Table 4). Including additional metric-aligned rewards such as PSNR or LPIPS did not yield further performance gains.

#### 4.5. User Study

A user preference study involving 20 participants indicated that AceTone's results were consistently favored for their stylistic coherence and faithful interpretation of prompts in both PST and IGG tasks (Table 5). AceTone received a higher average preference percentage (41.4% for PST, 46.9% for IGG) and a lower average rank compared to competing methods.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

AceTone represents a notable advancement in conditional image grading by introducing a generative system that integrates language and color. Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

#### 5.1. Novelty and Technical Contributions

- **First Multimodal Conditioned Color Grading Framework:** AceTone is presented as the first approach that supports multimodal conditioned color grading within a unified framework, generating 3D-LUTs directly from text prompts or reference images. This addresses the fragmented nature of previous solutions.
- **High-Fidelity LUT Tokenization:** The development of a 3D VQ-VAE-based tokenizer for 3D-LUTs provides a compact and perceptually accurate discrete representation of color transformations, which is a foundational element for integrating LUTs with VLMs.
- **Stable and Scalable Learning Strategy:** The phased training paradigm, involving generative pretraining, supervised fine-tuning, and reinforcement learning with GRPO, offers a robust and scalable method for aligning model outputs with aesthetic and perceptual preferences, mitigating issues associated with traditional adversarial training.
- **Comprehensive Dataset and Benchmarks:** The creation of the large-scale AceTone-800K dataset and accompanying AceTone-Bench benchmarks for both transfer and instruction-guided grading provides valuable resources for future research in semantic-aware color transformation tasks.

#### 5.2. Performance and Generalization

AceTone demonstrates state-of-the-art performance across quantitative metrics (PSNR, LPIPS, ∆E) and qualitative evaluations for both photorealistic style transfer and instruction-guided grading. It surpasses existing methods, including diffusion-based and agent-based models, in achieving visually pleasing and stylistically coherent results, as confirmed by both objective measures and subjective human preference studies. The model's ability to perform zero-shot on unseen styles highlights its generalization capabilities.

#### 5.3. Practical Applications

The capabilities of AceTone suggest several practical applications:
- **Universal Color Assistant:** As a multimodal prompt-driven grading system, AceTone could serve as a versatile tool in various visual content creation and editing platforms, enabling users to achieve complex tonal adjustments through intuitive text prompts or reference images.
- **Seamless Integration into Post-production Workflows:** Its LUT-based output ensures non-destructive color adjustments, allowing for seamless integration into professional post-production software and mobile editing applications.
- **Data Generation and Augmentation:** AceTone can function as a data generator, producing high-quality, controllable color transformations to augment datasets for tasks such as aesthetic learning, domain adaptation, or relighting.

#### 5.4. Future Research Directions

The work opens new avenues for research, including interactive grading systems that provide more nuanced control, modeling color dynamics in video, and broader investigations into computational aesthetics.

#### 5.5. Limitations

The paper acknowledges several limitations:
- Global LUTs may not fully capture local lighting variations or texture-dependent color shifts, leading to potential failure modes in scenes with extreme illumination or highly stylized content.
- The VLM's perception of color might have inherent flaws, potentially resulting in less informative or inaccurate annotations for instruction-guided grading.
- Ambiguity in culturally nuanced or abstract textual instructions (e.g., "nostalgic tone") could lead to misinterpretations by the model.
- The user study, while indicating preferences, was distributed online without enforced display calibration or vision screening for participants, which could introduce variability in subjective evaluations.
