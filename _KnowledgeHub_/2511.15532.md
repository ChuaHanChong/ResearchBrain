# NMPC-based Motion Planning with Adaptive Weighting for Dynamic Object Interception

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed and experimentally validated a real-time Nonlinear Model Predictive Control (NMPC) framework for cooperative manipulation, enabling two standard robots to intercept fast-moving objects in mid-air using a jointly held catcher. The Adaptive-Terminal (AT) NMPC formulation dynamically adjusts cost weights to regularize control effort, significantly reducing actuator power limit violations while maintaining real-time performance and achieving a 37.1% success rate in real-world catching tasks.

## Problem

- Catching fast-moving objects with cooperative, closed-chain manipulator systems presents significant challenges in real-time coordination, workspace characterization, and optimal pose selection.
- Aggressive maneuvers in stiff closed-chain systems often lead to actuator power limit violations in commercial robots, which are computationally expensive to model explicitly in real-time NMPC.
- Existing research largely focuses on single-arm systems or specialized end-effectors, neglecting the complexities of using standard, commercial-off-the-shelf manipulators with general-purpose grippers for dynamic interception.

## Method

- An integrated perception-prediction-planning-control pipeline was developed, using Kalman-filtered projectile motion predictions and a novel **NMPC-based** motion planner.
- The core solution is an Adaptive-Terminal (AT) NMPC formulation that dynamically modulates terminal and stage cost weights based on state error, promoting smoother trajectories and mitigating actuator power limit violations.
- The framework employs a simplified kinematic double integrator model for real-time tractability and explicitly enforces the rigid closed-chain constraint of cooperatively held manipulators.

## Results

- The AT-MPC framework achieved a **37.1%** success rate (13 out of 35 throws) in real-world dynamic object interception experiments with two Franka Emika Panda manipulators, with only **8.6%** of attempts aborted due to estimated power limit violations.
- Simulation analysis demonstrated that the AT mode required significantly lower average control effort compared to the Primitive-Terminal (PT) baseline, indicating reduced likelihood of power limit violations and smoother motion.
- The NMPC planner consistently met real-time constraints, with an average computation time of **19.21** ms and worst-case times within the 40 ms sampling interval, even with the adaptive weighting scheme.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dynamically adjusting NMPC cost weights based on state error (Adaptive-Terminal formulation) effectively regularizes control effort and generates smoother joint trajectories, implicitly addressing complex dynamic constraints like power limits without explicit modeling.
> - Aggressive terminal cost weighting in NMPC can lead to rapid convergence but often results in actuator power limit violations and overshoot in stiff closed-chain systems.
> - Real-time cooperative dynamic object interception with standard commercial manipulators and general-purpose grippers is feasible by adopting a tool-based, bimanual grasping strategy and carefully designed NMPC, despite the inherent closed-chain challenges.
