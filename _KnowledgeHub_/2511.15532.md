# NMPC-based Motion Planning with Adaptive Weighting for Dynamic Object Interception

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed and experimentally validated a real-time Nonlinear Model Predictive Control (NMPC) framework for cooperative manipulation, enabling two standard robots to intercept fast-moving objects in mid-air using a jointly held catcher. The Adaptive-Terminal (AT) NMPC formulation dynamically adjusts cost weights to regularize control effort, significantly reducing actuator power limit violations while maintaining real-time performance and achieving a 37.1% success rate in real-world catching tasks.

## Problem

- Catching fast-moving objects with cooperative, closed-chain manipulator systems presents significant challenges in real-time coordination, workspace characterization, and optimal pose selection.
- Aggressive maneuvers in stiff closed-chain systems often lead to actuator power limit violations in commercial robots, which are computationally expensive to model explicitly in real-time NMPC.
- Existing research largely focuses on single-arm systems or specialized end-effectors, neglecting the complexities of using standard, commercial-off-the-shelf manipulators with general-purpose grippers for dynamic interception.

## Method

- An integrated perception-prediction-planning-control pipeline was developed, using Kalman-filtered projectile motion predictions and a novel **NMPC-based** motion planner.
- The core solution is an Adaptive-Terminal (AT) NMPC formulation that dynamically modulates terminal and stage cost weights based on state error, promoting smoother trajectories and mitigating actuator power limit violations.
- The framework employs a simplified kinematic double integrator model for real-time tractability and explicitly enforces the rigid closed-chain constraint of cooperatively held manipulators.

## Results

- The AT-MPC framework achieved a **37.1%** success rate (13 out of 35 throws) in real-world dynamic object interception experiments with two Franka Emika Panda manipulators, with only **8.6%** of attempts aborted due to estimated power limit violations.
- Simulation analysis demonstrated that the AT mode required significantly lower average control effort compared to the Primitive-Terminal (PT) baseline, indicating reduced likelihood of power limit violations and smoother motion.
- The NMPC planner consistently met real-time constraints, with an average computation time of **19.21** ms and worst-case times within the 40 ms sampling interval, even with the adaptive weighting scheme.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dynamically adjusting NMPC cost weights based on state error (Adaptive-Terminal formulation) effectively regularizes control effort and generates smoother joint trajectories, implicitly addressing complex dynamic constraints like power limits without explicit modeling.
> - Aggressive terminal cost weighting in NMPC can lead to rapid convergence but often results in actuator power limit violations and overshoot in stiff closed-chain systems.
> - Real-time cooperative dynamic object interception with standard commercial manipulators and general-purpose grippers is feasible by adopting a tool-based, bimanual grasping strategy and carefully designed NMPC, despite the inherent closed-chain challenges.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research on robotic catching of fast-moving objects in flight represents a benchmark problem for robotic agility and coordination, demanding real-time performance across perception, prediction, planning, and control. Traditionally, this domain has been structured around a four-stage pipeline: object trajectory prediction, interception point selection, motion planning, and motion control. While single-arm manipulators have been extensively studied within this framework, the field is progressing towards more complex platforms, including mobile manipulators and humanoids, which necessitate whole-body coordination.

Current methodological approaches are broadly categorized into model-based optimal control and data-driven learning paradigms. Learning-based methods, such as imitation learning and reinforcement learning, offer capabilities to adapt to complex dynamics and real-world nuances. However, these often require extensive training data and may exhibit reduced robustness when encountering scenarios significantly outside their training distribution. Model Predictive Control (MPC), a prominent model-based approach, offers advantages in real-time performance, zero-shot adaptation, and explicit constraint handling, though it is sensitive to modeling errors. Recent comparative studies have indicated that while learning agents can achieve high peak performance, MPC-based agents often demonstrate greater robustness to varied or out-of-distribution tasks.

A significant gap in much of the existing research is the predominant focus on single-arm systems, frequently employing specialized end-effectors designed for catching. This work differentiates itself by addressing the less explored problem of dynamic object interception using standard, commercial-off-the-shelf (COTS) manipulators equipped with their original, general-purpose grippers. To achieve a secure catch with such grippers, a tool-based strategy is adopted, where the manipulators cooperatively grasp a separate passive catcher (e.g., a container). This bimanual, tool-based approach, while enabling robust "cradle-like" interception with standard hardware, fundamentally transforms the control problem by introducing a rigid closed-chain constraint. This closed-chain configuration presents specific challenges that render traditional single-arm pipelines suboptimal, including the complex characterization of the cooperative task space, the difficulty of optimal catching pose selection given combined kinematic redundancy, and the stringent coordination requirements for motion planning and execution. Aggressive maneuvers in such stiff, closed-chain systems often lead to actuator power limit violations, a critical issue not easily addressed by incorporating full dynamic constraints into real-time NMPC.

This paper positions itself as a contribution to the advancement of multi-robot coordination and dynamic object manipulation, specifically by developing an NMPC framework that directly addresses the challenges posed by cooperative, closed-chain manipulator systems using standard hardware. It aims to bridge the gap between agility and robust, dynamically feasible execution for next-generation collaborative and humanoid robots.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop and experimentally validate a real-time motion planning framework for dynamic object interception using cooperative manipulator systems under closed-chain constraints. This framework aims to enable two standard robotic manipulators, jointly holding a catcher, to intercept fast-moving objects in mid-air.

The motivation for this work stems from several key challenges and emerging needs:

1. **Bridging the Gap to Human-like Agility and Coordination:** Catching fast-moving objects is a complex task requiring rapid perception, prediction, planning, and control, representing a canonical benchmark for robotic agility. Extending this capability to cooperative multi-manipulator systems is considered a necessary step towards foundational, human-like intelligent behaviors for next-generation humanoid and collaborative robots.
2. **Addressing Challenges of Cooperative Closed-Chain Manipulation:**
 - **Enhanced Interception Geometries and Robustness:** A cooperative setup with a bimanual grasp allows for richer interception geometries and more secure "cradle-like" grasps, potentially enhancing robustness and safety compared to single-arm approaches.
 - **Control Complexity:** Cooperative manipulation introduces significant control challenges, including closed-chain geometry, inter-arm clearance, and kinematic redundancy. Traditional methods for single-arm systems are often inadequate for this coupled, constrained environment.
 - **Workspace Characterization and Optimal Pose Selection:** Characterizing the reachable workspace for a jointly held object in a closed-chain configuration is complex, and selecting an optimal catching pose *a priori* becomes intractable due to the numerous joint configurations that can realize a target pose with vastly different dynamic implications.
3. **Enabling Use of Commercial-Off-The-Shelf (COTS) Hardware:** Most existing robotic catching research employs specialized, custom-designed end-effectors. This work is motivated by the objective of using standard, general-purpose grippers on COTS manipulators. This necessitates a tool-based, bimanual grasping strategy, which inherently creates a rigid closed-chain constraint, further complicating the control problem.
4. **Mitigating Actuator Power Limit Violations:** Aggressive interception maneuvers, especially in stiff closed-chain systems, can demand significant instantaneous power. This frequently triggers power limit violations in commercial collaborative robots, leading to task abortion. Explicitly modeling such complex dynamic constraints (like power limits) within real-time NMPC is computationally prohibitive. Therefore, a key motivation is to develop a planning approach that implicitly addresses these limitations without explicit dynamic constraint modeling.
5. **Achieving Real-Time Performance:** Dynamic interception necessitates that the entire perception-prediction-planning-control loop operates in real-time under tight timing constraints. The planner must be capable of continuous re-planning to adapt to the non-deterministic nature of thrown object trajectories.

To address these motivations, the paper proposes a Nonlinear Model Predictive Control (NMPC) planner integrated into a holistic perception-prediction-planning-control pipeline. A core objective is the introduction of an **Adaptive-Terminal (AT) formulation** that employs a cost shaping approach to dynamically modulate pose tracking cost weights based on state error. This is specifically designed to regularize control effort, promote smoother joint velocities, and mitigate the actuator power limit violations frequently encountered with aggressive, fixed-weight approaches (Primitive-Terminal or PT) in such dynamically demanding, closed-chain systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology for dynamic object interception utilizes an integrated perception-prediction-planning-control pipeline, structured hierarchically to manage complexity and achieve real-time performance.

#### 3.1. System Architecture

The system comprises three main stages:
1. **Ball Trajectory Estimation and Target Point Selection:** Processes visual input to predict the ball's trajectory and identify a kinematically feasible interception pose.
2. **Nonlinear Model Predictive Control (NMPC) Planner:** The core component, generating coordinated joint trajectories for the cooperative manipulators.
3. **Low-Level Control Layer:** Executes the planned trajectories in real-time using joint PD controllers.

#### 3.2. Ball Trajectory Estimation and Target Point Selection

- **Perception:** RGB-D images are processed by a YOLOv8-based object detector to obtain initial 3D position estimates of the ball. This stage is designed for robustness against high-speed motion.
- **Prediction:** Time-stamped, potentially noisy, 3D position measurements are fed into a Kalman filter. This filter uses a discrete-time projectile motion model (including gravity and process noise) to provide smoothed estimates of the ball’s full state (position and velocity). These estimates serve as initial conditions for predicting the ball's future flight trajectory.
- **Target Point Selection:** From the predicted trajectory, an algorithm identifies a feasible interception pose for the catcher. This involves filtering candidate points within a predefined safe workspace envelope and selecting the point that minimizes the required average velocity for interception, balancing responsiveness with kinematic feasibility. The desired catcher orientation is also determined, often keeping the catcher opening upwards and aligned with the predicted impact direction.

#### 3.3. Motion Planning (NMPC)

The motion planner operates based on a simplified kinematic model to ensure real-time tractability, assuming a low-level controller can accurately track reference accelerations and that the container dynamics are negligible.

- **System Model:** A double integrator model is used for the overall system's joint dynamics: $\ddot{q}(t) = u(t)$, where $u(t) \in \mathbb{R}^{14}$ represents joint accelerations for the two 7-DoF manipulators.
- **Closed-Chain Constraint:** The two manipulators rigidly hold a catcher, forming a closed kinematic chain. This constraint, $h_o(q_l(t)) = h_o(q_r(t))$, is explicitly enforced within the NMPC formulation. This equality couples the configurations of both arms, ensuring kinematic consistency and defining the feasible manifold for system operation.
- **Constraints:** The NMPC problem is subject to:
 - **System Dynamics:** The discretized double integrator model.
 - **Actuator Limits:** Joint position, velocity, and acceleration limits ($q_{min} \le q(k) \le q_{max}$, $\dot{q}_{min} \le \dot{q}(k) \le \dot{q}_{max}$, $u_{min} \le u(k) \le u_{max}$).
 - **Workspace and Self-Collision Constraints:** Motion must remain within a predefined safe workspace, and minimum distance constraints between links are enforced.

- **Primitive MPC Formulation (PT):**
 - This baseline formulation prioritizes high-precision interception.
 - **Objective Function:** $J_{PT} = \|p_o(N)-p_{tg}\|^2_{P_e} + \|\phi_o(N)-\phi_{tg}\|^2_{O_e} + \sum_{k=0}^{N-1} (\|u(k)\|^2_R + \|\dot{q}(k)\|^2_W)$.
 - It uses heavily weighted terminal costs ($P_e, O_e$) to aggressively minimize the final pose error, combined with running costs ($R, W$) to penalize joint velocities and accelerations for smoothness.
 - **Limitation:** The fixed, large terminal weights can lead to aggressive accelerations and potential power limit violations in a stiff closed-chain system, as it does not explicitly consider dynamic constraints like torque or power limits.

- **Adaptive-Terminal MPC Formulation (AT):**
 - To enhance dynamic feasibility and mitigate power limit violations, the AT formulation introduces a cost shaping approach. It adaptively modulates both terminal and stage cost weights based on state error.
 - **Objective Function:** $J_{AT} = \|p_o(N)-p_{tg}\|^2_{P_{adp_e}} + \|\phi_o(N)-\phi_{tg}\|^2_{O_{adp_e}} + \sum_{k=0}^{N-1} (\|p_o(k) - p_{tg}\|^2_{Q_{adp_{pos}}(k)} + \|\phi_o(k) - \phi_{tg}\|^2_{Q_{adp_{ori}}(k)}) + \sum_{k=0}^{N-1} (\|u(k)\|^2_R + \|\dot{q}(k)\|^2_W)$.
 - **Adaptive Law:** A shared adaptive law, $W(e; W_{min}, W_{max}, \epsilon) = W_{min} + (W_{max} - W_{min}) \cdot \frac{e}{e+\epsilon}$, is used. Here, $e$ is the position or orientation error, $[W_{min}, W_{max}]$ defines the modulation range, and $\epsilon$ controls sensitivity.
 - **Stage Weights ($Q_{adp_{pos}}(k)$, $Q_{adp_{ori}}(k)$):** These are updated at each prediction step $k$ within the horizon, based on the predicted error $e(k)$ at that step.
 - **Terminal Weights ($P_{adp_e}$, $O_{adp_e}$):** These are determined once at the beginning of each MPC cycle, based on the initial state error $e(0)$ of the current cycle, and remain constant throughout that prediction horizon.
 - **Mechanism:** Terminal weights use a wider modulation range. Large initial errors lead to dominant terminal costs for aggressive convergence. As the system approaches the target (small errors), terminal weights decrease, increasing the relative influence of running costs and stage error penalization. This shifts the focus towards smoother motion during the final approach, improving feasibility.

#### 3.4. Implementation Details

- The MPC planner is formulated in CasADi and solved by IPOPT with the MA57 solver. Code generation is used for performance.
- The system utilizes ROS 2 Humble and ros2 control for dual-arm coordination.
- The MPC operates at 25 Hz (40 ms sampling time), providing joint references to 1 kHz low-level PD controllers on the physical hardware.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents experimental validation on a physical platform and comparative analysis in simulation to assess the performance of the proposed Adaptive-Terminal (AT) MPC framework against a Primitive-Terminal (PT) baseline.

#### 4.1. Real-World Catching Demonstration

- **Experimental Setup:** Two 7-DoF Franka Emika Panda manipulators cooperatively held a custom 3D-printed container. An extrinsically calibrated Intel RealSense D455 camera served as the perception system.
- **Success Rate (AT-MPC):** Out of 35 consecutive real-world throws, the AT-MPC framework successfully intercepted 13 throws, yielding a 37.1% success rate. Three attempts (8.6%) were aborted due to estimated actuator power limit violations, and other failures were attributed to camera perception instability or throws outside the safe interception zone.
- **Trajectory Tracking:** A representative successful catch using AT mode demonstrated effective tracking. Minimal deviation was observed between the MPC-generated reference and the trajectory executed by the low-level PD controllers in 3D space, indicating effective low-level control performance.
- **Closed-Chain Constraint Satisfaction:** The Euclidean distance between the two end-effector grasping points, which theoretically should remain constant due to the rigid grasp, showed minimal deviation. The average deviation was 2.8 mm, with a worst-case of 6.1 mm, primarily attributed to contact compliance and low-level tracking synchronization effects rather than planner constraint violations.
- **Real-time Performance:** The average MPC cycle computation time was measured at 19.21 ms. The worst-case computation time also remained within the 40 ms sampling interval, demonstrating the framework's real-time feasibility for dynamic tasks.

#### 4.2. Comparative Analysis of MPC Modes (AT vs. PT) in Simulation

- **Motivation for Simulation:** Real-world trials using the PT mode frequently triggered actuator power limit violations, leading to task abortion. The AT mode exhibited fewer such instances. A simulation-based comparison was conducted to isolate planner performance from hardware noise and external factors, using 500 trials with randomized start and target poses.
- **Control Effort:** Control effort, defined as the sum of squared joint accelerations over the planned trajectory, served as a proxy for energy consumption and likelihood of power limit violations. Results showed that the **AT mode required significantly lower average control effort** compared to the PT mode for both manipulators (Fig. 8). This supports the hypothesis that AT's adaptive weighting produces smoother acceleration profiles, moderating peak actuator demands.
- **Trajectory Smoothness and Overshoot:** Qualitative comparison of Cartesian X-trajectory profiles (Fig. 9) demonstrated that PT mode and an aggressively tuned AT mode (approximating PT) exhibited rapid convergence but with considerable overshoot. In contrast, balanced or smoother AT tunings resulted in more gradual approaches with significantly reduced overshoot. This indicates the AT formulation's flexibility in trading off convergence speed for trajectory smoothness via its adaptive parameters. The AT Smooth configuration, with near-zero adaptive terminal weights, specifically minimized terminal cost influence, prioritizing smoothness.
- **Computational Efficiency:** The AT formulation introduced minimal computational overhead. Its average computation time was only marginally higher than PT's, and worst-case times were comparable (39 ms for AT vs. 38 ms for PT). Both modes consistently met the 40 ms real-time constraint across all 500 simulated scenarios (Fig. 10, Fig. 11).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework for cooperative dynamic object interception, making several contributions and demonstrating significant potential impact:

1. **Advancement in Cooperative Robotic Manipulation:** The work addresses the complex problem of dynamic interception using two cooperative manipulators forming a closed kinematic chain. This configuration is inherently more challenging than single-arm systems but offers benefits such as enhanced grasping stability and versatility, particularly when using standard, general-purpose grippers.
2. **Mitigation of Dynamic Feasibility Challenges:** The introduction of the Adaptive-Terminal (AT) NMPC formulation provides a practical solution to balance aggressive task execution with intrinsic system limitations. By dynamically adjusting cost weights based on error, AT effectively regularizes control effort and generates smoother trajectories, which is shown to significantly mitigate actuator power limit violations that are common in demanding closed-chain tasks with kinematically planned systems. This implicitly addresses dynamic constraints without computationally expensive explicit modeling.
3. **Real-time Performance for Agile Tasks:** The demonstrated average computation time of 19.21 ms, significantly below the 40 ms sampling interval, establishes the framework's suitability for real-time, agile, and safety-critical cooperative tasks. This computational efficiency is maintained even with the added complexity of the adaptive weighting scheme.
4. **Enabling Standard Hardware Usage:** By facilitating robust "cradle-like" interception with commercial-off-the-shelf manipulators and their original grippers (via a cooperatively held tool), the research contributes to making advanced robotic capabilities more accessible and deployable without requiring specialized hardware modifications.
5. **Foundational for Future Collaborative Robots:** The ability to perform non-contact transfer methods, such as dynamic catching with coordinated manipulators, aligns with Industry 5.0 principles. This capability supports human workers and represents a step towards more intelligent and adaptable collaborative robots, particularly those designed for human-robot interaction or tasks requiring dexterous, multi-point manipulation.
6. **Improved Robustness:** The comparative analysis indicates that the AT approach offers increased robustness to execution challenges (e.g., power limits) compared to fixed-weight terminal cost methods, while retaining competitive real-time performance. This suggests more reliable operation in varied and dynamic environments.

The paper lays a foundation for future research, particularly in integrating learning-augmented hierarchical frameworks to further enhance robustness and success rates by predicting optimal interception poses directly from observations, potentially leading to improved overall system performance in dynamic cooperative tasks.
