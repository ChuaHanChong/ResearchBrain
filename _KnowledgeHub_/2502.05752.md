# PINGS: Gaussian Splatting Meets Distance Fields within a Point-Based Implicit Neural Map

> [!summary] Summary
> The Center for Robotics at the University of Bonn developed PINGS, a LiDAR-visual SLAM system that incrementally builds high-fidelity digital twins of large-scale environments by unifying Signed Distance Fields and Gaussian Splatting radiance fields. This method achieved superior novel view rendering quality, enhanced surface reconstruction, and improved SLAM localization accuracy while operating online in complex urban settings.

## Problem

- Existing robotic mapping methods either provided robust geometry (e.g., occupancy grids, SDFs) but lacked photorealism, or offered photorealistic rendering (e.g., NeRF, 3DGS) but with compromised geometric accuracy.
- High-fidelity radiance field techniques typically struggled with the scalability, incremental updates, and online operation required for autonomous robots in large, unbounded environments.
- A critical challenge was developing a unified environmental map that is both geometrically precise and visually photorealistic for robust robot perception and downstream tasks.

## Method

- **PINGS** integrates a continuous ==Signed Distance Field (SDF)== and a ==Gaussian Splatting (3DGS)== radiance field into a unified, point-based implicit neural map.
- The system employs ==Gaussian Surfels==, a variant of 3DGS, anchored by neural points to promote geometrically accurate depth and normal rendering.
- A joint optimization process includes a geometric consistency loss, enforcing alignment between SDF zero-level surfaces and gradients, and Gaussian surfel centers and normals.

## Results

- PINGS consistently achieved the best novel view rendering quality with higher PSNR and SSIM, and lower LPIPS and Depth-L1, across **five outdoor sequences**, demonstrating robust generalization.
- The system attained superior surface reconstruction, evidenced by the lowest Chamfer Distance and highest F-score (**0.1m** threshold) on the Oxford Spires dataset, surpassing dedicated geometric mapping methods.
- Localization accuracy improved, with PINGS yielding the lowest average relative and absolute trajectory errors on long in-house car sequences compared to other advanced SLAM systems.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Geometric consistency constraints between the SDF and radiance field lead to mutual improvements, allowing each modality to regularize and refine the other's representation.
> - The use of an elastic point-based implicit neural map enables compact storage, efficient incremental updates, and global consistency corrections via loop closures across large environments.
> - Gaussian Surfels, which flatten 3D Gaussian ellipsoids into 2D disks, are effective in producing more geometrically accurate depth and normal maps within a radiance field framework.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

PINGS addresses a critical challenge in robotics: creating high-fidelity environmental maps that are *both* geometrically accurate and photorealistic, suitable for large-scale, incremental, and online operation on autonomous robots. This area has historically been bifurcated, with different representations excelling in one aspect but failing in the other.

**Traditional Geometric Mapping:** For decades, robotics has relied on explicit map representations like occupancy grids, point clouds, surfels, or voxel grids (e.g., TSDF, VDB-Fusion) derived from range sensors like LiDAR or depth cameras. These methods provide robust geometry for tasks such as localization, collision avoidance, and path planning. More recently, implicit neural representations for geometry, such as Signed Distance Fields (SDFs) or occupancy fields, have gained traction for their compactness and continuous nature. PIN-SLAM, a direct predecessor to PINGS, successfully demonstrated a scalable, point-based implicit neural SDF for LiDAR SLAM, achieving globally consistent geometric maps. However, these geometric-centric maps inherently lack the photorealism needed for advanced applications like human-robot interaction, teleoperation, or high-fidelity simulation.

**Photorealistic Radiance Field Mapping:** The advent of Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) and, more recently, 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) revolutionized novel view synthesis, offering unprecedented photorealism. 3DGS, in particular, has seen rapid adoption due to its superior rendering speed compared to NeRF, making it more appealing for real-time robotic applications. These radiance fields are crucial for tasks demanding dense photometric information. However, they typically suffer from two major limitations in a robotic context: (1) **Geometric inaccuracy:** While visually stunning, their underlying geometry is often ambiguous or less precise than dedicated geometric maps, leading to issues in tasks like localization or planning. (2) **Scalability and Online Operation:** Most high-fidelity radiance field methods assume offline processing of well-curated, bounded scene datasets, struggling with the incremental, large-scale, and potentially unbounded environments encountered by mobile robots. Efforts to scale NeRF/3DGS (e.g., BlockNeRF, SiLVR, hierarchical 3DGS) often involve complex offline pre-processing, limiting their online applicability. While some incremental NeRF/3DGS SLAM systems exist, they are mostly confined to smaller, indoor scenes.

**Bridging the Gap:** PINGS directly addresses this void by proposing a novel, unified map representation. It acknowledges the strengths of both LiDAR-based geometric mapping and camera-based photorealistic rendering and, crucially, leverages their mutual consistency to overcome individual weaknesses. By integrating 3DGS (specifically, the geometrically more accurate Gaussian Surfels) into the point-based implicit neural framework of PIN-SLAM, PINGS aims to achieve both accurate SDFs and high-fidelity radiance fields simultaneously, incrementally, and consistently at a large scale. This represents a significant advancement in robot perception, moving towards a truly holistic environmental understanding.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of PINGS is to develop a robust LiDAR-visual SLAM system that can construct high-fidelity "digital twins" of large-scale environments, embodying both precise geometric accuracy and compelling photorealism. The authors highlight that "Robots benefit from high-fidelity reconstructions of their environment, which should be geometrically accurate and photorealistic to support downstream tasks." This core motivation underpins several specific objectives:

1. **Unified Map Representation:** The primary aim is to integrate two distinct, yet complementary, environmental representations – a continuous Signed Distance Field (SDF) and a Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) radiance field – into a single, cohesive, and compact data structure. This unification is key to avoiding redundant storage and enabling synergistic learning.

2. **Mutual Enhancement through Consistency:** A central objective is to leverage the strengths of each modality to improve the other. The geometric accuracy provided by LiDAR-derived SDFs should constrain and regularize the radiance field, reducing geometric ambiguities inherent in vision-only approaches. Conversely, the dense photometric cues and multi-view consistency from camera data and the radiance field should refine the SDF, especially in areas with sparse LiDAR measurements. The paper explicitly states: "By enforcing geometric consistency between these fields, we achieve mutual improvements by exploiting both modalities."

3. **Scalable and Incremental Mapping:** The system must be capable of operating online, incrementally building the map as the robot moves through large, unbounded environments (e.g., urban driving scenes). This involves efficient data structures and training strategies that prevent catastrophic forgetting and allow for continuous map expansion.

4. **Global Consistency with Loop Closures:** For large-scale applications, global consistency is paramount to prevent cumulative drift. PINGS aims to maintain a globally consistent map representation through loop closure detection and pose graph optimization, ensuring that revisited areas are accurately registered and map errors are corrected.

5. **Compact and Elastic Representation:** The chosen point-based implicit neural map representation is motivated by the need for compactness, efficient storage, and "elasticity" – the ability to deform and correct the map structure easily upon loop closure detection. This is crucial for real-world robotic systems with memory and computational constraints.

6. **Improved Downstream Performance:** Ultimately, the enhanced map representation should translate into tangible benefits for robotic applications, specifically:
 - Superior photorealistic and geometric rendering quality at novel views.
 - More accurate surface mesh reconstruction from the SDF.
 - Improved odometry estimation and global localization accuracy.

In essence, PINGS seeks to overcome the limitations of single-modality or decoupled multi-modality mapping systems by presenting a tightly integrated, mutually-supervised approach that achieves the best of both worlds for autonomous navigation and perception in complex, large-scale environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PINGS is designed as a LiDAR-visual SLAM system that incrementally builds a unified map of both Signed Distance Fields (SDFs) and Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) radiance fields. Its core innovation lies in the elastic point-based implicit neural map representation and the joint optimization with geometric consistency constraints.

**A. Point-Based Implicit Neural Map Representation:**
The map `M` is a collection of `N` neural points, `m_i`, each defined by:
- **Position (`x_i`) and Orientation (`q_i`):** In the world frame.
- **Geometric Feature Vector (`f_g_i`):** Encodes information for SDF and geometric attributes of Gaussian primitives.
- **Appearance Feature Vector (`f_a_i`):** Encodes information for color of Gaussian primitives.
- **Timestamps (`τ_c_i`, `τ_u_i`):** For tracking creation and last update, aiding in active status determination and associating points with LiDAR poses for map manipulation.
A voxel hashing structure `V` (with resolution `v_p`) is used for efficient neural point indexing and neighbor searches. New neural points are initialized from incoming LiDAR points if no active point exists in their corresponding voxel. A local map `M_l` within a radius `r_l` of the robot is maintained for efficient optimization and odometry estimation.

**B. Neural Signed Distance Field (SDF):**
Following PIN-SLAM, PINGS models a continuous SDF:
- **SDF Prediction:** For any query point `p`, its SDF value `S(p)` is predicted by a globally shared SDF decoder `D_d` that takes the geometric feature `f_g_j` and the relative coordinate `d_j` from `k`-nearest neural points `m_j`. These individual predictions `s_j` are then blended using inverse-distance weighting.
- **SDF Training:** The SDF is optimized using sampled points along LiDAR rays, providing pseudo-SDF labels. The loss `L_sdf` combines a binary cross-entropy term `L_bce` (for soft supervision of SDF values) and an Eikonal loss `L_eik` (to regularize SDF gradients to unit length).

**C. Neural Gaussian Splatting Radiance Field:**
PINGS uses camera images to construct a radiance field:
- **Gaussian Spawning:** Neural points act as anchors. Each neural point `m` (within the camera frustum) spawns `K` Gaussian primitives. Globally shared MLP decoders (`D_o`, `D_r`, `D_s`, `D_α`, `D_c`) map the neural point's features (`f_g`, `f_a`) to the Gaussian parameters (position, rotation, scale, opacity, color).
 - **Geometric Attributes (from `f_g`):** Position, rotation, and scale of Gaussians are predicted based on `f_g`. A key distinction is the use of **Gaussian Surfels**, which flatten 3D Gaussian ellipsoids into 2D disks (`s_z = 0`), promoting more geometrically accurate depth and normal rendering. The opacity decoder `D_α` also uses `f_g` and view distance `δ_v` for adaptive density.
 - **Appearance Attributes (from `f_a`):** Color is predicted by `D_c` based on `f_a` and the view direction.
- **Gaussian Splatting Rasterization:** Standard tile-based differentiable rasterization projects the spawned Gaussian surfels onto the 2D image plane to render RGB images, depth maps, and normal maps.
- **Radiance Field Training:** The `L_gs` loss combines:
 - `L_photo`: Photometric rendering loss (L1 and SSIM) against reference RGB images.
 - `L_depth`: Depth rendering loss (L1) against sparse depth maps projected from LiDAR.
 - `L_area`: Area regularization to minimize overlap of surfels.
 - **Pose Refinement:** To account for imperfect LiDAR odometry and sensor synchronization, camera poses and per-frame exposure parameters are jointly optimized during radiance field training.

**D. Joint Optimization with Geometric Consistency:**
This is the core of PINGS's mutual enhancement strategy:
- **Consistency Loss (`L_cons`):** Enforces alignment between the SDF's zero-level surface and gradients, and the Gaussian surfels' centers and normals.
 - `L_d_cons`: Samples points along surfel normals and pushes their SDF values to match the sample offset, effectively aligning surfel centers with the SDF zero-level.
 - `L_v_cons`: Aligns the SDF gradients at these sampled points with the surfel normals, ensuring consistent surface orientation.
- **Total Loss:** `L = L_sdf + L_gs + L_cons`. This combined loss is backpropagated to optimize neural point features, decoder parameters, camera poses, and exposure parameters.

**E. PINGS LiDAR-Visual SLAM System:**
PINGS operates by alternating between two main steps:
1. **Mapping:** Incremental joint learning of the local neural point map `M_l` (SDF and radiance field) using training pools `Q_p` (SDF samples) and `Q_i` (images). Decoder parameters are frozen after an initial convergence period (30 timesteps) to prevent catastrophic forgetting.
2. **Localization:** LiDAR odometry is estimated by aligning new scans to the learned SDF's zero-level set using Gauss-Newton optimization. Initial camera poses are derived from LiDAR odometry and refined during radiance field optimization.
- **Global Consistency:** Loop closures are detected using the local neural point map, followed by pose graph optimization. Neural points are then "elastically" transformed with their associated LiDAR frames, ensuring global map consistency for both SDF and radiance fields.
- **Dynamic Object Filtering:** Neural points likely representing dynamic objects (based on large SDF values in free space) are identified, and Gaussian spawning for them is disabled to prevent dynamic artifacts in the static map.

This integrated approach ensures that the geometric and photometric aspects of the map are not only represented but also mutually constrained and refined, leading to a more accurate and comprehensive environmental model.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

PINGS was extensively evaluated on challenging large-scale datasets, including self-collected in-house car datasets and the Oxford Spires dataset, to validate its core claims.

**1. Superior Novel View Rendering Quality (Claim i):**
- **Quantitative Results (Table I):** PINGS consistently achieved the best PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS (lower is better), and Depth-L1 (lower is better) metrics across five diverse outdoor sequences. This superiority held for both *in-sequence* and critically important *out-of-sequence* testing views, demonstrating robust generalization to truly novel viewpoints.
- **Qualitative Results (Figure 5):** PINGS produced noticeably smoother normal maps and visually more photorealistic RGB renderings compared to baselines (3DGS, GSS, Neural Point+3DGS, Neural Point+GSS), particularly in areas with challenging geometry like curbs and sidewalks.
- **Analysis:** The improvements were attributed to: (i) the adoption of Gaussian Surfels (GSS) over standard 3DGS, (ii) the neural point-based spawning mechanism providing better optimization control and reducing "floating" Gaussians, and most significantly, (iii) the geometric consistency loss `L_cons` which enforced surface alignment between surfels and the SDF.

**2. Enhanced Surface Reconstruction Quality and SDF Accuracy (Claim ii):**
- **Quantitative Results (Table II):** PINGS demonstrated superior performance in surface reconstruction on the Oxford Spires dataset, achieving the lowest Chamfer Distance and highest F-score (with a 0.1m threshold) among all compared methods, including offline vision-based (OpenMVS, Nerfacto) and online LiDAR-based (VDB-Fusion, PIN-SLAM) techniques.
- **Analysis:** PINGS consistently outperformed PIN-SLAM, its direct SDF-only predecessor, even when using the same neural point resolution. This directly validates that the "leveraging dense photometric cues and multi-view consistency from the radiance field" (via `L_gs` and `L_cons`) leads to a "more accurate signed distance field."

**3. Improved SLAM Localization Accuracy (Claim ii & iii):**
- **Quantitative Results (Table III):** PINGS achieved the lowest average relative translation error (ARTE) and absolute trajectory error (ATE) on two long in-house car sequences, outperforming state-of-the-art LiDAR odometry (F-LOAM, KISS-ICP, PIN-odometry) and SLAM systems (SuMa, MULLS, PIN-SLAM).
- **Analysis:** The enhanced accuracy of the SDF, resulting from the joint optimization with the radiance field and geometric consistency, directly translated into more precise LiDAR odometry and improved relocalization during loop closures. This demonstrates that better geometric mapping contributes to better localization.

**4. Large-Scale Globally Consistent Mapping with Loop Closures (Claim iii):**
- **Demonstration (Figure 1, Figure 7):** PINGS successfully built a globally consistent map over a challenging 5 km urban sequence involving approximately 10,000 LiDAR scans and 40,000 images. The figures visually demonstrate the efficacy of loop closure correction, showing how it rectifies accumulated odometry drift and eliminates visual artifacts (e.g., duplicate objects, misplaced trees) that arise without correction. This highlights the "elasticity of neural points" as a key enabler for large-scale consistency.

**5. Compact and Memory-Efficient Map Representation (Claim iv):**
- **Memory vs. Quality Trade-off (Figure 8):** PINGS proved more memory-efficient than competing methods. For equivalent memory usage, PINGS achieved better novel view rendering quality (lower LPIPS compared to GSS) and better surface reconstruction accuracy (lower Chamfer distance compared to VDB-Fusion).
- **Analysis:** This efficiency stems from its architecture: globally shared MLP decoders learn common patterns, while locally defined neural points compactly encode multiple Gaussian primitives and continuous SDF values through feature vectors, rather than explicitly storing individual primitives or discrete voxels. This allows PINGS to represent both fields efficiently within a single map.

In summary, the experimental evaluation strongly supports all four key claims, demonstrating PINGS's ability to create high-quality, geometrically accurate, and photorealistic maps for large-scale robotic applications with improved rendering, reconstruction, localization, and memory efficiency.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

PINGS represents a significant advancement in the field of robotic perception and environmental mapping, effectively bridging the long-standing gap between geometrically accurate representations and photorealistic rendering. Its impact can be discussed across several dimensions:

**1. Holistic Environmental Understanding for Robotics:**
The most profound impact of PINGS is enabling robots to construct a unified, high-fidelity "digital twin" of their surroundings. This means robots can now simultaneously perceive their environment with both centimeter-level geometric precision (for navigation, collision avoidance, and manipulation) and photorealistic visual fidelity (for human-robot interaction, teleoperation, and detailed scene understanding). This holistic understanding is crucial for the next generation of autonomous systems operating in complex, human-centric environments.

**2. Mutual Enhancement Paradigm:**
PINGS pioneers a robust framework for mutual supervision between LiDAR-derived distance fields and camera-derived radiance fields. This synergistic approach, where geometry constrains appearance and appearance refines geometry, is a powerful paradigm. It effectively addresses the inherent weaknesses of each modality when used in isolation (e.g., LiDAR's lack of texture, vision's geometric ambiguities), leading to a more reliable and accurate overall map. This mutual enhancement could inspire similar integration strategies in other multi-modal perception systems.

**3. Scalability and Online Operation for Real-World Robotics:**
By demonstrating incremental, online, and globally consistent mapping in large-scale urban environments (e.g., 5 km driving sequences), PINGS overcomes a major hurdle for deploying high-fidelity mapping systems on mobile robots. Previous radiance field methods were often limited to offline processing or smaller scenes. PINGS's ability to handle extensive, unbounded environments with loop closure correction makes it highly relevant for autonomous driving, large-area inspection, and urban robotics.

**4. Compact and Elastic Map Representation:**
The neural point-based implicit representation proves to be highly memory-efficient compared to explicit voxel grids or large collections of 3D Gaussians. Its "elasticity," inherited from PIN-SLAM, allows for efficient global consistency correction upon loop closures, which is critical for long-term mapping without accumulating drift. This efficiency and flexibility make the approach more practical for resource-constrained robotic platforms.

**5. Impact on Downstream Robotic Tasks:**
The benefits of PINGS directly translate into improved performance for numerous robotic tasks:
- **Navigation & Planning:** More accurate SDFs lead to safer and more efficient path planning and collision avoidance.
- **Simulation & Training:** High-fidelity photorealistic and geometrically accurate maps serve as superior world models for training reinforcement learning agents or for closed-loop autonomous driving simulations.
- **Human-Robot Interaction & Teleoperation:** Photorealistic rendering allows for more intuitive and immersive teleoperation experiences and richer visual feedback for human users.
- **Visual Localization:** The detailed radiance field provides rich visual cues that can enhance visual localization systems.
- **Asset Creation:** PINGS can efficiently generate high-quality 3D models (meshes and texture) of real-world environments, serving as a powerful tool for digital twin creation in various industries.

**6. Future Research Directions:**
While significant, the authors acknowledge limitations such as the current processing time (5 seconds per frame), the lack of pre-trained priors, and limited explicit 4D modeling for dynamic objects. These limitations, however, also highlight clear avenues for future research, such as integrating efficient optimization schemes, learning generalizable priors, and developing sophisticated dynamic object handling capabilities, all building upon the strong foundation laid by PINGS.

In conclusion, PINGS represents a major step towards truly versatile and robust environmental perception for autonomous robots, offering a unified, high-fidelity, and scalable solution that synergistically combines the best of geometric and photorealistic mapping. The open-source release further amplifies its potential impact, fostering wider adoption and accelerating research in multi-modal robotic mapping.
