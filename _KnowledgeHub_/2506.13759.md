# Discrete Diffusion in Large Language and Multimodal Models: A Survey

> [!summary] Summary
> This survey provides a systematic overview of Discrete Diffusion Language Models (dLLMs) and Multimodal Language Models (dMLLMs), detailing their mathematical foundations, modeling innovations, and diverse applications. It highlights how these models achieve up to 10 times faster inference and offer enhanced control compared to traditional autoregressive models, establishing them as a promising paradigm for future generative AI.

## Problem

- Autoregressive (AR) models suffer from slow sequential inference, preventing parallel generation of output tokens.
- AR models exhibit limited fine-grained control over output attributes, often requiring complex prompt engineering.
- The static, one-pass perception of input contexts in AR models hinders dynamic, task-aware comprehension.

## Method

- ==Discrete diffusion models== reframe generation as an ==iterative denoising process== over discrete token sequences, enabling parallel decoding for faster inference.
- These models provide enhanced controllability over output properties by treating generation as a denoising or infilling task.
- ==Bidirectional attention== in discrete diffusion models allows for continuous revision of context perception throughout the generation process.

## Results

- The survey systematically consolidates fragmented research on discrete diffusion models, tracing their evolution and categorizing their mathematical frameworks, architectures, and techniques.
- It details sophisticated training paradigms, such as diverse masking strategies, reweighting techniques, distillation, and reinforcement learning, alongside innovative inference methods like unmasking, remasking, and various guidance mechanisms (up to **10x** faster inference).
- The work identifies critical future research directions, including the development of robust infrastructure, further optimization of inference efficiency, and improved security and privacy safeguards tailored for d(M)LLMs.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Discrete diffusion models offer a viable alternative to autoregressive models, providing up to 10x faster inference speeds and improved control over generation.
> - The field is built on diverse mathematical foundations, including D3PMs, simplified masked diffusion, and continuous-time frameworks, each offering distinct approaches to the denoising process.
> - Discrete diffusion models have rapidly scaled and diversified across language and multimodal tasks, showcasing competitive performance and expanding applications in areas like reasoning, robotics, and drug discovery.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This survey is positioned at a crucial juncture in the development of artificial intelligence, specifically within the domain of large generative models. For several years, Large Language Models (LLMs) and Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have been predominantly built upon the autoregressive (AR) paradigm. AR models, which generate output tokens sequentially from left to right, have achieved remarkable success across diverse tasks, demonstrating capabilities that often rival or surpass human performance. However, as the paper highlights, this dominant AR paradigm inherently faces limitations:
- **Sequential Inference:** The left-to-right decoding process prevents parallel generation, leading to slower inference speeds, particularly for long sequences.
- **Limited Control:** Fine-grained control over output attributes like length, format, or specific structural elements remains challenging with AR models, often requiring complex prompt engineering or multi-round interactions.
- **Static Perception:** Causal attention restricts AR models to a one-pass, static perception of input contexts, making dynamic, task-aware comprehension difficult without costly chain-of-thought or iterative prompting.

Against this backdrop, Discrete Diffusion Large Language Models (dLLMs) and Discrete Diffusion Multimodal Large Language Models (dMLLMs) have emerged as a compelling alternative. Unlike AR models, discrete diffusion models frame generation as an iterative denoising process over discrete token sequences. This fundamental difference enables several advantages:
- **Parallel Decoding:** Multiple tokens can be generated simultaneously in each denoising step, significantly accelerating inference.
- **Enhanced Controllability:** By treating generation as a denoising or infilling task, d(M)LLMs offer precise control over output properties, facilitating structured generation.
- **Dynamic Perception:** Bidirectional attention allows continuous revision of context perception throughout the generation process, overcoming the static limitations of AR models.

The paper notes the growing presence of both industrial-scale proprietary d(M)LLMs (e.g., Mercury, Gemini Diffusion) and numerous open-source academic models (e.g., LLaDA, DREAM). These models have demonstrated performance comparable to AR counterparts while achieving up to 10× acceleration in inference speed. This survey, therefore, serves as a timely and critical resource for navigating this burgeoning field, systematically consolidating the fragmented research and highlighting the potential of discrete diffusion as a powerful alternative to traditional autoregressive approaches for building intelligent systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this paper is to provide a "systematic survey of Discrete Diffusion Language Models (dLLMs) and Discrete Diffusion Multimodal Language Models (dMLLMs)." This broad objective is driven by several key motivations stemming from the limitations of autoregressive models and the burgeoning potential of discrete diffusion models:

The core motivations for this survey are:
- **Addressing AR Limitations:** To draw attention to and systematically analyze a class of models (dLLMs/dMLLMs) that intrinsically overcome the limitations of the dominant autoregressive paradigm, particularly in terms of parallel inference, fine-grained control, and dynamic perception.
- **Consolidating Rapid Advances:** The rapid emergence of both industrial and open-source discrete diffusion models, demonstrating competitive performance and significant inference speedups, necessitates a comprehensive overview to guide future research and development.
- **Highlighting a Promising Alternative:** To position discrete diffusion models as a viable and highly promising alternative for next-generation large language and multimodal intelligence.

To achieve this overarching objective, the survey sets forth several specific aims:
- **Historical Tracing:** To document the historical evolution and foundational developments of dLLMs and dMLLMs.
- **Mathematical Formalization:** To formalize the underlying mathematical frameworks that govern discrete diffusion processes.
- **Methodological Cataloging:** To list and categorize commonly-used modeling methods and representative models within the discrete diffusion paradigm.
- **Technique Analysis:** To analyze key techniques employed across the entire lifecycle of these models, including training, inference, and quantization.
- **Trustworthy Issues Discussion:** To address the critical considerations of trustworthiness, encompassing privacy and safety aspects specific to dLLMs.
- **Application Summarization:** To summarize the diverse and emerging applications of discrete diffusion models across various domains, including language, vision-language, and biology.
- **Future Direction Discussion:** To identify and discuss potential future research directions and deployment opportunities, thereby guiding further innovation in the field.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

As a comprehensive survey paper, the methodology employed by the authors is a systematic and structured review of existing literature on discrete diffusion models, particularly their application to large language and multimodal contexts. The authors did not present novel experimental results or introduce new models, but rather synthesized, categorized, and critically analyzed the current state of research.

The approach taken involves:
- **Systematic Literature Review:** The authors gathered a wide range of relevant papers, from foundational works on discrete diffusion to recent advancements in scaling these models to LLM and MLLM sizes. The inclusion of both academic open-source models and references to industrial proprietary models (like Mercury and Gemini Diffusion) demonstrates a broad coverage of the field.
- **Categorical Organization:** The core of the methodology is a hierarchical organization of the reviewed literature. The paper is structured into distinct sections that progressively delve into different aspects of d(M)LLMs:
 - **Foundational Principles:** Beginning with the mathematical underpinnings (discrete diffusion models, transition matrices, continuous-time frameworks, concrete scores, discrete flow matching).
 - **Architectural and Modeling Innovations:** Discussing specific techniques used to adapt diffusion principles to language modeling (e.g., Block Diffusion, Flexible-Length Masking).
 - **Model Taxonomy:** Providing a timeline and categorization of representative models based on their scale and modality (1B-parameter models, large dLLMs, large dMLLMs, and unified models). This includes highlighting key contributions of each model family.
 - **Lifecycle Analysis:** A detailed breakdown of techniques used during different phases:
 - **Training:** Initialization strategies, masking schedules (uniform, token-wise, block-wise), reweighting, distillation, and reinforcement learning methods.
 - **Inference:** Unmasking and remasking techniques, prefilling and caching, guidance mechanisms (classifier-free, classifier, reward, energy-based), various sampling techniques, context length extension, and sparse computation.
 - **Post-Training Considerations:** Quantization and trustworthy issues like privacy and safety.
 - **Application Domains:** Summarizing the diverse real-world applications across various scientific and engineering disciplines.
 - **Future Outlook:** Concluding with identified challenges and potential future research directions.
- **Conceptual Unification:** The survey attempts to formalize different mathematical frameworks (e.g., D3PM, simplified masked diffusion, continuous time, concrete score, discrete flow matching) and highlight their interconnections and differences.
- **Comparative Analysis:** While not explicit experimental comparisons, the survey implicitly compares d(M)LLMs to AR models by constantly referring to the advantages of the former over the latter, such as parallel decoding speed and improved controllability.
- **Visual Aids:** The inclusion of a timeline (Fig. 1) and an overview diagram (Fig. 2) visually reinforces the systematic organization and historical development, making the complex landscape more accessible. Figure 3 provides a detailed visual comparison between AR and discrete diffusion models across architecture, attention, inference, and training. Figure 8, which plots publication trends, underscores the growing research interest, providing quantitative evidence for the timeliness of the survey.

By adopting this comprehensive and structured approach, the authors effectively synthesize a vast body of literature, providing a coherent and accessible resource for researchers, developers, and practitioners interested in discrete diffusion models for language and multimodal tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

As a survey paper, its "findings and results" are the comprehensive synthesis, categorization, and critical analysis of the existing research landscape rather than novel experimental outcomes. The main findings presented in this survey can be summarized as follows:

- **Emergence and Viability of Discrete Diffusion:** The survey firmly establishes discrete diffusion models (dLLMs and dMLLMs) as a strong, viable, and often superior alternative to the dominant autoregressive paradigm. They offer intrinsic advantages like parallel decoding (up to 10x faster inference), fine-grained output control, and dynamic context perception, while demonstrating performance comparable to their AR counterparts.
- **Diverse Mathematical Foundations:** The field is built upon several foundational mathematical frameworks, including Discrete Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (D3PMs), simplified masked diffusion models (which have become predominant), continuous-time discrete denoising models, concrete score matching, and discrete flow matching. Each framework offers unique properties and approaches to the denoising process.
- **Evolving Modeling Techniques:** Beyond the core mathematical formulations, researchers have introduced various modeling techniques to enhance flexibility and capabilities. These include block diffusion models (hybrid AR/diffusion), flexible-length masked diffusion (allowing variable-length generation), partial masking (decomposing tokens into sub-tokens for finer control), and diffusion with optimal transport position coupling (jointly denoising token values and positions).
- **Rapid Model Scaling and Diversification:**
 - **Early Models:** Initial efforts focused on models around 1 billion parameters, establishing feasibility on discrete data.
 - **Large dLLMs:** The field quickly scaled to large models like LLaDA, DREAM, DiffuGPT/DiffuLLaMA, TESS 2, and Seed Diffusion, demonstrating zero-shot generation, in-context learning, and reasoning capabilities, often outperforming AR models in planning-intensive tasks.
 - **Large dMLLMs:** Discrete diffusion extended to multimodal models with works like Dimple, LaViDa, and LLaDA-V, showcasing competitive performance in vision-language tasks through hybrid training and multimodal alignment.
 - **Unified Models:** Recent efforts like MMaDA, FUDOKI, and Muddit are developing single, unified diffusion architectures capable of handling multiple modalities (text and images) within a shared probabilistic framework.
- **Sophisticated Training Paradigms:** To address challenges like low corpus utilization and high variance, several advanced training techniques have emerged:
 - **Initialization:** Adapting pretrained BERT or AR models (e.g., GPT, LLaMA, Qwen) to diffusion objectives significantly accelerates training.
 - **Masking Strategies:** Complementary masking (ensuring all tokens are supervised), various masking schedules (linear, cosine, geometric, polynomial, spindle-shaped), and block-wise masking for reasoning tasks.
 - **Optimization:** Reweighting techniques (e.g., MGDM) to prioritize harder tokens, distillation for efficient few-step generation, and specialized reinforcement learning algorithms (UniGRPO, VRPO, SDPO, wd1, DCoLT) adapted for diffusion's unique properties.
- **Innovative Inference Techniques:** A rich set of techniques optimize the iterative denoising process:
 - **Unmasking:** Strategies for selecting and unmasking tokens (random, metric-based like confidence/margin/entropy, confident decoding, block-wise, dilated schedules).
 - **Remasking:** Techniques that allow reintroducing masked tokens to previously unmasked positions, enabling iterative refinement and error correction.
 - **Efficiency:** Adapting prefilling and KV-caching (dKV-Cache, dLLM-Cache, DualCache) for bidirectional attention, and sparse computation methods.
 - **Control:** Guidance techniques (classifier-free, classifier, reward, energy-based) to steer generation, various sampling strategies (temporal self-consistency, particle Gibbs sampling, early stopping), and dynamic response length control.
- **Emergent Security and Privacy Concerns:** dLLMs introduce unique vulnerabilities due to their bidirectional context modeling and parallel decoding. New jailbreak methods (DIJA, PAD) exploit these features, and novel alignment strategies (MOSA) are being developed to mitigate risks, particularly for middle-token manipulation.
- **Broadening Applications:** d(M)LLMs are being successfully applied across a wide spectrum of domains, including:
 - **Language-Related:** Style transfer, text embedding, controllable generation, summarization, poetry generation, text editing.
 - **Knowledge & Reasoning:** Chain-of-thought, commonsense knowledge inference, temporal knowledge graph reasoning, neurosymbolic diffusion.
 - **Vision & Multimodal:** Image enhancement, motion generation, asynchronous video generation.
 - **Robotics & Autonomous Driving:** Vision-language-action policies, multi-robot motion planning.
 - **Graph & Structured Predictions:** Molecular graph generation, node classification, 3D structure generation.
 - **Biological & Drug Discovery:** Molecular editing, protein generation, therapeutic peptide design.

In essence, the survey concludes that discrete diffusion models have rapidly matured from theoretical concepts to practical, high-performing systems that challenge the long-standing dominance of autoregressive models, demonstrating significant potential across numerous AI applications.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This survey paper holds significant importance for the scientific community and the broader field of artificial intelligence, particularly in its capacity to shape future research and development in generative models.

- **Consolidating a Rapidly Evolving Field:** The most immediate impact of this survey is its comprehensive and systematic consolidation of a fast-growing research area. Discrete diffusion models, while demonstrating immense promise, have seen fragmented development across various sub-fields. By tracing historical developments, formalizing mathematical frameworks, categorizing models and techniques, and summarizing applications, the survey provides a much-needed coherent overview. This makes it an indispensable resource for new researchers entering the field and for experienced practitioners seeking to understand the state-of-the-art.
- **Validating Discrete Diffusion as a Key Paradigm:** The paper's detailed exposition reinforces the argument that discrete diffusion models are not merely a niche area but a "promising alternative" to autoregressive models. By explicitly contrasting the advantages of d(M)LLMs—parallel decoding, fine-grained control, dynamic perception, and inference speed—against the limitations of AR models, the survey validates their fundamental utility and encourages wider adoption and exploration.
- **Accelerating Research and Development:** By clearly outlining key objectives, methodologies, findings, and, crucially, future directions (Appendix F), the survey acts as a roadmap for future research. It highlights critical challenges such as the need for robust infrastructure, further improvements in inference efficiency, and the development of stronger security and privacy safeguards. This structured identification of gaps will undoubtedly accelerate targeted research efforts.
 - **Infrastructure Development:** The call for standardized training frameworks, modular components, and openly available pretrained models is crucial for fostering a collaborative ecosystem, akin to what exists for AR LLMs.
 - **Inference Optimization:** Continued focus on optimizing sampling techniques, integrating efficient attention mechanisms, and exploring advanced quantization methods will be vital for scaling d(M)LLMs for real-world deployment.
 - **Trustworthy AI:** The identified security and privacy vulnerabilities, such as emergent jailbreak methods and data leakage risks, underscore an urgent need for research into robust alignment strategies and content moderation specific to the bidirectional nature of diffusion models.
- **Cross-Domain Fertilization:** The broad range of applications summarized in the survey—from natural language generation and reasoning to robotics, drug discovery, and image generation—demonstrates the versatility of discrete diffusion. This cross-domain perspective can inspire researchers from different fields to leverage d(M)LLMs for their specific problems, leading to innovative interdisciplinary solutions.
- **Educational Value:** For students and educators, the survey serves as an excellent pedagogical tool, offering a structured understanding of a complex and rapidly evolving technology. It distills dense technical information into digestible sections, making it easier to grasp the core concepts and advancements.

In conclusion, this survey is more than just a summary of existing work; it is a foundational document that validates the importance of discrete diffusion models, provides a comprehensive intellectual framework for the field, and explicitly charts a course for future innovation. Its impact will likely be seen in the accelerated development of dLLMs and dMLLMs, leading to more efficient, controllable, and versatile generative AI systems that push the boundaries of current capabilities.
