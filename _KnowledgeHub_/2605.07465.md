# SEIF: Self-Evolving Reinforcement Learning for Instruction Following

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Fudan University and Ant Group developed SEIF, a self-evolving reinforcement learning framework that continuously improves large language models' instruction-following capabilities on open-ended tasks. This framework adaptively generates challenging instructions and provides fine-grained rewards, achieving consistent performance gains across various models and benchmarks.

## Problem

- Existing self-play methods for instruction following often rely on instructions with static difficulty, limiting continuous advancement as model capabilities evolve.
- Ensuring the quality of self-generated instructions, especially as they become more complex, to avoid conflicting or invalid training data.
- Acquiring reliable, fine-grained reward signals for open-ended instruction following tasks is challenging due to the absence of unique ground-truth answers.

## Method

- **SEIF** introduces a ==self-evolving reinforcement learning== framework that forms a closed loop, consisting of an ==Instructor==, a ==Filter==, a ==Follower==, and a ==Judger==.
- The Instructor generates progressively challenging instructions, while the Follower learns to execute them, with both optimized in an ==alternating training procedure==.
- Auxiliary Filter and Judger roles, instantiated from the latest Follower, ensure instruction quality and provide ==adaptive, constraint-level reward signals== for open-ended tasks.

## Results

- **SEIF** consistently improved instruction-following across five different LLM families (1.5B to 14B parameters), with Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct showing **+4.7** on IFEval, **+4.0** on CFBench, and **+6.6** on WritingBench.
- Ablation studies confirmed that removing the Filter led to performance drops (e.g., **−3.2** on IFEval), and fixed (non-adaptive) Judger/Filter modules also decreased performance, highlighting their criticality.
- The framework successfully preserved or improved general LLM capabilities and dynamically evolved the training instruction distribution, introducing more complex constraint types over iterative turns.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dynamically evolving instruction difficulty is critical for sustained instruction-following improvement, significantly outperforming methods with static instruction sets.
> - Adaptive filtering of generated instructions and fine-grained, constraint-level reward signals are crucial for effective and stable training on open-ended tasks.
> - An optimal self-evolution strategy involves sufficient early-stage training followed by moderate late-stage refinement to achieve the best performance and prevent generalization degradation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Instruction following is a core capability for large language models (LLMs), enabling them to accurately interpret user requirements and generate appropriate responses. Enhancing this ability continuously and efficiently is a significant area of research given the widespread application of LLMs.

Current approaches to improving instruction following generally fall into two categories. The first category relies on external supervision, either through human annotations or feedback from powerful "teacher" models. While effective, these methods are often resource-intensive, costly, and do not scale well, particularly for complex or nuanced instructions.

The second category involves self-play training, where LLMs generate their own training data and learn from self-generated feedback. However, a limitation of many existing self-play methods is their reliance on instructions with static difficulty. As the model's capabilities evolve, the unchanging difficulty of the training instructions can restrict further advancements in the model's performance.

More recently, the self-evolution paradigm has gained traction, particularly in verifiable domains such as mathematical reasoning and code generation. In these fields, automated verifiers can readily provide objective reward signals, facilitating iterative improvement. However, applying self-evolution to open-ended tasks like instruction following presents distinct challenges, primarily due to the absence of unique ground-truth answers and the subjective nature of evaluating response quality.

This work addresses the limitations of existing paradigms by proposing SEIF, a self-evolving framework specifically designed for open-ended instruction following. SEIF aims to establish a continuous improvement loop that dynamically adapts instruction difficulty to the evolving capabilities of the LLM, thereby overcoming the static difficulty issue in traditional self-play and reducing the dependency on costly external supervision for open-ended tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a scalable and efficient method for continuously improving the instruction-following ability of Large Language Models (LLMs) without extensive reliance on external supervision or static training data. The motivation for SEIF stems from three specific challenges identified in current approaches to enhancing instruction following in LLMs:

1. **Instruction Difficulty Evolution:** Existing self-play methods often use a fixed distribution of instructions. As an LLM's instruction-following capability improves, these static instructions may no longer be sufficiently challenging to drive further growth. A key motivation is to design a mechanism that can continuously generate increasingly complex and challenging instructions that adapt to the model's current skill level. This dynamic adaptation is crucial for pushing the model's boundaries and enabling sustained improvement.

2. **Instruction Quality Assurance:** As instructions become more complex through an evolutionary process, there is an increased risk of generating conflicting, ambiguous, or invalid instructions. Such low-quality training data can misguide the learning process and hinder effective model development. Therefore, a significant motivation is to incorporate a robust mechanism to filter out or rectify such problematic instructions, ensuring the quality and consistency of the training dataset throughout the self-evolution process.

3. **Reward Signal Acquisition for Open-Ended Tasks:** Unlike verifiable tasks (e.g., math problems with a definitive correct answer), open-ended instruction following often lacks a clear, single ground-truth answer. This makes it challenging to automatically derive reliable and fine-grained reward signals necessary for reinforcement learning. The research is motivated to develop a method for obtaining meaningful and adaptive reward signals that can accurately assess the degree to which an LLM's response satisfies the constraints of an open-ended instruction, even without external human or strong teacher model input during the self-evolutionary loop.

By addressing these challenges, SEIF aims to create a closed, autonomous self-evolution loop where the difficulty of instructions and the capability of the instruction-following model mutually reinforce each other, leading to continuous enhancement in instruction-following performance.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SEIF (Self-Evolving Reinforcement Learning for Instruction Following) is a self-evolving framework designed to enhance the instruction-following ability of LLMs by forming a closed self-evolution loop. This loop involves the reciprocal improvement of instruction difficulty and model capability. The framework operates through four distinct roles: an Instructor, a Filter, a Follower, and a Judger.

#### 3.1. Overview of the SEIF Framework

The core idea of SEIF is to alternately train an Instructor and a Follower model. Both are initialized from a base LLM. The Instructor's role is to generate progressively challenging instructions tailored to the Follower's current abilities. The Follower then learns to execute these evolved instructions. To support this self-evolution on open-ended tasks, two auxiliary roles, a Filter and a Judger, are introduced. These auxiliary roles are instantiated from the latest Follower model and kept frozen during their respective operational stages, allowing their criteria for quality and evaluation to adapt as the Follower's capabilities advance.

#### 3.2. Roles and Their Functions

- **Instructor (I):** The Instructor's primary function is to take a seed instruction (z) and evolve it into a more complex instruction (x) by adding constraints. It is optimized to generate instructions that the current Follower finds challenging but feasible.
- **Filter (Q):** The Filter ensures the quality of the instructions generated by the Instructor. It checks if an evolved instruction (x) is conflicting or invalid. Invalid or conflicting instructions are removed from the training pipeline. The Filter adapts its criteria by being instantiated from the latest Follower.
- **Follower (F):** The Follower is the core instruction-following LLM that generates responses (y) to the evolved instructions (x). Its objective is to maximize the satisfaction of the constraints within the instructions.
- **Judger (J):** The Judger provides the reward signals for training. For each instruction-response pair (x, y), it evaluates the degree of constraint satisfaction. For an instruction with K constraints, it predicts a binary label (s_k ∈ {0, 1}) for each constraint and calculates a satisfaction rate (A_J(x, y) = (1/K) Σ s_k) as a scalar reward. The Judger also adapts its evaluation criteria by being instantiated from the latest Follower.

#### 3.3. Alternating Training Procedure

SEIF proceeds in iterative rounds, with each round comprising two stages of optimization:

1. **Instructor Optimization (Round T, Stage 1):**
 - The current Follower (F_θ_t), Filter (Q_t), and Judger (J_t) are frozen.
 - The Instructor (I_ψ_t) generates a group of evolved instructions (x_i) from seed instructions (z).
 - For each generated instruction (x_i):
 - The Filter (Q_t) determines if it is valid. If Q_t(x_i) = 0 (invalid/conflicting), the Instructor reward (R_I) is 0.
 - If valid, the frozen Follower (F_θ_t) generates a response (y_i).
 - The frozen Judger (J_t) calculates the constraint satisfaction rate (A_t(x_i, y_i)).
 - The Instructor reward is defined as R_I(z, x_i) = 1 - A_t(x_i, y_i). This reward structure encourages the Instructor to generate instructions that are difficult for the current Follower to satisfy, pushing the Follower toward its capability boundary.
 - The Instructor parameters (ψ) are updated using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) to maximize these rewards, resulting in I_ψ_(t+1).

2. **Follower Optimization (Round T, Stage 2):**
 - The newly updated Instructor (I_ψ_(t+1)), along with the Filter (Q_t) and a Judger (J'_t, instantiated from the *pre-update* Follower F_θ_t), are frozen.
 - The updated Instructor (I_ψ_(t+1)) generates new training instructions (x) from seed instructions (z).
 - The Filter (Q_t) validates these instructions. Invalid instructions are skipped.
 - For valid instructions, the Follower (F_θ_t) samples a group of responses (y_i).
 - The frozen Judger (J'_t) evaluates each response, providing a reward R_F(x, y_i) = A_J'_t(x, y_i). This reward encourages the Follower to generate responses that better satisfy the constraints.
 - The Follower parameters (θ) are updated using GRPO to maximize these rewards, resulting in F_θ_(t+1).

After the Follower update, the next iteration begins by refreshing the Filter and Judger from the latest Follower (F_θ_(t+1)), allowing the filtering and evaluation criteria to dynamically evolve with the model's improved capabilities.

#### 3.4. Reinforcement Learning Optimization (GRPO)

Both Instructor and Follower optimizations employ Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO). GRPO is a policy optimization algorithm that compares multiple candidate outputs sampled from the old policy for a given prompt and normalizes their rewards within that group. This approach eliminates the need for a separate value function. The objective function involves a clipped ratio of new to old policy probabilities and a KL regularization term to maintain policy stability. Advantages (A_i) are computed by standardizing rewards (r_i) within their respective groups. For the Instructor, the prompt is the seed instruction (z) and outputs are evolved instructions (x_i) with R_I rewards. For the Follower, the prompt is the evolved instruction (x) and outputs are responses (y_i) with R_F rewards.

#### 3.5. Data and Evaluation

- **Training Data:** 5120 seed instructions from sources like OpenAssistant Conversations, Super-NaturalInstructions, and Self-Instruct. Constraints are added to these seeds, including both verifiable hard constraints and semantically related soft constraints. Hard constraints are categorized into Lexical, Structural layout, Formatting, Language, and Special pattern types (24 subtypes). Soft constraints comprise 25 types including content, element, semantic, word/sentence/paragraph counts, tone, format, audience-specific, and complex context constraints.
- **Evaluation Benchmarks:** Six instruction-following benchmarks are used: IFEval, CFBench, FollowBench, WritingBench, AgentIF, and Multi-IF, covering constraint satisfaction, multi-turn dialogue, writing, agentic tool-use, and multilingual instruction following. General capabilities are assessed using GPQA-Diamond, MMLU-Pro, BBEH, and AIME.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted across various model scales and architectures yielded several key findings regarding the effectiveness and mechanisms of SEIF.

#### 4.1. Self-Evolving Enhances Instruction-Following

SEIF consistently improved instruction-following capabilities across five different model families, ranging from 1.5 billion to 14 billion parameters, over three iterative training stages. For instance, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct showed substantial gains, with a +4.7 improvement on IFEval, +4.0 on CFBench, and +6.6 on WritingBench. Similarly, Distill-Qwen-14B achieved an IFEval score of 80.0, representing a +5.1 increase. These results indicate that the self-evolving approach is effective in enhancing instruction-following performance, outperforming other instruction-following optimization baselines.

#### 4.2. Benefits for Small Models

The framework demonstrated utility even for smaller models. The Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct model achieved consistent gains through the self-evolution process, with notable improvements on AgentIF (+4.7) and IFEval (+3.9). This suggests that iterative self-training can enhance instruction-following behaviors in small-scale models, indicating that their capabilities are not solely limited by parameter size.

#### 4.3. Criticality of Instruction Difficulty Evolution

Comparison with other baselines, particularly those employing static instruction self-play, highlighted the importance of dynamic instruction difficulty evolution. Methods with static instruction difficulty (e.g., Self-Correct, Humpback, SELF, Self-Rewarding, I-SHEEP, Meta-Rewarding) yielded only marginal improvements over the base model. In contrast, SEIF, with its adaptive instruction difficulty evolution, achieved significantly better performance. For SEIF-7B, it scored 78.6 on IFEval, 51.0 on CFBench, and 59.0 on FollowBench, surpassing the strongest static baseline (Meta-Rewarding) by +2.0 on IFEval. A variant of SEIF without Instructor Evolving showed a notable drop to 75.9 on IFEval, underscoring that the adaptiveness of the training data distribution is critical.

#### 4.4. General Capability Preservation

Evaluations on general benchmarks (GPQA-Diamond, MMLU-Pro, BBEH, AIME) demonstrated that SEIF generally maintains or improves the models' broad general abilities. For example, Distill-Qwen-14B achieved the best average score of 60.3, and Llama-3.1-8B improved from 24.0 to 24.4. This indicates that the specialized self-evolution for instruction following does not compromise the models' general competencies.

#### 4.5. Ablation Study Findings

Ablation studies on SEIF-7B revealed the importance of specific framework components:
- **Filter:** Removing the Filter component ("w/o Filter") led to clear performance drops, such as -3.2 on IFEval and -6.0 on CFBench. This indicates that filtering invalid or conflicting instructions is crucial for maintaining data quality and preventing noisy training signals.
- **Shared Parameters (Adaptive Filter and Judger):** The "w/o Shared Parameters" variant, where the Judger and Filter were fixed rather than refreshed from the latest Follower, resulted in performance decreases across benchmarks. This suggests that adaptive filtering and evaluation criteria, which evolve with the model's capabilities, are important for aligned reward signals.
- **Constraint-Level Reward:** Using instruction-level binary rewards instead of fine-grained constraint-level rewards ("w/o Const.-Level Reward") also led to performance degradation (e.g., -2.6 on IFEval). This highlights the necessity of fine-grained reward signals for open-ended instruction following.

#### 4.6. Optimal Training Iterations

An analysis of the number of training iterations showed that model performance typically improved during the initial few turns and then saturated after approximately three turns. Further training yielded only marginal gains or, in some cases, slight performance degradation, leading to the adoption of three iteration turns in SEIF.

#### 4.7. Analysis of Performance Improvement Sources

Qualitative analysis provided insights into how instruction-following performance improves:
- **Instruction Distribution Evolution:** PCA visualizations of training instruction representations across turns showed noticeable shifts in distribution and centroid positions. This indicates that SEIF continuously updates and diversifies the training data distribution, introducing new variations rather than reusing a static set.
- **Constraint Type Evolution:** The distribution of constraint types evolved, with a decline in simple paragraph-level or sentence-level requirements and an increase in complex, structured, and semantically deep constraint types (e.g., formatting, role-based, audience-specific constraints). This evolution is driven by the Instructor's objective to generate increasingly challenging instructions that the Follower currently fails to satisfy.

#### 4.8. Self-Evolving Training Strategy

Investigation into epoch allocation strategies for SEIF-7B revealed that an asymmetric schedule, specifically "sufficient early-stage training followed by moderate late-stage training" (e.g., E1=3, E2=1, E3=1 epochs), achieved the best overall performance. Uniform schedules or late-intensive schedules were generally inferior. Excessive late-stage training was observed to potentially hurt generalization, while intensive early training provided a solid foundational capability, which was then moderately refined in later stages. Reward dynamics analysis supported this, showing higher and steadier reward growth for early-intensive strategies.

#### 4.9. Judger and Filter Reliability

Human agreement analysis on VerInstruct demonstrated the reliability of the self-instantiated Filter and Judger. The Filter achieved stable accuracy (0.79-0.80) and F1 (0.78-0.80) with human annotations across iterations. The Judger showed reasonable agreement with human judgments (Accuracy 0.73-0.74, F1 0.70-0.72). Minimal fluctuations across turns suggested no significant reliability degradation from self-instantiation. Further, an ablation study confirmed that refreshing both modules from the latest Follower yielded the best performance. Blind pairwise human evaluation confirmed that SEIF outputs were preferred over BASE and other training baselines, providing direct evidence of improved final instruction-following quality.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The proposed SEIF framework presents a novel approach to improving the instruction-following capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs) by introducing a self-evolving paradigm for open-ended tasks. This work offers several significant contributions and potential impacts:

1. **Reduced Reliance on External Supervision:** By establishing a closed self-evolution loop, SEIF significantly reduces the long-term dependence on costly human annotations or strong external teacher models during the continuous improvement process. This is particularly impactful for open-ended instruction following, where ground-truth answers are often ambiguous or non-existent, making traditional supervised learning or human feedback acquisition challenging and expensive.
2. **Enhanced Scalability and Autonomy:** The self-evolving nature of SEIF offers a path towards more scalable and autonomous post-training methods for LLMs. Models can continuously generate their own challenges, monitor their performance, and refine their instruction-following policies without constant human intervention. This fosters greater independence in model development and iteration.
3. **Adaptive Learning and Curriculum Generation:** SEIF's dynamic adjustment of instruction difficulty, driven by the Instructor's co-evolution with the Follower, represents an adaptive curriculum learning approach. This mechanism ensures that the training data remains appropriately challenging as the model improves, pushing the boundaries of its capabilities more effectively than static instruction sets.
4. **Guidance for Open-Ended Self-Evolution:** The detailed analyses, particularly regarding the optimal training strategy ("sufficient early-stage training, followed by moderate late-stage training"), provide practical guidance for future research in self-evolving systems for open-ended tasks. This understanding of reward dynamics and epoch allocation is valuable for designing more effective and stable self-improvement loops.
5. **Broader Applicability and Future Research:** The paradigm introduced by SEIF may extend beyond instruction following to other complex, open-ended tasks where objective ground truth is scarce. It suggests a general framework for adaptive AI systems that can improve through iterative cycles of data generation, quality control, reward modeling, and policy optimization. This could benefit research in areas such as model alignment, lifelong learning, and autonomous capability development.
6. **Community Engagement:** The public availability of the code and data associated with SEIF facilitates reproducibility, encourages further experimentation, and allows the broader research community to build upon this self-evolving framework.

While acknowledging that real-world user instructions can be substantially more complex than the current training data, SEIF's demonstrated effectiveness on benchmarks like AgentIF suggests its potential to handle intricate instruction-following scenarios. Future work could explore evolving even more realistic and complex instructions to further enhance models' abilities in diverse real-world applications.
