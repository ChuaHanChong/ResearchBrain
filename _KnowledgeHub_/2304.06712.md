# What does CLIP know about a red circle? Visual prompt engineering for VLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the Visual Geometry Group (VGG) at the University of Oxford explore "visual prompt engineering" for Vision-Language Models (VLMs), demonstrating that simple red circles can effectively steer a model's attention while preserving global context. This method achieves state-of-the-art zero-shot performance in referring expression comprehension tasks and, critically, reveals how such visual prompts can trigger latent biases within VLMs.

## Problem

- Adapting frozen Vision-Language Models (VLMs) like CLIP for fine-grained localization tasks (e.g., referring expression comprehension, keypoint localization) without requiring extensive fine-tuning.
- Traditional image transformation methods like cropping often remove valuable contextual information, which can be suboptimal for VLMs that benefit from holistic scene understanding.
- Understanding the emergent properties and potential biases embedded within large-scale VLMs trained on vast, uncurated internet data.

## Method

- Introduces =="visual prompt engineering"== as an alternative to image cropping, where ==graphical annotations (e.g., red circles)== are directly overlaid onto image pixels.
- Frames various computer vision problems as a question-answering task, where the VLM scores transformed image inputs (with marks) against textual queries.
- Empirically determined that a ==red circle== is the most effective visual marker for directing VLM attention across different tasks and VLM backbones.

## Results

- Red circle visual prompting nearly **doubled** the accuracy of cropping-based methods in the "Naming Keypoints" task.
- The approach achieved state-of-the-art zero-shot performance in referring expression comprehension on most RefCOCO benchmarks, without task-specific fine-tuning.
- Applying red circles to human images increased the probability of VLMs classifying individuals into negative or criminal categories by **17%** to over **32%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Visual marking, especially simple red circles, effectively directs VLM attention for localization tasks, outperforming traditional image cropping by preserving global context.
> - This capability is an emergent property, implicitly learned from rare but consistent visual cues in vast training datasets, and its effectiveness scales with VLM model capacity.
> - Visual prompts can inadvertently trigger or amplify undesirable biases within VLMs, revealing problematic latent associations linked to sensitive categories like criminal classifications.
