# Grasping Deformable Objects via Reinforcement Learning with Cross-Modal Attention to Visuo-Tactile Inputs

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Sogang University developed a deep reinforcement learning framework that enables a simple robotic gripper to grasp soft deformable objects stably and safely. The framework leverages a novel cross-modal attention module to effectively fuse visual and tactile sensory inputs, demonstrating superior and more stable learning performance compared to baseline multi-modal and visual-only approaches in simulation.

## Problem

- Manipulating soft deformable objects is challenging for robots due to their dynamically changing shapes and centers of mass, and their inherent fragility.
- Existing robotic solutions often rely on costly, dexterous hands, while simpler, more accessible grippers lack robust control methods for deformable items.
- Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) methods that use multi-modal data often employ simplistic fusion techniques, like concatenation, which may not fully exploit the complementary nature of visual (global) and tactile (local) information.

## Method

- An end-to-end ==Deep Reinforcement Learning== framework was developed using the ==Soft Actor-Critic (SAC)== algorithm to generate continuous control inputs for a simple two-jaw gripper.
- A ==Cross-modal Spatio-Channel Attention (CSCA)== module was integrated into the encoder network to selectively fuse complementary ==visual (semantic segmentation masks)== and ==tactile (pressure images)== inputs.
- The system was trained with a ==reward function== that encourages stable grip (based on contact area) and penalizes object dropping or breakage, ensuring safe manipulation.

## Results

- The proposed DRQ-CMA method exhibited a consistently stable and improving batch reward during training, outperforming early fusion, late fusion, and visual-only baselines.
- DRQ-CMA achieved the highest success rates across all test environments (basic, random robot movement, and unseen objects), demonstrating superior grasping performance.
- All multi-modal sensing methods significantly outperformed the visual-only baseline, which largely failed to learn the task, underscoring the necessity of tactile feedback.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Multi-modal visuo-tactile sensing is crucial for grasping deformable objects, as relying solely on visual information is insufficient for understanding contact dynamics and achieving stable control.
> - Cross-modal attention mechanisms effectively integrate global visual and local tactile information, leading to more stable learning and superior performance in DRL agents compared to simpler fusion strategies.
> - Using semantic segmentation masks instead of raw RGB images helps the DRL agent focus on object shapes and robot movement, reducing dependency on visual patterns and improving robustness.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic manipulation of objects has seen advancements, with a historical focus on rigid objects. However, there is a growing interest in manipulating deformable objects due to their prevalence in various applications. Deformable objects present significant challenges to robots because their center-of-mass and shapes change dynamically during manipulation, and they are often fragile. While dexterous robotic hands offer precision for such tasks, their high cost limits widespread adoption, necessitating methods for simpler, more accessible grippers.

Human manipulation relies heavily on the integration of multi-modal sensory inputs. Similarly, researchers have explored multi-modal sensing (e.g., visual and tactile) for robots to better understand grasping states. Visual data can provide global information, such as object shape and pose, while tactile data offers local information at the contact points, such as pressure distribution. These modalities offer complementary information that could be synergistically utilized.

Previous research in this domain has often employed supervised learning approaches to predict grasp states using visual and/or tactile information. However, these methods typically require extensive labeled data and an additional control policy to convert predictions into robot actions. Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) frameworks offer an alternative by enabling end-to-end control, directly generating actions from sensor data.

Existing DRL algorithms for robotic manipulation often use high-dimensional visual and tactile data. However, many current methods either process modalities separately for sequential decision-making or combine them through simple concatenation of feature vectors derived from separate encoders. Such straightforward fusion techniques may not fully exploit the complementary nature of the different sensory inputs, potentially leading to suboptimal representation learning for the DRL agent. Cross-modal attention mechanisms have shown utility in other computer vision tasks (e.g., RGB-D and RGB-T crowd counting) by enabling selective focus on relevant features across modalities. However, these applications typically involve cameras capturing the same spatial area. Applying cross-modal attention to visuo-tactile data for deformable object grasping is presented as a novel approach, given the distinct sensing areas and properties of visual and tactile information.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an end-to-end deep reinforcement learning (DRL) framework that enables a simple robotic gripper to grasp soft deformable objects stably and safely. This framework aims to generate appropriate control inputs for the gripper, preventing the object from being dropped or damaged during manipulation tasks.

The motivation for pursuing this objective stems from several challenges and limitations in current robotic grasping research:
- **Challenges of Deformable Objects:** Deformable objects pose difficulties for robots due to their dynamically changing center-of-mass and shape. They are also often fragile, making it challenging to apply appropriate grasping forces without causing damage.
- **Limitations of Dexterous Hands:** While dexterous hands can manipulate deformable objects with precision, their complexity and high cost restrict their general use. There is a need for robust control methods applicable to simpler, more affordable robotic grippers.
- **Inefficiency of Supervised Learning:** Supervised learning approaches for predicting grasp states require substantial amounts of labeled data and an additional mechanism to translate predictions into control inputs, making them less efficient for direct robot control.
- **Suboptimal Multi-Modal Data Fusion:** Existing DRL methods that utilize both visual and tactile information often employ simplistic fusion techniques, such as concatenating feature vectors or combining raw images on a channel axis. These methods may not effectively leverage the complementary nature of visual (global information like shape and pose) and tactile (local information like pressure at contact) data, thereby limiting the quality of learned representations.
- **Lack of Prior Work on Cross-Modal Attention for Visuo-Tactile Grasping:** The paper identifies a gap in existing literature regarding the application of cross-modal attention mechanisms specifically for fusing visuo-tactile information in DRL agents tasked with grasping deformable objects. Previous applications of cross-modal attention, such as in crowd counting, have dealt with modalities capturing the same spatial area, which differs from the complementary yet distinct sensing regions of visual and tactile sensors.

By addressing these points, the research aims to improve a robot's ability to interact with and manipulate deformable objects safely and effectively through enhanced multi-modal data processing within a DRL framework.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method is a DRL framework designed to generate continuous control inputs for a simple two-jaw robotic gripper, utilizing visuo-tactile sensing information. The core innovation lies in the use of a cross-modal attention module within the encoder network, which is trained in a self-supervised manner using the DRL agent's loss function.

**A. Markov Decision Process (MDP) Formulation:**
The grasping task is framed as an MDP, defined by a tuple (S, A, p, r, γ).
- **State (S):** The state `s_t` at time `t` comprises an aggregated feature vector `s_f` from the encoder network and a proprioceptive information vector `s_r`. `s_r` includes the 6-D pose of the end-effector, the gripper's joint position, and velocity.
- **Action (A):** The action `a_t` is the velocity (`v_t`) of the gripper joints, representing a continuous control input.
- **Reward Function (r):** The reward function encourages appropriate gripper control. It combines sparse rewards for success (`r_s`) and failure (`r_f`), and a continuous reward (`r_c`).
 - `r_s`: +1 if the object reaches the goal location (30cm above the start).
 - `r_f`: -0.3 if the object breaks, -0.7 if the object falls (dropping is penalized more severely than breaking).
 - `r_c`: A continuous reward `P/T` given if the episode is not terminated, where `P` is the proportion of tactile readings at least 30% of the maximum value (encouraging stable grip with more contact area), and `T` is the maximum episode step. The maximum total expected return is 2.

**B. DRL Algorithm:**
The framework adopts the Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) algorithm, which is known for learning stochastic policies by maximizing expected returns and entropy, promoting better exploration. SAC is combined with random data augmentation techniques, similar to Data Regularized Q (DrQv2), adapted for visual DRL. The SAC algorithm trains a state-action value function `Q_θ(s_t, a_t)`, a stochastic policy network `π_ϕ(a_t|s_t)`, and a temperature parameter `α` to find an optimal policy by minimizing soft Bellman residual for `Q` and an objective for `π`.

**C. Encoder Network with Cross-Modal Attention:**
The encoder network is central to the approach, designed to fuse visual and tactile inputs effectively.
- **Cross-modal Spatio-Channel Attention (CSCA) Module:** The network employs a CSCA module to selectively focus on complementary information from the multi-modal sensor data.
 - **Spatial-wise Cross-modal Attention (SCA) Block:** This block captures global feature correlations. It projects each modality's input into Query (q), Key (k), and Value (v) vectors. Unlike self-attention, cross-modal attention uses keys and values from one modality and queries from the other, enabling inter-modal information integration.
 - **Channel-wise Feature Aggregation (CFA) Block:** This block integrates the complementary information. It concatenates the cross-modal attention results into a feature map `F_c`. A 1x1 convolution and softmax operation generate weight maps (`W_a`, `W_b`) to re-weight the original feature maps. These weighted maps are summed to produce an aggregated feature map `F_agg`.
- **Input Data:**
 - **Visual Data:** Semantic segmentation masks (128x128) are used instead of raw RGB images. This focuses the agent on object shapes and robot movement, reducing dependency on visual patterns and improving robustness to changes in object appearance or background.
 - **Tactile Data:** One-channel tactile images (128x128) are derived from the sensors.
- **Training of Encoder:** The encoder is trained simultaneously with the critic network using backpropagation from the DRL agent's objective function, enabling it to learn state representations relevant for maximizing rewards.
- **Output:** The CSCA module generates an aggregated feature, which is then transformed into a 1x50 visual embedding `s_f` via a fully connected layer. This `s_f` is concatenated with the 1x7 proprioceptive vector `s_r` to form the final state `s_t` (1x57) for the critic and actor networks.

**D. Simulation Environment:**
- **Platform:** PyBullet is used as the dynamic simulator.
- **Robot:** A 6-DOF robotic arm equipped with a Robotiq 2F-85 gripper.
- **Tactile Sensors:** Simulated using TACTO.
- **Deformable Objects:** Six soft deformable objects with varying shapes (cylindrical, hexahedron), sizes, weights (0.25 kg, 0.5 kg, 2 kg), and colors are implemented. Two objects are used for training, and all six (including four unseen) are used for testing generalization.

**E. Experimental Setup and Baselines:**
- **Comparative Methods:** The proposed method, DRQ with the cross-modal attention module encoder (DRQ-CMA), is compared against:
 - DRQ with an Early Fusion encoder (DRQ-EF): Tactile and segmentation images are concatenated on the channel axis before a single convolutional encoder.
 - DRQ with a Late Fusion encoder (DRQ-LF): Images are processed by separate convolutional encoders, and their resulting feature maps are concatenated.
 - DRQ with a Segmentation Image Only encoder (DRQ-SO): Uses only visual information.
- **Training Details:** 450 million training episode steps, AdamW optimizer with a learning rate of 10^-4, batch size of 216, and an initial 20,000 steps of random exploration.
- **Evaluation Metrics:**
 - **Batch Reward:** Average reward from 256 MDP tuples sampled from the replay buffer at each training step.
 - **Success Rate:** Percentage of successful grasping tasks over 20 trials, where success means lifting the object to the goal location without dropping or breaking it.
- **Test Environments:**
 - **Basic:** Identical setup to the training environment (two training objects, straight vertical arm movement).
 - **Random Move:** Same test objects as Basic, but the robot arm moves with randomly chosen waypoints, resulting in zigzag patterns.
 - **More Objects:** Same arm movement as Basic, but uses all six deformable objects (including four unseen ones), randomly chosen per trial.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluated the proposed Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) framework with cross-modal attention (DRQ-CMA) against several baselines, focusing on both training stability and task performance in various environments.

**A. Training Process (Batch Reward):**
- **DRQ-CMA** demonstrated a consistent upward trend in batch reward throughout the training process, indicating stable and continuous optimization of the learning process.
- **DRQ-EF (Early Fusion)** and **DRQ-LF (Late Fusion)** exhibited less stable learning. Their batch rewards initially rose but then experienced drops and fluctuations before resuming an upward trend. While they eventually outperformed the visual-only method, their learning progression was not as smooth as DRQ-CMA.
- **DRQ-SO (Segmentation Only)** failed to learn effectively, with its batch reward consistently decreasing as training progressed. This indicates that relying solely on visual information was insufficient for the task.

**B. Effectiveness of Multi-Modal Sensing:**
The stark difference in performance between DRQ-SO and the multi-modal methods (DRQ-CMA, DRQ-EF, DRQ-LF) highlighted the importance of integrating both visual and tactile data. Visual information alone, despite providing object shape, did not equip the agent with an understanding of the contact dynamics crucial for stable grasping. Tactile information proved to be a necessary complementary modality for this task.

**C. Effectiveness of Cross-Modal Attention:**
The comparison of DRQ-CMA with DRQ-EF and DRQ-LF in terms of batch reward indicated that the cross-modal attention mechanism was effective. DRQ-CMA's more stable and continuously improving performance suggested that its encoder network was better at learning meaningful representations by selectively fusing complementary information from visual and tactile inputs, compared to simpler concatenation methods.

**D. Evaluation Results (Success Rate):**
The learned policies were evaluated in three distinct test environments: Basic (identical to training), Random Move (unseen robot motion patterns), and More Objects (unseen deformable objects).

- **DRQ-CMA** achieved the highest success rates across all test environments:
 - **Basic Environment:** DRQ-CMA consistently outperformed other methods.
 - **Random Move Environment:** This environment, involving zigzag arm movements, proved more challenging than handling different objects. Maintaining a stable grasp was difficult due to fluctuating object dynamics. DRQ-CMA experienced a performance drop but still maintained a higher success rate compared to baselines.
 - **More Objects Environment:** Grasping unseen objects was handled better than random arm movements by the multi-modal methods. This was attributed to tactile values providing meaningful pressure information at contact regardless of object shape. DRQ-CMA also showed the highest success rate in this environment.
- **DRQ-SO** consistently performed poorly, demonstrating minimal success (e.g., 5% in the basic environment), which was consistent with its training performance.
- **Performance Drop and Overfitting:** While DRQ-CMA achieved the highest overall success rates, it also exhibited a larger performance drop (up to 40%) when generalizing to new environments compared to methods with simpler encoders. This drop was attributed to potential overfitting of DRQ-CMA's policy and encoder network, which has more learnable parameters, to the specific trajectories and objects encountered during the less-randomized training environment. Despite this, its absolute success rate remained superior.

In summary, the results indicated that multi-modal sensing is critical for deformable object grasping, and the cross-modal attention mechanism significantly enhances the fusion of these modalities, leading to more stable learning and superior task performance, even with a tendency for overfitting in novel environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of robotic manipulation by addressing the challenging problem of grasping soft deformable objects with a simple gripper. The significance and potential impact of this work are multi-faceted:

- **Advancement in Deformable Object Manipulation:** The proposed DRL framework offers a method for robots to handle objects with dynamically changing shapes and centers of mass, which is a complex task for conventional robotic systems. This capability is crucial for applications in domestic robotics, logistics, manufacturing (e.g., handling food items, textiles), and even surgical robotics.
- **Effective Multi-Modal Sensor Fusion:** The introduction and demonstrated effectiveness of a cross-modal attention mechanism for fusing visual and tactile inputs in a DRL context represent a methodological advancement. This approach moves beyond simple concatenation strategies, enabling the DRL agent to learn more meaningful and correlated representations by selectively attending to relevant features across distinct sensing modalities. This could inspire similar fusion architectures in other robotic perception and control problems.
- **End-to-End Control for Simpler Robots:** By providing an end-to-end DRL framework that directly maps sensor data to gripper control inputs, the work reduces the complexity typically associated with tasks requiring separate grasp state prediction and control generation. This makes robust deformable object grasping more accessible for simpler, more cost-effective robotic grippers, potentially expanding the practical deployment of robots in various industries.
- **Enhanced Robustness and Safety:** The design of the reward function and the successful learning of a stable grasping policy demonstrate an ability to grasp objects without dropping or breaking them. This is critical for handling fragile deformable objects and ensuring task success in real-world scenarios.
- **Open-Source Simulation Environment:** The development of a training and test suite using PyBullet and TACTO, which can simulate various robot models, gripper attachments, and deformable objects with customizable properties, provides a valuable resource for future research. This simulation environment facilitates efficient iteration and comparison of new methods without immediate reliance on physical hardware.
- **Demonstrated Generalization Capabilities:** The experiments show that the proposed method exhibits a certain level of generalization to unseen robot motions and deformable objects. While the paper acknowledges potential overfitting and suggests future work on generalization, the current results indicate a foundational capability to adapt to variations in the environment and objects. This forms a basis for further research into more robust and widely applicable manipulation strategies.
- **Future Research Directions:** The identified areas for future work, such as addressing overfitting through regularization and curriculum learning, enhancing generalization via domain randomization, and transferring the learned policies to physical robot systems, highlight clear pathways for advancing the state of the art.

In conclusion, this research presents a robust deep reinforcement learning approach for grasping deformable objects, leveraging cross-modal attention for effective visuo-tactile data fusion. Its contributions are significant for advancing robotic capabilities in handling complex objects and expanding the practical utility of robotic systems.
