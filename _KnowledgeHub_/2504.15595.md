# Grasping Deformable Objects via Reinforcement Learning with Cross-Modal Attention to Visuo-Tactile Inputs

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Sogang University developed a deep reinforcement learning framework that enables a simple robotic gripper to grasp soft deformable objects stably and safely. The framework leverages a novel cross-modal attention module to effectively fuse visual and tactile sensory inputs, demonstrating superior and more stable learning performance compared to baseline multi-modal and visual-only approaches in simulation.

## Problem

- Manipulating soft deformable objects is challenging for robots due to their dynamically changing shapes and centers of mass, and their inherent fragility.
- Existing robotic solutions often rely on costly, dexterous hands, while simpler, more accessible grippers lack robust control methods for deformable items.
- Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) methods that use multi-modal data often employ simplistic fusion techniques, like concatenation, which may not fully exploit the complementary nature of visual (global) and tactile (local) information.

## Method

- An end-to-end ==Deep Reinforcement Learning== framework was developed using the ==Soft Actor-Critic (SAC)== algorithm to generate continuous control inputs for a simple two-jaw gripper.
- A ==Cross-modal Spatio-Channel Attention (CSCA)== module was integrated into the encoder network to selectively fuse complementary ==visual (semantic segmentation masks)== and ==tactile (pressure images)== inputs.
- The system was trained with a ==reward function== that encourages stable grip (based on contact area) and penalizes object dropping or breakage, ensuring safe manipulation.

## Results

- The proposed DRQ-CMA method exhibited a consistently stable and improving batch reward during training, outperforming early fusion, late fusion, and visual-only baselines.
- DRQ-CMA achieved the highest success rates across all test environments (basic, random robot movement, and unseen objects), demonstrating superior grasping performance.
- All multi-modal sensing methods significantly outperformed the visual-only baseline, which largely failed to learn the task, underscoring the necessity of tactile feedback.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Multi-modal visuo-tactile sensing is crucial for grasping deformable objects, as relying solely on visual information is insufficient for understanding contact dynamics and achieving stable control.
> - Cross-modal attention mechanisms effectively integrate global visual and local tactile information, leading to more stable learning and superior performance in DRL agents compared to simpler fusion strategies.
> - Using semantic segmentation masks instead of raw RGB images helps the DRL agent focus on object shapes and robot movement, reducing dependency on visual patterns and improving robustness.
