# GENIUS: Generative Fluid Intelligence Evaluation Suite

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Peking University, CUHK, StepFun, PolyU, and MSRA introduce GENIUS, the first benchmark dedicated to evaluating Generative Fluid Intelligence (GFI) in Unified Multimodal Models (UMMs). Their work reveals significant GFI deficits in state-of-the-art models and proposes a training-free attention adjustment mechanism that boosts performance by rectifying disordered attention distributions.

## Problem

- Existing benchmarks for Unified Multimodal Models (UMMs) primarily assess "Crystallized Intelligence" (memorization and retrieval), failing to evaluate their ability for dynamic reasoning and adaptation.
- A formal definition for Generative Fluid Intelligence (GFI) in visual generation was lacking, hindering systematic assessment and development of truly adaptable AI.
- UMMs often exhibit an "illusion of competence," where visually plausible outputs mask a fundamental inability to deeply comprehend context and logically adhere to novel, ad-hoc rules.

## Method

- Formally defined ==Generative Fluid Intelligence (GFI)== as a synthesis of Inductive Inference, Abstract Dynamic Reasoning, and Adaptive Inhibition, grounded in the Cattell-Horn-Carroll theory.
- Introduced ==GENIUS==, a multimodal, expert-curated benchmark with 510 samples across 3 dimensions and 5 tasks, designed to rigorously decouple static knowledge from fluid reasoning.
- Proposed a ==training-free attention adjustment mechanism== that distills task-critical keywords, maps their relevance to visual context, and injects a spatial bias into attention logits to focus on critical signals.

## Results

- Evaluations of **12** UMMs on GENIUS showed widespread GFI deficiencies, with the highest-performing proprietary model (Nano Banana Pro) achieving an overall score of only **57.19%**.
- The proposed attention adjustment mechanism improved the overall GFI score of an open-source model (Bagel) by **6.18%**, demonstrating its effectiveness in activating latent GFI capabilities.
- A high Pearson correlation (r = **0.9630** to **0.9659**) between LMM-based automated scoring (Gemini-3-Pro) and human expert judgments validated the reliability of the GENIUS evaluation framework.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current state-of-the-art UMMs exhibit substantial deficits in GFI, with even the best proprietary models scoring below 60%, indicating a significant gap towards general intelligence.
> - Models suffer from "cognitive inertia," frequently defaulting to pre-trained priors when instructions contradict established world knowledge, rather than adapting to new contextual rules.
> - There is an "execution gap" where models often demonstrate accurate comprehension of context (via VQA probes) but fail to translate this understanding into compliant visual outputs during generation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses a critical, yet often overlooked, challenge in the rapidly evolving field of Artificial Intelligence, particularly concerning Unified Multimodal Models (UMMs) capable of visual generation. UMMs have shown impressive progress, demonstrating remarkable abilities in generating high-quality visual content and processing complex, interleaved multimodal contexts. The current research landscape is largely dominated by efforts to enhance "Crystallized Intelligence" (CI) in these models – essentially their capacity for memorization, retrieval of pre-trained knowledge, and reproduction of learned schemas. This involves developing models that can generate realistic images of common objects, styles, or concepts learned from vast datasets. Existing benchmarks predominantly reflect this focus, assessing static world knowledge, generation quality, and adherence to simple text-to-image prompts.

However, the authors argue that this predominant focus on CI masks a fundamental deficiency in UMMs: their "Generative Fluid Intelligence" (GFI). Fluid intelligence, drawing from the Cattell-Horn-Carroll (CHC) theory of cognitive abilities, refers to the capacity to induce patterns, reason through novel constraints, and adapt to unforeseen scenarios independently of prior knowledge. While CI helps models recall that a "cat" has four legs and fur, GFI would enable them to generate a "cat with six legs if gravity worked differently on a new planet, whose rules are defined only in context."

The paper directly challenges the "illusion of competence" prevalent in current generative AI, where visually plausible outputs often conceal a lack of deep context comprehension and logical adherence. Many SOTA models excel at producing aesthetically pleasing images but struggle when faced with instructions that contradict their pre-trained priors or demand abstract, ad-hoc reasoning. While some benchmarks for Fluid Intelligence exist in other AI domains (e.g., ARC for logical reasoning in symbolic environments), none specifically address the generative aspect within a multimodal context where novel rules are defined on the fly.

GENIUS thus introduces a paradigm shift in evaluation, moving beyond mere reproduction and aesthetic quality to assess a model's true adaptability, reasoning capabilities, and ability to handle novel, context-dependent challenges. By formally defining GFI and proposing a dedicated benchmark, this work aims to guide the development of next-generation UMMs toward achieving a more robust and truly general form of intelligence, crucial for the long-term goal of Artificial General Intelligence (AGI). It emphasizes that for UMMs to be truly intelligent, they must not only know a lot (CI) but also be able to reason and adapt dynamically (GFI).

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to rigorously assess the Generative Fluid Intelligence (GFI) of Unified Multimodal Models (UMMs) in visual generation, thereby advancing the field beyond mere knowledge utilization towards dynamic, general-purpose reasoning.

The specific key objectives and their underlying motivations are:

1. **Formally Define Generative Fluid Intelligence (GFI):**
 - **Motivation:** The authors identify a significant "theoretical void" where GFI, despite its critical importance for general intelligence, lacks a formal definition within the context of visual generation. Without a clear theoretical framework, it is challenging to systematically guide the development and evaluation of UMMs towards this capability.
 - **Objective:** To formalize GFI as a synthesis of three core primitives, grounded in the Cattell-Horn-Carroll (CHC) theory of intelligence: Inducing Implicit Patterns (Inductive Inference), Executing Ad-hoc Constraints (Abstract Dynamic Reasoning), and Adapting to Contextual Knowledge (Adaptive Inhibition).

2. **Introduce GENIUS: A Dedicated Benchmark for Quantifying GFI:**
 - **Motivation:** Existing benchmarks primarily assess Crystallized Intelligence (CI), focusing on model memorization and retrieval of pre-trained knowledge. They fail to disentangle static knowledge from fluid reasoning, thus inadequately probing the true bounds of general intelligence in novel scenarios.
 - **Objective:** To construct GENIUS (GENerative Fluid Intelligence EvalUation Suite), the first benchmark specifically designed to purely quantify GFI. This suite features expert-curated samples, multi-image input, multimodal interleaved contexts, and novel rules that strictly decouple static knowledge.

3. **Systematically Evaluate Representative UMMs for GFI Deficits:**
 - **Motivation:** Despite rapid advancements, there is a lack of systematic analysis regarding how far current UMMs are from achieving true general intelligence in visual generation, particularly concerning fluid adaptability.
 - **Objective:** To conduct a comprehensive evaluation of 12 representative open-source and proprietary UMMs using the GENIUS benchmark. This aims to expose under-explored deficiencies and quantify the performance gap in GFI.

4. **Diagnose Failure Modes and Investigate Underlying Mechanisms:**
 - **Motivation:** The absence of investigations into *why* models fail in GFI tasks leaves critical questions unanswered, hindering targeted improvements. Understanding the root causes of these failures is essential for developing effective solutions.
 - **Objective:** To perform diagnostic analyses, including assessing the impact of inference-time strategies, context interventions, and reformulating generative tasks as VQA probes. This also involves visualizing attention distributions to identify mechanistic failures.

5. **Propose a Strategy to Enhance GFI Capabilities:**
 - **Motivation:** Beyond mere evaluation, the research aims to provide actionable insights and demonstrate potential pathways to bridge the identified performance gaps, thus actively steering UMMs towards general intelligence.
 - **Objective:** To propose a training-free attention intervention strategy, inspired by theoretical analysis of In-Context Learning (ICL) as Implicit Fine-Tuning, which can activate the model’s latent GFI by rectifying attention distribution.

Collectively, these objectives are motivated by the fundamental desire to move the field of generative AI beyond superficial prowess to genuinely intelligent systems capable of dynamic, context-aware reasoning and adaptation, which is a crucial step towards Artificial General Intelligence.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this paper is multi-faceted, combining theoretical formalization, rigorous benchmark construction, systematic empirical evaluation, and diagnostic analysis, culminating in a proposed intervention strategy.

#### 3.1. Formalization of Generative Fluid Intelligence (GFI)

Drawing from the Cattell-Horn-Carroll (CHC) theory of intelligence, the authors formally define GFI for visual generation as a synthesis of three core primitives:
- **Induce Implicit Pattern (Inductive Inference):** The ability to distill unstated visual patterns or intrinsic attributes from observations within the context (e.g., inferring personalized visual preferences).
- **Execute Ad-hoc Constraints (Abstract Dynamic Reasoning):** The capacity to perform logical reasoning and apply newly defined visual or symbolic rules within the immediate context (e.g., visualizing abstract metaphors or following arbitrary operational rules).
- **Adapt based on Contextual Knowledge (Adaptive Inhibition):** The skill to adjust generation based on novel contextual cues, even when these contradict established common sense or pre-trained world knowledge (e.g., simulating counter-intuitive physics or discerning literal vs. figurative meanings).

#### 3.2. Benchmark Construction: GENIUS Suite

GENIUS (GENerative Fluid Intelligence EvalUation Suite) is the first benchmark dedicated to GFI evaluation.
- **Structure:** It is hierarchically organized into three dimensions, five tasks, and 20 diverse sub-tasks, comprising a total of 510 expert-curated samples.
- **Key Design Principles:**
 - **Multimodal Interleaved Context:** Each sample includes multi-modal inputs (images and text) presented in an interleaved format, where removing any single modality renders the task unsolvable.
 - **Decoupling Static Knowledge:** Samples are meticulously designed to present dynamic and novel rules, strictly disassociating them from pre-trained world knowledge to ensure a pure quantification of GFI.
 - **Manual Curation:** All test cases are manually curated by multimodal experts, ensuring high quality and relevance.
- **Tasks within GENIUS:**
 - **Implicit Pattern Induction:** Features the "Implicit Pattern Generation" task, assessing the model's ability to deduce and apply unstated visual preferences.
 - **Ad-hoc Constraint Execution:** Includes "Visual Constraint Generation" and "Symbolic Constraint Generation," which assign novel meanings to visual elements or mathematical symbols, challenging abstract reasoning.
 - **Contextual Knowledge Adaptation:** Comprises "Prior-Conflicting Generation" (demanding reasoning based on new, even contradictory, common sense) and "Multi-Semantic Generation" (requiring discernment between literal and metaphorical interpretations based on context).

#### 3.3. Evaluation Metrics

A robust hybrid evaluation pipeline is constructed, utilizing the frontier LMM, Gemini-3-Pro, as the judge, augmented by manually-curated hints. Scores are assigned on a 0 (fail), 1 (partial), or 2 (perfect) scale.
- **Rule Compliance (RC):** Measures the precision in executing strict, ad-hoc rules. Human-curated "eval-hints" serve as gold standards for comparison against the generated output.
- **Visual Consistency (VC):** Assesses the model's ability to preserve the visual identity of original objects or characters under logical constraints, incorporating an anti-plagiarism screen.
- **Aesthetic Quality (AQ):** Evaluates the physical coherence, anatomical logic, rendering clarity, and absence of AI artifacts (e.g., distorted limbs).

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

- **Models Evaluated:** 12 representative models (6 open-source: Qwen-Image-Edit-2511, GLM-Image, FLUX.2-dev, NextStep-1, Emu3.5-Image, Bagel; 6 proprietary: Nano Banana, Nano Banana Pro, SeeDream 4.0/4.5, GPT-Image).
- **Evaluation Robustness:** Results are averaged over three independent runs to mitigate stochastic variance.
- **Input Formats:** Interleaved inputs are used where supported; otherwise, a decoupled format is adopted. Ablation studies explore the impact of different interleaved formats.

#### 3.5. Diagnostic Analysis

The authors conduct several analyses to understand failure modes:
- **Inference-time Strategies:** Testing pre-planning (reasoning mode) and post-reflection on models like Nano Banana Pro and Bagel.
- **Contextual Intervention:** Employing text-only and multimodal human-curated hints to guide generation.
- **Comprehension vs. Generation:** Reformulating generative tasks into Visual Question Answering (VQA) probes to assess context comprehension separately from generative execution.
- **Attention Visualization:** Visualizing attention distribution in Bagel to identify how models process multimodal context.

#### 3.6. Proposed Solution: Training-Free Attention Adjustment Mechanism

- **Theoretical Basis:** Inspired by In-Context Learning (ICL) as Implicit Fine-Tuning, the authors derive that attention magnitude directly dictates the norm of implicit gradient updates. Irregular attention leads to noisy gradients.
- **Mechanism:** A three-stage, training-free pipeline to recalibrate attention:
 1. **Keyword Distillation:** Using the model's semantic reasoning to extract task-critical visual cues into keywords.
 2. **Relevance Mapping:** Computing a semantic relevance map between keywords and visual context tokens.
 3. **Bias Injection:** Directly injecting a spatial bias based on the relevance map into attention logits, suppressing noise tokens and emphasizing critical signals, thereby steering the implicit gradient direction.

This comprehensive approach allows for a deep investigation into GFI, from its theoretical underpinnings to practical evaluation and potential solutions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation using the GENIUS benchmark revealed several critical insights into the Generative Fluid Intelligence (GFI) capabilities of current Unified Multimodal Models (UMMs).

1. **Significant Performance Deficits in GFI:**
 - Current state-of-the-art models, both proprietary and open-source, exhibit substantial gaps in GFI. Even the best proprietary model, Nano Banana Pro, achieved an overall score of only 57.19, falling short of a passing grade. Open-source models like Bagel scored considerably lower (26.74).
 - These deficits are particularly evident in tasks requiring ad-hoc reasoning and dynamic adaptation to novel rules, which are less reliant on pre-trained knowledge.

2. **"Cognitive Inertia" and Conflict with Pre-trained Priors:**
 - Models consistently struggle with the "Contextual Knowledge Adaptation" dimension, where performance drops significantly. When instructions contradict established world knowledge (e.g., counter-intuitive physics or remapped semantics), models frequently default to their pre-trained priors. This "cognitive inertia" indicates a lack of robust mechanisms to inhibit intrinsic priors and adapt dynamically.

3. **"Illusion of Competence": Aesthetic Fidelity Masks Logical Deficiencies:**
 - A critical finding is the consistent discrepancy between high Aesthetic Quality (AQ) scores and much lower Rule Compliance (RC) scores. Models are adept at generating visually plausible images, but often fail to adhere to the precise, context-dependent rules. This suggests that past model optimization has prioritized surface-level visual plausibility over deep context interpretation and logical correctness.

4. **Marginal Gains from Inference-Time Reasoning Strategies:**
 - Attempts to boost GFI through inference-time strategies like "pre-planning" (activating reasoning mode) and "post-reflection" (iterative refinement) yielded only marginal improvements for both proprietary and open-source models. This indicates that current architectures may struggle to effectively leverage explicit reasoning for generative tasks.

5. **Context Comprehension is Critical but Not Solely Sufficient:**
 - Intervention with human-curated hints significantly improved model performance, especially for stronger models like Nano Banana Pro. This highlights that accurate context comprehension is a critical factor in solving GFI tasks.
 - However, even with perfect hints, a gap remained, particularly for weaker base models, suggesting that while comprehension aids are crucial, they cannot fully compensate for inherent limitations in a model's intrinsic generative capabilities.

6. **Generative Failure Stems Primarily from an "Execution Gap":**
 - By reformulating generative tasks into Visual Question Answering (VQA) probes, the authors found that models often demonstrate accurate *understanding* of the context's intent (higher VQA accuracy) but fail to *translate* this understanding into compliant visual outputs. This "know-but-cannot-draw" phenomenon suggests that current UMM architectures struggle to effectively propagate rich semantic understanding from the encoder to the generative decoder, resulting in a crucial execution gap.

7. **Attention Mechanism Defects Identified:**
 - Visualization of attention distributions (e.g., in Bagel) revealed irregular noise and stochastic spikes across the multimodal context. This indicates that models struggle to accurately focus on critical new rules, instead spreading attention indiscriminately, leading to a failure in extracting specific signals for adaptation.

8. **Effectiveness of the Proposed Attention Adjustment Mechanism:**
 - The training-free attention intervention strategy successfully rectified the disordered attention landscape into a sharper distribution focused on critical tokens.
 - Quantitatively, this mechanism yielded consistent performance gains across almost all GFI dimensions (e.g., boosting Bagel's overall score by 6.18%). This validates the theoretical framework linking attention distribution to implicit gradient direction and demonstrates a simple yet effective way to activate latent GFI.

9. **Validation of LMM-as-a-Judge:**
 - A high Pearson correlation (r = 0.9630 for Nano Banana Pro, 0.9659 for Bagel) between LMM-based automated scoring (Gemini-3-Pro) and human expert judgments confirmed the reliability and accuracy of the evaluation framework.
 - Further validation with Qwen2.5-VL-72B as an alternative judge showed consistent relative performance trends and model rankings, reinforcing the generalizability of the results across different evaluators.

10. **Input Format Sensitivity and Context Indispensability:**
 - Ablation studies showed that models exhibited limited robustness to different input formats (decoupled vs. interleaved), with significant performance drops between them.
 - Removing contextual information led to a precipitous decline in rule compliance across all tasks, underscoring the indispensable role of rich context for accurate GFI.

These findings collectively highlight a significant frontier for research in multimodal AI, demonstrating that current models, despite their impressive generative prowess, lack robust fluid intelligence capabilities crucial for true general intelligence.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "GENIUS: Generative Fluid Intelligence Evaluation Suite" paper makes several significant contributions to the field of Artificial Intelligence, with substantial potential impact on future research and development of generative models.

1. **Pioneering a New Evaluation Paradigm for AGI:**
 - By formally defining Generative Fluid Intelligence (GFI) and introducing the first dedicated benchmark, GENIUS establishes a critical new standard for evaluating advanced AI systems. It shifts the focus from merely assessing "Crystallized Intelligence" (memorization and retrieval) to "Fluid Intelligence" (reasoning, adaptation, and problem-solving in novel scenarios). This is crucial for guiding the evolution of Unified Multimodal Models (UMMs) towards true Artificial General Intelligence (AGI), which requires dynamic adaptability beyond pre-trained knowledge.

2. **Exposing and Diagnosing Fundamental Model Deficiencies:**
 - The systematic evaluation of 12 state-of-the-art models reveals a stark reality: even the most advanced UMMs exhibit significant performance deficits in GFI. The findings highlight key weaknesses such as "cognitive inertia" (reliance on pre-trained priors over novel context), an "execution gap" (models understand but cannot accurately generate), and inadequate attention mechanisms. These diagnoses provide clear, actionable targets for future architectural innovations and training methodologies.

3. **Combating the "Illusion of Competence":**
 - GENIUS explicitly addresses the "illusion of competence" prevalent in generative AI, where aesthetically pleasing outputs often mask a lack of logical coherence or rule compliance. By introducing hybrid metrics that prioritize Rule Compliance and Visual Consistency alongside Aesthetic Quality, the benchmark encourages the development of models that are not only visually impressive but also logically sound and contextually accurate. This fosters a more transparent and trustworthy evaluation framework, which is vital for responsible AI development and deployment.

4. **Theoretical Contribution to In-Context Learning (ICL):**
 - The paper extends the theoretical understanding of In-Context Learning by linking attention distribution to the implicit gradient dynamics during generation. This theoretical analysis, coupled with empirical observations, provides a mechanistic explanation for GFI failures and forms the basis for potential interventions. Such theoretical grounding is critical for moving beyond heuristic improvements to principled advancements.

5. **Proposing an Actionable and Training-Free Solution:**
 - The proposed attention adjustment mechanism offers a practical, training-free strategy to boost GFI capabilities. Its success in rectifying attention distribution and improving performance provides a strong baseline and a simple paradigm for enhancing fluid intelligence. This demonstrates that significant improvements can sometimes be achieved through smart intervention at inference time, potentially inspiring more complex and integrated solutions.

6. **Enabling Development of More Robust and Versatile AI Tools:**
 - The long-term impact of improved GFI capabilities is profound. Models with higher GFI will be able to:
 - **Follow Complex, Ad-hoc Instructions:** Accurately interpret and execute intricate, novel instructions in creative tools (e.g., personalized art generation, complex design tasks).
 - **Handle Counter-Intuitive Scenarios:** Generate visualizations for scientific simulations, engineering designs, or fantasy worlds where physics or common sense are redefined.
 - **Reduce Hallucinations:** By better grounding generation in immediate context and rules, models are less likely to produce factually incorrect or illogical content.
 - **Improve Human-AI Collaboration:** By truly understanding and adapting to user intent, even when implicit or contradictory to pre-trained knowledge, generative AI can become a more intuitive and powerful collaborator.

In essence, GENIUS serves as a pivotal testbed for future research, pushing the boundaries of generative AI beyond mere pattern recognition and reproduction towards systems that can truly reason, adapt, and solve novel problems, thereby accelerating progress towards more generally intelligent and trustworthy AI.
