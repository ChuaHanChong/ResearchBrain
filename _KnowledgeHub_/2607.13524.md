# COLMAR: Cooperative View Policy Learning for Multi-Agent Active 3D Reconstruction

> [!summary] Summary
> A cooperative view policy learning framework, COLMAR, enables multiple autonomous agents to efficiently reconstruct 3D environments by integrating map-centric observations with a reconstruction-aware reward function. The framework consistently improves geometric accuracy by 54%, achieves 49% greater coverage, and enhances photorealistic rendering quality over existing active reconstruction methods.

## Problem

- Existing multi-agent active 3D reconstruction methods show limited coordination, leading to redundant observations and inefficient team-level coverage.
- Policy learning often uses sparse or terminal rewards, missing rich, dense feedback from incremental reconstruction updates for effective exploration guidance.
- Prior approaches, both classical and learning-based, can be brittle in complex scenes, producing suboptimal reconstruction quality and myopic exploration.

## Method

- A ==cooperative view policy learning== framework, **COLMAR**, trains a single, shared policy for multiple agents using ==Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)==.
- Each agent's policy input combines spatial (rendered depth, ==ego-centric occupancy==, global map) and temporal (pose history) observations.
- A dense, ==reconstruction-aware reward function== integrates unique coverage gain, team-level discovery bonuses, and penalties for stagnation, collisions, and crowding.

## Results

- COLMAR achieved **89.4%** reconstruction accuracy and **82.6%** coverage, outperforming baselines by up to **54%** and **49%** respectively, and yielded superior photorealistic rendering quality (PSNR **37.61**, LPIPS **0.0312**) on the Replica dataset.
- Performance scaled positively with the number of agents, increasing coverage from **72.4%** (1 agent) to **80.9%** (4 agents) and decreasing Chamfer distance from **6.48 cm** to **4.96 cm** on the GLEAM dataset.
- Ablation studies confirmed the criticality of the dense reconstruction-aware reward function, with regularization terms significantly reducing collision rates and improving geometric metrics.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Implicit coordination among agents can arise from a shared policy trained with a common understanding of the evolving environment map, removing the need for explicit inter-agent communication during deployment.
> - Dense, reconstruction-aware reward signals, incorporating local coverage, team-level discovery, and safety penalties, significantly improve policy learning for active reconstruction.
> - Effective view policy learning benefits from a fusion of both local (e.g., depth, ego-centric occupancy) and global (e.g., pose grid, aggregated map) map inputs, as these modalities are complementary.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

Autonomous 3D reconstruction is a foundational capability for applications such as robotic inspection, digital twinning, search-and-rescue operations, and embodied intelligence. The active variant of this problem involves a robot strategically selecting viewpoints to maximize reconstruction quality within a defined sensing budget.

Historically, solutions for active 3D reconstruction have relied on classical methods rooted in frontier expansion and next-best-view (NBV) criteria. These approaches typically use handcrafted scoring terms to select candidate views that maximize expected map gain while adhering to geometric constraints. While practical and robust in certain scenarios, these methods often exhibit brittleness when faced with complex geometries, partial observability, or non-trivial motion constraints, potentially leading to myopic or redundant trajectories.

More recent advancements have shifted towards learning-based active reconstruction. These methods, often employing reinforcement learning (RL) policies, have demonstrated an ability to surpass fixed NBV heuristics in challenging scenes. Examples include GenNBV, which learns a generalizable RL policy for active reconstruction in free-space actions, and GLEAM, which focuses on generalization in complex 3D indoor environments.

In parallel with active view planning, there have been rapid developments in 3D reconstruction backends. These range from neural implicit simultaneous localization and mapping (SLAM) systems (e.g., NICE-SLAM, NeRF-SLAM) to more efficient Gaussian representations like 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS). The field of multi-agent SLAM has also seen progress with systems such as MNE-SLAM, MAGiC-SLAM, and GRAND-SLAM, which aim for consistent and high-quality reconstructions from multiple robotic agents. Active mapping systems like ActiveGAMER further advocate for closer integration between mapping state and cooperative view planning.

Despite these advancements, the paper identifies two primary gaps in multi-agent active 3D reconstruction. First, many existing multi-agent approaches optimize exploration on a per-agent basis with limited coordination signals, which can result in redundant observations and inefficient team-level coverage. Second, policy learning in this domain often relies on coarse or terminal rewards, failing to fully leverage rich, dense reconstruction diagnostics such as local coverage growth, cues from under-explored regions, and motion validity information during training. These limitations can lead to a degradation in coordination quality and policy stability as scene complexity or team size increases. COLMAR directly addresses these gaps by proposing a cooperative view policy learning framework that integrates detailed reconstruction feedback and promotes coordinated view selection.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a cooperative view policy learning framework, named COLMAR, that enables multiple autonomous agents to efficiently reconstruct 3D environments. The work aims to overcome the limitations of existing multi-agent active 3D reconstruction methods, specifically addressing coordination inefficiencies and suboptimal utilization of reconstruction feedback during policy learning.

The detailed motivations and objectives are as follows:

1. **Enhance Coordination in Multi-Agent Active Reconstruction:** Existing multi-agent approaches frequently struggle with coordination, often resulting in agents making redundant observations or clustering spatially. This leads to inefficient team-level coverage and reduced overall reconstruction quality under limited sensing budgets. COLMAR aims to formulate viewpoint allocation as a shared policy optimization problem that explicitly promotes overlap-aware coverage and team-level discovery, thereby improving coordination without requiring explicit inter-agent message passing during deployment.

2. **Integrate Reconstruction-Aware Feedback into Policy Learning:** Many learning-based active reconstruction methods rely on sparse or final rewards, which may not adequately guide the agent's exploration behavior in a complex, dynamic mapping process. The authors hypothesize that dense feedback derived from incremental reconstruction updates, directly tied to downstream geometric quality, can significantly improve policy learning. COLMAR introduces a reconstruction-aware objective that incorporates reward components linked to local coverage gain, the discovery of under-explored regions, and collision-safe exploration. This objective is designed to align the exploration policy with the true goal of high-fidelity 3D reconstruction.

3. **Develop a Scalable and Generalizable Policy:** The research seeks to create a framework where a single, shared policy can be trained cooperatively across multiple agents and environments, and then executed independently by each agent during deployment. This approach, utilizing parameter-sharing Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) with map-centric observations, aims to ensure scalability to various team sizes and adaptability to different scene types.

4. **Improve Reconstruction Quality and Exploration Efficiency:** By addressing the aforementioned coordination and feedback challenges, a core objective is to achieve measurable improvements in both the geometric accuracy and completeness of the final 3D reconstruction, as well as the efficiency of the exploration process (e.g., higher coverage) under equivalent sensing budgets, when compared against current heuristic and non-cooperative learning baselines.

5. **Utilize Advanced Reconstruction Backends for Evaluation:** While policy learning uses a TSDF backend for efficient feedback during training, the motivation extends to evaluating the final reconstruction quality using state-of-the-art photorealistic techniques like 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS). This allows for a comprehensive assessment of the policy's ability to drive high-fidelity reconstruction, independent of the training mechanism.

In essence, COLMAR is motivated by the need for intelligent, cooperative, and feedback-driven multi-agent systems that can autonomously generate high-quality 3D reconstructions more efficiently than current methods.

### 3. Methodology and approach

COLMAR is a cooperative view policy learning framework designed for multi-agent active 3D reconstruction. Its methodology integrates map-centric observations with a reconstruction-aware reward function to train a shared policy for coordinated view selection.

**A. Problem Formulation**
The framework considers active 3D reconstruction in a static 3D indoor scene with a team of *N* agents. Each agent *i* receives a local observation and executes an action from a discrete set of motion primitives, which includes translation along x, y, z axes and rotation in yaw and pitch (roll is disabled). A mapping backend maintains a shared scene representation, which is a Truncated Signed Distance Function (TSDF) volume during training. This TSDF provides occupancy, frontier features, and reconstruction-aware reward signals to the policy. During deployment, policy parameters are fixed, and agents continue to use map-centric context from the fused team map to independently select actions. The collected viewpoints are then used for final 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) reconstruction and photometric evaluation, with 3DGS not involved in policy training. Coordination is implicit, arising from shared map evolution and shared policy parameters.

**B. Architecture Overview**
The COLMAR architecture operates in two distinct phases: training and deployment.
- **Training Phase:** Multiple agents collect map-centric observations from a shared TSDF managed by the mapping backend. A shared policy then predicts cooperative view actions based on these observations. Updates to the TSDF, resulting from agent actions, yield dense, reconstruction-aware rewards used for training the policy via Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO).
- **Deployment Phase:** The trained and frozen policy is used for independent, per-agent view planning. Agents receive the same type of map-centric observations derived from the fused team map and select actions without explicit inter-agent communication. The resulting trajectories are subsequently used to generate photorealistic 3D reconstructions with 3DGS for rendering-based evaluation.

**C. Policy Input and Network Architecture**
The policy input for each agent is structured into spatial and temporal modalities.
- **Spatial Input:** Comprises rendered depth images, an ego-centric occupancy map, and a global map that summarizes visited, free, occupied, and frontier regions. These spatial tensors are stacked channel-wise.
- **Temporal Input:** Includes the agent's current pose descriptor and a short history of recent poses.

The network architecture employs two encoding branches:
- A Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) processes the spatial input, generating a spatial latent representation ($h^{i}_{sp}$).
- A Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) encodes the pose history, which is then concatenated with the current pose and passed through a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) to produce a vector latent representation ($h^{i}_{vec}$).
These two latent representations are fused using a lightweight token-fusion module, which defaults to a transformer encoder performing self-attention over modality tokens. This produces a shared representation ($h^i$), which is then fed into actor and critic heads. The actor head outputs discrete-action logits ($z^i_t$), while the critic head provides a state-value estimate ($V^i_t$). The actor's output forms a categorical policy over a discrete action set, which consists of motion primitives for translation (up to 0.25m in x, y, z) and rotation (up to 15 degrees in yaw and pitch).

**D. Cooperative View Policy Learning**
COLMAR uses a single, shared policy (parameterized by $\theta$) for all agents and environments. During training, the policy receives map-centric observations ($o^i_t$) and predicts discrete action logits ($z^i_t$). The policy is modeled as a categorical distribution over the action set. At deployment, actions are sampled independently per agent using the frozen policy parameters. Coordination emerges implicitly from the shared map context and the shared policy parameters learned during joint training.

The policy parameters are updated using the standard PPO objective. This involves computing an importance ratio to measure policy deviation, estimating advantages using generalized advantage estimation, and forming a clipped PPO objective. The final training objective combines this policy term with a value-regression loss and entropy regularization, optimized over shuffled mini-batches for stability.

**E. Reconstruction-Aware Reward**
A dense cooperative reward function is designed to couple active view planning with reconstruction progress. This reward encourages agents to acquire non-redundant observations that expand the reconstructed surface while maintaining safe and spatially diverse team behavior. The total reward for agent *i* is formulated as:
$r_i = w_{ucov} g^{i}_{ucov} + b_{team} - p^{i}_{stag} - p^{i}_{col} - p^{i}_{crowd}$

- **Unique Coverage Gain ($g^{i}_{ucov}$):** This term is overlap-aware and computed from step-wise coverage growth ($\Delta\text{coverage}_i$) attributable to agent *i*'s action. $\Delta\text{coverage}_i$ is measured as a view-space gain from TSDF renders (pixels where rendered depth becomes closer after fusion). This gain is divided by $overlap_i$ (quantifying concurrent agent attention to the same region) and includes a `new-cell` bonus, encouraging complementary viewpoints and first-time discovery.
- **Team-Level Discovery Bonus ($b_{team}$):** This shared bonus rewards the team for discovering new cells ($n_{new}$) at the current step, normalized by the number of active agents ($n_{alive}$) to maintain consistent reward magnitudes across different team sizes.
- **Penalty Terms:**
 - $p^{i}_{stag}$: Penalizes stagnation, i.e., weak step motion or inefficient trajectories with small net displacement.
 - $p^{i}_{col}$: Penalizes unsafe contacts, including geometric collisions inferred from the map and motion-level checks.
 - $p^{i}_{crowd}$: Penalizes agents clustering too tightly, promoting spatial dispersion.

These reward components provide dense, long-horizon feedback, incentivizing novel map growth, coordination, and safe exploration while penalizing inefficient or unsafe behaviors. Training dynamics show stable optimization, with policy entropy decreasing and reward/map quality metrics improving.

### 4. Main findings and results

The evaluation of COLMAR was conducted on the GLEAM dataset for training and primary evaluation, with zero-shot generalization tested on the Replica dataset. Performance was measured using geometric metrics (global coverage, accuracy, Chamfer distance) and photometric metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, depth L1 error) where textured ground truth was available.

**A. Main Quantitative Comparison (on Replica)**
COLMAR demonstrated consistent and substantial improvements over all baselines, including random policy, greedy coverage, frontier-based exploration, and a single-agent PPO variant.
- **Geometric Quality:** COLMAR achieved 89.4% reconstruction accuracy (up to 54% higher than weaker baselines) and a Chamfer distance of 4.57 cm, significantly outperforming the single-agent PPO variant (77.6% accuracy, 6.80 cm Chamfer).
- **Exploration Efficiency:** It attained 82.6% coverage, indicating approximately 49% greater coverage compared to weaker baselines.
- **Photometric Quality:** COLMAR yielded the highest PSNR (37.61), SSIM (0.9786), and the lowest LPIPS (0.0312) and depth L1 errors (1.25 cm), demonstrating superior photorealistic reconstruction quality.

**B. Scaling With Number of Agents (on GLEAM)**
The study on agent scaling showed that COLMAR's performance improved consistently as the number of agents increased from 1 to 4.
- Coverage increased from 72.4% (1 agent) to 80.9% (4 agents).
- Chamfer distance decreased from 6.48 cm (1 agent) to 4.96 cm (4 agents).
These gains were most pronounced at smaller team sizes, with a tendency to saturate as more agents were added.

**C. Quality-Efficiency Trade-off (on GLEAM)**
COLMAR exhibited clear efficiency gains across varying sensing budgets (T, number of steps).
- With T increasing from 50 to 300, coverage improved from 65.1% to 80.9%.
- Chamfer distance dropped from 7.95 cm to 4.96 cm over the same budget range.
Improvements remained monotonic but showed a slowdown at larger budgets, suggesting diminishing returns beyond a certain exploration effort.

**D. Cross-Dataset Generalization**
COLMAR, trained on GLEAM, demonstrated effective zero-shot generalization to the Replica dataset. Performance on Replica was slightly better (e.g., 4.57 cm Chamfer) than on GLEAM (4.96 cm Chamfer), which is consistent with the typically simpler scene geometry of the Replica dataset.

**E. Qualitative Results**
Qualitative observations confirmed the learned coordination behavior. Agents under COLMAR spread out to complementary frontiers rather than repeatedly revisiting the same local regions. This spatial specialization contributed to better local surface consolidation and preserved team-level coverage diversity. The occupancy maps visually depicted steady growth of known free/occupied space and shrinking frontier regions, leading to more complete reconstructions with fewer holes. Failure cases were primarily noted in highly cluttered corners and regions with thin structures, where occlusion and long-horizon credit assignment presented challenges for optimal view allocation.

**F. Ablation Studies**
- **Reward Components:** Removing all penalty terms from the reward function resulted in the largest performance degradation (82.1% accuracy, 6.31 cm Chamfer, and a doubled collision rate from 0.07 to 0.14), underscoring the importance of regularization. The unique coverage gain and team-level discovery bonus also contributed positively to overall performance.
- **Cooperative vs. Non-Cooperative Training:** Multi-agent training, where agents learned cooperatively, significantly outperformed single-agent training (where a policy trained for one agent was deployed independently to multiple agents). Cooperative training achieved 88.2% accuracy and 4.96 cm Chamfer, compared to 84.5% accuracy and 5.89 cm Chamfer for single-agent training, highlighting the benefits of joint optimization.
- **Global vs. Local Input Ablation:** The full set of inputs (combining local depth and ego-centric occupancy with global pose-grid/map cues) yielded the best performance. Policies trained with only local or only global cues performed worse, confirming that these input modalities are complementary for effective view policy learning.

### 5. Significance and potential impact

The COLMAR framework presents a significant advancement in the field of multi-agent active 3D reconstruction. Its core contribution lies in demonstrating that a learned cooperative view policy, driven by a carefully designed reconstruction-aware objective, can substantially enhance both the quality and efficiency of 3D reconstructions. By formulating viewpoint allocation as a shared policy optimization over map-centric observations and introducing a dense reward mechanism that promotes overlap-aware coverage, team-level discovery, and collision-safe exploration, COLMAR addresses critical limitations of prior work, particularly the coordination inefficiencies and underutilization of reconstruction diagnostics.

The consistent outperformance of COLMAR against strong heuristic and non-cooperative learning baselines, across various metrics including geometric accuracy, coverage, and photorealistic rendering quality, indicates its effectiveness. The ability of the policy to scale with team size and exhibit good zero-shot generalization across different datasets further underscores its robustness and practical potential.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Enhanced Autonomous Systems:** COLMAR can improve the capabilities of autonomous robots in diverse applications requiring accurate 3D models, such as industrial inspection, rapid digital twin generation, search-and-rescue missions in complex environments, and sophisticated embodied intelligence platforms. By enabling more efficient and higher-quality data acquisition, it could reduce operational time and costs.
- **Foundation for Cooperative Robotics:** The framework provides a principled approach for cooperative multi-agent task allocation in perceptual tasks, where agents implicitly coordinate through shared environmental understanding and a common policy without explicit communication during decision-making. This could inspire further research in decentralized, scalable multi-robot systems.
- **Advancements in Reinforcement Learning for Robotics:** The proposed reconstruction-aware reward function, leveraging dense feedback from incremental mapping updates, offers a model for designing more effective reward signals for robotics tasks involving iterative environmental interaction and complex objectives.

**Limitations:**
Despite its strengths, the current study has several limitations:
- **Static Environments:** The current evaluation is restricted to static indoor scenes. Performance in dynamic environments, with moving objects or changing layouts, remains unexplored and may degrade.
- **Sensor Imperfections:** The framework's robustness to real-world sensor noise, calibration errors, and occlusions has not been fully characterized in the paper.
- **Domain Shift:** While zero-shot generalization to Replica was positive, significant domain shifts between training and deployment environments could still impact performance.
- **Scaling Saturation:** As observed in the experiments, the gains in performance tend to saturate as the team size increases, suggesting a point where additional agents provide diminishing returns without further algorithmic or environmental adaptation.
- **Real-World Deployment Challenges:** Practical deployment on physical robots would necessitate robust multi-agent localization, reliable communication infrastructure (even if not used for active decision-making, it's vital for map sharing), and safety-aware low-level control, which are beyond the scope of this view planning framework.

**Future Work:**
The authors suggest future research directions including investigating real-robot deployment with improved robustness to sensor imperfections, exploring communication-aware coordination under bandwidth constraints, and extending the framework to dynamic scenes with temporally consistent mapping and reward design. These efforts would address the identified limitations and move COLMAR closer to real-world applicability.
