# PAWS: Perception of Articulation in the Wild at Scale from Egocentric Videos

> [!summary] Summary
> PAWS is a training-free pipeline that extracts 3D object articulation (motion type, axis, origin) from in-the-wild monocular egocentric video, fusing smoothed hand-trajectory cues, multi-view geometric line/Manhattan recovery, and two-stage VLM reasoning; it beats supervised and tracking baselines and enables downstream finetuning plus real robot cabinet/drawer manipulation.

## Problem

- Supervised articulation predictors require costly high-quality 3D annotations and scanned meshes, limiting scalability and generalization to unseen objects and scenes.
- Static-input methods lack access to object motion cues, forcing reliance on learned category priors that fail under occlusion or novel geometries.
- Existing dynamic and tracking-based methods assume multi-view captures, stable lighting, depth sensors, or limited occlusion, breaking down on noisy monocular in-the-wild egocentric video.

## Method

- **Dynamic Interaction Perception**: ==MANO==-based 3D hand-trajectory extraction refined via ==Ornstein-Uhlenbeck/Rauch-Tung-Striebel smoothing== plus voxel-confidence coarse object localization from hand contact.
- **Static Scene Geometry Recovery**: multi-view ==line triangulation== with ==LO-RANSAC== yields revolute-axis candidates while ==Manhattan-world normal clustering== yields prismatic-axis candidates from reselected views.
- Two-stage **VLM-guided reasoning**: temporal motion-type VQA classifies revolute versus prismatic, then ==Set-of-Marks== spatial VQA grounds the physically plausible axis among geometric candidates.

## Results

- On HD-EPIC, scene-aggregated PAWS-s reaches **71.43%** match accuracy and **0.36** MAO score, beating ArtiPoint's **70.66%** match and **0.00** MAO.
- Ablation shows VLM axis reasoning raises motion-axis accuracy from **0.30** to **0.33** and full-parameter accuracy from **0.15** to **0.18** over geometry-only inference.
- Finetuning USDNet on PAWS-extracted EgoArti annotations lifts articulation-prediction MS from **0.02** zero-shot to **0.13**, improving cross-dataset generalization to Arti4D.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Hand-object contact trajectories are a robust physical grounding signal that survives monocular noise where pure geometric or tracking cues fail.
> - Combining foundation-model commonsense reasoning with geometric priors resolves ambiguity better than either signal alone, especially for low-texture or occluded objects.
> - Training-free, automatically extracted annotations from unlabeled egocentric video can substitute for costly 3D supervision while improving downstream prediction and robotic manipulation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses the challenge of articulation perception, which involves recovering the motion and kinematic structure of articulated objects (e.g., drawers, doors, cupboards) within 3D scenes. This capability is fundamental for advanced 3D scene understanding, with applications in robotics, simulation, and animation.

Existing approaches to articulation perception can be broadly categorized into methods relying on static inputs and those utilizing dynamic inputs. Learning-based methods for articulation prediction and generation often require extensive, manually annotated 3D datasets for training. These datasets are costly and labor-intensive to acquire, particularly at the scene level, limiting the scalability and diversity of models. Methods that infer articulation from static 3D geometry or single RGB(D) images typically rely on learned priors or category-level regularities, struggling with complex geometries, occlusions, or novel object categories due to the inherent lack of motion cues.

Dynamic input methods leverage multi-view observations, temporal data, or additional sensing modalities like depth. However, these often necessitate carefully collected data, stable lighting conditions, limited occlusions, or specific sensor setups. Tracking-based methods can degrade under occlusion, low texture, or motion blur. Approaches modeling human-object interactions using parametric human body models can suffer from self-occlusions.

This research distinguishes itself by focusing on in-the-wild egocentric videos, which offer large-scale, diverse, and natural human-object interaction data. While such videos are rich in information, their monocular, noisy, and unstructured nature, coupled with varying illumination and frequent occlusions, makes conventional multi-view or temporal methods less effective. PAWS positions itself as a training-free solution that directly extracts scene-level articulation from these challenging monocular egocentric videos, leveraging human hand-object interactions as a primary cue. This approach aims to bypass the limitations of labor-intensive 3D data annotation and controlled data collection, contributing a scalable method for articulation understanding in unconstrained real-world environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a method for accurately perceiving and localizing the articulation of human-made objects, such as furniture, directly from in-the-wild egocentric RGB videos. The system aims to estimate key articulation parameters: motion type (prismatic or revolute), motion axis, and motion origin.

The motivation for this work stems from several limitations in the current state of 3D scene understanding and articulation perception:

- **Scalability and Diversity of Training Data:** Current learning-based methods rely heavily on supervised training with high-quality 3D data and manual annotations. Collecting and annotating such data, especially at the scene level, is expensive and time-consuming. This dependency restricts the diversity of objects and scenes that models can generalize to, making real-world deployment challenging.
- **Limitations of Static and Controlled Dynamic Inputs:** Methods using static inputs inherently lack motion cues, making it difficult to reliably recover articulated motion and kinematic structure. Existing dynamic input methods often require controlled environments, multi-stage observations, stable lighting, limited occlusions, or additional sensors (e.g., RGB-D), which are not representative of unconstrained daily life.
- **Untapped Potential of Egocentric Videos:** In-the-wild egocentric videos contain abundant, naturally occurring human-object interaction information and implicit object kinematic structures. These videos are diverse and large-scale, offering a rich source of data. However, their monocular, noisy, unstructured nature, and variable lighting make them difficult to leverage with conventional computer vision techniques.
- **Bridging the Gap for Downstream Applications:** Reliable articulation perception is crucial for various downstream applications in robotics (e.g., robot manipulation of articulated furniture), 3D animation, and simulation. Enabling robots to understand and interact with articulated objects in human environments requires a robust, scalable method for identifying and characterizing these motions.

To address these challenges, the authors propose PAWS, motivated by the insight that human hand-object interactions provide strong physical cues about an object's articulation. By integrating estimated 3D hand trajectories with reconstructed scene geometry and leveraging foundation models for reasoning, the system aims to recover plausible articulation motion and kinematic structure from noisy, monocular egocentric video data without requiring task-specific supervised training. The ultimate goal is to generate high-quality articulation annotations at scale, which can then benefit existing articulation prediction models and facilitate real-world robotic manipulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The PAWS framework is a fully automatic, training-free pipeline designed to infer articulation types and parameters from untrimmed, in-the-wild egocentric RGB videos and associated language descriptions. The pipeline comprises four core components.

### 4. Dynamic Interaction Perception

This module processes a temporal sub-sequence of the video corresponding to a specific action (e.g., "open the cupboard").

- **Metric-Aware 3D Hand Reconstruction:** A frame-based 3D hand reconstruction method is applied to identify frames with visible hands and reconstruct the MANO hand pose. Fingertip landmarks (thumb, index, middle) are averaged to represent the 3D hand contact point over observed timestamps. This method directly estimates 3D hand poses, avoiding reliance on depth scaling.
- **Stochastic Hand Trajectories Refinement:** Raw hand trajectories, which can be noisy (due to estimation errors, pre/post-contact motions, unconstrained human movement), are refined. Hand-object contact detection filters out non-interaction segments. The remaining trajectory is modeled using a linear Gaussian state-space model with an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck (OU) motion prior, followed by forward Kalman filtering with $\chi^2$ outlier rejection and Rauch–Tung–Striebel (RTS) smoothing. This process yields refined, articulation-related trajectories crucial for determining the motion origin.
- **Object Coarse Localization:** A coarse 3D scene representation is reconstructed from the interaction clip using a method like Map-Anything. The resulting point cloud is discretized into a 3D voxel grid. Voxels observed consistently across multiple camera views are designated as high-confidence voxels, providing a robust spatial prior for the manipulated object, even with occlusions.

### 5. Static Scene Geometry Recovery

This module addresses the limited viewpoint variation and frequent occlusions inherent in egocentric videos by employing a coarse-to-fine geometric recovery strategy. It leverages motion type priors (revolute or prismatic) to reconstruct candidate articulation axes.

- **Frustum Intersection and View Reselection:** Based on the coarse object localization (high-confidence voxels), a subset of global camera views (N=50) is sampled from the full video. Frames whose camera frustums intersect the high-confidence voxel region are retained. Farthest point sampling on camera positions ensures spatial coverage, and object-specific semantic masks are used to refine view selection and ensure consistent object localization.
- **Multi-View Line Triangulation for Revolute Priors:** For revolute motion, 2D line segments are detected in selected views. Metric-scale depth maps are estimated monocularly. Sampled 2D pixels on these lines are unprojected to 3D using intrinsics and depth, then transformed to world coordinates. By aggregating 3D point sets across views based on 2D line correspondences, dense 3D point tracks are obtained. LO-RANSAC is then applied to fit 3D line models to these tracks, generating candidate revolute axis proposals.
- **Manhattan Spatial Priors for Prismatic Motion:** For prismatic motion, the Manhattan-world assumption is adopted. Surface normal maps and metric depth maps from the geometry estimation model are used to identify dominant orthogonal directions. Surface normals are aggregated and clustered across sampled images to extract these directions, which serve as candidate prismatic motion axes.

### 6. Vision-Language Guided Motion Reasoning

This module uses a Vision-Language Model (VLM, specifically GPT-4o) via a two-stage Visual Question Answering (VQA) pipeline to infer motion types and select plausible articulation axes.

- **Temporal Motion Type Classification:** A sparse sampling strategy is applied to the interaction clip, selecting K frames. For each frame, the VLM identifies the interacted object and its frame-level motion type. An LLM aggregates these per-frame predictions to make a robust global motion type decision (revolute or prismatic), filtering noise.
- **Spatial Axis Grounding via Set-of-Marks VQA:** To select the most plausible articulation axis from the 3D line candidates, the problem is framed as a constrained multiple-choice VQA. 3D line candidates are projected to 2D and filtered by the object's semantic mask. The N prominent candidates are overlaid as numbered visual prompts, and the VLM is queried to select the index that best represents the semantic articulation hinge based on physical commonsense.

### 7. Joint Articulation Inference

The final articulation parameters $\phi_i = \{c_i, a_i, o_i\}$ are estimated by integrating dynamic hand-object interaction information and static geometric structure. PCA and RANSAC are used to ensure robust parameter estimation.

- **Prismatic Motion:** If $c_i = \text{prismatic}$, the motion axis $a_i$ is selected as the dominant Manhattan direction that best aligns with the primary direction of the refined hand motion. The motion origin $o_i$ is set to the initial hand contact point.
- **Revolute Motion:** If $c_i = \text{revolute}$, the optimal rigid axis $a_i$ is selected from the VLM-verified 3D axis proposals by minimizing the variance of the orthogonal distance from the refined hand trajectory points to the candidate line. This enforces a consistent rotation radius. The motion origin $o_i$ is calculated as the orthogonal projection of the hand trajectory centroid onto the selected axis.

The pipeline is designed to be robust to scale variations and uses a single set of hyperparameters across all datasets.

### 8. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of PAWS was conducted through extensive experiments on public datasets, including HD-EPIC, Arti4D, and Epic-Fields, focusing on articulation perception accuracy and benefits for downstream tasks.

### 9. Accuracy of Articulation Perception

- **Datasets and Metrics:** The method was quantitatively evaluated on curated subsets of HD-EPIC (313 interaction clips, 80 furniture instances across 9 scenes) and Arti4D (standard evaluation splits across 34 scenes). Epic-Fields was primarily used for qualitative evaluation. Metrics included Match (%), which indicates the percentage of successfully detected articulation instances, and component-wise accuracy (M: motion type, MA: motion type + motion axis, MAO: motion type + motion axis + motion origin), evaluated under two settings: conditioned on successful detection and unconditionally over all clips.
- **Baselines:** PAWS was compared against Articulation3D (including two adapted variants, Articulation3D$^*$ and Articulation3D$^{**}$), ArtiPoint, Articulate-Anything, and iTACO.
- **Comparison on HD-EPIC Dataset:** PAWS consistently outperformed all baselines on HD-EPIC. Articulation3D variants struggled significantly due to the domain gap between their training data (Internet videos with sufficient viewpoint coverage) and in-the-wild egocentric videos (incomplete observations, rapidly changing viewpoints). ArtiPoint performed sub-optimally on HD-EPIC due to monocular depth prediction errors, lack of robust tracking points, and heavy occlusions, which are common in in-the-wild scenarios. PAWS achieved a Match (%) of 55.38%, with MAO of 0.20 for individual interactions and 71.43% (Match), 0.36 (MAO) for scene-level aggregated results (PAWS-s), demonstrating its robustness in challenging conditions.
- **Comparison on Arti4D Dataset:** On Arti4D, PAWS achieved comparable performance to HD-EPIC. ArtiPoint showed substantially better performance on Arti4D than on HD-EPIC, which was attributed to Arti4D's characteristics (more textured furniture, visual markers, stable hand/camera motion, and accurate depth information), making it more suitable for object tracking-based methods. Despite this, among successfully detected articulations, PAWS maintained competitive performance.

### 10. Ablation Study

Experiments on the HD-EPIC dataset analyzed the contribution of individual pipeline components:
- **Impact of Hand Contact Constraints:** Incorporating hand trajectory smoothing and contact-based filtering resulted in a slight improvement in accuracy. Trajectory smoothing reduced noise from the hand motion estimator, and the hand contact detector effectively filtered out non-interaction trajectory points.
- **Impact of VLM Reasoning:** VLM-based reasoning improved articulation accuracy compared to geometry-only inference. Specifically, applying the VLM filter increased MA from 0.30 to 0.33 and MAO from 0.15 to 0.18. This indicated that language-guided reasoning assisted in selecting more plausible articulation axes. However, VLM-based filtering alone was not sufficient for full articulation determination, particularly in ambiguous cases (e.g., low-texture furniture), highlighting the necessity of grounding articulation inference in physical hand-object interaction cues.

### 11. Downstream Applications

- **Cross-Data Set Generalization:** PAWS was used to generate articulation annotations on HD-EPIC, creating a dataset called EgoArti. Finetuning USDNet (an articulation prediction model) with EgoArti data significantly outperformed the zero-shot baseline on both EgoArti (in-domain) and Arti4D (cross-data set) datasets. This demonstrated that the automatically generated annotations provided high-quality supervision, improving both in-domain performance and generalization across datasets.
- **Robotic Manipulation:** The effectiveness of PAWS was validated in real-world settings using a Boston Dynamics Spot robot. Articulation parameters extracted by PAWS from human demonstration videos of an IKEA toy kitchen (cupboards, drawers) were used to infer opening orientation and suitable contact points. This information parameterized a Dynamic Movement Primitive (DMP), enabling the Spot robot to successfully execute desired opening or closing actions in an open-loop fashion.

The study identified limitations, including potential misclassification of visually ambiguous objects by VLMs and insufficient hand trajectory data in short or rapidly performed interactions (e.g., closing actions).

### 12. Significance and Potential Impact

The PAWS framework represents an advancement in articulation perception by offering a novel approach to extracting articulated object motion and structure from in-the-wild egocentric videos. Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

- **Scalable and Autonomous Annotation:** PAWS provides a fully automatic, training-free pipeline for generating 3D articulation annotations. This addresses a critical bottleneck in the field: the high cost and labor intensity associated with manual 3D data annotation and controlled data collection. By leveraging readily available egocentric videos, PAWS enables scalable annotation of articulation data, which can significantly expand the volume and diversity of training data for learning-based models.
- **Robustness in Unconstrained Environments:** The method demonstrates consistent performance in challenging, unconstrained egocentric settings, which are characterized by monocular input, noise, varying illumination, rapid camera motion, and frequent occlusions. This robustness contrasts with prior methods that often require controlled environments or specific sensing modalities, making PAWS more suitable for real-world applications.
- **Leveraging Hand-Object Interactions and Foundation Models:** The work highlights the power of integrating physical cues from human hand-object interactions with common-sense knowledge embedded in Vision-Language Models (VLMs). This combination allows PAWS to infer motion types and select plausible articulation axes, bridging the gap between low-level visual information and high-level kinematic reasoning.
- **Enhanced Downstream Applications:**
 - **Robotics:** The extracted articulation parameters can directly inform robot manipulation tasks. The demonstration with the Boston Dynamics Spot robot illustrates how PAWS can enable robots to understand and interact with articulated furniture in human environments, moving towards more intelligent and adaptable robotic systems.
 - **3D Scene Understanding and Simulation:** By providing richer, instance-specific articulation information, PAWS contributes to a more complete 3D scene understanding. This can benefit 3D animation, virtual reality, and physical simulation, allowing for the creation of more interactive and realistic digital environments.
 - **Articulation Prediction Models:** The high-quality, automatically generated annotations from PAWS can be used to fine-tune existing articulation prediction models, improving their performance and generalization capabilities across different datasets and object categories.

Overall, PAWS offers a practical and scalable solution for articulation perception, reducing reliance on expensive 3D supervision and controlled data. It establishes a pathway for future research to further exploit human interaction data for building more capable and adaptable artificial intelligence systems in robotics and computer vision.
