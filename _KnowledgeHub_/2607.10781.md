# Is Energy Guidance All You Need? Training-Free Norm Injection for Driving World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the Technical University of Munich demonstrate that training-free energy guidance can effectively steer the ego trajectory of a rectified-flow driving world model, reducing deviation from a braking target from 13.6 meters to 1.3 meters. However, the study reveals that this trajectory control does not translate into coherent changes in the jointly generated video stream due to insufficient cross-stream architectural coupling.

## Problem

- Autonomous driving world models struggle with dynamic controllability for safety-critical tasks, such as adhering to traffic norms or collision avoidance.
- Current control methods require expensive fine-tuning or pre-conditioning, making deployed models inflexible and unable to adapt to new rules at sampling time.
- It is challenging to ensure that steering a generated ego trajectory coherently influences the simultaneously generated video stream in multimodal world models.

## Method

- A training-free ==energy guidance== mechanism is integrated into a frozen ==rectified-flow== video-diffusion world model, built upon an **Open-Sora 2.0** backbone, at sampling time.
- Driving norms are encoded as differentiable ==energy functions== that are evaluated on the model's jointly generated future video and ego trajectory.
- During ==rectified-flow sampling==, an ==energy-descent step== perturbs the estimated clean trajectory towards lower-energy states, applying dynamic control without retraining the backbone.

## Results

- Energy guidance successfully steered the ego trajectory, reducing its mean deviation from a counterfactual braking target from **13.6 meters** (unguided) to **1.3 meters** (guided) across **35 clips**.
- Despite effective trajectory steering, the generated video stream did not consistently follow the adjusted trajectory; optical flow differences between guided and unguided videos were negligible (less than **2%**).
- The decoupling of video and trajectory was attributed to architectural limitations, specifically the bypassing of **38 single-stream blocks** in the Open-Sora 2.0 backbone, which limited essential cross-stream interaction.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dynamic, knowledge-based control over complex driving world models is achievable entirely at sampling time using training-free energy guidance, bypassing the need for costly retraining.
> - A single, sampling-time energy function can significantly and effectively steer the planned ego trajectory within a complex video world model.
> - Achieving coherent, end-to-end alignment between planned actions and generated visual outputs in multimodal generative models requires robust and deep cross-stream architectural coupling.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a challenge in the field of autonomous driving, specifically concerning the controllability of driving world models built on large video-diffusion backbones. These models are capable of generating realistic sensor streams, which makes them valuable for simulation and planning applications. However, ensuring that the generated scenes adhere to specific traffic norms or safety regulations remains a significant hurdle.

Current approaches to control these models typically involve either fine-tuning the diffusion backbone whenever a new constraint is introduced or conditioning the model on pre-defined layouts (such as maps or agent bounding boxes) that are "baked in" during the training phase. Both of these strategies require substantial computational resources, high-quality data, and couple new driving knowledge directly to the training process, limiting the model's adaptability once deployed. A deployed model cannot dynamically add or re-prioritize rules at sampling time with these methods.

This paper positions itself within the emerging domain of training-free guidance for diffusion models. Prior research has demonstrated that a frozen diffusion prior can be steered towards samples satisfying an external objective by perturbing its sampling trajectory using the gradient of a differentiable criterion. This concept has been explored for various generative tasks, including image and low-dimensional trajectory generation.

Within the context of robotic world models for driving, existing methods often achieve control through structured conditioning during training (e.g., GAIA-2) or by generating multiple futures and then selecting the most appropriate one (e.g., Drive-WM). While some recent models jointly generate ego trajectories alongside video (e.g., Epona, DriveLaW), their control mechanisms typically rely on learned conditioning rather than dynamic steering at sampling time. Similarly, rule-aware trajectory generation methods like CTG and Diffusion-Planner compile traffic rules into differentiable guidance for trajectory-only diffusion models, but they do not typically operate on frozen world models that jointly generate video.

The distinguishing aspect of this work lies in its specific conjunction: applying training-free energy guidance to a rectified-flow world model that jointly generates both future video and an ego trajectory. Unlike previous efforts, it aims to steer the jointly generated ego trajectory solely at sampling time without knowledge-specific conditioning or retraining of the backbone. Furthermore, a core contribution is the investigation into whether such trajectory guidance effectively propagates to and influences the generated video stream through the model's internal self-attention mechanisms, a question largely unaddressed by prior work.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the practical limitations of current driving world models. While these models excel at generating realistic visual data, their lack of dynamic controllability for safety-critical applications, such as ensuring adherence to traffic laws or preventing collisions, renders them inadequate for direct deployment. The authors identify the high cost and inflexibility associated with retraining backbones or pre-conditioning models on specific layouts as critical weaknesses. These methods prevent a deployed system from adapting to new or reprioritized rules in real-time.

Against this background, the central hypothesis of the paper is to investigate whether control over driving world models necessitates training at all, or if it can be achieved entirely at sampling time.

The key objectives are articulated through three contributions:

1. **Framing Driving-Norm Compliance as Training-Free Energy Guidance:** The first objective is to formalize how driving-norm compliance can be integrated into a frozen rectified-flow world model as training-free energy guidance. This involves evaluating differentiable energy functions on the model's jointly generated future video and planned ego trajectory, and applying these energies exclusively during the sampling process, without any retraining of the diffusion backbone.
2. **Experimental Validation of Trajectory Steering:** The second objective is to empirically test this hypothesis. This involves building a world model using an Open-Sora 2.0 backbone and demonstrating that training-free energy guidance can effectively steer the ego trajectory. A specific demonstration involves guiding the trajectory towards a counterfactual braking target, simulating a rear-end collision avoidance scenario, which the model was not explicitly trained to handle.
3. **Analysis of Video-Trajectory Coupling and Identification of Obstacles:** The third and critical objective is to address the question of whether steering the ego trajectory through energy guidance also causes the jointly generated video to coherently follow the steered trajectory. The paper aims to examine the effectiveness of the backbone's joint self-attention mechanism in propagating this control signal to the visual output. Based on the findings, the objective includes identifying architectural limitations preventing end-to-end control and proposing potential solutions.

In essence, the research seeks to develop a more flexible and less computationally intensive method for controlling driving world models, while rigorously assessing the extent to which internal planning adjustments translate into consistent visual outputs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research centers on integrating training-free energy guidance into a rectified-flow driving world model.

**A. Backbone and Model Architecture:**
The core of the system is a frozen video-diffusion world model built upon the Open-Sora 2.0 MM-DiT (DC-AE variant) backbone. This backbone, originally designed for video generation, comprises 19 double-stream blocks and 38 single-stream blocks. The authors repurpose the 'text stream' of this architecture for ego trajectory prediction, while the 'image stream' continues to predict future videos. The ego trajectory is represented as (x, y, θ, v) over 40 future steps. The model processes noisy future frames and a noisy ego trajectory, conditioned on observed past frames, without requiring explicit agent or map information during training.

**B. Rectified-Flow Sampling and Energy Guidance:**
The model utilizes a rectified-flow generative process, which involves integrating a deterministic ODE velocity field from a noisy state (t=1) to a clean sample (t=0). A key aspect of rectified-flow models is the ability to obtain a one-step estimate of the clean sample, denoted as $\hat{x}_0$, from the current noisy state $x_t$ and the predicted velocity $v_\theta(x_t, t)$.

The energy guidance mechanism operates exclusively at sampling time, without altering the pretrained weights of the backbone.
1. **Energy Function:** Driving knowledge is encoded as a differentiable energy function, $E(\cdot)$, which is evaluated on the predicted clean ego trajectory $\hat{x}_0$. For this paper, a single energy function is used: a Mean Squared Error (MSE) attraction towards a counterfactual braking target. This target simulates a slow lead vehicle, compelling the ego trajectory to brake.
2. **Guidance Step:** At each sampling step, the model first estimates $\hat{x}_0$. Then, an energy-descent step is performed in the clean-trajectory space: $\hat{x}'_0 = \hat{x}_0 - \lambda(t) \nabla_{\hat{x}_0} E(\hat{x}_0)$, where $\lambda(t)$ is an asymmetric schedule that dynamically adjusts the guidance strength, being zero near the beginning and end of the sampling process. This step effectively perturbs the estimated clean trajectory.
3. **Velocity Correction:** The perturbed clean estimate $\hat{x}'_0$ is then converted back into a velocity correction, which is added to the backbone's predicted velocity. This modified velocity guides the ODE integration process towards lower-energy trajectories.
4. **Stabilization:** To mitigate issues related to unreliable $\hat{x}_0$ estimates early in sampling or under multi-modality, the per-step guidance correction is capped at a fixed multiple of the backbone's own velocity.
5. **Computational Overhead:** The energy calculation and gradient descent step only involve the low-dimensional ego trajectory and do not require backpropagation through the large backbone, resulting in negligible computational overhead during sampling.

**C. Fine-tuning Details and Training:**
The Open-Sora 2.0 backbone is kept entirely frozen. To adapt it for driving world modeling and ego trajectory prediction, Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) adapters (rank 16, $\alpha = 32$) are attached to the modulation, attention, and MLP projections of both video and trajectory streams. Additionally, newly introduced modules, such as the ego encoder, ego output head, ego timestep MLP, and past-observation pooler, are initialized to zeros and trained from scratch. In total, 88.1 million parameters are trainable out of the 11 billion-parameter backbone.

The model is fine-tuned using a joint rectified-flow loss, which is a combination of MSE on the predicted velocities for both the video and ego streams. Optimization uses AdamW with gradient clipping and bf16 autocasting. Classifier-free guidance is also implemented during fine-tuning by occasionally dropping past conditioning to enhance video sharpness. The training data consists of 12-frame clips from the nuScenes v1.0-trainval dataset, captured by the front camera at 256x448 resolution. A frozen DC-AE autoencoder compresses video frames into latent representations.

**D. Experimental Design:**
The core experiment involves evaluating the effect of energy guidance on 35 sampled clips. For each clip, two rollouts are generated from identical noise: one unguided (factual) and one guided with the counterfactual braking energy (counterfactual). The performance is assessed by:
1. **Trajectory Deviation:** Measuring the L2 distance of the ego plan to the brake target across future steps.
2. **Video Consistency:** Quantifying the difference in per-frame optical flow between the guided and unguided videos to determine if the steered trajectory translates into visual changes.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments yielded two primary findings concerning the effect of training-free energy guidance on the driving world model:

1. **Effective Trajectory Steering:** The energy guidance mechanism successfully steered the planned ego trajectory. Across 35 sampled clips, the guided (counterfactual) trajectories converged significantly closer to the intended brake target compared to the unguided (factual) trajectories. Quantitatively, the guided trajectories ended a mean of 1.3 meters from the brake target, whereas the unguided trajectories ended an average of 13.6 meters away. This demonstrates that a single, sampling-time energy function can effectively influence the planning component of a complex video world model without any retraining. Per-clip visualizations of bird's-eye-view trajectories consistently show the counterfactual plan tracking the braked target, while the factual plan continues along its unguided path.

2. **Decoupling of Video and Trajectory:** Despite the successful steering of the ego trajectory, the generated video stream did not follow the adjusted trajectory. The per-frame optical flow of the guided video was found to be statistically indistinguishable from that of the unguided video. The measured difference in optical flow between the counterfactual and factual videos was negligible, typically less than 2% and within the natural variation observed across clips. This indicates that even though the ego vehicle's planned movement was significantly altered to simulate braking, the visual content of the generated video did not reflect this change in a discernible manner.

**Identified Cause of Decoupling:**
The authors attribute this decoupling to specific architectural modifications made to the Open-Sora 2.0 MM-DiT backbone. Specifically, the 38 single-stream blocks, which are part of the original Open-Sora 2.0 architecture and designed for joint processing, were bypassed in their modified model. Consequently, the interaction between the ego trajectory stream and the video stream primarily occurred only within the 19 double-stream blocks through joint self-attention. This limited cross-stream coupling proved insufficient for the steering signal from the ego trajectory to effectively propagate and influence the generated video pixels. The lack of deeper, more integrated joint processing is identified as a central obstacle to achieving end-to-end controllable rollouts where visual outputs align with planned actions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents several significant implications and potential impacts for the development of autonomous driving systems and generative AI models.

**Validation of Training-Free Control:** The primary significance lies in demonstrating the feasibility of controlling complex driving world models at sampling time through training-free energy guidance. This approach successfully steers the planned ego trajectory without requiring any fine-tuning or retraining of the large, pre-existing video-diffusion backbone. This outcome validates the hypothesis that knowledge injection and behavioral control do not necessarily require costly and time-consuming training phases.

**Enhanced Flexibility and Adaptability:** By decoupling control from training, the proposed method offers substantial flexibility. A deployed world model could dynamically add, remove, or re-prioritize driving norms and safety constraints during operation. This addresses a critical limitation of current methods, which are typically rigid once trained, enabling more adaptable and context-aware autonomous driving systems. For instance, a vehicle could react to unforeseen circumstances or temporary regulations by incorporating new rules without requiring a full model update.

**Reduced Computational and Data Costs:** Eliminating the need for retraining or extensive data re-collection for every new rule translates into significant reductions in computational resources and data acquisition efforts. This makes the development and deployment of controllable driving world models more efficient and scalable.

**Identification of Architectural Limitations for End-to-End Control:** A crucial contribution is the identification of a specific architectural bottleneck: the limited propagation of trajectory control to the video stream within the current model design. This finding highlights that simply having joint self-attention between streams is not always sufficient to ensure coherent, end-to-end alignment between planned actions and generated visual outputs in complex generative models. This insight is valuable for future architecture design in multimodal world models, emphasizing the need for robust cross-stream coupling.

**Direction for Future Research and Development:**
The paper proposes a clear path forward by suggesting that re-routing both video and trajectory streams through the full set of single-stream blocks (in addition to double-stream blocks) from the Open-Sora 2.0 backbone could provide the necessary deeper joint processing to ensure the video follows the steered trajectory. This specific architectural modification offers a concrete solution to the identified problem.

Furthermore, the work opens avenues for developing more sophisticated rule-based control systems:
- **Composition of Multiple Driving Norms:** The authors envision composing a library of differentiable energies representing various driving norms (e.g., collision avoidance, lane keeping, speed limits, comfort). This would allow for comprehensive control.
- **Prioritized Guidance:** Addressing the potential conflicts between multiple norms (e.g., comfort vs. safety), the paper proposes imposing a priority hierarchy at the gradient level. This would ensure that higher-priority constraints are met without being overridden by lower-priority ones, enhancing safety and robustness.

In summary, this research advances the state of controllable driving world models by proposing a flexible, training-free guidance mechanism. While it reveals a current architectural limitation in achieving full video-trajectory coherence, it also provides clear insights and a path toward developing truly end-to-end controllable, adaptable, and safer generative AI for autonomous systems.
