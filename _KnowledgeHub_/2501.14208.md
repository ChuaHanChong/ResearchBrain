# You Only Teach Once: Learn One-Shot Bimanual Robotic Manipulation from Video Demonstrations

> [!summary] Summary
> The You Only Teach Once (YOTO) framework enables dual-arm robots to learn complex bimanual manipulation skills from a single human video demonstration, leveraging binocular stereo vision for robust keypose extraction and a customized diffusion policy. It achieves an average success rate of nearly 76.8% across five intricate long-horizon tasks and demonstrates strong generalization capabilities to novel objects.

## Problem

- Bimanual robotic manipulation is challenging due to the complexities of coordinating two arms, managing high-dimensional action spaces, and ensuring collision avoidance.
- Traditional methods for teaching bimanual tasks, such as teleoperation, are often laborious, data-intensive, and difficult to scale.
- Learning from human action videos is promising but often limited by the instability of directly extracting continuous, noisy human trajectories and the need for large, diverse datasets.

## Method

- A one-shot teaching paradigm extracts stable 3D hand keyposes and a motion mask from a single binocular human video demonstration, simplifying complex human movements into robot-executable actions.
- A data proliferation strategy rapidly generates diverse training demonstrations through automated real-robot rollouts with precise keyframe editing and virtual geometric transformations of object point clouds.
- A customized Bimanual Diffusion Policy (BiDP) learns robust actions by focusing observations on manipulated objects, predicting sparse keyposes, and reorganizing the action space with a motion mask to handle dual-arm coordination.

## Results

- YOTO's keyframe extraction method yields stable 3D keyposes, crucial for successful robot action injection, addressing issues with direct 3D hand mesh reconstruction from monocular views.
- The Bimanual Diffusion Policy (BiDP) achieved an average success rate of nearly 76.8% across five complex bimanual tasks, significantly outperforming strong baselines (e.g., EquiBot at 23.4%, ACT at 5.7%).
- The framework demonstrated satisfactory out-of-domain generalization to novel, unseen objects and successfully applied to two additional bimanual tasks with just one-shot human teaching, showcasing its versatility.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A single human video demonstration, when processed into robust 3D keyposes and combined with binocular vision, contains sufficient information for one-shot skill transfer to dual-arm robots.
> - Rapid data proliferation through automated real-robot rollouts and virtual geometric transformations is essential for overcoming data scarcity and achieving high performance in bimanual manipulation.
> - Customizing diffusion policies by shrinking observations to task-relevant objects, predicting sparse keyposes, and reorganizing the action space with a motion mask significantly enhances their effectiveness for long-horizon bimanual tasks.
