# You Only Teach Once: Learn One-Shot Bimanual Robotic Manipulation from Video Demonstrations

> [!summary] Summary
> The You Only Teach Once (YOTO) framework enables dual-arm robots to learn complex bimanual manipulation skills from a single human video demonstration, leveraging binocular stereo vision for robust keypose extraction and a customized diffusion policy. It achieves an average success rate of nearly 76.8% across five intricate long-horizon tasks and demonstrates strong generalization capabilities to novel objects.

## Problem

- Bimanual robotic manipulation is challenging due to the complexities of coordinating two arms, managing high-dimensional action spaces, and ensuring collision avoidance.
- Traditional methods for teaching bimanual tasks, such as teleoperation, are often laborious, data-intensive, and difficult to scale.
- Learning from human action videos is promising but often limited by the instability of directly extracting continuous, noisy human trajectories and the need for large, diverse datasets.

## Method

- A one-shot teaching paradigm extracts stable 3D hand keyposes and a motion mask from a single binocular human video demonstration, simplifying complex human movements into robot-executable actions.
- A data proliferation strategy rapidly generates diverse training demonstrations through automated real-robot rollouts with precise keyframe editing and virtual geometric transformations of object point clouds.
- A customized Bimanual Diffusion Policy (BiDP) learns robust actions by focusing observations on manipulated objects, predicting sparse keyposes, and reorganizing the action space with a motion mask to handle dual-arm coordination.

## Results

- YOTO's keyframe extraction method yields stable 3D keyposes, crucial for successful robot action injection, addressing issues with direct 3D hand mesh reconstruction from monocular views.
- The Bimanual Diffusion Policy (BiDP) achieved an average success rate of nearly 76.8% across five complex bimanual tasks, significantly outperforming strong baselines (e.g., EquiBot at 23.4%, ACT at 5.7%).
- The framework demonstrated satisfactory out-of-domain generalization to novel, unseen objects and successfully applied to two additional bimanual tasks with just one-shot human teaching, showcasing its versatility.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A single human video demonstration, when processed into robust 3D keyposes and combined with binocular vision, contains sufficient information for one-shot skill transfer to dual-arm robots.
> - Rapid data proliferation through automated real-robot rollouts and virtual geometric transformations is essential for overcoming data scarcity and achieving high performance in bimanual manipulation.
> - Customizing diffusion policies by shrinking observations to task-relevant objects, predicting sparse keyposes, and reorganizing the action space with a motion mask significantly enhances their effectiveness for long-horizon bimanual tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of **bimanual robotic manipulation, embodied intelligence, visuomotor imitation learning, and advanced computer vision**. Bimanual manipulation is a long-standing challenge in robotics due to the complexities of coordinating two arms, managing high-dimensional action spaces, and ensuring collision avoidance in dynamic environments. The paper positions itself as a significant step towards enabling robots to perform complex daily tasks that humans execute with ease.

Historically, bimanual manipulation research has largely followed two main paths:
1. **Explicitly classifying tasks based on pre-defined taxonomies:** These approaches often lack versatility and scalability, limiting the robot's flexibility for arbitrary tasks.
2. **Implicitly learning from teleoperated demonstrations:** While this offers more flexibility, it typically requires a massive amount of training data collected through laborious and often non-stationary teleoperation, which is difficult to scale and generalize. Notable recent work in this area includes the ALOHA series, which has advanced dexterous teleoperation with low-cost hardware.

The authors explicitly choose a third, more promising route: **learning from human action videos**. They argue that human videos provide rich, "almost for free" features such as spatial-temporal positions, dynamic postures, and interaction states, which can be leveraged by "relatively mature vision techniques." This approach aims to circumvent the limitations of teleoperation by using a more scalable and accessible data source.

Within the human video-based learning landscape, the paper distinguishes itself from existing works like RSRD and OKAMI, which also aim to learn bimanual manipulation from single human demonstrations. The key differentiators of YOTO are:
- **Binocular camera usage:** YOTO leverages a binocular camera for more accurate 3D spatial information capture, contrasting with monocular RGB video approaches that might be less precise.
- **Keyframes-based motion extraction:** Instead of directly retargeting continuous, potentially noisy human trajectories, YOTO simplifies them into robust, discrete keyframes, which are more reliable and versatile for robot execution.
- **Focus on fixed-base arms with parallel grippers:** Unlike some research that uses humanoid robots, multi-fingered dexterous hands, or mobile bases to achieve human-like dexterity, YOTO aims for universal applicability with a more common and easier-to-control robot setup (two fixed-base arms with parallel-jaw grippers in a heterolateral configuration).

Furthermore, the paper builds upon recent advancements in **visuomotor imitation learning**, particularly diffusion-based policies. While these methods have shown impressive stability and ability to model high-dimensional action distributions, YOTO proposes a "customized bimanual diffusion policy (BiDP)" with specific enhancements to address the unique challenges of bimanual tasks. These enhancements include shrinking observations to task-relevant objects, predicting sparse keyposes, and reorganizing the action space with a "motion mask" to handle dual-arm coordination efficiently.

In summary, YOTO aims to bridge the gap between human demonstration and robotic bimanual execution by offering a practical, efficient, and scalable framework for data acquisition and policy learning, pushing the boundaries of generalizable bimanual manipulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching goal of this research is to **enable dual-arm robots to efficiently, precisely, and robustly accomplish various complex daily tasks through bimanual manipulation.**

The primary motivations and specific objectives driving the development of the YOTO framework are as follows:

- **Addressing Challenges of Bimanual Manipulation:** The authors highlight two main difficulties:
 - **Coordination:** Ensuring two arms move synchronously or alternately without collisions, which demands stringent control and scheduling.
 - **State Complexity:** The high-dimensional state space of two robot arms and their end effectors makes motion planning and action prediction challenging.
 - Traditional methods (pre-defined taxonomies or teleoperation) are deemed insufficient due to lack of simplicity, versatility, scalability, or data collection inefficiencies.

- **Leveraging Human Demonstrated Videos as a Data Source:** The core motivation is the belief that "the most effective and efficient way for teaching bimanual manipulation is learning from human demonstrated videos." This is because human demonstrations inherently contain rich, high-level features (spatial-temporal positions, dynamic postures, interaction states, dexterous transitions) that can be extracted and utilized "almost for free," offering a more scalable alternative to laborious teleoperation.

- **Developing a "You Only Teach Once" (YOTO) Paradigm:**
 - **Objective 1: One-Shot Skill Transfer:** To extract and inject patterns of bimanual actions from as little as a *single binocular observation* of human hand movements, facilitating rapid skill transfer to dual robot arms. This involves transforming noisy human hand movements into stable, robot-executable keyframes and deriving a motion mask for coordination.
 - **Objective 2: Rapid Training Data Proliferation:** To devise a subtle solution for rapidly generating a large volume of *diverse training demonstrations* from just this one-shot teaching. This involves "auto-rollout verification" on real robots for pose and category variations, and "geometric transformation" of object point clouds for virtual augmentation, significantly reducing data collection costs and time compared to teleoperation.
 - **Objective 3: Customized Bimanual Diffusion Policy (BiDP):** To learn a robust and efficient action policy specifically tailored for long-horizon dual-arm tasks. This involves improving existing diffusion policies by:
 - Focusing observations on manipulated objects (reducing irrelevant information).
 - Predicting essential keyposes (reducing action space dimensionality).
 - Utilizing the motion mask to determine arm movements (unified policy for synchronous/asynchronous tasks).

- **Demonstrating High Performance and Generalization:** To verify YOTO's effectiveness by achieving impressive performance in mimicking intricate long-horizon bimanual tasks, demonstrating strong generalization under different visual and spatial conditions, and outperforming existing visuomotor imitation learning methods in accuracy and efficiency.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The YOTO framework is composed of three interconnected modules, designed to efficiently acquire bimanual manipulation skills from human demonstrations and transfer them to dual-arm robots.

#### 3.1. Hand Motion Extraction and Injection

This module focuses on processing human video demonstrations to extract robot-executable actions.

- **Human Demonstration Capturing:** A single **binocular stereo camera (DexSense 3D)** captures dual-stream synchronized RGB videos of a human performing a bimanual task. The left camera is primarily observed for hand features, with the right camera activated only when accurate 3D depth information is needed for stereo matching.
- **High-Level Features Extraction:**
 - **3D Hand Point Trajectories:** The WiLoR method detects hand bounding boxes, and MANO estimates 3D hand shapes (H_L, H_R). To overcome the instability of direct 3D hand mesh reconstruction from monocular views, the authors propose a more robust approach: projecting the estimated 3D hand center points onto the 2D image plane, and then lifting these 2D points back into 3D using a **stereo matching algorithm**. This "back-projected" 3D trajectory (b_h_p,⋄,j) is more stable in the camera space.
 - **Hand States:**
 - **Gripper State:** The open/closed state of the gripper (h_g,⋄,j) is determined by detecting if the human hand is in contact with an object, providing a more reliable signal than relying solely on hand pose estimation.
 - **3D Hand Orientations:** Algorithm 1 is used to calculate 3D hand poses (h_r,⋄,j) by simplifying the human hand into a lower-dimensional gripper-like representation, analogous to eigengrasping.
 - **Manipulated Object Information:** State-of-the-art vision algorithms like **Florence-2 (vision-language model for open vocabulary detection) and SAM2 (segmentation)** are used to extract segmented manipulated objects from the initial left image. These 2D segments are then lifted to **3D point clouds (e_O)** using stereo matching.
- **Robot Actions Injection (Keyframes-based):**
 - **Keyframes Simplification:** The dense, continuous human hand trajectories are abstracted into discrete **keyframes (or keyposes)** {(e_h_p,⋄,k, e_h_r,⋄,k, e_h_g,⋄,k)}. Keyframes are identified using heuristics such as changes in gripper state or local extrema of velocity/acceleration. This significantly reduces the number of points (K ≈ 10 vs. J ≈ 100-200 frames), making verification and correction much faster.
 - **Robot Transformation:** These keyposes are transformed from camera coordinates to robot coordinates using pre-measured hand-eye calibration.
 - **Real-Robot Verification:** The extracted keyposes are played back on the real robot for rapid verification and manual correction of any errors (e.g., unreachable poses, collisions). This process typically takes about three minutes per task.
 - **Motion Mask Derivation:** A "motion mask" (C) is derived from the timestamps of the keyframes. This binary mask (0 for holding, 1 for moving) explicitly encodes the coordination strategy (synchronous or asynchronous) for dual-arm movements, which is crucial for long-horizon bimanual tasks.

#### 3.2. Demonstration Proliferation from One Teaching

This module addresses the data scarcity problem by expanding the single-shot human demonstration into a large training dataset.

- **Auto-Rollout Verification in Real-World:**
 - The refined keyframes-based robot actions (e_A) are interpretable and editable. For asynchronous tasks, e_A is split into individual arm trajectories (e_A_L, e_A_R) using the motion mask.
 - The system allows for **precise editing of keyframes** to adjust for variations in object 6-DoF poses or to replace objects with similar categories.
 - New demonstrations are collected by automatically rolling out these modified trajectories on the real robots. This automated process is significantly faster and more stable than traditional teleoperation (e.g., ~300 demonstrations in 8 hours).
- **Geometric Transformation of Point Clouds:**
 - This is a virtual augmentation scheme. Once the correspondence between manipulated objects and relevant keyframes is established, **geometric transformations (rotations and translations)** are applied directly to the extracted 3D point clouds (e_O) of manipulated objects.
 - The corresponding 6-DoF values in the keyframes (e_A) are updated accordingly. This allows for **batch generation of theoretically infinite (e_O, e_A) pairs** without needing real robot verification, making it the most efficient data expansion method. The transformations are constrained to the robot's reachable workspace, measured during auto-rollout.

#### 3.3. Bimanual Diffusion Policy (BiDP) Learning

This module trains a robust policy using the proliferated dataset.

- **Bimanual Dataset Composition:** The training dataset (e_D) comprises pairs of observations (e_O), actions (e_A), and the motion mask (C), generated from the proliferation strategies. The observations e_O consist of 3D point clouds of manipulated objects and robot proprioception states (S_L, S_R). The actions e_A are the discrete keyposes.
- **Diffusion-based Policy Representation:** BiDP is based on **Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPMs)**, specifically using the DDIM scheduler for faster inference (8 denoising steps). The policy learns to predict actions by iteratively denoising a random Gaussian noise, conditioned on observations.
- **Customized Bimanual Diffusion Policy:**
 - **Reduced Observation Space:** The input observation (e_O_k) is deliberately **shrunk to contain only the 3D point clouds of manipulated objects**, instead of the entire scene (as in). This aims to accelerate training convergence and eliminate irrelevant information.
 - **Keypose Prediction:** Instead of modeling continuous actions, BiDP is designed to **predict essential keyposes**. This significantly decreases the dimensionality of the diffusion space, simplifying the learning problem.
 - **Reorganized Action Space with Motion Mask:** For bimanual tasks, the action space is reorganized into A = {e_A_L, e_A_R} based on the motion mask C (Equation 2). This allows for a **unified policy network form** that handles both synchronous and asynchronous dual-arm movements by predicting time-ordered single-arm actions, effectively eliminating redundancy for asynchronous tasks.
 - **Network Architecture:** BiDP employs a **SIM(3)-equivariant PointNet++ encoder** for processing the 3D point cloud observations, making it robust to scale and position variations of objects. A convolutional U-Net is used as the backbone of the noise prediction network.

This comprehensive methodology allows YOTO to learn complex bimanual manipulation skills from minimal human input, leveraging advanced vision and learning techniques for efficient data generation and robust policy training.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted by the authors demonstrate the effectiveness and superiority of the YOTO framework across various challenging bimanual manipulation tasks.

- **Q1: Quality of Extracted Hand Motions:**
 - The direct application of advanced 3D hand mesh reconstruction methods (e.g., HaMeR, WiLoR) for extracting hand trajectories from monocular videos was found to be **unstable and difficult to parse** due to inherent errors and inconsistent camera space estimations.
 - YOTO's proposed method of **projecting 3D hand points to 2D, lifting them back to 3D using stereo matching, and then extracting sparse keyframes** yields significantly more stable and consistent 3D keyposes. This improved data quality is crucial for successful robot action injection.

- **Q2: Effectiveness of YOTO Strategies (Ablation Studies and Baselines Comparison):**
 - **Ablation Studies (Table III):** Incremental additions of YOTO's strategies on the "pull drawer" task demonstrated their individual and combined effectiveness:
 - Switching from entire scene point clouds to **purely object observations** (id-2) improved success.
 - Predicting **sparse keyframes** instead of continuous actions (id-3) also improved performance.
 - Combining both (id-4) yielded even better results.
 - **Reorganizing the action space** based on the motion mask (id-5) further enhanced the policy.
 - The **geometric transformation for data expansion** (id-6) showed the most significant performance boost, highlighting the critical role of data diversity and volume.
 - **BiDP (id-7), integrating all proposed strategies, achieved the highest success rate and average task completion length.**
 - **Comparison with Baselines (Table IV & V):** BiDP consistently and significantly **outperformed four strong baselines** (ACT, Diffusion Policy, 3D Diffusion Policy, EquiBot) across all five complex long-horizon bimanual tasks ("pull drawer," "pour water," "unscrew bottle," "uncover lid," "open box").
 - Diffusion-based policies (DP, DP3, EquiBot, BiDP) generally performed better than the transformer-based ACT, especially for modeling high-dimensional action spaces.
 - Policies leveraging more sophisticated 3D observation architectures (DP3, EquiBot) performed better than simpler ones.
 - BiDP's average success rate across all tasks was nearly **76.8%**, a substantial improvement over the baselines (e.g., EquiBot at 23.4%, ACT at 5.7%). This demonstrates the effectiveness of BiDP's customizations for bimanual tasks and the benefits of the data proliferation strategy.

- **Q3: Out-of-Domain (OOD) Generalization Ability (Table VI):**
 - When tested on novel, unseen objects, BiDP exhibited **satisfactory OOD generalization**, showing the least performance degradation among all compared methods.
 - BiDP maintained a clear advantage over EquiBot in OOD settings, attributed to its use of explicit 3D geometric transformations for data expansion (vs. EquiBot's SIM(3)-equivariant augmentation of the entire point cloud) and its reliance on object-centric point clouds, making it more robust.

- **Q4: Compatibility and Applicability to Diverse Bimanual Tasks:**
 - The YOTO framework was successfully applied to five representative long-horizon bimanual tasks, encompassing various skills (pull/push, pick/place, unscrew, lift), multi-object perception, and dual-arm coordination (synchronous and asynchronous).
 - Qualitative results (Fig. 7, 13) illustrate the successful real-robot execution of these complex tasks.
 - Furthermore, the paper demonstrates the framework's **simplicity, versatility, and scalability** by applying it to two *additional* typical bimanual tasks ("reorient pen" and "flip basket") with just one-shot human teaching (Fig. 8, 14), showcasing its potential for rapid skill transfer to new scenarios without extensive data collection.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The YOTO framework represents a significant advancement in the field of bimanual robotic manipulation, with several key implications and potential impacts:

- **Revolutionizing Data Collection for Robot Learning:** YOTO's most impactful contribution lies in its innovative **one-shot teaching and rapid data proliferation strategy**. By leveraging a single human video demonstration and intelligently augmenting it through automated real-robot rollouts and virtual 3D geometric transformations, it drastically reduces the time, cost, and effort traditionally associated with collecting large, diverse datasets for robot imitation learning. This is a game-changer for democratizing robot learning and scaling up capabilities.
- **Enhanced Bimanual Skill Acquisition:** The ability to extract fine-grained, stable keyframes and motion masks from human demonstrations provides a **standardized and robust method for transferring complex bimanual skills to robots**. This addresses a critical bottleneck in bimanual control: explicit coordination strategies.
- **Development of Robust and Generalizable Policies:** The specialized **Bimanual Diffusion Policy (BiDP)**, tailored with object-centric observations, keypose prediction, and a reorganized action space, yields policies that are not only highly effective but also robust and capable of **generalizing to novel visual and spatial conditions (unseen objects and locations)**. This is crucial for real-world deployment where environmental variability is common.
- **Practical Applicability for Complex Tasks:** The successful demonstration on five intricate, long-horizon bimanual tasks (including asynchronous and synchronous collaborations) on real Aubo i5 robots with standard parallel grippers highlights YOTO's immediate practical relevance. It moves beyond theoretical concepts to demonstrate tangible capabilities for tasks found in manufacturing, logistics, or household assistance.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** YOTO lays a strong foundation for future research in several directions. Its modular design allows for independent improvement of its components (e.g., more robust vision foundation models, advanced grippers, mobile robot integration). The success with basic fixed-base robots suggests that integrating more advanced hardware like multi-fingered dexterous hands or mobile platforms could further extend its capabilities, enabling even more intricate and dynamic human-robot interactions.
- **Efficiency and Scalability:** By streamlining the entire pipeline from demonstration to policy deployment, YOTO offers an unparalleled level of efficiency and scalability. This makes it a compelling solution for developing a wide range of bimanual robotic applications, moving closer to the vision of truly embodied intelligence.

**Limitations acknowledged by the authors** (inherent errors in vision-based hand trajectory extraction, fixed workbench, limited gripper flexibility, and unexplored ultra-difficult tasks) provide valuable insights for future work, but do not diminish the current impact of YOTO in pushing the boundaries of accessible and effective bimanual robotic manipulation. The framework's ability to "teach once" and rapidly proliferate learning opportunities marks a significant step forward in making complex robot skills attainable.
