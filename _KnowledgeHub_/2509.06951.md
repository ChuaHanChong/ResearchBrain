# F1: A Vision-Language-Action Model Bridging Understanding and Generation to Actions

> [!summary] Summary
> F₁, a Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model, integrates explicit visual foresight into its decision-making process, moving beyond purely reactive control. This approach yields enhanced robustness in dynamic environments and improved generalization across a range of real-world and simulated robotic manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models largely depend on reactive state-to-action mappings.
- This reactive paradigm leads to short-sighted behaviors and poor robustness in dynamic scenes, struggling with generalization and long-horizon tasks.
- Existing approaches lacked an explicit mechanism to reason about future states or temporal evolution, hindering adaptability in complex environments.

## Method

- **F₁** introduces a ==Mixture-of-Transformer (MoT)== architecture with dedicated Understanding, Generation, and Action ==expert modules==.
- It integrates ==explicit goal-conditioned visual foresight== into decision-making, reframing action generation as a ==foresight-guided inverse dynamics problem==.
- A ==three-stage progressive training recipe== is employed, involving ==generation-understanding alignment==, full model pretraining on large datasets, and task-specific adaptation.

## Results

- F₁ achieved an **82.2%** average success rate across **9** real-world Genie robot tasks, surpassing `π₀`'s **65.2%**, and demonstrated a **93.3%** success rate on the challenging 'Handover (R2H)' task compared to `π₀`'s **40%**.
- The model showed a **66.7%** overall success rate for continuous dual-arm dynamic grasping on a novel robot in moving belt scenarios and sustained meaningful success across a **10-step** long-horizon task, where `π₀` failed after basic grasps.
- Ablation studies confirmed that removing the generation expert significantly dropped performance by **17.2%** in simulation and similarly in real-world tasks, validating the critical role of visual foresight.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly generating and using goal-conditioned visual foresight allows VLA models to move beyond reactive control, enabling proactive planning and significantly improving robustness in dynamic scenes.
> - Even with imperfect pixel-level visual foresight, the 'actionable guidance' provided by anticipated future states is sufficient to greatly enhance downstream robotic control accuracy and reliability.
> - A progressive, multi-stage training approach, leveraging both pretrained multimodal understanding and large-scale robotics data, is crucial for developing stable, generalizable, and transferable visuomotor policies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of embodied AI, particularly the development of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, is rapidly evolving, aiming to enable robots to understand natural language instructions and execute tasks in complex, dynamic visual environments. Historically, robotic manipulation policies have progressed through several paradigms:

- **Reactive Action Experts:** Early works focused on end-to-end learning of state-to-action mappings (e.g., ACT, DP). While simple, these models often lacked semantic understanding and struggled with generalization and dynamic environments due to their purely reactive nature.
- **VLM-based Policies:** Subsequent approaches integrated Vision-Language Models (VLMs) (e.g., `π₀`, gr00t-N1) to inject rich semantic and scene understanding. These models improved language grounding but remained reactive, lacking an explicit mechanism to reason about future states or temporal evolution, which limits their robustness in dynamic or long-horizon tasks.
- **Visual Prediction-based Policies:** More recently, some methods (e.g., VPP, Genie Envisioner) attempted to incorporate future observation prediction as an auxiliary signal. However, without deep integration with semantic understanding from VLMs, their predictions often lacked semantic grounding, leading to brittle control and limited generalization.

F₁ directly addresses the core limitation across these paradigms: the reliance on reactive state-to-action mappings, which leads to "short-sighted behaviors and poor robustness in dynamic scenes." The paper explicitly positions F₁ as a novel paradigm that integrates "visual foresight generation into the decision-making pipeline." It draws inspiration from Predictive Inverse Dynamics Models (PIDMs), which infer actions required to achieve a desired future state. By explicitly generating and using goal-conditioned visual foresight, F₁ moves beyond mere reactivity, allowing actions to be planned based on anticipated visual outcomes. This foresight-guided approach allows F₁ to bridge "understanding, generation, and actions" within a unified framework, promising enhanced robustness and generalization in complex and dynamic real-world scenarios, thereby making a significant stride towards more intelligent and adaptable embodied agents.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop a robust and generalizable Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model that can effectively execute language-conditioned tasks in dynamic visual environments. The authors identify several key motivations for pursuing this objective:

- **Overcoming Reactive Limitations:** Existing VLA models predominantly rely on reactive state-to-action mappings. This leads to short-sighted behaviors, poor robustness to distribution shifts, and fragility in dynamic scenes where objects move, contexts shift, and instructions unfold over time. The primary motivation is to move beyond this reactive paradigm.
- **Enabling Foresight-Driven Planning:** To address the limitations of reactive policies, the authors propose to integrate explicit "visual foresight generation" into the decision-making process. This means the robot should not just react to the current state but anticipate plausible future visual states to guide its actions.
- **Bridging Understanding, Generation, and Actions:** The paper aims to unify these three cognitive functions—perception and semantic understanding, visual state prediction (generation), and motor control (actions)—within a single, coherent framework. This integration is crucial for holistic intelligence in embodied AI.
- **Improving Robustness and Generalizability:** The ultimate goal is to equip robots with policies that are robust to dynamic changes, can handle long-horizon tasks, and generalize well across diverse tasks and robot embodiments. This requires not only strong semantic grounding but also the ability to reason about temporal evolution and plan accordingly.
- **Learning from Large-Scale Data:** The motivation also includes developing a training strategy that can leverage large-scale datasets, both internet-scale and task-specific, to instill robust and transferable visuomotor knowledge.

In essence, the motivation for F₁ stems from the recognized gap between current VLA capabilities and the demands of real-world embodied tasks, particularly the need for proactive, foresight-guided planning rather than purely reactive execution.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

F₁ introduces a novel VLA framework built upon a **Mixture-of-Transformer (MoT)** architecture, specifically designed to bridge understanding, generation, and actions through explicit visual foresight.

#### 3.1. Architecture Overview (Mixture-of-Transformer)

The F₁ framework comprises three dedicated "experts," each implemented with a decoder-only transformer backbone, allowing for scalable autoregressive modeling while maintaining specialized functions:

- **Understanding Expert:** This expert is initialized from a pretrained Vision-Language Model (VLM), specifically `π₀` (Black et al., 2024). It takes the current visual observation (`o_t`) encoded by a SigLIP vision encoder (Zhai et al., 2023) and a natural language instruction (`l`). Its role is to generate a robust, semantically aligned multimodal representation of the scene and task goal, serving as the foundation for subsequent modules.
- **Generation Expert:** This expert is responsible for synthesizing a goal-conditioned visual foresight image (`ô_t+1`). Unlike reactive policies, it predicts a plausible future visual state. To achieve efficiency and accuracy, it employs a **next-scale prediction mechanism** (Tian et al., 2024b). Recent observations (`{o_t-m..., o_t}`) are encoded into multi-scale residual Vector Quantization (VQ) tokens (Lee et al., 2022). A temporal convolutional network aggregates motion-relevant features, which are then processed by a decoder-only transformer to autoregressively generate foresight tokens, decoded into `ô_t+1`.
- **Action Expert:** This expert maps the multimodal context into executable robot actions. Conditioned on the language goal (`l`), current observation (`o_t`), and crucially, the generated foresight image (`ô_t+1`), it predicts a short-horizon action sequence (`â_t:t+k`). This is framed as a **foresight-guided inverse dynamics problem**, where actions are derived to realize the anticipated visual outcome. The model uses **chunked action prediction** (Zhao et al., 2023) and a **flow-matching objective** (Lipman et al., 2023) in continuous action space.

#### 3.2. Unified Understanding-Generation-Action (UGA) Progressive Attention

To coordinate information flow between these experts, F₁ employs a hierarchical attention scheme. **Intra-expert attention** is bidirectional within each expert (except for causal token generation in the generation expert). **Inter-expert attention** follows a causal flow: the generation expert attends to the understanding expert, and the action expert attends to both. This prevents information leakage and ensures that control decisions are genuinely guided by predicted visual outcomes, stabilizing training and enhancing interpretability.

#### 3.3. Three-Stage Training Recipe

F₁'s robust capabilities are developed through a progressive three-stage training recipe on an extensive dataset of over 330k trajectories across 136 tasks and 5 embodiments:

- **Pretrain Stage I (Generation-Understanding Alignment):** The understanding expert (from `π₀`) is frozen. The randomly initialized generation expert is trained to synthesize future visual tokens (`z_j`) conditioned on historical observations and language instructions, aligning its outputs with the semantic space of the understanding expert. This stage uses a negative log-likelihood objective with teacher forcing.
- **Pretrain Stage II (Full Model Pretraining):** All three experts are jointly optimized on large-scale public robot datasets. This stage uses an **autoregressive next-scale prediction** objective for the generation expert (where future tokens are conditioned on *previously predicted* tokens, not ground truth, enhancing inference stability). Concurrently, a **flow matching-based action prediction** objective is used for the action expert, modeling the continuous transformation from noise to expert actions. The total objective is a weighted sum of these two losses.
- **Post-train Stage (Task-specific Adaptation):** The full model is fine-tuned on task-specific demonstrations for embodiment adaptation and learning fine-grained manipulation skills, using the same joint optimization as Stage II but on smaller, high-quality datasets.

This progressive scheme stabilizes optimization, imbues the model with transferable visual foresight, and facilitates generalization.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

F₁ demonstrates superior performance across diverse benchmarks, both in simulation and real-world settings, primarily due to its foresight-guided planning.

#### 4.1. Real-World Task Performance

- **Genie Robot (9 tasks):** F₁ consistently outperforms baselines (`π₀`, gr00t-N1, gr00t-N1.5) with an average grasp rate of 92.6% and success rate of 82.2%. `π₀` achieves 78.5% grasp and 65.2% success. F₁ shows significant gains on challenging tasks like "Handover (R2H)," achieving 93.3% success compared to `π₀`'s 40%, highlighting its ability to handle dynamic and precise coordination.
- **Franka Arm (Sweep, Sort):** F₁ achieves more efficient and reliable interactions in sweeping (7.1/8.0 successful objects vs. `π₀`'s 4.9/8.0, fewer empty sweeps) and higher success rates in sorting, especially for consecutive grasps (100% 2nd grasp vs. `π₀`'s 86.7%), indicating enhanced robustness in sequential tasks.
- **ARX LIFT II (Dynamic Manipulation, Long-Horizon Task):**
 - **Dynamic Environment (Moving Belt):** F₁ achieves a remarkable 66.7% overall success rate for continuous dual-arm dynamic grasping on a novel robot (ARX LIFT II) not present in pretraining. For specific items, it reaches 80% success, significantly outperforming `π₀` (33.3%). This confirms F₁'s ability to predict moving object positions and plan actions.
 - **Long-Horizon Task (10 steps, 2 mins):** F₁ completes the majority of the 10 sequential steps, maintaining meaningful success rates even in later, complex stages (e.g., pouring, wiping, sweeping). In stark contrast, `π₀` consistently fails beyond the simplest grasping actions (0% success from step 3 onwards), demonstrating F₁'s superior long-term planning and temporal consistency.

#### 4.2. Simulation Benchmark Performance

- **LIBERO Benchmark:** F₁ achieves the best overall performance, surpassing strong baselines like `π₀`, gr00t-N1, and CoT-VLA across LIBERO-Spatial, -Object, -Goal, and particularly -Long suites (91.3% vs. `π₀`'s 88.4%). This underscores its efficacy in tasks requiring precise spatial understanding, intricate object interaction, and long-horizon planning.
- **SimplerEnv Bridge Benchmark:** F₁ substantially outperforms other models in complex, multi-step manipulation tasks requiring fine-grained control, showing improved grasp and overall success rates. This is attributed to its enhanced generalization for precise pick-and-place positions.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Effectiveness of Generation Expert:** Removing the generation expert ("No-Gen" variant) leads to a significant performance drop from 77.5% to 60.3% average success rate (simulation), and similar drops in real-world tasks, highlighting its critical role in providing explicit visual foresight for effective planning.
- **Effectiveness of Pretraining (Stage II):** Removing large-scale robotics dataset pretraining ("Cotrain-Scratch") results in a 3.3% average performance drop in simulation and notable drops in real-world tasks, confirming Stage II's role in stabilizing optimization and inheriting foundational manipulation skills.
- **Training vs. Freezing Generation Expert:** Freezing the generation expert during later stages ("Frozen-Gen") leads to a moderate drop, indicating that while fixed foresight is useful, end-to-end fine-tuning is crucial for task alignment.
- **Impact of Planning Scales:** A 4-scale planning horizon (`ô_t+1` predictions) generally performs optimally, providing sufficient temporal abstraction without excessive noise or computational overhead, outperforming 2-scales and slightly outperforming 6-scales.

#### 4.4. Relationship between Generation Quality and Actions

- **Quantitative Analysis (Qwen2.5-VL-32B-Instruct):** Evaluated foresight images on scene consistency, object consistency, and task progress following. Scene consistency improves rapidly, while object consistency remains challenging due to lack of large-scale generative pretraining. Importantly, "Task Progress Following" improves steadily, suggesting F₁ captures high-level task logic even with imperfect pixel fidelity.
- **Correlation Study:** A consistent positive correlation is observed between image token accuracy (even modest at 40-45%) and action token accuracy across LIBERO suites. This implies that pixel-level perfection in foresight is not strictly necessary; sufficient task-relevant cues are enough to guide actions effectively, and improvements in visual prediction directly translate to action reliability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

F₁ represents a significant advancement in the field of embodied AI, particularly for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, with several key contributions and potential impacts:

- **Paradigm Shift to Foresight-Guided Control:** The most impactful contribution is the successful integration of explicit, goal-conditioned visual foresight into the perception-action loop. By reformulating action generation as a foresight-guided inverse dynamics problem, F₁ fundamentally shifts VLA models from reactive to proactive, planning-based processes. This is crucial for handling the inherent dynamism and long-horizon nature of real-world robotic tasks, where purely reactive policies often fail.
- **Enhanced Robustness and Generalization:** The extensive experimental results unequivocally demonstrate F₁'s superior robustness in dynamic environments (e.g., grasping moving objects) and enhanced generalization across diverse tasks and new robot embodiments. This capability is critical for deploying robots in unstructured and unpredictable settings, moving closer to general-purpose robot agents.
- **Unified Architecture for Cognitive Functions:** The Mixture-of-Transformer architecture with dedicated understanding, generation, and action experts provides a modular yet integrated framework. This structured approach facilitates clearer information flow, interpretability, and leverages the strengths of large-scale multimodal pretraining for semantic understanding, while adding a vital predictive component.
- **Systematic and Progressive Training Strategy:** The three-stage training recipe is a practical and effective method for building complex VLA models. It progressively aligns generative capabilities with semantic understanding, instills foundational visuomotor skills from large datasets, and then fine-tunes for task-specific adaptation. This systematic approach contributes to model stability, efficiency, and transferability.
- **Bridging Research Gaps:** F₁ effectively bridges the gap between state-of-the-art multimodal understanding (VLMs) and robust robotic control by explicitly modeling temporal evolution. It shows that even with imperfect pixel-level generation, the "actionable guidance" from foresight is sufficient to significantly improve downstream control, offering valuable insights for future generative AI in robotics.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Broader Real-World Deployment:** The increased robustness and generalization abilities of F₁ could accelerate the deployment of intelligent robots in complex real-world scenarios, from smart factories and warehouses to service robotics and assistive technologies.
- **Foundation for More Complex Skills:** By providing a robust base for planning and execution, F₁ could serve as a foundational architecture for learning even more complex skills, such as dexterous manipulation, multi-robot collaboration, or long-term human-robot interaction.
- **Advancement in Embodied AI Research:** This work provides a strong blueprint for developing truly intelligent embodied agents that can perceive, predict, and act coherently. It encourages further research into richer world models, physics-informed priors, and integrating reinforcement learning for continuous improvement beyond imitation.
- **Practical Implications for Robotics Engineering:** The open-source nature (Github, Hugging Face) of F₁ suggests its potential to become a widely adopted framework, providing a strong baseline for industrial and academic research in robot learning.

In conclusion, F₁ makes a significant stride towards creating more intelligent and adaptable robots by introducing a principled way to integrate predictive foresight into decision-making, setting a new benchmark for performance and robustness in Vision-Language-Action models.
