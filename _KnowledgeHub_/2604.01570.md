# Boosting Vision-Language-Action Finetuning with Feasible Action Neighborhood Prior

> [!summary] Summary
> A regularization method for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, termed Feasible Action Neighborhood (FAN) prior, enhances generalization and sample efficiency in robotic manipulation by aligning training objectives with the intrinsic tolerance of physical actions. This approach demonstrated up to an 11.7% increase in task success rates and achieved a 90% success rate with approximately one-third of the training steps required by baselines on standard benchmarks and real-world setups.

## Problem

- Existing VLA training methods, inherited from linguistic settings, overlook the 'intrinsic tolerance' or 'near-equivalence' of physical robotic actions, treating them as discrete tokens.
- Supervised Finetuning (SFT) often leads to overfitting and poor generalization in VLA models by collapsing probability mass onto single demonstrated actions.
- Reinforced Finetuning (RFT) for VLA models is typically sample-inefficient, requiring extensive exploration to implicitly discover robust action distributions for generalization.

## Method

- Formalized the concept of a ==Feasible Action Neighborhood (FAN)== to represent the local tolerance structure inherent in physical actions.
- Introduced a ==FAN-guided regularizer== based on ==Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence== to shape the VLA policy distribution towards a unimodal and smooth Gaussian target.
- Integrated the regularizer into ==Supervised Finetuning (FAN-SFT)== using a ==dynamic covariance matrix== and into ==Reinforced Finetuning (FAN-PPO)== with a fixed covariance matrix, preserving VLA model architectures.

## Results

- **FAN-SFT** achieved an average **+11.7%** increase in success rate for in-distribution tasks and **+5.2%** for out-of-distribution (OOD) tasks on ManiSkill, and improved **OpenVLA-OFT** success from 95.2% to **98.8%** on LIBERO-Spatial.
- **FAN-PPO** enhanced OOD performance by up to **+7.9%** and significantly accelerated convergence, reaching a **90%** success rate with approximately one-third of the training steps compared to PPO baselines on ManiSkill.
- Real-world experiments demonstrated that **FAN-SFT** policies exhibited stronger robustness to spatial perturbations in a pick-and-place task on a JAKA manipulator compared to baseline SFT.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Physical actions in robotic manipulation intrinsically possess a 'feasible action neighborhood' where multiple actions yield similar task progress, a property often ignored by linguistic-style VLA training.
> - Explicitly guiding VLA models to learn and encompass these feasible action neighborhoods through regularization significantly improves their ability to generalize to unseen or perturbed environments.
> - The regularization method provides a principled way to inject domain knowledge about action space geometry into VLA training, enhancing sample efficiency and robustness without architectural changes.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has seen advancements with the emergence of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which integrate visual perception, language understanding, and low-level control into a single framework. These models, often leveraging autoregressive language-model backbones, enable end-to-end mapping from sensory input and linguistic instructions to physical actions. The deployment of VLA models typically involves two stages: pretraining on extensive, heterogeneous VLA datasets to establish a general foundation, followed by finetuning on specific robot platforms or environments to adapt to particular embodiments, sensory configurations, and physical dynamics.

Current finetuning paradigms predominantly fall into two categories: Supervised Finetuning (SFT), which uses offline demonstration trajectories as labeled sequences, and Reinforced Finetuning (RFT), which optimizes policies through environment rewards via interaction. While SFT offers stability, RFT has shown potential for better generalization. A common practice in VLA training, inherited from linguistic settings, involves using next-token prediction with one-hot cross-entropy or its reinforcement learning analogues like PPO.

This paper identifies a fundamental mismatch between these inherited linguistic training methodologies and the inherent properties of physical robotic actions. Unlike linguistic tokens, which are often discrete and have a single "correct" interpretation, physical actions in manipulation tasks typically possess an "intrinsic tolerance" or "near-equivalence." This means that a neighborhood of actions around an optimal action can yield indistinguishable task progress. For example, grasping an object might tolerate slight variations in gripper position without compromising success.

Existing VLA training methods generally overlook this physical property, leading to limitations. In SFT, models tend to collapse their probability mass onto single demonstrated actions, resulting in overfitting and poor generalization, especially in out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios. In RFT, while policies can eventually learn broader action distributions for better generalization, this process is often highly sample-inefficient, requiring extensive exploration to implicitly discover the beneficial properties of action neighborhoods.

The presented work directly addresses this oversight. Instead of proposing new VLA architectures or entirely different finetuning algorithms, it introduces a novel regularization approach that explicitly incorporates the "Feasible Action Neighborhood" (FAN) property into existing SFT and RFT frameworks. By aligning the VLA training objective with the geometry of physical action spaces, the research aims to enhance sample efficiency and generalization, thereby providing a more principled and practical method for VLA adaptation in real-world robotic scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from an observed inconsistency between the training methodologies used for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models and the intrinsic nature of physical actions in robotic manipulation.

**Motivation:**
In linguistic tasks, models are typically trained to predict a single, correct token, and their loss functions reflect this "exclusive correctness." However, real-world robotic manipulation tasks differ in a fundamental way: for any given state, there is often a range of "feasible" actions, rather than just one optimal action, that can lead to successful or nearly equivalent task progress. This inherent tolerance in physical actions defines a "Feasible Action Neighborhood" (FAN). For instance, when commanded to grasp an object, the robotic gripper's exact position might vary slightly while still achieving a successful grasp.

The authors contend that current VLA training and finetuning methods, which are largely inherited from language model paradigms, fail to account for this FAN property. This oversight leads to specific challenges in both common finetuning paradigms:
- **Supervised Finetuning (SFT):** When trained on small, task-specific datasets, VLA models often exhibit "overfitting," collapsing their probability distributions onto the exact actions seen in demonstrations. This results in "spiky," narrow action distributions that poorly generalize when faced with minor perturbations or out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios. The model essentially learns a trivial or near-trivial FAN.
- **Reinforced Finetuning (RFT):** While RFT methods, through exploration, can eventually lead to policies with broader action distributions and improved generalization, this discovery process is highly sample-inefficient. Without an explicit mechanism to guide the policy towards a robust FAN, the agent expends significant training time to implicitly learn this property.

Empirical analysis in the paper supports this motivation, showing a correlation between the shape of the policy distribution (acting as a proxy for the FAN) and the model's generalization performance. Overly-peaked distributions learned during SFT warm-up stages correlate with poor generalization, whereas broader distributions emerging from RFT correlate with improved task success.

### 3. Key Objectives

Based on this motivation, the research sets out to achieve the following objectives:
1. **Formalize the concept of Feasible Action Neighborhood (FAN):** To define and mathematically characterize the local tolerance structure inherent in physical actions, highlighting the mismatch with standard language-style VLA training objectives.
2. **Develop a FAN-guided regularizer:** To introduce a novel regularization term that can be seamlessly applied to both SFT and RFT paradigms. This regularizer is designed to shape the VLA model's output distribution to align with the properties of the FAN, promoting locally smooth and unimodal predictions.
3. **Improve generalization and sample efficiency:** To empirically demonstrate that by explicitly aligning the training objective with the physical tolerance of real-world manipulation actions, the proposed method significantly enhances task success rates, OOD generalization, and sample efficiency in VLA finetuning.
4. **Maintain VLA model characteristics:** To ensure that the regularizer preserves the discreteness and autoregressive nature of VLA models, requiring no alterations to the model architecture or decoding scheme.

### 4. Methodology and Approach

The core of the proposed methodology is the introduction of a Feasible Action Neighborhood (FAN)-guided regularizer that shapes the policy distribution during VLA model finetuning. This approach is applied to both Supervised Finetuning (SFT) and Reinforced Finetuning (RFT).

#### 4.1. Formalization of Feasible Action Neighborhood (FAN)

The authors formally define the FAN, denoted as $N_\delta(s)$, for a given state $s$. If $a^*(s) = \text{arg max}_{a' \in A} Q(s, a')$ is the optimal action, then for a tolerance $\delta > 0$, the FAN is a connected set containing $a^*(s)$ within actions that yield nearly equivalent Q-values: $N_\delta(s) \subseteq \{a \in A : Q(s, a^*(s)) - Q(s, a) \leq \delta\}$. This definition highlights that a range of actions, not just a single one, can be nearly optimal.
The shape of the policy distribution $\pi(a|s)$ is considered a practical proxy for the learned FAN. A narrow, "spiky" distribution indicates a small FAN, whereas a broad, "smooth" distribution indicates a larger, more robust FAN.

#### 4.2. FAN-Guided Regularizer

To guide the policy distribution towards an ideal FAN, which is characterized by unimodality, smoothness, and local contiguity, the authors propose a regularizer based on the Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence between the policy $\pi$ and a target Gaussian distribution $N(\mu(s), \Sigma(s))$. The regularizer is defined as:
$L_{FAN} = E_s[D_{KL}(\pi(\cdot|s) \parallel N(\cdot|\mu(s), \Sigma(s)))]$
Here, $\mu(s) = \text{arg max}_a \pi(a|s)$ is the policy's own predicted optimal action (mean), and $\Sigma(s)$ is the covariance matrix. This regularizer is designed to be integrated into existing VLA training objectives without requiring architectural changes.

#### 4.3. SFT with FAN-Guided Regularization (FAN-SFT)

For supervised finetuning, the FAN-guided regularizer is added to the standard negative log-likelihood loss, resulting in the $L_{FAN-SFT}$ loss:
$L_{FAN-SFT}(\theta) = -\frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n \sum_{t=0}^{K_i-1} [\log \pi_\theta(a_t^i|s_t^i, l_i) + \alpha D_{KL}(\pi_\theta(\cdot|s_t^i, l_i) \parallel N(\cdot|\mu(s_t^i), \Sigma(s_t^i)))]$
where $\alpha$ is the regularization coefficient.
A crucial aspect for SFT is the *dynamic* definition of the covariance matrix: $\Sigma(s) = \text{diag}(\sum_{a \in A} \pi(a|s, l)(a - \mu(s))^2)$. This adaptive covariance encourages the policy to adopt a Gaussian shape based on its current geometry, which is considered stable within the supervised learning context.

#### 4.4. RFT with FAN-Guided Regularization (FAN-PPO)

For reinforced finetuning, the FAN-guided regularizer is incorporated into a trust-region policy optimization objective (e.g., PPO). The objective is formulated as a constrained optimization problem:
$\text{max}_\pi E_{s \sim d_{\pi_t}^\mu, a \sim \pi_t} [\frac{\pi(a|s, l)}{\pi_t(a|s, l)} A_{\pi_t}(s, a, l)] - \alpha E_{s \sim d_{\pi_t}^\mu} [D_{KL}(\pi(\cdot|s, l) \parallel N(\cdot|\mu(s), \Sigma))]$
$\text{s.t.} E_{s \sim d_{\pi_t}^\mu} [D_{KL}(\pi(\cdot|s, l) \parallel \pi_t(\cdot|s, l))] \leq \epsilon$
Here, $\alpha$ is the regularization coefficient. To ensure stability during RFT, a *fixed* covariance matrix $\Sigma = \sigma^2 I$ is used, where $\sigma > 0$ is a hyperparameter controlling the target FAN size. This provides a consistent target shape to anchor policy updates.
The authors provide Proposition 1, which states that the optimal policy $\pi_{t+1}$ in this formulation is proportional to a geometric interpolation between the previous policy $\pi_t$ and the target Gaussian $N(\mu(s), \Sigma)$, re-weighted by exponentiated Q-values. This highlights a competition between $\alpha$ (pull towards Gaussian) and $\epsilon$ (trust-region constraint).
For practical implementation, the regularizer is integrated into the PPO policy loss:
$L_{FAN-PPO}(\theta) = -\frac{1}{K} \sum_{k=0}^{K-1} \text{min} [I_k^t \hat{A}(s_k, a_k, l) - \alpha D_{KL}(\pi_\theta(\cdot|s_k, l) \parallel N(\cdot|\mu(s_k), \Sigma)), \text{Clip}(I_k^t, 1-\epsilon, 1+\epsilon) \hat{A}(s_k, a_k, l) - \alpha D_{KL}(\pi_\theta(\cdot|s_k, l) \parallel N(\cdot|\mu(s_k), \Sigma))]$
The advantage $\hat{A}$ is estimated using GAE, and the value function is updated via an MSE loss.

### 5. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluated the proposed FAN-guided regularization across both Supervised Finetuning (SFT) and Reinforced Finetuning (RFT) paradigms, utilizing two VLA models (OpenVLA and OpenVLA-OFT) on two robotic manipulation benchmarks (ManiSkill and LIBERO), along with real-world experiments.

#### 5.1. Supervised Finetuning (FAN-SFT)

- **ManiSkill Benchmark:**
 - FAN-SFT demonstrated an average improvement of +11.7% in success rate on in-distribution tasks and +5.2% on out-of-distribution (OOD) tasks compared to the OpenVLA + SFT baseline.
 - Consistent improvements were observed across all OOD categories: +5.1% in Vision OOD, +6.1% in Semantic OOD, and +4.4% in Execution OOD.
 - Specific tasks, such as 'Disturb Recep' (distractive receptacle) and 'Noise-s' (strong dynamic noise), showed absolute gains of +7.8% and +7.2% respectively, indicating enhanced robustness to various perturbations.
 - The method maintained its effectiveness across different training data scales, generally outperforming the baseline.
 - When compared to label smoothing, FAN-guided regularization was found to be more effective and stable, suggesting that its structured approach to probability spreading is superior for robotic actions.

- **LIBERO Benchmark (LIBERO-Spatial):**
 - FAN-SFT improved success rates from 84.7% to 87.2% for OpenVLA and from 95.2% to 98.8% for OpenVLA-OFT.
 - The approach yielded stronger resilience to positional shifts, for example, increasing success rate from 0.24 to 0.36 at a perturbation of (x=0.05, y=0.05).
 - Qualitative results indicated that vanilla SFT policies tended to overfit to seen spatial patterns, failing to adapt to perturbed target locations. In contrast, FAN-SFT policies successfully adapted to unseen positions, demonstrating stronger spatial generalization.

#### 5.2. Reinforced Finetuning (FAN-PPO)

- **ManiSkill Benchmark:**
 - Applying FAN-guided regularization to both OpenVLA and OpenVLA-OFT consistently improved performance across in-distribution and OOD settings.
 - OpenVLA + FAN-PPO showed an average improvement of +6.2% across OOD tasks compared to OpenVLA + PPO.
 - OpenVLA-OFT + FAN-PPO achieved an average improvement of +7.9% across OOD tasks compared to its PPO baseline.
 - The most significant performance gains were observed in the execution OOD setting, with improvements exceeding 10% in some cases (e.g., 'Obj. Pos.' for OpenVLA-OFT, +10.4%).
 - **Sample Efficiency and Convergence Speed:** For OpenVLA, FAN-PPO significantly accelerated convergence, achieving a 90% success rate with approximately one-third of the training steps required by the baseline. For OpenVLA-OFT, distinct performance gains and faster improvement were also observed throughout training, particularly in OOD evaluation.
 - Qualitative examples in the supplementary material illustrated that FAN-PPO executions exhibited better generalization compared to standard PPO.

- **Parameter Sensitivity Analysis (ManiSkill):**
 - Experiments demonstrated that while the method is sensitive to the regularization coefficient $\alpha$ and the standard deviation $\sigma$ of the target Gaussian, effective operating ranges were identified. Excessively large $\alpha$ or very small $\sigma$ could destabilize or degrade training.

#### 5.3. Real-World Experiment

- **JAKA Manipulator Platform:**
 - Evaluation on a physical robot platform for a "pick-and-place" task demonstrated that OpenVLA + FAN-SFT exhibited stronger robustness to spatial perturbations (e.g., perturbed object pose, manipulator initial pose, or target box position) compared to baseline OpenVLA + SFT.
 - In a standard in-distribution task, both methods performed similarly. However, under perturbed conditions, the baseline often failed to grasp or place correctly, while FAN-SFT successfully adapted to the disturbances.

In summary, the main findings consistently indicate that explicitly incorporating the Feasible Action Neighborhood property into VLA finetuning, through a structured regularization term, leads to substantial improvements in task success rates, generalization to OOD scenarios, and sample efficiency across different models, benchmarks, and real-world conditions.

### 6. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in this paper offers several points of significance and potential impact within the field of robotic manipulation and vision-language-action (VLA) models.

### 7. Significance

1. **Addressing a Fundamental Mismatch:** The work identifies and formally addresses a core conceptual discrepancy between the "single correct token" paradigm inherited from linguistic models and the "feasible action neighborhood" inherent in physical robotic actions. This insight is fundamental, as it highlights that ignoring the intrinsic tolerance of physical action spaces can lead to suboptimal learning outcomes.
2. **Principled Approach to Generalization:** By introducing the Feasible Action Neighborhood (FAN) concept and a corresponding regularization term, the paper provides a principled method to inject domain-specific knowledge about action space geometry directly into the learning objective. This moves beyond purely architectural or algorithmic advancements by leveraging the physical nature of the problem.
3. **Improved Robustness and Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Generalization:** The consistent and notable improvements in success rates across diverse OOD scenarios (vision, semantic, execution perturbations) demonstrate that explicitly shaping the policy distribution to encompass a FAN leads to more robust and generalizable robotic agents. This is crucial for deploying robots in varied and unpredictable real-world environments.
4. **Enhanced Sample Efficiency:** The significant reduction in training steps required to achieve target success rates, particularly evident in Reinforced Finetuning (RFT), indicates that the FAN-guided regularization effectively accelerates learning. This addresses a major practical challenge in robotics, where collecting large amounts of interaction data can be costly and time-consuming.
5. **Applicability to Existing Frameworks:** The proposed regularizer is designed to be compatible with existing VLA model architectures and finetuning paradigms (SFT and RFT) without requiring architectural modifications. This ease of integration facilitates its adoption by other researchers and practitioners.

**Potential Impact:**

1. **More Reliable Robotic Systems:** By improving generalization and robustness, the method could lead to the development of robotic systems that are less prone to failure in unforeseen or slightly perturbed situations. This could enhance the reliability and trustworthiness of robots in manufacturing, logistics, service industries, and hazardous environments.
2. **Reduced Data and Training Costs:** The demonstrated gains in sample efficiency could substantially lower the barrier to entry for developing and deploying VLA models. Less data collection and shorter training times translate directly into reduced operational costs and faster iteration cycles for robot development.
3. **Advancements in Foundation Models for Robotics:** As VLA models continue to evolve as foundation models for robotics, methods that enhance their adaptability and generalization capabilities for downstream tasks will be critical. This work contributes a key component towards making these models more effective and practical for real-world robotic applications.
4. **Inspiration for Future Research:** The success of explicitly modeling action geometry through FAN may inspire further research into other physically-informed priors or regularization techniques. Exploring alternative distributions beyond Gaussian, or more adaptive ways to define and learn the FAN, could lead to even more sophisticated and effective VLA training methodologies.
5. **Improved Sim-to-Real Transfer:** Enhanced robustness to minor variations in action execution and state perception could potentially bridge the gap between simulated and real-world robotic performance, a persistent challenge in robotics. Policies that inherently accommodate a feasible action neighborhood might transfer more effectively from simulation to physical hardware.

Overall, the research provides a practical and theoretically grounded approach that addresses a fundamental challenge in VLA model adaptation, with clear implications for developing more robust, efficient, and generalizable robotic manipulation capabilities.
