# Boosting Vision-Language-Action Finetuning with Feasible Action Neighborhood Prior

> [!summary] Summary
> A regularization method for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, termed Feasible Action Neighborhood (FAN) prior, enhances generalization and sample efficiency in robotic manipulation by aligning training objectives with the intrinsic tolerance of physical actions. This approach demonstrated up to an 11.7% increase in task success rates and achieved a 90% success rate with approximately one-third of the training steps required by baselines on standard benchmarks and real-world setups.

## Problem

- Existing VLA training methods, inherited from linguistic settings, overlook the 'intrinsic tolerance' or 'near-equivalence' of physical robotic actions, treating them as discrete tokens.
- Supervised Finetuning (SFT) often leads to overfitting and poor generalization in VLA models by collapsing probability mass onto single demonstrated actions.
- Reinforced Finetuning (RFT) for VLA models is typically sample-inefficient, requiring extensive exploration to implicitly discover robust action distributions for generalization.

## Method

- Formalized the concept of a ==Feasible Action Neighborhood (FAN)== to represent the local tolerance structure inherent in physical actions.
- Introduced a ==FAN-guided regularizer== based on ==Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence== to shape the VLA policy distribution towards a unimodal and smooth Gaussian target.
- Integrated the regularizer into ==Supervised Finetuning (FAN-SFT)== using a ==dynamic covariance matrix== and into ==Reinforced Finetuning (FAN-PPO)== with a fixed covariance matrix, preserving VLA model architectures.

## Results

- **FAN-SFT** achieved an average **+11.7%** increase in success rate for in-distribution tasks and **+5.2%** for out-of-distribution (OOD) tasks on ManiSkill, and improved **OpenVLA-OFT** success from 95.2% to **98.8%** on LIBERO-Spatial.
- **FAN-PPO** enhanced OOD performance by up to **+7.9%** and significantly accelerated convergence, reaching a **90%** success rate with approximately one-third of the training steps compared to PPO baselines on ManiSkill.
- Real-world experiments demonstrated that **FAN-SFT** policies exhibited stronger robustness to spatial perturbations in a pick-and-place task on a JAKA manipulator compared to baseline SFT.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Physical actions in robotic manipulation intrinsically possess a 'feasible action neighborhood' where multiple actions yield similar task progress, a property often ignored by linguistic-style VLA training.
> - Explicitly guiding VLA models to learn and encompass these feasible action neighborhoods through regularization significantly improves their ability to generalize to unseen or perturbed environments.
> - The regularization method provides a principled way to inject domain knowledge about action space geometry into VLA training, enhancing sample efficiency and robustness without architectural changes.
