# GaussianDream: A Feed-Forward 3D Gaussian World Model for Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> GaussianDream introduces a feed-forward 3D Gaussian world model that enhances language-conditioned robotic manipulation by integrating explicit 3D spatial grounding and short-horizon future environment anticipation. It improves real-robot task success rates from 34.4% to 50.0% by leveraging dense 3D supervision during training while maintaining efficient inference.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies suffer from spatial and geometric underspecification due to their 2D pixel-based nature, leading to errors in precise physical interactions.
- Current VLA frameworks underutilize dense visual and spatial supervision from robot trajectories, primarily supervising only immediate control commands.
- Most VLA policies lack explicit future environment evolution capabilities, limiting their generalization to dynamic and complex manipulation scenarios where state changes are crucial.

## Method

- **GaussianDream** augments a base VLA policy by injecting learned prefix tokens, representing a renderable 3D Gaussian scene state, derived from multi-view observations.
- It employs an asymmetric training-inference architecture, providing dense supervision via current 3D Gaussian reconstruction and future Gaussian prediction during training, while discarding auxiliary heads for lightweight inference.
- Pseudo depth maps and 3D scene-flow targets are generated from robot trajectories to provide dense supervision for current and predicted future Gaussian states.

## Results

- Achieved an average success rate of **98.4%** on the LIBERO benchmark and **54.8%** on RoboCasa Human-50, outperforming several existing 3D-enhanced and world model policies in simulation.
- Increased real-robot task success from a baseline of **34.4%** to **50.0%**, with notable improvements observed in spatial-relation and long-horizon manipulation scenarios.
- Maintained an efficient inference speed of 531 ms per action chunk on real robots due to its asymmetric design, supporting practical deployment for online control.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit 3D Gaussian representations can provide valuable spatial grounding for robotic manipulation, directly addressing geometric underspecification in VLA policies.
> - Dense supervision from pseudo 3D scene-flow and multi-view RGB/depth renderings effectively transforms ordinary robot trajectories into structured, rich learning signals.
> - An asymmetric training-inference design allows for comprehensive 3D supervision during training without incurring computational overhead for real-time robotic control during deployment.
