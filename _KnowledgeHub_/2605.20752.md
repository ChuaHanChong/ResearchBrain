# GaussianDream: A Feed-Forward 3D Gaussian World Model for Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> GaussianDream introduces a feed-forward 3D Gaussian world model that enhances language-conditioned robotic manipulation by integrating explicit 3D spatial grounding and short-horizon future environment anticipation. It improves real-robot task success rates from 34.4% to 50.0% by leveraging dense 3D supervision during training while maintaining efficient inference.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies suffer from spatial and geometric underspecification due to their 2D pixel-based nature, leading to errors in precise physical interactions.
- Current VLA frameworks underutilize dense visual and spatial supervision from robot trajectories, primarily supervising only immediate control commands.
- Most VLA policies lack explicit future environment evolution capabilities, limiting their generalization to dynamic and complex manipulation scenarios where state changes are crucial.

## Method

![[2605.20752-method.png]]
*The GaussianDream framework. The model uses a TGE module to encode temporal observations into a prefix Z t GD . During training, auxiliary heads reconstruct the current 3D scene and predict future Gaussian states to provide dense geometric supervision.*

- **GaussianDream** augments a base VLA policy by injecting learned prefix tokens, representing a renderable 3D Gaussian scene state, derived from multi-view observations.
- It employs an asymmetric training-inference architecture, providing dense supervision via current 3D Gaussian reconstruction and future Gaussian prediction during training, while discarding auxiliary heads for lightweight inference.
- Pseudo depth maps and 3D scene-flow targets are generated from robot trajectories to provide dense supervision for current and predicted future Gaussian states.

## Results

- Achieved an average success rate of **98.4%** on the LIBERO benchmark and **54.8%** on RoboCasa Human-50, outperforming several existing 3D-enhanced and world model policies in simulation.
- Increased real-robot task success from a baseline of **34.4%** to **50.0%**, with notable improvements observed in spatial-relation and long-horizon manipulation scenarios.
- Maintained an efficient inference speed of 531 ms per action chunk on real robots due to its asymmetric design, supporting practical deployment for online control.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit 3D Gaussian representations can provide valuable spatial grounding for robotic manipulation, directly addressing geometric underspecification in VLA policies.
> - Dense supervision from pseudo 3D scene-flow and multi-view RGB/depth renderings effectively transforms ordinary robot trajectories into structured, rich learning signals.
> - An asymmetric training-inference design allows for comprehensive 3D supervision during training without incurring computational overhead for real-time robotic control during deployment.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has seen significant advancements with the emergence of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies, such as RT-2, OpenVLA, and π0. These models leverage pretrained vision-language models (VLMs) to enable language-conditioned robot control, grounding semantic priors into physical actions and improving task generalization.

However, existing VLA paradigms, largely based on behavior cloning, exhibit limitations in tasks requiring precise physical interaction. These limitations stem primarily from three areas:
1. **Spatial and Geometric Underspecification**: VLMs primarily operate on 2D pixel grids. Consequently, 3D spatial structures and contact constraints critical for accurate manipulation are often encoded implicitly within visual latents and action labels. This implicit encoding can lead to geometric execution errors, such as misaligned grasp points.
2. **Underutilization of Dense Visual and Spatial Supervision**: Robot trajectories contain rich high-dimensional data including object layouts, appearance cues, and depth information. Standard action-imitation objectives typically supervise only the immediate control command, leaving a substantial amount of dense pixel-level geometric signals unexploited.
3. **Lack of Explicit Future Environment Evolution**: Most VLA policies acquire environment dynamics implicitly from action labels rather than through explicit internal environment emulation and future-state supervision. This restricts their ability to generalize to varied and complex manipulation scenarios where short-horizon state changes are crucial for task success.

To address these challenges, current research efforts largely divide into two categories. One category involves **3D-enhanced policy networks**, which aim to inject explicit geometric structures into VLA frameworks. These methods often employ representations like point clouds or depth maps to anchor the current scene configuration but typically provide limited mechanisms for modeling dynamic future environment evolution. The second category comprises **robotic world models**, which introduce proactive environment reasoning by forecasting future states in various modalities such as pixels, latent spaces, or action spaces. While some exploratory works have begun to integrate 3D Gaussian representations into predictive frameworks, their generative predictions often require computationally intensive iterative voxel optimization or heavy visual autoregressive rollouts, leading to substantial inference overhead. This computational burden makes them less suitable for deployment in high-frequency, real-time robotic control loops.

GaussianDream addresses these gaps by proposing a unified, feed-forward 3D Gaussian world model. It aims to combine the semantic understanding of pretrained VLAs with the geometric precision of 3D representations, specifically 3D Gaussians, while maintaining an efficient inference pipeline. The work differentiates itself by providing explicit 3D spatial grounding, leveraging dense supervision from robot videos, and enabling short-horizon future anticipation, all within an asymmetric training-inference architecture that ensures lightweight operation during deployment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop a robust and efficient framework for language-conditioned robotic manipulation that overcomes critical limitations observed in existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. The motivation for GaussianDream stems from a detailed analysis of these limitations and the shortcomings of current approaches to address them.

The key motivations and objectives are:

1. **Addressing Spatial and Geometric Underspecification**: Standard VLA models, often built upon 2D pixel-based pretrained Vision-Language Models (VLMs), implicitly encode 3D spatial structures. This implicit encoding can lead to a lack of geometric precision, making the robotic control loop susceptible to errors in tasks requiring fine-grained interaction, such as grasping objects accurately or placing them precisely. GaussianDream aims to provide explicit 3D spatial grounding by representing the environment as a 3D Gaussian scene state, thereby directly addressing the geometric underspecification.
2. **Maximizing Dense Visual and Spatial Supervision**: Robotic manipulation demonstrations capture rich visual and spatial information, including object layouts, appearance cues, and depth structures across continuous time. Traditional action-imitation learning primarily supervises immediate control commands, thus underutilizing these dense pixel-level geometric signals. A key motivation is to transform these ordinary robot trajectories into rich, structured learning signals by incorporating dense supervision from RGB renderings, depth estimation, and pseudo 3D scene-flow, thereby making more effective use of available data.
3. **Enabling Explicit Future Environment Anticipation**: Existing VLA policies often infer environment dynamics implicitly through action labels. This limits their ability to anticipate how the environment will evolve after an interaction, which is crucial for generalizing across varying and complex manipulation scenes where task success depends on understanding short-horizon state changes. GaussianDream seeks to integrate explicit future environment emulation by predicting short-horizon Gaussian evolution in structured 3D space. This allows the model to proactively reason about future states.
4. **Maintaining Inference Efficiency for Real-time Control**: While 3D-enhanced policies and robotic world models attempt to address some of the aforementioned issues, many introduce substantial computational overhead during inference. Generative 3D prediction, especially with explicit 3D Gaussian representations, often involves iterative optimization or heavy autoregressive rollouts, rendering them unsuitable for high-frequency, closed-loop robotic control. A critical motivation for GaussianDream is to achieve the benefits of explicit 3D grounding and future anticipation while maintaining an elegant and lightweight inference pipeline. This is addressed through an asymmetric training-and-inference architecture where auxiliary decoding heads are discarded at deployment.
5. **Unified Framework**: The paper aims to develop a single, unified framework that simultaneously tackles the challenges of explicit 3D space grounding, dense supervision, and future anticipation. This unified approach seeks to bridge the semantic understanding capabilities of pretrained VLAs with the geometric precision afforded by 3D Gaussian representations.

In summary, GaussianDream is motivated by the need for more geometrically precise, densely supervised, and temporally aware robotic manipulation policies that can operate efficiently in real-world scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

GaussianDream is designed as a feed-forward 3D Gaussian world model plug-in for language-conditioned robotic manipulation, employing an asymmetric training and inference strategy. The core idea is to leverage 3D Gaussian representations for dense geometric and temporal supervision during training, while ensuring a lightweight inference-time operation by distilling this knowledge into a compact prefix.

#### 3.1. Overview of GaussianDream

The system augments a base continuous-action VLA policy by injecting learned prefix tokens, denoted as $Z^{GD}_t$, into its multimodal context. This prefix is derived from a short temporal history of multi-view observations $o_{t-K:t}$ and learnable GaussianDream queries $Q^{GD}$.

During **training**, the $Z^{GD}_t$ prefix is processed by two auxiliary Gaussian heads:
1. A static reconstruction head ($R_\phi$) that produces a current 3D Gaussian scene state ($G_t$).
2. A future prediction head ($D_\psi$) that predicts horizon-conditioned future Gaussian changes ($\hat{G}_{t+\Delta}$).
These heads provide dense supervision using RGB rendering, depth, and pseudo 3D scene-flow signals.

During **inference**, all auxiliary decoding heads are discarded. Only the compact $Z^{GD}_t$ prefix is retained to condition the action generation of the policy ($\pi_\theta$), avoiding any test-time Gaussian reconstruction or future prediction.

#### 3.2. Current Gaussian Reconstruction

This branch focuses on providing explicit 3D spatial grounding. The learned prefix $Z^{GD}_t$ is intended to represent a renderable 3D scene state.
1. **Latent Grid Formation**: The 1024 GaussianDream tokens are reshaped into a $32 \times 32$ spatially organized latent grid. This structure helps preserve spatial locality and dense geometric information.
2. **Feature Map Generation**: A Gaussian decoder ($B_G$) upsamples this latent grid into a dense feature map $F^G_t \in \mathbb{R}^{256 \times 256 \times 128}$. This feature map encodes geometric and appearance information.
3. **Gaussian Attribute Prediction**:
 - A **geometry head** ($H_{geo}$) predicts attributes such as depth, rotation, scale, and opacity from $F^G_t$.
 - An **appearance head** ($H_{app}$) conditions on the current RGB observation $o_t$ to estimate spherical harmonics coefficients for Gaussian appearance.
4. **Gaussian Scene State Construction**: The predicted depth is unprojected to form Gaussian centers ($\mu^i_t$), which are then combined with the other attributes ($\theta^i_t$) to construct the current Gaussian scene state $G_t = \{(\mu^i_t, \theta^i_t)\}^{N_t}_{i=1}$.
5. **Supervision**: The reconstructed Gaussian scene $G_t$ is rendered to generate RGB and depth images, which are then supervised against ground-truth or pseudo-ground-truth targets.

#### 3.3. Future Gaussian Prediction

This branch aims to capture short-horizon geometric evolution and dense temporal structure.
1. **Multi-scale 3D-aware Feature Extraction**: Multi-scale 3D-aware patch features are extracted from the temporal observation sequence $o_{t-K:t}$ using VGGT (Visual Geometry Grounded Transformer). These features are pooled into a $32 \times 32$ grid and projected into temporal tokens.
2. **Temporal Gaussian Evolution (TGE) Module**: This module is introduced to aggregate spatial geometry and model future evolution. Learnable GaussianDream queries interact with the temporal tokens. The TGE module consists of 12 attention blocks that alternate between frame-wise spatial interaction and time-slot temporal attention. The output from the latest frame of the TGE module is projected to form the GaussianDream prefix $Z^{GD}_t$.
3. **Dynamic Head Prediction**: Given the current Gaussian state $G_t$, a dynamic head ($H_{vel}$) predicts horizon-conditioned center displacements ($\nu^{(\Delta)}_t$) for future steps (e.g., $t+1$ to $t+5$). A learnable horizon embedding ($e_\Delta$) and temporal scale factor ($\alpha_\Delta$) allow the model to distinguish dynamics across different prediction horizons.
4. **Future Gaussian State Construction**: The predicted displacements update only the Gaussian centers ($\hat{\mu}^i_{t+\Delta} = \mu^i_t + \Delta x^{(\Delta)}_i$), while non-positional attributes ($\theta^i_t$) are reused from the current Gaussian template. This focuses prediction on interaction-induced geometric changes.
5. **Supervision**: The predicted future Gaussian states ($\hat{G}_{t+\Delta}$) are supervised using future RGB renderings, depth, and pseudo 3D scene-flow signals.

#### 3.4. GaussianDream Training and Efficient Inference

The training process is divided into two stages:

1. **Stage I: GaussianDream Pretraining**: The reconstruction and prediction heads are trained independently of the action learning. For each demonstration sequence, pseudo depth maps (generated using Depth Anything V2) and pseudo 3D scene-flow targets (computed from adjacent frames using RAFT and back-projection) are used as dense supervision. The GaussianDream objective ($L_{GD}$) combines losses for current reconstruction ($L_{cur}$ - depth and RGB rendering) and future prediction ($L_{fut}$ - depth, RGB rendering, and 3D scene-flow). The prediction horizon is gradually expanded during this stage.
2. **Stage II: GaussianDream-Conditioned Policy Learning**: After pretraining, the VLA policy is jointly trained with the auxiliary Gaussian losses ($L_{GD}$). The action loss ($L_{act}$) is based on a flow-matching objective, as used in the base $\pi_{0.5}$ policy. The overall objective is $L = L_{act} + \lambda_{GD} L_{GD}$. This stage adapts the prefix for executable control while preserving its learned spatial-temporal structure.

**Inference**: A critical aspect of GaussianDream is its asymmetric inference design. At test time, all auxiliary decoding heads (for rendering, depth, velocity, and Gaussian decoding) are completely discarded. The temporal buffer is encoded into the compact $Z^{GD}_t$ prefix tokens, which are then injected into the VLA policy's context. The base policy generates actions using its standard denoising procedure, without requiring any future rollouts, test-time rendering, Gaussian decoding, or additional planning. This design maintains computational efficiency for real-time robotic control.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of GaussianDream was conducted across various robotic manipulation benchmarks in both simulated and real-world environments. The results demonstrate the framework's effectiveness in enhancing precise robotic interaction, explicit 3D spatial grounding, and future environment anticipation.

#### 4.1. Performance on Simulation Benchmarks

- **LIBERO Benchmark (Liu et al., 2023)**: GaussianDream achieved a high average success rate of **98.4%**. It recorded the best performance in the Spatial and Goal protocol categories, with 99.0% and 99.0% success rates, respectively. This performance surpassed several existing 3D-enhanced policies, including GeoVLA, VLA-4D, 3D-CAVLA, QDepth-VLA, and Spatial Forcing (PyTorch implementation). While LingBot-VA achieved a slightly higher average, it utilized a larger autoregressive video-action pipeline for control, whereas GaussianDream employs a more efficient prefix-based inference by using prediction solely as training-time supervision.
- **RoboCasa Human-50 Benchmark (Nasiriany et al., 2024)**: GaussianDream demonstrated a clear advantage in spatially precise pick-and-place tasks. It achieved the best average success rate of **54.8%** and the strongest performance in the Pick&Place category (43.8%). This indicates that the explicit 3D reconstruction and future prediction capabilities of GaussianDream contribute to improved localization-sensitive manipulation. It outperformed policies like $\pi_0$, $\pi_{0.5}$, GWM, and Being-H0.5 in overall average success.

#### 4.2. Performance on Real-Robot Tasks

- GaussianDream significantly improved physical execution on real-robot tasks. It increased the average success rate from 34.4% (achieved by the baseline $\pi_{0.5}$ policy) to **50.0%**.
- Gains were observed across all tested scenario groups (attribute grounding, spatial relations, stacking/unstacking, long-horizon execution). The most notable improvements occurred in spatial-relation and long-horizon scenarios, suggesting that the GaussianDream prefix provides valuable geometric context for action generation in complex physical settings.
- Supplementary analysis indicated that GaussianDream reduced abrupt trajectory changes compared to the $\pi_{0.5}$ baseline, contributing to smoother execution.
- Regarding inference speed, the asymmetric design allowed GaussianDream to achieve 531 ms per action chunk during deployment (with auxiliary heads discarded), which was faster than a diagnostic configuration retaining the heads (569 ms) and a World Action Model baseline (over 700 ms per action chunk), supporting its practicality for online robot control.

#### 4.3. Qualitative Analysis

- **Current Reconstruction**: Visualizations of depth renderings for current reconstruction showed accurate object layouts in the observed frame, indicating that the GaussianDream prefix can be decoded into a spatially grounded 3D representation.
- **Future Prediction**: Predicted future states maintained temporal coherence across consecutive steps and effectively modeled object interactions and coordinated motions. This suggests that GaussianDream learns structured spatial-temporal representations from sequences of observations.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Component Analysis**:
 - Using current Gaussian reconstruction alone achieved 97.0% success on LIBERO, confirming that explicit 3D spatial grounding through Gaussian states provides a strong prior.
 - Adding future Gaussian prediction further improved performance to 97.5%, demonstrating that supervision on short-horizon state changes provides complementary benefits beyond static current-frame grounding.
- **Supervision Analysis**:
 - The inclusion of RGB rendering loss improved the reconstruction-only variant from 97.0% to 97.3%, suggesting that image-level consistency helps align predicted Gaussians with observations.
 - Removing depth supervision (while retaining future prediction and rendering) resulted in a performance drop to 97.2%, indicating that RGB consistency alone is insufficient to fully constrain metric geometry.
 - The full GaussianDream model, utilizing reconstruction, prediction, RGB rendering, and depth supervision, achieved 98.4%, validating the complementary roles of all these components.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

GaussianDream represents a notable advancement in language-conditioned robotic manipulation by effectively addressing several critical limitations of existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enhanced Geometric Precision and Spatial Reasoning**: By explicitly grounding actions in reconstructed 3D Gaussian states, GaussianDream directly tackles the problem of spatial and geometric underspecification in VLA policies. This leads to more precise robotic interactions, as evidenced by its strong performance on tasks requiring accurate localization and manipulation, such as pick-and-place and spatial-relation tasks on LIBERO and RoboCasa. This capability is crucial for deploying robots in environments demanding fine-grained physical interaction.
2. **Effective Utilization of Dense Data Supervision**: The framework demonstrates a method to extract rich, structured learning signals from standard robot trajectories. By generating pseudo depth and 3D scene-flow targets from consecutive frames and using them for dense RGB, depth, and flow supervision, GaussianDream transforms underutilized visual data into potent training signals. This approach could potentially reduce the reliance on specialized 3D annotations and make better use of large-scale robot demonstration datasets.
3. **Proactive Environment Anticipation**: The integration of horizon-conditioned future Gaussian prediction enables the model to explicitly anticipate short-horizon environment evolution. This capability allows the policy to reason about the dynamic consequences of its actions, enhancing generalization to varying and complex manipulation scenes where interaction outcomes depend on future state changes.
4. **Practical Efficiency for Real-time Control**: A key contribution is the asymmetric training and inference design. By utilizing full 3D Gaussian reconstruction and prediction for dense supervision during training but discarding all auxiliary decoding heads at deployment, GaussianDream maintains a lightweight, feed-forward inference pipeline. This addresses a major bottleneck of many 3D-aware world models, which often introduce prohibitive computational overheads for real-time robotic control, making GaussianDream a viable option for online operation on physical robots.
5. **Robustness and Generalization to Real-World Scenarios**: The demonstrated performance improvements on real-robot tasks, particularly in spatial-relation and long-horizon scenarios, indicate that the learned GaussianDream prefix provides a robust geometric context for action generation in physical environments. This suggests an improved ability to transfer knowledge from simulation or large datasets to real-world deployment, even in the presence of real-world noise and embodiment mismatch.
6. **Pioneering Integration of 3D Gaussian Splatting**: GaussianDream provides a concrete example of how 3D Gaussian Splatting, a representation known for its efficiency in novel view synthesis, can be effectively adapted and integrated into a robotic world model for control. This opens new avenues for leveraging explicit 3D representations within reinforcement learning and imitation learning frameworks.

In conclusion, GaussianDream offers a significant step towards more capable, geometrically aware, and efficient general-purpose robotic manipulation. Its approach of unifying explicit 3D grounding, dense supervision, and future anticipation within an inference-efficient architecture has the potential to influence the design of future VLA models and accelerate the development of robust, generalist robots.
