# BeyondMimic: From Motion Tracking to Versatile Humanoid Control via Guided Diffusion

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from UC Berkeley and Stanford University developed BeyondMimic, a framework combining scalable reinforcement learning for motion tracking with guided latent diffusion for versatile humanoid control. This enabled a Unitree G1 robot to master diverse, agile human motions and adapt to unseen tasks, demonstrating a 70.8% user preference for naturalness and successfully performing complex acrobatics and zero-shot task composition.

## Problem

- Existing humanoid control methods for diverse tasks either produce unnatural motions or require extensive, motion-specific tuning, limiting scalability.
- Prior versatile control approaches often sacrifice agility and naturalness or struggle to generalize to novel tasks with implicit or hard-to-specify objectives.
- Robust sim-to-real transfer of complex, dynamic learned behaviors traditionally relies on heavy domain randomization and complex observation/reward engineering.

## Method

- A scalable reinforcement learning stage uses anchor-centered tracking and a compact, motion-agnostic reward function to acquire a broad repertoire of agile human-like movements with minimal domain randomization.
- A versatile control stage leverages a latent state-action diffusion model, trained on VAE-compressed motion, to enable online classifier guidance for adapting to novel, unseen tasks at inference time without retraining.
- An optimized C++ deployment framework, utilizing CPU for RL policies and mobile GPU for diffusion inference, ensures low-latency, real-time control and robust zero-shot transfer to hardware.

## Results

- Successfully acquired over **2.5** hours of diverse human motions, with 30 representative clips demonstrating zero-shot transfer to a physical Unitree G1 robot, performing complex acrobatics like aerial cartwheels.
- Achieved human-level agility (e.g., peak accelerations of 31 m/s² during cartwheels) and naturalness, with a user study showing **70.8%** preference for BeyondMimic's motions over native controllers.
- Demonstrated versatile, zero-shot control for unseen tasks including waypoint navigation, joystick teleoperation, obstacle avoidance, and seamless composition of complex acrobatic maneuvers with walking/running.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - High-fidelity and robust humanoid motion tracking can be achieved with a minimalist RL formulation and targeted domain randomization, challenging the necessity for extensive heuristics.
> - Latent diffusion models, when combined with classifier guidance, provide a powerful mechanism for transforming imitative skills into a generative control system capable of zero-shot adaptation and dynamic skill composition.
> - The inherent ability of diffusion models to learn data distribution gradient fields allows for online optimization towards arbitrary task objectives while preserving the naturalness and physical feasibility of learned human-like motions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of humanoid robots capable of agile, natural, and versatile movement in human-centric environments has been a long-standing grand challenge in robotics. BeyondMimic addresses critical limitations in existing approaches by presenting a unified framework that scales to diverse human motions and enables versatile skill composition for unseen tasks.

Historically, humanoid control relied on **model-based paradigms** (e.g., Zero-Moment Point, capture point control, whole-body control). While providing stable locomotion, these methods often produced unnatural or stiff movements, were restricted to limited motion ranges (e.g., persistently bent knees), and struggled with unmodeled dynamics in highly dynamic or contact-rich scenarios. Simplifications inherent in these models often led to "unnatural motions" (Ref. 13-15) and difficulties in real-world transfer for dynamic skills (Ref. 18).

The advent of **learning-based approaches**, particularly reinforcement learning (RL), offered a promising alternative. RL-trained humanoids achieved diverse locomotion (walking, running, climbing stairs, traversing challenging terrains) and some compliance (Ref. 20-28). However, these successes often depended on **hand-crafted reward functions** specific to each task, making scalability to a large repertoire of skills prohibitively costly and yielding unnatural movements that lacked human-likeness (Ref. 29).

A more recent trend, and a direct precursor to BeyondMimic, involves **learning from human motion data**. Early RL-based motion tracking methods, like DeepMimic (Ref. 32-35), demonstrated impressive agile skills but often required motion-specific tuning and lacked generalization beyond trained motions. Adversarial Motion Priors (AMPs) (Ref. 36-37) improved generalization by learning motion "styles" but were generally not reusable across different tasks without retraining.

To achieve **versatility**, prior work explored two main routes:
1. **Hierarchical control:** Combining a task-agnostic motion tracker with a task-level planner (Ref. 38-43). This allowed skill reuse but typically sacrificed agility and naturalness due to decoupled training and planner-controller mismatches (Ref. 44-45).
2. **Multi-task generative models:** Approaches using Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) (Ref. 46-47) showed promise in skill composition but were limited by explicit goal conditioning during training and struggled to generalize to tasks with implicit or hard-to-specify objectives, leading to "jittery motions and degraded naturalness" (Ref. 48).

BeyondMimic directly tackles these limitations. It enters the landscape by presenting a framework that **overcomes the need for motion-specific tuning** in learning diverse, agile, and natural human behaviors. Crucially, it moves beyond mere imitation by leveraging **diffusion models** for generative control. Diffusion models are a cutting-edge technique in generative AI (Ref. 49, 64-68), offering a unique advantage in their ability to learn the gradient fields of the data distribution, which allows for **classifier guidance** – online optimization towards novel objectives at test time. This positions BeyondMimic at the forefront of combining high-fidelity physics-based control with advanced, adaptive generative AI, paving the way for more intelligent and autonomous humanoid behaviors that generalize beyond their training data.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching motivation behind BeyondMimic is to advance the capabilities of humanoid robots towards a future where they can seamlessly integrate into human environments, possessing the agility, naturalness, and versatility commonly envisioned in science fiction (e.g., Data, Astro, Baymax). To achieve this ambitious vision, the authors outline several key objectives:

1. **Scalable Acquisition of Human-like Motor Skills:** A core objective is to enable humanoid robots to learn a broad repertoire of complex, agile, and natural motor skills directly from human demonstrations. The critical challenge here is scalability: previous methods either produced unnatural motions when trying to be general or required extensive motion-specific tuning for each new skill. BeyondMimic aims to overcome this by developing a "one-recipe-fits-all" framework that uses a single setup and shared hyperparameters to master a wide range of diverse behaviors, from aerial cartwheels to sprinting, while maintaining human-like performance.

2. **Versatile Control for Unseen Tasks and Seamless Skill Composition:** Moving beyond simply imitating existing motions, the research seeks to empower humanoids with the versatility to compose these learned skills dynamically and adapt to novel, unseen downstream tasks. This means the robot should be able to switch tasks, integrate different skills, and achieve goals (e.g., obstacle avoidance, teleoperation) without requiring any task-specific training or fine-tuning. The motivation is to enable "generalization and versatility beyond training setups."

3. **Robust and High-Fidelity Sim-to-Real Transfer:** A significant hurdle in humanoid robotics is transferring learned behaviors from simulation to physical hardware. Previous works often relied on heavy domain randomization, reward regularization, and complex observations to bridge the "sim-to-real gap," often at the cost of performance or requiring motion-specific tuning. BeyondMimic aims to achieve zero-shot transfer of complex, dynamic, and agile behaviors to real hardware using a more principled and compact formulation, challenging the conventional wisdom of extensive heuristics.

4. **Achieve Human-level Agility and Naturalness:** The goal is not just to perform tasks, but to perform them with the dynamic quality and aesthetic naturalness characteristic of human movement. This includes mastering highly dynamic and acrobatic feats, exhibiting biomechanical similarities in gaits (like walking and running), and demonstrating perceptually natural responses to disturbances.

In essence, BeyondMimic is motivated by the desire to push the limits of both the breadth and depth of motor skills that humanoids can acquire and the flexibility with which they can apply these skills to novel situations in the real world.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

BeyondMimic's framework is structured into two interconnected stages: **(1) Scalable Human Motion Tracking via Reinforcement Learning (RL)** and **(2) Versatile Humanoid Control via Guided Diffusion**.

#### 3.1. Scalable Human Motion Tracking via RL

This first stage focuses on enabling the robot to accurately imitate a diverse set of human motions with high fidelity and robustness, particularly for zero-shot sim-to-real transfer.

- **Problem Formulation:** Motion tracking is formulated as a Markov Decision Process (MDP). The goal is to reproduce human-retargeted reference motions (generalized positions and velocities) on a humanoid robot.
- **Anchor-Centered Tracking:** To enhance robustness to global perturbations and sim-to-real discrepancies while preserving motion style, the policy tracks *relative* body poses rather than global ones. An "anchor body" (typically the root/torso) follows the reference motion directly, while other target bodies track their desired poses relative to this anchor. This allows for global drift without sacrificing local coordination and style.
- **Simple, Motion-Agnostic Reward Function:** Unlike prior methods with numerous ad-hoc regularizations, BeyondMimic uses a compact reward composed of:
 - **Unified Task Term:** Gaussian-shaped exponential functions penalizing mean-squared errors in position, orientation, linear velocity, and angular velocity across a compact set of target bodies (including end-effectors).
 - **Three Lightweight Regularization Penalties:** Joint limits, action rate (for smoothness), and self-contact forces (for collision avoidance). This minimalist design promotes generalizability across motions without specific tuning.
- **Continuous, Robot-Centric Observation and Action Spaces:**
 - **Observation:** Includes motion phase (progress cue), anchor pose error (minimal global cue for balance), IMU twist, relative joint positions, joint velocities, and the previous action. This input is kept minimal and robot-centric, avoiding complex temporal stacking.
 - **Action:** Normalized joint position setpoints sent to low-level motor PD controllers. The PD gains are heuristically set based on the reflected inertia (armature) of the joints, promoting compliance while ensuring stability.
- **Minimal Domain Randomization:** Challenging the assumption of heavy randomization, the authors found that randomizing only truly uncertain physical properties (ground friction, restitution, default joint positions/offsets, torso center of mass) and applying random velocity perturbations was sufficient for robust sim-to-real transfer. This prevents diluting the control objective.
- **Adaptive Sampling:** For long and complex motion sequences, an adaptive sampling strategy is employed during RL training. This prioritizes segments with higher empirical failure rates, leading to faster learning and better convergence on difficult parts of the trajectory before gradually reverting to uniform sampling.

#### 3.2. Versatile Humanoid Control via Guided Diffusion

This second stage focuses on taking the diverse skills learned in Stage 1 and enabling their seamless composition and adaptation to novel, unseen tasks at inference time without retraining.

- **Motivation for Diffusion Models:** Diffusion models are chosen because they implicitly learn the *gradient fields* (score function) of the data distribution, which allows for "classifier guidance" – an online optimization process that steers unconditional generation towards task-specific conditional generation via gradient ascent.
- **Latent State-Action Diffusion Model:** To bridge the gap between state-space task objectives and action-space policies, and to handle high-dimensional, irregular action spaces, a latent state-action diffusion model is proposed:
 - **Stage 1: Variational Autoencoder (VAE) for Motion Representation:** A conditional VAE is trained (using DAgger for robustness) to compress reference motion components (motion phase, anchor pose error) into a smooth, lower-dimensional latent representation `z`. This latent, combined with proprioceptive inputs (gravity, IMU twist, joint states, previous action), is then used by the VAE decoder to reconstruct the actions. This creates a structured, smooth latent space suitable for diffusion.
 - **Stage 2: State-Latent Diffusion Model Training:** The VAE is rolled out on diverse human motions to collect *state-latent trajectories*. These trajectories (which include root and body features parameterized in a hybrid character-yaw-centric frame) are then used to train a denoising diffusion probabilistic model (DDPM). The trajectories are augmented with Ornstein-Uhlenbeck noise during collection to improve robustness. The model is self-supervised, learning to reconstruct clean trajectories from noisy versions.
- **Online Optimization via Classifier Guidance:** At inference time, the trained diffusion model (which has learned the distribution of human-like trajectories in its latent space) can be guided towards novel objectives *without retraining*. This is achieved by incorporating arbitrary, differentiable task-specific cost functions `G(tau)` into the denoising process. The model iteratively refines its predictions by adding a conditional gradient term (`-nabla_tau G(tau)`) to the unconditional score function, effectively "steering" the generated trajectory towards minimizing the cost while staying within the learned distribution of feasible human-like motions.
 - **Examples of Task Costs:** Joystick steering (minimizing velocity difference from command), waypoint navigation (combining position tracking and velocity minimization near goal), and obstacle avoidance (using a signed distance field (SDF) with a barrier function to steer away from obstacles).
- **Deployment Architecture:** A highly optimized C++ deployment framework ensures low-latency, real-time control and precise synchronization. RL policies run on the robot's CPU (using ONNX Runtime), while the diffusion model runs on a mobile GPU for inference acceleration (using TensorRT), with the VAE decoder running on CPU. For some tasks, state estimation (low-level generalized momentum observer + Kalman filter, or LiDAR-inertial odometry) is used, and motion capture provides ground truth for environmental context in complex navigation.

In summary, BeyondMimic uniquely combines robust, scalable RL for foundational skill acquisition with the powerful generative and optimization capabilities of latent diffusion models, enabling a new level of versatility and generalization for humanoid control.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

BeyondMimic delivers impressive results across multiple dimensions: scalable learning, human-level agility, naturalness, and versatile control.

#### 4.1. Scalable Learning from Human Motions

- **Diverse Skill Repertoire:** The framework successfully learned from approximately 2.5 hours of diverse human motions, validating all in high-fidelity simulation. Crucially, 30 representative clips (totaling 15 minutes) transferred **zero-shot** to a physical Unitree G1 robot. This repertoire included static poses (single-leg standing), highly dynamic acrobatics (single-leg jumps, turn kicks, cartwheels), and stylized behaviors (elderly walking, dance sequences, sport moves).
- **Unified Formulation:** A single formulation and shared hyperparameters were used across all motions, demonstrating that motion-specific tuning is not required to acquire a broad and agile skill set.

#### 4.2. Human-level Agility

- **Complex Acrobatics:** The robot performed highly dynamic sequences in challenging outdoor environments (soft soil, uneven ground), including aerial cartwheels, 180° side spin kicks, and 360° flip kicks.
- **Quantitative Metrics:** During aerial cartwheels, the robot achieved peak accelerations of 31 m/s² and pelvic angular velocities up to 20 rad/s (mean 7.01 rad/s), comparable to skilled human performance (average 7.75 rad/s). Landings were clean with minimal recovery.
- **Robust Contact-Rich Control:** Demonstrated agility in two consecutive cartwheels, crawling, and jumping up from the ground. It also reproduced complex athletic gestures, like Cristiano Ronaldo's celebration jump-turns, five times consecutively without stability or style loss, surpassing prior work that reported only a single execution.

#### 4.3. Natural, Human-like Behaviors

- **Biomechanics:** The robot's Ground Reaction Force (GRF) profiles during walking and running were comparable to human data, exhibiting distinct heel-strike and propulsive push-off peaks in walking, and single peaks in running. The only noted difference was sharper force peaks in walking due to the lack of a toe joint.
- **Perceptual Naturalness (User Study):** In a user study (N=77), BeyondMimic's motions were significantly preferred (70.8% vs. 29.2%, p < .001) over Unitree's native controller for overall human-likeness and naturalness, with strong preferences for both walking (57.0%) and running (84.7%).
- **Robust Recovery:** The robot demonstrated compliant and natural recovery under external disturbances, pausing and stabilizing when gently held while walking, then smoothly resuming once released.

#### 4.4. Versatile Humanoid Control (Zero-Shot)

- **Command-Conditioned Locomotion:**
 - **Waypoint Navigation:** Generated smooth, stable trajectories to reach waypoint goals from various starting positions.
 - **Joystick Teleoperation:** Exhibited smooth omnidirectional walking, reliably tracking commanded linear and yaw velocities, and robustly recovering from large external perturbations (kicks) while pursuing objectives.
 - **Multimodal Gaits and Transitions:** Produced distinct human-like gaits (walking/jogging) for similar low-velocity commands and achieved smooth gait transitions from walking to running *without explicit labels* or prior training on transitions, inferring them naturally.
- **Motion Inpainting and Task Transitioning:**
 - Guided by sparse future keyframes (e.g., for cartwheels), the diffusion policy smoothly inpainted the transitions and intermediate motions, demonstrating online insertion and composition of agile skills.
 - Achieved seamless mode switching between different tasks (e.g., joystick-controlled walking, keyframe-conditioned cartwheels, then running), showcasing versatility in task specification over long horizons (e.g., three consecutive cartwheels interwoven with walking/running).
- **Task Composition:**
 - Demonstrated intuitive task composition by combining multiple objectives (waypoint tracking + obstacle avoidance via a Signed Distance Field (SDF)) without retraining. The robot successfully detoured around obstacles to reach target waypoints, showcasing scene-aware navigation.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies

- **MDP Parameters:** Validation showed that continuous orientation representations (Rot6D), the absence of temporal history in observations, accurate armature settings, and minimal deployment latency are critical for successful sim-to-real transfer of complex motions.
- **Adaptive Sampling:** Proven essential for learning difficult segments within long motion sequences, accelerating training convergence, and preventing failure on challenging parts (e.g., cartwheels).
- **Latent Diffusion:** Achieved a 95% success rate for cartwheels in sim-to-sim transfer (vs. 5% without latent encoding), highlighting its crucial role in enhancing robustness for complex acrobatic motions and transferring this performance to hardware.

These findings collectively demonstrate that BeyondMimic not only achieves state-of-the-art performance in individual aspects of humanoid control but also unifies them into a comprehensive framework for scalable, agile, natural, and versatile robot behavior.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

BeyondMimic represents a significant advancement in the field of humanoid robotics, pushing the boundaries of what these complex machines can achieve in terms of motor skill acquisition and versatile control. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Redefining Scalability in Humanoid Skill Learning:** The work introduces a "one-recipe-fits-all" framework for learning hundreds of diverse human motions with a single set of hyperparameters and a compact reward formulation. This dramatically reduces the engineering effort required to teach new skills to humanoids, making the acquisition of large, versatile skill repertoires truly scalable. This is a critical step towards creating humanoids with broad general-purpose capabilities.

2. **Bridging the Gap Between Imitation and Generalization:** A core contribution is moving beyond mere imitation to *generative, adaptive control*. By leveraging latent diffusion models and classifier guidance, BeyondMimic enables robots to synthesize and compose learned skills into novel trajectories that solve unseen tasks at inference time, *without any task-specific retraining*. This ability to adapt and generalize on-the-fly, even to implicitly defined objectives like obstacle avoidance, is a crucial step towards truly intelligent and autonomous humanoid behavior.

3. **Challenging Sim-to-Real Transfer Dogma:** The paper successfully demonstrates robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer of highly dynamic and agile behaviors (like aerial cartwheels) with a minimalistic approach to domain randomization and reward shaping. This challenges the prevailing assumption that heavy heuristics and extensive tuning are always necessary for real-world deployment of complex learned skills, suggesting that a principled formulation and careful system implementation can be more effective.

4. **Advancing Human-like Naturalness and Agility:** BeyondMimic achieves unprecedented levels of human-like naturalness and agility in learned motions, validated through both biomechanical analysis (GRF profiles) and user studies (perceptual preference). This not only improves the aesthetic quality of robot movement but also potentially enhances human-robot interaction and acceptance in shared environments.

5. **Paving the Way for Foundational Models in Robotics:** The framework's task-agnostic training and ability to generalize across motions and objectives positions it as a significant step towards "foundational models for humanoid behavior." Such models, learned directly from human demonstrations, could serve as powerful, adaptable priors for a wide array of future humanoid applications, much like large language models have transformed natural language processing.

6. **Practical Impact and Open-Source Contribution:** The open-source release of BeyondMimic and its adoption in popular reinforcement learning repositories (MJLab, Unitree RL Lab) underscores its practical utility and potential to accelerate research within the broader robotics community. This directly facilitates reproducibility and further development by other researchers.

While the paper acknowledges limitations such as reliance on state estimation, short prediction horizons, and challenges with fine-grained control guidance, these open avenues for future research. Overall, BeyondMimic significantly pushes the state of the art in humanoid control, offering a scalable, robust, and versatile paradigm that brings the vision of agile, natural, and intelligent humanoid robots closer to reality.
