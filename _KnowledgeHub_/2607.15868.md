# EgoExoMoCap: Distributed Ego-Exo Human Motion Capture

> [!summary] Summary
> EgoExoMoCap is a distributed motion capture framework that fuses continuous egocentric head/wrist tracking with intermittent exocentric camera views from multiple HMD wearers, using DINOv3-gated ray canonicalization to combine signals. It reconstructs full-body motion robustly under occlusion and out-of-view scenarios, achieving state-of-the-art results on Nymeria and EgoHumans versus ego-only and exo-only baselines.

## Problem

- Egocentric-only HMD tracking cannot faithfully reconstruct invisible body parts like lower-body poses since only head and wrist signals are observed directly.
- Exocentric monocular capture struggles with global motion ambiguity, occlusions, fast camera motion, and image blur when using a single external RGB camera.
- Multi-human motion estimation methods rely on external observations or individually worn sensors, without exploiting mutual observations among co-located wearers.

## Method

- **EgoNet**: a temporal ==MLP-Mixer== network that coarsely estimates ==SMPL poses== from egocentric signals alone, providing region proposals for localizing the wearer in exocentric images.
- ==Wearer-conditioned ray canonicalization==: unprojects ==ViTPose== 2D keypoints into 3D rays, scales by observer-wearer distance, and transforms into the wearer's head-local frame for rotation-invariant features.
- Learned ==DINOv3== visibility gating (**ScoreNet**): predicts per-joint confidence scores from CLS-token context to softly suppress unreliable exocentric rays under occlusion before ==spatial-temporal transformer== fusion.

## Results

- On Nymeria three-point tracking, EgoExoMoCap achieves **5.72cm** MPJPE versus **6.47cm** for a naive PromptHMR+EgoPoser fusion baseline and **7.74cm** for the best ego-only method.
- On EgoHumans, scaling to a multi-observer setup drops MPJPE from **8.80cm** (single observer) to **7.11cm**, and qualitative multi-view fusion reduces error from about **9-11cm** to **6.43cm**.
- Ablations show removing egocentric input raises MPJPE to **11.45cm**, and Gaussian noise up to **10cm** on EgoNet's coarse pose only degrades final MPJPE by **0.54cm**, showing graceful robustness.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Turning each HMD wearer into both a motion subject and a mobile observer of others creates a natural distributed sensing opportunity without added hardware.
> - Canonicalizing cross-view geometric features into a rotation- and position-invariant local frame is critical for generalization across scenes and camera motions.
> - Learned semantic gating from holistic image context outperforms raw 2D-detector confidence, since detectors can be confidently wrong under occlusion by scene objects.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Accurate and robust human motion capture in unconstrained, real-world environments is a critical area of research for applications in embodied AI, virtual and augmented reality (VR/AR), robotics, and human-computer interaction. Traditional motion capture (mocap) systems typically rely on bulky multi-camera setups or obtrusive suits, limiting their scalability and use outside controlled studio environments.

Existing research approaches in motion capture from head-mounted devices (HMDs) generally fall into two categories:
- **Egocentric body tracking**: Focuses on estimating the motion of the subject wearing the HMD. While offering scalability and lightweight use, these methods often face challenges such as sensor noise, limited visibility of the wearer's body from their own egocentric cameras, and difficulty in faithfully reconstructing lower body movements. Approaches like AvatarPoser, EgoPoser, EgoAllo, and RPM are examples in this domain, primarily relying on head and sometimes wrist trajectories.
- **Exocentric body tracking**: Focuses on capturing the movements of people in the wearer's surroundings, typically from external RGB cameras (e.g., a phone or another HMD's camera). These methods, such as PromptHMR, frequently struggle with reconstructing global motion in world space due to inherent ambiguities, and are susceptible to body occlusions, fast camera movements, and image blur.

Recent advancements have also explored multi-human motion estimation, recognizing the importance of capturing coordinated behaviors and interactions. However, these methods often rely on external observations or individually worn sensors without exploiting the potential for mutual observations among multiple users. Prior work has largely explored egocentric and exocentric sensing in isolation, with limited efforts using third-person views primarily for training-time supervision for egocentric pose estimation.

This research, EgoExoMoCap, addresses a gap in the landscape by proposing a distributed framework that *jointly* leverages both egocentric self-motion cues and exocentric tracking signals from multiple HMDs. It capitalizes on the capabilities of modern HMDs (such as Project Aria glasses) that provide multi-modal data streams and support distributed information exchange and accurate time alignment. By enabling each participant to act as both a motion subject and a mobile observer of others, the work explores a collaborative motion capture paradigm that aims to overcome the individual limitations of purely egocentric or exocentric systems, particularly in challenging real-world scenarios involving occlusions and out-of-view movements.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a lightweight, distributed, and robust human motion capture system that can operate in unconstrained, in-the-wild environments using only head-mounted devices (HMDs). This overarching goal is driven by several motivations and broken down into specific contributions:

**Motivation:**
1. **Demand for Real-World Motion Data:** There is a growing need for accurate and scalable acquisition of real-world human motion and interaction data. This data is crucial for advancing applications in embodied artificial intelligence (AI), virtual reality (VR), augmented reality (AR), robotics, and human-computer interaction.
2. **Limitations of Current Mocap Systems:** Traditional motion capture systems, which typically involve bulky multi-camera setups or require subjects to wear obtrusive specialized suits, are not suitable for scalable, natural, and unobtrusive data collection in everyday environments.
3. **Inherent Challenges of Isolated Sensing Paradigms:**
 - **Egocentric Tracking Limitations:** While HMDs offer a portable solution, egocentric tracking (estimating the wearer's own motion) suffers from sensor noise and the limited visibility of the wearer's body from their own camera, making faithful reconstruction of full-body motion (especially the lower body) difficult.
 - **Exocentric Tracking Limitations:** Exocentric tracking (capturing others' motions from an external viewpoint) struggles with global motion estimation, severe body occlusions, and the unreliability introduced by dynamic camera movements and image blur.
4. **Emergence of Capable HMDs:** The increasing sophistication of consumer-grade HMDs, such as Project Aria, which provide multi-modal streams (head and hand trajectories, stereo/RGB images) and support accurate time alignment and distributed information exchange, creates a new opportunity for collaborative motion capture. These devices allow each participant to concurrently be a subject whose motion is captured and an observer who captures the motion of others.
5. **Addressing Data Heterogeneity and Unreliability:** A significant challenge lies in effectively combining heterogeneous signals (e.g., continuous head/wrist trajectories vs. intermittent image features) and managing the unreliability of exocentric streams due to occlusions or subjects being out-of-view.

**Key Objectives and Contributions:**
1. **Lightweight and Portable Solution:** To propose a human motion capture system that is lightweight, portable, and operates in-the-wild, requiring only a set of HMDs (e.g., Aria glasses) worn by participants. This reduces reliance on centralized, extensive hardware.
2. **Multi-Modal Framework Design:** To design a novel framework that jointly leverages heterogeneous egocentric self-motion cues and exocentric tracking signals. This fusion is intended to provide robust motion estimates even in challenging real-world conditions like out-of-view motions and severe body occlusions.
3. **Scalability:** To ensure that the proposed approach is scalable, functioning effectively with a minimum of two subjects and naturally extending to multi-subject setups with multiple observers.
4. **Robustness via Context-Aware Features:** To develop mechanisms that address the unreliability of exocentric streams by leveraging contextual image features (e.g., DINOv3) to learn per-keypoint confidence scores, thereby adaptively balancing the contributions of egocentric and exocentric signals.
5. **Performance Evaluation:** To rigorously evaluate the system's performance on existing in-the-wild datasets, demonstrating state-of-the-art results in real-world scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

EgoExoMoCap employs a distributed framework for full-body motion capture of a "wearer" (target subject) by combining their egocentric HMD data with exocentric views from an "observer" (nearby subject also wearing an HMD). The system is designed to scale from a two-person scenario to multiple observers.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

- **Inputs:** At each timestep `t`, the system takes:
 - An RGB image (`I^o_t`) from the observer's HMD camera.
 - The observer's head position (`p^o_t`) and orientation (`θ^o_t`).
 - The wearer's head position (`p^w,head_t`) and orientation (`θ^w,head_t`).
 - *Optionally*, the wearer's left and right wrist positions (`p^w,lrw_t`) and orientations (`θ^w,lrw_t`).
 - Camera intrinsics and extrinsics for both HMDs are assumed to be known.
- **Outputs:** The system predicts the sequence of full-body poses for the wearer in global space, represented by SMPL body model parameters (joint rotations, root orientation, and root position). The model can handle different subject-specific or mean body shapes.

#### 3.2. Method Overview (Refer to Figure 2)

The process generally follows these steps:
1. **Egocentric Tracking (EgoNet):** Initial coarse 3D body pose estimation from the wearer's egocentric signals.
2. **Projection & Region Proposal:** The coarse pose is projected onto the observer's image to localize the wearer and define a region of interest.
3. **Exocentric Tracking (2D Keypoints):** 2D body keypoints are detected within the proposed region.
4. **Ray-based Canonicalization:** These 2D keypoints are unprojected into 3D rays, depth-scaled by inter-person distance, and rigidly transformed into the wearer's head-local coordinate frame.
5. **Learned Visibility Gating (ScoreNet):** DINOv3 features from the exocentric image are used to learn per-keypoint confidence scores, weighting the reliability of the rays.
6. **Ego-Exo View Aggregation (Spatial Transformer):** Egocentric and gated exocentric signals are fused.
7. **Temporal Modeling (Temporal Transformer):** Fused features are processed across a temporal window for smoothness.
8. **Final Full-Body Motions:** The network outputs the wearer's full-body SMPL pose.

#### 3.3. Ego-Guided Wearer Localization

- **Egocentric Signal Encoding:** The wearer's head (and optionally wrist) positions, orientations, and their velocities are encoded into a high-dimensional vector. Spatial normalization (wrist relative to head) and temporal normalization (head position relative to first frame) are applied to remove global position dependence and produce a robust egocentric feature vector (`x^ego_t`).
- **Coarse Pose Estimation (EgoNet):** A temporal network, EgoNet (comprising a linear projection, sinusoidal temporal positional encoding, an MLP-Mixer block, and two MLP heads), takes `x^ego_t` to predict initial global orientation and local body poses of the SMPL model. This provides a rough pose estimate.
- **Region Proposal:** The 3D joint positions derived from EgoNet's coarse estimates are projected onto the observer's exocentric image using known camera parameters. An expanded bounding box around these projected joints defines a region of interest, localizing the wearer for subsequent exocentric processing. This is crucial for robustness as generic bounding box detectors often fail under severe occlusions.

#### 3.4. Exocentric Ray-based Pose Canonicalization

To handle observer viewpoint changes and scale ambiguities, a ray-based geometric representation is developed:
- **From 2D Detections to 3D Rays:** ViTPose detects `K=13` 2D body keypoints within the bounding box. Each 2D keypoint `x_t,j` is unprojected into a 3D ray in global coordinates using the observer's camera intrinsics (`K_obs`) and extrinsics rotation (`R_obs`), resulting in a unit direction vector (`d_hat_t,j`). This step removes observer camera rotation dependence.
- **Depth Scaling:** To reintroduce spatial distance information lost in unit vectors, `d_hat_t,j` is scaled by the Euclidean distance between the observer's and wearer's head positions, yielding `d_tilde_t,j`.
- **Proxy 3D Endpoint:** This scaled vector is then anchored to the observer's global head position to compute a proxy 3D endpoint position (`e_t,j`).
- **Ego-Space Canonicalization:** Finally, `e_t,j` is transformed into the wearer's head-local coordinate frame (`r_t,j`). This canonical representation ensures the relationship between proxy endpoints and ground-truth body pose is invariant to the wearer's global trajectory and camera rotation, improving generalization.

#### 3.5. Learned Visibility Gating

The reliability of exocentric signals can be compromised by occlusions or subjects moving out of view. Traditional 2D detector confidences are often insufficient.
- **DINOv3 Feature Extraction:** From the same cropped region used for keypoint detection, a DINOv3 CLS token (`F_CLS`) is extracted.
- **ScoreNet:** A two-layer MLP (ScoreNet) with a sigmoid function maps `F_CLS` to `K` per-joint confidence scores (`w`), ranging from 0 to 1. This learned gating function leverages global semantic context to assess visibility.
- **Gating:** Each canonicalized ray `r_t,j` is element-wise scaled by its corresponding confidence score `w_t,j`. This mechanism suppresses unreliable exocentric signals, allowing the network to fall back on egocentric data when necessary.

#### 3.6. Ego-Exo View Aggregation

- **Token Construction:**
 - **Ego Token:** Comprises the egocentric input signals and the coarse pose predicted by EgoNet, projected into a 512-dimensional embedding.
 - **Exo Token:** Comprises the gated exocentric rays (`r_hat_t,j`), also projected to a 512-dimensional embedding.
- **Spatial Fusion:** A Spatial Transformer Encoder processes a short sequence containing the Ego and Exo Tokens. Self-attention enables the Ego Token to integrate relevant information from the exocentric observation. The output of the Ego Token is retained as the fused feature (`e_tilde_t`), maintaining an egocentric inductive bias. This architecture inherently scales to multiple observers.
- **Temporal Modeling and Decoding:** The sequence of fused features `{e_tilde_t}` from all frames (augmented with learnable temporal positional embeddings) passes through a Temporal Transformer Encoder. This performs bidirectional self-attention over a window of `T=96` frames to capture long-range motion dynamics and ensure smoothness.
- **Decoding:** Two lightweight MLP heads independently decode each output frame: one for root orientation (`θ_hat_w,root_t`) and one for body joint rotations (`θ_hat_w,body_t`). Root translation (`p_w,root_t`) is analytically recovered by subtracting the predicted head-to-root offset (via forward kinematics) from the known HMD head position.

#### 3.7. Training

- **Two-stage Training:**
 1. EgoNet is trained using only egocentric signals.
 2. The full ego-exo fusion network (ray embedding, DINO confidence gate, Spatial Transformer, Temporal Transformer) is trained end-to-end, with EgoNet's coarse predictions frozen. The DINOv3 backbone is also frozen; only ScoreNet's MLP is trained.
- **Loss Function:** The training objective combines three L1 losses: root orientation loss (`L_orient`), body joint rotation loss (`L_rot`), and 3D joint position loss (`L_pos`), which penalizes errors on joint positions obtained via forward kinematics. Weights (`λ_orient=0.02, λ_rot=1.0, λ_pos=1.0`) are applied to balance these objectives.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of EgoExoMoCap was evaluated through extensive experiments on two in-the-wild datasets, Nymeria and EgoHumans, comparing against various state-of-the-art egocentric and exocentric baselines.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Evaluation on Nymeria Dataset (Table 1)

- **Overall Superiority:** EgoExoMoCap consistently demonstrated superior performance across nearly all metrics (MPJPE, Upper-body PE, Lower-body PE, MPJVE) for both 1-point (head tracking only) and 3-point (head and wrist tracking) setups. For example, in the 3-point setup, EgoExoMoCap achieved an MPJPE of 5.72 cm, significantly lower than the best egocentric baseline (EgoPoser at 7.74 cm) and exocentric baseline (PromptHMR-Finetuned at 10.65 cm).
- **Jitter:** The only exception was Jitter, where RPM achieved slightly lower values, attributed by the authors to its PCAF module which prioritizes smoothness. However, EgoExoMoCap's jitter was still competitive (2.16 vs 1.29 for RPM in 3-point).
- **Limitations of Baselines:**
 - Egocentric methods (AvatarPoser, EgoPoser, EgoAllo, RPM) generally produce plausible motions but struggle to faithfully reconstruct invisible body parts (e.g., lower body movements during sitting or kneeling).
 - Exocentric methods (PromptHMR) showed difficulty with extreme occlusions and rapid observer HMD camera motion, even when provided with ground-truth camera parameters and wearer global position.
- **Benefit of Fusion:** A custom ego-exo baseline (PromptHMR+EgoPoser) demonstrated the general benefit of combining both information sources, yielding lower MPJPE than individual methods. However, its "naive" fusion led to decreased accuracy and higher velocity error/jitter compared to EgoExoMoCap, highlighting the effectiveness of EgoExoMoCap's robust fusion strategy.

#### 4.2. Quantitative Evaluation on EgoHumans Dataset (Table 2)

- **Generalization:** Results on the EgoHumans dataset, an outdoor multi-person dataset featuring dynamic interactive activities, confirmed the trends observed in Nymeria. EgoExoMoCap again outperformed baselines in MPJPE, Upper/Lower-body PE, and MPJVE for both 1-point and 3-point tracking.
- **Multi-Observer Benefits:** The study evaluated EgoExoMoCap in multi-observer scenarios (using a subset of EgoHumans with multiple observer streams).
 - Single-observer EgoExoMoCap (EgoExo-single-observer) showed higher MPJPE (e.g., 8.80 cm in 3-point) compared to the full multi-observer setup.
 - A multi-view triangulation baseline (EgoExo-triangulation) performed better than single-observer but still worse than EgoExoMoCap's multi-observer fusion (e.g., 8.49 cm vs. 7.11 cm in 3-point).
 - EgoExoMoCap with multi-observer fusion (EgoExo-multi-observer) achieved the best performance (7.11 cm MPJPE in 3-point), demonstrating that aggregating views from multiple observers significantly improves accuracy, particularly when single views are partial or far away (Figure 5).

#### 4.3. Qualitative Results (Figure 4)

Visual comparisons illustrate EgoExoMoCap's ability to reconstruct motion robustly even in challenging scenarios, such as when other baselines fail to recover from inaccurate estimates or cannot reconstruct occluded body parts. Examples include diverse activities from Nymeria and tennis interactions from EgoHumans.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies (Table 3)

- **Necessity of Modalities:** Removing either egocentric inputs (`w/o Ego`) or exocentric images (`w/o Exo`) led to a substantial drop in all metrics, confirming that both modalities are crucial for the system's performance.
- **Wearer Localization:** Replacing EgoNet's region proposals with a generic bounding box detector (YOLO - `w/o Ego BBX`) worsened metrics, validating EgoNet's role in robust wearer localization under occlusion.
- **Ray-based Representation:** Design choices for ray-based pose canonicalization were validated: omitting depth scaling, not transforming rays into the wearer's head-local frame (Ray in World-Space, Ray in Exo-Space), or not lifting to 3D rays (`w/o Lifting`) all negatively impacted performance.
- **Learned Gating:** The DINOv3-based learned gating mechanism was shown to be effective. Removing DINO scores (`w/o DINO Score`), using ViT-provided confidences (`w/ ViT Score`), or simple masking (`w/ Masking`) all resulted in worse performance, especially for lower-body accuracy. Figure 6 qualitatively illustrates how DINO scores help assess signal reliability in occluded scenarios where 2D detectors might still provide high-confidence but erroneous keypoints.
- **Robustness to EgoNet:** The system showed graceful degradation and limited sensitivity to noise in EgoNet's coarse pose estimates, indicating that the fusion network is not overly dependent on the initial coarse estimation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

EgoExoMoCap presents a notable advancement in human motion capture technology, with several significant implications and potential impacts:

1. **Increased Accessibility and Reduced Obtrusiveness:** By enabling full-body motion capture using only commonly available head-mounted devices (HMDs) like smart glasses, EgoExoMoCap significantly lowers the barrier to entry for motion data collection. This eliminates the need for expensive, bulky multi-camera setups or restrictive motion capture suits, making high-fidelity motion capture more accessible and less intrusive for researchers and developers alike.
2. **Scalable Data Collection in Real-World Environments:** The approach facilitates the scalable collection of diverse, real-world whole-body motion and interaction data outside controlled laboratory settings. This capability is particularly valuable for applications that require authentic human behavior in natural environments, rather than stylized or constrained movements. The system's ability to scale from two subjects to multiple participants further enhances its utility for capturing complex social interactions.
3. **Enhanced Robustness to Real-World Challenges:** EgoExoMoCap specifically addresses and mitigates common challenges in in-the-wild motion capture, such as out-of-view motions and severe body occlusions. By intelligently combining complementary egocentric and exocentric signals with context-aware confidence estimation, the system maintains robust performance in scenarios where individual sensing modalities would typically fail.
4. **Advancements in Embodied AI and XR (VR/AR):** The provision of higher-fidelity, diverse, and natural human motion data has a direct and substantial impact on the development of embodied AI agents, interactive characters in VR/AR environments, and robotics. More realistic and varied training data can lead to more naturalistic robot behaviors, more immersive virtual experiences, and improved human-computer interaction paradigms.
5. **Foundation for Future Research:** The framework lays a foundation for future work in several areas:
 - **Multi-Modal Integration:** It opens avenues for incorporating additional multi-modal streams, such as stereo cameras on HMDs or more diverse body-worn sensors, to further enrich motion cues and enhance capture accuracy.
 - **Crowded Scenarios and Non-HMD Participants:** The distributed nature of the system could be extended with tracklet association mechanisms from exocentric people tracking systems to handle crowded environments or include individuals not wearing HMDs.
 - **Human-Scene Interaction:** Leveraging the 3D scene reconstruction capabilities inherent in many HMDs could lead to more accurate modeling and reconstruction of human interactions within complex environments.
 - **Real-time Applications:** While currently focused on offline reconstruction, the lightweight nature of the system suggests potential for future adaptation to online and real-time tracking, contingent on hardware and software advancements.

In conclusion, EgoExoMoCap represents a pragmatic and effective step towards democratizing full-body motion capture, enabling the collection of valuable real-world human motion data with minimal setup and maximum flexibility.
