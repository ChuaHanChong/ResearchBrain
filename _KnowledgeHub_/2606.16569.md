# PROSE: Training-Free Egocentric Scene Registration with Vision-Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at ETH Zurich and the University of Oxford developed PROSE, a training-free pipeline that uses off-the-shelf Vision-Language Models to perform cross-time egocentric scene registration from only RGB input. PROSE achieved higher Registration Recall and lower Relative Rotation and Translation Errors compared to geometric and learned baselines on the ADT and AEA datasets, and produced instance-level open-vocabulary 3D scene graphs that supported higher success rates in a simulated path planning task.

## Problem

- Aligning two captures of the same indoor environment taken at different times (cross-time scene registration) is challenging, especially with noisy, sparse, and blurry egocentric RGB data from head-mounted cameras.
- Existing purely geometric methods struggle with degraded geometry and lack semantic understanding crucial for dynamic scenes with object rearrangements.
- Learned scene-graph methods require expensive pre-built or annotated scene graphs and often exhibit brittle generalization to novel egocentric data distributions.

## Method

- **PROSE** (Prompted Scene rEgistration) is an RGB-only, training-free pipeline that orchestrates off-the-shelf foundation models for scene parsing, instance correspondence, and pose estimation.
- It uses a geometric foundation model for per-frame geometry, a ==prompted VLM== (Qwen3.6-27B) for open-vocabulary object listing, and SAM 3 for instance segmentation to build temporally consistent 3D scene graphs.
- Cross-scan instance correspondences are established using height-based binning and VLM-driven 'double verification' prompts, with final rigid transforms estimated via RANSAC on point-level correspondences from matched instance pairs.

## Results

- PROSE consistently outperformed geometric (TEASER++, GeoTransformer) and learned scene-graph (SG-Reg) baselines on ADT and AEA benchmarks for scene registration, achieving higher Registration Recall and lower Relative Rotation and Translation Errors, particularly with degraded geometry.
- The method demonstrated substantially higher precision (**70.4%** NP on ADT) and F1 scores in cross-scan instance matching compared to open-vocabulary detection-and-embedding baselines and SG-Reg.
- PROSE's generated open-vocabulary scene graphs supported a higher number of usable nodes (192 vs. 33 for ConceptGraphs) and achieved higher path planning success rates in a simulated environment, validating their utility for downstream robotic navigation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Pretrained Vision-Language Models can effectively serve as a unified engine for both semantic scene understanding (object listing) and robust cross-scan instance matching without requiring task-specific training.
> - Strategic VLM prompting, including specific design choices for object listing and a 'double verification' strategy for instance matching, significantly improves precision and reduces false positives in challenging egocentric data.
> - Object-level semantic correspondences provide robust cues for registration in noisy egocentric settings, allowing for effective alignment even when underlying geometry is degraded, and also generate valuable open-vocabulary 3D scene graphs for downstream tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research addresses the challenge of aligning two captures of the same indoor environment taken at different times, a task known as cross-time scene registration. This capability is fundamental for developing persistent spatial memory in autonomous systems such as robots and Augmented Reality (AR) headsets. The paper specifically targets the "egocentric RGB" setting, where data is captured by head-mounted cameras (e.g., Meta Aria Glasses) and consists solely of RGB video streams.

Existing methods for scene registration generally fall into two categories:

1. **Pure Geometric Methods**: These approaches (e.g., TEASER++, GeoTransformer) rely on dense, high-quality point clouds for feature extraction and matching. However, egocentric RGB capture inherently produces noisy, sparse, and blurry data due to rapid viewpoint changes, motion blur, and narrow fields of view, making it difficult to recover reliable dense geometry. Furthermore, these methods typically disregard semantic information, which can be crucial for identifying stable landmark objects in dynamic scenes where furniture might be rearranged.
2. **Learned Scene-Graph Registration Methods**: These methods (e.g., SGAligner, SG-PGM, SG-Reg) incorporate semantic priors by representing scenes as graphs of objects and their relationships. While addressing the semantic gap, they often require a pre-built or annotated scene graph as input, which can be expensive to obtain for new environments. Additionally, their trained matchers are sometimes found to be brittle and do not consistently generalize to the specific data distributions encountered in egocentric captures. These methods also typically decouple the scene graph construction from the matching process.

The presented work, PROSE, positions itself by taking a different approach. It leverages the capabilities of *pretrained Vision-Language Models (VLMs)* to perform both scene understanding (i.e., identifying objects to build a scene graph) and cross-scan instance matching. This represents an extension of recent work applying VLMs to single-scene 3D understanding into the more complex cross-time, cross-scan correspondence task. By being training-free and RGB-only, PROSE aims to overcome the limitations of prior methods, particularly in the challenging egocentric domain, by offering a scalable solution that simultaneously produces a useful scene representation and the desired rigid transform.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to achieve robust and accurate cross-time egocentric scene registration using only RGB input, without requiring depth sensors, training on specific datasets, or pre-annotated scene graphs. This involves estimating a rigid transformation (T ∈ SE(3)) that aligns two egocentric RGB sequences of the same indoor scene captured at different times, even when there is partial overlap and objects within the scene may have been moved or removed.

Beyond the core registration task, PROSE also aims to produce two critical byproducts:
- An instance-level open-vocabulary 3D scene graph for each captured sequence (G_ref and G_src).
- A set of cross-scan instance correspondences (C) that are used to drive the registration process.

The motivation for these objectives stems from several real-world requirements and limitations of current technologies:

- **Enabling Persistent Spatial Memory**: For applications like robotic navigation and AR systems, the ability to re-localize within an environment and maintain a persistent understanding of it over time is crucial. A robot revisiting a room or an AR headset anchoring virtual content across multiple sessions depends on accurately aligning new observations with previous maps.
- **Addressing Egocentric Data Challenges**: Head-mounted cameras, while offering a natural perspective for embodied agents, introduce significant challenges. The captured RGB data is often characterized by motion blur, rapid viewpoint changes, and inherently noisy geometric signals, which severely degrade the performance of traditional geometric registration techniques that rely on clean point clouds.
- **Limitations of Existing Paradigms**:
 - **Geometric methods** are ill-suited for the noisy and partial geometry derived from egocentric RGB. They also discard semantic information, which human agents naturally use for relocalization (e.g., recognizing landmark objects).
 - **Learned scene-graph methods** necessitate either manual annotation of 3D scene graphs or the training of dedicated front-end models for graph construction, which is resource-intensive and often does not generalize well to novel or egocentric data distributions. Their learned matchers can also be brittle when faced with realistic egocentric variations.
- **Leveraging Vision-Language Models**: The authors observe that while geometry may be unreliable in egocentric captures, prominent objects remain visually recognizable across time. They hypothesize that pretrained VLMs, which excel at visual comparison and understanding, can serve as a unified engine for both identifying objects (scene understanding) and matching them across different scans (cross-scan correspondence). This approach seeks to provide semantic understanding as an inherent part of the alignment process, rather than assuming it as prior input.
- **Training-Free Scalability**: By relying entirely on off-the-shelf foundation models, PROSE aims to be training-free. This significantly reduces the overhead associated with data annotation and model training, offering a more scalable solution for diverse and dynamic indoor environments.

In essence, PROSE is motivated by the need for a robust, scalable, and semantic-aware registration system that can operate directly on challenging egocentric RGB video, addressing a gap left by both purely geometric and existing learned semantic-geometric methods.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PROSE (Prompted Scene rEgistration) is an RGB-only, training-free pipeline designed for cross-time egocentric scene alignment. It processes two egocentric RGB sequences (V_ref and V_src) to output a rigid transform T ∈ SE(3), along with an instance-level open-vocabulary scene graph for each scan (G_ref, G_src) and cross-scan instance correspondences (C). The pipeline consists of three main stages: Scene Parsing, Cross-scan Instance Correspondence, and Pose Hypothesis and Voting.

#### 3.1. Scene Representation: From RGB to Per-Instance 3D

This stage processes each RGB sequence independently to construct an object-level 3D scene graph.

- **Per-frame Geometry**: A geometric foundation model, VGGT-Ω, is used off-the-shelf. It takes an RGB sequence and predicts dense depth maps, camera intrinsics, and camera poses for every frame. These per-frame predictions are then used to unproject and concatenate the frames into a single per-subscan point cloud. A 2D-to-3D index is maintained to link each 3D point back to its original pixel and frame.
- **Object Listing**: A pretrained Vision-Language Model (VLM), Qwen3.6-27B, is prompted with a subsampled set of frames from the RGB sequence. Its role is to generate a flat list of object names present in the scene. This open-vocabulary approach is chosen over closed-vocabulary detectors to identify scene-specific objects relevant for alignment. The base prompt asks for at most 20 concrete, distinct items. This is augmented with five specialized design choices to improve robustness in egocentric settings:
 1. **Landmark-Salience**: Prioritizes large, scene-anchoring objects.
 2. **Static-Rigid**: Focuses on static, rigid items, excluding dynamic or non-rigid entities.
 3. **Hallucination-Guard**: Prevents the VLM from inventing objects or speculative category expansions.
 4. **Occlusion-Recovery**: Encourages the inclusion of partially visible or briefly seen objects.
 5. **Scene-Prior**: Infers the room type and suggests typical functional objects within it. The final prompt combines all these choices.
- **Instance Segmentation**: SAM 3 is utilized for open-vocabulary concept segmentation. It takes the VLM-generated object names and segments/tracks every instance of that concept across the video, producing temporally consistent masks for each object and frame. This avoids the need for separate detectors and trackers which often fail under egocentric viewpoint changes.
- **Per-instance Fusion**: This step unifies the per-frame SAM 3 masks into consistent 3D instances for the scene graph.
 1. **Mask-to-3D Lifting**: Masked pixels are back-projected into 3D using the 2D-to-3D index. For nested masks, the smallest-area mask claims the pixel.
 2. **Per-Voxel Majority Voting**: The combined 3D point cloud, where points may have multiple instance IDs from different frames, is voxelized at 5 cm resolution. Each voxel's points are relabeled to the majority instance ID within that voxel, resolving temporal ID inconsistencies.
 3. **Absorption**: If at least 50% of an instance's points are reallocated to a single other instance after voting, the smaller instance is absorbed into the larger one to clean up over-segmentation.
 4. **Scene-Wide Near-Duplicate Merge**: A final pass merges instances that describe the same physical object but did not co-occupy voxels (e.g., due to appearing/disappearing). This involves a geometric overlap test based on voxel containment (ρ_vox ≥ 0.6) and Chamfer distance (d_cham ≤ 3 cm) between their point clouds. Transitively connected groups of qualifying pairs are merged.
 The output of this stage is a scene graph G = (V, E), where V are temporally consistent 3D object instances (each with a fused point set and PCA-based oriented bounding box) and E connects instances with nearby centroids (k=5 nearest neighbors within 1 meter).

#### 3.2. Cross-Scan Instance Correspondence

Given the two scene graphs G_ref and G_src, this stage identifies instance correspondences C ⊂ V(G_ref) × V(G_src) using the same VLM. This is designed to overcome challenges like the large search space, visually similar objects at different heights, and VLM hallucinations.

- **Height-Based Binning**: Object instances are binned based on their centroid height along the gravity axis. K=5 equal-count quantile bins are created from the pooled heights of both scans, with a 20% overlap between adjacent bins to ensure boundary objects remain matchable. The VLM is then queried separately within each bin, reducing query complexity and eliminating implausible matches across vastly different heights. A final cross-bin pass addresses any remaining unmatched instances.
- **VLM Prompting**: Within each bin, the VLM is presented with three images:
 1. **REF Crop Grid**: A grid of zoomed-in crops for each reference object in the bin, with a green silhouette outline and a unique numeric Set-of-Marks ID.
 2. **SRC Crop Grid**: Similar grid for source objects, with a distinct set of numeric IDs.
 3. **Context Frame**: One wide RGB frame per scan (REF on top, SRC on bottom) showing the objects in their spatial layout, outlined and labeled with the same IDs, providing contextual information.
 The markers used for REF and SRC crops are from a single, shared, disjoint namespace (REF: 1..N, SRC: N+1..N+M). The VLM is prompted to return a JSON array of {"ref": id, "src": id} pairs, which directly represent candidate cross-scan instance pairs.
- **Pairwise Same/Different Verification**: Each candidate pair from the binning stage undergoes a verification step. The two instance crops are rendered side-by-side, and two complementary yes/no questions are posed to the VLM:
 1. "Is the object on the LEFT the same physical object as the object on the RIGHT?" (Affirmative check)
 2. "Are the object on the LEFT and the object on the RIGHT DIFFERENT physical objects?" (Negated check)
 A candidate pair is retained only if the VLM's answers agree (e.g., "yes" to the affirmative and "no" to the negated query). This dual-prompt approach helps mitigate the VLM's inherent "yes-bias" in single-affirmative queries, sharply reducing false positives at a small cost in recall. The surviving pairs form the final correspondence set C.

#### 3.3. Pose Hypothesis and Voting

Given the set of instance correspondences C, this stage estimates the rigid transform T ∈ SE(3) that aligns the source scan to the reference.

- The method assumes a single dominant rigid motion relates the two scans. Instead of fitting T to all correspondences jointly (which can be sensitive to outliers), PROSE generates a transform hypothesis for each matched pair.
- For each matched pair (A_i, A'_i), a candidate transform T_i is computed by running RANSAC on the point-level correspondences between the two instances' fused point sets. These point-level correspondences are extracted using an off-the-shelf 3D descriptor (e.g., FCGF, FPFH, GeoTransformer) under mutual nearest-neighbor matching.
- Each candidate T_i is then scored by its inlier ratio: the fraction of the entire source point cloud that, after being transformed by T_i, falls within a fixed distance (3 cm threshold) of the reference point cloud.
- The transform T_i with the highest inlier ratio is selected as the final rigid transform T. This voting scheme provides robustness to individual mismatched pairs, as transforms derived from incorrect matches will typically have low inlier ratios across the entire scene.

The entire pipeline is implemented using off-the-shelf checkpoints for all foundation models and runs on a single H200 GPU.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of PROSE was evaluated on the egocentric Aria Digital Twin (ADT) and Aria Everyday Activities (AEA) datasets, across tasks of scene registration, instance correspondence, and object listing, including a downstream path planning application.

**Scene Registration (Table 2):**
- **Superior Performance**: PROSE consistently achieved higher Registration Recall (RR) and lower Relative Rotation Error (RRE) and Relative Translation Error (RTE) compared to both geometric (TEASER++, BUFFER-X, GeoTransformer) and learned scene-graph (SG-Reg) baselines on both ADT and AEA benchmarks.
- **Robustness to Degraded Geometry**: On point clouds reconstructed from RGB by VGGT-Ω (the sensor-free setting PROSE targets), PROSE maintained usable accuracy and extended its performance lead. Geometric baselines and SG-Reg showed a significant drop in performance as input geometry degraded, indicating their sensitivity to noise and partial coverage. PROSE's object-level semantic correspondence was less affected.
- **Memory Efficiency**: While GeoTransformer failed due to Out of Memory (OOM) when applied at the full scene level, it functioned effectively as a backend descriptor within PROSE's per-instance pose estimation stage, as each sub-problem was small enough to fit in memory.
- **Correspondence-Driven Gains**: The performance gains of PROSE were consistent across different point-level descriptor backends (FPFH, FCGF, GeoTransformer), suggesting that the primary advantage comes from the semantic instance correspondence stage rather than the choice of low-level geometric descriptor.

**Scene-Graph Correspondence (Table 4):**
- PROSE demonstrated substantially higher precision (NP) and F1 scores in cross-scan instance matching compared to an open-vocabulary detection-and-embedding baseline (GDino+CLIP) and SG-Reg on both ADT and AEA.
- The precision gap was notable, with PROSE achieving 70.4% NP on ADT compared to 14.2% for GDino+CLIP and 20.8% for SG-Reg. This is attributed to the VLM's reasoning capabilities over shared-namespace Set-of-Marks prompts, which effectively disambiguated visually similar objects and reduced false positives.
- SG-Reg, designed for curated RGB-D graphs, showed weak performance on egocentric data, consistent with its poor registration results.

**Ablation Study (Table 3):**
- **Object Listing (Table 3a)**: Each of the five prompting design choices (Landmark-Salience, Static-Rigid, Hallucination-Guard, Occlusion-Recovery, Scene-Prior) incrementally contributed to improved object listing metrics (Anchor Recall, Precision, Recall, F1), with the combined prompt yielding the best performance.
- **Instance Matching (Table 3b)**: The two most impactful components for instance matching F1 and Coverage Ratio were the "double verification" (pairwise same/different prompts) and "height binning," which sharpened precision and reduced distractors, respectively.
- **VLM Backbone (Table A4)**: Registration accuracy was found to correlate with the underlying VLM's capability. Qwen3.6-27B, the default backbone, outperformed other open- and closed-source VLMs, including larger models, indicating that VLM quality directly translates to PROSE's performance without specific training.
- **Object Lister Comparison (Table A5)**: PROSE's VLM-based object lister, with its emphasis on precision, yielded better registration results than open-vocabulary taggers (RAM, RAM++, Tag2Text), even those with higher recall. This suggests that precise semantic information is more critical than exhaustive but noisy object enumeration for downstream registration.

**Downstream Application: Path Planning (Table 5, Figure 3, Sec. A.2):**
- PROSE's generated open-vocabulary scene graphs were evaluated in a simulated path planning task and a real-robot navigation experiment.
- PROSE produced significantly more usable nodes (192) and a broader set of routing targets compared to ConceptGraphs (33 nodes) and FM-Fusion (5 nodes), which enabled higher path planning success rates across different distance bands (e.g., 49.6% in-room SR for PROSE vs. 46.2% for ConceptGraphs).
- The real-robot experiment with a Boston Dynamics Spot robot demonstrated that the PROSE scene graph successfully supported object-level navigation in real indoor scenes, validating its utility as a semantic map.

**Inference Time (Table A6):**
- An end-to-end registration for a subscan pair took approximately 49.37 seconds on a single H200 GPU. The VLM-driven cross-scan correspondence (22.34 s) and instance segmentation (13.84 s) were the most time-consuming stages, consistent with the use of large foundation models. This latency is deemed acceptable for offline registration tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of PROSE holds significance for several areas within robotics, augmented reality, and general 3D scene understanding.

**Addressing Core Challenges in Egocentric Perception:**
PROSE directly confronts critical limitations of existing scene registration methods when applied to challenging egocentric RGB data. By effectively handling motion blur, rapid viewpoint changes, and object rearrangements without relying on dense, clean geometry, it demonstrates a robust approach to a problem that often defeats traditional techniques. Its ability to generate reliable semantic correspondences from noisy inputs is a notable advancement.

**Leveraging a Training-Free Paradigm:**
A key aspect of PROSE's significance is its training-free nature. By orchestrating a pipeline of off-the-shelf, pretrained foundation models (VLMs, geometric backbones, segmenters), the method bypasses the extensive data annotation and model training typically required for learned scene graph methods. This reduces development costs, enhances scalability to new environments, and enables rapid deployment without the need for domain-specific fine-tuning.

**Joint Scene Understanding and Registration:**
PROSE uniquely integrates scene understanding (generating open-vocabulary 3D scene graphs) with cross-scan instance matching and rigid transform estimation within a unified pipeline. The resulting scene graph is not merely an intermediate step but a valuable, reusable semantic representation of the environment, supporting downstream applications like path planning and robotic navigation, as demonstrated in the experiments. This contrasts with approaches that treat scene graph construction and registration as decoupled problems or discard semantics entirely.

**Impact on Persistent Spatial Memory:**
The work contributes directly to the realization of persistent spatial memory for autonomous agents. By providing a reliable mechanism for robots and AR headsets to re-localize and re-anchor content in environments that may have changed over time, PROSE can enable more intelligent and context-aware interactions in dynamic human-centric spaces.

**Future-Proof Modularity:**
The modular architecture of PROSE means that advancements in any of its constituent foundation models (e.g., more capable VLMs, improved geometric reconstruction models, or faster segmentation tools) can directly translate into performance improvements for the entire pipeline without requiring architectural changes or retraining. This positions PROSE to benefit from ongoing progress in the broader field of AI.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
Despite its contributions, the paper acknowledges several limitations. VLM spatial reasoning can be sensitive to prompt phrasing, though this is an active area of VLM research. The method's reliance on static landmarks means performance may degrade in highly dynamic or sparse environments. As a multi-stage pipeline, errors can compound. Future work could focus on tighter coupling of semantic and geometric matching, exploring new VLM architectures specifically pretrained for 3D understanding, and integrating fallbacks for challenging scenarios to further enhance robustness.
