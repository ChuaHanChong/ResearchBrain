# SA-VLA: Spatially-Aware Flow-Matching for Vision-Language-Action Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> SA-VLA introduces a framework for robust reinforcement learning adaptation of robotic manipulation policies, integrating spatial awareness into state representations, reward design, and exploration. This approach improves zero-shot spatial generalization by 2.25% and enhances fine-tuning stability under spatial distribution shifts.

## Problem

- Pretrained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies often lose robustness during reinforcement learning (RL) fine-tuning, particularly under spatial distribution shifts.
- The erosion of spatial inductive bias and ineffective spatially agnostic exploration during RL lead to brittle behaviors, failing to generalize across varying viewpoints or object layouts.
- Current RL approaches for continuous-time, noise-driven flow-matching VLA policies struggle with precise geometric reasoning essential for real-world robotic interaction.

## Method

- **SA-VLA** employs ==spatial token fusion==, augmenting 2D visual tokens with implicit spatial representations derived from multi-view features.
- Implements ==spatially-aware step-level dense rewards== by decomposing tasks into generic phases (Reach, Place, Leave) and providing feedback based on signed changes in normalized geometric distances.
- ==Spatially-Conditioned Annealed Noise (SCAN)== guides exploration with a hybrid noise model, combining learned, geometry-aware stochasticity with an annealed minimum floor to ensure persistent and targeted exploration during ==flow-matching RL==.

## Results

- Spatial token fusion alone improved zero-shot success rates on spatial perturbation tasks by **+2.25%** compared to the baseline, reaching **83.25%**.
- Spatially-aware step-level dense rewards consistently led to faster convergence and smoother training dynamics during RL fine-tuning.
- The full SA-VLA framework achieved an **83.75%** success rate, demonstrating complementary benefits from all components and superior performance compared to baselines and ablated versions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly preserving and reinforcing spatial inductive bias throughout the RL pipeline is critical for stable and effective fine-tuning of VLA policies.
> - A holistic approach, integrating spatial awareness into state representation, reward design, and exploration strategy, synergistically improves robustness and generalization.
> - Step-level dense rewards derived from task geometry significantly stabilize optimization by providing consistent feedback, while spatially-conditioned exploration enhances the discovery of meaningful states.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper operates at the intersection of several rapidly evolving fields: embodied artificial intelligence, robotic manipulation, Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, and reinforcement learning (RL).

In recent years, VLA models have emerged as a powerful paradigm for robotic manipulation, demonstrating impressive generalization capabilities across diverse tasks by integrating visual observations, language instructions, and action generation within a single framework. Approaches utilizing diffusion and flow-matching policies, such as $\pi^{0.5}$, have shown particular promise for continuous, high-dimensional control and multimodal action generation. These models represent a significant step towards "foundation models" for robotics, aiming to provide broad capabilities rather than task-specific solutions.

However, a critical challenge remains: adapting these powerful pretrained VLA policies to novel environments or tasks via reinforcement learning (RL) often leads to a degradation of robustness. Specifically, these policies tend to become brittle under *spatial distribution shifts*, such as changes in camera viewpoint, object initial configurations, or increased environmental clutter. The paper identifies the core reason for this fragility: the erosion of spatial inductive bias during RL fine-tuning. Traditional RL, with its sparse rewards and spatially agnostic exploration, tends to overfit to short-horizon visual cues, neglecting the crucial underlying geometric reasoning that is essential for real-world robotic interaction.

The broader landscape of VLA research acknowledges the importance of spatial information. While many existing VLA methods primarily rely on 2D appearance cues or shallow depth priors, recent efforts (e.g., BridgeVLA, StereoVLA) have begun to incorporate more explicit 3D structure. SA-VLA builds upon this by leveraging *implicit spatial representations*, which infer geometric understanding from multi-view imagery without requiring explicit 3D reconstruction. This approach offers a balance, avoiding the discretization artifacts and scalability issues of explicit 3D methods (like point clouds or voxels) while still providing robust geometric cues.

Furthermore, the paper situates itself within the context of flow-matching-based policy optimization, which has gained traction for its stability and gradient consistency compared to diffusion models or traditional RL baselines. Methods like ReinFlow have successfully integrated flow matching with online RL. SA-VLA extends this line of work by introducing *step-level progress modeling* and explicitly incorporating *implicit spatial representations* and *spatially-conditioned exploration*, addressing limitations where existing flow-matching RL methods largely operate at the trajectory level and are purely reward-driven.

By tackling the specific problem of spatial inductive bias collapse during RL fine-tuning of flow-matching VLA policies, SA-VLA aims to bridge the gap between the strong generalization of pretrained VLA models and the robust, adaptable behaviors required for real-world robotic deployment. It highlights the necessity of a holistic approach that integrates spatial awareness across all critical components of the RL pipeline: state representation, reward design, and exploration strategy.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this research stems from a critical limitation observed in current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models: while they demonstrate impressive generalization capabilities in robotic manipulation after pretraining, their robustness often degrades when fine-tuned using reinforcement learning (RL), particularly under spatial distribution shifts. Robots operating in the physical world frequently encounter variations in object placement, viewpoint, or environmental clutter. Current RL fine-tuning processes for VLA policies, especially those based on flow matching, tend to lose their inherent geometric understanding—their "spatial inductive bias"—leading to inconsistent and brittle behaviors. This is problematic because reliable robotic manipulation fundamentally requires precise reasoning about object geometry, spatial relationships, and contact dynamics.

The paper argues that this degradation is primarily caused by two factors during RL adaptation:
1. **Erosion of Spatial Inductive Bias:** High-variance updates during on-policy RL can overwrite the delicate, pretrained geometric regularities within the VLA policy.
2. **Ineffective Feedback and Exploration:** Sparse or weakly shaped rewards, combined with spatially agnostic exploration, encourage the policy to overfit to superficial visual cues or short-horizon reward-action correlations. This results in behaviors that fail to generalize across varying viewpoints or layouts. For continuous-time, noise-driven flow-matching VLA policies, where spatial behaviors are often implicitly maintained, this issue is particularly acute.

Given this motivation, the **key objective** of SA-VLA is to develop a spatially-aware RL adaptation framework that explicitly preserves and reinforces spatial grounding throughout the policy optimization process. The overarching goal is to enable stable and effective RL fine-tuning that not only improves in-distribution performance but also maintains or enhances robustness under spatial distribution shifts, thus yielding more transferable and reliable robotic behaviors.

To achieve this, SA-VLA pursues three specific objectives:
1. **Inject Spatial Structure into State Representations:** To ensure the policy always "sees" and reasons about task geometry, SA-VLA aims to augment visual tokens with robust, implicit spatial representations.
2. **Design Geometrically Meaningful Dense Rewards:** To provide clear and consistent learning signals, the framework seeks to provide step-level dense rewards that directly reflect geometric progress aligned with the task's spatial objectives.
3. **Implement Spatially-Conditioned Exploration:** To guide the agent efficiently through the complex manipulation space, SA-VLA aims to develop an exploration strategy that is sensitive to local geometry and uncertainty, promoting the discovery of meaningful states rather than random wandering.

By addressing these objectives concurrently, SA-VLA intends to mitigate catastrophic forgetting of spatial priors, reduce overfitting to spurious correlations, and ensure that RL fine-tuning preserves both semantic and spatial consistency, ultimately leading to policies that generalize effectively in cluttered and dynamic environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SA-VLA proposes a holistic, spatially-aware framework for adapting flow-matching Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies through reinforcement learning. It integrates spatial understanding into three critical components of the RL pipeline: state representation, reward design, and exploration.

#### 3.1. Spatial Token Fusion

To ensure the policy's state representations are inherently geometry-aware, SA-VLA augments 2D visual tokens with *implicit spatial tokens*.
- **Implicit Spatial Tokens ($z_t$):** Unlike explicit 3D reconstructions (e.g., point clouds), these tokens are learned embeddings derived from multi-view features (e.g., from VGGT), capturing scene layout, object geometry, and coarse spatial structure directly within a differentiable feature space. This avoids the computational overhead and discretization issues of explicit 3D methods, aligning better with RL optimization.
- **Fusion Mechanism:** The spatial tokens are first projected into the visual embedding space and enriched with positional and view encodings ($\tilde{z}_t$). Then, visual tokens ($x_t$) attend to these processed spatial tokens via *unidirectional cross-attention*. This asymmetric design is crucial: visual tokens query spatial context, but spatial tokens remain stable and unaffected by noisy, appearance-driven gradients, serving as a robust geometric anchor.
- **Channel-wise Gating:** A learnable channel-wise gate ($g$) modulates the contribution of spatial features to the visual tokens, allowing the model to selectively amplify or suppress geometric cues along different semantic dimensions. This gate is bounded by a hyperbolic tangent, preventing unstable amplification during early RL training.
- **Output:** The process yields `h_t`, geometry-aware embeddings that combine semantic, positional, and multi-view spatial information, forming a robust input for the policy.

#### 3.2. Spatially-Aware Step-Level Dense Reward

To provide informative and stable learning signals, SA-VLA introduces a dense reward system that aligns feedback with task geometry.
- **Phase Decomposition:** Robotic manipulation tasks are decomposed into three generic, semantically distinct phases: **Reach**, **Place**, and **Leave**. These correspond to approaching an object, moving it to a destination, and disengaging the end-effector. This is not a rigid script but a conceptual breakdown of geometric objectives.
- **Geometric Distances:** Two normalized geometric distances are defined at each step $t$: end-effector-object distance ($d_{ro}^{(t)}$) and object-destination distance ($d_{od}^{(t)}$). These are normalized by reference distances measured at the first valid interaction step.
- **Phase-Specific Rewards:** The dense reward is computed as a phase-specific *signed change* in the relevant normalized distance. For example, in the Reach phase, reducing $d_{ro}^{(t)}$ yields a positive reward. In the Leave phase, increasing $d_{ro}^{(t)}$ is rewarded. This provides immediate, phase-consistent feedback on geometric progress.
- **Online Phase Inference:** Phase transitions are inferred *online* based on low-level interaction signals (e.g., gripper state stability, relative object poses, object-destination proximity) rather than fixed temporal boundaries. This adaptive inference ensures rewards are consistent with actual execution dynamics and avoids premature or spurious phase switches. This structure preserves temporal flexibility while embedding manipulation priors.

#### 3.3. Spatially-Conditioned Annealed Noise (SCAN)

Effective exploration is crucial for leveraging dense rewards and geometry-aware representations. SCAN addresses this by injecting spatially-conditioned stochasticity while ensuring persistent exploration.
- **Hybrid Noise Model:** SCAN defines an effective noise scale $\sigma_t(x_t)$ as a combination of a *learned, geometry-aware noise component* ($\sigma_{learned}(x_t)$) and an *annealed minimum floor* ($\sigma_{min}(t)$).
 - $\sigma_{learned}(x_t)$ is predicted directly from the fused visual-spatial embedding ($h_t$), allowing the policy to adapt exploration variance based on local geometric and perceptual uncertainty (e.g., higher noise in contact-sensitive or ambiguous regions). This component is fully parameterized as part of the flow-matching policy.
 - $\sigma_{min}(t)$ is an annealing lower bound that decreases over training steps. It ensures strictly positive noise, preventing premature collapse to deterministic behaviors and encouraging broad exploration in early stages, then gradually refining it.
- **PPO Compatibility:** By explicitly parameterizing $\sigma_{learned}(x_t)$ as part of the policy, SCAN ensures that both the mean and variance contribute to the likelihood ratio in PPO's surrogate objective, maintaining theoretical consistency. The annealed floor reconciles early-stage external noise with a policy-dependent distribution after annealing.
- **Goal:** SCAN aligns exploration with the geometric structure encoded in the policy input and reward design, increasing the likelihood of visiting geometrically meaningful states where dense rewards provide informative gradients. This balances exploration and exploitation, stabilizes fine-tuning, and reinforces generalizable behaviors.

In summary, SA-VLA's methodology is a tightly integrated system where geometry-aware representations inform both the reward signals and the exploration strategy, all working in concert to preserve and enhance the spatial inductive bias of VLA policies during RL adaptation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of SA-VLA was conducted on the LIBERO and LIBERO-PLUS benchmarks, with a particular focus on robustness under spatial distribution shifts (viewpoint changes and initial state perturbations). The experiments systematically investigated the contribution of each SA-VLA component to spatial generalization and training stability.

#### 4.1. Zero-Shot Spatial Generalization Enabled by Spatial Token Fusion (RQ1)

- **Finding:** Before any RL adaptation, simply incorporating **spatial token fusion** into the pretrained flow-matching VLA policy significantly improved zero-shot success rates on the spatial-perturbation subset of LIBERO-PLUS.
- **Quantification:** An overall gain of +2.25% in success rate was observed compared to the baseline ($\pi^{0.5}$), reaching 83.25%.
- **Breakdown:** The improvement was more pronounced for camera-view perturbations (+3.83%) than for initial-state perturbations (+0.52%), indicating that multi-view spatial cues are particularly effective in enhancing robustness to observational changes.
- **Significance:** This establishes that the explicit encoding of geometric structure through spatial token fusion provides a stronger spatial inductive bias, enhancing the policy's fundamental ability to reason about geometry even before task-specific RL fine-tuning.

#### 4.2. Effect of Reward Density on Spatial Generalization (RQ2)

- **Finding:** Utilizing **spatially-aware step-level dense rewards** consistently led to faster convergence and smoother training dynamics during RL fine-tuning, compared to sparse rewards.
- **Observation:** This stabilizing effect was evident in both zero-shot evaluation and few-shot RL settings.
- **Mechanism:** Dense rewards provide granular, intermediate feedback aligned with geometric progress, which significantly reduces gradient variance and stabilizes policy updates, especially under the stochasticity introduced by exploration noise. In contrast, sparse rewards offer weak guidance, leading to high-variance gradients and instability.
- **Significance:** This highlights that appropriate reward shaping is crucial not just for achieving higher final performance, but fundamentally for ensuring optimization stability and preventing the erosion of spatial consistency during RL adaptation, particularly in spatially complex tasks.

#### 4.3. Spatially-Conditioned Exploration Beyond Dense Rewards (RQ3)

- **Finding:** Combining dense rewards with **Spatially-Conditioned Annealed Noise (SCAN)** further improved final success rates and overall robustness compared to using dense rewards with conventional learned flow noise.
- **Comparison:** While dense rewards stabilized optimization, in scenarios with limited trajectory coverage (few-shot RL), the agent might still miss critical geometric configurations. SCAN addresses this by actively guiding exploration.
- **Mechanism:** SCAN injects spatially-conditioned stochasticity, dynamically adjusting noise based on the fused visual-spatial embeddings. This encourages the agent to explore under-sampled, geometrically relevant regions of the state space in a controlled manner, preventing local optima and promoting better state visitation.
- **Significance:** This demonstrates that exploration and reward design are complementary. Dense rewards provide direction for learning, while spatially-conditioned exploration ensures that the agent experiences the necessary geometric interactions to fully leverage those rewards, leading to a more comprehensive and robust acquisition of skills.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study

- **Finding:** A cumulative ablation study confirmed that all three components of SA-VLA (spatial token fusion, step-level dense rewards, and SCAN) provide complementary benefits, with the highest performance achieved when all are enabled.
- **Quantitative Impact:**
 - Full SA-VLA: 83.75% success rate.
 - SA-VLA w/o SCAN: Performance dropped to 83.00%.
 - SA-VLA w/o Dense Rewards (and w/o SCAN): Performance dropped significantly to 77.50%.
 - SA-VLA w/o Fusion (and w/o DR, w/o SCAN): 81.00% (this is essentially a baseline pretrained policy fine-tuned with sparse rewards, showing that sparse RL without spatial awareness performs worse than the non-RL baseline).
- **Conclusion:** The ablation confirms that the robust performance of SA-VLA is a result of the synergistic interaction between its spatially-aware components. Removing any element systematically degrades spatial robustness during RL adaptation.

In summary, the results convincingly demonstrate that SA-VLA effectively addresses the problem of spatial inductive bias collapse. Its multi-faceted approach, incorporating geometry-aware representations, dense geometric rewards, and spatially-conditioned exploration, enables stable RL fine-tuning and significantly improves generalization capabilities under diverse spatial perturbations in robotic manipulation tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The SA-VLA framework represents a significant advancement in the field of embodied artificial intelligence and robotic manipulation, with several profound implications and potential impacts:

1. **Enhanced Robustness and Generalization for Robotic Manipulation:** The primary significance of SA-VLA lies in its ability to enable stable reinforcement learning fine-tuning for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies while *preserving and enhancing spatial grounding*. This directly addresses a critical vulnerability of current VLA models: their fragility under spatial distribution shifts (e.g., varying viewpoints, object placements, clutter). By yielding more robust and transferable behaviors, SA-VLA brings VLA-powered robots closer to real-world deployment in unstructured and dynamic environments, where variations are the norm rather than the exception.

2. **Unlocking the Full Potential of Flow-Matching VLA Policies:** Flow-matching models like $\pi^{0.5}$ represent a powerful new generation of foundation models for robotics. SA-VLA provides a crucial missing piece by offering a reliable method to adapt these policies via RL without undermining their inherent strengths. This extends their utility beyond supervised pretraining, allowing them to continuously learn and improve in new scenarios while retaining their sophisticated understanding of space.

3. **Holistic Approach to RL Design:** The success of SA-VLA highlights the importance of a *holistic and aligned* approach to reinforcement learning. Instead of focusing on isolated improvements in policy architecture, reward design, or exploration, SA-VLA demonstrates that integrating spatial awareness across **all three** components (representation, reward, and exploration) is key to achieving robust learning in complex, visually-grounded tasks. This could serve as a blueprint for designing more effective RL systems for other domains where specific inductive biases are crucial.

4. **Bridging the Gap Between Pretraining and Fine-tuning:** The paper effectively identifies and addresses the "catastrophic forgetting" or "erosion of inductive bias" problem that often plagues fine-tuning of large pretrained models in RL settings. By explicitly preserving spatial inductive bias, SA-VLA offers a method to gracefully transfer knowledge from large-scale pretraining to task-specific adaptation, a central challenge in modern AI.

5. **Interpretability and Credit Assignment:** The use of phase-consistent, step-level dense rewards based on geometric progress not only stabilizes learning but also offers more interpretable learning signals. This clarity in credit assignment helps the policy understand *why* certain actions are beneficial, leading to more meaningful and less spurious action-reward correlations.

6. **Guidance for Future Research:** While SA-VLA demonstrates impressive results, it also openly discusses its limitations, such as potential challenges with deformable objects or the reliance on pretrained spatial encoders. This provides clear directions for future research, including exploring richer geometric representations, more flexible phase abstractions, and alternative interaction modalities. The availability of code and a project page further ensures that this work can be built upon by the wider research community.

In conclusion, SA-VLA represents a significant step towards developing truly intelligent and adaptable robotic systems. By strategically embedding spatial awareness throughout the entire reinforcement learning pipeline, it overcomes a critical barrier in VLA policy adaptation, paving the way for more capable and reliable robots in the physical world.
