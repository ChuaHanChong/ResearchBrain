# Identity-Preserving Image-to-Video Generation via Reward-Guided Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Huazhong University of Science and Technology and Alibaba introduced IPRO, a reinforcement learning framework that significantly enhances facial identity preservation in image-to-video generation without modifying the foundational diffusion model's architecture. The approach led to improvements in FaceSim by up to 45.9% while maintaining overall video quality.

## Problem

- Maintaining consistent human identity in image-to-video (I2V) generation is challenging, especially with varying expressions, movements, or small input faces, leading to identity degradation and drift.
- Existing identity preservation methods, often designed for text-to-video (T2V), are architecturally intrusive for I2V or suffer from exposure bias when using supervised finetuning.
- A general-purpose solution is needed that improves identity fidelity in I2V foundation models without compromising their original capabilities or requiring per-scene/per-person fine-tuning.

## Method

- The ==Identity-Preserving Reward==-guided Optimization (**IPRO**) framework optimizes diffusion models via a differentiable facial reward function, training on-policy to mitigate exposure bias.
- A novel Facial Scoring Mechanism (FSM) computes rewards by comparing generated faces to a multi-view reference pool from the ground-truth video, allowing for natural variations while preserving identity.
- KL-divergence regularization is incorporated to stabilize training and prevent 'reward hacking' by constraining the optimized model's deviation from the original model's distribution.

## Results

- IPRO consistently improved Face Similarity (FaceSim) across foundation models, with increases of up to **45.9%** for an in-house I2V model (**0.4769** to **0.6960**) and **20.1%** for Wan 2.2 **27B**-A14B (**0.5780** to **0.6942**).
- The method achieved superior FaceSim (**0.6942**) compared to adapted state-of-the-art T2V identity-preserving methods (e.g., MoCA† **0.5820**, Concat-ID† **0.6056**) and other RL approaches like DPO (**0.6284**).
- Ablation studies demonstrated that the Facial Scoring Mechanism and KL-divergence regularization effectively mitigated reward hacking from **58%** to **10%** while maintaining high FaceSim, and ArcFace embeddings significantly outperformed CLIP for facial identity scoring.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reinforcement learning, guided by a differentiable identity reward, can effectively enhance identity preservation in I2V models without altering their architecture, directly addressing long-term consistency and exposure bias.
> - Using a multi-view reference pool for facial scoring allows generated subjects to resemble ground-truth identities while accommodating natural variations in pose and expression, preventing rigid 'copy-paste' artifacts.
> - Truncating gradient backpropagation to the final sampling steps efficiently optimizes fine-grained identity details, and KL-divergence regularization is essential to balance identity fidelity with maintaining overall video quality and preventing unnatural generated content.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of video generation has witnessed advancements, particularly with diffusion transformer-based models that synthesize high-quality, temporally coherent videos. Within this domain, human-centric video generation constitutes a significant application area. However, a persistent challenge in image-to-video (I2V) generation is maintaining consistent identity between the input image and the generated video sequence, especially when the subject in the video undergoes substantial expression changes, movements, or when the face occupies a small portion of the input image. This issue can lead to progressive identity degradation and drift from the initial appearance.

Previous research in identity preservation largely concentrates on text-to-video (T2V) generation. These T2V methods often integrate auxiliary identity modules, such as face adapters, 3D facial geometry, or scene-specific LoRA adapters, and utilize curated datasets to inject identity information. However, the authors note that applying such architecturally-intrusive methods to I2V models, where identity is theoretically already encoded in the first frame, can be redundant, disrupt the original structure of foundation models, and increase complexity. Furthermore, supervised finetuning (SFT) methods, commonly used in subject-driven generation, can suffer from exposure bias, which manifests as error accumulation and identity drift over time due to discrepancies between training and inference dynamics. These methods are also often designed for single subjects, limiting scalability to multi-individual scenes.

This work addresses the I2V identity preservation problem from the perspective of reinforcement learning (RL). Prior RL-based approaches, such as ReFL, DRaFT, AlignProp, and VADER, have utilized differentiable reward functions to supervise the output of diffusion models, enabling adaptation to task-specific objectives. This paper introduces an RL framework specifically tailored for identity preservation in I2V generation, aiming to enhance identity fidelity without modifying the underlying model architecture or introducing additional identity networks. It positions itself as a direct optimization approach that leverages a face identity model as a reward, contrasting with direct preference optimization (DPO) methods that learn from relative preferences and may lack absolute calibration.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an I2V generation framework that significantly enhances identity preservation, particularly for human faces, without requiring modifications to the foundational diffusion model's architecture or the introduction of auxiliary identity networks.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges observed in existing I2V models:

- **Identity Degradation in I2V:** Current I2V models struggle to maintain the identity of the input human face throughout a generated video. This issue is particularly pronounced when the input face is low-resolution, when the subject performs large-scale movements (e.g., jumping, turning), or when significant expression changes occur. The propagation of errors across frames can lead to identity drift, where the generated subject diverges from the appearance in the initial frame.
- **Limitations of Existing Identity Preservation Methods:**
 - **T2V vs. I2V:** Many successful identity-preserving methods are developed for T2V generation and rely on injecting explicit identity features or architectural changes. Adapting these to I2V, where identity is inherently present in the first frame, may be redundant and structurally disruptive.
 - **Exposure Bias:** Supervised finetuning (SFT) methods, common in subject-driven generation, are susceptible to exposure bias. This discrepancy between training on ground-truth inputs and inferring on self-generated inputs can lead to the accumulation of subtle errors that result in long-term identity inconsistency.
 - **Architectural Intrusiveness and Scalability:** Methods that introduce new modules or significantly alter architectures increase model complexity and are often limited to single-subject generation, restricting their applicability in real-world scenarios with multiple individuals.
- **Need for a General-Purpose Solution:** The authors seek a method that can improve identity preservation in general-purpose I2V foundation models without compromising their original capabilities or requiring per-scene/per-person fine-tuning, thereby enhancing practicality and scalability.

To address these motivations, the specific objectives of the proposed Identity-Preserving Reward-guided Optimization (IPRO) framework are:

- To introduce a direct and effective tuning algorithm that optimizes diffusion models using a face identity scorer, guided by reinforcement learning principles.
- To mitigate exposure bias by training on-policy, initializing trajectories from pure Gaussian noise, and directly optimizing for long-term identity consistency.
- To accelerate convergence and improve performance by backpropagating the reward signal through the final steps of the sampling chain, concentrating gradient feedback where fine-grained details are most influential.
- To develop a robust multi-view facial scoring mechanism that uses facial feature pools from ground-truth videos to enhance generalization to variations in pose, expression, and lighting, while preventing the "copy-paste" phenomenon where generated faces become overly rigid.
- To stabilize training and prevent reward hacking by incorporating a KL-divergence regularization that constrains the deviation of the tuned model from the original diffusion model.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed Identity-Preserving Reward-guided Optimization (IPRO) framework is a reinforcement learning-based approach designed to enhance facial identity preservation in I2V generation. It operates by optimizing diffusion models using a differentiable facial reward function, without altering their underlying architecture.

The methodology consists of three main components:

#### 3.1. Identity-Preserving Reward

IPRO leverages a differentiable facial reward function to guide the video generation process.
- **Facial Reward Feedback Learning:** The core idea is to maximize face identity consistency between generated video frames and ground-truth video frames. A facial reward score, `R_face`, is computed based on the cosine similarity between ArcFace embeddings of the ground-truth (GT) video and the generated video. ArcFace is selected for its discriminative power in capturing fine-grained facial features. The goal is to fine-tune the diffusion model's parameters (θ) such that the sampling process maximizes `R_face`.
- **Mitigation of Exposure Bias:** Unlike supervised fine-tuning (SFT) with teacher forcing, which trains on real samples but infers on self-generated states, IPRO trains on-policy. It initializes trajectories from random noise and generates videos in an inference-like manner. This approach aligns the training and inference distributions, directly optimizing for long-term identity consistency and thereby mitigating exposure bias, which can lead to identity drift over time.
- **Efficient Gradient Backpropagation:** To optimize memory usage and accelerate convergence, IPRO adopts a truncation strategy inspired by DRaFT. Gradients from the reward function are backpropagated only through the last `K` sampling steps. This strategy is based on the observation that later timesteps are more influential for fine-grained appearance details, which are critical for identity preservation, while also preventing drift in global dynamics. The truncated gradient computation involves summing the product of the reward gradient with respect to `x_t` and `x_t`'s gradient with respect to `θ` over the last `K` steps.

#### 3.2. Facial Scoring Mechanism (FSM)

IPRO introduces a novel Facial Scoring Mechanism (FSM) to provide a robust and informative reward signal.
- **Reference Face Pool:** Instead of comparing generated frames to a single reference image (which can cause a "copy-paste" phenomenon, suppressing facial expression diversity) or to time-aligned ground-truth frames (which provides weak supervision under teacher forcing), FSM treats all faces in the ground-truth video as a multi-view reference pool.
- **Multi-view Similarity Calculation:** For each generated frame `i` (`ˆx_i`), its ArcFace embedding (`ϕ(ˆx_i)`) is compared via cosine similarity to the embeddings of all faces (`ϕ(x_j)`) in the ground-truth video's `F` frames (`x_1..., x_F`). An average similarity score `s_i` is computed for each generated frame.
- **Overall Face Reward:** The final `R_face` reward is the average of these `s_i` scores across all `F'` generated frames. This mechanism encourages the generated subjects to resemble those in the ground-truth video while allowing for natural variations in pose, expression, and lighting, thereby improving generalization.

#### 3.3. KL-Divergence Regularization

To stabilize the training process and prevent "reward hacking"—where the model over-optimizes for the reward function at the expense of naturalness or adherence to other prompts—IPRO incorporates a multi-step KL-divergence regularization.
- **Constraining Deviation:** This regularization penalizes deviations between the optimized model (θ) and the original, untuned model (θ_ref) across the reverse-time sampling trajectory. It measures the KL-divergence between the path distributions induced by the two models.
- **Formulation:** The regularization term is defined as a sum of weighted squared differences between the velocity parameterizations (`v_θ` and `v_θ_ref`) of the optimized and reference models over `K` steps. This constraint ensures that while the model learns to preserve identity, it largely retains its original capabilities and distribution, preventing large-scale shifts and maintaining diversity in generated content.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

- **Foundation Models:** Experiments utilized an in-house I2V model (15B dense model with MMDiT and single DiT blocks), Wan 2.2 5B, and Wan 2.2 27B-A14B. For Wan 2.2 27B-A14B, only the low-noise expert model was trainable.
- **Training Framework:** ROLL was employed for training efficiency. Wan2.2-Lightning, a distilled version of Wan2.2, was used, requiring only 8 steps without Classifier-free Guidance (CFG).
- **Optimization:** Adam optimizer with a learning rate of 2e-5, trained for 100 steps with a batch size of 64. The truncation gradient step `K` was set to 4. Facial reward weight was 0.1, and KL-divergence loss weight was 1.
- **Datasets:** High-quality videos (960p resolution) were collected from the Internet. SCRFD was used for face detection, with a focus on "small-face scenarios" (largest face bounding box per frame not exceeding 100x100 pixels). Videos with insufficient face coverage or occluded faces (identified by Qwen2.5-VL) were discarded. Face embeddings were extracted from remaining frames.
- **Evaluation Metrics:**
 - **Identity Consistency:** Face Similarity (FaceSim) between generated frames and the input image, evaluated on a small-face evaluation set of 600 scenes.
 - **Overall Video Quality:** VBench-I2V metrics: Subject Consistency (SC), Background Consistency (BC), Aesthetic Quality (AQ), Imaging Quality (IQ), Time Flickering (TF), Dynamic Degree (DD), and Motion Smoothness (MS).
 - **Reward Hacking:** Measured qualitatively and quantitatively using a Vision-Language Model (VLM) (Gemini 2.5 Pro) to identify unnatural rigidity or lack of expression.
 - **Human Evaluation:** A user study comparing IPRO against baselines on Identity Preservation, Visual Quality, Text Alignment, and Motion Amplitude.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate that IPRO consistently improves identity preservation in I2V generation across various foundation models and evaluation scenarios, without compromising overall video quality.

#### 4.1. Comparisons on Facial Reward

- **Quantitative Improvement:** The FaceSim metric showed substantial improvements when IPRO was integrated. For the in-house I2V model, FaceSim increased by 45.9% (from 0.4769 to 0.6960). For Wan 2.2 5B, it improved by 44.1% (from 0.3788 to 0.5460). For Wan 2.2 27B-A14B, it increased by 20.1% (from 0.5780 to 0.6942).
- **General Video Quality:** The introduction of the reward model did not degrade other general video quality metrics as assessed by VBench-I2V, indicating that identity preservation was enhanced without negative side effects on aspects like subject consistency, aesthetic quality, or temporal flickering.
- **Qualitative Observations:** Visual comparisons (Figure 4) indicated that IPRO produced more stable generation and stronger identity preservation compared to the baseline models.

#### 4.2. Comparisons with Other Methods

- **Adapted T2V Baselines:** IPRO was compared against two state-of-the-art T2V identity-preserving methods, MoCA† and Concat-ID†, adapted for I2V.
- **Superior FaceSim:** IPRO achieved a FaceSim of 0.6942, outperforming MoCA† (0.5820) and Concat-ID† (0.6056) when integrated with Wan 2.2. This indicates better alignment with identity-specific goals through reward-guided learning.
- **Qualitative Advantage:** Qualitative comparisons (Figure 5) further illustrated IPRO's superior identity retention. IPRO also demonstrated natural generalization to multi-person scenarios (Supplementary Figure 11), a challenge for methods that inject identity features.

#### 4.3. Comparison with DPO and GRPO

- **Reward-Based vs. Preference-Based RL:** IPRO, using an explicit identity reward model, was compared to DPO and GRPO.
- **Higher FaceSim:** IPRO achieved a FaceSim of 0.6942, surpassing DPO (0.6284) and GRPO (0.6334). The authors attributed this to the direct, calibrated signals provided by the identity reward model, contrasting with DPO's relative preference optimization and GRPO's limited efficiency in I2V due to low response diversity.
- **Qualitative Consistency:** Visual results (Figure 6) supported IPRO's superior identity preservation over DPO and GRPO.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study

- **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** When facial similarity was integrated as an auxiliary loss in SFT, the FaceSim was 0.6392. IPRO's reward-based approach achieved a higher FaceSim of 0.6942, indicating its robustness over explicit supervision.
- **Reward Model Choice:** Using the ArcFace model for facial embeddings (FaceSim 0.6942) significantly outperformed using the CLIP image encoder (FaceSim 0.6099), demonstrating ArcFace's suitability for fine-grained facial identity features in this framework.
- **Gradient Steps Truncation (K):** Experiments showed that using the final (low-noise) gradient steps for backpropagation (K=4, FaceSim 0.6942) resulted in better identity consistency compared to high-noise initial steps (FaceSim 0.6456). Increasing K beyond 4 yielded diminishing returns.
- **Reward Hacking Mitigation:**
 - Without KL-divergence regularization (FaceSim 0.7544, Hacking 58%) or the Facial Scoring Mechanism (FSM) (FaceSim 0.7388, Hacking 52%), the model exhibited higher FaceSim but a significant increase in reward hacking, characterized by unnaturally stiff faces, lack of expression, and poor responsiveness to prompts (e.g., failing to open eyes as prompted).
 - IPRO, with both KL regularization and FSM, achieved a FaceSim of 0.6942 with a hacking rate of 10%, demonstrating its ability to balance identity consistency with expressive, prompt-following behavior.
 - Ablation on loss weights for reward and KL-divergence showed that a ratio of λ2/λ1 = 10 (KL weight / reward weight) provided the optimal balance between FaceSim and hacking rate.

#### 4.5. Human Evaluations

A user study involving 96 volunteers compared IPRO against baseline I2V models (in-house and Wan 2.2). Participants rated videos based on Identity Preservation, Visual Quality, Text Alignment, and Motion Amplitude. The results indicated that IPRO significantly outperformed baselines in Identity Preservation, while performing comparably in other dimensions. Specifically, IPRO was preferred for identity preservation over the in-house I2V model in 67.2% of cases and over Wan 2.2 in 62.1% of cases, with low "no preference" rates.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces a method for enhancing identity preservation in image-to-video (I2V) generation, addressing a challenge within human-centric video synthesis.

The significance of this work stems from several contributions:

- **Non-Intrusive Identity Preservation:** The IPRO framework achieves improved identity consistency without requiring modifications to the architecture of pre-trained I2V diffusion models or the addition of auxiliary identity networks. This approach promotes the reusability and adaptability of foundational video generation models.
- **Leveraging Reinforcement Learning:** The paper demonstrates the efficacy of a reward-guided optimization strategy for a fine-grained perceptual task like facial identity preservation in I2V. This contrasts with more common supervised fine-tuning methods and highlights the potential of RL for adapting generative models to specific objectives, particularly in scenarios prone to exposure bias.
- **Robust Facial Scoring Mechanism (FSM):** The proposed FSM, which utilizes ground-truth video frames as a multi-view facial feature pool, provides a more comprehensive and flexible reward signal. This mechanism is designed to prevent static "copy-paste" phenomena and encourage natural facial variations while maintaining identity, thereby improving generalization across different expressions and poses.
- **Training Stability and Generalization:** The incorporation of KL-divergence regularization is a notable technical contribution, as it helps to stabilize the RL training process and mitigate reward hacking. This ensures that identity preservation is achieved without excessively distorting the original capabilities of the foundation model or leading to unnatural artifacts.
- **Scalability to Multi-Person Scenarios:** The methodology naturally extends to scenes with multiple individuals without requiring architectural adjustments, a capability that can be challenging for some identity-specific methods.

The potential impact of this work includes:

- **Enhanced Human-Centric Video Generation:** Improved identity preservation could lead to more realistic and controllable generation of human-centric videos, benefiting applications in digital content creation, virtual try-ons, personalized media, and synthetic avatar generation.
- **Advancement in Foundational Models:** By demonstrating an effective way to refine the capabilities of general-purpose I2V models without structural changes, this research could pave the way for more adaptable and controllable generative AI systems across various tasks.
- **Motivation for Further Research:** The authors express hope that their work will bring identity preservation in I2V and its reward framework into broader community focus, motivating further exploration into unified identity reward models that encompass non-facial attributes (e.g., jewelry, accessories, clothing) in future work.

The authors also acknowledge potential social impacts, noting that while the technology offers benefits for creative production and research, it may also amplify risks associated with non-consensual deepfakes and misinformation. They express a commitment to working with legal and ethics experts to advance safety measures while realizing the technology's benefits.
