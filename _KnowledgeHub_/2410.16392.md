# Scaffolded Language Models with Language Supervision for Mixed-Autonomy: A Survey

> [!summary] Summary
> This survey organizes the literature on "scaffolded language models" and "language supervision," identifying a distinct learning paradigm that optimizes non-parametric variables (prompts and tools) using language-based feedback. It provides a taxonomy for these compound AI systems and outlines a research agenda for continuous learning in mixed-autonomy scenarios.

## Problem

- Integrating Large Language Models (LLMs) into complex, multi-step automation tasks with external tools effectively.
- Overcoming limitations of traditional parametric training for LLMs, such as catastrophic forgetting and challenges with ambiguous human feedback.
- Lack of a unified framework to categorize and understand fragmented research areas related to compound AI systems and LM pipelines.

## Method

- Introduces the concept of ==scaffolded LMs== -- structures that integrate LMs into multi-step processes with tools, differentiating between autonomous "agents" and pre-structured "workflows."
- Proposes ==language supervision== as a ==non-parametric training== paradigm where LMs optimize external components (prompts and tools) using language-based feedback.
- Presents a comprehensive taxonomy that categorizes approaches to training scaffolded LMs with language supervision, including ==prompt optimization==, ==experiential learning==, and ==AutoDiff-style frameworks==.

## Results

- A coherent conceptual framework and taxonomy were established, unifying diverse research on integrating LLMs with external tools and optimizing them via language.
- The survey identified and systematized "training scaffolded LMs with language supervision" as a distinct and powerful learning paradigm for mixed-autonomy AI.
- Outlined key future research directions for continuous learning in AI agents, emphasizing capabilities like scalability, plasticity, efficiency, and interpretability in dynamic environments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Non-parametric optimization with language supervision offers advantages over traditional parametric training, such as mitigating catastrophic forgetting and supporting closed-source models.
> - Language-based feedback provides rich, interpretable objectives, enabling LLMs to refine their behavior and external tool use effectively in mixed-autonomy settings.
> - Future research should focus on enabling "agents that inhabit streams of experience" to learn continuously and adaptively from ongoing language-based feedback, moving beyond episodic learning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This survey paper is positioned within the rapidly evolving landscape of Large Language Models (LLMs) and their application in complex automation tasks, specifically focusing on "scaffolded LMs" and a novel training paradigm termed "language supervision." The work aims to provide a structured overview of the literature surrounding the integration of LMs into multi-step processes with external tools, collectively referred to as "compound AI systems" or "language agents."

The authors distinguish their contribution from existing surveys by adopting a broad "scaffold abstraction" that encompasses both autonomous agents and pre-structured workflows, rather than solely focusing on agents. This approach provides a framework to unify diverse research areas such as prompt optimization, experiential learning, and AutoDiff-style frameworks. The paper identifies a paradigm shift from traditional parametric training (where LM weights are updated) to non-parametric training (where external components like prompts and tools are optimized using language-based feedback). This shift is presented as crucial for developing AI systems capable of operating effectively in mixed-autonomy settings, where humans and AI collaboratively share control and decision-making. The survey situates itself as an organizational effort to synthesize and categorize the fragmented but related literature on compound AI systems, LM pipelines, prompt optimization, language agents, AI workflow optimization, and experiential learning agents.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this survey is to organize the existing intricate literature on the design and optimization of structures built around post-trained LMs, which the authors term "scaffolded LMs." The core focus is on a specific scaffold that integrates LMs in a multi-step process with tools, and particularly on the paradigm of "training scaffolded LMs with language supervision."

The motivation for this work is multi-faceted:

- **Emergence of Scaffolded LMs:** The paper highlights that scaffolded LMs represent a new type of program for digital automation, extending LMs beyond traditional NLP tasks. These systems leverage natural language as an interface for human-digital system interaction, facilitating mixed-autonomy scenarios.
- **Limitations of Parametric Training:** While parametric training (e.g., supervised learning, reinforcement learning, unsupervised learning with well-defined loss functions) has achieved significant breakthroughs, it faces challenges such as catastrophic forgetting. It also often struggles with the rich, ambiguous, and open-ended feedback naturally provided by humans.
- **Advantages of Non-Parametric Training with Language Supervision:** The authors emphasize that optimizing prompts and tools (referred to as "non-parametric variables") through language supervision offers several advantages:
 - **Rich, Interpretable, and Expressive Objectives:** Language enables the formulation of complex, human-understandable objectives.
 - **Mitigation of Catastrophic Forgetting:** Unlike updating LM weights, non-parametric updates are less prone to overwriting previously learned knowledge.
 - **Compatibility with Closed-Source Models:** Non-parametric methods can optimize external components without requiring access to the internal weights of proprietary LMs.
 - **Learning from Human Feedback:** In mixed-autonomy settings, humans provide frequent and rich language-based feedback (corrections, error identification). Language supervision allows LMs to learn directly from this feedback, improving continuously.
 - **Efficiency and Interpretability:** Optimization in the language space offers an efficient and interpretable way to modify system behavior, especially with fuzzy objectives.
- **Need for a Unified Taxonomy:** The diverse and rapidly growing body of research in areas like compound AI systems, language agents, and prompt optimization requires a coherent framework to understand their interconnections and advancements. This survey aims to provide such a taxonomy, distilling a coherent structure from the evolving field.
- **Beyond Episodic Learning:** A key motivation is to address the limitation of current LM systems that learn only within individual episodes and fail to accumulate knowledge across experiences. The paper motivates the need for agents that "inhabit streams of experience" by continuously learning from language-based feedback in real-world, mixed-autonomy deployments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The survey adopts a structured approach to categorize and explain the literature on scaffolded LMs and language supervision.

The methodological breakdown is as follows:

- **Defining Scaffolded LMs:** The paper begins by defining a scaffolded LM as a structure that integrates an LM into a multi-step process with tools. It views these as semi-parametric models where the LM is the parametric component, and prompts and tools are the non-parametric variables.
- **Post-trained Language Models and Tools:**
 - **Post-trained LM Interface:** It describes how post-trained LMs operate within a "chat format" protocol, involving various roles (system, user, assistant, tool) and special tokens. It clarifies how prompts (system and user messages) steer model behavior and how non-parametric training optimizes these prompts. The concept of "privilege ordering" of messages in a chat format is introduced to explain how LMs prioritize information and mitigate prompt injection.
 - **Tools:** The paper broadly defines tools as external capabilities (e.g., APIs, code interpreters, web search, vector databases, other LMs, human input) that LMs can invoke. It discusses "function calling" via JSON schemas, the challenges of tool composition (sequential vs. parallel), the use of executable Python code for unified tool calls (CodeAct), and issues such as invalid tool calls, execution failures, and security vulnerabilities (code injection). The importance of clear tool descriptions for reliability is highlighted.
- **Multi-Step Process: Agents vs. Workflows:** The survey differentiates between two primary types of scaffolds:
 - **Agents:** Characterized by high autonomy, agents decide which tools to use and when to terminate, adapting to unstructured scenarios. They typically rely on a single, comprehensive prompt and update a continuous chat history.
 - **Workflows:** Designed for structured scenarios where the solution path can be pre-determined. Workflows involve pre-defined steps, each with tailored prompts, limiting the LM's autonomy to achieve greater predictability and cost-efficiency. They can leverage meta-generation techniques for parallel processing and robust outputs.
- **Benchmarks for Mixed-Autonomy:** The paper reviews recent benchmarks (e.g., SWE-Bench, SWE-Lancer, HCAST, RE-Bench, PaperBench, τ-Bench, SWEET-RL, BrowseComp) that reflect a shift from measuring pure AI intelligence to evaluating productivity gains in mixed-autonomy settings. These benchmarks often align agent performance with human effort, measure monetary return, simulate human collaboration, and use real-world software/APIs.
- **Training with Language Supervision Taxonomy:** This section forms the core of the methodology, categorizing approaches that use an LM as an optimizer for non-parametric variables:
 - **Prompt Optimization:** Methods that optimize the prompt of a *single* LM call (not a scaffolded LM). This includes generating variations, using evolutionary algorithms (crossover, mutation), optimizing based on scores (OPRO), fine-grained adjustments (SAMMO, ABO), generating textual feedback for instruction improvement, and structuring output for guided reasoning (Self-discover).
 - **Experiential Learning:** Focuses on training *agents* by extracting insights or refining tools from execution traces.
 - **Insights Extraction:** Approaches like ExpeL extract general heuristics or decision rules from successful/unsuccessful execution traces and integrate them into the agent's system prompt. Follow-up works explore selective insight addition (AutoGuide) and preventing distributional shifts (AutoManual).
 - **Tools Optimization:** Methods aim to refine developer-defined code into more effective, reusable tools from successful traces (ASI, SkillWeaver) or address failures (LearnAct, AgentOptimizer).
 - **AutoDiff Frameworks:** Applies optimization principles inspired by deep learning's automatic differentiation to *workflows*.
 - **Graph Abstraction:** Workflows are represented as directed acyclic graphs (DAGs) with nodes as variables (prompts, intermediate values) and operations (Python functions, LM calls). TextGrad focuses on textual variables, while Trace supports arbitrary Python functions as operations, allowing for tool optimization.
 - **Graph Execution and Optimization:** Involves forward computation (collecting feedback), backward feedback propagation (using a chain rule metaphor or formatting execution traces), and variable updates. TextGrad's chain rule propagates textual feedback, while Trace autoregressively generates updated variables from a formatted trace.
- **Beyond Episodic Learning:** The paper concludes by outlining a research agenda for "agents that inhabit streams of experience," focusing on continuous learning from language-based feedback. This includes discussing desired capabilities like scalable learning (insight caching, skill induction), plasticity and stability (scoped edits, gated promotion, versioned artifacts), efficiency (compacting experience into durable artifacts, sleep-time compute), and interpretability (human-readable diffs, provenance). It contrasts this non-parametric approach with parametric continual learning and test-time training methods.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

As a survey, this paper primarily synthesizes and categorizes existing research rather than presenting novel empirical findings. The main "results" are the insights derived from this synthesis:

- **Emergence of a New Learning Paradigm:** The central finding is the identification and systematization of "training scaffolded LMs with language supervision" as a distinct and powerful learning paradigm. This approach, which optimizes non-parametric variables (prompts and tools) using language-based feedback, offers a viable alternative or complement to traditional parametric training.
- **Advantages of Non-Parametric Optimization:** The survey consolidates evidence suggesting that language supervision in non-parametric training addresses several critical limitations of parametric methods. These include mitigating catastrophic forgetting, enabling compatibility with closed-source LMs, supporting interpretable and expressive objectives, and leveraging rich, natural language feedback prevalent in human-AI collaboration.
- **Categorization of Scaffold Designs:** The work effectively categorizes scaffolded LMs into "agents" (high autonomy, flexible) and "workflows" (structured, predictable), illustrating their respective strengths and suitable application domains. This distinction highlights the trade-offs between flexibility and control in designing LM-based systems.
- **Evolution of Language-Based Optimization Techniques:** The survey traces the progression of language-based optimization from simple prompt optimization for single LM calls to complex AutoDiff-style frameworks for entire computational workflows. This demonstrates an increasing sophistication in how LMs are used to self-optimize and improve their own behavior and the surrounding systems.
- **Impact of Language-based Feedback:** The literature reviewed consistently shows that LMs can effectively interpret diverse forms of language feedback—ranging from error messages and execution traces to explicit human corrections and high-level objectives—to refine their internal logic (via insights) or external capabilities (via tools).
- **Shift in Benchmarking Focus:** The survey observes a significant trend in benchmarks moving from isolated performance metrics to evaluating real-world productivity gains and effectiveness in mixed-autonomy settings. This reflects the practical application and increasing demand for robust, collaborative AI systems.
- **Identification of Key Future Research Avenues:** A critical finding is the articulation of "beyond episodic learning" as a crucial missing capability. The paper identifies the core requirements for agents to continuously learn from "streams of experience," including scalability, plasticity, stability, efficiency, and interpretability. This provides a roadmap for future research in developing truly adaptive and self-improving AI systems.
- **Interpretability and Control:** The nature of language and code as non-parametric variables inherently offers higher interpretability and easier human oversight compared to opaque internal neural network weights. This makes non-parametric updates particularly suitable for mixed-autonomy where human understanding and intervention are necessary.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This survey holds substantial significance for the fields of artificial intelligence, natural language processing, and human-computer interaction, with several potential impacts:

- **Conceptual Framework for AI System Design:** The paper provides a unifying conceptual framework that brings together disparate lines of research under the umbrella of "scaffolded LMs with language supervision." This can help researchers and developers better understand, categorize, and design complex AI systems that leverage LMs and external tools.
- **Advancement of Mixed-Autonomy Systems:** By highlighting the unique advantages of non-parametric training with language supervision, the survey contributes significantly to the development of AI systems capable of effective collaboration with humans. This is crucial for real-world applications where AI functions as a co-worker, adapting to human feedback and shared goals (e.g., software co-pilots, digital assistants, scientific discovery platforms).
- **Guidance for Future Research:** The detailed articulation of "beyond episodic learning" and its required capabilities (scalability, plasticity, stability, efficiency, interpretability) offers a clear roadmap for future research. It can inspire new investigations into continuous learning mechanisms, novel architectural designs for agents, and improved methods for integrating human feedback.
- **Addressing Practical Deployment Challenges:** The emphasis on mitigating catastrophic forgetting, ensuring compatibility with closed-source models, and enhancing interpretability directly addresses key practical challenges in deploying and maintaining AI systems in real-world environments. This can lead to more robust, reliable, and trustworthy AI products.
- **Shift Towards Adaptive AI:** The survey advocates for a shift from static, pre-trained models to dynamic, adaptive AI systems that continuously evolve and improve based on ongoing experience and user interaction. This paradigm shift could accelerate the development of more intelligent and versatile AI agents.
- **Influence on Benchmark Development:** By analyzing current benchmarks and highlighting the need for metrics aligned with real-world productivity and mixed-autonomy, the paper implicitly encourages the development of more relevant and challenging evaluation standards for future AI systems.
- **Interdisciplinary Impact:** The discussion touches upon aspects of cognitive science (e.g., how agents learn from experience), software engineering (e.g., tool reusability, code actions), and human factors (e.g., interpretability, human-AI collaboration), suggesting a broad interdisciplinary impact.
