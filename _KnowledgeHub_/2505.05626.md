# Perceiving Beyond Language Priors: Enhancing Visual Comprehension and Attention in Multimodal Models

> [!summary] Summary
> PERCEPTLLM, a multimodal large language model, integrates architectural disentanglement, auxiliary visual supervision, and input masking to enhance visual grounding and reduce reliance on language priors. This approach achieves superior performance on spatial reasoning benchmarks, outperforming larger models while maintaining inference efficiency.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) exhibit weak visual grounding, frequently generating plausible but factually incorrect responses by over-relying on linguistic patterns.
- Existing MLLMs struggle with fine-grained visual details, precise spatial reasoning, and object attribute identification due to sparse visual training signals and strong language priors.
- A single Transformer backbone faces a tension between optimizing for causal language modeling and rich spatial representation learning, hindering robust multimodal integration.

## Method

- Introduced ==VISUALLOSS==, an auxiliary loss leveraging powerful self-supervised vision encoders (I-JEPA/AIM-v2) to compel the LLM backbone to build rich, ==disentangled visual representations==.
- Implemented ==BLANKTOKENS==, a strategy that masks portions of input text during training, forcing the model to initiate and sustain responses based primarily on visual information.
- Developed a ==disentangled architecture== with independent weights for vision and text pathways within the LLM backbone, optimizing each modality's processing without incurring additional inference overhead.
- Generated a specialized synthetic dataset featuring programmatically arranged objects and spatially-focused questions to explicitly train for precise visual parsing and spatial reasoning.

## Results

- PERCEPTLLM demonstrated consistent reductions in next-token prediction loss on visually-dependent datasets, with the disentangled architecture alone contributing a notable drop from **3.48** to **3.20**.
- On the SpatialEval benchmark, the full PERCEPTLLM model achieved an average normalized accuracy improvement of **1.52** compared to the baseline, outperforming the larger Llama 3.2 **11B** model.
- The combination of all proposed techniques, including the advanced AIM-v2 encoder, yielded superior performance while maintaining a smaller model size (**8B** vs **11B**) and lower input resolution (**224x224** vs **560x560**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - MLLMs inherently develop a preliminary capability to associate semantic labels with image patches, indicating a foundation for enhanced visual understanding that can be explicitly guided.
> - Decoupling visual and textual processing with independent weights within the LLM backbone resolves the conflict between non-autoregressive spatial representation and autoregressive language generation, improving overall performance and efficiency.
> - Targeted, explicit visual supervision and the systematic weakening of language priors during training are crucial for MLLMs to move beyond superficial visual processing toward truly visually faithful reasoning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of multimodal artificial intelligence, particularly MLLMs, has seen rapid advancements, demonstrating impressive capabilities in processing and integrating information from various modalities like vision and language. Models such as LLaVA and Llama 3.1 have showcased general-purpose abilities across a wide range of tasks, from image captioning to visual question answering.

However, a critical limitation observed in many state-of-the-art MLLMs is their "weak visual grounding" and an "over-reliance on learned language priors." Research by others (e.g.,) has revealed that MLLMs often generate plausible-sounding but factually incorrect responses, effectively "hallucinating" or ignoring fine-grained visual details in favor of statistical correlations present in their vast textual training data. This leads to models struggling with tasks requiring nuanced visual comprehension, precise spatial reasoning, or accurate object attribute identification. The challenge lies in ensuring that visual input genuinely grounds the model's reasoning and generation processes, rather than merely serving as a weak contextual cue.

Previous attempts to address these challenges include:
- **Strengthening visual signals:** Providing embeddings from multiple visual encoders (e.g.,) to offer diverse visual information. However, this often adds complexity and does not fundamentally alter how the LLM integrates the visual data.
- **Auxiliary losses:** Introducing additional loss terms to help the LLM build richer visual representations or promote region-level understanding (e.g.,). A common drawback is the potential need for extra visual annotations.
- **Synthetic or augmented data:** Generating specialized datasets (e.g.,) to provide targeted training for specific capabilities like spatial reasoning.

This paper, "Perceiving Beyond Language Priors," positions itself as a fundamental advancement beyond these existing approaches. Instead of merely augmenting inputs or adding superficial losses, the authors aim to **enhance the MLLM's internal visual representation and its ability to actively guide language generation**. They claim their innovations fundamentally differ by not requiring extra visual annotations or additional encoder overhead at inference time, thereby offering a more integrated and efficient solution to the pervasive problem of weak visual grounding in MLLMs. By focusing on intrinsic improvements within the LLM backbone and its interaction with visual tokens, the work seeks to enable MLLMs to move beyond superficial visual processing towards a truly "visually faithful reasoning and generation."

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to **overcome the limitations of current MLLMs that often default to strong language priors and fail to fully leverage visual input**, leading to poor visual comprehension and attention. The authors aim to enable MLLMs to build a deeper, more nuanced understanding of visual content and actively guide language generation based on these visual insights.

The motivation stems from several identified challenges:
1. **Weak Visual Understanding:** MLLMs often struggle with fine-grained visual details, object attributes, states, or intricate scene information. This is partly attributed to vision encoders like CLIP, which, while good for global semantics, may lack the granularity needed for deep comprehension.
2. **Sparse Training Signals:** The standard next-token prediction loss, calculated only on text, provides an indirect and often weak signal for visual learning. Only a fraction of text tokens might strongly depend on visual content, limiting the LLM's opportunity to develop comprehensive internal visual representations.
3. **Strong Language Priors:** Even with relevant visual information, MLLMs tend to prioritize learned linguistic patterns, leading to "hallucinations" or ignoring visual evidence, especially in tasks requiring spatial reasoning or precise grounding.

To address these challenges, the paper proposes a novel methodology with the following key contributions and specific objectives:
- **Enhanced Visual Representation:** Introduce a novel auxiliary visual loss (VISUALLOSS) combined with an architectural modification to ensure the LLM backbone builds rich, disentangled representations of image regions independent of associated text.
- **Weakening Language Priors:** Develop a technique (BLANKTOKENS) and a specialized synthetic dataset to explicitly encourage the model to reduce reliance on language priors and increase sensitivity to fine-grained visual details.
- **Improved Multimodal Alignment:** Provide insights into how MLLMs internally understand visual information and demonstrate significant improvements in the model's ability to integrate visual and linguistic information, leading to more visually faithful reasoning and generation.

Ultimately, the goal is to develop **PERCEPTLLM**, a multimodal model that exhibits superior visual comprehension and attention, setting a new standard for grounding language generation in visual reality.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed approach, embodied in PERCEPTLLM, introduces several interconnected techniques designed to enhance visual comprehension and attention within MLLMs. The methodology consists of an initial analysis, three core training innovations, and a specialized synthetic dataset.

#### 3.1. Dissecting Visual Tokens in MLLMs

The authors begin by analyzing how existing MLLMs internally represent visual information. They probe the visual token embeddings generated by the LLM backbone by feeding them through its language modeling head and identifying the top predicted token for each visual patch. Their key observation is that MLLMs, even without explicit supervision, develop an emergent capability to associate meaningful semantic labels (e.g., "handle," "canoe," "cow") with corresponding image patches. This suggests that MLLMs perform localized scene interpretation, capturing both foreground and background elements, and fine-grained structures. This "nascent ability" provides the motivation to further enhance and leverage this internal visual understanding.

#### 3.2. Visual Representation using VISUALLOSS

To enrich the LLM's internal processing of visual signals, the authors introduce **VISUALLOSS**. This auxiliary loss function leverages a powerful, self-supervised auxiliary vision encoder, I-JEPA (and later AIM-v2 for advanced versions), during the training phase. The LLM backbone is tasked with predicting the corresponding rich visual representations from I-JEPA based on its own visual token features. By minimizing the Mean Squared Error (MSE) between the LLM's visual features (projected into the auxiliary vision encoder's space) and the target I-JEPA representations, VISUALLOSS provides a strong, targeted supervisory signal. This forces the LLM to build a comprehensive and nuanced understanding of the entire visual content, independent of what is explicitly mentioned in the text. The loss is combined with the standard next-token prediction loss using a weighting coefficient $\beta$.

#### 3.3. Weakening Language Priors using BLANKTOKENS

To mitigate the MLLM's over-reliance on language priors, the **BLANKTOKENS** strategy is introduced. This technique involves strategically masking portions of the input text during training. Specifically, the first `N` text tokens (e.g., `N=5`) are consistently blanked out, compelling the model to initiate its response based primarily on visual information. Additionally, a fraction (e.g., 20%) of subsequent input tokens are randomly masked. This disruption of straightforward language-based auto-completion forces the model to depend more heavily on its visual comprehension capabilities to generate coherent and visually grounded outputs throughout the sequence.

#### 3.4. Disentangling Visual Representation and Autoregressive Prediction

The authors identify an inherent tension when a single set of Transformer weights attempts to optimize for two conflicting objectives: causal language modeling for text (autoregressive) and rich spatial representation learning for images (non-autoregressive). To resolve this, they introduce a **key architectural modification: decoupling the processing of image and text tokens by assigning them independent sets of weights** within the LLM backbone, inspired by Mixture-of-Transformers. This creates specialized pathways where image tokens can optimize for non-autoregressive spatial representations, while text tokens can retain their autoregressive capabilities for fluent language generation. Crucially, this disentanglement maintains deployment efficiency, as only the pathway corresponding to the token's modality is activated at inference time, incurring no additional computational overhead.

#### 3.5. Targeted Synthetic Data Generation

To further encourage spatial reasoning and fine-grained visual attribute identification, a **specialized synthetic dataset** is created. This dataset consists of images programmatically generated as grids, where real-world object instances (with segmentation masks from Open-Images-v7) are placed at distinct locations. For each scene, questions focusing on object identification ("What are the objects?"), relative direction ("In which direction is...?"), and simple distance ("What is the distance...?") are automatically formulated. The ground truth answers are derived directly from the programmed spatial layout, ensuring that correct answers require accurate visual parsing and spatial reasoning, thereby inherently resisting solutions based purely on language priors.

The training setup builds upon the LLaVA framework with a Llama 3.1 8B backbone and CLIP as the base vision encoder, following a three-stage procedure: feature alignment, end-to-end continued pretraining (where VISUALLOSS and BLANKTOKENS are applied), and end-to-end instruction fine-tuning (incorporating synthetic data).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of PERCEPTLLM is demonstrated through both upstream analysis and evaluation on a challenging visual benchmark, showcasing consistent improvements as the proposed techniques are incrementally introduced.

#### 4.1. Upstream Analysis (SpatialMM Dataset)

An upstream analysis was conducted on the SpatialMM dataset, which is specifically designed with text heavily dependent on visual content. Table 1 presents the next-token prediction (NTP) loss:

| Technique | NTP |
|:------------------------ |:----- |
| Baseline | 3.59 |
| + VISUALLOSS | 3.54 |
| + BLANKTOKENS | 3.48 |
| + ind. weights | 3.20 |
| + AIMv2 encoder (PERCEPTLLM) | 2.96 |
| + VISUALLOSSADV (AIM-Radio) | 2.94 |

The results show a consistent decrease in cross-entropy loss with each added technique, indicating improved next-token prediction capability, especially for visually relevant tokens. This suggests that the model is better integrating visual information during language generation. Qualitative visualizations further confirm lower loss values for tokens referencing visual objects, attributes, or relationships.

#### 4.2. Evaluation on SpatialEval Benchmark

The primary evaluation was performed on the SpatialEval benchmark, designed to probe spatial understanding capabilities. Table 2 summarizes the accuracy across different subsets (Grid, MazeNav, Map, Real):

| Technique | Grid | MazeNav | Map | Real | Avg. |
|:----------------------------- |:--- |:------ |:--- |:--- |:--- |
| Baseline | 34.5 | 15.1 | 44.1 | 50.4 | 1 |
| + VISUALLOSS | 31.3 | 17.0 | 50.7 | 56.3 | 1.075|
| + BLANKTOKENS | 30.1 | 22.9 | 47.1 | 52.6 | 1.125|
| + Synthetic | 41.1 | 26.3 | 55.2 | 53.3 | 1.31 |
| + VISUALLOSSADV (AM-Radio) | 43.5 | 26.7 | 64.1 | 53.3 | 1.385|
| **PERCEPTLLM (full model)** | **50.8**| **35.0**| **55.13**| **52.6**| **1.52**|
| Llama 3.2 11B | 50.1 | 28.1 | 48.2 | 53.3 | 1.365|

Key findings from the SpatialEval benchmark:
- **VISUALLOSS Impact:** The addition of VISUALLOSS significantly improves performance, particularly on SpatialMap and SpatialReal, attributed to strengthened visual content understanding.
- **BLANKTOKENS Effectiveness:** While its direct impact on raw scores can vary per task, its role in reducing reliance on language priors is crucial and contributes to the overall system’s gains.
- **Synthetic Data Contribution:** The targeted synthetic data provides consistent improvements across all subsets, demonstrating its effectiveness in enhancing visual grounding even with minimal overlap with benchmark tasks. It helps narrow the performance gap with larger models like Llama 3.2 11B despite PERCEPTLLM being smaller (8B vs 11B) and using lower input resolution (224x224 vs 560x560).
- **Auxiliary Encoder Advancement:** Replacing I-JEPA with the AM-Radio encoder as the auxiliary vision encoder (VISUALLOSSADV) yields an additional gain of approximately 9.1 percentage points on SpatialMap, matching the performance of Llama 3.2 11B on this specific task.
- **PERCEPTLLM Superiority:** The final model, PERCEPTLLM (integrating all proposed enhancements: auxiliary visual loss, input masking, disentangled architecture, synthetic data, and the advanced AIM-v2 image encoder), achieves the strongest results. It significantly outperforms the larger Llama 3.2 11B model, achieving a 1.52 average normalized accuracy improvement compared to the baseline, while being more efficient in terms of model size, architecture, and input resolution. The disentangled architecture (independent weights) is particularly highlighted in the upstream analysis as yielding a substantial drop in NTP loss from 3.48 to 3.20.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "Perceiving Beyond Language Priors" holds significant implications for the development of more robust, reliable, and intelligent MLLMs. By directly addressing the critical issue of weak visual grounding and over-reliance on language priors, the authors pave the way for MLLMs that are less prone to "hallucinations" and more capable of genuine visual comprehension.

The key significance points are:
- **Enhanced Visual Grounding:** PERCEPTLLM demonstrates a fundamental improvement in how MLLMs process and integrate visual information, moving beyond superficial cues to deeper, fine-grained understanding. This directly mitigates the problem of MLLMs generating plausible but factually incorrect responses.
- **Scalable Path for MLLM Evolution:** The approach offers a scalable method to leverage advancements in image encoders. By using an auxiliary loss to transfer rich visual representations from powerful vision models into the LLM's latent space, the framework allows MLLMs to inherently benefit from state-of-the-art vision research without requiring complex architectural redesigns or additional inference-time overhead.
- **Efficient Architectural Innovation:** The disentangled architecture, which assigns independent weights for vision and text pathways, is a clever solution to a fundamental training conflict. Its ability to significantly boost performance without incurring additional computational or memory access overhead during inference is a crucial practical advantage, especially for deployment on specialized hardware platforms like those developed by Cerebras. This makes the approach highly attractive for real-world applications where efficiency is paramount.
- **Targeted Training for Core Capabilities:** The introduction of BLANKTOKENS and a specialized synthetic dataset provides effective mechanisms to explicitly force models to rely on visual input and practice spatial reasoning. This targeted training helps instill foundational visual intelligence, making the models more robust to challenging visual scenarios.
- **Superior Performance with Efficiency:** PERCEPTLLM’s ability to outperform larger, more complex models (e.g., Llama 3.2 11B) with a smaller backbone and lower input resolution highlights its efficiency and effectiveness. This suggests that the quality of multimodal integration can be more impactful than simply scaling model size or input resolution.

**Potential Impact:**
The advancements proposed in this paper could lead to:
- **More Trustworthy AI Systems:** MLLMs that exhibit fewer hallucinations and provide more accurate, visually grounded responses would be more reliable for critical applications in fields like medical imaging analysis, autonomous driving, and detailed content creation.
- **Improved Human-AI Interaction:** More visually intelligent MLLMs could better understand and respond to nuanced visual queries, enhancing user experience in multimodal assistants, accessibility tools, and educational platforms.
- **New Research Avenues:** The concept of disentangled processing pathways for modalities and the self-supervised transfer of visual knowledge open new research directions in multimodal architecture design and training strategies. The authors themselves suggest future work on optimizing data mixtures and conducting ablation studies to further fine-tune PERCEPTLLM.

In conclusion, "Perceiving Beyond Language Priors" presents a comprehensive and impactful solution to a fundamental challenge in MLLMs. By focusing on enhancing the intrinsic visual understanding and attention mechanisms of the LLM backbone, the work pushes the boundaries towards truly intelligent multimodal AI.
