# Neural Material Adaptor for Visual Grounding of Intrinsic Dynamics

> [!summary] Summary
> NeuMA introduces a learning-based framework that integrates established physical models with neural adaptors to accurately infer intrinsic dynamics from visual observations. It achieves superior accuracy in object dynamics grounding (average Chamfer distance of 1.31) and high-fidelity dynamic scene rendering by using a differentiable Material Point Method simulator and a novel Particle-GS renderer.

## Problem

- AI systems struggle with visual grounding of intrinsic dynamics, failing to interpret and reason about physical world dynamics from visual observations, a capability effortless for humans.
- Black-box neural network methods for dynamics lack physical constraints, leading to physically implausible motions and limited generalization.
- White-box physical simulators, while interpretable, rely on expert-defined equations that may not perfectly capture the complex, nuanced dynamics observed in real-world materials.

## Method

- **NeuMA** models material dynamics as a residual adaptation, combining expert-designed physical models (M0M_0M0) with a learnable neural adaptor (ΔMθΔ M_\thetaΔMθ) to enhance accuracy.
- It utilizes a differentiable ==Material Point Method== (==MPM==) simulator for handling complex material behaviors and allows for end-to-end optimization through back-propagation.
- A novel differentiable renderer, Particle-GS, robustly connects particle-based physics simulations with visual observations by dynamically deforming Gaussian kernels based on simulated particle states.

## Results

- NeuMA significantly reduced Chamfer distance for object dynamics grounding (average **1.31**) compared to baselines like PAC-NeRF (**114.80**) and NCLaw (**12.18**), indicating superior accuracy in motion inference.
- The framework achieved higher-fidelity dynamic scene rendering, outperforming baselines in visual quality metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) on synthetic and real-world datasets.
- NeuMA demonstrated strong generalization capabilities to novel object shapes and multi-object interactions, and accurately predicted future dynamics for durations three times longer than observed sequences.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Representing material models as a residual correction to established physical priors allows for accurate data-driven adaptation while preserving physical interpretability and generalizability.
> - A robust hierarchical particle binding mechanism effectively bridges the gap between discrete simulation particles and continuous rendering primitives (Gaussian kernels), ensuring consistent deformation and visual grounding.
> - End-to-end training with visual supervision, enabled by differentiable simulation and rendering, can effectively infer complex intrinsic dynamics without explicit particle-level ground truth supervision.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a fundamental challenge in artificial intelligence: enabling machines to interpret and reason about the physical world's dynamics from visual observations, a capability termed visual grounding of intrinsic dynamics. Humans effortlessly perform this task, discerning material properties and predicting object interactions from simple observations. However, modern AI systems generally struggle with this cognitive ability.

Existing approaches to visual grounding of dynamics broadly fall into two categories:
- **Black-box methods:** These use pure neural networks to directly model dynamic transitions. While flexible, they often lack physical constraints, leading to potential violations of physical laws and limited generalization capabilities. Examples include approaches that directly learn motion transitions without explicit physics (e.g.,).
- **White-box methods:** These leverage traditional physical simulators, such as those based on the Material Point Method (MPM), which approximate object dynamics using partial-differential equations. These methods typically back-propagate pixel differences to estimate physical coefficients (e.g., Young’s modulus). While offering interpretability and generalizability, their reliance on expert-defined equations means they may not perfectly capture the *actual* complex dynamics observed in the real world (e.g.,).

The presented work, NeuMA, positions itself as a hybrid approach that seeks to bridge the limitations of both black-box and white-box methods. It aims to integrate established physical laws with data-driven learned corrections. By doing so, NeuMA intends to achieve the accuracy and flexibility needed to capture actual intrinsic dynamics, while simultaneously retaining the generalizability and interpretability afforded by physical priors. This approach contributes to the field of intuitive physics, differentiable simulation, and differentiable rendering, aiming to enhance AI's ability to understand and predict dynamic scenes.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observed disparity between human and AI capabilities in understanding the physical world. Humans, even children, can infer an object's intrinsic dynamics, adapt to new scenarios, and predict interactions based on visual cues. Modern AI systems, however, often fail to replicate this visual grounding ability.

The specific limitations of current AI methodologies further motivate this work:
- **Inaccuracy and Lack of Generalization in Black-Box Models:** Purely neural network-based simulators, without explicit physical constraints, tend to produce physically implausible motions and struggle to generalize to unseen scenarios or conditions due to deep coupling between extrinsic (e.g., geometry) and intrinsic (e.g., physical motion) attributes.
- **Imperfection of Expert-Designed Models in White-Box Methods:** Traditional physical simulators rely on expert-defined equations, which, while physically grounded, may not perfectly align with the complex and nuanced dynamics observed in real-world materials and interactions. This "gap" between theoretical models ($M_0$) and actual dynamics ($\Delta M$) prevents accurate inference.

Given these challenges, the core question addressed by this research is: **"How can actual intrinsic dynamics be accurately inferred from visual observations?"**

To answer this, the key objectives of the NeuMA framework are:
1. **To develop a learning-based model (NeuMA) that formulates the learning of intrinsic dynamics as a residual adaptation paradigm.** This involves starting with widely accepted expert-designed physical models ($M_0$) and learning a correction term ($\Delta M$) that grounds these models to observed images. The goal is to model actual intrinsic dynamics more accurately and flexibly than white-box methods, while maintaining the interpretability and generalizability of physical priors.
2. **To preserve the generalizability and physical interpretability of established physical laws.** By building upon existing physical models, NeuMA aims to prevent the "black box" nature of pure neural networks and ensure that learned dynamics remain physically plausible and understandable.
3. **To create a differentiable rendering mechanism (Particle-GS) capable of effectively bridging the gap between particle-based physics simulations and visual image observations.** This rendering component is essential for enabling the back-propagation of image gradients, which are used to optimize the underlying physical simulator and material parameters based on pixel-level supervision from videos.

In essence, the overarching goal is to equip AI systems with a robust, accurate, and interpretable mechanism for visual grounding of intrinsic dynamics, thereby advancing machine understanding and reasoning about the physical world.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

NeuMA (Neural Material Adaptor) is a learning-based framework designed for visually grounding intrinsic dynamics from sequences of visual observations ($I = \{I_1, I_2, \dots, I_T\}$). Its core methodology integrates existing physical laws with learned corrections, combining a differentiable simulator with a novel differentiable renderer.

The framework describes dynamic systems using the elastodynamic equation, where material models $M$ (parameterized by $\theta$) define the stress response. The dynamic system transitions from state $s_t$ to $s_{t+1}$ via a transition model $S(s_t; M_\theta)$. The optimal material parameters $\theta^*$ are learned by minimizing the visual difference ($L_v$) between rendered images ($\hat{I}_t = R(s_t; K_t, P_t)$) and ground-truth observations ($I_t$).

**Neural Material Adaptor (NeuMA) Formulation:**
The central concept of NeuMA is to implicitly represent the material model $M_\theta$ as a residual term added to widely accepted physical priors $M_0$:
$M_\theta:= M_0 + \Delta M_\theta$
- **$M_0$ (Expert-designed Physical Models):** These are fixed, pre-optimized material models (e.g., from NCLaw) that encode existing physical priors and constraints for elastic and plastic materials.
- **$\Delta M_\theta$ (Neural Adaptor):** This is the learnable correction term, implemented using Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA). LoRA is chosen for its efficiency in adaptation and its ability to preserve the underlying prior $M_0$, preventing drastic deviations from known physics.

The entire material model $M_\theta$ is embedded into a differentiable simulator $S$, which is instantiated as the **Material Point Method (MPM)**. MPM is selected for its capability to handle complex motion simulation across various materials and its suitability for back-propagating gradients during end-to-end training.

**Overall Pipeline (Three Stages):**

1. **Initial State Acquisition:**
 - **3D Gaussian Kernels Reconstruction:** From initial multi-view observations $I_0$, foreground objects are separated using image matting. Standard 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) is then used to reconstruct the object as a set of Gaussian kernels $K^{(i)} = \{x^{(i)}, \alpha^{(i)}, A^{(i)}, c^{(i)}\}$, representing center, opacity, covariance matrix, and spherical harmonic coefficients.
 - **Initial Particle State:** A surface mesh is explicitly reconstructed from the object's 3D representation. Initial physical particle positions ($p_0$) are obtained by uniformly sampling points within this mesh volume. Initial velocities ($v_0$) are acquired by optimizing the visual difference loss $L_v$ over the first few frames. Initial elastic deformation gradients ($F^e_0$) are set to identity matrices. Particle mass is derived from a constant density and estimated particle volume.
 - **Particle-GS and Particle Binding:** A novel component called Particle-GS is introduced to establish a robust hierarchical relationship between Gaussian kernels (for rendering) and simulation particles (for physics). Instead of a direct one-to-one mapping, a sparse binding matrix $B$ is created. Each Gaussian kernel $K^{(i)}$ is bound to nearby particles based on their Mahalanobis distance. A confidence threshold and chi-squared test determine binding. This mechanism aims to make the visual grounding robust to potentially uneven Gaussian kernel distributions from 3DGS, and to enforce relatively uniform physical states among particles associated with the same object part, facilitating realistic simulation.

2. **Physics Simulation:**
 - Given the initial particle state $s_0$, the MPM-based differentiable simulator $S$ performs time integration.
 - Within each time step, the material model $M_\theta$ (composed of $M_0 + \Delta M_\theta$) is applied:
 1. **Stress Computation:** The first Piolar-Kirchhoff stress $P_t$ is computed using the elastic material model $M^e_\theta(F^e_t)$.
 2. **MPM Time Integration:** Updated positions ($p_{t+1}$), velocities ($v_{t+1}$), and trial elastic deformation gradients ($F^{e,trial}_{t+1}$) are obtained through the MPM algorithm's time integration scheme (detailed in Appendix E.6).
 3. **Plasticity Post-processing:** The trial elastic deformation gradient is projected back to an admissible elastic region using the plastic material model $M^p_\theta(F^{e,trial}_{t+1})$.

3. **Dynamic Scene Rendering:**
 - Particle-GS extends static Gaussian kernels to be time-dependent ($K_t^{(i)} = \{x_t^{(i)}, \alpha^{(i)}, A_t^{(i)}, c^{(i)}\}$), with opacity and spherical harmonics assumed constant over time.
 - **Kernel Deformation:** Gaussian kernel centers ($x_{t+1}$) and covariance matrices ($A_{t+1}$) are deformed based on the binding matrix $B$ (computed in Stage I) and the updated particle states from the simulator $S$.
 - Center update: $\Delta x_{t+1} = B \times (p_{t+1} - p_t)$, then $x_{t+1} = x_t + \Delta x_{t+1}$.
 - Covariance update: $\bar{F}^e_{t+1} = B \times F^e_{t+1}$, then $A_{t+1} = \bar{F}^e_{t+1} A_0 (\bar{F}^e_{t+1})^\top$.
 - The deformed Gaussian kernels are then splatted onto the 2D image plane to synthesize the predicted image $\hat{I}_{t+1}$.

**Training Details:**
NeuMA is trained end-to-end using the rendering loss $L_v$. An RAdam optimizer with a cosine learning rate scheduler is used for 1,000 iterations per scene. Physical priors (frame indifference, undeformed state equilibrium) are explicitly regularized. The Low-Rank Adaptation component $\Delta M_\theta$ typically uses a rank of 16.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates NeuMA across synthetic and real-world datasets, using metrics for object dynamics grounding (Chamfer distance) and dynamic scene rendering (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS).

**Performance on Object Dynamics Grounding:**
- **Superior Accuracy:** NeuMA consistently achieved lower Chamfer distances across synthetic benchmarks compared to baselines like PAC-NeRF and NCLaw. On average, NeuMA yielded a Chamfer distance of 1.31, significantly outperforming PAC-NeRF (114.80) and NCLaw (12.18). This improvement is attributed to the learnable residual term $\Delta M_\theta$, which corrects physical priors.
- **Effectiveness of Visual Supervision:** In some instances, NeuMA, trained solely with visual supervision, outperformed its variant trained with ground-truth particle supervision (NeuMA w/ P.S., average Chamfer 1.51). This suggests the model's ability to infer dynamics from visual observations can match or exceed direct particle-level tuning in specific contexts, possibly due to the regularization provided by the physical priors.
- **Importance of Particle Binding:** Ablation studies demonstrated that the proposed particle binding mechanism (NeuMA w/o Bind, average Chamfer 6.60) is crucial for robust performance. It mitigates issues arising from uneven Gaussian kernel distribution in 3DGS, ensuring consistent physical states within object parts.
- **Validation of Residual Adaptation:** Directly training $M_0$ without the residual term ($\Delta M_\theta$) resulted in poorer performance (NeuMA w/o $\Delta M_\theta$, average Chamfer 1.79), validating that the residual adaptation paradigm is more effective than directly finetuning pre-trained models.
- **Consistent Performance Over Time:** The Chamfer distance for NeuMA remained consistently low throughout the simulation sequence, indicating accurate learning of intrinsic dynamics over extended periods.

**Performance on Dynamic Scene Rendering:**
- **High-Fidelity Rendering:** NeuMA generally outperformed all baselines in visual quality metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) on synthetic data, producing high-fidelity and physically plausible image sequences even from single-view input.
- **Handling Complex Geometries:** Unlike PAC-NeRF, which produced blurry artifacts on intricate geometries, NeuMA demonstrated robust performance. Combining NCLaw with Particle-GS showed improved visual quality but failed to capture accurate material motion, highlighting the necessity of NeuMA's dynamics grounding.
- **Real-World Applicability:** Evaluations on real-world data showed NeuMA's rendered sequences to be more aligned with observations compared to Spring-Gaus, confirming its effectiveness in practical scenarios.

**Experimental Analysis:**
- **Dynamics Interpolation:** NeuMA demonstrated the ability to smoothly interpolate between dynamics dictated by initial material priors ($M_0$) and those derived from visual observations by adjusting the weight of the $\Delta M_\theta$ term. This indicates the flexibility and control offered by the residual design.
- **Dynamics Generalization:**
 - **Novel Objects:** Trained NeuMA models successfully generalized to new object shapes (e.g., letters of "NeurIPS"), exhibiting similar learned motion patterns.
 - **Multi-Object Interactions:** Multiple instances of trained NeuMA models could interact seamlessly and generate physically plausible dynamics in multi-object collision scenarios. This generalization capacity is attributed to the refinement of expert-designed $M_0$, which preserves physical interpretability.
- **Dynamics Prediction:** NeuMA was able to predict future dynamics accurately for a duration three times longer than the observed sequence, highlighting its potential for long-term dynamic video generation.
- **Prior Correction Analysis:** Gradient norm analysis showed that NeuMA adaptively adjusted the elastic or plastic material models based on the observed dynamics. For instance, in "BouncyBall", the elastic model saw more significant changes, while in "RubberPawn", the plastic model was adjusted more. This provides interpretability into how NeuMA corrects physical priors to align with visual data.
- **Uneven Mass Distribution:** NeuMA demonstrated the capability to handle objects with uneven mass distributions (e.g., a ClayCat with varying densities), achieving comparable grounding accuracy.
- **Robustness to Inaccurate Priors:** NeuMA performed well with moderate inaccuracies in the initial physical prior ($M_0$) but showed significant performance degradation when the prior was fundamentally incorrect for the material.
- **Ablation on $\Delta M_\theta$ Rank:** A rank of 16 was found to be optimal for $\Delta M_\theta$, balancing fitting capacity and avoiding saturation.
- **Compatibility with Image-to-3D Models:** NeuMA successfully drove 3D models generated from single images by image-to-3D methods, producing physically plausible 4D dynamic sequences, showcasing its potential for advanced content generation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

NeuMA represents a notable advancement in the field of AI's understanding and reasoning of physical dynamics from visual data. Its significance and potential impact stem from its core methodological contributions and demonstrated capabilities:

- **Bridging the Gap between Physics-based and Data-driven Models:** NeuMA successfully integrates the interpretability and generalizability of traditional, white-box physical simulators with the adaptability and accuracy of modern, data-driven neural networks. This hybrid approach mitigates the limitations of both pure neural models (lack of physical adherence) and purely analytical models (inability to capture complex, real-world dynamics not perfectly described by equations). This paradigm offers a more robust and physically consistent framework for learning dynamics.

- **Enhanced Accuracy and Generalization:** The extensive experiments demonstrate that NeuMA achieves superior accuracy in visually grounding intrinsic dynamics and generates higher-fidelity dynamic scene renderings compared to prior methods. Crucially, its ability to generalize to novel object shapes, multi-object interactions, and predict dynamics over extended periods indicates that it learns underlying physical principles rather than merely memorizing observed motions. This generalization is a critical step towards more intelligent and adaptable AI systems.

- **Interpretability through Residual Adaptation:** The design of $\Delta M_\theta$ as a residual correction to expert-designed physical models ($M_0$) provides a degree of interpretability. By analyzing which components of the material model are most significantly adjusted, researchers can gain insights into the discrepancies between theoretical physics and observed reality, and how the model learns to compensate.

- **Potential for Advanced 4D Content Creation:** The demonstrated compatibility of NeuMA with state-of-the-art image-to-3D generative models opens avenues for physically plausible 4D (3D + time) content generation from minimal inputs, such as a single image. This has significant implications for industries like digital entertainment, virtual reality, architectural design, and product prototyping, enabling users to generate dynamic simulations of objects with realistic physical behaviors.

- **Advancement in Robotics and Simulation:** An accurate visual grounding of intrinsic dynamics is fundamental for robotic systems that need to interact with deformable objects or operate in dynamic environments. NeuMA's contributions could lead to more intelligent robots capable of predicting and adapting to the physical properties of objects they encounter, improving manipulation, planning, and control in complex real-world scenarios.

Despite its strengths, the paper also acknowledges limitations, such as the reliance on multi-view surface reconstruction for initial particles (requiring calibrated cameras), the challenge of cumulative errors in long-term predictions, and susceptibility to motion blur. Addressing these aspects represents clear directions for future research. However, NeuMA's novel paradigm for learning and adapting physical models from visual observation marks a significant step towards more sophisticated and human-like machine understanding of the physical world.
