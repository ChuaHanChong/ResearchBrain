# VLM2Vec-V2: Advancing Multimodal Embedding for Videos, Images, and Visual Documents

> [!summary] Summary
> VLM2Vec-V2, developed by researchers from Salesforce Research and collaborating universities, introduces a unified multimodal embedding model capable of processing and aligning videos, images, and visual documents with text. The model achieves the highest overall score of 58.0 on the newly introduced MMEB-V2 benchmark, which expands evaluation to 78 tasks across these diverse visual modalities.

## Problem

- Existing state-of-the-art multimodal embedding models predominantly focus on natural images and text, limiting their applicability to real-world scenarios involving diverse visual content.
- There is a lack of unified support for dynamic content like videos and structured visual forms such as documents (PDFs, slides) within a single multimodal embedding framework.
- A comprehensive and standardized benchmark was missing to effectively evaluate embedding models across images, videos, and visual documents.

## Method

- The paper introduces ==VLM2Vec-V2==, a unified multimodal embedding model trained using an ==instruction-guided contrastive learning== approach on the Qwen2-VL backbone.
- A new comprehensive benchmark, ==MMEB-V2==, is developed with 9 meta-tasks and 78 specific tasks to evaluate multimodal embeddings across text, images, videos, and visual documents.
- Sophisticated data sampling strategies, including on-the-fly batch mixing and ==interleaved sub-batching==, are employed during multi-task training to handle heterogeneous data sources.

## Results

- VLM2Vec-V2 achieves the highest overall average score of **58.0** across all 78 datasets in the MMEB-V2 benchmark, outperforming existing generalist models like GME and previous VLM2Vec versions.
- It demonstrates strong performance on image tasks (average **64.9**), competitive results on video tasks (average **34.9**), and significantly improved performance on visual document retrieval (**65.4**) compared to other generalist VLMs.
- Ablation studies confirm that training with all three modalities (images, videos, visual documents) consistently leads to the best overall performance and that increased training steps further improve results.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Comprehensive multi-modal training across images, videos, and visual documents is crucial for achieving broad generalization in embedding models.
> - Instruction-guided contrastive learning with a robust Vision-Language Model backbone (Qwen2-VL) enables effective alignment of diverse visual and textual data into a unified embedding space.
> - Data sampling strategies like interleaved sub-batching and specific LoRA ranks are important for optimizing performance across heterogeneous modalities during training.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper "VLM2Vec-V2" addresses a critical limitation in the rapidly evolving field of multimodal embedding models. These models are fundamental for enabling various downstream AI applications, including semantic similarity search, information retrieval, clustering, and retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), by encoding diverse data modalities into a shared, dense representation space.

**Current Landscape and Gaps:**
Existing state-of-the-art multimodal embedding models, such as the original VLM2Vec, E5-V, and GME, have predominantly focused on natural images and text. They are typically trained and evaluated on datasets like MMEB (Jiang et al., 2024) and M-BEIR (Wei et al., 2023), which primarily derive visual content from sources like MSCOCO, Flickr, and ImageNet. While these models have shown remarkable success in tasks involving natural images, their applicability is severely constrained in real-world scenarios that demand understanding a broader spectrum of visual information. This includes dynamic content like videos and structured visual forms such as documents, PDFs, websites, and slides. The current lack of unified support for these diverse visual forms limits their utility in emerging applications like sophisticated AI agents, comprehensive multi-modal search and recommendation systems, and advanced RAG systems that need to process various document types.

**Contribution to the Landscape:**
VLM2Vec-V2 directly tackles this gap by extending the capabilities of multimodal embeddings beyond natural images to encompass videos and visual documents.
1. **Unified Multimodal Embedding:** It proposes a unified framework that learns embeddings across these diverse visual forms, moving towards a truly general-purpose multimodal understanding. This is a significant step towards models that can handle "any visual input" rather than being specialized.
2. **Comprehensive Benchmarking:** Crucially, the work introduces MMEB-V2, a comprehensive benchmark that significantly expands upon prior efforts. By integrating new task types focused on videos (retrieval, moment grounding, classification, QA) and visual documents (retrieval), MMEB-V2 provides a much-needed standardized framework for training and evaluating multimodal embedding models on a wider, more realistic range of visual data. This benchmark will likely become a new standard, driving future research in this direction.
3. **Instruction Tuning and VLMs:** The work aligns with the broader trend of leveraging large foundation models, particularly Vision-Language Models (VLMs), and instruction tuning. Just as instruction tuning has revolutionized text embeddings and VLMs for general-purpose understanding, VLM2Vec-V2 demonstrates how these techniques can be adapted to create highly effective general-purpose multimodal embedding models.
In essence, VLM2Vec-V2 pushes the boundary of multimodal representation learning by addressing the critical challenge of unifying static images, dynamic videos, and structured visual documents within a single embedding space, thus expanding the practical applicability of AI in real-world, heterogeneous data environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind VLM2Vec-V2 stems from the recognition that existing multimodal embedding models are narrowly focused, primarily on natural images, which severely limits their utility in practical, real-world applications. The paper identifies two key objectives to address this limitation:

1. **Develop a Unified Multimodal Embedding Framework:** The primary objective is to create a single, general-purpose embedding model that can effectively process and generate unified representations for diverse visual forms, including natural images, videos, and visual documents, alongside text inputs. This unified representation is crucial for enabling applications such as AI agents that interact with various data types, multi-modal search and recommendation systems that span media, and retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) that can leverage information from visual documents.
2. **Construct a Comprehensive Evaluation Benchmark:** To accurately assess the performance and generalizability of such a unified model, a robust and comprehensive benchmark is essential. Therefore, a secondary but equally critical objective is to introduce MMEB-V2, an extended version of the existing MMEB benchmark, designed to evaluate embedding models across all targeted modalities and a wider array of tasks.

The authors explicitly articulate the research questions they aim to investigate:
- How well can a multimodal embedding generalize across diverse visual modalities (images, videos, visual documents)?
- What are the key ingredients and effective strategies for training robust and versatile multimodal embedding models that can handle such diversity?
- What are the specific challenges involved in representing temporal information inherent in videos and structured information found in visual documents within a unified embedding space?

By pursuing these objectives, the authors seek to close the applicability gap of current multimodal embeddings and lay the groundwork for more scalable and adaptable representation learning in both research and real-world settings.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VLM2Vec-V2's methodology centers on two main pillars: the development of a comprehensive benchmark (MMEB-V2) and the training of a unified embedding model (VLM2Vec-V2) using an instruction-guided contrastive learning approach.

#### 3.1. MMEB-V2: Expanding the Benchmark

MMEB-V2 is introduced as a significant expansion of the original MMEB, specifically designed to evaluate multimodal embedding models across text, image, video, and visual document modalities.
- **Structure:** It comprises 9 meta-tasks and a total of 78 specific tasks. Building upon the original MMEB's five image- and text-based task categories, MMEB-V2 introduces **five new meta-tasks**:
 - **Video Retrieval (V-RET):** Text/Video query to retrieve corresponding video.
 - **Moment Retrieval (M-RET):** Text (+ optional full video) query to retrieve specific temporal segments within a video.
 - **Video Classification (V-CLS):** Video query to predict a class label.
 - **Video QA (V-QA):** Textual question + video query to select the correct answer from options.
 - **Visual Document Retrieval (VisDoc):** Natural language query to retrieve structured visual documents (e.g., multi-page PDFs, slide decks). This category includes 5 datasets, encompassing 24 tasks, covering a broad range of document types and use cases (e.g., charts, slides).
- **Input Formatting:** For video-based tasks, videos are represented as uniformly sampled sequences of frames. Instructions play a crucial role in specifying the model's objective (e.g., "Recognize the category of the video contents."). Query texts can be questions, descriptions, or commands.
- **Data Sourcing and Practicality:** The datasets within MMEB-V2 span diverse domains (sports, object recognition, daily life, movie clips) and sources (YouTube, professional productions, crowd-sourced content), ensuring diversity and real-world relevance. To ensure practicality for research, certain datasets are downsampled to allow the full benchmark to be run within a reasonable timeframe.

#### 3.2. VLM2Vec-V2: Unified Embedding Model Training

The VLM2Vec-V2 model is designed to align data from different modalities into a shared embedding space, guided by natural language instructions.

- **Backbone Model:** The chosen backbone is **Qwen2-VL (Wang et al., 2024b)**. This VLM is selected for its key capabilities:
 - **Naive Dynamic Resolution:** Efficiently processes variable resolution inputs.
 - **Multimodal Rotary Position Embedding (M-RoPE):** Captures spatial and temporal structure across modalities.
 - **Unified Architecture:** Integrates 2D and 3D convolutions for consistent image and video understanding, critical for handling both static and temporal visual data.
- **Learning Paradigm:** **Contrastive Learning** is adopted to adapt the pretrained VLM into an embedding model. The standard InfoNCE loss (Oord et al., 2018) is used, leveraging in-batch negatives and hard negatives to maximize similarity between query-target pairs and dissimilarity with negative samples. The matching score uses temperature-scaled cosine similarity.
- **Multi-modal Data Formatting:** A standardized instruction-conditioned format is used for all query-target pairs (q, t+).
 - Queries are formatted as `[VISUAL TOKEN] Instruct: {task instruction} \n Query: {q}`, where `[VISUAL TOKEN]` (`<|image pad|>` or `<|video pad|>`) indicates the modality.
 - Targets are optionally formatted as `[VISUAL TOKEN] {target instruction}`.
 - This unified formatting, combined with explicit task instructions, helps the model contextualize and generalize across heterogeneous tasks and modalities.
- **Data Sampling Strategies:** To manage heterogeneous data sources during multi-task training:
 - **On-the-fly Batch Mixing:** A pre-defined sampling weight table controls the relative probabilities of sampling from each dataset, ensuring balanced exposure and preventing overfitting.
 - **Interleaved Sub-batching:** Each full batch (e.g., 1024 samples) is divided into smaller sub-batches (e.g., 64 samples), sampled independently. This increases intra-sub-batch homogeneity, making contrastive discrimination harder, while preserving cross-task diversity within the full batch, leading to more stable and robust optimization.
- **Training Data Curation:** A diverse training dataset is curated from three main sources:
 - **Video-language instruction data:** From LLaVA-Hound (300k video-caption pairs, 240k video QA pairs), formatted for video retrieval or video-to-text retrieval.
 - **Visual document retrieval data:** From ViDoRe (ColPali train set, 118k) and VisRAG (synthetic 239k, in-domain 123k).
 - **Image-text datasets:** From MMEB-train (Jiang et al., 2024), covering image QA, classification, retrieval, and visual grounding.
- **Training Setting:** VLM2Vec-V2 is trained using Qwen2-VL 2B, a batch size of 1,024 for 2K/5K steps, an interleaved sub-batch size of 64, and a loss temperature of 0.02. GradCache is used for large global batch sizes, and LoRA tuning (rank 16, alpha 32) enables parameter-efficient training on 8 H100 GPUs. Videos are represented by 8 uniformly sampled frames.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** Hit@1 is the primary metric for image and video tasks, while NDCG@5 is used for visual document tasks (consistent with prior work).
- **Baselines:** Performance is compared against VLM embedding models like GME, VLM2Vec (previous version), and LamRA, as well as modality-specialized models such as ColPali (for document retrieval).

This comprehensive methodology, spanning benchmark creation, model architecture choice, and sophisticated training strategies, enables VLM2Vec-V2 to achieve broad multimodal understanding.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate VLM2Vec-V2's strong performance and the efficacy of its unified training approach across diverse visual modalities.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance

- **Dominant Performance:** VLM2Vec-V2 (2B) achieves the highest overall average score of **58.0** across all 78 datasets in MMEB-V2. This outperforms all other strong baselines, including various sizes of GME, LamRA, and the previous VLM2Vec models, some of which use larger backbones (e.g., GME 7B, LamRA 7B, VLM2Vec 7B). This indicates that VLM2Vec-V2’s unified training strategy is highly effective and balanced.

#### 4.2. Performance Across Modalities

- **Image Tasks:** VLM2Vec-V2 demonstrates strong performance on image-based tasks, achieving an average of **64.9**. This is comparable to, and in some cases even surpasses, the performance of VLM2Vec-7B (65.5), despite VLM2Vec-V2 utilizing a smaller 2B parameter backbone. This highlights efficient knowledge transfer and adaptation for image understanding.
- **Video Tasks:** On video tasks, VLM2Vec-V2 achieves a competitive average of **34.9**. While this is not as high as its image performance, it is competitive with and often outperforms other VLM-based generalist models (e.g., VLM2Vec-7B at 34.0, GME-7B at 38.6), especially considering it was trained on a relatively smaller amount of video-specific data. This indicates promising generalization to temporal data.
- **Visual Document Retrieval (VisDoc):** For visual document retrieval, VLM2Vec-V2 achieves **65.4**. It significantly outperforms all other VLM2Vec variants (e.g., VLM2Vec-7B at 46.4) and generalist VLMs like GME (72.7) and LamRA (50.2). However, it still trails behind ColPali (71.0), a model specifically designed and optimized for visual document retrieval using a late interaction matching mechanism, suggesting room for further specialization in this domain.

#### 4.3. Ablation Analysis

The ablation studies provide valuable insights into the training strategies:

- **Generalization Across Modalities (Training Data Mix):**
 - Training solely on image data yielded the highest average performance for image tasks but struggled significantly on video and visual document tasks.
 - Combining two modalities (e.g., Image+Video or Image+VisDoc) showed noticeable gains, particularly for image benchmarks.
 - **Incorporating all three modalities (Image+Video+VisDoc) during training consistently led to the best overall performance (49.1 average) and specifically boosted performance on visual document tasks (52.2).** This strongly supports the hypothesis that comprehensive multi-modal training is crucial for broad generalization.
- **Data Sampling Strategies (Interleaved Sub-batching):**
 - Increasing the interleaved sub-batch size (IB) generally improved performance for VisDoc and Video modalities. This suggests that grouping similar samples within sub-batches, making contrastive discrimination harder, benefits learning for these complex modalities. The best performance for VisDoc and Video was achieved with larger sub-batch sizes (IB=128 or IB=1024, implying less mixing across diverse tasks within the *sub-batch* but still diverse *full batch*).
 - For the Image modality, an "inverted U-shaped" trend was observed, with optimal performance at an intermediate sub-batch size (IB=64). This implies that while some grouping helps, excessive homogeneity might hinder generalization for image tasks.
- **Model Settings (LoRA Rank and Training Steps):**
 - **LoRA Rank:** A LoRA rank of 16 yielded the best overall performance across all modalities. This indicates that a moderate number of tunable parameters in parameter-efficient fine-tuning is optimal for balancing performance across diverse modalities, with higher ranks (e.g., 32) not providing additional benefits.
 - **Training Steps:** Performance for all three modalities (Image, VisDoc, Video) consistently improved with increased training steps, up to 5K steps. Crucially, there was no clear sign of saturation, especially for VisDoc and Video, suggesting potential for further gains with extended training.

In summary, VLM2Vec-V2 sets a new benchmark for general-purpose multimodal embeddings. Its success is attributed to its unified training framework, leveraging a robust VLM backbone, instruction-guided contrastive learning, and a well-designed data sampling strategy that balances diversity and hardness during training.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "VLM2Vec-V2" holds significant implications for the fields of multimodal AI, representation learning, and real-world AI applications.

1. **Bridging Critical Modality Gaps:** The most immediate significance is its direct addressal of a critical limitation in existing multimodal embedding models. By extending support beyond natural images to include videos and visual documents, VLM2Vec-V2 unlocks the potential for these models to operate effectively in much broader, more realistic environments where information is multimodal and comes in diverse formats. This is crucial for truly general-purpose AI.

2. **Establishing a New Benchmark (MMEB-V2):** The introduction of MMEB-V2 is a foundational contribution. It provides the research community with a comprehensive, standardized, and challenging benchmark for systematically evaluating multimodal embedding models across static, temporal, and structured visual data. This benchmark will serve as a crucial tool for guiding future research, enabling fair comparisons, and pushing the boundaries of what multimodal models can achieve. Its breadth of tasks (78 across 9 meta-tasks) ensures a holistic assessment.

3. **Demonstrating Feasibility of Unified Embedding Learning:** VLM2Vec-V2 itself, as a strong baseline, demonstrates that learning a single, unified embedding space for text, images, videos, and visual documents is not only feasible but also highly effective. Its strong overall performance, especially when considering its relatively smaller size compared to some baselines, validates the instruction-guided contrastive learning approach and the choice of the Qwen2-VL backbone.

4. **Providing Insights into Effective Training Strategies:** The detailed ablation studies offer valuable insights into the "key ingredients" for training robust and versatile multimodal embedding models. Findings regarding the benefits of comprehensive multi-modal data mixing, the optimal effect of interleaved sub-batching for different modalities, and the performance curve related to LoRA ranks and training steps provide practical guidance for researchers and practitioners developing similar systems.

5. **Enabling Advanced Real-World Applications:** The advancements laid out by VLM2Vec-V2 have profound implications for various practical applications:
 - **AI Agents:** Future AI agents that need to interpret and respond to information from diverse sources (e.g., a user speaking to a video, analyzing a document, or discussing an image) will heavily rely on such unified embeddings.
 - **Multimodal Search and Recommendation:** The ability to retrieve and recommend content across text, images, videos, and documents from a single query (e.g., "Find videos and articles about quantum computing") will significantly enhance user experience in search engines and recommendation systems.
 - **Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG):** RAG systems, a cornerstone of current advanced AI, can become more powerful by leveraging visual documents (charts, slides, reports) and videos as knowledge sources, directly integrating their content into generative processes. This moves beyond traditional text-only RAG.

In conclusion, VLM2Vec-V2 is a significant step forward in making multimodal AI more generalized, practical, and scalable. By addressing critical gaps in both benchmarking and model capabilities, it lays robust groundwork for future research and development in universally applicable representation learning for a wide array of AI systems.
