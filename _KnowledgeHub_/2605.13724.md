# AnyFlow: Any-Step Video Diffusion Model with On-Policy Flow Map Distillation

> [!summary] Summary
> NVIDIA and National University of Singapore researchers developed AnyFlow, a two-stage distillation framework that enables video diffusion models to generate high-quality content across a flexible range of inference steps. This approach, which leverages flow maps and on-policy distillation, continuously improves video quality as sampling steps increase, achieving performance comparable to non-distilled models at significantly fewer NFEs and surpassing previous consistency-based methods.

## Problem

- Existing video diffusion models often have fixed inference step budgets, limiting user flexibility in balancing latency and output quality.
- Prior few-step distillation methods, such as consistency models, suffer from performance degradation when more sampling steps are used, failing to provide reliable "any-step" generation.
- Consistency distillation introduces cumulative bias by repeatedly re-noising intermediate states, causing sampling trajectories to diverge from the ideal Probability-Flow ODE path.

## Method

- A two-stage training pipeline converts a pretrained video diffusion model into an "any-step" flow map model, capable of learning transitions between arbitrary time points (z_t "to" z_r).
- The first stage, Forward Flow Map Training, adapts MeanFlow with interpolated timestep conditioning, a reweighted time sampler, guidance-fused training, and adaptive loss reweighting.
- The second stage, On-Policy Flow Map Distillation, refines the model using a novel Flow Map Backward Simulation strategy that efficiently decomposes rollout trajectories via shortcut transitions, mitigating test-time errors and exposure bias.

## Results

- AnyFlow demonstrates robust "any-step" scaling, with performance that matches or surpasses consistency models in the few-step regime and consistently improves with increased NFEs, unlike previous methods that show degradation.
- Achieved VBench scores of 84.04 (bidirectional T2V) and 84.05 (causal T2V) at 4 NFEs for 14B models, with quality further improving to 84.10 and 84.41 respectively at 32 NFEs.
- Qualitative and quantitative evaluations showed AnyFlow outperforming competing methods (e.g., rCM, Krea-Realtime, FastVideo) in T2V and I2V tasks, producing videos with higher visual quality, more realistic motion, and improved temporal stability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Shifting the distillation target from endpoint-focused consistency mapping (z_t 
"to" z_0) to generalized flow-map transition learning (z_t 
"to" z_r) inherently supports variable step sizes and adaptable inference budgets.
> - Flow Map Backward Simulation enables efficient distribution matching distillation by leveraging the composition property of flow maps, allowing the simulation of different inference step budgets with consistent computational cost during training.
> - AnyFlow preserves the instantaneous flow field of the base model, enabling continued fine-tuning on downstream datasets, a capability often lost in highly distilled models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Video diffusion models have demonstrated the capability to generate high-quality video content. A significant area of research within this field focuses on accelerating the inference process, particularly through diffusion distillation techniques. Consistency Models (CMs) represent a prominent approach in this area, enabling few-step video generation by learning a direct mapping from a noisy latent state (z_t) to a clean one (z_0) along the probability-flow ODE (PF-ODE) trajectory. Methods such as rCM for bidirectional models and Self-Forcing for causal models have built upon this foundation.

However, a noted limitation of consistency-based distillation methods is their performance degradation as the number of sampling steps (or Neural Function Evaluations, NFEs) increases during test time. This degradation constrains their utility for "any-step" generation, where users require flexible control over the trade-off between latency (fewer steps) and output fidelity (more steps). The paper posits that this issue arises because consistency distillation replaces the original PF-ODE trajectory with a consistency-sampling trajectory, which involves repeatedly re-noising intermediate states and introduces cumulative bias, causing trajectories to drift away from the target path.

AnyFlow addresses this limitation by introducing a framework based on flow map models. Flow maps offer a generalized perspective on diffusion by learning transition operators between arbitrary time points (z_t → z_r) rather than only to the endpoint (z_t → z_0). While flow map models (e.g., MeanFlow) have been explored, their application to scalable any-step video diffusion, particularly in conjunction with efficient on-policy distillation, represents an active area of investigation. This work distinguishes itself from concurrent efforts, such as Transition Matching Distillation (TMD), by focusing on flow-trajectory decomposition for rollout efficiency, enabling broader architectural generalization. By integrating flow map principles with an optimized on-policy distillation strategy, AnyFlow aims to overcome the "any-step" scaling issues inherent in previous consistency-based approaches and extend capabilities to both bidirectional and causal video generation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an "any-step" video diffusion model that can consistently deliver high-quality video generation across a flexible range of inference step budgets. This entails achieving efficient, high-quality output with minimal sampling steps for rapid previews, while also allowing quality to progressively improve as more computational resources (i.e., more NFEs) are allocated for final, higher-fidelity outputs, all without necessitating model retraining.

The motivation for this objective stems from practical user requirements for video generation. Current state-of-the-art video diffusion models, while achieving high generation quality, often operate under fixed inference-step budgets. This rigidity limits user flexibility in controlling the latency-quality trade-off. Existing few-step distillation methods, predominantly based on consistency models, have advanced rapid generation but exhibit a critical weakness: their performance tends to decline as more sampling steps are introduced. This counter-intuitive behavior means that additional computation does not reliably lead to improved quality, thus failing to meet the demand for "any-step" flexibility.

The authors attribute this structural limitation in consistency models to their design, which primarily focuses on learning fixed-point mappings to the z_0 (clean image) state. During multi-step inference with such models, the process often involves re-noising intermediate states and repeatedly projecting back to z_0. This iterative re-noising can lead to cumulative bias, causing the sampling trajectories to deviate from the underlying Probability-Flow ODE (PF-ODE) path that governs the diffusion process. Consequently, instead of a refined output, increasing sampling steps may result in a degraded output.

To address these issues, AnyFlow proposes to:
1. **Shift the distillation target:** Move from endpoint-focused consistency mapping (z_t → z_0) to a more generalized flow-map transition learning (z_t → z_r) across arbitrary time intervals. This approach is intended to inherently support variable step sizes and adaptable inference budgets.
2. **Mitigate test-time errors:** Forward flow map training alone is identified as insufficient to fully address issues such as discretization error in low-NFE sampling and exposure bias in causal generation. Therefore, a subsequent on-policy distillation stage is a key objective.
3. **Develop efficient on-policy distillation:** Design a new backward simulation strategy suitable for flow map models. Existing consistency-based backward simulation techniques are not directly applicable or efficient for the arbitrary time-pair transitions learned by flow map models, particularly when aiming for scalable, multi-step inference.
4. **Support diverse architectures:** Ensure the framework is applicable to both bidirectional and causal video diffusion models, at various scales, and capable of jointly supporting text-to-video (T2V), image-to-video (I2V), and video-to-video (V2V) generation.
5. **Enable continued fine-tuning:** Preserve the model's ability to be further adapted to downstream datasets, which is often challenging with highly distilled models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

AnyFlow is structured as a two-stage training pipeline designed to distill pretrained video diffusion models into "any-step" flow map models.

**Stage 1: Forward Flow Map Training**
This stage aims to convert a pretrained video diffusion model into a flow map model that learns the transition operator `f_θ: (z_t, t, r) ↦ z_r`. The core objective is based on MeanFlow, which parameterizes the averaged transport velocity `u_θ(z_t, r, t)` between time `t` and `r`. The transition is approximated as `f_θ(z_t, t, r) = z_t - (t-r) u_θ(z_t, r, t)`, and `u_θ` is optimized using a loss that targets a specific `u_tgt` incorporating a differential derivation term (Equation 3). To handle the derivative term `d u_θ(z_t, r, t) / dt` efficiently and ensure compatibility with Fully Sharded Data Parallel (FSDP), the differential derivation equation (Equation 4) approximates it using a finite difference method, requiring two forward passes.

Several modifications are introduced to adapt MeanFlow for post-training video diffusion:
- **Interpolated Timestep Conditioning:** Instead of directly adding a new embedding for `r` (which can cause instability and over-saturation), AnyFlow uses an interpolated scheme: `g ⋅ emb(t) + (1-g) ⋅ emb'(r)`. Here, `emb'` is initialized from the pretrained `emb`, and `g` is set to 0.25. This maintains embedding norm stability.
- **Time Sampler:** Timestamps `t` and `r` are sampled uniformly and reordered to ensure `t > r`. A reweighting function `w(t)` (specifically, Beta(2, 1.5)) is applied to balance the training objective across different noise levels, assigning more probability mass to high-noise regions.
- **Guidance-Fused Training:** Classifier-free guidance (CFG) is integrated into the model's prediction `u` (Equation 6), `u = (1/g_cfg) (u_c - (1-g_cfg) sg(u_∅))`, where `g_cfg` is the CFG scale. This fusion ensures better alignment with the guidance scale of the pretrained diffusion model and allows CFG to be omitted during inference for speed.
- **Adaptive Loss Reweighting:** To stabilize training, especially for `t ≠ r` cases, an adaptive weight `w_t,r` (Equation 7) is introduced. This weight dynamically scales the loss for `t ≠ r` cases based on the average regression loss observed at boundary cases (`t = r`), preventing gradient instability and preserving the learned instantaneous velocity field.

**Stage 2: On-Policy Flow Map Distillation**
This stage refines the model obtained from Stage 1 to mitigate test-time errors, such as discretization error at low NFEs and exposure bias in causal generation. It follows the paradigm of Distribution Matching Distillation (DMD), which involves the student model performing a self-rollout to generate a sample, followed by re-noising and computing a Kullback–Leibler (KL) gradient to guide the model (Equation 5).

The central innovation in this stage is **Flow Map Backward Simulation**:
- **Motivation:** Traditional consistency backward simulation (used in rCM, Self-Forcing) requires re-noising intermediate states and rolling out the full trajectory, which is computationally expensive for arbitrary step counts and can lead to trajectory drift.
- **Mechanism:** AnyFlow leverages the learned `z_t → z_r` transition capability of flow map models. For a target sampling budget `N`, the full rollout trajectory from `T` to `0` is decomposed into three segments: `T → t`, `t → r`, and `r → 0`. The segments `T → t` and `r → 0` are efficiently handled by "shortcut" transitions using the learned flow map, while `t → r` represents a single step of the Euler scheduler (`t - r = T/N`). This decomposition exploits the composition property of flow maps.
- **Efficiency:** By varying `N` during training, this method allows for efficient simulation of different inference step budgets with a consistent computational cost. The KL gradient, once computed at `z_0`, is backpropagated through this shortcut chain.
- **Inference:** After distillation, a standard Euler scheduler can be directly applied for sampling, as the model is trained to handle transitions across various time intervals.

**AnyFlow Applications:**
- **Bidirectional and Causal Video Diffusion:** The pipeline is applied directly for bidirectional models. For causal models, it integrates with the FAR training pipeline for efficient context compression and KV-cache management. Non-uniform chunk partitioning is used to support I2V and T2V jointly (e.g., first chunk size 1 for I2V, subsequent chunks size 3).
- **Continued Training:** A key advantage is that AnyFlow preserves the instantaneous flow field, allowing the distilled model to be further fine-tuned on downstream datasets, unlike some consistency-distilled models that lose this adaptability.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

AnyFlow demonstrates improved performance and scalability in few-step video generation compared to existing consistency-based distillation methods.

#### 4.1. Test-Time Scaling and Any-Step Performance

- A central finding is that AnyFlow exhibits robust scaling behavior: its performance matches or surpasses consistency-based models in the few-step regime and, importantly, continues to improve as the number of sampling steps increases. This is in contrast to methods like rCM and Self-Forcing, which show performance degradation with more NFEs (Figure 1, Table 2).
- The model achieves quality comparable to or better than its non-distilled teacher model in terms of test-time scaling, while significantly improving the overall trajectory (Figure 1).

#### 4.2. Quantitative Text-to-Video (T2V) Evaluation (VBench Scores, Table 3)

- **Bidirectional Models (14B scale):** AnyFlow-Wan2.1-T2V-14B achieved a VBench score of 84.04 at 4 NFEs, outperforming rCM-Wan2.1-T2V-14B (83.73 at 4 NFEs). It further improved to 84.10 at 32 NFEs, demonstrating its multi-step scaling.
- **Causal Models (14B scale):** AnyFlow-FAR-Wan2.1-14B reached 84.05 at 4 NFEs and further improved to 84.41 at 32 NFEs. This result surpassed Krea-Realtime-14B (83.25 at 4 NFEs) and other competitive causal models in the 14B category, indicating strong performance and scaling in the causal setting.

#### 4.3. Quantitative Image-to-Video (I2V) Evaluation (VBench-I2V Scores, Table 4)

- AnyFlow-FAR-Wan2.1-14B achieved a VBench-I2V score of 87.87 at 4 NFEs. This performance is comparable to Wan2.1-I2V-14B, which used 50x2 NFEs (87.71), and surpassed FastVideo-CausalWan2.2-A14B-Preview (86.82), indicating high visual quality and strong first-frame faithfulness with high sampling efficiency.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Comparisons (Figure 9)

- **Causal T2V:** AnyFlow-FAR-Wan2.1-14B produced video clips with higher visual quality and more realistic motion at 4 NFEs compared to FastVideo (blurry details), LightX2V (flickering artifacts), and Krea-Realtime-14B (unrealistic motion).
- **Bidirectional T2V:** AnyFlow-Wan2.1-T2V-14B demonstrated slightly better motion quality than rCM-Wan2.1-T2V-14B.
- **I2V:** AnyFlow maintained good first-frame faithfulness and smooth motion, showing comparable temporal stability and motion quality to Wan2.1-I2V-14B, while outperforming FastVideo, which exhibited flickering and inconsistencies.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies

- **Methodology Impact (Table 2):** Flow map training provided a stronger initialization for few-step sampling than standard flow matching and Consistency ODE-Init. The addition of on-policy distillation, particularly with flow map backward simulation, was crucial for mitigating test-time errors and achieving the highest performance across both few-step and multi-step regimes.
- **Flow Map Backward Simulation:** This component was shown to produce straighter sampling trajectories, leading to consistent quality improvements with increased sampling budgets. In contrast, consistency backward simulation led to over-smoothed textures and degraded motion when more steps were allocated (Figure 6).
- **Timestep Conditioning:** The proposed interpolated timestep conditioning method maintained embedding norms consistent with pretrained models, preventing the over-saturated visual results observed with zero-initialized timestep conditioning (Figure 11).
- **Comparison to Consistency ODE-Init (Figure 12):** AnyFlow consistently generated clearer results and better preserved the knowledge from the flow-matching-pretrained base model compared to Consistency ODE-Init, both before and after on-policy distillation.
- **Training Cost (Table 5):** While flow map backward simulation incurred a slightly higher training cost for 4 steps (15.7-22.5% more) compared to consistency backward simulation, its efficiency significantly improved for larger step counts (e.g., 43.4-47.0% reduction in cost for 16 steps), owing to its shortcut transition mechanism.

#### 4.6. Adaptability to Downstream Datasets

- AnyFlow models were shown to preserve the instantaneous flow field, making them compatible with continued training on downstream datasets. This enables efficient adaptation to specialized domains, such as robotics or driving scenes, to improve identity preservation and trajectory accuracy without requiring full retraining (Figure 7, 8).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

AnyFlow introduces a new paradigm for video diffusion model distillation by leveraging a two-time flow map formulation. This framework directly addresses a key limitation of prior consistency-based methods, which often degrade in performance with increased sampling steps. By enabling true "any-step" video generation, AnyFlow allows a single model to support a flexible trade-off between inference latency and output fidelity. This capability holds practical significance for users who require both rapid previews during creative iteration and high-quality final outputs without needing to retrain or manage multiple specialized models.

The primary impacts of this work include:
- **Enhanced User Experience and Workflow Flexibility:** Users can dynamically adjust the number of inference steps based on their immediate needs, leading to a more adaptable and efficient video generation workflow. This addresses a real-world demand for flexible control over generation speed and quality.
- **Improved Model Robustness and Scaling Behavior:** AnyFlow models demonstrate consistent quality improvement with increased sampling steps, unlike consistency models. This reliable scaling behavior enhances the robustness of distilled video diffusion models.
- **Increased Efficiency and Performance:** By achieving performance comparable to or surpassing larger, non-distilled models (e.g., Wan2.1-I2V-14B at 50x2 NFEs vs. AnyFlow-FAR at 4 NFEs for I2V) with significantly fewer NFEs, AnyFlow contributes to more efficient high-quality video generation. The flow map backward simulation further optimizes training efficiency for multi-step distillation.
- **Broader Applicability:** The framework is validated across both bidirectional and causal architectures, and across different model scales (1.3B to 14B parameters). Its ability to jointly support text-to-video, image-to-video, and video-to-video generation within a unified model enhances its versatility.
- **Adaptability for Specialized Domains:** The preservation of the instantaneous flow field allows for continued fine-tuning on downstream datasets. This feature is particularly valuable for adapting general-purpose models to niche applications (e.g., robotics, specific driving scenarios) without the prohibitive cost and complexity of retraining a full generative model. This makes advanced video generation more accessible and practical for diverse use cases.
- **Advancement in Diffusion Distillation Research:** AnyFlow provides a scalable recipe for learning any-step causal video diffusion, establishing a foundation for future research. This includes potential improvements in flow map training methodologies for better robustness across various NFE regimes and extensions to long-video generation tasks.

In summary, AnyFlow represents a step towards more practical and adaptable high-quality video generation, offering a versatile solution that bridges the gap between rapid prototyping and high-fidelity production within a single, efficient model.
