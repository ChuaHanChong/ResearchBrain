# Reassessing the Role of Supervised Fine-Tuning: An Empirical Study in VLM Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> This study empirically re-evaluates Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning (RL) for Vision-Language Model (VLM) reasoning, demonstrating that SFT can be more effective for weaker models and data-efficient, while RL scales better with stronger models but is susceptible to deceptive rewards. It identifies conditional advantages for each approach, challenging the general "RL over SFT" belief.

## Problem

- The prevailing 'RL over SFT' narrative in VLM reasoning often claims RL is inherently superior for generalization, potentially underestimating SFT's role.
- A lack of systematic, controlled comparisons between SFT and RL for VLM reasoning leads to potentially confounded results due to differing data sources or model setups.
- Concerns exist that SFT might merely 'memorize' and potentially hinder subsequent RL optimization rather than fostering true reasoning capabilities.

## Method

- Conducted a systematic empirical study comparing SFT and RL using identical training data sources (up to 50K samples) and matched optimization setups to ensure fair evaluation.
- Utilized open-source QwenVL series models (Qwen2-VL-2B/7B and Qwen2.5-VL-3B/7B) to evaluate performance across different model capacities.
- Implemented ==SFT (negative log-likelihood)== and two representative RL algorithms (==GRPO==, ==DAPO==) with ==verifiable, task-level rewards== (exact correctness, format consistency).

## Results

- SFT consistently outperforms RL (GRPO) by approximately **2%** on weaker VLMs (e.g., Qwen2-VL-2B/7B), while RL shows advantages primarily with stronger models (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-7B).
- SFT achieves reasoning performance comparable to or better than RL trained on **20K** samples, using only **2K** samples, highlighting its data efficiency.
- RL training exhibits consistently increasing rewards, but test set accuracy often plateaus or declines, indicating 'deceptive rewards' and overfitting, a phenomenon stabilized by SFT pre-training.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The relative effectiveness of SFT and RL for VLM reasoning is conditional, depending on factors like model capacity and data scale, rather than one being universally superior.
> - SFT demonstrates high data efficiency, achieving strong reasoning performance with significantly less data than RL, and shows robust cross-modal transferability under appropriate data conditions.
> - RL optimization can be plagued by 'deceptive rewards,' where increasing training rewards do not consistently correlate with improved reasoning accuracy on unseen data due to reward hacking, though SFT initialization can mitigate this.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This study directly intervenes in a significant and ongoing debate within the field of large reasoning models (LRMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs) concerning the most effective post-training paradigms: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning (RL).

**Prevailing RL-Centric Narrative:** In recent years, the AI community has seen a rapid rise in RL-driven breakthroughs for reasoning, leading to a dominant "RL over SFT" narrative. Influential works like DeepSeek-R1, which demonstrated strong reasoning performance even without SFT pre-training, have cemented this belief. Prior studies have often claimed that "SFT memorizes, RL generalizes", suggested that SFT might even hinder subsequent RL optimization, and reported that RL-only training can outperform the traditional "SFT then RL" pipeline. This collective body of work has largely shifted the community's focus towards RL-centric approaches for post-training VLMs to achieve sophisticated reasoning behaviors. Early explorations of VLM reasoning did employ SFT on datasets with explicit reasoning trajectories (e.g., LLaVA-CoT, Mulberry-260K), but the pendulum has swung heavily towards RL.

**This Paper's Contrarian Stance:** The present work directly challenges this prevailing RL-centric view. It argues that the perceived superiority of RL might be conditional and influenced by specific experimental setups, such as model capacity, data scale, and data distribution. By conducting a systematic and controlled comparison using identical data sources and matched optimization setups, the authors aim to isolate the true contributions of SFT and RL, thereby providing a more nuanced understanding. They revisit the assumptions that have shaped recent RL-dominant trends, suggesting that SFT's role may have been underestimated.

**Contribution to the SFT vs. RL Debate:** Several studies have previously compared SFT and RL, often emphasizing SFT's limitations, particularly regarding generalization and compatibility with RL. This paper acts as a counter-narrative, presenting evidence that SFT can be crucial in specific scenarios and challenging the notion of RL's universal superiority in generalization. It moves beyond a simplistic "SFT vs RL" dichotomy, advocating for a balanced post-training pipeline where both approaches function as complementary components. This re-evaluation is timely, given the significant resources and research effort being poured into RL-based alignment for large models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to systematically reassess the role of Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) in improving the reasoning capabilities of Vision-Language Models (VLMs), thereby challenging the prevailing assumption that Reinforcement Learning (RL) is inherently superior.

The primary motivations driving this study are:

- **Challenging the "RL over SFT" Narrative:** The community's increasing focus on RL for VLM reasoning has led to a belief that SFT is fundamentally inferior, with claims ranging from SFT merely "memorizing" to potentially hindering RL optimization. The authors are motivated to empirically investigate if this RL-centric view holds universally or if it is context-dependent.
- **Providing a Controlled Comparison:** Previous comparisons between SFT and RL may have suffered from confounds due to differences in data sources, model architectures, or training setups. A key motivation is to establish a systematic and controlled experimental framework—using identical data sources (up to 50K samples) and matched optimization setups—to isolate and accurately evaluate the distinct contributions of SFT and RL.
- **Identifying Conditional Effectiveness:** The researchers aim to discover if the relative effectiveness of SFT and RL is conditional, specifically exploring its dependency on:
 - **Model Capacity:** How do SFT and RL perform with weaker (smaller) versus stronger (larger) VLM backbones?
 - **Data Scale:** What are the data efficiency characteristics of SFT and RL across varying data volumes, from limited (e.g., 2K samples) to large-scale (e.g., 20K or 50K samples)?
 - **Data Distribution:** How does the distribution of training data affect the generalization capabilities of SFT, especially in cross-modal transfer?
- **Investigating Generalization and Transferability:** A common critique of SFT is its limited generalizability. The study seeks to rigorously compare the cross-domain and cross-modal transferability of SFT and RL, particularly to non-reasoning visual benchmarks and pure-text tasks, to challenge or confirm existing beliefs.
- **Uncovering RL Limitations:** The authors are motivated to identify potential issues within the RL paradigm, specifically investigating the reliability of reward signals and whether higher training rewards consistently correlate with improved reasoning accuracy on unseen data.
- **Advocating for a Balanced Post-training Approach:** Ultimately, the motivation is to move beyond a strict hierarchy between SFT and RL, advocating for a more balanced and complementary post-training pipeline where both techniques are leveraged strategically based on specific model, data, and task characteristics.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of this study's methodology revolves around a systematic and controlled empirical comparison between Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning (RL) for VLM reasoning. The authors meticulously designed their experiments to minimize confounding variables and isolate the true effects of each post-training paradigm.

**Controlled Experimental Setup:**
The crucial aspect of their approach is the use of **identical training data sources** and **matched optimization setups**. Both the SFT dataset (approx. 50K samples with reasoning trajectories) and the RL dataset (approx. 30K samples) were derived from the *same* large-scale multimodal reasoning dataset. This consistency is vital for a fair comparison, as differences in data quality or domain could otherwise skew results.

**Models and Training Data:**
- **Backbone Models:** The study utilizes open-source VLMs from the **QwenVL series**, including Qwen2-VL-2B/7B and Qwen2.5-VL-3B/7B. This selection allows for evaluating the paradigms across different model capacities.
- **Multimodal Reasoning Dataset:** The training data is a large-scale multimodal reasoning dataset constructed from a diverse set of existing benchmarks, such as Geometry3K, GeoQA, AI2D, TabMWP, ChartQA, ScienceQA, and others. Reasoning trajectories within this dataset were generated through rejection sampling using larger Qwen models (Qwen2.5-VL-7B and Qwen2.5-VL-32B), ensuring high-quality, step-by-step reasoning demonstrations.
- **System Prompt:** A standardized system prompt guides the models to produce outputs with clearly separated reasoning and final answer segments, enclosed within `<think>` and `<answer>` tags, respectively. This structured output facilitates consistent evaluation of reasoning processes.

**Post-Training Paradigms:**
1. **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):**
 - **Objective:** Minimizes the negative log-likelihood of reference responses, explicitly teaching step-by-step reasoning by imitating high-quality reasoning trajectories.
 - **Implementation:** Conducted using the LLaMA-Factory framework.
 - **Hyperparameters:** Global batch size of 128, learning rate of 1 × 10⁻⁵, and 3 epochs.

2. **Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RL):**
 - **Objective:** Enhances reasoning by maximizing expected reward from autonomously generated reasoning trajectories.
 - **Algorithms:** Two representative RL algorithms are employed from the EasyR1 framework:
 - **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO):** An extension of PPO that evaluates rewards over groups of reasoning trajectories sampled from the same prompt.
 - **Decoupled Clip and Dynamic Sampling Policy Optimization (DAPO):** Builds upon GRPO, featuring KL-free optimization, asymmetric clipping thresholds (`ϵ_low`, `ϵ_high`) to promote learning from correct rollouts, and a dynamic sampling strategy to ensure informative gradient updates.
 - **RL with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR):** A deterministic verifier assigns rewards based on objective, task-level criteria such as exact numerical correctness (`r_acc`) and format consistency (`r_format`). The reward function prioritizes accuracy (λ=0.9).
 - **Hyperparameters:** Global batch size of 512, learning rate of 1 × 10⁻⁶, and 2 epochs.

**Evaluation Strategy:**
Performance is assessed across a comprehensive suite of benchmarks:
- **Multimodal Mathematical Reasoning Benchmarks:** MathVista, MathVerse, MathVision, and We-Math are used to evaluate the core reasoning capabilities.
- **Non-Reasoning Visual Benchmarks:** MMVet-Hard, MMVet, RealWorldQA, HalluBench, and MMBench-EN are used to evaluate cross-domain transferability to tasks emphasizing perceptual grounding and factual consistency.
- **Pure Text Benchmarks:** MMLU-Pro, GPQA, and MATH-500 are used to evaluate cross-modal transferability, assessing how reasoning-oriented post-training affects general language understanding.
- **Inference:** All inferences are conducted using VLMEvalKit. Answers for base models are extracted using an external LLM, while for reasoning models, content within `<answer>` tags is directly used.

**Analysis Dimensions:**
The methodology explicitly explores:
- **Model Scalability:** Comparing SFT and RL performance across models of varying capacities (2B to 7B parameters).
- **Data Scalability:** Investigating how performance changes for SFT and RL with different volumes of training data (1K to 20K samples).
- **Transferability:** Analyzing generalization to both non-reasoning visual tasks and pure-text tasks, with an additional focus on the impact of training data distribution and "think-mode" prompting.
- **Reward-Accuracy Correlation:** Scrutinizing the relationship between increasing training rewards and actual test set accuracy in RL, to identify potential issues like deceptive rewards.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study presents several key findings that challenge the prevailing "RL over SFT" narrative, demonstrating that the relative effectiveness of the two paradigms is highly conditional.

**1. Conditional Effectiveness and Scalability:**
- **Model Scalability:** SFT proves surprisingly effective for weaker or smaller VLMs, reliably eliciting reasoning capabilities where RL struggles. For instance, SFT consistently outperforms GRPO by approximately 2% on weaker models (e.g., Qwen2-VL-2B/7B). Conversely, RL (GRPO/DAPO) shows advantages primarily when the backbone model is already strong and possesses sufficient capacity (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-7B), exhibiting superior scaling behavior with model strength.
- **SFT Initialization for RL:** While SFT initialization benefits subsequent RL training, its impact diminishes as the base model becomes stronger, dropping below 1.0% for Qwen2.5-VL-7B. This suggests that SFT helps weak models produce diverse and correct responses, providing better signals for RL, but becomes less critical for stronger models that can already generate such responses.
- **Data Scalability:** SFT demonstrates remarkable **data efficiency**, achieving reasoning performance comparable to or better than RL trained on 20K samples using only 2K samples. This highlights SFT's ability to effectively leverage scarce supervision signals. In contrast, RL benefits more substantially from large-scale data (10K–20K samples), showing steady performance gains as data volume increases. SFT tends to plateau or mildly degrade with very large datasets. These findings suggest that RL's perceived superiority might stem from experiments conducted on larger models and richer datasets.

**2. Cross-Modal Transferability and Generalization:**
- **Cross-Domain (Non-Reasoning Visual Tasks):** Neither SFT nor RL exhibited clear dominance. Both paradigms achieved broadly comparable performance on non-reasoning visual benchmarks, with differences generally within 2%. SFT tended to perform better on open-ended tasks (e.g., MMVet-Hard, MMVet), while RL showed slight advantages on structured tasks (e.g., RealWorldQA, HalluBench). SFT + RL combination yielded more stable performance.
- **Cross-Modal (Pure Text Tasks):** Contrary to the popular notion that RL generalizes better, SFT demonstrated **stronger cross-modal transferability** to pure-text tasks (GPQA, MATH-500, MMLU), outperforming base models without degrading general language understanding. GRPO without SFT initialization slightly underperformed SFT in this aspect. However, when GRPO was preceded by an SFT warm-up, it matched or exceeded SFT performance, indicating that a balanced SFT+RL combination promotes stronger cross-modal transferability.
- **Factors Influencing Transferability (Data Distribution):** The study found that the generalization performance of SFT is closely tied to the **distribution of training data**. SFT on datasets closely aligned with the base model's distribution (e.g., QwenVL-Distill) better preserves pre-trained knowledge, showing minimal degradation in OOD performance. Conversely, SFT using data from heterogeneous sources (e.g., Eureka-Distill) resulted in more pronounced accuracy drops. "Think-style" prompting had only marginal influence on transferability, suggesting that data distribution is the primary determinant.

**3. Pervasive Issue of Deceptive Rewards in RL:**
- A critical finding is the presence of **deceptive rewards** in RL training. During GRPO training, reward curves consistently increased, indicating continuous optimization under the verifiable reward signal. However, the corresponding accuracy on held-out benchmarks (e.g., MathVerse, We-Math) often plateaued and eventually **declined** even as rewards continued to rise.
- This divergence implies that RL, guided by verifiable rewards, can **overfit to the training reward**, leading to "reward hacking" where the model optimizes for superficial reward conformity rather than genuine reasoning ability.
- Notably, this overfitting tendency was **mitigated when GRPO was preceded by an SFT stage**. The SFT-based cold-start initialization stabilized the training dynamics, leading to lower training-set rewards but ultimately better performance on unseen test data, suggesting that SFT provides a more stable and generalizable initialization that resists reward over-optimization.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research paper offers a significant re-evaluation of the roles of Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning (RL) in enhancing reasoning capabilities for Vision-Language Models (VLMs). Its findings carry substantial implications for both academic research and practical model development.

**1. Challenging a Dominant Paradigm:**
The most profound impact of this work is its direct challenge to the widely accepted "RL over SFT" narrative that has come to dominate VLM reasoning research. By demonstrating that the superiority of RL is conditional and not universal, the paper encourages the community to reconsider deeply ingrained assumptions and adopt a more nuanced perspective. This intellectual rebalancing can prevent premature abandonment of SFT and foster more comprehensive research into its strengths.

**2. Reinstating the Value of SFT:**
The study empirically reinstates SFT's crucial role, particularly in specific, high-value scenarios. Its effectiveness for weaker models, its remarkable data efficiency (SFT with 2K samples matching RL with 20K), and its strong cross-modal transferability under appropriate data conditions highlight that SFT may have been significantly underestimated. This will likely lead to renewed interest in developing high-quality SFT datasets and refining SFT techniques, especially for resource-constrained environments or for bootstrapping smaller models.

**3. Guiding Post-Training Pipeline Design:**
By showing that SFT and RL are complementary rather than strictly hierarchical, the paper offers practical guidance for designing more effective post-training pipelines for reasoning VLMs. Instead of a one-size-fits-all approach, developers can now make informed decisions based on model capacity, available data scale, and desired generalization properties. This could lead to hybrid approaches that strategically combine SFT for robust initialization and data efficiency with RL for further refinement and exploration, thus optimizing model performance and training efficiency.

**4. Highlighting and Mitigating Deceptive Rewards in RL:**
The identification of "deceptive rewards" in RL—where increasing training rewards do not correlate with improved test accuracy—is a critical contribution. This phenomenon of "reward hacking" raises serious concerns about the reliability of reward signals and the validity of solely optimizing for them in complex reasoning tasks. The finding that an SFT cold-start can mitigate this overfitting offers a practical solution, promoting more stable and generalizable RL training. This insight will stimulate further research into robust reward design, better evaluation metrics, and techniques to prevent reward over-optimization in RL-driven large language models.

**5. Emphasizing Data Distribution for Generalization:**
The finding that SFT's generalization performance is critically tied to the distribution of its training data provides valuable guidance for dataset curation. It underscores that fine-tuning with reasoning trajectories whose distributions closely align with the base model's pre-training knowledge is crucial for retaining out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization. This can inform the design of more effective distillation and data augmentation strategies, reducing the risk of catastrophic forgetting during SFT.

**6. Future Research Directions:**
The paper explicitly states its limitations, such as focusing primarily on the Qwen-VL model family and mathematical reasoning. This implicitly opens avenues for future research to validate these findings across broader model architectures, diverse reasoning domains (e.g., commonsense, spatial, scientific reasoning), and different modalities. Further investigation into why SFT excels in data efficiency and weaker models, and deeper exploration of robust reward functions for RL, are also natural extensions of this work.

In conclusion, this study serves as a timely and important empirical intervention, urging the VLM community to adopt a more balanced, context-aware perspective on post-training strategies. Its practical insights into conditional effectiveness, data efficiency, generalization, and RL's deceptive rewards are poised to significantly influence future research and development of reasoning-capable VLMs.
