# Co-Reinforcement Learning for Unified Multimodal Understanding and Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Shanghai Jiao Tong University and Nanyang Technological University developed CoRL, a co-reinforcement learning framework that applies Group Relative Policy Optimization to Unified Multimodal Large Language Models (ULMs). This framework enabled the ULM-R1 model to achieve an average 7% improvement in text-to-image generation and 23% in multimodal understanding benchmarks, surpassing previous unified models and narrowing the gap with specialized counterparts.

## Problem

- Existing applications of reinforcement learning, specifically Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), in multimodal large language models (MLLMs) largely focused on understanding tasks, with limited exploration for visual generation.
- A significant gap existed in applying GRPO to Unified Multimodal Large Language Models (ULMs) to concurrently improve both understanding and generation capabilities within a single model.
- Direct, task-specific reinforcement learning approaches often failed to achieve anticipated improvements in multimodal generation and could degrade other abilities in ULMs.

## Method

- Developed CoRL (==Co-Reinforcement Learning==), a two-stage framework built on Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) to synergistically enhance Unified Multimodal Large Language Models (ULMs).
- Implemented a "Foundation" stage (Unified RL) with a comprehensive joint reward function, incorporating bidirectional cycle consistency and text-image matching for generation, alongside accuracy and format for understanding.
- Followed by a "Specialization" stage (Refined RL) using targeted, task-specific reward functions to further refine performance for text-to-image generation and multimodal understanding tasks.

## Results

- The ULM-R1 model, resulting from CoRL, achieved average performance improvements of **7%** on three text-to-image generation benchmarks and **23%** on nine multimodal understanding benchmarks.
- ULM-R1 attained state-of-the-art performance among unified models on benchmarks like MMStar (47.6), WeMath (21.1), and LogicVista (34.5), and notably surpassed some dedicated generation-only models.
- Qualitative analysis confirmed ULM-R1's superior text-to-image alignment, object grounding, and enhanced mathematical reasoning capabilities compared to its Janus-Pro baseline.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Unified reinforcement learning (RL) significantly outperforms separate or cyclical RL strategies for co-evolving understanding and generation capabilities in multimodal models.
> - The proposed reward functions, including bidirectional cycle consistency and text-image matching, effectively guide multimodal generation by capturing fine-grained semantic and perceptual alignment.
> - Unified Multimodal Large Language Models (ULMs) demonstrate strong cross-task synergy, allowing for data-efficient co-optimization and mutual enhancement of understanding and generation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of several rapidly evolving fields in artificial intelligence: large foundation models (LFMs), multimodal AI, and reinforcement learning (RL) for model alignment and capability refinement.

**Advancements in Foundation Models and Post-Training:** The paper acknowledges the significant progress in general capabilities and knowledge breadth of LFMs. It highlights post-training (e.g.,) as a crucial paradigm for refining these pre-trained models towards specialized applications, task adaptation, and human-aligned behaviors. This work contributes to this paradigm by focusing on RL-based post-training for multimodal models.

**The Rise of Reinforcement Learning for LLM Alignment:** Reinforcement learning has emerged as a powerful technique for fine-tuning LLMs, exemplified by approaches like Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) and direct policy optimization (DPO). This paper specifically draws inspiration from the success of RL-based approaches using *verifiable rewards* and Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), as showcased by DeepSeek-R1. This paradigm is attractive due to its data efficiency and ability to achieve strong alignment without explicit preference modeling or reward model learning. The current work aims to extend the benefits of this RL paradigm to the multimodal domain.

**Bridging Multimodal Understanding and Generation:** In the multimodal AI community, previous applications of GRPO in MLLMs predominantly focused on multimodal *understanding*, particularly complex visual and mathematical reasoning tasks (e.g.,). Its application to visual *generation* has been limited to pioneering explorations (e.g.,). A more significant gap exists in extending GRPO to *Unified Multimodal Large Language Models (ULMs)* that are designed to perform both understanding and generation tasks concurrently within a single model. ULMs offer inherent advantages like cross-task synergy and shared LLM components, enabling better mitigation of reward imbalance and knowledge transfer across tasks. This paper directly addresses this under-explored area, positioning itself as a pioneering effort to apply verifiable-reward GRPO for the *unified* reinforcement of both understanding and generation capabilities in ULMs.

**Architectural Context for ULMs:** The paper explicitly mentions that its work builds upon the fully autoregressive (F-AR) model for unified multimodal understanding and generation, citing models like Janus-Pro, TokenFlow, and Emu3. This indicates a commitment to an architecture where visual inputs are encoded into discrete tokens, similar to text, and images are generated in an autoregressive manner, which aligns with the LLM-centric approach.

In essence, this research pushes the boundaries of RL applications in multimodal AI by demonstrating that GRPO with verifiable rewards can effectively co-optimize and enhance both understanding and generation capabilities within a unified multimodal model, thereby fostering cross-task synergy and data efficiency.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **enhance the understanding and generation capabilities of Unified Multimodal Large Language Models (ULMs) without relying on extensive supervised data.**

This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Leveraging Reinforcement Learning's Potential:** The recent success of RL-based approaches, particularly those utilizing verifiable rewards and Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) like DeepSeek-R1, demonstrated significant promise in acquiring advanced capabilities and generalizing effectively without large-scale, high-quality supervised data. This motivated the authors to explore if this paradigm could be successfully applied to multimodal contexts.

2. **Addressing the Gap in Multimodal RL:** While GRPO has been applied to MLLMs primarily for multimodal *understanding* (e.g., visual reasoning), its application to visual *generation* and, more critically, to *unified* MLLMs (ULMs) capable of both understanding and generation, remains significantly under-explored. The authors identified this as a crucial area for advancement.

3. **Exploiting Cross-Task Synergy in ULMs:** ULMs, by their nature, possess inherent advantages through LLM sharing and potential cross-task synergy. The authors hypothesized that these benefits could be amplified under a shared RL policy optimization framework. This would allow ULMs to share reward signals across diverse tasks and mitigate reward imbalance, especially as GRPO inherently operates by ranking outputs within task-agnostic groups.

4. **Evidence from Pilot Studies:** The authors' initial pilot experiments revealed two critical findings:
 - Direct task-specific RL often fails to deliver anticipated improvements, especially in visual generation, and can even degrade other abilities.
 - Unified RL, which involves jointly optimizing both understanding and generation, showed comprehensive advantages across tasks. These findings provided strong empirical evidence for the synergistic co-evolution of dual capabilities within a shared training framework, motivating the development of a dedicated co-reinforcement learning framework.

In summary, the motivation stems from the desire to harness the data efficiency and strong alignment capabilities of GRPO for ULMs, specifically to enable them to simultaneously improve both their understanding and generation skills by fostering cross-task synergy in a unified optimization setting.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of this paper's methodology is **CoRL (Co-Reinforcement Learning)**, a novel framework designed to synergistically improve ULMs' understanding and generation capabilities. CoRL is built upon the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) algorithm and follows a "Foundation-then-Specialization" paradigm implemented through a two-stage RL procedure. The baseline ULM used is Janus-Pro-1B.

#### 3.1. Preliminary: Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)

GRPO is a value-free policy optimization algorithm that enhances training stability and sample efficiency by introducing a group-wise relative advantage. For a given input, it samples a group of outputs, evaluates them with predefined verifiable reward functions, and normalizes these rewards to compute group-relative advantages. The policy is then optimized by maximizing an objective that involves the ratio of new to old policy probabilities, weighted by these advantages. The paper notes an optional KL-divergence constraint to limit policy deviation.

#### 3.2. Pilot Exploration

Before proposing CoRL, the authors conducted pilot studies comparing four rule-based training strategies on a curated dataset of 16K samples (COCO 2017 with image, caption, and QA):
1. **Separate RL:** Independent optimization for understanding and generation.
2. **Separate RL with Weight Merging:** Separate optimization followed by Gaussian distribution-based weight merging.
3. **Cycle RL:** Scheduled alternation between the two tasks.
4. **Unified RL:** Joint optimization of both tasks within a single paradigm.
The results demonstrated that unified RL significantly outperformed other strategies, highlighting the benefits of co-evolving dual capabilities.

#### 3.3. Co-Reinforcement Learning (CoRL) Framework

CoRL consists of two stages:

**Stage 1: Unified RL for Joint Optimization (Foundation)**
This stage aims to establish a robust foundation by jointly enhancing both understanding and generation capabilities.
- **Reward Function (`R_Uni-S1`):** A comprehensive joint reward function is formulated:
 `R_Uni-S1 = R_cycle + R_TIM + λ * (R_Acc + R_Format)`
 - **Bidirectional Cycle Consistency Reward (`R_cycle`):** Encourages generated images to faithfully depict prompts by measuring consistency in both visual (LPIPS between real and generated image) and textual spaces (SPICE score between original prompt and BLIP-generated re-caption of generated image).
 - **Text-Image Matching Reward (`R_TIM`):** Addresses limitations of holistic measures like CLIP Score by leveraging the ULM itself to evaluate fine-grained cross-modal alignment at the token level, using maximum cosine similarity between textual and visual tokens.
 - **Accuracy Reward (`R_Acc`):** Binary reward for multimodal question answering, assigning 1 for correct answers (parsed from `<answer>` tags).
 - **Format Reward (`R_Format`):** Assigns 1 for strict compliance with the expected output format (e.g., reasoning within `<think>` tags, final answer in `<answer>` tags).
 - `λ` is a balancing coefficient.
- **Training Objective:** Maximizes a GRPO-based objective (Eq. 6) using the group-relative advantages derived from `R_Uni-S1`. Notably, the KL-divergence constraint is *omitted* in this stage for improved efficiency and generalization, based on recent empirical findings.
- **Training Data:** A curated dataset (`x2x_rft_22k`) of 22K samples, each including a real image, a prompt for visual generation, and a CoT-format QA pair for multimodal understanding.

**Stage 2: Refined RL for Task-specific Enhancement (Specialization)**
After unified RL, this stage applies targeted learning to further refine task-specific performance.
- **Reward Functions:**
 - **Text-to-Image Generation (`R_T2I-S2`):** `R_cycle + R_TIM`.
 - **Multimodal Understanding (Multiple-Choice) (`R_MCQ-S2`):** `R_MCQ-Acc + R_Format`.
 - **Multimodal Understanding (Open-Ended) (`R_OE-S2`):** `R_OE-Acc + R_Format`.
- **Training Objective:** Adheres to the standard GRPO formulation (Eq. 2), with the appropriate task-specific reward replacing `A_i`. The KL-divergence constraint is *reintroduced* here for stable optimization.
- **Training Data:**
 - Text-to-Image Generation: `x2x_rft_16k` (curated dataset with COCO captions).
 - Multimodal Understanding: `mcot_r1_mcq` for multiple-choice and `mcot_r1_vqa` for open-ended questions. These datasets are derived from diverse sources like A-OKVQA, MCoT-Instruct, and MAVIS-Instruct.

#### 3.4. Implementation Details

The RL training is built upon the `trl` framework. Specific hyperparameters like learning rates, batch sizes, number of sampled responses (G), and prompt/completion lengths are detailed. All training is performed on 8 NVIDIA H20 (96G) GPUs. Greedy decoding is used for text generation, and classifier-free guidance (CFG) for image generation during inference.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The application of the CoRL framework to the Janus-Pro baseline ULM results in **ULM-R1**, a unified model with significantly reinforced capabilities.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance

ULM-R1 achieves average improvements of **7% on three text-to-image generation benchmarks** and **23% on nine multimodal understanding benchmarks**, demonstrating the effectiveness of CoRL.

#### 4.2. Text-to-Image Generation (Table 1)

- **Strong Performance among Unified Models:** ULM-R1 ranks second on both GenEval and WISE benchmarks among unified understanding and generation models.
- **Balanced Capabilities:** Achieves a balanced performance across diverse task categories within GenEval, with the best score of 0.71 in object counting.
- **Competitiveness with Specialized Models:** Notably, ULM-R1 surpasses some top-performing *generation-only* models, such as SD3-Medium (0.77 vs. 0.74 on GenEval), indicating its strong generative prowess despite being a unified model.
- **Consistent Improvements over Baseline:** ULM-R1 shows consistent and substantial improvements across all generation benchmarks compared to its Janus-Pro baseline.

#### 4.3. Multimodal Understanding (Table 2)

- **Superiority among Unified Models:** ULM-R1 markedly outperforms existing unified models across most understanding benchmarks.
- **Narrowing the Gap with Understanding-Only Models:** It substantially narrows the performance gap with leading understanding-only MLLMs of comparable scale.
- **State-of-the-Art in Specific Benchmarks:** Achieves state-of-the-art performance among unified models on MMStar (47.6), WeMath (21.1), LogicVista (34.5), and mixed-format math benchmarks like MathVerse (25.4) and MathVision (22.0).
- **Significant Gains in Reasoning:** Demonstrates considerable improvements in mathematical and logical reasoning tasks over its base model, with gains of +15.2 on WeMath and +10.6 on LogicVista.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Results (Figures 3 & 4)

- **Improved Visual Generation:** ULM-R1 shows superior text-to-image alignment and object grounding, with notable enhancements in spatial arrangement and compositional consistency compared to Janus-Pro.
- **Enhanced Multimodal Understanding:** Exhibits significantly improved understanding capabilities, particularly in mathematical reasoning, showcasing more accurate and logical thought processes.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies and Discussion (Tables 3-7)

- **Efficacy of Unified RL:** The pilot study and ablation show that Unified-RL significantly enhances both generation and understanding, outperforming Cold-SFT and other RL paradigms. CoRL consistently surpasses both baseline and task-specific RL variants.
- **Effectiveness of Proposed Rewards:** Combining the bidirectional cycle consistency reward (`R_cycle`) and text-image matching reward (`R_TIM`) yields the best generation performance, suggesting modest but complementary effects. `R_TIM` is shown to be superior to CLIP score in capturing fine-grained semantic alignment.
- **Optimal Visual Consistency Metric:** LPIPS is identified as the best visual consistency measure within `R_cycle` due to its robustness to minor perceptual variations.
- **Hyperparameter `λ`:** A moderate value of `λ` (around 0.8) achieves a balanced trade-off between generation and understanding performance during unified RL.
- **Scalability:** CoRL demonstrates consistent improvements across different model scales (Janus-1.3B and Janus-Pro-7B), confirming its scalability, although complex reasoning gains do not scale linearly with model size.

These findings collectively underscore the substantial benefits of reinforcement learning, particularly the CoRL framework, in facilitating cross-task synergy and optimization for ULMs, enabling them to achieve robust generalization and sophisticated reasoning capabilities with data efficiency.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work represents a significant contribution to the fields of large language models, multimodal AI, and reinforcement learning, with several important implications and potential impacts:

1. **Pioneering Unified RL for Multimodal Models:** This paper is a pioneering exploration of applying reinforcement learning via Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) for *unified* multimodal large language models (ULMs). Previous applications of GRPO in multimodal contexts largely focused on understanding or reasoning, while generation capabilities and the joint optimization of both were considerably under-explored. CoRL addresses this gap, establishing a novel paradigm for enhancing holistic multimodal intelligence.

2. **Demonstrating Cross-Task Synergy and Co-evolution:** The systematic pilot studies and subsequent results compellingly demonstrate that ULMs possess significant potential for the synergistic co-evolution of understanding and generation capabilities within a shared policy optimization framework. This finding validates the architectural premise of ULMs and suggests that treating multimodal tasks as inherently intertwined, rather than separate, leads to superior overall performance.

3. **Data-Efficient Capability Enhancement:** By leveraging verifiable rewards and GRPO, the CoRL framework offers a data-efficient paradigm for enhancing ULM capabilities. This reduces the reliance on large-scale, high-quality supervised data, which can be expensive and time-consuming to curate, making the training process more practical and accessible. This is a critical factor for the sustainable development of increasingly complex foundation models.

4. **Novel Reward Mechanisms for Multimodal Generation:** The introduction of the bidirectional cycle consistency reward (`R_cycle`) and text-image matching reward (`R_TIM`) specifically designed for text-to-image generation in an RL setting is a key methodological advancement. These rewards facilitate fine-grained semantic alignment and perceptual consistency, crucial for high-quality visual synthesis.

5. **Setting New Benchmarks for Unified Models:** The resulting model, ULM-R1, achieves state-of-the-art performance among unified multimodal models across both generation and understanding benchmarks. It substantially narrows the performance gap with specialized, understanding-only MLLMs and even surpasses some dedicated generation-only models, demonstrating that a single unified model can achieve highly competitive performance across diverse tasks.

6. **Advancing Complex Multimodal Reasoning:** The significant improvements observed in mathematical and logical reasoning tasks (e.g., +15.2 on WeMath, +10.6 on LogicVista) highlight the ability of CoRL to foster sophisticated reasoning capabilities. This is particularly impactful for applications requiring deep semantic understanding and problem-solving in visual contexts.

7. **Guidance for Future RL Research in ULMs:** The paper provides valuable insights into the design of RL frameworks for multimodal models, including the benefits of a "unified-then-refined" strategy and the importance of specific reward functions. Its identified limitations, such as the remaining performance gap between generation and understanding tasks and the simplicity of some understanding rewards, naturally point towards promising avenues for future research, stimulating further innovation in the field.

In conclusion, this research not only presents an effective framework for simultaneously reinforcing both understanding and generation in ULMs but also fundamentally advances our understanding of how these dual capabilities can synergistically co-evolve, paving the way for more intelligent, versatile, and data-efficient multimodal AI systems.
