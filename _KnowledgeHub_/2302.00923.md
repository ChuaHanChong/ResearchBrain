# Multimodal Chain-of-Thought Reasoning in Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> A Multimodal Chain-of-Thought (Multimodal-CoT) framework for language models under 1 billion parameters is developed to integrate language and vision, mitigating hallucinated rationales in smaller models. The framework achieves 90.45% accuracy on the ScienceQA benchmark, outperforming prior state-of-the-art for models of its scale.

## Problem

- Existing Chain-of-Thought (CoT) methods primarily focus on text-only inputs, limiting their applicability to real-world problems that involve multiple modalities.
- Smaller language models (under 1 billion parameters) often generate hallucinated rationales that mislead answer inference when attempting CoT.
- Converting visual inputs to text captions for multimodal large language models leads to significant information loss and lacks synergistic representation.

## Method

- A ==two-stage Multimodal Chain-of-Thought (Multimodal-CoT)== framework is introduced, explicitly separating ==rationale generation and answer inference==.
- The model integrates language and vision features through a ==Transformer-based encoder-decoder architecture==, utilizing a language encoder and a vision extractor.
- A ==gated fusion mechanism== dynamically combines cross-attended vision features with language representations to create a fused multimodal input for the decoder.

## Results

- The Multimodal-CoT model (**738M parameters**) achieved **90.45%** accuracy on the ScienceQA benchmark, establishing a new state-of-the-art for models under 1 billion parameters.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the two-stage framework and the inclusion of vision features are crucial, with vision features correcting **60.7%** of previous hallucination errors.
- The framework demonstrates effectiveness on the A-OKVQA benchmark and proves adaptable by leveraging machine-generated rationales for training, showing comparable performance to human annotations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Unlike very large language models, smaller models trained for Chain-of-Thought reasoning often produce hallucinated rationales that degrade overall performance.
> - Integrating multimodal (vision and language) context during rationale generation is essential to mitigate hallucination and improve reasoning accuracy in smaller models.
> - A two-stage approach, separating rationale generation from answer inference, is more effective for smaller models than a single-stage Chain-of-Thought due to the hallucination issue.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses a crucial and rapidly evolving area within artificial intelligence: the development of advanced reasoning capabilities in large language models (LLMs). The broader research landscape is characterized by:

- **Emergence of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Reasoning:** CoT prompting (Wei et al., 2022b; Kojima et al., 2022) has been a significant breakthrough, enabling LLMs to solve complex reasoning problems by generating intermediate reasoning steps (rationales) before producing a final answer. This technique has vastly improved LLMs' performance on tasks requiring multi-step thinking, moving beyond simple factual recall to more complex problem-solving.
- **Unimodal Limitation of Existing CoT:** A major gap identified by the authors is that most existing CoT studies have primarily focused on the *language modality* (text-only inputs and outputs). Real-world problems, however, often involve multiple modalities, such as text, images, audio, and video.
- **Rise of Multimodal LLMs:** Concurrently, there has been a surge in the development of Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) like GPT-4V, Gemini, Flamingo, and InstructBLIP, which aim to process and understand information across different modalities. These models often employ strategies like transforming visual input into textual descriptions (e.g., captioning) to be processed by a language model or integrating vision features directly into a language model architecture.
- **Challenges with Current Multimodal Approaches:**
 - **Information Loss in Captioning:** A common approach for multimodal reasoning with LLMs is to convert images into text captions and then feed the combined text to the LLM. The authors highlight that this "captioning process still incurs significant information loss when transforming vision signals into textual descriptions," leading to a lack of "mutual synergy in the representation space of different modalities."
 - **Resource Intensiveness of LLMs:** Deploying and prompting very large LLMs (e.g., models with hundreds of billions of parameters) can be computationally expensive and may involve paywalls, limiting their accessibility and local deployment.
 - **Hallucination in Smaller Models:** When attempting to fine-tune *smaller* language models (under 100 billion parameters, and specifically under 1 billion parameters as targeted by this paper) to perform CoT reasoning, a critical challenge arises: these models tend to generate "hallucinated rationales that mislead the answer inference." This issue significantly hampers their ability to leverage CoT effectively.

This paper directly addresses the aforementioned challenges by focusing on **Multimodal Chain-of-Thought (Multimodal-CoT)**, specifically for **smaller models**. It proposes a novel framework that integrates language and vision modalities directly, aiming to mitigate hallucination and improve reasoning accuracy without relying on the massive scale or external captioning models often associated with larger LMMs. By achieving state-of-the-art performance with models under 1 billion parameters, this work carves out a niche in making advanced multimodal reasoning more efficient and accessible, bridging the gap between unimodal CoT and resource-intensive large multimodal models. It also distinguishes itself by being one of the first formal, peer-reviewed studies on CoT reasoning in different modalities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop an effective methodology for Multimodal Chain-of-Thought (Multimodal-CoT) reasoning in language models, particularly focusing on those under 1 billion parameters. The authors explicitly state their aim to incorporate language (text) and vision (images) modalities into a two-stage framework that separates rationale generation and answer inference.

The key motivations driving this research stem from the limitations of existing CoT approaches and the inherent multimodal nature of human understanding:

- **Mimicking Human Cognition:** The paper opens with a compelling analogy: "Imagine reading a textbook with no figures or tables." This highlights that humans naturally integrate information from various modalities (like text and images) for knowledge acquisition and reasoning. The motivation is to imbue AI models with a similar capability, moving beyond unimodal processing.
- **Addressing Unimodal CoT Limitations:** Existing CoT research has predominantly focused on text-only scenarios. This work is motivated by the need to extend CoT reasoning to multimodal inputs, acknowledging that many real-world problems (e.g., science questions, visual QA) inherently require processing information from multiple sources.
- **Mitigating Hallucination in Smaller LMs:** A critical observation and primary motivation is the challenge faced by smaller language models (under 100 billion parameters) in generating coherent and non-hallucinatory rationales. Unlike very large LLMs that exhibit "emergent abilities" for CoT, smaller models often produce misleading reasoning steps, which can degrade overall performance rather than improve it. The authors aim to combat this "misleading by hallucinated rationales" phenomenon by providing necessary visual context.
- **Overcoming Information Loss from Captioning:** While larger multimodal models might rely on image captioning to convert visual information into text, this process inevitably leads to information loss. The authors are motivated to explore direct multimodal feature fusion to preserve richer information and enable "mutual synergy in the representation space of different modalities."
- **Improving Accessibility and Deployability:** The focus on models "under 1 billion parameters" is a strong practical motivation. Such models are significantly more resource-efficient than LLMs like GPT-3.5 or GPT-4, making them more amenable to fine-tuning, local deployment on consumer-grade GPUs, and circumventing paywalls. This democratizes access to advanced reasoning capabilities.

In summary, the core objective is to propose a robust, efficient, and hallucination-mitigating Multimodal-CoT framework that allows smaller language models to perform complex reasoning by effectively integrating visual and linguistic information, leading to more accurate and reliable answers.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology, named **Multimodal-CoT**, is a two-stage framework designed to leverage both language (text) and vision (images) modalities for improved reasoning. It builds upon the insights gained from an initial empirical analysis of why unimodal CoT fails for smaller models.

#### 3.1. Core Design Principle: Two-Stage Framework

Multimodal-CoT explicitly separates the problem into two distinct stages:
1. **Rationale Generation (QCM → R):** Given a multimodal input (Question, Context, Image, Options), the model first generates an intermediate reasoning chain (rationale).
2. **Answer Inference (QCMR → A):** The generated rationale is then concatenated with the original language input, and this combined information, along with the original vision input, is fed into the model to infer the final answer.

Both stages utilize the **same underlying model structure** but are trained independently on their respective tasks (generating rationales vs. generating answers). During inference, the rationale generated by the first-stage model serves as input for the second-stage model.

#### 3.2. Model Architecture: Multimodal Transformer Backbone

The framework adopts a Transformer-based encoder-decoder architecture, specifically using variants of the T5 model (FLAN-Alpaca, UnifiedQA, FLAN-T5) as the backbone. The key modifications for multimodal processing occur in three main procedures:

- **Encoding:**
 - **Language Encoding:** The textual input (question, context, options in stage 1; question, context, options, *and rationale* in stage 2) is processed by a `LanguageEncoder` (a Transformer encoder) to obtain textual representations (`H_language`).
 - **Vision Extraction:** The image input is fed into a `VisionExtractor` (e.g., a frozen ViT-large encoder) to extract vision features. A learnable projection matrix (`W_h`) then converts these features to match the hidden dimension of the language representation, resulting in `H_vision`.
 - **Handling Missing Vision:** For questions without associated images, all-zero vectors are used as "blank features," signaling the model to disregard visual input.

- **Interaction (Multimodal Fusion):** This is where language and vision representations are combined.
 - **Cross-Attention:** A single-head attention network is employed to correlate text tokens with image patches. The language representation (`H_language`) acts as the query (Q), while the vision features (`H_vision`) act as both key (K) and value (V). This step generates `H_attn_vision`, a vision representation aligned to the language sequence.
 - **Gated Fusion Mechanism:** To integrate `H_language` and `H_attn_vision`, a gated fusion mechanism is applied. This mechanism uses a sigmoid gate (`λ`) to dynamically control the contribution of each modality to the final fused representation (`H_fuse`). This allows for adaptive weighting of language and vision information based on the input.
 - `λ = Sigmoid(W_l * H_language + W_v * H_attn_vision)`
 - `H_fuse = (1 - λ) * H_language + λ * H_attn_vision`

- **Decoding:** The `H_fuse` (the combined multimodal representation) is then fed into a Transformer decoder to generate the target text sequence: either the rationale (in stage 1) or the final answer (in stage 2).

#### 3.3. Motivation for the Two-Stage Design (from initial analysis)

The authors performed an insightful initial experiment using a text-only baseline on the ScienceQA dataset:
- **One-stage "No-CoT" (QCM→A):** Directly predicting the answer achieved 81.63% accuracy.
- **One-stage "Reasoning" (QCM→RA):** Generating rationale *before* answer in a single sequence drastically *decreased* accuracy to 69.32%.
- **One-stage "Explanation" (QCM→AR):** Generating rationale *after* answer also performed poorly (69.68%).

This surprising performance drop with in-sequence CoT for smaller models, contrary to observations in large LLMs, revealed a key problem: **hallucinated rationales**. Analysis showed that the text-only model often generated incorrect rationales that *misled* the subsequent answer inference. This led to the hypothesis that multimodal context is crucial for generating *effective* rationales. Incorporating vision features significantly improved rationale quality and, consequently, answer accuracy, validating the need for multimodal input *during rationale generation* and the separation into two stages to ensure the answer inference benefits from accurate rationales.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of Multimodal-CoT on ScienceQA and A-OKVQA datasets yielded several significant findings:

1. **State-of-the-Art Performance on ScienceQA:**
 - Multimodal-CoT (Large, 738M parameters) achieved an average accuracy of **90.45%** on the ScienceQA benchmark. This represents a substantial improvement over the prior published best result (Chameleon (GPT-4) at 86.54%) and significantly outperforms other VQA models, language-only baselines (UnifiedQA at 74.11%), and even much larger LLMs/LMMs (e.g., GPT-4 at 83.99%, LLaVA 13B at 90.92% which is actually slightly higher but within a very close range and the InstructBLIP at 90.70% is concurrent and the authors achieved SOTA *upon release*). The paper specifically highlights that their model, *under 1 billion parameters*, achieves SOTA. This demonstrates that effective multimodal reasoning is achievable without relying on models of hundreds of billions of parameters.
 - The smaller Multimodal-CoT (Base, 223M parameters) also achieved a strong 85.31% accuracy, outperforming many significantly larger models.

2. **Effectiveness on A-OKVQA:**
 - On the A-OKVQA benchmark, Multimodal-CoT (Base) achieved 50.57% accuracy, outperforming the language-only baseline (47.86%) and other VQA models like ViLBERT (49.1%). This indicates the generalization capability of the approach to different multimodal reasoning tasks.

3. **Validation of Two-Stage Framework and Vision Features (Ablation Study):**
 - Ablation studies (Table 6) conclusively showed the importance of both core components:
 - **Two-Stage Framework:** Removing the two-stage framework (i.e., reverting to a one-stage approach) led to a significant performance drop (e.g., 90.45% to 84.56% for Large model), confirming that separating rationale generation from answer inference is crucial for smaller models.
 - **Vision Features:** Removing vision features (i.e., making it a text-only CoT) caused an even larger drop (e.g., 90.45% to 83.97% for Large model, and 85.31% to 78.57% for Base model). This empirically validates the core hypothesis that multimodal information is essential for generating effective rationales and mitigating hallucination.

4. **Mitigation of Hallucination:**
 - The initial analysis revealed that a significant portion (56%) of errors in the text-only baseline were due to "hallucinated rationales" (e.g., misinterpreting visual information when only provided with text).
 - The integration of vision features in Multimodal-CoT successfully corrected **60.7%** of these hallucination mistakes, as exemplified by the magnet attraction problem where the model without vision hallucinated the pole types, leading to a wrong answer, while the multimodal model correctly identified them.

5. **Enhanced Convergence Speed:**
 - Training curves (Figure 5) showed that Multimodal-CoT (both Base and Large variants) achieved higher accuracy earlier in the training process compared to one-stage baselines. This suggests that the two-stage framework with multimodal input facilitates faster and more stable learning.

6. **Generality Across Backbones and Vision Features:**
 - Multimodal-CoT proved generally effective when implemented with different language model backbones (UnifiedQA, FLAN-T5, FLAN-Alpaca), consistently outperforming prior best unimodal methods.
 - The approach also worked with various vision features (ViT, CLIP, DETR, ResNet), with ViT performing marginally best, demonstrating robustness to feature choices.

7. **Feasibility of Adapting to Non-Annotated Rationales:**
 - A crucial finding for broader applicability is that Multimodal-CoT can leverage rationales generated by *other large models* (e.g., InstructBLIP for image-based, ChatGPT for text-based) instead of relying solely on human-annotated rationales. Using these "pseudo-rationales" for training achieved performance (87.76%) comparable to using human annotations (90.45%), suggesting the method's potential for tasks where human-annotated rationales are unavailable.

8. **Generalization to Out-of-Domain Benchmarks:**
 - Evaluated on the MMMU benchmark (without further training), Multimodal-CoT (738M) achieved 28.7% accuracy. While much lower than large models like GPT-4V or Gemini Ultra (which are orders of magnitude larger), it outperformed several other larger multimodal models (Kosmos-2, Fuyu, OpenFlamingo-2, MiniGPT4-Vicuna), indicating decent zero-shot generalization capabilities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Multimodal Chain-of-Thought Reasoning in Language Models" paper makes several significant contributions that have both scientific and practical implications for the field of artificial intelligence:

1. **Formalizing Multimodal CoT Reasoning:** This work is a pioneering formal study of Chain-of-Thought reasoning in a multimodal context within peer-reviewed scientific literature. By explicitly separating rationale generation and answer inference and integrating vision and language, it lays a foundational framework for future research in multimodal reasoning beyond simple question answering.

2. **Addressing Hallucination in Smaller Models:** A critical scientific contribution is the identification and effective mitigation of the "hallucination" problem in CoT reasoning for smaller language models. The empirical analysis demonstrates that providing relevant visual context during rationale generation is key to preventing models from generating illogical or misleading intermediate steps. This insight is vital for making CoT reasoning robust across different model scales.

3. **Democratizing Advanced AI Reasoning:** By achieving state-of-the-art performance on challenging multimodal reasoning benchmarks with models *under 1 billion parameters*, Multimodal-CoT significantly lowers the barrier to entry for deploying advanced AI capabilities. Unlike massive LLMs that require immense computational resources and often incur costs (paywalls), smaller models can be fine-tuned and run on more accessible hardware, such as consumer-grade GPUs (e.g., 32G memory). This democratizes access to sophisticated multimodal reasoning for researchers and developers with limited resources.

4. **Enhancing Model Efficiency and Stability:** The demonstrated benefits of improved convergence speed highlight the efficiency gains offered by the Multimodal-CoT framework. Faster convergence means less training time and computational cost, contributing to more sustainable AI development. The two-stage design also appears to provide a more stable learning objective for generating complex reasoning chains.

5. **Practical Applicability and Scalability:**
 - The method's general effectiveness across various backbone language models (T5 variants) and different vision feature extractors (ViT, CLIP, DETR, ResNet) demonstrates its robustness and flexibility for integration into diverse systems.
 - The successful demonstration of using *machine-generated rationales* (from larger LLMs/LMMs like InstructBLIP and ChatGPT) instead of human annotations is a critical step towards broader practical applicability. This eliminates the often-prohibitive cost and effort of creating new human-annotated CoT datasets for every new task, paving the way for scaling multimodal CoT to a wider array of real-world problems.

6. **Guiding Future Research:** The detailed error analysis (revealing prevalent commonsense and logical mistakes, particularly concerning map interpretation, counting, and alphabetization) provides clear directions for future research. This includes:
 - Developing more informative visual features and stronger vision-language interaction for fine-grained understanding.
 - Integrating external commonsense knowledge into models.
 - Implementing filtering mechanisms for CoTs to ensure only relevant and correct rationales influence the final answer.

In essence, this paper does not just push the state-of-the-art; it also makes advanced multimodal reasoning more practical and accessible, while rigorously analyzing the underlying mechanisms and pointing towards crucial avenues for future exploration in building more robust and human-like AI reasoning systems. The public availability of the code further contributes to its impact by fostering reproducibility and community-driven development.
