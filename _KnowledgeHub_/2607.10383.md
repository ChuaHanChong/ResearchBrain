# ABot-N1: Toward a General Visual Language Navigation Foundation Model

> [!summary] Summary
> ABot-N1, a Visual Language Navigation foundation model from AMAP CV Lab at Alibaba Group, employs a hierarchical slow-fast architecture to decouple cognition from control, establishing new state-of-the-art performance on five diverse embodied navigation tasks. The model demonstrated substantial improvements, such as an 11.7-point increase in object-goal success rate and a 35.0-point increase for POI-goal navigation, and successful deployment on real-world robots.

## Problem

- Embodied navigation capabilities were traditionally addressed in isolation, leading to task-specific architectures, narrow training data, and limited cross-task transfer, hindering general-purpose agent development.
- Monolithic policy architectures suffered from 'spectral mismatch' between slow semantic reasoning and fast motor control, and 'optimization interference' from conflicting task gradients, resulting in issues like coordinate drift and brittle semantic handling.
- Current policies lacked interpretability, operating as black-box systems without human-readable decision traces, making debugging and safety audits challenging for real-world deployment.

## Method

- Developed **ABot-N1**, a ==hierarchical slow-fast architecture== that asynchronously couples a larger, deliberative ==slow system== (4B VLM) for high-level cognition with a smaller, reactive ==fast system== (2B VLM) for low-level control.
- Implemented a unified ==pixel-goal interface==, where the slow system generates explicit ==Chain-of-Thought (CoT)== reasoning and 2D pixel anchors (affordance or target pixels) as a ==structured bottleneck== for the fast system.
- Pre-trained both systems independently via ==supervised imitation== on a heterogeneous 30M sample corpus covering five navigation tasks, incorporating robustness augmentation, and applied ==Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)== to the slow system for episode-level success alignment.

## Results

- Achieved new state-of-the-art performance on R2R-CE Val-Unseen (NE **3.32m**, SR **70.9%**, SPL **67.5%**), and demonstrated substantial improvements on object-goal (**11.7-point** SR increase) and POI-goal (**35.0-point** SR<2m increase) tasks over previous baselines.
- The multi-task ABot-N1 consistently matched or exceeded single-task specialists across all five navigation tasks, indicating positive cross-task transfer and effective scaling without destructive interference.
- Successfully deployed on an AMap TuTu quadrupedal robot in diverse real-world indoor and outdoor environments, demonstrating robust performance in complex tasks like obstacle avoidance, object search, POI localization, and person tracking.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling cognition from control through a hierarchical slow-fast architecture effectively mitigates 'spectral mismatch' and 'optimization interference', enhancing generality and robustness in embodied navigation.
> - The explicit Chain-of-Thought (CoT) and visually grounded pixel goals serve as a transparent, structured bottleneck, providing fine-grained interpretability and improving the ability to diagnose navigation failures.
> - A unified pixel-goal interface and multi-task pretraining facilitate positive cross-task transfer, allowing the model to leverage shared physical and semantic priors across diverse navigation tasks without negative interference.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Embodied navigation is a core capability for general-purpose physical agents, requiring agents to perform tasks such as reaching metric targets, following natural language instructions, searching for objects, locating points of interest (POIs), and tracking people in complex, open-world environments. Traditionally, these capabilities have been addressed in isolation, leading to task-specific architectures with bespoke goal interfaces, narrow training data, and limited cross-task transfer. This fragmentation results in a proliferation of specialized models that are challenging to integrate into a single robotic system.

Recent advancements have focused on developing general-purpose navigation foundation models, aiming to unify diverse tasks within a single architecture and training corpus. While these models have demonstrated improved cross-task transfer, the paper identifies several persistent challenges, particularly concerning their transition from benchmarks to real-world robotic deployment:
- **To-Point Navigation:** Inaccuracies in map routing or localization can misplace target coordinates in physically infeasible regions, necessitating explicit traversability reasoning for safe arrival.
- **To-Target Navigation:** End-to-end fine-tuning on object-search trajectories can degrade pre-trained semantic priors essential for open-vocabulary recognition. Moreover, combining "search" and "approach" phases complicates training convergence and failure attribution.
- **Interpretability & Safety:** Monolithic policies that directly map observations to actions often operate as black-box systems, lacking human-readable decision traces or intermediate reasoning steps. This opacity hinders root-cause analysis and safety audits.

The paper posits that these issues stem from monolithic policies that bypass intermediate reasoning, creating a mismatch between semantic reasoning (slow dynamics) and motor control (fast dynamics), and leading to optimization interference from conflicting task gradients. ABot-N1 positions itself within this landscape by proposing a modular, factorized approach that decouples cognition from control, aiming to enhance generality, robustness, and transparency in visual language navigation. This approach builds upon the trend of generalist navigation foundation models (e.g., NavFoM, ABot-N0) and dual-system Vision-Language Navigation (VLN) architectures (often termed "brain-body" decoupling), while also integrating explicit linguistic (Chain-of-Thought - CoT) and structured visual representations (pixel goals) for embodied reasoning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a general Visual Language Navigation (VLN) foundation model that addresses the limitations of current approaches, specifically focusing on generality, robustness, and transparency. The motivation for this objective stems from an analysis of existing monolithic policy architectures, which are identified as the root cause of several challenges in real-world embodied navigation.

The key motivations are:
- **Overcoming Architectural Limitations:** Current monolithic policies struggle with a "spectral mismatch" between the slow dynamics of high-level semantic reasoning and the fast dynamics of low-level motor control. They also suffer from "optimization interference," where diverse task objectives lead to conflicting gradients during training, impeding positive transfer.
- **Mitigating Coordinate Drift and Poor Semantic Handling:** In real-world point-goal navigation, localization or map errors can shift target coordinates into non-traversable areas. For object-goal tasks, end-to-end fine-tuning can erode pre-trained semantic priors and conflate stochastic exploration with deterministic execution, leading to brittle performance on long-tail semantics.
- **Enhancing Interpretability and Safety:** The black-box nature of monolithic policies hinders interpretability, making it difficult to understand decision-making processes, debug failures, and ensure safety in critical applications. The goal is to provide explicit, human-readable decision traces.
- **Unifying Diverse Navigation Tasks:** The research aims to consolidate disparate navigation tasks (point-goal, object-goal, POI-goal, instruction-following, person-following) within a single framework, facilitating the extraction of physical and semantic priors from heterogeneous data and moving beyond a "zoo" of specialized models.

To achieve these objectives, the research proposes a hierarchical slow-fast architecture that decouples cognition from control. This design is intended to enable a low-frequency reasoner to perform explicit planning and a high-frequency controller to handle reactive execution, interacting through a transparent, structured bottleneck.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ABot-N1 employs a hierarchical slow-fast architecture designed to decouple high-level cognition from low-level control. This dual-system approach is complemented by a unified pixel-goal interface and a specialized training methodology.

#### 3.1. Model Architecture

ABot-N1 consists of two asynchronously coupled sub-networks: a slow system for deliberative reasoning and a fast system for reactive control. They share a visual front-end but differ in capacity, operating frequency, and function.

- **Slow System - Deliberative Reasoner:**
 - **Backbone:** Built on a 4B-parameter Vision-Language Model (VLM), specifically Qwen-3.5-4B.
 - **Inputs:** At each invocation, it receives a short buffer of historical front-view frames ($I_{ref,mem}$), a tri-view capture ($I_{ref,tri}$) from left, front, and right cameras, the task specification as a natural language instruction ($g = \ell$), and the previous decision (Chain-of-Thought $C_{n-1}$ and pixel goals $p_{n-1}$) for temporal consistency.
 - **Outputs:**
 - **Chain-of-Thought (CoT) ($C_n$):** Explicit natural-language reasoning traces that interpret intent and ground language to visual entities. Emitted for semantically demanding tasks like instruction-following and object-goal.
 - **Pixel Goals ($p_n$):** A set of 2D anchor points projected onto the current tri-view images. These come in two forms:
 - **Affordance Pixel:** An image-space point indicating a safe, traversable waypoint for forward locomotion (e.g., next safe spot on a path).
 - **Target Pixel:** An image-space point marking the final goal (e.g., an object, POI entrance, tracked person), emitted only when the target is visible or within final approach distance.
 - **Task Specificity:** Different tasks instantiate these pixels distinctly. For point-goal, only an affordance pixel is emitted for traversable paths. For instruction-following, affordance pixels guide along the path, and a target pixel activates near the endpoint. Object-goal uses affordance for exploration and target for the discovered object. POI-goal uses affordance for approach and target for the localized POI entrance. Person-following anchors both to the person's bounding box bottom-center when visible, or the affordance pixel falls back to a future waypoint if the person is temporarily out of view.

- **Fast System - Action Expert:**
 - **Backbone:** Uses Qwen-3.5-2B.
 - **Inputs:** Current memory frames ($I_{cur,mem}$), current real-time tri-view observation ($I_{cur,tri}$), the slow system’s CoT trace ($C_n$) and pixel goals ($p_n$), the slow system’s reference observation ($I_{ref,tri}$) to anchor pixel goals, and the task specification ($g$).
 - **Outputs:** A sequence of $H=5$ continuous SE(2) actions ($a_{t:t+H}$) in the ego-centric frame. Each action includes a 2D position (x, y), a heading angle (sin $\theta$, cos $\theta$), and a binary completion flag ($c_i$). Step sizes are adaptive, larger outdoors and smaller indoors.

- **Asynchronous Inference:** The slow system operates at a low frequency due to its larger parameter count, suitable for deliberative reasoning. The fast system runs at a higher frequency, utilizing the most recent slow-system context to generate smooth, responsive trajectories. It densely tracks the cached pixel goal between slow-system updates, compensating for reasoning latency.

#### 3.2. Pretraining

Both systems are pre-trained independently via supervised imitation on a curated heterogeneous corpus covering five navigation tasks.

- **Training Protocol:**
 - **Initialization:** From pre-trained Qwen-3.5 checkpoints.
 - **Slow System Supervision:** Cross-entropy language-modeling loss for CoT tokens and pixel coordinates.
 - **Fast System Supervision:** Smooth-L1 loss on position and heading angle, combined with a binary arriving loss. Smooth-L1 is viable due to the slow system's commitment to a discrete decision (CoT + pixel goals), which concentrates the waypoint distribution, simplifying the regression problem.
 - **Robustness Augmentation:** Both systems are trained with noise-injected inputs (perturbed target coordinates, noisy prior pixel predictions, simulated asynchronous lag) to tolerate imperfect upstream signals.

- **Pretraining Data Recipe:** A 30M sample corpus spanning photorealistic indoor simulations (Habitat-style, proprietary InteriorGS) and outdoor driving/street-view trajectories, and real-robot teleoperation. Each episode is enriched with CoT rationales and pixel-goal annotations. The data is split into high-level data (13.3M) for the slow system and low-level data (16.4M) for the fast system, with specific pixel configurations per task.
 - **Point-Goal:** 800+ indoor, 22 outdoor 3DGS scenes annotated with occupancy maps.
 - *Slow System (∼2.36M):* Inputs include tri-view, noisy global target, and sometimes history. Perturbs target coordinates to robustify against real-world coordinate drift, forcing the model to rely on visual evidence and walkable-region semantics.
 - *Fast System (∼6.20M):* Inputs include current tri-view, history, slow system’s affordance and reference views, and global target. Supervised by next 5 ego-centric waypoints from sub-optimal and OOD-correction trajectories.
 - **Instruction-Following:** Four streams from ABot-N0 (VLN-CE R2R/RxR on Matterport3D, InteriorGS auxiliaries). Pixel supervision uses a unified geometric pipeline.
 - *VLN-CE R2R/RxR (slow ∼2.25M, fast ∼2.10M):* CoT labels by a two-stage Qwen-3.5-27B annotator (decomposing instructions, aligning sub-instructions).
 - *InteriorGS Auxiliaries (slow ∼2.86M, fast ∼2.57M):* Door-traversal, office-building, and short-horizon primitives. Slow count is slightly larger due to stronger terminal-segment augmentation.
 - **Object-Goal:** OVON 145-scene Habitat subset, and proprietary InteriorGS scenes for long-tail categories.
 - *Slow System (∼110K):* Outputs natural-language path plan, affordance pixel, and object pixel. CoT labels generated via a data flywheel, iteratively refining a distilled VLM (Qwen-2.5-VL) from high-capacity VLM seeds using A* consistency filtering and self-play.
 - *Fast System (∼2.10M):* Inputs current frame with slow system reference (plan, pixels). Randomly masks reference channels to prevent over-reliance. Outputs 5 ego-centric waypoints, supervised by sub-optimal and OOD-correction trajectories.
 - **POI-Goal:** No 3D simulator for commercial-street settings, so only the slow system is supervised.
 - *Seed Annotation (∼275K):* From BridgeNav and POINav, providing (POI name, signage bbox, entrance bbox). MoGe-V2 for monocular depth, occupancy map, and target pixel projection.
 - *VLM Annotator Scaling:* Qwen-3.5-4B annotator distilled from MoGe-V2 to scale to ∼31M street-view pairs, yielding ∼8M valid positive labels. Handles positive (feasible affordance pixel, blocked path) and negative (POI absent) cases.
 - *N1 Slow-System Corpus (∼3.0M):* Tri-views synthesized by stitching positive with negative images.
 - **Person-Following:** Avatar-tracking sequences in Habitat using TrackVLA’s motion trajectories, with A* planning at three following distances.
 - *Scenario Composition:* Single-Target Tracking (STT), Distracted Tracking (DT), and Ambiguity Tracking (AT).
 - *Slow System (∼2.7M):* Affordance/target pixels anchored to target bounding box; target pixel null and affordance fallback to future waypoint when target leaves view.
 - *Fast System (∼3.4M):* Current frame paired with reference frame (0-4 steps earlier). Predicts waypoint sequence, heading, and safety-clearance flag.
 - **Asymmetric Noise Schedules:** Slow system tolerates aggressive noise due to temporal context; fast system uses a conservative schedule, as it is more sensitive to single-frame pixel perturbations.

#### 3.3. Post-Training

Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) is applied to the slow system to align pixel-goal generation with environment-grounded signals, bridging the gap between imitation and episode-level success.

- **Formulation:** For each state, $G$ candidate outputs ($C^{(i)}, p^{(i)}$) are sampled, scored with a composite reward $R^{(i)}$, and group-relative advantages $A^{(i)}$ are formed. An L_GRPO loss is optimized, including a KL anchor against a reference model to prevent unstable exploration.
- **Reward Design:** A composite reward $R = w_f R_{format} + w_t R_{target} + w_o R_{safety}$ (weights sum to one) targets three failure modes.
 - $R_{format}$: Binary indicator for JSON schema adherence.
 - $R_{target}$: Average of per-view matching scores, with an exponential decay of L2 distance. Penalizes missing predictions.
 - $R_{safety}$: Asymmetric exponential penalty based on distance to the nearest non-traversable region. Flat in safe zones, sharp in danger margins.
- **Balance Sampling for GRPO Training Dataset:** GRPO training data is built on the point-goal pretraining corpus, stratified into Safe, Critical, and Danger zones based on proximity to non-traversable regions. Unstable samples are discarded. The sampling proportions (P_safe : P_critical : P_danger = 5:3:2) are guided by Gradient Signal-to-Noise Ratio (GSNR) to ensure balanced gradient signals, with Danger Zone prompts providing crucial high-magnitude repulsive gradients.
- **Training Dynamics and Generality:** Action-balanced batch sampling prevents biased preference. Reward-hacking (target withholding, permanent inaction) is mitigated by balancing reward magnitudes and imposing missing-prediction penalties. The factorization is designed to be task-agnostic, extensible by redefining $R_{target}$.

#### 3.4. Benchmarks

The paper introduces two novel benchmarks to address gaps in existing evaluation frameworks for city-scale navigation.

- **ABotN-PointBench:**
 - **Motivation:** Addresses scene homogeneity, open-loop protocols, and ignored social rules in prior point-goal benchmarks. It is a closed-loop, high-fidelity benchmark for indoor and outdoor point-goal navigation.
 - **Scene Construction:** 31 real-world scenes (16 indoor, 15 outdoor) reconstructed as high-fidelity 3DGS environments, manually annotated with fine-grained walkability maps for legal traversable regions.
 - **Episode Generation:** A* on occupancy maps for shortest feasible paths (465 reference trajectories). Targets stratified by distance (5–50m outdoors, 3–20m indoors) for short- to long-horizon tasks.
 - **Difficulty Stratification:** Outdoor (15 scenes, 5 episodes/scene/tier): Low (5–20m), Medium (20–35m), High (35–50m). Uses SR<3col (success with fewer than 3 collisions) to account for annotation noise. Indoor (16 scenes, 8 Low + 8 High): 3–20m. Uses SR<1col (strict zero-collision) due to precise annotations.
 - **Metrics:** Success Rate (SR) and Success weighted by Path Length (SPL).

- **ABotN-POIBench:**
 - **Motivation:** Addresses limitations of open-loop or coarse-grained POI-goal benchmarks, demanding final-meters arrival at physical entrances. First closed-loop, high-fidelity benchmark for real-world POI-goal navigation.
 - **Scene Construction:** 11 real commercial regions (∼126,398 m^2) reconstructed as 3DGS scenes using LiDAR-Inertial SLAM, global multi-sensor optimization, 3D Gaussian initialization, and dense triangle mesh extraction for collision geometry.
 - **POI and Entrance Annotation:** 163 distinct POIs, each with a name and a ground-flush horizontal bounding frame as the physical entrance (ground-truth arrival region).
 - **Start Sampling:** Multiple start poses per POI within a radius, under traversability-aware constraints (walkable regions, no grass/steps/large obstacles), ensuring partial signage visibility. Reference trajectories generated by A* on 2D top-down occupancy grids.
 - **Metrics:** Success Rate at a 2m entrance threshold (SR<2m) and SPL.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

ABot-N1 was evaluated across five core tasks in simulation and further validated through real-world deployment. The results demonstrate its capabilities in general visual language navigation.

#### 4.1. Simulation Evaluation

ABot-N1 (the joint multi-task checkpoint) and ABot-N1† (the single-task fine-tuned variant) were compared against baselines using standard metrics for each task.

- **Instruction-Following (VLN-CE R2R/RxR):**
 - ABot-N1 established new state-of-the-art performance on R2R-CE Val-Unseen with an NE of 3.32m, SR of 70.9%, and SPL of 67.5%. This surpassed all baselines, including those using depth and odometry, while utilizing only a front-left-right tri-view of RGB.
 - On RxR-CE Val-Unseen, ABot-N1 achieved the best NE (3.13m) and competitive SR (73.9%) and SPL (63.9%), ranking second to Qwen-RobotNav-4B which used larger in-domain RxR data.
 - ABot-N1 (multi-task) demonstrated positive cross-task transfer, matching or exceeding ABot-N1† (single-task specialist) on both splits (e.g. +2.63 SR on R2R, +2.95 SR on RxR). The affordance pixel re-grounding and explicit CoT were attributed to these gains.

- **Object-Goal (Short-Horizon OVON):**
 - ABot-N1 improved SR by 11.7 points and SPL by 16.4 points over ABot-N0, reducing the average Distance-to-Goal (DTG) at termination from 1.44m to 0.82m (a 1.8x reduction).
 - ABot-N1† achieved slightly higher SR (85.5%) and lower DTG (0.59m), while ABot-N1 still exceeded the specialist on SPL (51.8% vs. 50.8%). This indicates strong cross-task transfer and a framework advantage by decoupling recognition (object pixel) from approach (affordance pixel).

- **Point-Goal (ABotN-PointBench):**
 - **Outdoor:** ABot-N1 achieved 92.9% overall SR<3col, improving over ABot-N0 by 16.0 SR. Gains were pronounced on the hardest tier (88.0% vs. 65.3% SR, a 22.7-point jump), demonstrating safe and socially compliant navigation by re-grounding trajectories onto traversable pathways. ABot-N1† (92.0% SR) already outperformed baselines, and ABot-N1 further improved, suggesting transfer of social-rule semantics from other tasks.
 - **Indoor:** ABot-N1 achieved 95.4% SR<1col, a 5.8-point gain over ABot-N0 (89.6%), with improvements concentrated on the High-difficulty set (95.8% vs. 87.5%), indicating robust generalization in complex interior layouts.

- **POI-Goal (ABotN-POIBench):**
 - ABot-N1 achieved new state-of-the-art with 77.3% SR<2m (+35.0 points over POINav) and 72.6% SPL (+32.3 points).
 - ABot-N1† already achieved 69.9% SR<2m, confirming framework advantage. ABot-N1 benefited from cross-task synergy, with its fast controller leveraging point-goal data and its slow system benefiting from object-goal-style visibility reasoning.

- **Person-Following (EVT-Bench):**
 - ABot-N1 led on all SR metrics and on TR for Distracted Tracking (DT) and Ambiguity Tracking (AT) splits, matching the top STT TR within 0.2 points.
 - On STT, it achieved 90.1% SR / 89.8% TR, surpassing ABot-N0 by +3.2 SR and +2.2 TR.
 - On AT, it achieved 70.0% SR / 87.8% TR, improving over ABot-N0 by +2.7 SR and +8.3 TR, confirming re-identification across severe occlusions.
 - On DT, TR increased from 75.4% to 84.4% (+9.0 points), indicating strengthened identity reasoning under distractor interference. A trade-off was observed with slightly higher CR on DT/AT (17.9% vs. 11.6% / 7.05%).

- **Cross-Task Summary:** ABot-N1 consistently improved over ABot-N0 across all five tasks' primary metrics. The multi-task model either matched or exceeded single-task specialists, indicating positive cross-task transfer and effective scaling without destructive interference, attributed to the unified pixel-goal interface.

#### 4.2. Real-World Deployment

ABot-N1 was deployed on an AMap TuTu quadrupedal robot in diverse indoor and outdoor environments.

- **Hardware Setting:** AMap TuTu, equipped with omnidirectional LiDAR, RTK-GNSS, and a 270° Tri-Camera array. Compute module upgraded to Jetson AGX Orin 64 GB for concurrent dual slow-fast VLN inference with real-time perception and control.
- **Edge Deployment Scheme:** Full slow-fast pipeline deployed at 10 Hz. Slow system used Qwen-3.5-2B, fast system used a DiT with 306M parameters and a DINOv2-Base encoder. Asynchronous execution maximized GPU utilization while maintaining responsiveness.
- **Deployment Results (Qualitative):**
 - **Point-Goal Navigation:** Demonstrated obstacle avoidance on narrow roads, construction-zone detours, correct fork selection, and traffic-light-compliant crosswalk traversal. The affordance pixel guidance enabled safe, rule-compliant navigation in complex outdoor scenarios.
 - **Object-Goal Navigation:** Located a park bench under challenging lighting, identified a specific chair with a water bottle (spatial reasoning), and found a partially occluded fire extinguisher. The affordance pixel guided on safe surfaces, while the target pixel locked onto the object, validating the decoupled recognition-and-approach.
 - **POI-Goal Navigation:** Successfully located commercial storefronts by recognizing signage (e.g., Lanzhou noodle restaurant at oblique angles, McDonald’s with obstacle avoidance, Luckin Coffee with slope navigation and staircase avoidance). This demonstrated both fine-grained visual recognition and path-feasibility reasoning.
 - **Instruction-Following:** Executed a multi-step instruction (e.g., "Walk down the stairs... approach and stop directly in front of the bar counter"). The explicit CoT decomposed long instructions into sub-goals, with affordance and target pixels guiding each segment.
 - **Person-Following:** Maintained stable tracking despite distractors, followed a target ascending stairs, and smoothly tracked a target around sharp indoor corners with temporary occlusion. This confirmed the slow system's identity persistence and the fast system's reactive control and stable locomotion.

- **Summary of Deployment:** The real-world experiments validated the slow-fast architecture's ability to combine high-level semantic reasoning (instruction decomposition, open-vocabulary recognition, spatial reasoning, rule-aware decision-making, identity persistence) from the slow system with smooth, precise, and reactive control from the fast system. Asynchronous coupling managed latency and odometric drift.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ABot-N1 contributes to the field of embodied artificial intelligence by presenting a vision-language navigation foundation model that addresses several challenges in achieving general, robust, and interpretable navigation.

- **Architectural Advancement:** The introduction of a hierarchical slow-fast architecture that explicitly decouples high-level cognition from low-level control represents an architectural approach to VLN. This design is posited to mitigate coordinate drift, improve handling of long-tail semantics, and enhance interpretability by allowing distinct computational processes to operate at appropriate frequencies and interact through a structured bottleneck (CoT and pixel goals).
- **Enhanced Generality and Robustness:** The model demonstrates state-of-the-art performance across five diverse navigation tasks (point-goal, instruction-following, object-goal, POI-goal, and person-following) from a single multi-task checkpoint. The multi-task model consistently matches or exceeds the performance of single-task specialists, indicating positive cross-task transfer rather than negative interference. This suggests a step toward truly versatile navigators that can operate in open-world environments with varying conditions.
- **Improved Interpretability and Safety:** By generating explicit Chain-of-Thought reasoning alongside visually grounded pixel anchors, ABot-N1 offers fine-grained interpretability. This transparency is critical for safety-sensitive applications, as it enables clearer root-cause analysis of failures, distinguishing between semantic reasoning errors and spatial targeting inaccuracies.
- **Advancement in Urban-Scale Navigation:** The research introduces and publicly releases two novel benchmarks, ABotN-PointBench and ABotN-POIBench, for city-scale navigation. These benchmarks cover complex indoor and outdoor environments, and realistic commercial districts, facilitating the comprehensive evaluation and advancement of methods for robust long-horizon autonomy, particularly in scenarios where traditional high-precision mapping infrastructure is not available.
- **Real-World Applicability:** The successful deployment of ABot-N1 on a quadrupedal robot in diverse real-world environments validates the model's practical applicability and its ability to generalize from simulation. This demonstrates a viable path toward deployable embodied navigation agents.
- **Future Directions:** The slow-fast separation treats semantic recognition and motor action as distinct data streams, enabling independent expansion of training corpora for perception and control. This data-centric scaling strategy is planned for further verification in future iterations, indicating a roadmap for continued development in open-world scaling.
