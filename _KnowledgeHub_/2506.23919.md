# Goal-VLA: Image-Generative VLMs as Object-Centric World Models Empowering Zero-shot Robot Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the National University of Singapore developed Goal-VLA, a hierarchical framework for zero-shot robot manipulation that utilizes image-generative Vision-Language Models as an object-centric world model. The system translates visual goal states into precise 3D object poses, achieving an average 59.9% success rate on RLBench tasks and 60% in real-world experiments without task-specific training.

## Problem

- Robotic manipulation methods struggle with generalization across varied environments, tasks, objects, and robot embodiments.
- Existing end-to-end Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models rely heavily on extensive, costly datasets of paired instruction-vision-action data, limiting their zero-shot generalization capabilities.
- Hierarchical VLA models face challenges with intermediate representations, as sparse ones lack geometric precision and dense visual ones often require specific training for low-level policies, while VLMs demonstrate inaccuracies in direct 3D spatial reasoning.

## Method

- **Goal-VLA** uses image-generative VLMs (e.g., Gemini 2.5 Flash-image) as an object-centric world model to generate concrete visual goal images from abstract natural language instructions and initial observations.
- It incorporates a "Reflection-through-Synthesis" loop, where a VLM (e.g., Gemini 2.5 Pro) iteratively critiques and refines generated goal images for semantic plausibility and physical feasibility.
- A dedicated spatial grounding module computes precise 3D target object poses by establishing pixel-level correspondences and performing point-cloud registration, enabling training-free low-level control for the robot manipulator.

## Results

- Goal-VLA achieved an average success rate of **59.9%** across eight diverse zero-shot RLBench simulation tasks, substantially outperforming baselines like OpenVLA (**0.2%**) and MOKA (**26.0%**).
- In real-world experiments, the framework demonstrated a **60%** average success rate on four diverse manipulation tasks with a UFACTORY robot, surpassing real-world baselines such as MOKA (**22.5%**) and MolmoAct (**27.5%**).
- Ablation studies confirmed that both Input Enhancement and the Reflection-through-Synthesis process are critical, with the latter boosting the average success rate from **40.0%** to **88.8%** with three reflection iterations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling high-level semantic goal reasoning (via VLM-generated images) from precise low-level spatial grounding is key to achieving robust zero-shot generalization in robotic manipulation.
> - An object-centric visual goal state, derived from an image-generative VLM, can serve as a potent interface, providing both semantic understanding and explicit spatial guidance for robot control.
> - An iterative self-correction mechanism, termed "Reflection-through-Synthesis," significantly enhances the robustness and accuracy of generated visual goals by allowing the system to refine potentially infeasible or incorrect outcomes.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation faces a fundamental challenge in generalization, where methods performing well in specific settings often falter under minor variations in environments, tasks, objects, or robot embodiments. Recent advancements in foundation models, particularly Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs), have spurred the development of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. These models aim to connect multimodal perception (vision and language) with robotic action.

Current VLA paradigms are broadly categorized into two approaches:
1. **End-to-end VLA models:** These models directly map visual and linguistic inputs to low-level robot actions. Examples include RT-1/2, OpenVLA, and MolmoAct. A significant limitation of this approach is its heavy reliance on extensive datasets of paired instruction-vision-action data, which are costly and time-consuming to acquire in sufficient diversity, thus restricting their zero-shot generalization capabilities.
2. **Hierarchical VLA models:** This approach decouples the problem by using a VLM for high-level planning to generate an intermediate representation, which then guides a separate low-level policy. However, the design of this intermediate representation presents a critical challenge:
 - **Sparse or symbolic representations** (e.g., language descriptions, keypoints, symbolic skills, or code) often lack the precise geometric detail required for complex manipulation tasks (e.g., SayCan, MOKA).
 - **Dense visual representations** (e.g., subgoal images or videos) provide rich information but typically necessitate training the low-level policy to interpret them, thereby reintroducing data dependency and undermining the zero-shot objective (e.g., SUSIE, 3D-VLA).
 - **Implicit spatial guidance** (e.g., value maps or reward functions) requires the VLM to perform precise 3D spatial reasoning, a capability where current VLMs often demonstrate inherent limitations and inaccuracies (e.g., VoxPoser, IKER).

Goal-VLA positions itself within the hierarchical VLA paradigm. It addresses the limitations of existing hierarchical approaches by leveraging the strengths of image-generative VLMs for semantic goal reasoning while explicitly decoupling precise spatial grounding. The paper argues that an object-centric goal state, derived from a VLM-generated image, can serve as a "golden interface" that provides both semantic understanding and explicit spatial guidance, enabling zero-shot, training-free low-level control, and cross-embodiment generalization. This approach distinguishes itself from agent-centric world models by focusing on the semantic goal in image space, thereby enhancing generalizability across different robot kinematics.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the generalization capabilities of robotic manipulation systems, specifically focusing on achieving robust zero-shot performance in diverse and unstructured environments.

The motivation for Goal-VLA stems from the identified limitations within current VLA models:
- **Lack of Zero-shot Generalization:** Existing end-to-end VLA models are fundamentally constrained by the scarcity of diverse paired instruction-vision-action data. This data dependency impedes their ability to generalize to novel tasks, objects, or environments without extensive fine-tuning.
- **Intermediate Representation Dilemma in Hierarchical VLAs:**
 - **Insufficient Spatial Precision:** Sparse intermediate representations in hierarchical models often do not provide enough geometric detail for complex manipulation, limiting their effectiveness for tasks requiring fine motor control.
 - **Data Dependency for Dense Visual Goals:** While dense visual representations like goal images offer rich context, they typically require the low-level policy to be specifically trained to interpret them, negating the zero-shot objective.
 - **VLM Weakness in Spatial Reasoning:** VLMs, while strong in semantic understanding, frequently exhibit inaccuracies when required to ground their reasoning precisely in 3D space for generating implicit spatial guidance (e.g., value maps).

To address these issues, the authors propose a core insight: effective zero-shot generalization in a low-level policy necessitates explicit spatial guidance. They argue that a decoupled architecture can capitalize on VLMs' semantic reasoning strengths for high-level goal generation while delegating precise spatial grounding to a dedicated module.

The specific objectives of Goal-VLA are:
1. **Develop an Object-Centric World Model:** To utilize Image-Generative VLMs as an object-centric world model that generates a visual representation of the desired goal state (a goal image). This model focuses on the semantic goal in image space, abstracting away robot kinematics for broader generalizability.
2. **Derive Precise Target Object Poses:** To translate the VLM-generated visual goal into explicit and precise 3D target object poses. This object-centric representation is intended to be inherently generalizable across various tasks, environments, object categories, and robot embodiments.
3. **Implement Robust Goal Refinement:** To introduce an iterative refinement process, termed "Reflection-through-Synthesis," that validates and improves the generated goal image for plausibility and accuracy before execution. This mechanism aims to enhance robustness by enabling self-correction of potentially infeasible or incorrect initial visual goals.
4. **Enable Training-Free Low-Level Control:** To provide an explicit, spatially rich object pose that can effectively guide a low-level policy without requiring task-specific training or fine-tuning, thereby achieving true zero-shot manipulation.
5. **Demonstrate Cross-Domain Generalization:** To evaluate the framework's effectiveness and generalizability across diverse manipulation tasks in both simulated (RLBench) and real-world settings, using only a user’s task description and single-view RGB-D input.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Goal-VLA is a decoupled hierarchical framework that translates a single-view RGB-D image observation and a natural language task description into executable robot actions. The framework is structured into three main stages: Goal State Reasoning, Spatial Grounding, and Low-level Policy.

#### 3.1. Goal State Reasoning

This module translates the user's abstract language instruction into a concrete and plausible visual goal, represented by a goal image (I') and an associated metric depth map (D').
- **Prompt Enhancement:** The initial RGB image (I) and the natural language task description (L) are fed into a text-output VLM (e.g., Gemini 2.5 Pro). This VLM enriches the concise instruction with context-specific details inferred from the visual input, generating a more descriptive and detailed enhanced prompt (L_e).
- **Reflection-through-Synthesis Loop:** This iterative refinement process generates and validates candidate goal images.
 - **Goal Image Generation:** The enhanced prompt (L_e) and the initial image (I) are provided to a pre-trained image-generation VLM (e.g., Gemini 2.5 Flash-image, also known as Nano Banana). This VLM produces a candidate goal image (I'_cand) depicting the desired outcome.
 - **Synthesis for Inspection:** To facilitate evaluation, the target object is segmented from I'_cand using a segmentation model (e.g., Grounded SAM). This segmented "virtual image" of the target object is then overlaid onto the initial scene (I) with partial transparency. This in-context visualization helps bridge the semantic gap for robust evaluation.
 - **Reflection and Refinement:** The synthesized image (I_synth), along with the original instruction (L) and current enhanced prompt (L_e), is passed to a Reflector VLM (e.g., Gemini 2.5 Pro). The Reflector assesses if the synthesized outcome aligns with the task's semantic requirements and physical plausibility. If the goal is deemed valid, the loop terminates. If not, the Reflector generates a revised prompt (L_revised) containing corrective feedback, which is then used for the subsequent image generation iteration. This loop continues for a maximum number of iterations.
- **Final Output Generation:** Once a goal image is accepted, a depth estimation model (e.g., Depth-Anything V2) generates a relative depth map (D') for the goal image. Simultaneously, Grounded SAM extracts the target object masks (M and M') for both the initial and the validated goal images.

#### 3.2. Spatial Grounding

This module computes the precise 3D transformation (rotation R and translation t) required to move the object from its initial pose to its goal pose.
- **Semantic Matching:** Robust pixel-level correspondences are established between the initial image (I) and the goal image (I'). Traditional methods are insufficient due to potential appearance changes in VLM-generated images. Therefore, the Geo-Aware model is used to extract pixel-wise semantic features (f, f') from I and I'. Pixels are matched by finding the highest cosine similarity between their semantic features, generating a pixel mapping F: (x, y) → (x', y').
- **Point-cloud Registration:** This stage computes the 3D spatial transformation using depth information.
 - **Depth Alignment:** The estimated depth map for the goal image (D') is scaled to match the metric scale of the ground-truth initial depth map (D). A linear transformation (scale factor s1 and bias b) is calibrated via least squares regression on background pixels (pixels not covered by initial or goal object masks) to prevent manipulation-induced changes from skewing the alignment. The learned parameters are applied to D' to yield D'_rescaled.
 - **Object Point Cloud Extraction:** Object-specific 3D point clouds (P and P') are extracted from the initial and goal frames using their respective masks (M and M') and depth maps (D and D'_rescaled).
 - **Transformation Computation:** Using the established 2D pixel correspondences (F), corresponding point pairs in P and P' are identified and filtered. The Umeyama algorithm is then applied to these point sets to compute a similarity transformation (scale factor s2, rotation R, and translation t) that maps the initial object point cloud to the goal object point cloud.

#### 3.3. Low-level Policy

This module translates the object goal pose into executable robot actions.
- **Contact Module:** A multi-stage, sample-based method determines a feasible initial contact pose (P_contact) on the object's point cloud. Candidate poses are generated on the object's surface (oriented along local normals), pruned for valid orientation and collision-free interaction with the robot's end-effector, and then scored using geometric heuristics. The highest-scoring pose is selected.
- **Goal Pose Computing:** Assuming the relative pose between the gripper and the object remains constant after initial contact, the gripper's final goal pose (P_goal) is calculated by applying the object's transformation (R, t) from the Spatial Grounding module to the gripper's initial pose (derived from P_contact).
- **Motion Planning Module:** A collision-free trajectory is generated for the robot manipulator from its current configuration to the computed target goal pose (P_goal). In simulation, a built-in sample-based planner is used; in real-world experiments, a sample-based motion planner generates the path in the robot's configuration space.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research conducted experiments in both simulated (RLBench) and real-world environments to evaluate Goal-VLA's performance, the effectiveness of its components, and its generalizability.

#### 4.1. Simulation Experiments (Q1)

Goal-VLA was evaluated on eight diverse manipulation tasks from the RLBench benchmark in a zero-shot setting, meaning no task-specific fine-tuning was performed. Each task was evaluated across 10 random seeds, with 10 trials per seed (totaling 100 runs per task).

- **Overall Performance:** Goal-VLA achieved an average success rate of 59.9% across all tasks.
- **Comparison with Baselines:**
 - **End-to-end VLAs:** OpenVLA and Pi0 demonstrated minimal to no success (0.2% and 0% respectively), indicating their reliance on extensive paired action data and brittleness in zero-shot scenarios.
 - **Hierarchical VLAs:** MOKA (keypoints) achieved 26.0%, MolmoAct (trajectories) achieved 11.3%, and VoxPoser (3D value maps) achieved 5.8%. These methods generally lagged behind Goal-VLA, suggesting limitations in their intermediate representations for precise and generalizable manipulation.
- **Conclusion:** Goal-VLA's object-centric goal state, providing explicit 3D pose information, demonstrated superior precision and robustness compared to other methods that either depend on task-specific training or utilize less precise intermediate representations.

#### 4.2. Ablation Study (Q2)

An ablation study was conducted to quantify the contribution of two key components: Input Enhancement and the Reflection-through-Synthesis process.

- **Impact of Input Enhancement:** Adding the Input Enhancement module, which enriches the user's prompt with visual context, led to a substantial improvement in success rate, increasing it by 27.5 percentage points from a baseline of 40.0% to 67.5%.
- **Impact of Reflector (Reflection-through-Synthesis):** The Reflector module alone, enabling iterative critique and refinement of generated goals, also yielded a significant gain of 11.2 percentage points.
- **Combined Effect and Iterations:** The full Goal-VLA model, combining both Input Enhancement and the Reflection-through-Synthesis process, performed optimally. With a maximum of 1 reflection iteration, the success rate reached 83.8%. Increasing the maximum reflection iterations to 3 further boosted the average success rate to 88.8%.
- **Conclusion:** Both Input Enhancement and the Reflection-through-Synthesis mechanism are critical and complementary components that contribute to the robustness and accuracy of the high-level reasoning process.

#### 4.3. Real-World Experiments (Q3)

Goal-VLA was tested on four diverse real-world manipulation tasks: Tomato Placement, Table Sweeping, Weighing Duck, and Bottle Stand-Up. Each task involved 10 trials.

- **Overall Performance:** Goal-VLA achieved a 60% average success rate across the real-world tasks.
- **Comparison with Baselines:** Goal-VLA significantly outperformed real-world baselines: MOKA (22.5%), MolmoAct (27.5%), and OpenVLA (0%).
- **Task-Specific Performance:** Goal-VLA demonstrated strong performance in Tomato Placement (9/10 success) and Weighing Duck (7/10 success). It achieved moderate success in Table Sweeping (4/10 success) and Bottle Stand-Up (4/10 success).
- **Generalization:** The framework's success across diverse tasks, objects, environments (simulated and real), and different robot embodiments (Franka in simulation, UFACTORY in real-world) without task-specific fine-tuning indicates strong generalization capabilities.

#### 4.4. Failure Cases Analysis

Analysis of real-world experiments identified typical failure modes:
- **Spatial Grounding Module Failures:** This was a primary cause of failure in precision-demanding tasks. Inaccuracies in estimated depth (e.g., for "Weighing Duck") and orientation (e.g., for "Bottle Stand-Up") from this module often led to task failure.
- **High-level Reasoning Module Failures:** For tasks requiring complex semantic understanding to generate feasible goal images (e.g., "Table Sweeping" for a sweeping motion), occasional failures occurred at the reasoning stage.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Goal-VLA presents a contribution to the field of robotic manipulation by addressing the challenge of zero-shot generalization and the limitations of existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models.

- **Enhanced Generalization:** By decoupling high-level semantic reasoning from precise low-level spatial control, Goal-VLA enables a VLM to function as an object-centric world model. This approach moves toward more robust generalization across varied tasks, objects, environments, and robot embodiments, a critical hurdle for deploying autonomous robots in unstructured settings.
- **Enabling Zero-shot Manipulation:** The framework’s ability to generate an explicit 3D object pose from a visual goal state allows a low-level policy to perform manipulation tasks without requiring paired action data or task-specific fine-tuning. This significantly reduces the data dependency and cost associated with training traditional robotic policies.
- **Leveraging and Mitigating VLM Capabilities:** Goal-VLA effectively utilizes the strong semantic understanding and image generation capabilities of modern VLMs for high-level planning. Simultaneously, it strategically mitigates the inherent imprecision of VLMs in direct 3D spatial reasoning by externalizing the exact 3D transformation computation to a dedicated spatial grounding module.
- **Improved Robustness through Reflection:** The introduction of the Reflection-through-Synthesis mechanism adds a self-correction loop to the goal generation process. This iterative refinement of visual goals based on contextual feedback enhances the plausibility and feasibility of the robot's intended actions, contributing to overall system robustness.
- **Pathway for Future Research:** The object-centric world model paradigm and the decoupled architecture proposed in Goal-VLA could serve as a foundation for developing more advanced and generalizable robotic systems. It suggests a promising direction for creating agents that can interpret abstract instructions and execute them physically with minimal prior knowledge.
- **Practical Implications:** The demonstrated performance in both simulation and real-world scenarios, coupled with its zero-shot nature, indicates a potential for practical applications where robots need to adapt to novel situations without extensive reprogramming or training. This could accelerate the deployment of intelligent robots in domains such as household assistance, manufacturing, and logistics.
