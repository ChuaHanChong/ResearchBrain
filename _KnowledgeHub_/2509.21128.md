# RL Squeezes, SFT Expands: A Comparative Study of Reasoning LLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from The University of Tokyo quantitatively compared reasoning in large language models after Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning (RL), establishing that RL prunes incorrect reasoning paths and concentrates functionality, while SFT expands the diversity of correct trajectories and distributes functional importance. This work provides a mechanistic explanation for the empirical success of combined SFT+RL training strategies in enhancing LLM reasoning.

## Problem

- Existing research predominantly relies on outcome-based metrics like Pass@k, overlooking the qualitative changes in the underlying reasoning processes of LLMs.
- The distinct and complementary roles of SFT and RL in shaping LLM reasoning mechanisms remain largely unclarified, leading to 'trial-and-error' approaches in current training methodologies.
- A debate persists regarding whether RL with verifiable rewards (RLVR) develops new reasoning capabilities or primarily elicits existing ones from pre-trained base models.

## Method

- The authors developed a novel analytical framework to investigate LLM reasoning paths at two granularities: a ==trajectory-level analysis== and a ==step-level analysis==.
- Trajectory-level analysis involved clustering unique correct and incorrect reasoning outputs based on character n-gram similarity (chrF) to quantify their diversity.
- Step-level analysis constructed =='reasoning graphs'== by segmenting responses into logical expressions, embedding them into a shared space, and then analyzing their global topological properties and local ==graphlet structures==.

## Results

- RL consistently reduced the number of unique incorrect trajectories and, notably, also reduced the number of unique correct trajectories, which helps explain why base models might outperform RL models at high Pass@k values.
- RL models exhibited significantly steeper exponential decay rates (e.g., ~**2.5 times** higher than Base models) for reasoning graph metrics, indicating a concentration of key functionalities into fewer steps.
- SFT models showed flatter decay rates (e.g., reduced to about **one-third** of Base models), signifying a more uniform distribution of reasoning functionalities and a shift from modular to globally connected graph structures.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - RL 'squeezes' reasoning by pruning incorrect trajectories and concentrating functional importance into a smaller, more specialized subset of reasoning steps, leading to a hub-centralized graph structure.
> - SFT 'expands' reasoning by increasing the diversity of correct trajectories and distributing functional importance more uniformly across a broader range of reasoning steps, creating a globally connected graph.
> - The two-stage SFT+RL procedure achieves maximal Pass@1 performance by first leveraging SFT to expand the repertoire of correct solution paths, then using RL to effectively compress and eliminate incorrect ones.
