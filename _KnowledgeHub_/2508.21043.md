# HITTER: A HumanoId Table TEnnis Robot via Hierarchical Planning and Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> UC Berkeley researchers developed "HITTER," a humanoid table tennis robot employing a hierarchical planning and learning framework that enables agile, whole-body striking motions. The system achieved a 92.3% return rate for incoming balls and sustained 106 consecutive shots in rallies against human opponents on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots traditionally struggle with rapid and agile interaction with dynamic environments through manipulation, especially in tasks requiring sub-second reaction times.
- Prior humanoid table tennis efforts were limited to static robot positions, severely restricting effective hitting range and agility.
- Integrating precise perception, prediction, planning, and whole-body action in dynamic scenarios like table tennis presents a significant challenge for humanoids.

## Method

- A hierarchical framework combines a model-based planner for precise ball trajectory prediction and racket target planning with a reinforcement learning-based whole-body controller for motion execution.
- The learning-based controller, trained in simulation using PPO and human motion references, generates agile, human-like striking motions, including rapid base repositioning.
- The system leverages high-precision motion capture for ball tracking and deploys the learned policy zero-shot on a general-purpose ==Unitree G1== humanoid robot.

## Results

- The model-based planner achieved position prediction errors below **7.5** cm and time prediction errors below 20 ms, **0.5** and **0.3** seconds before a strike, respectively.
- The integrated system demonstrated a **96.2%** hit rate and a **92.3%** return rate for incoming balls, consistently using strategic forehand/backhand strokes.
- The robot engaged in extended rallies, achieving a record of 106 consecutive shots against a human opponent and sustaining continuous rallies in humanoid-humanoid matches.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The hierarchical approach effectively combines the strengths of model-based planning (prediction accuracy) and learning-based control (adaptability, human-like motion generation) for complex dynamic tasks.
> - Incorporating human motion references into the reward function significantly enhances the naturalness and effectiveness of the robot's striking movements and whole-body coordination, including waist rotation.
> - Commanding target base positions to the controller, rather than velocities, enables rapid, single-step lateral movements for repositioning, crucial for covering the table effectively.
