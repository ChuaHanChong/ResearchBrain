# Spatial-Aware Decision-Making with Ring Attractors in Reinforcement Learning Systems

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the University of Manchester and Zhejiang Normal University developed a framework integrating neuroscience-inspired ring attractors into Reinforcement Learning systems to improve spatial-aware decision-making. This approach, implemented as a Deep Learning module, enhances action selection by explicitly encoding spatial relationships and uncertainty, yielding an average 53% improvement on the Atari 100k benchmark.

## Problem

- Traditional Reinforcement Learning (RL) often struggles with efficient action selection in spatially structured environments, as flattened action spaces can obscure critical spatial relationships.
- Existing spatial awareness methods in RL frequently lack the biological plausibility and inherent continuity observed in neural circuits.
- There is a need to effectively integrate uncertainty quantification into spatially aware action selection to improve learning reliability in complex environments.

## Method

- The authors developed a Continuous-Time Recurrent Neural Network (CTRNN) ==ring attractor model== that processes Q-values and uncertainty estimates to dynamically select actions in a circular action space.
- A Deep Learning (DL) module was created using Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) with a fixed, distance-dependent circular input-to-hidden connectivity and learnable hidden-to-hidden recurrent connections, enabling efficient integration into Deep RL agents.
- Uncertainty quantification was incorporated using Bayesian Linear Regression and Thompson Sampling to generate mean and variance estimates of Q-values, which then inform the ring attractor's input signals for more informed action selection.

## Results

- The CTRNN-based ring attractor models (BDQNRA, BDQNRA-UA) consistently outperformed the baseline BDQN in Super Mario Bros and Highway environments, with the uncertainty-aware variant (BDQNRA-UA) achieving the highest cumulative reward.
- The DL-based ring attractor (DDQNRA) demonstrated consistent outperformance against standard DDQN in both Super Mario Bros and Highway, indicating enhanced learning speed and overall performance.
- The EffZeroRA model achieved an average **53%** increase in human-normalized scores across the Atari 100k benchmark, with improvements exceeding **100%** in games with strong spatial components such as Asterix (**110%**) and Boxing (**105%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Ring attractors provide a biologically plausible and effective mechanism for explicitly encoding spatial information and relationships between actions, which accelerates the learning rate of RL agents.
> - Integrating uncertainty awareness with spatial action distribution through ring attractors leads to more robust and higher performance in sequential decision-making tasks.
> - A recurrent neural network architecture can successfully emulate the dynamics of ring attractors, offering a computationally efficient and highly integrable solution for modern Deep Reinforcement Learning frameworks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research integrates neuroscientific models, specifically ring attractors, into Reinforcement Learning (RL) systems to enhance action selection, particularly in environments characterized by spatial structures. The work is positioned at the intersection of several established research areas:

**Spatial Awareness in RL:** Previous studies have demonstrated the benefits of incorporating spatial awareness into RL. Approaches include relational RL using attention mechanisms to reason about entity relations (Zambaldi et al., 2019), DRL agents for navigation that use auxiliary tasks (Mirowski et al., 2017), and cognitive mapping and planning frameworks that combine spatial memory with neural planners (Gupta et al., 2017; Bapst et al., 2019). While these methods highlight the importance of spatial awareness, the current work posits that they often lack the biological plausibility observed in neural circuits.

**Biologically Inspired Machine Intelligence:** Research in this domain leverages insights from neuroscience to improve RL algorithm efficiency, adaptability, and interpretability. Examples include incorporating grid-like representations inspired by mammalian grid cells (Banino et al., 2018; Cueva & Wei, 2018) for navigation tasks, observing emergent insect-like behaviors in RL agents (Singh et al., 2023), and meta-RL algorithms mimicking prefrontal cortex and dopamine functions (Wang et al., 2018). These studies underscore the potential of biologically inspired representations for robust learning.

**Uncertainty Quantification (UQ) Methods:** Various techniques have been developed to quantify uncertainty in RL, primarily to improve exploration and decision-making under partial information. These include bootstrapped Deep Q-networks (DQNs) (Osband et al., 2016), random network distillation (Burda et al., 2018), 'masksemble' approaches (Durasov et al., 2020; Bykovets et al., 2022) for diverse predictions, and Bayesian methods for risk assessment in non-stationary environments (Jain et al., 2021). Bayesian ring attractors have also been developed to dynamically adjust activity based on evidence quality (Kutschireiter et al., 2023). Specifically, Bayesian Deep Q-networks (BDQNs) integrate Thompson sampling and Bayesian linear regression for uncertainty-aware action-value estimates (Azizzadenesheli et al., 2018).

The authors identify a research gap in integrating these three elements—spatial awareness, biological inspiration, and uncertainty quantification—into a cohesive framework. This paper aims to bridge this gap by proposing ring attractors as a biologically plausible model that inherently captures spatial relations and can incorporate uncertainty, potentially leading to more robust and efficient RL agents.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the efficiency and accuracy of action selection in Reinforcement Learning (RL) systems, particularly within environments that possess inherent spatial structures. This objective is motivated by several challenges and opportunities:

**Challenge of Efficient Action Selection in Spatial Environments:** Traditional RL frameworks, especially those using flattened action spaces, may fail to adequately represent or exploit the spatial relationships between actions. For example, in robotic manipulation, joint movements are coupled, and in game-playing, tactical decisions often depend on positional awareness. Flattening the action space can obscure the continuity between rotation angles or the adjacency between tactical moves, potentially hindering learning speed and accuracy.

**Motivation for Biologically Plausible Ring Attractors:**
- **Neuroscience Inspiration:** Ring attractors are a mathematical model inspired by neural circuit dynamics, originally proposed by Zhang (1996) and empirically observed by Kim et al. (2017). They serve as specialized brain-inspired structures that encode spatial information and uncertainty.
- **Explicit Spatial Encoding:** Ring attractors offer a mechanism to explicitly encode the action space in a continuous and stable manner (Sun et al., 2020). This facilitates the organization of neural activity and enables the distribution of spatial representations across the neural network, which can preserve relationships between actions (e.g., continuity of angles or adjacency of moves).
- **Improved Decision-Making:** By exploiting spatial relations between actions, ring attractors are posited to contribute to more informed transitions between actions in sequential decision-making tasks, thereby improving learning speed and efficiency in RL.
- **Uncertainty Awareness:** The model provides a framework to integrate uncertainty estimation directly into the action selection process, yielding more efficient and reliable learning in complex environments.

**Specific Contributions as outlined by the authors:**
- **Novel RL Policy Approach:** To propose a new method for incorporating ring attractors into RL to explicitly capture spatial information and relationships between actions, which is hypothesized to significantly accelerate the agent's learning rate.
- **Uncertainty-Aware Capabilities:** To integrate a Bayesian approach for constructing ring attractor input signals, thereby enabling the encoding of uncertainty estimations to guide action selection and demonstrate performance improvements compared to methods without uncertainty awareness.
- **Deep Learning (DL) Module Development:** To develop a reusable DL module based on recurrent neural networks (RNNs) that can integrate ring attractors into existing Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) agents. This module is intended to facilitate integration and comparison with current DRL frameworks and baselines, promoting the adoption of the ring attractor approach across diverse RL models and tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research investigates the application of ring attractors in RL through two distinct methodological approaches: an exogenous Continuous-Time Recurrent Neural Network (CTRNN) model and an integrated Deep Learning (DL) module within DRL agents. Both methods aim to leverage ring attractors' spatial encoding capabilities for enhanced action selection.

#### 3.1. Continuous-Time RNN Ring Attractor Model (Exogenous)

This initial approach focuses on developing a self-contained, exogenous ring attractor implemented as a CTRNN. This model is designed to perform action selection by integrating with the output of a value-based policy model.

#### 3.1.1. Ring Attractor Architecture and Dynamics

- **Configuration:** The ring attractor consists of excitatory and inhibitory neurons arranged in a circular pattern, based on the Touretzky ring attractor network. Each excitatory neuron connects to all others, and a single inhibitory neuron is centrally placed with equal weighted connections to all excitatory neurons.
- **Input Signal:** The total input to an excitatory neuron $n$ is a sum of weighted Gaussian functions. The magnitude $K_i$ of each Gaussian is set to the Q-value $Q(s, a)$ of a state-action pair, ensuring that actions with higher estimated values contribute more to the network dynamics. The mean position $\mu_i$ of the Gaussian corresponds to the angle $\alpha_a(a)$ of action $a$ in the circular action space, while the standard deviation $\sigma_i$ represents the uncertainty $\sigma_a$ of the action value estimate.
- **Neuron Activation:** A Rectified Linear Unit (ReLU) function $f(x) = \max(0, x+h)$ is employed, with $h$ as a positive threshold for non-linear behavior.
- **Excitatory Neuron Dynamics:** The activation $v_n$ of an excitatory neuron evolves over time based on its external input (Q-value-weighted Gaussian), the weighted influence from other excitatory neurons $\epsilon_n$, the weighted influence from the inhibitory neuron $\eta_n$, and a time integration constant $\tau$.
- **Inhibitory Neuron Dynamics:** The activation $u$ of the inhibitory neuron updates based on the sum of excitatory activations $\epsilon_n$ and its self-inhibition term $\eta_n$.
- **Synaptic Weighted Connections:** Connection strength between neurons decreases with their distance. For excitatory neurons, the weight is $e^{-d(m,n)^2}$, where $d(m,n)$ is the distance. The single inhibitory neuron is set at a distance of 1 unit from all excitatory neurons.
- **Action Selection:** The network's final output is translated into an action by selecting the action corresponding to the neuron with the maximum activation $V$, assuming a uniform distribution of neurons and actions around the ring.

#### 3.1.2. Uncertainty Quantification Integration

- **Bayesian Linear Regression (BLR):** To incorporate uncertainty into action-value estimates, BLR is used as the output layer of the neural network. This allows for the computation of a posterior distribution for the Q-value $Q(s, a) = \Phi_\theta(s)^T w_a$, where $w_a$ are weights sampled from the posterior distribution.
- **Thompson Sampling:** This method is employed to construct the Gaussian BLR prior distribution and sample weights $w_{a,i}$ from the posterior distribution, enabling the estimation of both the mean $\bar{Q}(s, a)$ and variance $\bar{\sigma}^2_a$ of Q-values for each action. The variance $\bar{\sigma}^2_a$ then feeds into the $\sigma_a$ parameter of the Gaussian input functions for the ring attractor.

#### 3.2. Deep Learning Ring Attractor Model

To address the computational overhead of the CTRNN model and improve integration with modern DRL agents, a DL-based ring attractor is developed using Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs).

#### 3.2.1. DRL Agent Integration

- **RNN Structure:** RNNs are utilized for their ability to model sequential data and temporal dependencies, mirroring the dynamics of ring attractors.
- **Circular Connectivity:** Weighted connections are structured to establish a circular topology.
 - **Input-to-hidden connections ($V(s)$):** These connections, from the previous layer (output of $\Phi_\theta(s)$) to the RNN layer, are designed to maintain the spatial structure of the ring. They are fixed and decrease exponentially with distance $d(m,n)$ between neurons.
 - **Hidden-to-hidden connections ($U(v)$):** These recurrent connections model the attractor state, integrating information from previous time steps. They are learnable and also decay exponentially with distance, allowing the network to capture emergent action relationships.
- **Learnable Time Constant ($\tau$):** An inherited parameter from the CTRNN, $\tau$ controls the contribution of input states to the current hidden state, effectively regulating the temporal evolution and adaptability of the attractor state within the RNN.
- **Action-Value Function:** The Q-value $Q(s, a)$ is derived from the RNN layer's output using a hyperbolic tangent (tanh) activation function, scaled by a learnable scalar $\beta$ to map the output to the appropriate reward range ($Q(s, a) = \beta \tanh((V(s) + U(v)))$).

#### 3.2.2. Ring Configurations for Action Spaces

- **Single Ring:** Used for action spaces with a primary directional component (e.g., movement actions), where actions map directly to positions on the ring circumference.
- **Double Ring:** Employed for more complex action spaces that combine independent action dimensions (e.g., movement and combat). This configuration uses block matrices for both input-to-hidden and hidden-to-hidden connections, with a learnable cross-coupling parameter $\kappa$ to manage interaction strength between the two rings.
- **Neutral Inhibitory Action:** For games with a 'no-op' or neutral action, it is positioned centrally, equidistant from all other actions. This ensures consistent, strong connections to all other actions, preserving spatial relationships while maintaining equal accessibility.
- **Extension to N-Ring Configurations:** The double ring architecture can be extended to multiple rings ($R$) through block matrix structures, allowing decomposition of complex action spaces into semi-independent dimensions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted aimed to validate the proposed ring attractor integration into RL algorithms, evaluating both the CTRNN exogenous model and the DL-based implementation across various environments and benchmarks.

#### 4.1. CTRNN Ring Attractor Model Performance

Experiments for the CTRNN ring attractor model (Section 3.1.2), integrated with BDQN, were performed on the OpenAI Super Mario Bros environment (discrete action space) and OpenAI Highway (continuous 1D circular action space).
- **Performance Improvement:** Both the standard ring attractor model (BDQNRA) and its uncertainty-aware variant (BDQNRA-UA) consistently outperformed the baseline standard BDQN in terms of cumulative reward over 1 million frames.
- **Uncertainty Awareness Benefit:** BDQNRA-UA, which incorporated uncertainty quantification (Section 3.1.3), demonstrated the highest overall performance, indicating a benefit from combining spatial action distribution with uncertainty-aware action selection.
- **Computational Overhead:** Empirical evaluations revealed that CTRNN-based ring attractor models incurred a significant mean computational overhead of 297.3% (SD = 14.2%) compared to the baseline, highlighting a performance bottleneck. This observation motivated the development of the DL implementation.

#### 4.2. DL Ring Attractor Model Performance

The DL-based ring attractor implementation (Section 3.2), integrated as DDQNRA, was evaluated against standard DDQN in Super Mario Bros and Highway environments.
- **Consistent Outperformance:** DDQNRA consistently outperformed standard DDQN in both navigation and game-like decision-making scenarios. This suggests that the ring attractor's ability to encode spatial relationships significantly contributes to the agent's learning speed.
- **Enhanced Learning Speed:** The results indicate that integrating ring attractors into DRL architectures can lead to improvements in learning speed and overall performance, particularly in environments with strong spatial cues.

#### 4.3. Performance on Atari 100k Benchmark

A comprehensive analysis of the DRL model integrated with EfficientZero (EffZeroRA) was conducted on the Atari 100k benchmark, comparing it with state-of-the-art algorithms like SPR and CURL.
- **Significant Average Improvement:** The EffZeroRA model demonstrated an average 53% increase in human-normalized scores across all tested games compared to prior baselines.
- **Exceptional Gains in Spatial Games:** Games with strong inherent spatial components, such as Asterix and Boxing, showed improvements exceeding 100% (110% for Asterix, 105% for Boxing). This highlights the particular benefit of spatial encoding provided by ring attractors in such environments.
- **Ring Configurations:** The paper outlines specific ring configurations for different game types:
 - **Single Ring:** Used for games with primarily directional movement actions (e.g., Asterix, Ms Pacman).
 - **Double Ring:** Employed for games combining movement with independent action dimensions (e.g., Seaquest, BattleZone), where one ring handles movement and another handles secondary mechanics.
- **General Enhancement:** Even in games where the improvement was less dramatic, substantial increases in performance were observed, suggesting that the benefits of the ring attractor extend across a broad range of RL tasks.
- **Fair Comparison:** Baseline EffZero and EffZeroRA models were re-implemented and evaluated under identical experimental conditions to ensure a fair comparison.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies (Appendix A.2)

- **Exogenous Model Ablation:** Comparing the full BDQNRA model with versions where the action space was randomly distributed (BDQNRA-RM) in the ring showed a performance drop for BDQNRA-RM. This confirmed that the correct topological mapping of actions to the ring structure is crucial for performance.
- **DL Model Ablation:** Removing the circular weight distribution (fixed input-to-hidden, learnable hidden-to-hidden) in the RNN-based ring attractor led to a significant performance drop in games like Ms Pacman and Chopper Command. This emphasizes the role of the circular topology in encoding spatial information and enhancing learning.

#### 4.5. DL Ring Attractor Model Evolution (Appendix A.2.3)

Analysis of trainable forward pass and hidden-to-hidden weights in the DL model revealed that:
- **Forward Pass Weight Preservation:** Trainable forward pass connections naturally preserved the ring structure with strong distance-dependent decay patterns throughout training, suggesting an inherent benefit of this topology for transmitting per-frame sensory information.
- **Hidden-to-Hidden Weight Specialization:** Hidden-to-hidden connections evolved beyond their initial ring structure, developing specialized patterns to encode environment-specific relationships between neurons. This supports the design choice of fixing forward pass connections and making hidden-to-hidden weights trainable.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework for integrating neuroscience-inspired ring attractors into Reinforcement Learning (RL) systems, demonstrating a novel approach to action selection that has several implications and potential impacts.

**Enhanced Learning Efficiency and Stability:** The integration of ring attractors, particularly through the Deep Learning (DL) module, demonstrably improves the learning capabilities of value-based RL agents. This leads to more stable and efficient action selection, which is particularly advantageous in tasks with inherent spatial structures. The observed average 53% performance increase on the Atari 100k benchmark, with over 100% gains in specific spatial games, indicates a substantial advancement in RL agent performance for such environments.

**Biologically Plausible Framework:** By drawing inspiration from neural circuit dynamics, the work bridges a gap between neuroscience-inspired models and practical RL agents. It offers a biologically plausible mechanism for encoding spatial information and uncertainty, which could provide insights into how biological systems make spatially-aware decisions. This interdisciplinary approach contributes to the broader field of biologically inspired artificial intelligence.

**Uncertainty-Aware Decision-Making:** The explicit integration of uncertainty quantification into the ring attractor's action selection process contributes to more reliable and robust learning. This capability is valuable in complex and uncertain environments where agents must account for the reliability of their value estimates.

**General Applicability and Integrability:** The development of a reusable DL module facilitates the integration of ring attractors into existing DRL architectures. Its demonstrated effectiveness across various RL tasks and agents, including standard benchmarks, suggests a general enhancement that can benefit a wide range of applications beyond those explicitly tested.

**Potential Future Research Directions:**
- **Scalability:** Investigating the effectiveness and scalability of this approach in high-dimensional action spaces and domains where spatial relationships are less straightforward.
- **Broader RL Paradigms:** Extending the current work to multi-agent scenarios and policy-based RL agents, which could broaden the applicability and impact of ring attractors.
- **Explicit Uncertainty Mapping:** Further leveraging the spatial structure of attractor networks to explicitly map and manage uncertainty within the action space, potentially leading to advanced risk assessment and decision-making capabilities.
- **Safe RL Applications:** Exploiting the stability properties of ring attractors to enforce constraints and ensure predictable behavior in safety-critical RL applications.
- **Cognitive Science Insights:** The approach offers a tangible model for exploring spatial encoding of actions and decision-making processes, potentially informing cognitive science research.

Overall, this work contributes to the advancement of RL by offering a mechanism that improves performance through spatial awareness and uncertainty integration, while also providing a biologically inspired perspective on intelligent decision-making.
