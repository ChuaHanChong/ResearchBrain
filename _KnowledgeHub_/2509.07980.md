# Parallel-R1: Towards Parallel Thinking via Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Parallel-R1, a reinforcement learning framework developed by a collaborative team including Tencent AI Lab Seattle, teaches large language models to perform parallel thinking for complex mathematical reasoning tasks. The method effectively addresses cold-start challenges and achieves robust performance gains, including a 42.9% improvement on the AIME25 benchmark by utilizing parallel thinking as a mid-training exploration scaffold.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) struggle to engage in "parallel thinking," which involves exploring multiple reasoning paths concurrently, a capability inspired by human cognition.
- Existing methods, such as test-time strategies (e.g., CoT, ToT) or Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), are computationally intensive at inference or lead to superficial imitation, failing to instill intrinsic, generalizable parallel thinking.
- Applying reinforcement learning (RL) to teach novel complex behaviors like parallel thinking faces a "cold-start" problem, where the model cannot initially generate the target behavior, and challenges in designing effective generalizable reward functions.

## Method

- Parallel-R1 formalizes LLM ==parallel thinking== into explicit exploration (generating N independent paths for a critical step) and summary phases, controlled by specific architectural tags.
- A scalable data pipeline generates high-quality ==parallel thinking data== for easier problems (Parallel-GSM8K) to teach the model the format and basic behavior, effectively addressing the ==cold-start problem== for subsequent RL.
- The framework employs Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with a ==progressive training curriculum==, including a crucial Stage 1 RL on easy math to stabilize parallel thinking, followed by Stage 2 RL on general math with nuanced reward designs that balance task accuracy and explicit parallel behavior.

## Results

- Parallel-R1 consistently outperformed all baselines, with the Parallel-R1-Seen (causal) variant achieving the highest average score of **48.9** across mathematical reasoning benchmarks.
- Stage 1 RL on GSM8K was crucial for causal models, improving performance by **2.3%** on average, but was detrimental for structured models, indicating that attention masks learned on easy tasks do not generalize well to harder problems.
- The "alternating accuracy and parallel" reward schedule for Parallel-R1-Unseen achieved a high parallel ratio (**63.0%**) while maintaining strong task performance, and as an exploration scaffold, it led to a **42.9%** improvement on AIME25 over baseline GRPO models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The progressive curriculum effectively overcomes the cold-start problem, enabling LLMs to learn structured reasoning behaviors from scratch, demonstrating that initial SFT for format followed by staged RL is a practical approach.
> - Analysis of LLM behavior during RL training reveals a strategic evolution: models shift from using parallel paths for early-stage computational exploration to late-stage multi-perspective verification as their core reasoning improves.
> - Parallel thinking can serve as a "mid-training exploration scaffold," where temporarily forcing diverse exploration during RL leads to higher performance ceilings even if the final policy relies less on explicit parallel structures.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Large Language Models (LLMs) has seen rapid advancements, particularly in their ability to perform complex reasoning tasks. However, a significant challenge remains in enabling LLMs to engage in "parallel thinking" – the cognitive process of exploring multiple reasoning paths concurrently before synthesizing a conclusion. This capability is inspired by human cognition, which often involves divergent thought and multi-perspective verification to avoid premature commitment to suboptimal solutions.

Existing approaches to elicit parallel thinking in LLMs largely fall into two categories:
- **Test-time strategies**: Methods like Chain-of-Thought (CoT), Tree-of-Thoughts (ToT), and self-consistency techniques encourage LLMs to explore multiple reasoning paths during inference. While effective, these strategies come with a substantial computational overhead and do not permanently instill the capability within the model's parameters. They are external mechanisms rather than intrinsic skills.
- **Training-based methods (Supervised Fine-Tuning - SFT)**: Some prior work attempts to teach parallel thinking through SFT on synthetically generated data. However, as the authors highlight, SFT inherently performs "behavioral cloning," leading to teacher-forced imitation. This approach is highly dependent on the quality and complexity of pre-generated data, which is often costly and difficult to scale for challenging, real-world problems. It primarily encourages superficial pattern matching rather than the acquisition of a deep, generalizable reasoning skill, severely limiting the model's ability to discover novel reasoning patterns or adapt to unseen scenarios.

The current research by Zheng et al. identifies a critical gap: the lack of an effective and scalable reinforcement learning (RL) framework to instill *intrinsic, adaptive parallel thinking* capabilities in LLMs for complex, real-world reasoning tasks. While some earlier RL applications demonstrated parallel thinking on narrow, synthetic domains (e.g., the CountDown task), they struggled with the "cold-start" problem—where a model unfamiliar with parallel thinking cannot generate such trajectories for RL to learn from—and the design of effective reward functions for general tasks.

This paper positions itself as the *first RL framework* to tackle these challenges directly. It builds upon the advancements in Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR), a paradigm that optimizes LLMs using outcome-based, automatically checkable rewards, thus eliminating the need for trained reward models or extensive human annotations. By extending RLVR to teach a *structured reasoning capability* rather than just improving sequential reasoning, Parallel-R1 aims to move beyond mere "lossless transformation" of existing reasoning chains and instead uncover novel, highly adaptive reasoning behaviors. This work represents a significant step towards enabling LLMs to truly learn and generalize complex cognitive processes, thereby fundamentally enhancing their intrinsic reasoning capabilities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The motivation for developing Parallel-R1 stems from the observed benefits of parallel thinking in advanced AI systems and human cognition, coupled with the limitations of current LLM training paradigms. The authors were particularly inspired by reports of Google's Gemini crediting parallel thinking for its success in the International Mathematical Olympiad, underscoring its potential value beyond a mere technical trick. Cognitive science further supports this by illustrating how humans engage in parallel thought to explore possibilities, prevent premature conclusions, and facilitate structured reasoning.

Given this context, the research outlines several key objectives:

1. **Develop a Reinforcement Learning Framework for Parallel Thinking**: The primary goal is to propose and implement the first RL framework capable of teaching LLMs to perform parallel thinking *from scratch* on *general mathematical reasoning tasks*. This moves beyond supervised imitation and limited synthetic environments.
2. **Address the Cold-Start Problem**: A significant challenge in applying RL to novel behaviors like parallel thinking is the "cold-start" problem, where the LLM initially lacks the ability to generate the target behavior. The objective is to devise a progressive training curriculum that effectively bootstraps this capability.
3. **Design Effective Reward Functions**: Traditional RL often relies on final correctness as a reward. For parallel thinking, a key objective is to explore and design reward schemes that can balance the incentive for task accuracy with the explicit encouragement of parallel thinking behaviors, without forcing the model into unnecessary or suboptimal strategies.
4. **Analyze the Strategic Role and Evolution of Parallel Thinking**: To move beyond a "black box" understanding, the authors aim to investigate how the model's parallel thinking strategy evolves throughout the RL training process. This includes understanding when and why the model chooses to employ parallel paths.
5. **Validate Parallel Thinking as an Exploration Scaffold**: A novel objective is to empirically test the hypothesis that parallel thinking can serve as a *mid-training exploration scaffold*—a temporary phase that forces diverse exploration, ultimately leading to a higher performance ceiling even if the final policy shifts away from explicit parallel structures.
6. **Compare Model Architectures**: The work also aims to provide robust insights into architectural design by comparing causal (standard) and structured (with explicit attention masking for path isolation) model variants in learning parallel thinking.

In essence, the research is motivated by a desire to enable LLMs to learn a more human-like, robust, and generalizable form of reasoning, moving beyond pattern matching to acquire a deeper, intrinsic skill set through the power of reinforcement learning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Parallel-R1 addresses the challenges of instilling parallel thinking in LLMs through a multi-faceted methodology that combines a progressive curriculum, a novel data pipeline, specialized reward modeling, and architectural considerations.

#### 3.1. Formulation of Parallel Thinking Behaviors

The authors formalize LLM parallel thinking into two key phases, inspired by human problem-solving:
1. **Exploration**: When the model identifies a "critical step," it pauses the main reasoning chain and concurrently generates *N* independent trajectories within separate reasoning paths.
2. **Summary**: After exploration, the model aggregates insights from these parallel paths into a concise summary, resolves conflicts, and then resumes the main reasoning chain.
To implement this, three control tags are introduced: `<Parallel>...</Parallel>` for the exploration block, `<Path>...</Path>` for individual reasoning threads, and `<Summary>...</Summary>` for aggregating insights. The inference workflow allows the model to dynamically insert these tags, execute parallel generation, summarize, and continue auto-regressively.

#### 3.2. The Simple and Scalable Data Pipeline for Parallel Thinking (Addressing Cold-Start)

A core challenge is the scarcity of high-quality parallel thinking data for complex problems. The authors' *Key Finding 1* reveals that simple prompting works well for generating parallel thinking traces for *easy* tasks (83.6% success on GSM8K) but fails for *challenging* tasks (0.0% on DAPO).
Based on this, they propose a scalable data pipeline:
- Utilize detailed zero-shot prompts with a strong distilled Qwen-3 - 8B model (DeepSeek-R1-0528-Qwen-3-8B) to generate a large, high-quality corpus of parallel thinking data specifically for *easier problems* like GSM8K, creating the **Parallel-GSM8K** dataset.
- Implement a **Parallel Thinking Format Check (Algorithm 1)** to filter for strict adherence to the defined tag structure, especially crucial for structured model variants.
The strategic insight is that this "cold-start" data is used *not* to solve the final target tasks, but *solely to teach the model the *format* and *basic behavior* of parallel thinking*, enabling a transition to RL for generalization.

#### 3.3. Reinforcement Learning Algorithm

The framework employs **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)** (Shao etal., 2024), an RL algorithm suitable for optimizing LLMs using verifiable rewards. GRPO calculates advantages based on the relative performance of candidate responses from the old policy and uses a clipped policy ratio and a KL divergence penalty to ensure stable updates. The multi-turn interactive rollout process involves alternating between autoregressive generation, parallel exploration, and summarization.

#### 3.4. Training Recipes and Reward Modeling

The authors explore two distinct settings: causal models (no architectural modification) and structured models (with architectural modification).

- **Parallel-R1-Seen (Causal Model)**:
 1. **Cold-Start Stage (SFT)**: Fine-tune the initial LLM (Qwen-3 - 4B -Base) on the `Parallel-GSM8K` dataset to instill basic format knowledge.
 2. **RL on Easy Math (Stage 1)**: Small-scale RL on `Parallel-GSM8K` to stabilize the parallel thinking format. The reward is `R_final = R_<Parallel> * R_acc`, which is +1 if the output contains parallel units *and* is correct, else -1. This explicitly incentivizes both behavior and correctness on easy tasks.
 3. **RL on General Math (Stage 2)**: RL on the `DAPO` dataset (a more challenging general math benchmark) to generalize the parallel thinking ability. The reward is `R_acc` only (solely based on final answer correctness), aiming to improve task performance.

- **Parallel-R1-Unseen (Structured Model)**:
 This variant explicitly enforces path isolation through architectural modifications to the attention mechanism.
 1. **Structured Attention Mechanism**:
 - **Path-window masking**: Restricts attention within a `<Path>` block to only its own path and the shared context, preventing cross-path information leakage.
 - **Multiverse position encodings**: Assigns disjoint sets of position indices to each path, ensuring positional embedding spaces do not overlap. This maintains isolation while allowing the `<Summary>` block to see all paths.
 2. **Training Recipe Redesign**: Due to findings that attention masks learned on easy tasks might not transfer well, Stage 1 RL on GSM8K is *removed* for this variant.
 3. **Reward Schedules (for RL on General Math)**: Two schemes are investigated for the RL phase on general math:
 - **(S1) Accuracy-only**: Optimize solely for task correctness.
 - **(S2) Alternating accuracy and parallel**: Switches between `R_acc` (80% of steps) and a tiered reward for parallel thinking (20% of steps): +1.2 (parallel AND correct), +1.0 (no parallel BUT correct), -1.0 (other cases). This aims for a nuanced balance between incentivizing parallel behavior and overall performance.

#### 3.5. Experimental Setups

- **Model**: Qwen-3 - 4B -Base.
- **Evaluation Benchmarks**: AIME’24, AIME’25, AMC’23 (Mean@16 and Pass@16), and MATH (Mean@1).
- **Baselines**: Standard GRPO on DAPO, and GRPO trained first on GSM8K then DAPO (without parallel thinking capabilities) for fair comparison.

This comprehensive approach allows for systematic investigation into how parallel thinking can be effectively instilled in LLMs, considering both algorithmic and architectural dimensions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on various mathematical reasoning benchmarks provide compelling evidence for the effectiveness and nuanced behavior of the Parallel-R1 framework.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance Improvements (Table 2)

- **Superiority of Parallel-R1**: The progressive Parallel-R1 framework consistently outperformed all baselines. The top-performing causal variant, **Parallel-R1-Seen**, achieved the highest average score of 48.9 across benchmarks.
- **Limitations of SFT**: While SFT (e.g., Parallel-SFT-Unseen at 31.7 average) provided a significant initial boost over the base model (6.6 average), it proved insufficient for advanced reasoning compared to RL-based approaches. It fell considerably short of the standard GRPO baseline's 45.1.
- **Validation of Cold-Start Strategy**: A naive sequential RL training (GRPO on GSM8K then DAPO) offered only marginal benefit (45.3 average) compared to GRPO on DAPO alone (45.1 average). This validates the paper's specific approach of using cold-start for *targeted format and behavior learning*, rather than just general pre-training on easy data.
- **Architectural Trade-offs**: Causal (Seen) models generally outperformed structured (Unseen) models. This suggests that explicit architectural modifications, while intuitively appealing for isolation, can be detrimental to RL training if not carefully managed, potentially due to difficulties in transferring learned attention masks.
- **Importance of Reward Design**: The comparison between reward schedules for Parallel-R1-Unseen (S1: Accuracy-only) and (S2: Alternating Acc./Parallel) highlighted that reward design is crucial for balancing parallel ratio and overall performance.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies on Training Approach (Table 3)

- **Role of Stage 1 RL for Causal Models**: For the causal (Parallel-R1-Seen) variant, removing Stage 1 RL on GSM8K led to a consistent performance drop (2.3% on average). This confirms that SFT alone is insufficient; Stage 1 RL is crucial for the model to acquire the *adaptive ability* to trigger and utilize parallel thinking before tackling harder problems.
- **Detrimental Effect of Stage 1 RL for Structured Models**: Conversely, for the structured (Parallel-R1-Unseen) variant, *adding* Stage 1 RL on GSM8K severely *hurt* performance (8.6% on average). This supports the hypothesis that attention masks learned on easy math (GSM8K) do not generalize well to the distribution shift of harder problems, leading to overfitting to superficial patterns.
- **Impact of Parallel Thinking Prompt**: Removing the detailed parallel thinking prompt caused a 1.8% average performance degradation, indicating that clear instructions aid the model in understanding the reasoning process rather than mere memorization.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies on Reward Modeling (Parallel-R1-Unseen) (Table 4)

- **Accuracy-only Reward**: While optimizing solely for accuracy yields high performance on some benchmarks (e.g., 69.7 on AMC23), it resulted in a very low parallel ratio (13.6%). This indicates accuracy alone does not intrinsically stimulate parallel thinking.
- **Parallel-only Reward**: Directly rewarding parallel structure led to an impressively high parallel ratio (80.3%) but caused a significant performance drop across most benchmarks. This shows that unconstrained encouragement of parallel behavior can lead the model to prioritize form over logical correctness.
- **Alternating Accuracy and Parallel Reward**: This strategy provided the best balance, achieving a high parallel ratio (63.0%) *without sacrificing performance*. On some challenging benchmarks like AIME25, it even surpassed the accuracy-only approach (19.0% vs. 17.7%), demonstrating its effectiveness in fostering both structural behavior and problem-solving capability.

#### 4.4. Evolution of Parallel Thinking Behavior (Key Finding 2, Figure 3, Case Studies)

- **Strategic Shift**: Analysis revealed a clear strategic evolution in the model's use of parallel thinking during RL training. The average *relative position* of the `<Parallel>` block steadily increased as training progressed.
- **Interpretation: Exploration to Verification**: This shift indicates that the model initially used parallel paths for *early-stage computational exploration* to discover solutions when its reasoning was weak. As its core reasoning ability improved, it adopted a more risk-averse strategy, deploying `<Parallel>` blocks for *late-stage multi-perspective verification* to confirm an already derived solution. This behavior is shaped by the final-answer-dominated reward design.

#### 4.5. Parallel Thinking as a Mid-Training Exploration Scaffold (Key Finding 3, Figure 4)

- **Hypothesis Validation**: The authors empirically validated the hypothesis that parallel thinking can serve as an effective structured exploration mechanism for RL training.
- **Two-Stage Curriculum**:
 - **Stage 1 (Exploration)**: Using alternating ACC/PAR rewards for 200 steps successfully maintained a high parallel ratio, forcing the model to explore diverse reasoning paths.
 - **Stage 2 (Exploitation)**: Switching to accuracy-only reward after 200 steps resulted in continued performance improvement, peaking at 25.6% on AIME25 (a 42.9% improvement over the baseline GRPO model), *even as the parallel ratio decreased*.
- **Key Insight**: This finding suggests that the value of parallel thinking extends beyond its direct application in the final solution. The initial forced exploration, acting as a scaffold, guides the model to a more effective region in the policy space, unlocking a higher performance ceiling even if the model's final policy relies less on explicit parallel structures.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Parallel-R1 framework marks a significant advance in the field of large language model reasoning, offering several key contributions and substantial potential impact:

1. **Pioneering RL for Intrinsic Structured Reasoning**: This work presents the first successful reinforcement learning framework to instill a complex, structured reasoning capability like parallel thinking *from scratch* into LLMs for challenging, real-world tasks. This moves beyond the limitations of supervised fine-tuning (SFT) that often lead to superficial pattern matching and restricted generalization, paving the way for LLMs to acquire more human-like, intrinsic reasoning skills.

2. **Effective Solution to the Cold-Start Problem**: The proposed progressive curriculum, starting with SFT on easily generated "cold-start" data for format learning, followed by staged RL, provides a practical and scalable solution to the long-standing cold-start problem in teaching novel behaviors to LLMs via RL. This methodology can be generalized to instilling other complex cognitive abilities.

3. **Advanced Reward Design for Complex Behaviors**: The detailed ablation studies on reward modeling highlight the critical importance of carefully designed reward functions. The "alternating accuracy and parallel" reward strategy, in particular, demonstrates an effective method for balancing performance goals with the explicit encouragement of desired complex behaviors. This insight is highly valuable for future research in Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) and Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR).

4. **Deepened Understanding of LLM Learning Dynamics**: By analyzing the evolution of parallel thinking behavior, the paper offers crucial insights into how LLMs adapt their strategies under RL. The discovery that models shift from early-stage exploration to late-stage multi-perspective verification provides a rare glimpse into the "black box" of LLM decision-making, contributing to more interpretable and controllable AI systems.

5. **Novel RL Exploration Paradigm**: The most significant conceptual contribution is the validation of parallel thinking as a "mid-training exploration scaffold." This demonstrates that temporarily forcing structured exploration can guide the RL process to discover more robust and higher-performing policy spaces, even if the explicit parallel structure is later de-emphasized. This finding has profound implications for RL algorithm design, suggesting new paradigms for overcoming local optima and enhancing exploration in complex environments.

6. **Enhanced Mathematical Reasoning Performance**: Empirically, Parallel-R1 achieves consistent and substantial accuracy improvements on challenging mathematical benchmarks (e.g., 8.4% improvement over sequential RL models, and a remarkable 42.9% improvement on AIME25 through scaffolding). This directly enhances the practical utility of LLMs in critical STEM domains, potentially aiding in scientific discovery and problem-solving.

7. **Guidance for Architectural Design**: The comparison between causal and structured model variants offers practical guidance, indicating that explicit architectural modifications, while conceptually sound, require careful integration with RL training to avoid pitfalls like poor generalization of learned attention patterns.

In summary, Parallel-R1 not only pushes the boundaries of LLM reasoning capabilities but also contributes significantly to the understanding and application of reinforcement learning for complex AI behaviors. Its open-source release of code, model, and data will undoubtedly foster further research and innovation in this rapidly evolving field.
