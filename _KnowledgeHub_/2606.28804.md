# ViPSim: Collaborating Visual and Parameter Spaces for Consistent Long-Horizon Embodied World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Huawei Technologies and Huazhong University of Science and Technology developed ViPSim, a framework that enhances Embodied World Models (EWMs) by collaborating visual and parameter spaces to generate consistent, high-fidelity, long-horizon visual rollouts. The approach significantly improves perceptual quality and motion correctness, with ViPSim(DiT) achieving PSNR 20.35, SSIM 0.80, and an EWMBench overall score of 5.5697.

## Problem

- Existing action-conditioned Embodied World Models (EWMs) struggle to maintain consistency over long predictive horizons, leading to cumulative trajectory drift.
- A "representation gap" exists between low-dimensional action inputs and high-dimensional visual synthesis, causing insufficient geometric correspondence in robot-object interactions.
- Traditional evaluation methods are either costly and risky (real-world) or lack visual fidelity and realism (procedural rendering), creating a domain gap.

## Method

- **ViPSim** formulates long-horizon robot video generation as a ==chunk-based autoregressive process==, predicting future frames conditioned on multi-scale context.
- It introduces a ==dual-space collaboration== strategy, integrating ==Visual Space Representations== (pixel-aligned priors like action maps, depth, masks) and ==Parameter Space Representations== (numerical motion guidance like camera extrinsics and end-effector poses).
- A ==collaborative interaction mechanism== fuses these spaces by using ==geometric grounding== via dense structural priors and ==physical modulation== via precise numerical drivers injected into the generative model's ==cross-attention== layers, using ==Plücker embeddings== for camera pose.

## Results

- ViPSim (DiT backbone) achieved improved perceptual quality, with **PSNR 20.35**, **SSIM 0.80**, and **LPIPS 0.19**, showing higher fidelity in generated visual sequences compared to baselines.
- The framework demonstrated superior motion correctness, increasing the EWMBench Motion Sum to **2.4754** for ViPSim(DiT) from a baseline of **2.0504**, indicating better synchronization with input actions.
- Qualitative results showed high visual persistence and scene consistency over long horizons, preserving object identity and restoring occluded elements, along with robust manipulation of both deformable and rigid objects.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Bridging the "representation gap" between low-dimensional actions and high-dimensional visual outputs is critical for consistent long-horizon EWMs.
> - Synergistically combining explicit spatial priors (Visual Space) with precise numerical motion commands (Parameter Space) effectively addresses this gap.
> - Incorporating fine-grained geometric grounding (depth, masks) and leveraging Plücker embeddings for camera pose provides robust spatial and morphological constraints.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Embodied Artificial Intelligence (AI) and general-purpose robotics has seen significant advancements, with Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models emerging as a core component for connecting perception and execution. However, the development and scaling of VLA systems are challenged by the lack of efficient and reliable evaluation frameworks. Traditional real-world evaluation methods are often costly and pose safety risks, while existing procedural rendering engines frequently fail to accurately simulate complex visual details, particularly those involving non-rigid objects, leading to a substantial domain gap.

Embodied World Models (EWMs) have recently gained prominence as data-driven, high-fidelity alternatives for evaluating robot policies. These models predict future visual states based on current observations and actions, offering high-throughput, risk-free environments for exploring diverse scenarios without the risk of hardware damage or physical collisions. Beyond benchmarking, high-fidelity EWMs have the potential to serve as foundational simulators for model-based reinforcement learning, active inference, and predictive control pipelines, thereby accelerating the development cycle of embodied agents.

Despite their potential, existing action-conditioned EWMs encounter difficulties in maintaining consistency over extended predictive horizons. A primary challenge identified in the literature is the "representation gap" between low-dimensional action inputs and high-dimensional visual synthesis. This gap can result in insufficient geometric correspondence between numerical actions and pixel-level scene dynamics, leading to cumulative trajectory drift and inconsistent robot-object interactions during long-horizon rollouts. While some prior work has introduced auxiliary visual cues to enhance global scene coherence, they often do not fully integrate explicit spatial specifications with precise numerical guidance, leading to fidelity mismatches, especially in high-contact regions where robot morphology and scene geometry are tightly coupled.

ViPSim addresses these specific limitations by proposing a framework designed to bridge this representation gap. It aims to achieve consistent long-horizon generation by synergistically collaborating between explicit spatial priors (Visual Space) and precise numerical motion commands (Parameter Space), thereby enhancing the reliability of EWMs as both evaluation benchmarks and foundational simulators.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop a framework that enables Embodied World Models (EWMs) to generate consistent, high-fidelity, and long-horizon visual rollouts, addressing critical challenges in the evaluation and development of embodied intelligence.

The key motivations driving this work are:

1. **Addressing the Representation Gap:** Existing action-conditioned EWMs struggle with a fundamental "representation gap" between low-dimensional action inputs (e.g., robot joint angles or end-effector poses) and the high-dimensional visual synthesis outputs (e.g., video frames). This disparity often results in a lack of rigorous geometric correspondence, leading to problems such as:
 - **Accumulated Trajectory Drift:** Over long prediction horizons, the simulated robot's trajectory can diverge significantly from the intended path.
 - **Inconsistent Robot-Object Interactions:** Interactions with objects may lack physical plausibility or visual fidelity, especially in complex manipulation tasks.

2. **Enhancing EWM Reliability as Evaluation Benchmarks:** The reliability of EWMs as tools for evaluating Vision-Language-Action (VLA) systems is hindered by these consistency issues. A primary motivation is to create more dependable and accurate synthetic environments that can serve as robust benchmarks for testing embodied policies. This includes enabling:
 - **Safety-critical evaluation:** Performing evaluations in a risk-free virtual environment, eliminating the inherent dangers and costs associated with real-world robot testing.
 - **High-throughput analysis:** Allowing for rapid and extensive testing of policies across numerous scenarios, including edge cases.

3. **Establishing Foundational Simulators for Advanced AI:** Beyond evaluation, a core motivation is to elevate EWMs to the status of foundational simulators for advanced AI paradigms such as model-based reinforcement learning, active inference, and predictive control. For these applications, high-fidelity, long-horizon consistency, and accurate physical interactions are paramount for effective agent training and deployment.

4. **Overcoming Limitations of Current Simulation Approaches:**
 - **Real-world evaluation:** Prohibitively expensive, time-consuming, and carries inherent safety risks, especially when dealing with complex or novel scenarios.
 - **Procedural rendering engines:** While offering some control, they often fail to capture the visual complexity and realism of real-world scenarios, particularly concerning non-rigid interactions and deformable objects, leading to a "domain gap" that limits transferability to the real world.

To achieve these objectives, the research proposes ViPSim, a framework designed to bridge the representation gap by introducing a synergistic collaboration between two complementary domains: the Visual Space, which provides explicit spatial and morphological grounding through pixel-aligned projections and depth information, and the Parameter Space, which injects precise numerical motion guidance via raw action sequences and camera matrices. This dual-space approach is motivated by the need to ensure that generated states are simultaneously constrained by geometric boundaries and precisely steered by numerical commands, thereby resolving issues of inconsistency and drift in long-horizon embodied world models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ViPSim addresses the challenge of consistent long-horizon embodied generation by formulating the task as a chunk-based autoregressive process and employing a dual-space collaboration strategy.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The framework is built upon video diffusion models and decomposes long-horizon robot video generation into discrete chunks of length L. At each step, the model predicts L future frames ($\hat{X}_{next} = \{x_{t_1}, \ldots, x_{t_L}\}$) conditioned on a multi-scale context. This context includes a current state frame ($x_{t_0}$), sparse historical frames ($X_{hist}$) for long-range temporal consistency, and future control sequences comprising camera parameters ($c_t$) and end-effector (EEF) poses ($a_t$). The model learns to approximate $p(x_{t_1:t_L} | x_{t_0}, X_{hist}, a_{t_1:t_L}, c_{t_1:t_L})$. For long-horizon generation, an autoregressive sliding window promotes the last predicted frame to the new current state for the subsequent chunk.

#### 3.2. Framework Overview (Dual-Space Collaboration)

ViPSim's core architecture integrates Visual Space Representations and Parameter Space Representations through a collaborative interaction mechanism.

#### 3.3. Visual Space Representations

This space establishes explicit spatial and morphological grounding by transforming numerical data into pixel-aligned visual priors.

- **Action Maps:** Raw action sequences ($a_{t_1:t_L}$) are converted into spatial-aware action maps ($M \in \mathbb{R}^{L \times 3 \times H \times W}$). This involves projecting the 3D end-effector (EEF) pose onto the 2D image plane using camera intrinsics (K) and extrinsics (R, t). A unit circle is rendered at the projected pixel coordinates (u, v) for each robotic arm, forming a trajectory video. Distinct color schemes are used for dual-arm scenarios. This provides explicit spatial guidance to the generative backbone.

- **Camera Pose (Plücker Embeddings):** Instead of raw matrices, pixel-wise camera perspectives are represented using Plücker embeddings ($p_{u_t,v_t,t} = (o_t \times d_{u_t,v_t,t}, d_{u_t,v_t,t}) \in \mathbb{R}^6$). For each pixel (u, v) at time t, $o_t$ is the camera center in world coordinates, and $d_{u_t,v_t,t}$ is the unit direction vector from the camera center to the pixel, calculated via inverse projection. These embeddings form a spatial embedding map $P \in \mathbb{R}^{L \times 6 \times H \times W}$, enabling the model to leverage spatial geometric priors more effectively.

- **Scene Geometry (Depth Maps):** Relative depth sequences ($D \in \mathbb{R}^{L \times 1 \times H \times W}$) are generated using Video Depth Anything. These sequences provide persistent spatial layout and object-distance priors, ensuring consistency with the underlying 3D environment.

- **Embodiment Morphology (Mask Maps):** Robotic morphological masks ($S \in \mathbb{R}^{L \times 1 \times H \times W}$) are produced via an automated segmentation pipeline combining a fine-tuned robotic arm detection model with SAM2 and a dynamic template update strategy. These masks provide joint-level and body-shape grounding, mitigating geometric inconsistencies and self-intersections.

#### 3.4. Parameter Space Representations

This space injects precise numerical motion guidance.

- **Camera Pose:** The camera extrinsic parameters ([R|T]) are processed using a flattening operation into a 12-dimensional numerical vector ($c^{param}_t \in \mathbb{R}^{12}$) for each timestep, providing a rigorous numerical cue for view synthesis.

- **Dual-Arm Action:** Euler angles from raw action ($a_t$) are converted into a 4-dimensional unit quaternion ($q_t$) for numerical stability. A single arm's state is re-parameterized as an 8-dimensional vector ([x, y, z, qx, qy, qz, qw, o]$_t$), where 'o' is gripper state. For dual-arm, these are concatenated into a 16-dimensional vector ($a^{param}_t \in \mathbb{R}^{16}$).

- **Structured Physical Embedding:** The concatenated action and camera features are fed into a lightweight Physics Encoder (linear layers, non-linear activations) to map them into the latent space of the generative model, forming embeddings ($E_{phys}$) that retain quantitative information.

#### 3.5. Collaborative Interaction Mechanism

This mechanism integrates the two spaces into the generative world model.

- **Geometric Grounding via Dense Structural Priors:** RGB input frames, action maps (M), depth maps (D), and morphological masks (S) are individually encoded into the latent space using a pre-trained VAE. For future L frames, depth and mask sequences are populated with values from the current frame ($x_{t_0}$) as a steady anchor. Plücker embeddings (P) are processed by a Camera Adapter. Reducers (1x1 convolutions) compress auxiliary map features for efficiency. The final fused latent input ($z_{in}$) is constructed by element-wise adding camera perspectives and concatenating reduced spatial priors: $z_{in} = [z_{rgb} + Adapter(P)] \oplus z_{spatial}$, where $z_{spatial} = Red.(z_{act}) \oplus Red.(z_{dep}) \oplus Red.(z_{mask})$. This `z_in` serves as the primary input to the denoising backbone.

- **Physical Modulation via Precise Numerical Drivers:** The physics embedding ($E_{phys}$) from the Parameter Space is integrated with global visual features ($E_{clip}$) and optional textual instructions ($E_{text}$) to form a unified semantic sequence ($C_{global} = [E_{phys} \oplus E_{clip} \oplus E_{text}]$). This $C_{global}$ is injected into the generative backbone via cross-attention layers, where latent spatial features act as the query and $C_{global}$ as key/value. This ensures synthesized motion is spatially consistent and tethered to numerical commands.

#### 3.6. Implementation Details

ViPSim is backbone-agnostic, demonstrated by integration with UNet-based (DynamiCrafter) and DiT-based (Wan2.2-TI2V-5B) architectures, using standard noise prediction or flow-matching objectives respectively. Training is performed on the AgiBotWorld-Beta dataset (10 tasks, 1000 trajectories). For efficiency, geometric and semantic features are pre-computed offline during training. During online inference, feature extraction is only performed on-the-fly for sparse reference frames, minimizing latency (1.918s per chunk end-to-end). Evaluation metrics include standard fidelity (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) and EWMBench for Motion Correctness (HSD, NDTW, DYN), Semantic Alignment (BLEU, CLIP, Logics, Diversity), and Scene Consistency.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents quantitative and qualitative evidence demonstrating the effectiveness of ViPSim in achieving consistent long-horizon embodied world models.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Analysis

- **Perceptual Quality (Table I & II):**
 - ViPSim(UNet) exhibited improved perceptual quality metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) compared to the EnerVerse-AC baseline, showing gains of +0.49 PSNR, +0.01 SSIM, and -0.02 LPIPS.
 - ViPSim(DiT) achieved a more substantial performance improvement, reaching PSNR 20.35, SSIM 0.80, and LPIPS 0.19, indicating higher fidelity in generated visual sequences.
 - Statistical analysis over 15 independent runs for ViPSim(DiT) confirmed the stability of these results, with low standard deviations across all metrics (e.g., PSNR ±0.0831, SSIM ±0.0024, LPIPS ±0.0044).

- **EWMBench Protocol (Table III):**
 - ViPSim demonstrated consistent improvements across all EWMBench categories, which evaluate motion correctness, semantic alignment, and scene consistency.
 - **Motion Correctness:** ViPSim showed a notable lead in metrics related to EEF trajectory adherence (DYN, HSD, nDTW). ViPSim(UNet) improved the Motion Sum from 2.0504 (EnerVerse-AC) to 2.4030, and ViPSim(DiT) further increased it to 2.4754. This indicates superior synchronization between generated visuals and input actions.
 - **Semantic Alignment & Scene Consistency:** The framework also showed improvements in maintaining spatiotemporal coherence and instruction-following, with ViPSim(DiT) achieving the highest overall score of 5.5697, compared to EnerVerse-AC's 5.0386.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Results

- **Visual Persistence and Scene Consistency (Figure 4):**
 - ViPSim demonstrated resilience to recursive error accumulation over long horizons. For instance, in "Wiping the table" and "Shelving shampoo" tasks, ViPSim(DiT) preserved object identity and maintained photometric/semantic attributes (e.g., shampoo bottle texture) more effectively than the baseline, which exhibited chromatic drift and material degradation.
 - The model also showed proficiency in restoring environmental elements that were temporarily occluded by the robot, maintaining intricate scene details and exhibiting superior spatiotemporal anchoring compared to the baseline, where background components underwent geometric distortion.

- **Deformable and Rigid Object Manipulation (Figure 5):**
 - **Deformable Objects:** In tasks like "Folding Shorts" and "Wiping the Stain," ViPSim maintained fabric fidelity and structural coherence (e.g., fine-grained folding gaps), whereas baselines showed progressive textural degradation and morphological warping.
 - **Rigid Objects:** For tasks such as "Pick up a Cup" and "Release a Dumbbell," ViPSim ensured superior object permanence, preserving global geometry and fine surface details even under complex occlusions, unlike baselines where interactions with the gripper triggered structural collapse.

- **Dynamic Compatibility (Figure 6b):** Under stress tests with highly subsampled (10-frame interval) end-effector poses, ViPSim inferred stable robot motion aligned with ground truth, demonstrating robustness under sparse motion constraints.

- **Action Faithfulness (Figure 7):** Cross-action swap experiments confirmed that ViPSim precisely followed transplanted reference trajectories, irrespective of the initial scene context, validating the efficacy of its parameter-space embeddings in guiding the generative process with quantitative precision.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study (Table IV & V)

- **Effectiveness of Dual-Space Collaboration:**
 - The baseline using only Action Map performed the lowest, indicating that pixel-level cues alone are insufficient for precise motion scaling.
 - Integrating Raw Action into the Parameter Space significantly improved performance, demonstrating the critical role of numerical grounding.
 - The "Full Strategy" (incorporating Action Map, Plücker embeddings, Depth Map, Mask Map, Raw Action, and concatenated RT matrix) achieved the best performance across all metrics, underscoring the synergistic benefits of combining parametric precision with geometry-aware visual grounding.

- **Impact of Fine-grained Geometric Grounding:**
 - Incrementally adding Depth Map, then Mask Map, progressively enhanced structural fidelity, object-background boundary resolution, and handling of complex interactions.
 - The Double Strategy (full visual grounding combined with parametric guidance) consistently yielded the highest scores in motion correctness and scene consistency.

- **Motion-Centric Metric Prioritization:** While general quality metrics showed marginal differences, ViPSim consistently produced clear and stable improvements in motion-related metrics, emphasizing its utility for long-horizon embodied evaluation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ViPSim represents a notable advancement in the field of Embodied World Models (EWMs) by addressing critical limitations related to long-horizon consistency and the representation gap between actions and visual synthesis. Its significance and potential impact span several areas:

1. **Enhanced Reliability of EWMs:** By effectively bridging the representation gap between low-dimensional actions and high-dimensional video synthesis, ViPSim mitigates cumulative trajectory drift and ensures consistent robot-object interactions over extended rollouts. This significantly enhances the reliability of EWMs, making them more dependable as evaluation benchmarks for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) systems.

2. **Foundational Simulator for Embodied AI Development:** The framework's ability to generate high-fidelity, physically plausible, and long-horizon visual sequences positions EWMs as robust foundational simulators. This has the potential to accelerate the development cycle of embodied agents by providing high-throughput, risk-free environments for model-based reinforcement learning, active inference, and predictive control pipelines.

3. **Safe and Scalable Evaluation:** ViPSim provides an alternative to expensive and safety-critical real-world robot evaluations. It enables researchers to explore complex, edge-case scenarios without the risk of hardware damage or hazardous physical collisions, fostering safer and more scalable research in embodied intelligence.

4. **Handling Complex Interactions:** The framework demonstrates emergent capabilities in generating complex interactions, particularly with deformable objects like cloth, maintaining structural integrity and material fidelity over time. This extends the applicability of EWMs to more intricate manipulation tasks that have traditionally been challenging for simulation.

5. **Robust Generalization:** ViPSim exhibits robust performance in out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios and cross-embodiment tasks. Its ability to generalize beyond training data, such as emulating actions for an unseen robot type, suggests its potential for building more general-purpose embodied agents and reducing the need for extensive, specific training data.

6. **Backbone-Agnostic Design:** The demonstration of ViPSim's compatibility with both UNet-based and DiT-based generative backbones highlights its flexibility and potential for integration with future advancements in generative models, ensuring its longevity and adaptability within the evolving AI landscape.

In summary, ViPSim contributes to overcoming key challenges in embodied AI by providing a high-fidelity and consistent world modeling paradigm. Its capabilities offer a solid foundation for automated evaluation, predictive control, and accelerated development of embodied agents, ultimately paving the way for more capable and versatile general-purpose robots. Future work on integrating VLA policies promises to further enhance its utility in a complete closed-loop evaluation workflow.
