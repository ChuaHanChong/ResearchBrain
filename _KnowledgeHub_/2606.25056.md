# BFMTrack: Latent Sequence Optimization for Physics-Based Motion Tracking with Behavioral Foundation Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Disney Research developed BFMTrack, a framework that extends Behavioral Foundation Models (BFMs) to perform physics-based motion tracking by optimizing temporal sequences of latent behaviors. This approach achieves superior tracking fidelity and motion smoothness over heuristic BFM methods and enables robust, dynamic locomotion on a real bipedal robot, including autonomous recovery from falls.

## Problem

- Traditional physics-based motion tracking methods often require extensive retraining for new motions or environments, limiting scalability and versatility.
- Existing Behavioral Foundation Models (BFMs) primarily focus on time-invariant tasks, and their latent spaces do not directly support time-varying objectives like dynamic motion tracking.
- Current BFM-based tracking heuristics using moving-window averages suffer from a fundamental coherence-precision trade-off and suboptimal behavior due to naive latent concatenation.

## Method

- The method leverages pre-trained latent-conditioned BFM policies and a backward map, which embeds character states into a latent space, trained using the Forward-Backward (FB) framework.
- It introduces ==Latent Sequence Optimization (LSO)==, a REINFORCE-style policy gradient approach, to directly optimize a temporal sequence of latent codes for the BFM policy over a given motion horizon.
- The optimization utilizes a self-contained reward signal derived from the BFM's backward map and incorporates temporally correlated colored noise for exploration to bias towards smooth, coherent latent trajectories, extending to sparse keyframe tracking via SLERP initialization.

## Results

- BFMTrack significantly outperforms the optimal Empirical Reward (ER) baseline in dense motion tracking across all positional metrics (lower EMD, DTW, MPJPE, MMPJPE) and achieves superior smoothness when using temporally correlated noise.
- The Latent Sequence Optimization (LSO) framework consistently achieves better tracking accuracy and motion quality in sparse keyframe tracking scenarios compared to piecewise constant and spherical spline baselines, demonstrating effective in-betweening.
- Qualitative sim-to-real deployment on a 20-DoF Lima bipedal robot demonstrates robust execution of dynamic, contact-rich motions and the ability to autonomously recover from unplanned falls, showcasing real-world applicability and inherent stability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Optimizing an entire sequence of BFM latents, rather than instantaneous selections, is crucial for achieving high-fidelity, time-varying motion tracking with physics-based characters.
> - Injecting temporally correlated noise during latent sequence optimization significantly improves both tracking precision and motion smoothness, effectively mitigating the inherent coherence-precision trade-off in heuristic BFM tracking.
> - Behavioral Foundation Models, when properly prompted through latent sequence optimization, can generate robust and physically plausible motions for diverse tasks, including synthesizing complex actions from sparse keyframes and autonomously recovering from unexpected robot falls.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work operates within the field of physics-based character control, particularly at the intersection of reinforcement learning (RL) and motion tracking for virtual characters and humanoid robots. The broader landscape has seen significant advancements in teaching characters to perform complex behaviors that adhere to physical laws.

Traditional approaches to physics-based motion tracking, such as DeepMimic, often involve training specialized RL policies for each motion clip. While effective for specific tasks, these methods typically require extensive retraining for new motions or environments, limiting their scalability and versatility. Subsequent efforts have aimed to scale motion imitation through techniques like multiple experts, model-based components, learned motion priors, diffusion-based policies, and large-scale training. However, these methods often rely on dense, frame-by-frame supervision, which can lead to issues like overcorrection, jitter, or unnatural behavior if the reference motion is infeasible or the character's state deviates. Adversarial methods have been introduced to loosen this strict supervision by implicitly learning a natural motion distribution, but they can face stability issues and mode collapse. A distinct challenge is tracking sparse keyframes, where supervision signals are minimal, requiring policies that can infer plausible motions between specified points.

More recently, the field has seen the emergence of Behavioral Foundation Models (BFMs). These models aim to provide a universal control policy for physics-based characters by learning a rich repertoire of physically plausible behaviors in a reward-free, unsupervised manner, guided by large-scale motion datasets. BFMs organize these behaviors into a latent space, allowing for zero-shot adaptation to various tasks by prompting with reward functions or target states. This approach yields stable and versatile character performance by encoding solutions to a broad range of environmental interactions.

However, a key limitation of existing BFMs is their primary focus on time-invariant tasks, such as goal-reaching or state-based reward optimization. Their latent space does not directly support time-varying objectives like tracking a dynamic motion sequence. Current BFM-based tracking heuristics, which involve aggregating reference segments into single latents using moving-window averages, suffer from a fundamental coherence-precision trade-off. This trade-off either compromises local precision for global coherence (large windows) or lacks anticipatory context for local precision (small windows), and also leads to suboptimal behavior due to naive latent concatenation.

This research addresses this specific gap by proposing a method that repurposes BFMs for precise, time-varying motion tracking. It seeks to combine the stability and versatility of BFMs with the ability to accurately reproduce dynamic reference motions, including both dense trajectories and sparse keyframes, without requiring task-specific reward engineering or policy retraining. By doing so, it extends the applicability of BFMs to a broader range of real-world scenarios, particularly in robotics and character animation where robust and adaptable motion generation is crucial.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this work arises from a recognized limitation in the current capabilities of Behavioral Foundation Models (BFMs). While BFMs have demonstrated potential in learning universal control policies for physics-based characters, organizing a diverse range of physically plausible behaviors into a latent space, and excelling at time-invariant tasks (e.g., reaching a static goal, optimizing a constant reward), their application to time-varying objectives, such as motion tracking, has been constrained.

The key objectives and motivations are detailed as follows:

- **Address the shortcomings of existing BFM-based motion tracking heuristics:** Current BFM approaches for motion tracking rely on heuristic methods like moving-window averaging to compress reference motion segments into single latent vectors. This aggregation is typically permutation-invariant, meaning it discards the temporal order of states within the look-ahead window. This design leads to a fundamental "coherence-precision trade-off":
 - **Large windows** provide better motion coherence by considering a longer context but can smooth out or "wash out" fast transitions and dynamic nuances.
 - **Small windows** preserve local precision but lack the necessary context for anticipatory movements, potentially leading to jerky or reactive behavior.
 - Furthermore, BFMs are not trained for frequent latent switches during inference, leading to suboptimal or unnatural behavior when latents are concatenated naively on a per-timestep basis. The researchers are motivated to develop a method that resolves this trade-off, enabling both global coherence and local precision.

- **Enable precise motion tracking for physics-based characters using BFMs:** The core objective is to extend the capabilities of BFMs to accurately reproduce time-varying reference trajectories with physics-based characters. This involves moving beyond instantaneous latent selection to optimizing an entire sequence of latents. The aim is to generate motions that are not only kinematically accurate but also physically plausible and stable, leveraging the inherent physics-awareness and diverse skill repertoire learned by the BFM.

- **Eliminate the need for reward engineering and tuning for tracking tasks:** Traditional reinforcement learning approaches for motion tracking often require meticulous design and tuning of reward functions, which can be a complex and time-consuming process. A key motivation is to develop a self-contained optimization loop that derives its reward signal directly from the BFM's backward map, thereby removing the need for manual reward engineering for each tracking task.

- **Generalize motion tracking to sparse keyframe inputs:** Beyond dense motion tracking, the researchers aim to extend the method to synthesize expressive, full-body motions from sparse sets of keyframes. This "inbetweening" capability is crucial for animation and control scenarios where only critical poses or moments are specified. This distinguishes the approach from purely kinematic methods (which lack physical guarantees) and dedicated tracking policies (which require dense references).

- **Demonstrate robustness and real-world applicability:** A practical motivation is to validate the framework's versatility across different input densities (dense vs. sparse) and its applicability to real robotic hardware. This includes evaluating performance in simulation on a complex humanoid model and demonstrating sim-to-real deployment on a bipedal robot, emphasizing the method's ability to handle perturbations and generate natural recovery motions.

In summary, the motivation stems from the desire to unlock the full potential of BFMs for dynamic, time-varying tasks, specifically motion tracking, by developing an optimization framework that is precise, coherent, robust, and requires minimal human intervention in terms of reward design.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology proposed in this paper, BFMTrack, is a two-stage approach designed to enable physics-based motion tracking using Behavioral Foundation Models (BFMs).

**Stage I: BFM Training (Background and Intuition)**

The foundation of BFMTrack is a pre-trained latent-conditioned policy `π(s, z)`, where `s` is the character's state and `z` is a latent code. This policy is trained using the Forward-Backward (FB) framework, which allows for learning a diverse set of behaviors through unsupervised, reward-free interactions with an environment.

- **FB Framework Components:**
 - **Latent-conditioned policy `π(s, z)`:** Determines actions `a` given a state `s` and a latent `z`.
 - **Backward map `B: s → B(s) ∈ Z`:** Embeds any state `s` into the latent space `Z` (unit hypersphere in `R^d`). Conceptually, `B(s)` encodes the reward required to reach state `s`.
 - **Forward map `F: (s, a, z) → F(s, a, z) ∈ R^d`:** Predicts the "value" of an action `a` for achieving the goal encoded by `z` when starting from `s`.
- **Training Process:** Training is performed off-policy using collected transitions `(s_t, a_t, s_{t+1})`. The forward and backward maps are jointly optimized such that `π(s, z) := argmax_a F(s, a, z)^T z`, and `F(s, a, z)^T B(s')` approximates the probability of reaching `s'` from `s` by following `π`. Transitions are replayed with multiple latent codes to teach the policy how to realize any latent from any state.
 - **Extensions for Humanoid Control:** For practical application to humanoids, two extensions are incorporated:
 - **Conditional Policy Regularization (FB-CPR):** Introduces a latent-conditioned discriminator that guides the learned policy towards human-like behaviors, leveraging a large-scale human motion dataset.
 - **Auxiliary Reward Terms:** Includes reward terms (e.g., action rate, torque regularization) to facilitate sim-to-real transfer, particularly for robotic platforms.
 - **Critic Network:** A critic network `Q: (s, a, z) → Q(s, a, z) ∈ R` is trained to integrate additional reward feedback from the discriminator and simulator. An actor network `π(s, z)` is explicitly trained for inference.
- **Inference Limitations (Existing Methods):** For dense tracking, prior BFM-based methods approximate motion over a horizon `L` as a stationary behavior by inferring an "empirical reward (ER) imitation latent" (`z_t = 1/L Σ_{τ=t+1}^{t+L} B(g_τ)`). This aggregation is permutation-invariant and discards temporal order, leading to the coherence-precision trade-off. Additionally, per-timestep latent switching is outside the policy's training distribution.

**Stage II: Latent Sequence Optimization (LSO)**

This stage addresses the limitations of zero-shot BFM tracking by optimizing a temporal sequence of latents `{z_t}^T_{t=1}` directly, using simulation rollouts and policy gradients.

- **Problem Formulation:** For a target motion of length `T`, the latent sequence is treated as a continuous stochastic optimization problem. Gaussian distributions `z_t ~ N(μ_t, σ^2 I)` are defined for each timestep, where `σ` is a fixed standard deviation and the means `{μ_t}^T_{t=1}` are the optimization variables.
- **Initialization:** The sequence of means is initialized using the backward map, encoding the task of reaching the target pose at each timestep: `μ_t = B(g_t)`.
- **Optimization Loop (REINFORCE-style Policy Gradient):**
 1. **Sampling:** For each optimization step, `N` latent sequences are sampled: `z_t^{(n)} ~ N(μ_t, σ^2 I)` for `t=1...,T` and `n=1...,N`.
 2. **Simulation Rollouts:** These `N` sampled latent sequences are rolled out in `N` parallel simulation environments, yielding observed states `s_t^{(n)}`.
 3. **Reward Computation:** The tracking reward `r_t^{(n)}` is computed directly in the latent space using the cosine similarity between the backward embeddings of the simulated state and the target goal: `r_t^{(n)} = (B(s_t^{(n)})^T B(g_t)) / (||B(s_t^{(n)})||_2 ||B(g_t)||_2)`. This makes the optimization self-contained, requiring no additional reward design.
 4. **Discounted Returns and Advantage Estimation:** Discounted returns `R_t^{(n)}` are computed, and an advantage estimate is derived using a leave-one-out baseline to reduce variance.
 5. **Mean Latent Trajectory Update:** The mean latent trajectory `μ_t` at each timestep is updated along the direction `Δμ_t = 1/N Σ_{n=1}^N R_t^{(n)} ∇_{μ_t} log N(z_t^{(n)} | μ_t, σ^2 I)`.
 6. **Projection:** After each update, `μ_t` is `L_2` normalized to project it back onto the unit hypersphere, maintaining geometric feasibility.
- **Temporally Correlated Noise for Exploration:** To bias exploration towards smooth and coherent latent trajectories, sampling is performed using temporally correlated colored noise (e.g., pink noise, `β=1`, where power spectral density `S(f) ∝ 1/f^β`). This helps overcome the issue of uncorrelated perturbations causing frequent, unrelated motion transitions and averaging out high-frequency variations.
- **Sparse Keyframe Tracking Extension:**
 - **Initialization:** For sparse keyframes, `μ_t` is initialized by encoding `B(g_t^k)` at keyframe timesteps `t_k` and interpolating the intermediate `μ_t` values using Spherical Linear Interpolation (SLERP).
 - **Reward:** The tracking reward `r_t` is set to 0 for intermediate timesteps where no reference is available.
 - **Structural Prior:** The correlated Gaussian sampling noise naturally imposes a structural prior on the optimization, regularizing the under-determined problem of synthesizing plausible motion between sparse keyframes.
- **Implementation Details:**
 - **Simulator & Embodiments:** Isaac Sim is used with a 69-DoF SMPL humanoid and a 20-DoF Lima bipedal robot (for real-world deployment).
 - **Control:** Actions are position targets for Proportional-Derivative (PD) controllers.
 - **State Representations:** Two representations are used: `s_act` for the actor (root orientation, velocities, joint states, previous actions) and `s_mot` for the backward map/discriminator (includes root height and rigid body kinematics in a local path frame).
 - **Domain Randomization:** Applied for Lima (foot friction, mass, external forces) with an asymmetric actor-critic architecture (privileged critic). Symmetric setup for SMPL.
 - **BFM Training:** Utilizes FB-CPR within a parallel training framework (1024 environments), fast off-policy learning techniques (UTD ratio 1/64), 256-dimensional latent space, 3M gradient steps. AMASS dataset is used with prioritized motion sampling based on imitation accuracy. Auxiliary regularization rewards are employed.
 - **Latent Optimization:** Performed over 24 iterations with `N=128` parallel environments using Adam optimizer. Specific learning rates, momentum, discount factor, and exploration noise standard deviation are used.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates the BFMTrack framework across dense motion tracking, sparse keyframe tracking, and real-world robot deployment.

**Evaluation Metrics:**
- **Motion Preservation:** Earth Mover’s Distance (EMD) and Dynamic Time Warping (DTW) measure the overall style and temporal structure preservation against the reference.
- **Tracking Accuracy:** Mean Per-Joint Position Error (MPJPE) and Mean Max Per-Joint Position Error (MMPJPE) quantify Euclidean distance errors in Cartesian joint positions. For sparse tracking, these are evaluated only at keyframes.
- **Motion Smoothness:** Mean Per-Joint Acceleration Error (MPJAE) measures the error in joint linear acceleration.

**Dense Motion Tracking:**
- **Comparison to ER Baseline:** The work establishes a baseline using the Empirical Reward (ER) formulation, which approximates motion tracking by inferring a single latent for a short look-ahead horizon `L`. Extensive ablation studies demonstrate that `L=5` provides the optimal balance between anticipation and precision for the ER baseline, illustrating the inherent coherence-precision trade-off. Shorter horizons lead to lag, while longer horizons result in premature actions (e.g., in a kick motion).
- **LSO Performance:**
 - **LSO (β=0, white noise):** This variant, using independent latent optimization at each timestep, demonstrates a substantial improvement in positional tracking accuracy (lower EMD, DTW, MPJPE, MMPJPE) compared to the optimal ER baseline. However, it introduces increased high-frequency jitter, reflected in a higher MPJAE.
 - **LSO (β=1, pink noise):** By incorporating temporal structure through pink noise, this variant achieves the best overall tracking fidelity across all positional metrics. It significantly reduces EMD, DTW, MPJPE, and MMPJPE compared to both the ER baseline and LSO (β=0). It also substantially reduces the MPJAE compared to LSO (β=0), indicating smoother motion.
 - **LSO (β=2, red noise):** This variant, imposing stronger temporal correlations, yields the smoothest motion (lowest MPJAE) among all tested methods. This smoothness comes at the cost of a marginal reduction in tracking precision compared to LSO (β=1), but still outperforms the ER baseline in positional accuracy.
- **Conclusion:** The Latent Sequence Optimization (LSO) framework consistently outperforms zero-shot BFM inference (ER baseline) across all evaluated metrics. The strategic injection of temporally correlated noise is identified as crucial for achieving both high tracking fidelity and motion smoothness.

**Sparse Keyframe Tracking:**
- **Baselines:** Since no established BFM-based baseline exists for sparse keyframe tracking, the researchers compare LSO against two alternative strategies for imposing structural priors:
 - **Piecewise Constant:** Assigns a single latent per keyframe, held constant between frames.
 - **Spherical Spline:** Parameterizes the latent sequence using continuous spherical splines for smoother transitions.
 - Both baselines are evaluated with and without latent optimization via policy gradients.
- **LSO Performance:**
 - **Impact of Optimization:** Latent optimization significantly improves overall tracking performance and motion quality for all methods (Piecewise Constant, Spherical Spline, and LSO) compared to their zero-shot initializations. This highlights the critical role of the optimization step.
 - **Comparative Performance:** LSO with correlated noise outperforms the alternative baselines across most sparse keyframe scenarios.
 - Piecewise Constant performs reasonably well with denser keyframes but degrades in sparser settings due to abrupt latent switches.
 - Spherical Spline successfully smooths transitions but its constrained structure is less effective for dense motions.
 - **Balance:** LSO demonstrates a balance between tracking accuracy and natural motion generation, effectively leveraging flexible trajectories guided by correlated noise to synthesize plausible in-between motions even for challenging, highly dynamic, and contact-rich movements (e.g., a rolling motion tracked from sparse keyframes).

**Robot Deployment:**
- The method is qualitatively demonstrated to transfer to a real 20-DoF bipedal robot (Lima).
- The robot can execute dynamic and contact-rich motions (running, dancing) and exhibits robustness to perturbations and disturbances.
- A notable finding is the robot's ability to recover from an unplanned fall by generating natural, contact-rich getting-up sequences, while remaining consistent with the training motion distribution. This illustrates the BFM's inherent physics-awareness and ability to synthesize plausible behaviors even under significant state deviations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research introduces BFMTrack, a method for physics-based motion tracking that leverages Behavioral Foundation Models (BFMs) by optimizing latent sequences. This work contributes to the field by addressing limitations of BFMs in handling time-varying objectives and offers several significant implications and potential impacts.

#### 5.1. Key Implications

- **Bridging a Gap in BFM Capabilities:** The primary significance lies in extending the utility of BFMs from predominantly time-invariant tasks to high-fidelity, time-varying motion tracking. By enabling BFMs to accurately track dense and sparse reference motions, the research broadens their applicability in animation, robotics, and virtual character control.
- **Enhanced Motion Fidelity and Plausibility:** Unlike purely kinematic tracking methods, BFMTrack generates motions that are inherently grounded in physics and guided by a large-scale motion dataset. This results in physically plausible and natural-looking motions, even when interpolating sparse keyframes or recovering from disturbances.
- **Reduced Engineering Effort:** The method's self-contained optimization loop, which derives its reward signal from the BFM's backward map, eliminates the need for manual reward engineering and tuning for each new tracking task. This streamlining can accelerate development and deployment in practical applications.
- **Robustness to Perturbations and Sim-to-Real Transfer:** BFMs are designed to handle a broad range of interactions and possess an inherent ability to recover from unexpected states. This robustness, combined with the proposed latent sequence optimization, allows the system to synthesize natural-looking recovery behaviors (e.g., getting up from a fall) and perform well in the face of sim-to-real gaps, which are inevitable in robotic deployments. This is particularly valuable for developing robust control policies for physical robots.
- **Generalization to Sparse Inputs:** The method's ability to track sparse keyframes and synthesize plausible "in-between" motions without specialized training for sparse inputs highlights its generalization capabilities. This is advantageous for animation workflows and human-robot interaction where only key poses are specified.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

- **Advanced Character Animation:** For animation studios, BFMTrack could offer a tool for generating high-quality, physically accurate character motions from either dense motion capture data or sparse keyframes, reducing manual labor and increasing realism. It could facilitate more expressive and robust character control in interactive experiences.
- **Robotics Control:** The demonstrated sim-to-real transfer and robustness to perturbations suggest a direct impact on humanoid robotics. BFMTrack could enable robots to execute complex, dynamic behaviors, recover autonomously from falls or disturbances, and adapt to unforeseen environmental interactions, leading to more resilient and capable robotic systems.
- **Foundation for More Complex Tasks:** The framework provides a foundation for future research into more complex control problems. For instance, extending the method to track partial states (e.g., only end-effector positions) or supporting global (world-frame) tracking targets could enable advancements in loco-manipulation, navigation of challenging terrain, or long-horizon tasks for both virtual and physical agents.
- **Further BFM Research:** The work highlights the untapped potential of BFMs beyond their initial scope. By demonstrating how their latent spaces can be effectively utilized for dynamic, time-varying control, it encourages further exploration into designing and optimizing latent behaviors for a wider array of intelligent agent tasks.

#### 5.3. Limitations and Future Work

- **Computational Cost:** The latent sequence optimization is currently computationally expensive, making it unsuitable for interactive animation workflows or real-time onboard robot computation. This limits its use in scenarios requiring real-time adaptation to uncertain or complex environments.
- **Data Requirements:** BFMs, especially those trained with FB-CPR, necessitate large, representative motion datasets. Such datasets may not be readily available for non-humanoid characters or for stylized/caricatured motions.
- **Future Directions:** The authors propose extending the work to:
 - Handle partial state keyframes (e.g., only end-effector targets).
 - Support global (world-frame) tracking targets, which is crucial for temporally extended tasks like loco-manipulation or navigating complex terrain.

In conclusion, BFMTrack marks a notable step towards harnessing the full potential of Behavioral Foundation Models for dynamic control. By offering a robust, adaptable, and less labor-intensive approach to motion tracking, it promises to advance both the realism of virtual characters and the capabilities of humanoid robots.
