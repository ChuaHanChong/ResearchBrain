# Learn More with Less: Uncertainty Consistency Guided Query Selection for RLVR

> [!summary] Summary
> An active learning framework is introduced for Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Reward (RLVR) in mathematical reasoning tasks, utilizing an "uncertainty consistency" metric. This approach enables RLVR to achieve performance comparable to full-dataset training, such as 91.7% on GSM8K and 72.9% on MATH with Qwen2.5-7B, while using only 30% of the training data.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Reward (RLVR) methods for LLMs in mathematical reasoning require substantial query budgets (tens of thousands of examples), incurring high annotation costs.
- The role of query selection strategies in RLVR for reasoning tasks has been largely overlooked, leading to potential issues like model bias, entropy collapse, and gradient instability.
- Traditional active learning (AL) strategies, including uncertainty-based and feature-space coverage methods, fail to significantly improve performance over random selection in the RLVR setting.

## Method

- Proposed an "==uncertainty consistency==" metric to quantify the alignment between subjective model uncertainty (e.g., perplexity) and objective ground-truth correctness (rule-based reward).
- Introduced an offline Point-Biserial Correlation Coefficient (rpbr_{pb}rpb​) for static dataset selection and an online ronlinepbr_{online_pb}ronlinep​b​ metric based on advantage estimates for dynamic, real-time query selection during RL training.
- Developed a selection strategy where, at each online training step, a top percentage (e.g., 30%) of queries exhibiting the largest ronlinepbr_{online_pb}ronlinep​b​ values are selected for computing RL gradients, facilitating stable and efficient learning.

## Results

- The online uncertainty consistency (ronlinepbr_{online_pb}ronlinep​b​) method achieved performance comparable to or slightly exceeding full-dataset training (e.g., **91.7%** on GSM8K vs. **91.5%** for full data with Qwen2.5-**7B**) while utilizing only **30%** of the data.
- All tested classic active learning baselines consistently performed worse than full-dataset training and showed only marginal improvements over random sampling, underscoring the limitations of prior methods in this domain.
- Consistency sampling generalized effectively across various RLVR algorithms (GRPO, RLOO, DAPO, REINFORCE++) and different LLM architectures, maintaining higher policy entropy during early training and ensuring stable learning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Classic active learning methods, which primarily rely on subjective uncertainty, are insufficient for data-efficient training in RLVR contexts for mathematical reasoning.
> - "Inconsistent samples"—where high subjective uncertainty does not align with objective incorrectness—generate extreme policy gradients, leading to high variance and instability during RL training.
> - Prioritizing "uncertainty consistent" samples, where subjective and objective uncertainties are aligned, promotes better exploration, maintains higher policy entropy, and stabilizes RL training, leading to improved sample efficiency.
