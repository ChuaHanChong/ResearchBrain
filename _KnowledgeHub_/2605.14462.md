# Real2Sim in HOI: Toward Physically Plausible HOI Reconstruction from Monocular Videos

> [!summary] Summary
> HA-HOI reconstructs physically plausible 4D human-object interaction from monocular RGB video using a human-first, object-follow formulation: world-grounded SMPL-X motion anchors the metric frame, object mesh and 6-DoF pose follow, VLM-proposed contact priors drive staged optimization, and a PPO residual controller replays the trajectory in simulation. It reaches best human Chamfer distance 7.11 cm and penetration 0.013 cm on BEHAVE.

## Problem

- Prior monocular HOI trackers reconstruct in image or camera space without a stable gravity-aware interaction frame, which breaks on moving-camera in-the-wild videos.
- Existing contact cues are coarse, latent, or unreliable, and pipelines never recover fine hand-object articulation as a first-class part of the interaction.
- Methods are scored as reconstruction systems, not as teacher trajectories, so plausible-looking tracks lose stable contact and dynamic plausibility under physical rollout.

## Method

- Human-centric interaction anchor: world-grounded ==SMPL-X== plus fused ==MANO== hands defines the metric frame, and monocular depth is rescaled to the human region rather than used globally.
- Object-follow reconstruction: inpainting plus single-image 3D generation builds a canonical mesh, scale is picked by silhouette IoU and depth agreement, then health-gated ==FoundationPose== tracking recovers pose.
- Semantic contact refinement with ==InteractVLM== contact proposals over a four-stage objective (contact, penetration, smoothness, anchor, prior), switching from bidirectional chamfer to object signed-distance-field contact loss.

## Results

- On BEHAVE, best human accuracy with CD-h **7.11 cm** versus **7.51**, lowest human acceleration error **0.52** versus **0.54**, all without ground-truth object templates or camera.
- Penetration drops from **0.084 cm** for the strongest baseline to **0.013 cm**, the metric that most directly indicates simulation-ready, contact-faithful interaction geometry.
- Ablating contact optimization slightly improves some surface metrics while penetration worsens from **0.013** to **0.097 cm**; ground-truth mesh only improves CD-o **15.68** to **13.96**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Anchor an under-constrained multi-entity reconstruction on the most reliably estimated agent, then solve the rest relative to it, instead of independent tracks.
> - Surface-distance metrics can reward geometrically close but physically invalid states, so contact and penetration terms must be evaluated separately from Chamfer distance.
> - A residual policy over a reconstructed reference turns kinematic tracks into stable rollouts, letting simulation absorb small errors that would otherwise cause slipping.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses the reconstruction of 4D Human-Object Interaction (HOI), which involves recovering temporally coherent and spatially consistent motions of humans and objects from visual observations. This capability is foundational for applications such as 3D content creation, augmented/virtual reality (AR/VR), embodied artificial intelligence (AI), and simulation-based learning, particularly for reinforcement learning in acquiring human-like behaviors.

Traditionally, obtaining 4D HOI animation has relied on specialized setups like human and object motion capture, multi-view RGB-D systems, or extensive manual post-processing. While effective in controlled environments, these methods are resource-intensive, costly, and lack scalability for widespread use. Monocular RGB videos, in contrast, are abundant and easily captured, offering a more scalable approach despite the inherent under-constraint of monocular reconstruction.

Recent advancements in monocular human-object tracking and reconstruction, exemplified by methods such as VisTracker, CARI4D, and THO, have improved the recovery of human and object trajectories in 3D space. Some of these approaches also incorporate contact-aware modules or learned interaction constraints. However, the authors identify a "fundamental interaction gap" in these existing methods. Their outputs often remain as visually plausible 4D tracks rather than interaction motions that are physically stable, transferable, or directly usable as animation in humanoid-object simulations.

The paper outlines three specific limitations of prior work relevant to the goal of physically plausible HOI animation:
1. **Interaction Frame:** Most existing methods are designed for image- or camera-space reconstruction, lacking explicit organization around a stable, gravity-aware interaction frame, which is crucial for in-the-wild videos with moving cameras.
2. **Contact Detail:** While some contact cues are introduced, they are often coarse, latent, or unreliable, and current pipelines typically do not explicitly recover fine hand-object articulation as an integral part of the interaction.
3. **Simulation Readiness:** These methods are primarily evaluated as reconstruction systems rather than as teacher trajectories for physical humanoid-object rollout. Consequently, even visually reasonable trajectories may fail to maintain stable contact, functional manipulation, or dynamic plausibility when instantiated in a physical simulation.

The core argument of this paper is that HOI is defined by the evolving functional relationship between a human and an object, not merely their co-existence in 3D space. The "interaction gap" signifies that existing pipelines often fail to recover the physical and semantic relation that binds trajectories into a coherent interaction, thus limiting their utility for downstream applications requiring contact, affordance, and action-level consistency. The present work aims to bridge this gap by focusing on generating physically plausible interaction sequences.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a framework capable of reconstructing physically plausible 4D Human-Object Interaction (HOI) animations from in-the-wild monocular videos. This extends beyond merely recovering visually aligned human-object trajectories to producing interaction sequences that can robustly guide a simulated humanoid toward realistic object-interaction behaviors.

The detailed motivations and objectives are as follows:

- **Addressing the "Interaction Gap":** The authors identify a critical limitation in current monocular HOI reconstruction, where methods often provide visually coherent human and object trajectories but fail to capture the underlying physical and semantic relationships that constitute a true interaction. The motivation is to move beyond independent entity tracking and instead treat the interaction itself as the organizing principle of reconstruction.
- **Achieving Physical Plausibility:** A central goal is to produce HOI animations that are not only visually accurate but also physically stable under gravity and rigid-body dynamics. This means ensuring stable contact, functional manipulation, and dynamic plausibility, which are often absent in purely kinematic reconstructions.
- **Generating Simulation-Ready Trajectories:** The reconstructed HOI sequences are intended to serve as "teacher trajectories" for physics-based humanoid-object simulations. This requires outputs that can reliably guide a humanoid controller in a simulated environment, correcting for small errors that might otherwise lead to slipping, jitter, or unstable contact.
- **Adopting a Human-Centric Formulation:** The motivation for the proposed "human-first, object-follow" approach stems from the observation that in most HOI scenarios, the human is the active agent whose actions dictate the object's motion. Therefore, treating the human body as the organizing coordinate system for the interaction is posited to yield more coherent and stable reconstructions.
- **Enhancing Fine-Grained Interaction Details:** The work aims to explicitly recover fine hand-object articulation and precise contact geometry, moving beyond coarse or latent contact cues provided by previous methods. This is crucial for realistic and functional interactions.
- **Enabling Scalable 3D Content Creation and Embodied AI:** By transforming readily available monocular videos into high-fidelity, simulation-ready HOI data, the research seeks to provide a scalable foundation for various applications, reducing reliance on expensive and labor-intensive traditional capture methods.

In essence, the research is motivated by the need for HOI reconstructions that are deeply grounded in physical reality and robust enough for deployment in dynamic simulation environments, thereby unlocking new possibilities for learning and content generation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, HA-HOI (Human-Anchored HOI), processes monocular RGB videos to reconstruct physically plausible 4D human-object interaction trajectories. The core design principle is a human-centric approach, where human motion serves as the interaction anchor, followed by object reconstruction relative to the human, semantic contact refinement, and finally, projection into a physics-based simulation.

The methodology consists of four sequential stages:

#### 3.1. Human-Centric Interaction Anchor

This stage establishes a stable, world-grounded human motion to serve as the organizing coordinate system for the interaction.
- **Human Motion Recovery:** World-space body motion is recovered using the SMPL-X model. To capture fine-grained interaction, MANO hand poses are estimated and fused into the SMPL-X hand parameters. This results in an articulated human sequence providing both global body motion and detailed hand articulation.
- **Depth Calibration:** Monocular depth predictions, which are often unstable and metric-ambiguous, are calibrated to this human anchor. A scale factor is estimated by aligning unprojected depth points within the human region with the recovered human mesh. This calibrated depth provides local geometric evidence for object placement while maintaining the temporal and metric consistency established by the human motion.

#### 3.2. Object-Follow Reconstruction

Following the human anchor, the object's geometry and motion are recovered, ensuring visual grounding and compatibility with human motion.
- **Canonical Object Mesh Construction:** A canonical 3D object mesh is constructed. This involves selecting frames where the object is sufficiently visible, potentially using image inpainting for occluded regions, and then applying a single-image 3D object reconstruction model.
- **Metric Scale Estimation:** Since monocular reconstruction does not provide reliable metric scale, an object scale (s\*o) is estimated. This is achieved by evaluating candidate scales against reference frames using a combined metric of silhouette IoU (Intersection over Union) and calibrated depth consistency (Equation 1). The median over reference frames ensures robustness. If the estimated scale deviates significantly from a unit scale, the object pose registration is re-run.
- **Object Pose Tracking:** The scaled object mesh's pose is tracked across the video using FoundationPose. To enhance robustness against occlusion or motion blur, a "health-gated tracking" mechanism is employed. This mechanism maintains a health score based on depth consistency and mask IoU (Equation 6) and operates in three modes: healthy tracking (standard FoundationPose), degraded tracking (increased refinement iterations), and re-registration (full pose estimation from scratch). Low-confidence segments can be interpolated.
- **Transformation to Human-Centric Frame:** The tracked camera-space object poses are transformed into the human-centric world frame established in the first stage.
This stage yields a coarse HOI trajectory, which is visually grounded and temporally coherent but may still lack precise interaction details like stable contact.

#### 3.3. Semantic Contact Refinement

This stage refines the coarse trajectory to express a coherent body-object relationship, moving beyond mere visual alignment.
- **Contact Proposal:** InteractVLM is utilized as a contact proposal module. Given representative interaction frames, it predicts probable contact regions on both the human (mapped to SMPL-X vertices, Ch) and the object (mapped to reconstructed object mesh vertices, Co).
- **Optimization Framework:** The interaction is refined by optimizing a subset of variables (selected body joints, hand pose, and object pose) to minimize a loss function (Equation 2). This function combines several terms:
 - **Contact Loss (Lc):** Initially, a bidirectional nearest-neighbor distance between contact regions. Once coarse alignment is achieved, it switches to an object signed-distance-field contact objective (Equation 3), which pulls human contact vertices onto the object surface while allowing local hand/body and object pose adaptation.
 - **Penetration Loss (Lp):** Penalizes interpenetration between the human and object meshes using a signed distance metric (Equation 12).
 - **Temporal Smoothness Loss (Ls):** Encourages smooth motion by penalizing acceleration (Equation 13).
 - **Anchor Loss (La):** Keeps the refinement anchored to the coarse reconstruction from the previous stage.
 - **Regularization Loss (Lr):** Regularizes body and hand poses, including HaMeR priors for hand articulation.
- **Progressive Optimization:** A four-stage progressive optimization strategy is used:
 1. **Depth Pre-optimization:** Adjusts SMPL translation for depth map alignment while maintaining contact.
 2. **Object Pose Pre-optimization:** Optimizes object 6-DoF poses with contact and temporal constraints.
 3. **Joint Optimization:** Jointly optimizes SMPL body pose and object poses, allowing them to adapt to each other.
 4. **Hand Refinement:** Refines wrist and finger poses using contact, coverage, penetration, non-contact repulsion, and hand-pose priors, with HaMeR priors for stability.
The output of this stage is a contact-refined kinematic trajectory, which is visually aligned and geometrically interaction-aware.

#### 3.4. Physics-Based Interaction Projection

The final stage projects the refined kinematic trajectory into a physics simulator to ensure dynamic stability and plausibility.
- **Humanoid-Object System Instantiation:** The optimized human and object sequences are converted into reference motions for a corresponding humanoid-object system in a physics simulator (IsaacGym). The object is modeled as a dynamic rigid body, and the humanoid is controlled through joint-level actuation. Both human and object meshes undergo convex decomposition (e.g., V-HACD for objects, custom MuJoCo XML for humanoids) to prepare them for collision detection.
- **Sequence-Specific Residual Controller:** Instead of direct replay, a sequence-specific residual controller is trained using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO). The policy observes the current simulated state and the reference state, outputting a residual action that modifies the reference joint target. This allows the simulator to correct small kinematic errors.
- **Reward Function:** The controller is trained with a reward function (Equation 4) that balances motion fidelity and interaction stability. This includes terms for human pose tracking, object pose tracking, hand articulation, maintaining predicted body-object contacts, and penalizing implausible velocities (Appendix A.5.2).
- **Simulation Rollout:** After training, the controller is rolled out once, and the simulated states are converted back into SMPL-X parameters and object poses.
This final step yields a physically plausible humanoid-object animation that closely adheres to the input video while demonstrating stable interaction under physical simulation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The HA-HOI framework was evaluated using the BEHAVE dataset, a benchmark for human-object interaction captured with multi-view RGB-D sensors, providing pseudo-ground-truth SMPL body and object registrations. The method takes monocular RGB video as input and is evaluated end-to-end without BEHAVE-specific training or ground-truth object templates.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Comparison on BEHAVE

(Referencing Table 1 in the paper)
HA-HOI demonstrated competitive performance against existing monocular HOI reconstruction and tracking methods (CHORE, InterTrack, HOI-TG, VisTracker, CARI4D, THO), despite operating in a more general setting (not requiring predefined object meshes or pose initialization).

- **Human Reconstruction Accuracy:** HA-HOI achieved the best human reconstruction accuracy, with a Chamfer Distance for the human mesh (CD-h) of 7.11 cm, lower than the 7.51 cm reported for CARI4D. It also obtained the lowest human acceleration error (Acc-h) at 0.52 cm/s², indicating superior temporal stability and accurate body geometry. These results support the proposed human-centric formulation, which anchors the reconstruction around a coherent human motion.
- **Interaction-Specific Physical Plausibility:** A significant finding is HA-HOI's substantial improvement in the physical plausibility of the reconstructed sequences. The method achieved the lowest human-object penetration score of 0.013 cm, a notable reduction from 0.084 cm (CARI4D). This metric directly reflects the objective of producing physically meaningful contact relations suitable for simulation.
- **Object Reconstruction:** While some template-based methods achieved lower object Chamfer distance (CD-o) by leveraging strong object geometry priors, HA-HOI, which reconstructs object geometry without ground-truth templates at test time, demonstrated that optimizing for interaction consistency leads to more contact-faithful and simulation-ready 4D HOI reconstruction. The combined human-object Chamfer distance (CD-c) for HA-HOI was 10.74 cm.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Results

(Referencing Figure 3 in the paper)
Qualitative comparisons on BEHAVE test sequences, training sequences, and in-the-wild videos illustrated the practical advantages of HA-HOI. These results are presented after physics simulation, representing a more stringent evaluation than visual reconstruction alone.

- **Stability and Coherence:** HA-HOI consistently produced stable, temporally coherent, and visually aligned HOI animations across diverse scenarios.
- **Functional Relation Preservation:** The method effectively preserved the functional relationship between the human body and the object. Examples included maintaining plausible hand-object contact and stable object placement (chair, table interactions) and preserving grasp-like relations (weightlifting example), ensuring the object remained physically connected to the human action after simulation.
- **Contact Region Superiority:** Compared to CARI4D, HA-HOI showed a clear advantage in contact regions. Prior methods often depicted visually aligned global human-object layouts, but with body parts that did not reliably explain the interaction (e.g., floating or penetrating fingers, lack of stable contact). HA-HOI's approach explicitly addressed this by reconstructing contact-aware HOI animations with articulated hands and stable object support.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

(Referencing Table 2 in the paper)
An ablation study on BEHAVE isolated the contributions of different components:

- **Ground-Truth Object Mesh (w/ GT mesh):** Using ground-truth object meshes moderately improved object (CD-o) and combined (CD-c) Chamfer distances (13.96 and 10.12, respectively). Human metrics remained largely unchanged. This indicated that while open-world object reconstruction is a source of error, the human-centered approach is robust and not overly dependent on object templates.
- **Ground-Truth Camera Parameters (w/ GT cam.):** Replacing estimated camera parameters with ground truth yielded only a small improvement in object and combined Chamfer distances (CD-o: 15.19, CD-c: 10.45). This suggested that camera estimation is not the primary bottleneck and HA-HOI maintains stability in a fully automatic setting.
- **Without Contact Optimization (w/o contact opt.):** Removing the semantic contact refinement step resulted in a significant increase in human-object penetration (0.097 cm, compared to 0.013 cm for the full model). While some visual-alignment scores might even marginally improve without contact optimization, the drastic increase in penetration confirmed that purely surface-distance metrics can favor geometrically close but physically invalid reconstructions. This finding is critical, supporting the argument that contact refinement introduces a deliberate bias towards physical compatibility, substantially reducing implausible intersections without compromising meaningful reconstruction accuracy.

In summary, the findings indicate that HA-HOI successfully moves beyond independent entity tracking to generate physically plausible, contact-consistent, and temporally stable 4D HOI animations, which are notably more suitable for physics-based humanoid-object simulation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The HA-HOI framework represents a methodological advancement in the field of 4D human-object interaction (HOI) reconstruction from monocular videos. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Shift Towards Physically Grounded Interaction:** The work challenges the conventional focus on merely visually plausible human and object trajectory reconstruction. By explicitly emphasizing the recovery of the functional and physical relationship between the human and object, HA-HOI contributes to a paradigm shift towards generating physically grounded interaction animations. This is crucial for applications that demand more than superficial visual fidelity.
- **Improved Simulation Readiness:** A core contribution is the generation of "simulation-ready" HOI trajectories. The integration of physics-based interaction projection and a sequence-specific residual controller ensures that the reconstructed motions are dynamically stable and can guide simulated humanoids effectively. This directly addresses a critical limitation of prior kinematic-only reconstructions, which often fail under physical rollout. This capability has a substantial impact on embodied AI, where robust, physically plausible demonstrations are essential for training agents to perform human-like behaviors.
- **Scalability from Monocular Video:** By demonstrating a robust pipeline for converting in-the-wild monocular RGB videos into high-quality, simulation-ready HOI data, the research offers a scalable solution for data acquisition. This has the potential to democratize access to rich interaction data, reducing the reliance on expensive and labor-intensive multi-view or motion capture systems. This scalability is vital for large-scale content creation and diverse learning scenarios in virtual environments.
- **Enhanced Contact and Articulation Fidelity:** The human-first, object-follow formulation combined with VLM-guided semantic contact proposals and progressive optimization stages enables the recovery of more fine-grained hand-object articulation and consistent contact. This level of detail is critical for realistic manipulation tasks and provides a richer understanding of human-object interactions.
- **Broader Impact on Downstream Applications:** The outputs of HA-HOI can serve as foundational data for various downstream applications:
 - **3D Content Creation:** Enabling the rapid generation of realistic human-object animations for games, film, and virtual reality.
 - **Robotics and Embodied AI:** Providing high-fidelity demonstrations for reinforcement learning, allowing robots to learn complex human-like manipulation skills in simulation before transfer to real-world deployment.
 - **Human-Computer Interaction:** Facilitating the development of more intuitive and responsive interactive systems.
 - **Biomechanics and Ergonomics:** Offering a tool for analyzing human movement and interaction with objects in diverse contexts.

**Limitations and Future Work (as identified by the authors):**
The authors acknowledge that the reconstruction stage remains vision-based, implying that severe occlusion could still lead to inaccuracies in human body pose or object geometry. Future work aims to deploy the reconstructed interactions on real humanoid robots, which introduces additional challenges such as sim-to-real transfer, hardware limitations, sensing noise, and contact robustness. Addressing these gaps would further solidify the vision of using general monocular HOI videos as scalable demonstrations for learning humanoid-object behavior in real-world scenarios.

In conclusion, this work represents a significant step towards leveraging ubiquitous monocular video data as a rich source for understanding and synthesizing physically plausible human-object interactions. By prioritizing interaction coherence and simulation readiness, HA-HOI contributes to advancing fields that rely on realistic human-like behavior in virtual and potentially physical environments.
