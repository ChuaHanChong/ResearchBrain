# Diff4Splat: Controllable 4D Scene Generation with Latent Dynamic Reconstruction Models

> [!summary] Summary
> DIFF4SPLAT is a feed-forward framework for generating dynamic 4D scenes, represented as deformable 3D Gaussian fields, from a single image, text prompt, and camera poses. This method synthesizes high-fidelity, controllable dynamic 3D content in approximately 30 seconds, achieving a 60-fold acceleration over previous optimization-based approaches while demonstrating strong geometric integrity and precise camera control.

## Problem

- Multi-stage pipelines for dynamic 3D scene generation are computationally expensive, time-consuming (tens of minutes to GPU hours), and prone to error accumulation across stages.
- Existing feed-forward methods are typically limited to producing 2D videos or static 3D scenes, lacking explicit, dynamic 3D geometry and often suffering from visual artifacts.
- There is a lack of frameworks that can efficiently and directly synthesize an explicit, controllable dynamic 4D scene representation without per-scene optimization.

## Method

- Introduces **DIFF4SPLAT**, a feed-forward framework that integrates a video ==diffusion model== backbone with a deformable ==3D Gaussian== field representation.
- Develops a Latent Dynamic Reconstruction Model (**LDRM**) that directly processes 3D-aware latent tensors from a video diffusion model to regress static and dynamic 3D Gaussian attributes and deformations.
- Employs a progressive training scheme: initial pre-training on static scenes at low then high resolution, followed by fine-tuning on dynamic datasets with a unified loss function including ==Flow Matching==, photometric, geometric, and motion components.

## Results

- Generates dynamic 4D scenes in approximately 30 seconds, demonstrating a 60-fold acceleration and **3.2x** memory reduction compared to optimization-based baseline methods.
- Achieved a lower FVD of 210.153 (compared to 235.961 for AC3D + Mosca) and higher CLIP-Score (23.123) and CLIP-Aesthetic score (5.231), indicating superior visual quality and semantic alignment.
- Exhibited strong geometric integrity (highest MASt3R correspondence matches) and precise camera control with significantly lower Relative Pose Error (RPE) for translation (0.012 vs 3.001) and rotation (0.008 vs 0.810) compared to implicit baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Video diffusion models can be repurposed to directly predict explicit, dynamic 4D scene representations by translating 2D-oriented latent features into structured deformable 3D Gaussians.
> - A progressive training strategy, starting with static scene learning before introducing dynamics, is crucial for achieving stable and high-fidelity 4D scene generation.
> - Integrating explicit camera trajectory control (via Plücker embeddings) and motion-aware losses directly into the generative process is essential for producing geometrically accurate and temporally consistent 4D scenes.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The generation of dynamic 3D scenes from a single image represents a complex challenge in computer vision. Prior approaches to this problem have generally followed two main paradigms, each with limitations that this work aims to address.

One paradigm involves multi-stage pipelines that sequentially generate a 2D video and then apply 3D reconstruction techniques to infer a 3D scene. Examples of such methods include those combining video generation with 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) reconstruction, such as AC3D followed by Mosca. While capable of producing explicit 3D representations, these methods are often characterized by significant computational costs, long processing times (ranging from tens of minutes to several GPU hours per video), and susceptibility to errors accumulated across stages. They also generally lack end-to-end control over the final 4D output.

The alternative paradigm focuses on direct feed-forward generation. However, existing methods in this category have largely been confined to producing 2D video sequences or static 3D scenes. They typically do not capture explicit, dynamic 3D geometry, which is considered essential for a comprehensive 4D representation that enables applications like novel view synthesis, depth extraction, and real-time interaction. Some feed-forward approaches generate dynamic pointmaps, but these often struggle to achieve photorealism, resulting in visual artifacts.

This research identifies a critical gap in the existing landscape: the absence of a framework that can directly and efficiently synthesize an explicit, controllable dynamic 4D scene representation. Video diffusion models have demonstrated capabilities in generating high-quality, temporally coherent 2D videos and are being explored for 3D-aware consistency. Static 3D scene generation has also seen advancements leveraging image diffusion models. However, integrating the generative power of diffusion models with the explicit representation of dynamic 3D geometry in an efficient, feed-forward manner remained an active area of research.

DIFF4SPLAT addresses this by introducing a unified framework that combines a video diffusion model backbone with a deformable 3D Gaussian field representation. It aims to bridge the gap between efficient 2D video generation and detailed 4D scene synthesis, moving beyond the limitations of both multi-stage and static-only feed-forward methods by directly generating a 4D representation without per-scene optimization. This positions DIFF4SPLAT as an advancement in the field of dynamic 3D scene generation, emphasizing efficiency and explicit geometric control.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce DIFF4SPLAT, a feed-forward framework designed for the generation of dynamic 4D (3D + time) scenes from a single input image. This framework aims to directly predict a dynamic scene, represented as a deformable 3D Gaussian field, in a single forward pass.

The specific goals and motivations underpinning this work are detailed as follows:

- **Addressing Computational Inefficiency:** A significant motivation stems from the high computational cost and time requirements of existing methods for generating explicit 4D representations. Current state-of-the-art optimization-based approaches, even for a single video, can demand substantial GPU hours or at least half an hour of processing time (e.g., DimensionX, Mosca). Such inefficiencies render these methods impractical for many real-world applications where rapid content generation is necessary. DIFF4SPLAT aims to collapse this multi-stage pipeline into approximately 30 seconds, representing a substantial acceleration over existing methods.

- **Unifying Generation and Explicit Representation:** The research seeks to overcome the "fundamental dilemma" identified in the introduction: the trade-off between efficient feed-forward generation (often limited to 2D outputs or static 3D) and the ability to produce explicit, dynamic 3D geometry. The objective is to enable a diffusion model to directly synthesize a complete 4D representation that includes appearance, geometry, and motion within a single, end-to-end trainable model, thereby eliminating the need for test-time optimization or post-hoc processing steps.

- **Enhancing Controllability and Consistency:** Existing video diffusion models, while effective for 2D video generation, often lack precise camera motion control and may exhibit inconsistencies in dynamic content, which can hinder robust 3D reconstruction. A key objective is to integrate explicit camera trajectory control (via Plücker embeddings) and motion constraints directly into the generation process. This aims to produce 4D scenes that are not only visually high-fidelity but also geometrically accurate and temporally consistent, aligning with specified camera movements and text prompts.

- **Facilitating Diverse Applications:** By generating an explicit deformable 3D Gaussian field, the framework aims to inherently support a wide range of downstream applications. These include high-fidelity video generation, accurate depth rendering, novel view synthesis, and potentially real-time interaction, which are challenging to achieve with implicit or 2D-only representations.

- **Bridging Representational Gaps:** The research is motivated by the need for an architectural solution that can effectively translate the latent features generated by 2D-oriented video diffusion models into structured 4D representations. This involves designing a component, such as a Video Latent Transformer, capable of interpreting spatio-temporal features as dynamic point clouds and regressing parameters for deformable 3D Gaussians.

- **Enabling Data-Driven Learning of 4D Priors:** To achieve these objectives, a significant motivation involves the creation of a large-scale, high-quality 4D dataset. Such a dataset, annotated with metric-scale geometry and motion labels, is considered crucial for supervising the model to learn robust geometric and dynamic priors, moving beyond existing datasets that often lack metric scale or comprehensive dynamic annotations.

In summary, the core motivation for DIFF4SPLAT is to develop an efficient, end-to-end, and controllable feed-forward method that directly generates explicit, high-fidelity dynamic 3D scenes from a single image, thereby making advanced 4D content creation more practical and accessible.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DIFF4SPLAT addresses the generation of dynamic 4D scene representations from a single input image ($I_0$), a text prompt ($C_{ctx}$), and camera poses ($P$) represented by Plücker embeddings. The methodology integrates a video diffusion model with a novel latent reconstruction Transformer to combine 2D appearance priors, geometry, and motion cues for 4D synthesis.

The overall approach involves several key innovations:

1. **Data Curation:**
 - A scalable 4D data annotation pipeline was developed to convert real-world videos into metric-scale spatio-temporal point maps.
 - **Synthetic Datasets:** Seven synthetic datasets (TartanAir, MatrixCity, PointOdyssey, DynamicReplica, Spring, VKITTI2, MultiCamVideo) were leveraged. These provide ground-truth annotations for geometry and motion and diverse camera trajectories essential for learning robust geometric and dynamic priors.
 - **Real-world Datasets:** Two real-world datasets (RealEstate10K, Stereo4D) were incorporated to enhance generalization. To address their common lack of metric scale and limited camera motion, VideoDepthAnything and MegaSaM were used to recover metric depth maps and re-estimate camera extrinsics, enabling aligned geometry across frames. This process generated approximately 130,000 diverse 4D training scenes, which underwent quality control, including dynamic object masking and reprojection error filtering.

2. **Latent Dynamic Reconstruction Model (LDRM):**
 - The LDRM is designed to avoid per-scene optimization by directly processing latents from a video diffusion model.
 - A pre-trained video diffusion model, conditioned on $I_0$ and $P$, generates a compact, 3D-aware latent tensor $z \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times h \times w \times c}$ that aims to ensure structural and appearance consistency across views.
 - This latent tensor $z$ and camera pose conditions are used to create and concatenate latent and pose tokens.
 - These tokens are processed by Transformer blocks.
 - A lightweight decoder then regresses 3D Gaussian attributes from the Transformer output, followed by a 3D deconvolutional layer to map these attributes to source video pixels.

3. **Deformable Gaussian Fields:**
 - The method extends the static 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) representation to model dynamic scenes.
 - A static scene is represented by a collection of $M$ Gaussian primitives, each defined by its mean location ($\mu_p$), scaling factors ($s_p$), orientation quaternion ($q_p$), opacity ($\alpha_p$), and color features ($c_p$). Spherical Harmonics (SH) are used for view-dependent effects.
 - For dynamic scenes, an inter-frame deformation model is introduced. For each Gaussian $p$ at time step $t$, the LDRM predicts a displacement ($\Delta\mu_p^t$), a rotation adjustment ($\Delta q_p^t$), and a scale modification ($\Delta s_p^t$).
 - The deformed parameters at time $t$ are updated: $\mu_p^t := \mu_p^0 + \Delta\mu_p^t$, $q_p^t := q_p^0 \otimes \Delta q_p^t$, and $s_p^t := s_p^0 + \Delta s_p^t$.
 - The LDRM generates a Gaussian feature map $G$ (for static parameters) and a deformation map $D$ (for $\Delta\mu, \Delta q, \Delta s$). The deformation map has 10 channels for offsets.
 - During rendering, a pruning strategy removes Gaussians with opacity below a threshold ($\tau_{opacity} = 0.005$).

4. **Training Objective:**
 - The network is optimized using a unified supervision scheme with a total loss function ($L$) that combines four components:
 - **Flow Matching (FM) Loss ($L_{FM}$):** Applied to the base video diffusion model parameters to finetune it on the curated 4D dataset, ensuring generated latent sequences match the data distribution.
 - **Photometric Loss ($L_{photo}$):** A composite loss (MSE + LPIPS) between rendered images ($\hat{I}_k$) and ground-truth images ($I_k$) to ensure high-fidelity appearance.
 - **Geometric Loss ($L_{geo}$):** Enforces accurate depth relationships using a covariance-based term between rendered depth maps ($\hat{D}_k$) and ground-truth depth ($D^*_k$), along with a total variation loss ($L_{TV}$) for local smoothness.
 - **Motion Loss ($L_{motion}$):** Utilizes 3D point tracking data (ground-truth for synthetic data, CoTracker for real-world) to penalize discrepancies between predicted displacements ($\Delta\hat{x}_j$) and ground-truth displacements ($\Delta x_j$), with an additional L1 regularization.

5. **Progressive Training Scheme:**
 - **Static Geometry Pre-training (40K iterations):** LDRM is initially trained on static scenes at low resolution (256x256) using only photometric and geometric losses. The deformation module is frozen during this stage.
 - **High-Resolution Refinement (40K iterations):** The model continues training on static scenes at a higher resolution (512x512) with the deformation module still frozen, enhancing reconstruction fidelity.
 - **Dynamic Scene Fine-tuning (20K iterations):** The entire model, including the deformation module, is unfrozen and fine-tuned on dynamic datasets, incorporating the full loss function, including the motion loss. This stage focuses on learning complex temporal deformations.

The framework is implemented using CogVideoX, a pretrained Video Diffusion Transformer, with 16 standard Transformer blocks in the LDRM. Training is conducted for 100,000 iterations on 32 A100 GPUs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of DIFF4SPLAT demonstrates its performance across video generation, novel view synthesis, and geometry extraction, particularly in terms of efficiency, visual quality, and geometric fidelity.

1. **Efficiency:**
 - A primary finding is the significant reduction in generation time. DIFF4SPLAT can generate a dynamic scene within approximately 30 seconds.
 - This represents a substantial acceleration (approximately 60-fold) compared to optimization-based methods, which, despite utilizing state-of-the-art components (e.g., AC3D + Mosca), require around 45 minutes for a single video. This efficiency gain makes dynamic 3D scene generation more practical for real-world applications.
 - The approach also demonstrates a reduction in memory consumption during reconstruction, from 80GB to 25GB (a 3.2x reduction) in comparable settings, due to the elimination of gradient computation for optimization.

2. **Quantitative Performance (Appearance and Aesthetic Quality):**
 - **Video Generation & Aesthetic Quality (Table 1):**
 - DIFF4SPLAT achieved competitive or superior metrics compared to baselines, including optimization-based methods. It recorded an FVD of 210.153, which is lower (better) than the 235.961 of "AC3D + Mosca."
 - The method showed the highest CLIP-Score (23.123) and CLIP-Aesthetic score (5.231), indicating strong semantic alignment with text prompts and high visual appeal.
 - Its QA-Quality score (2.813) was competitive with the best baseline (2.842).

3. **Quantitative Performance (Geometric Integrity and Controllability):**
 - **Geometric Integrity (Table 2):**
 - DIFF4SPLAT exhibited strong geometric integrity, achieving the highest average number of matches (5114.22) in MASt3R correspondence matching.
 - It also scored highest in Subject Consistency (88.32) and highly in Background Consistency (89.89), suggesting stable and coherent scene geometry over time.
 - **Generation Controllability (Table 3):**
 - The explicit 4D representation significantly improved camera pose fidelity. DIFF4SPLAT achieved substantially lower Relative Pose Error (RPE) values compared to implicit baselines: 0.012 for translation (vs. 3.001 for AC3D) and 0.008 for rotation (vs. 0.810 for AC3D). This indicates precise adherence to specified camera paths.

4. **Qualitative Results:**
 - Visually, the generated 4D scenes were observed to be more appealing, temporally consistent, and geometrically accurate than those produced by baseline methods (Figure 3). Rendered videos showed smoother motion and fewer artifacts. This is attributed to the model's ability to directly predict a deformable 3D Gaussian field, providing a continuous representation of scene dynamics.
 - The framework supports diverse applications, including novel view synthesis for dynamic scenes, depth map extraction, and real-time interactive exploration (Figure 5).

5. **Ablation Studies:**
 - **Effect of Deformation Gaussian Field:** Ablation studies confirmed that the deformation Gaussian module is critical for distinguishing camera motion from foreground object movement. Its removal led to motion blur, spike artifacts, and degradation in image quality (Figure 6, Table 4). The motion loss component was found to significantly improve dynamic video generation quality across multiple metrics.
 - **Effect of Explicit Representation:** The explicit 3D Gaussian Splatting representation was shown to be advantageous for camera controllability (lower RPE) and enabling additional functionalities like depth rasterization and real-time interaction (Table 3). Interactive exploration latency was drastically reduced to 6.7 ms from 28000 ms of implicit models.
 - **Importance of Progressive Training:** The progressive training scheme was crucial for robust 4D scene generation. Direct dynamic training without static pre-training resulted in unstable training and degraded scene quality, highlighting the value of establishing a static scene representation foundation before learning dynamics (Figure 7).
 - **Joint vs. Separate Training:** Joint training of the diffusion model and LDRM yielded improved performance over separate training, indicating the benefit of end-to-end learning for compatibility between components (Table C).
 - **Comparison with Two-Stage Baselines:** The unified approach consistently outperformed a two-stage "generate-video-then-reconstruct" baseline, especially in temporal consistency and efficiency, by avoiding the overhead of pixel-space decoding and mitigating multi-view inconsistencies (Table C).

Overall, the findings suggest that DIFF4SPLAT represents an advancement in dynamic 3D scene generation by combining efficiency, high-fidelity synthesis, and precise geometric control within a single, end-to-end framework.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of DIFF4SPLAT represents an advancement in the field of dynamic 3D scene generation, addressing several limitations of prior approaches and demonstrating the feasibility of an efficient, end-to-end feed-forward pipeline.

**Key areas of significance and potential impact include:**

1. **Bridging the Efficiency-Fidelity Gap:** The work unifies dynamic 3D generation and representation into a single feed-forward pass, which is a departure from conventional multi-stage pipelines that are often slow and computationally expensive. By generating dynamic 4D scenes in approximately 30 seconds, DIFF4SPLAT offers a substantial acceleration (up to 60-fold compared to certain optimization-based methods). This efficiency gain has the potential to make high-fidelity dynamic 3D scene generation practical for a wider range of real-world applications where speed is critical, such as interactive content creation, rapid prototyping, and real-time simulation.

2. **Advancing Explicit 4D Representation:** DIFF4SPLAT directly synthesizes a deformable 3D Gaussian field, an explicit representation that captures appearance, geometry, and motion. This contrasts with methods that produce only 2D videos or implicit 3D representations, which often require additional, costly processing steps to extract useful geometric information or enable explicit control. The direct generation of explicit 4D geometry enhances the interpretability and manipulability of the generated scenes.

3. **Enhanced Controllability and Consistency:** The framework incorporates precise camera trajectory control and motion consistency constraints, leading to generated scenes that adhere faithfully to specified camera paths and exhibit high temporal and geometric stability. This level of control is valuable for professional content creation workflows requiring specific camera movements or scene dynamics. The low Relative Pose Error (RPE) further validates the accuracy of the camera control.

4. **Novel Architectural Contributions:** The introduction of the Video Latent Transformer architecture is a notable contribution. This architecture effectively bridges the gap between 2D latent features from video diffusion models and a structured 4D representation, enabling the direct prediction of deformable 3D Gaussian parameters. This demonstrates a novel way to leverage the powerful priors learned by diffusion models for 3D generation.

5. **Facilitating Research through Dataset Release:** The creation of a large-scale 4D dataset with metric-scale geometry and motion annotations is a significant contribution. The intention to release this dataset will provide a valuable resource for the research community, potentially fostering further developments in 4D scene understanding, generation, and reconstruction.

6. **Enabling Diverse Applications:** The explicit 4D representation naturally supports a range of downstream applications beyond video generation. These include highly accurate novel view synthesis, direct depth map rasterization, geometry extraction, and real-time interactive exploration (e.g., in virtual reality, augmented reality, or robotics simulations). The reduction in interactive exploration latency to milliseconds further underscores this potential.

7. **Potential for Hybrid Approaches:** While designed as a feed-forward method, the highly efficient and effective initialization provided by DIFF4SPLAT could also serve as a strong starting point for optimization-based 4D reconstruction methods, potentially accelerating their convergence and improving their robustness. This suggests a synergistic potential with existing techniques.

**Broader Implications:**
While DIFF4SPLAT advances the capability to generate dynamic 4D content from single images, the ethical implications of such powerful generative models, particularly the potential for creating deceptive content ("deepfakes"), are acknowledged. The authors highlight the importance of implementing safeguards, such as watermarking, to promote responsible deployment and encourage the community to adopt best practices for ethical use.

In conclusion, DIFF4SPLAT offers a significant step forward in making high-quality, controllable, and efficient dynamic 3D scene generation practical, with broad implications for content creation, simulation, and interactive digital experiences.
