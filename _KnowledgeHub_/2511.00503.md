# Diff4Splat: Controllable 4D Scene Generation with Latent Dynamic Reconstruction Models

> [!summary] Summary
> DIFF4SPLAT is a feed-forward framework for generating dynamic 4D scenes, represented as deformable 3D Gaussian fields, from a single image, text prompt, and camera poses. This method synthesizes high-fidelity, controllable dynamic 3D content in approximately 30 seconds, achieving a 60-fold acceleration over previous optimization-based approaches while demonstrating strong geometric integrity and precise camera control.

## Problem

- Multi-stage pipelines for dynamic 3D scene generation are computationally expensive, time-consuming (tens of minutes to GPU hours), and prone to error accumulation across stages.
- Existing feed-forward methods are typically limited to producing 2D videos or static 3D scenes, lacking explicit, dynamic 3D geometry and often suffering from visual artifacts.
- There is a lack of frameworks that can efficiently and directly synthesize an explicit, controllable dynamic 4D scene representation without per-scene optimization.

## Method

- Introduces **DIFF4SPLAT**, a feed-forward framework that integrates a video ==diffusion model== backbone with a deformable ==3D Gaussian== field representation.
- Develops a Latent Dynamic Reconstruction Model (**LDRM**) that directly processes 3D-aware latent tensors from a video diffusion model to regress static and dynamic 3D Gaussian attributes and deformations.
- Employs a progressive training scheme: initial pre-training on static scenes at low then high resolution, followed by fine-tuning on dynamic datasets with a unified loss function including ==Flow Matching==, photometric, geometric, and motion components.

## Results

- Generates dynamic 4D scenes in approximately 30 seconds, demonstrating a 60-fold acceleration and **3.2x** memory reduction compared to optimization-based baseline methods.
- Achieved a lower FVD of 210.153 (compared to 235.961 for AC3D + Mosca) and higher CLIP-Score (23.123) and CLIP-Aesthetic score (5.231), indicating superior visual quality and semantic alignment.
- Exhibited strong geometric integrity (highest MASt3R correspondence matches) and precise camera control with significantly lower Relative Pose Error (RPE) for translation (0.012 vs 3.001) and rotation (0.008 vs 0.810) compared to implicit baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Video diffusion models can be repurposed to directly predict explicit, dynamic 4D scene representations by translating 2D-oriented latent features into structured deformable 3D Gaussians.
> - A progressive training strategy, starting with static scene learning before introducing dynamics, is crucial for achieving stable and high-fidelity 4D scene generation.
> - Integrating explicit camera trajectory control (via Plücker embeddings) and motion-aware losses directly into the generative process is essential for producing geometrically accurate and temporally consistent 4D scenes.
