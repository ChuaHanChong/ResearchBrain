# Bag of Tricks for Image Classification with Convolutional Neural Networks

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Amazon Web Services systematically evaluated a collection of training procedure refinements and minor model architecture adjustments for Convolutional Neural Networks. They demonstrated that by combining these 'tricks,' ResNet-50's Top-1 accuracy on ImageNet improved by nearly 4% to 79.29%, and these enhanced models consistently performed better when applied to object detection tasks.

## Problem

- The primary focus in deep learning research for image classification was on developing novel model architectures, often overlooking the impact of training procedure refinements.
- Crucial training details (e.g., data preprocessing, optimization methods, specific hyperparameter choices) were often undocumented or hidden in source code, leading to a lack of systematic understanding of their individual and combined effects on model performance.
- Existing baselines for widely-used CNN models could be improved without resorting to fundamentally new and more complex architectures.

## Method

- The researchers systematically evaluated and quantified the impact of various training procedure refinements and minor architectural tweaks, categorizing them into ==efficient training techniques==, ==model tweaks==, and ==training refinements==.
- They established a strong baseline and then conducted extensive ablation studies, sequentially applying and stacking these 'tricks' to widely-used CNN architectures like ==ResNet-50==, ==Inception-V3==, and ==MobileNet==.
- The improved models were then tested for their transfer learning capabilities by using them as backbones for object detection and semantic segmentation tasks to assess their broader utility.

## Results

- ResNet-50's Top-1 validation accuracy on ImageNet increased from an initial **75.3%** to **79.29%** by systematically applying the identified 'tricks'.
- Efficient training techniques, including large-batch training with specific heuristics and FP16 low-precision training, significantly reduced training time (e.g., ResNet-50 from **13.3 min/epoch** to **4.4 min/epoch**) while maintaining or slightly improving accuracy.
- Enhanced pre-trained models led to a **4% mAP** gain in Faster-RCNN for object detection on PASCAL VOC, demonstrating improved transferability, though some refinements were found to be suboptimal for semantic segmentation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Training procedure refinements and subtle model tweaks, often overlooked, contribute as much to CNN accuracy gains as architectural innovations.
> - Systematically combining a collection of well-known or slightly modified training practices can yield substantial performance improvements for established deep learning models.
> - Models pre-trained with these refined techniques show improved performance when transferred to downstream tasks like object detection, though some techniques might be task-specific in their efficacy (e.g., semantic segmentation).

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of image classification using Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) has witnessed rapid and significant advancements since AlexNet's breakthrough in 2012. Historically, a dominant narrative in this progress has been the relentless pursuit of novel model architectures. From VGG, Inception, and ResNet to DenseNet and NASNet, researchers have primarily focused on designing more intricate and efficient network structures to achieve state-of-the-art accuracy on challenging benchmarks like ImageNet. The "model accuracy improvement" trend, as the authors note, has largely been attributed to these architectural innovations.

However, the authors point out a critical, yet often overlooked, parallel development: "training procedure refinements." These refinements, encompassing changes in data preprocessing, data augmentation, optimization methods, loss functions, and even minor architectural tweaks, have also played a substantial, albeit less celebrated, role. In much of the existing literature, these crucial details are frequently relegated to brief mentions in implementation sections or are only discoverable by sifting through accompanying source code. This lack of systematic documentation and empirical evaluation has created a "dark matter" of practical knowledge in deep learning, where seemingly minor adjustments can lead to significant performance gains but are not formally studied or quantified.

This paper directly addresses this gap. It shifts the spotlight from architectural novelty to the cumulative power of these often-underestimated "tricks." By systematically examining and quantifying the impact of a collection of such refinements, the paper provides a crucial perspective. It demonstrates that substantial performance improvements can be achieved not just by designing entirely new networks, but by meticulously optimizing and combining well-known, or even subtly modified, training practices. This work acts as a demystifier, bringing these 'hidden' heuristics to the forefront and proving their collective efficacy. It suggests that a significant portion of the observed accuracy gains in recent years might stem from these procedural details rather than solely from groundbreaking architectural paradigms. Furthermore, by improving the baseline performance of established architectures like ResNet-50, it sets new, higher standards for what can be achieved, influencing future benchmarking and model development efforts.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind this research can be summarized as follows:

- **Systematic Evaluation of Training Refinements:** The core objective was to systematically examine and empirically evaluate a collection of training procedure refinements and minor model architecture tweaks. The authors recognized that while these refinements significantly contribute to model accuracy, they are often briefly mentioned or hidden in source code, leading to a lack of clear understanding of their individual and combined impact.
- **Quantify the Impact of "Tricks":** The paper aimed to quantify the magnitude of improvement that these "tricks" – which range from optimization strategies to data augmentations and subtle architectural adjustments – bring to the final model accuracy. This involves conducting thorough ablation studies to isolate the contribution of each refinement.
- **Improve Baselines for Established Models:** A key motivation was to significantly boost the accuracy of widely-used CNN models, such as ResNet-50, Inception-V3, and MobileNet, without resorting to fundamentally new and more complex architectures. The goal was to show that by combining these refinements, even older architectures could outperform newer, supposedly more advanced models trained with standard pipelines.
- **Demonstrate Transferability:** The authors intended to prove that the accuracy improvements achieved on large-scale image classification tasks (like ImageNet) translate into better performance when these enhanced models are used as pre-trained backbones for other computer vision tasks, specifically object detection and semantic segmentation. This highlights the practical utility of their findings for a broader range of applications.
- **Provide a "Bag of Tricks" for Practitioners:** Implicitly, the paper aimed to offer a readily applicable "recipe" or set of guidelines for practitioners and researchers to achieve higher performance with existing or new CNN models, by making these effective techniques explicit and their benefits well-documented.
- **Promote Reproducibility:** By making their implementations and training scripts publicly available in GluonCV, the authors sought to enhance the reproducibility of their findings and facilitate the adoption of these beneficial training practices within the community.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors adopted a systematic, empirical approach centered around extensive ablation studies to evaluate the impact of various "tricks." Their methodology can be broken down into the following key steps:

- **Baseline Establishment:**
 - They first defined a **baseline training procedure** for three common CNN architectures: ResNet-50, Inception-V3, and MobileNet. This baseline mirrored a widely used implementation for ResNet, including specific data preprocessing steps (random cropping, flipping, color jittering, PCA noise, normalization), Xavier weight initialization, and Nesterov Accelerated Gradient (NAG) descent.
 - Models were trained for 120 epochs on 8 Nvidia V100 GPUs with a total batch size of 256, using an initial learning rate of 0.1, decayed by 10 at 30, 60, and 90 epochs. ImageNet was the primary dataset for evaluation.

- **Categorization and Evaluation of Refinements:** The "tricks" were broadly categorized and evaluated:

 - **Efficient Training Techniques (Section 3):** These techniques aim to leverage modern hardware capabilities without sacrificing accuracy, sometimes even improving it.
 - **Large-batch Training:**
 - **Linear scaling learning rate:** Adjusting the initial learning rate proportionally to the batch size.
 - **Learning rate warmup:** Gradually increasing the learning rate from a small value at the beginning of training.
 - **Zero $\gamma$ initialization:** Initializing the $\gamma$ parameter of Batch Normalization layers in residual blocks to zero, effectively making the residual connection an identity mapping initially.
 - **No bias decay:** Applying L2 regularization (weight decay) only to weights, not biases or BN parameters.
 - **Low-precision Training:** Using FP16 (half-precision floating point) instead of FP32 for computations to accelerate training, alongside techniques like mixed-precision training (FP16 activations/gradients, FP32 master weights).
 - Ablation studies were performed on ResNet-50 to quantify the impact of each large-batch heuristic and FP16.

 - **Model Tweaks (Section 4):** Minor adjustments to the network architecture that barely change computational complexity but can affect accuracy. They focused on ResNet architecture.
 - **ResNet-B:** Modifying the stride of the first two convolutions in Path A of the downsampling block to avoid ignoring input feature map information.
 - **ResNet-C:** Replacing the initial 7x7 convolution in the input stem with three smaller 3x3 convolutions to reduce computational cost and potentially improve representation.
 - **ResNet-D (Proposed):** Adding a 2x2 average pooling layer before the 1x1 convolution in Path B of the downsampling block to avoid ignoring input information.
 - These tweaks were evaluated individually and cumulatively on ResNet-50-D, which represents ResNet-50 with all three tweaks applied.

 - **Training Refinements (Section 5):** Additional techniques primarily focused on optimizing the training process for better convergence and generalization.
 - **Cosine Learning Rate Decay:** Replacing the step-wise learning rate decay with a cosine annealing schedule.
 - **Label Smoothing:** Modifying the one-hot target distribution to a smoothed version during training to prevent overfitting and encourage better generalization.
 - **Knowledge Distillation:** Using a larger, pre-trained "teacher" model (ResNet-152-D) to guide the training of a "student" model (ResNet-50-D) through an additional distillation loss.
 - **Mixup Training:** Generating new training examples by linearly interpolating two random examples and their labels, aiming for improved generalization and robustness.
 - These refinements were applied sequentially (stacked) to the enhanced ResNet-50-D model and also evaluated on Inception-V3 and MobileNet. The training epochs were increased to 200 for Mixup training.

- **Evaluation on Different Datasets:** While ImageNet (ILSVRC2012) was the primary dataset, they also evaluated the best combination of tricks on the MIT Places365 dataset to demonstrate generalizability.

- **Transfer Learning Evaluation (Section 6):** To assess the broader utility of these improved pre-trained models, they evaluated their performance as backbones for:
 - **Object Detection:** Using Faster-RCNN on the PASCAL VOC dataset.
 - **Semantic Segmentation:** Using Fully Convolutional Network (FCN) on the ADE20K dataset.
 - The base models were swapped with various improved versions, keeping other task-specific settings constant to isolate the impact of the pre-trained backbone. Performance was measured using mAP for detection and Pixel Accuracy (PixAcc) and mean Intersection over Union (mIoU) for segmentation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents compelling empirical evidence supporting the effectiveness of combining various training procedures and minor model tweaks.

- **Baseline Improvements from Efficient Training:**
 - Switching from a baseline of Batch Size (BS) 256 with FP32 to BS 1024 with FP16 significantly reduced training time (e.g., ResNet-50 from 13.3 min/epoch to 4.4 min/epoch).
 - Crucially, by combining all large-batch heuristics (linear scaling LR, warmup, zero $\gamma$, no bias decay) and FP16, the models not only trained faster but also achieved slightly *higher* accuracy compared to the FP32/BS256 baseline (e.g., ResNet-50 improved from 75.87% to 76.21% Top-1 accuracy). The ablation study showed that individual heuristics like linear scaling alone could degrade performance, but the combination compensated for this.

- **Impact of Model Tweaks:**
 - The model tweaks (ResNet-B, ResNet-C, and the proposed ResNet-D) cumulatively improved ResNet-50-D's Top-1 accuracy by 1% over the initial ResNet-50.
 - ResNet-D, specifically, led to a Top-1 accuracy of 77.16% for ResNet-50 (when combined with efficient training settings). These tweaks had minimal impact on computational complexity (e.g., ResNet-50-D was only 3% slower than ResNet-50 despite a 15% FLOPs increase).

- **Effectiveness of Training Refinements (Stacked Approach):**
 - By sequentially stacking the refinements on the improved ResNet-50-D model, significant accuracy gains were observed.
 - **Cosine decay** provided a notable boost (e.g., ResNet-50-D from 77.16% to 77.91% Top-1).
 - **Label smoothing** further improved accuracy (e.g., ResNet-50-D to 78.31% Top-1).
 - **Mixup training** proved highly effective, pushing ResNet-50-D to 79.15% Top-1 (without distillation).
 - **Knowledge distillation** from a ResNet-152-D teacher, particularly when combined with mixup, yielded the highest accuracy.
 - **Overall, the combination of all tricks boosted ResNet-50's Top-1 validation accuracy from an initial 75.3% to an impressive 79.29% on ImageNet.** This 4% absolute gain is substantial and allows ResNet-50 to outperform newer architectures like SE-ResNeXt-50 trained with standard pipelines (Table 1).

- **Generalizability Across Models and Datasets:**
 - The improvements were not limited to ResNet-50; Inception-V3 and MobileNet also showed consistent accuracy gains when applying the efficient training and training refinements.
 - The refinements successfully improved performance on the MIT Places365 dataset, demonstrating their transferability across different large-scale image classification tasks.

- **Transfer Learning Performance:**
 - **Object Detection (PASCAL VOC):** Models trained with the "bag of tricks" consistently yielded better Faster-RCNN performance. The highest ImageNet accuracy (79.29%) directly translated to the best mAP (81.33%) for object detection, outperforming the standard model by 4%. This indicates a strong positive correlation between source task accuracy and downstream task performance.
 - **Semantic Segmentation (ADE20K):** While cosine learning rate decay improved FCN performance, other refinements like label smoothing, distillation, and mixup provided *suboptimal or even slightly degraded* results for semantic segmentation. The authors hypothesize that pixel-level prediction tasks might be negatively affected by "softened labels" or "blurred pixel-level information" introduced by these techniques, suggesting a potential trade-off or task-specific optimal parameterization.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The paper's significance and potential impact are profound, particularly for the practical application and advancement of deep learning in computer vision:

- **Demystification and Democratization of Performance Gains:** The most significant contribution is the systematic aggregation and empirical validation of seemingly minor "tricks." By bringing these largely undocumented or briefly mentioned implementation details to the forefront, the paper demystifies a critical aspect of achieving state-of-the-art performance. This work provides a clear, actionable roadmap for practitioners, enabling them to significantly boost model accuracy without requiring years of experience in hyperparameter tuning or architectural design. It democratizes access to high-performance deep learning.

- **New Baselines and Benchmarking:** The substantial accuracy improvements achieved on classic architectures like ResNet-50 (e.g., 75.3% to 79.29% on ImageNet) effectively establish new, higher baselines for these models. This forces the research community to reconsider what constitutes "state-of-the-art" and potentially shifts the focus of future research. Instead of merely comparing novel architectures, researchers might now need to ensure that all models are trained with these optimized procedures for a fair comparison.

- **Practical Utility and Cost-Effectiveness:** The "bag of tricks" offers a highly practical and cost-effective way to improve model performance. Instead of investing heavily in developing or searching for new, often computationally expensive, architectures (e.g., through Neural Architecture Search), practitioners can apply these validated techniques to existing, well-understood models to achieve comparable or even superior results. This is particularly valuable for industries where quick, reliable performance boosts are essential.

- **Emphasis on Training Procedures:** The paper re-emphasizes that training procedures are not mere implementation details but are as critical as architectural design in achieving high performance in deep learning. This could encourage more systematic research into optimization algorithms, learning rate schedules, data augmentation strategies, and regularization techniques.

- **Improved Transfer Learning:** The consistent positive impact on object detection highlights the value of pre-training models with these refined techniques. Stronger classification backbones lead to better performance on downstream vision tasks, accelerating progress in various application domains. The nuanced results for semantic segmentation also open up new avenues for research, prompting investigations into task-specific optimization strategies for different computer vision problems.

- **Enhanced Reproducibility:** By meticulously documenting each trick, presenting detailed ablation studies, and making their code publicly available via GluonCV, the authors significantly enhance the reproducibility of deep learning research. This transparency is vital for scientific progress and building upon existing work.

In conclusion, "Bag of Tricks for Image Classification with Convolutional Neural Networks" is a landmark paper that brought clarity and quantification to the often-overlooked practical aspects of deep learning. Its findings have profoundly impacted how deep learning models are trained, setting new standards for performance and highlighting the immense power of systematic engineering and empirical validation in the pursuit of more accurate and efficient AI systems.
