# ELVIS: Ensemble-Calibrated Latent Imagination for Long-Horizon Visual MPC

> [!summary] Summary
> ELVIS, a latent model predictive controller from KU Leuven and Flanders Make, enables reliable long-horizon planning in visual control tasks, particularly under partial observability and occlusions. It integrates a recurrent state-space model with ensemble-calibrated latent imagination, outperforming existing baselines in DeepMind Control Suite and achieving robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer on a challenging sand-spraying task.

## Problem

- Reliable long-horizon planning in visual Model Predictive Control (MPC) is challenging due to compounding model errors and computational expense.
- Existing visual MPC methods often rely on short receding horizons (e.g., 3-5 steps) and are brittle under partial observability or occlusions, leading to suboptimal policies when future trajectories are multimodal.
- A consistent framework is needed where model-based reinforcement learning objectives and online planner optimization are aligned, especially concerning the treatment of uncertainty.

## Method

- **ELVIS** integrates a ==Recurrent State-Space Model== (**RSSM**) for maintaining a latent belief state under partial observability with an extended Model Predictive Path Integral (**MPPI**) control.
- It introduces ==GMM-MPPI==, a Gaussian Mixture Model-based **MPPI** controller, to manage branching imagined futures and multimodal action-value distributions over long horizons, preventing mode collapse.
- An ensemble-UCB-gated, time-varying ==lambda-return== is employed to adaptively control the influence of uncertain imagined futures, enabling uncertainty-aware soft truncation in both learning and planning.

## Results

- ELVIS achieved state-of-the-art performance across 14 DeepMind Control Suite visual tasks, consistently improving both sample efficiency and final return compared to TD-MPC2 and DreamerV3.
- Demonstrated robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer on a sand-spraying task, yielding superior surface quality (Rrms: 2.2 ± 0.4 mm) and robustness to dust and sensory noise under severe occlusions, significantly outperforming baselines.
- Ablation studies confirmed that increased planning horizon, uncertainty-aware truncation, and multimodal action proposals are critical components contributing to ELVIS's performance gains.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Maintaining multiple action sequence hypotheses via GMM-MPPI is crucial for effective long-horizon planning, especially when optimal future trajectories are inherently multimodal.
> - Dynamically adjusting the effective planning horizon based on epistemic uncertainty, using UCB-gated lambda-returns, significantly improves robustness against compounding model errors under partial observability.
> - Aligning the uncertainty treatment in both actor-critic learning and online MPC planning through a shared uncertainty-aware return enhances overall system coherence and performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is situated within the field of model-based reinforcement learning (RL), particularly focusing on visual control and model predictive control (MPC) in challenging environments. Model-based RL has demonstrated capabilities in achieving sample efficiency compared to model-free counterparts by learning a world model and leveraging it for planning or policy learning. Existing approaches generally fall into two categories: those that use imagined rollouts to augment policy/value learning (e.g., Dreamer-style agents) and those that employ MPC for online trajectory optimization in a learned latent space (e.g., TD-MPC, TD-MPC2).

A significant challenge in current visual control with model-based RL is reliable long-horizon planning. Existing methods often rely on short receding horizons (e.g., 3-5 steps) due to the computational expense and increasing error-proneness of longer latent rollouts. Model errors compound over extended horizons, leading to unreliable imagined returns. This issue is amplified under partial observability and visual occlusions, which reduce visual evidence and force the planner to depend more on latent imagination. Furthermore, many MPC instantiations rely on the Markov assumption, often using heuristically stacked frames as a proxy state. This approach can fail under severe occlusions or viewpoint shifts, necessitating memory or a belief state rather than short-term visual history.

This paper specifically addresses these limitations by integrating a recurrent state-space model (RSSM) for maintaining a latent belief under partial observability, a characteristic of Dreamer-style agents, with an extended form of latent planning inspired by TD-MPC2. It aims to make long-horizon planning practical by mitigating trajectory branching and managing compounding epistemic uncertainty, thereby advancing the state of the art in robust visual control under challenging conditions. The approach differentiates itself from prior work that uses uncertainty primarily for dynamics propagation, exploration, or safety triggering, by integrating it into the return propagation mechanism for both learning and planning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a latent model predictive controller capable of performing reliable long-horizon planning in visual control tasks, particularly under conditions of partial observability and visual occlusions. The motivations stem from two key limitations observed in existing model-based RL approaches:

1. **Challenges of Long-Horizon Planning:**
 - **Computational Expense and Error Accumulation:** Long rollouts with learned latent dynamics are computationally intensive and prone to compounding model errors. As the planning horizon increases, predicted returns become less reliable.
 - **Branching Futures and Multimodal Action-Value Distributions:** Over long horizons, imagined rollouts can diverge into multiple distinct future trajectories, each potentially leading to high rewards. Standard unimodal MPC approaches, such as those relying on a single Gaussian proposal (e.g., in MPPI), tend to average across these incompatible action-sequence hypotheses, leading to conservative or suboptimal policies and mode collapse.
 - **Brittleness under Occlusions:** Compounding model error is particularly problematic under partial observability, where visual occlusions reduce sensory information, forcing the agent to rely more heavily on its internal latent imagination. This can make deep imagination brittle and unreliable.

2. **Limitations of Existing Control Paradigms:**
 - **Short Receding Horizons:** Current visual MPC often operates with very short horizons (e.g., 3-5 steps) due to the aforementioned issues, limiting the agent's foresight and potential for global optimization.
 - **Inadequate Handling of Partial Observability:** Many RL methods rely on stacking a short history of frames as a Markov proxy, which is insufficient for tasks with significant occlusions, viewpoint shifts, or distractors. Such scenarios require a robust memory or belief state to maintain coherence.
 - **Misalignment between RL Objectives and Planner Optimization:** A consistent framework is needed where the learning objectives (e.g., actor-critic training) and the planner's long-horizon optimization are aligned, especially concerning the treatment of uncertainty.

To address these, the paper proposes ELVIS, which is designed to make long-horizon planning practical by introducing mechanisms to manage multi-modal trajectory distributions and adaptively control the influence of uncertain imagined returns. The core motivation is to enhance data efficiency, robustness under partial observability, and zero-shot transfer performance in complex visual control settings.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ELVIS (Ensemble-Calibrated Latent Imagination for Long-Horizon Visual MPC) integrates several components to facilitate long-horizon, uncertainty-aware planning under partial observability. The framework builds upon a Recurrent State-Space Model (RSSM) and extends Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) control.

**A. Recurrent State-Space Model (RSSM) Foundation:**
ELVIS uses an RSSM, similar to Dreamer-style agents, to maintain a latent belief `s_hat_t = (h_t, z_t)` under partial observability. Here, `h_t` is a deterministic memory state summarizing the observation history, and `z_t` is a stochastic latent state capturing aleatoric uncertainty in high-dimensional observations. The RSSM is trained to maximize a variational evidence lower bound (ELBO), encouraging accurate observation reconstruction, reward prediction, and consistency between the filtered posterior and latent prior. This memory-augmented belief state serves as the starting point for latent imagination and downstream planning.

**B. GMM-MPPI for Multi-Modal Long-Horizon Planning:**
To address the issue of branching imagined futures and multimodal action-value distributions over long horizons, ELVIS replaces standard unimodal MPPI with a Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) based MPPI.
- **Multiple Gaussian Proposals:** Instead of a single Gaussian, ELVIS maintains `M` parallel Gaussian proposals, `q_m(a_0:H-1) = N(a_0:H-1; μ_m, Σ_m)`, for action sequences. Each mode `m` has its own mean `μ_m` and covariance `Σ_m`.
- **Sample Generation and Scoring:** For each mode, `K` candidate action sequences are drawn. These sequences are rolled out under the learned RSSM latent dynamics from the current belief state. Each rollout is assigned a scalar score `G^(k)_m` using a shared uncertainty-aware return mechanism (described below).
- **Mode-wise Weighting and Update:** To normalize scores across modes, a global best score `G_star` is computed. Mode-wise weights `w^(k)_m` are then calculated using a relative-to-best softmax, ensuring consistent normalization. Each mode's moments (`μ_m`, `Σ_m`) are updated by weighted moment matching, preserving multiple coherent hypotheses. This prevents mode collapse that can occur with a single-Gaussian update when optimal trajectories are multimodal.
- **Policy-Random Initialization and Warm-start:** Planning is warm-started across time using a receding-horizon shift of mean sequences. To diversify hypotheses within a planning call, each mode is initialized with a distinct mixing ratio `α_m` of a learned actor prior `a_π` and a random sequence `a_rand`. After `L` MPPI iterations, the first action of the highest-scoring mode's mean sequence is executed.

**C. Uncertainty-aware Learning and Planning via UCB-gated λ_t-returns:**
To control compounding model error, especially under visual occlusions, ELVIS introduces a shared uncertainty-aware return.
- **Ensemble-UCB Uncertainty:** An ensemble of `M` latent critics `{V_i(s_hat)}` is maintained. Along imagined rollouts, the mean `μ_t` and standard deviation `σ_t` of the ensemble's value predictions are computed. An optimism-flavored importance score, `UCB(s_hat_t) = μ_t + βσ_t`, is then derived. High UCB indicates a valuable and epistemically informative future, suitable for exploration.
- **Soft Truncation with Time-Varying λ_t:** The UCB score is mapped to a time-dependent `λ_t` for the λ-return calculation: `λ_t = λ_max - (λ_max - λ_min) * norm(UCB(s_hat_t))`. This means high UCB values lead to a smaller `λ_t` (more bootstrapping, shorter effective horizon), while low UCB values lead to a larger `λ_t` (deeper look-ahead). This mechanism allows for uncertainty-triggered soft truncation: reliance on deeper, noisier imagination is reduced when a high-UCB future is found, but rewards are propagated further if the rollout remains uninformative.
- **Shared Return for Imagined TD Learning and MPPI Scoring:** The time-varying λ-return `G_t` is defined recursively. Crucially, this `G_0` serves as the TD target for training the critic ensemble and an actor prior `π_θ(a|s_hat)`. This actor then warm-starts the GMM-MPPI by initializing action sequences. This shared return aligns the objectives of policy learning and online planning, allowing RL to provide a guiding prior for the sampling-based planner, which in turn refines proposals.

The overall ELVIS algorithm involves: (1) updating the belief state using the RSSM posterior, (2) executing the `PLAN` function (GMM-MPPI with UCB-gated λ_t) to select an action, (3) storing the transition, (4) updating the RSSM, and (5) updating critics and the actor prior using the shared uncertainty-aware return.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of ELVIS was structured to address its performance on visual control tasks and its robustness under extreme partial occlusions and sim-to-real transfer.

**A. Visual Control on DeepMind Control Suite (DMC)**
- **Overall Performance:** ELVIS demonstrated state-of-the-art performance across 14 DeepMind Control Suite visual tasks, consistently improving both sample efficiency and final return compared to TD-MPC2 and DreamerV3. ELVIS ranked either first or second on every task, indicating robust performance across diverse environments. This suggests that the design choices for handling partial observability and long-horizon planning also contribute to performance gains in standard visual control benchmarks.
- **Ablation Studies:** Controlled ablations on the DMC suite isolated the contributions of ELVIS's core components:
 - **Planning Horizon:** Increasing the MPC horizon from H=5 to H=15 substantially improved aggregated performance, indicating that deeper foresight is beneficial when combined with a learned world model.
 - **Uncertainty-Aware Truncation:** Replacing the uncertainty-based λ-return truncation with a non-uncertainty variant (fixed-λ / no-UCB) led to a significant drop in aggregated score, demonstrating the necessity of uncertainty awareness in managing compounding model error.
 - **Multimodal Action Proposals:** Replacing the GMM action proposal with a unimodal Gaussian (No GMM) also reduced performance, supporting the claim that long-horizon latent planning benefits from multimodal action proposals.

**B. Sand Spray Sim-to-Real Zero-Shot Transfer**
- **Task Setup:** ELVIS was evaluated on a real-world sand-spraying task designed as a proxy for industrial operations in harsh, low-visibility settings, featuring severe visual occlusions and sensory noise. The sim-to-real interface was primarily geometric (heightmaps), with significant real-world discrepancies (dust, sensor noise, dynamics mismatch). Performance was assessed using surface-quality metrics: root-mean-square roughness (Rrms), peak-to-valley roughness (Rt), and waste volume ratio (rwv).
- **Simulation Learning Performance:** In the simulated sand spray task, both ELVIS and TD-MPC2 significantly outperformed DreamerV3, highlighting the advantage of model-based planning over purely policy-based action selection in challenging visual control. ELVIS also exhibited more stable learning and reduced sensitivity to random seeds compared to TD-MPC2.
- **Real-World Evaluation:** Zero-shot transfer to the physical sand-spraying testbed revealed a change in relative ranking. While ELVIS outperformed baselines in simulation, its advantage became more pronounced in the real world: ELVIS ≫ DreamerV3 > TD-MPC2.
 - **ELVIS:** Achieved the best surface quality (Rrms: 2.2 ± 0.4 mm, Rt: 17.5 ± 0.5 mm, rwv: 6.3 ± 1.1%). It produced uniform sand deposition and was robust to dust and sensory noise, maintaining homogeneous coverage even under severe occlusions.
 - **TD-MPC2:** Degraded sharply after transfer, often collapsing to repeatedly spraying a single corner, indicating brittle planning under partial observability and unmodeled real-world perturbations.
 - **DreamerV3:** Transferred more reliably than TD-MPC2, supporting the benefit of recurrent memory, but its lack of online MPC refinement and explicit uncertainty handling limited its ability to recover from accumulated errors and maintain consistent coverage.

These results collectively indicate that ELVIS's combination of long-horizon planning, recurrent belief tracking, and uncertainty-aware truncation is crucial for robust zero-shot real-world performance in environments with severe partial observability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of ELVIS represents a notable step in addressing the challenges of long-horizon planning and robust control in visual model-based reinforcement learning, particularly under conditions of partial observability and visual occlusions.

The primary significance lies in demonstrating a practical approach to making long-horizon latent imagination reliable. By introducing **GMM-MPPI**, ELVIS provides a mechanism to maintain multiple coherent hypotheses over long planning horizons, effectively counteracting mode averaging that can render unimodal planners ineffective when future trajectories are inherently multimodal. This capability is critical for complex tasks where optimal actions might lead to distinct, high-reward outcomes.

Furthermore, the **ensemble-UCB-gated, time-varying λ_t-return** offers an adaptive strategy for managing compounding model error. By dynamically trading off bootstrapping with look-ahead based on critic uncertainty, ELVIS stabilizes deep imagination and prevents unreliable distant predictions from dominating control decisions. This "soft truncation" is particularly impactful in visually complex or occluded environments where the reliability of observations and model predictions can fluctuate. The consistent use of this uncertainty-aware return for both actor-critic learning and MPPI trajectory scoring creates a strong alignment between the learning and planning objectives, enhancing overall system coherence and performance.

The experimental results highlight several areas of potential impact:
- **Improved Performance and Sample Efficiency:** ELVIS's state-of-the-art performance on standard visual control benchmarks indicates its general applicability beyond niche, challenging scenarios. Its ability to achieve high data efficiency can accelerate the development and deployment of RL agents.
- **Enhanced Robustness under Partial Observability:** The strong zero-shot sim-to-real transfer performance on the sand-spraying task, characterized by severe occlusions and sensory noise, demonstrates ELVIS's robustness in real-world conditions. This is a crucial step towards deploying RL systems in industrial or unstructured environments where visual sensing is often imperfect. The comparison with baselines underscores the importance of recurrent belief tracking and explicit uncertainty management for maintaining coherent control when observations are unreliable.
- **Advancement in Model-Based RL Methodology:** ELVIS provides a recipe for combining the strengths of Dreamer-style recurrent world models with online MPC, pushing the boundaries of what is achievable with learned latent dynamics.

While the current study is limited to dense-reward continuous-control settings and incurs increased computational costs due to long-horizon rollouts, the proposed methods lay foundational groundwork. Future research could focus on computational efficiency, extending to sparse-reward manipulation tasks, and developing a more rigorous theoretical understanding of mixture-based planning under model uncertainty. The public availability of the code supports further research and practical application of these advancements.
