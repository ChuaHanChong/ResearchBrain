# ELVIS: Ensemble-Calibrated Latent Imagination for Long-Horizon Visual MPC

> [!summary] Summary
> ELVIS, a latent model predictive controller from KU Leuven and Flanders Make, enables reliable long-horizon planning in visual control tasks, particularly under partial observability and occlusions. It integrates a recurrent state-space model with ensemble-calibrated latent imagination, outperforming existing baselines in DeepMind Control Suite and achieving robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer on a challenging sand-spraying task.

## Problem

- Reliable long-horizon planning in visual Model Predictive Control (MPC) is challenging due to compounding model errors and computational expense.
- Existing visual MPC methods often rely on short receding horizons (e.g., 3-5 steps) and are brittle under partial observability or occlusions, leading to suboptimal policies when future trajectories are multimodal.
- A consistent framework is needed where model-based reinforcement learning objectives and online planner optimization are aligned, especially concerning the treatment of uncertainty.

## Method

- **ELVIS** integrates a ==Recurrent State-Space Model== (**RSSM**) for maintaining a latent belief state under partial observability with an extended Model Predictive Path Integral (**MPPI**) control.
- It introduces ==GMM-MPPI==, a Gaussian Mixture Model-based **MPPI** controller, to manage branching imagined futures and multimodal action-value distributions over long horizons, preventing mode collapse.
- An ensemble-UCB-gated, time-varying ==lambda-return== is employed to adaptively control the influence of uncertain imagined futures, enabling uncertainty-aware soft truncation in both learning and planning.

## Results

- ELVIS achieved state-of-the-art performance across 14 DeepMind Control Suite visual tasks, consistently improving both sample efficiency and final return compared to TD-MPC2 and DreamerV3.
- Demonstrated robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer on a sand-spraying task, yielding superior surface quality (Rrms: 2.2 ± 0.4 mm) and robustness to dust and sensory noise under severe occlusions, significantly outperforming baselines.
- Ablation studies confirmed that increased planning horizon, uncertainty-aware truncation, and multimodal action proposals are critical components contributing to ELVIS's performance gains.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Maintaining multiple action sequence hypotheses via GMM-MPPI is crucial for effective long-horizon planning, especially when optimal future trajectories are inherently multimodal.
> - Dynamically adjusting the effective planning horizon based on epistemic uncertainty, using UCB-gated lambda-returns, significantly improves robustness against compounding model errors under partial observability.
> - Aligning the uncertainty treatment in both actor-critic learning and online MPC planning through a shared uncertainty-aware return enhances overall system coherence and performance.
