# Enhancing Train-Free Infinite-Frame Generation for Consistent Long Videos

> [!summary] Summary
> MIGA is a method developed by a team at Alibaba Group and the University of Chinese Academy of Sciences to enhance train-free infinite-frame video generation. It tackles the training-inference gap and improves long-term consistency through a two-stage training-inference alignment and dual consistency enhancement, yielding state-of-the-art results on VBench and NarrLV benchmarks and raising the overall VBench score by 2.03% (TTA) and 1.73% (DCE) over the FIFO-Diffusion baseline.

## Problem

- Existing train-free infinite-frame video generation methods, like FIFO-Diffusion, suffer from a "training-inference gap" where foundation models trained on uniform noise are applied to inputs with varying noise levels during autoregressive inference.
- This mismatch leads to issues such as content drift and visual artifacts in generated long videos, as foundation models cannot fully realize their generative potential under these conditions.
- Current autoregressive approaches lack explicit mechanisms for modeling long-range temporal dependencies, resulting in suboptimal consistency for subjects and backgrounds across very extended video sequences.

## Method

- Introduce Two-Stage Training-Inference Alignment (TTA) to bridge the training-inference gap by decomposing the generation process into two stages that progressively reduce the noise span of latents.
- Implement Stage 1 as "Zigzag Iterative Denoising" to create a smoother noise span across input latents, followed by Stage 2, "Denoising at a Unified Noise Level," where final denoising occurs under conditions matching the model's training.
- Integrate Dual Consistency Enhancement (DCE) using Self-Reflection for efficient anomaly detection and correction in high-noise latents, and Long-Range Frame Guidance to enable feature interaction between distant frames for improved temporal coherence.

## Results

- MIGA established state-of-the-art performance for train-free long video generation, achieving higher scores across all evaluated metrics on VBench and NarrLV benchmarks compared to existing baselines like FIFO-Diffusion.
- Quantitative results include MIGA improving Subject Consistency for Wan2.1-based models from 92.67 to 96.46, and VBench ablation studies showing TTA alone increased the overall score by **2.03%** and DCE by **1.73%** over the baseline.
- A human evaluation confirmed MIGA's superiority in consistency, with **62.23%** preference for subject consistency over FIFO-Diffusion, and the method achieved quality levels comparable to several training-based long video generation models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The discrepancy between uniform noise training and varying noise inference conditions (the "training-inference gap") is a primary cause of quality degradation in train-free autoregressive long video generation.
> - Decomposing the denoising process into stages that gradually unify noise levels for the foundation model's input significantly improves video quality and reduces artifacts.
> - Maintaining long-term temporal consistency benefits from a dual approach: efficient anomaly detection and correction using latent space similarity (Self-Reflection) and incorporating information from distant, clean frames during local denoising (Long-Range Frame Guidance).

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of video generation has made progress in synthesizing short video clips using foundation models. However, practical applications in areas like film production, game development, and world simulation often require long, coherent videos. Building such models from scratch is computationally and data-intensive. Consequently, a research direction has emerged that focuses on extending the generation length of existing foundation video models in a training-free manner.

Early efforts in train-free long video generation, such as FreeNoise, FreeLong, and FreePCA, typically employ strategies like reorganizing initial noise, leveraging frequency distributions, or integrating global and local information. While these methods demonstrate promising results, their memory requirements scale with the number of generated frames, limiting the achievable video length.

A subsequent development in this landscape introduced infinite-frame generation methods, notably FIFO-Diffusion. These approaches utilize frame-level autoregressive frameworks, maintaining a noise queue where noise levels increase sequentially along the frame dimension and employing a first-in-first-out denoising process. This design allows for infinite video generation with constant memory consumption, addressing a key limitation of earlier train-free methods. Other related autoregressive diffusion models include Diffusion-Forcing and AR-Diffusion.

This paper positions itself within the context of these train-free, frame-level autoregressive models. It identifies two primary limitations of existing methods like FIFO-Diffusion:
1. A significant "training-inference gap": Foundation models are typically trained on inputs with uniform noise levels, but during autoregressive inference, they process latents with multiple, varying noise levels. This mismatch can lead to issues such as content drift and visual artifacts.
2. Insufficient attention to long-term consistency: Existing autoregressive methods often lack explicit mechanisms to model dependencies across distant frames, leading to suboptimal temporal consistency in long videos.

MIGA aims to address these identified limitations, thereby enhancing the effective utilization of foundation models for consistent long video generation within the train-free, infinite-frame paradigm.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are to improve the quality and consistency of long video generation using train-free, infinite-frame autoregressive frameworks. The motivation stems from specific limitations observed in current methods, particularly those building upon the frame-level autoregressive paradigm like FIFO-Diffusion.

The detailed motivations are:

- **Mitigating the Training-Inference Gap**: Foundation video generation models are typically trained on input latents that possess a single, uniform noise level across all frames. However, in train-free frame-level autoregressive inference, the model is required to process a sequence of latents where noise levels progressively increase or vary across frames within a single input window. This discrepancy between the training conditions (unified noise) and inference conditions (varying noise) is identified as a "training-inference gap." This gap can hinder the foundation model from realizing its full generative potential, potentially leading to visual artifacts and a lack of coherence (content drift) in the generated long videos. A key objective is to reduce this mismatch.
- **Enhancing Long-Term Consistency**: A central challenge in long video generation is maintaining visual and semantic consistency across many frames. While autoregressive frameworks enable extended video lengths, existing methods often provide insufficient explicit mechanisms for modeling long-range temporal dependencies. For instance, FIFO-Diffusion primarily facilitates feature interaction between neighboring chunks through lookahead denoising but lacks broader mechanisms to enforce consistency over extended durations. The objective is to develop new mechanisms that explicitly promote and maintain consistency, particularly for subject and background elements, over very long sequences.

To achieve these objectives, the authors propose MIGA, which introduces two main mechanisms designed to directly address these issues within the existing train-free autoregressive generation framework.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MIGA proposes a novel approach that enhances train-free infinite-frame video generation by addressing the training-inference gap and improving long-term consistency. The methodology builds upon existing frame-level autoregressive generation frameworks.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries: Train-Free Frame-Level Autoregressive Generation

The foundation of MIGA is the frame-level autoregressive generation, exemplified by FIFO-Diffusion. This process involves:
- **Latent Representation**: Video data is converted into compact latents ($z_0 \in \mathbb{R}^{f \times l \times d}$) using a Variational Autoencoder (VAE), where $f$ is frame count, $l$ is tokens per frame, and $d$ is token dimension. Each frame's latent ($z_i^0 \in \mathbb{R}^{l \times d}$) is a basic unit.
- **Denoising Process**: A noise prediction network $\epsilon_\theta(\cdot)$ progressively refines Gaussian noise ($z_T \sim N(0, I)$) over $T$ timesteps to recover clean latents ($z_0$). A sampler $\phi(\cdot)$ (e.g., DDPM) performs each denoising step.
- **Autoregressive Queue**: To generate videos longer than a foundation model's default capacity ($f_0$ frames), a queue $Q = \{z_1^{\tau_1}; \dots; z_T^{\tau_T}\}$ of $T$ latents is maintained, with progressively increasing noise levels (from $\tau_1$ to $\tau_T$).
- **Iterative Generation**: In each iteration, one inference step is applied to all latents in $Q$, reducing their noise levels. The first latent ($z_1^{\tau_0}$), now fully denoised, is dequeued and saved. A new Gaussian latent ($z_T^{\tau_T}$) is then enqueued at the end, continuing the process.
- **Sliding Window Inference**: Since the queue length $T$ often exceeds $f_0$, the sampler $\Phi(\cdot)$ processes $Q$ in multiple executions using a sliding window of size $f_0$ and a stride of $\lfloor f_0/2 \rfloor$.

#### 3.2. Two-Stage Training-Inference Alignment (TTA)

The TTA mechanism is designed to mitigate the training-inference gap by reducing the noise span of latents fed to the model during inference. It decomposes the generation process into two stages:

- **Stage 1: Zigzag Iterative Denoising**: This stage modifies the noise queue structure. Instead of changing the noise level with every single latent frame, the noise level is altered every $L_{zig}$ latents. This "zigzag" structure ($Q_{s1} = \{z_1^{\tau_e}, \dots, z_{L_{zig}}^{\tau_e}, z_{L_{zig}+1}^{\tau_{e+1}}, \dots\}$) creates a smoother noise span across input latents, bringing the inference condition closer to the uniform noise condition seen during training. In each iteration, $L_{zig}$ partially denoised latents are dequeued, and $L_{zig}$ new Gaussian latents are enqueued. This stage performs partial denoising, bringing latents to a state where their noise levels are still above $\tau_0$. The initialization of the queue for this stage involves progressively guiding the noise latents using initial clean latents, rather than simple duplication.
- **Stage 2: Denoising at a Unified Noise Level**: After $n$ iterations in Stage 1, $nL_{zig}$ latents are obtained, all at the same noise level $\tau_{e-1}$. These latents form a new queue $Q_{s2} = \{z_1^{\tau_{e-1}}, \dots, z_{nL_{zig}}^{\tau_{e-1}}\}$. In this stage, all latent frames share the identical noise level, which perfectly aligns with the model's training condition. A sliding-window denoising process is applied over $Q_{s2}$ to fully denoise these frames ($e-1$ steps). This stage ensures that the final denoising steps occur under conditions closely matching the model's training.

#### 3.3. Dual Consistency Enhancement (DCE)

The DCE mechanism is introduced to specifically promote long-term temporal consistency, leveraging the characteristics of the maintained latent queue. It comprises two complementary approaches:

- **Self-Reflection**: This approach adapts Test-Time Scaling (TTS) for long videos. It aims to efficiently detect and correct consistency anomalies in newly generated high-noise latents (at the tail of the queue).
 - **Consistency Evaluation**: Instead of relying on external models requiring clean pixel inputs and additional denoising, MIGA proposes an efficient internal consistency metric. It leverages the interpretability of the VAE latent space, using cosine similarity between latents as a measure of frame difference. An important insight is that the cosine similarity between early high-noise latents strongly correlates with that of final clean latents, allowing for early detection of consistency disruptions without full denoising. The consistency score ($C_{score}$) is computed based on consecutive evaluation latents ($q_{eval}$) and preceding reference latents ($q_{ref}$).
 - **Anomaly Detection and Expanded Search**: A judgment index ($f_{judg}$) is defined at the tail of the queue. At each iteration, latents around $f_{judg}$ are evaluated. If the decrease in $C_{score}$ between adjacent chunks exceeds a threshold ($\delta_{adju}$), an anomaly is detected, and an expanded search for correction is triggered.
 - **Progressively Guided Search**: Upon detection, a small number of latents after $f_{judg}$ are considered for correction. Multiple candidate latent sequences ($n_{samp}$) are generated by sampling Gaussian noise and guiding them using preceding, evaluated clean latents ($q_{guid}$). The candidate with the highest consistency score is selected to replace the problematic sequence if it improves the initial score. This mechanism focuses computational resources where anomalies are likely, rather than at every step.

- **Long-Range Frame Guidance**: This method facilitates feature interactions between distant frames to enhance consistency during the sliding-window inference.
 - **Sparse Latent Sampling**: When the foundation model processes a local window of $f_0$ nearby latents, MIGA explicitly samples $m_{guid}$ latents sparsely from earlier, relatively clean positions in the queue.
 - **Guided Denoising**: These $m_{guid}$ sparsely sampled (and clean) latents are concatenated with the local dense latents and fed as input to the foundation model for denoising. This allows the model to leverage information from distant, already refined frames to guide the denoising of the current local window, thereby promoting consistency across broader temporal ranges.

#### 3.4. Implementation and Generalizability

MIGA is applied to popular foundation models like VideoCrafter2 (generating animation-style videos by default) and the latest Wan2.1-1.3B (generating realistic-style videos). The core modifications involve adjusting the noise prediction model and sampler to handle varying noise levels per frame. The framework also supports multi-text conditional generation by providing different text prompts to latents at various temporal positions. The authors discuss that the frame-level autoregressive framework, and thus MIGA, is adaptable to models where noise and text conditions interact with latents via cross-attention. However, it may face challenges with architectures like MMDiT, which concatenate text and video features for joint interaction with noise conditions, as distinguishing noise conditions at the frame level for text features becomes problematic.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of MIGA was conducted on two benchmarks, VBench and NarrLV, using two foundation models: VideoCrafter2 and Wan2.1-1.3B.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Results

- **VBench Evaluation**: MIGA demonstrated performance improvements across all evaluated metrics compared to existing train-free baselines (FreePCA, FreeLong, FIFO-Diffusion, ScalingNoise).
 - **VideoCrafter2-Based Models**: For 128-frame videos, MIGA achieved a Subject Consistency (S.C.) of 97.66 and a Background Consistency (B.C.) of 96.99, outperforming the next best, FreeLong (S.C. 95.72, B.C. 96.42). MIGA also showed higher Motion Smoothness (M.S.), Temporal Flicker (T.F.), and an Overall Score (O.S.) of 97.82.
 - **Wan2.1-Based Models**: For 161-frame videos, MIGA significantly improved over FIFO-Diffusion, with S.C. increasing from 92.67 to 96.46, B.C. from 93.37 to 95.50, and O.S. from 95.29 to 97.24. The paper notes that Wan2.1-based MIGA's consistency scores were slightly lower than VideoCrafter2-based MIGA, attributed to VideoCrafter2's tendency to generate animation-style videos, which are generally easier to maintain consistency in than the realistic-style videos from Wan2.1.
- **NarrLV Evaluation**: On NarrLV, a benchmark for narrative expressiveness, MIGA consistently improved performance across different Temporal Narrative Atom (TNA) counts (2, 3, and 4) and metrics (scene attributes ($s_{att}$), target attributes ($t_{att}$), and target actions ($t_{act}$)).
 - **VideoCrafter2-Based MIGA**: It achieved higher scores (e.g., $s_{att}$ 69.78 for TNA=2) compared to FIFO-Diffusion ($s_{att}$ 67.02), indicating better narrative expressiveness.
 - **Wan2.1-Based MIGA**: Demonstrated further substantial gains, with $s_{att}$ reaching 79.32 for TNA=2, surpassing FIFO-Diffusion ($s_{att}$ 67.77). This suggests Wan2.1's capability to generate richer narrative content, further enhanced by MIGA.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Results

Visualizations of 1000+ frame videos generated by Wan2.1-based MIGA (Figure 1 and Appendix B.6) demonstrate that the generated content closely follows text prompts and maintains strong subject and background consistency over extended durations. Comparison between VideoCrafter2-based and Wan2.1-based MIGA shows that while VideoCrafter2 produces animation-like videos with high consistency, Wan2.1 generates more detailed, realistic-style videos.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies confirmed the effectiveness of MIGA's core mechanisms:
- **Core Mechanisms (TTA and DCE)**: Individually, the Two-Stage Training-Inference Alignment (TTA) and Dual Consistency Enhancement (DCE) mechanisms improved the overall VBench score by 2.03% and 1.73% respectively, compared to the FIFO-Diffusion baseline. Their combined application yielded further performance gains.
- **TTA Components**: Sequentially introducing Stage 1 (zigzag iterative denoising) and Stage 2 (unified noise level denoising) of TTA led to progressive performance improvements. Stage 1 reduced drastic anomalies, while Stage 2 effectively suppressed visual noise. The optimal zigzag width ($L_{zig}=4$) was identified, showing that reducing the noise span enhances performance up to a certain point. Omitting Stage 1 entirely and performing only Stage 2 (i.e., synchronous denoising of independent noise latents) resulted in a sharp performance drop, indicating the importance of the autoregressive property for latent information transfer.
- **DCE Components**:
 - **Self-Reflection**: The adjustment threshold ($\delta_{adju}$) for anomaly detection was studied. A smaller threshold increased the frequency of expanded searches, which correlated with higher overall scores, demonstrating the effectiveness of the test-time scaling capability. A default $\delta_{adju}=0.01$ was selected to balance performance and computational cost.
 - **Long-Range Frame Guidance**: Varying the number of guidance frames ($m_{guid}$) showed optimal performance at $m_{guid}=6$.

#### 4.4. Computational Efficiency

- **TTA**: Introducing TTA did not alter the computational efficiency of FIFO-Diffusion, as the number of denoising steps per latent remained constant. Memory overhead increased only moderately with video length due to intermediate variable storage, confirming its support for infinite frame generation with constant per-window memory.
- **DCE**: Long-range frame guidance introduced negligible additional noise prediction steps. Self-reflection, as a Test-Time Scaling approach, increased computational burden through expanded sampling, proportional to the number of anomalies detected and samples generated. However, this is an optional mechanism where the trade-off between quality and inference time can be controlled. Quantitative analysis indicated MIGA (with DCE) delivered substantial performance gains for a moderate increase in average generation time per frame compared to the baseline.

#### 4.5. Human Evaluation

A user study comparing MIGA with FIFO-Diffusion on 48 video pairs indicated that MIGA consistently outperformed FIFO-Diffusion across subject consistency (62.23% vs 15.89%), background consistency (61.72% vs 17.45%), motion smoothness (66.14% vs 14.06%), and temporal flicker (66.14% vs 16.15%).

#### 4.6. Comparison with Training-Based Methods

Although MIGA is a train-free method, it achieved comparable performance across all VBench metrics to several recent training-based autoregressive long video generation models (e.g., CausVid, Self-Forcing, LongLive, Infinity-RoPE, Reward Forcing). This suggests that MIGA can achieve competitive quality without requiring extensive training on long video datasets.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of video generation by enhancing the capabilities of train-free, infinite-frame long video generation. The proposed MIGA method addresses identified limitations in existing autoregressive frameworks, specifically the training-inference gap and insufficient long-term consistency modeling.

The significance of this work lies in several aspects:
- **Improved Efficiency and Accessibility**: By enabling foundation models to generate significantly longer and more consistent videos without requiring extensive re-training or fine-tuning, MIGA offers a resource-efficient solution. This increases the accessibility of high-quality long video generation for researchers and practitioners who may lack the computational resources for large-scale training.
- **Enhanced Video Quality and Coherence**: The two-stage training-inference alignment mechanism directly tackles the discrepancy between training and inference conditions, leading to more stable and artifact-free video content. The dual consistency enhancement mechanism, through self-reflection and long-range frame guidance, actively promotes and corrects temporal consistency, which is crucial for the realism and utility of long videos.
- **State-of-the-Art Performance in Train-Free Settings**: MIGA demonstrates state-of-the-art performance on established benchmarks (VBench and NarrLV) within the train-free paradigm. Its ability to generate coherent long narratives and maintain consistency over thousands of frames, even when compared favorably against some training-based methods, highlights its effectiveness.
- **Foundation for Future Work**: MIGA's mechanisms provide a foundation for further improvements in train-free long video generation. The insights into mitigating the training-inference gap and enhancing consistency are transferable and could inspire future developments in autoregressive generation or other extended video generation techniques.

Potential impacts include:
- **Accelerated Content Creation**: Industries like film production, animation, and game development could leverage MIGA to rapidly prototype, extend, or generate long video sequences, reducing manual effort and production costs.
- **Advanced Simulation and Virtual Worlds**: The generation of infinitely long and consistent videos could be applied in creating more dynamic and immersive virtual environments, detailed simulations, or AI-driven world models.
- **Research on Generative AI**: This work advances the understanding of how to effectively extend the capabilities of large foundation models to complex, sequential tasks without incurring the high costs of retraining, contributing to the broader field of generative AI.

The paper acknowledges limitations, such as the occurrence of "hallucinations" or lack of physical knowledge in very long videos, where subjects might unexpectedly change attributes or exhibit non-physical behaviors. Future work could focus on incorporating additional conditioning signals beyond text to address these challenges and generate more robust and realistic long videos.
