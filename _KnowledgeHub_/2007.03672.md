# Long-term Human Motion Prediction with Scene Context

> [!summary] Summary
> Long-term 3D human motion prediction conditioned on scene context. From a single scene image plus 2D pose history, a three-stage pipeline runs GoalNet (cVAE sampling plausible 2D destinations), PathNet (3D torso path as 2D heatmaps plus depth), and PoseNet (transformer refining per-step 3D poses along the path). A synthetic GTA-IM dataset of one million RGBD frames supports training; average error drops to 165 mm versus 193 mm for the best baseline.

## Problem

- Prior motion predictors ignore scene context, so long-horizon rollouts drift into implausible states such as walking through tables or sofas.
- Global trajectory prediction and local pose prediction were treated as separate lines of work, leaving no single framework covering both over long horizons.
- Real human-scene datasets carry noisy 3D pose labels, few frames, few scenes, and depth-sensor range limits of roughly 4 meters.

## Method

- **GoalNet**, a ==conditional variational auto-encoder== over scene image and pose heatmaps, samples multiple 2D motion destinations via ==soft-argmax==, trained with an L1 destination loss plus KL divergence.
- **PathNet**, an ==hourglass backbone== with heatmap and depth branches, plans the 3D torso path as 2D path heatmaps plus center depth, supervised by 2D, 3D, and smoothness losses.
- **PoseNet**, a ==transformer==, refines a coarse 3D sequence built by back-projecting 2D pose history and replicating the present pose onto each predicted path location; **GTA-IM** supplies one million synthetic RGBD frames with 98-joint labels.

## Results

- On GTA-IM the deterministic model averages **173 mm** error versus **193 mm** for the strongest baseline combination, and ten sampled hypotheses cut this to **165 mm**.
- On PROX the method reaches **270 mm** mean MPJPE versus **282 mm** for the prior state of the art, improving to **249 mm** with ten samples.
- Ablations show heatmap-plus-depth path encoding beats direct xyz regression (**173** vs **191 mm**); removing the image costs **23 mm** and masking scene pixels costs **16 mm**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Full-scene imagery, not just cropped human pixels, supplies the constraints that keep long-horizon predictions physically plausible; the gain grows with horizon length.
> - Predicting a spatial goal first, then planning toward it, turns a compounding step-by-step rollout into a tractable goal-conditioned problem.
> - Representing 3D location as image-space heatmaps plus depth exploits appearance correlation better than regressing raw coordinates, and RGB alone matches RGBD input.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of human motion prediction is an active area of research, with significant progress driven by deep learning. Existing approaches generally fall into categories of global trajectory prediction and local pose prediction. However, a common limitation across many current methods is the insufficient integration of scene context, particularly for long-term predictions.

Traditional human pose sequence prediction frameworks often focus on local human appearance dynamics without considering the surrounding environment. This typically restricts their predictions to short durations (around 1 second) and local movements, such as a person walking in place without significant global displacement.

In global trajectory prediction, methods have modeled social and scene interactions using physical forces, continuum dynamics, Hidden Markov Models, game theory, or more recently, data-driven neural network approaches. While some of these implicitly account for scene influence, many rely on specific input formats like bird-eye view images or known 3D scene information, limiting their applicability to diverse camera viewpoints.

Local pose prediction methods typically leverage local image context around the human figure to guide future pose generation. However, they generally do not utilize the broader background image or global scene context. Similarly, approaches that predict 3D pose solely from 3D pose history ignore visual scene information, which can lead to predictions that are physically inconsistent with the environment, such as a person walking through a wall. While some methods like iMapper consider joint reasoning of scene and human motion, they often rely on computationally intensive offline optimization. Furthermore, current learning-based methods typically do not holistically model scene context and human pose for extended time instances.

Another aspect of the broader landscape concerns data availability. Training high-capacity neural models for human-scene interaction tasks requires large-scale and diverse datasets. Existing real-world datasets for 3D human motion often feature contrived environments, suffer from relatively noisy 3D annotations, or have limited motion ranges due to sensor constraints. While synthetic datasets generated from gaming engines exist, they tend to focus on tasks like human pose estimation or segmentation in outdoor settings, lacking detailed human-scene interaction within indoor environments.

This work positions itself to bridge these gaps by developing a framework that integrates scene context into long-term 3D human motion prediction from 2D inputs. It combines aspects of global trajectory and local pose prediction within a unified, scene-aware system, and addresses data limitations by contributing a new synthetic dataset designed for human-scene interaction.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to advance the field of human motion prediction by enabling accurate long-term 3D human motion prediction that is influenced by and consistent with the scene context, given only a single scene image and 2D pose histories.

The motivation for this work stems from several key observations and requirements:
1. **Goal-Directed Human Movement:** Human actions are inherently goal-directed and are significantly influenced by the spatial layout of objects in the environment. For example, a person navigating a room will naturally avoid obstacles and move towards a specific destination, potentially adapting their path to sit on a chair. Existing motion prediction models that neglect scene context struggle to capture these fundamental aspects of human behavior, leading to implausible or limited predictions.
2. **Limitations of Existing Methods in Long-Term Prediction:** Current approaches, by largely ignoring scene context, are confined to short-term predictions (approximately one second) and localized movements. To achieve predictions that cover larger spatio-temporal neighborhoods, incorporating scene context is deemed essential.
3. **Need for Multi-Modality:** Human future actions are often uncertain and multi-modal; a person might have several plausible goals or paths depending on their intentions. The framework aims to reflect this uncertainty by generating multiple hypotheses for future motion.
4. **Learning Scene Constraints:** Instead of relying on classical 3D path planning methods from robotics, which can be complex and model-dependent, the authors aim to implicitly learn scene constraints from extensive human-scene interaction data using neural networks. This approach uses 2D images to represent the scene, making the method applicable to readily available visual data.
5. **Challenges with Existing Datasets:** The development and evaluation of learning systems for long-term, scene-aware human motion prediction are hampered by limitations in current datasets. Real datasets often contain noisy 3D annotations, lack diversity in human-scene interactions, or feature restricted motion ranges. Synthetic datasets, while offering clean annotations, typically do not focus on diverse indoor human-scene interactions crucial for this task. This necessitates the creation of a new, high-quality dataset.

In summary, the key objectives are:
- To formally define and tackle the task of long-term 3D human motion prediction using 3D poses and locations, explicitly integrating scene context.
- To design a novel three-stage computational framework that is goal-oriented and leverages scene context for robust motion prediction.
- To develop and contribute a comprehensive synthetic dataset (GTA-IM) featuring diverse human-scene interactions and precise annotations to support the training and rigorous evaluation of such models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology is a three-stage learning framework designed to factorize the complex problem of long-term human motion prediction with scene context. Given a single scene image and N-step 2D human pose histories, the system predicts T-step 3D human poses and their locations.

**Overall Pipeline:**
The framework processes information sequentially through three modules: GoalNet, PathNet, and PoseNet (Figure 3 in the paper).
1. **GoalNet:** Predicts multiple possible 2D human motion destinations (goals) within the image space.
2. **PathNet:** Plans 3D human paths (sequence of human center locations) towards each predicted goal, incorporating scene context.
3. **PoseNet:** Generates detailed 3D human pose sequences at each timestep, following the planned 3D paths.

The modular design allows for both deterministic (single-mode) and stochastic (multi-modal) predictions, as GoalNet can sample multiple destination hypotheses.

**Detailed Breakdown of Modules:**

#### 3.1. GoalNet: Predicting 2D Path Destination

- **Purpose:** To predict potential future destinations of human motion, modeled as 2D points in the image space at the end of the prediction horizon (time N+T). This stage addresses the multi-modal nature and uncertainty of future human behavior.
- **Input:** The scene image `I` and `N` previous 2D human pose heatmaps `H_1:J_1:N` (where `J` is the number of keypoints).
- **Architecture:** A conditional Variational Auto-Encoder (CVAE).
 - An encoder `F_enc` maps the input `(H_1:J_1:N, I)` to the parameters `(μ, σ)` of a latent Gaussian distribution `z`.
 - A latent vector `z` is sampled from this distribution `Q(z|H_1:J_1:N, I)`.
 - A decoder `F_dec` takes the sampled `z` and the scene image `I` to predict a heatmap `ˆH_r_N+T` for the human center destination.
- **Output:** The 2D human motion destination `ˆX_r_N+T` is extracted from `ˆH_r_N+T` using a differentiable soft-argmax operation.
- **Training:** Optimized with two objectives:
 - `L_dest2D`: An L1 loss comparing the predicted 2D destination `ˆX_r_N+T` with the ground-truth `X_r_N+T`.
 - `L_KL`: A KL-divergence loss regularizing the latent distribution `Q` towards a standard normal distribution `N(0,1)`.
- **Inference:** During testing, `z` vectors are sampled from `N(0,1)` to generate multiple distinct 2D destination hypotheses.

#### 3.2. PathNet: Planning 3D Path towards Destination

- **Purpose:** To plan a 3D path (sequence of human center locations in 3D camera coordinates) towards a given 2D destination, while integrating scene context from the image.
- **Input:** The scene image `I`, 2D pose history `H_1:J_1:N`, and the predicted 2D destination heatmap `ˆH_r_N+T` from GoalNet.
- **Representation:** The 3D path is not directly regressed as 3D coordinates. Instead, it is represented as a sequence of 2D path heatmaps `ˆH_r_N+1:N+T` and corresponding depth values `ˆd_r_1:N+T` for the human center. This representation is stated to facilitate training.
- **Architecture:** An Hourglass54 network serves as the backbone. It has two output branches: one predicts 2D path heatmaps, and the other predicts the human torso depth over time.
- **Output:** The 2D path `ˆX_r_N+1:N+T` is extracted from `ˆH_r_N+1:N+T` via soft-argmax. This 2D path, combined with the predicted depth `ˆd_r_1:N+T` and camera intrinsics `K`, is back-projected to obtain the 3D path `ˆY_r_1:N+T`.
- **Training:** Two supervision losses are used:
 - `L_path2D`: An L1 loss on the predicted 2D path `ˆX_r_N+1:N+T` against the ground-truth `X_r_N+1:N+T`.
 - `L_path3D`: An L1 loss on the predicted 3D path `ˆY_r_1:N+T` against the ground-truth `Y_r_1:N+T`, augmented with a term penalizing large positional changes between consecutive frames to encourage smoothness.
- **Deterministic Mode:** For deterministic predictions, GoalNet is bypassed, and PathNet directly generates a single 3D path. During training, ground-truth destinations are used for PathNet; during inference, GoalNet's predictions are used.

#### 3.3. PoseNet: Generating 3D Pose following Path

- **Purpose:** To predict detailed 3D human pose sequences (all keypoints) at each timestep, refining an initial estimation based on the predicted 3D path.
- **Input:** The predicted 3D path `ˆY_r_1:N+T` from PathNet and the 2D pose history `X_1:N`.
- **Initial 3D Pose Estimation (`¯Y_1:N+T`):**
 - The 2D pose history `X_1:N` is lifted to noisy 3D poses `¯Y_1:N` using the predicted human torso depth `ˆd_r_1:N` and camera intrinsics `K`.
 - The current 3D pose `¯Y_N` is replicated and translated according to the predicted future 3D path locations to form an initial estimation for future poses `¯Y_N+1:N+T`.
 - These are concatenated to form `¯Y_1:N+T`.
- **Architecture:** A Transformer network is adapted for this task, with specific modifications (e.g., removal of certain embedding layers and softmax).
- **Output:** Refined 3D human pose sequences `ˆY_N+1:N+T`.
- **Training:** Optimized by `L_pose3D`, an L1 loss comparing the predicted 3D pose `ˆY_N+1:N+T` against the ground-truth `Y_N+1:N+T`.
- **Training vs. Inference:** Ground-truth 3D paths are used for training PoseNet, while PathNet's predictions are used during inference.

#### 3.4. GTA Indoor Motion (GTA-IM) Dataset

- **Contribution:** A new large-scale synthetic dataset designed to overcome the limitations of existing datasets for scene-aware human motion prediction.
- **Generation:** An automated data pipeline was developed using the Grand Theft Auto (GTA) gaming engine, allowing control over actors, scenes, cameras, lighting, and motions.
- **Diversity:** Randomizes goal destinations, tasks (e.g., climbing stairs, sitting, opening doors), walking styles (22 types), movement speeds, lighting conditions, and camera viewpoints (over a sphere around the actor).
- **Annotations:** Provides 1 million RGBD frames at 1920x1080 resolution, with ground-truth 3D human pose (98 joints), human segmentation, and camera pose.
- **Scale:** Features 50 human characters across 10 distinct large indoor scenes (multi-floor environments like living rooms, bedrooms, kitchens, balconies).
- **Role:** Used for training and evaluation, and for pre-training models to stabilize training and enhance performance on real datasets.

#### 3.5. Implementation Details

- All modules are implemented in PyTorch and trained using the ADAM optimizer.
- Input image and heatmap sizes are 256x448; output heatmap resolution is 64x112.
- Depth values are capped and normalized.
- The three modules are trained separately, which was found to yield better performance than joint training.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates the proposed framework through extensive quantitative and qualitative analyses on both a synthetic dataset (GTA-IM) and a real-world dataset (PROX), comparing it against several strong baselines.

#### 4.1. Datasets and Metrics

- **GTA-IM:** The newly introduced synthetic dataset, split into 8 scenes for training and 2 for evaluation, using 21 selected human joints.
- **PROX:** A real-world dataset comprising 12 3D scenes and RGB sequences of 20 subjects, split into 52 training sequences and 8 testing sequences, using 18 joints from the SMPL-X model.
- **Metric:** Mean Per Joint Position Error (MPJPE) in millimeters, reported for 3D path and 3D pose predictions at various future time steps (0.5, 1, 1.5, 2 seconds) and as a mean over all time steps ("All").

#### 4.2. Baselines

Three baselines were established for comparison, as no prior work directly addresses 3D human motion prediction with global movement from 2D input while incorporating scene context:
1. **TR:** A Transformer network directly performing 3D prediction from 2D inputs as a single-stage sequence-to-sequence task.
2. **TR + VP:** Predicts future 2D human pose using Transformer, then lifts the predicted 2D pose to 3D using VideoPose3D.
3. **VP + LTD:** Lifts 2D pose history to 3D using VideoPose3D, then predicts future 3D human pose using Learning Trajectory Dependencies (LTD). This baseline is the most competitive.

All models were trained for 2-second-long predictions based on a 1-second-long history.

#### 4.3. Quantitative Results

**GTA-IM Dataset (Table 1):**
- **Overall Performance:** The proposed deterministic model achieved an average error of 173 mm, outperforming the best baseline (VP + LTD) which had an average error of 193 mm.
- **Stochastic vs. Deterministic:**
 - The stochastic model, when evaluating the best of 4 samples, matched the deterministic model's performance (173 mm).
 - With 10 samples, the error further decreased to 165 mm, demonstrating the advantage of stochastic prediction in handling uncertainty.
- **Ablation Studies:**
 - **3D Path Representation:** Directly regressing 3D coordinates ("Ours w/ xyz. output") resulted in an 18 mm higher error (191 mm vs. 173 mm), confirming the benefit of representing the 3D path as 2D heatmaps and depth.
 - **Scene Context Input:**
 - Removing image input ("Ours w/o image") increased the average error by 23 mm (196 mm), highlighting the importance of scene context.
 - Using only masked images ("Ours w/ masked image") led to a 16 mm higher error (189 mm), indicating that the full scene image is more informative than cropped human images for long-term prediction.
 - **RGBD Input:** Using both RGB and depth images ("Ours w/ RGBD input") yielded similar performance (172 mm) to RGB-only input, suggesting the model implicitly learns depth information from 2D.
 - **Ground-truth Destination:** When provided with ground-truth 2D destinations ("Ours w/ GT destination"), the average error significantly dropped to 137 mm, implying that the uncertainty in predicting future destination is a major source of challenge.

**PROX Dataset (Table 2):**
- **Overall Performance:** The proposed deterministic method achieved an average MPJPE of 270 mm, outperforming the best baseline (VP + LTD) at 282 mm.
- **Stochastic vs. Deterministic:** The stochastic model again showed advantages. With 3 samples, it outperformed the deterministic model (264 mm vs. 270 mm). With 10 samples, the error further reduced to 249 mm. The improvements from stochastic predictions were more pronounced on the PROX dataset, attributed to higher uncertainty in real-world scenarios.
- **Pre-training:** Pre-training on the GTA-IM dataset improved performance on PROX (270 mm vs. 280 mm without pre-training), indicating that the synthetic dataset aids in preventing overfitting, especially given PROX's less diverse camera poses and background appearance.

**Longer-term Predictions (up to 3 seconds, Figure 6, 10):**
- For longer prediction horizons (e.g., 3 seconds), the stochastic models consistently outperformed their deterministic counterparts. The error gap between deterministic and stochastic predictions widened significantly at later time steps (e.g., over 100 mm at 3 seconds), underscoring the benefits of multi-modal predictions for extended durations.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Results

- **Comparison with Baselines (Figure 5, 11):** Qualitative visualizations demonstrated that baseline methods, lacking scene context awareness, often produced physically implausible motions such as humans walking through tables or sofas. In contrast, the proposed model implicitly learned scene constraints and generated more plausible 3D human motions.
- **Destination Sampling (Figure 7):** The GoalNet effectively generated diverse and plausible 2D destination samples, reflecting different potential activities (e.g., sitting or standing).
- **Long-term Stochastic Predictions (Figure 9):** The model showed an ability to generate varied and realistic 3D future motions, including turning, walking straight, U-turns, climbing stairs, standing up, or lying on a sofa, demonstrating its capacity for goal-directed and scene-consistent behavior.

#### 4.5. Failure Cases (Figure 8)

Despite generally plausible results, the model exhibited minor failure cases. Small intersections between human feet and the 3D scene mesh (e.g., floor or bed) were observed. This limitation is attributed to the implicit learning of scene constraints from data, without explicit integration of pre-reconstructed 3D meshes or physically-based collision detection often seen in offline optimization methods.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of computer vision and human behavior understanding by addressing the challenging problem of long-term 3D human motion prediction within diverse scene contexts, from limited 2D input.

**Key Contributions to Research:**
- **Problem Formulation:** The work formulates a new task of long-term 3D human motion prediction that explicitly incorporates scene context in terms of 3D poses and locations.
- **Novel Framework:** It introduces a three-stage, modular computational framework that leverages scene context for goal-oriented motion prediction. This framework is capable of generating stochastic (multi-modal) predictions, which is crucial for handling the inherent uncertainty of future human actions.
- **Dataset Contribution:** The creation of the GTA-IM synthetic dataset is a notable contribution. This large-scale dataset, with its diverse human-scene interactions and clean 3D annotations, addresses significant limitations in existing real and synthetic datasets, providing a valuable resource for training and robustly evaluating models in this domain.
- **Performance Advancement:** The proposed method demonstrates consistent quantitative and qualitative improvements over existing methods across both synthetic and real-world datasets, particularly in long-term predictions and generating scene-plausible movements.

**Potential Impact and Applications:**
The ability to predict feasible human motion while considering the surrounding environment has relevance across several real-world applications:
- **Robotics:** Home service robots can benefit from anticipating human movements and intentions, enabling safer and more effective collaboration with people in dynamic indoor environments.
- **Augmented Reality (AR):** AR systems could provide more intelligent and context-aware navigation guidance, such as for visually impaired individuals, by predicting their future movements and adapting instructions accordingly.
- **Autonomous Vehicles:** For autonomous vehicles, predicting pedestrian behavior in complex urban or indoor settings, including their intended paths and potential interactions with the environment, is critical for safety and efficient path planning.
- **Human-Computer Interaction:** More intuitive and responsive interactive systems could be developed by forecasting user movements and adapting interfaces or environments.
- **Virtual Reality and Gaming:** Generating realistic and context-aware character animations could enhance immersion and realism.

**Future Directions:**
The authors identify several avenues for future work, including the development of robust evaluation metrics for the naturalness and feasibility of stochastic human motion predictions, incorporating dynamic objects and multiple interacting people into the scene context, and exploring methods to integrate multi-view or temporal image inputs to reconstruct the 3D scene more accurately for explicit physical constraint enforcement.
