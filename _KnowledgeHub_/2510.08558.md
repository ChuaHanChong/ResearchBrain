# Agent Learning via Early Experience

> [!summary] Summary
> The "early experience" paradigm enables autonomous language agents to learn and continuously improve from their own interactions with the environment, leveraging implicit feedback from observed future states rather than explicit reward signals. This approach consistently improves task effectiveness, enhances out-of-domain generalization, and provides a robust foundation for subsequent reinforcement learning.

## Problem

- Existing language agent training (supervised fine-tuning) is limited by expensive, non-scalable human expert data, leading to brittle agents with poor generalization.
- Applying reinforcement learning to complex, real-world language agent tasks is challenging due to absent/sparse reward signals and inefficient long-horizon rollouts.
- There is a critical need for language agents to learn continuously from their own interactions and consequences in reward-free, unstructured environments.

## Method

- The =="early experience" paradigm== collects interaction data by executing alternative agent actions and observing the resulting future states, forming a reward-free `D_rollout` dataset.
- ==Implicit World Modeling (IWM)== trains the agent to predict future states from current states and actions, thereby implicitly learning environment dynamics directly within the policy.
- ==Self-Reflection (SR)== generates natural language chain-of-thought explanations that compare expert actions to suboptimal alternatives based on their observed outcomes, providing contrastive supervision for refining agent reasoning.

## Results

- Both IWM and SR consistently improve agent success rates across eight diverse language-agent environments and multiple model sizes, with gains ranging from **+2.3%** to **+18.4%** success rate.
- Early experience methods substantially enhance out-of-domain generalization, helping agents recover a significant portion of performance loss in novel scenarios compared to imitation learning.
- Models pre-trained with early experience provide a superior initialization for subsequent reinforcement learning, achieving higher ultimate performance ceilings during the RL phase.
- The paradigm reduces reliance on expert data, demonstrating comparable or superior performance with significantly fewer human demonstrations, and scales effectively across various model sizes (**3B to 70B** parameters).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Agents can achieve substantial self-improvement and develop a deeper understanding of environment dynamics by learning from the observed consequences of their own actions, even without explicit reward signals.
> - Integrating implicit world modeling and grounded self-reflection provides scalable, reward-free supervision that enhances policy robustness and decision-making beyond rote imitation.
> - "Early experience" serves as a practical, effective bridge, enabling experience-driven learning before full reinforcement learning infrastructure is available and providing a superior warm-start for downstream RL.
> - The effectiveness of these methods stems from converting off-expert rollouts into grounded supervision derived from actual environment outcomes, unlike ungrounded reasoning methods.
