# Compliant Non-Prehensile Pushing Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed a control framework enabling impedance-controlled robots to perform compliant non-prehensile pushing while ensuring safety during human interaction. The framework combines an extended Model Predictive Control (MPC) with an energy tank passivity filter, achieving sub-centimeter positional accuracy (mean 1.65 x 10^-2 m) and preventing unsafe force escalation during external disturbances.

## Problem

- Existing non-prehensile pushing strategies are largely designed for stiff, position-controlled robots, limiting their safe deployment in environments shared with humans.
- Impedance-controlled robots, while offering compliance, struggle to maintain manipulation accuracy during pushing tasks and can exhibit uncontrolled energy buildup (leading to unsafe forces) when external interactions impede desired motion.
- Integrating advanced manipulation precision with robust safety guarantees during physical human-robot interaction in compliant pushing scenarios presents a significant control challenge.

## Method

- An extended ==Model Predictive Control== (MPC) framework is developed to enable compliant non-prehensile pushing, optimally modulating the robot's position/velocity set-point to control contact force and desired sliding velocity.
- The planar outputs from the MPC are transformed into 3D Cartesian space for execution by an impedance-controlled manipulator.
- An energy tank passivity filter is integrated to monitor system energy and dynamically adjust the robot's velocity set-point, preventing passivity violations and uncontrolled force increases during external interactions such as human interference.

## Results

- Simulations and real-world experiments confirmed that the energy tank passivity filter prevents unbounded pushing force escalation during external interactions, maintaining task execution safety and limiting positional errors to below **1.5** x 10^-2 m in simulations.
- On an ABB YuMi robot, the system achieved high-precision tracking during human obstruction (positional error < 2 x 10^-3 m), with the passivity filter activating to safely manage interaction forces and resume the task upon release.
- A quantitative analysis on a KUKA LBR iiwa across 216 tests yielded a mean positional error of **1.65** x 10^-2 m and a mean rotational error of **0.10** rad, demonstrating robustness to object mass and friction variations, though performance degraded with higher velocities and curvilinear paths.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A virtual spring model effectively links an impedance-controlled robot's displacement from a set-point to contact force, allowing force control through optimal set-point modulation.
> - The inherent underactuation in compliant pushing can be addressed by deriving a velocity set-point that mediates between controlling the contact force and achieving desired sliding velocity.
> - An energy tank passivity filter can actively detect and prevent unbounded energy accumulation and force escalation in compliant systems during external disturbances, ensuring safe human-robot interaction by dynamically limiting control output.
