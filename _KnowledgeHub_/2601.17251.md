# EMPM: Embodied MPM for Modeling and Simulation of Deformable Objects

> [!summary] Summary
> The EMPM framework models and simulates deformable objects by integrating differentiable Material Point Method physics with 3D Gaussian Splatting for geometry and appearance, enabling physically plausible and data-efficient reconstruction of elastic and elastoplastic materials from visual inputs. It incorporates an online adaptive identification scheme to refine material parameters based on real-time sensory feedback.

## Problem

- Existing deformable object models like spring-mass systems oversimplify complex dynamics of continuum materials, leading to inaccuracies for large deformations, fracture, or elastoplastic behaviors.
- Learning-based physics models require large datasets and exhibit poor generalization to novel objects or unseen manipulation scenarios.
- Integrating accurate physics-based dynamics, geometry reconstruction, and photorealistic appearance modeling from visual inputs for deformable objects remained a challenge for creating generalizable digital twins.

## Method

- Integrates a differentiable Material Point Method (MPM) simulator, 3D Gaussian Splatting for appearance, and geometry reconstruction from multi-view RGB-D inputs into a comprehensive framework.
- Employs an offline optimization phase to identify material parameters by minimizing the discrepancy between simulated predictions and real-world observations in 3D geometry and appearance.
- Includes an online adaptive identification scheme for continuous refinement of material parameters based on live sensory feedback during real-time interactions, using 3D observations and 2D mask alignment.

## Results

- EMPM qualitatively reproduced physically plausible deformations for both elastic and elastoplastic objects, including complex behaviors like fracture and permanent deformation, outperforming spring-mass and learning-based baselines.
- The framework demonstrated superior quantitative geometric accuracy (e.g., Chamfer distance 0.0082 for elastoplastic objects, compared to 0.0177 for PhysTwin) and photorealistic visual quality across diverse materials.
- Online adaptive optimization successfully refined material parameters during robotic interaction, improving simulation-to-real alignment and reducing error metrics for both elastic rope and elastoplastic bread dough.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Differentiable Material Point Method (MPM) can accurately model complex continuum material behaviors like large deformations, fracture, and elastoplasticity, surpassing simpler physics models.
> - Integrating physics-based simulation with photorealistic appearance rendering and robust geometry reconstruction from visual data enables a powerful real-to-sim-to-real pipeline for deformable objects.
> - An online adaptive identification scheme is critical for refining material parameters in real-time, making the simulation robust to uncertainties and suitable for dynamic robotic interaction.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper addresses a fundamental and persistent challenge within 3D vision, computer graphics, and robotic manipulation: accurately modeling and simulating deformable objects, particularly continuum materials, in a physically plausible, generalizable, and data-efficient manner from visual information.

Traditional approaches to modeling deformable objects in simulation often involve particle-based models or spring-mass systems. While these methods are computationally efficient for certain scenarios, they frequently oversimplify the complex dynamics of real-world deformable materials, leading to inaccuracies, especially for large deformations, fracture, or elastoplastic behaviors. For instance, spring-mass systems are typically limited to elastic behaviors and struggle with phenomena like permanent deformation or tearing.

More recent advancements have introduced learning-based intuitive physics models. These models aim to capture complex behaviors through statistical approximations, but they typically require substantial amounts of training data. A significant limitation of these data-driven approaches is their often-poor generalization to novel objects or unseen deformation scenarios, restricting their real-world applicability in dynamic and diverse environments.

Concurrently, radiance field methods, such as Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) and Gaussian Splatting (GS), have advanced appearance modeling for both static and dynamic scenes. These differentiable rendering techniques have enabled high-fidelity visual reconstruction but generally do not inherently integrate physics-based dynamics.

Within this context, the Material Point Method (MPM) has emerged as a promising approach for simulating deformable objects composed of continuum materials. MPM is well-suited for handling complex interactions such as large deformations, contact, fracture, and tearing, making it more physically accurate than simpler models. The development of differentiable MPM-based methods is a relatively recent but active area, aiming to allow physical parameters to be optimized by minimizing discrepancies between simulated and real-world observations. Prior work has demonstrated the potential of differentiable MPM for tasks like system identification from visual information.

EMPM contributes to this landscape by combining differentiable MPM simulation with robust geometry reconstruction and photorealistic appearance rendering using Gaussian Splatting. It aims to overcome the limitations of oversimplified physics models and data-hungry learning-based methods by leveraging a physically grounded, differentiable simulator. The paper also extends the applicability of differentiable MPM by demonstrating its effectiveness across a wider range of materials (both elastic and elastoplastic) and introducing an online parameter optimization scheme, which is crucial for real-time adaptation in robotic manipulation tasks. This work positions itself as a comprehensive real-to-sim-to-real pipeline that bridges the gap between visual perception and physics-aware interaction with complex deformable objects.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a comprehensive framework, called Embodied MPM (EMPM), for modeling and simulating deformable objects. This framework aims to be physically plausible, generalizable, and data-efficient, addressing significant challenges in 3D vision, computer graphics, and robotic manipulation.

The key motivations driving this work are as follows:

- **Addressing Limitations of Existing Deformable Object Models:** Many current methods for simulating deformable objects, such as particle-based models and spring-mass systems, often oversimplify the complex dynamics of continuum materials. These simplifications lead to inaccuracies, particularly for large deformations, material damage (like fracture or tearing), and non-elastic behaviors (such as permanent shape change in elastoplastic materials). The authors aim to overcome these limitations by employing the Material Point Method (MPM), which is inherently more capable of capturing the rich dynamics of challenging materials.
- **Improving Generalization and Data Efficiency:** Learning-based physics models, while capable of capturing complex behaviors, typically demand large datasets for training and often struggle to generalize to novel objects or unseen scenarios. This data intensity and limited generalization hinder their practical applicability in diverse real-world settings. EMPM seeks to provide a physics-aware alternative that can infer material properties from relatively limited visual data, thereby enhancing generalization and data efficiency.
- **Enabling Differentiable Real-to-Sim-to-Real Pipelines:** The long-term goal is to establish a robust pipeline that can perform accurate interactive simulation of deformable objects directly from visual inputs, leveraging gradient-based optimization. This capability is critical for constructing photorealistic and physics-realistic digital twins, which are essential for various applications.
- **Facilitating Physics-Aware Robotic Manipulation:** A significant motivation is to enable robots to manipulate complex deformable objects effectively. For robust robotic control, an accurate understanding of an object's physical properties and dynamic responses is necessary. By providing an adaptive, robust, and physics-aware object representation, EMPM aims to unlock new possibilities for robotic tasks involving complex deformables, such as stretching dough, squeezing plasticine, or tearing bread.
- **Integrating Geometry, Appearance, and Physics:** The authors aim to jointly model and simulate deformable objects by integrating geometry reconstruction, appearance rendering, and physics-based simulation into a cohesive framework. While recent advances in radiance fields have improved appearance modeling, the challenge of physics-based simulation remains. EMPM seeks to combine these elements to create a more complete and useful digital representation.
- **Online Adaptation to Sensory Feedback:** Recognizing that material properties might vary or initial estimations might be imprecise, a key objective is to enable online correction and adaptation of MPM parameters using live sensory observations. This capability allows the model to continuously refine its understanding of the object's physics during interaction, making it more robust to real-world variability and suitable for real-time robotic deployment.
- **Handling a Broad Range of Materials:** The framework is explicitly designed to handle a diverse array of deformable materials, including both elastic objects (e.g., rope, cloth, soft elastic toys) and elastoplastic materials (e.g., plasticine, bread dough, pita bread), which exhibit distinct behaviors under deformation.

In summary, the core objective is to develop a versatile, physically accurate, and adaptable simulation framework for deformable objects that can be learned from visual data, continuously refined online, and directly applied to complex robotic manipulation tasks, thereby overcoming the limitations of current modeling paradigms.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Embodied MPM (EMPM) framework integrates geometry reconstruction, appearance rendering, and a differentiable Material Point Method (MPM) physics simulation to model and simulate deformable objects from multi-view RGB-D video inputs. The approach involves both offline parameter identification and online adaptive correction.

#### 3.1. Overall Architecture

EMPM operates as a real-to-sim-to-real pipeline. It starts with multi-view RGB-D observations from a pre-calibrated camera rig. These observations are used to reconstruct the object's 3D geometry and build a 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) model for appearance. This combined geometry-appearance model is then interfaced with a differentiable MPM simulator. The MPM's material parameters (e.g., Young’s modulus, Poisson’s ratio, density, plastic yield stress) are optimized by minimizing the discrepancy between the simulator's predictions and real-world observations in terms of 3D geometry and visual appearance over time.

#### 3.2. MPM and Continuum Mechanics

The core of EMPM is a differentiable Material Point Method (MPM) simulator, implemented using the Warp framework. MPM is a hybrid particle-grid method well-suited for simulating continuum materials, large deformations, contact, and elastoplastic flow. The simulation process involves a series of steps:

- **Particle to Grid (P2G):** Particle mass and momentum (including affine velocity terms for stability, APIC scheme) are transferred to a background grid.
- **Grid Update:** Grid node velocities are updated by applying internal forces (stress, gravity), external forces, and boundary conditions. Robot/hand motions are incorporated as Dirichlet boundary velocities on grid nodes in contact with the controller. Coulomb friction is applied for contact with surfaces like tables or grippers.
- **Grid to Particle (G2P):** Updated grid velocities are interpolated back to the particles, which are then advected to new positions.
- **Deformation Update:** Particle deformation gradients (F) are updated based on grid velocity gradients.
- **Elastoplasticity:** To model permanent shape changes, the deformation gradient is factored into elastic (F_E) and plastic (F_P) parts (F = F_E * F_P). Stress is computed only from the elastic part. The framework uses a Fixed Corotated stress model for elasticity and a von Mises return mapping for plasticity, allowing it to simulate both elastic and elastoplastic behaviors.

#### 3.3. Offline Simulation and Optimization

The offline phase focuses on identifying the material parameters ($\theta$) from pre-collected multi-view RGB-D video sequences.

- **Real-to-Sim Data Processing:**
 - **Segmentation:** Grounded-SAM2 is used to segment the target object and controllers (e.g., hands) in each image frame based on language prompts.
 - **3D Reconstruction:** Segmented depth images are lifted into 3D point clouds ($\tilde{X}_t$).
 - **3D Point Tracking:** An off-the-shelf point tracking model is used on offline sequences to compute 3D point trajectories ($\tilde{X}^{trk}_t$).
 - **Control Input:** Controller actions ($u_t$) from reconstructed end-effector motion are enforced as boundary velocities in the MPM grid update.
 - **Particle Initialization:** MPM particles ($X_0$) are sampled from the initial reconstructed point cloud ($\tilde{X}_0$), with optional point densification.
- **Objective Function:** The material parameters $\theta$ are optimized by minimizing a loss function ($L_{offline}$) that quantifies the discrepancy between MPM model predictions ($\hat{X}_t$) and real observations ($\tilde{X}_t$, $\tilde{X}^{trk}_t$) over time. This loss comprises two main terms:
 - **Chamfer Distance:** Measures the 3D geometric difference between the predicted and reconstructed point clouds ($\text{Chamfer}(\hat{X}_t, \tilde{X}_t)$).
 - **L2 Tracking Loss:** Computes the squared distance between individual predicted particles and their corresponding tracked points ($\| \hat{X}_{t,j} - \tilde{X}^{trk}_{t,j} \|_2^2$). Occlusion masks filter out invalid particles.
- **Optimization:** The Warp framework's automatic differentiation capabilities (via PyTorch integration) are leveraged to compute gradients of the loss with respect to material parameters ($\nabla_\theta L_{offline}$). An AdamW optimizer is used for gradient-based updates. A zero-order optimization method (CMA-ES) is also available as an alternative, potentially for memory reduction or more aggressive parameter search.

#### 3.4. Online Adaptive Identification

EMPM also includes an online scheme for continuously optimizing material parameters during real-time interactions using streaming 3D observations.

- **Streaming Supervision:**
 - An initial point cloud ($\tilde{X}_0$) is fused from multi-view RGB-D images and sampled as MPM particles.
 - Grounded SAM2 continuously segments the target object, and depth images are back-projected to generate real-time 3D point clouds ($\tilde{X}_t$).
 - Robot gripper positions are recorded as control inputs ($u_t$) for boundary conditions.
 - Online point tracking is not performed due to its unreliability under occlusion and indefinite sequences.
- **Simulation and Online Correction:**
 - Simultaneous simulation of the object is performed, creating a synchronized digital twin ($\hat{X}_t$).
 - Differentiable simulation steps are executed to generate gradient flows for parameter estimation.
 - Optimization steps are restricted to quasi-static states, assuming the object is near stable equilibrium. A fixed-step forward simulation ($H$ steps) predicts future states ($\check{X}_{t+H}$) from current simulated states ($\hat{X}_t$) and control inputs ($u_t$).
 - **Online Loss Function ($L_{online}$):** This loss aims to penalize discrepancies caused by inaccurate parameters:
 - **Chamfer Distance ($L_{dist}$):** Aligns the predicted point cloud ($\check{X}_{t+H}$) with the current real-time 3D observation ($\tilde{X}_{t+H}$).
 - **2D Mask Loss ($L_{mask}$):** Serves as a regularizer, aligning the simulated particles' projection with the segmented 2D mask ($\mathcal{M}_{t+H}$) since direct particle correspondence is unavailable online.
 - The gradient from $L_{online}$ is back-propagated to update $\theta'$, which then replaces the current parameters in the simulation.

#### 3.5. Applications

The EMPM simulator provides an action-conditioned predictive engine. By binding end-effector motions to grid boundary conditions, it can generate rollouts ($\hat{X}_{t:t+H}$) for candidate robot trajectories. This capability supports interactive simulation, "what-if" analysis for manipulation strategies, and forms a basis for model-based planning and control in autonomous deformable object manipulation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted aimed to demonstrate EMPM's capability in modeling diverse deformable objects, its accuracy compared to baseline models, and the effectiveness of its online optimization.

#### 4.1. Experiment Setup

- **Object Selection:** Objects were categorized into two main types:
 - **Elastic Objects:** Rope segment, cloth, soft elastic toy.
 - **Elastoplastic Objects:** Plasticine, bread dough, pita bread.
- **Manipulations:** Various manipulations were performed, including extending, stretching, squeezing, and tearing, to cover a range of deformations.
- **Sensor Setup:** A multi-view stereo RGB-D camera rig (three RealSense D455 cameras) was used for perception.
- **Data Collection:** Initial data was collected using human hands to capture complex interactions for offline optimization. For robotic manipulation experiments, a bimanual setup with two Franka arms was used for online optimization.
- **Implementation:** Training and online optimization of Young’s modulus ($E$) and Poisson’s ratio ($\nu$) were performed on a single Nvidia A6000 GPU using Warp's automatic differentiation and the AdamW optimizer.

#### 4.2. Offline Simulation and Optimization

- **Qualitative Results (Figures 4 & 5):**
 - EMPM's simulated particle behavior exhibited physically plausible deformations that closely matched real-world observations for both elastic and elastoplastic objects.
 - Photorealistic visualizations using Gaussian Splatting demonstrated high-fidelity appearance reproduction of dynamic simulations. Gaussian centers were interpolated with neighboring MPM particle motions using Linear Blend Skinning (LBS).
 - **Comparison with PhysTwin (spring-mass system):** EMPM demonstrated significant advantages, particularly for elastoplastic materials. PhysTwin failed to accurately model fracture (e.g., in pita bread) and exhibited unrealistic deformations (e.g., over-bending in plasticine). EMPM successfully captured these complex phenomena.
- **Quantitative Results (Table 1):**
 - EMPM was compared against PhysTwin and a learning-based dynamic model, PGND, in terms of geometric and visual metrics.
 - **Geometric Accuracy:** Measured by Chamfer distance (Dist $\downarrow$) and L2 tracking error (Track $\downarrow$). EMPM consistently achieved lower (better) distance and tracking errors across both elastic and elastoplastic objects. For elastoplastic objects, EMPM's Chamfer distance was 0.0082, significantly lower than PhysTwin's 0.0177 and PGND's 0.0245.
 - **Visual Quality:** Evaluated using IoU (Intersection over Union, % $\uparrow$), PSNR ($\uparrow$), SSIM (Structural Similarity Index, $\uparrow$), and LPIPS (Learned Perceptual Image Patch Similarity, $\downarrow$). EMPM generally outperformed baselines in these metrics, indicating better photorealistic rendering. For elastoplastic objects, EMPM achieved an IoU of 0.7768, PSNR of 27.82, SSIM of 0.9725, and LPIPS of 0.0291, all superior to the baselines.
 - **Material Versatility:** A key finding was EMPM's ability to handle a broad spectrum of materials, including both hyperelastic and elastoplastic behaviors, which simpler spring-mass systems cannot capture (e.g., permanent shape change, fracture).

#### 4.3. Online Optimization

- **Qualitative Results (Figure 6):**
 - Experiments on a rope and bread dough using bimanual robotic arms demonstrated that EMPM's simulated digital twins closely followed the motion of real objects during teleoperation.
 - The alignment between simulation and observation progressively improved over time with online optimization, visually confirming the adaptive capabilities.
- **Quantitative Results (Table 2):**
 - The online optimization process (performing one optimization step every 5 streaming steps when the object was quasi-steady, simulating for 10 forward steps) improved simulation accuracy.
 - For the rope, $L_{mask}$ decreased from 0.0456 to 0.0428, and $L_{dist}$ decreased from 0.0057 to 0.0054.
 - For bread dough, $L_{mask}$ decreased from 0.0031 to 0.0024, and $L_{dist}$ decreased from 0.0060 to 0.0059. These reductions in error confirm the efficacy of online correction.
- **Running Time Analysis (Table 3):**
 - EMPM demonstrated efficient training times, with 161.80s for elastic objects and 171.60s for elastoplastic objects, making it the most efficient compared to PGND (50675.50s, 48464.40s) and PhysTwin (496.84s, 589.51s).
 - Inference (testing) times were comparable to PhysTwin but higher than PGND, which had the lowest inference time due to its small feedforward network, albeit at the cost of significantly longer training.

#### 4.4. Applications (Figure 7)

- A proof-of-concept application with real robotics hardware illustrated the potential for using EMPM in model-based planning and control for deformable object manipulation tasks, where the robot could track changes predicted by the simulator.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The EMPM framework represents a development in the field of deformable object modeling and simulation, with several points of significance and potential impact:

- **Enhanced Physical Plausibility and Realism:** By leveraging the Material Point Method (MPM), EMPM offers a more physically accurate representation of continuum materials compared to simpler models like spring-mass systems. This is particularly significant for objects undergoing large deformations, fracture, or exhibiting elastoplastic behavior, which were previously challenging to simulate realistically from visual data. The ability to model complex phenomena like tearing bread or squeezing plasticine with fidelity contributes to more realistic digital twins.
- **Robustness to Diverse Material Properties:** The framework's capacity to handle both elastic and elastoplastic materials broadens its applicability across a wide range of real-world objects. This versatility is a notable improvement over methods often restricted to purely elastic or specific types of deformable materials.
- **Data-Efficient and Generalizable Modeling:** Unlike many learning-based approaches that require extensive training data and often struggle with generalization to novel objects, EMPM grounds its dynamics in a differentiable physics engine. This approach allows for the identification of physical parameters from visual observations with potentially less data, contributing to better generalization across different object instances or manipulation scenarios.
- **Online Adaptability for Dynamic Environments:** The introduction of an online adaptive identification scheme is a critical advancement. This feature enables continuous refinement of material parameters based on live sensory feedback during real-time interactions. This capability makes EMPM robust to uncertainties, initial parameter inaccuracies, or material variations, which is vital for real-world robotic applications where conditions can change dynamically.
- **Foundation for Physics-Aware Robotic Manipulation:** EMPM provides a direct interface for incorporating accurate deformable dynamics into embodied workflows. By generating action-conditioned rollouts that predict object responses to robot actions, it lays a foundation for model-based robotic planning and control. This could lead to more intelligent and dexterous robotic manipulation of deformable objects, allowing robots to perform complex tasks that require an understanding of material behavior, such as dressing, cooking, or assembly with soft components.
- **Improved Digital Twin Capabilities:** The joint modeling of geometry, photorealistic appearance (via 3D Gaussian Splatting), and physics-based dynamics allows for the creation of high-fidelity digital twins. These twins can accurately mirror real-world objects, enabling "what-if" analysis for manipulation strategies, immediate feedback on feasibility, and visualization of object responses under hypothetical control inputs, thereby facilitating simulation-to-real transfer.
- **Efficient Parameter Optimization:** The use of differentiable MPM with frameworks like Warp enables efficient gradient-based optimization of material parameters. This is a significant advantage over manual tuning or computationally expensive trial-and-error methods for system identification. The reported training efficiency further supports its practicality.

**Potential Impact:**

The direct impact of EMPM could be seen in:
- **Robotics:** Revolutionizing soft robot control and manipulation, allowing robots to handle delicate and complex deformable objects with greater precision and adaptability.
- **Virtual Reality/Augmented Reality:** Creating more immersive and interactive virtual environments where virtual objects respond realistically to user interactions.
- **Manufacturing and Design:** Enabling better simulation tools for product design involving deformable materials, from textiles to food processing.
- **Medical Simulation:** Improving models for surgical training and planning that require accurate soft tissue interaction.

While the work identifies limitations such as challenges in obtaining reliable online point tracking under occlusion and large deformations, it proposes promising future directions, including enhanced appearance modeling and integrating model-based control for autonomous deformable manipulation. Overall, EMPM contributes a framework for robust and adaptive modeling of deformable objects, bridging perception and physics for advanced interactive applications.
