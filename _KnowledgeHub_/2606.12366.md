# APT: Action Expert Pretraining Improves Instruction Generalization of Vision-Language-Action Policies

> [!summary] Summary
> Zhejiang University researchers introduced Action expert PreTraining (APT), a two-stage method for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies, designed to improve instruction generalization by pretraining the action expert on visuomotor correlations before language exposure. This approach achieved an average success rate of 27% on the LIBERO-PRO benchmark and demonstrated 90% success on complex compositional real-world tasks, outperforming existing baselines.

## Problem

- Continuous-action Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models exhibit limited generalization to out-of-distribution (OOD) language instructions, including paraphrases, novel objects, and compositional tasks.
- A structural imbalance in VLA datasets, where a single language instruction corresponds to numerous vision-action frames, causes policies to learn 'visual shortcuts' and bypass genuine language understanding.
- Randomly initialized action experts can generate noisy gradients that interfere with and potentially corrupt the pre-trained language representations of the Vision-Language Model (VLM) backbone.

## Method

- **APT** uses a ==Bayesian factorization== of the VLA policy, decomposing it into a language-agnostic ==Vision-Action (VA) prior== and a language-conditioned VLA likelihood.
- A two-stage training procedure is implemented: Stage 1 pretrains the ==action expert== solely on balanced vision-action pairs to establish a robust ==visuomotor prior==; Stage 2 jointly trains the full VLA policy, refining the action expert to align with language instructions.
- The action expert, a ==Transformer==-based ==diffusion model==, employs a novel ==layer-wise gated fusion== mechanism to effectively inject intermediate VLM features, integrating language conditioning while preserving the learned VA prior.

## Results

- APT achieved an average success rate of **19%** on LIBERO-PRO OOD tasks (**27%** with VLM finetuning), demonstrating substantial gains over baselines like π0.5 and LangForce.
- In real-world compositional tasks, APT maintained **90%** success when given chained instructions in a single prompt, while π0.5's performance collapsed to **20%**.
- The action expert pretraining method (APT) consistently improved performance across various VLA architectures (e.g., π-style, GR00T-style), indicating its broad applicability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Pretraining the action expert on balanced vision-action data before exposing it to language significantly mitigates the formation of visual shortcuts and improves language generalization.
> - Decoupling the learning of fundamental visuomotor control (VA prior) from language alignment (VLA likelihood) via Bayesian factorization provides a more principled and effective training paradigm for VLA policies.
> - Careful, gated integration of VLM features into the action expert is crucial for leveraging language understanding without corrupting the action expert's pretrained visuomotor capabilities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic control has increasingly focused on Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which integrate large pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs) with robotic action generation to enable robots to follow natural language instructions. This paradigm aims to develop generalizable robot policies capable of operating across diverse tasks and environments.

Early approaches to VLA models involved training transformer-based policies from scratch on large robot datasets. Subsequent advancements leveraged pre-trained VLMs, initially by discretizing action spaces and co-training with vision-language data. This approach demonstrated improved language capabilities but often struggled with the continuous and dexterous nature of real-world manipulation tasks. To address action quality, continuous-action experts, often diffusion- or flow-based, were introduced and coupled with VLMs. These dual-system architectures have become prevalent, with recent efforts scaling them further through VLM reasoning co-training and auxiliary objectives based on world models.

Despite significant progress in in-distribution performance, a persistent challenge for VLA models, particularly those with continuous action experts, is their limited generalization to out-of-distribution (OOD) language instructions. This includes unseen paraphrases, novel object references, or compositional task specifications. Prior research has attributed this issue, in part, to a structural imbalance in VLA datasets. In these datasets, a single language instruction typically corresponds to numerous vision-action frames within a trajectory. This imbalance can lead policies to develop "visual shortcuts," where actions are primarily derived from visual cues, bypassing the language instruction and compromising genuine language understanding.

Existing attempts to mitigate this issue include "knowledge insulation" (stopping gradients from the action expert to the VLM backbone to prevent corruption of pre-trained language representations) and co-training on additional vision-language reasoning corpora. While discrete-action methods can utilize VL co-training to preserve VLM language capabilities, continuous action experts often start from random initialization and learn entirely from imbalanced VLA data. This process can produce noisy gradients that negatively affect the VLM and prevent it from fully leveraging its language grounding abilities.

This paper positions its work, APT (Action expert PreTraining), as directly addressing this initialization gap in continuous-action VLA models. It proposes that by appropriately pretraining the action expert, a tendency toward visual shortcuts can be relieved, thereby improving instruction following and OOD language generalization. This approach extends existing Bayesian frameworks for VLA policy decomposition by applying it specifically to the pretraining of the action expert, contrasting with methods that primarily focus on architectural modifications or gradient flow control during joint training.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the generalization capabilities of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies, specifically their ability to follow out-of-distribution (OOD) language instructions. This includes reliably executing tasks based on unseen paraphrased commands, novel object references, and compositional task specifications, which is a critical requirement for real-world robot deployment.

The motivation for this work stems from an identified bottleneck in current continuous-action VLA models:
1. **Poor OOD Language Generalization:** While existing VLA policies often perform well on tasks seen during training, they exhibit substantial performance degradation when faced with instructions or task compositions not encountered in the training data. This limits their applicability in dynamic, unstructured environments.
2. **Structural Imbalance in VLA Data:** A fundamental issue identified is the imbalance in VLA datasets. A single, static language instruction is typically associated with a long sequence of varied visual observations and corresponding actions throughout a robot trajectory. This makes the vision-action data significantly more diverse than the language data.
3. **Tendency Towards Visual Shortcuts:** Due to this data imbalance, VLA policies, especially those with randomly initialized action experts, are prone to learning "visual shortcuts." This means the policy learns to infer actions primarily from visual cues, effectively bypassing the language instruction. When the visual context is sufficiently predictive of the action, the language signal becomes underutilized or ignored.
4. **Corruption of VLM Language Capabilities:** When a randomly initialized action expert, biased towards visual shortcuts, is trained jointly with a pre-trained Vision-Language Model (VLM), the noisy or misaligned gradients generated by the action expert can interfere with and potentially corrupt the VLM's pre-trained language representations. This undermines the VLM's ability to provide robust language understanding for the downstream robot control.
5. **Lack of Principled Action Expert Initialization:** Unlike VLMs which benefit from extensive pretraining on balanced vision-language data (e.g., internet-scale image-text pairs), the continuous action experts in VLA models typically start from random initialization. This lack of a pre-trained foundation makes them susceptible to the issues arising from imbalanced VLA data.

The core hypothesis motivating APT is that if the action expert could be properly pre-trained to understand visuomotor correlations *before* being exposed to language, it would be less prone to visual shortcuts. This would enable it to effectively integrate language signals from the VLM, leading to improved generalization. The research thus poses a specific question: how can the action expert be pre-trained using only the available VLA data in a principled manner that avoids the language-vision imbalance?

The objective is to answer this by proposing a two-stage training method, APT, that leverages a Bayesian factorization of the VLA policy. This factorization separates action generation into a language-agnostic Vision-Action (VA) prior and a language-conditioned VLA likelihood, allowing for targeted pretraining of the action expert on balanced vision-action pairs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Action expert PreTraining (APT), addresses the instruction generalization bottleneck in continuous-action VLA policies through a two-stage training approach grounded in a Bayesian factorization of the policy.

#### 3.1. Bayesian Factorization of the VLA Policy

The VLA policy, π(a | v, ℓ), which models the probability of an action *a* given visual observations *v* and language instruction *ℓ*, is decomposed as:
π(a | v, ℓ) ∝ πp(a | v) · L(ℓ | v, a)
This factorization separates the action generation into two components:
- **Vision-Action (VA) Prior πp(a | v):** This component models the distribution of robot actions conditioned solely on visual observations, without any language input. It is designed to capture fundamental visuomotor control. The key insight is that within a VLA dataset, individual vision-action pairs are inherently balanced (each visual frame is uniquely paired with an action), thus avoiding the shortcut incentives present in VLA triplets where one instruction covers many frames. Pretraining the action expert on these balanced vision-action pairs provides a principled initialization that captures coherent visuomotor control.
- **VLA Likelihood L(ℓ | v, a):** This component introduces language understanding. With a well-initialized VA prior, the likelihood model's task is simplified to aligning the pre-trained action distribution with specific task instructions, rather than learning action generation from scratch alongside language grounding.

#### 3.2. Two-Stage Training Procedure

The Bayesian factorization naturally leads to a two-stage pretraining procedure:

- **Stage 1: VA Prior Pretraining:**
 - The action expert is trained exclusively on vision-action pairs from the pretraining data.
 - Language input is completely masked or excluded from the training process.
 - The VLM backbone parameters are kept frozen during this stage.
 - This stage focuses on developing a robust visuomotor prior that can generate actions based purely on visual observations, free from language-induced shortcuts.
 - The action expert utilizes N/2 of its attention layers for this stage. Visual and action tokens are processed, using Projective Positional Encoding (PRoPE) to embed camera extrinsics.

- **Stage 2: VLA Joint Training (Likelihood Alignment):**
 - After Stage 1, language tokens are introduced, and the full VLA policy (incorporating both the VA prior and the VLA likelihood) is jointly trained on the complete VLA pretraining data.
 - The action expert's parameters are initialized from the Stage 1 checkpoint, leveraging the learned VA prior.
 - The language mask is removed, allowing language tokens to participate in attention computations.
 - This stage refines the action expert to align its pretrained action distribution with specific task instructions.
 - The action expert expands from N/2 to N attention layers. The original N/2 layers retain PRoPE, while the newly inserted N/2 layers use Multimodal Rotary Position Embedding (mRoPE) to handle interleaved vision, language, and action tokens. Joint optimization of all N layers is performed.

- **Post-Training:** After these two pretraining stages, the full policy can be finetuned on task-specific data to adapt to target-domain distributions.

#### 3.3. Action Expert Design

The action expert is a Transformer-based diffusion model with a novel design for integrating VLM features:

- **Multimodal Self-Attention:** At each denoising step, visual tokens (*v*), language tokens (*ℓ*), and action tokens (*a*) are concatenated into a single sequence. This sequence is then processed via block-wise causal self-attention. The action tokens consist of executed action history, current proprioceptive state, and noisy action tokens to be denoised. Denoising timestep is incorporated via Feature-wise Linear Modulation (FiLM).
- **Layer-wise VLM Feature Gated Fusion:** Qwen3-VL is used as the VLM backbone. To effectively leverage its semantic representations, intermediate features from Qwen3-VL are injected into *every* self-attention layer of the action expert. N intermediate features are uniformly sampled from Qwen3-VL. A learnable scalar, modulated by a sigmoid function, acts as a gate to control the influence of each VLM layer's features on the action expert, enabling flexible integration while preserving the action expert's own processing.

#### 3.4. Action Representation and Training Details

- **Action Space:** Actions are represented in the SE(3) manifold (3D translation, 6D continuous rotation), plus normalized gripper width, resulting in a 10-dimensional action vector. End-effector poses are expressed in the camera coordinate frame for embodiment equivariance.
- **Pretraining Datasets:** The model is pretrained on a diverse set of large-scale datasets, including DROID (real-world Franka trajectories), AgiBotWorld-Alpha (real-world dual-arm trajectories), InternData-A1 (high-fidelity synthetic data across diverse embodiments and skills), and InternVLA-M1 (generated pick-place trajectories with dense language-object correspondences). These datasets are sampled with specific weights during pretraining.
- **Hyperparameters:** AdamW optimizer, specified learning rates for action expert and VLM (when finetuned), and DDPM for training/DDIM for inference with a fixed number of diffusion/denoising steps.

#### 3.5. Baselines for Comparison

The proposed APT method is evaluated against several representative end-to-end VLA policies:
- **OpenVLA:** A VLA policy built on Prismatic VLM, pretrained on diverse datasets, and finetuned with continuous action representations.
- **π0:** Combines PaliGemma VLM with a diffusion-based action expert, pretrained on a subset of OXE and the π dataset.
- **π0.5:** An extension of π0, utilizing a hybrid pretraining procedure that jointly trains on internet-scale reasoning data and large-scale robot demonstration data, incorporating Knowledge Insulation (KI) to prevent gradient corruption.
- **LangForce:** A concurrent work that enforces language following via latent action queries and maximizes mutual information between actions and instructions using a dual-branch architecture.
- **CaP-X:** A code-as-policies framework that generates structured task programs via a language model and executes them through learned robot primitives.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides comprehensive experimental validation across simulation and real-world settings, demonstrating the effectiveness of Action expert PreTraining (APT) in improving language generalization for VLA policies.

#### 4.1. Simulation Experiments

- **LIBERO-PRO Benchmark:**
 - OpenVLA and π0 achieved 0% success under both "Pos" (swapped object positions) and "Task" (replaced manipulated object) perturbations, indicating a failure to generalize when language matters.
 - π0.5, which uses Knowledge Insulation (KI), improved significantly on "Pos" tasks (e.g., 20% to 38% across suites), suggesting effective preservation of in-distribution language following. However, its performance remained near zero on "Task" perturbations, highlighting KI's limitation for OOD language generalization.
 - LangForce showed improvements on "Task" but exhibited degradation on "Pos," suggesting a trade-off between language sensitivity and visual adaptability.
 - APT demonstrated substantial performance gains over both π0.5 and LangForce, with an average success rate of 19%. For instance, on "Spatial-Pos," APT achieved 44% compared to π0.5's 20% and LangForce's 11%. On "Spatial-Task," APT matched LangForce's 48%. This indicates that APT's two-stage training (VA prior for visuomotor competence, VLA likelihood for language alignment) effectively resolves this trade-off.
 - When APT was trained with VLM finetuning (APT (Ft VLM)), it further improved across most suites, reaching an average success rate of 27%. This suggests that with a well-initialized action expert, stopping gradients (KI) is not strictly necessary and joint VLM finetuning can yield additional benefits. APT (Ft VLM) also outperformed the agent-level CaP-X baseline on average.

- **Rigid Object Pick-Place Benchmark:**
 - Ablation studies on this benchmark confirmed the impact of KI, 2-Stage training (APT's core), and VLM finetuning.
 - π0 (no KI, Ft VLM) showed the weakest generalization (e.g., 42% on Seen Object (SO)).
 - π0.5 (KI, VLM co-training) improved substantially (84% on SO, 70% on Unseen Object (UO)).
 - APT with KI and 2-Stage training surpassed π0.5 (88% on SO, 56% on UO) without requiring external VL reasoning data, attributing the gains directly to action expert pretraining.
 - The best overall results were achieved by APT with 2-Stage training and joint VLM finetuning (98% on SO, 84% on UO, 92% on Unseen Container (UC)), further supporting that KI is not a prerequisite when the action expert is properly initialized.

- **Architectural Generalization:** APT's action pretraining method was successfully applied to π-style and GR00T-style architectures, showing consistent performance improvements across nearly all settings. The gains were most pronounced for APT's own design and GR00T-style architectures, which both employ VLM fusion designs that better preserve the learned VA prior.

- **Effectiveness of Large-Scale Pretraining:** A variant of APT trained solely on task-specific datasets (without large-scale pretraining) showed some generalization, but significantly underperformed the full APT model on unseen categories and environments (UO and UOUE). This highlights that diverse action priors from large-scale heterogeneous data are crucial for robust generalization.

- **Language Injection Mechanism:** The gated fusion mechanism for VLM feature injection outperformed a direct "Token Insertion" method across all dimensions. This indicates that carefully modulating language conditioning, rather than abruptly modifying the pretrained VA distribution, helps preserve the VA prior and enables more effective language-guided refinement.

#### 4.2. Real-World Experiments

- **Single Task Generalization (Pick-Place & Clutter Pick-Place):**
 - On pick-place tasks, APT maintained robust performance across increasing OOD difficulty (Unseen Object, Unseen Container, Unseen Environment), while π0.5 degraded significantly. For instance, in the most challenging UOUCUE setting, APT achieved 28/40 successful trials compared to π0.5's 16/40. APT demonstrated better grasping of unseen objects and precise placement into unseen containers.
 - In clutter pick-place tasks (requiring pushing to clear space before manipulation), APT again outperformed π0.5 across all settings (e.g., 25/30 vs 18/30 on SO). π0.5 often struggled with sub-task transitions (e.g., from push to grasp) or misgrounded objects, whereas APT exhibited better performance in these long-horizon, cluttered conditions.

- **Compositional Task Generalization (Table Storage + Pick-Place):**
 - Both APT and π0.5 performed comparably and robustly under "Task Coaching" (sequential instructions, seen or unseen language). This indicated that both policies had sufficient individual task competence and could handle paraphrased or swapped-object instructions when given one at a time.
 - However, under "Task Chaining" (a single concatenated prompt specifying multiple tasks), π0.5 nearly collapsed (e.g., 20% success), while APT maintained strong performance (90% success). π0.5's typical failure mode was over-executing the first task (e.g., placing all objects into the storage box) and failing to transition to the subsequent task, indicating an inability to parse and execute multi-task instructions from a single, unsegmented prompt. APT's success here demonstrates better preservation of the VLM's language understanding for complex, multi-step instructions.

#### 4.3. Failure Analysis

- **π0.5 Failures:** In cluttered scenes, π0.5 often failed at the push-to-grasp transition due to confusing target objects with distractors of similar colors or continuously pushing after targets were exposed. In compositional tasks, it failed to parse chained instructions, often over-executing the first sub-task.
- **APT Failures:** APT's remaining failures were less frequent and typically involved minor issues such as occasionally continuing to push after grasping (clutter pick-place) or skipping a final "close the box" action in chained compositional tasks. These indicate a remaining gap in precisely detecting sub-task termination rather than fundamental language grounding issues.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant contribution to the development of generalizable robot policies, particularly in the domain of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. The proposed Action expert PreTraining (APT) method offers a principled approach to enhance the out-of-distribution (OOD) language generalization capabilities of continuous-action VLA systems, which is a critical step for real-world robotic deployment.

The significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Improved Language Generalization:** APT directly addresses a core limitation of current continuous-action VLA models: their poor performance on unseen instructions, novel objects, and compositional tasks. By enabling policies to genuinely attend to language, rather than relying on visual shortcuts, APT makes robots more adaptable and robust to variations in human commands.
- **Principled Training Paradigm:** The Bayesian factorization of the VLA policy into a Vision-Action (VA) prior and a VLA likelihood provides a theoretically grounded framework for training. This decomposition allows for focused learning of visuomotor skills (Stage 1) before integrating language guidance (Stage 2), leading to a more stable and effective learning process.
- **Effective Action Expert Initialization:** The work highlights the crucial role of pretraining the continuous action expert. By initializing the action expert on balanced vision-action data, APT prevents the early formation of visual shortcuts and mitigates the negative impact of noisy gradients on the VLM backbone. This contrasts with previous methods that often train randomly initialized action experts directly on imbalanced VLA data.
- **Architectural Flexibility:** The demonstrated applicability of APT to diverse mainstream VLA architectures (e.g., π-style, GR00T-style) suggests its broad utility beyond the specific action expert design presented. This indicates that the core concept of action expert pretraining can be integrated into various existing or future VLA frameworks.
- **Enhanced Real-World Capabilities:** The consistent performance gains observed in both simulation and real-world experiments translate into more capable robots. This includes improved semantic grounding for novel objects, more robust execution in cluttered environments, and a superior ability to understand and execute complex, multi-task instructions presented in a single, chained prompt. These advancements move closer to the goal of truly generalist robots.
- **Implications for Foundation Models in Robotics:** The research underscores that even with powerful pre-trained VLMs, their effective integration into robotic control systems requires careful consideration of the downstream action expert's training. APT demonstrates that specialized pretraining for the action component is essential to fully leverage the language understanding capabilities of foundation models.
- **Future Research Directions:** The identified limitations, such as the current design not explicitly modeling long-horizon memory or being primarily evaluated on tabletop manipulation, open avenues for future research. Extending APT to tasks requiring multi-step progress tracking, locomotion, or mobile manipulation could further broaden its impact.

In conclusion, APT provides a valuable contribution by offering a robust and principled method to improve language generalization in VLA policies, thereby advancing the capabilities of robots in understanding and executing diverse human instructions in complex environments.
