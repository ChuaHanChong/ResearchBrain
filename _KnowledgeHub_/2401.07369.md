# CoVO-MPC: Theoretical Analysis of Sampling-based MPC and Optimal Covariance Design

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Carnegie Mellon University established the first theoretical convergence analysis for Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) control, characterizing its contraction properties for quadratic and strongly convex cost functions. This theoretical foundation enabled the development of CoVO-MPC, an algorithm that dynamically adapts its sampling covariance matrix based on the cost function's Hessian, achieving 43-54% improved performance over standard MPPI in robotic tasks.

## Problem

- Sampling-based Model Predictive Control (MPC) methods, such as MPPI, lacked a comprehensive theoretical understanding of their convergence and contraction properties.
- Critical hyperparameters, particularly the sampling covariance matrix (Σ), in sampling-based MPC were typically tuned heuristically, limiting performance and robustness.
- Existing sampling-based MPC algorithms often did not explicitly leverage information about the system's dynamics or the optimization landscape for efficient control.

## Method

- Performed a theoretical analysis of ==MPPI=='s single-step update rule, proving at least linear ==convergence== for quadratic cost functions and convergence in probability to a neighborhood for strongly convex costs.
- Derived a principled method for optimally designing the ==sampling covariance matrix (Σ)== by minimizing the ==contraction rate==, showing it asymptotically simplifies to be proportional to the inverse square root of the cost function's ==Hessian==.
- Developed **CoVO-MPC**, a novel sampling-based MPC algorithm that dynamically computes and schedules an optimal sampling covariance matrix online, or uses a pre-computed offline approximation for reduced computational cost.

## Results

- CoVO-MPC consistently outperformed standard MPPI across CartPole, Quadrotor simulation, and real-world Quadrotor tasks, demonstrating **43-54%** reduced tracking errors or overall cost.
- The algorithm exhibited higher sampling efficiency, achieving better performance with fewer samples and generating more concentrated, lower-mean cost distributions for sampled trajectories.
- An offline approximation variant of CoVO-MPC also significantly improved performance (e.g., **22%** in real-world quadrotor control) while maintaining a computational cost comparable to standard MPPI.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - MPPI's convergence rate is precisely characterized by the sampling covariance (Σ), temperature (λ), and the cost function's Hessian (D), highlighting the importance of landscape awareness.
> - Optimal sampling efficiency is achieved when the covariance matrix adapts to the local curvature of the cost function, suggesting a non-isotropic sampling distribution that compresses along 'smoother' directions and stretches along 'steeper' ones.
> - Even with nonlinear systems and non-quadratic costs, MPPI maintains contraction properties, albeit with bounded error terms related to the magnitude of the nonlinear residuals.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Broader Research Landscape

Model Predictive Control (MPC) is a well-established control paradigm with applications across diverse fields, including process control, robotics, transportation, and power systems. Within MPC, sampling-based approaches have garnered attention due to their flexibility in handling complex dynamics and cost functions, as well as their amenability to massive parallelization on modern computing architectures like GPUs. These characteristics have led to their adoption in areas such as path planning and control, and notably, as a key subroutine in model-based reinforcement learning (MBRL) frameworks, where they can effectively manage learned, often nonlinear and nonconvex, dynamics and reward functions.

Prominent sampling-based MPC algorithms include Model Predictive Path Integral Control (MPPI), the Cross-Entropy Method (CEM), and Covariance Matrix Adaptation Evolution Strategy (CMA-ES). While these methods have demonstrated empirical success in real-world robotic control and MBRL contexts, a comprehensive theoretical understanding of their properties, particularly regarding convergence, contraction, and principled hyperparameter tuning, has been less developed. Existing implementations often rely on heuristic adjustments for critical parameters such as the temperature (λ) and the sampling covariance matrix (Σ), which can limit performance and robustness. This research addresses this gap by providing theoretical insights into the convergence of sampling-based MPC and utilizing these insights to develop an improved algorithm.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to establish a theoretical understanding of the convergence properties of sampling-based Model Predictive Control (MPC), specifically focusing on Model Predictive Path Integral Control (MPPI). The authors aim to characterize whether and how rapidly MPPI converges to an optimal control sequence when sampling around a suboptimal one.

A second key objective is to leverage this theoretical analysis to develop a novel sampling-based MPC algorithm that can optimally tune its hyperparameters, particularly the sampling covariance matrix (Σ), to enhance convergence rates.

The motivation for these objectives arises from several factors:
- **Empirical Performance vs. Theoretical Gap:** Sampling-based MPC methods, especially MPPI, have shown promising empirical results in complex domains like agile robotics and model-based reinforcement learning. However, this practical success is not uniformly supported by rigorous theoretical guarantees regarding their convergence or contraction properties.
- **Lack of Principled Hyperparameter Tuning:** Critical hyperparameters in sampling-based MPC, such as the temperature (λ) and the sampling covariance (Σ), are typically tuned heuristically. This process is often time-consuming, task-specific, and lacks a theoretical foundation for optimal selection, potentially limiting the algorithm's performance and generalizability. For instance, standard MPPI often uses isotropic Gaussian sampling (Σ = σI) without explicit consideration of the underlying system dynamics or optimization landscape.
- **Optimization Landscape Awareness:** Most existing sampling-based MPC algorithms operate without explicitly leveraging information about the system's dynamics or the shape of the optimization landscape. The authors hypothesize that integrating this information could lead to more efficient and robust control.

By addressing these motivations, the research aims to both advance the theoretical foundations of sampling-based MPC and provide practical tools for designing more effective and efficient control strategies.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a multi-faceted approach involving theoretical analysis, algorithmic design, and empirical validation.

#### 3.1. Theoretical Analysis of MPPI Convergence

The authors initiate their theoretical investigation by analyzing the single-step update rule of MPPI (Equation 2 in the paper), which involves generating a new control sequence as a softmax-weighted average of sampled trajectories.

- **Quadratic Cost Functions (LQR Systems):** The initial analysis focuses on scenarios where the total cost function J(U) is quadratic with respect to the control sequence U. This encompasses time-varying Linear Quadratic Regulator (LQR) systems. Under this condition, the authors prove that MPPI exhibits at least linear convergence (contraction) towards the optimal control sequence U* and optimal cost J*. A precise characterization of the contraction rate is provided, expressed as a function of the sampling covariance Σ, the temperature λ, and the system's Hessian matrix D. This analysis highlights the importance of Σ and D in determining the convergence speed.
- **Generalization Beyond Quadratic Costs:** The theoretical framework is extended to more general nonlinear systems and non-quadratic cost functions through three scenarios:
 - **Strongly Convex J(U):** For β-strongly convex cost functions with Lipschitz continuous derivatives, the analysis demonstrates that MPPI converges in probability to a neighborhood of the optimal point U*. The contraction rate is of order O(λ/β), and a residual error of order O(√λ/β) is identified, indicating that convergence is maintained but to a small region around the optimum.
 - **Linear Systems with Nonlinear Residuals:** The analysis considers linear dynamics augmented with bounded nonlinear residual dynamics, where the total cost J(U) includes higher-order terms. By leveraging the framework of exponential families, the authors demonstrate that MPPI still guarantees contraction, though with an associated error term. This error is shown to be proportional to a Lipschitz constant of the log-partition function's derivative and the magnitude of the Hessian difference introduced by the residual dynamics.
 - **General Systems:** As a direct extension of the residual dynamics case, a corollary is provided for general systems where direct access to the cost function's structure (specifically, the difference in Hessian matrices) is assumed. The contraction error is directly related to the discrepancy between the Hessian of the nominal dynamics and the actual Hessian under the system's true dynamics.

#### 3.2. Optimal Covariance Design

Based on the convergence analysis, particularly the role of Σ and D in the quadratic case, the authors develop a principle for optimally designing the sampling covariance matrix.

- **Optimization Problem Formulation:** The problem is formulated as minimizing the contraction rate (Equation 5) subject to a constraint on the determinant of Σ (to maintain a consistent sampling volume).
- **Derivation of Optimal Σ:** Theorem 2 describes the optimal structure for Σ: its singular value decomposition shares the same eigenvector matrix with the Hessian matrix D, and its eigenvalues (diagonal elements in Λ) satisfy a specific algebraic relationship involving D's eigenvalues (O) and the initial error (Λ_in).
- **Practical Approximation:** To overcome the practical challenge of requiring knowledge of the optimal solution U* to apply Theorem 2, a simplified approximation is derived (Corollary 3). Under the assumptions of an isotropic gap between the current and optimal control sequences (U_in - U* ∼ N(0, I)) and a small λ regime, the optimal covariance Σ asymptotically simplifies to be proportional to D^(-1/2). This approximation is efficient to compute and forms the basis for the proposed algorithm. This principle suggests that the sampling distribution should be compressed along directions where the cost function is "smoother" and stretched along directions aligned with the cost's gradient, adapting to the optimization landscape.

#### 3.3. CoVO-MPC Algorithm Development

The derived optimal covariance design principle is integrated into a practical MPC algorithm named CoVO-MPC (Covariance-Optimal MPC).

- **Online Implementation:** At each time step, CoVO-MPC first computes the Hessian matrix D_t = ∇^2 J_t(U_in|t) around the current sampling mean U_in|t. It then uses the derived optimal covariance mapping C(D_t) (specifically the D^(-1/2) approximation from Corollary 3) to generate the sampling covariance Σ_t. This Σ_t is then used in the standard MPPI framework for trajectory sampling, weighting, and mean update.
- **Offline Approximation Variant:** To mitigate the computational cost of real-time Hessian calculation, an "offline approximation" variant is proposed. In this approach, covariance matrices Σ_t are pre-computed and cached offline for all time steps using a nominal controller to roll out trajectories. During online operation, CoVO-MPC retrieves these pre-calculated Σ_t values from the buffer, reducing online computational overhead.

#### 3.4. Empirical Validation

The proposed CoVO-MPC algorithm is empirically evaluated and compared against standard MPPI across three nonlinear robotic tasks:
- **CartPole:** A simulation environment.
- **Quadrotor Simulation:** Tracking challenging zig-zag trajectories.
- **Real-world Quadrotor:** Using a Bitcraze Crazyflie 2.1 platform for agile control tasks identical to the simulation.

Performance metrics include tracking error (cost), sampling efficiency (performance across varying numbers of samples N), and computational cost. Additionally, visualizations of sampled trajectory cost distributions and covariance matrices are used to provide insight into the algorithm's behavior. The experiments are designed to ensure a fair comparison by keeping the determinant (sampling volume) of the covariance matrices identical between CoVO-MPC and MPPI.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents both theoretical characterizations and empirical validations for sampling-based Model Predictive Control.

#### 4.1. Theoretical Contributions

- **Convergence of MPPI for Quadratic Costs:** The paper formally demonstrates that MPPI, when applied to optimization problems with a quadratic total cost (e.g., time-varying LQR systems), exhibits at least linear convergence (contraction) towards the optimal control sequence. The theoretical analysis provides a precise mathematical expression for this contraction rate, linking it to the sampling covariance matrix (Σ), the temperature parameter (λ), and the system's Hessian matrix (D). This offers a foundational understanding of MPPI's behavior in a common class of control problems.
- **Generalization to Non-Quadratic Costs:**
 - **Strongly Convex Functions:** For cost functions that are β-strongly convex, MPPI is shown to converge in probability to a neighborhood around the optimal solution. The rate of contraction is characterized as O(λ/β), with a residual error of O(√λ/β). This indicates that for sufficiently small λ relative to β, MPPI maintains effective convergence, albeit to an approximate optimum.
 - **Linear Systems with Nonlinear Residuals:** The analysis extends to linear systems featuring bounded nonlinear residual dynamics. By employing an exponential family framework, the authors establish that MPPI can still guarantee contraction, with the presence of a bounded error term. This error is related to the Lipschitz continuity of the log-partition function's derivative and the difference in Hessian matrices caused by the nonlinear residuals.
 - **General Systems:** A further generalization indicates that for systems with direct access to cost function structure, the contraction error is directly proportional to the difference between the actual Hessian and a nominal Hessian without residual dynamics.
- **Optimal Covariance Design Principle:** The theoretical analysis directly leads to a principled method for designing the sampling covariance matrix Σ. For optimal convergence, Σ's singular value decomposition is shown to share eigenvectors with the Hessian matrix D, and its eigenvalues must satisfy a specific relationship. A practical approximation, particularly relevant for small λ, proposes Σ ∝ D^(-1/2). This principle suggests adapting the sampling distribution to the local curvature of the cost function, moving away from isotropic sampling.

#### 4.2. Algorithmic Innovation and Empirical Performance

- **CoVO-MPC Algorithm:** The theoretical insights culminated in the development of CoVO-MPC, a novel sampling-based MPC algorithm that dynamically computes and schedules an optimal sampling covariance matrix based on the cost function's Hessian. This approach enables CoVO-MPC to adapt its sampling strategy to the underlying optimization landscape.
- **Significant Performance Gains:** CoVO-MPC consistently demonstrated superior performance compared to the standard MPPI algorithm across all evaluated robotic tasks (CartPole, Quadrotor simulation, and real-world Quadrotor control). Performance enhancements ranged from 43% to 54%, measured by reduced tracking errors or overall cost.
- **Effectiveness of Offline Approximation:** The "offline approximation" variant of CoVO-MPC, which pre-computes and caches covariance matrices, also outperformed standard MPPI by a substantial margin. Although its performance slightly degraded in the real-world quadrotor task due to sim-to-real gaps (22% improvement over MPPI), its consistent superiority suggests that the optimized *pattern* of the covariance matrix, rather than its real-time recalculation precision, is a primary driver of the performance gains.
- **Improved Sampling Efficiency:** CoVO-MPC exhibited higher sampling efficiency. Empirical results show that CoVO-MPC achieved better performance with fewer samples than standard MPPI, implying a more targeted and effective exploration of the control space.
- **Concentrated Cost Distributions:** Analysis of the cost distributions of sampled trajectories revealed that CoVO-MPC generates samples with a more concentrated distribution and a lower mean cost compared to MPPI. This indicates that CoVO-MPC's optimal covariance design effectively guides sampling towards more favorable regions of the control space.
- **Computational Cost Considerations:** While the online version of CoVO-MPC incurs increased computational cost due to real-time Hessian calculation, the offline approximation variant maintains a computational cost comparable to standard MPPI while still delivering performance improvements.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research significantly advances the theoretical understanding of sampling-based Model Predictive Control (MPC), addressing a previously identified gap between its empirical success and formal guarantees. By providing the first convergence analysis for Model Predictive Path Integral Control (MPPI), the paper establishes a foundational theoretical framework that characterizes the algorithm's contraction properties, particularly under quadratic and strongly convex cost functions, and extends this to systems with nonlinear residuals.

The immediate and practical impact of this theoretical work is the development of CoVO-MPC, a novel sampling-based MPC algorithm. CoVO-MPC moves beyond heuristic hyperparameter tuning by offering a principled method for optimally scheduling the sampling covariance matrix based on the cost function's Hessian. This data-driven adaptation to the optimization landscape contrasts with traditional isotropic sampling, leading to more efficient and effective exploration of the control space.

The empirical results demonstrate a substantial performance advantage, with CoVO-MPC outperforming standard MPPI by 43-54% across various robotic tasks, including complex real-world quadrotor agile control. This highlights the practical utility of the theoretical findings in enhancing control performance and robustness. Furthermore, the demonstrated effectiveness of CoVO-MPC's offline approximation variant suggests that the structural design of the covariance matrix is crucial, offering a pathway to high-performance control without a significant increase in online computational burden.

The work opens several avenues for future research. It lays the groundwork for more detailed finite-sample analysis of sampling-based MPC, considering the variance characteristics of the algorithm's output. Generalizing these results to multi-step receding-horizon settings and further integrating CoVO-MPC within advanced model-based reinforcement learning (MBRL) frameworks, leveraging learned dynamics or value functions, represents a promising direction. By bridging the gap between theoretical understanding and practical implementation, this research contributes to the development of more reliable, efficient, and intelligent control systems with broad implications for robotics, autonomous systems, and machine learning.
