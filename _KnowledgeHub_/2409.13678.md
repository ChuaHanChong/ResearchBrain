# SoloParkour: Constrained Reinforcement Learning for Visual Locomotion from Privileged Experience

> [!summary] Summary
> SoloParkour presents a two-stage constrained reinforcement learning approach for training end-to-end visual locomotion policies that enable a lightweight quadruped robot to perform agile parkour skills, such as climbing, leaping, and crawling, using only depth camera inputs. The method leverages privileged simulation experience to efficiently warm-start visual policy learning, achieving real-world performance on the Solo-12 robot comparable to more powerful platforms, while ensuring safety through explicit constraint enforcement.

## Problem

- Existing visual locomotion methods based on distilling privileged policies suffer from an "observability gap," where the visual policy cannot fully replicate behaviors learned from comprehensive simulator data due to limited onboard sensors.
- Direct end-to-end reinforcement learning from pixel inputs is computationally impractical due to the high cost of rendering depth images for every interaction step in simulation.
- Ensuring robot safety for real-world deployment, especially for lightweight robots like Solo-12, requires explicit mechanisms to prevent exceeding physical limits, which is often difficult with traditional reward-penalty methods.

## Method

- A two-stage learning process is employed: first, a privileged policy is trained with full simulator information using PPO and "Constraints as Terminations" (CaT) for safety. Second, an end-to-end visual policy is trained using a variant of DDPG, warm-started with privileged experience and an off-policy CaT formulation.
- The visual policy uses an asymmetric actor-critic architecture: the actor processes depth images, while the critic uses the simpler, cheaper-to-compute privileged state to improve training stability and speed.
- Privileged experience is used to populate a replay buffer (`D_priv`), which is sampled alongside experiences from the visual policy's own explorations (`D_online`), allowing the visual policy to adapt behaviors to its actual visual limitations rather than merely imitating.

## Results

- SoloParkour achieved performance in simulation comparable to the privileged policy and significantly outperformed all imitation-based distillation baselines on challenging terrains (hurdles, steps, crawl, leap).
- The method successfully deployed on a real Solo-12 robot, performing agile parkour skills including climbing a 40cm step (**1.5x** robot height), leaping over a 35cm gap (**78%** body length), and crawling under 20cm obstacles, without real-world fine-tuning.
- The policies maintained excellent compliance with safety constraints in both simulation and reality, with torque violations consistently under **4%** for successful trajectories, demonstrating the effectiveness of the constrained RL approach.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Warm-starting an off-policy visual RL algorithm with privileged experiences is critical for sample efficiency and learning complex behaviors, significantly outperforming learning from scratch.
> - The "observability gap" inherent in distillation methods can be overcome by allowing the visual policy to adapt its behaviors to its own sensory inputs through end-to-end RL, rather than simply imitating an 'ideal' privileged policy.
> - Integrating the "Constraints as Terminations" (CaT) framework into both on-policy and off-policy RL is crucial for training safe and robust policies that adhere to physical limits, simplifying reward design and improving sim-to-real transfer.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research sits at the intersection of reinforcement learning (RL), robot locomotion, and visuomotor control, specifically addressing the challenging domain of "robot parkour." The broader landscape has seen significant progress in training agile locomotion policies in simulation and transferring them to real robots, with dynamic skills like high-speed running, recovery from falls, jumping, and climbing now achievable. Recent works have even demonstrated parkour-like maneuvers on powerful quadruped robots like Atlas, Go-1, and Anymal, often relying on sim-to-real transfer.

However, a critical challenge in this field is the development of robust visual locomotion policies, where robots learn to navigate complex environments using onboard cameras. Traditional approaches often employ a two-stage process: first, training a "privileged policy" with full access to simulator-only information (e.g., perfect heightmaps, hidden object states), and then distilling this knowledge into a "visual policy" that only observes camera inputs (e.g., depth images) using imitation learning techniques like DAgger or Behavior Cloning.

This paper identifies a crucial limitation of these distillation-based methods: the "observability gap." Privileged information can reveal details obscured from the robot's camera (e.g., behind obstacles, outside the field of view, or due to sensor lags), meaning the visual policy might not be able to accurately replicate behaviors learned from such comprehensive data. Simply imitating the privileged policy can lead to suboptimal or unsafe behaviors when the visual input is insufficient. While direct end-to-end RL from pixels could, in theory, address this by forcing the policy to learn behaviors suited to its actual sensors, it is computationally impractical due to the high cost of rendering depth images in simulation for every interaction step.

SoloParkour addresses this precise gap by proposing a novel, computationally efficient end-to-end RL method that leverages privileged experience without simply imitating it. Instead of merely distilling behaviors, it uses privileged experience to warm-start an off-policy RL algorithm, allowing the visual policy to *adapt* and refine its behaviors based on its own sensory limitations. Furthermore, the work integrates a constrained RL framework, "Constraints as Terminations (CaT)," directly into both stages of learning. This is a significant contribution to safe locomotion, moving beyond simple reward penalties to actively prevent unsafe states, which is particularly vital for a lightweight robot like Solo-12 that is more susceptible to damage. This novel combination of constrained RL and sample-efficient visual policy learning from privileged experience sets it apart from prior works, pushing the boundaries of what is achievable with end-to-end visuomotor control, especially on resource-constrained hardware.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel and effective method for training end-to-end visual policies for agile and safe quadruped locomotion, specifically targeting "robot parkour" skills such as walking, climbing high steps, leaping over gaps, and crawling under obstacles, using only depth pixel inputs.

The motivation behind pursuing this objective is multifaceted:

- **Addressing the Parkour Challenge:** Robot parkour represents a pinnacle challenge for legged robots, demanding high agility, precision, and robust navigation in dynamic and complex environments. Successfully tackling parkour demonstrates advanced capabilities in perception, control, and coordination.
- **Overcoming Limitations of Existing Visual Locomotion Methods:** Current state-of-the-art approaches for visual locomotion, primarily based on distilling privileged policies, suffer from the "observability gap." This gap arises because privileged information available in simulation (e.g., perfect heightmaps, full environmental states) often cannot be fully reconstructed from limited onboard visual sensors (e.g., egocentric depth cameras). Consequently, distilled visual policies may struggle to replicate optimal behaviors or even generate unsafe actions when their visual inputs are insufficient.
- **Mitigating High Computational Costs:** While direct end-to-end reinforcement learning from raw pixel inputs would ideally allow policies to learn behaviors best suited to their actual sensors, the computational expense of rendering high-fidelity depth images for every simulation step makes this approach impractical for training from scratch.
- **Ensuring Robot Safety and Transferability:** For real-world robot deployment, especially with lightweight and delicate robots like Solo-12, safety is paramount. Exceeding physical limits (e.g., motor torques, joint accelerations, or causing unexpected impacts) can lead to damage. Therefore, integrating mechanisms that explicitly enforce safety constraints during learning is a critical motivation, simplifying the reward tuning process and enhancing sim-to-real transfer.
- **Pushing the Capabilities of Solo-12:** The researchers specifically aim to demonstrate these advanced parkour skills on the Solo-12, a lightweight, open-hardware quadruped robot. Solo-12 has significantly more limited torque ranges and is more prone to damage than the heavier, more powerful robots (e.g., Atlas, Go-1, Anymal) typically used in parkour experiments. This presents an additional challenge and motivation: to show that sophisticated, agile behaviors can be achieved even with constrained hardware, effectively pushing the robot to its physical limits in a safe manner.

By addressing these motivations, SoloParkour seeks to enable robots to learn intrinsically safe and adaptive visual locomotion policies that are robust to sensory limitations and efficient to train.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SoloParkour introduces a novel, two-stage reinforcement learning methodology to train end-to-end visual locomotion policies for agile and safe quadruped parkour, leveraging privileged experience while directly addressing the computational and observability challenges.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation: Constrained Reinforcement Learning (CRL)

The core of the approach formulates robot parkour as a constrained Markov Decision Process (CMDP). The objective is to maximize the discounted sum of future rewards while satisfying a set of explicit safety constraints. This is critical for robot safety, especially for the Solo-12.
- **States and Actions:** The robot's state (`s_t`) includes a history of proprioceptive measurements (joint positions, velocities, base orientation, angular velocity), previous actions, user-commanded velocity vectors, and crucially, depth images (`I_depth`). Actions (`a_t`) are desired joint position offsets, converted to torques by a low-level PD controller.
- **Rewards:** A simple, progress-based reward function (Equation 3) is used, measuring alignment with commanded velocity, encouraging forward motion and agile skills. A survival bonus is added, and rewards are clipped below zero for stability.
- **Constraints:** To ensure safe and transferable behaviors, a comprehensive set of constraints (`c_i`) is introduced, limiting physical parameters such as joint torques, velocities, and accelerations, joint position limits, action rates, and preventing unwanted contacts (e.g., knee/base collisions). Other constraints promote a specific walking style (e.g., base orientation, foot air time, heading). These are divided into "hard" (immediate termination) and "soft" (gradually enforced) constraints. The key innovation is the adoption of "Constraints as Terminations" (CaT), which reformulates the CMDP by assigning termination probabilities (`δ`) to episodes whenever a constraint is violated. This allows the RL agent to aggressively optimize for rewards within safe boundaries.

#### 3.2. Two-Stage Visual Policy Learning

The methodology is divided into two distinct stages:

**Stage 1: Privileged Policy Learning**
- **Objective:** To learn a highly capable and safe locomotion policy (`π_priv`) with access to privileged, simulator-only information.
- **Observation Space:** The `π_priv` observes a "privileged state" (`s_priv`), which includes proprioceptive measurements, velocity commands, previous actions, a height-scan map of its surroundings (a compact representation of terrain geometry), and the ceiling height (for crawling tasks). This information is "cheap-to-compute" in simulation.
- **Algorithm:** The privileged policy is parameterized by a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) and trained using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), a widely-used on-policy RL algorithm for locomotion.
- **Constraint Enforcement:** CaT is integrated with PPO to ensure `π_priv` adheres to all defined safety constraints from the outset.

**Stage 2: End-to-End Vision-Based RL from Privileged Experience**
- **Objective:** To train an end-to-end visual policy (`π`) that takes raw depth pixels as input, can adapt behaviors from the privileged policy, and adheres to safety constraints, all while circumventing the high computational cost of direct pixel-RL.
- **Core Idea:** This stage adapts Reinforcement Learning with Prior Data (RLPD) to warm-start an off-policy RL algorithm using experiences generated by the Stage 1 privileged policy.
- **Algorithm:** A variant of Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (DDPG) is employed, known for its sample efficiency. This algorithm uses a high update-to-data ratio, minimizing the number of expensive depth image renderings in simulation.
- **Dual Replay Buffers:**
 - `D_online`: Stores experiences collected by the visual policy itself.
 - `D_priv`: A "privileged experience buffer" populated by running the fully trained `π_priv` from Stage 1 through various terrains. Crucially, this buffer includes the depth image modality (`I_depth`) along with other state information.
- **Training Strategy:** During learning, batches of experience are sampled in equal proportions from both `D_online` and `D_priv`. This allows the visual policy to learn from both its own explorations and the expert demonstrations.
- **Visual Policy Architecture:** The visual actor (`π`) uses a ConvNet to process depth images, a Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) to handle observation histories (including depth embeddings), and an MLP head to output joint position offsets. Random augmentations (translation, noise, cutout) are applied to depth images during training to enhance robustness.
- **Asymmetric Actor-Critic:** To further improve training stability and speed, an asymmetric actor-critic design is used. While the visual actor observes the full state (including depth images), the critic is trained on the simpler, cheaper-to-compute privileged state (`s_priv`). This provides a more stable value estimate without the overhead of processing high-dimensional visual inputs for the critic.
- **Off-Policy CaT:** The CaT framework is extended to the off-policy setting, ensuring that the visual policy also learns to satisfy safety constraints, even when learning from a mix of its own and privileged experiences.
- **Stabilization:** Techniques like Randomized Ensembled Double Q-Learning (REDQ) and Layer Normalization are incorporated to stabilize Q-learning at high update-to-data ratios.

The entire training pipeline is conducted in the IsaacGym simulator using massively parallel environments, allowing for efficient policy learning on a single GPU. The trained policies are then directly deployed on the real Solo-12 robot for validation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides compelling evidence for the effectiveness of SoloParkour through extensive simulation studies and successful real-robot deployments.

#### 4.1. Simulation Experiments

The policies were evaluated on diverse, challenging terrains (crawl, climb, leap, hurdle) in the IsaacGym simulator, measuring terrain completion rates across increasing difficulty levels.

- **Comparison to Supervised Distillation:**
 - SoloParkour achieved performance comparable to the privileged policy itself, excelling across hurdles, steps, and crawl tracks, and showing only a marginal decrease on leap terrains.
 - Crucially, SoloParkour significantly outperformed all imitation-based distillation baselines (DAgger, Behavior Cloning (BC), and Privileged Reconstruction) on all terrains and at most difficulty levels.
 - The performance gap was particularly pronounced on complex "leap" terrains, where precise timing and forceful movements are required. DAgger and BC often failed to clear gaps or missed landings.
 - Privileged Reconstruction struggled on terrains with occlusions (crawl, climb), where the visual input could not accurately reconstruct the privileged terrain information.
 - **Conclusion:** These results underscore that end-to-end visual RL allows for a tighter coordination between vision and control, enabling the policy to adapt behaviors to its visual limitations more effectively than mere imitation or reconstruction.

- **Ablative Analysis:**
 - **"From Scratch" (visual RL without privileged experience):** This ablation performed poorly across all terrains, demonstrating significantly lower sample efficiency. This result strongly validates the critical importance of warm-starting the visual policy learning with privileged experience.
 - **"No Priv. Critic" (critic processes full depth observations):** This variant, where the critic also processes high-dimensional visual input, showed lower performance than the full SoloParkour method given the same computational budget.
 - **Conclusion:** This validates the design choice of using an asymmetric actor-critic architecture, where the critic leverages privileged information for faster and more stable learning without sacrificing the quality of the visual actor.

- **Constraint Satisfaction:**
 - Detailed analysis (Figure 5, 6, 7) showed that SoloParkour maintained excellent compliance with safety constraints (e.g., joint torques, velocities, limits, base orientation, collision avoidance). Torque violations were consistently under 4%, and base orientation violations under 10% for successful trajectories.
 - The level of constraint satisfaction was comparable to both the privileged policy and the supervised distillation baselines, indicating that the off-policy CaT mechanism effectively transferred and maintained safety.
 - **"Visual RL w/o CaT":** This ablation achieved higher terrain completion rates but at the severe cost of significantly increased constraint violations, rendering these policies unsuitable and unsafe for real-world deployment.
 - **Conclusion:** The constrained RL formulation with CaT is crucial for developing safe and robust policies, allowing for aggressive optimization of agility without compromising robot integrity.

#### 4.2. Real-Robot Experiments

The most compelling validation came from the direct deployment of the SoloParkour policy on a real Solo-12 quadruped robot, without any real-world fine-tuning.

- **Agile Parkour Skills:** The robot successfully performed a variety of challenging parkour skills with high repeatability (Table 1):
 - **Climbing:** Cleared a 40cm step (1.5 times the robot's height).
 - **Leaping:** Jumped over a 35cm gap (78% of its body length).
 - **Crawling:** Navigated under obstacles 20cm above the ground.
- **Robust Vision Integration:** The policy effectively processed real-time depth vision signals from an Intel RealSense D-405 camera, synchronously responding to obstacles.
- **Pushing Hardware Limits:** Despite Solo-12 being significantly less powerful than robots typically used in parkour experiments (e.g., 2-3 times lower thrust-to-weight ratio than Unitree Go-1), SoloParkour enabled it to achieve performances comparable to recent parkour achievements, pushing the robot to its physical limits in a controlled and safe manner.

These results demonstrate that SoloParkour effectively addresses the core challenges, enabling a lightweight, open-hardware robot to perform complex, agile visuomotor parkour skills safely and robustly in the real world.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

SoloParkour represents a significant step forward in the field of agile legged robot locomotion and visuomotor control, with several key areas of significance and potential impact:

- **Advancement in End-to-End Visual Locomotion:** The paper introduces a novel and highly effective method for training end-to-end visual policies from depth pixels to robot control commands. By leveraging privileged experience to warm-start a sample-efficient off-policy RL algorithm, SoloParkour successfully addresses the long-standing challenges of high computational costs associated with direct pixel-RL and the inherent "observability gap" limitations of imitation-based distillation methods. This approach allows the robot to truly *adapt* to its visual sensor limitations, learning optimal behaviors based on what it can actually perceive, rather than attempting to replicate actions from an "ideal" privileged teacher.
- **Enhanced Safety and Robustness through Constrained RL:** The integration of "Constraints as Terminations" (CaT) in an off-policy framework is a crucial contribution to robot safety. By explicitly defining and enforcing physical limits (e.g., joint torques, velocities, collisions) as termination conditions, the method ensures that the robot aggressively explores for agile skills without compromising its physical integrity. This not only simplifies the reward design process (avoiding tedious penalty tuning) but also leads to more reliably transferable policies from simulation to the real world, a critical factor for practical robot deployment.
- **Pushing the Boundaries of Lightweight Robotics:** The successful demonstration of complex parkour skills (climbing 1.5 times its height, leaping over 78% of its body length) on the Solo-12 robot is particularly impactful. Solo-12 is a lightweight, open-hardware platform with inherently limited actuation capabilities compared to commercial quadruped robots often used in similar research. SoloParkour shows that advanced, dynamic behaviors are not exclusively reserved for high-power, high-cost robots, thereby making cutting-edge robotics research more accessible and potentially accelerating innovation in the broader community. This performance demonstrates the method's ability to extract maximum performance from constrained hardware within safe operating limits.
- **Greater Real-World Applicability:** The ability to learn robust visual policies that transfer directly from simulation to a real robot without extensive fine-tuning highlights the method's strong potential for real-world applications. Robots equipped with such policies could autonomously navigate complex, unstructured environments that require dynamic, multi-skill locomotion, ranging from search and rescue missions to inspection in industrial settings or even agile delivery.
- **Inspiration for Future Research:** The identified limitation regarding battery current limits (which was not modeled) opens up an exciting avenue for future research: incorporating more comprehensive physical constraints into the constrained MDP framework. Furthermore, the reliance on manually constructed simulation terrains suggests future work could explore procedural or generative simulators to create even more diverse and realistic training environments. The successful use of depth vision also paves the way for investigations into more complex visual modalities like RGB-D or full RGB vision for enhanced perception.

In conclusion, SoloParkour offers a robust, safe, and computationally efficient framework that significantly advances the state-of-the-art in visuomotor control for agile legged robots. Its impact extends from enabling more sophisticated behaviors on less powerful hardware to enhancing the safety and real-world applicability of learning-based robotic systems.
