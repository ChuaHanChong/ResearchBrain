# SoloParkour: Constrained Reinforcement Learning for Visual Locomotion from Privileged Experience

> [!summary] Summary
> SoloParkour presents a two-stage constrained reinforcement learning approach for training end-to-end visual locomotion policies that enable a lightweight quadruped robot to perform agile parkour skills, such as climbing, leaping, and crawling, using only depth camera inputs. The method leverages privileged simulation experience to efficiently warm-start visual policy learning, achieving real-world performance on the Solo-12 robot comparable to more powerful platforms, while ensuring safety through explicit constraint enforcement.

## Problem

- Existing visual locomotion methods based on distilling privileged policies suffer from an "observability gap," where the visual policy cannot fully replicate behaviors learned from comprehensive simulator data due to limited onboard sensors.
- Direct end-to-end reinforcement learning from pixel inputs is computationally impractical due to the high cost of rendering depth images for every interaction step in simulation.
- Ensuring robot safety for real-world deployment, especially for lightweight robots like Solo-12, requires explicit mechanisms to prevent exceeding physical limits, which is often difficult with traditional reward-penalty methods.

## Method

- A two-stage learning process is employed: first, a privileged policy is trained with full simulator information using PPO and "Constraints as Terminations" (CaT) for safety. Second, an end-to-end visual policy is trained using a variant of DDPG, warm-started with privileged experience and an off-policy CaT formulation.
- The visual policy uses an asymmetric actor-critic architecture: the actor processes depth images, while the critic uses the simpler, cheaper-to-compute privileged state to improve training stability and speed.
- Privileged experience is used to populate a replay buffer (`D_priv`), which is sampled alongside experiences from the visual policy's own explorations (`D_online`), allowing the visual policy to adapt behaviors to its actual visual limitations rather than merely imitating.

## Results

- SoloParkour achieved performance in simulation comparable to the privileged policy and significantly outperformed all imitation-based distillation baselines on challenging terrains (hurdles, steps, crawl, leap).
- The method successfully deployed on a real Solo-12 robot, performing agile parkour skills including climbing a 40cm step (**1.5x** robot height), leaping over a 35cm gap (**78%** body length), and crawling under 20cm obstacles, without real-world fine-tuning.
- The policies maintained excellent compliance with safety constraints in both simulation and reality, with torque violations consistently under **4%** for successful trajectories, demonstrating the effectiveness of the constrained RL approach.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Warm-starting an off-policy visual RL algorithm with privileged experiences is critical for sample efficiency and learning complex behaviors, significantly outperforming learning from scratch.
> - The "observability gap" inherent in distillation methods can be overcome by allowing the visual policy to adapt its behaviors to its own sensory inputs through end-to-end RL, rather than simply imitating an 'ideal' privileged policy.
> - Integrating the "Constraints as Terminations" (CaT) framework into both on-policy and off-policy RL is crucial for training safe and robust policies that adhere to physical limits, simplifying reward design and improving sim-to-real transfer.
