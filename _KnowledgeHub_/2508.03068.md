# Hand-Eye Autonomous Delivery: Learning Humanoid Navigation, Locomotion and Reaching

> [!summary] Summary
> The Hand-Eye Autonomous Delivery (HEAD) framework, developed at Stanford University, enables humanoid robots to autonomously navigate 3D environments and interact with objects by coordinating whole-body locomotion and reaching. The system leverages diverse human data to achieve a 71% success rate in real-world object delivery tasks while demonstrating robust whole-body control tracking sparse targets (eyes, hands).

## Problem

- Humanoid robots face challenges in performing coordinated whole-body navigation and manipulation in complex 3D environments, often treating these capabilities separately.
- Learning complex, multi-modal skills (vision, locomotion, manipulation) from human demonstrations is data-intensive and difficult due to the 'embodiment gap' and 'observation gap' between humans and robots.
- Existing whole-body control methods often require dense human pose data or privileged information, hindering robust tracking of sparse, high-level commands and real-world deployment.

## Method

- A modular '**Hand-Eye Autonomous Delivery** (HEAD)' framework that decouples high-level decision-making (navigation and reaching) from low-level whole-body physical execution.
- A low-level ==Whole-Body Controller (WBC)== is developed using imitation-based Reinforcement Learning (RL) combined with a GAN-like approach, employing decoupled discriminators for upper and lower body motion to track sparse eye and hand targets.
- High-level navigation and reaching modules: the navigation module predicts camera trajectories from ego-centric images using a Transformer, trained on mixed human and robot data, while the reaching module uses a downward-facing RGB-D camera and Inverse Kinematics for precise interaction.

## Results

- The integrated HEAD system achieved a **71%** success rate in real-world object delivery tasks across varied indoor environments, demonstrating autonomous navigation and reaching.
- The low-level whole-body controller with decoupled discriminators outperformed single-discriminator approaches, achieving significantly lower tracking errors (e.g., 0.075m position, **0.120** rad orientation in Isaac Gym) and greater robustness in sim-to-sim transfer.
- The high-level navigation module showed that combining in-domain robot data with out-of-distribution human egocentric data (ADT) significantly improved generalization to novel scenes, boosting success rates in a new environment (kitchen) from **35%** to **60%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A modular architecture efficiently bridges the embodiment and observation gaps, allowing for effective utilization of heterogeneous human data (motion capture, egocentric vision) for complex humanoid skills.
> - Using decoupled discriminators for upper and lower body during RL training significantly improves whole-body controller robustness, tracking accuracy, and motion composability for humanoids.
> - Incorporating large-scale out-of-distribution human egocentric vision datasets is crucial for enhancing the navigation module's generalization to novel real-world scenes.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research by Chen et al. directly addresses several critical and evolving challenges in the broader landscape of robotics, particularly humanoid autonomy:

**A. The Rise of Humanoid Robotics:** Recent advancements in hardware have made humanoid robots more accessible for academic research. However, controlling their complex, high-degree-of-freedom bodies for practical tasks in human-centric environments remains a significant challenge. This paper contributes to the growing body of work aiming to unlock the full potential of humanoids beyond structured industrial settings.

**B. Learning from Human Data:** A central theme in modern robot learning is leveraging the abundance of human data. The paper situates itself within this trend, acknowledging the promise of large-scale human motion capture (mocap) data and egocentric vision data (e.g., from AR/VR glasses or internet videos). It addresses the inherent challenges of using such data, specifically the "embodiment gap" (differences between human and robot morphology/dynamics) and the "observation gap" (differences in sensory input). By proposing a modular system that can utilize heterogeneous human data effectively, the authors push the boundaries of data-driven robot learning. This contrasts with purely synthetic data approaches or reliance solely on robot-specific demonstrations, which can be costly and difficult to scale.

**C. Whole-Body Control (WBC):** The paper advances the state-of-the-art in WBC for humanoids. Traditional optimal control methods often require detailed kinematic trajectories, which are hard to generate and highly specific to robot kinematics. Reinforcement Learning (RL) based WBC has shown impressive results, but many existing methods track full human joint positions, demanding dense and precise human pose data. This work distinguishes itself by developing a WBC that effectively tracks *sparse* targets (eyes and hands), which are much easier to obtain from portable devices like VR headsets or AR glasses. This focus on sparse, task-space targets simplifies the human-to-robot transfer interface, a key innovation.

**D. Visual Navigation for Articulated Robots:** Conventional visual navigation research often abstracts robots as point masses operating in a 2D plane, suitable for wheeled robots. However, this simplification is insufficient for humanoids that need to navigate complex 3D spaces, stepping over obstacles, or reaching for objects at varying heights while coordinating their entire body. This paper specifically tackles 3D navigation for humanoids, addressing the limitations of 2D abstraction and integrating it seamlessly with manipulation capabilities. It builds upon but differentiates itself from prior humanoid navigation work like NaVila, which might decouple perception from locomotion and ignore full-body reaching.

**E. Modular vs. End-to-End Learning:** While end-to-end visuomotor policies are gaining traction, they often require massive, heterogeneous datasets and face significant challenges in training stability and scalability. The authors advocate for a *modular approach*, decoupling ego-centric vision perception from physical actions. This design choice is a crucial part of how their work fits in, offering a practical alternative that promotes efficient learning, allows for flexible data sources, and mitigates the complexities of training a single, unified policy for such a broad range of skills. This modularity allows them to specialize different learning components for different data types and objectives.

In summary, this paper contributes a novel, integrated framework that addresses the complex interplay of navigation, locomotion, and reaching for humanoids in human-centric environments, effectively bridging the gap between human data and robust robot capabilities through a carefully designed modular learning architecture.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of the "Hand-Eye Autonomous Delivery (HEAD)" framework is to empower humanoids to autonomously navigate through complex, human-designed 3D environments and interact with objects by reaching and touching them. This goal is driven by several specific motivations:

1. **Enabling Fundamental Manipulation:** The authors highlight that any human manipulation task begins with the ability to move close to a target object for perception and interaction. Therefore, a primary objective is to equip humanoids with this fundamental skill: delivering their end-effectors (hands) and cameras (eyes) to the required locations in a coordinated manner.

2. **Overcoming Integration Challenges:** Existing approaches often combine separate navigation methods (for mobile robots, often simplified as 2D point masses) with manipulation methods (for manipulators, often spatially isolated from locomotion). This leads to control strategies that lack seamless coordination between upper-body manipulation and lower-body locomotion, resulting in disjointed and unnatural behavior. The paper's objective is to achieve concurrent navigation, locomotion, and reaching in a fully coordinated way, leveraging the humanoid's morphology.

3. **Efficient Learning from Heterogeneous Human Data:** Learning complex, multi-modal skills (vision, locomotion, manipulation) end-to-end from human demonstrations is data-intensive and challenging, requiring heterogeneous data involving both egocentric vision and full-body motion. The authors aim to overcome this by adopting a modular approach that decouples perception from physical actions. This allows them to use different sources and types of human data (e.g., large-scale motion capture for whole-body control, Aria glasses data for visual navigation) and different algorithms for distinct components, promoting efficient learning and scalability.

4. **Robust Whole-Body Control with Sparse Inputs:** Training a whole-body controller for humanoids faces specific hurdles:
 - **Sparse Targets:** Unlike full-body tracking, they aim to control the entire humanoid based on only three sparse points (eyes, left hand, right hand).
 - **Diverse Skill Coordination:** The upper and lower body need to perform different tasks simultaneously (e.g., navigating while preparing to reach).
 - **Real-world Observability:** Avoiding reliance on privileged information like global root position and velocity, which are difficult to obtain accurately in real-world deployments.
 The objective is to develop a robust low-level policy that addresses these challenges, facilitating effective sim-to-real transfer.

5. **Achieving 3D Navigation:** Unlike robots constrained to 2D movement, humanoids need to navigate 3D spaces, interacting with objects at various heights and navigating around obstacles. A key motivation is to enable this full 3D navigation capability through coordinated whole-body movements.

In essence, the research aims to develop a practical and robust framework for complex humanoid behaviors by intelligently decomposing the problem, leveraging diverse human data, and designing specialized learning components that work in harmony.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The HEAD framework adopts a **modular approach**, decoupling high-level decision-making (navigation and reaching) from low-level physical execution (whole-body control). This modularity is designed to promote efficient learning and scalability by allowing different components to be trained with distinct data sources and algorithms.

**A. System Architecture:**
HEAD consists of two main layers:
1. **High-Level Policy:** Commands target positions and orientations for the humanoid's eyes and hands. This policy itself is composed of two modules: Navigation and Reaching.
2. **Low-Level Policy (Whole-Body Controller):** Controls the humanoid's full-body movements to track the commanded eye and hand targets.

**B. Low-Level Whole-Body Controller (WBC):**
- **Objective:** Learn to track the 6D poses (position and orientation) of three key points: the eyes, left hand, and right hand, while exhibiting human-like whole-body movements.
- **Control Mechanism:** Controls the humanoid via PD servos.
- **Training Paradigm:** Imitation-based Reinforcement Learning (RL) combined with a GAN-like approach. This allows the policy to imitate the *distribution* of human demonstrations rather than strictly tracking specific full-body trajectories, while simultaneously performing goal-directed target tracking.
- **Human Motion Dataset:** A 5-hour dataset curated by retargeting large-scale human motion capture data (AMASS and OMOMO) to the Unitree G1 robot. This dataset encompasses diverse manipulation and locomotion behaviors.
- **Key Design Choices for Robustness and Real-world Deployment:**
 - **Deployable Observation Space:** The policy's observation vector consists only of robot link poses and joint velocities in the robot's local coordinate frame, avoiding reliance on global root position or velocity, which are difficult to obtain in the real world.
 - **Decoupled Motion Imitation:** Instead of a single discriminator for whole-body motion, two separate discriminators are employed to reward upper and lower body groups independently. This promotes composability, allowing the policy to learn combinations of poses from different body parts (e.g., walking while manipulating) and preventing entanglement of irrelevant motions.
 - **Sparse Target Tracking:** Target inputs to the policy are represented as relative transformations (e.g., `p_tar_eye - p_eye`), avoiding global coordinates. Goal-directed rewards are defined based on these relative errors.
 - **Sim-to-Real Considerations:** Regularization terms (e.g., penalizing in-air time, sliding, and energy consumption) and extensive domain randomization (dynamics parameters, sensor noise, action delay) are used during training to improve robustness for real-world transfer.
- **Optimization:** A multi-objective learning framework balances imitation objectives (from discriminators) and the goal-directed tracking objective (manual reward function) using PPO.

**C. High-Level Navigation Module:**
- **Objective:** Guide the robot toward a goal specified as a 2D point in its initial ego-centric RGB image, predicting a future eye (camera) trajectory (position and orientation).
- **Architecture:** Input image (I_t) and tracked 2D goal (g_t, using an off-the-shelf point tracker) are processed. DINO features are extracted from the image, positional embeddings are added to the goal, and these are fed into a Transformer decoder to predict a sequence of future camera transformations (C_t:t+T) relative to the previous frame.
- **Data Collection (Human Data):** An automatic method using Aria Glasses collects goal-conditioned human training data (images, future camera trajectories, 2D goals approximated via gaze and static point clouds).
- **Domain Shift Mitigation:**
 - **Generalization to New Scenes:** Incorporates the large-scale egocentric dataset Aria Digital Twin (ADT) (400 minutes of diverse indoor activities) converted into goal-conditioned data.
 - **Visual Embodiment Gap:** Applies undistortion and homography transforms to Aria Glasses images to virtually match the robot's narrower camera view and pitch angle.
 - **Kinematic Embodiment Gap (Speed):** Robot videos are subsampled as robots move slower than humans.
 - **Co-training:** The navigation module is trained with a mix of human data (Aria Glasses, ADT) and a small amount of robot-specific data (collected by commanding the robot to navigate and recording head poses with a mocap system).
 - **Shared Decoder Branch:** A single decoding branch is shared for both human and robot data during training, found to improve scene generalization for navigation.

**D. High-Level Reaching Module:**
- **Objective:** Handle the final precise approach to touch an object once the robot is near.
- **Camera System:** Uses a second, downward-facing RGB-D camera with a narrower field-of-view (FoV) for reaching.
- **Navigation-Reaching Transition:** Control switches from navigation to reaching when the target object becomes visible in the downward-looking RGB-D camera and is within arm's reach. The goal is transferred to the RGB-D frame using correspondence matching.
- **Action Generation:** The reaching module approximates the goal as a 3D hand position and uses Inverse Kinematics (IK) (Mink) to infer the full 6D poses for the hands and head for the low-level policy. IK optimization is initialized from the current robot state, with objectives to ensure smooth transitions and natural postures (small changes in CoM and pelvis orientation).

**E. Hardware Setup:** The system is deployed on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot. A wide-angle USB webcam is used for navigation, and the G1's built-in Realsense D435 RGB-D camera is used for reaching. All computations run on a PC with an RTX4090 GPU and i9-14900K CPU, controlling the robot via Ethernet.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of the HEAD system was conducted in both simulation and the real world, assessing the overall system and individual components.

**A. Whole-Body Reaching with Different Scenes (Real-World Deployment):**
- **Overall Success Rate:** The integrated HEAD system achieved a **71% success rate** in reaching novel objects placed at different locations and heights across three distinct layouts in two different indoor environments (a lab and a kitchen).
- **Environment Impact:** Success rate was higher in the lab room (training environment) than in the kitchen (deployment environment). The kitchen's narrower corridors and reflective surfaces posed greater challenges for the goal tracker, navigation transformer, and transition module.
- **Failure Analysis:** Identified common failure modes:
 - **Motion Blur:** Robot movement could cause the point tracker to lose or misidentify the target.
 - **Aggressive Paths:** Human data, which often includes faster and more aggressive movements, sometimes led to robot collisions when those paths were not feasible for the humanoid.

**B. Performance of Whole-Body Controller (Simulation):**
- **Effectiveness of GAN-like RL:** Guiding RL exploration with GANs significantly improved sample efficiency compared to policies trained without discriminators, which showed very high failure rates (97-99%).
- **Advantage of Decoupled Discriminators:** Using *two separate discriminators* for upper and lower body motion (as in the "Ours" method) substantially outperformed a single whole-body discriminator.
 - **Improved Tracking Accuracy:** Ours achieved significantly lower positional and orientation errors in both Isaac Gym (0.075m, 0.120 rad) and MuJoCo (0.153m, 0.326 rad) compared to a single discriminator (Isaac Gym: 0.149m, 0.169 rad; MuJoCo: 0.525m, 1.015 rad).
 - **Enhanced Robustness and Composability:** This decoupling prevented the policy from entangling irrelevant motions (e.g., arm-swing patterns during walking) and enabled the controller to produce more diverse and composable motions, better handling unseen tasks like carrying a box while walking.
- **Sim-to-Sim Transfer:** The decoupled discriminator approach showed significantly greater robustness when transferring from Isaac Gym to MuJoCo, a more realistic contact simulation environment.

**C. Performance of Navigation Module (Open-Loop Prediction):**
- **Importance of Robot Data:** Training solely with in-domain human data (H-Lab) resulted in poor success rates (0.14 SR) due to the embodiment gap. In-domain robot data (R-Lab) was essential for achieving reliable performance (0.71 SR).
- **Benefits of Combined Data:** Combining R-Lab data with either in-domain human data (H-Lab) or large-scale out-of-distribution human data (ADT, 'O') further improved performance in the lab room.
- **Generalization to Novel Scenes (Kitchen):**
 - Training only with R-Lab data generalized poorly to the new deploy room (kitchen), resulting in a low success rate (0.35 SR).
 - Crucially, incorporating the **out-of-distribution ADT dataset ('O')** significantly boosted success rates in the deploy room (from 0.35 to 0.60 SR for the "Ours" configuration), despite not making a huge difference in the training room. This highlights ADT's importance for cross-scene generalization when robot training data is unavailable.
 - Adding in-domain human data from the kitchen (+ H-Kit) also helped improve performance in that specific environment.
- **Shared Decoder Branch:** The chosen architecture, which shares a single decoding branch for both human and robot navigation data, was found to improve scene generalization, suggesting that for short-term navigation, embodiment differences might be less critical than for manipulation tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The HEAD framework represents a significant step forward in enabling humanoids to operate autonomously and effectively in human environments. Its potential impact spans several key areas:

1. **Advancing Humanoid Autonomy in Unstructured Environments:** By demonstrating robust navigation, locomotion, and reaching capabilities in complex, human-designed indoor spaces, this work moves humanoids closer to practical applications in homes, offices, and warehouses. It tackles the fundamental challenge of making humanoids truly useful by allowing them to perceive and interact with objects in a coordinated, human-like manner.

2. **Effective Utilization of Diverse Human Data:** The paper provides a compelling methodology for leveraging various forms of human data—from high-fidelity motion capture to egocentric video from AR glasses and large-scale public datasets—despite inherent embodiment and observation gaps. This modular strategy for data integration and learning is a valuable blueprint for future research in robot learning from human demonstrations, potentially reducing the need for expensive and time-consuming robot-specific data collection.

3. **Robust and Deployable Whole-Body Control:** The development of a whole-body controller that can track sparse, high-level commands (head and hands) and operates without relying on privileged world-coordinate information is crucial for real-world deployment. The innovations in using decoupled discriminators and addressing sim-to-real transfer considerations make this WBC highly practical and robust, offering a foundation for complex, dynamic humanoid behaviors.

4. **Integrated 3D Navigation and Manipulation:** Unlike prior works that often treat navigation and manipulation as separate problems, HEAD successfully integrates these skills into a seamless, coordinated whole-body control strategy. This is essential for humanoids, which require intricate coordination between their upper and lower bodies to navigate obstacles and interact with objects at varying heights and locations in 3D space.

5. **Modular Learning for Scalability and Efficiency:** The modular design of HEAD is a key takeaway. It demonstrates that decomposing a complex problem into specialized, learnable components can be more efficient and scalable than attempting an end-to-end approach, especially when dealing with heterogeneous data and the complexities of humanoid morphology. This approach allows for easier debugging, component-specific optimization, and potential transfer of learned modules to new tasks or robots.

6. **Future Directions for Humanoid Robotics:** The paper also implicitly sets the stage for future research. The authors acknowledge limitations, such as the robot's conservative approach to navigation and the ambiguity in lower-body control from sparse head and wrist poses. This suggests future work could focus on more fine-grained whole-body navigation with collision avoidance, integrating additional egocentric information (like foot pose), and developing more general grasping frameworks.

In conclusion, "Hand-Eye Autonomous Delivery" presents a practical, robust, and intelligently designed framework that significantly advances the capabilities of humanoid robots. Its modular architecture, innovative use of human data, and focus on deployable whole-body control offer valuable insights and tools for the ongoing quest to enable humanoids to seamlessly integrate into human-centric environments.
