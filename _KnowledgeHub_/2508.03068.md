# Hand-Eye Autonomous Delivery: Learning Humanoid Navigation, Locomotion and Reaching

> [!summary] Summary
> The Hand-Eye Autonomous Delivery (HEAD) framework, developed at Stanford University, enables humanoid robots to autonomously navigate 3D environments and interact with objects by coordinating whole-body locomotion and reaching. The system leverages diverse human data to achieve a 71% success rate in real-world object delivery tasks while demonstrating robust whole-body control tracking sparse targets (eyes, hands).

## Problem

- Humanoid robots face challenges in performing coordinated whole-body navigation and manipulation in complex 3D environments, often treating these capabilities separately.
- Learning complex, multi-modal skills (vision, locomotion, manipulation) from human demonstrations is data-intensive and difficult due to the 'embodiment gap' and 'observation gap' between humans and robots.
- Existing whole-body control methods often require dense human pose data or privileged information, hindering robust tracking of sparse, high-level commands and real-world deployment.

## Method

- A modular '**Hand-Eye Autonomous Delivery** (HEAD)' framework that decouples high-level decision-making (navigation and reaching) from low-level whole-body physical execution.
- A low-level ==Whole-Body Controller (WBC)== is developed using imitation-based Reinforcement Learning (RL) combined with a GAN-like approach, employing decoupled discriminators for upper and lower body motion to track sparse eye and hand targets.
- High-level navigation and reaching modules: the navigation module predicts camera trajectories from ego-centric images using a Transformer, trained on mixed human and robot data, while the reaching module uses a downward-facing RGB-D camera and Inverse Kinematics for precise interaction.

## Results

- The integrated HEAD system achieved a **71%** success rate in real-world object delivery tasks across varied indoor environments, demonstrating autonomous navigation and reaching.
- The low-level whole-body controller with decoupled discriminators outperformed single-discriminator approaches, achieving significantly lower tracking errors (e.g., 0.075m position, **0.120** rad orientation in Isaac Gym) and greater robustness in sim-to-sim transfer.
- The high-level navigation module showed that combining in-domain robot data with out-of-distribution human egocentric data (ADT) significantly improved generalization to novel scenes, boosting success rates in a new environment (kitchen) from **35%** to **60%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A modular architecture efficiently bridges the embodiment and observation gaps, allowing for effective utilization of heterogeneous human data (motion capture, egocentric vision) for complex humanoid skills.
> - Using decoupled discriminators for upper and lower body during RL training significantly improves whole-body controller robustness, tracking accuracy, and motion composability for humanoids.
> - Incorporating large-scale out-of-distribution human egocentric vision datasets is crucial for enhancing the navigation module's generalization to novel real-world scenes.
