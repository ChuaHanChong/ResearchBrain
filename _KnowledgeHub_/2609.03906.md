# Revisiting Topological Graphs for Macro Action based Closed-loop Reinforcement Learning of Vision Language Navigation in Continuous Environment

> [!summary] Summary
> The paper reformulates vision-language navigation in continuous environments as a hierarchical MDP: a Graph Transformer high-level policy picks frontier nodes on a topological graph while a training-free rotate-then-forward controller executes them. This compresses hundreds of steps to 5-20, making closed-loop PPO fine-tuning with an action-aware value head tractable and reaching state-of-the-art on R2R-CE and RxR-CE.

## Problem

- Behavior cloning suffers distribution shift, and DAgger assumes an oracle expert whose actions turn ambiguous once the agent deviates from the reference trajectory.
- Applying RL directly over micro motor actions spans hundreds of steps, so sparse terminal rewards give uninformative credit assignment and poor sample efficiency.
- Prior RL fine-tuning either trains open-loop on offline single-turn outputs or leans on distance-based dense rewards that reward greedy shortcuts over instruction fidelity.

## Method

- ==Hierarchical MDP==: a macro MDP over unvisited frontier nodes plus STOP, with the micro MDP low-level heuristic controller serving as its state-transition function.
- ==Action-aware value head==: a learnable query attention-pools the full graph including frontier tokens, sharing the ==cross-modal backbone== so the critic sees dead-end topology.
- ==Graph-based PPO== with outcome-driven terminal reward summing Success, SPL and nDTW, ==KL-regularized== against the ==DAgger== reference, after pretraining and DAgger value warm-up.

## Results

- Reinforcement fine-tuning lifts R2R-CE Val-Unseen success from **65.1%** to **68.1%** and SPL **53.5%** to **57.3%**; RxR-CE reaches **62.3%** success and **66.8%** nDTW at **0.5B** parameters.
- Ablations: history-only critic gives only **+1.1** success versus **+3.0** and costs **25** versus **16** hours; PPO beats SAC (**+2.1**) and GRPO (**+1.7**); dense rewards and multi-epoch updates both underperform.
- A matched micro-action baseline gains **+0.6** success after **120** hours of closed-loop fine-tuning, and zero from open-loop; RFT adds a stable **+6.2** to **+7.0** across four pretraining data scales.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Choosing the right action abstraction, not the RL algorithm, is what makes closed-loop fine-tuning viable on long-horizon embodied tasks.
> - Critics over dynamic candidate action sets must observe the candidates themselves; conditioning only on history leaves the value function partially observable.
> - Dense distance-shaped rewards quietly trade instruction fidelity for geometric shortcuts, and reusing batches over-optimizes pretrained cross-modal representations onto local noise.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Vision-Language Navigation in Continuous Environments (VLN-CE) tasks an autonomous agent with interpreting natural language instructions to navigate through unseen, continuous 3D environments. This domain presents significant challenges, primarily related to distribution shift between training and test conditions, error accumulation over long navigation horizons, and the inherent complexity of decision-making in large action spaces.

Existing approaches to VLN-CE can be broadly categorized into two main paradigms. The first involves graph-based methods, which abstract the continuous environment into a topological graph of navigable waypoints or nodes. These methods define "macro actions" as the selection of a frontier node, thereby compressing the decision horizon and reducing the impact of error accumulation. The second paradigm leverages Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs), which, empowered by internet-scale pre-training, exhibit strong semantic priors and generalization capabilities. However, LVLM-based methods typically operate on the original "micro action" space (e.g., move forward, turn left/right, stop), which can still lead to long decision horizons.

Both paradigms commonly employ Imitation Learning (IL), often augmented with DAgger, to tackle distribution shift. However, DAgger's reliance on an expert oracle faces limitations in closed-loop navigation; expert actions can become ambiguous when the agent deviates from the reference trajectory, suggesting a mismatch between IL and autonomous exploration in such dynamic scenarios. Reinforcement Learning (RL) offers a more natural solution by learning directly from task outcomes, but its direct application to VLN-CE with micro action spaces is often sample-inefficient due to reward sparsity and the extensive decision horizons (hundreds of steps). Prior attempts to apply RL in VLN-CE have frequently resorted to restrictive workarounds, such as constraining optimization to single-turn language outputs with offline datasets (open-loop RL) or employing distance heuristics as dense reward shaping. These compromises, while facilitating some learning, often bypass genuine autonomous exploration and limit the full potential of RL.

This research positions itself within this landscape by addressing the bottleneck of sample-efficient, closed-loop RL in VLN-CE. It specifically revisits and extends the structural abstraction provided by topological graphs. By reformulating VLN-CE as a hierarchical Markov Decision Process (MDP), the authors aim to decouple high-level planning from low-level control, thereby making closed-loop RL tractable on a significantly compressed macro action space. This approach seeks to overcome the limitations of both traditional IL and current RL applications in VLN-CE, while also benefiting from the strengths of graph-based environment abstraction.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop a robust and sample-efficient framework for closed-loop Reinforcement Learning (RL) in Vision-Language Navigation in Continuous Environments (VLN-CE) by leveraging a macro action space. The authors aim to overcome the challenges associated with applying RL directly to micro action spaces in long-horizon navigation tasks.

The motivation for this research stems from several identified limitations in existing VLN-CE methodologies:

1. **Distribution Shift and Error Accumulation:** In continuous, unseen environments, agents trained via Imitation Learning (IL) often suffer from distribution shift, where errors accumulate over a long sequence of micro actions, leading to task failure. While DAgger attempts to mitigate this, expert supervision becomes ambiguous when the agent deviates from the pre-defined optimal path, undermining the effectiveness of IL in truly closed-loop scenarios.
2. **Sample Inefficiency and Credit Assignment in Micro-Action RL:** Directly applying RL to the hundreds of micro actions required for VLN-CE results in an extremely long decision horizon and sparse terminal rewards. This exacerbates the credit assignment problem, making RL training highly sample-inefficient and often intractable without restrictive reward shaping or offline training paradigms. Such workarounds (e.g., using distance heuristics as dense rewards) can lead to agents exploiting greedy shortcuts that do not align with natural language instructions, or limit the learning to open-loop settings without environmental interaction.
3. **Need for Tractable Closed-Loop RL:** The authors hypothesize that a naturally occurring, closed-loop RL paradigm is essential for learning truly robust navigation policies that can adapt to errors and generalize effectively. However, previous attempts at closed-loop RL in VLN-CE have struggled with the aforementioned sample efficiency and credit assignment issues when operating on micro action spaces.
4. **Leveraging Topological Abstraction:** Topological graphs have demonstrated effectiveness in compressing the decision horizon for VLN-CE by abstracting environments into navigable nodes and macro actions. The authors are motivated to explore whether this structural abstraction can be leveraged to make closed-loop RL sufficiently sample-efficient and tractable.

In summary, the key motivation is to bridge the gap between the theoretical benefits of closed-loop RL and its practical applicability in VLN-CE by introducing a hierarchical decision-making framework that leverages macro actions in a topological graph representation, thereby making RL optimization feasible and effective.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology reformulates the VLN-CE task as a Hierarchical Markov Decision Process (MDP), which decouples high-level planning from low-level control. This structure enables the application of Reinforcement Learning (RL) to a compressed macro action space.

**Problem Formulation: A Hierarchical MDP Perspective**
- **High-Level Planner (Macro MDP):** This layer addresses the long-horizon task by operating on a topological graph.
 - **State ($S_H$):** A macro state $s_H_t$ is defined by the natural language instruction ($I$) and a holistic topological graph ($G_t$). $G_t$ includes both explored nodes ($H_t$, representing the agent's history) and dynamically generated unvisited frontier nodes ($A_H_t$, representing potential next macro actions).
 - **Action ($A_H$):** A macro action $a_H_t$ involves selecting one of the unvisited frontier nodes as a subgoal or choosing a special STOP action.
 - **Transition ($T_H$):** Transitions are implicitly managed by the low-level controller, which executes the chosen macro action and updates the graph with new observations.
 - **Reward ($R_H$) and Discount ($\gamma_H$):** The agent receives an outcome-based terminal reward ($R_T$) at the episode's end, evaluating the final trajectory's success, path efficiency, and instruction fidelity.
- **Low-Level Controller (Micro MDP):** This layer handles short-horizon routing to execute a selected high-level frontier.
 - **State ($S_L$):** A micro state $s_L_k$ consists of the current panoramic observation and the agent's relative pose to the assigned macro subgoal.
 - **Action ($A_L$):** The micro action space consists of primitive motor commands (move forward, turn left, turn right, stop).
 - **Controller:** A deterministic heuristic policy, specifically a "rotate-then-forward with tryout strategy," is employed to navigate to the selected frontier, avoiding the need for low-level RL training.

**Closed-loop RFT via Graph-based PPO**
The core of the RL optimization is a closed-loop Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT) paradigm using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), chosen for its on-policy nature and ability to provide fine-grained, step-level credit assignment.

- **Action-Aware Value Estimation:** To address the dynamic nature of the macro action space, a novel Action-Aware Value Head is proposed. Instead of predicting the state value solely based on historical context, the Critic network evaluates the state $s_H_t = (I, G_t)$, where $G_t$ explicitly includes the current set of frontier nodes. This allows the Critic to account for available future paths, distinguishing topological dead-ends from well-connected hubs. The Actor and Critic networks share a common cross-modal representation backbone for efficiency.
- **Outcome-Driven Reward and GAE:** The terminal reward function ($R_T$) is a composite of three metrics:
 - **Success:** Binary indicator for reaching the target within 1.5m.
 - **SPL (Success weighted by Path Length):** Penalizes inefficient paths.
 - **nDTW (normalized Dynamic Time Warping):** Measures path fidelity to the reference trajectory, providing a positive signal even in failed episodes.
 Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE) is used to compute advantages from Temporal Difference (TD) errors, which then guide the PPO objective for updating the shared-backbone network.

**Hierarchical Policy Architecture**
The system comprises an online graph constructor, a multimodal high-level planner, and a heuristic low-level controller.

- **Topological Graph Construction:** An online graph $G_t = (V_t, E_t)$ is built. $V_t$ includes explored nodes (agent's path) and unvisited frontier nodes (potential waypoints). A waypoint predictor extracts navigable locations as frontiers, pruning redundant ones. Explored nodes store panoramic RGB-D images, while frontier nodes inherit features from their parent explored nodes.
- **Modality Encoding:**
 - **Language:** Instructions are tokenized and processed by a BERT-style encoder.
 - **Visual:** 12 panoramic RGBD views for each explored node are processed by a CLIP-pretrained ViT-B/32 (RGB) and a DD-PPO-pretrained ResNet-50 (Depth), then compressed into four directional tokens via attention pooling. Frontier nodes are encoded as single token vectors.
 - **Embeddings:** Step and pose embeddings are added to capture chronological order and relative spatial orientation.
- **Graph-Aware Cross-Modal Fusion:** Encoded language ($I$) and graph ($G$) features are fed into a Cross-Modal Fusion Network. This network employs cross-attention between modalities and Graph-Aware Self-Attention (GASA) within graph features. GASA incorporates a spatial bias matrix ($M$) based on topological path distance and relative orientation, explicitly capturing node relationships. Navigational cues from text are distilled to enhance graph representation.
- **Dual-Head Decision and Low-Level Control:**
 - **Actor Head:** A Feed-Forward Network (FFN) scores frontier and STOP tokens from the refined graph representation ($G_{out}$), yielding macro action logits.
 - **Value Head:** A learnable query performs attention pooling over $G_{out}$ to compress the topological graph into a single vector, which an FFN then projects to the scalar state value ($V_\pi(s_H_t)$), implementing the Action-Aware Value Estimation.
 - **Low-Level Controller:** Executes the selected frontier using the "rotate-then-forward with tryout" strategy.

**Training Paradigm**
A three-stage curriculum is adopted:
1. **Pretraining:** Initial visual-linguistic alignment and navigational priors are established using a unified dataset from multiple sources (Prevalent, Prevalent LVLM Aug, RxR-Marky, R2R train, RxR train).
2. **DAgger with Value Warm-up:** The pre-trained model is fine-tuned using DAgger for imitation. Crucially, the Action-Aware Value Head is simultaneously warmed up using a joint loss combining cross-entropy for imitation and a clipped MSE loss for value prediction.
3. **Closed-Loop RFT:** PPO optimization is applied to the high-level policy. The PPO objective includes policy and value losses, and a KL divergence regularization term against the DAgger-trained reference model to prevent policy degradation. Visual encoders are frozen, while other modules are trainable throughout.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research demonstrates the effectiveness of applying closed-loop Reinforcement Learning (RL) to a macro action space for Vision-Language Navigation in Continuous Environments (VLN-CE).

**State-of-the-Art Performance:**
- The proposed model, particularly after Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT), achieves state-of-the-art performance on both R2R-CE and RxR-CE Val-Unseen benchmarks.
- On R2R-CE, the RFT stage boosts the Success Rate (SR) from 65.1% (DAgger baseline) to 68.1% and Success weighted by Path Length (SPL) from 53.5% to 57.3%. The Navigation Error (NE) decreases from 4.06 to 3.89.
- On RxR-CE, similar improvements are observed, with SR reaching 62.3%, SPL 50.2%, and normalized Dynamic Time Warping (nDTW) reaching 66.8%.
- These results surpass both other graph-based IL baselines (e.g., HNR) and contemporary LVLM-based approaches (e.g., StreamVLN).

**Ablation Studies on the RL Framework:**
- **Action-Aware Value Head:** The explicit incorporation of the dynamic macro action space into the Critic's input (Action-Aware Value Head) is shown to be crucial. Compared to a "History-only Value Critic" (which cannot see generated frontiers), the Action-Aware design yields significantly higher performance gains (+3.0 ∆SR vs. +1.1 ∆SR) and is more computationally efficient (approximately 16 hours vs. 25 hours for RFT). This confirms its role in accurately evaluating topological states and penalizing dead ends.
- **RL Algorithm Comparison:** PPO is identified as the most effective algorithm within the proposed architecture.
 - PPO achieves a +3.0 ∆SR and +3.8 ∆SPL over the DAgger baseline.
 - Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) shows moderate gains (+1.7 ∆SR, +2.3 ∆SPL), limited by its lack of a critic for step-level credit assignment.
 - A discrete variant of Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) performs better than GRPO (+2.1 ∆SR, +2.6 ∆SPL) due to its Q-networks, but is outperformed by PPO. SAC's limitations include issues with stale data from off-policy replay buffers, which destabilize cross-modal representations, and the computational overhead of maintaining target networks.
- **Reward Formulation:** An outcome-driven sparse reward (Success + SPL + nDTW) is found to be more effective than a "Dense Soft Reward" based on geodesic distance reduction. Dense rewards can lead the agent to exploit greedy geometric shortcuts, potentially deviating from instruction fidelity.
- **Update Epochs:** Performing "Strict On-Policy Updates" (one optimization epoch per collected batch) consistently outperforms multi-epoch updates. Reusing the same batch for multiple gradient steps leads to overfitting of the cross-modal backbone to local environmental noise, degrading generalizable priors.

**Comparison with Micro Action Space Paradigms:**
- A baseline using an identical visual-linguistic backbone, but operating on micro action space, was developed for comparison.
- **Open-Loop RFT:** When trained offline on fixed datasets with a time-decayed reward, open-loop RFT on micro actions yields no performance gains over the DAgger baseline, instead showing signs of overfitting.
- **Closed-Loop RFT:** Closed-loop RFT on micro actions, despite a significantly longer training time (approximately 120 hours compared to 16 hours for the macro-action RFT), achieves only marginal gains (+0.6 SR). This is attributed to the drastically extended decision horizon, which exacerbates credit assignment and renders sparse terminal rewards uninformative.
- **Advantage of Macro Action Space:** These comparisons validate that macro action spaces are a prerequisite for effective and sample-efficient closed-loop RL in VLN-CE, by compressing the decision horizon from hundreds to typically 5-20 steps.

**Orthogonality of RFT and Dataset Scaling:**
- Experiments investigating RFT's interaction with pretraining dataset scale reveal that RFT provides a consistent absolute performance boost (∆Total navigation performance between +6.2 and +7.0) regardless of the initial competency of the DAgger-trained base model.
- This suggests that RFT and dataset scaling offer orthogonal performance gains. RFT addresses a fundamental mismatch between DAgger's supervision signals and instruction fidelity, a structural limitation of the IL paradigm that cannot be resolved solely by increasing pretraining data.

**Case Study:**
- A case study demonstrates the RFT model's superior error recovery capabilities. In a situation of initial heading ambiguity, the RFT model recognized the instruction-trajectory mismatch and successfully leveraged its topological memory to backtrack to a correct path, exhibiting a sharp action distribution for the corrective action. The DAgger model, in contrast, failed to recover.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant contribution to the field of Vision-Language Navigation in Continuous Environments (VLN-CE) and potentially to embodied AI in general. Its primary significance lies in demonstrating a tractable and effective method for applying closed-loop Reinforcement Learning (RL) to a complex, long-horizon task that has historically struggled with RL's sample efficiency and credit assignment challenges.

**Key areas of significance and potential impact include:**

1. **Enabling Tractable Closed-Loop RL for VLN-CE:** By reformulating VLN-CE as a Hierarchical MDP and leveraging topological graphs to create a macro action space, the work effectively compresses the decision horizon. This makes RL optimization practical and sample-efficient, moving beyond the limitations of open-loop RL or dense reward shaping, which often compromise true autonomous exploration and instruction fidelity. This framework could unlock the full potential of RL for robust, adaptable navigation.
2. **Novel Architectural Contributions:** The introduction of the Action-Aware Value Head is a specific architectural innovation that addresses the challenge of dynamic action spaces in graph-based navigation. By allowing the critic to explicitly observe the available frontier nodes, it enhances state value estimation and improves the learning process without incurring substantial computational overhead. This design principle could be applicable to other RL problems with dynamic action sets.
3. **Advancement in Navigation Performance:** The achievement of state-of-the-art performance on challenging benchmarks like R2R-CE and RxR-CE indicates the practical efficacy of the proposed hierarchical RL framework. This pushes the boundaries of current navigation capabilities, particularly in unseen environments with complex language instructions.
4. **Differentiated Role of RL vs. Imitation Learning (IL):** The ablation studies and comparison with micro action space paradigms provide valuable insights into the fundamental limitations of IL and the distinct advantages of closed-loop RL. The finding that RFT offers orthogonal performance gains to dataset scaling suggests that RL addresses structural flaws in IL's supervision (e.g., mismatch between expert actions and instruction fidelity) that cannot be resolved merely by larger datasets. This clarifies the complementary roles of pre-training, imitation, and reinforcement learning in embodied agents.
5. **Foundation for Future Embodied AI Research:** The approach of decoupling high-level planning from low-level control using a hierarchical MDP and macro actions is a generalizable paradigm. It could inspire similar solutions for other long-horizon embodied AI tasks, such as robotic manipulation, exploration, or complex task execution, where sparse rewards and extensive action spaces are common challenges.
6. **Towards Real-World Deployment:** While the current low-level controller is heuristic, the paper identifies future work in replacing it with robust local navigation algorithms for physical robot deployment. By making high-level planning more robust through RL, this research contributes a crucial component towards building more capable and generalizable navigation systems for real-world robotics.

In summary, this research not only delivers a high-performing VLN-CE agent but also offers significant methodological and theoretical contributions that could broadly impact the development of intelligent embodied agents.
