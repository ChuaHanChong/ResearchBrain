# ReTac-ACT: A State-Gated Vision-Tactile Fusion Transformer for Precision Assembly

> [!summary] Summary
> ReTac-ACT extends Action Chunking with Transformers into a vision-tactile imitation policy for precision assembly. Separate visual and tactile encoders feed bidirectional cross-attention, then a proprioception-conditioned gate reciprocally reweights modalities so the policy shifts from vision-led approach to tactile-led insertion, while an auxiliary tactile reconstruction loss preserves contact geometry. It reaches 90% peg-in-hole success and 80% at 0.1 mm clearance.

## Problem

- Vision-only imitation policies over-rely on visual perception and fail in last-millimeter contact regions where the gripper and workpiece occlude the hole.
- Parallel early-fusion visuo-tactile models suffer modality imbalance: dense visual signals dominate sparse tactile ones, diluting touch during fine manipulation.
- Precision-assembly evaluation used custom setups with undisclosed tolerances and clearances, making reported success rates non-reproducible and incomparable across methods.

## Method

- **ReTac-ACT**'s modality-specific encoders: ImageNet ==ResNet-18== for three RGB views, plus a dedicated 5-layer CNN tactile encoder producing 196 tokens, avoiding natural-image backbones on contact images.
- ==Cross-Modal Dynamic Fusion==: ==bidirectional cross-attention== mutually enriches visual and tactile tokens, then a proprioception-conditioned ==MLP gate== with learnable temperature reciprocally blends them as an information bottleneck.
- Auxiliary losses on top of ==ACT=='s L1 and KL terms: ==transposed-convolution tactile reconstruction== plus ==InfoNCE== contrastive vision-tactile alignment, weighted 0.5 and 0.1 respectively.

## Results

- On NIST ATB M1 at 3 mm clearance ReTac-ACT hits **90%** peg-in-hole with **0%** missed grasps, versus **40%** for ACT and **20%** for both Diffusion Policy and the pi05 generalist.
- Tightening clearance to **0.1 mm** costs only **11%** relative (**90%** to **80%**), while ACT falls **62.5%** relative (**40%** to **15%**) and Diffusion Policy collapses to **0%**.
- Ablations: removing cross-attention or reciprocal fusion drops insertion to **5%**, dropping tactile reconstruction to **15%**, and static gating to **35%**, from the full **90%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Modality reliability shifts across task phases, so a state-conditioned gate beats fixed concatenation; static fusion left multimodal information available but underexploited during occluded insertion.
> - Reconstruction supervision stops tactile encoders collapsing to trivial features when signals are static in free space, preserving the high-frequency deformation cues precision needs.
> - Large generalist VLA pretraining does not substitute for dedicated contact sensing; specialized tactile fusion trained on 50 to 100 demonstrations vastly outperformed it.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research addresses challenges within robotic manipulation, specifically focusing on precision assembly tasks that require sub-millimeter accuracy in contact-rich environments. Human operators frequently integrate visual and tactile perception to achieve fine manipulation, where vision guides coarse alignment and tactile feedback enables real-time, millimeter-level adjustments during contact. The paper identifies a significant limitation in current vision-only imitation learning (IL) methods, such as Diffusion Policy, ACT, and pi05. While these methods demonstrate performance in general manipulation tasks, they often struggle with precision operations like peg-in-hole assembly, particularly in "last-millimeter" regions. This struggle is attributed to an over-reliance on visual perception, which becomes unreliable due to occlusion from the robot's end-effector or workpiece, and the ambiguity of geometric features during direct contact.

The paper notes the development of high-precision tactile sensors, such as GelSight, as a hardware foundation for integrating rich contact information into robotic control. Previous vision-tactile learning and fusion approaches have explored deep neural networks and self-attention mechanisms to process multimodal data. However, many of these methods, particularly those adopting early fusion with parallel multimodal inputs, face a "modality imbalance" issue. In such cases, the model can be dominated by visual signals, diminishing the effective contribution of tactile information during fine manipulation where it is most critical. Efforts to enhance tactile representation through masking and reconstruction have been made, but these often do not account for the dynamic nature of assembly tasks, which transition between vision-dominant free-space motion and tactile-dominant contact phases.

ReTac-ACT positions itself by extending the Action Chunking with Transformers (ACT) framework to natively incorporate and effectively utilize high-dimensional tactile feedback. By introducing mechanisms for dynamic modality fusion and tactile representation learning, the work aims to overcome the limitations of both vision-only policies and existing vision-tactile fusion strategies that lack adaptive control over modality reliance. It seeks to enable a seamless transition between sensory modalities based on the robot's state, thereby enhancing robustness in complex, occluded, and contact-intensive assembly scenarios. The evaluation on the standardized NIST Assembly Task Board M1 benchmark also addresses a broader issue in the field: the historical lack of reproducible and comparable results due to custom, non-standardized experimental setups.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and effective robotic policy for high-precision assembly tasks, specifically focusing on contact-rich operations where traditional vision-based methods encounter significant limitations. The authors highlight the necessity for robotic systems to emulate human-like synergistic use of vision and touch to achieve fine-grained manipulation.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges inherent in precision assembly:
1. **Visual Occlusion and Failure in Contact-Rich Regions:** In the "last-millimeter" phase of assembly, the robot's end-effector and the workpiece can obscure critical visual feedback, rendering vision-only policies ineffective for the sub-millimeter corrections required.
2. **Geometric Ambiguity:** Close contact during insertion can make distinct visual features difficult to discern, further hindering purely visual guidance.
3. **Limitations of Vision-Only Imitation Learning:** Despite advances in vision-based imitation learning (e.g., ACT, Diffusion Policy), these methods are observed to struggle with the stringent accuracy demands of tasks like millimeter-level peg-in-hole assembly due to their sole reliance on visual input.
4. **Ineffective Multimodal Fusion:** Existing vision-tactile fusion strategies often exhibit "modality imbalance," where the more dominant visual signals overshadow the crucial, but often weaker in scale, tactile information. This can lead to a diminished contribution of tactile feedback during the contact phase.
5. **Suboptimal Tactile Representation Learning:** Standard visual backbones, often pre-trained on natural images, are ill-suited for processing tactile images. They tend to prioritize semantic features over the high-frequency contact deformations vital for precision manipulation, leading to a loss of critical information.
6. **Static Modality Reliance:** The assembly process naturally transitions from a free-space approach phase, where vision is dominant, to a contact-rich insertion phase, where tactile feedback becomes paramount. Most existing methods lack mechanisms to dynamically adapt the policy's reliance on different modalities across these distinct task phases.
7. **Lack of Standardized Evaluation:** The research community has historically suffered from a lack of standardized benchmarks for precision assembly, hindering reproducible research and objective performance comparisons.

To address these motivations, the key objectives of ReTac-ACT are formulated around three synergistic mechanisms:
- To enable **reciprocal visuo-tactile feature enhancement** through bidirectional cross-attention, allowing modalities to mutually refine their representations before fusion.
- To implement a **proprioception-conditioned gating network** that dynamically adjusts the policy's reliance on tactile feedback, particularly elevating its importance when visual occlusion is likely to occur during contact.
- To introduce a **tactile reconstruction objective** during training to ensure that the tactile encoder learns manipulation-relevant contact information and fine-grained deformation patterns, rather than generic visual textures, thereby preventing feature collapse and enhancing sensitivity to sub-millimeter deviations.
- To evaluate the proposed policy rigorously on the **standardized NIST Assembly Task Board M1 benchmark** to ensure reproducibility and provide a meaningful comparison against existing methods.
- To **release the codebase and a vision-tactile demonstration dataset** to support the broader research community.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ReTac-ACT is presented as a state-gated vision-tactile imitation learning policy built upon the Action Chunking with Transformers (ACT) framework. Its architecture integrates visual, tactile, and proprioceptive inputs to predict future action chunks for precision assembly.

#### 3.1. Architecture Overview

The ReTac-ACT architecture comprises three main modules:
1. **Multi-Modal Encoders:** Separate encoders process visual and tactile inputs to extract specialized features.
2. **Cross-Modal Dynamic Fusion Module:** This module dynamically integrates the encoded features, adapting the weighting between modalities based on the robot's proprioceptive state.
3. **Action Generator:** A CVAE-based transformer decoder predicts a chunk of future actions.

#### 3.2. Multi-Modal Encoders

- **Visual Encoder:** For each of three RGB camera inputs (two wrist-mounted, one third-person), a ResNet-18 backbone (pre-trained on ImageNet) extracts multi-scale features. These feature maps are then spatially flattened and linearly projected to form visual tokens $V \in \mathbb{R}^{3N_v \times D}$, where $N_v$ is the number of tokens per camera and $D$ is the feature dimension.
- **Tactile Encoder:** A dedicated 5-layer Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) encoder is employed for tactile images. It uses progressively increasing channels ({32, 64, 128, 256, 512}), kernel size 4, and stride 2. The resulting feature map is flattened and projected into tactile tokens $T \in \mathbb{R}^{N_t \times D}$ ($N_t=196$). This encoder is regularized during training by an auxiliary tactile reconstruction objective to capture high-frequency contact deformations relevant for manipulation, as opposed to generic visual textures.

#### 3.3. Cross-Modal Dynamic Fusion Module

This module is designed to handle the shift in modality reliability during assembly, transitioning from vision-dominant (free-space) to tactile-dominant (contact-rich).
- **Bidirectional Cross-Modal Attention:** Prior to fusion, visual and tactile tokens are mutually enhanced through cross-attention. Tactile tokens query visual tokens to generate visual-context-enriched tactile features ($\tilde{T}$), and visual tokens query tactile tokens to produce tactile-refined visual features ($\tilde{V}$). This allows for reciprocal information exchange.
- **Gating Mechanism:** A 3-layer MLP, $g(\cdot)$, processes the robot's proprioceptive state $x_t$ to compute a scalar modality gate $\alpha_t \in (0,1)$. This gate is passed through a sigmoid function with a learnable temperature parameter $\tau_g$. An $\alpha_t$ value near 0 indicates vision preference (free-space), while a value near 1 indicates tactile preference (contact phase).
- **Reciprocal Fusion:** The enhanced features are fused using the computed gate $\alpha_t$: $V^{\star} = (1 - \alpha_t)V + \alpha_t\tilde{V}$ and $T^{\star} = \alpha_t T + (1 - \alpha_t)\tilde{T}$. This reciprocal weighting ensures that increasing reliance on one modality proportionally decreases the weight of the other, enforcing adaptive switching. The final fused representation, $[V^{\star}; T^{\star}]$, is then passed to the transformer encoder.

#### 3.4. Action Generator

The action generator follows the ACT framework. A 4-layer Transformer Encoder processes the concatenated fused tokens $[V^{\star}; T^{\star}]$ along with an embedding of the proprioceptive state. A 1-layer Transformer Decoder, conditioned on the encoder output and a latent variable $z$ (from a Conditional VAE), predicts an action chunk $\{a_t, \dots, a_{t+k-1}\}$ of length $k$. During inference, $z$ is sampled from the prior mean. Actions consist of 14-DoF bimanual joint targets and 2 gripper commands.

#### 3.5. Loss Function

The total loss function is a combination of several objectives:
- **Action Prediction Loss ($L_{\ell_1}$):** A standard L1 loss for predicting actions.
- **VAE KL-Divergence Loss ($L_{KL}$):** Standard loss for the Conditional VAE component.
- **Auxiliary Tactile Reconstruction Loss ($L_{rec}$):** A transposed-convolution decoder (mirroring the encoder) reconstructs the raw tactile image $\hat{I}_t^t$ from the encoded tactile tokens. An L2 loss ensures the encoder preserves fine-grained contact geometry, preventing feature collapse.
- **Contrastive Alignment Loss ($L_{con}$):** An InfoNCE-style loss that projects globally pooled visual and tactile tokens into a shared latent space. It maximizes similarity for synchronized vision-tactile pairs and minimizes it for mismatched pairs, promoting semantic alignment between modalities.

The total objective is $L = L_{\ell_1} + \lambda_{KL}L_{KL} + \lambda_{rec}L_{rec} + \lambda_{con}L_{con}$, with weights $\lambda_{KL}=10, \lambda_{rec}=0.5, \lambda_{con}=0.1$. The model is trained end-to-end using AdamW optimizer with specific learning rates and weight decay.

#### 3.6. Experimental Setup

- **Task:** NIST ATB M1 precision assembly benchmark: inserting cylindrical pegs into holes at 3 mm, 1 mm, and 0.1 mm clearances. Pegs are 304 stainless steel with sub-10 µm CNC tolerance.
- **Hardware:** Bimanual system with two Realman RM75-6F-V 7-DOF arms, each with a parallel gripper.
- **Sensors:** Proprioception (joint positions, Cartesian poses, gripper states), multi-view RGB cameras (480x640), and four Xense optical tactile sensors (480x640, two per gripper).
- **Data Collection:** Over 5,000 expert demonstration trajectories were collected via VR teleoperation, covering 5 geometric shapes and 4 clearance levels, time-synchronized at 30 Hz. The dataset includes visual, proprioceptive, and tactile data, standardized to LeRobot dataset V3.0 format. Initial robot and peg poses were randomized, and multiple operators collected data. ReTac-ACT policies were trained with 50-100 trajectories for specific clearance levels.
- **Evaluation:** Three metrics: Missed (grasp failure), Grasp (successful grasp), and Peg-in-hole (insertion success). 20 trials per experiment (2 initial positions, 10 trials each).
- **Baselines:** ACT (vision-only, CVAE-transformer), Diffusion Policy (DP) (visuomotor policy via action diffusion, vision-only), and pi05 (generalist VLA model, vision-only).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate ReTac-ACT's performance in precision peg-in-hole assembly, particularly its robustness in tight-clearance and contact-rich scenarios.

#### 4.1. Comparison with Baseline Methods

On the NIST ATB M1 benchmark with a 3 mm clearance cylindrical peg, ReTac-ACT achieved superior performance:
- **Grasping Phase:** ReTac-ACT recorded a 0% miss rate and 100% grasp success. In contrast, ACT missed 40% of grasps, Diffusion Policy missed 70%, and pi05 missed 55%. This improvement is attributed to the bidirectional cross-attention mechanism, which allowed tactile features to enhance visual localization and visual context to refine tactile-informed grasping.
- **Insertion Phase:** ReTac-ACT achieved a 90% peg-in-hole insertion success rate. ACT achieved 40% (2.25 times lower), while Diffusion Policy and pi05 both achieved 20% (4.5 times lower). The state-gated dynamic fusion mechanism is identified as critical here, enabling adaptive reliance on tactile feedback during contact-rich insertion.
- **VLA Generalist Limitation:** The pi05 model, despite being a state-of-the-art pre-trained VLA, performed comparably to other vision-only baselines (20% success), suggesting that generalist pre-training without specific tactile integration is insufficient for high-precision contact-rich tasks.

#### 4.2. Ablation Study

A systematic ablation study validated the contribution of each component of ReTac-ACT:
- **Removal of Reciprocal Fusion or Cross-Attention:** Eliminating either bidirectional cross-attention or reciprocal fusion led to a significant drop in insertion success from 90% to 5%. This indicates that explicit, bidirectional information exchange between visual and tactile modalities is crucial for effective integration, particularly during contact-rich manipulation.
- **Removal of Tactile Reconstruction (TacRecon):** Without the tactile reconstruction objective, insertion success fell to 15%, grasp success dropped to 95%, and the miss rate increased to 5%. This demonstrates that TacRecon is essential for the tactile encoder to learn manipulation-relevant contact geometry and deformation patterns, rather than generic visual textures, which are necessary for sub-millimeter corrections.
- **Removal of State-Gated Fusion (StateGate):** Disabling the state-gated dynamic fusion mechanism (effectively using static fusion weights) reduced insertion success from 90% to 35%, while grasp performance remained at 100%. This highlights the importance of adaptively reweighting modalities; without it, the policy could not sufficiently leverage tactile information during the visually occluded insertion phase.

#### 4.3. Robustness to Tighter Tolerances

ReTac-ACT's performance was evaluated across three clearance levels (3 mm, 1 mm, and 0.1 mm):
- **Absolute Performance:** At the tightest clearance (0.1 mm), ReTac-ACT maintained an 80% insertion success rate. In contrast, ACT dropped to 15%, and Diffusion Policy failed completely with 0% success.
- **Degradation Rate:** As clearance tightened from 3 mm to 0.1 mm, ReTac-ACT experienced an 11% relative degradation (from 90% to 80%). ACT degraded by 62.5% (from 40% to 15%), and Diffusion Policy degraded by 100% (from 20% to 0%). This indicates that tactile sensing is critical for maintaining performance in highly precise assembly scenarios where visual information becomes severely limited.
- **Grasp Reliability:** ReTac-ACT consistently maintained a 0% miss rate across all clearance levels, further demonstrating its robustness in the initial grasping phase.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Analysis: Attention Visualization

Attention heatmaps and the evolution of the modality gate $\alpha_t$ provided insights into the policy's behavior:
- **State-Dependent Focus:** ReTac-ACT consistently focused its attention on task-relevant objects (peg and hole), unlike ACT which showed scattered attention. This focused attention is attributed to the bidirectional cross-attention mechanism.
- **Instant Modality Switching:** At the moment of contact, the attention heatmap was observed to instantly concentrate on the contacted assembly components. Concurrently, the fusion weight parameter $\alpha_t$ increased, reflecting an adaptive shift in reliance from visual to tactile information as the task transitioned from free-space to contact-rich insertion.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of ReTac-ACT offers several significant contributions and potential impacts on the field of robotic manipulation and assembly:

- **Addressing a Fundamental Challenge:** ReTac-ACT directly tackles the fundamental challenge of performing precision assembly in contact-rich "last-millimeter" regions, where visual feedback is often compromised by occlusion. This represents a significant step beyond the capabilities of vision-only robotic policies.
- **Advancement in Multimodal Perception for Robotics:** The proposed architectural innovations—bidirectional cross-attention for feature enhancement, a proprioception-conditioned state-gated dynamic fusion mechanism, and an auxiliary tactile reconstruction objective—provide a principled and effective method for integrating vision and tactile modalities. This approach addresses the prevalent issue of modality imbalance and ensures the extraction of manipulation-relevant tactile features.
- **Enhanced Precision and Robustness:** The experimental results demonstrate a substantial improvement in peg-in-hole assembly success rates, particularly at industrial-grade clearances (0.1 mm), where ReTac-ACT significantly outperforms existing vision-only and generalist baseline methods. This enhanced precision and robustness have direct implications for industrial automation, where tasks require sub-millimeter tolerances.
- **Validation of Tactile Sensing Importance:** The stark performance degradation of vision-only policies at tighter clearances, contrasted with ReTac-ACT's sustained high performance, empirically validates the indispensable role of high-resolution tactile sensing in fine-grained, contact-rich manipulation tasks.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The commitment to open-sourcing the ReTac-ACT codebase and releasing a large-scale, standardized vision-tactile demonstration dataset based on the NIST ATB M1 benchmark is a significant contribution to the research community. This will facilitate reproducible research, enable comparative studies, and provide resources for developing more advanced multimodal robotic policies.
- **Path Towards Generalization:** While the current evaluation focuses on cylindrical pegs, the outlined future work to extend evaluation to non-axially-symmetric shapes and integrate with large-scale Vision-Language-Action (VLA) pre-training suggests a pathway toward combining specialized tactile reasoning with more generalist robotic knowledge. This could lead to more versatile and adaptable robotic systems capable of handling a wider array of complex assembly tasks.
- **Potential for Industrial Adoption:** The demonstrated ability to achieve high success rates at 0.1 mm clearance, which approaches ISO IT6-IT7 grade tolerances, positions ReTac-ACT as a promising technology for real-world industrial assembly applications requiring high precision and reliability.
