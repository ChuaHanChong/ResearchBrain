# From Seeing to Doing: Bridging Reasoning and Decision for Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> The paper introduces FSD (From Seeing to Doing), a Vision-Language Model that enhances robotic manipulation by generating intermediate, embodiment-agnostic "visual aids" through structured spatial reasoning. FSD achieves robust zero-shot generalization, demonstrating a 72% success rate on real-world tasks with an xArm 6 robot and outperforming state-of-the-art baselines.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models often exhibit suboptimal zero-shot generalization in robotic manipulation despite leveraging pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs).
- Limitations stem from data scarcity in embodied robotics and the heterogeneity of robotic embodiments, hindering scaling laws and robust end-to-end supervised learning.
- Current approaches often lack fine-grained guidance and explicit spatial reasoning for complex manipulation tasks, relying on direct input-to-action mapping.

## Method

- Developed **FSD**, a Vision-Language Model that generates intermediate, embodiment-agnostic "visual aids" (spatial affordance boxes/points, object-centric visual traces) via a Spatial Relationship-Focused Visual ==Chain-of-Thought== (SrCoT) mechanism.
- Implemented a hierarchical "weak-to-strong" dataset construction pipeline to progressively enhance capabilities in region grounding, spatial understanding, reasoning, and visual aid generation.
- Introduced a self-consistency alignment mechanism to improve the model's understanding and generation of spatial coordinates by framing generation tasks as inverse understanding problems.

## Results

- FSD achieved an average rank of 1.3 across five spatial reasoning benchmarks, outperforming 13B open-source models and matching GPT-4o's performance, with **88.0%** accuracy in 3D depth perception.
- On the VABench benchmark for visual aid generation, FSD demonstrated significantly higher accuracy for spatial affordance points (**61.82%** vs. RoboPoint's **19.09%**) and lower RMSE/MAE for visual traces.
- FSD attained a **72%** success rate in zero-shot real-world robotic manipulation tasks with an xArm 6 robot, outperforming baselines by over **30%**, and exhibited lower execution latency compared to other pipelines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly generating intermediate, embodiment-agnostic visual aids through structured spatial reasoning significantly enhances zero-shot generalization in robotic manipulation.
> - A hierarchical data construction approach, combining large-scale embodied datasets with common sense data, effectively builds capabilities from basic visual grounding to complex visual trace generation.
> - Self-consistency training, by aligning spatial annotations with physical meaning through bidirectional tasks, strengthens spatial understanding and generation, crucial for robust visual aid prediction.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation is actively pursuing the development of generalized agents capable of performing diverse actions across various platforms, tasks, objects, and dynamic environments. A prominent approach involves leveraging pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs) by transforming them into Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models through fine-tuning on large-scale embodied datasets. The expectation is that VLMs, pre-trained on extensive internet-scale data, would imbue VLAs with robust generalization capabilities, particularly for zero-shot performance on novel tasks and unseen scenarios.

However, existing literature indicates that directly applying VLMs for this purpose often results in suboptimal zero-shot performance. This limitation is attributed primarily to two factors: the scarcity of embodied robotics data compared to language and vision datasets, which hinders scaling laws similar to those observed in other domains, and the heterogeneity inherent in robotic embodiments, which can lead to significant variations in VLA outputs and complicates end-to-end supervised learning for generalization.

Within this landscape, several related areas of research are relevant. Spatial understanding and reasoning with VLMs aim to infer spatial information beyond 2D RGB images, a capability crucial for embodied AI. Works such as SpatialVLM, SpatialRGPT, and SpatialBot have focused on incorporating spatial primitives, coordinate systems, and precise spatial relationship modeling to enhance geometric reasoning. Another area is Visual Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning, which extends the step-by-step reasoning paradigms from Large Language Models (LLMs) to multimodal contexts. Approaches like Shikra, VoCoT, and EmbodiedCoT have explored integrating visual regions or enhancing intermediate reasoning for embodied AI tasks. Finally, research into visual aids for robotic manipulation investigates the use of embodiment-agnostic representations, such as visual traces and spatial affordances, to improve training efficiency and transferability. While many existing works in this area, including LLaRVA and various spatial affordance models, have shown promise, they often face challenges in generalizing to novel tasks without task-specific fine-tuning.

This work positions itself within this context by attempting to bridge VLM reasoning with embodied decision-making, aiming to overcome the limitations of data scarcity and heterogeneity by explicitly generating intermediate, embodiment-agnostic visual aids through structured spatial reasoning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the generalization capabilities of robotic manipulation systems, particularly for unseen scenarios and novel tasks, by addressing the limitations of existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. The authors specifically aim to achieve robust zero-shot performance in robotic manipulation.

The motivation behind this work stems from the identified shortcomings of current VLA models, which, despite leveraging general Vision-Language Models (VLMs), struggle with zero-shot generalization. The authors attribute these limitations to two main challenges:
1. **Data Scarcity and Heterogeneity:** Embodied robotics datasets are significantly smaller and less diverse than those used for training general vision and language models. This prevents VLA models from benefiting from similar scaling laws and acquiring the broad generalization abilities observed in other domains. The varied nature of robotic platforms (embodiment heterogeneity) also leads to diverse action outputs, complicating the development of robust end-to-end supervised learning solutions that can generalize across different robots.
2. **Lack of Fine-Grained Guidance and Reasoning:** Current VLA models often rely on direct mapping from visual-language inputs to actions. This approach may not adequately capture the complex spatial relationships and multi-step reasoning required for nuanced manipulation tasks, especially in novel or unstructured environments. The authors draw an analogy to human cognition, where complex tasks are broken down into object localization, planning based on relative positions, and obstacle avoidance, suggesting that a similar structured reasoning process could benefit robotic systems.

To address these motivations, the specific objectives of the FSD (From Seeing to Doing) model are:
- To develop a novel vision-language model that generates intermediate representations, termed "visual aids," through spatial relationship reasoning. These visual aids are intended to provide fine-grained, embodiment-agnostic guidance for robotic manipulation.
- To integrate a hierarchical data construction pipeline that combines large-scale embodied datasets with common sense data, fostering a progressive enhancement of model capabilities from weak to strong.
- To implement a self-consistency mechanism that aligns spatial coordinates with visual signals, thereby strengthening the model's understanding and generation abilities in the spatial domain.
- To enable zero-shot robot manipulation by leveraging these reasoning-driven visual aids, allowing robots to perform tasks in novel scenarios without prior task-specific fine-tuning.

By pursuing these objectives, the research aims to unlock the generalization potential of VLMs for robotic manipulation, moving beyond purely data-driven approaches to integrate explicit spatial reasoning into the decision-making process.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The FSD (From Seeing to Doing) model proposes a pipeline to bridge VLM reasoning and embodied decisions via the generation of intermediate visual aids. The approach focuses on leveraging VLMs' visual understanding while augmenting it with step-by-step reasoning to extract unified, mid-level structural representations independent of specific robot embodiments.

#### 3.1. Definition of Visual Aids

FSD utilizes three types of visual aids, all represented as normalized 2D image coordinates $x = (p, q) \in^2$:
- **Spatial affordance boxes ($\mathcal{B}$):** Rectangular regions defining target areas for object placement. These require inferring coordinates for unmarked free space.
- **Spatial affordance points ($\mathcal{P}$):** More precise and flexible sets of points $\{(x_i, y_i) \mid i = 1, \dots, n\}$ for placement, reducing redundancy.
- **Object-centric visual traces ($\tau$):** Ordered sequences of coordinates $\{x_t \mid t = 1, \dots, T\}$ describing manipulation trajectories. These facilitate complex instruction execution, cross-embodiment transfer, and collision avoidance.
These aids are object-centric, not agent-centric, which helps circumvent limitations of heterogeneous embodied data and leverages general visual datasets without robots.

#### 3.2. Spatial Relationship-Focused Visual Chain-of-Thought (SrCoT)

SrCoT guides VLMs to generate visual aids through structured reasoning based on spatial relationship graphs. This approach aims to stimulate VLMs' spatial reasoning rather than relying solely on extensive demonstrations. SrCoT comprises two phases:
1. **Description:** Object-centric region captions are generated to establish a task-relevant spatial relationship graph. Nodes represent objects with their coordinates, and edges denote relative relationships (e.g., above, below, left, right).
2. **Reasoning:** Using the spatial relationship graph as anchor points, the model determines start and end coordinates through object references and free space reasoning. Intermediate points are then iteratively derived with explicit logical connections. This templated reasoning path enables analogical reasoning in the spatial domain. The model is required to generate coordinates in a specified format (e.g., `<ref>` for objects, `<point>` for points, `<box>` for boxes) and bind them to objects, ensuring explicit visual-spatial coordinate alignment. All coordinates are discretized as integers normalized between 0 and 999.

#### 3.3. Weak-to-Strong Capability Dataset Construction Pipeline

A hierarchical data construction pipeline was designed to progressively develop the capabilities required by SrCoT, addressing precise reference grounding, spatial understanding, and complex instruction following. The pipeline consists of five capability levels:
1. **Region Grounding:** Enables focusing on key objects in scenes, integrating grounding with captioning for positional information. Task-relevant objects are nominated by GPT-4o, and bounding boxes/segmentation masks are generated by GroundedSam.
2. **Spatial Relationship Understanding:** Establishes prerequisite knowledge for spatial reasoning. 3D semantic scene graphs are reconstructed using Metric3Dv2 for depth estimation, along with WildCamera and PerspectiveFields for camera parameters. This enables 2D-3D mapping and spatial relationship graph construction, with a focus on objects having a relative depth gap of at least 20%.
3. **Spatial Reasoning:** Builds upon the previous levels to perform multi-hop analysis of object positions and relationships. GPT-4o generates diverse multi-turn Question-Answering (QA) data based on image captions, object coordinates, and relationships.
4. **Spatial Affordance Generation:** Creates actionable spatial guidance by extracting the manipulated object’s final position from human demonstrations in embodied datasets. The precise affordance region is calculated by combining this with reference object positioning and re-rendered onto the initial frame.
5. **Visual Trace Generation:** Employs a two-stage approach: self-supervised keypoint extraction (Huang et al., 2024) identifies grasp points, followed by CoTracker (Karaev et al., 2024) to capture temporal dynamics from human demonstrations, projecting trajectories onto the initial frame. Eight equidistant points are uniformly sampled from smoothed trajectories.
Automatic dataset construction leveraged large-scale robot data from BridgeDataV2, RT-X, and Droid, producing approximately 300K Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) data points. Strict rule-based filters and iterative manual validation were used to ensure data quality.

#### 3.4. Self-Consistent Alignment for Spatial Understanding and Generation

To align the model's understanding of spatial annotations with their physical meaning, a self-consistency mechanism is proposed. Generation tasks are framed inversely as understanding problems. For example, if the forward task is $(X_v, X_q) \to \tau$ (inferring visual trace $\tau$ from image $X_v$ and instruction $X_q$), the inverse task becomes $(X_v, \tau) \to X_q$ (predicting possible instructions given an image and visual traces). This bidirectional approach helps the model comprehend spatial coordinates' meanings and aligns coordinate space with image-text modalities, enhancing spatial reasoning.

#### 3.5. Training and Action Execution

- **Training:** FSD is trained in two stages, building on the LLaVA-1.5 instruction tuning pipeline and ASMv2. The architecture comprises a CLIP-ViT-L-336px image encoder, a linear projector, a language tokenizer, and a Vicuna-13B LLM. Only the projector and LLM weights are updated.
 1. **General Spatial Reasoning Enhancement:** Uses data from Levels 1-3, mixed with 1.4M samples of general visual question answering (VQA) data.
 2. **Visual Aids Generation and Understanding:** Uses data from Levels 4-5 with the self-consistency mechanism. FSD predicts a fixed set of 8 points for visual traces.
- **Action Execution:**
 - FSD reasons from initial or intermediate task steps, selecting visual aids.
 - For bounding boxes, the center is sampled as the target point. For affordance points, one point is directly sampled. CuRobo is used as the motion planner for these.
 - For visual traces, 2D traces $\tau$ are generated, and preliminary depth information is obtained from depth cameras. A pinhole camera model maps these to 3D space ($\tau_{3D}$). The path trajectory is then optimized using gradient descent-based interpolation, generating complete motion trajectories in SE(3) space. GraspNet (or predetermined poses) is used to match grasp poses based on the first point's position.

#### 3.6. Visual Aids Generation Benchmark (VABench)

A new benchmark, VABench, was manually annotated with 300 problems from real-world and simulation datasets (OXE, BridgeData, Droid, Libero) to evaluate visual aid generation.
- **VABench-Point:** Measures the proportion of predicted points falling within ground truth target regions (for models outputting bounding boxes, points are uniformly sampled within them).
- **VABench-VisualTrace:** Computes Mean Absolute Error (MAE) and Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE) between predicted and ground truth trajectories. For differing lengths, trajectories are interpolated. Coordinates are normalized to 1000x1000 space. An MLLM-based qualitative scoring (GPT Score, 1-10) assesses visualized trajectories based on task alignment, feasibility, and obstacle avoidance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of FSD encompassed both "seeing" (spatial understanding and reasoning) and "doing" (zero-shot robot manipulation) capabilities across various benchmarks and real-world settings.

#### 4.1. Evaluation of Spatial Understanding and Reasoning Capabilities ("Seeing")

- **General Spatial Reasoning:** FSD-13B demonstrated strong performance across five spatial reasoning benchmarks (CVBench, BLINK, CRPE, SAT, EmbSpatial-Bench), which include 15 subtasks. FSD achieved an average rank of 1.3, outperforming other 13B open-source models and exhibiting performance comparable to the closed-source GPT-4o. Specifically, the model achieved 88.0% accuracy in 3D depth perception, 86.7% in distance estimation, and 78.3% in spatial relationship understanding. This indicates robust spatial reasoning capabilities developed through the data-centric approach.

- **Object and Free Region Reference Capabilities:** FSD exhibited enhanced abilities in accurately identifying objects and localizing free spaces based on natural language instructions. On the RoboRef It benchmark for object reference, FSD achieved 56.7% accuracy, surpassing GPT-4o (15.3%) and specialized models like RoboPoint (49.8%). For the Where2Place benchmark, which assesses free space reference, FSD performed comparably to RoboPoint while significantly outperforming other baselines. The authors attribute this improvement to the enhanced spatial understanding facilitated by the SrCoT mechanism.

- **Spatial Visual Aids Capabilities (VABench):** FSD demonstrated improved performance in generating precise spatial affordances and visual traces on the newly introduced VABench benchmark.
 - **VABench-Point:** FSD achieved 61.82% accuracy, which is more than three times higher than RoboPoint (19.09%).
 - **VABench-VisualTrace:** FSD obtained lower RMSE (78.26) and MAE (63.44) values compared to baselines such as the DINOv2 Predictor (RMSE 128.32, MAE 117.49) and RoboBrain (RMSE 121.6, MAE 103.8). FSD also achieved a higher LLM Score (6.21) compared to the DINOv2 Predictor (4.01) and RoboBrain (4.5), indicating better qualitative trajectory generation. Ablation studies confirmed that both SrCoT and the self-consistency alignment mechanism contributed to FSD’s performance, suggesting the effectiveness of the reasoning-based approach over purely data-driven methods for visual aid prediction.

#### 4.2. Evaluation of Decision Capability ("Doing")

- **SimplerEnv Evaluation (WidowX Robot):** FSD was deployed on a WidowX robotic arm in SimplerEnv for zero-shot generalization tasks. It achieved an average success rate of 40.6%, outperforming standard zero-shot baselines like RoboPoint (17.7%) and demonstrating competitive performance with some fine-tuned end-to-end VLAs (e.g., $\pi_0$-fast 48.3%, OpenVLA-OFT 41.8%). The authors note that FSD maintained robust zero-shot generalization, unlike some end-to-end VLAs that experienced significant performance degradation when faced with variations in backgrounds and instructions without dedicated fine-tuning.

- **Real-World Robot Evaluation (xArm 6 Robot):** FSD was tested on an xArm 6 robot across 8 tabletop manipulation tasks in a zero-shot setting. It achieved a 72% success rate, outperforming the strongest baseline (unspecified, but contextually referring to MOKA or RoboPoint) by more than 30%. FSD successfully completed complex tasks, such as towel folding, by generating visual traces, which was beyond the capabilities of the baseline methods.

- **Qualitative Experiments on Challenging Tasks:** FSD demonstrated the ability to infer visual traces for tasks requiring continuous contact (e.g., "Wiping the whiteboard") and kinematic understanding of articulated objects (e.g., "Opening the drawer"). While downstream action control requires further optimization for these tasks, these qualitative results suggest FSD's potential in bridging perception and complex control for challenging scenarios.

- **Real-World Execution Latency:** FSD exhibited low execution latency compared to end-to-end OpenVLA (fine-tuned) and modular MOKA pipelines (e.g., 14s vs. 23s for a sponge task, 10s vs. 12s for a cucumber task). This efficiency is attributed to its single-pass, single-model architecture that generates visual aids in a zero-shot manner, avoiding costly fine-tuning and multi-component system overhead.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in this paper, through the introduction of FSD, offers a notable contribution to the field of robotic manipulation, particularly concerning generalization in novel and unseen scenarios.

The primary significance of this work lies in its novel paradigm that effectively bridges Vision-Language Model (VLM) reasoning with embodied decision-making. By generating intermediate spatial representations (visual aids) through a Spatial Relationship-Focused Visual Chain-of-Thought (SrCoT) mechanism, FSD provides a structured approach to leverage the broad visual understanding capabilities of VLMs for robotic control. This method addresses the persistent challenges of data scarcity and heterogeneity that often limit the zero-shot generalization performance of purely data-driven VLA models.

The demonstrated robust zero-shot generalization capabilities in both simulated (SimplerEnv) and real-world (xArm 6) robot settings highlight the practical applicability of FSD. Achieving a 72% success rate across diverse real-world tasks, significantly outperforming baseline methods, suggests that FSD moves towards more versatile and adaptable robotic assistants. The ability to successfully infer visual traces for complex tasks like towel folding and even challenging scenarios involving contact-rich interactions or articulated objects indicates a step towards enabling robots to handle more intricate and open-world manipulation problems.

Furthermore, the hierarchical data construction pipeline and the self-consistency alignment mechanism are significant methodological contributions. These components systematically enhance the spatial understanding, reasoning, and visual aid generation capabilities of VLMs, providing a scalable framework for developing embodied intelligence. The introduction of the Visual Aids Generation Benchmark (VABench) is also impactful, as it provides a standardized tool for evaluating an under-explored yet critical aspect of visually-guided robotic manipulation.

The approach of using visual aids as explicit, embodiment-agnostic guidance can potentially lead to more interpretable and controllable robotic systems. By transforming prediction tasks into reasoning tasks, FSD capitalizes on visual-spatial common knowledge, reducing the reliance on scenario-specific fine-tuning. This could simplify the deployment of robotic solutions in dynamic and varied environments. The low execution latency achieved by FSD, compared to fine-tuned end-to-end or multi-component modular pipelines, also points towards efficiency gains that are crucial for practical real-world robotic applications.

In summary, FSD represents an advancement in integrating high-level reasoning with low-level action planning for robotic manipulation. Its success in zero-shot generalization has the potential to broaden the scope of tasks that robots can perform autonomously and adaptably, fostering the development of more intelligent and general-purpose robotic agents. Future work on task decomposition for long-horizon instructions, integration with closed-loop control, and extension to 3D visual aids could further amplify FSD's impact.
