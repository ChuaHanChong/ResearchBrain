# Video-R1: Reinforcing Video Reasoning in MLLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers introduced Video-R1, the first framework to apply a rule-based reinforcement learning paradigm for enhancing temporal reasoning capabilities in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) for video. Video-R1, leveraging a new temporal-aware RL algorithm and dedicated video reasoning datasets, consistently outperforms previous state-of-the-art models, achieving 37.1% accuracy on VSI-Bench, surpassing GPT-4o's 34.0%.

## Problem

- Existing MLLMs primarily excel at video perception tasks but lack robust, explicit temporal reasoning abilities to understand dynamic events and relationships over time.
- Current reinforcement learning (RL) algorithms for reasoning, designed for text, do not inherently provide reward signals that encourage temporal understanding in videos.
- A scarcity of high-quality, complex video reasoning datasets hinders the effective training and generalization of MLLMs for sophisticated video-based reasoning tasks.

## Method

- Constructed two comprehensive datasets, ==Video-R1-CoT-165k== (for SFT) and ==Video-R1-260k== (for RL), incorporating a hybrid of high-quality image and video reasoning data to build foundational and temporal reasoning skills.
- Introduced ==Temporal Group Relative Policy Optimization (T-GRPO)==, an RL algorithm that explicitly incentivizes temporal reasoning using a ==contrastive reward mechanism== based on comparing responses to ordered versus randomly shuffled video frames.
- Implemented a two-stage training strategy: an initial Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) cold start on Video-R1-CoT-165k, followed by RL training with T-GRPO on Video-R1-260k, enhanced with a ==length-based reward== for response coherence.

## Results

- Video-R1 consistently and significantly outperformed previous state-of-the-art MLLMs across **six** challenging video benchmarks, with Video-R1-7B (**64** frames) achieving **37.1%** accuracy on VSI-Bench, surpassing GPT-4o (**34.0%**).
- Reinforcement learning, even after only **1k** steps, yielded significant performance improvements, demonstrating its effectiveness in generalizing video reasoning capabilities beyond SFT.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical role of both the hybrid image-video data for general reasoning and the T-GRPO algorithm for explicit temporal understanding, as removing either component led to noticeable performance drops.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reinforcement Learning (RL) with a temporal-aware algorithm (T-GRPO) is crucial for unlocking generalized and robust video reasoning in MLLMs, offering performance improvements over SFT-only approaches.
> - The explicit incentivization of temporal understanding through a contrastive reward mechanism effectively guides the MLLM to leverage correct temporal order, leading to deeper temporal reasoning rather than relying on superficial cues.
> - Combining image-based reasoning data with video data during training provides a robust foundation of general reasoning skills that significantly boosts performance on complex video reasoning tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research directly addresses a significant gap in the rapidly evolving landscape of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). Historically, Large Language Models (LLMs) have demonstrated impressive reasoning capabilities, especially with the advent of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting and rule-based Reinforcement Learning (RL), as exemplified by models like DeepSeek-R1. This "R1 paradigm" uses outcome-only rewards to elicit complex reasoning in text.

More recently, MLLMs have expanded the capabilities of LLMs to incorporate visual modalities, primarily images, enabling tasks like image captioning, visual question answering, and even image-based reasoning. Efforts such as Kimi k1.5 and Skywork R1V have begun to extend the R1 paradigm to image-text MLLMs, showing promising results in static visual reasoning.

However, the authors correctly identify that **video reasoning in MLLMs remains largely underexplored, especially when it comes to leveraging the R1 paradigm for explicit temporal reasoning.** Existing video MLLMs (e.g., LLaMA-VID, VideoLLaMA2, LongVA, VISA) have predominantly focused on video *perception* tasks, such as event recognition, object tracking, or efficient processing of long video sequences. While these models can interpret dynamic content, they often lack robust, explicit reasoning abilities over temporal sequences, leading them to sometimes "shortcut" reasoning by focusing on individual frames rather than the holistic temporal flow. This paper aims to bridge this critical gap by systematically investigating how to elicit strong *temporal reasoning* capabilities in MLLMs using a sophisticated rule-based RL approach inspired by DeepSeek-R1. It moves beyond mere video understanding to genuine video *reasoning*.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to introduce **Video-R1**, the first systematic attempt to explore the "R1 paradigm" for incentivizing and reinforcing robust video reasoning within MLLMs. This involves enabling MLLMs to not just "see" videos but to "think" and "reason" about the dynamic events and temporal relationships within them.

The authors are driven by two key motivations, stemming from challenges in applying the R1 paradigm directly to video reasoning:

1. **Lack of Temporal Modeling in Existing RL Algorithms:** Current RL training algorithms, like the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) used in DeepSeek-R1, are designed for text-based reasoning and do not inherently provide explicit reward signals that encourage temporal understanding in videos. Without such signals, MLLMs might default to superficial visual patterns or static frame analysis, failing to grasp event progression, motion, or causality over time. This leads to "shortcuts" in reasoning and hinders generalization. The motivation is to devise an algorithm that explicitly pushes the model to leverage temporal information.
2. **Scarcity of High-Quality Video Reasoning Data:** Training powerful reasoning models, especially with RL, requires abundant and diverse data that demands complex, multi-step thought processes. The authors observe that most existing video datasets focus on perception rather than reasoning, and data requiring long chain-of-thought for video is particularly scarce. This data bottleneck limits the effectiveness of RL and the emergence of robust reasoning behaviors. The motivation is to create high-quality, comprehensive datasets that can support both supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and RL training for video reasoning.

By addressing these motivations, the authors aim to unlock emergent and robust video reasoning abilities in MLLMs, akin to the breakthroughs seen in text-based LLMs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose a multi-faceted approach to achieve their objectives, combining algorithmic innovation with strategic data curation and a two-stage training strategy.

#### 3.1. Dataset Construction

To overcome the data scarcity issue, the authors construct two datasets:
- **Video-R1-CoT-165k:** Used for the Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) cold start.
- **Video-R1-260k:** Used for the Reinforcement Learning (RL) training.

Crucially, both datasets incorporate a **hybrid training strategy**, mixing high-quality **image-based reasoning data** with **video-based reasoning data**. The image data (e.g., math, spatial, knowledge, chart, OCR) provides a foundation for general reasoning skills in static contexts, while the video data (general video, specific video QA) introduces the temporal complexity.

The **Video-R1-260k** dataset's composition is carefully balanced across various domains, with a significant portion dedicated to general video (44%) and various image-based reasoning tasks (e.g., Math 14%, Knowledge 14%, Spatial 8%, Chart 8%, OCR 6%, General Image 6%). This balance ensures broad reasoning skill development.

For **CoT Annotation**, they leverage a powerful MLLM, Qwen2.5-VL-72B-Instruct, to generate Chain-of-Thought rationales for the Video-R1-260k samples. After filtering, this forms Video-R1-CoT-165k, essential for the SFT cold start.

The **Rule-based Reward Design** for RL training is meticulously defined for various data types, ensuring reliable and precise reward signals:
- **Multiple Choice & Numerical QA:** Binary reward for exact matches.
- **OCR:** Reward based on Word Error Rate (WER).
- **Free-form QA:** Reward calculated as the average of ROUGE-1, ROUGE-2, and ROUGE-L scores.
- **Regression:** Reward based on inverse relative error.
This focus on verifiable outputs enables stable and effective RL training.

#### 3.2. Temporal Group Relative Policy Optimization (T-GRPO)

To address the lack of temporal modeling, the authors introduce **T-GRPO**, an extension of the GRPO algorithm specifically designed to encourage temporal reasoning.
The core idea is a **contrastive reward mechanism**: for each video question, the model processes two inputs:
1. **Temporally ordered frame sequence.**
2. **Randomly shuffled frame sequence.**

The model generates responses for both. A **temporal reward `r_t` (set to α=0.3)** is assigned *only when the proportion of correct answers from the ordered group (p) exceeds that from the shuffled group (p̃)*. This encourages the model to prefer reasoning strategies that exploit the correct temporal order. This temporal reward is then added to the base correctness and format rewards (`r_i`) *only for correct responses*, forming the final reward `R_i`. This selective application ensures that only meaningful positive advantages are reinforced, pushing the model toward truly temporally-aware policies. The advantage `A_i` is then computed within each group using normalized rewards, and the policy is updated using a modified PPO-like objective function that includes a KL divergence term to maintain proximity to a reference policy. T-GRPO is applied exclusively to video-based inputs.

#### 3.3. Training Strategies

The training process follows a two-stage approach, using Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct as the base MLLM:
1. **SFT Cold Start:** The model is initially fine-tuned on the **Video-R1-CoT-165k** dataset. This stage equips the model with foundational multimodal reasoning capabilities and serves as a strong initialization.
2. **RL Training:** The SFT-initialized model is further trained on the broader **Video-R1-260k** dataset using the proposed **T-GRPO algorithm**. This phase leverages RL to refine and generalize reasoning strategies beyond the patterns learned during SFT.

An additional **length-based reward** is introduced during RL training. If a correct response's length falls within a predefined interval (`l_min`=320, `l_max`=512), an extra reward (`ω`=0.2) is given. This balances encouraging deeper reasoning without excessive verbosity or "overthinking."

#### 3.4. Aha Moment in Video Reasoning

The authors observe that Video-R1, particularly after RL training, exhibits "aha moments"—self-reflective behaviors where the model revisits its interpretation or reflects on previous outputs when faced with ambiguous temporal cues or complex tasks. This indicates an active internal feedback loop and deeper reasoning, rather than mere memorization.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results demonstrate the significant effectiveness of Video-R1 across a range of benchmarks, validating both the T-GRPO algorithm and the data curation strategy.

- **Superior Performance of Video-R1:** Video-R1 consistently and significantly outperforms previous state-of-the-art models across all six challenging video benchmarks.
 - **Video Reasoning Benchmarks:** On VSI-Bench, VideoMMMU, and MMVU (mc), Video-R1 achieves notable gains. For instance, **Video-R1-7B (64 frames) attains 37.1% accuracy on VSI-Bench, surpassing even the proprietary GPT-4o (34.0%)**. This is a particularly strong result given VSI-Bench's focus on complex video spatial reasoning.
 - **General Video Benchmarks:** On MVBench, TempCompass, and VideoMME, Video-R1 also shows strong improvements, indicating enhanced general video understanding beyond pure reasoning tasks.
- **RL Works Better Than SFT:** The SFT-only model (Qwen2.5-VL-7B-SFT) does not consistently improve performance and sometimes even drops slightly (e.g., VideoMME), suggesting potential overfitting or limited generalization. In contrast, after **only 1k steps of reinforcement learning**, Video-R1 achieves significant performance boosts, underscoring the crucial role of RL in unlocking generalizable video reasoning capabilities.
- **More Frames Lead to Better Reasoning:** Increasing the number of input frames (from 16 to 32 to 64) consistently improves performance across almost all benchmarks for Video-R1. This highlights the importance of richer temporal context for complex video reasoning.
- **Ablation Study Validates Key Components:**
 - **Video-R1-7B-wo-image:** Removing image-based data leads to a noticeable performance drop across all benchmarks, confirming the critical role of image data in bootstrapping general reasoning skills.
 - **Video-R1-7B-wo-temporal:** Replacing T-GRPO with the original GRPO results in weaker performance, especially on reasoning benchmarks, validating the effectiveness of explicit temporal-aware training.
 - **Video-R1-7B-zero:** Skipping the SFT cold start stage leads to worse performance, demonstrating the importance of the Video-R1-CoT-165k dataset and the SFT initialization.
- **RL Training Curves & Temporal Reward Analysis:**
 - The **accuracy reward** and **temporal reward `r_t`** (scaled) both show generally upward trends during RL training, indicating continuous improvement in correctness and the adoption of temporally grounded reasoning strategies.
 - Analysis shows that Video-R1 trained with T-GRPO incorporates temporal reasoning in **75.0% of responses** for relevant questions, significantly higher than Video-R1-wo-temporal (60.2%), directly proving T-GRPO's effectiveness.
 - Sensitivity analysis on the temporal reward hyperparameter `α` reveals robustness to its choice within a reasonable range (0.2-0.3 yielded optimal results).
- **Effect of Length Reward:** The length-based reward effectively encourages the model to maintain a moderate response length during RL training, which is crucial for preserving a deep reasoning style and preventing overly short or uninformative responses that can lead to weaker generalization.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Video-R1: Reinforcing Video Reasoning in MLLMs" paper represents a significant step forward in the field of multimodal AI, with several potential impacts:

1. **Advancing Video Reasoning in MLLMs:** This work is the first systematic attempt to apply the "R1 paradigm" of rule-based reinforcement learning to video reasoning. By demonstrating its effectiveness, Video-R1 establishes a new foundation and a promising direction for developing MLLMs that can genuinely "reason" about dynamic visual content, moving beyond mere perception or descriptive understanding.
2. **Bridging the Gap between Text and Video Reasoning:** The success of extending text-based RL reasoning to the complex domain of video opens avenues for more unified and powerful general-purpose MLLMs. It suggests that similar sophisticated reasoning capabilities observed in LLMs can be instilled in MLLMs for dynamic data.
3. **Novel Algorithmic Contributions (T-GRPO):** The introduction of T-GRPO is a key algorithmic innovation. By explicitly incentivizing temporal reasoning through a contrastive reward mechanism, it overcomes a fundamental limitation of prior RL methods for video. This algorithm could inspire future research into more sophisticated temporal modeling techniques in reinforcement learning for sequential data.
4. **High-Quality Dataset Curation:** The construction and release of Video-R1-CoT-165k and Video-R1-260k datasets are invaluable contributions to the research community. These datasets, combining both image and video reasoning samples, address a critical data scarcity issue and will serve as vital resources for training and evaluating future video reasoning MLLMs.
5. **Outperforming State-of-the-Art and Proprietary Models:** Video-R1's ability to outperform advanced models, including GPT-4o on a challenging benchmark like VSI-Bench, highlights the power of specialized RL-based training for specific reasoning tasks. This demonstrates that open-source models can achieve competitive or superior performance through targeted methodological innovations.
6. **Insights into RL for MLLMs:** The paper provides valuable insights into the dynamics of RL training for MLLMs, including the observation that "RL generalizes" better than SFT for reasoning, the benefit of more frames, and the "aha moments" of self-reflection. These findings contribute to a deeper understanding of how MLLMs learn and refine complex reasoning behaviors.
7. **Open-Source Contribution:** The release of all code, models, and data is a substantial contribution, fostering transparency, reproducibility, and accelerated research in the field. This allows other researchers to build upon Video-R1's foundations.

**Limitations and Future Work:** The authors acknowledge limitations, including the current constraint to 16 video frames (limiting long-range temporal dependencies), the computational overhead of T-GRPO, and the need for more adaptive response length control and refined image-to-video knowledge transfer. Future work will focus on scaling to longer videos, more efficient temporal modeling, and developing generalist video reward models, pointing toward continued exciting research in this area.
