# Open-Vocabulary Multi-Label Classification via Multi-Modal Knowledge Transfer

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Shenzhen University, Tsinghua University, and Tencent's YouTu Lab developed the Multi-modal Knowledge Transfer (MKT) framework for open-vocabulary multi-label classification. This framework leverages pre-trained vision-language models to achieve state-of-the-art performance, demonstrated by an 11.7% absolute mAP gain over prior methods on NUS-WIDE ZSL, and accurately classifies unseen and complex multi-word labels.

## Problem

- Traditional multi-label classification systems are limited to predefined labels, failing to recognize new, unseen categories in dynamic environments.
- Existing Multi-Label Zero-Shot Learning methods rely on single-modal textual knowledge, neglecting rich visual semantic information and struggling with complex multi-word 'text labels'.
- Adapting powerful vision-language pre-training (VLP) models for open-vocabulary *multi-label* classification, especially involving unseen text labels, had remained largely unexplored.

## Method

- The ==Multi-modal Knowledge Transfer (MKT)== framework is introduced, utilizing a Vision Transformer as an image encoder and leveraging a pre-trained CLIP model for multi-modal knowledge.
- A ==two-stream module== within the vision transformer extracts both global image features and discriminative local patch features for comprehensive multi-object recognition.
- ==Knowledge distillation== aligns the student model's global image features with robust multi-modal representations from the CLIP image encoder, while ==prompt tuning== refines label embeddings generated by the CLIP text encoder for the multi-label task.

## Results

- The MKT framework achieved new state-of-the-art performance, consistently outperforming prior Multi-Label Zero-Shot Learning methods and a fine-tuned CLIP baseline on NUS-WIDE and Open Images datasets across ZSL and GZSL tasks.
- On NUS-WIDE ZSL, MKT demonstrated an **1**1.7%** absolute mAP gain** over the BiAM method (**37.6%** vs. **25.9%**), and a **6.**5%** mAP gain** over ZS-SDL on GZSL (**18.3%** vs. **12.1%**).
- The framework successfully classifies complex multi-word 'text labels' and demonstrates enhanced localization of objects through Grad-CAM visualizations, all while exhibiting competitive efficiency in terms of parameters and FLOPs.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Multi-modal knowledge from Vision and Language Pre-training (VLP) models provides superior semantic and visual consistency for label embeddings compared to single-modal language models.
> - Knowledge distillation effectively transfers powerful VLP capabilities, aligning image embeddings and enhancing generalization to unseen labels while preventing overfitting.
> - Prompt tuning offers an efficient method to adapt VLP text embeddings, making them more discriminative for specific downstream multi-label tasks without extensive fine-tuning.
> - Combining global and local image feature streams within a two-stream module leads to more robust and discriminative multi-label predictions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses a critical and evolving challenge within computer vision: **open-vocabulary multi-label classification**. It builds upon existing work in multi-label classification and zero-shot learning while pushing into the new frontier of open-vocabulary recognition powered by large-scale pre-trained models.

**Traditional Multi-Label Classification:** The core task involves identifying all relevant labels present in an image. Early methods focused on training independent binary classifiers or exploiting label correlations through structure learning and graph methods. While successful for *seen* labels, these methods fundamentally struggle with *unseen* labels.

**Multi-Label Zero-Shot Learning (ML-ZSL):** To overcome the limitation of unseen labels, ML-ZSL emerged. These methods typically leverage semantic information (like attributes or word embeddings such as GloVe) to transfer knowledge from seen to unseen categories. The primary challenge in ML-ZSL has been aligning image embeddings with relevant label embeddings and understanding relationships between seen and unseen label embeddings. However, existing ML-ZSL methods largely suffer from two key limitations:
1. **Single-modal knowledge:** They primarily rely on textual label embeddings (e.g., GloVe, BERT), which exploit only language-based semantic information. This ignores the rich *visual semantic information* inherent in image-text pairs.
2. **Limited label representation:** While effective for single-word labels, these language-based models often cannot easily extend to or effectively represent complex "text labels" or multi-word phrases (e.g., "black dog," "white cat"), hindering model flexibility and applicability in real-world scenarios where labels are often compositional.

**Emergence of Open-Vocabulary (OV) Recognition and Vision-Language Pre-training (VLP):** In parallel, the field has witnessed a paradigm shift with the advent of large-scale Vision and Language Pre-training (VLP) models, such as CLIP and ALIGN. These models are trained on billions of diverse image-text pairs, acquiring a powerful ability to match images with arbitrary textual descriptions. This "open-vocabulary" capability allows them to recognize concepts not explicitly seen during their pre-training, simply by comparing image features with text embeddings of new concepts. OV methods have achieved impressive performance in tasks like object detection and image segmentation by replacing traditional classification heads with label embeddings derived from VLP text encoders.

**The Gap This Work Addresses:** Despite the success of OV methods, their application to *multi-label classification* has been "less explored." Most existing OV works focus on single-label classification or tasks where bounding box annotations guide localized recognition. Adapting VLP models for multi-label classification, especially to robustly identify multiple objects, including complex unseen text labels, presents unique challenges, such as the need for discriminative feature extraction beyond a single global image representation and handling the lack of direct contrastive loss training for multiple labels.

**This paper's contribution** therefore precisely fits into this gap. It pioneers the integration of the powerful multi-modal knowledge from VLP models into a dedicated framework for open-vocabulary *multi-label* classification. By doing so, it transcends the limitations of traditional ML-ZSL (single-modal knowledge, poor handling of text labels) and extends the state-of-the-art in open-vocabulary recognition to the more complex and practical multi-label setting. It leverages VLP's strengths while introducing specific mechanisms (knowledge distillation, prompt tuning, two-stream feature extraction) to tailor it for the multi-label task, thereby establishing a new research direction.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel and effective open-vocabulary framework for multi-label classification, capable of accurately recognizing both seen and unseen labels, including complex multi-word "text labels," by leveraging multi-modal knowledge.

This overarching objective is driven by several key motivations, primarily arising from the limitations of prior work and the untapped potential of emerging technologies:

1. **Addressing the Challenge of Unseen Labels in Real-World Scenarios:** Traditional multi-label classification systems are limited to a predefined set of labels, failing when encountering new, unseen categories. Real-world applications (e.g., scene understanding, surveillance, self-driving cars) demand systems that can adapt to an ever-expanding vocabulary of objects and concepts, often involving tens of thousands of labels. This necessitates a framework that can generalize to unseen labels without explicit training data for those labels.

2. **Overcoming the Limitations of Single-Modal Knowledge Transfer in ML-ZSL:** Existing Multi-Label Zero-Shot Learning (ML-ZSL) methods primarily rely on single-modal knowledge, typically from pre-trained textual label embeddings (e.g., GloVe, BERT).
 - **Ignoring Rich Semantic Information:** This approach overlooks the rich, visually-grounded semantic information inherent in image-text pairs, which can provide a more robust and comprehensive understanding of concepts.
 - **Ineffectiveness with Text Labels:** These single-modal language models struggle to accurately represent and classify complex, multi-word "text labels" (e.g., "black dog," "white flower") because they lack visual context to interpret the compositionality of such phrases effectively.

3. **Extending Open-Vocabulary Capabilities to Multi-Label Classification:** While recently developed open-vocabulary (OV) methods, based on Vision and Language Pre-training (VLP) models like CLIP, have achieved impressive performance in tasks such as object detection and image segmentation, their application to multi-label classification, especially including unseen text labels, remains largely unexplored. The motivation is to harness the powerful image-text matching ability of VLP models, trained on billions of image-text pairs, and adapt it for the more challenging multi-label recognition task.

4. **Ensuring Consistency and Adaptability of Embeddings:** The success of VLP models hinges on aligning visual and textual embeddings. For multi-label classification, it's crucial to:
 - **Align Image and Label Embeddings:** Ensure that image features from the classification backbone are consistent with the multi-modal label embeddings derived from VLP models.
 - **Optimize Label Embeddings for the Task:** Recognize that generic VLP text embeddings might not be optimal for a specific downstream task like multi-label classification and thus require adaptation.

5. **Robust Feature Extraction for Multiple Objects:** Multi-label classification requires the ability to recognize multiple distinct objects or attributes within a single image. Relying solely on a global image feature might be insufficient. The motivation is to develop a feature extraction mechanism that can capture both broad contextual (global) and specific object-level (local) information to improve multi-object recognition.

In summary, the paper is motivated by the need for more adaptable, robust, and generalizable multi-label classification systems for real-world scenarios, seeking to achieve this by intelligently integrating and transferring multi-modal knowledge from powerful VLP models, while addressing the specific challenges of multi-label recognition.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose a novel open-vocabulary framework called **Multi-modal Knowledge Transfer (MKT)** for multi-label classification. The methodology is designed to leverage the rich multi-modal knowledge from Vision and Language Pre-training (VLP) models and adapt it specifically for the multi-label task, addressing the challenges of unseen labels and complex text labels.

**Overall Framework (Figure 2):**
MKT primarily consists of two main components: a vision transformer acting as the image encoder (student model) and a pre-trained VLP model (specifically CLIP) serving as the source of multi-modal knowledge (teacher model).

1. **Vision Transformer with Two-Stream Module (Image Encoder):**
 - **Backbone:** A Vision Transformer (ViT-B/16), pre-trained on ImageNet-1K, is used as the backbone to extract semantic features from input images. It processes an image by reshaping it into a sequence of flattened 2D patches.
 - **Feature Extraction:** The ViT outputs two types of features: a class token output (`o_cls`) representing the **global feature** of the image, and patch token outputs (`o_patch`) representing **local features** from different regions of the image.
 - **Two-Stream Module:** A simple yet effective module is introduced to process these features. It comprises:
 - **Global Head (Θ_G):** A linear projection layer that maps `o_cls` into a global feature embedding (`e_cls`).
 - **Local Head (Θ_L):** Two linear layers that map `o_patch` into a set of local feature embeddings (`e_patch`).
 - **Final Prediction Score:** The prediction score `s_i` for a given label `i` is calculated by combining the similarity of the label embedding (`z_i`) with both the global and local image embeddings:
 `s_i = 1/2 * <z_i, e_cls> + 1/2 * TopK([<z_i, e_1>, <z_i, e_2>..., <z_i, e_N>])`
 Here, `<..>` denotes inner product, and `TopK` performs top-k mean pooling over the similarities with local patch embeddings, ensuring robustness to localized information.

2. **Vision and Language Pre-training (VLP) Model (CLIP):**
 - **Role:** CLIP (ViT-B/16 image encoder) serves as the pre-trained VLP model. Its image encoder provides knowledge distillation targets, and its text encoder generates initial label embeddings.
 - **Image-Text Matching Ability:** CLIP's training objective is to match paired images and texts, thus its image encoder's output for an image is naturally aligned with its relevant text labels generated by the text encoder.

3. **Knowledge Transfer Mechanisms:**
 - **Knowledge Distillation for Alignment:** To transfer the powerful image-text matching ability from CLIP to the student vision transformer, knowledge distillation is employed. The global feature `o_cls` from the student's vision backbone is encouraged to be similar to the image embedding `o_dist` generated by the **CLIP image encoder** (teacher model).
 `L_dist = ||o_dist - o_cls||_1` (L1-norm loss)
 This ensures the student's global representation aligns with the robust, multi-modal representation learned by CLIP, facilitating generalization to unseen labels. Distillation is applied to global features because `o_cls` and CLIP's image output both correspond to class tokens, and local features need to remain discriminative for multiple objects.
 - **Prompt Tuning for Label Embedding:** Initial label embeddings (`z_i`) are generated by passing a manually designed prompt template, "There is a {label} in the scene," filled with the label name, through the **CLIP text encoder**. Recognizing that these generic embeddings might not be optimal for multi-label classification, **prompt tuning** is introduced. Instead of fine-tuning the entire CLIP text encoder (which could lead to mode collapse due to insufficient data), only a learnable "context embedding" within the prompt template is updated. This adapts the label embeddings to better support the specific classification task while preserving the rich semantic space of CLIP.

4. **Loss Functions and Two-Stage Training:**
 The training process is divided into two stages:
 - **Stage 1:** The vision encoder (ViT backbone and two-stream module) is trained. The label embeddings are fixed using the pre-trained CLIP text encoder with the manual prompt. The loss function combines a ranking loss (`L_rank`) to correctly rank positive labels higher than negative ones for multi-label prediction, and the knowledge distillation loss (`L_dist`):
 `L_stage1 = L_rank + λL_dist` (where λ is a weight factor).
 - **Stage 2:** The vision encoder parameters are frozen. Only the learnable "context embedding" within the prompt template for label generation is fine-tuned using the ranking loss:
 `L_stage2 = L_rank`. This stage refines the label embeddings to be more discriminative for the multi-label task.

**Implementation Details:**
- **Datasets:** NUS-WIDE and Open Images (v4) datasets are used for evaluation, with standard seen/unseen splits for ZSL and GZSL tasks.
- **Hyperparameters:** Specific values for top-k pooling (`k=18`), distillation weight (`λ=1`), learning rates, and batch sizes are meticulously chosen for each stage and dataset.

This systematic approach combines a robust vision backbone with advanced VLP knowledge transfer techniques and a tailored multi-label feature processing module, positioning MKT as a comprehensive solution for open-vocabulary multi-label classification.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The MKT framework demonstrates significant advancements in open-vocabulary multi-label classification, establishing new state-of-the-art performance and providing comprehensive insights into the effectiveness of its components.

1. **State-of-the-Art Performance:**
 - **Outperformance over ML-ZSL Methods:** MKT consistently and significantly outperforms previous Multi-Label Zero-Shot Learning (ML-ZSL) methods (e.g., LESA, ZS-SDL, BiAM) on both NUS-WIDE and Open Images datasets across both Zero-Shot Learning (ZSL) and Generalized Zero-Shot Learning (GZSL) tasks. For instance, on NUS-WIDE ZSL, MKT achieves 37.6% mAP, an absolute gain of 11.7% over BiAM (25.9%). In GZSL, MKT shows 18.3% mAP, surpassing ZS-SDL (12.1%) by 6.5%.
 - **Superiority over Fine-tuned CLIP Baseline (CLIP-FT):** Even a direct fine-tuning of CLIP (CLIP-FT) with ranking loss serves as a strong baseline, often outperforming traditional ML-ZSL methods. However, MKT consistently surpasses CLIP-FT, demonstrating the added value of its specific design choices for multi-label recognition.

2. **Effectiveness of Knowledge Distillation:**
 - Ablation studies reveal that incorporating knowledge distillation substantially improves performance on both ZSL and GZSL tasks. The distillation loss helps align the student image embeddings with the robust, multi-modal representations learned by the CLIP image encoder. This not only facilitates generalization to unseen labels but also helps suppress overfitting to seen labels during training. The optimal performance is observed when the distillation weight `λ` is around 1, indicating a balanced influence.

3. **Impact of Prompt Tuning:**
 - Prompt tuning further enhances the model's performance beyond the benefits of knowledge distillation. By adapting the context embedding within the prompt template, the label embeddings generated by the VLP text encoder become more optimized and discriminative for the specific multi-label classification task. Qualitative analysis (label retrieval and t-SNE visualization) shows that prompt-tuned embeddings capture both semantic and *visual* consistency more effectively than standard CLIP embeddings or other language models.

4. **Superiority of VLP-based Label Embeddings:**
 - A critical finding is that VLP model-based label embeddings (e.g., from CLIP) significantly outperform traditional language model embeddings like GloVe. VLP embeddings capture both semantic and visual consistency among labels, which is crucial for generalizing to unseen categories and recognizing subtle visual differences. Retrieval experiments vividly illustrate this, showing VLP embeddings retrieving visually and semantically similar labels (e.g., "girls" retrieving "man", "kid", "person") more accurately than GloVe or BERT.

5. **Effectiveness of the Two-Stream Module:**
 - Ablation studies confirm the value of combining local and global features. The global head performs well on mAP, while the local head contributes to better F1 scores, indicating that global representations are more general, and local representations are more discriminative. The combination of both streams in the proposed module leverages these complementary strengths, leading to more discriminative predictions while maintaining robustness to noise, resulting in the best overall performance.

6. **Enhanced Text-Label Classification Capability:**
 - MKT demonstrates superior handling of "text labels" (multi-word labels like "black dog"). On a specially constructed text-label dataset from Open Images, MKT significantly outperforms BiAM, affirming its flexibility and robust representation of complex concepts by treating them as a coherent whole rather than individual words.

7. **Robustness to Backbone Variants and Efficiency:**
 - The MKT framework is shown to be robust and adaptable to different vision backbones (e.g., VGG-19, DINO ResNet-50), consistently outperforming prior ML-ZSL methods with comparable backbones. Furthermore, MKT achieves its superior performance with fewer parameters and FLOPs compared to the BiAM baseline, indicating practical efficiency.

8. **Qualitative Insights:**
 - Visualizations of predictions show that MKT produces more diverse and precise labels compared to baselines. Grad-CAM visualizations demonstrate that MKT can localize relevant image regions more accurately, focusing on specific objects (e.g., "boat") rather than broad irrelevant areas.

In conclusion, the main findings strongly validate the MKT framework's ability to effectively integrate multi-modal knowledge for open-vocabulary multi-label classification, setting new performance standards and showcasing the critical contributions of its core components.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in "Open-Vocabulary Multi-Label Classification via Multi-Modal Knowledge Transfer" holds significant implications and potential impact across several dimensions of computer vision and artificial intelligence.

1. **Pioneering Open-Vocabulary Multi-Label Classification:** This work is explicitly stated as "the first work to explore open-vocabulary multi-label classification task." This establishes a new research frontier by bridging the gap between existing multi-label zero-shot learning and the powerful capabilities of vision and language pre-training (VLP) models. It opens up a new avenue for research, encouraging the community to further explore and refine methodologies for this challenging and practical task.

2. **Enhanced Real-World Applicability of Multi-Label Systems:** The ability to classify unseen labels, including complex multi-word "text labels," is a critical requirement for real-world computer vision applications. Systems that can only recognize a fixed, pre-defined set of labels quickly become obsolete or limited in dynamic environments. MKT's capacity to generalize to arbitrary new concepts significantly boosts the practical utility of multi-label recognition in areas such as:
 - **Scene Understanding:** More nuanced descriptions of complex scenes, going beyond pre-programmed object lists.
 - **Surveillance Systems:** Detecting novel events or objects not explicitly trained for.
 - **Self-Driving Cars:** Adapting to new road conditions, signs, or unexpected objects.
 - **Image Retrieval and Annotation:** Enabling more flexible and human-like querying and description of image content.

3. **Demonstrating the Power of Multi-Modal Knowledge Transfer:** The paper strongly validates the hypothesis that multi-modal knowledge from large-scale VLP models (like CLIP) is vastly superior to single-modal semantic embeddings (like GloVe) for robust visual recognition tasks. This encourages a broader shift in the community towards integrating vision and language understanding for more effective AI systems, particularly when dealing with semantic generalization.

4. **Methodological Contributions for VLP Adaptation:** The specific techniques introduced within MKT—knowledge distillation for aligning image embeddings with VLP representations, prompt tuning for adapting label embeddings to a specific downstream task, and the two-stream module for capturing both local and global features—are valuable contributions in their own right. These methods can be adapted and extended to other open-vocabulary and multi-modal tasks, providing a blueprint for effectively transferring knowledge from powerful pre-trained models. Prompt tuning, in particular, offers an efficient way to adapt large language models without extensive fine-tuning, a topic of significant interest in current AI research.

5. **Setting New Benchmarks:** By achieving new state-of-the-art results on challenging datasets like NUS-WIDE and Open Images, MKT provides strong baselines for future research in open-vocabulary multi-label classification. These benchmarks serve as crucial reference points for subsequent improvements and comparisons, driving further innovation in the field.

6. **Addressing Compositionality in Labels:** The demonstrated ability to handle "text labels" effectively (e.g., "black dog") is a significant step towards enabling AI systems to understand and recognize compositional concepts, which is a hallmark of human language and perception. This moves beyond simple object recognition to more complex attribute-object relationships.

7. **Efficiency and Accessibility:** Achieving superior performance with fewer parameters and FLOPs compared to a competitive baseline (BiAM) suggests that MKT is not only effective but also relatively efficient, making it more accessible for deployment in resource-constrained environments or for large-scale applications.

In essence, MKT significantly pushes the boundaries of what is achievable in multi-label classification, making AI systems more adaptable, intelligent, and capable of operating in dynamic, open-ended environments. Its contributions are poised to inspire a wave of future research at the intersection of computer vision and natural language processing, particularly in leveraging multi-modal pre-training for challenging real-world tasks.
