# AbstRaL: Augmenting LLMs' Reasoning by Reinforcing Abstract Thinking

> [!summary] Summary
> The ABSTRAL framework augments Large Language Models' reasoning capabilities by training them to perform abstract thinking using structured data and reinforcement learning. It notably improves model robustness to various input perturbations and boosts out-of-distribution generalization on mathematical and general reasoning benchmarks.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) exhibit fragility and poor out-of-distribution generalization, especially when faced with subtle shifts in problem statements like varied numbers, rephrasing, or distracting information.
- LLMs experience significant performance degradation on reasoning tasks due to 'instantiation shifts' and more challenging 'interferential shifts'.
- Current data augmentation methods aimed at improving robustness are computationally intensive, demanding vast amounts of varied training instances.

## Method

- Introduction of the AbstRaL framework, which integrates neural language understanding with symbolic reasoning through a four-step pipeline: ==Condition Recognition, Abstract Reasoning, Abstraction Retrieval, and Symbolic Derivation==.
- Development of ==Granularly-decomposed Abstract Reasoning (GranulAR)== data to teach LLMs to generate abstract answers with explicit reasoning steps and symbolic derivations.
- Application of ==Reinforcement Learning (RL) using GRPO== with novel model-free rewards, specifically a binary answer correctness reward and a ==symbolic distance reward==, to guide the learning of faithful abstractions.

## Results

- AbstRaL almost entirely reverted performance drops on instantiation shifts, achieving a **-1.27%** change for Qwen2.5-0.5B on GSM-Symbolic (compared to a **10.58%** drop for CoT-8S).
- Significantly mitigated the negative impact of distracting information (interferential shifts), raising accuracy for Qwen2.5-0.5B to **36.5%** on GSM-Plus 'Distract' versus **15.2%** for CoT-RL.
- Demonstrated improved zero-shot performance across a wide range of out-of-distribution datasets, including diverse mathematical (e.g., MATH, Minerva MATH) and general reasoning tasks (e.g., MMLU, BBH).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly teaching LLMs to identify and operate on the abstract, high-level reasoning schema underlying problems significantly improves their invariance to surface-form variations.
> - The GranulAR data format, which provides fine-grained sub-questions and explicitly quotes input conditions, is crucial for structured planning and effectively filtering out distracting information.
> - Reinforcement learning with specific model-free rewards (answer correctness and symbolic distance) is vital for training LLMs to generate faithful and accurate abstract reasoning, moving beyond supervised fine-tuning limitations.
