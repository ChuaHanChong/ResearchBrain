# AbstRaL: Augmenting LLMs' Reasoning by Reinforcing Abstract Thinking

> [!summary] Summary
> The ABSTRAL framework augments Large Language Models' reasoning capabilities by training them to perform abstract thinking using structured data and reinforcement learning. It notably improves model robustness to various input perturbations and boosts out-of-distribution generalization on mathematical and general reasoning benchmarks.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) exhibit fragility and poor out-of-distribution generalization, especially when faced with subtle shifts in problem statements like varied numbers, rephrasing, or distracting information.
- LLMs experience significant performance degradation on reasoning tasks due to 'instantiation shifts' and more challenging 'interferential shifts'.
- Current data augmentation methods aimed at improving robustness are computationally intensive, demanding vast amounts of varied training instances.

## Method

- Introduction of the AbstRaL framework, which integrates neural language understanding with symbolic reasoning through a four-step pipeline: ==Condition Recognition, Abstract Reasoning, Abstraction Retrieval, and Symbolic Derivation==.
- Development of ==Granularly-decomposed Abstract Reasoning (GranulAR)== data to teach LLMs to generate abstract answers with explicit reasoning steps and symbolic derivations.
- Application of ==Reinforcement Learning (RL) using GRPO== with novel model-free rewards, specifically a binary answer correctness reward and a ==symbolic distance reward==, to guide the learning of faithful abstractions.

## Results

- AbstRaL almost entirely reverted performance drops on instantiation shifts, achieving a **-1.27%** change for Qwen2.5-0.5B on GSM-Symbolic (compared to a **10.58%** drop for CoT-8S).
- Significantly mitigated the negative impact of distracting information (interferential shifts), raising accuracy for Qwen2.5-0.5B to **36.5%** on GSM-Plus 'Distract' versus **15.2%** for CoT-RL.
- Demonstrated improved zero-shot performance across a wide range of out-of-distribution datasets, including diverse mathematical (e.g., MATH, Minerva MATH) and general reasoning tasks (e.g., MMLU, BBH).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly teaching LLMs to identify and operate on the abstract, high-level reasoning schema underlying problems significantly improves their invariance to surface-form variations.
> - The GranulAR data format, which provides fine-grained sub-questions and explicitly quotes input conditions, is crucial for structured planning and effectively filtering out distracting information.
> - Reinforcement learning with specific model-free rewards (answer correctness and symbolic distance) is vital for training LLMs to generate faithful and accurate abstract reasoning, moving beyond supervised fine-tuning limitations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of artificial intelligence is actively pursuing advancements in reasoning capabilities for large language models (LLMs), considering it an essential quality for artificial general intelligence. While LLMs have demonstrated impressive reasoning abilities across general and specialized domains such as mathematics, a persistent challenge remains in their robustness and out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization. Specifically, LLMs, particularly smaller models, frequently exhibit performance degradation when faced with distribution shifts in reasoning tasks.

Previous research has identified these robustness issues in grade school math (GSM) tasks, where performance drops occur with changes to numerical or nominal variables (instantiation shifts) or the introduction of distracting clauses (interferential shifts). A common strategy to address this involves data augmentation, where synthetic data is generated to "instantiate" reasoning problems with various surface-form contexts. This approach aims to equip LLMs with a broader understanding of high-level reasoning schemas.

This paper positions itself as an alternative to this data augmentation paradigm. Instead of synthesizing more instances, the authors propose a strategy focused on "abstracting" reasoning problems. This approach is rooted in the abstract reasoning literature and aims to teach LLMs to directly learn the underlying abstract structure of problems. This is hypothesized to not only counteract distribution shifts by promoting invariant reasoning but also facilitate the integration of symbolic tools for solution derivation. The work thus contributes to the ongoing effort to enhance LLM reasoning beyond rote memorization or surface-level pattern matching, moving towards more generalized and robust cognitive processes.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the reasoning robustness and OOD generalization capabilities of large language models (LLMs), particularly smaller ones, within the domain of grade school mathematics (GSM).

The motivation stems from observed limitations in current LLMs:
- **Lack of Robustness to Distribution Shifts:** Existing LLMs, especially smaller variants, exhibit performance drops when presented with variations in problem surface forms. These variations include "instantiation shifts," such as altering numerical values or nominal variables (e.g., names), and "interferential shifts," which involve adding distracting, yet topic-related, clauses. Despite solving original problems correctly, LLMs often struggle with these perturbed versions, indicating a lack of robust reasoning.
- **Inefficiency of Data Augmentation:** While synthesizing additional instances of reasoning problems through paraphrasing or templating can improve robustness, this strategy typically requires generating a large volume of synthetic data. This can be computationally expensive and may not fully address the underlying issue of generalized abstract understanding.
- **Limitations of Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) for Abstraction:** Initial attempts to teach abstract reasoning through SFT have shown that LLMs often fail to produce "faithful" abstractions. This is attributed to the auto-regressive nature of SFT, which can lead models to overfit to specific contexts within the training data, hindering the acquisition of more general abstract thinking strategies.

To address these motivations, the paper outlines several key objectives:
- **Develop a Novel Learning Strategy:** Propose and implement a learning framework, termed AbstRaL (Reinforced Abstraction Learning), that teaches LLMs to directly learn the abstract, high-level reasoning schema underlying GSM problems.
- **Integrate Abstract Thinking with Symbolic Tools:** Design a pipeline that enables LLMs to generate mathematical abstractions from input problems and then leverage external symbolic tools (e.g., equation solvers) to stably derive solutions based on these abstractions and identified input conditions.
- **Construct Granular Abstract Reasoning Data:** Create a specialized dataset, GranulAR (Granularly-decomposed Abstract Reasoning), which integrates abstract reasoning with existing fine-grained reasoning strategies like socratic problem decomposition and chain-of-thought (CoT) explanations. This data format aims to facilitate the gradual construction of abstractions within a structured reasoning chain.
- **Apply Reinforcement Learning (RL) for Abstraction Faithfulness:** Introduce an RL approach, building upon SFT, that utilizes a new set of model-free rewards to specifically improve the fidelity and correctness of the generated abstractions.
- **Evaluate Robustness against Perturbations:** Assess AbstRaL’s effectiveness in mitigating performance degradation on established GSM perturbation benchmarks (GSM-Symbolic and GSM-Plus), focusing on its ability to handle variations in relevant conditions and the interference of distracting information.
- **Investigate Generalizability to OOD Tasks:** Determine whether improving abstract thinking in the GSM domain implicitly benefits LLMs’ capabilities on a wider range of OOD mathematical and general reasoning tasks, thereby indicating broader generalizability.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology, AbstRaL (Reinforced Abstraction Learning Framework), aims to teach LLMs abstract thinking for GSM problem-solving. It consists of a four-step pipeline, supported by a novel data construction process and a reinforcement learning scheme.

**AbstRaL Framework (Four Steps):**
The framework decomposes the task of inferring an abstraction (A) from an input question (X) into a fine-grained pipeline: X → X_A → Y_A → A → Y.

1. **Condition Recognition:**
 - The initial step involves parsing the input GSM question (X) to identify all relevant numerical and nominal conditions (C).
 - These conditions are then formulated using abstract symbols (e.g., `in0`, `in1`) to represent input variables.
 - An "abstract input question" (X_A) is constructed by replacing specific values or texts in X with their assigned abstract symbols, enclosed in square brackets.
 - This step can be implemented using either a symbolic tool (e.g., regex-matching for numerical values) or a neural parser (e.g., a prompted oracle LLM). In the experiments, a Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct model with few-shot prompting was used.

2. **Abstract Reasoning:**
 - This is the core learning step where LLMs are trained to generate an "abstract answer" (Y_A) in response to the abstract question (X_A).
 - Y_A is designed to use abstract symbols for both input variables (quoted from X_A) and derived output variables (e.g., `out0`), with mathematical derivations enclosed in double angle brackets.
 - The learning for this step is driven by GranulAR data through a combination of Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning (RL).

3. **Abstraction Retrieval:**
 - Following the generation of Y_A, the de-contextualized "abstraction" (A) is extracted.
 - This abstraction consists of the sequence of mathematical derivations defined by abstract symbols (e.g., `in0*in1=out0`).
 - A regex-matching tool is employed to extract all derivations enclosed in double angle brackets from Y_A.

4. **Symbolic Derivation:**
 - In the final step, the retrieved abstraction (A) is combined with the specific input conditions (C) identified in step 1.
 - These are treated as a system of equations and fed into an external symbolic tool (e.g., SymPy equation solver) to derive the concrete, final answer (Y) to the original question.

**Granularly-decomposed Abstract Reasoning (GranulAR) Data:**
- **Motivation:** To integrate abstract reasoning with existing LLM fine-grained reasoning strategies (like Chain-of-Thought (CoT) and socratic problem decomposition), making abstract thinking acquisition easier.
- **Format (Y_A):** GranulAR answers first decompose the abstract question (X_A) into a list of sub-questions for holistic planning. Each sub-question is then answered with CoT, quoting relevant input conditions or prior sub-question answers using abstract symbols, followed by a symbolic derivation. The final answer is explicitly linked to an output abstract symbol.
- **Construction:**
 - Start with original GSM8K socratic CoT data (X, Y).
 - Perform condition recognition to get C and X_A.
 - An oracle LLM (Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct) is prompted to rewrite the gold socratic CoT answer (Y) into the GranulAR format (Y_A), incorporating X_A for context. This involves a two-step rewriting process: (1) replacing numerical values with abstract symbols and identifying derivations, and (2) restructuring into the granularly-decomposed format.
 - Abstraction (A) is retrieved from Y_A.
 - A verification step ensures that A, combined with C via symbolic derivation, yields the correct final answer (Y). Only correctly rewritten and verified instances are retained for training.

**Learning of Abstract Reasoning:**
- **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** LLMs are initially fine-tuned to auto-regressively generate Y_A given X_A, using a causal language modeling loss. This teaches the model the desired format.
- **Reinforcement Learning (RL) with Abstraction Rewards:**
 - To address SFT's tendency to overfit contexts and improve the faithfulness of generated abstractions, RL is applied on top of SFT.
 - The RL algorithm used is GRPO (Shao etal., 2024).
 - **Model-free Rewards:**
 - `r_answer (eA, C, Ans)`: Rewards are given based on the correctness of the final answer derived from the *model-generated* abstraction (`eA`) and conditions (`C`) compared to the *gold* answer (`Ans`). A positive reward (`r_correct`) is given for a match, otherwise zero.
 - `r_symbolic (eA, A)`: This reward granularly measures the alignment between the *model-generated* tokenized abstraction (`eA`) and the *gold* tokenized abstraction (`A`). It is calculated as `r_max * (1 - EditDistance(eA, A) / max_length_of_abstractions)`. A higher reward is granted for closer similarity, providing fine-grained feedback.
 - The GRPO objective is maximized, using a group relative advantage calculated from the sum of `r_answer` and `r_symbolic`, alongside a KL divergence regularization against the SFT-trained reference policy.

**Experimental Settings:**
- **LLMs Tested:** Llama3 (1B, 3B, 8B), Qwen2.5 (0.5B, 1.5B, 3B, 7B, Math-7B), and Mathstral-7B.
- **Baselines:** 8-shot Chain-of-Thought (CoT-8S), 8-shot Program-of-Thought (PoT-8S), CoT with RL (CoT-RL), Chain-of-Abstraction (CoA), Abstraction-of-Thought (AoT), and Symbolic Reasoning Language Model (SyReLM).
- **Evaluation Datasets:** GSM-Symbolic (perturbations on numbers, names, or both) and GSM-Plus (digit expansion, integer-decimal-fraction conversion, numerical substitution, rephrasing, distractor insertion), plus a wide range of OOD math and general reasoning datasets (e.g., MATH, AIME, MMLU, BBH).
- **Ablation Studies:** Conducted to isolate the contributions of symbolic tools, intermediate contexts, RL, specific reward components, and the GranulAR data format.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of AbstRaL yielded several findings regarding its effectiveness in improving reasoning robustness and generalizability.

#### 4.1. GSM Reasoning Robustness

- **Mitigation of Performance Degradation:** AbstRaL consistently improved reasoning robustness across all tested LLMs (ranging from 0.5B to 7B/8B parameters) on both GSM-Symbolic and GSM-Plus datasets. It effectively mitigated the performance drops associated with distribution shifts.
- **Variations of Relevant Conditions (GSM-Symbolic, GSM-Plus "Num. Pert."):** On the GSM-Symbolic dataset's "Vary Both" (numerical and nominal variable changes), LLMs trained with AbstRaL exhibited consistently higher average accuracy and lower standard deviations across multiple evaluation rounds compared to baseline methods. The performance drop (Δ) from original problems to perturbed ones was significantly reduced, sometimes even showing a slight increase in performance (negative Δ), suggesting that AbstRaL helps overcome overfitting to specific input conditions. Similarly, on GSM-Plus's numerical perturbation categories (Digit Expansion, Int-Dec-Fra, Num. Sub.), AbstRaL almost reverted the performance degradation.
- **Contextual Variations (GSM-Plus "Rephrase"):** AbstRaL maintained robustness to problem rephrasing, performing comparably or better than baseline methods.
- **Distracting Conditions (GSM-Plus "Distract"):** AbstRaL significantly mitigated the performance interference caused by distracting clauses inserted into problems. In contrast, LLMs using baseline reasoning methods experienced more substantial performance drops when faced with these interferential shifts. This improvement is attributed to AbstRaL's granularly-decomposed abstract reasoning (GranulAR) data, which supports holistic planning of reasoning steps and selective identification of useful input conditions.
- **Overfitting Reduction:** For some larger LLMs (e.g., Qwen2.5-Math-7B-Instruct), AbstRaL, while occasionally scoring slightly lower on the "Origin 100" (original GSM8K) dataset compared to some CoT baselines, demonstrated superior performance on perturbed samples (e.g., "Vary Both"). This suggests that AbstRaL may reduce LLMs' overfitting to existing input conditions that could be a result of data contamination during pre-training or post-training.

**Ablation Studies:**
- **Contribution of Abstract Reasoning (w/o Tools):** Replacing external symbolic tools (for condition recognition, abstraction retrieval, and symbolic derivation) with the LLM itself resulted in only a minor performance drop. This indicates that the primary source of improvement is the LLM's learned abstract reasoning capability, rather than merely the integration of powerful external tools.
- **Importance of Contexts (w/o Contexts):** Training LLMs to directly generate de-contextualized abstractions (A) from abstract questions (X_A) without generating intermediate abstract answers (Y_A) led to a substantial performance decrease. This suggests that narrowing the gap between symbolic abstraction and natural language through intermediate contextual representations (Y_A) is crucial for faithful abstract thinking.
- **Role of Reinforcement Learning (w/o RL):** Removing the RL component entirely resulted in significantly lower scores on both original and perturbed datasets, demonstrating the necessity of RL for acquiring robust abstract reasoning.
- **Significance of Symbolic Distance Reward (w/o r_symbolic):** Ablating only the symbolic distance reward (`r_symbolic`) also caused a notable performance drop, even below some prompting baselines. This highlights that `r_symbolic` provides essential fine-grained feedback for steering the model towards generating correct abstractions.
- **Impact of Granular Data (w/o GranulAR):** Training LLMs on a simplified abstract data format (without explicit sub-question listing and quoting of input conditions at each step) resulted in a drastic performance drop on the "Distract" testing set. This confirms that the fine-grained reasoning format of GranulAR data is critical for identifying and ignoring distracting conditions effectively.

#### 4.2. Generalization to OOD Tasks

- **Implicit Benefit to General Reasoning:** AbstRaL-trained LLMs consistently outperformed baseline methods (Original SFT, CoT-RL, CoA) across a broad spectrum of OOD mathematical datasets (e.g., MATH, AIME, SVAMP, TABMWP) and general reasoning datasets (e.g., MMLU, BBH, ARC-Challenge, OpenBookQA).
- **Beyond Domain Specificity:** This generalization occurred even though the GSM reasoning scheme learned by AbstRaL might not be directly applicable to solving all OOD tasks, indicating that the acquisition of abstract thinking in one domain (GSM) can implicitly enhance broader mathematical and general reasoning capabilities.
- **Specificity of Learning Approach:** In contrast to AbstRaL, baselines like CoT-RL and CoA did not consistently improve upon the original model checkpoints for OOD tasks, suggesting that the "implicit benefit" of abstract thinking requires a specific and carefully designed learning methodology like AbstRaL.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "ABSTRAL: AUGMENTING LLMS’ REASONING BY REINFORCING ABSTRACT THINKING" carries several significant implications and potential impacts on the field of large language models and artificial intelligence.

**1. Enhanced Reasoning Robustness:** The primary significance lies in AbstRaL's demonstrated ability to substantially improve the robustness of LLMs, particularly smaller models, against various forms of distribution shifts. By effectively mitigating performance degradation due to instantiation shifts (e.g., changes in numerical or nominal variables) and interferential shifts (e.g., distracting clauses), the method addresses a critical vulnerability in current LLM reasoning. This makes LLMs more reliable in real-world scenarios where input variations are common.

**2. Promotion of Abstract Thinking:** The research underscores the importance of abstract thinking as a mechanism for robust generalization in LLMs. By explicitly teaching models to learn and utilize the underlying abstract structure of problems, AbstRaL moves beyond surface-form pattern matching, fostering a deeper, more generalized understanding of reasoning principles. This aligns with the concept of general fluid intelligence, suggesting that cultivating abstract thought processes in LLMs can broadly enhance their cognitive abilities.

**3. Novel Methodological Contributions:**
 - **Abstraction-Focused Learning:** AbstRaL offers a distinct paradigm compared to prevalent data augmentation strategies. Instead of computationally expensive generation of numerous reasoning instances, it focuses on teaching LLMs to identify and leverage abstract schemas, potentially offering a more efficient route to robustness.
 - **GranulAR Data Format:** The introduction of Granularly-decomposed Abstract Reasoning (GranulAR) data provides a structured, fine-grained approach to teaching abstract reasoning. By integrating socratic problem decomposition and CoT with abstract symbols, it facilitates gradual and comprehensible abstraction construction, particularly valuable for handling complex reasoning steps and identifying relevant information.
 - **Model-Free Abstraction Rewards:** The development of model-free rewards (answer correctness and symbolic distance rewards) for reinforcement learning offers a practical and efficient mechanism to guide LLMs towards generating faithful abstractions without requiring a separate reward model.

**4. Demonstrated Generalizability to OOD Tasks:** A key impact of this work is the finding that improving abstract thinking in one domain (GSM) implicitly enhances LLMs' capabilities across a wide range of OOD mathematical and general reasoning tasks. This suggests that the abstract reasoning skills acquired are not merely task-specific but contribute to a more fundamental improvement in general reasoning abilities, indicating a pathway towards more generally intelligent AI systems.

**5. Potential for Smaller, More Efficient Models:** The improvements observed across a spectrum of LLM sizes, including smaller models (e.g., 0.5B), suggest that AbstRaL could enable more capable and robust reasoning in models with fewer parameters. This has implications for deploying efficient AI solutions in resource-constrained environments.

**6. Future Research Directions:** The paper opens several avenues for future theoretical and practical research. This includes formalizing the theoretical benefits of abstraction on learning complexity and robustness, extending the method to other complex domains beyond mathematics, exploring more computationally efficient training schemes (like LoRA), and investigating advanced decoding strategies.

In summary, AbstRaL represents a significant step towards developing LLMs that reason more robustly and generalize more effectively by grounding their problem-solving in abstract principles. This approach has the potential to contribute to the development of more reliable, intelligent, and broadly capable AI systems.
