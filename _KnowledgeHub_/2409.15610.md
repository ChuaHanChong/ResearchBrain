# Full-Order Sampling-Based MPC for Torque-Level Locomotion Control via Diffusion-Style Annealing

> [!summary] Summary
> DIAL-MPC, a sampling-based Model Predictive Control framework, enables real-time optimization using full-order robot dynamics by applying principles from diffusion models. The approach demonstrates 13.4 times lower tracking error than standard MPPI and outperforms trained reinforcement learning policies by 50% in challenging climbing tasks, all without requiring any training.

## Problem

- Controlling legged robots in real-time using full-order dynamics models is computationally challenging for traditional nonlinear model predictive control (NMPC) methods.
- Reinforcement learning (RL) approaches require extensive training and often lack generalization capabilities for diverse legged locomotion tasks.
- Simplified models used in traditional MPC (e.g., LIPM, SRBM) limit the robot's ability to perform highly dynamic behaviors requiring precise joint-level dynamics and contact force modeling.

## Method

- ==DIAL-MPC== introduces a dual-loop annealing process for sampling-based Model Predictive Control, systematically refining control sequences through trajectory-level and action-level adjustments.
- The framework iteratively refines a running estimate of the optimal control sequence by sampling perturbations around it, evaluating costs using a full-order dynamics model, and updating the estimate with importance-weighted averaging.
- It leverages parallel computation capabilities (e.g., JAX on GPUs) to evaluate thousands of sampled trajectories in parallel, making full-order dynamics optimization computationally feasible for real-time applications.

## Results

- DIAL-MPC achieves **13.4** times lower tracking error compared to standard MPPI in quadrupedal velocity tracking tasks.
- The method outperforms trained RL policies by **50%** in challenging climbing tasks without requiring any prior training.
- Despite optimizing over full-order dynamics, DIAL-MPC maintains real-time performance with control frequencies up to 50 Hz, validated through successful real-world experiments on a quadrupedal robot performing precise velocity tracking and payload jumping.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) can be viewed as a single-step diffusion process, providing a theoretical foundation for applying diffusion-inspired annealing to improve sampling-based MPC.
> - The proposed dual-loop annealing strategy, with decreasing trajectory-level sampling variance and time-varying action-level variance, enables the algorithm to escape local minima early while converging to precise solutions later.
> - Applying different sampling variances across the prediction horizon (higher for future actions, lower for immediate actions) optimizes control sequences more effectively by reflecting the varying importance of actions over time.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

Controlling legged robots in real-time using full-order dynamics models presents a significant computational challenge. Traditional nonlinear model predictive control (NMPC) methods struggle with the high-dimensional, contact-rich nature of legged locomotion, while reinforcement learning (RL) approaches require extensive training and lack generalization capabilities. This paper introduces DIAL-MPC (Diffusion-Inspired Annealing for Locomotion Model Predictive Control), a novel sampling-based MPC framework that bridges these limitations by incorporating principles from diffusion models into trajectory optimization.

The research addresses a fundamental tension in robotics control: the trade-off between model accuracy and computational efficiency. While simplified models enable real-time control, they sacrifice the precision needed for highly dynamic maneuvers. DIAL-MPC resolves this by making full-order dynamics optimization computationally tractable through a principled annealing process that gradually refines control sequences.

### 2. Background and Problem Setting

Model Predictive Control has been a cornerstone of robotics control, particularly for systems with complex dynamics and constraints. In legged robotion, MPC typically operates on simplified models such as the Linear Inverted Pendulum Model (LIPM) or Single Rigid Body Model (SRBM) to maintain computational efficiency. However, these simplifications limit the robot's ability to perform highly dynamic behaviors that require accurate modeling of joint-level dynamics, contact forces, and system nonlinearities.

Sampling-based MPC methods, particularly Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) control, offer an alternative by using Monte Carlo sampling to approximate optimal control solutions. MPPI formulates control as a path integral problem where the optimal control is computed as a weighted average of sampled trajectories. The weight of each trajectory is determined by its cost through the relation:

```
w_i = exp(-J(τ_i)/λ)
```

where `J(τ_i)` is the cost of trajectory `τ_i` and `λ` is a temperature parameter. While MPPI can handle complex dynamics and constraints, it suffers from high variance and sensitivity to hyperparameter tuning, particularly in high-dimensional control spaces.

Recent advances in deep learning, particularly diffusion models, have demonstrated remarkable success in generating high-quality samples through iterative denoising processes. Diffusion models work by gradually adding noise to data during training, then learning to reverse this process for generation. This iterative refinement principle offers insights for improving sampling-based optimization in robotics.

### 3. The DIAL-MPC Framework

DIAL-MPC introduces a dual-loop annealing process that systematically refines control sequences through both trajectory-level and action-level adjustments. The framework operates on two nested loops:

**Trajectory-level Annealing**: This outer loop progressively reduces the sampling variance across iterations, starting with broad exploration and gradually focusing on promising regions of the control space. The temperature parameter λ follows an annealing schedule that balances exploration and exploitation.

**Action-level Annealing**: This inner loop applies different sampling variances across the prediction horizon, with higher variance for actions further in the future and lower variance for immediate actions. This reflects the principle that near-term actions should be more precisely optimized than distant future actions.

The algorithm maintains a running estimate of the optimal control sequence that is iteratively refined through the annealing process. At each iteration, the algorithm samples perturbations around the current best estimate, evaluates their costs using the full-order dynamics model, and updates the estimate using importance-weighted averaging.

The mathematical formulation of DIAL-MPC builds upon the MPPI framework but introduces time-varying covariance matrices that encode the annealing schedule:

```
Σ_t^{(k)} = σ_traj^{(k)} · σ_action(t) · I
```

where `σ_traj^{(k)}` decreases with iteration `k` and `σ_action(t)` varies with time horizon `t`.

### 4. Theoretical Foundation

A key theoretical contribution of this work is establishing the connection between MPPI and diffusion processes. The authors demonstrate that MPPI can be viewed as a single-step diffusion process where the sampling distribution evolves according to the cost landscape. This insight motivates the use of diffusion-inspired annealing to improve convergence properties.

The theoretical analysis shows that the proposed annealing schedule provides better coverage of the solution space while maintaining convergence guarantees. Unlike standard MPPI, which uses fixed sampling variance, DIAL-MPC's adaptive variance allows it to escape local minima early in the optimization while converging to precise solutions in later iterations.

The connection to diffusion models also provides theoretical justification for the action-level annealing strategy. Just as diffusion models apply different noise levels at different time steps, DIAL-MPC applies different sampling variances across the prediction horizon to reflect the varying importance of actions at different time scales.

### 5. Implementation and Experimental Setup

DIAL-MPC is implemented in JAX using the Brax physics simulator, which provides automatic differentiation and parallel computation capabilities essential for real-time performance. The implementation leverages GPU acceleration to evaluate thousands of sampled trajectories in parallel, making full-order dynamics optimization computationally feasible.

The experimental evaluation covers multiple challenging locomotion tasks:

1. **Velocity Tracking**: Precise control of quadruped locomotion at various speeds
2. **Agile Climbing**: Navigating steep terrain and obstacles
3. **Payload Jumping**: Dynamic jumping maneuvers while carrying additional mass
4. **Humanoid Locomotion**: Extending the approach to bipedal robots

The full-order dynamics model includes detailed joint-level dynamics, contact modeling, and actuator constraints. The cost function typically includes terms for tracking error, control effort, stability, and task-specific objectives.

### 6. Results and Performance Analysis

The experimental results demonstrate significant improvements over existing methods across multiple metrics:

**Tracking Performance**: DIAL-MPC achieves 13.4 times lower tracking error compared to standard MPPI in quadrupedal velocity tracking tasks. The improved precision stems from the algorithm's ability to escape poor local minima through the annealing process.

**Comparison with RL**: In challenging climbing tasks, DIAL-MPC outperforms trained RL policies by 50% without requiring any training. This demonstrates the algorithm's ability to leverage accurate dynamics models for superior performance.

**Computational Efficiency**: Despite optimizing over full-order dynamics, DIAL-MPC maintains real-time performance with control frequencies up to 50 Hz. The parallel implementation and efficient sampling strategy enable this computational efficiency.

**Robustness**: The algorithm demonstrates robustness to model mismatch and external disturbances, maintaining performance even when the dynamics model differs from the true system dynamics.

### 7. Real-World Validation

The practical effectiveness of DIAL-MPC is validated through real-world experiments on a quadrupedal robot. The experiments include:

**Precision Jumping**: The robot successfully performs jumping maneuvers while carrying a 7kg payload, demonstrating the algorithm's ability to handle dynamic tasks with changing system properties.

**Velocity Tracking**: Real-world tracking experiments show that DIAL-MPC maintains precise control at speeds up to 0.8 m/s with minimal tracking error.

The real-world experiments confirm that the simulation results translate effectively to physical systems, validating the approach's practical utility. The robot demonstrates stable, precise control across various challenging scenarios without requiring task-specific tuning or training.

### 8. Significance and Future Directions

DIAL-MPC represents a significant advancement in legged robot control by making full-order dynamics optimization practical for real-time applications. The algorithm's training-free nature addresses a major limitation of RL-based approaches, while its superior performance compared to traditional MPC methods opens new possibilities for highly dynamic robot behaviors.

The theoretical connection between sampling-based MPC and diffusion processes provides a new framework for understanding and improving trajectory optimization algorithms. This insight could inspire further research in applying diffusion model principles to robotics optimization problems.

The work demonstrates several key advantages over existing approaches:

- **Immediate Deployment**: Unlike RL methods, DIAL-MPC requires no training, enabling immediate deployment on new tasks and robot platforms
- **Superior Precision**: The annealing process enables more precise control than traditional sampling-based methods
- **Model Flexibility**: The approach can leverage any differentiable dynamics model, including learned models
- **Generalization**: The algorithm generalizes across different tasks, robot morphologies, and environmental conditions

Future research directions include extending the approach to manipulation tasks, multi-robot coordination, and adaptive model learning. The diffusion-inspired annealing principle could also be applied to other optimization problems in robotics beyond trajectory optimization.

The success of DIAL-MPC in enabling real-time full-order dynamics control represents a step toward more capable and versatile legged robots that can perform complex behaviors in challenging real-world environments. As computational resources continue to improve and the theoretical understanding of diffusion-inspired optimization advances, this approach could become a standard tool for high-performance robot control.
