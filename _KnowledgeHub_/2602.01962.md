# Zero-Shot Off-Policy Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers introduce Zero-Shot Off-policy Learning (ZOL), a method for Behavioral Foundation Models (BFMs) that enables effective test-time adaptation to new tasks without online interaction or additional training. ZOL improves policy reliability by using a novel connection between successor measures and stationary density ratios to explicitly account for distributional shifts in an offline manner.

## Problem

- Distributional shift between a BFM's inferred policy and the pretraining data leads to inaccurate value estimates and suboptimal behavior in zero-shot adaptation.
- Existing off-policy evaluation methods typically require task-specific optimization, which is incompatible with the rapid, training-free adaptation goal of BFMs.
- BFM inference often results in suboptimal policies due to an 'inference gap,' where latent selection is biased by empirical correlations on high-density offline data, ignoring critical information in low-support areas.

## Method

- **ZOL** (Zero-Shot Off-policy Learning) is a test-time, ==occupancy-aware== adaptation procedure that leverages a theoretical connection between successor measures and stationary density ratios.
- The method approximates stationary density ratios for a target policy using pre-trained Forward and Backward embeddings, allowing for offline, training-free distribution correction.
- ZOL optimizes a task-specific latent variable via gradient ascent on a regularized, reward-weighted return objective, incorporating the approximated stationary density ratio, a chi-square-like variance penalty, and a trust region penalty.

## Results

- ZOL consistently outperformed baseline promptable RL methods, including HILP, FB, LoLA, ReLA, and TD-JEPA, on ExORL continuous control tasks, demonstrating an average **10.03%** improvement over vanilla FB.
- Achieved superior success rates on challenging OGBench long-horizon and sparse-reward tasks (e.g., AntMaze, Cube), obtaining an average return of **0.47** without any additional environment sampling at test time.
- Successfully mitigated data support bias in a 2D toy environment, accurately reconstructing task reward structures where vanilla Forward-Backward inference failed due to reliance on high-density regions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An exact identity demonstrates that stationary density ratios can be expressed in terms of successor densities, which are implicitly available within low-rank Forward-Backward representations.
> - This identity enables training-free, occupancy-corrected adaptation for BFMs, allowing rapid policy inference without retraining or requiring online environment interaction at test time.
> - Explicitly accounting for a candidate policy's induced state visitation via occupancy correction helps overcome the 'inference gap' in BFMs, leading to the selection of policies that are aligned with dataset-consistent behavior.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is positioned within the rapidly evolving field of Reinforcement Learning (RL), specifically addressing challenges in unsupervised pretraining and zero-shot adaptation for generalist agents. The concept of "Behavioral Foundation Models (BFMs)" is central, wherein agents acquire broad behavioral priors from large, unlabeled, offline datasets, which can then be adapted to various tasks at deployment without further training. This paradigm aims to emulate the success of foundation models in other domains, such as natural language processing and computer vision.

A core challenge in this BFM-style zero-shot RL is the "off-policy problem." When a policy derived from a BFM is applied to a new task, its visitation distribution may significantly diverge from the distribution of the data used for pretraining (the "behavior policy" data). This distributional shift can lead to "value function overestimation bias" and "out-of-distribution (OOD) queries," where the agent attempts actions in regions of the state-action space that are poorly represented in the training data, resulting in suboptimal behavior.

Existing research has explored "stationary occupancy correction" to address this mismatch, notably the DICE family of algorithms (e.g., DualDICE, OptiDICE, Diffusion-DICE). These methods estimate stationary density ratios ($w_{\pi/\beta} = d_{\pi}/d_{\beta}$) to quantify how a target policy $\pi$ shifts visitation relative to the dataset-collecting behavior $\beta$. However, these approaches typically require task-specific optimization, which is not conducive to the fast, zero-shot adaptation desired in BFMs.

Other recent approaches for fast adaptation with BFMs, such as ReLA and LoLA, perform adaptation through online interaction or rollouts. While effective, these methods necessitate access to the environment at test time, incurring computational costs and potentially limiting applicability in scenarios where online interaction is expensive or prohibited.

This paper positions itself by aiming to bridge the gap between robust off-policy learning methods and the zero-shot adaptation requirements of BFMs, specifically seeking to achieve effective adaptation *without* requiring per-task online optimization or environment interaction at test time.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable effective zero-shot off-policy learning for Behavioral Foundation Models (BFMs) by overcoming the inherent challenges of distributional shift and value function overestimation, all while avoiding computationally expensive per-task online optimization. The central motivating question guiding this work is:

"Can we estimate optimal stationary occupancy ratios for a whole family of BFM-induced policies without per-task online optimization, enabling fast test-time adaptation while avoiding the pathologies of off-policy learning?"

This motivation arises from several observed difficulties:
1. **Distributional Shift and Overestimation Bias:** In zero-shot RL, an agent trained on reward-free data must adapt to new tasks at test time. The policy inferred for a new task may induce a state-action visitation distribution significantly different from the pretraining data, leading to inaccurate value estimates and suboptimal behavior.
2. **Limitations of Existing Off-Policy Methods:** While methods like DICE address stationary occupancy correction, they typically involve solving task-specific optimization problems, which contradicts the goal of rapid, training-free zero-shot adaptation.
3. **Suboptimality of Current BFM Inference:** Recent studies indicate that even when a BFM possesses strong behaviors within its latent policy space, the inference procedure used to select a policy for a new task can result in a suboptimal representation. This "inference gap" often stems from the latent selection process being dominated by empirical correlations on a biased offline support, rather than being self-consistent with the visitation distribution induced by the candidate policy. This leads to policies that look good under extrapolated values but induce unsupported visitation.
4. **Need for Interaction-Free Adaptation:** Current fast adaptation methods (e.g., ReLA, LoLA) often rely on online interaction or rollouts at test time, which can be impractical or costly. The goal is to achieve robust adaptation entirely offline, using only the pretrained BFM and task information.

To address these points, the authors aim to discover a theoretical connection that allows inferring optimal importance sampling ratios, effectively performing a stationary distribution correction with an optimal policy for any task, on the fly and without additional training. This is intended to mitigate overestimation and extrapolation errors during zero-shot adaptation, thereby improving the reliability and robustness of BFMs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, **Zero-Shot Off-policy Learning (ZOL)**, is a test-time procedure designed for occupancy-aware fast adaptation without altering the BFM's training. It leverages a theoretical connection between successor measures (SMs) and stationary density ratios within the Forward-Backward (FB) representation framework.

**Core Theoretical Insight:**
The paper establishes an exact identity: the stationary density ratio $w_{\pi/\beta}(s,a)$ for a policy $\pi$ relative to a behavior policy $\beta$ (that collected the dataset $D$) is given by:
$w_{\pi/\beta}(s,a) = (1 - \gamma) E_{(s_0,a_0)\sim\rho_0} [m_{\pi}(s_0,a_0,s,a)]$
where $\gamma$ is the discount factor, $\rho_0$ is the initial state distribution, and $m_{\pi}(s_0,a_0,s,a)$ is the successor density, which characterizes the discounted future visitation under $\pi$ starting from $(s_0,a_0)$, relative to $d_{\beta}$.

The Forward-Backward (FB) framework approximates this successor density using a low-rank factorization:
$m_{\pi_z}(s_0,a_0,s,a) \approx F(s_0,a_0,z)^T B(s,a)$
where $F$ is a forward embedding, $B$ is a backward embedding, and $z$ is a latent variable indexing a policy $\pi_z$.
By substituting this approximation and taking the expectation over initial states, the stationary density ratio for a policy $\pi_z$ becomes:
$w_{\pi_z/\beta}(s,a) \approx (1 - \gamma) W_{\pi_z}^T B(s,a)$
where $W_{\pi_z} := E_{(s_0,a_0)\sim\rho_0} [F(s_0,a_0,z)]$ is the expected forward embedding vector.

This equation indicates that FB representations implicitly contain the necessary distribution-correction weights, allowing for a training-free approximation of occupancy-corrected adaptation for task latents $z$ at test time. Importantly, this ratio is only ever evaluated on states $(s,a)$ supported by the dataset $d_{\beta}$, avoiding extrapolation to unseen regions.

**ZOL Algorithm Steps:**

1. **Forward-Backward Representation Training:**
- The forward ($F_{\theta}$) and backward ($B_{\psi}$) embedding networks are pretrained using a temporal-difference objective that enforces a Bellman fixed-point for the successor density. An additional loss encourages $B^T B = I$ for better expressivity. This step occurs offline and is task-agnostic.

2. **Task-Specific Latent Inference (Initialization):**
- Given a new task $T$ with reward $r_T$ and a dataset $D$, an initial task-specific latent vector $c_T$ (or $z_0$) is inferred. This is typically done by aggregating reward information via the backward features from the dataset: $c_T := E_{(s,a)\sim D} [B_{\psi}(s,a) r_T(s,a)]$. This $c_T$ serves as the starting point for the subsequent optimization.

3. **Latent Variable Optimization (Test-Time Adaptation):**
- ZOL optimizes the latent variable $z$ (initialized at $c_T$) using gradient ascent to maximize a regularized weighted-return objective. This step is performed purely offline, using the pre-trained $F$ and $B$ networks and the provided dataset $D$.
- **Objective Function:** The algorithm maximizes:
 $J_{total}(z) = J_{ret}(\pi_z) - \lambda_1 \chi^2(z) - \lambda_2 L_{trust}(z)$
 - **Reward-Weighted Return ($J_{ret}(\pi_z)$):** This is an offline estimate of the target policy's return, corrected by the stationary density ratio: $J_{ret}(\pi_z) = E_{(s,a,r)\sim d_{\beta}} [w_{\pi_z/\beta}(s,a) (r(s,a) - \bar{r})]$. Reward centering ($r - \bar{r}$) is applied to prevent optimizing trivial constant reward offsets.
 - **Stationary Density Ratio ($w_{\pi_z/\beta}(s,a)$):** Approximated as $(1 - \gamma) W_{\pi_z}^T B(s,a)$. The expected forward embedding $W_{\pi_z}$ is estimated via Monte Carlo sampling, where a batch of start states is sampled, the policy $\pi_z$ is executed, and the resulting $F$ embeddings are averaged.
 - **Regularization Terms:**
 - **Positivity and Self-Normalization:** The raw logits for the ratio are passed through a `softplus` activation to ensure positivity, and then self-normalized over the batch to enforce $E[w] \approx 1$.
 - **Weight Clipping:** The weights $w_i(z)$ are clipped ($w_i(z) \leftarrow \min\{w_i(z), w_{max}\}$) to prevent numerical instability and extreme values.
 - **Chi-square-like Ratio Variance Penalty ($\chi^2(z)$):** Defined as $E[(w-1)^2]$, this term penalizes high variance in the importance weights, encouraging $w$ to stay close to 1, thereby preventing ratio blow-up and promoting conservatism. This term is mathematically equivalent to the $\chi^2$-divergence between $d_{\pi}$ and $d_{\beta}$.
 - **Trust Region Penalty ($L_{trust}(z)$):** This term, defined as $\|\text{proj}(z) - \text{proj}(z_{init})\|^2$, ensures that the latent search stays close to the initial inferred latent $z_{init}$, preventing catastrophic drift to degenerate skills.

The optimization process uses Adam optimizer to update $z$, with optional gradient norm clipping for stability. This entire process is training-free and interaction-free, operating solely on the pretrained FB model and offline data.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper evaluates ZOL across diverse zero-shot adaptation settings, demonstrating its effectiveness in mitigating issues arising from distribution shift and inference-time suboptimality.

1. **2D Toy Example (Donut Environment):**
- A minimal 2D environment (a donut-shaped state space with radially-decaying data density) was used to visualize the core mechanism.
- Vanilla FB inference (using Equation 8) for various tasks (SQUARE, TWOCIRCLES, CROSS) produced "smoother, support-biased explanations" for rewards, failing to reconstruct the true task structure accurately. This was attributed to the inference process being dominated by high-density regions of the offline dataset, even when task-critical information lay in low-support areas.
- ZOL, by explicitly reasoning about the candidate policy's induced state visitation through occupancy correction, reliably reconstructed the intended "sharp reward structure" across all tasks. This finding highlights ZOL's ability to correct for inference-time suboptimality caused by data support bias, even with expressive underlying representations.

2. **ExORL DeepMind Control Benchmark:**
- ZOL was evaluated on 16 continuous control tasks across four domains (Walker, Cheetah, Quadruped, Pointmass) using the RND exploratory dataset.
- The method consistently outperformed recent promptable baselines, including HILP, FB (vanilla), LoLA, ReLA, and TD-JEPA.
- Particular improvements were observed in high-dimensional domains like Quadruped. For instance, ZOL showed an average return of 627.2 compared to FB's 592.5, yielding an overall average improvement of 10.03% over FB across all tasks. Notable gains included a 51.79% relative gain for "Cheetah-Run" and 35.98% for "Pointmass-Bottom Right."

3. **OGBench (Long-Horizon Goal-Conditioned Control):**
- On challenging long-horizon and sparse-reward tasks (AntMaze-medium, AntMaze-large, Cube-double, Cube-single, Puzzle-3x3), ZOL achieved superior success rates.
- ZOL's average return across 5 tasks was 0.47, surpassing FB (0.45), LoLA (0.42), and ReLA (0.39).
- A key finding was that ZOL achieved these results *without any additional environment sampling* at test time, unlike LoLA and ReLA, which require online interaction. This empirically validated the hypothesis that the distribution correction ratio is sufficient to recover near-optimal policies offline.

4. **SMPL Humanoid (MoCap Imitation):**
- Using a pretrained FB-CPR model for HumEnv (a 358-dimensional state and 69-dimensional action space humanoid), ZOL showed gains over the baseline FB-CPR in motion tracking tasks.
- For tasks like "RAISEARMS-H-L" and "ROTATE-X-5-0.8," ZOL exhibited substantial relative gains of 467.33% and 832.18% over FB, respectively, finding better policies for realistic motion capture trajectories.

5. **Ablation Study:**
- A systematic ablation study on ZOL's hyperparameters (optimization steps, trust regularization, chi-square penalty, weight clipping) on both ExORL and SMPL Humanoid benchmarks indicated that ZOL's performance is largely robust and insensitive to specific hyperparameter choices within reasonable ranges.
- Mild preferences were observed for fewer inner optimization steps, moderate trust region penalties, and smaller $\chi^2$ penalties, suggesting a balance between exploration and conservatism.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work makes several contributions to the fields of reinforcement learning and foundation models, with notable significance and potential impact:

1. **Bridging Research Areas:** The paper successfully bridges off-policy learning methods and zero-shot adaptation for Behavioral Foundation Models (BFMs). By theoretically connecting successor measures to stationary density ratios, it provides a principled approach to apply distribution correction in a zero-shot setting, which has been a challenge for traditional off-policy evaluation/optimization methods.

2. **Mitigating the "Inference Gap" in BFMs:** A key impact is addressing the observed suboptimality in BFM inference. ZOL directly tackles the issue where a BFM's latent inference process might select policies that appear optimal under extrapolated values but induce visitation distributions unsupported by the pretraining data. By steering latent policy selection toward "task-aligned, dataset-consistent behavior," ZOL improves the reliability and robustness of zero-shot adaptation.

3. **Enabling Training-Free and Interaction-Free Adaptation:** A significant practical impact is the ability to perform test-time adaptation *without* requiring additional training or online environment interaction. This differentiates ZOL from methods like LoLA and ReLA, which rely on rollouts or online fine-tuning. This interaction-free characteristic makes ZOL particularly valuable for real-world applications where online data collection is costly, unsafe, or impossible, thereby reducing deployment barriers for BFMs.

4. **Enhanced Performance and Robustness:** Across various benchmarks, including continuous control (ExORL), long-horizon manipulation (OGBench), and humanoid motion tracking (SMPL Humanoid), ZOL consistently outperforms or provides substantial gains over existing zero-shot baselines. This indicates that occupancy correction provides a tangible benefit for improving policy quality and stability during zero-shot transfer.

5. **Theoretical Advancements:** The theoretical connection derived in the paper, showing how Forward-Backward representations implicitly contain distribution-correction weights, offers a novel perspective and a low-rank, data-only estimator for occupancy correction. This theoretical grounding strengthens the method's validity and opens avenues for further research in representation learning for off-policy problems.

6. **Future Directions:** While the method inherits representational biases from the learned FB factorization, the work lays a foundation for future advancements. Potential impacts include the development of uncertainty-aware occupancy correction, stronger and richer ratio parameterizations, and extensions to more complex domains like stochastic or partially observable environments, further enhancing the capabilities and applicability of BFMs.
