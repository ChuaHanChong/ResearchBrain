# Real-to-Sim Robot Policy Evaluation with Gaussian Splatting Simulation of Soft-Body Interactions

> [!summary] Summary
> A simulation framework for robot policy evaluation combines 3D Gaussian Splatting for photorealistic rendering with physics-informed soft-body digital twins to accurately model deformable object interactions. The approach achieved a strong correlation (Pearson r > 0.9) between simulated and real-world policy performance across various manipulation tasks and policies, addressing the sim-to-real gap.

## Problem

- Evaluating visuomotor robot policies in the real world is costly, time-consuming, and difficult to reproduce, especially for tasks involving deformable objects.
- The "sim-to-real gap" limits the use of simulation for reliable policy evaluation, as policies successful in simulation often fail in physical environments.
- Existing simulators struggle to accurately represent both the photorealistic appearance (e.g., color for robot cameras) and complex dynamics of deformable objects.

## Method

- A unified simulation framework was developed, integrating 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) for high-fidelity rendering with physics-informed soft-body digital twins (PhysTwin) for dynamic object modeling.
- High-fidelity digital twins of the robot workspace and objects were created using 3DGS reconstructions from video, and physical parameters for deformable objects were optimized from interaction videos.
- The system implemented positional and color alignment for visual consistency with robot cameras and a custom NVIDIA Warp-based physics engine for accurate soft-body deformation and collision handling.

## Results

- The simulator demonstrated strong correlation between simulated and real-world policy success rates across all evaluated tasks (plush toy packing, rope routing, T-block pushing) and state-of-the-art policies, with Pearson correlation coefficients consistently exceeding 0.9.
- Compared to the NVIDIA IsaacLab baseline, the proposed method yielded substantially higher sim-to-real correlation (e.g., r=0.915 vs. r=0.649 for T-block pushing), particularly for tasks involving deformable objects.
- Ablation studies confirmed that independently removing either color alignment or physics optimization significantly reduced the sim-to-real correlation, validating the importance of both components for reliable policy evaluation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Both high-fidelity appearance (e.g., through color alignment to robot cameras) and accurate dynamics modeling (e.g., optimized material properties) are critical for achieving trustworthy sim-to-real correlation in robot policy evaluation.
> - Different robot policies exhibit varying sensitivities to visual and dynamic discrepancies, underscoring that a comprehensive approach to bridging the sim-to-real gap is essential for robust evaluation.
> - The framework can effectively monitor policy learning dynamics and aid in selecting optimal training checkpoints, demonstrating its utility beyond just final performance evaluation.
