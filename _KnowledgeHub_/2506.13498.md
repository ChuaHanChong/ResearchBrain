# A Survey on Imitation Learning for Contact-Rich Tasks in Robotics

> [!summary] Summary
> A survey mapping imitation learning methods for contact-rich robotic manipulation: it taxonomizes demonstration-teaching methods (online/offline teaching by online/offline learning), reviews behavior cloning, movement primitives, generative models, and inverse RL, and surveys industrial, household, and healthcare applications to identify open research directions.

## Problem

- No prior survey specifically targets imitation learning for contact-rich tasks; the closest existing survey reviews reinforcement learning approaches instead, leaving IL research trends unorganized.
- Contact dynamics are highly nonlinear and sensitive to small positional deviations, making classical Lagrangian or PDE-based models computationally intractable and pushing research toward scattered model-free methods.
- Tactile and force-sensing technologies remain confined to research labs, and third-person imitation plus multimodal demonstration collection for contact tasks lack a consolidated methodological overview.

## Method

- Introduces a four-way ==teaching taxonomy== (interactive imitation, demo-augmented RL, direct imitation, observational learning) crossing online/offline teaching with online/offline learning to classify demonstration-collection methods.
- Organizes learning-approach coverage around ==behavior cloning== (RNN/LSTM/Transformer/ACT/Mamba), ==dynamic movement primitives== and their force-aware/Riemannian extensions, and generative methods (VAE, CVAE, ==diffusion policy==, foundation-model ==VLA==).
- Synthesizes ==inverse-RL== and ==adversarial imitation== (IRL, AIL, ==GAIL==) alongside ==offline-RL== pretraining/fine-tuning strategies, then maps these methods onto industrial, household, and surgical/rehabilitation application cases.

## Results

- Cites a GAIL-based peg-in-hole insertion achieving **6 micrometer** peg-hole clearance on an industrial Yaskawa GP8 arm, evidencing adversarial imitation's precision on genuinely contact-rich assembly.
- Reports the dataset scale underpinning behavior-cloning progress: DROID's **76,000** trajectories across **564** scenes and Open X-Embodiment's aggregation of over **1 million** trajectories across **22** embodiments.
- Notes ALOHA-collected ACT policies solving complex contact tasks like page-turning and pen disassembly from only **50** teleoperated demonstrations, showing transformer action-chunking's demonstration efficiency.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - No single sensing modality suffices for contact-rich manipulation; fusing force, vision, and tactile signals is a design principle that generalizes well beyond any specific benchmark.
> - Plain behavior cloning compounds errors over time; pairing it with residual RL correction or DAgger-style human intervention is a transferable remedy across contact-rich task domains.
> - The sim-to-real gap widens as contact complexity grows; domain randomization alone rarely closes it, so physics-aware augmentation or hybrid sim/real data remains necessary caution.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper positions itself as a critical and timely survey addressing a specific, yet highly impactful, gap in the existing robotics literature. The broader research landscape in robotics has long grappled with "contact-rich tasks," which involve complex physical interactions with the environment (e.g., assembly, manipulation of deformable objects, human-robot physical interaction). These tasks are inherently challenging due to their nonlinear dynamics, sensitivity to small positional deviations, and the difficulties in accurate modeling and control.

Traditionally, approaches to contact-rich tasks have been categorized into model-based and model-free methods. While model-based methods have seen practical application, their limitations in dealing with highly nonlinear and unpredictable behaviors, especially in "everyday physics," have become increasingly apparent. This has fueled a shift towards machine learning-based, model-free approaches, primarily reinforcement learning (RL) and imitation learning (IL).

The authors acknowledge the existence of numerous surveys in related areas:
- **Contact-rich tasks/manipulation:** Several surveys have covered this domain (e.g., Cui and Trinkle, 2021; Jiang et al., 2022; Kroemer et al., 2021; Suomalainen et al., 2022), often focusing on general robot learning for manipulation or specific applications like assembly.
- **Imitation Learning (IL):** Other surveys have addressed IL more broadly (e.g., Argall et al., 2009; Celemin et al., 2022; Fang et al., 2019; Hua et al., 2021; Urain et al., 2024), covering its foundational paradigms, challenges in skill transfer, interconnections with RL, interactive IL, and the role of deep generative models.

**The unique contribution and niche of this survey** lie in its specific focus: **"No survey exists that investigates imitation learning research in contact-rich tasks."** The closest existing work surveyed RL technologies in contact-rich tasks (Elguea-Aguinaco et al., 2023). However, the authors emphasize that "significant differences in methodology and effects between RL technology and imitation learning make this contrast valuable for organizing trends in this field."

This survey, therefore, fills a crucial void by systematically organizing the recent research trends in IL specifically tailored for the complexities of contact-rich robotic tasks. It consolidates knowledge on demonstration collection, learning algorithms, and real-world applications in this specialized domain. By doing so, it provides a dedicated resource for researchers and practitioners, fostering advancements at the intersection of IL and robust physical interaction. The work is particularly timely given the rapid advancements in multimodal learning, large language models (LLMs), and foundation models, which are increasingly being leveraged in robotics to enhance capabilities in complex contact tasks across industrial, household, and healthcare domains.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this survey is to comprehensively review and systematically organize the research trends in imitation learning (IL) applied to contact-rich robotic tasks. The motivation stems from several interconnected challenges and opportunities in modern robotics:

1. **Complexity of Contact-Rich Tasks:** Many fundamental robotic tasks inherently involve physical interaction with the environment, characterized by multiple contact states, nonlinear dynamics, and extreme sensitivity to minor positional deviations. Understanding and controlling these "everyday physics" phenomena (e.g., friction, elasticity, plastic deformation) is a long-standing and ongoing challenge in robotics.
2. **Limitations of Traditional Approaches:** Classical model-based control methods struggle with the high nonlinearity and variability of real-world contact dynamics. While reinforcement learning (RL) offers autonomous acquisition of complex movements, it suffers from extensive trial-and-error, hardware wear-and-tear, safety concerns, and a significant sim-to-reality gap in contact scenarios.
3. **Potential of Imitation Learning (IL):** IL offers a compelling alternative by allowing robots to efficiently acquire expert skills, including the subtle adjustments of contact forces and positions inherent in human dexterous manipulation. Humans possess tacit knowledge and empirical rules for contact tasks that are difficult to explicitly program. IL aims to leverage this human expertise to fundamentally enhance robot capabilities.
4. **Rapid Advancements in AI and Robotics:** The proliferation of Large Language Models (LLMs) and their extension to other modalities (images, speech) signals a paradigm shift towards foundation models in AI. In robotics, these models are expected to abstract human tacit knowledge and empirical rules, enabling more sophisticated and adaptable robotic behaviors. This surge in AI capabilities has led to a significant increase in IL papers in robotics, necessitating a systematic organization of these advancements.
5. **Filling a Research Gap:** As highlighted in the broader landscape section, there was a clear lack of a dedicated survey focusing specifically on IL for *contact-rich* tasks, differentiating it from broader IL surveys or RL-specific contact task reviews. This survey aims to bridge that gap.

In summary, the paper's key objectives are to:
- Provide a systematic organization of existing research methods in imitation learning for contact-rich tasks.
- Present recent research trends and clarify the challenges faced in this field.
- Identify and outline future research directions and practical application possibilities.

By achieving these objectives, the authors aim to lay a foundational understanding for future advancements in robust, adaptable, and human-like contact-rich robotic manipulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

As a survey paper, its "methodology and approach" refers to the structured framework used to analyze and present the current state of imitation learning for contact-rich robotics. The authors adopt a comprehensive, multi-faceted approach, systematically dissecting the field into logical components:

1. **Defining the Domain (Preliminaries):** The survey begins by establishing a clear understanding of "contact-rich robotics," detailing its challenges (e.g., scalability of classical control, sensitivity to conditions) and drawing insights from human motor control. This includes discussions on how humans manage contact (compliance, predictive control, robust feedback) and how these biological principles inspire robotic control strategies like impedance and admittance control. Key challenges specific to contact-rich imitation learning are then enumerated, such as the nonlinear nature of contact dynamics, data collection scarcity (especially for tactile sensing), the partial observability of contact forces from vision alone, the difficulties of third-person imitation, and data efficiency/generalization.

2. **Demonstration Collection Methodologies:** Recognizing that IL fundamentally relies on expert demonstrations, the survey dedicates a significant section to data collection.
 - **Data Modalities:** It analyzes the crucial role of diverse sensory inputs (position, force, vision, tactile) in capturing the complexity of contact tasks, emphasizing their fusion for enhanced robustness. Emerging modalities like EMG and soft/flexible sensors are also discussed.
 - **Teaching Methods:** A classification of teaching methods is presented, distinguishing between online teaching (kinesthetic, teleoperation, VR-based) and offline teaching (observation). Importantly, it clarifies the distinction between teaching (data collection) and learning (algorithm processing) as independent concepts.
 - **Synthetic Data Generation:** The increasing importance of simulation environments (e.g., MuJoCo, PyBullet, Isaac Gym, Drake) for generating scalable and diverse data, particularly for complex contact dynamics, is explored, along with techniques like domain randomization and physics-based augmentation to bridge the sim-to-real gap.
 - **Available Datasets:** A review of notable, large-scale, and multimodal datasets specifically designed for contact-rich tasks (e.g., TVL, Touch100k, BridgeData V2, DROID, Open X-Embodiment, Sparsh) is provided, highlighting their contribution to addressing data scarcity and generalization.

3. **Analysis of Imitation Learning Approaches:** The core of the survey systematically reviews various IL algorithms and paradigms, detailing their principles, applications, and limitations in the context of contact-rich tasks.
 - **Behavior Cloning (BC):** Discusses its supervised learning nature, application of sequence models (RNN, LSTM, Seq2Seq, Transformers like ACT), and recent generative models (Diffusion, Mamba, Implicit BC). It also addresses limitations like adaptability and compounding error, and how human feedback is integrated.
 - **Dynamic Movement Primitives (DMPs):** Explores DMPs as a generalizable motion representation, its extensions (ProMPs, KMPs, G-DMPs) for multi-dimensionality and variance, and how they are reformulated or parallelized with other modalities (force, impedance) for contact tasks.
 - **Generative Methods:** Delves into the use of Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) and Conditional VAEs (CVAEs) for learning compact representations and adaptive behavior. A significant subsection is dedicated to **Foundation Models (FMs)**, particularly Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, examining their role in interpreting instructions and generating actions for robotics, while acknowledging the challenge of their application to *dynamic* contact force regulation. Other generative methods like Diffusion Policy and Action Chunking with Transformers (ACT) are also covered.
 - **Inverse Reinforcement Learning (IRL):** Explains IRL's approach to inferring reward functions from expert demonstrations, with a focus on Adversarial Imitation Learning (AIL) and its variant, Generative Adversarial Imitation Learning (GAIL), for learning complex manipulation tasks.
 - **Multi-modal IL:** Systematically categorizes how different modalities (proprioception, force, tactile, vision, language, video) are integrated, whether through parallelization, reformulation of models, or hierarchical approaches, to achieve context-awareness and improved performance in contact tasks.
 - **Offline Reinforcement Learning (Offline RL):** Examines its role in leveraging pre-collected datasets to learn contact-rich skills without extensive real-world interaction, addressing safety and sample efficiency concerns. It discusses pre-training, fine-tuning strategies, and architectural innovations.
 - **Other Methods:** Briefly touches upon less common but emerging approaches, including world models, zero/one-shot IL, IL on Riemannian manifolds, learning from limited/suboptimal demonstrations, unsupervised IL, and optimization-based IL.

4. **Application Cases:** The survey then illustrates the practical impact of IL in contact-rich tasks across three major domains: industrial robots (assembly, deburring, polishing), service and household robots (table wiping, cloth folding, interacting with objects), and health-care robots (surgical procedures, rehabilitation).

5. **Future Research Directions:** Finally, the paper concludes by identifying critical challenges and proposing three key research directions: dual-process hierarchical architectures, advanced multimodal sensing, and bridging the sim-to-real gap, underscoring the influence of foundation models across these areas.

This structured approach allows the authors to provide a clear, detailed, and comprehensive overview, highlighting both the successes and persistent challenges in the field.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

As a survey paper, its "main findings and results" are not experimental outcomes but rather a synthesis of the current state of the art, identification of key trends, clarification of challenges, and articulation of future directions within imitation learning for contact-rich robotic tasks.

The main findings and insights derived from this survey are:

1. **Imitation Learning (IL) as a Crucial Paradigm for Contact-Rich Tasks:** The survey strongly establishes IL as an increasingly vital approach for handling the complexities of contact-rich tasks. It highlights that IL's ability to efficiently learn from expert (human) demonstrations, including subtle force and position adjustments, makes it uniquely suited for tasks where traditional model-based control struggles due to nonlinearity and sensitivity.

2. **Importance of Multimodal Data Collection:** The success of IL in contact-rich scenarios heavily relies on rich, diverse data modalities. The survey underscores that positional, force, visual, and tactile information are all crucial for capturing interaction dynamics. Challenges in data collection, particularly the scarcity and limitations of tactile sensing in industrial applications, are identified as a significant bottleneck. Advances in synthetic data generation (simulators, domain randomization) and the proliferation of large, multimodal datasets (e.g., TVL, Touch100k, Open X-Embodiment) are shown to be critical enablers.

3. **Evolution of IL Approaches:**
 - **Behavior Cloning (BC)** remains a foundational and effective supervised learning paradigm, particularly with the advent of large-scale datasets and powerful sequence models like LSTMs and Transformers (e.g., ACT). It excels in learning intricate manipulation sequences.
 - **Dynamic Movement Primitives (DMPs)** continue to be relevant, offering a structured, generalizable, and sample-efficient way to represent motions, especially when extended to incorporate force and other modalities, or integrated with reinforcement learning.
 - **Generative Models**, including VAEs, Diffusion Models, and particularly **Foundation Models (FMs)** like Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, are emerging as powerful tools. They enable robots to interpret high-level instructions, generalize across tasks, and map complex inputs to actions. However, the application of FMs to *dynamic force regulation* in truly contact-rich tasks remains an active challenge.
 - **Inverse Reinforcement Learning (IRL)**, especially Adversarial Imitation Learning (AIL) and Generative Adversarial Imitation Learning (GAIL), offers a way to infer reward functions directly from expert demonstrations, reducing the need for hand-crafted rewards and improving scalability.
 - **Multi-modal IL** is a key trend, combining various sensory inputs (vision, language, force, proprioception, tactile) to enhance context-awareness, adaptability, and generalization, often through techniques like policy composition or hierarchical models.
 - **Offline Reinforcement Learning (Offline RL)** is gaining prominence by allowing policies to be learned from pre-collected datasets, mitigating safety and hardware wear concerns associated with online trial-and-error in real-world contact.

4. **Persistent Challenges in Contact-Rich IL:** The survey systematically highlights several critical hurdles:
 - **Highly Nonlinear Contact Dynamics:** Difficulty in accurately modeling these dynamics (e.g., soft contacts, friction) leads to the reliance on model-free learning but also exacerbates the sim-to-real gap.
 - **Data Scarcity and Quality:** Despite new datasets, obtaining high-quality, comprehensive demonstration data for contact-rich tasks, especially involving subtle force interactions and tactile feedback, remains challenging. Wearable tactile technologies are not yet widely adopted.
 - **Non-Full Observability:** Vision alone is often insufficient, as contact forces are not directly observable and visual occlusions are common.
 - **Sim-to-Real Gap:** Policies learned in simulation often fail to transfer effectively to the real world due to discrepancies in contact models and environmental variations.
 - **Adaptability and Generalization:** Current IL methods can struggle to adapt to unforeseen environmental variations or generalize to novel tasks or objects beyond the demonstration distribution, leading to compounding errors.
 - **Safety:** In real-world contact, ensuring safe operation during learning and execution, especially with limited data or exploration, is paramount.

5. **Diverse Application Landscape:** IL for contact-rich tasks is finding increasing utility across various domains:
 - **Industrial Robotics:** Enhancing precision and robustness in assembly, insertion, deburring, polishing, and deformable object manipulation.
 - **Service and Household Robotics:** Enabling interaction with unstructured environments for tasks like cleaning, cloth folding, object manipulation (doors, drawers), and tool use.
 - **Health-Care Robotics:** Offering substantial benefits in surgical procedures (tissue manipulation, knot-tying) and rehabilitation (passive exercises, adaptive therapies) where dexterity, compliant behavior, and human-robot physical interaction are critical.

Overall, the survey concludes that while significant progress has been made, particularly with the integration of advanced machine learning techniques, robustly deploying IL for dynamic, generalizable, and safe contact-rich tasks in the real world still requires addressing fundamental challenges related to data, models, and control architectures.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This survey paper holds significant importance and has the potential for substantial impact on the field of robotics and artificial intelligence:

1. **Definitive Resource and Knowledge Consolidation:** By systematically organizing the vast and rapidly growing body of research on imitation learning for contact-rich tasks, the survey provides a much-needed, comprehensive, and up-to-date reference. This is invaluable for new researchers entering the field, helping them grasp the fundamental challenges, existing solutions, and state-of-the-art developments without having to sift through disparate literature. For seasoned researchers, it offers a structured overview to identify gaps and synergies.

2. **Highlighting a Critical Research Niche:** By explicitly identifying and focusing on the intersection of imitation learning and contact-rich tasks—a unique and under-surveyed area—the paper draws attention to its importance. This specialization helps to define the scope of future research and encourages a more targeted effort within the robotics community to address these specific, challenging problems.

3. **Guiding Future Research Directions:** The detailed articulation of key challenges (nonlinear dynamics, data scarcity, observability, sim-to-real gap, generalization) and the proposed future research directions (dual-process hierarchical architectures, multimodal sensing, improved sim-to-real transfer, foundation models) serve as a roadmap for the field. It points to where research efforts are most needed and where the greatest breakthroughs are likely to occur, fostering innovation and interdisciplinary collaboration.

4. **Promoting Advanced AI Integration:** The survey emphasizes the transformative potential of recent advancements in large-scale AI, particularly foundation models (e.g., LLMs, VLMs) and generative models (e.g., Diffusion Models), for robotics. By detailing how these models are being applied or could be applied to contact-rich tasks, it encourages further exploration of embodied AI and the transfer of general web knowledge to physical robot control. This could accelerate the development of more intelligent, versatile, and adaptable robotic systems.

5. **Accelerating Real-World Applications:** The inclusion of diverse application cases (industrial, household, healthcare) demonstrates the practical relevance and immediate potential of IL for contact-rich tasks. By outlining current successes and remaining challenges in these domains, the survey can inspire industry adoption, inform product development, and facilitate the transition of research prototypes into commercially viable solutions. For example, advancements in robot assembly, assistive technologies, and surgical automation can directly benefit from the insights provided.

6. **Fostering Interdisciplinary Collaboration:** The diverse authorship from prominent institutions (IIT, Google Deepmind, University of Tokyo) itself underscores the interdisciplinary nature of this field. The survey further highlights the need for continued collaboration between experts in machine learning, control theory, sensor development, and human motor neuroscience to achieve robust and safe human-robot interaction in contact-rich environments.

In essence, this survey serves as a pivotal piece of literature that not only synthesizes current knowledge but also actively shapes the trajectory of future research in a highly complex and impactful domain of robotics. Its insights are crucial for developing robots that can seamlessly and safely interact with the physical world, ultimately paving the way for more autonomous and intelligent robotic systems in our daily lives and industries.
