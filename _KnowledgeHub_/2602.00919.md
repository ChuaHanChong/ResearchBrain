# Green-VLA: Staged Vision-Language-Action Model for Generalist Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> Developed by Sber Robotics Center, Green-VLA presents a staged Vision–Language–Action framework that integrates a multi-stage training curriculum and data quality assurance to enable generalist robots to perform diverse, long-horizon tasks across various embodiments. The approach achieved an average success rate of 90% on Green humanoid tasks and improved object picking success to 95.4% with guidance, demonstrating enhanced robustness and generalization for real-world robotic deployment.

## Problem

- Robotic datasets are heterogeneous in observations, action spaces, and sampling rates, often suffering from quality issues like jitter, blurriness, and inconsistent execution.
- Current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models struggle with generalization across diverse robot embodiments due to varying kinematics and action spaces.
- Behavior cloning, a prevalent training method, often saturates and is insufficient for long-horizon, contact-rich tasks, leading to brittle policies and poor out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization.

## Method

- **Green-VLA** uses a five-stage ==staged training curriculum== (L0 to R2) that progressively builds semantic understanding, multi-embodiment priors, and task-level policy alignment, culminating in ==RL refinement==.
- A robust data framework with multi-stage ==Data Quality Assurance (DataQA)== processes and ==temporal alignment== for heterogeneous robotics datasets.
- A unified ==transformer==-based model architecture with a ==fixed-semantic action space==, ==dynamic embodiment prompting==, and ==action retargeting== for generalization across diverse robot platforms.

## Results

- Demonstrated an average success rate of **90%** for various instruction-conditioned pick-and-place tasks on the Green humanoid robot, exhibiting robustness to OOD layouts and bimanual control.
- Improved success rates for Out-of-Domain items in e-commerce shelf picking from **10.2%** without guidance to **72.8%** with JPM guidance, showcasing enhanced precision for novel objects.
- RL fine-tuning (R2 stage) significantly boosted success rates for challenging grasping tasks (e.g., "Cookies" from **30.4%** to **82.7%**) and enhanced long-horizon consistency on CALVIN benchmarks (ACL of **4.5**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A structured, staged training curriculum from web-scale data to embodiment-specific adaptation and RL refinement effectively develops generalist robot policies capable of multi-embodiment control.
> - Integrating a robust Data Quality Assurance (DataQA) pipeline and a unified, semantically fixed action space is essential for leveraging heterogeneous datasets and achieving cross-embodiment generalization.
> - Reinforcement learning refinement (R2 stage) improves long-horizon task execution, robustness, and grasping reliability, addressing the inherent limitations of behavior cloning in complex, contact-rich scenarios.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision–Language–Action (VLA) models represent an emerging area in embodied AI, aiming to integrate perception, reasoning, and action for robotic control through multimodal context and natural language instructions. This paradigm seeks to enable generalist robots that can perform diverse, long-horizon tasks across various environments and adapt to novel objects, goals, and robotic embodiments. Recent VLA initiatives, such as $\pi_0$, Gemini Robotics, GR00T N1, and AgiBot GO-1, have demonstrated progress by aggregating large-scale data and employing unified architectures for tasks like manipulation and reasoning.

However, existing VLA approaches face several challenges that hinder real-world deployment. Robotic datasets are characterized by heterogeneity in observations, action spaces, and sampling rates. Data quality varies, often including issues such as jitter, blurry frames, inconsistent execution, and limited scene diversity. Additionally, the predominant training method, behavior cloning (BC), tends to saturate and may not align policies effectively with long-horizon objectives or task-level rewards, leading to models with limited generalization across different embodiments and environments. While approaches integrating explicit reasoning have shown improvements in long-horizon planning, they often introduce inference latency that can impede real-time control in robotics. Green-VLA addresses these challenges by emphasizing data quality alignment, action space unification, and reinforcement learning refinement, aiming to create more robust and deployable VLA models for generalist robots, particularly humanoids.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of the Green-VLA framework are to overcome the limitations of current VLA models, particularly in real-world deployment on diverse robotic platforms, and to enhance their generalization capabilities, robustness, and efficiency. The core motivations driving this work include:

1. **Addressing Data Heterogeneity and Quality:** Existing robotic datasets are often disparate in observations, action spaces, and sampling rates. Furthermore, data quality issues—such as jitter, blurriness, and inconsistent task execution—can undermine model stability and performance. Green-VLA aims to develop a robust data processing pipeline that filters, normalizes, and aligns heterogeneous robotics data to ensure quality and semantic consistency, thereby providing a more stable foundation for generalist learning.

2. **Achieving Multi-Embodiment Generalization:** Traditional VLA models often struggle to generalize across different robot embodiments (e.g., humanoids, mobile manipulators, fixed-base arms) due to differing kinematics and action spaces. The objective is to establish a unified action interface and training methodology that supports multiple embodiments under a consistent semantic layout, enabling positive knowledge transfer and allowing a single policy to control a fleet of diverse robots. This is particularly motivated by the challenges of controlling high-DoF humanoids, which require coordinated bimanual manipulation, whole-upper-body motion, and fine-grained fingertip control.

3. **Improving Long-Horizon Task Execution and Robustness:** Behavior cloning, a common training paradigm, frequently encounters limitations in long-horizon and contact-rich manipulation tasks, often failing to assign credit effectively over extended action sequences. This can lead to brittle models that generalize poorly and struggle with out-of-distribution (OOD) states. A key objective is to integrate reinforcement learning (RL) refinement to align policies with task-level rewards, enhance long-horizon success, improve failure recovery mechanisms, and mitigate issues with OOD actions, moving beyond the saturation points of pure behavior cloning.

4. **Ensuring Real-Time Control and Efficiency:** While explicit reasoning modules can enhance planning, they may introduce significant inference latency, preventing real-time robotic control. Green-VLA seeks to develop an architecture that supports both sophisticated reasoning and low-latency execution. This involves architectural optimizations and auxiliary signals, such as episode progress prediction and OOD detection, to ensure efficient and safe task completion without compromising real-time performance.

5. **Developing a Staged Training Curriculum:** The motivation behind a staged training pipeline is to systematically build semantic and physical priors, learn shared affordances, and align policies. This progression is designed to leverage different data regimes—from web-scale multimodal data for common-sense grounding to robotics-specific data for grounded control, and finally embodiment-specific adaptation and RL refinement—to balance generalization, efficiency, and real-world reliability.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Green-VLA employs a staged Vision–Language–Action framework optimized for real-world deployment on the Green humanoid robot, while designed for generalization across diverse embodiments. The methodology is structured around a five-stage curriculum and supported by a unified data and control stack.

#### 3.1. Staged Training Curriculum

The framework progresses through five distinct stages:

- **L0: Foundational VLMs (Base VLM):** This initial stage utilizes an existing vision-language model pretrained on large-scale image/video-text data. At this point, the model does not include robot actions and serves as a base for acquiring general semantic understanding.
- **L1: Multimodal Grounding (Web Pretrain for Physical World Understanding):** The L0 model is further pretrained on 24 million non-robotics, internet-scale multimodal samples. This stage aims to build general reasoning and semantic grounding, allowing the model to acquire priors about physics, object affordances, and task structure from diverse web data (e.g., VQA, pointing, bounding box prediction, multi-view VQA, captioning).
- **R0: Multi-Embodiment Pretraining (General Robotics Pretrain):** In this stage, the model is pretrained on a large corpus of robotics-domain data, comprising over 3,000 hours and 184 million samples from various humanoids and manipulators. This stage focuses on learning broad affordance priors and abstracting embodiment-specific details to capture cross-domain invariants.
- **R1: Embodiment-Specific Adaptation (Effective Tuning):** Following general robotics pretraining, the model undergoes fine-tuning tailored to a specific target embodiment (e.g., the Green humanoid). This involves targeted hyperparameter searches, architectural adjustments for embodiment-aware state/action heads, and efficiency improvements for inference.
- **R2: RL-based Policy Alignment (RL Alignment):** The final stage refines policies using reinforcement learning objectives. This addresses limitations of behavior cloning in long-horizon and contact-rich tasks by incorporating task rewards and failure recovery mechanisms, aiming to improve success rate and robustness beyond what is achievable with supervised learning alone.

#### 3.2. Green-VLA Data Framework

The data framework is central to Green-VLA, integrating diverse data sources and ensuring quality.

#### 3.2.1. Data Pipeline

The pipeline combines web/multimodal grounding data (L1) with large-scale robotics data (R0). All RGB streams and proprioception data are temporally normalized to align physical progress across sources.

#### 3.2.2. Dataset

- **Multi-modal Web Data (L1):** A corpus of 24 million samples from datasets like RefSpatial, AgibotWorld (subset for VQA), RoboPoint, ShareRobot, Robo2VLM, PixMo-Points, MS COCO, A-OKVQA, OpenSpaces, and Sun RGB-D. These datasets contribute to learning general visual-language priors and multi-view/physical understanding.
- **Robotics Data (R0):** A total of 184 million robotics-domain samples (over 3,000 hours) from open-source and internally collected datasets. These include:
 - **Humanoids:** AgiBotWorld\_twofinger (774 hours), AgiBotWorld\_dexhand (82 hours), Action\_net (143 hours), RoboMind (33 hours), Green Humanoid (48 hours, augmented to 167 effective hours).
 - **Mobile Manipulators / Dual-Arm:** Galaxea-Open-World-Dataset (477 hours), RDT (59 hours), BiPlay (9.7 hours), ALOHA any\_pick (11.2 hours).
 - **Single-Arm:** DROID (512 hours), Bridge (105 hours), Fractal (350 hours).
 Each episode is annotated with a language instruction. The Green Humanoid dataset is augmented through bilateral symmetry mirroring and time-reversed demonstrations for reversible tasks.

#### 3.2.3. Data Quality Assurance (DataQA)

A multi-stage filtering process is applied to robotics datasets:
- **Filtering Criteria:** Identification and removal of episodes with missing cameras/frames, inappropriate lengths, insufficient motion activity, and invalid gripper action patterns.
- **Quality Metrics:** Trajectories are scored and filtered using:
 - **Jitter (S\_tremble):** Measures erratic motions by comparing raw and smoothed velocity trajectories.
 - **Sharpness (S\_sharp):** Assesses image quality using median Laplacian std score in local regions.
 - **Visual Diversity (D\_vis):** Quantified by analyzing standard deviation of DINOv3 features over time.
 - **State Variance (D\_state):** Measured by the Frobenius norm of the covariance matrix of robot states.
- **Temporal Alignment:** Optical flow magnitude from wrist cameras is used to estimate execution speed, and trajectories are resampled via monotonic cubic splines to normalize motion per step, ensuring consistent dynamics across datasets.
- **Dynamic Dataset Sampling:** During training, a scheduled mixture of datasets is used, starting with a uniform mixture and gradually converging to a biased distribution based on target weights (relevance, size, DataQA quality) to prevent early collapse onto dominant embodiments.

#### 3.3. Green-VLA Model Architecture and Components

The Green-VLA model (approximately 5 billion parameters) uses a unified transformer-based architecture with a Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct backbone (or PaliGemma 3B in earlier versions) and a flow-matching action expert.

- **Architecture (Figure 1):** A multimodal vision-language encoder processes RGB observations, proprioceptive state, and natural language instructions, fusing them into a shared token sequence. This is augmented with an embodiment/control-type prompt. A flow-matching action expert then predicts unified action chunks.
- **Task Planner Module:** A high-level GigaVision VLM (based on GigaChat) decomposes user goals into symbolic subtasks (e.g., "pick [item]"), which are translated into structured prompts for Green-VLA. It provides feedback and replanning for subtask completion, operating as a frozen model during Green-VLA training.
- **Unified Action Space:** Green-VLA utilizes a fixed-semantic layout (A\_u $\subset \mathbb{R}^{64}$) where each index range has a consistent physical meaning across all robots. Native actions are mapped into corresponding unified slots with a binary mask $m_e$ indicating valid dimensions for each embodiment $e$. A masked behavior cloning objective $L_{uni}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}[\left\|m_e \odot (\pi_\theta(x_e^t, c_e) - \Phi_e(a_e^t))\right\|^2_2]$ is used, where no loss is applied to unused slots.
 - **Dynamic Embodiment and Control-Type Prompting:** A structured control prompt $c_e$ (e.g., "#arms, gripper/dex-hand, joint/cartesian") conditions the policy, explicitly controlling action modalities.
 - **Retargeting for Target Humanoid:** Actions from diverse training robots are transformed into the configuration space of the target humanoid (e.g., Green robot) to imitate the intent of source trajectories.
 - **Temporal Scale Conditioning:** A speed-conditioned augmentation (scalar factor $v$) warps target trajectories, allowing the model to represent both fine-grained and coarse motions within the same policy. This is applied via an RMS-style modulation on the action expert's hidden state.
- **Episode Progress Prediction:** The action expert jointly predicts a normalized episode progress signal $\hat{\rho}_t \in$, enabling downstream planners to manage long-horizon tasks.
- **Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Detector:** A Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) fitted on robot states detects OOD states. If predicted actions lead to an OOD state, actions are corrected using the GMM density gradient to nudge the robot back toward the training distribution.
- **Guidance with Joint Prediction Module (JPM):**
 - **JPM:** A specialized Vision-Language Model predicts a 2D affordance point $(u,v)$ on the image, which is lifted to a 3D target point $p^*$ in the robot's workspace using depth information and camera intrinsics/pose. Inverse kinematics (IK) computes a feasible joint configuration $q^*$ for the end-effector.
 - **Guidance Module:** A pseudoinverse guidance scheme (ΠGDM) modifies the learned flow-matching velocity field $v$ to bias trajectories toward the JPM-predicted target $p^*$, aiding in precise object targeting, especially for visually dense or novel items.

#### 3.4. R2: RL Fine-tuning Approaches

Green-VLA employs two RL fine-tuning approaches in the R2 stage:

- **Trajectory Optimization with Native Fine-tuning:** A separate critic (Q-function) is trained on the R1 dataset using sparse rewards and Implicit Q-Learning (IQL). Trajectories generated by the R1 base model are improved by adding normalized gradients of the Q-function with respect to actions. These optimized trajectories are validated in the environment and, if successful, added back to the R1 dataset for further native R1 fine-tuning, repeating from R0-trained weights. This avoids direct modification of base model weights with RL gradients.
- **Optimization of Source Distribution:** An actor-critic algorithm trains a small actor network that generates the random noise fed into the flow-matching base model. This actor learns a new noise distribution $p'_0$ that maximizes the return obtained by the base model in the environment, effectively steering the base model's behavior by optimizing its input noise, while keeping the base model weights largely unchanged.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

Green-VLA was evaluated across its staged training phases and various robot embodiments, focusing on task-following, long-horizon robustness, and efficiency. The model, in its latest configuration, is a mid-scale $\sim$5B-parameter VLA model.

#### 4.1. R0 Phase Performance

Green-VLA (R0) demonstrated initial capabilities before embodiment-specific adaptation or RL.

- **ALOHA Table-Cleaning Task (Bimanual Cobot Magic):**
 - Green-VLA (R0) achieved an average success rate (SR) of 69.5% and an average time-to-clear of 1 minute 35 seconds. This compared to $\pi_0$ (35.6% SR, 2m59s), GR00T N1 (33.2% SR, >5m), WALL-OSS (12.1% SR, >5m), and AgiBot GO-1 (38.4% SR, 3m57s).
 - Green-VLA (R0) exhibited higher task-following accuracy and SR, indicating that multi-embodiment pretraining with quality alignment yielded effective initial performance without specific fine-tuning.

- **Simpler Benchmarks (Google Robot and WidowX):**
 - **Google Robot:** Green-VLA (R0, Paligemma 3B) achieved an average Visual Matching (VM) success rate of 53.6% and Visual Action (VA) success rate of 36.7%. These figures were comparable to or exceeded several fine-tuned baselines. For example, $\pi_0$ (Fine-tune) showed 44.1% VM and 41.1% VA.
 - **WidowX:** Green-VLA (R0, Paligemma 3B) demonstrated a pick success rate of 75.0% and a grasp success rate of 45.0%. This was competitive with various foundation policies like $\pi_0$ (Fine-tune, 53.1% pick SR) and RT-1X (11.5% pick SR).

#### 4.2. R1 Phase Performance: Efficient Fine-Tuning

The R1 stage, involving embodiment-specific adaptation, showed further improvements.

- **Simpler Benchmarks (Google Robot and WidowX):**
 - **Google Robot:** Green-VLA (R1, Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct) achieved 77.0% VM and 66.7% VA, surpassing its R0 performance and exceeding several other models like EO-1 (76.5% VM, 63.0% VA) and X-VLA (65.3% VM, 62.3% VA).
 - **WidowX:** Green-VLA (R1, Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct) achieved 89.6% pick success and 72.9% grasp success, demonstrating a notable increase over R0 (75.0% pick SR).

- **Guidance with JPM (E-commerce Shelf Picking):**
 - The JPM with guidance module significantly improved success rates in visually challenging e-commerce tasks.
 - Without guidance, Green-VLA achieved 62.3% (In-Domain Coarse), 36.7% (In-Domain SKU), and 10.2% (Out-of-Domain).
 - With JPM guidance, these rates increased to 95.4% (In-Domain Coarse), 93.1% (In-Domain SKU), and 72.8% (Out-of-Domain), indicating its effectiveness in precise object targeting, especially for novel or specific items.

- **Humanoid Evaluation (Green Robot):**
 - The Green robot successfully executed instruction-conditioned pick-and-place tasks, demonstrating robustness to OOD scene layouts and coordinated bimanual control.
 - Average success rates for various tasks included: Pick [item] (98%), Place in [basket] (100%), Pick [item] from [basket] (77%), Give [item] to user (99%), Hand over [item] (84%), and Clean full [table] (87%). The overall average across all tasks was 90%.
 - The system showed capability in long-horizon tasks with an average chain length (ACL) of 3.4 for specific tasks (e.g., sort apples and oranges), and efficient table cleaning.

#### 4.3. R2 Phase Performance: RL Alignment

The R2 stage, incorporating reinforcement learning fine-tuning, provided further gains.

- **Simpler BRIDGE WidowX:** R2 alignment improved the R1 model's success rate by an absolute 24%, achieving 91.7% pick success and 79.1% grasp success (Paligemma 3B), and 94.6% pick success and 80.5% grasp success (Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct). These results were competitive with or surpassed state-of-the-art fine-tuned baselines. For example, DB-MemVLA achieved 88.6% pick SR.
- **CALVIN ABC$\to$D Benchmark:**
 - R2 RL alignment markedly improved long-horizon consistency, error recovery, and compositional task success, raising the average chain length (ACL).
 - Green-VLA R2 (Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct) achieved an ACL of 4.5, comparable to $\pi_0$ (R, 4.1) and Flower (R, 4.5), and outperforming Green-VLA R1 (Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct, 4.2).
- **E-commerce Environment (Challenging Grasping):**
 - R2 fine-tuning specifically targeted physically reliable grasping of challenging objects (e.g., cookies, shampoo, pet food).
 - Success rates for items like "Cookies" increased from 30.4% (R1) to 82.7% (R2), "Shampoo" from 25.3% (R1) to 62.8% (R2), and "Pet food" from 12.6% (R1) to 88.3% (R2), indicating improved contact geometry and fewer drops.

In summary, Green-VLA demonstrated performance improvements across its staged training phases. The R0 phase established a strong multi-embodiment foundation. R1 provided gains through embodiment-specific adaptation and effective guidance for precise object interactions. R2 further enhanced robustness, long-horizon task execution, and grasping reliability through RL alignment.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Green-VLA presents a methodical framework for developing generalist robot policies, emphasizing structured data curation, action space unification, and a multi-stage training curriculum that extends to reinforcement learning. This approach offers several areas of significance and potential impact:

1. **Enhanced Generalization and Robustness:** By systematically addressing data heterogeneity and quality through its DataQA pipeline and unified action space, Green-VLA promotes positive knowledge transfer across diverse robot embodiments. The staged training, particularly the R0 (general robotics pretraining) and R1 (embodiment-specific adaptation) phases, supports the development of policies that can operate on humanoids, mobile manipulators, and fixed-base arms from a single model. This increases the potential for deploying generalist robots in varied real-world scenarios, reducing the need for separate models for each robot type.

2. **Improved Long-Horizon Task Execution:** The integration of reinforcement learning in the R2 phase addresses a significant limitation of traditional behavior cloning by aligning policies with task-level rewards. This capability is critical for complex, multi-step tasks that require sustained interaction and recovery from errors. The demonstrated gains in average chain length (ACL) and success rates for challenging manipulation tasks suggest that Green-VLA can execute longer, more intricate workflows reliably.

3. **Real-world Applicability and Efficiency:** Components like the episode progress prediction, out-of-distribution (OOD) detection, and the Joint Prediction Module (JPM) with guidance contribute to a deployment-ready design. OOD detection enhances safety and stability by nudging the policy back to known state distributions, while JPM allows for precise interaction with novel or visually ambiguous objects, important for dynamic environments like e-commerce. The focus on low-latency inference, achieved through architectural optimizations, further supports real-time robotic control.

4. **Foundation for Future Research and Development:** Green-VLA provides a structured recipe for progressing from web-scale multimodal pretraining to real-world robot deployment. This modular approach, combining large vision-language models with specialized robotics components, can serve as a foundation for future research in embodied AI. Envisioned future work includes incorporating multilingual instruction following for broader accessibility, integrating lightweight reasoning modules for more sophisticated task decomposition without sacrificing latency, and exploring embodied memory and trajectory replay for enhanced long-horizon performance.

5. **Addressing Data Scalability Challenges:** The framework moves beyond merely scaling data by emphasizing quality alignment and intelligent utilization of heterogeneous datasets. The DataQA pipeline and balanced-to-target sampling schedule for multi-embodiment training represent practical solutions to leverage large, noisy datasets effectively, contributing to more stable and efficient model learning.

Overall, Green-VLA represents an advancement in creating generalist, responsive, and reliable robot policies by tackling fundamental challenges in data quality, cross-embodiment generalization, and long-horizon control, thereby contributing to the practical realization of embodied AI systems.
