# Simultaneous Collision Detection and Force Estimation for Dynamic Quadrupedal Locomotion

> [!summary] Summary
> A new Interacting Multiple-Model Momentum-Based Kalman Observer (IMM-MBKO) enables quadrupedal robots to simultaneously detect unexpected collisions and accurately estimate contact forces using only proprioceptive sensors. This approach achieves a 95% collision detection success rate within 20ms and reduces force estimation error by 40% compared to previous methods, enhancing robot robustness in unstructured environments.

## Problem

- Quadrupedal robots require accurate detection of unexpected collisions and estimation of associated contact forces for maintaining balance and executing recovery behaviors in unstructured environments.
- Existing proprioceptive methods for collision detection often lack accurate force estimation or depend on specific gait patterns, while external sensors can be unreliable in adverse conditions.
- Distinguishing between intentional ground contact (stance) and unexpected impacts (collision) using only internal sensors is challenging.

## Method

- Researchers at Mitsubishi Electric Research Laboratories developed an Interacting Multiple-Model Momentum-Based Kalman Observer (IMM-MBKO) to simultaneously distinguish between swing, stance, and collision modes.
- The system accurately estimates external forces acting on each foot by integrating momentum dynamics with a disturbance observer within mode-dependent process and measurement models.
- Detected collisions trigger reactive control strategies, including reflex motions to increase step height and admittance control to modify swing leg trajectories, allowing the robot to comply with obstacles.

## Results

- The IMM-MBKO achieved a **95%** collision detection success rate within an average delay of 20ms, with low false positive (**5%**) and false negative (**5%**) rates.
- Force estimation error (RMSE) was **40%** lower compared to baseline methods, demonstrating superior accuracy in estimating external forces across different contact modes.
- Real-world experiments on a Unitree A1 robot validated reliable collision detection and effective obstacle negotiation across various gaits and speeds, confirming the approach's gait-invariance and real-time applicability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An Interacting Multiple-Model Kalman Filter (IMM-KF) can effectively distinguish between swing, stance, and collision contact modes by running parallel filters with mode-specific dynamic models and measurement assumptions.
> - Modeling the differences between stance and collision contacts, specifically through distinct friction cone constraints in measurement models, allows for accurate differentiation and force estimation.
> - Integrating collision detection with reactive control strategies, such as reflex motions and admittance control, enables robots to respond effectively to unexpected impacts and navigate obstacles.
