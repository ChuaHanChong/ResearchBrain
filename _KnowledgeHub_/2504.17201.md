# Simultaneous Collision Detection and Force Estimation for Dynamic Quadrupedal Locomotion

> [!summary] Summary
> A new Interacting Multiple-Model Momentum-Based Kalman Observer (IMM-MBKO) enables quadrupedal robots to simultaneously detect unexpected collisions and accurately estimate contact forces using only proprioceptive sensors. This approach achieves a 95% collision detection success rate within 20ms and reduces force estimation error by 40% compared to previous methods, enhancing robot robustness in unstructured environments.

## Problem

- Quadrupedal robots require accurate detection of unexpected collisions and estimation of associated contact forces for maintaining balance and executing recovery behaviors in unstructured environments.
- Existing proprioceptive methods for collision detection often lack accurate force estimation or depend on specific gait patterns, while external sensors can be unreliable in adverse conditions.
- Distinguishing between intentional ground contact (stance) and unexpected impacts (collision) using only internal sensors is challenging.

## Method

- Researchers at Mitsubishi Electric Research Laboratories developed an Interacting Multiple-Model Momentum-Based Kalman Observer (IMM-MBKO) to simultaneously distinguish between swing, stance, and collision modes.
- The system accurately estimates external forces acting on each foot by integrating momentum dynamics with a disturbance observer within mode-dependent process and measurement models.
- Detected collisions trigger reactive control strategies, including reflex motions to increase step height and admittance control to modify swing leg trajectories, allowing the robot to comply with obstacles.

## Results

- The IMM-MBKO achieved a **95%** collision detection success rate within an average delay of 20ms, with low false positive (**5%**) and false negative (**5%**) rates.
- Force estimation error (RMSE) was **40%** lower compared to baseline methods, demonstrating superior accuracy in estimating external forces across different contact modes.
- Real-world experiments on a Unitree A1 robot validated reliable collision detection and effective obstacle negotiation across various gaits and speeds, confirming the approach's gait-invariance and real-time applicability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An Interacting Multiple-Model Kalman Filter (IMM-KF) can effectively distinguish between swing, stance, and collision contact modes by running parallel filters with mode-specific dynamic models and measurement assumptions.
> - Modeling the differences between stance and collision contacts, specifically through distinct friction cone constraints in measurement models, allows for accurate differentiation and force estimation.
> - Integrating collision detection with reactive control strategies, such as reflex motions and admittance control, enables robots to respond effectively to unexpected impacts and navigate obstacles.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

#### 1.1. Background and Context

Quadrupedal robots are increasingly being deployed in complex, unstructured environments where they must navigate obstacles and uncertain terrain. In these scenarios, the ability to detect unexpected collisions and accurately estimate contact forces is crucial for maintaining balance and executing appropriate recovery behaviors.

#### 1.2. Paper Overview

The paper "Simultaneous Collision Detection and Force Estimation for Dynamic Quadrupedal Locomotion" by researchers from Mitsubishi Electric Research Laboratories introduces a novel approach that addresses a significant challenge in legged robotics: how to accurately distinguish between intentional contacts (normal locomotion) and unexpected collisions while simultaneously estimating the forces involved using only proprioceptive sensors.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

#### 2.1. Motivation and Limitations of Existing Approaches

Current approaches often rely on external sensors like cameras or LiDAR, which may be unreliable in dusty, smoky, or poorly lit environments. Alternatively, some methods use only internal joint encoders but typically require specific gait patterns or lack accurate force estimation capabilities. The method proposed in this paper overcomes these limitations by developing a proprioceptive sensing approach that is independent of gait patterns and provides both collision detection and force estimation simultaneously.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

#### 3.1. Core Principles and Methodology

The core innovation of this work is an Interacting Multiple-Model Momentum-Based Kalman Observer (IMM-MBKO) that can simultaneously:

1. Distinguish between three different contact modes (swing, stance, and collision)
2. Accurately estimate external forces acting on the robot
3. Function independently of specific gait patterns or controllers

This approach builds upon momentum-based observers that have been used for contact detection in the past, but significantly extends their capabilities through a multiple-model framework.

The methodology integrates three key components:

1. **Contact Mode Estimation**: Using an Interacting Multiple-Model Kalman Filter to estimate the probability of each leg being in swing, stance, or collision mode.
2. **Force Estimation**: Estimating the external forces acting on each foot based on the robot's dynamic model and the estimated contact mode.
3. **Reactive Control**: Implementing reflex motions and admittance control to respond to detected collisions, helping the robot navigate obstacles safely.

#### 3.2. Interacting Multiple Model Kalman Filter Design

The paper introduces an Interacting Multiple-Model Kalman Filter (IMM-KF) that runs three parallel Kalman filters, each corresponding to one of the possible contact modes:

1. **Swing Mode**: The foot is in the air with no contact force
2. **Stance Mode**: The foot is in intentional contact with the ground
3. **Collision Mode**: The foot has experienced an unexpected collision

The state vector for each filter includes the generalized momentum and the external force components. The process model combines momentum dynamics with a disturbance observer for contact forces:

$$\begin{bmatrix} \dot{\boldsymbol{h}} \\ \dot{\boldsymbol{f}}_{ext} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} -\boldsymbol{A}\boldsymbol{h} + \boldsymbol{S}_{m}\boldsymbol{f}_{ext} + \boldsymbol{\tau} \\ \boldsymbol{0} \end{bmatrix}$$

Where:
- $\boldsymbol{h}$ is the generalized momentum
- $\boldsymbol{f}_{ext}$ is the external force
- $\boldsymbol{A}$ is the momentum dissipation matrix
- $\boldsymbol{S}_{m}$ is a mode-dependent selection matrix
- $\boldsymbol{\tau}$ represents joint torques

What makes this approach innovative is that the selection matrix $\boldsymbol{S}_{m}$ depends on the contact mode, creating different process models for each mode. This allows the system to better capture the specific dynamics of each contact situation.

#### 3.3. Contact Modes and Measurement Models

A key contribution is how the paper models the differences between stance and collision contacts. While both involve forces applied to the foot, they differ significantly in their characteristics:

- **Stance contacts**: Have a primary vertical force component with lateral friction limited by a narrow friction cone
- **Collision contacts**: Can have forces in any direction with a wider friction cone

These differences are captured in the measurement models for each Kalman filter:

1. **Swing Mode**: The measurement model assumes zero contact force
2. **Stance Mode**: The model incorporates friction cone constraints with a narrow cone
3. **Collision Mode**: The model allows for forces in different directions with a wider friction cone

The IMM-KF computes mode probabilities by evaluating the likelihood of measurements under each model. When a collision occurs, the collision mode quickly becomes the most probable.

#### 3.4. Reaction Strategies: Reflex Motion and Admittance Control

Once a collision is detected, the robot must respond appropriately. The paper implements two complementary reaction strategies:

1. **Reflex Motion**: When a collision is detected, the robot automatically increases the step height of the affected leg to clear the obstacle. This is triggered when the collision probability exceeds a threshold.

2. **Admittance Control (AC)**: This approach modifies the swing leg trajectory based on the estimated external force, allowing the leg to comply with obstacles rather than rigidly pushing against them.

The difference in foot acceleration between these approaches is significant:

The admittance control modifies the swing leg command based on the estimated external force:

$$\boldsymbol{p}_{d} = \boldsymbol{p}_{ref} + \boldsymbol{M}_{AC}^{-1}\boldsymbol{D}_{AC}\int \hat{\boldsymbol{f}}_{ext} dt$$

Where:
- $\boldsymbol{p}_{d}$ is the desired foot position
- $\boldsymbol{p}_{ref}$ is the reference trajectory
- $\boldsymbol{M}_{AC}$ and $\boldsymbol{D}_{AC}$ are admittance parameters
- $\hat{\boldsymbol{f}}_{ext}$ is the estimated external force

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. Implementation and Evaluation

The researchers implemented and evaluated their approach on a Unitree A1 quadrupedal robot, using only the robot's onboard joint encoders for state estimation. The system was tested in both Gazebo simulations and real-world hardware experiments.

The force estimation performance was compared against several baseline methods:

1. **MBKO**: A standard momentum-based Kalman observer
2. **FOMBO**: A fixed-order momentum-based observer
3. **PM-MBKO**: A probabilistic mixture momentum-based Kalman observer

The results show that the proposed IMM-MBKO approach achieves superior performance in estimating external forces accurately across different contact modes.

#### 4.2. Performance and Comparative Results

The paper provides comprehensive quantitative results comparing the proposed method with baseline approaches. Key performance metrics include:

1. **Detection Success Rate**: The IMM-MBKO achieved a 95% success rate in detecting collisions compared to 82% for the best baseline method.
2. **Detection Delay**: The approach detected collisions within 20ms on average, significantly faster than other methods.
3. **False Positive/Negative Rates**: The method achieved the lowest false positive rate (5%) and false negative rate (5%) among all compared approaches.
4. **Force Estimation Error**: The root mean square error (RMSE) for force estimation was 40% lower than the best baseline method.

The computational performance was also evaluated. For a single leg, the IMM-MBKO requires approximately 1.8ms of computation time, while the four-leg version takes about 5.0ms. This is well within the typical control loop time of 10ms for most quadrupedal robots, making the approach suitable for real-time implementation.

#### 4.3. Real-World Validation

The real-world experiments on the Unitree A1 robot validated the simulation results and demonstrated the practical utility of the approach. The robot was tested in scenarios where it had to navigate over obstacles and respond to unexpected collisions:

The experiments confirmed that:

1. The system could reliably detect collisions in real-world settings
2. The reflex motion and admittance control strategies enabled effective obstacle negotiation
3. The force estimation was accurate enough to support improved balancing through MPC

One particularly important result was that the approach worked consistently across different gaits and walking speeds without requiring recalibration, demonstrating its gait-invariant properties.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

#### 5.1. Conclusion and Significance

The paper presents a significant advancement in proprioceptive sensing for legged robots. By enabling simultaneous collision detection and force estimation using only joint encoders, it addresses a critical gap in the capabilities of quadrupedal robots operating in unstructured environments.

Key contributions include:

1. A novel IMM-MBKO framework that distinguishes between different contact modes while estimating external forces
2. Mode-dependent process and measurement models that capture the different dynamics of swing, stance, and collision contacts
3. Integration of collision detection with reactive control strategies (reflex motion and admittance control)
4. Demonstration of gait-pattern invariance, making the approach widely applicable

This work has important implications for the deployment of legged robots in challenging environments where external sensing may be unreliable, such as search and rescue operations, industrial inspection, or exploration of hazardous environments. By enhancing the proprioceptive capabilities of these robots, they can operate more safely and effectively without relying on external sensors that may fail in adverse conditions.

The approach also provides a foundation for developing more sophisticated obstacle negotiation behaviors that can help legged robots navigate complex terrains with greater autonomy and robustness.
