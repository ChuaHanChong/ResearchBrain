# REASONING GYM: Reasoning Environments for Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards

> [!summary] Summary
> Open-Thought developed REASONING GYM, a library of over 100 procedurally generated environments with verifiable rewards, enabling large language models (LLMs) to acquire transferable reasoning skills. This library addresses data scarcity for RLVR training, showing that models can improve on challenging tasks and achieve positive transfer to established external benchmarks like MATH and Big-Bench Hard.

## Problem

- The scarcity of high-quality, diverse, and robust training data limits the advancement of reasoning capabilities in Large Language Models (LLMs) using Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR).
- Existing fixed datasets are prone to model memorization and do not allow for continuous challenging or precise evaluation of generalizable reasoning skills.
- Traditional data sources, such as expensive human-curated datasets or noisy internet-scraped content, lack scalability and reliability for continuous RLVR training.

## Method

- Introduces ==REASONING GYM (RG)==, an open-source library comprising over 100 ==procedurally generated== reasoning environments across eleven distinct categories.
- Each RG task includes configurable parameters (difficulty, structural, stylistic) for fine-grained control over problem characteristics, enabling ==dynamic curriculum learning==.
- Integrates algorithmic ==verifiers== for every task, providing automatic, objective, and verifiable rewards crucial for efficient and robust RLVR training.

## Results

- Frontier LLMs exhibited low zero-shot performance and pronounced 'difficulty cliffs' on many challenging RG tasks, particularly in visual-spatial, algorithmic, and graph reasoning.
- RLVR training on RG tasks resulted in consistent intra-domain performance improvements (e.g., Algebra **+11.7%**) and strong cross-domain transfer (e.g., algorithmic training boosted algebra by **+29.1%**).
- Skills developed through RG training positively transferred to external benchmarks, with the RG-Math trained model achieving gains of **+9.7%** on MATH and **+7.7%** on Big-Bench Hard.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Procedural generation offers a scalable and verifiable alternative to traditional datasets, effectively addressing the data scarcity bottleneck for RLVR in LLMs.
> - Reasoning skills acquired through RG training demonstrate significant intra-domain transfer and surprising cross-domain transfer between different reasoning categories (e.g., algorithmic training improving algebra and geometry).
> - Curriculum learning, which adaptively increases task difficulty, consistently leads to superior performance across various difficulty levels compared to fixed-difficulty training.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of "REASONING GYM" (RG) is situated within the rapidly advancing field of Large Language Models (LLMs) and their increasingly sophisticated reasoning abilities. The authors specifically highlight recent breakthroughs by models such as OpenAI-o1, DeepSeek-R1, and QwQ-32B, attributing much of this progress to Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR). RLVR is a powerful paradigm that enables models to learn open-ended reasoning processes by receiving outcome-based feedback.

However, the paper identifies a critical bottleneck in the continued success and scalability of RLVR: the scarcity of high-quality, diverse, and robust training data. Current approaches typically rely on either expensive human-curated datasets, which are not scalable, or internet-scraped content, which often suffers from issues of sustainability, reliability, noise, and susceptibility to memorization. This data limitation hinders the ability of LLMs to develop truly generalizable reasoning skills and prevents researchers from continuously challenging models with new, harder problem instances.

REASONING GYM directly addresses this challenge by providing a novel alternative: procedurally generated reasoning environments. This approach fundamentally differs from traditional fixed-dataset benchmarks (e.g., GSM8K, MATH, Big-Bench Hard, GPQA, various coding benchmarks), which, despite their utility, are prone to models overfitting or simply memorizing solutions rather than learning underlying reasoning strategies. While other procedurally generated benchmarks exist, particularly in games and puzzles, RG distinguishes itself by its vast scope, covering over 100 distinct data generators across an extensive array of reasoning domains.

In the landscape of RLVR environments, RG positions itself alongside efforts like Tülu 3, DeepSeek-R1, TextArena, Logic-RL, Absolute Zero Reasoner, SYNTHETIC-1, KORGym, Reasoning Core, and GEM. These related works also aim to generate data for RLVR or provide environments for LLM evaluation. RG differentiates itself by its comprehensive nature, offering a singular, extensive library of algorithmically verifiable tasks designed for continuous, adaptive training and evaluation. Its broad domain coverage, from symbolic algebra to visual cognition and formal logic, coupled with fine-grained control over problem complexity, positions it as a foundational resource for pushing the boundaries of reasoning in LLMs beyond the "dataset ceiling."

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of REASONING GYM is to overcome the fundamental data scarcity bottleneck that impedes the advancement of reasoning abilities in large language models, particularly when trained with Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR). The authors are motivated by the need for an open-ended, scalable, and robust environment that can continuously challenge and evaluate reasoning models.

Specifically, the key objectives and motivations behind RG are:

- **Scalability and Data Abundance:** To provide a virtually infinite supply of high-quality training data for RLVR. Unlike fixed datasets, RG's procedural generation ensures that models can be exposed to endless, unique problem instances, thereby eliminating concerns about data exhaustion and memorization.
- **Controllable Complexity:** To allow for adjustable difficulty levels and structural variations within tasks. This enables dynamic curriculum learning, where models can progressively tackle harder problems as their capabilities improve, fostering genuine skill acquisition rather than rote learning.
- **Algorithmic Verifiability:** To ensure that all task solutions can be automatically and unambiguously verified. This objective is crucial for reliable RLVR training, as it eliminates the need for expensive human annotation and subjective evaluation, providing clear, objective rewards.
- **Elimination of Memorization:** By generating unique instances, RG ensures that models cannot simply memorize solutions, forcing them to develop robust and generalizable reasoning strategies.
- **Precise Experimental Control:** To offer researchers the ability to isolate and systematically study specific reasoning capabilities (e.g., algebraic manipulation, graph traversal, logical deduction) by varying task parameters.
- **Continuous Evaluation:** To provide a framework for evaluating model performance across a continuous spectrum of difficulty, offering a more nuanced understanding of capabilities and identifying "performance cliffs" as complexity increases.
- **Pushing "Dataset Ceiling":** To create an environment where models can be continuously challenged with increasingly difficult and novel instances, allowing for genuine advances in reasoning rather than merely demonstrating familiarity with existing benchmarks.

These motivations are underpinned by three core design principles: (P1) Algorithmic Verifiability, ensuring objective feedback; (P2) Large Solution Spaces, promoting generalizable strategies; and (P3) Parametric Difficulty Control, enabling adaptive learning curricula.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

REASONING GYM's methodology centers on the procedural generation of a diverse suite of reasoning environments. This approach is designed to produce an unlimited supply of training and evaluation data with verifiable rewards.

**Core Components:**
The library comprises over 100 data generators and corresponding verifiers, spanning eleven distinct high-level categories that mirror human problem-solving abstractions:
1. **Mathematical domains:** Algebra, Arithmetic, Geometry
2. **Algorithmic thinking:** Algorithms, Code
3. **Logical reasoning:** Logic, Induction
4. **Pattern recognition:** Cognition (+ARC)
5. **Constraint satisfaction:** Games
6. **Discrete structures:** Graphs

Each "task" within RG is not a fixed question-answer pair, but rather a generative algorithm. These algorithms include configurable parameters that allow for fine-grained control over problem characteristics:
- **Difficulty Parameters:** Directly modulate problem complexity (e.g., number of nodes in a graph, polynomial degrees, word lengths).
- **Structural Parameters:** Define fundamental properties of the problem (e.g., dimensionality of a matrix, types of constraints, depth of a logic proof).
- **Stylistic Parameters:** Vary the presentation of the problem without altering its underlying difficulty (e.g., variable names, number formats, problem phrasing).

**RLVR Integration:**
A key innovation is the inclusion of "verifiers" for every task. These are algorithmic mechanisms that automatically and objectively check the correctness of a model's proposed solution. This allows for the assignment of "verifiable rewards," which are essential for Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) training, providing direct and unambiguous feedback to the agent.

**Experimental Approach:**
The authors conducted a series of comprehensive experiments using Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct as the base model and GRPO (Generalized Advantage Estimation with Proximal Policy Optimization) as the RL algorithm, consuming approximately 1500 A6000 GPU hours.

1. **Zero-shot Performance Evaluation:** State-of-the-art LLMs, including reasoning-optimized (e.g., o3-mini, DeepSeek-R1, Grok 3 Mini) and general-purpose models (e.g., Llama 4 Maverick, Claude 3.5 Sonnet, Gemma 3 27B), were evaluated on both "easy" and "hard" configurations of RG tasks to assess their innate reasoning capabilities and susceptibility to increased difficulty.

2. **Intra-Domain Transfer:** Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct was RL-fine-tuned on a composite of tasks within a specific reasoning category (e.g., Algebra, Algorithmic, Games). Its performance was then evaluated on *held-out* tasks from the *same* category to determine if learned skills generalize within a domain.

3. **Cross-Domain Transfer:** Separate instances of Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct were trained on individual RG categories. Their performance was subsequently assessed on held-out tasks from *different* reasoning domains to investigate whether reasoning skills acquired in one domain could benefit performance in others.

4. **Transfer to External Benchmarks:** Models trained on RG's mathematical and algorithmic categories were evaluated on established external reasoning benchmarks like GSM8K, MATH, Big-Bench Hard, and MMLU-Pro to validate the real-world applicability and transferability of skills learned within RG.

5. **Curriculum RLVR:** The benefits of curriculum learning were explored by comparing a training setup that progressively increases task difficulty based on model performance against one that samples uniformly from all difficulty levels.

The methodology emphasizes the dynamic, scalable, and verifiable nature of RG, enabling rigorous experimentation into the fundamental aspects of reasoning in LLMs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental investigation using REASONING GYM yielded several significant findings:

- **Low Zero-Shot Performance on Challenging Tasks:** Even frontier LLMs exhibited low zero-shot performance on many RG tasks, particularly those requiring visual-spatial reasoning presented in text format, such as tasks in the ARC, cognition, and games categories. This indicates that these areas remain significant challenges for current models.

- **Pronounced Difficulty Cliffs:** Increasing task complexity led to sharp performance drops, a phenomenon termed "difficulty cliffs." The most severe declines were observed in algorithmic reasoning (28% drop), code generation (62% drop), and graph problems (30% drop) when transitioning from easy to hard configurations. This suggests that current LLM reasoning capabilities are often fragile and may rely on pattern recognition rather than robust, generalizable strategies.

- **Superiority of Reasoning-Optimized Models:** Models explicitly trained for reasoning (e.g., OpenAI-o3-mini, DeepSeek-R1, Grok 3 Mini) consistently and substantially outperformed general-purpose models (e.g., Llama 4 Maverick, Claude 3.5 Sonnet, Gemma 3 27B) across RG tasks, demonstrating a significant average performance gap (e.g., 22% between the best reasoning and non-reasoning models). This highlights the value of reasoning-specific training data.

- **Consistent Intra-Domain Transfer:** RLVR training on subsets of tasks within a reasoning domain consistently improved performance on held-out tasks from the same domain. Improvements ranged from modest (e.g., arithmetic +6.3%, cognition +2.0%) to substantial (e.g., algebra +11.7%, algorithmic +7.4%). Notably, the "Games" category, where the base model initially achieved 0% accuracy, showed measurable capability (3.3%) after RLVR training, indicating that domain-specific training can bootstrap entirely new skills.

- **Surprising Cross-Domain Transfer:** The experiments provided strong evidence of cross-domain transfer of reasoning skills:
 - Models trained on algorithmic tasks showed significant improvements in distinct mathematical domains, specifically algebra (+29.1%) and geometry (+22.3%).
 - Training on logic tasks enhanced performance in cognition (+13.3%) and graph reasoning (+9.1%).
 - Even models trained on games tasks, despite poor in-domain performance, showed gains in algebra (+21.8%) and cognition (+13.1%). These results suggest that RLVR instills fundamental, transferable reasoning primitives.

- **Positive Transfer to External Benchmarks:** Skills developed through RG training successfully transferred to established reasoning benchmarks:
 - The RG-Math trained model achieved significant gains on MATH (+9.7%) and Big-Bench Hard (+7.7%), and a marginal gain on GSM8K (+0.5%).
 - Both RG-Math and RG-Algorithmic models demonstrated noticeable improvements across several tasks within MMLU-Pro, including Math (+5.62%), Computer Science (+4.40%), and Psychology (+6.02%) for the RG-Math model. This validates the real-world utility of RG-based training.

- **Effectiveness of Curriculum RLVR:** Curriculum learning, where task difficulty adaptively increases, generally led to superior performance across difficulty levels compared to fixed-difficulty training. For example, on "Spell Backwards" (word length 4), the curriculum model outperformed the non-curriculum by +40.67%. This highlights the efficiency and effectiveness of structured progression in learning complex tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

REASONING GYM represents a significant advancement in the research and development of artificial intelligence, particularly concerning the reasoning capabilities of large language models. Its potential impact spans several critical areas:

- **Solving the Data Scarcity Problem:** RG directly tackles the fundamental bottleneck of high-quality training data for RLVR. By offering over 100 procedurally generated environments with virtually infinite, verifiable instances, it provides a sustainable and scalable alternative to expensive human-curated or noisy internet-scraped datasets. This ensures a continuous supply of novel challenges, preventing model memorization and enabling continuous progress.

- **Advancing LLM Reasoning Capabilities:** The demonstrated intra-domain and, more notably, cross-domain transfer of skills from RG training provides compelling evidence that models can acquire truly generalizable reasoning primitives. This moves beyond the mere recognition and application of solution templates, suggesting a deeper understanding and fostering the development of robust, broadly applicable reasoning skills crucial for advanced AI. The ability to bootstrap entirely new capabilities (e.g., in Games) after RLVR training is particularly impactful.

- **Rigorous and Unbiased Evaluation Framework:** RG serves as a powerful evaluation tool. Its procedural nature eliminates memorization concerns inherent in fixed benchmarks, allowing for a more accurate assessment of genuine reasoning abilities. The parametric control over difficulty enables researchers to precisely probe "difficulty cliffs" and understand where model competencies break down, providing clear targets for future research.

- **Facilitating Research and Development:** By open-sourcing the complete library, including generators, verifiers, and training infrastructure, Open-Thought empowers the broader research community. This resource enables systematic experimentation, allowing researchers to isolate specific reasoning capabilities, study their development, and compare models under standardized, reproducible conditions. It democratizes access to high-quality reasoning data, accelerating the pace of innovation.

- **Towards More Robust and General AI:** The positive transfer of skills to established external benchmarks like MATH, Big-Bench Hard, and MMLU-Pro validates RG's utility for building models that are not only proficient in synthetic environments but also perform better on real-world reasoning tasks. This contributes to the broader goal of developing more robust, adaptable, and generally intelligent AI systems.

- **Driving Curriculum Learning Innovations:** The findings on the benefits of curriculum RLVR underscore the importance of structured learning paths for complex reasoning tasks. RG provides a flexible platform for developing and testing advanced curriculum strategies, further optimizing the training process for more efficient and effective skill acquisition.

While the authors acknowledge limitations, such as the difficulty of capturing domains requiring extensive domain knowledge or creativity with procedural generators, and the current focus on single-turn, text-based reasoning, RG lays a strong foundation. It inspires future work in areas like multimodal and multi-turn reasoning and continual learning, ultimately contributing to the long-term vision of developing AI with human-like reasoning capabilities.
