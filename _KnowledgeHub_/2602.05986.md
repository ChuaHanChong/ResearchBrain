# RISE-Video: Can Video Generators Decode Implicit World Rules?

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Shanghai Jiao Tong University and Tencent Youtu Lab developed RISE-Video, a comprehensive benchmark for evaluating the implicit world reasoning capabilities of Text-Image-to-Video (TI2V) models. This framework, which leverages an LMM-based automated judging pipeline, revealed that even leading TI2V models like Hailuo 2.3 achieved only 22.5% accuracy, consistently struggling with higher-level reasoning despite their visual fidelity.

## Problem

- Existing video generation benchmarks predominantly emphasize surface-level qualities such as perceptual realism and temporal consistency, lacking diagnostic granularity for higher-level cognitive reasoning.
- The capacity of current Text-Image-to-Video (TI2V) models to internalize and reason over implicit world rules (e.g., physical laws, social norms, logical inferences) remains largely unquantified.
- Without a dedicated framework, models might generate visually convincing but physically implausible, logically inconsistent, or experientially nonsensical videos, hindering the development of true 'world simulators'.

## Method

- Introduced ==RISE-Video==, a reasoning-oriented benchmark featuring ==467 human-annotated samples== across eight distinct reasoning categories, from perceptual to logical knowledge.
- Developed a ==multi-dimensional evaluation protocol== using four complementary metrics: Reasoning Alignment, Temporal Consistency, Physical Rationality, and Visual Quality, with specialized handling for abstract tasks.
- Proposed an ==automated LMM-based judging pipeline==, primarily utilizing GPT-5, to efficiently assess videos while maintaining high alignment with human judgments.

## Results

- The top-performing TI2V model, Hailuo 2.3, achieved only **22.5%** accuracy on the RISE-Video benchmark, indicating pervasive deficiencies in implicit reasoning across all **11** evaluated models.
- Models performed best on low-level Perceptual Knowledge tasks but showed consistently low performance in Logical Capability, highlighting a fundamental limitation in abstract, rule-based reasoning.
- Sora 2 showed notable strength in Temporal Consistency, while Hailuo 2.3 demonstrated a clear advantage in Reasoning Alignment, outperforming the second-ranked model by **6.6%** in this metric.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current state-of-the-art TI2V models, despite their impressive visual fidelity, systematically struggle with implicit world reasoning, particularly in logical and complex experiential scenarios.
> - There is a significant performance gap between closed-source and open-source models, with open-source models exhibiting more visual artifacts and lower reasoning capabilities.
> - The LMM-based judging pipeline, validated through ablation studies, demonstrates robust alignment with human preferences, enabling scalable and reliable evaluation for reasoning in video generation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of video generation has experienced rapid and transformative advancements in recent years, primarily driven by innovations in large-scale generative models, especially diffusion models. Early work laid the groundwork for Text-to-Video (T2V) and Text-Image-to-Video (TI2V) synthesis, with models demonstrating remarkable progress in visual fidelity, realism, and temporal coherence. Architectures like Lumiere, CogVideoX, VideoPoet, and systems such as Sora 2, Veo 3.1, and Kling 2.6 have pushed the boundaries of video duration, resolution, and quality. These models can now generate increasingly plausible and engaging video content from textual prompts or combined text-image inputs.

Despite these significant strides, the authors pinpoint a critical yet under-explored frontier: the capacity of these models to internalize and reason over *implicit world rules* that extend beyond explicit textual instructions. While existing benchmarks for video generation have evolved from coarse perceptual metrics to more structured protocols, most predominantly emphasize surface-level qualities such as perceptual realism, temporal consistency, and adherence to explicit prompts. Benchmarks like VBench offer comprehensive evaluations across various dimensions, and others focus on specific aspects like physical commonsense (e.g., VideoPhy-2, T2VBench) or video dynamics (e.g., ChronoMagic-Bench). However, the explicit evaluation of *higher-level cognitive reasoning*—encompassing logical, societal, experiential, or subject-specific knowledge—has remained conspicuously scarce, particularly within the TI2V paradigm.

RISE-Video strategically intervenes to bridge this gap. It represents a pioneering effort to shift the evaluative focus from merely assessing *what* models generate visually to *how well* they understand and apply underlying cognitive and physical constraints. By introducing a dedicated, reasoning-oriented diagnostic framework, RISE-Video positions itself as a crucial tool for assessing whether video generators are merely sophisticated "pixel manipulators" or are beginning to function as rudimentary "world simulators" capable of decoding and applying implicit rules. The work also aligns with the growing trend of leveraging Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) as automated judges in evaluating generative AI, but it customizes this approach specifically for the nuanced assessment of reasoning abilities. This benchmark is thus timely, addressing a fundamental limitation in the current generation of TI2V models and providing a structured pathway for future research to develop more intelligent and rule-aware generative systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind RISE-Video stems from a fundamental unanswered question in the field of generative video models: can contemporary Text-Image-to-Video (TI2V) models reliably internalize and reason over implicit world rules that extend beyond explicit textual instructions? While models have achieved impressive visual fidelity and structural controllability, their "intelligence" in understanding the underlying dynamics, causality, and constraints of the real world remains largely unquantified and often overlooked by existing evaluation paradigms.

The authors identify a critical gap: current evaluation benchmarks, whether general-purpose like VBench or task-specific, predominantly emphasize perceptual quality, temporal coherence, and faithful instruction following at a superficial level. They lack the diagnostic granularity to probe deeper cognitive reasoning abilities, such as common sense, physical laws, social norms, or logical inferences. This deficiency means that models might generate visually convincing videos that are nonetheless physically implausible, logically inconsistent, or experientially nonsensical. Without a dedicated framework to expose these reasoning failures, the community risks developing models that excel aesthetically but fundamentally misunderstand the world they are attempting to simulate.

Against this backdrop, the key objectives of the RISE-Video project are:

1. **To introduce a pioneering reasoning-oriented benchmark for TI2V synthesis:** The primary goal is to shift the evaluative focus from surface-level aesthetics to deep cognitive reasoning, providing a structured testbed for probing model intelligence.
2. **To develop a comprehensive dataset spanning diverse reasoning dimensions:** RISE-Video aims to cover a wide spectrum of reasoning types—from low-level perceptual cues to high-level abstract inferences—by meticulously curating 467 human-annotated samples across eight distinct categories (Experiential, Commonsense, Temporal, Societal, Perceptual, Spatial, Subject-Specific, and Logical Reasoning).
3. **To establish a multi-dimensional evaluation protocol:** The research seeks to define a set of complementary metrics—Reasoning Alignment, Temporal Consistency, Physical Rationality, and Visual Quality—that collectively provide a holistic appraisal of whether generated videos are not only visually plausible but also strictly adhere to cognitive and physical constraints.
4. **To propose an automated and scalable evaluation pipeline leveraging Large Multimodal Models (LMMs):** Recognizing the limitations of manual human evaluation for large-scale assessment, an objective is to develop an LMM-based judging system that can emulate human-centric assessment while maintaining high alignment with human judgments, thereby enabling efficient and reproducible evaluation.
5. **To conduct extensive experiments on state-of-the-art TI2V models to reveal their current reasoning limitations:** By applying the new benchmark to a range of existing generative models, the project aims to systematically identify pervasive deficiencies and provide critical insights that can guide the advancement of future world-simulating generative models.

In essence, the motivation is to elevate the evaluation of video generation beyond mere visual realism, pushing the field towards models that possess a more profound understanding of the implicit rules governing our world.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of RISE-Video is meticulously designed to address the challenges of evaluating implicit reasoning in TI2V models. It encompasses a structured data construction process, a multi-dimensional evaluation protocol, and an innovative LMM-based automated judging pipeline.

#### 3.1. Data Construction

The cornerstone of RISE-Video is its dataset, explicitly partitioned based on the types of reasoning knowledge required. This systematic categorization ensures comprehensive coverage of diverse reasoning scenarios. The benchmark comprises 467 meticulously curated samples, each human-annotated to ensure ground-truth reliability.

**Eight Reasoning Categories:**
1. **Commonsense Knowledge:** Evaluates understanding of everyday physics (e.g., vase breaking), human life (e.g., mosquito bites leading to swelling), and basic healthcare (e.g., dental decay).
2. **Subject Knowledge:** Probes structured, discipline-specific knowledge beyond general common sense, including physics (e.g., electricity, mechanics), chemistry (e.g., reactions), geography (e.g., river formations), and sports (e.g., soccer shooting).
3. **Perceptual Knowledge:** Assesses the model's capacity to capture and manipulate low-level visual attributes: size, color, count, position, and challenging occlusion scenarios.
4. **Societal Knowledge:** Evaluates understanding of social and cultural contexts, encompassing emotion recognition, social rules (e.g., disposing trash), and cultural customs (e.g., dietary traditions).
5. **Logical Capability:** Requires systematic application of explicit rules over visual elements. This includes game actions (e.g., Super Mario rules), puzzle solving (e.g., mazes, Gomoku), and geometric reasoning.
6. **Experiential Knowledge:** Examines human-like, experience-based knowledge for interpreting intentions (e.g., spoon near mouth implies eating), identities, procedures (e.g., peeling an orange before eating), and contextual understanding.
7. **Spatial Knowledge:** Assesses understanding of 3D spatial relationships and object manipulation, covering viewpoint transformations, object arrangement (relative size, shape), and structural inference from incomplete components.
8. **Temporal Knowledge:** Evaluates reasoning across different time spans and ordering patterns: short-term, medium-term, long-term, and reverse temporal reasoning (e.g., an elephant transforming backward in time).

Input images for the benchmark are sourced from high-quality image generation models, websites with permissive licenses, and adapted samples from the RISEBench dataset. To address privacy concerns, image stylization is applied to tasks involving real individuals where original appearance is not crucial for reasoning evaluation.

#### 3.2. Evaluation Metrics

RISE-Video employs a multi-dimensional evaluation protocol consisting of four complementary metrics, each designed to assess a specific aspect of video generation quality and reasoning.

1. **Reasoning Alignment (RA):** This metric assesses whether the generated video demonstrates correct knowledge-based reasoning. For most samples, LMM-as-a-Judge is used, answering manually designed, knowledge-aware questions in a binary (Yes/No) format, yielding a 0-1 score. Reasoning-aware frame sampling strategies are adopted, varying rates based on whether full event progression or only final states need evaluation.
 - **Specialized Handling for Schematic Puzzles (Logical Capability):** For tasks like Maze Navigation, Symmetry Generation, and Board Games, which are ill-suited for linguistic LMM judgment, specialized strategies are implemented:
 - **Maze Navigation:** Agent trajectories are tracked via color matching across frames to verify "no wall-crossing" and "reach target" constraints, with scores mapped from the number of satisfied conditions ({0, 0.5, 1}).
 - **Symmetry Generation:** Grid-level positional alignment between the last frame and the Ground Truth (GT) reference is calculated using HSV-identified cells, leading to an accuracy score discretized into {0, 0.5, 1}.
 - **Board Games:** The LMM judge is provided with both the generated last frame and an auxiliary GT reference image for precise visual and structural comparison, scoring on a 1-5 scale.

2. **Temporal Consistency (TC):** This metric evaluates if the video only exhibits changes explicitly or implicitly required by the instruction, while preserving all other irrelevant aspects (e.g., object attributes, scene layout, character identity). An LMM judge, guided by the instruction, assesses consistency of non-instructed elements on a 1-5 scale, using uniform frame sampling.

3. **Physical Rationality (PR):** This metric assesses adherence to fundamental laws of physics and real-world logic (e.g., gravity, object permanence, collision dynamics). The LMM judge verifies physical accuracy and logical coherence of movements and environmental reactions on a 1-5 scale. Abstract logical tasks are excluded.

4. **Visual Quality (VQ):** This metric evaluates perceptual fidelity and technical integrity, focusing on subject sharpness, texture preservation, and lighting consistency. To ensure fair assessment, super-resolution is manually applied to low-clarity images before evaluation to prevent LMMs from misinterpreting low native resolution as technical blur. An LMM (GPT-5-mini) assesses a batch of 6 uniformly sampled frames (excluding first/last) on a 1-3 scale, classifying results from severe failure to professional-standard clarity.

**Overall Scores:** The benchmark aggregates results into two overall scores:
- **Weighted Score (W.Score):** Assigns weights of 0.4 (RA), 0.25 (TC), 0.25 (PR), and 0.1 (VQ), reflecting the emphasis on reasoning.
- **Accuracy:** A strict measure where a case is counted as correct only if all four dimensions achieve full scores, normalized to a 100-point scale.

#### 3.3. Automated LMM-based Judging Pipeline

The research leverages Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) for scalable evaluation. Specifically, GPT-5 is used as the primary judge for Reasoning Alignment, Temporal Consistency, and Physical Rationality, while a more cost-effective GPT-5-mini handles Visual Quality assessment. This pipeline is guided by carefully designed, reasoning-aware questions and prompts to ensure fair and objective evaluation. An ablation study is conducted to validate the LMMs' alignment with human judgments, confirming GPT-5's robust performance compared to other LMMs like Qwen3-VL-235B and Gemini-3-Flash.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

The methodology concludes with an extensive evaluation of 11 representative TI2V models, including 6 closed-source systems (Hailuo 2.3, Wan 2.6, Sora 2, Veo 3.1, Kling 2.6, Seedance 1.5-pro) and 5 open-source models (Wan2.2-I2V, HunyuanVideo-1.5 and its distilled variant, Wan2.2-TI2V-5B, CogVideoX1.5-5B). This broad selection provides a comprehensive snapshot of current TI2V model capabilities.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive evaluation conducted using RISE-Video reveals pervasive and systematic deficiencies in the reasoning capabilities of current state-of-the-art Text-Image-to-Video (TI2V) models, despite their remarkable advancements in visual fidelity.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance and Reasoning Limitations

- **Pervasive Deficiencies:** All evaluated models, including the best-performing ones, achieved relatively low accuracy scores. Hailuo 2.3, the top model, attained an accuracy of only 22.5%, followed closely by Veo 3.1 (22.3%) and Sora 2 (21.3%). This starkly highlights that satisfying reasoning-oriented requirements remains a significant challenge for contemporary TI2V systems.
- **Open vs. Closed-Source Gap:** Open-source models consistently underperformed closed-source models across both reasoning capability and visual quality. Open-source models often exhibited more visual artifacts such as blurring, ghosting effects, and degraded spatial sharpness, which not only reduced their Visual Quality scores but also hindered reliable reasoning assessment.

#### 4.2. Metric-Specific Insights

- **Reasoning Alignment (RA):** Hailuo 2.3 demonstrated a clear advantage in Reasoning Alignment, outperforming the second-ranked model, Wan 2.6, by 6.6%. This indicates its relatively stronger capacity for knowledge-based reasoning compared to its peers.
- **Temporal Consistency (TC):** Sora 2 exhibited a notable strength in Temporal Consistency, suggesting its effectiveness in preserving non-instructed elements and maintaining stable generation behavior throughout videos.
- **Physical Rationality (PR) and Visual Quality (VQ):** While models generally scored higher on VQ, suggesting decent aesthetic quality, PR scores were moderate, indicating that physically implausible scenarios are still common.

#### 4.3. Category-Specific Performance Breakdown

The performance varied significantly across the eight reasoning categories:

- **Perceptual Knowledge:** Models generally performed best on Perceptual Knowledge tasks (e.g., color, size, count). This suggests that current models are relatively strong at capturing and manipulating low-level visual attributes.
- **Logical Capability:** This category proved to be the major bottleneck, showing consistently low performance across all models. Logical Capability tasks, requiring the integration of perceptual evidence with abstract, rule-based reasoning (e.g., game actions like Gold Miner where models failed to capture objects along a trajectory), highlight a fundamental limitation.
- **Experiential Knowledge:** Hailuo 2.3 and Veo 3.1 showed advantages in this category. For instance, in the example of a person drinking from a bottle, only these two models inferred the necessary action of unscrewing the bottle cap, demonstrating a rudimentary understanding of procedural knowledge.
- **Dynamic Behavior and Responsiveness:**
 - **Kling 2.6** frequently produced videos with minimal motion or near-static content, often failing to perform required transformations (e.g., chameleon camouflage, capillary action in a rose).
 - **Veo 3.1 and Sora 2** showed relatively weaker responsiveness to dynamic instructions, sometimes only partially following the prompt or exhibiting temporal discontinuities (abrupt changes between frames). Veo 3.1, for example, failed to accurately model chameleon camouflage where the color did not sufficiently adapt.
 - **Wan 2.6 and Hailuo 2.3** generally demonstrated stronger instruction following and more dynamic generation behavior.

#### 4.4. LMM-as-Judge Validation

The ablation study confirmed the reliability of the LMM-based evaluation pipeline. GPT-5 demonstrated the most robust alignment with human preferences across most metrics (RA, TC, PR), with lower Mean Absolute Error (MAE) and Standard Deviation (STD). For Visual Quality, the cost-effective GPT-5-mini also showed tighter alignment with human ratings than other tested LMMs. This validation underscores the potential for scalable and accurate automated evaluation using LMMs.

In summary, the main findings consistently point to a significant gap between the visual realism that TI2V models can achieve and their ability to decode and apply implicit world rules. While perceptual tasks are handled reasonably well, higher-level reasoning, especially logical and complex experiential scenarios, remains a profound challenge for current generative video systems.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of RISE-Video holds significant implications for the future direction of research and development in generative video models. Its contributions extend beyond merely providing a new dataset; it fundamentally reshapes how the "intelligence" of these models is assessed and understood.

1. **Filling a Critical Evaluation Gap:** RISE-Video uniquely addresses the under-explored frontier of implicit reasoning in Text-Image-to-Video (TI2V) generation. By shifting the evaluation paradigm from surface-level aesthetics and explicit instruction following to deeper cognitive reasoning, it fills a crucial void in existing benchmarks. This re-orientation is vital for pushing models beyond mere visual synthesis towards genuine world simulation capabilities.

2. **Comprehensive Diagnostic Framework:** The benchmark's structured taxonomy of eight distinct reasoning categories (Experiential, Commonsense, Temporal, Societal, Perceptual, Spatial, Subject-Specific, and Logical) combined with four complementary evaluation metrics (Reasoning Alignment, Temporal Consistency, Physical Rationality, and Visual Quality) provides an unprecedentedly holistic and fine-grained diagnostic tool. Researchers can now pinpoint specific strengths and weaknesses of models across diverse intelligence dimensions, enabling more targeted and efficient model development.

3. **Enabling Scalable and Reliable Evaluation:** The proposed automated LMM-based judging pipeline is a significant step towards practical and sustainable evaluation. Its demonstrated high alignment with human judgments, coupled with its scalability, means that researchers can continuously and cost-effectively assess new model iterations without extensive manual effort. This accelerates the research cycle and fosters more rigorous comparative analysis within the community. The specialized handling for "Schematic Puzzles" further exemplifies the thoughtful design to ensure robust evaluation even for challenging, abstract reasoning tasks.

4. **Highlighting Pervasive Deficiencies and Guiding Future Research:** The extensive evaluation of 11 state-of-the-art TI2V models definitively reveals that current systems, despite their impressive visual fidelity, systematically struggle with higher-level and implicit reasoning. The low accuracy scores across reasoning tasks underscore that models are still far from truly understanding and applying world rules. This finding is critical; it provides concrete evidence of a fundamental limitation, thereby stimulating and guiding future research efforts. It will encourage the development of:
 - **Reasoning-aware Architectures:** New model designs that are explicitly engineered to internalize and leverage implicit knowledge.
 - **Improved Training Paradigms:** Innovative training strategies that go beyond surface statistics to imbue models with a deeper understanding of causality, physics, social norms, and logical inference.
 - **Advanced Data Curation:** Methodologies for creating datasets that better capture and convey implicit world rules to generative models.

5. **Fostering Development of "World Simulators":** Ultimately, RISE-Video serves as a foundational step towards building generative models that function as robust "world simulators." These models would not only render visually realistic content but also produce videos that are physically plausible, logically coherent, and socially appropriate, reflecting a more profound understanding of how the world operates. This vision has broad implications for applications ranging from realistic virtual environments and advanced content creation tools to intelligent agents capable of complex decision-making and interaction.

In conclusion, RISE-Video is a landmark contribution that refocuses the generative video community on the crucial aspect of implicit reasoning. By providing a comprehensive benchmark, a robust evaluation framework, and clear evidence of current model limitations, it is poised to inspire and accelerate the development of a new generation of more intelligent, reasoning-aware, and truly world-understanding generative AI systems.
