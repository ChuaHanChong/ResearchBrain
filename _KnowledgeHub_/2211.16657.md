# Task-Driven Hybrid Model Reduction for Dexterous Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Arizona State University and the University of Pennsylvania developed a task-driven hybrid model reduction method, which identifies a simplified explicit hybrid model (LCS) sufficient for achieving high-performance, real-time control of contact-rich manipulation tasks. This approach enables a three-fingered robotic hand to precisely manipulate an unknown object with minimal data and computational time.

## Problem

- Robotic tasks involving frequent contact, such as dexterous manipulation and locomotion, are multi-modular hybrid systems with a combinatorial explosion of contact choices, making them challenging to model and control.
- Traditional planning and control methods for these systems are often computationally intractable due to the vast number of potential hybrid modes, while existing reduced-order models are typically manually designed and may not capture all critical dynamics.
- Data-driven modeling approaches, especially model-free reinforcement learning, require millions or billions of environment samples, posing a significant bottleneck for real-world robotic applications.

## Method

- The proposed method identifies a ==task-relevant reduced-order Linear Complementarity System (LCS)== containing only a limited number of hybrid modes, which is sufficient for achieving high-performance control for specific tasks.
- An ==iterative learning algorithm== minimizes the ==task performance gap== between a **Model Predictive Control (MPC)** strategy utilizing the reduced-order model and one using the full dynamics, guided by theoretical insights on model prediction errors.
- The approach combines a ==violation-based loss== for learning LCS parameters, a ==trust-region LCS-MPC== for real-time control, and a ==rollout buffer== to collect task-relevant interaction data from the full-order system.

## Results

- For synthetic hybrid systems, the method achieved multiple orders of magnitude reduction in active hybrid modes (e.g., from thousands to **~7 active modes**) with **less than 5%** task performance loss compared to full-order MPC.
- On a three-fingered robotic hand manipulating an unknown cube, the system learned effective control policies for turning and moving tasks within approximately **4.1-4.6 minutes** of wall-clock time, using **fewer than 4,000 environment samples**.
- The learned `g-MPC` controller achieved high accuracy (e.g., **3.4%** relative reorientation error, **3.8%** relative position error) and operated in real-time (**>50 Hz** for turning, **>30 Hz** for moving), demonstrating robustness against external disturbances.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - While contact-rich tasks can involve thousands of potential hybrid modes, far fewer modes are often necessary to accomplish specific tasks, enabling significant model reduction without substantial performance loss.
> - Minimizing the task performance gap can be achieved by accurately fitting the reduced-order model to the full dynamics based on data generated by the reduced-order MPC policy itself, thereby making the learning task-driven.
> - Explicitly learning a reduced-order hybrid model allows for sample-efficient and real-time capable control of complex, contact-rich manipulation tasks, even without prior knowledge of the system's geometry or physical properties.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic tasks such as legged locomotion and dexterous manipulation inherently involve frequent making and breaking of contact, rendering them multi-modular and hybrid systems. These systems are characterized by discrete contact modes and continuous physical dynamics within each mode. The hybrid nature presents challenges for both model representation and control.

In the realm of data-driven modeling, deep learning networks have demonstrated limitations in representing the stiffness and multi-modality of physical systems, leading to the development of frameworks specifically designed for hybrid dynamics. These include methods that learn smoothing approximations by relaxing hybrid mode boundaries, incurring some approximation error, and approaches that focus on learning explicit hybrid structures such as piecewise-affine (PWA) models, often compactly represented as Linear Complementarity Systems (LCS). While PWA models can describe multi-modality and are locally linear, their general identification is NP-hard.

For planning and control, the combinatorial explosion of discrete contact choices and continuous actuation inputs makes algorithms intractable as the number of potential hybrid modes and planning depth increase. Traditional approaches often rely on predefined mode sequences. More recent advancements include LCS linearization of nonlinear multi-contact robot dynamics and methods that decouple combinatorial complexity from planning depth. Reinforcement learning (RL) has achieved results in contact-rich manipulation but model-free RL typically demands millions or billions of environment samples. Model-based RL, while more sample-efficient, still requires substantial data for unstructured deep neural networks, and controlling with such models can be challenging.

The concept of reduced-order models has been applied to hybrid robotic tasks, particularly in locomotion, to simplify planning. However, these models are often manually designed and may not capture all critical dynamics. Recent work has explored optimizing for reduced-order models that retain full-order capabilities, primarily focusing on reducing state dimensions. This paper positions itself within this landscape by addressing the comparatively less explored problem of *hybrid mode reduction* for contact-rich tasks. It aims to develop a data-efficient and real-time capable control framework for complex contact-rich manipulation, leveraging learned explicit hybrid models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this research stems from the observation that while contact-rich tasks like dexterous manipulation can involve thousands of potential hybrid modes, far fewer modes are often necessary to accomplish specific tasks. This leads to the overarching hypothesis that identifying and utilizing a full hybrid dynamics model for control may be unnecessarily complex and computationally intractable for real-time applications.

The primary objective of this work is to develop a method for "task-driven hybrid model reduction." This involves finding a simplified hybrid model, specifically a reduced-order Linear Complementarity System (LCS), that contains only a limited number of "task-relevant" hybrid modes. The goal is for this reduced model to be sufficient for achieving high-performance, real-time control of contact-rich manipulation tasks.

More precisely, the paper aims to:

1. **Formulate Task-Driven Hybrid Model Reduction:** Define the problem as minimizing the "task performance gap" between a Model Predictive Control (MPC) strategy utilizing the reduced-order hybrid model and an MPC strategy utilizing the full, complex hybrid dynamics. This minimization is framed as both MPC controllers operating on the original, full-order robot system.
2. **Develop a Provably Bounded Learning Approach:** Establish theoretical insights indicating that minimizing the task performance gap can be achieved by fitting the reduced-order model to the full dynamics well, specifically on data generated by the reduced-order MPC policy. This theoretical grounding aims to justify an iterative learning method.
3. **Integrate Existing Technologies:** Leverage prior work on learning LCS representations for systems with many modes and recent advancements in real-time LCS-based control to construct a practical and efficient learning algorithm.
4. **Demonstrate Effectiveness on Complex Systems:** Apply the proposed method to both synthetic hybrid control systems and a high-dimensional, three-fingered robotic hand manipulating an unknown object. The objective is to demonstrate significant mode reduction while maintaining high task performance and enabling real-time closed-loop control with minimal prior knowledge and sample efficiency.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology centers on an iterative learning algorithm designed to identify a reduced-order hybrid model (represented as an LCS) that is task-relevant.

**A. Problem Formulation**

The full-order hybrid dynamics `f` are abstractly represented as a general complementarity system, encompassing all potential contact modes. A reduced-order LCS `g` is chosen as the proxy, sharing the same state and input dimensions as `f` but with a significantly smaller dimension for its complementarity variable (dim λ < dim Λ), thereby restricting the number of hybrid modes it can represent (2^dim λ).

The core problem is to find `g` such that when a reduced-order MPC (g-MPC) based on `g` controls the true full-order system `f`, its task performance is as close as possible to that of a full-order MPC (f-MPC) based on `f` controlling `f`. This is formalized by minimizing a task performance gap `L(g)`, which measures the difference in task cost `J_β` between the two control strategies, averaged over a distribution of tasks `p(β)` and initial states `p_β(x_0)`.

**B. Theoretical Analysis**

The paper provides two theoretical lemmas to guide the practical algorithm:
- **Lemma 1:** Under the assumption of Lipschitz continuity for the task cost function, it proves that the task performance gap `L(g)` is upper-bounded by a term proportional to the sum of two model prediction errors: the error between `g` and `f` when `g-MPC` control inputs are applied to `f`, and the error between `g` and `f` when `f-MPC` control inputs are applied to `f`. This suggests that if `g` accurately predicts `f`'s behavior on task-relevant trajectories, the performance gap will be small.
- **Lemma 2:** Assuming Lipschitz continuity of task cost gradients and bounded gradients for `G(u, x_0)`, this lemma demonstrates that if `g` closely approximates `f` in both zeroth and first-order predictions on the `g-MPC` generated data, then `g-MPC` solutions are approximately stationary solutions for `f-MPC`. This indicates that `g-MPC` data can serve as a proxy for `f-MPC` data for model fitting.

These lemmas collectively imply that by minimizing the zeroth-order prediction error of `g` relative to `f` on data generated by the evolving `g-MPC` policy, the algorithm can implicitly minimize the task performance gap.

**C. Practical Algorithm (Algorithm 1)**

The methodology is implemented as an iterative process with three main components:

1. **Learning Reduced-Order LCS (Section V-A):**
 - The parameters `θ` of the LCS `g` are learned by minimizing a "violation-based loss" (`L_vio`). This loss measures the discrepancy between the observed environment transitions (from the Rollout Buffer) and the predictions of the LCS model, encompassing both affine dynamics and complementarity equations.
 - The loss formulation involves an inner convex quadratic program to solve for complementarity variables `λ` and slack variables `φ`, which can be efficiently optimized. The gradient of `L_vio` with respect to `θ` is analytically derivable using the Envelope Theorem, avoiding differentiation through the inner optimization. This approach is designed to handle stiff and multi-modal dynamics.

2. **Trust-Region LCS Model Predictive Controller (Section V-B):**
 - At each iteration, the currently learned LCS `g` is used within an MPC framework. To manage model inaccuracies and restrict exploration to regions where `g` is likely valid, a trust region constraint `u_t ∈ [¯u − ∆, ¯u + ∆]` is imposed on the control inputs.
 - The center `¯u` and size `∆` of this trust region are dynamically updated. They are calculated as the mean and `η` times the standard deviation, respectively, of all control inputs present in the `Rollout Buffer` from previous iterations.
 - The LCS-MPC problem is solved using a direct trajectory optimization method, treating the LCS equations and trust region bounds as constraints. CasADi with IPOPT is utilized for optimization, enabling real-time performance due to the reduced complexity of `g`.

3. **Rollout Buffer:**
 - This buffer stores environment interaction data in the form of `(x_f,k, u_g-MPC,k, x_f,k+1)` tuples. These samples are collected by executing the trust-region `g-MPC` controller on the actual robot system (the full-order dynamics `f`). The buffer is periodically updated with new rollouts and has a maximum capacity.

The algorithm begins with an initial guess for `g` parameters and an initial buffer filled by random policies. It then iteratively updates `g`, adjusts the trust region, and collects new data, continually refining the reduced-order model and its associated controller.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents findings from two primary application domains: synthetic hybrid control systems and a three-fingered robotic hand manipulating unknown objects.

**A. Model Reduction for Synthetic Hybrid Control Systems (Section VI)**

- **Significant Mode Reduction:** The method successfully reduced the hybrid mode count by multiple orders of magnitude. For instance, in a 20-dimensional state system with 15 complementarity variables (potentially thousands of modes), the method learned a reduced-order LCS with only 3 complementarity variables (~7 active modes), while the full system exhibited ~2.8k modes under random policies.
- **Minimal Task Performance Loss:** Despite substantial mode reduction, the `g-MPC` controller achieved a task performance loss of less than 5% (often around 1-2%) relative to the full-order `f-MPC` controller.
- **Task-Driven Model Accuracy:** The learned reduced-order LCS `g` demonstrated high prediction accuracy (relative prediction error < 2%) on data generated by its *own* `g-MPC` policy (task-relevant data). In contrast, its accuracy was significantly lower (15-30%) on randomly sampled data, confirming the task-driven nature of the learning.
- **Qualitative Performance:** Phase portraits illustrated that the trajectories generated by the `g-MPC` closely resembled those of the `f-MPC`, indicating effective control.
- **Hyperparameter Robustness:** An ablation study indicated that the algorithm's performance was largely robust to variations in MPC horizon, number of new rollouts, buffer size, and trust-region parameters.

**B. Three-Finger Dexterous Manipulation (Section VII)**

The method was applied to a simulated three-fingered robotic hand manipulating a cube, without prior knowledge of the system's geometry or physical properties. The state space included cube position/orientation and fingertip positions (9 DoF), with fingertip incremental positions as inputs (6 DoF). A reduced-order LCS with 5 complementarity variables (max 32 modes) was used.

- **Cube Turning Task:**
 - **Data Efficiency and Learning Speed:** The system learned an effective control policy within approximately 4.1 minutes of wall-clock time, collecting only about 4,000 environment samples (200 rollouts * 20 steps/rollout).
 - **High Accuracy:** The `g-MPC` achieved a cube terminal orientation error of 0.06 ± 0.02 radians, with a relative error of 3.4% ± 2.3% for reorienting the cube to various targets.
 - **Real-time Control:** The learned `g-MPC` operated at a frequency exceeding 50 Hz, enabling real-time closed-loop control.
 - **Task-Relevant Modes:** The learned LCS actively utilized approximately 14 hybrid modes, which empirically correlated with diverse physical contact interactions, including fingertip separation, sticking, slipping, and various rotational slips between the cube and the table. More than 70% of rollouts exhibited sequences of stick-slip-separate modes.
 - **Robustness:** The `g-MPC` controller demonstrated high robustness, capable of stabilizing the cube against external disturbance torques equivalent to an angular acceleration of 5000 rad/s² before exceeding a 0.3 rad orientation error.

- **Cube Moving Task:**
 - **Data Efficiency and Learning Speed:** Similar to the turning task, the method achieved successful control within approximately 4.6 minutes of wall-clock time, using less than 4,000 environment samples.
 - **High Accuracy:** The `g-MPC` achieved a terminal position error of 0.0076 ± 0.0010 meters (relative error 3.8% ± 1.3%) and a terminal orientation error of 0.065 ± 0.021 radians for moving the cube to random target poses.
 - **Real-time Control:** The `g-MPC` maintained a running frequency exceeding 30 Hz.
 - **Complex Strategies and Modes:** The learned model, utilizing around 15 active modes, enabled the generation of diverse manipulation strategies for different target poses, involving choices of contact faces (right, left, front, back) and various contact interactions (separate, stick, slip) between fingertips and the cube, as well as complex translational and rotational slips between the cube and table. It also captured fingertip-fingertip contact.
 - **Robustness:** The `g-MPC` was robust against external disturbance wrenches, handling forces equivalent to 0.9 m/s² acceleration before significant position error occurred.
 - **Observed Limitations:** Occasional reorientation failures were observed, potentially attributed to insufficient sampling in certain complex regions of the target space during training.

**C. Discussion (Section VII-E)**

- **Mode Budget:** Analysis showed that while a minimum `dim λ` is required for successful manipulation (e.g., `dim λ >= 4` for Cube Turning), increasing `dim λ` beyond a certain point yielded diminishing performance returns and increased computational cost for the `g-MPC`.
- **Cost Weights:** The algorithm's performance was found to be robust to a wide range of cost weight choices, suggesting that careful tuning is not overly critical, provided the weights ensure similar scaling for different cost terms.
- **Task Distribution:** The method performed effectively across different task distributions (fixed target, Gaussian target, uniform target), demonstrating its ability to minimize the task performance gap in expectation over the given distribution.
- **PWA Model Limitations:** The paper acknowledges that PWA models, being based on linearization, may not optimally capture large nonlinearities (e.g., full 360-degree rotations) unless more "pieces" are added, which diverges from the core intent of capturing hybrid structure.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work contributes to the field of robotics by addressing the fundamental challenge of modeling and controlling contact-rich hybrid systems, particularly in dexterous manipulation. Its significance and potential impact include:

- **Enabling Real-time Control:** By effectively reducing the complexity of hybrid models, the research makes real-time, closed-loop Model Predictive Control (MPC) tractable for systems with a combinatorial number of contact modes. This is a critical step towards deploying advanced manipulation capabilities on physical robots.
- **High Data Efficiency:** The proposed method achieves high performance with significantly fewer environment samples (on the order of thousands) and training time (minutes) compared to typical model-free reinforcement learning approaches that often require millions or billions of samples and days of training. This addresses a major bottleneck in robot learning.
- **Reduced-Order Hybrid Modeling:** It offers a principled, data-driven approach to identify task-relevant hybrid modes, avoiding the need for manual design of reduced models which can miss crucial dynamics. This could generalize to other complex hybrid robotic systems, such as legged locomotion, where only a subset of modes might be critical for specific behaviors.
- **Unknown Object Manipulation:** The demonstration on a three-fingered hand manipulating an *unknown* object without prior knowledge of its geometry or physics highlights the method's potential for learning versatile manipulation skills in unstructured environments.
- **Robustness in Contact:** The inherent closed-loop nature of MPC, combined with the learned contact dynamics, provides a degree of robustness against external disturbances, an essential feature for real-world robotic operations.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The work establishes a robust framework for hybrid model learning and control, paving the way for extensions to nonlinear complementarity models and further integration with real-world robotic hardware.

The paper suggests a viable pathway to overcoming the long-standing intractability of planning and control in contact-rich robotic tasks, moving towards more autonomous and adaptable robotic systems.
