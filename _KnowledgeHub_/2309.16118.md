# D$^3$Fields: Dynamic 3D Descriptor Fields for Zero-Shot Generalizable Rearrangement

> [!summary] Summary
> A new scene representation, D3 Fields, provides 3D, dynamic, and semantic understanding of environments for robots by constructing implicit fields from multi-view RGBD observations in 0.166 seconds. This enables zero-shot rearrangement tasks using diverse 2D goal images, including stylized and AI-generated ones, without prior knowledge of objects or scenes.

## Problem

- Robotic manipulation systems often lack scene representations that are simultaneously 3D, dynamic, and semantically rich for complex real-world interaction.
- Generalizing robotic rearrangement tasks to novel objects, scenes, and diverse task specifications (e.g., varied 2D goal images) typically requires extensive retraining or object-specific models.
- Traditional methods for specifying robotic tasks are often rigid, relying on explicit 3D models or complex programming, which limits intuitive human-robot interaction.

## Method

- **D3 Fields** is introduced as a novel ==implicit 3D representation== that outputs ==signed distance==, ==dense semantic features==, and instance probability distributions for any 3D point.
- The representation is constructed in a zero-shot, training-free manner directly from sparse multi-view RGBD observations by fusing features from pre-trained ==2D visual foundation models==.
- D3 Fields integrate with ==3D keypoint tracking== and a learned ==Graph Neural Network (GNN) dynamics model== to enable ==Model-Predictive Control (MPPI)== for generalizable object rearrangement based on 2D goal images.

## Results

- D3 Fields builds implicit 3D representations significantly faster (**0.166 seconds** average per scene) than existing methods like F3RM (**88.379 seconds**), while generating more accurate 3D meshes and dense semantic correspondences.
- The framework demonstrates robust zero-shot generalization, successfully performing object rearrangement using diverse 2D goal images, including AI-generated and stylized inputs, across novel object instances and granular materials.
- Analysis shows D3 Fields produce consistent 3D instance segmentations and maintain category-level semantic consistency, with features for similar object parts clustering regardless of instance or pose.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Off-the-shelf 2D visual foundation models can be effectively grounded and fused into a coherent 3D dynamic representation without scene-specific training.
> - The combination of 3D geometry with dense, category-level semantic features enables robust zero-shot correspondence across diverse object instances and visual styles in goal images.
> - A differentiable dynamic implicit representation facilitates gradient-based 3D keypoint tracking, which is then used to learn dynamics models for real-time manipulation planning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation is continuously striving for more intelligent, adaptive, and autonomous systems. A core challenge lies in how robots perceive and represent their surrounding environment. Historically, scene representations have varied, often prioritizing certain properties over others. Early methods might focus on precise 3D geometry (e.g., point clouds, meshes) but lack semantic understanding or the ability to model dynamic changes. Conversely, 2D vision systems could process semantic information or track dynamics but failed to provide the necessary 3D context for physical interaction.

This paper addresses a critical gap in existing scene representations: the simultaneous achievement of **3D, dynamic, and semantic properties**.
- **3D Representation:** Many works leverage 3D data (e.g.,), but often without rich semantic understanding.
- **Dynamic Modeling:** Some approaches focus on dynamic changes (e.g.,) but often operate in 2D or lack a comprehensive 3D semantic grounding.
- **Semantic Information:** Others prioritize object instance and category recognition (e.g.,), frequently limited to static scenes or lacking robust 3D geometry.

The emergence of **visual foundation models** (VFMs) like DINOv2, Segment Anything Model (SAM), and Grounding-DINO has revolutionized 2D computer vision, offering unprecedented zero-shot generalization capabilities for tasks like object detection and segmentation. A significant trend in robotics research is to leverage these powerful 2D models by "grounding" them in 3D environments for robotic tasks. However, effectively transferring their 2D generalization power to 3D, especially for dynamic scenes and without extensive retraining, remains a challenge. Existing works in this vein (e.g.,) have made progress but often don't emphasize dynamics learning or fully achieve zero-shot generalization across novel objects and styles.

Furthermore, **neural implicit representations** (or neural fields), such as Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs) and their derivatives, have gained traction for their ability to represent continuous 3D scenes with high fidelity. While many neural field approaches for manipulation (e.g.,) exist, they often suffer from significant drawbacks: requiring dense camera views, needing scene-specific retraining, or struggling to generalize to novel instances and scenes (e.g., F3RM, FeatureNeRF). These limitations make them impractical for real-time, zero-shot robotic tasks in unstructured environments.

D3 Fields aims to synthesize these advancements by:
1. **Integrating 2D Visual Foundation Models into 3D:** It directly leverages the zero-shot semantic capabilities of state-of-the-art VFMs (DINOv2, SAM, Grounding-DINO) and an instance tracking model (XMem) to imbue 3D points with rich semantic and instance-level information.
2. **Developing a Dynamic Implicit 3D Representation:** Unlike static NeRFs or other neural fields that require retraining, D3 Fields are constructed on-the-fly from sparse multi-view RGBD observations, making them dynamic and adaptable to changing scenes. This avoids expensive retraining.
3. **Enabling Zero-Shot Generalization:** By creating dense 3D semantic correspondences and combining them with learned dynamics, the system can perform rearrangement tasks specified by arbitrary 2D goal images (even AI-generated or stylistically varied) without prior knowledge of the specific objects or scenes.

This work distinguishes itself by offering a representation that is not only 3D and semantic but also dynamic and, critically, constructible in a zero-shot manner without scene-specific training. This addresses key bottlenecks in deploying general-purpose manipulation robots, positioning D3 Fields as a significant step forward in making robots more versatile and easier to instruct in complex, dynamic environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce **D3 Fields (Dynamic 3D Descriptor Fields)**, a novel scene representation that simultaneously possesses three crucial properties for robotic manipulation: being **3D, dynamic, and semantic**.

The motivations underpinning this objective are rooted in the limitations of existing robotic manipulation systems and the desire to create more capable, flexible, and user-friendly robots:

- **Bridging the Gap in Scene Representation:** Previous approaches to scene representation in robotics often compromise on one or more of the ideal properties (3D, dynamic, semantic). For instance, many 3D representations lack semantic understanding, dynamic models are often limited to 2D, and semantic models might not capture full 3D geometry or dynamics. D3 Fields aims to be a unified solution that incorporates all three, providing a richer, more comprehensive understanding of the robot's workspace.
- **Enabling Zero-Shot Generalizable Rearrangement:** A major bottleneck in current robotic manipulation is the difficulty of generalizing to novel objects, scenes, and task specifications without extensive retraining or manual programming. The authors specifically aim to enable "zero-shot generalizable rearrangement tasks," where the robot can understand and execute tasks even when the goal is presented via diverse 2D images (e.g., internet photos, smartphone pictures, or AI-generated images) that differ significantly in style, context, and specific instances from the robot's current environment. This eliminates the need for object-specific models or pre-labeled datasets for each new task.
- **Simplifying Human-Robot Interaction:** The reliance on flexible 2D goal images, rather than explicit 3D models or complex programming, offers a significantly more intuitive and convenient interface for humans to specify tasks. This approach reduces the burden on human operators and makes robots more accessible to a wider range of users.
- **Efficiency and Adaptability:** Traditional 3D representations or neural fields often require time-consuming training or re-training for new scenes. D3 Fields are motivated by the need for an **efficient** representation that can be constructed in a zero-shot manner, leveraging pre-trained visual foundation models. This allows for rapid adaptation to novel environments without significant computational overhead, a critical factor for real-world deployment.
- **Robustness to Diverse Conditions:** The ability to handle varied styles (e.g., sketch drawings, AI-generated art) and contexts (e.g., different lighting, backgrounds) in goal images, along with diverse object instances and materials (e.g., granular debris), underscores the motivation to create a representation robust to the inherent variability of real-world scenarios.

In essence, the paper's core objective is to move beyond specialized robotic systems toward truly general-purpose manipulators that can understand and act upon high-level, human-friendly instructions in dynamic and unstructured environments, enabled by a powerful and flexible scene representation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The D3 Fields framework tackles the problem of zero-shot generalizable robotic rearrangement by introducing a novel implicit 3D representation that leverages visual foundation models for semantics and integrates dynamics learning. The overall approach can be broken down into three main components: D3 Fields Representation, Keypoints Tracking and Dynamics Learning, and Zero-Shot Generalizable Robotic Rearrangement Planning.

#### 3.1. D3 Fields Representation

D3 Fields are defined as implicit functions, $F(\cdot|W)$, which take an arbitrary 3D coordinate $x$ in the world frame as input and output a tuple $(d, f, p)$. Here, $d \in \mathbb{R}$ is the signed distance to the object surface (for geometric information), $f \in \mathbb{R}^N$ is a dense semantic descriptor feature, and $p \in \mathbb{R}^M$ is an instance probability distribution across $M$ potential objects (for instance-level segmentation). Crucially, the construction of these fields requires no training and is performed in a zero-shot manner by utilizing off-the-shelf, powerful 2D visual foundation models.

The process to construct D3 Fields from multi-view RGBD observations is as follows:
1. **2D Feature Extraction from Multi-view Observations:** Multi-view RGBD images are fed into various pre-trained 2D visual foundation models:
 - **DINOv2** extracts dense semantic feature volumes ($W_f$) from RGB images, providing rich, generalizable visual descriptors.
 - **Grounded-SAM** (combining Grounding-DINO for open-set object detection and SAM for segmentation) extracts instance mask volumes ($W_p$), allowing for robust object instance segmentation in 2D.
 - **XMem** is used for long-term video object segmentation, likely contributing to dynamic instance tracking coherence.
2. **3D Point Projection and Depth-based Weighting:** For any given arbitrary 3D point $x$, it is projected into the image space of each of the $K$ cameras. For each viewpoint $i$, the projected pixel $u_i$ and the distance $r_i$ from $x$ to camera $i$ are computed. The corresponding depth reading $r'_i$ is interpolated from the camera's depth image $R_i$.
 - A **truncated depth difference** $d_i = r_i - r'_i$ is calculated. This value, truncated by a threshold $\mu$ (Equation 3), indicates how close $x$ is to the observed surface from that view and whether it's in front or behind it.
 - **Visibility and Confidence Weights:** Two types of weights are derived from $d_i$:
 - $v_i$: A binary visibility indicator ($1$ if $x$ is within $\mu$ of the surface and visible, $0$ otherwise).
 - $w_i$: A decaying confidence weight (exponentially decreasing as $|d_i|$ increases, and zero if far away) (Equation 4). This prioritizes views where $x$ is close to a perceived surface.
3. **Feature Interpolation and Fusion:** For each viewpoint $i$, the 2D semantic feature $f_i$ and instance mask $p_i$ are interpolated from the pre-extracted 2D feature volumes ($W_f$ and $W_p$) at the projected pixel $u_i$ (Equation 5). These instance masks are ensured to have consistent indexing across views. Finally, the signed distance $d$, semantic feature $f$, and instance probability $p$ for the 3D point $x$ are fused from all $K$ viewpoints using a weighted sum based on $v_i$ and $w_i$ (Equation 6). This process is differentiable, allowing for gradient-based optimization when required.

#### 3.2. Keypoints Tracking and Dynamics Learning

To enable dynamic manipulation, D3 Fields are used for object tracking and dynamics model learning:
1. **Keypoint Tracking:** For a target object instance, initial 3D keypoints $s_0$ are sampled close to its surface. These keypoints are then tracked over time ($s_t \to s_{t+1}$) by minimizing the feature distance between the current keypoints' D3 Field features $F_f(s_{t+1}|W)$ and the initial keypoints' features $F_f(s_0|W)$ (Equation 7). The differentiability of D3 Fields allows for gradient-based optimization. For rigid objects, additional rigid body constraints and distance regularization can be applied for enhanced stability.
2. **Dynamics Model Learning:** The tracked 3D keypoints provide state-action trajectories, which are then used to train a dynamics model $f(\cdot, \cdot)$. The authors instantiate this model using **Graph Neural Networks (GNNs)**, following prior work, to predict future object states $s_{t+1}$ given current state $s_t$ and action $a_t$. This learned dynamics model is crucial for model-predictive control.

#### 3.3. Zero-Shot Generalizable Robotic Rearrangement

The final stage integrates the D3 Fields representation and learned dynamics into a planning framework for zero-shot rearrangement tasks:
1. **Goal Specification and Correspondence:** The task goal is specified by a 2D image $I_{goal}$. To bridge the 3D workspace and the 2D goal, dense correspondences are established. Initial 3D keypoints $s_0$ from the workspace (with associated DINOv2 features $f_0$) are mapped to corresponding 2D points $s_{goal}$ in the goal image. This is achieved by calculating feature distances between goal image pixels (using DINOv2 features $W_f^{goal}$) and $f_0$, then normalizing these distances using softmax to obtain weights ($\beta_{ij}$), and finally computing $s_{goal}$ as a weighted sum of image pixels (Equation 8).
2. **Cost Function Definition:** A virtual reference camera is used to project the current 3D keypoints $s_t$ into a 2D plane, yielding $s_t^{2D}$. The planning cost function $c(s_t, s_{goal})$ is then defined as the L2 distance between $s_t^{2D}$ and $s_{goal}$ (Equation 9), directly measuring how well the current object configuration matches the 2D goal image.
3. **Model-Predictive Control (MPC):** With the defined cost function and the learned GNN dynamics model, an MPC framework, specifically **Model-Predictive Path Integral (MPPI)**, is used. At each time step, MPPI samples multiple action sequences, predicts their outcomes using the dynamics model, evaluates their costs, and then updates the action sequence to minimize the cost. This process is repeated iteratively until the task is completed.

This comprehensive methodology allows D3 Fields to effectively perceive, understand, model, and manipulate objects in 3D dynamic environments based on flexible 2D goal specifications, all in a zero-shot, generalizable manner.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted demonstrate the effectiveness and efficiency of D3 Fields across various dimensions, including comparison with state-of-the-art implicit representations, zero-shot generalization capabilities, and internal analysis of the representation itself.

#### 4.1. D3 Fields Efficiency and Effectiveness

- **Correspondence Quality (Qualitative Comparison - Figure 5):** D3 Fields significantly outperform baseline implicit 3D representations like F3RM and FeatureNeRF.
 - D3 Fields consistently reconstruct clear 3D meshes and establish accurate, semantically consistent correspondences between 2D source images and 3D reconstructed meshes. It highlights semantically similar areas even across different instances and contexts (e.g., matching parts of shoes).
 - F3RM can reconstruct a reasonable mesh in some scenarios but often fails to establish accurate correspondences and struggles in diverse scenes.
 - FeatureNeRF, being pre-trained on a smaller dataset, shows poor generalization to novel scenes, producing inaccurate meshes and correspondences.
- **Computational Efficiency:** D3 Fields demonstrate superior efficiency in constructing the implicit representation. For four RGBD views across four scenes, D3 Fields take an average of **0.166 ± 0.002 seconds**, which is orders of magnitude faster than F3RM's **88.379 ± 5.306 seconds**. This efficiency is crucial for real-time robotic applications.
- **Quantitative Correspondence (Supplementary):** While not detailed in the main text, the paper states that quantitative comparisons in the supplementary material further confirm the effectiveness.

#### 4.2. Zero-Shot Generalizable Rearrangement

The qualitative evaluations (Figures 1 and 6) highlight the framework's ability to handle diverse rearrangement tasks in a zero-shot manner:

- **Generalization to AI-Generated Goal Images:** D3 Fields successfully guide manipulation tasks even when the goal image has a highly stylized appearance (e.g., Van Gogh style for shoe organization in Figure 1) that is distinct from the real-world workspace. This confirms the representation's ability to capture semantic information independent of superficial visual style.
- **Compositional Goal Images:** The system can handle multi-stage or complex tasks by combining multiple goal image specifications. For example, in the office desk organization task, the robot sequentially arranges objects based on different goal images.
- **Generalization Across Instances and Materials:** The framework demonstrates robust instance-level generalization, successfully manipulating novel instances of objects (e.g., different types of shoes, utensils, fruits) and even granular materials like debris (Figure 1), which typically pose challenges for robotic systems. Figure 4 shows the wide variety of objects used, reinforcing this claim.
- **Generalization Across Simulation and Real World:** D3 Fields enable successful task execution in both real-world setups (using a Kinova Gen3 arm) and simulation (OmniGibson with a Fetch robot). The ability to use real-world goal images to manipulate objects in simulation (and vice versa, as implied) highlights strong generalization capabilities across domains.

#### 4.3. D3 Fields Analysis

Internal analysis of the D3 Fields representation itself provides insight into its generalization capabilities (Figure 7):

- **Instance Mask Fields:** The representation effectively generates distinct **3D instance segmentations** for objects in the scene, even for clustered items (e.g., cans, toothpaste). This ability to differentiate individual objects in 3D is fundamental for multi-object manipulation.
- **Semantic Descriptor Fields:** Visualizing the semantic features using Principal Component Analysis (PCA) reveals **consistent color patterns** for semantically similar parts across different instances of the same object category. For example, shoe heels consistently appear green and shoe toes red, regardless of the shoe's overall appearance or pose. This category-level semantic consistency is crucial for establishing accurate correspondences with novel instances in goal images, explaining the zero-shot generalization capability.
- **3D Consistency (Supplementary):** The paper mentions that D3 Fields achieve better 3D consistency compared to simpler methods like point cloud stitching, further validating the robustness of the implicit representation.

In summary, D3 Fields represent a significant leap in efficiently creating 3D, dynamic, and semantic scene representations, enabling a wide array of zero-shot rearrangement tasks that were previously challenging due to limitations in generalization and goal specification flexibility.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of D3 Fields represents a substantial contribution to the field of robotic manipulation, with significant implications for the development of more autonomous, adaptable, and user-friendly robots.

- **Unified and Comprehensive Scene Representation:** D3 Fields addresses a long-standing challenge in robotics by providing a scene representation that is simultaneously **3D, dynamic, and semantic**. This trifecta is essential for robots to truly understand and interact intelligently with complex, changing real-world environments. By implicitly encoding geometry, dense semantic features, and instance masks, D3 Fields offers a richer and more actionable understanding of the workspace than previous methods.
- **Breakthrough in Zero-Shot Generalization for Manipulation:** One of the most impactful aspects of this work is its demonstration of **zero-shot generalizable rearrangement**. Robots equipped with D3 Fields can perform novel manipulation tasks without needing prior training on specific objects or scenes. This dramatically reduces the need for extensive data collection and domain-specific engineering, a major bottleneck in deploying robots in diverse settings. The ability to interpret goal images of varying styles, contexts, and instances (including AI-generated images) is a significant step towards truly flexible task specification.
- **Enhanced Human-Robot Interaction:** By accepting diverse 2D images as goal specifications, D3 Fields simplifies the human-robot interface. Humans can naturally instruct robots using readily available images from their daily lives or even conceptual images, making robots more accessible, intuitive, and convenient for a broader range of users. This moves away from rigid programming paradigms towards more natural communication.
- **Efficient and Real-time Operation:** The method's ability to construct the 3D representation in a zero-shot manner, leveraging pre-trained foundation models, without scene-specific training, and with remarkable speed (0.166 seconds), is critical. This efficiency paves the way for real-time adaptation and execution in dynamic environments, a requirement for practical robotic applications.
- **Effective Grounding of 2D Foundation Models in 3D Robotics:** D3 Fields provides a highly effective framework for extending the immense zero-shot generalization capabilities of powerful 2D visual foundation models (like DINOv2, SAM, Grounding-DINO) into the 3D realm for robotic control. This demonstrates a powerful paradigm for building on state-of-the-art vision models to enhance robot intelligence.
- **Foundation for Future Advanced Manipulation:** This work lays a strong foundation for more complex and sophisticated manipulation tasks. The robust 3D instance segmentation and category-level semantic consistency inherent in D3 Fields could be leveraged for tasks requiring fine-grained understanding of object parts, tool use, or even more abstract human instructions. It opens avenues for future research in areas like long-horizon planning, collaborative manipulation, and learning from demonstration in dynamic environments.

While the authors acknowledge limitations, such as the need for more fine-grained semantic features and improved task planning for collision avoidance in crowded scenes, these are natural next steps that build upon the strong foundation established by D3 Fields. The work significantly advances the state-of-the-art in robotic perception and control, bringing us closer to a future where robots can adapt intelligently and flexibly to the unstructured and dynamic demands of the real world.
