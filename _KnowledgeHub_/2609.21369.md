# ProTracer: Proprioception-Guided Failure Diagnosis in Robot Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> ProTracer is a training-free framework that adds proprioception to off-the-shelf vision-language models for post-hoc manipulation failure diagnosis, including a new task: pinpointing when a failure first begins. Gripper and end-effector change points pick keyframes, and rule-based narratives describe robot state in language. It localizes onset within 0.5 s on 71% of failed FailTime-Short episodes zero-shot, 77% with reflective memory.

## Problem

- Prior failure-diagnosis work asks only whether and what went wrong, detecting or categorizing failures, while ignoring when the failure first began inside a rollout.
- Online anomaly detectors see only past observations, so a first alarm is an unreliable onset estimate, and they need task-specific nominal models plus threshold calibration.
- Dense video prompting floods VLMs with redundant frames while sparse visual sampling misses brief contact-rich transitions, and no benchmark provided frame-level onset labels with synchronized proprioception.

## Method

- **Sign-CPD**: gripper-aperture velocity signs and motion indicators form a symbolic matrix scored by ==modal-Hamming cost==, with ==PELT change points== greedily pruned to a fixed budget of temporal phases.
- **Rule-based signal narratives** turn the 8D end-effector trajectory into hallucination-free, embodiment-agnostic text, feeding a two-round VLM pass: ==keyframe scene captioning==, then joint diagnosis over vision plus proprioception.
- Optional **reflective memory** converts calibration-set prediction errors into textual lessons, accepted only if re-inference fixes the episode, kept compact by ==ADD, MERGE, UPDATE and COMPRESS ops== without updating weights.

## Results

- On FailTime-Short ProTracer reaches **71.18%** acc@0.5s and **0.59 s** MAE zero-shot, **77.34%** with reflective experiences, versus **46.06%** for trained ViFailback-8B and **27.83%** for dense-prompted Gemini.
- On FailTime-Long, averaging **54 s** with new embodiments, ProTracer holds **40.35%** acc@0.5s while ViFailback-8B collapses to **5.26%**; on Short, label-free runtime detectors reach at most **1.72%**, supervised GRU **18.97%**.
- Ablations: removing proprioception drops acc@0.5s to **30.79%**, removing vision collapses failure-type accuracy to **15.02%**; Sign-CPD recalls **69.68%** of annotated action boundaries versus **40.80%** for optical flow.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Temporal precision and semantic content come from different modalities: proprioception decides when to look, vision-language reasoning decides what failed.
> - Discrete state switches, grasp open or close and moving or still, segment manipulation primitives better than motion amplitude, so discard magnitudes when detecting boundaries.
> - A runtime detector's first alarm is not an onset estimate; its error spread of 3-4 seconds cannot be removed by any median offset calibration.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Overview

Robot-failure diagnosis usually tells us whether a rollout failed and what went wrong. It does not tell us when the robot first stopped being on track. That moment matters for credit assignment, salvaging successful prefixes from failed demonstrations, and tracing causes. It can precede the visible consequence by seconds. In one illustrative episode, a gripper releases prematurely at 13.7 seconds while the scene still looks normal. The spill becomes visible at 16.4 seconds, and the robot continues.

The authors' approach reads the robot's own motion and gripper signals to find when to look, then uses vision to interpret what went wrong. They frame this as a new retrospective task plus a training-free method, ProTracer. Proprioception identifies informative action transitions, and a vision-language model (VLM) uses those cues alongside visual context to explain what happened.

In the illustrated rollout, premature release at 13.7 seconds precedes the spill becoming visible at 16.4 seconds; the robot continues afterward. The example shows why onset and visible consequence are different moments.

### 2. From robot signals to useful moments

Proprioception is robot-internal state information. Here it includes gripper aperture and end-effector pose and motion. ProTracer represents gripper opening or closing direction and whether the end effector is moving. Change-point detection groups stable patterns and identifies transitions, and the candidate boundaries are used to select representative video frames and intervals. Deterministic rules then turn the fuller robot-state signal into short physical descriptions, such as gripper opening, holding, displacement, direction, and wrist rotation.

The VLM works in two stages. It first describes the selected visual scenes. It then considers those scene descriptions, the robot-state narratives, the task instruction, and the failure taxonomy to produce a verdict, onset, type, explanation, and avoidance suggestion.

ProTracer uses proprioceptive dynamics both to select temporal evidence and to produce structured state narratives; the VLM combines these with visual scene descriptions and task context.

### 3. Measuring onset, and testing the mechanism

Onset is the earliest deviation in a rollout that ultimately fails after which execution does not return to a successful task-completion trajectory. A temporary error that is corrected, such as a failed grasp followed by a successful retry, is not an onset. The FailTime benchmark includes 1,539 FailTime-Short episodes built from ViFailback material and 100 newly collected FailTime-Long real-world episodes on ALOHA and SO-101.

Two metrics score onset estimates. [Acc@0.5s](mailto:Acc@0.5s) is the share of estimates with absolute error under 0.5 seconds. Mean absolute error (MAE) is in seconds and is computed only over correctly detected failure episodes.

On FailTime-Short, with Gemini-3.1-pro as the backbone, ProTracer reaches 71.18% [acc@0.5s](mailto:acc@0.5s) and 0.59 seconds MAE. The same dense-frame Gemini baseline reaches 48.03% and 0.90 seconds when it receives raw proprioceptive values. This supports the full structured pipeline over simply appending raw sensor values. By itself, it does not establish that either keyframe selection or narratives alone explain the gain. All methods' onset results degrade on the longer horizon, though ProTracer is best among listed methods there.

A controlled selector swap tests the temporal guidance directly. With the ProTracer pipeline held fixed, the Sign-CPD selector reaches 71.18% [acc@0.5s](mailto:acc@0.5s) and 0.59-second MAE. Optical flow reaches 66.50% and 0.73 seconds, and continuous CPD reaches 66.26% and 0.79 seconds. A separate 50-episode boundary-alignment test measures recall of manually annotated action boundaries within 0.5 seconds. Sign-CPD recalls 69.68% of them, versus 40.80% for optical flow and 55.28% for continuous CPD. This supports the authors' interpretation that discrete state switches better align with primitive boundaries, though other factors may also contribute to the full method's improvement.

### 4. When is the failure?

A visually normal scene can conceal a decisive robot-state transition. ProTracer's results support using proprioception to guide where the VLM looks, while vision supplies context for what that transition means.

The main boundary is that ProTracer is retrospective and uses the completed rollout, so it is not an online policy-stopping detector. "Training-free" means no additional model training for the zero-shot pipeline; it does not mean cost-free inference. Gemini-3.1-pro processing takes about 40–60 seconds and costs about $0.09 per episode, dominated by two VLM calls.
