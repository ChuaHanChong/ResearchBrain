# Overthink-Triggered Slowdown Attacks on LVLM-Based Robotic Systems

> [!summary] Summary
> Research identifies and validates "overthink-triggered slowdown attacks" on Large Vision-Language Model (LVLM)-based robotic systems, demonstrating that physically realizable scene text can induce excessive reasoning and significantly amplify inference latency. The study found that specifically crafted visual text prompts could increase decision-making delays by up to 6.96x on some models and are transferable across different LVLM architectures.

## Problem

- LVLMs are increasingly integrated into robotic systems, introducing new vulnerabilities due to their unique operational characteristics.
- Robotic operations often require real-time decision-making, and any delays in LVLM inference due to "overthinking" can compromise safety and operational correctness.
- Existing adversarial methods for LLMs (e.g., text prompt manipulation, pixel-level image perturbations) are not directly applicable or practical for black-box, physical-world LVLM scenarios in robotics.

## Method

- A ==three-stage framework== was developed for black-box discovery of **overthinking triggers**: ==data-driven lexical mining==, ==evolutionary text optimization== using a ==genetic algorithm==, and ==black-box transfer evaluation==.
- The framework efficiently searches for triggers by employing a ==prefix-based proxy fitness function== during optimization to estimate overthinking potential from early output tokens.
- Candidate triggers are rendered as physically realizable visual patches, embedded into road-scene images, and tested on multiple ==LVLM== architectures, including physical validation with printed text.

## Results

- The optimized scene-text triggers consistently induced latency amplification across **three** distinct LVLM architectures, with the strongest single case achieving a **6.96x** increase in inference latency.
- The attack was validated in a real-world physical setup using printed adversarial text and live camera input, demonstrating up to **4.74x** latency amplification on one model despite real-world sensing complexities.
- The proposed methodology significantly outperformed baseline methods (random text, naive Chain-of-Thought) and direct transfers of text-only overthinking prompts from prior research, highlighting the necessity of modality-specific trigger optimization for LVLMs.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Overthinking in LVLMs can be systematically triggered by specific, contextually relevant scene text that elevates perceived reasoning demand, rather than arbitrary text.
> - Effective overthinking triggers are compositional, often combining scenario descriptions, ambiguity, reasoning requirements, and constraints to induce extended processing.
> - Triggers optimized on one LVLM architecture successfully transfer to other, unseen architectures, indicating a general vulnerability in how LVLMs process complex visual text instructions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) are increasingly integrated into robotic systems to facilitate complex tasks such as perception, decision reasoning, and high-level planning. These models combine visual understanding with natural language processing capabilities, enabling robots to operate in dynamic and uncertain environments. However, the integration of LVLMs introduces new vulnerabilities, particularly those exploiting their unique operational characteristics.

One such vulnerability is the "overthinking" phenomenon, where language models generate excessively long reasoning traces or outputs, even for simple queries. Prior research (e.g.,) has demonstrated this behavior in reasoning-oriented Large Language Models (LLMs), showing that it can lead to increased response times and computational overhead. In the context of robotic systems, where operations are often time-constrained, such delays can compromise safety, stability, and operational correctness.

Existing attack vectors on LLMs primarily involve textual prompt manipulation, either by injecting decoy reasoning tasks or optimizing black-box prompts to induce extended generation. Some works also explore resource amplification through tool-calling chains or poisoning attacks to implant over-generation behaviors. Another line of research has explored adversarial images that can increase output length and latency in LVLMs by inducing hidden token tails or delaying end-of-sequence termination. However, these often rely on pixel-level perturbations or require white-box access, which may not be practical for robotic systems.

A distinct category of attacks, typographic attacks, targets LVLMs by embedding human-readable text into images. These attacks have shown that visually embedded text can override or redirect model reasoning, mislead LVLMs through self-generated typography, or influence downstream agent actions in robotic contexts. However, these typographic attacks primarily focus on degrading correctness or hijacking instructions, rather than systematically studying and inducing overthinking behaviors to amplify inference latency.

This work bridges a gap in the existing literature by focusing specifically on "overthinking-induced slowdown attacks" in LVLM-based robotic systems, utilizing physically realizable, human-readable scene text as the attack vector. It differentiates itself by:
1. **Targeting Latency**: Unlike previous typographic attacks focusing on decision content, this research targets system availability and real-time performance by inducing prolonged inference.
2. **Modality-Specific Triggers**: It recognizes that prompts effective in text-only LLMs may not transfer directly to image-based LVLM inputs due to the visual processing pipeline and scene plausibility constraints.
3. **Black-box and Physical Realizability**: It operates under a practical black-box threat model and validates attacks using physically printed scene text, addressing real-world deployment challenges.

By identifying and validating transferable scene-text triggers, this research contributes to understanding a novel availability risk for time-sensitive cyber-physical systems that rely on LVLMs, offering insights critical for their robust and safe deployment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to systematically identify and validate transferable scene-text triggers capable of inducing "overthinking-triggered slowdown attacks" in Large Vision-Language Model (LVLM)-based robotic systems. This involves developing a framework to discover human-readable text that, when embedded in a robot's visual scene, causes LVLMs to generate excessively long reasoning traces, thereby increasing inference latency and slowing down decision-making.

The motivation for this work is multi-faceted and rooted in the increasing reliance on LVLMs in critical robotic applications:

1. **Time-Critical Operations in Robotics**: Robotic systems, particularly in domains like autonomous driving, operate under strict time constraints. Timely decision-making is paramount for ensuring safety, maintaining stability, and achieving correct task execution. Any significant delay in the LVLM's inference process can directly compromise these critical operational requirements, leading to hazardous situations or system failures.
2. **Exploiting LVLM Overthinking Behavior**: Recent findings indicate that LLMs and LVLMs can exhibit "overthinking," producing verbose or extended responses even for straightforward queries. This behavior, if deliberately triggered, can be exploited by an adversary to amplify the computational load and inference time of a victim LVLM.
3. **Unique Attack Vector for LVLMs**: Unlike text-only LLMs, LVLMs process both visual and linguistic inputs. This characteristic opens a novel attack surface where adversaries can embed instruction-like natural language text directly into the visual scene (e.g., as signs or displays). Since LVLMs are trained to read and reason over scene text as part of their environmental understanding, such embedded text can be interpreted as input, potentially initiating an overthinking response without direct access to the model's internal parameters or explicit text prompts.
4. **Practical Black-Box Threat Model**: The research focuses on a realistic adversarial scenario where the attacker has no access to the LVLM's internal architecture, weights, gradients, or even the system's explicit user/system prompts. The adversary's capabilities are limited to introducing physically realizable, human-readable text into the camera's field of view. This black-box setting is common in real-world deployments and necessitates novel discovery methods.
5. **Challenges in Trigger Identification**: Identifying effective scene-text triggers presents two significant technical challenges:
 - **Vast Search Space**: The space of possible human-readable text strings is immense, yet only a small subset is likely to induce overthinking. Brute-force searching is computationally infeasible, and triggers for robotic systems must also be contextually plausible (e.g., road signs, instructions) and camera-readable.
 - **Evaluation Bottleneck**: The very nature of an "overthinking" attack means that effective triggers cause prolonged generation, making full end-to-end latency measurement for every candidate prohibitively expensive during the search process. An efficient evaluation mechanism is required.
6. **Need for Defensive Measures**: Understanding these attacks is crucial for developing robust defenses. The identified triggers and their characteristics can guide defenders in evaluating the vulnerability of their LVLM-based systems, informing safety verification, and aiding in the design of runtime filtering or mitigation strategies to detect and neutralize high-risk inputs.

In essence, the motivation stems from the recognition of a new, practical availability threat to LVLM-powered robotics, driven by the models' inherent overthinking tendencies and their multimodal input processing, requiring efficient and effective black-box methods for trigger discovery.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

This research introduces a three-stage framework designed to systematically identify and validate high-impact, transferable scene-text triggers for overthinking-induced slowdown attacks on LVLM-based robotic systems under a black-box setting.

**A. Overview of the Three-Stage Framework**

The framework operates sequentially:
1. **Stage 1: Data-driven Lexical Mining**: Generates a pool of candidate scene texts, evaluates their overthinking intensity on a reference LVLM, and extracts lexical features correlated with high latency from output prefixes.
2. **Stage 2: Evolutionary Text Optimization**: Employs a genetic algorithm (GA) to efficiently search and compose optimized triggers from the lexical insights and high-impact seeds derived in Stage 1, using a prefix-based proxy for fitness evaluation.
3. **Stage 3: Black-box Transfer Evaluation**: Tests the optimized triggers on unseen images and multiple victim LVLMs, including physical validation, to assess transferability and real-world effectiveness.

**B. Design Details of Each Stage**

1. **Stage 1: Data-driven Lexical Mining**
 - **Design Rationale**: The premise is that overthinking is not triggered by arbitrary text but by inputs that elevate the perceived reasoning demand. This stage aims to narrow the search space to more promising text categories and identify key linguistic elements associated with extended generation.
 - **Calibration Prompt Families**: Five categories of reasoning-intensive text, extended from prior work and refined through experimentation, are used to generate a diverse corpus of candidate scene texts (N_cal = 1500). These categories are:
 - Quantitative and calculation-based scenarios.
 - Algorithmic or planning-oriented formulations.
 - Ethical and moral trade-offs.
 - Introspective or meta-cognitive prompts.
 - Instruction-heavy tasks with recursive constraints.
 - **Overthinking Intensity Metric (r)**: For each candidate text, the LVLM's response to a fixed robotic decision task is measured with (`L_adv`) and without (`L_base`) the injected text. The intensity score is calculated as `r = L_adv / L_base`, representing the token count ratio. A higher `r` indicates a stronger overthinking effect.
 - **Stability-based Selection of Extremes**: To robustly identify keywords, the top `s` (high-ratio) and bottom `s` (low-ratio) samples are selected based on their `r` values. The optimal `s` is determined using the Jaccard index `J(S_s, S_s+Δ)` (where `S_s` is the keyword set from top `s` samples) to ensure the extracted keyword set has converged and is stable (threshold 0.8, with ∆=50). This process yielded `s=150`.
 - **Contrastive Lexical Score**: A discriminative weight `Score(w) = log((Count(w, H) + 1) / (Count(w, L) + 1))` is computed for each token `w`. `Count(w, H)` is its frequency in the first `K_p` (32) tokens of high-ratio responses, and `Count(w, L)` in low-ratio responses. Tokens with high scores are indicative of overthinking. This forms a sensitivity lexicon `L`.

2. **Stage 2: Evolutionary Text Optimization**
 - **Design Rationale**: Given the discrete, combinatorial nature of natural language and the compositional structure of effective triggers, a Genetic Algorithm (GA) is chosen over gradient-based or Bayesian methods. The GA's crossover operation can recombine functional components, exploiting the insight that high-impact triggers are compositional rather than single sentences.
 - **Sentence-level Genetic Representation**: An auxiliary text-only LLM is used to decompose high-impact seeds (from Stage 1) into five functional slots: (1) Scenario description, (2) Ambiguity/conflict, (3) Reasoning requirement, (4) Constraints/formatting, and (5) Final action request. These components form a gene pool `G = {G_j}^5_{j=1}`. Each candidate trigger `g` is a tuple `(g1, g2, g3, g4, g5)`.
 - **Prefix-based Proxy Fitness (F_proxy)**: To overcome the evaluation bottleneck, fitness is estimated by generating only the first `K_p` (32) tokens of a candidate's response. `F_proxy(g) = sum(Score(t_i) * I[t_i ∈ L])` where `Score(t_i)` comes from the sensitivity lexicon. This proxy is motivated by the observation that overthinking signals appear early in the output.
 - **Genetic Algorithm (Algorithm 1)**:
 - **Initialization**: Population `P_0` of `P=50` individuals is created by sampling from gene pools `G_j`.
 - **Iteration (G=15 generations)**:
 - Calculate `F_proxy` for all individuals in the current population.
 - **Hybrid Evaluation**: Run full inference (measuring actual latency and length) for a small elite set (`m=5`) of top-ranked candidates (and `m=5` bottom candidates for diagnostic analysis) per generation.
 - **Selection**: Parents are chosen via tournament selection based on `F_proxy`.
 - **Crossover**: Offspring are generated by composition-aware crossover, swapping functional components between parents.
 - **Mutation**: Slot-wise mutations replace a component with another from its gene pool, or paraphrases it.
 - **Population Renewal**: The next generation `P_t` combines elite individuals from `P_t-1` with newly generated offspring.
 - **Output**: A set of top-ranked, optimized trigger texts.

3. **Stage 3: Black-box Transfer Evaluation**
 - **Design Rationale**: This stage assesses the robustness and generalizability of the discovered triggers by evaluating them on unseen LVLMs and images, mirroring a practical threat scenario where an adversary optimizes on a local model and deploys against an unknown victim.
 - **Setup**: The top-N optimized triggers from Stage 2 are evaluated on `N_test = 3000` held-out road-scene images from the BDD100K dataset (disjoint from Stages 1 and 2 scenes).
 - **Visual Text Injection**: Trigger text is rendered as a physically realizable visual patch and overlaid onto images.
 - **Target LVLMs**:
 - **Cross-architecture transfer**: Kimi-VL-A3B-Thinking-2506 (Kimi-VL) and Qwen3-VL-8B-Thinking (Qwen3-VL), representing different model families and scales from the reference Gemma3-27B-it.
 - **Held-out scene generalization**: Gemma3-27B-it (the same architecture as the reference, but on disjoint test images).
 - **Metrics**:
 - End-to-end inference latency and generated token length are measured.
 - Latency amplification ratio `r = L_attack / L_clean` (median of 3 repetitions per scene-trigger pair).
 - Attack success rate (ASR) at thresholds `τ ∈ {1.5×, 2×, 5×, 10×}`.
 - **Physical Validation**: A static indoor office setup with a robotic vehicle, a physically printed sign, and live camera input is used to confirm real-world feasibility.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation addressed four research questions (RQs) concerning the framework's design, attack effectiveness, physical realizability, and necessity compared to baselines.

**RQ1: Framework Design Validation (Lexical Stability and Proxy Accuracy)**
- **Stability of Lexical Mining**: Jaccard index analysis, which measures the overlap of keyword sets, indicated that a stable core set of overthinking-related keywords was achieved when `s ≥ 150` (Figure 3), consistently exceeding a Jaccard index of 0.8. This supported the selection of `s=150` for subsequent lexical scoring and gene-pool construction, confirming the robustness of the lexical mining approach.
- **Proxy Fitness Validity and Efficiency**:
 - **Efficiency**: Evaluating only the first 32 tokens (Prefix-32) significantly reduced inference time compared to full generation. In high-latency cases, full inference could exceed 80 seconds, while Prefix-32 consistently stayed around 2.5–3.5 seconds (Figure 4), demonstrating substantial computational savings.
 - **Proxy–Latency Association**: Spearman's rank correlation coefficient between proxy fitness and full inference latency on 3,000 evaluated records was `ρ = 0.73`. This value indicates a strong monotonic association, supporting the use of prefix-based proxy fitness as an effective ranking signal to prioritize full evaluations for promising candidates.

**RQ2: Attack Effectiveness across Different LVLMs**
- **Consistent Latency Amplification**: The top-ranked optimized triggers (T1–T4) consistently induced latency amplification across all three target LVLMs. All model-trigger pairs resulted in a slowdown ratio `r > 1.0x` (Figure 6).
- **Magnitude of Slowdown**: The strongest single-trigger case, T3 on Gemma3, achieved a latency amplification of 6.96x. Other notable results included T2 on Qwen3-VL (3.11x) and T1 on Kimi-VL (2.24x).
- **Transferability**: Triggers optimized on Gemma3 demonstrated effective transferability to cross-architecture models like Kimi-VL and Qwen3-VL, indicating that the identified overthinking mechanisms are not model-specific artifacts but represent general vulnerabilities in LVLMs.
- **Tail-Risk Distribution**: Analysis of the empirical Cumulative Distribution Function (CDF) of added latency revealed model-dependent tail behavior. Qwen3-VL exhibited the heaviest tail, with its 95th percentile added latency reaching 69.53 seconds, suggesting a higher frequency of severe slowdown cases compared to Kimi-VL (29.54s) and Gemma3 (36.73s) (Figure 10).

**RQ3: Real-World Validation with Physical Visual Text**
- **Physical Realizability**: The overthink-triggered slowdown attack was validated under real-world conditions using physically printed adversarial text and live camera input on a robotic vehicle (Figures 7, 8).
- **Physical Amplification**: The attack persisted despite the complexities of real-world sensing. Kimi-VL showed significant amplification with 4.74x latency and 4.85x token ratio. Qwen3-VL and Gemma3 exhibited moderate amplification, with ratios around 2.0x (Table II). These results confirm the feasibility of the attack surviving the full camera-to-model sensing pipeline and inducing measurable decision delays.

**RQ4: Method Necessity and Comparison to Prior Overthinking Attacks**
- **Ablation Studies (Table III)**:
 - **Random Text**: Arbitrary long-form scene text had negligible impact, yielding slowdown ratios near 1.0x (e.g., 0.97x on Kimi-VL), indicating that specific textual content is required.
 - **Stage1-Best**: The strongest trigger identified after Stage 1 (without Stage 2 optimization) significantly increased slowdown ratios (e.g., 2.72x on Kimi-VL, 2.65x on Qwen3-VL), validating the effectiveness of data-driven lexical mining in narrowing the search space.
 - **Optimized Trigger (T1)**: T1, resulting from Stage 2 optimization, substantially amplified slowdown beyond Stage1-Best (e.g., 4.54x on Kimi-VL, 16.75x on Gemma3). This demonstrated that the evolutionary optimization effectively combined slowdown-inducing components into a more potent, conflict-heavy trigger.
 - **Naive Chain-of-Thought (CoT)**: A generic task-relevant reasoning baseline (Naive-CoT) only achieved modest increases (e.g., 1.32x on Kimi-VL), highlighting that unoptimized reasoning prompts are less effective than the developed triggers.
- **Comparison with Prior Overthinking Prompts (Table IV)**:
 - When transferred directly into the image-based LVLM setting, attack prompts from text-only overthinking research (OVERTHINK and THINKTRAP) were significantly less effective. OVERTHINK prompts achieved slowdowns up to 3.84x on Gemma3 but mostly remained near 1.0x on other models. THINKTRAP prompts consistently stayed near 1.0x across all LVLMs.
 - This contrasts sharply with the optimized triggers from this work, which achieved 4.54x on Kimi-VL, 3.52x on Qwen3-VL, and 16.75x on Gemma3. This comparison underscores the necessity of modality-specific trigger optimization for LVLMs, as text-only prompts do not automatically translate to effective visual scene-text attacks.

The findings collectively establish that the proposed three-stage framework successfully identifies potent, transferable, and physically realizable scene-text triggers that consistently induce substantial overthinking-triggered slowdowns in LVLM-based robotic systems, surpassing the efficacy of generic prompts and direct transfers from text-only attacks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant contribution to the understanding of vulnerabilities in Large Vision-Language Model (LVLM)-based robotic systems, particularly concerning their real-time operational constraints. The findings have implications for both the security and robustness of these emerging cyber-physical systems.

**Novel Vulnerability and Threat Model**:
- The work identifies and validates a novel "overthinking-induced slowdown attack" that targets the availability of LVLM-based robotic systems. Unlike prior typographic attacks focused on decision correctness or content manipulation, this attack specifically exploits the computational burden of excessively long reasoning traces, directly impacting time-sensitive operations.
- By demonstrating the attack under a strict black-box setting and with physically realizable scene text, the research highlights a practical threat vector that adversaries could exploit without privileged access to the victim system's internals or explicit textual prompts. This expands the understanding of real-world attack surfaces for LVLMs in robotics.

**Comprehensive Trigger Discovery Methodology**:
- The proposed three-stage framework (data-driven lexical mining, evolutionary text optimization, black-box transfer evaluation) offers a systematic and efficient approach to discover effective, transferable multimodal triggers.
- The integration of stability-based lexical analysis, a compositional genetic algorithm, and a prefix-based proxy fitness addresses the inherent challenges of searching a vast, discrete language space and overcoming the computational bottleneck of evaluating overthinking behaviors. This methodology is a valuable contribution for future research into multimodal adversarial examples.

**Empirical Validation and Transferability**:
- The extensive experimental evaluation across multiple LVLM architectures and thousands of held-out scenes provides robust evidence of the attack's effectiveness. The demonstration of significant latency amplification (up to 6.96x on Gemma3) and successful transferability across different LVLMs (Kimi-VL, Qwen3-VL) indicates a general vulnerability rather than a model-specific artifact.
- The physical-world validation with printed scene text and live camera input confirms the practical feasibility of the attack, demonstrating its resilience against real-world imaging conditions and sensing pipelines.

**Implications for System Safety and Robustness**:
- **Security Risk**: The identified attacks pose a critical availability risk for autonomous systems, where delays in decision-making can lead to accidents or operational failures. For example, an autonomous vehicle facing an ethical dilemma described in scene text might overthink, delaying critical braking or steering maneuvers.
- **Defensive Guidance**: The research provides actionable insights for developers and defenders. The discovered triggers and their underlying lexical and structural patterns can be used to:
 - **Benchmark and Validate**: Test the robustness and real-time performance guarantees of LVLM-based robotic systems against known overthinking triggers.
 - **Runtime Monitoring**: Inform the design of runtime monitors that detect early signals of overthinking (e.g., from output prefixes or unusual lexical patterns) and trigger mitigation strategies.
 - **Policy Switching**: Guide the implementation of safety mechanisms like runtime budgets, early termination policies, or fallback to simpler, faster models when overthinking is detected or budgets are approached, ensuring continued (albeit potentially degraded) operation.
 - **Input Filtering**: Develop mechanisms to filter or deprioritize high-risk scene text inputs.
- **Future Research Directions**: This work opens avenues for further research into:
 - The precise cognitive mechanisms within LVLMs that lead to overthinking when presented with conflicting or complex scene text instructions.
 - More sophisticated, context-aware defensive strategies that can differentiate legitimate complex reasoning from adversarial overthinking.
 - The impact of environmental factors (lighting, occlusion, motion blur) and hardware specifications (camera resolution, processing power) on the attack's efficacy.

**Ethical Considerations**:
- The authors acknowledge the dual-use nature of this research, emphasizing its role in diagnosis and defense. All experiments were conducted offline in controlled settings, and no attacks were tested on real-world robotic deployments, adhering to responsible disclosure principles.

In conclusion, this paper uncovers a pertinent and practical security vulnerability in a rapidly evolving technological domain. By meticulously detailing the attack methodology and providing robust empirical evidence, it lays the groundwork for developing more resilient and safer LVLM-based robotic systems.
