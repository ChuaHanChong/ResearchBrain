# Listwise Policy Optimization: Group-based RLVR as Target-Projection on the LLM Response Simplex

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Tsinghua University and Tencent developed Listwise Policy Optimization (LPO), a framework for large language model post-training that reinterprets existing group-based policy gradient methods as implicit target-projections. LPO explicitly constructs a reward-weighted target distribution and performs divergence minimization, leading to improved training performance and stability across various reasoning tasks.

## Problem

- Existing group-based Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) methods for LLMs lacked a unifying theoretical understanding, often viewed through empirical refinements.
- Current policy gradient approaches relied on first-order approximations, potentially limiting optimization stability and efficiency in complex reasoning tasks.
- There was a need for a more explicit and theoretically grounded framework to leverage the finite set of sampled responses in group-based RLVR for better LLM optimization.

## Method

- Reinterpreted group-based policy gradients as ==implicit reverse KL projections== towards a reward-weighted softmax target on a finite ==response simplex==.
- Introduced **Listwise Policy Optimization (LPO)** to explicitly decouple ==target construction== (via a proximal RL objective that defines a ==Listwise Gibbs target==) from ==policy optimization== (by minimizing divergence to the target).
- Developed two LPO instantiations, **Forward KL (LPO_fwd)** and **Reverse KL (LPO_rev)**, which offer distinct theoretical properties and practical advantages in optimization.

## Results

- **LPO** consistently outperformed group-based policy gradient baselines (**GRPO**, **Dr.GRPO**, **MaxRL**) in both **Pass@1** and **Pass@k** accuracy across diverse reasoning tasks (logic, math, programming) and various LLM backbones.
- LPO variants demonstrated **higher response entropy** and exhibited **lower, more stable gradient norms** during training, indicating improved exploration and optimization stability.
- The performance gains were primarily attributed to the **listwise nature** of the projection, and LPO showed robust generalization across different group sizes and LLM families.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Standard group-based policy gradients are equivalent to a first-order approximation of a reverse KL projection towards an implicit reward-weighted softmax target distribution on the response simplex.
> - Explicitly constructing a listwise target distribution and projecting towards it offers provable benefits, including monotonic improvement on the listwise objective and gradients that are bounded, zero-sum, and self-correcting, enhancing stability.
> - Different divergence choices (e.g., Forward KL) within the projection step can promote desirable properties like mode-coverage, helping to preserve response diversity during training.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work is situated within the field of post-training optimization for large language models (LLMs), specifically focusing on Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR). RLVR has gained prominence for enhancing reasoning capabilities in LLMs on complex problem-solving tasks.

Current state-of-the-art in RLVR largely employs critic-free, group-based reinforcement learning (RL) paradigms, such as Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO). These methods involve sampling a group of responses for a given prompt, scoring them using a verifier, and subsequently updating the policy using relative advantage signals derived from within the group. Subsequent research has introduced various refinements, particularly focusing on advantage normalization schemes and strategies for training stabilization.

This paper proposes a unified geometric perspective on these existing group-based RL algorithms. It suggests that their optimization strategies implicitly define a target distribution on a "response simplex" (a probability simplex formed by the sampled responses) and attempt to project the policy towards this target using first-order approximations. This interpretation positions much of the existing work as variations of an underlying target-projection mechanism.

The paper contrasts this with classical RL approaches that explicitly use target projection. While classical methods often deal with continuous action spaces, necessitating function approximation, the group-based RLVR setting for LLMs, with its finite set of sampled responses, allows for exact computation of both the target distribution and the projection in a closed form. This distinction facilitates a transition from implicit approximations to explicit, exact listwise optimization.

Furthermore, the work draws connections and distinctions to other LLM alignment methods. It relates to DPO (Direct Preference Optimization) and its extensions in the context of pairwise and listwise preference optimization, noting that LPO operates in an online RLVR setting with absolute rewards, whereas DPO typically works offline with ranked preference data. It also acknowledges concurrent efforts like FlowRL and TPO, which similarly explore explicit target formulation, but highlights LPO's contribution in offering a unifying analytical framework that recovers existing methods and supports diverse divergences with provable properties.

By providing a unified analytical framework and introducing an explicit projection mechanism, this research aims to offer deeper insights into policy optimization for RLVR and identify avenues for improved algorithmic design.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation for this research stems from an analytical observation regarding existing group-based RLVR algorithms. The authors identify that prevalent group-based policy gradient methods, despite their empirical effectiveness, implicitly construct a reward-weighted softmax target distribution on the "response simplex" (a probability distribution over the sampled responses for a given prompt). The standard policy gradient update, in this view, functions as a first-order approximation of a reverse Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence projection towards this implicit target. The sharpness of this implicit target is controlled by the advantage normalization scheme employed.

This revelation leads to the central research question: "What properties emerge when this target-projection is made explicit, and how does this decoupled optimization space influence RLVR of LLMs?" The paper posits that this implicit, approximate nature of current methods might limit their efficiency and stability, motivating the development of an explicit and exact target-projection framework.

The key objectives are detailed as follows:
1. **Provide a unifying analytical perspective:** To demonstrate that existing group-based policy gradient methods for RLVR share a common geometric structure, where they implicitly perform approximate target-projections on the response simplex. This involves formalizing the concept of a listwise distribution on the simplex and showing the equivalence of policy gradient updates to a negative reverse KL divergence gradient at the on-policy point.
2. **Develop Listwise Policy Optimization (LPO):** To introduce a novel framework that explicitly performs target-projection on the response simplex. This framework aims to:
 - **Explicate the implicit target:** By defining a local proximal RL objective constrained to the sampled responses, yielding a closed-form Gibbs distribution as the target, with a controllable temperature parameter. This demystifies the origin and characteristics of the target distribution.
 - **Decouple target construction from divergence projection:** This decoupling allows for flexibility in choosing projection divergences, moving beyond the inherent reverse KL approximation of policy gradients.
 - **Optimize the policy via exact divergence minimization:** To project the current policy towards the explicit target distribution on the response simplex using exact divergence minimization, rather than first-order approximations.
3. **Demonstrate and analyze the benefits of LPO:** To show that this explicit target-projection framework provides:
 - **Monotonic improvement:** Prove that LPO guarantees monotonic improvement on the listwise objective per iteration.
 - **Stable optimization:** Show that the exact projection results in gradients that are bounded, zero-sum, and self-correcting, which are hypothesized to intrinsically reduce variance and stabilize training.
 - **Flexibility in divergence selection:** Implement and evaluate both forward and reverse KL divergences as examples, highlighting their distinct structural properties (e.g., mode-coverage for forward KL).
 - **Improved training performance:** Empirically validate LPO's effectiveness over typical policy gradient baselines on diverse reasoning tasks and LLM backbones, under matched target configurations.
 - **Preservation of response diversity:** Demonstrate that LPO inherently supports response diversity, addressing issues like entropy collapse observed in other RLVR methods.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of this work is structured into two primary components: an analytical reinterpretation of existing methods and the proposal of a new explicit optimization framework, Listwise Policy Optimization (LPO).

#### 3.1. Group-based Policy Gradient as Implicit Target-Projection

The paper first establishes a unified geometric understanding of group-based policy gradient methods.
- **Listwise Distribution on Response Simplex:** For a given prompt `x` and a group of `K` sampled responses `{y1..., yK}`, a listwise distribution `Pθ` is defined. This distribution `Pθ,k = softmax(sθ)` reflects the policy `πθ`'s relative preference for each response `yk` compared to a behavior policy `πb`, where `sθ,k = log(πθ(yk|x) / πb(yk|x))`. This `Pθ` vector lies on the probability simplex `∆K-1`, termed the "response simplex."
- **Approximate Reverse KL:** Proposition 1 demonstrates that at the exact on-policy point (where `πθ = πb`), the standard group-based policy gradient (`gPG = (1/K) Σk Ak ∇θ log πθ(yk|x)`) is equivalent to the negative gradient of the reverse KL divergence `DKL(Pθ || w*)`. Here, `A` is a zero-mean advantage vector, and `w* = softmax(A)` is identified as the *implicit target distribution* on the response simplex. The paper quantifies the off-policy approximation error, showing it grows linearly with policy drift.
- **Implicit Targets of Existing Methods:** The work analyzes several existing group-based policy gradient algorithms (e.g., GRPO, Dr.GRPO, MaxRL) and shows that their advantage normalization schemes implicitly configure the temperature `τ` of this `w* = softmax(R/τ)` target, thus affecting its sharpness without changing the reward ordering.

#### 3.2. Listwise Policy Optimization (LPO) Framework

LPO is proposed to transition from implicit approximations to explicit, exact target-projection. It decouples the optimization into two steps per iteration:
- **(i) Target Construction (`w* = argmax_w∈∆K-1 ˆJ(w)`):**
 - A local proximal RL objective `ˆJ(w) = Σk wk Rk - τ DKL(w || Pt)` is defined on the response simplex. `Pt` is the listwise distribution induced by the pre-update policy `πt`.
 - Theorem 1 proves that the unique maximizer of `ˆJ(w)` is `w* = softmax(ϕ)`, where `ϕk = Rk/τ + st,k`. This is termed the "Listwise Gibbs target."
 - This formulation explicitly defines `τ` as a trust-region hyperparameter. At the on-policy point (`πt = πb`), `Pt` becomes uniform, recovering `w* = softmax(R/τ)`, consistent with the implicit targets.
 - Theorem 2 provides a monotonic improvement guarantee for the listwise reward, showing that `ˆR(Pt+1) ≥ ˆR(Pt) + τ[DKL(w* || Pt) + DKL(Pt || w*)] - 2Rmax ϵproj`, where `ϵproj` is the projection error.
 - Proposition 2 establishes idealized full-space convergence, where iterating exact proximal updates leads to the reward-maximizing policy.

- **(ii) Projection for Policy Optimization (`θ' = argmin_θ D(w* || Pθ)`):**
 - The policy `πθ` is updated by minimizing a chosen divergence `D` between the target `w*` and the current policy's listwise distribution `Pθ`.
 - **Forward KL (DKL(w* || Pθ)):** Minimizing this divergence yields a gradient `∇θ L_LPO_fwd = Σk (Pθ,k - w*k) ∇θ log πθ(yk|x)`.
 - Corollary 1 describes the desirable properties of its coefficients `c_fwd_k = Pθ,k - w*k`: (a) bounded (between -1 and 1), (b) zero-sum (Σk c_fwd_k = 0), and (c) self-correcting (approaches 0 as `Pθ` approaches `w*`).
 - Corollary 2 provides a mode-coverage guarantee, acting as a log-barrier against mode collapse, ensuring response diversity.
 - **Reverse KL (DKL(Pθ || w*)):** Minimizing this divergence yields `∇θ L_LPO_rev = Σk Pθ,k (dk - d̄) ∇θ log πθ(yk|x)`, where `dk` is the logit gap and `d̄` is its `Pθ`-weighted mean. This also exhibits zero-sum and self-correcting properties. It is shown to be equivalent to maximizing the proximal objective `ˆJ` and implicitly includes an entropy bonus.

#### 3.3. Practical Implementation

Algorithm 1 outlines the LPO procedure. It integrates seamlessly into existing group-based RLVR pipelines without additional computational overhead. The temperature `τ` is adapted from the group-relative advantage normalization statistics of existing baselines (e.g., `τ = σG` for GRPO), enabling a rigorous comparison by isolating the effects of the gradient formulation. The authors also use token-level clipping for log-density ratios, consistent with PPO.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

LPO is evaluated across four reasoning domains: logic (Countdown Game), mathematics (MATH dataset), programming (PRIME code dataset), and multi-modal geometry (Geometry3k dataset). Diverse LLM backbones (Qwen3, Qwen2.5-VL, DeepSeek, Llama, Mistral) ranging from 1.5B to 14B parameters are used. Baselines include GRPO, Dr.GRPO, and MaxRL. Evaluation metrics include expected Pass@1 (average accuracy) and Pass@k, with varying `k` based on the benchmark. `K=8` responses are sampled per prompt during training.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluation of Listwise Policy Optimization (LPO) yields several findings across diverse reasoning tasks and LLM backbones.

- **Consistent Performance Gains:** LPO consistently demonstrated improved training performance compared to the group-based policy gradient (PG) baselines (GRPO, Dr.GRPO, MaxRL) under matched implicit target constructions.
 - **Pass@1 Accuracy:** LPO variants (LPO_fwd and LPO_rev) achieved higher expected Pass@1 accuracy in 13 out of 15 settings compared to their corresponding PG baselines.
 - **Pass@k Accuracy:** This advantage extended to Pass@k evaluations, with LPO_fwd surpassing implicit PG methods in 15 out of 15 scenarios, and LPO_rev in 11 out of 15. These consistent gains suggest that explicit listwise projection improves training efficiency and performance in RLVR.

- **Projection Divergence Effects:** An empirical distinction was observed between the two LPO variants. LPO_fwd generally outperformed LPO_rev in Pass@k across 13 out of 15 scenarios. This is attributed to the mode-coverage property inherent in forward-KL, which actively preserves reasoning diversity and allows for a broader exploration of valid solution paths. This finding highlights the flexibility offered by the decoupled target-projection framework in selecting divergence measures with specific optimization properties.

- **Robustness Across Temperature Parameterizations:** The study noted that the optimal implicit temperature (`τ`) strategy varies significantly across tasks for PG baselines. Despite this task-dependent behavior, LPO consistently delivered stable performance gains under all tested `τ` designs, indicating that the benefits of exact listwise projection are largely independent of the specific temperature heuristic.

- **Training Dynamics:** Analysis of training dynamics provided insights into LPO's optimization behavior:
 - **Response Entropy:** Both LPO variants generally maintained higher response entropy compared to PG baselines. This sustained diversity is linked to the Pass@k improvements and suggests LPO acts as a remedy for entropy collapse in RLVR.
 - **Gradient Norms:** LPO variants exhibited lower and more stable gradient norms. This empirical stability is consistent with the theoretical prediction of bounded, zero-sum, and self-correcting gradients arising from exact projection on the response simplex.
 - **Response Length:** LPO tended to generate longer responses, which often correlates with more detailed reasoning chains, further supporting its tendency for extensive exploration, particularly for LPO_fwd.

- **Listwise vs. Pointwise Projection Ablation:** An ablation study comparing listwise LPO with a pointwise projection variant (where the listwise policy distribution was removed) showed that the pointwise approach suffered a significant performance drop. This indicates that LPO's performance gains are not solely due to the target design but critically depend on the coupled competitive mechanism and structural variance reduction provided by listwise projection.

- **Effect of Group Size K:** LPO variants remained competitive with GRPO across tested group sizes (K ∈ {2, 4, 8, 16, 32}), showing pronounced advantages at smaller `K`. This suggests improved sample efficiency. LPO_rev showed stronger Pass@1, while LPO_fwd scaled well on Pass@k, validating their theoretical diversity properties.

- **Generalization Across LLM Families:** Experiments across various LLM families (Qwen, DeepSeek, Mistral, Llama) demonstrated that LPO consistently improved performance on the Countdown task, regardless of the underlying model architecture or training paradigm. This indicates that LPO offers model-agnostic benefits for improving reasoning performance in RLVR.

- **Fully On-Policy Optimization:** In a strictly on-policy regime, LPO_rev's training curves were almost identical to GRPO, empirically validating the theoretical equivalence between group-based policy gradient and approximate reverse KL projection at the on-policy point. LPO_fwd still showed superior sample efficiency and Pass@k accuracy in this setting.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work introduces Listwise Policy Optimization (LPO) as a contribution to the field of reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) for large language models (LLMs).

**Theoretical and Algorithmic Significance:**
- **Unified Framework:** The paper's primary theoretical contribution is providing a unified geometric framework that reinterprets existing group-based RLVR methods. By demonstrating that these methods implicitly define a target distribution on the response simplex and perform approximate target-projections, it offers a deeper understanding of their underlying mechanics.
- **Explicit Optimization:** LPO transitions from these implicit approximations to an explicit, exact target-projection mechanism. This shift is significant because it leads to provable properties that enhance optimization stability.
- **Robust Gradient Properties:** LPO's exact projection on the response simplex yields gradient coefficients that are bounded, zero-sum, and self-correcting. These properties contribute to variance reduction and stable optimization trajectories, addressing common challenges in RL training.
- **Monotonic Improvement Guarantee:** The framework offers a theoretical guarantee of monotonic improvement on the listwise reward objective, indicating more reliable and predictable learning.
- **Decoupled Design Space:** By decoupling the target construction from the projection divergence, LPO opens up a flexible design space. This allows for the exploration and selection of different statistical divergences (e.g., forward KL, reverse KL), each with distinct structural properties that can be leveraged for specific optimization goals, such as mode-coverage or exploitation.

**Practical Impact:**
- **Improved Performance:** The empirical results demonstrate that LPO consistently improves training performance in terms of Pass@1 and Pass@k accuracy across diverse reasoning tasks (logic, mathematics, programming, multi-modal geometry) and various LLM backbones. This suggests that LPO can lead to more capable and accurate reasoning models.
- **Enhanced Stability and Diversity:** LPO fosters more stable optimization trajectories and intrinsically preserves response diversity. This can mitigate issues like "entropy collapse," where models converge to a narrow set of solutions, which is particularly crucial for complex reasoning problems requiring exploration of multiple valid paths.
- **Model-Agnostic Applicability:** The method's effectiveness across different LLM families (Qwen, DeepSeek, Mistral, Llama) indicates its generalizability and potential as a model-agnostic approach for improving RLVR.
- **Computational Efficiency:** LPO integrates into existing group-based RLVR pipelines without incurring additional computational costs, making it a practically viable improvement for current LLM post-training workflows.

**Potential Future Directions:**
The work identifies several avenues for future research, including extending the framework to step-level listwise projections for finer-grained control, exploring off-policy replay mechanisms for improved sample efficiency, and investigating broader classes of divergences beyond KL to unlock further optimization properties or adapt to specific task requirements. These extensions could broaden the applicability and further enhance the capabilities of RLVR for LLMs.
