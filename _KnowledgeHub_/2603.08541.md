# EquiBim: Learning Symmetry-Equivariant Policy for Bimanual Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Purdue University's EquiBim framework enhances bimanual robotic manipulation by enforcing bilateral equivariance in policies through a model-agnostic regularization term. This approach leads to improved robustness and generalization across various observation-action configurations in both simulated and real-world tasks.

## Problem

- Many existing robot learning methods implicitly handle physical symmetries, particularly in dual-arm manipulation, leading to inconsistent or asymmetric behaviors under symmetric observations.
- Suboptimal policy performance can result from neglecting inherent bilateral symmetry in dual-arm systems and tasks, especially with limited or imbalanced demonstration data.
- Prior methods for incorporating symmetry often rely on specialized architectures or are specific to certain observation modalities/action representations, limiting their general applicability and transferability.

## Method

- Formulates bilateral symmetry as a group action that operates jointly and consistently on both observation and action spaces.
- Introduces a symmetry-consistent regularization term in the training objective to encourage equivariant policy behavior, defined as ||π(S(O)) - S(π(O))||^2.
- Achieves model-agnostic integration by defining consistent symmetry transformations across diverse modalities (e.g., images, point clouds) and action representations (e.g., end-effector, joint space) without architectural modifications.

## Results

- EquiBim consistently improved success rates across all tested observation-action configurations in simulation on the RoboTwin benchmark, showing gains up to +9.5% (absolute) for Image + Joint policies.
- Real-world evaluations demonstrated enhanced robustness and generalization, with a +30% absolute improvement for Banana Handover under nominal conditions and 5/10 success under mirrored object orientations where the baseline failed (0/10).
- The framework improved generalization under distribution shifts and compensated for asymmetric demonstration quality, indicating better transfer of symmetric structure and improved robustness to varying initial conditions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly enforcing bilateral equivariance via an output-level regularization effectively injects a fundamental physical prior into robot policies, improving consistency under symmetric transformations.
> - The model-agnostic nature of the regularization allows seamless integration into a wide range of existing imitation learning pipelines, supporting diverse observation modalities and action representations.
> - Symmetry regularization provides a strong structural prior, particularly beneficial for observation-action configurations (e.g., Image + Joint) where implicit geometric information is weaker, compensating for this limitation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated within the field of robotic imitation learning, a paradigm that enables robots to acquire complex manipulation skills by observing human demonstrations. Recent advancements in this area have facilitated the learning of various manipulation tasks, including dexterous, high-precision, and bimanual operations, through the use of expressive policy representations and rich sensory inputs.

A recognized limitation in many existing robot learning methods is their implicit handling of physical symmetries inherent in robotic systems and tasks. This is particularly relevant in dual-arm manipulation, where bilateral symmetry is a fundamental characteristic of both robot morphology and many task structures. Policies trained without explicitly accounting for these symmetries may exhibit inconsistent or asymmetric behaviors when presented with symmetric observations, potentially compromising coordination and robustness.

Prior research has explored incorporating symmetry and structural priors into robot learning, utilizing methods such as group-equivariant neural network architectures, data canonicalization, augmentation strategies, and invariant feature representations. While these approaches have demonstrated benefits in generalization and sample efficiency, they are often tailored to specific model designs, observation modalities, or action parameterizations. This specificity can limit their general applicability and transferability across diverse robot learning pipelines.

EquiBim addresses this gap by proposing a model-agnostic framework that enforces symmetry as a general inductive bias, aiming for broader applicability across different observation modalities and action representations in bimanual manipulation. This approach seeks to provide a universally applicable mechanism for leveraging bilateral symmetry, rather than relying on architecture-specific or modality-specific designs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observation that while bilateral symmetry is intrinsic to dual-arm robotic systems and many bimanual manipulation tasks, most current imitation learning approaches do not explicitly leverage this structural property. This oversight can lead to suboptimal policy performance, characterized by asymmetric or inconsistent robotic behaviors even when faced with symmetrically equivalent task configurations. Such inconsistencies can negatively impact the quality of coordination and the overall robustness of the robot's operation, particularly in scenarios with limited or imbalanced demonstration data.

Existing methods that attempt to incorporate symmetry often rely on specialized architectures or are specific to certain observation modalities or action representations. This dependency limits their generalizability and makes them difficult to integrate as a universal inductive bias across diverse robotic learning setups. The authors identify a need for a more flexible and plug-and-play solution.

Given these motivations, the key objectives of the EquiBim framework are:
- **To explicitly enforce bilateral equivariance**: The core objective is to ensure that a robotic policy's predictions transform consistently under symmetric transformations, meaning that if an observation is symmetrically transformed, the policy's output action should also be symmetrically transformed.
- **To formulate symmetry as a group action**: The framework aims to define physical symmetry formally as a group action that operates jointly on both the observation and action spaces. This formalization underpins the equivariance constraint.
- **To introduce an equivariance-based regularization**: Instead of requiring architectural modifications, the goal is to implement symmetry enforcement as a regularization term in the training objective. This approach aims to constrain policy predictions to be consistent under symmetric transformations.
- **To achieve model-agnostic integration**: A significant objective is to design EquiBim as a flexible framework that can be seamlessly incorporated into a wide range of existing imitation learning pipelines. This includes compatibility with diverse observation modalities (e.g., point clouds, images) and action representations (e.g., end-effector space, joint space) without requiring modifications to the underlying network architectures or optimization procedures.
- **To validate performance and robustness**: The research aims to evaluate the effectiveness of EquiBim in improving performance and robustness in bimanual manipulation tasks, both in simulated environments and on physical hardware, especially under conditions involving distribution shifts.

By addressing these objectives, the research seeks to provide a practical and general method for enhancing the learning of bimanual manipulation skills by systematically leveraging an inherent physical prior.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

EquiBim is presented as a symmetry-aware training framework designed for dual-arm robot learning, specifically enforcing bilateral equivariance between observations and actions. The methodology formulates symmetry as a group action and applies a consistency objective during policy training.

**A. Problem Setup and Bimanual Symmetry**
The problem is framed as learning a robotic control policy `π` through behavior cloning. The policy maps a history of observations `O` to a sequence of future actions `A`. Observations can be heterogeneous, combining visual (images, point clouds) and proprioceptive (end-effector states, joint configurations) data. The framework makes no specific assumptions about modality or action representation.
The focus is on bimanual tasks where the physical setup and task specification exhibit inherent left-right symmetry. This implies that exchanging the roles of the two arms and applying a corresponding geometric reflection to the scene results in an equivalent task instance with an equally valid expert behavior. This structural property is denoted by a symmetry transformation `S(·)`, which acts consistently on both observations and actions.

**B. Symmetry-Consistent Regularization**
The core of EquiBim is a symmetry-consistent regularization that encourages equivariant policy behavior. Unlike methods that enforce symmetry through architectural constraints, EquiBim imposes a consistency objective on the policy outputs.
Given an observation history `O` and its symmetrically transformed counterpart `S(O)`, an ideal symmetry-aware policy should produce predictions such that the action sequence predicted from the transformed observation, `π(S(O))`, matches the symmetrically transformed prediction from the original observation, `S(π(O))`. This equivariant consistency is enforced using a regularization loss:
`L_sym = ||π(S(O)) - S(π(O))||^2`
This objective encourages compatibility between actions for symmetrically equivalent task instances without altering the policy parameters or inference process. The regularization is independent of the policy architecture, observation modality, or action representation, provided `S` can be consistently defined for the given inputs and outputs.

**C. Symmetry Transformation across Modalities**
The symmetry transformation `S` is defined consistently across different observation modalities and action representations, typically centered on a left-right (lateral) reflection of the robot workspace. It is assumed that visual observations from a head camera align the image's horizontal axis with the robot's lateral (y) direction.

- **For Image-based Observations**: `S` is defined as a horizontal flip of the image.
- **For Point Cloud Observations**: Points are first transformed from the robot or world frame into the image coordinate frame using known camera extrinsics. The symmetry transformation is then applied as a reflection along the lateral axis, represented by `R_y = diag(1, -1, 1)`, followed by an inverse transformation back to the original frame. Formally, for a point `p`, `S(p) = T_cam^-1 R_y T_cam p`, where `T_cam` is the transformation from robot to image frame.
- **For End-Effector Actions**: End-effector poses are first expressed in the image or robot base coordinate frame. A sagittal reflection is applied to both position and orientation components, and the transformed pose is then converted back to the original control frame.
- **For Joint-Space Actions**: The symmetry transformation `S` is defined based on the robot's kinematic structure. Each joint is assigned a symmetry sign indicating whether its motion changes the left-right spatial configuration under arm exchange. The transformed joint command is obtained by applying joint-wise sign flips: `S(q) = Dq`, where `q` is the joint action vector and `D` is a diagonal matrix of joint-specific symmetry signs.

Actions are parameterized in absolute form to ensure well-defined and comparable symmetry transformations across different action spaces. This unified definition of `S` allows the same symmetry-consistent regularization to be applied to policies with diverse input and output configurations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of EquiBim was conducted in both simulation and real-world environments, utilizing a controlled and modular experimental setup.

**A. Experimental Setup**
- **Simulation**: Experiments were performed on RoboTwin, a dual-arm robotic simulation suite with symmetric kinematics, featuring eight bimanual tasks (e.g., Beat Block Hammer, Handover Block, Press Stapler) selected for their inherent left-right symmetry.
- **Observation-Action Configurations**: Four combinations were tested: Image + Joint, Image + End-Effector (EE), Point Cloud + Joint, and Point Cloud + EE.
- **Baseline Policies**: Diffusion Policy (for image-based) and DP3 (for point cloud-based) were used as backbones. EquiBim was integrated without modifying these architectures.
- **Real-World**: A dual-arm LeRobot SO101 platform with a Logitech C920x RGB camera (mimicking a head-mounted configuration) was used for real-world validation. Three tasks were evaluated: Banana Handover, Drumstick Hook Hanging, and Toy Chicken Hook Hanging. Human demonstrations (50 per task) were collected using a teleoperation system. The ACT policy was used as the backbone.
- **Evaluation Metric**: Average success rate.

**B. Effect of Symmetry Regularization in Simulation**
EquiBim consistently improved performance across all tested observation-action configurations on the RoboTwin benchmark.
- **Overall Performance Gains**:
 - Image + Joint: Success rate increased from 34.1% to 43.6% (+9.5% absolute).
 - Image + EE: Success rate increased from 37.3% to 40.0% (+2.7% absolute).
 - Point Cloud + Joint: Success rate increased from 73.5% to 77.9% (+4.4% absolute).
 - Point Cloud + EE: Success rate increased from 74.5% to 77.8% (+3.3% absolute).
- **Trend Analysis**: The most substantial gains were observed in the Image + Joint configuration. The authors suggest this is due to image observations lacking explicit 3D structure and joint-space actions not directly reflecting task-space spatial relationships. In this setting, symmetry regularization provides a strong structural prior, compensating for the implicitly weaker geometric information. Point cloud–based observations and end-effector actions already encode more explicit geometric information, leading to comparatively smaller, yet consistent, gains.
- **Per-Task Analysis**:
 - Significant improvements were noted on tasks with clear bilateral structure or interchangeable arm roles, such as Beat Block Hammer (+22% Image + Joint), Move Can Pot (+23% Image + Joint), Place Empty Cup (+28% Image + Joint), and Press Stapler (+15% Point Cloud + Joint). This indicates that enforcing symmetry reduces policy variance and improves generalization for these tasks.
 - Moderate performance drops were observed on tasks like Handover Block (-15% Image + EE) and Pick Dual Bottles (-13% Image + EE). The authors attribute this to these tasks potentially having instance-level asymmetries in optimal control strategies, such as temporal role assignments in handover, which symmetry regularization might suppress. However, it is noted that this does not imply inherent incompatibility, as real-world handover tasks showed improvement.

**C. Real-World Evaluation**
Real-world experiments validated EquiBim's effectiveness in enhancing robustness and generalization.
- **Training Distribution**:
 - Banana Handover: ACT achieved 3/10 success, while ACT + EquiBim achieved 6/10 success (+30% absolute improvement).
 - Drumstick and Toy Chicken Hook Hanging: Both methods showed comparable performance under nominal conditions, with EquiBim exhibiting consistent but smaller gains.
- **Shifted Distribution (Generalization)**:
 - **Banana Handover (mirrored object orientation, varied placement)**: The baseline ACT failed (0/10 success), whereas ACT + EquiBim achieved 5/10 success. This demonstrates EquiBim's ability to transfer learned left-right interaction patterns across mirrored configurations, improving robustness to symmetric shifts.
 - **Drumstick Hook Hanging (asymmetric demonstration quality)**: With uneven supervision (e.g., weaker right-side demonstrations), ACT achieved 1/10 success, while ACT + EquiBim reached 4/10 success. This suggests symmetry regularization can leverage stronger symmetric data (e.g., from left-side demonstrations) to regularize and compensate for weaker data, effectively transferring symmetric structure across arms.
 - **Toy Chicken Hook Hanging (randomized initializations)**: Under broader positional shifts, ACT achieved 4/10 success, while ACT + EquiBim achieved 6/10 success (+20% absolute improvement). This indicates improved generalization under varying initial conditions.

Overall, the real-world findings reinforced that symmetry-consistent regularization contributes to improved robustness and generalization in visually symmetric bimanual manipulation scenarios, across both nominal and shifted object distributions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work presents EquiBim, a framework that establishes explicit enforcement of bilateral equivariance as a valuable inductive bias for bimanual robotic manipulation policies. By formulating symmetry as a group action consistently across observation and action spaces and imposing an output-level regularization, EquiBim offers a method that is model-agnostic and can be integrated into existing imitation learning pipelines without requiring architectural modifications.

The significance of this approach lies in several aspects:
- **Enhanced Robustness and Generalization**: The consistent performance improvements observed in both simulation and real-world experiments, particularly under distribution shifts and varying initial conditions, suggest that EquiBim improves the robustness and generalization capabilities of learned bimanual policies. This is crucial for deploying robotic systems in dynamic and unstructured environments.
- **Data Efficiency Potential**: By explicitly encoding a fundamental physical prior, EquiBim may reduce the reliance on extensive and perfectly balanced demonstration datasets. The ability to leverage strong symmetric data to regularize weaker or less abundant data, as demonstrated in the Drumstick Hook Hanging task, implies potential for improved sample efficiency.
- **Broad Applicability**: The framework's model-agnostic nature is a key strength, allowing its application across diverse observation modalities (images, point clouds) and action representations (end-effector space, joint space). This flexibility positions EquiBim as a general, plug-and-play module for a wide array of bimanual robot learning scenarios.
- **Addressing Fundamental Limitations**: EquiBim directly addresses the limitation of many current robot learning methods that neglect inherent physical symmetries, providing a mechanism to prevent asymmetric or inconsistent behaviors under symmetric observations.

The potential impact of EquiBim includes facilitating the development of more reliable and versatile bimanual robotic systems. By providing a principled way to incorporate prior knowledge about physical symmetries, this work contributes to the broader objective of making robot learning more efficient, robust, and generalizable. It highlights that explicitly leveraging task-level inductive biases like symmetry can offer a practical and effective strategy for advancing the capabilities of robotic manipulation.
