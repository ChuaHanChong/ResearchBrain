# ReflectWorld-MM: An Entity-Oriented Multimodal Memory System for Open-Ended Video Streams

> [!summary] Summary
> Rightly Robotics developed ReflectWorld-MM, an entity-oriented multimodal memory system enabling AI assistants to continuously perceive, remember, and reason over open-ended video streams. The system integrates cognitively-inspired hierarchical memory with an externalized database, achieving superior accuracy across six long-video and entity-centric benchmarks, with up to a 10.4 percentage point increase over prior methods on entity-focused tasks.

## Problem

- Existing AI agents struggle with memory scalability and persistence when confronted with arbitrarily long, open-ended multimodal streams, often relying on bounded context windows or flat feature stores.
- Prior multimodal memory systems lack effective entity-oriented organization, making it difficult to track persistent identities or revise semantic knowledge about individuals and objects over time.
- Current solutions often do not fully integrate multi-scale episodic, evolving semantic, and procedural memory within a unified, deployable architecture, nor do they enable perception to be actively informed by accumulated context.

## Method

- **ReflectWorld-MM** introduces a ==cognitively grounded hierarchical memory system== with ==episodic==, ==evolving entity-centric semantic==, and ==procedural== components, all externalized into a persistent, indexed database.
- A sophisticated ==perception front-end== processes live video by resolving entities and enriching vision-language model prompts with ==bounded working memory==, ==camera-specific semantic context==, and ==entity history==, allowing for context-aware interpretation.
- The system is realized as a deployable, database-backed service capable of ingesting arbitrary live video sources and exposing APIs, ensuring continuous operation with bounded per-step computational cost.

## Results

- ReflectWorld-MM achieved the highest accuracy across all six evaluated benchmarks, including VideoMME-Long, LVBench, HippoVlog, EgoLife-QA, and M3-bench (robot and web splits).
- On entity-centric M3-bench tasks, the system demonstrated substantial improvements, outperforming M3-Agent by **10.4 percentage points** on the web split (**56.0%** vs. **45.6%**) and **9.1 points** on the robot split (**37.4%** vs. **28.3%**).
- The system exhibits high answer efficiency, achieving strong accuracy with minimal reliance on re-accessing source video (e.g., only **4.6% fallback** on EgoLife-QA), indicating that its constructed memory effectively stores necessary evidence for queries.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Organizing memory around persistent entities, rather than frames or tokens, is crucial for accumulating durable knowledge and facilitating long-term reasoning about individuals and objects.
> - Integrating multiple levels of abstraction in memory (fine-grained entity observations, segment traces, and narrative schemas) allows for efficient retrieval and reasoning over vastly different time scales.
> - Actively feeding back accumulated context and entity history into the perception process transforms passive observation into an informed, narrative-continuous understanding, significantly improving the quality of observations stored in memory.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of intelligent assistants capable of continuously observing, remembering, and reasoning about the world from multimodal data streams represents a significant goal in artificial intelligence. This work positions itself within the evolving landscape of multimodal agents, particularly those equipped with long-term memory.

Current approaches to memory in AI agents can be broadly categorized. One category involves streaming and long-video models that primarily store memory implicitly within the model context or as a flat feature store, often organized by frames or tokens. Examples include MovieChat, MA-LMM, Flash-VStream, VideoLLM-online, and ReKV. While effective for bounded videos, these systems typically exhibit single-scale, content-agnostic memory, which tends to degrade when confronted with streams significantly exceeding their training video lengths. Such methods struggle with tracking persistent entities or recalling information over extended, unbounded periods.

A separate line of research focuses on explicit memory systems for language agents, drawing inspiration from cognitive theories of human memory by decomposing it into episodic, semantic, and procedural components. Works like MemGPT, Generative Agents, A-MEM, Mem0, and MemoryBank fall into this category. However, these systems are predominantly text-only and conversation-driven, lacking the capability to perceive video streams or resolve persistent visual entities, which is crucial for continuous multimodal understanding.

Closer to the present work are multimodal agents that incorporate explicit long-term memory over video. M3-Agent organizes memory as an entity-centric multimodal graph and employs a multi-turn retrieval policy. WorldMM constructs multi-scale episodic, semantic, and visual memories to support long-video question answering. Despite these advancements, several limitations persist in these prior multimodal memory agents. M3-Agent's memory update mechanism, primarily frequency-based, results in largely append-only semantic knowledge that cannot be effectively revised or removed. WorldMM, while utilizing multi-scale memory, relies on hand-set temporal scales per dataset and does not explicitly maintain entity identities, thereby hindering its ability to answer identity-dependent questions. More fundamentally, these systems generally do not integrate multi-scale episodic memory, a continuously evolving entity-centric semantic memory, and procedural memory within a unified architecture, nor are they realized as complete services capable of operating on arbitrary live streams.

ReflectWorld-MM addresses these identified gaps by proposing a system that unifies entity-oriented multimodal memory with open-ended streaming perception. Its design incorporates cognitively grounded hierarchical memory organization and is engineered for real-world deployment, aiming to provide a comprehensive solution for lifelong multimodal learning in AI assistants.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of ReflectWorld-MM is to enable intelligent assistants to operate continuously on open-ended multimodal streams, perceiving, remembering, and reasoning over their accumulated experiences. This overarching goal is driven by several specific motivations to overcome the limitations of existing approaches:

1. **Overcoming Memory Scalability and Persistence Issues**: A core motivation is to move beyond models that store memory implicitly within context windows or flat feature banks. These methods are inherently limited by bounded input lengths and suffer from performance degradation as video streams become arbitrarily long. ReflectWorld-MM aims to provide a persistent, externalized memory system that allows for indefinite operation without the per-step computational cost increasing with stream length.

2. **Shifting to Entity-Oriented Memory Organization**: Traditional video understanding often organizes memory around frames or sparse tokens, which are content-agnostic and do not explicitly track persistent subjects. The paper argues for organizing memory around "entities"—persons or objects that are consistently re-identified over time. This entity-centric approach is motivated by the need to accumulate durable knowledge about individuals and objects, facilitating reasoning about "who" and "what" reappears and evolves over time.

3. **Implementing a Cognitively Grounded Hierarchical Memory**: Drawing inspiration from human memory theories (specifically Tulving's episodic, semantic, and procedural memory taxonomy and Conway's autobiographical memory structure), the system aims to build a hierarchical memory. This is motivated by the need to support retrieval at multiple levels of abstraction (fine-grained events, segment summaries, high-level narratives) and to store different types of knowledge (event-specific experiences, durable facts, operational rules) in appropriate, interconnected structures.

4. **Enabling Evolving and Revisable Semantic Knowledge**: Existing entity-centric multimodal memory systems often treat semantic knowledge as append-only, meaning new information is added but old facts are rarely revised or removed. ReflectWorld-MM seeks to implement an evolving semantic memory where new evidence can reinforce, update, or delete existing facts, reflecting a more dynamic and accurate understanding of entities over time. This is critical for maintaining up-to-date and consistent knowledge.

5. **Integrating Multimodal Perception with Contextual Awareness**: A significant motivation is to ensure that the perception process itself is informed by accumulated context, rather than interpreting each segment in isolation. By feeding back working memory, scene context, and entity history into the perception front-end, the system aims to achieve "narrative continuity" and actively make sense of the present in light of the past, thereby enhancing the quality of observations written to memory.

6. **Ensuring Real-World Operability and Deployability**: The research is motivated by the need to develop a complete system, not just a theoretical model. This includes designing ReflectWorld-MM as a deployable, database-backed service that can ingest arbitrary live video sources, externalize memory into a persistent store, and expose APIs for integration with off-the-shelf assistant systems. This focus on practical realization aims to bridge the gap between research prototypes and functional AI applications.

7. **Facilitating Proactive Response Mechanisms**: By incorporating a procedural memory for user-defined rules, the system aims to enable proactive behaviors, such as raising alerts based on perceived situations, without requiring direct model action or generating spurious notifications. This allows the system to act as an intelligent monitor rather than just a passive recall system.

In summary, the key motivation is to advance the state of AI assistants by endowing them with a robust, cognitively inspired, scalable, and continuously evolving multimodal memory system that can understand and reason over open-ended real-world video streams.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ReflectWorld-MM is designed as an entity-oriented multimodal memory system for open-ended video streams, built around three core components: a perception front-end, a hierarchical long-term memory, and a complete system realization. The design is guided by principles of separating evidence from decisions, organizing memory by time scale and abstraction level, and enabling the past to participate in perceiving the present.

#### 3.1. Perception Front-End

The perception front-end is responsible for converting raw audiovisual streams into entity-resolved observations. It operates in four main stages:

1. **Streaming Ingestion**: The system utilizes a common gateway to accept arbitrary live video sources (e.g., network cameras, webcams, local files, HTTP streams, smartphone cameras). This gateway normalizes incoming streams and segments them into activity-coherent audiovisual units, complete with transcripts and speaker diarization when available. This process ensures the front-end operates on truly open-ended streams without a predetermined end.

2. **Entity Recognition**: Within each segment, the system detects persons and objects and attempts to re-identify them against previously seen entities using face and body appearance. Identity evidence (candidate matches with similarity scores) is generated. Crucially, a dedicated *resolver* is the sole component permitted to make final identity decisions, ensuring that noisy matches do not corrupt the memory and that all identity decisions are auditable.

3. **Context-Enhanced Perception**: A distinguishing feature is that the vision-language model (VLM) does not interpret segments in isolation. Instead, its prompt is enriched with three layers of accumulated context before processing:
 - **Bounded Working Memory**: Maintains the current event state, including a rolling event summary, the three most recent segment summaries, active entities, and up to eight tracked targets, providing within-event narrative continuity.
 - **Per-Camera Semantic Context**: Supplies cross-event scene background knowledge (e.g., scene type, typical activities, routines), which is periodically revised by an LLM and incorporated into the prompt as directives.
 - **Entity History**: Upon re-identification of an entity, its accumulated episodic and semantic knowledge is retrieved and added to the prompt, enabling the VLM to understand the current frame in light of the entity's history.

4. **Agent-Steered Adaptive Perception**: Perception is not a fixed process but is actively steered by a high-level agent. The agent injects its understanding of the scene—including a scene description, focus targets, and active user rules—into the VLM prompt. This mechanism directs the VLM's attention and reporting focus. A controller, using deterministic frame-level and semantic segment-level policies, adaptively decides the level of analysis for each segment (e.g., lightweight vision-only vs. fuller analysis with detection and re-identification). Static or silent segments are processed cheaply, while information-rich or rule-relevant segments receive more thorough analysis. Hard guards prevent the skipping of critical evidence, and detection anchoring ensures that any identity targets are grounded in visual evidence.

#### 3.2. Hierarchical Long-Term Memory

Inspired by human memory theory, the long-term memory is externalized into a persistent, indexed store and organized into three main types:

1. **Episodic Memory**: Records "what happened" at three levels of abstraction:
 - **Entity Level**: Stores fine-grained observations for each resolved entity within a segment (appearance, behavior, interactions), keyed by the entity's persistent identifier.
 - **Trace Level**: Stores a summary for each segment, describing the event that occurred and listing the participating entities, linking back to their respective entity-level observations. This serves as the default unit of recall.
 - **Schema Level**: Aggregates multiple closed events into narrative "chapters" once a certain budget of events or time interval is reached. These chapter-level summaries provide a coarse index, limiting the number of fine-grained traces an agent needs to consider for long-history queries.

2. **Evolving Entity-Centric Semantic Memory**: This component distills durable knowledge about each entity, such as attributes, habits, and relationships. It is updated and consolidated after every *N* new observations of an entity (N=5 in experiments). The consolidator processes the entity's prior episodic and semantic records to generate one of four edit decisions: Add, Update, Delete, or no change. This ensures the memory is evolving and revisable, rather than append-only. Facts carry an importance score (`w`) that increases with re-confirmation (`w ← w + (1 − w) γ`), making well-confirmed facts more stable and newly added ones easier to revise.

3. **Procedural Memory**: Stores user-supplied rules and preferences (e.g., conditions for alerts). These rules are integrated into the perception prompt, allowing the VLM to emit semantic signals (e.g., "situation looks risky," "rule satisfied"). Following the evidence-versus-decision principle, a separate deterministic resolver combines these signals with camera-specific notification policies and recent notification history to decide whether and what to deliver, preventing spurious or repeated alerts.

#### 3.3. System Realization and Retrieval

- **Externalized Persistent Store**: The memory is housed in an indexed database rather than being kept in the model's context or main memory. This architecture allows the total store to grow indefinitely while maintaining bounded per-segment working state and query context.
- **Retrieval Mechanism**: Queries are answered by an agent (GPT-5 in experiments) interacting with the externalized store via tool calls. The agent selects the appropriate memory level (entity observations, traces, schemas, semantic facts, or procedural rules) and retrieves only necessary items. Shared entity identifiers connect different memory levels. An importance score aids in ranking retrieved facts.
- **Implementation Details**: The system employs GPT-5-mini for memory extraction and semantic consolidation, and OpenAI’s `text-embedding-3-small` for embedding memory items. Local, open-source ONNX models (e.g., YOLO26m for object detection, RetinaFace-ResNet50 for face detection, ArcFace-MobileFaceNet for face ReID) provide bounded visual/audio signals. The system supports `offline_quality` and `live_latency` execution modes, with `live_latency` prioritizing low-latency commits of critical fields.
- **Deployment**: ReflectWorld-MM is packaged as a plugin for off-the-shelf assistant runtimes like OpenClaw, exposing its functionalities (capture control, perception, memory query, notification policy, context management) through a uniform set of tool-based APIs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

ReflectWorld-MM was evaluated across six benchmarks, encompassing general long-video understanding, egocentric, and entity-centric scenarios. The results consistently demonstrated its performance advantages over compared baselines.

#### 4.1. Main Results (Accuracy)

- **Superior Performance Across Benchmarks**: ReflectWorld-MM achieved the highest accuracy on all six benchmarks: VideoMME-Long, LVBench, HippoVlog, EgoLife-QA, M3-bench-robot, and M3-bench-web.
- **Significant Gains on Entity-Centric Tasks**: The largest improvements were observed on the M3-bench, an entity-centric benchmark. ReflectWorld-MM outperformed M3-Agent, a strong prior method, by 10.4 percentage points on the web split (56.0% vs. 45.6%) and 9.1 points on the robot split (37.4% vs. 28.3%). While absolute accuracies on M3-bench remained low across all systems, the relative gains indicate the effectiveness of ReflectWorld-MM's entity-oriented design.
- **Validation of Entity Identity Tracking**: On EgoLife-QA, a benchmark highly sensitive to entity identity, ReflectWorld-MM achieved the best accuracy of 46.8%. This compares favorably to GPT-5 (42.6%) and M3-Agent (30.8%). Notably, this accuracy surpasses the 45.5% reported by the original EgoLife paper, which used manually annotated descriptions with identity IDs, highlighting ReflectWorld-MM's capability with automatically constructed memory. WorldMM, which does not explicitly maintain entity identities, was not reported on this benchmark, further emphasizing the importance of entity-oriented design.
- **Improved Long-Video Understanding**: When compared against GPT-5 alone as a reference for raw video understanding, ReflectWorld-MM consistently improved long-video question answering performance. Improvements ranged from 2.6 points on the shorter VideoMME-Long benchmark (76.9% vs. 74.3%) to 9.0 points on LVBench (69.4% vs. 60.4%), indicating that its memory system enhances the reasoning capabilities of the underlying language model over extended durations.

#### 4.2. Memory Quality (Answer Efficiency)

Beyond raw accuracy, the study evaluated memory quality through "answer efficiency," measuring accuracy alongside the average number of answer tokens per question and the percentage of questions requiring video fallback.

- **High Accuracy with Low Fallback**: ReflectWorld-MM achieved high accuracy with minimal reliance on re-accessing the source video, exhibiting only 4.6% fallback on EgoLife-QA and 6.8% on VideoMME-Long. This suggests that the system's constructed memory effectively stores the necessary evidence to answer most queries directly.
- **Comparison to Baselines**: M3-Agent showed 0.0% fallback but significantly lower accuracy, implying its compact traces might omit crucial evidence. WorldMM, while strong on VideoMME-Long, required video fallback for 34.0% of questions, indicating that its memory might function more as an index to the source material rather than a comprehensive store of answerable evidence. ReflectWorld-MM demonstrated a favorable profile of both high accuracy and efficient, memory-based answering.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

An answer-time ablation study on the entity-sensitive M3-bench-robot and EgoLife-QA benchmarks investigated the contributions of different memory components.

- **Contribution of Memory Components**: Even under this conservative setting (where memory is fixed and components are blocked at answer time), removing specific components led to reduced accuracy. The largest drop was observed when schema memory was removed on M3-bench-robot (37.4% to 33.6%). Entity association and semantic memory also contributed positively to performance on both datasets. These results suggest that the proposed entity association, hierarchical schema-level organization, and semantic consolidation are integral to the system's measured gains.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Analysis

Three case studies illustrated the "write side" of ReflectWorld-MM:

- **Entity-Centric Semantic Memory Evolution**: A week-long vlog example demonstrated how observations from different days, linked to the same person, are consolidated into durable semantic facts (e.g., work/study routines, exercise habits). This illustrated the evolving nature of semantic memory, where new evidence strengthens or revises existing knowledge, contrasting with append-only systems.
- **Task-Adaptive Extraction**: In a coffee-making sequence, initial recognition of "coffee preparation" as the scene context guided subsequent perception. Later segments were interpreted as stages of the same pour-over workflow, showcasing how accumulated scene context influences the meaning of new observations and allows for adaptive, rather than fixed, feature extraction.
- **Procedural Memory and Proactive Response**: An example depicted a user-defined rule ("Notify me when the dog is on the sofa") triggering a notification. Visual evidence of the dog near, then on, the sofa was converted into semantic signals, which a deterministic resolver then combined with policy and cooldowns to trigger an alert. This demonstrated the system's capability for rule-based, proactive responses grounded in perception, without direct VLM action.

#### 4.5. Real-World Operation

ReflectWorld-MM has been implemented as a complete service, capable of ingesting arbitrary live video streams and persisting its memory in an indexed database. This externalized memory is accessible to any downstream agentic system via APIs, underscoring its practical deployment readiness. The code has been open-sourced, and a dashboard provides an interactive interface to the system.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ReflectWorld-MM represents a notable advancement in the development of AI systems capable of continuous perception, memory, and reasoning over open-ended multimodal streams. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enabling True Lifelong Learning for AI Agents**: By providing an externalized, persistent, and continually evolving memory, ReflectWorld-MM addresses a fundamental limitation of current AI models that struggle with unbounded input lengths and transient context. This architecture facilitates truly lifelong learning, allowing agents to accumulate and adapt knowledge over indefinitely long durations, a crucial step towards robust general-purpose AI.

2. **Bridging Cognitive Theory and Practical AI Systems**: The system's design, explicitly grounded in human memory theory (episodic, semantic, procedural memory, and autobiographical hierarchy), provides a robust conceptual framework for managing complex, multi-scale information. This integration of cognitive science principles into a deployable AI architecture offers a pathway for developing more human-like and intuitive memory systems.

3. **Advancing Entity-Centric Understanding**: The emphasis on entity-oriented memory, where observations and knowledge are organized around persistent persons and objects, is a key contribution. This enables agents to track identities, accumulate durable facts about entities, and reason about their evolution over time, significantly improving understanding in dynamic, real-world environments compared to frame or token-centric approaches.

4. **Enhancing Multimodal Perception with Context**: The methodology of feeding back accumulated context (working memory, scene context, entity history) into the perception front-end is significant. It transforms perception from a passive extraction process into an active, context-aware interpretation, leading to more coherent and narratively continuous understanding of events. This has implications for more robust and accurate scene interpretation in complex scenarios.

5. **Practical Deployability and Scalability**: ReflectWorld-MM's realization as a database-backed service that accepts arbitrary live streams and exposes standard agent APIs makes it highly practical. Its bounded per-step computational cost, independent of stream length, ensures scalability for real-world deployments in applications such as household robotics, personal assistants, surveillance, and industrial monitoring.

6. **Improved Performance and Efficiency**: The empirical results, showing superior accuracy across a range of long-video and entity-centric benchmarks, particularly with efficient memory-based answering (low video fallback), demonstrate the system's tangible benefits. This provides strong evidence that the architectural choices lead to more effective and efficient information retrieval and reasoning.

7. **Foundation for Proactive and Intelligent Assistants**: The inclusion of procedural memory for user-defined rules and proactive response mechanisms opens avenues for building more interactive and assistive AI systems. Agents equipped with ReflectWorld-MM could not only answer questions about the past but also proactively alert users to relevant situations or deviations from established norms.

In conclusion, ReflectWorld-MM offers a comprehensive and validated approach to multimodal memory, moving beyond transient, implicit methods towards a persistent, entity-oriented, and cognitively inspired system. This work is likely to influence future research in memory architectures for AI, especially in embodied AI, lifelong learning, and the development of intelligent agents operating continuously in dynamic environments.
