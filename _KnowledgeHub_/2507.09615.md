# Towards Fine-Grained Adaptation of CLIP via a Self-Trained Alignment Score

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework called Fine-grained Alignment and Interaction Refinement (FAIR) enhances the performance of Vision-Language Models like CLIP in unsupervised fine-grained classification by introducing a learned alignment score and adaptively refining cross-modal interactions. The approach achieved an average accuracy gain of 2.78% over the prior state-of-the-art, DPA, across 13 fine-grained datasets.

## Problem

- Pretrained Vision-Language Models (VLMs) like CLIP struggle with fine-grained classification due to their reliance on global features and generic text prompts, failing to capture subtle visual distinctions.
- Acquiring labeled data for fine-grained domains is often costly or infeasible, necessitating effective unsupervised adaptation (UA) methods.
- Existing UA methods use fixed alignment scores, limiting their adaptability to evolving class distinctions, or computationally expensive localized feature processing for pseudo-labeling.

## Method

- Introduces ==Fine-grained Alignment and Interaction Refinement (FAIR)==, an unsupervised adaptation framework for CLIP that dynamically aligns localized image features with adaptive language embeddings.
- Employs ==learnable Class Description Anchors (CDAs)== initialized by an LLM, which adapt concurrently with the image encoder during self-training to capture evolving class semantics.
- Utilizes ==selective crop relevance weighting== to choose only the top-k most informative localized image crops, significantly reducing computational cost while enhancing local feature utilization.
- Integrates a ==confidence-adjusted weighting mechanism== during self-training to refine pseudo-labels, especially for visually similar classes, thereby mitigating ambiguity.

## Results

- FAIR improved average classification accuracy by **2.78%** across **13** fine-grained datasets compared to the state-of-the-art DPA, and by over **9%** compared to zero-shot CLIP and CuPL.
- The framework achieved a notable performance of **91.92%** accuracy on the EuroSAT dataset, significantly outperforming DPA's **79.94%**, demonstrating strong capability in distinguishing highly similar classes.
- FAIR exhibited computational efficiency, achieving competitive performance with only **45.1K** trainable parameters (for ViT-B/32 on EuroSAT) and faster convergence (**15** epochs), while also outperforming prior methods in transductive settings.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dynamically learning and adapting class representations through a Learned Alignment Score (LAS) is critical for capturing fine-grained distinctions, contributing a 7.04% performance boost.
> - Refining pseudo-labels with an adaptive confidence-adjusted weighting mechanism significantly enhances self-training robustness by 1.84%, particularly for ambiguous fine-grained classes.
> - Selectively weighting and utilizing only the most relevant localized image crops ([CLS]-based top-k selection) provides a computationally efficient way to leverage fine-grained visual cues.
> - Parameter-efficient fine-tuning of only layer normalization weights and Class Description Anchors enables competitive performance with a small number of trainable parameters.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research operates within the rapidly evolving landscape of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and their application to real-world computer vision challenges. Pretrained VLMs, such as OpenAI's CLIP (Contrastive Language-Image Pre-training), have revolutionized zero-shot learning by effectively aligning image and text representations through massive-scale contrastive pretraining. This capability allows CLIP to perform diverse tasks like image classification, object detection, and semantic segmentation without requiring task-specific labeled data.

However, a significant hurdle remains in adapting these powerful models to **fine-grained classification** tasks, where subtle visual distinctions differentiate categories (e.g., distinguishing between different car models or species of birds). CLIP's reliance on global image features and generic text prompts (e"a photo of a {CLASS}") often falls short in capturing these nuanced cues, leading to degraded performance. Furthermore, acquiring labeled data for such specialized fine-grained domains is often costly or infeasible, motivating the need for **unsupervised adaptation (UA)** strategies.

Existing unsupervised adaptation methods for CLIP typically fall into two categories:
1. **Fixed Alignment Scores:** Methods like LaFTer, DPA, and ReCLIP often rely on either frozen pretrained classifiers or handcrafted prompt ensembles. While LLMs can generate richer class descriptions, the alignment mechanism itself remains static throughout adaptation. This limits their ability to capture evolving, subtle class distinctions, especially when pseudo-labels are generated using these fixed scores.
2. **Computationally Expensive Pseudo-labeling:** Approaches like WCA improve zero-shot fine-grained classification by considering localized image features and fine-grained text descriptions. However, applying such methods directly for pseudo-labeling in unsupervised adaptation can be prohibitively computationally expensive due to the need to generate and process numerous image crops for each unlabeled image.

FAIR positions itself as a crucial advancement by addressing these limitations. It seeks to develop an **adaptive and computationally efficient** approach to unsupervised adaptation for fine-grained classification. By introducing a "Learned Alignment Score" and dynamically refining the interaction between localized image features and class descriptions, FAIR aims to overcome the rigidity of fixed classifiers and the computational burden of naive crop-based methods. This work contributes to making powerful VLMs like CLIP more robust and adaptable for real-world applications where fine-grained distinctions matter, and labeled data is scarce, thereby pushing the state-of-the-art in unsupervised domain adaptation for VLMs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind FAIR stems from the recognition that while VLMs like CLIP are powerful, their application to fine-grained classification in unsupervised settings is hindered by specific limitations of existing methods. The paper identifies two primary shortcomings:

1. **Inflexibility of Existing Alignment Scores:** Current unsupervised adaptation (UA) approaches for fine-grained classification often rely on fixed alignment scores. These scores, derived from either a frozen pretrained classifier or a static ensemble of prompts, cannot dynamically adapt to the evolving, subtle class distinctions inherent in fine-grained tasks. This leads to inaccurate pseudo-labeling and limits the model's ability to learn nuanced visual cues from unlabeled data. The paper highlights this problem with examples like LaFTer, where a frozen classifier for pseudo-labeling struggles with visually similar classes (Fig. 4 and 5).
2. **Computational Inefficiency of Localized Feature Methods:** While methods like WCA demonstrate the benefit of localized image features for fine-grained classification in a zero-shot context, directly applying them for pseudo-labeling during unsupervised adaptation is computationally prohibitive. Generating and processing a large number of image crops (e.g., 60 crops in WCA) for every unlabeled image significantly increases training time and GPU memory requirements (Fig. 2), hindering scalability.

To overcome these challenges, the authors set forth the following key objectives for FAIR:

- **Develop a Learned Alignment Score (LAS):** The primary objective is to introduce an adaptive mechanism that allows for dynamic cross-modal interactions. This LAS should enable a more accurate capture of fine-grained class distinctions by aligning localized image features with adaptive language embeddings.
- **Enhance Pseudo-labeling Accuracy:** By making the alignment score learnable and incorporating fine-grained visual cues, the framework aims to generate more class-discriminative and reliable pseudo-labels for self-training.
- **Improve Computational Efficiency:** The approach seeks to leverage localized image features effectively without incurring excessive computational costs. This involves a selective mechanism for choosing the most relevant image crops.
- **Mitigate Inter-class Ambiguities:** In fine-grained domains, visually similar classes can lead to ambiguous pseudo-labels. A specific mechanism is needed to refine these uncertain labels during self-training, making the adaptation more robust.
- **Achieve Substantial Performance Gains:** Ultimately, the framework aims to significantly outperform existing state-of-the-art zero-shot and unsupervised adaptation methods across a diverse range of fine-grained datasets.

By achieving these objectives, FAIR aims to make CLIP a more versatile and practical tool for real-world fine-grained classification tasks in data-scarce, unsupervised environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

FAIR (Fine-grained Alignment and Interaction Refinement) is a novel unsupervised adaptation framework for CLIP designed to enhance fine-grained classification by dynamically aligning localized image features with descriptive language embeddings. The methodology builds upon insights from existing alignment score functions (CLIP, CuPL, WCA) but introduces critical innovations to improve efficiency and adaptiveness.

**Overall Framework (Illustrated in Fig. 3):**
FAIR operates as a self-training pipeline. Given unlabeled images from a target domain and a predefined set of class names, it adapts a pretrained CLIP model (visual encoder $E_v$ and textual encoder $E_t$) to excel in the target task. The framework involves generating pseudo-labels for weakly augmented images, which then guide the self-supervision of strongly augmented counterparts.

**Key Components and Innovations:**

1. **Class Description Anchors (CDA) Initialization and Adaptiveness:**
 - **Initialization:** Unlike previous methods that use fixed text embeddings, FAIR initializes a set of Class Description Anchors ($Z^* \in R^{c \times d}$) for each class. These anchors are formed by averaging multiple descriptive text embeddings generated by a Large Language Model (LLM, e.g., GPT-3) in response to a query like "what does a {CLASS} look like?". The original text encoder $E_t$ is then discarded for subsequent training steps.
 - **Adaptiveness:** Crucially, these CDAs are **learnable parameters** and are updated concurrently with the image encoder during the self-training process. This allows the class representations to dynamically adapt and evolve, capturing more nuanced fine-grained semantic relationships between visual and textual modalities.

2. **Selective Crop Relevance Weighting:**
 - **Localized Crops:** For each weakly augmented image $\alpha(x)$, a set of $N$ random localized crops $p(x)$ are generated.
 - **`[CLS]`-based Weighting:** Instead of WCA's approach, FAIR refines the crop weighting. It computes relevance weights ($fw_i$) by comparing the `[CLS]` token feature of each local crop ($f_{i}^{[\text{CLS}]}$) to the `[CLS]` token feature of the global image view ($f^{[\text{CLS}]}$). The `[CLS]` token is known to capture comprehensive global semantic and structural details, making it more effective for similarity tasks.
 - **Top-k Crop Selection:** A crucial efficiency improvement is the selection of only the **top-k most relevant crops** (where $k < N$) based on these calculated weights. This significantly reduces computational overhead by focusing on the most informative visual regions, rather than processing all randomly sampled crops. The weighting mechanism also avoids softmax normalization, which can over-emphasize high weights and is less effective for top-k selection (Fig. 7).

3. **Learned Alignment Score (LAS) via Fine-grained Interaction:**
 - The core of FAIR is the Learned Alignment Score ($\psi_{\text{FAIR}}$), which facilitates fine-grained interactions between the selected top-k localized image features ($f_i = E_v(p_i)$) and the adaptive CDAs ($Z^*$).
 - The alignment score is computed by summing the similarities between each of the top-k crop features and the respective CDA for a given class, weighted by their relevance ($fw_i$). This allows the model to leverage local visual cues for classification against a dynamically evolving textual class representation.

4. **Self-Training with Pseudo-Label Refinement:**
 - **Pseudo-Label Generation:** For each unlabeled image $x$, the LAS (Eq. 14) is used to predict a pseudo-label $\hat{y}$ by taking the class with the maximum alignment score.
 - **Self-Supervision Loss:** A standard cross-entropy loss ($L_{st}$) is applied between the pseudo-label $\hat{y}$ and the probabilistic output ($p_{A(x)}$) of the strongly-augmented counterpart $A(x)$, obtained by comparing its global visual feature with the CDAs.
 - **Adaptive Weighting Mechanism:** To mitigate noise and ambiguity in pseudo-labels, especially for visually similar classes, FAIR introduces a confidence-adjusted weighting mechanism ($\gamma_x$). This weight is calculated as $S_{i,x} \cdot |S_{i,x} - S_{j,x}|$, where $S_{i,x}$ and $S_{j,x}$ are the highest and second-highest alignment scores from LAS. This penalizes pseudo-labels where the top two predictions are very close, indicating uncertainty, and effectively down-weights their contribution to the loss. This improves robustness to fine-grained distinctions (Fig. 8).
 - **Fairness Regularization:** A fairness regularization loss ($L_{reg}$) is also incorporated to encourage uniform predictions across classes and reduce overfitting to biased pseudo-labels.
 - **Overall Loss:** The total loss is $L = L_{st} + L_{reg}$.

5. **Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning:**
 - FAIR fine-tunes only the **layer normalization weights** of the CLIP image encoder and the Class Description Anchors ($Z^*$). This strategy is known to be stable and effective for adapting models with noisy supervision, while also being computationally efficient by minimizing the number of trainable parameters (Fig. 9).

By integrating these components, FAIR creates a robust, adaptive, and efficient framework for unsupervised fine-grained classification, capable of learning nuanced class distinctions without relying on human-labeled data.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

FAIR demonstrates significant advancements in unsupervised adaptation (UA) for fine-grained classification, outperforming both zero-shot and existing UA methods across a wide range of datasets. The key findings and results are:

1. **Superior Overall Performance:**
 - FAIR achieves a **substantial average gain of 2.78%** across 13 diverse fine-grained datasets compared to the previous state-of-the-art (SOTA) method, DPA (Table 1).
 - It consistently outperforms other UA methods, including POUF, LaFTer, and ReCLIP, with average gains of 9.54%, 9.82%, and 5.67% respectively.
 - Compared to zero-shot methods, FAIR delivers even larger improvements, surpassing CLIP, CuPL, and WCA by average margins of 11.45%, 9.89%, and 10.96% respectively.
 - Notably, on datasets like EuroSAT, FAIR achieves particularly striking improvements (e.g., 91.92% vs. DPA's 79.94%), indicating its strong capability in distinguishing highly similar classes (Fig. 1).
 - These performance benefits are maintained even when using a stronger backbone like ViT-B/16 (Table 2) and with a different VLM (MetaCLIP, Table 8).

2. **Effectiveness of Key Components (Ablation Studies):**
 - The **Learned Alignment Score (LAS)** is identified as the most critical component, with its removal leading to a substantial performance drop of 7.04% (Table 3, FAIR w/o LAS). This confirms the importance of dynamic, adaptive class representations and fine-grained cross-modal interaction.
 - The **adaptive weighting mechanism** for pseudo-labels also plays a significant role, contributing a 1.84% performance boost (Table 3, FAIR w/o PL weight vs. FAIR). Further ablation on this component (Table 5) shows that both the top-1 score ($S_{i,x}$) and the difference between top-1 and top-2 scores ($|S_{i,x} - S_{j,x}|$) are essential for refining pseudo-labels and mitigating ambiguity.
 - Using **[CLS]-based crop weighting** and **top-k crop selection** effectively refines the processing of localized features, leading to incremental but consistent improvements over a naive WCA-based pseudo-labeling approach (Table 3).

3. **Efficiency and Computational Advantages:**
 - FAIR significantly improves efficiency compared to methods that extensively use localized crops for pseudo-labeling, such as WCA (Fig. 2). By selectively choosing only top-k relevant crops, it reduces computational cost while enhancing accuracy.
 - The parameter-efficient fine-tuning strategy, focusing on layer normalization weights and CDAs, allows FAIR to achieve competitive performance with a relatively small number of trainable parameters (45.1K for ViT-B/32 on EuroSAT), comparable to DPA and significantly less than LaFTer (Table 9). This contributes to faster convergence (15 epochs vs. 50 for ReCLIP).

4. **Robustness in Transductive Settings:**
 - FAIR maintains its superior performance even in a transductive setting (where the model adapts to and is evaluated on the unlabeled test split). It achieves an average accuracy of 75.57%, outperforming SOTA transductive methods like DPA (72.86%) and ReCLIP (69.27%) (Table 4). This demonstrates the framework's strong adaptability and generalization capabilities across different evaluation paradigms.

5. **Discriminative Feature Learning:**
 - Visualizations (t-SNE plots in Fig. 4) illustrate that FAIR, by leveraging LAS, effectively separates class clusters and enhances discriminability of image embeddings and classifiers, contrasting sharply with the class confusion observed in methods relying on fixed alignment-based pseudo-labels (e.g., LaFTer). Corresponding confusion matrices (Fig. 5) further highlight reduced misclassifications with FAIR's learned alignment.

In summary, the results unequivocally establish FAIR as a new state-of-the-art in unsupervised adaptation for fine-grained classification with CLIP, showcasing its ability to learn more robust and discriminative features through dynamic cross-modal alignment and efficient pseudo-label refinement.

### 5. Significance, Potential Impact, and Limitations

FAIR makes a significant contribution to the field of vision-language models and their practical applications, particularly in scenarios characterized by fine-grained distinctions and a scarcity of labeled data. Its primary significance lies in:

1. **Advancing Unsupervised Adaptation for Fine-grained Tasks:** FAIR pushes the state-of-the-art in unsupervised adaptation (UA) for fine-grained classification. By demonstrating substantial performance improvements over existing methods across diverse datasets, it addresses a crucial bottleneck in deploying powerful VLMs like CLIP in specialized domains where labeling data is costly or impractical.
2. **Enhancing CLIP's Versatility:** The framework increases CLIP's adaptability and robustness, enabling it to excel in tasks that were previously challenging due to its reliance on global features and generic prompts. This opens up new possibilities for applying CLIP in domains such as specialized scientific imaging, industrial quality control, environmental monitoring (e.g., satellite imagery), and medical diagnostics, where nuanced visual cues are paramount.
3. **Introducing Dynamic and Adaptive Alignment:** The concept of a Learned Alignment Score (LAS) and adaptive Class Description Anchors (CDA) is a powerful innovation. It moves beyond static alignment mechanisms, allowing the model to dynamically learn and refine cross-modal relationships during training. This adaptive nature is key to capturing the subtle, evolving class distinctions inherent in fine-grained tasks.
4. **Improving Computational Efficiency:** By introducing selective crop relevance weighting and focusing on parameter-efficient fine-tuning, FAIR offers a practical solution to the computational challenges often associated with localized feature analysis. This makes advanced fine-grained adaptation more accessible and scalable for researchers and practitioners.
5. **Robust Pseudo-Labeling:** The novel self-training weighting mechanism is crucial for refining pseudo-labels, especially in the presence of inter-class ambiguities. This enhances the reliability of the self-training process, a core component of unsupervised adaptation, and improves the model's ability to learn from noisy signals.

The potential impact of FAIR extends to broader computer vision applications. As foundation models become more prevalent, efficient and effective unsupervised adaptation methods are essential for their widespread adoption. FAIR provides a blueprint for how to unlock the full potential of VLMs for specialized tasks, reducing the dependency on extensive human annotation and accelerating the development of AI solutions in data-limited environments.

**Limitations and Future Work:**

Despite its notable achievements, FAIR, like any research, presents certain limitations that also illuminate avenues for future research:

1. **Challenges with Extreme Fine-grained Similarities:** The paper explicitly acknowledges a performance degradation on highly challenging datasets like StanfordCars. This dataset features extreme intra-class variations (e.g., different model years of the same car) and high inter-class similarities (e.g., visually analogous cars from different manufacturers). In such cases, the reliance on pseudo-labeling without human supervision can lead to unreliable distinctions, as shown by overlapping embeddings in t-SNE plots (Fig. 12) and confusion in classifications (Fig. 8a).
2. **Specificity of LLM-Generated Descriptions:** The current LLM-generated descriptions (derived from CuPL) may lack the necessary specificity to differentiate between extremely similar subclasses. As illustrated in Fig. 11c and 11d, generic descriptions might not capture the minute design elements that differentiate two very similar car models, exacerbating intra-class ambiguity.
3. **Domain-Specific Biases:** Pretrained models, optimized for common object categories, may carry domain-specific biases that hinder their ability to capture the nuanced distinctions required in highly specialized fine-grained recognition tasks.

To address these limitations, the authors propose several directions for future work:

- **Generating Denser, Finer-grained Descriptions:** Future efforts could focus on developing advanced LLM prompting strategies or specialized text generation models that can produce more detailed, class-specific descriptions. These descriptions would need to highlight subtle structural components, stylistic cues, or functional differences that are critical for fine-grained disambiguation.
- **Improving Pseudo-label Generation for Ambiguous Cases:** Research could explore more sophisticated pseudo-labeling mechanisms specifically designed to handle extreme visual similarities and ambiguities. This might involve uncertainty quantification, hierarchical classification, or multi-view consistency beyond simple top-1/top-2 score differences.
- **Domain-Adaptive Fine-Tuning:** Investigating advanced domain-adaptive fine-tuning techniques could help mitigate domain-specific biases in pretrained models, allowing them to better focus on relevant details within a target domain. This could involve architectural modifications or novel training objectives that encourage disentanglement of highly correlated visual features.
- **Incorporating Attention Mechanisms:** Future work could explore integrating explicit attention mechanisms within the visual encoder to guide the model's focus more precisely on distinguishing local details, further enhancing the fine-grained interaction with text descriptions.

By acknowledging these limitations, the authors provide a clear roadmap for future research, ensuring continued progress in making VLMs more robust and effective for complex, real-world fine-grained recognition problems.
