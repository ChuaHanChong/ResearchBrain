# Towards Physically Executable 3D Gaussian for Embodied Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> Zhejiang University and Manycore Tech Inc developed SAGE-3D, a framework that transforms 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) into a physically executable, semantically and physically aligned environment for embodied navigation. This work introduces the InteriorGS dataset and SAGE-Bench benchmark, enabling training and evaluation of embodied AI agents in high-fidelity, interactive simulations, and leading to more generalizable navigation policies.

## Problem

- Traditional 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) is a perceptual representation lacking object-level semantics, making it unsuitable for language-grounded navigation tasks.
- 3DGS inherently lacks explicit surface information or collision geometries, preventing realistic physical interaction and simulation for embodied agents.
- Existing embodied navigation benchmarks often rely on less photorealistic mesh-based representations or simplified 'A-to-B' tasks, limiting the complexity and realism of embodied AI training and evaluation.

## Method

- Proposes the SAGE-3D paradigm to augment 3DGS with semantic and physics layers, creating executable environments for embodied navigation, and releases InteriorGS, a large-scale dataset of 1,000 high-fidelity indoor 3DGS scenes with object-level annotations.
- Implements a 3DGS-Mesh hybrid representation, leveraging original meshes for precise collision bodies for physics simulation, while 3DGS provides photorealistic rendering.
- Develops SAGE-Bench, a 3DGS-based VLN benchmark, with hierarchical instructions and new metrics (Continuous Success Ratio, Integrated Collision Penalty, Path Smoothness) for nuanced evaluation.

## Results

- State-of-the-art VLN models achieved significantly lower success rates on SAGE-Bench (e.g., NaVILA with 0.39 SR vs. 0.54 SR on VLN-CE R2R Val-Unseen) and struggled with high-level instructions (SR dropped from 0.56 to 0.39).
- Models trained on SAGE-Bench's 3DGS data demonstrated superior generalization, achieving up to a 34% relative increase in Oracle Success Rate on unseen R2R Val-Unseen tasks.
- 3DGS scene data offered faster rendering (6.2 ms/frame) and lower memory usage (220 MB) than scanned mesh data, despite requiring more training iterations to converge for a given success rate.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - SAGE-Bench offers a significantly more challenging Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) task than previous benchmarks, pushing the capabilities of current state-of-the-art models.
> - 3DGS scene data, while requiring more training iterations for convergence, yields models with strong generalizability to unseen environments.
> - The introduced 'natural continuity metrics' provide a more comprehensive assessment of navigation performance, revealing limitations of traditional binary success metrics in capturing real-world feasibility.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in SAGE-3D is situated at the intersection of computer vision, embodied artificial intelligence (AI), and robotics, specifically focusing on **Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN)**. VLN is a crucial capability for embodied AI agents, enabling them to interpret natural language instructions and navigate complex real-world or simulated environments.

Historically, VLN research has heavily relied on simulated environments to overcome the high costs and risks associated with real-world training. This "sim-to-real" paradigm has driven the evolution of scene representations. Early benchmarks like **Matterport3D** (Chang et al., 2017) and **HM3D** (Ramakrishnan et al., 2021) utilized scanned mesh reconstructions from RGB-D data. While these provided detailed 3D geometry, they often suffered from:
1. **Imperfect Geometry and Texture:** Scans are prone to noise, leading to incomplete or inaccurate meshes and textures that can break under novel viewpoints, creating visual artifacts (seams, stretching, blur).
2. **Lack of Fine-grained Semantics:** Objects often merge into continuous surfaces, making it challenging to extract distinct object instances or apply semantic labels without complex post-processing.
3. **Limited Physical Executability:** Deriving reliable collision geometries from noisy, merged meshes is difficult, hindering realistic physical interaction and navigation.

The advent of **3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS)** (Kerbl et al., 2023) has been a significant development in 3D scene representation. 3DGS offers two key advantages over traditional mesh-based representations:
1. **Photorealistic Real-time Rendering:** It can render highly photorealistic views from arbitrary viewpoints in real-time, crucial for visual consistency in navigation.
2. **Easier Reconstruction:** It can be reconstructed from sparse RGB images, bypassing the need for depth sensors and simplifying the data acquisition process.

Despite these benefits, traditional 3DGS, as highlighted in this paper, is primarily a perceptual representation focused on rendering. It suffers from two major limitations for embodied navigation:
1. **Deficiency in Fine-grained Object-level Semantics:** 3DGS primitives (Gaussians) inherently lack object instance IDs or semantic attributes, making it impossible to ground language instructions that refer to specific objects (e.g., "go to the red chair").
2. **Lack of Physically Executable Structure:** Being a volumetric rendering technique, 3DGS doesn't natively provide explicit surface information or collision geometries, making physical simulation (e.g., detecting collisions, interacting with objects) highly challenging.

SAGE-3D directly addresses these critical gaps. It aims to elevate 3DGS from a purely visual representation to an **executable, semantically and physically aligned environment foundation** for embodied AI. By integrating object-level semantics and robust physical executability into 3DGS, SAGE-3D pushes the boundaries of sim-to-real realism, allowing VLN agents to operate in environments that are both visually indistinguishable from the real world and physically interactive, thereby significantly narrowing the sim-to-real gap for future embodied intelligence research. This work positions itself as a critical enabler for more complex, interactive, and human-centric navigation tasks that go beyond simple point-to-point movements.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of SAGE-3D is to transform 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) from a purely perceptual scene representation, primarily used for photorealistic rendering, into a **fully executable, semantically and physically aligned environment foundation** suitable for training and evaluating embodied AI agents in tasks like Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN).

The motivation stems directly from the limitations of existing scene representations and the specific shortcomings of traditional 3DGS for embodied AI:

1. **Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap:** Real-world training for embodied agents is expensive, time-consuming, and risky. High-fidelity simulations are essential to bridge this gap. While 3DGS offers unparalleled photorealism and view consistency compared to scanned meshes, its lack of semantic and physical information prevents it from being a comprehensive simulation environment. The objective is to leverage 3DGS's visual fidelity while adding the necessary components for practical embodied learning.

2. **Addressing Lack of Fine-grained Semantics in 3DGS:** Traditional 3DGS scenes only encode color and density. This makes it impossible for VLN agents to understand and act upon instructions that refer to specific objects or their attributes (e.g., "find the red chair," "open the white bookshelf"). An agent cannot meaningfully ground language in the visual scene without object-level semantic annotations. The motivation is to provide these fine-grained semantics, enabling richer, more human-like instructions and understanding.

3. **Overcoming Lack of Physical Executability in 3DGS:** 3DGS is a volumetric technique that doesn't inherently provide explicit surface geometry required for physics simulations. This leads to issues like object penetration and unrealistic interactions, which are detrimental to embodied learning. The goal is to embed robust collision objects and physical interfaces, allowing agents to navigate and interact with the environment in a physically realistic manner, detecting collisions, and understanding traversable spaces.

4. **Enabling Complex VLN Tasks:** Current VLN benchmarks often rely on simplified "A-to-B" navigation tasks. The lack of detailed semantics and physics in existing representations limits the complexity of instructions and interactions. SAGE-3D aims to enable more complex, multi-step, and causal instructions (e.g., "I'm thirsty, get water from the table") by providing an environment where such tasks can be unambiguously grounded and physically executed.

In essence, SAGE-3D seeks to create a **unified, high-fidelity, and interactive simulation platform** by augmenting the visual prowess of 3DGS with essential semantic understanding and physical simulation capabilities, thereby opening new avenues for advanced embodied intelligence research.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SAGE-3D proposes a new paradigm built on two core components: (1) Object-Level Semantic Grounding and (2) Physics-Aware Execution Jointing. This is complemented by the release of a new dataset, InteriorGS, and a benchmark, SAGE-Bench.

#### 3.1. SAGE-3D Paradigm Definition

The authors formally define SAGE-3D as the transformation of a Gaussian primitive set `G` from a 3DGS scene, augmented with a semantic layer `M` and a physics layer `Φ`, into an executable environment `E_exec`. This environment is framed as a semantics- and physics-augmented Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP), supporting continuous Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) and related tasks.

#### 3.2. Object-Level Semantic Grounding

To address the lack of semantics in traditional 3DGS, the authors introduce:

- **InteriorGS Dataset:** This is a large-scale dataset of 1,000 high-fidelity indoor 3DGS scenes. These scenes are sampled from artist-created mesh models, ensuring high quality and detail. Crucially, each scene undergoes **manual object-level annotation and double verification**, providing:
 - Object categories (e.g., "chair," "table")
 - Instance IDs (unique identifier for each object)
 - Bounding box information
 - Over 554k object instances across 755 categories are included, encompassing both residential interiors and public spaces.
- **3DGS Data Sampling:** To reconstruct reliable 3DGS models in occlusion-rich indoor environments, an average of 3,000 camera views per scene are rendered using a ray tracing renderer. These views and their poses are then used with the open-source GSplat pipeline to estimate 3DGS parameters.
- **2D Semantic Top-Down Map Generation:** Since 3DGS lacks inherent discrete entities like NavMeshes, the authors design a 2D semantic top-down map. This is generated by projecting annotated 3D objects from InteriorGS onto the ground plane. Object footprints are refined into irregular masks by sampling surface points and taking a 2D convex hull. This map is vital for instruction generation and path planning.

#### 3.3. Physics-Aware Execution Jointing

To enable physical interaction and prevent issues like mesh penetration, the authors implement:

- **3DGS-Mesh Hybrid Representation:** This is a key innovation. Instead of trying to infer physics directly from Gaussians, the approach leverages the **artist-created triangle meshes** from which the 3DGS data was sampled.
 - **Collision Body Extraction:** For each object, convex decomposition (using CoACD) is applied to its original mesh to extract accurate per-object collision bodies.
 - **Integration with 3DGS:** In the simulation environment (e.g., Isaac Sim), a USDA scene is authored where these extracted collision bodies act as invisible rigid shapes, driving contact and dynamics. Simultaneously, the 3DGS file remains visible, providing photorealistic appearance. This **decoupled design** ensures both high-fidelity rendering (from 3DGS) and accurate physical simulation (from mesh-based collision bodies).
 - Static-scene objects are configured as static bodies, while a curated subset is movable or articulated to support interactions.
- **Agents, Control, and Observations:** The simulator exposes rich robotics APIs for various platforms (e.g., legged, wheeled, aerial robots), supporting both discrete and continuous control commands (e.g., velocity commands). The environment provides synchronized multimodal observations (RGB, depth, semantic segmentation, poses, contact events) and built-in collision detection and recovery mechanisms.

#### 3.4. SAGE-Bench: A 3DGS-Based VLN Benchmark

Building on InteriorGS and the SAGE-3D paradigm, the authors introduce SAGE-Bench, the first fully 3DGS-based VLN benchmark:

- **Hierarchical Instruction Generation:** To overcome the limitations of "A-to-B" navigation, SAGE-Bench employs a hierarchical instruction scheme combining:
 - **High-level instructions:** Emphasize task semantics and human-oriented intent, categorized into: Add Object, Scenario Driven, Relative Relationship, Attribute-based, and Area-based.
 - **Low-level instructions:** Focus on control and kinematic evaluation, including primitive actions and point-to-point navigation (e.g., Room-to-Room, Object-to-Object).
 - Instructions are generated using an MLLM with prompts based on object categories, attributes, and spatial relations derived from 2D semantic maps.
- **Trajectory Generation:** A*-based shortest-path search is used on a navigation map (combining occupancy and 2D semantic maps) to generate 2 million instruction-trajectory pairs.
- **Three-Axis Evaluation Framework:** This framework provides discrete evaluation slices based on:
 - **Task Types:** VLN and No-goal Navigation (exploration).
 - **Instruction Level:** High-level vs. Low-level.
 - **Episode Complexity:** Scene complexity (asset density) and Path complexity (path length).
- **Navigation Natural Continuity Metrics:** To address limitations of conventional metrics (like Success Rate (SR) which is binary and only at endpoint), three new metrics are introduced:
 - **Continuous Success Ratio (CSR):** Proportion of time the agent stays within a permissible corridor around the reference path while satisfying task conditions.
 - **Integrated Collision Penalty (ICP):** Time-averaged collision intensity, differentiating between occasional contact and persistent scraping.
 - **Path Smoothness (PS):** Normalized smoothness score based on consecutive heading changes, promoting natural, feasible robot motion.

In summary, the methodology systematically enhances 3DGS with essential AI and robotics capabilities through careful data creation, hybrid representation design, and a comprehensive benchmarking framework, moving beyond purely visual rendering to physically executable, semantically rich environments.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on SAGE-Bench and comparative analyses yielded several key insights:

1. **Challenging Nature of SAGE-Bench:** SAGE-Bench presents a significantly more difficult VLN task than existing benchmarks. Even state-of-the-art VLN models like NaVILA, which achieves 0.54 SR on VLN-CE R2R Val-Unseen, only reached 0.39 SR and 0.47 OSR on SAGE-Bench. Other models performed even lower (SR values typically no higher than 0.15). This indicates that the enriched semantics, detailed physics, and complex instructions of SAGE-Bench effectively push the boundaries of current VLN model capabilities.

2. **3DGS Scene Data: Faster Rendering but Slower Convergence:**
 - **Rendering Speed:** 3DGS scene data demonstrated superior rendering performance, achieving a per-frame rendering time of 6.2 ms and an average memory usage of 220 MB, significantly outperforming scanned mesh data (16.7 ms and 850 MB).
 - **Training Convergence:** Despite faster rendering, models trained on 3DGS-based data required more iterations to converge to the same success rate (e.g., 160 iterations / 6.2 hours for 40% SR) compared to scanned mesh data (120 iterations / 4.8 hours). The authors attribute this slower convergence to the higher demands of 3DGS data, as its richness and photorealism better mirror real-world complexity, making the learning task more challenging.

3. **Strong Generalizability of 3DGS Scene Data:** Models trained exclusively on SAGE-Bench's 3DGS data showed significant generalizability when evaluated on the unseen environments of the VLN-CE benchmark.
 - NaVILA-SAGE, trained only on SAGE-Bench, achieved a 31% relative increase in Success Rate (from 0.29 to 0.38) and a 34% relative increase in Oracle Success Rate (from 0.38 to 0.51) on R2R Val-Unseen tasks compared to its NaVILA-base counterpart (trained on VLN-CE). Similar improvements were observed for the NaVid model. This finding strongly supports the data's alignment with real-world scenarios and its effectiveness in promoting generalizable navigation policies.

4. **Effectiveness of New Navigation Natural Continuity Metrics:**
 - The newly proposed metrics—Continuous Success Ratio (CSR), Integrated Collision Penalty (ICP), and Path Smoothness (PS)—provided deeper insights into navigation performance beyond conventional metrics like Success Rate (SR) and Collision Rate (CR).
 - CSR was generally higher than SR, indicating a more inclusive measure that allows for deviations from the exact ground-truth path while still satisfying task conditions.
 - For ICP and PS, NaVILA, despite achieving relatively high task completion (0.39 SR), showed poor natural motion continuity with a high ICP of 0.61 (indicating sustained collisions) and a PS of 0.68 (reflecting unsmooth, mechanical turns). Visual examples corroborated this, showing models "hugging walls" for long periods, where traditional CR might only register "1" but ICP captures the persistent nature of the collision (e.g., 0.87). This highlights how traditional metrics can fail to capture critical real-world feasibility issues.

5. **High-Level vs. Low-Level Instructions:** VLN models performed significantly worse when executing high-level, semantically rich instructions compared to low-level, basic action-oriented instructions. For NaVILA, the SR dropped from 0.56 on low-level instructions to 0.39 on high-level instructions. This suggests that understanding and executing complex, human-centric goals remains a major challenge for current VLN models, underscoring the value of SAGE-Bench's hierarchical instruction design.

6. **Impact of Scene Diversity vs. Sample Size:** Experiments showed that increasing the **number of scenes** in the training data had a greater impact on performance gains than merely increasing the **number of samples** from a fixed set of scenes. This indicates that **environmental diversity is more critical** for learning robust and generalizable VLN policies.

7. **Performance under Different Evaluation Slices:** VLN models exhibited poorer performance on "Relative Relationship" and "Attribute-based" instruction types, as well as in scenarios with increased trajectory length and scene complexity. This confirms the challenges posed by fine-grained semantic grounding and navigating complex environments.

In summary, the findings validate SAGE-3D's contributions: it provides a challenging, high-fidelity environment that drives model improvements, offers a more nuanced evaluation of continuous navigation, and highlights critical areas for future research in embodied AI.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The SAGE-3D paradigm and its associated datasets (InteriorGS) and benchmark (SAGE-Bench) represent a significant contribution to the fields of computer vision, robotics, and embodied artificial intelligence. Its potential impact is multi-faceted:

1. **Advancing Sim-to-Real Transfer:** SAGE-3D directly addresses the long-standing challenge of the sim-to-real gap by providing an environment that combines the photorealistic rendering capabilities of 3DGS with accurate physical executability and fine-grained semantics. This makes simulated training more closely mirror real-world complexities, leading to policies that are more robust and readily transferable to physical robots.

2. **Enabling Richer Embodied AI Research:** By endowing 3DGS environments with object-level semantics and physical interaction capabilities, SAGE-3D opens the door for a new generation of embodied AI tasks. Researchers can now explore:
 - **Complex Multi-Step and Causal Navigation:** Agents can follow instructions like "I'm thirsty, get water from the table," requiring semantic understanding, planning, and interaction.
 - **Interactive Manipulation:** The hybrid 3DGS-Mesh representation with accurate collision bodies facilitates training for tasks involving object pushing, grasping, or re-arrangement, moving beyond passive navigation.
 - **Human-Robot Collaboration:** The detailed semantic grounding can improve natural language understanding for robots in shared environments.

3. **New Standard for VLN Benchmarking:** SAGE-Bench, as the first 3DGS-based VLN benchmark, sets a new standard for evaluating embodied agents.
 - **Hierarchical Instructions:** The inclusion of high-level, human-centric instructions alongside low-level commands pushes models to develop deeper semantic understanding and reasoning.
 - **Natural Continuity Metrics:** The novel metrics (CSR, ICP, PS) provide a more comprehensive and realistic assessment of navigation quality, moving beyond binary success to evaluate smoothness, collision avoidance, and sustained goal-consistent behavior—aspects crucial for real-world robot deployment. These metrics can become a standard for evaluating continuous control in embodied tasks.

4. **Community Resource and Foundation:** The release of InteriorGS, a large-scale dataset of 1K object-annotated 3DGS indoor scenes, is a valuable public resource. This dataset will likely become a fundamental building block for future research, enabling researchers worldwide to train and test their models in high-fidelity, semantically rich, and physically valid environments without the prohibitive cost of real-world data collection.

5. **Catalyst for 3DGS Application Beyond Rendering:** SAGE-3D demonstrates a powerful way to augment 3DGS for functional applications beyond its initial purpose of novel view synthesis. It showcases how 3DGS can be integrated into a broader AI and robotics pipeline, inspiring further innovation in combining visual fidelity with actionable intelligence.

In conclusion, SAGE-3D is not merely an incremental improvement; it proposes a foundational shift in how embodied AI environments are constructed and utilized. By providing a coherent pipeline from high-fidelity data generation to physically valid evaluation, it promises to accelerate progress in creating more intelligent, robust, and capable embodied agents that can truly understand and interact with the world.
