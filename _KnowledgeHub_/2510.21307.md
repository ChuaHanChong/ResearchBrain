# Towards Physically Executable 3D Gaussian for Embodied Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> Zhejiang University and Manycore Tech Inc developed SAGE-3D, a framework that transforms 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) into a physically executable, semantically and physically aligned environment for embodied navigation. This work introduces the InteriorGS dataset and SAGE-Bench benchmark, enabling training and evaluation of embodied AI agents in high-fidelity, interactive simulations, and leading to more generalizable navigation policies.

## Problem

- Traditional 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) is a perceptual representation lacking object-level semantics, making it unsuitable for language-grounded navigation tasks.
- 3DGS inherently lacks explicit surface information or collision geometries, preventing realistic physical interaction and simulation for embodied agents.
- Existing embodied navigation benchmarks often rely on less photorealistic mesh-based representations or simplified 'A-to-B' tasks, limiting the complexity and realism of embodied AI training and evaluation.

## Method

- Proposes the SAGE-3D paradigm to augment 3DGS with semantic and physics layers, creating executable environments for embodied navigation, and releases InteriorGS, a large-scale dataset of 1,000 high-fidelity indoor 3DGS scenes with object-level annotations.
- Implements a 3DGS-Mesh hybrid representation, leveraging original meshes for precise collision bodies for physics simulation, while 3DGS provides photorealistic rendering.
- Develops SAGE-Bench, a 3DGS-based VLN benchmark, with hierarchical instructions and new metrics (Continuous Success Ratio, Integrated Collision Penalty, Path Smoothness) for nuanced evaluation.

## Results

- State-of-the-art VLN models achieved significantly lower success rates on SAGE-Bench (e.g., NaVILA with 0.39 SR vs. 0.54 SR on VLN-CE R2R Val-Unseen) and struggled with high-level instructions (SR dropped from 0.56 to 0.39).
- Models trained on SAGE-Bench's 3DGS data demonstrated superior generalization, achieving up to a 34% relative increase in Oracle Success Rate on unseen R2R Val-Unseen tasks.
- 3DGS scene data offered faster rendering (6.2 ms/frame) and lower memory usage (220 MB) than scanned mesh data, despite requiring more training iterations to converge for a given success rate.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - SAGE-Bench offers a significantly more challenging Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) task than previous benchmarks, pushing the capabilities of current state-of-the-art models.
> - 3DGS scene data, while requiring more training iterations for convergence, yields models with strong generalizability to unseen environments.
> - The introduced 'natural continuity metrics' provide a more comprehensive assessment of navigation performance, revealing limitations of traditional binary success metrics in capturing real-world feasibility.
