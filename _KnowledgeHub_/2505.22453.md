# First SFT, Second RL, Third UPT: Continual Improving Multi-Modal LLM Reasoning via Unsupervised Post-Training

> [!summary] Summary
> MM-UPT establishes an Unsupervised Post-Training (UPT) paradigm for multi-modal large language models, allowing them to continually refine reasoning capabilities without relying on human-annotated data. The framework employs an online reinforcement learning approach with a majority-voting self-reward mechanism and enables the models to generate their own synthetic training data, leading to enhanced performance across diverse multi-modal benchmarks.

## Problem

- Multi-modal Large Language Models (MLLMs) heavily depend on extensive, costly human-annotated data for post-training stages like Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning (RL).
- This dependency creates an unsustainable bottleneck for the continuous improvement and scalability of MLLMs, particularly as real-world tasks grow in complexity and diversity.
- Prior unsupervised self-improvement methods for MLLMs often involve complex, multi-stage pipelines or yield only marginal gains, lacking a simple, online approach for continuous enhancement.

## Method

- MM-UPT formalizes ==Unsupervised Post-Training (UPT)== as a distinct third stage for MLLMs, designed to enable continuous self-improvement in the absence of external supervision.
- It adapts the GRPO algorithm for an unsupervised context by introducing a novel ==self-rewarding mechanism based on majority voting==. The MLLM samples multiple responses, extracts answers, identifies a pseudo-label from the majority vote, and calculates rewards based on agreement.
- The framework integrates strategies for ==autonomous synthetic data generation==, where the MLLM itself creates new questions from images (in-context or direct synthesizing), feeding these unlabeled samples back into the unsupervised reinforcement learning pipeline.

## Results

- MM-UPT consistently improved the base Qwen2.5-VL-7B model's performance across all multi-modal mathematical reasoning benchmarks (e.g., average score increased from **49.47%** to **53.17%** on MMR1).
- The approach significantly outperformed existing unsupervised self-improvement baselines and achieved performance competitive with strong supervised post-training methods, demonstrating its efficacy without ground-truth labels.
- Models trained on MLLM-generated synthetic data performed comparably to, and in some cases surpassed, those trained on original human-written questions, validating the effectiveness of autonomous data creation across Geometry3K and GeoQA datasets.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Majority voting effectively generates reliable pseudo-reward signals from an MLLM's moderately competent responses, allowing the model to bootstrap its own knowledge and improve answer consistency in an unsupervised manner.
> - MLLMs are capable of autonomously synthesizing high-quality, diverse training questions solely from visual input, creating a scalable and fully self-sufficient loop for data generation.
> - The effectiveness of unsupervised post-training depends on a foundational level of competence in the base MLLM; models with insufficient prior knowledge may amplify errors through majority voting.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Multi-modal Large Language Models (MLLMs) has seen rapid progress, enabling advanced capabilities in various vision-language tasks, from image captioning to complex visual reasoning. The dominant paradigm for improving these models post-initial training (e.g., pre-training) has historically relied on two main stages: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning (RL), often with human feedback (RLHF) or verifiable rewards (RLVR).

This paper argues that while SFT and RL have been highly effective, they share a critical dependency: the availability of vast amounts of high-quality, manually annotated multi-modal data. As real-world tasks become increasingly complex and diverse, the cost and effort associated with curating and annotating such data become unsustainable. This data scarcity represents a fundamental bottleneck for the continual improvement and scalability of MLLMs.

In response to this challenge, the broader research landscape has seen a growing interest in "self-improvement" or "unsupervised" paradigms for LLMs. Prior works have explored using LLMs to generate synthetic instruction data for offline training via SFT or DPO (e.g., Genixer, STIC, SENA). However, these methods often involve complex, multi-stage pipelines (data generation, verification, filtering) that are difficult to iterate and scale for online, continuous improvement. Concurrently, online reinforcement learning techniques like GRPO have shown promise in enhancing MLLM reasoning with verifiable rewards, and test-time RL has been applied to LLMs (TTRL).

MM-UPT critically positions itself as proposing and formalizing a **"third stage of post-training"**: Unsupervised Post-Training (UPT), designed to follow initial SFT and RL. It addresses the limitations of prior unsupervised methods by offering a *simple, online reinforcement learning-based approach* that directly tackles the data dependency problem for MLLMs, specifically focusing on multi-modal reasoning. By leveraging GRPO with a novel self-rewarding mechanism (majority voting), MM-UPT aims to provide a scalable and autonomous path for MLLMs to continually enhance their capabilities without any external human supervision or explicit reward models. This work contributes to shifting the paradigm towards true self-sufficiency for advanced AI models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation behind MM-UPT stems from the unsustainable reliance of current MLLM post-training methods (SFT, RL) on expensive and limited human-annotated multi-modal data. The authors highlight that the increasing complexity and quantity of real-world tasks necessitate a paradigm shift towards models that can continually improve themselves using unlabeled or synthetically generated data.

Based on this overarching motivation, the key objectives of the MM-UPT framework are:

1. **Formalize Unsupervised Post-Training (UPT):** To define and establish UPT as a distinct, critical third stage of MLLM post-training, enabling continual improvement in the absence of external supervision.
2. **Develop a Simple and Effective UPT Framework:** To propose MM-UPT, a practical and easy-to-implement framework for unsupervised post-training of MLLMs, specifically tailored for multi-modal reasoning tasks.
3. **Overcome Reward Calculation Challenges in Unsupervised Settings:** To adapt online reinforcement learning algorithms (like GRPO) to an unsupervised context by devising a robust self-rewarding mechanism that does not require ground-truth labels. This involves leveraging the MLLM's own responses to generate implicit training signals.
4. **Explore Scalability through Data Self-Generation:** To extend the unsupervised paradigm by enabling MLLMs to autonomously synthesize new, high-quality training samples, thereby creating a truly scalable and self-improving loop without human intervention.
5. **Demonstrate Effectiveness in Multi-modal Reasoning:** To empirically validate MM-UPT's ability to significantly enhance the multi-modal reasoning capabilities of state-of-the-art MLLMs across various benchmarks and scenarios.

In essence, the authors aim to pioneer a pathway for MLLMs to learn and refine their skills autonomously, making their development more scalable, cost-effective, and ultimately, more aligned with the vision of truly intelligent and self-improving AI systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The MM-UPT framework addresses the challenge of unsupervised MLLM improvement by adapting an online reinforcement learning (RL) approach, GRPO (Group-based Reinforcement Policy Optimization), to operate without ground-truth labels. This is achieved through a novel self-rewarding mechanism based on majority voting, complemented by strategies for MLLM-driven data self-generation.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The problem is defined as improving a pre-trained MLLM $\pi_\theta$ using a collection of unlabeled multi-modal data $Q = \{(I_i, q_i)\}_{i=1}^N$, where $I_i$ is an image and $q_i$ is a question. The crucial constraint is the absence of ground-truth answers or external supervision, requiring the model to generate its own training signals.

#### 3.2. Training Method: Self-Rewarding via Majority Voting with GRPO

MM-UPT builds upon the GRPO algorithm, known for its stability and computational efficiency by directly estimating advantages without a separate value model. The core innovation lies in how rewards are calculated in an unsupervised setting:

1. **Response Sampling:** For a given unlabeled multi-modal input $(I, q)$, the MLLM ($\pi_{\theta_{old}}$) samples a group of $G$ responses $O = \{o_i\}_{i=1}^G$.
2. **Answer Extraction:** An rule-based answer extractor $E(\cdot)$ is applied to each response to extract a concise answer, resulting in $\hat{Y} = \{\hat{y}_i\}_{i=1}^G$.
3. **Majority Voting for Pseudo-Label:** The most frequent answer among $\hat{Y}$ is identified as the majority-voted answer $y^* = \arg\max_{y \in \hat{Y}} \sum_{i=1}^G I[y = \hat{y}_i]$. This $y^*$ serves as a dynamic pseudo-label.
4. **Pseudo-Reward Calculation:** Rewards $r_i$ are assigned based on agreement with the pseudo-label: $r_i = 1$ if $\hat{y}_i = y^*$, and $r_i = 0$ otherwise.
5. **Advantage Estimation and Policy Update:** These pseudo-rewards are then used within the standard GRPO framework to calculate advantage estimates $\hat{A}_i = \frac{r_i - \text{mean}(\{r_1, \dots, r_G\})}{\text{std}(\{r_1, \dots, r_G\})}$. The policy model $\pi_\theta$ is updated by maximizing the GRPO objective, which includes a KL divergence constraint to prevent drastic deviations from a reference model $\pi_{ref}$. This process is summarized in Algorithm 1.

This majority-based reward mechanism encourages the model to converge towards consistent, high-consensus responses, effectively bootstrapping its own knowledge and improving answer stability.

#### 3.3. Synthetic Data Generation

To enhance scalability beyond existing unlabeled datasets, MM-UPT incorporates two strategies for the MLLM to synthesize new training samples:

1. **In-Context Synthesizing:** The MLLM is provided with an original example (image, question, and answer triplet) and prompted to generate a *new question* that is semantically distinct but relevant to the same image. This strategy aims for task-relevant variations.
2. **Direct Synthesizing:** The MLLM receives only the image and is prompted to *freely create a new question* without any textual context from original questions. This approach encourages greater diversity and novelty in generated questions, relying solely on visual input.

These self-generated samples, inherently lacking ground-truth labels, are then fed into the same unsupervised reinforcement learning pipeline, enabling a fully autonomous self-improvement loop.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

- **Base MLLMs:** Primarily Qwen2.5-VL-7B, with ablation studies on Qwen2.5-VL-3B, MM-Eureka-7B, and ThinkLite-VL-7B.
- **Training Data (Unlabeled):** Standard datasets with masked labels (Geometry3K, GeoQA, MMR1) and synthetic datasets generated by the MLLM itself.
- **Evaluation Benchmarks:** Four multi-modal mathematical reasoning benchmarks (MathVision, MathVerse, MathVista, We-Math). Also, non-mathematical VQA (ChartQA, IconQA) and pure-language math (MATH, Omni-MATH) for generalization.
- **Implementation Details:** Utilizes the EasyR1 framework based on GRPO, with specific hyperparameters for training episodes, optimizer, learning rate, weight decay, gradient clipping, KL divergence constraint, and rollout temperature. Answer correctness is evaluated using Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate the effectiveness and scalability of MM-UPT across various scenarios and models.

#### 4.1. Scenario 1: Unsupervised Training on Standard Datasets

- **Consistent Performance Gains:** MM-UPT consistently improves the performance of the base Qwen2.5-VL-7B model across all evaluated multi-modal mathematical reasoning benchmarks (MathVision, MathVerse, MathVista, We-Math). For instance, with the MMR1 dataset, the average score increased from 49.47% (base model) to 53.17%.
- **Outperforms Unsupervised Baselines:** MM-UPT significantly outperforms existing unsupervised self-improvement methods like SRLM, LMSI, Genixer, and STIC. These baselines often showed only marginal gains or even performance degradation on certain benchmarks when applied to strong MLLMs.
- **Competitive with Supervised Methods:** Remarkably, MM-UPT achieves performance competitive with strong *supervised* post-training methods such as rejection sampling-based SFT and supervised GRPO, underscoring its efficacy despite the lack of ground-truth labels.

#### 4.2. Scenario 2: Unsupervised Training on Synthetic Datasets

- **Effective Data Self-Generation:** Both "In-Context Synthesizing" and "Direct Synthesizing" strategies lead to notable performance improvements over the base model.
- **Comparable to Human-Written Questions:** Models trained on synthetic data achieve performance comparable to, and in some cases even surpass, those trained on original human-written questions (e.g., direct synthesizing on Geometry3K and GeoQA datasets). This highlights the MLLM's ability to generate high-quality textual questions solely from images, fostering scalable and fully autonomous self-improvement.
- **Diversity in Synthetic Questions:** Manual inspection reveals that in-context synthesizing tends to generate rephrased questions, while direct synthesizing produces more diverse and novel questions, even with some occasional hallucinations.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study: Generality Across Backbone Models

- **Robustness Across Scales:** MM-UPT consistently improves the performance of various MLLM backbones of different scales, including Qwen2.5-VL-3B, MM-Eureka-7B, and ThinkLite-VL-7B. For example, Qwen2.5-VL-3B saw a 7.4% average increase in accuracy.
- **Compatibility with Supervised GRPO:** MM-UPT proves effective even for models already optimized with supervised GRPO (e.g., MM-Eureka-7B and ThinkLite-VL-7B), providing further performance boosts. This demonstrates its value as a lightweight refinement step that can leverage new unlabeled data.

#### 4.4. Deeper Analysis

- **Why MM-UPT Works:** The effectiveness of majority voting stems from its ability to enhance reliability when individual predictions have a probability $p > 0.5$ of being correct (as shown in a simplified binary example). This allows MM-UPT to derive reliable pseudo-reward signals from the MLLM's own moderately competent responses.
- **When MM-UPT Might Fail:** The method's effectiveness diminishes when the base model lacks sufficient prior knowledge or performs poorly on a dataset (e.g., ThinkLite-11K, designed for difficult samples). In such scenarios, majority voting can amplify incorrect answers, leading to performance degradation. This suggests a requirement for a certain level of initial competence in the base MLLM.
- **Trade-Offs:** MM-UPT improves pass@1 accuracy but leads to a consistent decrease in pass@n (e.g., pass@10) performance, indicating a reduction in response diversity. This behavior is similar to supervised GRPO, where models converge to high-consensus patterns. However, MM-UPT shifts computational cost from inference (repeated sampling) to a one-time training phase, making it more efficient for deployment.
- **Generalization Beyond Mathematical Reasoning:** MM-UPT not only avoids performance degradation but yields improvements on non-mathematical visual question answering benchmarks (ChartQA, IconQA), suggesting that reinforcing consistent answers is a beneficial trait that transfers positively.
- **Adaptability to Pure Language Tasks:** The framework, when applied to a pure language model (Qwen2.5-MATH-7B) on language-based mathematical reasoning (MATH, Omni-MATH), also results in substantial performance gains, demonstrating its broader applicability.
- **Training Dynamics:** Monitoring training curves shows that majority voting reward consistently increases, semantic entropy decreases, and average benchmark accuracy along with effective rank improve over time, indicating stable convergence towards more confident and consistent predictions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The MM-UPT framework represents a significant step forward in the field of MLLM development, offering several profound implications and potential impacts:

1. **Enabling Continual, Autonomous Self-Improvement:** By formalizing Unsupervised Post-Training (UPT) as a critical third stage, this work provides a blueprint for MLLMs to move towards true autonomy. This paradigm shift could allow models to continually refine their capabilities even after the initial waves of human-annotated data are exhausted, leading to more robust and adaptable AI systems.
2. **Addressing Data Scarcity and Cost:** The most direct impact of MM-UPT is its ability to significantly reduce the dependency on expensive and labor-intensive human annotations. This democratizes MLLM development, making advanced models more accessible and sustainable for a wider range of researchers and applications, especially for niche or rapidly evolving domains where data collection is particularly challenging.
3. **Simplicity and Accessibility:** MM-UPT's simple yet effective approach, leveraging majority voting within the stable GRPO framework, makes it a practical solution. Its open-source code further enhances accessibility, encouraging rapid iteration and adoption within the research community.
4. **Scalable Data Generation:** The proposed data self-generation strategies, particularly "Direct Synthesizing," unlock a powerful pathway for infinite, high-quality training data. This offers a scalable solution for expanding the training corpus without any human intervention, paving the way for truly self-evolving multi-modal AI.
5. **Broad Applicability and Generalization:** The demonstrated effectiveness of MM-UPT across diverse multi-modal reasoning benchmarks, its generalization to non-mathematical VQA tasks, and its successful adaptation to purely language-based mathematical tasks highlight its versatility. This suggests that the core mechanism of reinforcing consistent, high-confidence responses is a generally beneficial learning principle for large models.
6. **Insights into Model Learning Dynamics:** The deeper analysis into "why it works" and "when it might fail" provides valuable insights into the conditions under which unsupervised learning is most effective. Understanding the trade-offs between accuracy and diversity, and the necessity of initial model competence, guides future research in building more robust self-improvement mechanisms.

Future work, as suggested by the authors, could explore more fine-grained pseudo-reward signals (e.g., LLM-as-Judge, model collaboration) to overcome limitations in low-competence scenarios, and investigate the scaling laws of UPT with synthetic data. Overall, MM-UPT lays a foundational stone for the next generation of MLLMs that are not only powerful but also self-sufficient and capable of continuous, unsupervised evolution.
