# First SFT, Second RL, Third UPT: Continual Improving Multi-Modal LLM Reasoning via Unsupervised Post-Training

> [!summary] Summary
> MM-UPT establishes an Unsupervised Post-Training (UPT) paradigm for multi-modal large language models, allowing them to continually refine reasoning capabilities without relying on human-annotated data. The framework employs an online reinforcement learning approach with a majority-voting self-reward mechanism and enables the models to generate their own synthetic training data, leading to enhanced performance across diverse multi-modal benchmarks.

## Problem

- Multi-modal Large Language Models (MLLMs) heavily depend on extensive, costly human-annotated data for post-training stages like Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning (RL).
- This dependency creates an unsustainable bottleneck for the continuous improvement and scalability of MLLMs, particularly as real-world tasks grow in complexity and diversity.
- Prior unsupervised self-improvement methods for MLLMs often involve complex, multi-stage pipelines or yield only marginal gains, lacking a simple, online approach for continuous enhancement.

## Method

- MM-UPT formalizes ==Unsupervised Post-Training (UPT)== as a distinct third stage for MLLMs, designed to enable continuous self-improvement in the absence of external supervision.
- It adapts the GRPO algorithm for an unsupervised context by introducing a novel ==self-rewarding mechanism based on majority voting==. The MLLM samples multiple responses, extracts answers, identifies a pseudo-label from the majority vote, and calculates rewards based on agreement.
- The framework integrates strategies for ==autonomous synthetic data generation==, where the MLLM itself creates new questions from images (in-context or direct synthesizing), feeding these unlabeled samples back into the unsupervised reinforcement learning pipeline.

## Results

- MM-UPT consistently improved the base Qwen2.5-VL-7B model's performance across all multi-modal mathematical reasoning benchmarks (e.g., average score increased from **49.47%** to **53.17%** on MMR1).
- The approach significantly outperformed existing unsupervised self-improvement baselines and achieved performance competitive with strong supervised post-training methods, demonstrating its efficacy without ground-truth labels.
- Models trained on MLLM-generated synthetic data performed comparably to, and in some cases surpassed, those trained on original human-written questions, validating the effectiveness of autonomous data creation across Geometry3K and GeoQA datasets.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Majority voting effectively generates reliable pseudo-reward signals from an MLLM's moderately competent responses, allowing the model to bootstrap its own knowledge and improve answer consistency in an unsupervised manner.
> - MLLMs are capable of autonomously synthesizing high-quality, diverse training questions solely from visual input, creating a scalable and fully self-sufficient loop for data generation.
> - The effectiveness of unsupervised post-training depends on a foundational level of competence in the base MLLM; models with insufficient prior knowledge may amplify errors through majority voting.
