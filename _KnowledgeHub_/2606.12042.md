# KinematicRL: A Sim-to-Real Reinforcement Learning Framework For Social Navigation With Kinodynamic Feasibility

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Tongji University developed KinematicRL, a deep reinforcement learning framework that enables mobile robots to navigate socially in real-world, pedestrian-rich environments by addressing the sim-to-real gap. The framework integrates higher-order control, a robust LiDAR-only human tracking pipeline, and a gated spatio-temporal transformer, achieving smoother, kinodynamically feasible trajectories and successful real-world deployment.

## Problem

- Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) policies for social navigation often assume simplified first-order robot dynamics, leading to dynamically infeasible actions in real robots.
- Existing DRL methods typically assume perfect knowledge of human states, whereas real-world data from noisy sensors requires robust estimation.
- DRL architectures struggle to adapt to time-varying crowd sizes and maintain stability under sensor noise, especially with common transformer limitations.

## Method

- Proposed using ==higher-order control== inputs (accelerations) as the DRL action space, warm-starting the policy with a stochastic iLQR using a divergence minimization objective to ensure dynamic feasibility.
- Developed a robust, LiDAR-only human tracking pipeline featuring cluster-based detection, historical averaging for velocity estimation, and spatial-velocity data association to handle noisy sensor data.
- Introduced a gated spatio-temporal transformer with unbiased residual gating and scenario-based domain randomization to adapt to variable crowd densities and enhance learning stability.

## Results

- KinematicRL achieved a simulated success rate of **0.84** with zero velocity oscillation frequency and low jerk and curvature, demonstrating smoother and dynamically feasible trajectories compared to baselines.
- The LiDAR-only tracking pipeline attained a Mean Squared Error of **0.11m**/s for velocity estimation, outperforming other methods and improving robustness by incorporating velocity similarity in data association.
- The learned policy successfully transferred to a real differential drive robot, exhibiting proactive, socially compliant, and smooth collision avoidance in a human-populated environment, robust to sensor limitations and occlusions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Higher-order control, specifically using accelerations, significantly reduces the tracking error between simulated and actual robot positions, effectively bridging the sim-to-real gap for actuator dynamics.
> - Initializing DRL policies with expert demonstrations from stochastic iLQR using a mode-covering M-projection objective improves training convergence and transfers action uncertainty for better policy quality.
> - A custom gated spatio-temporal transformer architecture, combined with scenario-based domain randomization, is crucial for developing proactive navigation behaviors and robustly handling fluctuating crowd sizes and sensing inaccuracies.
