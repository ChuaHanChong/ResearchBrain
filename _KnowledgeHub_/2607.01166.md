# Structured 4D Latent Predictive Model for Robot Planning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at MIT and Harvard developed a Structured 4D Latent Predictive Model, which learns and predicts the 3D structure and dynamics of a scene in a compact latent space for robot planning. This framework achieved an average 61.3% success rate on complex ManiSkill3 manipulation tasks, significantly outperforming 2D video-generative baselines, and demonstrated robust zero-shot generalization to novel visual conditions.

## Problem

- Policy-based robot agents struggle with generalization under minor environmental shifts due to reliance on scarce, task-specific interactive data.
- Existing video-based predictive models for robot planning operate in 2D pixel space, leading to physical inconsistencies and limited 3D spatial understanding for precise manipulation tasks.
- Direct 3D dynamics modeling is challenging; traditional 3D representations lack visual detail, while photorealistic ones are computationally prohibitive for dynamic scenarios.

## Method

- A ==Structured 3D Latent== representation, consisting of ==sparse 3D voxels== with feature vectors, is introduced to capture holistic scene information efficiently, which can be decoded into ==3D Gaussians== for rendering.
- A **Structured 4D Latent Predictive Model** is developed to learn temporal dynamics by ==autoregressively predicting== future 3D latent states through a ==two-step conditional flow-matching== process: one for coarse geometry (==Single Dynamics model==) and another for detailed features (==Latent Generator==).
- A planning framework leverages the predicted 3D latent subgoals, translating them into robot actions using a ==goal-conditioned inverse dynamics module==, which can be either learned or based on ==learning-free pose estimation==.

## Results

- The model achieved an average success rate of **61.3%** on ManiSkill3 tasks (e.g., StackCube, PullCubeTool), surpassing video-generative planning baselines like UniPi (**29.3%**) and TesserAct (**24.7%**).
- It demonstrated strong zero-shot generalization capabilities, maintaining high success rates (**78-85%**) on StackCube-v1 under various visual perturbations, including changes in lighting, noise, background, and camera viewpoint shifts.
- In real-world experiments, the model achieved an **80%** success rate on a block-in-basket task using a learning-free pose estimation method, outperforming a Diffusion Policy baseline (**62%**) and confirming practical applicability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Modeling scene evolution directly in a structured 3D latent space inherently enhances physical consistency, leading to superior multi-view and temporal coherence compared to 2D pixel-space methods.
> - Decomposing the prediction of 4D scene dynamics into predicting coarse 3D geometry and then refining with detailed features allows for efficient and expressive scene representation and future state generation.
> - Integrating geometrically rich 3D future state predictions as subgoals within a closed-loop inverse dynamics planning framework enables more precise and spatially aware robot actions, thereby improving manipulation performance and robustness.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of general-purpose agents capable of performing a diverse range of real-world tasks is a central objective in robotics research. Current approaches in robot learning broadly fall into two categories: policy-based agents and dynamics model-based agents.

Policy-based agents, which directly map sensory inputs to actions, have shown proficiency in constrained, well-instrumented environments. However, they frequently exhibit limitations in generalization when confronted with even minor distribution shifts, such as variations in lighting, viewpoint, or the composition of unseen tasks. This is primarily attributed to the scarcity of large-scale, task-diverse, and interactive robotic data required for robust training.

An alternative paradigm involves learning dynamics models that predict the consequences of actions, thereby facilitating planning and enhancing generalization. Historically, dynamic models have been integral to robotic tasks like locomotion and manipulation, enabling effective planners such as model predictive control (MPC). More recently, this approach has been revitalized in robot learning through the integration of video generative models, trained on large datasets, with planners or inverse dynamics modules. These models predict environmental evolution conditioned on high-level instructions, offering advantages in interpretability, flexibility, and long-horizon planning.

Despite these advancements, prevailing video-based predictive models are inherently 2D and operate in pixel space. This 2D nature introduces physical inconsistencies with the real 3D world, limiting accurate spatial understanding, a factor particularly critical for fine-grained manipulation tasks. Modeling dynamics directly in 3D presents its own set of challenges; traditional 3D representations (e.g., point clouds, meshes) often lack rich visual detail, while photorealistic representations (e.g., Neural Radiance Fields, 3D Gaussian Splatting) are computationally intensive for dynamic modeling. Intermediate solutions, such as predicting RGB video augmented with depth and normals, offer partial 3D cues but remain surface-level projections susceptible to occlusions and viewpoint shifts, lacking comprehensive multi-view coherence.

This research addresses the aforementioned limitations by proposing a method that bridges the gap between the generalization capabilities of dynamics models and the need for robust 3D spatial understanding. It aims to develop a predictive model that inherently simulates dynamic 3D structures, moving beyond 2D or 2.5D representations to a holistic 3D understanding, thereby enhancing consistency and planning accuracy in complex robotic tasks. This positions the work as an advancement towards more capable and generalizable robot learning systems by providing a foundation for more physically consistent world models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a general-purpose agent capable of solving a wide array of real-world robotic tasks with improved generalization, long-horizon planning, and flexible decision-making. This overarching goal is motivated by several identified limitations in current robot learning paradigms:

1. **Addressing Limitations of Policy-Based Agents**: Existing policy-based agents, which directly map observations to actions, struggle with generalization under even minor distribution shifts (e.g., changes in lighting, viewpoint, or unseen task compositions). This is partly due to the scarcity of diverse, large-scale interactive robotic data. The research aims to overcome this by shifting towards a model that predicts scene dynamics, thereby enabling planning and improving robustness to environmental variations.

2. **Overcoming Shortcomings of Video-Based Predictive Models**: While video generative models offer an alternative for robot planning, their inherent 2D nature poses significant challenges. They operate in pixel space, leading to physical inconsistencies with the real 3D world and limiting precise spatial understanding. This is particularly problematic in fine-grained manipulation tasks where accurate 3D cues are essential. The motivation is to move beyond these 2D limitations to enable physically consistent and spatially aware predictions.

3. **Tackling the Complexity of 3D Dynamics Modeling**: Direct 3D dynamics modeling is challenging. Traditional 3D representations often compromise visual detail for geometry, while photorealistic representations are computationally expensive for dynamic scenarios. Partial 3D cues, like depth and normals, are surface-level and vulnerable to occlusions and viewpoint changes, failing to provide holistic 3D understanding. The paper is motivated by the question of whether a model can inherently simulate dynamic 3D structures, ensuring complete and 3D consistent scene understanding.

To address these motivations, the specific objectives of this work are:
- **Introduce a Structured 4D Latent Representation**: Develop a compact yet expressive 3D latent representation that captures holistic scene information over time, allowing for efficient dynamic modeling while preserving fine details. This representation is designed to be decodable into various explicit 3D formats (e.g., point clouds, 3D Gaussian Splatting) to support flexible generalization to novel viewpoints and scene representations.
- **Develop a Structured 4D Latent Predictive Model**: Construct a model that predicts the evolution of a scene's 3D structure in this structured latent space, conditioned on current observations and textual instructions. This model aims to achieve superior visual quality, strong 3D consistency, and robust multi-view and viewpoint generalization compared to existing video-based methods.
- **Integrate with a Planning Framework**: Propose a planning framework that leverages the detailed 3D predictions from the latent predictive model. These geometrically rich future states serve as subgoals for a goal-conditioned inverse dynamics module, which translates them into precise, spatially-aware robot actions.
- **Validate Performance and Generalization**: Empirically demonstrate that the proposed model and planning pipeline achieve superior performance on complex manipulation tasks, exhibit robust generalization to novel visual conditions (e.g., lighting, background, noise, viewpoint shifts), and prove effective on real-world robotic platforms, thereby validating its practical applicability.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of this work is the **Structured 4D Latent Predictive Model** for robot planning, designed to predict the evolution of a scene's 3D structure in a structured latent space. The overall framework is illustrated in Figure 2.

#### 3.1. Structured 3D Latent for Scene Representations

The method employs a structured, sparse 3D latent representation, denoted as $z_t = \{(p_i, f_i)\}_{i=1}^L$, to capture the complete 3D scene at time $t$. This representation is inspired by SLAT (Xiang et al., 2025) and consists of a set of $L$ active voxels. Each voxel $p_i$ represents a 3D coordinate within a discretized $N \times N \times N$ grid, and $f_i \in \mathbb{R}^d$ is a feature vector encoding local geometry and color. This design balances computational efficiency (sparse, low-dimensional features) with expressive power for capturing fine details.

- **Encoding from images to structured 3D latent**: The encoding process takes calibrated multi-view RGB-D observations (along with camera intrinsics and extrinsics) as input. First, depth maps are unprojected using camera geometry and merged into a 3D point cloud. This point cloud is then voxelized to define the sparse grid of active voxel coordinates $\{p_i\}_{i=1}^L$. For each image, a pre-trained DINOv2 encoder extracts patch-level embeddings, which are then unprojected into the voxel grid using corresponding camera poses. Features arriving at the same voxel are averaged, and a latent encoder E processes these features to produce the compact feature vectors $f_i$.
- **Decoding from structured 3D latent to images**: To convert the latent representation back into a renderable scene, a latent decoder D maps each latent voxel feature $f_i$ to a set of $K$ 3D Gaussians. Each Gaussian is parameterized by its position, color, scale, opacity, and rotation. The center of each Gaussian is constrained to be near its corresponding active voxel. These decoded 3D Gaussians can then be rendered into images from arbitrary viewpoints or converted into point clouds by extracting their centers.

#### 3.2. Structured 4D Latent Predictive Model (Dynamics Learning)

The predictive model learns the temporal dynamics of 3D scenes by generating future 3D structures conditioned on current observations and textual instructions. This is formulated as a conditional generator $g(z_{t+1}..., z_{t+T} | z_t, c)$, operating autoregressively to predict $z_{t+1}$ from $z_t$ and a text instruction $c$. Due to the complexity of directly generating a full 3D latent state, a two-step pipeline is adopted:

- **Single Dynamics Model (SD)**: This component, $SD(\{p_i\}_{t+1} | z_t, c)$, focuses on predicting the coarse geometry of the next state, specifically the sparse voxel positions $\{p_i\}_{t+1}$. It uses a conditional flow matching framework (Lipman et al., 2023). The input voxel grid is encoded by 3D convolutional blocks into a lower-resolution latent tensor. A transformer denoiser operates in this compressed space. Text instructions are encoded using a pre-trained CLIP model and injected into each transformer block via cross-attention, along with the current latent $z_t$ (processed by 3D convolutions). Condition augmentation, including random dropout of voxel features and Gaussian noise, is used to enhance robustness.
- **Latent Generator (LG)**: The latent generator, $LG(\{f_i\}_{t+1} | \{p_i\}_{t+1}, z_t, c)$, predicts the detailed feature vectors $\{f_i\}_{t+1}$ for the voxel structure provided by the SD model. Similar to SD, it employs a flow-matching framework with a transformer backbone, conditioned on text and 3D latent features via cross-attention.

SD and LG are trained independently using a conditional flow-matching objective, $L_{FM}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{t,\epsilon,x_0} \|v_{\theta}(x(t), t, c) - (\epsilon - x_0)\|^2$, where $x_0$ is the clean data sample, $x(t)$ is an interpolation between $x_0$ and Gaussian noise $\epsilon$, and $v_{\theta}$ is the model's velocity prediction. After training, they are applied iteratively to generate complete 3D latents $z_{t+1}..., z_{t+T}$.

#### 3.3. Planning with Inverse Dynamics

The predicted sequence of 3D latent subgoals $(z_1..., z_T)$ serves as a high-level plan for robotic control. A goal-conditioned inverse dynamics module, $ID(z_t, z_{t+1}) \rightarrow a_{1:H}$, translates these latent subgoals into short-horizon robot command sequences ($a_{1:H}$, typically joint positions). Two implementations are explored for this module:

- **Learned inverse dynamics model**: This model decodes $z_t$ and $z_{t+1}$ into point clouds $pc_t$ and $pc_{t+1}$. These point clouds are then encoded using a pyramid convolutional backbone (Ze et al., 2024a), concatenated with the robot's proprioceptive state, and fed into a diffusion head to predict the action sequence $a_{1:H}$. This module is trained separately from the predictive model using a diffusion objective.
- **Learning-free pose estimation with motion planning**: This approach converts predicted 3D subgoals into executable motions geometrically. The predicted latent subgoal $z_{t+1}$ is decoded into a point cloud $pc_{t+1}$. By using a template point cloud of the robot gripper, a rigid transformation is estimated to align the gripper template with the predicted gripper geometry in $pc_{t+1}$. This involves FPFH features for coarse matching, RANSAC for initial alignment, and ICP for refinement. The resulting end-effector pose $T_{ee,t+1}$ is then passed to a motion planner to generate a feasible robot trajectory.

#### 3.4. Planning Pipeline

The complete planning pipeline (Algorithm 1) proceeds as follows:
1. Initial multi-view observations are encoded into the initial 3D latent state $z_0$.
2. In a loop for $t=0..., T-1$:
 - The next latent subgoal $\hat{z}_{t+1}$ is predicted using the SD and LG models, conditioned on the current state $z_t$ and the task instruction $c$.
 - $z_t$ and $\hat{z}_{t+1}$ are decoded into point clouds $pc_t$ and $\hat{pc}_{t+1}$.
 - The inverse dynamics module $ID(pc_t, \hat{pc}_{t+1})$ predicts the action chunk $a_{1:H}$.
 - The action chunk is executed on the robot.
 - For closed-loop planning, the latent state $z_{t+1}$ is updated from new observations; otherwise, $z_{t+1}$ is set to $\hat{z}_{t+1}$.
The agent repeatedly replans until the subgoal is reached, then proceeds to the next.

**4.5. Training Data**: The models are trained on demonstration data from ManiSkill3 (Tao et al., 2025) and LIBERO (Liu et al., 2023) tasks, paired with language instructions. Intermediate timesteps from trajectories are sampled, and 40 spherical multi-view cameras render observations, which are then encoded into 3D latents for training. The inverse dynamics module is trained on 1,000 demonstration trajectories with point cloud observations and corresponding action sequences for specific ManiSkill3 tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of the Structured 4D Latent Predictive Model encompassed 4D generation quality, robot planning performance, generalization capabilities, and real-world applicability.

#### 4.1. 4D Generation Results

- **Visual Quality**: The model autoregressively generates sequences of future 3D latents that maintain multiview and temporal consistency, exhibiting high visual fidelity in rendered images and point clouds (Figure 3).
- **Multiview Consistency**: The proposed method significantly outperforms video generation-based models (Wan 2.1, TesserAct, OpenSora 1.3) in maintaining 3D consistency. Quantitatively, it shows substantially lower Chamfer Distance and depth error, and higher cPSNR and cSSIM, demonstrating improved 3D consistency and multi-view coherence (Table 1a). This is attributed to the explicit 3D latent representation, which inherently enforces consistent 3D structure.
- **Viewpoint Generalization**: The model demonstrated robust ability to integrate diverse multi-view information and generalize to previously unseen viewpoints, preserving consistent 3D structure and object interactions (Figure 4).
- **Connection to Robot Policy Performance**: The quality of the predictive rollout directly correlates with robot policy performance. The model generated robot masks with higher Intersection over Union (IoU) scores compared to ground truth, indicating more stable and accurate predictions for robot action execution (Table 1b).

#### 4.2. Robot Planning Results

- **Manipulation Planning Performance (ManiSkill3)**: The structured 4D latent predictive model, when integrated with a learned inverse dynamics module, achieved an average success rate of 61.3% across ManiSkill3 tasks (StackCube-v1, PullCubeTool-v1, PegInsertionSide-v1). This performance significantly surpassed video-generative planning baselines (UniPi: 29.3%, TesserAct: 24.7%) and was comparable to specialized imitation learning policies (DP: 55.7%, DP3: 49.3%), as shown in Table 2.
- **Manipulation Planning Performance (RLBench)**: On selected RLBench tasks (CloseBox, SweepToDustpan, WaterPlants), the method achieved an average success rate of 75.3%, outperforming UniPi (55.0%) and TesserAct (61.7%) as reported in their respective publications (Table 3).
- **Zero-shot Generalization to Visual and Viewpoint Changes**: The model demonstrated strong zero-shot generalization to novel visual conditions. On the StackCube-v1 task, it maintained high success rates (78-85%) under various perturbations, including reduced lighting, additive Gaussian noise, background color shifts, and horizontal camera rotations (5° and 10°). This significantly outperformed diffusion policy baselines (DP and DP3), highlighting the robustness provided by the explicit 3D latent representation (Table 4).

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

- **Inputs to the Inverse Dynamics Module**: Comparing different input representations for the inverse dynamics module (decoded point clouds, 3D latents, 3D voxels) on StackCube-v1, decoded point clouds consistently outperformed voxel-based inputs and achieved performance comparable to latent-based representations while being computationally lighter. Closed-loop execution substantially improved success rates across all input types, especially with limited (4-camera) inputs, demonstrating its corrective power for imperfect rollouts (Table 5).
- **Number of Camera Views**: Increasing the number of training-time camera views (from 4 to 40) generally improved both 3D consistency metrics and planning success rates. However, even with only 4 training views, the method substantially outperformed video-based baselines. Closed-loop replanning provided significant gains, particularly when training views were limited, suggesting its effectiveness in mitigating supervision deficiencies (Table 6a, 6b).

#### 4.4. Real-World Experiments

The model was evaluated on a real-world block-in-basket task using a dataset of 200 human demonstrations collected with four RGB-D cameras.
- Qualitative results showed that the generated point cloud sequences exhibited temporal and 3D consistency in real-world scenarios (Figure 5b, 5c).
- Utilizing a learning-free point cloud registration method for end-effector pose estimation and motion planning, the method achieved an 80% success rate over 50 episodes, outperforming a Diffusion Policy baseline (62%) (Figure 5g). This demonstrated the model's ability to learn meaningful dynamics from real-world data and effectively transfer to real robot manipulation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces a Structured 4D Latent Predictive Model that represents a shift in robot planning from 2D video-based approaches to an explicit 3D dynamics framework. The significance and potential impact of this work are multifaceted:

- **Enhanced Spatial Understanding and Consistency**: By modeling scene evolution directly in a structured 3D latent space, the work addresses a fundamental limitation of pixel-space methods: the lack of explicit 3D geometric understanding and physical consistency. This results in future scene predictions that are geometrically consistent across multiple views and temporally coherent, which is crucial for tasks requiring precise spatial reasoning.
- **Improved Generalization Capabilities**: The explicit 3D latent representation inherently provides robustness against common distribution shifts, such as changes in viewpoint, lighting, background, or the presence of noise. The demonstrated strong zero-shot generalization to novel visual conditions is a critical advancement for deploying robotic systems in diverse and unstructured real-world environments, where fixed environmental conditions cannot be guaranteed.
- **More Capable Robotic Manipulation**: The integration of the 3D predictive model with a goal-conditioned inverse dynamics module enables the generation of geometrically rich subgoals. This allows for more precise and spatially aware manipulation, particularly beneficial for fine-grained tasks that demand high accuracy in object interaction and placement, moving beyond the capabilities of methods relying on less accurate 2D cues.
- **Foundation for Generalist Agents**: This work contributes to the long-term goal of developing general-purpose robotic agents. By learning a dynamic model of the 3D world, the framework provides a more robust and flexible foundation for planning across various tasks and environments, reducing reliance on task-specific policies or extensive, task-specific data collection. The ability to reason about 3D structure and its evolution is a step towards more intelligent and adaptable robotic behaviors.
- **Modular and Flexible Planning Framework**: The methodology separates the world modeling (4D latent prediction) from the action execution (inverse dynamics), promoting modularity. This allows for flexible inverse dynamics implementations (learned or learning-free), potentially enabling easier transfer across different robot platforms or scenarios where action-labeled data might be scarce.
- **Broader Applicability and Future Directions**: While the current implementation relies on calibrated multi-view inputs, the success in simulation and real-world experiments suggests a strong potential for broader applicability. Future work can explore extensions to less constrained input settings (e.g., sparse, monocular, uncalibrated observations) and further address the challenge of achieving fine geometric precision for contact-rich manipulation tasks, paving the way for even more robust and versatile robot autonomy.
