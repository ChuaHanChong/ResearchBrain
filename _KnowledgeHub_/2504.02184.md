# Model Predictive Control with Visibility Graphs for Humanoid Path Planning and Tracking Against Adversarial Opponents

> [!summary] Summary
> UCLA researchers introduced a real-time framework combining Dynamic Augmented Visibility Graphs for path planning and Collision-Free Model Predictive Control for trajectory tracking, specifically designed for adult-sized humanoid robots in dynamic, adversarial environments. This system allowed the ARTEMIS robot to achieve an undefeated record and win the RoboCup 2024 championship, demonstrating robust navigation and collision avoidance.

## Problem

- Developing real-time path planning and collision avoidance for adult-sized humanoids in dynamic, adversarial, and sensor-noisy environments.
- Accounting for high turning costs inherent to large humanoid robots to generate kinematically feasible and efficient paths.
- Ensuring computational efficiency for on-board processing while achieving smooth and robust trajectory tracking with integrated collision avoidance, avoiding abrupt control changes due to mode switching.

## Method

- A two-stage approach comprising Dynamic Augmented Visibility Graphs (DAVG) for long-term path planning and Collision-Free ==Model Predictive Control== (cf-MPC) for short-term trajectory tracking.
- DAVG extends traditional visibility graphs by augmenting states to account for turning costs and employs active region preprocessing to enhance computational efficiency in complex, crowded environments.
- The cf-MPC utilizes a unified nonlinear or linearized MPC formulation that integrates collision avoidance directly into the objective function with slack variables, ensuring robust and smooth tracking without explicit mode switching.

## Results

- DAVG generated optimal paths considering turning costs in simulations, with average solving times ranging from **0.33** ms to **8.0** ms for various obstacle configurations.
- cf-MPC achieved accurate trajectory tracking with an average solving time of **0.65**-**1.4** ms for LMPC (5-20 step horizon) and **4.2**-**84.6** ms for NMPC, enabling control loops up to 400 Hz and 100 Hz respectively on hardware.
- The framework was successfully implemented on the ARTEMIS humanoid robot during RoboCup 2024, contributing to an undefeated record in seated matches and winning the championship game (6-1), demonstrating robust real-time obstacle avoidance and competitive maneuvering.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Augmenting visibility graph states with orientation information effectively incorporates turning costs, yielding kinematically more efficient paths for humanoid robots.
> - A preprocessing step to select 'active regions' for graph construction significantly reduces computational load in environments with many obstacles, maintaining real-time performance.
> - Integrating collision avoidance with slack variables directly into the MPC formulation enables robust and smooth trajectory tracking, even when robot pose estimates or obstacle perceptions are noisy.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper addresses fundamental challenges in robotic autonomy, specifically in the areas of real-time path planning, obstacle avoidance, and trajectory tracking for complex, dynamic systems. The context of adult-sized autonomous humanoid soccer in RoboCup 2024 provides a demanding testbed for these capabilities, characterized by a crowded, dynamic, and adversarial environment.

Traditional approaches to path planning encompass a wide array of methods, including learning-based techniques, nonlinear optimization, control Lyapunov and barrier functions (CLF/CBF), heuristic methods like potential fields, reachability methods, rapidly-exploring random trees (RRT), and graph-based search algorithms such as A*. Among graph-based methods, visibility graphs are a classical approach for finding shortest paths among polygonal obstacles. The paper positions its Dynamic Augmented Visibility Graphs (DAVG) as an advancement in this domain, specifically tailored to address the computational demands and kinematic constraints (e.g., turning costs) pertinent to large humanoid robots in real-time scenarios. Previous visibility graph methods exist, some attempting to select active regions or nodes to improve efficiency, but the authors highlight the complexity or limitations of these approaches for their specific application.

For trajectory tracking, Model Predictive Control (MPC) is a widely adopted technique due to its ability to handle nonlinear models and constraints directly. Existing MPC implementations for mobile robots often involve linearizing dynamics or, in some cases, switching between different control modes (e.g., tracking vs. obstacle avoidance) to manage complex constraints. The presented Collision-Free MPC (cf-MPC) contributes to this landscape by offering a unified formulation that integrates collision avoidance and robot dynamics without requiring explicit mode switching. This approach is intended to provide smoother transitions and improved robustness in environments with sporadic obstacle appearance and sensor noise, which are critical factors in competitive robotic events like RoboCup.

The paper aims to bridge the gap between theoretical planning and practical real-time execution on complex hardware by developing methods that are both computationally efficient and robust to environmental uncertainties. This is particularly relevant for bipedal humanoids, which possess unique dynamic characteristics and operational constraints compared to wheeled or aerial robots.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research was to develop a robust and computationally efficient framework for path planning and trajectory tracking that enabled an adult-sized autonomous humanoid robot to successfully navigate and compete in the RoboCup 2024 soccer league. This overarching goal was driven by several specific motivations and challenges encountered in such a dynamic and adversarial environment:

- **Real-time Collision Avoidance in Dynamic Environments:** The RoboCup field is populated by multiple moving entities, including human referees, robot handlers, and opponent robots. This necessitates continuous, real-time path planning and re-planning to avoid collisions, a non-trivial task when obstacles frequently appear or disappear from the robot's perception.
- **Robustness to Measurement Noise:** Humanoid bipedal locomotion inherently introduces noise into sensor measurements. Coupled with a limited field of view, this noise can lead to inaccuracies in obstacle localization and the robot's own pose estimation. Planners must be robust to these uncertainties, even when the estimated robot position might momentarily violate hard constraints.
- **Computational Efficiency:** Humanoid robots typically operate with on-board computational resources that must concurrently manage vision, localization, locomotion, and high-level decision-making. Any planning and tracking algorithm must, therefore, be highly efficient to ensure real-time responsiveness (e.g., at rates of 80-100 Hz or higher) without compromising other critical functions.
- **Considering Turning Costs for Humanoids:** For large humanoid robots, sharp turns can be more costly in terms of time and energy expenditure compared to moving in a straight line. Traditional shortest-path algorithms often prioritize Euclidean distance, which can result in paths with frequent or severe turning angles. A planning strategy that explicitly accounts for these turning costs was motivated to generate more realistic and efficient trajectories for bipedal robots.
- **Smooth Transition in Trajectory Tracking:** In a highly dynamic environment, obstacles can suddenly "flash" into or out of the robot's planned path. Existing MPC formulations that switch between different objective functions for tracking and obstacle avoidance can lead to abrupt control changes. The motivation was to develop a unified MPC formulation that seamlessly balances trajectory tracking and collision avoidance, even when noise temporarily places the robot inside a constraint boundary, thus ensuring smoother and more stable robot behavior.
- **Long-term and Short-term Planning Integration:** A comprehensive solution requires both a long-term strategy for overall path generation and a short-term reactive mechanism for immediate obstacle avoidance and precise trajectory following. The work aimed to integrate these two layers effectively.

These motivations collectively guided the development of DAVG for efficient long-term path planning that considers turning angles, and cf-MPC for robust, real-time, short-term trajectory tracking with integrated collision avoidance.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology integrates a two-stage approach: a long-term path planner called Dynamic Augmented Visibility Graphs (DAVG) and a short-term trajectory tracker named Collision-Free Model Predictive Control (cf-MPC).

**A. Path Planning by Dynamic Augmented Visibility Graphs (DAVG)**

DAVG is an extension of traditional visibility graphs designed to efficiently find collision-free paths while accounting for the turning costs specific to humanoid robots.

1. **Obstacle Representation:** Obstacles, including opponent robots and humans, are modeled as regular polygons in the 2D environment.
2. **Preprocessing for Active Regions:** To manage computational load in complex environments with numerous obstacles, DAVG employs a preprocessing step to identify and select only "active" obstacles that are likely to influence the optimal path.
 - A straight line connecting the start (S) and goal (G) points (S-G line) is defined as a baseline.
 - Initially, obstacles that intersect the S-G line are identified as active obstacles.
 - An "active region" is then defined by finding the farthest vertices of these active obstacles along and perpendicular to the S-G line, creating a bounding box.
 - The algorithm iteratively checks for any other obstacles that intersect this active region. If new obstacles are found, they are added to the set of active obstacles, and the active region is re-calculated. This process continues until no new obstacles intersect the active region, ensuring that only relevant obstacles are considered for graph construction.
3. **Visibility Graph Construction (Euclidean Distance):**
 - The nodes of the graph include the starting point (S), the goal point (G), and the vertices of all active obstacles that are not covered by other obstacles.
 - An edge is created between any two nodes if the straight line segment connecting them does not intersect any obstacle. The weight of this edge is the Euclidean distance between the nodes.
 - A special condition is handled for cases where the starting or ending point is initially inside an obstacle. In such situations, the algorithm identifies the closest "leaving" or "entering" vertex on the obstacle boundary, respectively (e.g., in the heading direction for the start, or coming direction for the goal), and an edge is defined between this vertex and the respective S or G point.
4. **Augmentation for Turning Angle (AVG):** To account for the time-consuming nature of turning for humanoid robots, the graph states are augmented.
 - Instead of just nodes (vertices), the augmented states represent a transition between two vertices (V_i, V_j), effectively representing an incoming edge to V_j.
 - The weight of an edge between an augmented state (V_i, V_j) and another (V_j, V_k) combines both the Euclidean distance of the segment (V_j, V_k) and a cost associated with the turning angle formed by the segments (V_i, V_j) and (V_j, V_k). This encourages paths with larger radii of curvature or fewer sharp turns.
5. **Path Generation:** Once the shortest path in the DAVG is found, discrete points along this path are linearly interpolated to form a continuous trajectory. This linear interpolation is selected to maintain the shortest path property in Euclidean space and potentially reduce swings in updated paths during re-planning due to the lack of curvature.

**B. Trajectory Tracking by Collision-Free Model Predictive Control (cf-MPC)**

The cf-MPC is designed to track the DAVG-generated trajectory while simultaneously ensuring short-term collision avoidance and respecting the robot's dynamics, even in the presence of noise.

1. **Dynamic Model:** A discretized 2D pose model is used, representing the robot's state as `X_k = [x_k, y_k, θ_k]^T` (position and orientation) and control input as `u_k = [v_xk, v_yk, ω_k]^T` (linear velocities in x and y, and angular velocity). The model accommodates both forward and sideways motion, though a simplified mobile robot model can be used if sideways motion is limited.
2. **Nonlinear Model Predictive Control (NMPC) Formulation:**
 - **Objective Function:** Minimizes a weighted sum of the squared differences between the predicted robot states and a reference trajectory (tracking error), and the squared control inputs (control effort), over a prediction horizon `N`.
 `J_0 = Σ_{i=1 to N} (X_i - X_{r,i})^T Q (X_i - X_{r,i}) + Σ_{i=0 to N-1} u_i^T R u_i`
 - **Dynamic Constraints:** The robot's motion is constrained by its nonlinear dynamics: `X_{k+1} = f_d(X_k, u_k)`.
 - **Collision-Free Constraints:** Obstacles are represented as circular regions. The constraint ensures that the robot's predicted position `(x_i, y_i)` remains outside a safe radius `R` from each obstacle `j`: `(x_i - x_{obs,j})^2 + (y_i - y_{obs,j})^2 >= R^2`.
 - **Slack Variables:** To guarantee feasibility, particularly when the robot's estimated position might momentarily fall within an obstacle's constraint due to noise, positive slack variables `δ_j` are introduced. These relax the collision constraint: `(x_i - x_{obs,j})^2 + (y_i - y_{obs,j})^2 + δ_j >= R^2`. The squared slack variables, weighted by a penalty `ρ`, are added to the objective function `J = J_0 + ρ Σ δ_j^2`, making the constraint softer.
 - **Speed Constraints:** Maximum and minimum limits are imposed on the robot's linear and angular velocities.
 - **Key Feature:** This NMPC formulation integrates collision avoidance and robot dynamics into a single optimization problem, avoiding a hard switch between different modes of operation.
3. **Linear Model Predictive Control (LMPC) Formulation:**
 - To improve computational speed, the NMPC can be linearized.
 - **Linearized Dynamics:** The nonlinear dynamics `f_d(X_k, u_k)` are linearized around the reference trajectory `(X_{r,k}, u_{r,k})` using Jacobian matrices `A_k` (state) and `B_k` (input).
 - **Linearized Collision Constraints:** The circular obstacle avoidance constraints are simplified by replacing them with tangent planes around the current reference point for each obstacle. This transforms the quadratic collision constraint into a linear one.
 - **Quadratic Program (QP):** With linearized dynamics and constraints, the LMPC problem becomes a Quadratic Program, which can be solved more efficiently than a general nonlinear program. The objective function, slack variables, and speed constraints remain similar to the NMPC.

The combination of DAVG for long-term strategic path planning and cf-MPC for reactive, dynamic-aware trajectory tracking forms a comprehensive framework designed for robust operation in challenging, real-world robotic competitions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents findings from both simulation and hardware implementation, demonstrating the performance and capabilities of the proposed DAVG and cf-MPC framework.

**A. DAVG Performance:**
- **Optimal Path Generation:** Simulations, such as the example in Fig. 10, illustrate DAVG's ability to generate optimal paths around multiple polygonal obstacles of varying sizes. The path correctly navigates complex environments.
- **Turning Angle Consideration:** Fig. 11 (a) and (b) demonstrate how augmenting the graph with turning angle costs influences path selection. The algorithm effectively chooses longer paths with gentler turns over shorter paths with sharper turns when the turning cost is factored in, which is beneficial for humanoid robots.
- **Obstacle Exit Strategy:** Fig. 11 (c) and (d) confirm the method's functionality in specific challenging scenarios where the robot's starting or ending point is inside an obstacle. DAVG successfully identifies appropriate vertices on the obstacle boundary to initiate or conclude movement, facilitating an efficient exit or entry.
- **Computational Efficiency:** Table I provides average solving times for DAVG. For 2 to 8 obstacles, with polygons represented by 4 to 10 arcs, the solving times ranged from approximately 0.33 ms to 8.0 ms. These figures indicate that DAVG operates within real-time requirements for path planning in dynamic environments.

**B. cf-MPC Performance:**
- **Trajectory Tracking in Simulation:** The LMPC formulation was validated in simulation, demonstrating accurate tracking of the paths generated by DAVG. Fig. 12 shows the robot's trajectory closely following the planned path while managing obstacles.
- **Computational Efficiency:** Table II details the average solving times for both LMPC and NMPC with two obstacles. LMPC consistently solved faster, ranging from 0.65 ms for a 5-step prediction horizon to 1.4 ms for a 20-step horizon. NMPC solving times were higher, ranging from 4.2 ms for 5 steps to 84.6 ms for 20 steps. These times suggest that LMPC is particularly suitable for high-frequency control loops.

**C. Hardware Implementation (ARTEMIS in RoboCup 2024):**
- **Real-time Obstacle Avoidance:** The framework was successfully implemented on the ARTEMIS humanoid robot during the RoboCup 2024 competition. Fig. 13 illustrates the robot's ability to react to sudden obstacle appearances. The system rapidly re-planned and adjusted the path, even when ARTEMIS was already within an obstacle's theoretical constraint boundary, demonstrating robustness to perception noise and sudden environmental changes.
- **Competitive Maneuvering:** Fig. 14 showcases ARTEMIS's performance in a competitive scenario, successfully bypassing an opponent robot to retrieve the soccer ball and clear it from the penalty area. This real-world application validates the integrated system's capability in a high-stakes, adversarial environment.
- **Operational Frequency:** During the competition, the path planning and tracking processes ran at approximately 80-100 Hz using NMPC and around 400 Hz using LMPC. These speeds were achieved concurrently with other computationally intensive tasks such as vision, localization, locomotion, and high-level decision-making on the same on-board computer.
- **Competitive Success:** The implemented system played a significant role in the team's performance at RoboCup 2024. The team was undefeated in all seated matches, scoring 45 goals over 6 games, and ultimately won the championship game with a final score of 6 to 1.

The results collectively indicate that the DAVG and cf-MPC framework achieves its design objectives of efficient path planning with turning cost consideration, robust and smooth collision-free trajectory tracking, and high computational efficiency for real-time operation on complex humanoid robots in dynamic, adversarial environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in this paper offers several significant contributions and has potential impacts across multiple domains of robotics.

**A. Advancements in Autonomous Humanoid Robotics:**
The primary significance lies in the development of a robust and efficient framework for path planning and trajectory tracking specifically tailored for adult-sized humanoid robots. Humanoids present unique challenges due to their complex dynamics, limited field of view, and the inherent noise associated with bipedal locomotion. The demonstrated success in RoboCup 2024—a highly dynamic, adversarial, and real-time environment—serves as a strong validation of the framework's practical utility for such complex robotic systems.

**B. Methodological Contributions:**
- **Dynamic Augmented Visibility Graphs (DAVG):** DAVG addresses a critical gap in traditional graph-based planning by explicitly incorporating turning angle costs into path optimization. For large, dynamic robots like humanoids, turning maneuvers are energetically and temporally expensive. By augmenting graph states to consider orientation, DAVG generates paths that are not merely shortest in Euclidean distance but also more kinematically feasible and efficient for the robot, thereby improving overall trajectory quality and execution speed. The active region selection further enhances efficiency by reducing the computational load for graph construction in crowded environments.
- **Collision-Free Model Predictive Control (cf-MPC):** The proposed cf-MPC provides a unified and smooth approach to integrating collision avoidance with trajectory tracking. Unlike methods that switch between different control objectives, cf-MPC's single formulation, along with the use of slack variables, enables continuous and robust operation even when the robot's estimated state or environmental perception is noisy, or when obstacles appear suddenly. This contributes to safer and more predictable robot behavior in highly uncertain conditions, which is crucial for close-proximity interactions with other robots and humans.

**C. Computational Efficiency for Real-time Applications:**
The achievement of high operational frequencies (80-100 Hz for NMPC and ~400 Hz for LMPC) on hardware with shared computational resources is a notable impact. This demonstrates that the proposed methods are not merely theoretical but are engineered for practical, real-time deployment on embedded systems common in autonomous robots. The ability to perform complex planning and control at these rates allows for rapid reaction to environmental changes, a necessity for competitive and safety-critical applications.

**D. Proven Performance in a Competitive Environment:**
The empirical validation through winning RoboCup 2024 provides substantial evidence of the framework's effectiveness. Achieving an undefeated record in seated matches and a dominant championship victory underscores the practical advantages of DAVG and cf-MPC in a demanding, real-world scenario. This success can motivate further research and adoption of similar approaches within the RoboCup community and beyond.

**E. Potential for Broader Applicability:**
While demonstrated in a 2D soccer context, the fundamental principles of DAVG and cf-MPC, such as augmented state planning for kinematic constraints and unified MPC for robust collision avoidance, are noted to be extensible to higher dimensions and other robotic platforms. This suggests potential applications in diverse fields, including logistics, search and rescue, service robotics, and manufacturing, where autonomous systems operate in dynamic, cluttered, and potentially adversarial environments.

In summary, this work advances the state of the art in path planning and trajectory tracking for humanoid robots by introducing computationally efficient and robust methods that explicitly address the unique challenges of bipedal locomotion and dynamic environments, with practical validation through competitive success.
