# Model Predictive Control with Visibility Graphs for Humanoid Path Planning and Tracking Against Adversarial Opponents

> [!summary] Summary
> UCLA researchers introduced a real-time framework combining Dynamic Augmented Visibility Graphs for path planning and Collision-Free Model Predictive Control for trajectory tracking, specifically designed for adult-sized humanoid robots in dynamic, adversarial environments. This system allowed the ARTEMIS robot to achieve an undefeated record and win the RoboCup 2024 championship, demonstrating robust navigation and collision avoidance.

## Problem

- Developing real-time path planning and collision avoidance for adult-sized humanoids in dynamic, adversarial, and sensor-noisy environments.
- Accounting for high turning costs inherent to large humanoid robots to generate kinematically feasible and efficient paths.
- Ensuring computational efficiency for on-board processing while achieving smooth and robust trajectory tracking with integrated collision avoidance, avoiding abrupt control changes due to mode switching.

## Method

- A two-stage approach comprising Dynamic Augmented Visibility Graphs (DAVG) for long-term path planning and Collision-Free ==Model Predictive Control== (cf-MPC) for short-term trajectory tracking.
- DAVG extends traditional visibility graphs by augmenting states to account for turning costs and employs active region preprocessing to enhance computational efficiency in complex, crowded environments.
- The cf-MPC utilizes a unified nonlinear or linearized MPC formulation that integrates collision avoidance directly into the objective function with slack variables, ensuring robust and smooth tracking without explicit mode switching.

## Results

- DAVG generated optimal paths considering turning costs in simulations, with average solving times ranging from **0.33** ms to **8.0** ms for various obstacle configurations.
- cf-MPC achieved accurate trajectory tracking with an average solving time of **0.65**-**1.4** ms for LMPC (5-20 step horizon) and **4.2**-**84.6** ms for NMPC, enabling control loops up to 400 Hz and 100 Hz respectively on hardware.
- The framework was successfully implemented on the ARTEMIS humanoid robot during RoboCup 2024, contributing to an undefeated record in seated matches and winning the championship game (6-1), demonstrating robust real-time obstacle avoidance and competitive maneuvering.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Augmenting visibility graph states with orientation information effectively incorporates turning costs, yielding kinematically more efficient paths for humanoid robots.
> - A preprocessing step to select 'active regions' for graph construction significantly reduces computational load in environments with many obstacles, maintaining real-time performance.
> - Integrating collision avoidance with slack variables directly into the MPC formulation enables robust and smooth trajectory tracking, even when robot pose estimates or obstacle perceptions are noisy.
