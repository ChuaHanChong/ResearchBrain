# Improving Robotic Imitation Learning via Trajectory Standardization

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed Information-Standardized Trajectory Resampling (ISR), an offline preprocessing method that enforces consistent kinematic-dynamic information density across demonstration trajectories. This approach led to an average increase of 24.0 percentage points in task success rate for robotic manipulation tasks compared to time-uniform downsampling, while often reducing dataset size and improving robustness to varied human operator styles.

## Problem

- Human-generated demonstration trajectories in imitation learning often exhibit temporal non-uniformity and spatial redundancy, leading to non-Markovian contradictions and inefficient learning.
- Variations in operator speed, intermittent pauses, and inconsistent action density across demonstrations introduce skewed data distributions and dominate training gradients with low-value motions.
- Existing preprocessing methods, such as time-uniform downsampling or arc-length parameterization, fail to account for critical kinematic-dynamic information, potentially discarding dynamic intent or oversimplifying task-critical segments.

## Method

- The Information-Standardized Trajectory Resampling (**ISR**) method defines an ==information-intensity field==, L(t), as a weighted sum of velocity and acceleration norms (λ_vel ||v(t)|| + λ_acc ||a(t)||).
- This field warps the raw trajectory into a 1D Riemannian manifold where geodesic distance represents kinematic-dynamic information content.
- ISR uses a bottom-up dynamic programming approach to extract a resampled trajectory, ensuring consecutive points are separated by approximately equal information distance, thereby standardizing the data's kinematic-dynamic properties.

## Results

- ISR consistently increased average task success rates: by **24.0** percentage points for π0.5_{**0.5**}**0.5**​ (from **47.8%** to **71.8%**) and **17.5** percentage points for VO-DP (from **62.7%** to **80.2%**) compared to time-uniform 3× downsampling.
- Achieved these performance gains while retaining comparable or fewer action points (**28%** to **40%** of original data) than 3× downsampling, demonstrating improved data quality rather than increased volume.
- ISR exhibited improved robustness to cross-operator variability, achieving **45.8%** success with mixed operator data on Place&Stack, more than doubling the **22.2%** achieved by time-uniform downsampling.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Standardizing demonstration trajectories by enforcing consistent kinematic-dynamic information density across points significantly improves the performance and robustness of imitation learning policies.
> - A decoupled information distance formulation, combining secant distance for spatial uniformity (velocity term) and integrated acceleration for dynamic intent preservation (acceleration term), effectively addresses both spatial redundancy and dynamic fidelity.
> - The acceleration term is particularly critical for contact-rich or force-sensitive manipulation tasks, as it preserves subtle dynamic cues (e.g., deceleration phases) that are essential for precise execution and often overlooked by purely spatial methods.
