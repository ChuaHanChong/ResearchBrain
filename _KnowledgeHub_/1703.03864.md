# Evolution Strategies as a Scalable Alternative to Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> OpenAI demonstrates that Evolution Strategies (ES), when combined with massive parallelism, can achieve performance competitive with state-of-the-art gradient-based deep reinforcement learning methods on complex tasks like MuJoCo and Atari games. The approach dramatically reduces training time, for example, solving the 3D Humanoid walking task in 10 minutes using 1,440 CPU cores, by leveraging an efficient communication strategy that enables near-linear speedup.

## Problem

- Traditional deep reinforcement learning (RL) algorithms faced limitations in massive parallelization due to high communication bandwidth requirements and inherent sequential dependencies, hindering wall-clock training speed.
- Evolution Strategies (ES) were historically perceived as less effective and scalable for solving complex deep RL problems, especially those involving high-dimensional neural network policies.
- Gradient-based RL methods often grapple with challenges like sensitivity to reward distribution (sparse rewards), the need for backpropagation, issues with long time horizons, and hyperparameter tuning for temporal discounting and action frequency.

## Method

- Applied a variant of **Evolution Strategies** (==Natural Evolution Strategies==) to optimize deep neural network policies by sampling perturbed parameters from a Gaussian distribution and estimating gradients from collected returns.
- Implemented a highly scalable parallelization strategy using ==Common Random Numbers==, allowing workers to reconstruct perturbation vectors locally and only exchange scalar episode returns, minimizing communication overhead.
- Incorporated variance reduction (==antithetic sampling==, ==fitness shaping==, ==weight decay==) plus ==Virtual Batch Normalization (VBN)== for Atari and ==action discretization== for MuJoCo, boosting stability and exploration sensitivity.

## Results

- ES matched or exceeded the final policy performance of TRPO on nearly all MuJoCo continuous control tasks and achieved competitive scores on **51 Atari 2600 games** compared to A3C and DQN.
- The 3D Humanoid walking task was solved by ES in just **10 minutes** using **1,440 CPU cores**, demonstrating a **two orders of magnitude** reduction in training time from typical **11-hour** benchmarks.
- Strong, robust results across Atari/MuJoCo with fixed hyperparameters and different exploration patterns than policy gradients; ES tolerates delayed/sparse rewards and skips value functions/backprop, at lower per-episode compute cost.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - ES achieves performance competitive with SOTA gradient-based deep RL while being dramatically faster in wall-clock time; using Common Random Numbers (CRN) and exchanging only scalar returns enables near-linear speedups with thousands of CPU cores.
> - Effective exploration for ES on deep neural networks requires careful policy parameterization, such as Virtual Batch Normalization for Atari games, to ensure sensitivity to parameter perturbations and varied action choices.
> - ES inherently bypasses several complexities of traditional deep RL, including the need for value functions, backpropagation, and sensitivity to reward sparsity or action frequency, simplifying its application.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of artificial intelligence, particularly in developing agents capable of complex tasks in uncertain environments, has been largely dominated by **Reinforcement Learning (RL)** algorithms based on the Markov Decision Process (MDP) formalism. Techniques such as Q-learning and Policy Gradients (e.g., Deep Q-Networks (DQN), Asynchronous Advantage Actor-Critic (A3C), Trust Region Policy Optimization (TRPO)) have achieved remarkable successes, ranging from mastering Atari games from pixels to helicopter aerobatics and grandmaster-level Go. These methods typically rely on calculating and propagating gradients through a policy or value function.

However, an alternative, less prevalent approach exists: **black-box optimization**, also known as direct policy search or neuro-evolution when applied to neural networks. This class of algorithms does not rely on gradient information from the environment or policy, instead treating the optimization problem as a "black box" where only fitness (return) values are observed for given parameters. Historically, black-box optimization methods, including Evolution Strategies (ES), have been perceived as less effective at solving challenging RL problems, especially those involving high-dimensional neural network policies, compared to their gradient-based counterparts. This perception often stemmed from concerns about their sample efficiency and scalability to complex tasks.

This paper directly challenges that long-standing perception. It strategically re-introduces Evolution Strategies as a viable and, critically, **highly scalable** alternative to modern deep RL techniques. The core contribution lies in demonstrating that with careful implementation and leveraging contemporary distributed computing capabilities, ES can achieve competitive performance on complex benchmarks like MuJoCo continuous control tasks and Atari games.

The work shifts the focus from optimizing *data efficiency* (number of environment interactions) to optimizing *wall-clock time* by exploiting massive parallelism. While acknowledging that ES might still be less data-efficient than some state-of-the-art gradient-based methods, the authors argue that its unique parallelization properties can compensate for this, leading to significantly faster training times.

Furthermore, the paper highlights several intrinsic advantages of ES that address known limitations of gradient-based RL:
- **Insensitivity to reward distribution:** ES handles sparse or dense rewards equally well, as it only uses total episode return.
- **No backpropagation required:** This simplifies the computational graph, reduces memory requirements, and avoids issues like exploding gradients, particularly in recurrent networks. It also makes ES compatible with non-differentiable components and low-precision hardware (e.g., specialized AI chips like TPUs) where backpropagation might be impractical.
- **Tolerance of arbitrarily long time horizons:** Unlike policy gradient methods that often rely on temporal discounting to reduce variance (which can bias gradients for long-lasting effects), ES's gradient estimate is independent of episode length.
- **Invariance to action frequency (frameskip):** A crucial hyperparameter in many traditional RL setups, frameskip, can be problematic. ES, by perturbing parameters rather than actions, is inherently robust to this.

In essence, this work acts as a significant re-evaluation and revitalization of Evolution Strategies within the deep RL landscape. It argues that given the availability of vast parallel compute resources, ES offers a compelling alternative that is simpler, more robust to certain RL challenges, and significantly faster in terms of wall-clock training time for complex problems. This could lead to a renewed interest in derivative-free optimization methods and their hybrids for future AI research.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **systematically explore and validate Evolution Strategies (ES) as a practical, scalable, and competitive alternative to the dominant Markov Decision Process (MDP)-based Reinforcement Learning (RL) techniques**, such as Q-learning and Policy Gradients, particularly in the context of deep neural network policies.

The motivations behind this exploration are multi-faceted:

- **Addressing Scalability Limitations of Traditional RL:** Mainstream deep RL algorithms, especially those relying on value function approximation and backpropagation, often face challenges in massively parallelizing their training processes due to high communication bandwidth requirements (exchanging full gradients or experiences) and inherent sequential dependencies (e.g., value function updates needing to "catch up" with policy changes). The authors were motivated to find an approach that could truly leverage hundreds or thousands of CPUs for faster training.
- **Exploiting ES's Inherent Parallelizability:** Evolution Strategies inherently involve evaluating multiple perturbed policies independently. The authors recognized that if communication overhead could be minimized, ES could achieve near-linear speedups with increasing workers. This led to the development of a novel communication strategy.
- **Challenging the Perception of ES:** Historically, ES and other black-box optimization methods have been considered less effective for complex deep RL problems. A key motivation was to demonstrate that this perception, in the era of large-scale compute and refined ES techniques, is no longer accurate.
- **Simplifying RL Training:** Traditional RL methods can be complex, requiring careful tuning of hyperparameters like temporal discounting, managing exploding gradients, and ensuring effective value function approximation. ES, by contrast, operates on complete episodes, needs no value function, and implicitly handles sparse rewards and long horizons without explicit discounting. This simplicity and robustness were strong motivators.
- **Enabling New Applications and Architectural Flexibilities:** The gradient-free nature of ES allows for optimization of policies with non-differentiable components (e.g., hard attention, discrete decision modules) or on specialized low-precision hardware (e.g., TPUs) where traditional backpropagation is difficult or impossible. This opens doors for exploring novel neural network architectures and deployment scenarios.
- **Improving Wall-Clock Training Time:** For many research and development cycles, wall-clock time (how long it takes to get results) is more critical than raw sample efficiency. Even if ES might use more environment steps, a drastic reduction in training time through parallelism could make it a more practical choice for rapid iteration and problem-solving.

In essence, the paper aims to shift the practical landscape of deep RL by showcasing a robust, conceptually simpler, and dramatically faster-to-train alternative that can stand toe-to-toe with established, more complex methods on challenging benchmarks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the methodology revolves around applying a specific variant of Evolution Strategies, adapted for massive parallelism and high-dimensional neural network policies.

#### 3.1. Evolution Strategies (ES) Core Algorithm

The authors employ a form of **Natural Evolution Strategies (NES)**, where the objective is to maximize the expected return $E_{\theta \sim p_\psi} F(\theta)$, where $F(\theta)$ is the policy's fitness (total return from an episode) with parameters $\theta$, and $p_\psi(\theta)$ is a distribution over parameters. In this work, the distribution is simplified to an isotropic multivariate Gaussian with mean $\psi$ (which effectively becomes the policy parameters $\theta$) and a fixed standard deviation $\sigma$.
The stochastic objective is thus viewed as a Gaussian-blurred version of the original objective $F(\cdot)$, which makes it smooth and differentiable in parameter space. The gradient of this objective with respect to the mean parameter $\theta$ is estimated using the score function estimator:
$$ \nabla_\theta E_{\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0,I)} \{F(\theta + \sigma\epsilon)\} = \frac{1}{\sigma} E_{\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0,I)} \{F(\theta + \sigma\epsilon) \epsilon\} $$
This gradient is approximated with samples: given $n$ noise vectors $\epsilon_1, \ldots, \epsilon_n \sim \mathcal{N}(0,I)$, the policy parameters are updated as $\theta_{t+1} \leftarrow \theta_t + \alpha \frac{1}{n\sigma} \sum_{i=1}^n F_i \epsilon_i$, where $F_i$ is the return from policy $\theta_t + \sigma\epsilon_i$. This process involves two main phases per iteration:
1. **Perturbation and Evaluation:** Generate perturbed policy parameters and evaluate their performance by running episodes in the environment.
2. **Update:** Aggregate returns and update the main policy parameters.

#### 3.2. Scaling and Parallelization (Key Innovation)

The most significant methodological contribution is the parallelization strategy, designed for extremely low communication bandwidth:
- **Common Random Numbers (CRN):** Instead of workers communicating full policy gradients (which are high-dimensional), they only communicate their scalar return ($F_i$). Each worker is initialized with the same random seed, allowing them to independently generate and agree upon the same sequence of random perturbation vectors ($\epsilon_j$). This means each worker can reconstruct all $\epsilon_j$ used by other workers.
- **Reduced Bandwidth:** Since all workers know all $\epsilon_j$ vectors by sharing a single random seed (or a sequence of seeds), they only need to exchange the *scalar* returns $F_i$ to collectively compute the parameter update $\frac{1}{n\sigma} \sum F_j \epsilon_j$. This drastically reduces communication overhead, making scaling to thousands of workers feasible.
- **Practical Implementation:** Workers pre-instantiate large blocks of Gaussian noise, then randomly index into them for perturbations in each iteration, which, while not strictly independent across iterations, worked well in practice.
- **Computational Efficiency:** The ES approach avoids backpropagation and value function estimation, significantly reducing computation per episode and memory usage per worker.

#### 3.3. Variance Reduction and Exploration Techniques

To enhance the robustness and performance of ES:
- **Antithetic Sampling (Mirrored Sampling):** For every sampled noise vector $\epsilon$, its negative $-\epsilon$ is also evaluated. This pairs perturbations, reducing the variance of the gradient estimate.
- **Fitness Shaping (Rank Transformation):** Before computing the parameter update, a rank transformation is applied to the episode returns. This normalizes the scores, making the update less sensitive to outlier performances and potentially aiding in escaping local optima.
- **Weight Decay:** A regularization technique applied to policy network parameters to prevent them from growing excessively large, which could lead to unstable behavior or hinder the effect of perturbations.
- **Dynamic Episode Length Capping:** To maintain high CPU utilization, episode lengths are capped at a dynamically adjusted constant (e.g., twice the mean episode length).

#### 3.4. Impact of Network Parameterization on Exploration

A crucial insight for ES's success with deep neural networks:
- **Virtual Batch Normalization (VBN):** For Atari environments, standard Gaussian perturbations on policy parameters sometimes led to policies that always took one action. Incorporating VBN (from Salimans et al., 2016) significantly improved exploration. VBN uses fixed statistics from an initial batch for normalization, making the policy more sensitive to small weight changes early in training, thus encouraging a wider variety of actions and more effective exploration.
- **Action Discretization:** For some MuJoCo continuous control tasks, discretizing the continuous actions into bins (e.g., 10 bins) was found to encourage more exploration, forcing non-smooth action choices with respect to parameter perturbations.

#### 3.5. Experimental Setup

- **Environments:** Evaluated on standard reinforcement learning benchmarks from OpenAI Gym:
 - **MuJoCo:** Continuous robotic control tasks (e.g., HalfCheetah, Hopper, Humanoid). Policies used were standard multilayer perceptrons (MLPs).
 - **Atari 2600:** 51 classic Atari games with pixel inputs. Policies used convolutional neural networks (CNNs) identical to those in DeepMind's A3C work.
- **Baselines:** Compared against highly-tuned implementations of state-of-the-art policy gradient algorithms:
 - **MuJoCo:** Trust Region Policy Optimization (TRPO).
 - **Atari:** Asynchronous Advantage Actor-Critic (A3C) and DQN.
- **Computational Resources:** Experiments were conducted on Amazon EC2, demonstrating scalability up to 1,440 CPU cores.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents several compelling findings that fundamentally challenge the prevailing views on the effectiveness and scalability of Evolution Strategies for complex deep reinforcement learning tasks.

#### 4.1. Performance Competitiveness on Benchmarks

- **MuJoCo:** ES was able to match the final policy performance of TRPO, a highly optimized policy gradient method, on nearly all continuous control tasks. While ES sometimes required more environment interaction steps (up to 10x more data on harder tasks like Hopper and Walker2d), it achieved better sample efficiency on simpler environments (up to 3x better on Swimmer, HalfCheetah). This demonstrates that ES can achieve state-of-the-art performance.
- **Atari:** ES achieved competitive results on 51 Atari 2600 games. When trained for 1 billion frames (equivalent neural network computation to A3C's 1-day results at 320 million frames due to ES's lack of backpropagation and value function), ES performed better than A3C on 23 games and worse on 28. This indicates that ES is a viable, high-performing alternative across a broad range of challenging tasks.

#### 4.2. Exceptional Parallel Scalability and Wall-Clock Time Reduction

- This is the most striking result. Due to its minimal communication requirements (only scalar returns exchanged, with perturbations reconstructed locally via common random numbers), ES exhibits near-linear speedup with increasing CPU cores.
- **Dramatic Speedup:** The 3D Humanoid walking task, which typically takes about 11 hours to solve on a single 18-core machine (comparable to state-of-the-art RL), was solved by ES in **just 10 minutes** using 1,440 CPU cores. This represents a two orders of magnitude reduction in training time, making experiment turnaround times significantly faster.

#### 4.3. Crucial Role of Policy Parameterization for Exploration

- The study found that simply applying Gaussian noise to standard deep neural network parameters (like those used in DQN/A3C) was not always sufficient for adequate exploration, especially for Atari games. Policies could become "stuck," always taking the same action.
- The introduction of **Virtual Batch Normalization (VBN)** into the policy architecture for Atari environments was critical. VBN made the policy more sensitive to small parameter changes, ensuring more varied actions and effective exploration.
- For some MuJoCo tasks, **discretizing continuous actions** also encouraged better exploration by forcing non-smooth action choices. This highlights that the interaction between parameter perturbation and policy output is key to ES's exploratory behavior.

#### 4.4. Qualitatively Different Exploration Behavior

- ES exhibited distinct exploration patterns compared to policy gradient methods like TRPO. On the MuJoCo humanoid task, ES learned a diverse range of gaits, including unusual ones like walking sideways or backward, which were never observed with TRPO. This suggests that parameter-space exploration can lead to different, potentially more diverse, optimal or near-optimal solutions.

#### 4.5. Robustness to Hyperparameters

- The authors successfully achieved strong results using fixed hyperparameters for all Atari environments and a different fixed set for all MuJoCo environments (with one minor binary exception). This indicates that ES can be surprisingly robust to hyperparameter choices across a variety of domains, simplifying its application.

#### 4.6. Confirmed Inherent Advantages

- **Invariance to Temporal Resolution:** Demonstrated on the Atari game Pong, ES's learning curves remained very similar regardless of the `frame-skip` parameter, confirming its robustness to action frequency, a known challenge for many RL algorithms.
- **Tolerance of Delayed and Sparse Rewards:** As ES only relies on total episode return, it is inherently robust to these aspects, simplifying problem formulation.
- **No Value Function or Discounting:** The method does not require complex value function approximation or temporal discounting, simplifying the learning setup and avoiding potential biases.
- **Computational Efficiency Per Episode:** By not requiring backpropagation, ES reduces computation per episode by roughly two-thirds and significantly lowers memory usage, making it suitable for hardware with limited memory or computational power.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The findings presented in this paper carry significant implications for the field of reinforcement learning and broader AI research, potentially leading to a re-evaluation of established paradigms and opening new avenues for development.

#### 5.1. Reshaping the RL Landscape

The most immediate and profound impact is the demonstration that **Evolution Strategies are a viable and highly competitive alternative to gradient-based deep RL methods**. For years, ES has been relegated to a niche status for complex tasks. This paper emphatically pushes back on that perception, showing that when properly implemented and leveraged with modern compute, ES can match or even exceed the wall-clock performance of state-of-the-art methods. This could inspire renewed research interest in neuro-evolution, derivative-free optimization, and black-box methods, potentially fostering a more diverse methodological ecosystem in deep RL.

#### 5.2. Unlocking Massive Parallelism for RL

The paper's breakthrough in achieving near-linear speedups up to thousands of CPU cores is a game-changer. By demonstrating that complex tasks like 3D Humanoid walking can be solved in minutes rather than hours or days, the authors provide a practical blueprint for **drastically accelerating RL research and development cycles**. This reduced turnaround time means researchers can iterate on ideas much faster, test more hypotheses, and solve more challenging problems within reasonable timeframes, driving innovation forward. The novel communication strategy based on common random numbers is a key technical enabler for this.

#### 5.3. Simplifying RL and Broadening Accessibility

ES inherently bypasses several complexities prevalent in traditional deep RL, such as:
- The need for value function approximation.
- The intricacies of temporal discounting.
- The computational and memory demands of backpropagation.
- Challenges with exploding/vanishing gradients in recurrent networks.
- Sensitivity to reward sparsity and action frequency.
This inherent simplicity makes ES potentially **easier to implement, debug, and apply** for researchers and practitioners, lowering the barrier to entry for effective deep RL.

#### 5.4. Enabling Novel Applications and Architectures

The gradient-free nature of ES opens up new possibilities for AI systems that are difficult or impossible to train with gradient-based methods:
- **Non-differentiable Components:** Policies incorporating modules that are inherently non-differentiable (e.g., hard attention mechanisms, symbolic reasoning modules, or discrete decision-making processes) can be seamlessly optimized with ES.
- **Specialized Hardware:** ES is particularly well-suited for deployment on energy-efficient, low-precision AI hardware like Google's TPUs, which are optimized for inference but often have limited memory for backpropagation.
- **Real-world Robotics:** The robustness to long horizons, delayed rewards, and varying action frequencies makes ES an attractive candidate for challenging real-world robotics problems where these issues are common.

#### 5.5. Advancing Meta-Learning and Automated Machine Learning (AutoML)

The authors explicitly mention meta-learning as a key future direction. ES's ability to optimize over policy parameters directly, without requiring internal differentiability of the learning process itself, makes it a powerful candidate for "learning-to-learn" problems where the outer loop optimizes the learning algorithm or hyperparameter configurations. This could significantly contribute to AutoML efforts, where the goal is to automate the design and optimization of machine learning systems.

In conclusion, this paper is a landmark contribution that not only validates an often-underestimated optimization paradigm but also provides a pragmatic path for leveraging modern computational power to accelerate fundamental AI research. Its impact extends beyond simply solving benchmarks faster, offering a simpler, more robust, and more flexible approach to developing intelligent agents.
