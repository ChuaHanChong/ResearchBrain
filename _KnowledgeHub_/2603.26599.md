# VGGRPO: Towards World-Consistent Video Generation with 4D Latent Reward

> [!summary] Summary
> VGGRPO, a framework developed at Google, enhances video generation by integrating a Latent Geometry Model with latent-space Group Relative Policy Optimization, enabling "world-consistent" videos with stable camera motion and consistent 3D scene structure. The method improves geometric consistency and overall video quality, particularly in dynamic scenes, while reducing computational overhead by 24.5% compared to RGB-based approaches.

## Problem

- Large-scale video diffusion models often produce geometrically inconsistent videos, exhibiting issues like 3D/motion inconsistency, geometric drift, and unstable camera trajectories.
- Existing architectural modifications for geometric consistency increase complexity, computational overhead, and can compromise the broad generalization capabilities of pretrained models.
- Prior post-training alignment methods (e.g., RL-based) are computationally inefficient due to repeated VAE decoding for RGB-based rewards, susceptible to noise, and primarily limited to static scenes.

## Method

- **VGGRPO** introduces a ==Latent Geometry Model== (LGM) by stitching a pretrained ==geometry foundation model== to the ==VAE latent space==, allowing direct ==4D geometry prediction== from latents without RGB decoding.
- It employs ==latent-space Group Relative Policy Optimization== (**GRPO**) to align the video diffusion model, converting the deterministic ==denoising process== into an ==SDE== for stochastic exploration.
- Two novel rewards, ==Camera Motion Smoothness== and ==Geometry Reprojection Consistency==, are computed entirely from the LGM's latent-space geometric predictions, guiding the model towards world-consistent generation.

## Results

- VGGRPO consistently outperformed all baselines in geometric consistency metrics, achieving higher Motion Quality and Visual Quality win rates and lower Sampson epipolar error, especially in dynamic scenes.
- The framework maintained coherent scene structure and temporal continuity in challenging dynamic videos, demonstrating robust performance where prior geometry-aligned baselines struggled.
- Latent-space reward computation reduced peak GPU memory by **8.23 GB** and computation time by **24.5%** (from 54.73s to 41.33s) compared to RGB-based rewarding, also showing higher robustness to latent perturbations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Efficiently integrating powerful geometry priors can be achieved by stitching a geometry foundation model directly into the VAE latent space, circumventing the need for computationally expensive RGB decoding.
> - Optimizing video diffusion models with latent-space geometric rewards significantly improves both geometric and perceptual quality, even for complex dynamic scenes, without compromising the base model's generalization.
> - A combination of camera motion smoothness and 3D reprojection consistency rewards effectively addresses both temporal and spatial geometric inconsistencies, leading to holistically more coherent video outputs.
