# VGGRPO: Towards World-Consistent Video Generation with 4D Latent Reward

> [!summary] Summary
> VGGRPO, a framework developed at Google, enhances video generation by integrating a Latent Geometry Model with latent-space Group Relative Policy Optimization, enabling "world-consistent" videos with stable camera motion and consistent 3D scene structure. The method improves geometric consistency and overall video quality, particularly in dynamic scenes, while reducing computational overhead by 24.5% compared to RGB-based approaches.

## Problem

- Large-scale video diffusion models often produce geometrically inconsistent videos, exhibiting issues like 3D/motion inconsistency, geometric drift, and unstable camera trajectories.
- Existing architectural modifications for geometric consistency increase complexity, computational overhead, and can compromise the broad generalization capabilities of pretrained models.
- Prior post-training alignment methods (e.g., RL-based) are computationally inefficient due to repeated VAE decoding for RGB-based rewards, susceptible to noise, and primarily limited to static scenes.

## Method

- **VGGRPO** introduces a ==Latent Geometry Model== (LGM) by stitching a pretrained ==geometry foundation model== to the ==VAE latent space==, allowing direct ==4D geometry prediction== from latents without RGB decoding.
- It employs ==latent-space Group Relative Policy Optimization== (**GRPO**) to align the video diffusion model, converting the deterministic ==denoising process== into an ==SDE== for stochastic exploration.
- Two novel rewards, ==Camera Motion Smoothness== and ==Geometry Reprojection Consistency==, are computed entirely from the LGM's latent-space geometric predictions, guiding the model towards world-consistent generation.

## Results

- VGGRPO consistently outperformed all baselines in geometric consistency metrics, achieving higher Motion Quality and Visual Quality win rates and lower Sampson epipolar error, especially in dynamic scenes.
- The framework maintained coherent scene structure and temporal continuity in challenging dynamic videos, demonstrating robust performance where prior geometry-aligned baselines struggled.
- Latent-space reward computation reduced peak GPU memory by **8.23 GB** and computation time by **24.5%** (from 54.73s to 41.33s) compared to RGB-based rewarding, also showing higher robustness to latent perturbations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Efficiently integrating powerful geometry priors can be achieved by stitching a geometry foundation model directly into the VAE latent space, circumventing the need for computationally expensive RGB decoding.
> - Optimizing video diffusion models with latent-space geometric rewards significantly improves both geometric and perceptual quality, even for complex dynamic scenes, without compromising the base model's generalization.
> - A combination of camera motion smoothness and 3D reprojection consistency rewards effectively addresses both temporal and spatial geometric inconsistencies, leading to holistically more coherent video outputs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of video generation has seen considerable advancements with large-scale video diffusion models achieving high visual fidelity and broad generalization. However, a persistent challenge in these models is their frequent failure to preserve geometric consistency over time. Generated videos often exhibit issues such as 3D and motion inconsistency, geometric drift, unstable camera trajectories, and incoherent scene structures. These inconsistencies limit their applicability in domains requiring precise spatial and temporal coherence, such as embodied AI, robotics, and physics-aware simulations.

Existing research efforts to address these inconsistencies generally fall into two categories. The first approach involves augmenting the generative model architecture with additional modules or loss components that explicitly incorporate geometric priors. Examples include point cloud-conditioned diffusion models, which use 3D inputs to improve static-scene consistency, and approaches that add auxiliary geometry prediction modules or loss terms (e.g., depth prediction with cross-frame consistency). While these methods can be effective, they often increase architectural complexity, computational overhead, and may restrict the model's flexibility, potentially compromising the broad generalization capabilities inherited from internet-scale pretraining.

The second paradigm focuses on post-training alignment, frequently inspired by reinforcement learning (RL) techniques. Recent work in this area includes adapting Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) or Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) to diffusion models. These methods typically compute rewards based on geometric cues, such as epipolar constraints or dense correspondences, using external geometry models. However, these post-training methods often face their own set of limitations. Many rely on offline preference data collection, leading to off-policy optimization. Crucially, rewards are commonly evaluated in the pixel (RGB) space, necessitating repeated decoding of VAE latents, which incurs substantial computational and memory costs. RGB-based rewards can also be sensitive to decoding noise and minor pixel variations, weakening the optimization signal. Furthermore, many existing geometric reward formulations are constrained to static scenes, as their underlying assumptions and correspondence pipelines do not readily extend to complex dynamic videos.

Concurrently, the emergence of geometry foundation models has demonstrated the capability of feed-forward networks to recover dense geometry and camera motion from image sequences, learning robust geometric priors from large datasets. This development presents an opportunity to integrate strong geometric knowledge into video generation without extensive architectural modifications. VGGRPO positions itself within this landscape by seeking to leverage these powerful geometry priors efficiently, directly in the latent space, and extend their application to dynamic scenes, thereby addressing the limitations of prior work regarding computational efficiency, robustness to dynamic scenes, and preservation of the base model's generalization capacity.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to advance video generation towards "world-consistent" outputs. World consistency, in this context, implies that generated videos should not only possess high visual fidelity but also maintain geometric coherence, exhibit stable camera motion, and represent a consistent 3D scene structure across frames.

The motivation for pursuing this objective stems from several limitations observed in current large-scale video diffusion models:

1. **Geometric Inconsistency in State-of-the-Art Models:** Despite impressive visual quality and generalization, existing video diffusion models frequently produce videos with 3D and motion inconsistencies. These issues manifest as geometric drift (objects shifting unnaturally in space), unstable or jittery camera trajectories, and inconsistent scene structures over time.
2. **Importance for Downstream Applications:** These inconsistencies are critical impediments for emerging downstream applications that rely on accurate 3D understanding and stable temporal dynamics. Examples include embodied AI (where agents interact with simulated environments), physics-aware simulation (requiring physically plausible object behavior), and various forms of 3D content creation, all of which necessitate stable camera motion and coherent 3D geometry.
3. **Drawbacks of Existing Architectural Modifications:** Prior attempts to inject geometric structure into generators often involve architectural changes or additional loss components. While sometimes effective, these modifications can increase the model's complexity, computational requirements, and, critically, might compromise the broad generalization abilities inherent in models pretrained on vast internet-scale datasets. The goal is to improve consistency without sacrificing this generalization capacity.
4. **Inefficiencies and Limitations of RGB-based RL Alignment:** Post-training alignment methods, particularly those based on reinforcement learning, have shown promise. However, many calculate rewards in the pixel (RGB) space, which necessitates repeated VAE decoding of latents. This process is computationally expensive, memory-intensive, and susceptible to noise and subtle variations introduced during VAE decoding. Furthermore, most existing geometric reward formulations are designed for static scenes, failing to generalize to complex and dynamic real-world scenarios.
5. **Leveraging Geometry Foundation Models:** Recent advances in geometry foundation models demonstrate their capability to infer dense 4D geometry (including depth, camera pose, point maps, and scene flow) from image sequences. The motivation is to efficiently integrate these strong geometric priors directly into the video generation process, bypassing the RGB decoding bottleneck and extending consistency to dynamic scenes.

In summary, the key objectives are to:
- Develop a method that preserves the generalization capabilities of pretrained video diffusion models while significantly improving geometric consistency.
- Enable efficient, geometry-aware post-training by computing rewards directly in the latent space, eliminating the computational overhead of repeated RGB decoding.
- Extend geometric consistency methods to dynamic, 4D scenes, overcoming the static-scene limitations of prior approaches.
- Achieve both temporally smooth camera motion and cross-view coherent 3D scene structure, leading to truly "world-consistent" video generation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VGGRPO (Visual Geometry GRPO) is a latent geometry-guided framework designed for geometry-aware video post-training. It integrates two main components: a Latent Geometry Model (LGM) and a latent-space Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) training procedure with custom rewards.

#### 3.1. Latent Geometry Model (LGM)

The LGM's purpose is to enable direct prediction of 4D scene geometry from the VAE latent space of a video diffusion model, thereby avoiding the need for computationally expensive RGB decoding.

- **Construction through Model Stitching:** A pretrained geometry foundation model ($\Phi$), which typically operates on RGB image sequences, is "stitched" to the VAE latent space. This is achieved by replacing the initial layers of $\Phi$ with a lightweight, learned 3D convolutional "connector" layer ($S_\psi$). This connector maps the video diffusion latents (from the VAE encoder E) directly into the intermediate feature space expected by the subsequent layers of the geometry foundation model.
 - The LGM is formally represented as $\hat{\Phi}_\psi = \Phi_{\hat{\ell}+1:L} \circ S_\psi$, where $\hat{\ell}$ denotes the number of initial layers replaced.
- **Training the LGM:**
 1. **Feature Alignment:** The optimal stitching layer $\hat{\ell}$ and initial parameters $\psi$ for the connector $S_\psi$ are found by minimizing the feature alignment error between the output of $S_\psi$ (fed with VAE-encoded videos) and the output of the original geometry model's initial layers $\Phi_{1:\hat{\ell}}$ (fed with corresponding RGB videos). This is done over a calibration dataset of real videos.
 2. **Geometric Prediction Alignment:** After initialization, $\psi$ and the remaining layers of the geometry foundation model ($\Phi_{\hat{\ell}+1:L}$) are fine-tuned. This fine-tuning aims to minimize an alignment loss ($L_{\text{align}}$) between the geometric predictions of the stitched LGM (from VAE latents) and those of the original RGB-based geometry model (from RGB videos). This loss considers multiple geometric modalities (camera pose $C$, depth $D$, point map $P$, and scene flow $F$).
- **Output:** Once trained, the LGM ($\hat{\Phi}_\psi$) can directly output per-frame geometric representations (camera pose, depth map, 3D point map, and scene flow) from video latents, without requiring RGB frames. The use of a geometry model supporting dynamic 4D reconstruction (e.g., Any4D) allows the LGM to handle dynamic scenes by predicting scene flow.

#### 3.2. VGGRPO Training

VGGRPO employs a latent-space Group Relative Policy Optimization framework to align the video diffusion model towards world-consistent generation. This framework avoids repeated VAE decoding, making it efficient.

- **GRPO Framework Adaptation:** The denoising process of rectified flow models (used by state-of-the-art video diffusion models) is cast as a multi-step Markov Decision Process (MDP). GRPO, a critic-free reinforcement learning algorithm, is applied to optimize a policy that maximizes an expected reward while maintaining a KL divergence regularization to the reference (pretrained) policy.
- **Stochastic Exploration:** To enable diverse trajectory sampling for GRPO, the deterministic ODE-based denoising process is converted into an equivalent Stochastic Differential Equation (SDE). This SDE introduces controlled noise at each denoising step, allowing for exploration and providing a tractable log-probability for reverse-step transitions, essential for computing importance ratios.
- **Latent-Space Rewards:** Two complementary rewards are designed, both computed entirely from the geometric predictions of the LGM ($\hat{\Phi}_\psi(z_0)$) on the final denoised latent $z_0$:
 1. **Camera Motion Smoothness Reward ($r_{\text{motion}}$):**
 - **Motivation:** Address jittery or unstable camera motion.
 - **Calculation:** Based on predicted camera poses ($C_i$). It quantifies translational smoothness by normalizing the magnitude of acceleration by velocity magnitudes across frames ($e_{\text{trans}}$). Similarly, rotational smoothness is measured using angular velocities and accelerations ($e_{\text{rot}}$).
 - **Reward Function:** $r_{\text{motion}}(z_0) = \frac{1}{2} \left( \frac{1}{1 + e_{\text{trans}}(z_0)} + \frac{1}{1 + e_{\text{rot}}(z_0)} \right)$. This function maps error terms to a range, where higher values indicate smoother motion.
 2. **Geometry Reprojection Consistency Reward ($r_{\text{geo}}$):**
 - **Motivation:** Enforce consistent 3D structure across different viewpoints and over time.
 - **Calculation:** Uses predicted point maps ($P_i$), depths ($D_i$), camera parameters ($C_i$), and scene flow ($F_i$). For static scenes, all points are aggregated. For dynamic scenes, scene flow $F_i$ helps filter static regions to build a stable scene representation. This aggregated point cloud is then reprojected into each view $i$ using $C_i$ to generate a rendered depth map $\hat{D}_i$.
 - **Error Measurement:** The pixel-wise L1 difference between the rendered depth map $\hat{D}_i$ and the predicted depth map $D_i$ is computed for valid pixels ($e^{(i)}_{\text{geo}}$).
 - **Reward Function:** $r_{\text{geo}}(z_0) = - \frac{1}{3} \sum_{i \in \text{top-3}} e^{(i)}_{\text{geo}}(z_0)$, focusing on the three worst-performing views to penalize local failure cases.
- **Alignment Policy Update:** For each training prompt, a group of $K$ latent video samples ($z_0^k$) are generated from the current policy. Each sample is scored by both rewards. A group-relative advantage ($A_k$) is computed for each sample by normalizing and averaging the $r_{\text{motion}}$ and $r_{\text{geo}}$ scores within the group. The diffusion model's policy parameters (specifically, the velocity field $v_\theta$) are then updated by maximizing a clipped surrogate objective, incorporating $A_k$ and a KL regularization term, using importance ratios calculated from the latent-space SDE transitions. This entire process operates without decoding latents to RGB, significantly boosting efficiency.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

VGGRPO was evaluated on both static and dynamic video generation benchmarks, demonstrating consistent improvements over baseline models and prior post-training alignment methods.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Evaluation

- **Improved Geometric Consistency:** VGGRPO consistently outperformed all baselines (Base Model, Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), Epipolar-DPO, VideoGPA) on geometry-related metrics. This included higher Motion Quality (MQ) and Visual Quality (VQ) win rates (reflecting human preference) and lower Sampson epipolar error (Epi.), particularly on static scenes where the metric is applicable.
- **Robustness in Dynamic Scenes:** On challenging dynamic benchmarks with significant non-rigid motion, VGGRPO demonstrated robust performance, unlike prior geometry-aligned baselines whose performance degraded noticeably. This highlights the effectiveness of VGGRPO's 4D-aware latent geometry model and reward design in complex dynamic scenarios.
- **General Video Quality Preservation:** VGGRPO also improved most general video quality metrics from VBench (subject consistency, background consistency, aesthetic quality, imaging quality, motion smoothness). This indicates that the gains in geometric consistency were achieved without compromising the model's perceptual quality or broad generalization capabilities. A slight decrease in "Dynamic Degree" was observed, which the authors attribute to reduced camera jitter from the motion reward, as this metric relies on optical flow magnitude.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Results

- **Enhanced World Consistency:** Visual comparisons showed that baseline models struggled with world consistency, exhibiting unstable camera motion, geometric drift, and temporally inconsistent scene structures. For example, the base model introduced spurious content, while SFT, Epipolar-DPO, and VideoGPA displayed temporal flicker and geometric instability. In contrast, VGGRPO produced more coherent scene structures and smoother camera motion in both static and dynamic settings.
- **Superiority in Dynamic Scenarios:** The performance gap widened in dynamic examples, where baselines suffered from severe scene blur and significant geometric deformation. VGGRPO maintained coherent scene structure and temporal continuity under complex dynamics, reinforcing its robustness.

#### 4.3. Additional Studies

- **Impact of Geometry Foundation Models:** VGGRPO demonstrated flexibility across different geometry foundation models. While a VGGT-based LGM (designed for static scenes) achieved slightly lower epipolar error on static content, an Any4D-based LGM (supporting 4D dynamics) yielded higher VQ and MQ due to its ability to handle dynamic scenes. Both variants outperformed prior post-training baselines.
- **Complementary Reward Components:** Ablation studies confirmed the complementary roles of the two rewards. Optimizing only the camera motion smoothness reward ($r_{\text{motion}}$) significantly stabilized camera trajectories but left geometric artifacts. Adding the geometry reprojection consistency reward ($r_{\text{geo}}$) effectively mitigated these inconsistencies, leading to more coherent scene geometry and improved perceptual quality, affirming the necessity of both terms.
- **Test-Time Reward Guidance:** The differentiable nature of the LGM enabled a training-free, gradient-based test-time guidance mechanism. Applying this guidance in latent space at selected denoising steps consistently improved geometric consistency with minimal runtime overhead, suggesting utility beyond training-time alignment.
- **Efficiency Analysis:** A direct comparison of reward computation showed that latent-space rewarding (VGGRPO) reduced peak GPU memory (from 76.80 GB to 68.57 GB) and sped up computation time (from 54.73 s to 41.33 s, a 24.5% reduction) compared to RGB-based rewarding. Furthermore, the latent geometry model demonstrated significantly higher robustness to latent perturbations compared to an RGB-based geometry model, which degraded rapidly even with visually imperceptible changes in RGB space.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The VGGRPO framework represents a notable advancement in the field of video generation by addressing the critical challenge of geometric inconsistency in an efficient and robust manner. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **World-Consistent Video Generation:** The core contribution of VGGRPO is its ability to generate videos that are not only visually high-quality but also consistently coherent in their 3D structure and camera motion. This moves beyond merely plausible appearance to geometrically faithful representations, a crucial step for many real-world applications.
- **Efficiency through Latent-Space Optimization:** By introducing the Latent Geometry Model (LGM) and performing Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) directly in the VAE latent space, VGGRPO eliminates the computational and memory overhead associated with repeated RGB decoding. This makes geometry-aware post-training significantly more efficient and scalable, transforming it from a prohibitively expensive process to a practical solution for large-scale video diffusion models.
- **Robustness to Dynamic Scenes:** Unlike many prior methods that are limited to static scenes, VGGRPO's integration of a 4D-aware geometry foundation model (like Any4D) and its corresponding reward design allows it to maintain geometric consistency even in challenging dynamic environments with complex non-rigid motion. This expands the applicability of consistent video generation to a much broader range of real-world scenarios.
- **Preservation of Generative Capacity:** A key advantage is that VGGRPO achieves geometric consistency without compromising the broad generalization and visual fidelity inherited from large-scale pretrained models. The method acts as a general-purpose regularizer that improves overall generation quality across diverse metrics and caption sets, rather than specializing at the cost of versatility.
- **Novel Reward Design:** The complementary design of the camera motion smoothness reward and the geometry reprojection consistency reward effectively targets two primary sources of inconsistency. This joint optimization strategy has been shown to be more effective than optimizing for either factor alone, leading to holistic improvements in world consistency.
- **Flexible Framework:** The LGM is model-agnostic, allowing it to be instantiated with different geometry foundation models. This flexibility ensures that VGGRPO can benefit from future advancements in geometry estimation, extending its utility and longevity.
- **Inference-Time Guidance Potential:** The differentiability of the latent geometry model opens avenues for training-free, gradient-based guidance at inference time. This provides a mechanism for users to further fine-tune geometric consistency for specific generation tasks without retraining the model, offering a practical tool for controlling generation outputs.

The collective impact of these contributions is a more practical, scalable, and versatile framework for generating geometrically consistent videos. This has direct implications for advancing research in embodied AI, robotic simulation, 3D content creation, and potentially various creative industries, by providing more reliable and controllable synthetic visual data.
