# Reinforcement Learning for Reasoning in Small LLMs: What Works and What Doesn't

> [!summary] Summary
> Knovel Engineering Lab and VNU University of Science developed a reinforcement learning approach to enhance mathematical reasoning in 1.5-billion-parameter language models. The method achieved competitive performance against larger, more expensive models, including outperforming OpenAI's o1-preview on AIME24, with an estimated training cost of only $42 using a curated dataset and rule-based rewards.

## Problem

- Advanced reasoning capabilities are typically tied to massive LLMs with immense computational demands, limiting accessibility for most researchers and organizations.
- Existing RL-based fine-tuning methods for smaller LLMs often still incur high computational costs or require extensive datasets, hindering practical deployment.
- There is a need for resource-efficient approaches to elevate reasoning performance in small LLMs (1-10 billion parameters) to democratize access to advanced AI capabilities.

## Method

- A **1.5B**-parameter model (DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B) was fine-tuned using the ==Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)== algorithm under strict constraints (4 NVIDIA A40 GPUs, 24-hour training, 1 epoch).
- A compact, high-quality mathematical reasoning dataset of **39,659** examples was meticulously curated from existing sources to maximize learning efficiency.
- A ==rule-based reward system==, comprising accuracy, cosine, and format rewards, was implemented to guide RL optimization without the need for a resource-intensive neural critic model.

## Results

- The fine-tuned models (Open-RS1, Open-RS2, Open-RS3) achieved average zero-shot pass@1 scores of **53.0%** to **56.3%** on math benchmarks, competing with and in some cases exceeding other 1.5B and even some 7B models.
- Open-RS3 achieved a **46.7%** AIME24 score, surpassing OpenAI's o1-preview (**44.6%**) and DeepScaleR-1.5B-Preview (**43.1%**) on this specific benchmark.
- The approach demonstrated significant cost and data efficiency, using only **7,000** samples and an estimated training cost of **$42**, a substantial reduction compared to baseline models costing thousands of dollars.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Small LLMs can achieve rapid reasoning improvements (within 50-100 steps) with limited high-quality data, but prolonged training under fixed length constraints can lead to performance degradation and output instability.
> - Incorporating a balanced mix of easy and hard problems stabilizes initial reasoning performance, though long-term issues like language drift in multilingual base models persist.
> - Cosine rewards are effective for stabilizing output lengths, but extending length limits is crucial for handling extremely complex reasoning tasks, especially with multilingual base models prone to language drift.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The pursuit of Artificial General Intelligence (AGI) has seen remarkable progress with the advent of Large Language Models (LLMs) such as GPT-4o, Claude 3.5 Sonnet, and Gemini 1.5. A critical component of this progress involves enhancing LLMs' reasoning capabilities, often through post-training techniques like supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and reinforcement learning (RL). These methods are crucial for improving reasoning accuracy, aligning models with human values, and adapting them to diverse user preferences, all while requiring fewer resources than the initial pre-training phase.

Within this landscape, a significant innovation is OpenAI's o1 series, which demonstrates impressive mathematical, coding, and scientific reasoning through inference-time scaling via extended Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning. However, the proprietary nature and immense computational demands of such cutting-edge models (often hundreds of billions of parameters, like DeepSeek-R1 built on the 671-billion-parameter DeepSeek-V3) present a substantial barrier to entry for the broader research community and organizations outside major tech firms. This limitation creates a critical need for more accessible and resource-efficient approaches to enhance LLM reasoning.

Previous efforts to improve reasoning have explored process-based reward models, RL approaches, and search algorithms like Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS). While these methods have yielded incremental gains, they often fall short of the general reasoning prowess of state-of-the-art large models or themselves demand significant computational investment. For instance, some RL-based fine-tuning efforts on smaller LLMs still rely on expansive datasets (hundreds of thousands to millions of samples) or incur high computational costs, undermining their practical accessibility.

This paper directly addresses this gap by focusing on **small LLMs** (1 to 10 billion parameters), which represent a resource-efficient alternative with potential for widespread deployment. The authors investigate whether powerful RL techniques, specifically adapting the GRPO algorithm used in models like DeepSeek-R1, can be effectively applied to *small LLMs under stringent resource constraints*. This work is positioned as a direct challenge to the prevailing notion that advanced reasoning capabilities are exclusively tied to massive models and exorbitant computational budgets, aiming to democratize access to high-performance AI reasoning. By focusing on a 1.5-billion-parameter model and strict time/hardware limits, the research aims to provide a blueprint for achieving competitive reasoning abilities in resource-constrained environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The research is fundamentally driven by the challenge of bridging the gap between the advanced reasoning capabilities of state-of-the-art LLMs and the practical limitations faced by most organizations in terms of computational resources and budget. This tension crystallizes into two central research questions:

1. **How do small LLMs behave when fine-tuned under strict resource constraints, such as limited computational power and training time?** This question probes the scalability and deployment feasibility of smaller models in real-world, restricted settings.
2. **Can their reasoning performance be elevated using an RL-based approach akin to DeepSeek-R1’s methodology, and if so, how?** This investigates the direct applicability and effectiveness of advanced RL techniques, traditionally used for much larger models, on more compact architectures.

These questions naturally extend to a practical inquiry: If viable, how should such an approach be implemented for small LLMs, and if not, what are the fundamental limitations? The overarching motivation is to develop **cost-effective and accessible alternatives** to large-scale, proprietary LLMs for complex reasoning tasks. The authors aim to demonstrate that significant reasoning gains can be achieved in small models with minimal resources, thereby contributing to the democratization of advanced AI technologies and enabling their deployment in resource-limited environments where self-hosting large models is infeasible.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors adopted a multi-faceted approach to optimize the reasoning capabilities of small LLMs under strict computational constraints.

**Base Model Selection:**
The study utilized **DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B**, a 1.5-billion-parameter model distilled from larger architectures. This choice reflects a strategic balance between efficiency and reasoning potential. Crucially, the authors bypassed the typical Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) phase, hypothesizing that the model's pre-training was sufficient to directly leverage Reinforcement Learning (RL).

**Computational Constraints:**
Training was conducted on a cluster of **4 NVIDIA A40 GPUs (48GB VRAM each)**, within a strict **24-hour window**. These constraints limited the number of sampled outputs per step to 6 and the maximum completion length to 4096 tokens (later reduced to 3584 tokens in subsequent experiments). The total training was restricted to a single epoch.

**Reinforcement Learning Algorithm:**
The **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)** algorithm (Shao et al. 2024), as implemented in DeepSeek-R1, was adapted for this study. GRPO is computationally efficient as it eliminates the need for a separate, often equally large, critic model by estimating baselines directly from group scores. This makes it particularly suitable for resource-constrained environments. The objective function involves maximizing the expected reward while incorporating a clipping mechanism and a KL-divergence penalty term to maintain policy stability and prevent excessive deviation from a reference policy.

**High-Quality Dataset Curation:**
To maximize reasoning performance while minimizing training costs, a compact, high-quality **mathematical reasoning dataset** was curated. This dataset was derived from two existing sources:
- **s1 dataset:** Initially 59,029 general-purpose reasoning questions. Filtering steps included:
 1. Retaining questions with solutions containing the LaTeX command `\boxed{}` (31,323 examples).
 2. Using DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B to remove trivial questions (21,533 examples).
 3. Using Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct to remove noisy or multi-part questions, resulting in **18,615 examples (open-s1)**.
- **DeepScaleR dataset:** Initially 40,315 mathematics-specific questions. Refinement included:
 1. Using Qwen2.5-Math-7B-Instruct to exclude easy questions, resulting in **21,044 examples (open-deepscaler)**.
The final dataset combined these two refined sets, totaling **39,659 high-quality mathematical reasoning questions**. This curated corpus aimed for a balance of scale and specificity for efficient training. For the experiments, smaller subsets were often used (e.g., 7,000 samples for Experiments 2 and 3).

**Reward Models:**
A **rule-based reward system** was implemented to guide RL optimization, deliberately avoiding resource-intensive neural reward models. It comprised three components:
- **Accuracy Reward:** A binary score (1 for correct, 0 for incorrect), requiring the final answer in `\boxed{}` format for objective verification.
- **Cosine Reward:** Scales the accuracy reward based on response length. Shorter correct solutions receive higher rewards, while longer incorrect solutions are penalized less severely, incentivizing concise yet accurate reasoning.
- **Format Reward:** Awards a positive score for enclosing the reasoning process within `<think>` and `</think>` tags, enforcing structural clarity.

**Experimental Design:**
Three distinct experiments were conducted to analyze the training behavior of small LLMs:
1. **Experiment 1: Impact of High-Quality Data.** Training on the open-s1 dataset (18,615 samples) with accuracy and format rewards, max completion length 4096 tokens.
2. **Experiment 2: Balancing Easy and Hard Problems.** Training on a 7,000-sample dataset (mix of open-s1, open-deepscaler, and easier DeepScaleR problems) with accuracy and format rewards, max completion length 3584 tokens.
3. **Experiment 3: Controlling Length with Cosine Reward.** Training on the same 7,000-sample dataset as Experiment 2, but replacing accuracy reward with cosine reward and adding a "Reply in English only" system prompt instruction, max completion length 3584 tokens.

**Evaluation:**
Performance was measured using the **zero-shot pass@1 metric** across five mathematics-focused benchmark datasets: AIME24, MATH-500, AMC23, Minerva, and OlympiadBench. This metric assesses the model's ability to solve problems correctly on the first attempt without prior examples, emphasizing intrinsic reasoning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments yielded several key insights into the behavior and performance of small LLMs fine-tuned with RL under strict resource constraints.

**Experiment 1: Impact of High-Quality Data**
- **Observation:** The model achieved rapid reasoning improvements on AMC23 (63% to 70%) and MATH-500 (83% to 84%) within the first 50–100 global steps.
- **Challenge:** After approximately 200 global steps, performance significantly degraded, dropping below 60% on AMC23 and to 80% on MATH-500. This degradation was accompanied by unstable accuracy rewards, fluctuating completion lengths (initially decreasing then increasing beyond 4000 tokens), and the emergence of unreadable, non-English outputs.
- **Insight 1:** Small LLMs can achieve rapid reasoning improvements with limited high-quality data within 50–100 steps, but performance degrades with prolonged training under strict length constraints, indicating challenges with handling complexity and output coherence.

**Experiment 2: Balancing Easy and Hard Problems**
- **Observation:** By incorporating a mix of easy and hard problems in a 7,000-sample dataset and reducing the max completion length to 3584 tokens, initial performance improved significantly. AMC23 accuracy rose from 63% to 80%, and MATH-500 from 83% to 85% within 50–100 steps. Initial completion lengths dropped to ~2800 tokens.
- **Challenge:** Similar to Experiment 1, performance declined after 150–200 steps (around 4000 local steps), accompanied by unstable KL divergence and the reemergence of mixed-language outputs.
- **Insight 2:** Incorporating a mix of easy and hard problems under reduced length constraints enhances early performance and stabilizes reasoning behavior, though long-term stability and multilingual tendencies remain persistent challenges.

**Experiment 3: Controlling Length with Cosine Reward**
- **Observation:** Using the cosine reward function successfully stabilized completion lengths, keeping them consistently between 1000 and 3500 tokens. Performance on AMC23 and MATH-500 increased modestly compared to the baseline (63% to 72.5% and 83% to 84.4%, respectively) within 50 steps.
- **Challenge:** While length was controlled, the peak accuracy lagged behind Experiment 2. Critically, mixed-language content persisted after 200 steps despite a system prompt instruction to "Reply in English only," indicating the deep-seated multilingual nature of the base model.
- **Insight 3:** Cosine rewards effectively stabilize completion lengths, improving training consistency. However, extending length limits is necessary for extremely hard tasks, especially when working with multilingual base models that exhibit language drift.

**Overall Comparison and Cost Efficiency:**
The three best checkpoints from the experiments (Open-RS1, Open-RS2, Open-RS3) were evaluated against various general-purpose and mathematics-focused baseline models (Table 1).

- **Performance:**
 - Our models achieved average zero-shot pass@1 scores of 53.0% (Open-RS1), 55.7% (Open-RS2), and 56.3% (Open-RS3). This is highly competitive with, and in some cases surpasses, other 1.5B models (e.g., DeepScaleR-1.5B-Preview at 57.0%) and even some 7B models.
 - **Open-RS3** achieved the highest AIME24 score (46.7%), **outperforming OpenAI's o1-preview (44.6%)** and DeepScaleR-1.5B-Preview (43.1%).
 - **Open-RS2** excelled on AMC23 (80.0%), and both Open-RS1 and Open-RS2 outperformed DeepScaleR-1.5B-Preview on OlympiadBench.
 - Minerva scores lagged behind 7B models, indicating continued challenges with cross-disciplinary reasoning in smaller models.

- **Cost and Data Efficiency:**
 - The most striking result is the efficiency: Our approach used only **7,000 samples** (generating 6 outputs per step, totaling 42,000 samples over one epoch) and incurred an estimated **training cost of approximately $42** on 4x A40 GPUs over 24 hours.
 - In stark contrast, baseline 7B models like Qwen2.5-7B-SimpleRL cost $1633 and Eurus-2-7B-PRIME cost $1088. Even other 1.5B models such as DeepScaleR-1.5B-Preview and Still-3-1.5B-Preview incurred costs of $3629 and $2268, respectively, and required significantly more data (e.g., 40,000 x 16 samples for DeepScaleR).

These findings demonstrate that small LLMs can achieve competitive reasoning performance with remarkably minimal data and computational cost, presenting a scalable and highly cost-effective alternative to resource-intensive larger models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This study makes several significant contributions to the field of large language models and artificial intelligence, particularly concerning the democratization and accessibility of advanced AI capabilities.

**Democratization of Advanced AI:** The most profound impact of this research lies in its demonstration that sophisticated reasoning abilities, typically associated with massive, resource-intensive LLMs, can be effectively cultivated in **small LLMs (1.5 billion parameters) under stringent computational and temporal constraints**. Achieving performance competitive with, and in some cases surpassing, larger and more expensively trained models (e.g., Open-RS3 outperforming o1-preview on AIME24) at a fraction of the cost ($42 vs. thousands of dollars) is a critical step towards **democratizing advanced AI technologies**. This means that organizations and researchers with limited budgets and infrastructure can now realistically aspire to develop and deploy AI models capable of complex reasoning, rather than being solely dependent on proprietary, large-scale models from major tech companies.

**Efficiency and Scalability:** The work provides compelling evidence for the efficacy of **RL-based fine-tuning** for small LLMs, offering a highly cost-effective alternative to traditional large-scale approaches. The compact, high-quality mathematical reasoning dataset and the use of the GRPO algorithm (which eliminates the need for a separate critic model) are key components of this efficiency. This research lays a foundation for developing lightweight, reasoning-capable LLMs suitable for resource-constrained environments, making them more scalable and deployable across a wider range of applications, including those with privacy concerns where self-hosting is essential.

**Practical Insights into LLM Training:** The three experiments yield actionable insights into the nuances of training small LLMs with RL:
- **Rapid Gains:** Small, high-quality datasets can quickly bootstrap reasoning capabilities, highlighting the importance of data curation.
- **Optimization Instability:** The observed performance degradation with prolonged training under fixed length constraints points to critical challenges in managing the optimization process and preventing "catastrophic forgetting" or reward hacking.
- **Length Management:** The study underscores the importance of output length control (e.g., via cosine rewards) and the limitations of predefined completion lengths for complex reasoning tasks.
- **Multilingual Challenges:** The persistent language drift in the multilingual base model highlights an often-overlooked challenge in fine-tuning such models for monolingual tasks, suggesting a need for more robust language control mechanisms.

**Open Science and Reproducibility:** The authors' decision to release their code and curated datasets as open-source resources significantly contributes to the research community. This commitment fosters reproducibility, encourages further exploration, and provides a valuable foundation for future work on efficient reasoning in LLMs, allowing others to build upon these findings and address the identified challenges.

**Future Directions:** While the study demonstrates significant promise, it also highlights areas for future research:
- **Extended Training and Adaptive Lengths:** Investigating longer training durations or dynamic length scheduling could mitigate truncation issues for complex problems.
- **Monolingual Optimization:** Developing lightweight language rewards or employing monolingual pre-filtering could address language drift in multilingual base models.
- **Generalizability:** Expanding evaluation to non-mathematical domains (e.g., scientific reasoning, coding) would test the broader applicability of the approach.
- **Hybrid Methods:** Combining GRPO with search algorithms like MCTS could potentially deepen reasoning capacity without excessive resource demands.

In conclusion, this paper presents a compelling argument that advanced reasoning capabilities are not solely the domain of massive, expensive LLMs. By meticulously exploring the application of RL to small models under strict resource constraints, the authors have demonstrated a viable path towards accessible, high-performance AI, setting a new benchmark for efficiency and opening exciting avenues for future research and widespread adoption.
