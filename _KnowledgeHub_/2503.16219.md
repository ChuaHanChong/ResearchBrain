# Reinforcement Learning for Reasoning in Small LLMs: What Works and What Doesn't

> [!summary] Summary
> Knovel Engineering Lab and VNU University of Science developed a reinforcement learning approach to enhance mathematical reasoning in 1.5-billion-parameter language models. The method achieved competitive performance against larger, more expensive models, including outperforming OpenAI's o1-preview on AIME24, with an estimated training cost of only $42 using a curated dataset and rule-based rewards.

## Problem

- Advanced reasoning capabilities are typically tied to massive LLMs with immense computational demands, limiting accessibility for most researchers and organizations.
- Existing RL-based fine-tuning methods for smaller LLMs often still incur high computational costs or require extensive datasets, hindering practical deployment.
- There is a need for resource-efficient approaches to elevate reasoning performance in small LLMs (1-10 billion parameters) to democratize access to advanced AI capabilities.

## Method

- A **1.5B**-parameter model (DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B) was fine-tuned using the ==Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)== algorithm under strict constraints (4 NVIDIA A40 GPUs, 24-hour training, 1 epoch).
- A compact, high-quality mathematical reasoning dataset of **39,659** examples was meticulously curated from existing sources to maximize learning efficiency.
- A ==rule-based reward system==, comprising accuracy, cosine, and format rewards, was implemented to guide RL optimization without the need for a resource-intensive neural critic model.

## Results

- The fine-tuned models (Open-RS1, Open-RS2, Open-RS3) achieved average zero-shot pass@1 scores of **53.0%** to **56.3%** on math benchmarks, competing with and in some cases exceeding other 1.5B and even some 7B models.
- Open-RS3 achieved a **46.7%** AIME24 score, surpassing OpenAI's o1-preview (**44.6%**) and DeepScaleR-1.5B-Preview (**43.1%**) on this specific benchmark.
- The approach demonstrated significant cost and data efficiency, using only **7,000** samples and an estimated training cost of **$42**, a substantial reduction compared to baseline models costing thousands of dollars.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Small LLMs can achieve rapid reasoning improvements (within 50-100 steps) with limited high-quality data, but prolonged training under fixed length constraints can lead to performance degradation and output instability.
> - Incorporating a balanced mix of easy and hard problems stabilizes initial reasoning performance, though long-term issues like language drift in multilingual base models persist.
> - Cosine rewards are effective for stabilizing output lengths, but extending length limits is crucial for handling extremely complex reasoning tasks, especially with multilingual base models prone to language drift.
