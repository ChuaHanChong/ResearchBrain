# Large Video Planner Enables Generalizable Robot Control

> [!summary] Summary
> A research team from MIT and UC Berkeley developed the Large Video Planner (LVP), a 14-billion parameter video foundation model trained on a curated 1.4 million video dataset (LVP-1M), to enable generalizable robot control. The LVP achieved 59.3% average success for "Task complete" on 100 novel "in-the-wild" tasks and demonstrated superior zero-shot task-level generalization on real robots compared to existing Vision-Language-Action models.

## Problem

- Developing general-purpose robots capable of reliably operating across diverse tasks and novel, unseen environments remains a significant challenge in embodied AI.
- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) robot foundation models suffer from limited generalization due to their reliance on scarce robot action data, leading to suboptimal performance in novel scenarios.
- The 'asymmetric transfer' paradigm, where broad knowledge from MLLMs is finetuned on small robot datasets, fails to effectively bridge the gap to robust embodied control.

## Method

- A large video foundation model, the Large Video Planner (LVP) with 14 billion parameters, is developed using a latent diffusion framework enhanced for temporal coherence.
- LVP is trained on LVP-1M, a curated 1.4 million video dataset emphasizing diverse human and robot interactions with action-centric annotations.
- The system extracts executable robot actions from generated video plans by estimating human hand motion via 4D reconstruction and pose estimation, followed by retargeting to robot grippers or dexterous hands.

## Results

- On 100 novel "in-the-wild" tasks, LVP achieved an average success rate of 59.3% for "Task complete" and 44.0% for "Perfect task complete" in video plan generation, outperforming baselines like Wan 2.1 (39.3% and 13.5% respectively).
- Real-world robot experiments demonstrated LVP's superior zero-shot generalization, with 6 out of 10 successes on out-of-distribution parallel-gripper tasks, whereas VLA baselines achieved 1 or 0 successes.
- The approach successfully enabled dexterous manipulation with multi-fingered hands, performing complex tasks like scooping coffee beans and tearing tape, in addition to standard gripper tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Videos, with their inherent spatio-temporal sequences of states and actions, provide a more direct and effective modality for pretraining generalizable robot foundation models than static images and language.
> - Large-scale online video data, implicitly encoding actionable information, offers a scalable and rich source for robot learning, mirroring the success of MLLMs.
> - Techniques like Diffusion Forcing and History Guidance are crucial for generating temporally coherent and causally consistent video plans that accurately depict instructed actions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of general-purpose robots capable of operating reliably across diverse tasks and novel environments represents a significant challenge in embodied intelligence. A key component for such robots is a planning algorithm that can interpret unseen tasks, adapt to new scenes, and generate physically coherent behaviors.

Recent advancements in foundation models, particularly Large Language Models (LLMs) and Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), have demonstrated broad competence through large-scale data pretraining. This success has inspired the development of "robot foundation models," often instantiated as Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. These VLAs extend MLLMs by incorporating an action output modality, aiming to transfer pretraining knowledge from language and images to robotic control.

However, existing VLA models face limitations. They primarily rely on scarce robot action data, which is not comparable in scale to the text and image datasets used for MLLMs. This leads to an "asymmetric transfer" where pretrained MLLM knowledge is finetuned on a small amount of robot-specific data, resulting in suboptimal generalization to new robot tasks in unseen situations.

This work proposes an alternative paradigm. Instead of building upon MLLMs and their image/language pretraining, it advocates for using large-scale *video pretraining* as the primary modality for constructing robot foundation models. The rationale is that videos inherently capture spatio-temporal sequences of states and actions, which are directly aligned with physical world interactions and robotic behavior. This contrasts with static images and language, offering a richer representation of continuous actions. Furthermore, video data is abundant online, containing implicit action information that can be leveraged for pretraining, akin to how MLLMs benefit from vast text and image corpora. This video-centric approach aims to provide a more directly grounded source of knowledge transfer to embodied tasks compared to the asymmetric transfer of VLAs.

The paper differentiates its approach from other lines of research involving video generation for robotics. While some work uses video generation for visual policies or as dynamics models to predict future frames conditioned on actions, this research frames video generation as a *visual motion planner* that produces realistic interaction videos from which executable actions are subsequently extracted and retargeted, thereby forming a unified robot foundation model.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable generalizable robot control by developing decision-making models that can operate across diverse tasks and novel environments. The core motivation stems from identified limitations in current robot foundation models, specifically Vision-Language-Action (VLA) systems.

The key motivations and objectives are detailed as follows:

- **Addressing Generalization Limitations in VLA Models:** Existing VLA models, while inspired by the success of MLLMs, face challenges in achieving strong generalization. This is attributed to the scarcity of large-scale robot action data compared to the vast internet-scale text and image datasets used for MLLMs. As a result, current VLAs rely on an "asymmetric transfer" strategy, where broad pre-trained knowledge from MLLMs is fine-tuned on relatively narrow robot interaction data. This often leads to poor performance when robots encounter novel tasks or operate in unseen situations. The paper aims to overcome this limitation by proposing an alternative pretraining paradigm.

- **Leveraging Video as a Primary Modality:** The central motivation is to establish video as the foundational data type for robot learning. Unlike static images and language, videos naturally capture the temporal progression of states and actions, providing an inherent encoding of physical world dynamics and agent-environment interactions. This spatio-temporal richness is considered more aligned with robotic behavior than image-text pairs. The objective is to harness this inherent property of video to train more effective and generalizable robot foundation models.

- **Exploiting Internet-Scale Video Data Abundance:** The internet contains a vast amount of video data depicting human activities, instructional tutorials, and task demonstrations. Each such video implicitly encodes actionable information. The motivation is to leverage this abundant, naturally occurring data, mirroring the successful large-scale pretraining paradigm of LLMs and MLLMs, but directly focused on action dynamics. This provides a scalable data source that can offer a stronger bridge to downstream embodied tasks compared to traditional robot datasets.

- **Developing a Purpose-Built Video Foundation Model for Embodied AI:** The research aims to develop a novel video generative model, named Large Video Planner (LVP), specifically designed for embodied decision-making and action extraction. Unlike existing video generation models often optimized for content creation or aesthetic quality, LVP is motivated by the need to prioritize physical consistency, adherence to real-world image observations, and faithful execution of task instructions. It is designed to take textual instructions and initial observation frame(s) as input and generate predictive video plans from which executable actions can be reliably extracted.

- **Curating a Specialized Dataset (LVP-1M):** A critical objective is to address the data requirements of this new paradigm. The authors are motivated to curate and release a large-scale, open dataset (LVP-1M) that emphasizes diverse object interactions and is paired with action-centric text annotations. This dataset is meticulously processed from a mix of internet and robot videos, ensuring high temporal coherence and tight alignment with language instructions, which is crucial for learning robust embodied behaviors.

- **Enhancing Temporal Coherence and Causal Consistency:** The success of video-based planning relies heavily on the temporal coherence and causal consistency of the generated video frames. The research is motivated to integrate specific model design advancements, such as History Guidance and Diffusion Forcing, to explicitly enhance these properties. These techniques aim to ensure that generated video plans are not only plausible but also temporally smooth and logically consistent in their depiction of actions.

- **Demonstrating Zero-Shot Task-Level Generalization:** A key objective is to rigorously evaluate the model's ability to perform drastically different tasks it has never encountered during training. This involves a strong form of generalization, moving beyond object-level or configuration-level variations. The motivation is to use an independent evaluation protocol involving third-party selected, challenging, and diverse tasks in "in-the-wild" settings, followed by real-robot experiments to validate end-to-end physical execution.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on a two-stage process: generating a video plan using a large video foundation model and then extracting executable robot actions from that plan.

#### 3.1. Large Video Planner (LVP): A Video Foundation Model for Generative Planning

The LVP is a 14-billion parameter video foundation model constructed using a latent diffusion framework with specialized enhancements for temporal coherence.

- **Latent Diffusion Framework:**
 - **Video Compression:** A temporally causal 3D Variational Autoencoder (VAE) compresses input video clips (e.g., [1+T, 3, H, W] frames) into a compact, lower-dimensional latent representation (e.g., [1+⌈T/4⌉, 16, ⌈H/4⌉, ⌈W/4⌉]). The first frame is repeated four times before compression to facilitate co-training with single-frame image data. The VAE is then frozen.
 - **Diffusion Model Training:** A video diffusion model, a variant of Diffusion Transformer (DiT), is trained in this compressed latent space. It uses a flow matching objective with a shifted schedule to emphasize higher noise levels. The model is trained to predict the flow (difference between noise and clean latent) conditioned on the noisy latent, an input image, a text instruction, and the noise level.

- **Diffusion Forcing Transformer:**
 - **Flexible Conditioning:** To improve temporal coherence and allow conditioning on initial frames (image-to-video, I2V) or multiple preceding frames (video-to-video, V2V), a modified Diffusion Forcing framework is adopted. Instead of adding uniform noise, independent noise levels are applied to different segments of the video during training.
 - **Training Strategy:** A random history length (0 to 6 latent frames) is sampled, splitting the video into history and future segments. Independent noise levels are applied to each segment. With 50% probability, the history segment's noise level is set to zero, enabling the model to learn to condition on a clean context. This approach unifies I2V and V2V training, is compatible with existing DiT weights (e.g., WAN 2.1 14B), and eliminates the need for extra cross-attention layers.
 - **Contextual Inputs:** The model cross-attends to CLIP features of the first frame and text embeddings extracted by a UMT5 encoder. Mask and guidance channels for image conditioning, present in baseline WAN models, are removed.

- **Enhanced Temporal Coherence with History Guidance:**
 - LVP employs history guidance, an extension of Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG), to improve adherence to context frames.
 - During sampling, a combined score is used: `s_final = (1 + w_hist)∇ log p(x_k|x_hist, c_text) - w_hist∇ log p(x_k|c_text) + (1 + w_text)∇ log p(x_k|x_hist, c_text) - w_text∇ log p(x_k|x_hist)`. This linearly combines scores estimated with and without history conditioning and text conditioning, enhancing both temporal grounding and instruction following.

- **Autoregressive Extension:** The model can extend previously generated or captured video segments autoregressively, using up to 24 frames (6 latent frames) as context. This enables the generation of multi-stage video plans.

- **Training Protocol:**
 - **Stage 1 (Continue Pretraining):** Initializes from WAN I2V 14B weights, discards mask/image guidance channels. Trained for 60,000 steps with a batch size of 128 (200 billion tokens total), focusing on dynamics and instruction following.
 - **Stage 2 (Low Camera Motion Finetuning):** A smaller subset of videos from Ego4D, Epic-Kitchens, and Panda datasets, specifically filtered for low optical flow magnitude, is used for an additional 10,000 steps of finetuning. This stage aims to reduce unwanted camera motion and improve temporal smoothness.
 - Total training time was approximately 14 days using 128 H100 SXM5 GPUs.

#### 3.2. LVP-1M: A Video Dataset of Human and Robot Actions

To train LVP, a large-scale, open dataset, LVP-1M, was curated from diverse sources, totaling 1.4 million short clips.

- **Video Sources:**
 - **Web Crawls:** Filtered subset of Pandas 70M for human interactions.
 - **Egocentric Human Activity:** Ego4D, Epic Kitchens, Something-something, providing human-object interactions.
 - **Robotics Datasets:** Bridge, Droid, Language Table, AgiBot-World, featuring teleoperated robots.
- **Data Curation Pipeline:**
 1. **Aggressive Filtering:** Clips are removed if they are low-resolution, too short/long, poorly lit, or exhibit rapid camera motions (based on optical flow statistics). Object detectors ensure the embodiment (hand or gripper) is visible in the first frame. Suboptimal robot trajectories are also filtered out. A multi-stage filtering process using keywords and Gemini-2.0 Flash is applied to the Pandas-70M subset to focus on human hand interactions.
 2. **Temporal Alignment:** All video clips are resampled and aligned to a standardized human-speed of 3 seconds at 16 frames per second, regardless of original frame rate or teleoperation speed. Long tasks are segmented into atomic actions, and clips are trimmed precisely.
 3. **Action-Centric Re-Captioning:** Gemini Flash is used to generate multiple (2-5) high-quality, action-centric captions for each video, expanding on short or missing original annotations to enhance linguistic diversity and instruction following.
- **Dataset Composition:** The final dataset comprises 1.4 million clips, with a reweighted sampling strategy during training to balance contributions from different sources.

#### 3.3. Robot Actions from Video Plans

The generated video plans, depicting a human hand or robot gripper executing a task, are converted into executable robot actions through a multi-stage pipeline.

- **Human Hand Motion Estimation:**
 1. **Per-frame Hand Pose Estimation:** HaMeR independently predicts MANO hand mesh vertices and a global wrist orientation (`R_t`) for each video frame.
 2. **4D Consistent Alignment:** MegaSAM, a 4D reconstruction model, is used to estimate per-frame depth maps, camera intrinsics (`K`), and extrinsics. This information refines HaMeR's translation estimates by backprojecting hand pixels into 3D using the depth and intrinsics, enforcing temporal smoothness, and resolving monocular scale ambiguity. The first frame's camera coordinate system serves as the world reference.
 3. **Temporal Completion and Smoothing:** Missing frames are linearly interpolated. Quaternions use SLERP for continuity, and a causal Savitzky-Golay filter is applied to positions and quaternion components.

- **Robot Finger Motion Retargeting:**
 1. **Dexterous Hands:** Dex-Retargeting is used to map human hand keypoints (detected from RGB) to robot joint configurations by solving an optimization objective, yielding robot finger joint angles.
 2. **Parallel-Jaw Grippers:** GraspNet predicts candidate grasp poses based on scene geometry. Grasp execution is triggered by heuristics detecting grasping intent from the human hand motion.

- **Real-Robot Execution:**
 1. **Camera-to-Robot Alignment:** The recovered wrist poses (translation and rotation) are transformed from the initial camera coordinate frame (`C_0`) into the robot control frame (`R`) using an extrinsic calibration (fixed rotation `M` and translation `t`).
 2. **Robot Wrist and Finger Execution:** For the robot arm, cuRobo solves inverse kinematics to generate arm joint trajectories that follow the transformed wrist poses. The retargeted finger joint sequences are directly sent to the robot hand controller. The robot control API executes these synchronized arm and hand trajectories. The overall execution framework operates in an open-loop manner.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation focused on assessing the model's zero-shot task-level generalization, meaning its ability to perform substantially novel tasks not explicitly encountered during training.

#### 4.1. Third-Party Selection of Novel Tasks

- **Data Collection:** An independent evaluation protocol involved crowdsourcing approximately 200 manipulation tasks from third-party participants. Participants were instructed to propose short (3–5 seconds) tasks from their everyday surroundings, provide a photo of the scene with the hand and target object, and a brief text description, emphasizing diversity and challenge.
- **Quality Filtering:** A separate group of third-party annotators filtered these submissions, resulting in a set of 100 high-quality tasks, each comprising an observation image and a refined instruction text.

#### 4.2. Evaluating Video Motion Planning (LVP Standalone Performance)

The LVP's ability to generate video plans was qualitatively and quantitatively assessed against baseline video generation models.

- **Qualitative Examples:** Figure 6 presents generated video plans for various "in-the-wild" tasks. Figure 7 illustrates the model's capability for multi-stage planning through autoregressive video extension, showing sequentially generated action plans.
- **Baseline Comparison:** LVP was compared with three video generation baselines: Wan 2.1 I2V 14B, Cosmos-Predict 2 14B, and Hunyuan I2V 13B. Each method generated four videos per prompt for the 100-task test set.
- **Four-Level Evaluation Metric:** Third-party annotators scored the generated videos based on:
 1. **Level 1: Correct contact:** Hand makes contact with the specified object at a correct location.
 2. **Level 2: Correct end state:** The final frame achieves the instructed goal (motion quality not considered).
 3. **Level 3: Task complete:** Correct contact and correct end state, with plausible, continuous motion (minor physics artifacts allowed).
 4. **Level 4: Perfect task complete:** Task completed with visually flawless physics and no noticeable artifacts.
- **Quantitative Results (Table 2):**
 - LVP demonstrated higher success rates across all four levels compared to the baselines.
 - Notable gains were observed at higher difficulty levels: For "Task complete" (Level 3), LVP achieved an average success rate of 59.3% and a Best@4 rate of 82.0%, significantly outperforming Wan 2.1 (39.3% average, 76.0% Best@4), Hunyuan (13.5% average, 42.0% Best@4), and Cosmos-Predict 2 (7.5% average, 24.0% Best@4).
 - At the highest level, "Perfect task complete" (Level 4), LVP achieved 44.0% average success and 71.0% Best@4, indicating its ability to generate physically viable and visually coherent motion plans for novel tasks in uncontrolled settings.
 - The results suggest that while baselines could initiate correct interactions (high Level 1 scores), they struggled to produce coherent, task-complete motion trajectories. LVP exhibited a stronger grasp of contact dynamics, goal reasoning, and end-to-end plan generation.

#### 4.3. Evaluating Real-World Robot Manipulation

The complete pipeline, from video generation to action extraction and physical execution, was evaluated on real robotic platforms.

- **Robot Platforms:** Experiments were conducted on a Franka Emika Arm with a parallel-jaw gripper and a Unitree G1 Arm equipped with an Inspire dexterous hand.
- **Task Sets:** Tasks included standard manipulation (e.g., pick-and-place, open drawer) for the parallel gripper, and fine-grained dexterous manipulation (e.g., open door, scoop coffee beans, tear tape) for the dexterous hand. An out-of-distribution (OOD) task set for the gripper involved novel objects and scenes.
- **Baselines:** Performance was compared against existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models: π₀ and OpenVLA. These baselines were only tested on parallel-gripper tasks due to compatibility limitations with dexterous hands.
- **Results (Table 8, Figure 9):**
 - LVP demonstrated strong zero-shot generalization and successful execution on a range of challenging novel tasks, including those requiring dexterous manipulation like scooping coffee beans and tearing tape.
 - It consistently outperformed the VLA baselines across task suites. For instance, in "Pick Objects (OOD Scene)" for the parallel gripper, LVP achieved 6/10 successes, whereas π₀ managed 1/10 and OpenVLA 0/10.
 - Baselines exhibited strong performance on tasks similar to their training distributions (e.g., picking up common objects) but showed limited generalization to task-level variations or entirely new tasks.
 - The findings suggest that models trained on imitation learning with limited task diversity may struggle to generalize robustly to novel scenarios, highlighting the advantage of LVP's video-based approach.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a notable shift in the paradigm for developing robot foundation models, with several potential impacts on the field of embodied AI.

- **Validation of Video-Centric Robot Learning:** The work effectively demonstrates the feasibility and advantages of using large-scale video pretraining as a primary modality for robot foundation models. This offers an alternative to approaches that predominantly leverage vision-language pretraining from static images and text, suggesting that the spatio-temporal richness of video data provides a more direct and effective source of knowledge for embodied control. This validation could inspire increased research into video-based learning for robotics.

- **Enhanced Generalization Capabilities:** The Large Video Planner (LVP) exhibits notable zero-shot task-level generalization, a capability that has been a significant challenge for existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. By successfully generating plans and executing tasks never explicitly seen during training, LVP pushes the boundaries of how generalizable robotic policies can be, potentially enabling robots to perform a wider array of novel tasks in uncontrolled, real-world environments.

- **Foundation for Open Research:** The release of the LVP model, the curated LVP-1M dataset, and the associated training code represents a significant contribution to the open science community. This promotes reproducibility and provides valuable resources for other researchers to build upon, fostering further exploration and development in video-based robot learning and generalist robot policies.

- **Real-World Feasibility for Diverse Morphologies:** The successful deployment of actions extracted from generated video plans on multiple physical robotic platforms, including a parallel-jaw gripper and a dexterous multi-fingered hand, demonstrates the practical applicability and real-world feasibility of the approach. This suggests that video-based planning can be retargeted to various robot embodiments, broadening the potential impact across different robotic systems.

- **Inspiration for Future Embodied AI Systems:** By demonstrating robust instruction following, strong generalization, and real-world execution, this work lays a foundation for future embodied AI systems. It suggests a path towards robots that can understand high-level human instructions and autonomously devise physically plausible action sequences in complex, unseen scenarios.

However, the paper acknowledges several limitations that highlight areas for future development:
- **Inference Speed:** The generation of a single video plan currently takes several minutes on high-performance GPUs, which precludes real-time deployment on robots. Addressing this through techniques like step-distillation or causal video models is necessary for practical applications.
- **Action Extraction Robustness:** The reliance on open-source models for 4D reconstruction and hand pose estimation introduces potential points of failure, which can lead to task execution errors. Improvements in the robustness and accuracy of these perception modules are required.
- **Retargeting Challenges:** Retargeting human hand motions to robots, especially to parallel-jaw grippers with fewer degrees of freedom or to certain dexterous hands with specific kinematic constraints, remains challenging.
- **Open-Loop Execution:** The current robot execution framework operates in an open-loop manner, which may not be sufficient for complex, dexterous tasks that require real-time feedback and adaptation. Integrating closed-loop control mechanisms would enhance performance and robustness.
