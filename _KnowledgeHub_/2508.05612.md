# Shuffle-R1: Efficient RL framework for Multimodal Large Language Models via Data-centric Dynamic Shuffle

> [!summary] Summary
> The Shuffle-R1 framework optimizes reinforcement learning fine-tuning for Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) by dynamically structuring trajectory sampling and batch composition. This data-centric approach enhances training efficiency and performance, achieving higher accuracy on various benchmarks with significantly fewer training steps and reduced computational overhead.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning (RL) for MLLMs suffers from 'Advantage Collapsing,' where most computed advantages in a batch are near zero, diluting informative gradient signals.
- A second issue, 'Rollout Silencing,' involves a consistent decline in the proportion of rollouts contributing non-zero gradients as training progresses, leading to wasted computational resources.
- Standard RL pipelines use a static sampling paradigm, overlooking the varying informativeness of learning signals and the potential for reusing valuable data.

## Method

- ==Pairwise Trajectory Sampling== (PTS) selects trajectories with stronger learning signals by generating an extended rollout pool and forming max-min contrastive pairs based on sorted advantages, then selecting top-k pairs for gradient updates.
- Advantage-based Batch Shuffle (ABS) dynamically reshapes training batches by assigning importance weights to trajectory pairs based on the sum of their absolute advantages. It then sub-samples and reshuffles these pairs, allowing high-advantage trajectories to be sampled and reused more frequently.
- The framework theoretically demonstrates that PTS and ABS amplify the expectation of the advantage and the gradient norm, leading to larger update magnitudes and a positive bias towards high-advantage trajectories.

## Results

- Shuffle-R1 **7B** achieved a **1.4%** accuracy gain over DAPO on Geometry3K and was competitive with proprietary models like GPT-4o on several out-of-domain benchmarks.
- The framework converged to comparable performance as GRPO in approximately half the training steps and with about **60%** of the total wall-clock time, incurring only **4%** to **7.7%** additional GPU time.
- It demonstrated scalability across Qwen2.5-VL models (**3B**, **7B**, **32B**) and showed consistent performance improvements across diverse tasks including visual reasoning, referring expression comprehension, and language-only reasoning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dynamically prioritizing and reusing high-advantage trajectories can significantly mitigate the issues of Advantage Collapsing and Rollout Silencing in RL training.
> - A data-centric approach, focusing on *which* data is learned from and *how* it's presented, is as crucial as optimizing the learning algorithm or reward function for efficient RL fine-tuning.
> - Structured contrastive sampling and advantage-aware batch shuffling concentrate gradient exposure on informative samples, leading to more effective and stable policy updates.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has become an established post-training methodology for enhancing the reasoning capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs) and Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). This includes applications in complex reasoning domains such as mathematical problem solving, code generation, and various multimodal tasks like object detection, reasoning segmentation, and video understanding. Recent advancements, such as DeepSeek-R1, have demonstrated the efficacy of RL in improving reasoning by leveraging verifiable outcome-based reward signals.

Current research efforts in RL for LLMs and MLLMs often focus on two primary areas: optimizing the RL framework itself (e.g., objective functions, gradient update mechanisms) and refining reward functions (e.g., contrastive rewards, active reflection tokens, diverse rollouts). However, the majority of these approaches typically operate under a static sampling paradigm, where trajectories are sampled and processed uniformly. This practice overlooks the inherent variability in the informativeness and quality of learning signals that evolve throughout the training process. The paper identifies this oversight as a critical limitation, suggesting that an optimal framework should dynamically prioritize and utilize valuable signals while filtering out less informative or noisy ones.

"SHUFFLE-R1" addresses this gap by proposing a data-centric approach that dynamically restructures trajectory sampling and batch composition. It shifts the focus from solely optimizing the learning algorithm or reward function to actively managing *which* data the model learns from and *how* that data is presented during training. This work contributes to the growing body of research exploring the efficiency and effectiveness of RL training, particularly for complex MLLMs, by advocating for adaptive mechanisms that interact with training dynamics.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to improve the efficiency of RL fine-tuning for Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). The authors identify two underexplored issues within current RL pipelines that contribute to training inefficiencies and hinder long-term learning:

1. **Advantage Collapsing:** This phenomenon occurs when the majority of computed advantages within a training batch concentrate excessively near zero. In policy gradient methods, advantages quantify how much better an action was compared to the expected outcome, guiding policy updates. When most advantages are near zero, the gradient signals become weak or negligible. This effectively drowns out the informative signals from the few trajectories that possess large-magnitude advantages, leading to suboptimal gradient updates and inefficient learning. While increasing the total number of rollouts can marginally increase the chance of sampling valuable trajectories, it also significantly increases computational overhead without addressing the fundamental issue of signal dilution.

2. **Rollout Silencing:** This issue refers to the consistent decline in the proportion of rollouts that contribute non-zero gradients as training progresses. This silencing can arise from several factors, including advantages genuinely being zero, aggressive gradient clipping mechanisms, or excessive truncation of trajectories. The authors observe that this leads to a substantial waste of computational resources, as a significant portion of the processed rollouts do not contribute meaningfully to learning. This problem is exacerbated by the observation that simple queries may converge early and cease generating informative rollouts, while difficult queries remain inaccurate, also failing to produce useful learning signals. Furthermore, standard pipelines typically use each rollout only once, preventing the full exploitation of valuable data.

Motivated by these limitations, the authors pose a fundamental question: "Can dynamically prioritizing trajectories provide richer gradient information and lead to more effective training?" They contend that an adaptive framework is necessary to prioritize, reuse, and reallocate gradient exposure towards informative samples. The overarching goal is to concentrate updates on "golden signals" while effectively filtering out noisy ones, thereby maximizing the utility of each training step and improving both the efficiency and performance of RL training for MLLMs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Shuffle-R1 framework addresses the identified issues by dynamically structuring trajectory sampling and batch composition through two main modules: Pairwise Trajectory Sampling (PTS) and Advantage-based Batch Shuffle (ABS).

#### 3.1. Preliminaries

The work builds upon the widely used policy gradient algorithm for MLLMs, which aims to maximize the expected return. For a given query `q`, `N` responses `O = {o_1..., o_N}` are sampled from the current policy `π_θ'`. Each response `o_i` receives a reward `r_i` from verifiable reward functions. Advantages `ˆA = {ˆA_1..., ˆA_N}` are estimated to guide policy updates. The core objective function is defined as:

$J(θ) = E_{q∼D, \{o_i\}_{i=1}^N ∼π_{θ'}(·|q)} \frac{1}{\sum_{i=1}^N |o_i|} \sum_{i=1}^N \sum_{t=1}^{|o_i|} \min(\gamma_t(θ) \hat{A}_i, \text{clip}(\gamma_t(θ), 1-\epsilon, 1+\epsilon) \hat{A}_i)$

where $\gamma_t(θ) = \frac{\pi_θ(o_{i,t}|q, o_{i,<t})}{\pi_{θ'}(o_{i,t}|q, o_{i,<t})}$ is the importance ratio, and $\hat{A}_i = \frac{r_i - \text{mean}(R)}{\text{std}(R)}$ is the normalized advantage, with `ε` as a clipping hyperparameter. This static paradigm, despite its practicality, is where Advantage Collapsing and Rollout Silencing manifest.

#### 3.2. Problem Analysis (Further Details)

- **Advantage Collapsing:** The authors' analysis reveals that a large number of advantages cluster near zero, meaning only a small fraction of rollouts possess high-magnitude advantages crucial for meaningful updates. While increasing rollout size (`N`) can slightly improve signal capture, it incurs significant computational cost (Figure 3a) without addressing the core problem of signal quality and concentration.
- **Rollout Silencing:** The proportion of rollouts contributing non-zero gradients declines over time (Figure 1b). This is compounded by varying learning rates for different query difficulties (Figure 3b); simple queries converge early and provide diminishing returns, while difficult ones remain challenging, offering noisy or unhelpful rollouts. Moreover, the single-use nature of rollouts in standard pipelines limits the utility of informative samples.

#### 3.3. Pairwise Trajectory Sampling (PTS)

To mitigate Advantage Collapsing, PTS aims to select trajectories with stronger learning signals. It leverages an extended rollout pool (2N trajectories per query, twice the number used for gradient calculation) to increase the probability of capturing high-advantage samples. Instead of evaluating trajectories in isolation, PTS organizes them into structured contrastive pairs.

1. **Rollout Generation and Advantage Calculation:** For each query `q`, 2N rollouts are generated, and their corresponding rewards and advantages are computed.
2. **Max-Min Pairing:** The computed advantages are sorted in descending order: $\hat{A}_{(1)} \ge \hat{A}_{(2)} \ge \dots \ge \hat{A}_{(2N)}$. Pairs are then formed by matching the highest advantage trajectory with the lowest, the second highest with the second lowest, and so on. This creates `N` pairs: $P = \{(o_{(i)}, o_{(2N-i+1)})\}_{i=1}^N$. This pairing mechanism is designed to capture both high and low advantage signals jointly, forming "positive-negative" contrastive pairs.
3. **Top-k Selection:** A subset of `M = αN` pairs (where `α ∈ (0, 1)` is a hyperparameter) with the largest advantage contrasts are selected from the `N` pairs. Only the trajectories within these `M` selected pairs are used for subsequent gradient updates.

This structured contrastive sampling scheme filters out low-signal trajectories and sharpens the model's policy gradient by direct comparison, thereby focusing computational bandwidth on diverse and gradient-rich signals.

#### 3.4. Advantage-based Batch Shuffle (ABS)

To address Rollout Silencing and further amplify the gradient exposure of informative samples, ABS dynamically reshapes training batches. It operates on the batch `B` provided by PTS, which consists of `M × G` trajectory pairs (where `G` is the number of queries in a batch).

1. **Importance Weighting:** For each trajectory pair $p_j = (o_{j,1}, \hat{A}_{j,1}, o_{j,2}, \hat{A}_{j,2}, q_j)$ in the batch `B`, an importance weight $W(p_j) = |\hat{A}_{j,1}| + |\hat{A}_{j,2}|$ is assigned, based on the sum of the absolute advantages.
2. **Sampling Distribution:** These weights are normalized to form a sampling distribution $\Phi(p_j)$ over the entire batch `B`.
3. **Sub-sampling and Reshuffling:** `S` rounds of sub-sampling are performed from the original batch `B` based on the distribution $\Phi$. Each sub-sampling selects `T` distinct pairs (2T trajectories). The `S` sub-sampled batches are then concatenated to form a reshuffled batch `B'`, ensuring that $|B'| = |B|$, so the total number of samples processed per gradient update remains the same. This allows high-advantage trajectories to be sampled and reused more frequently within a single training epoch.

ABS optimizes the learning process through "Advantage-aware Shuffling" and "Sub-batch Resampling." It increases the update frequency for high-advantage trajectories and reinforces high-value samples through repeated exposure, transforming each batch into a soft-prioritized structure.

Theoretically, the authors demonstrate that PTS and ABS amplify the expectation of the advantage and the gradient norm, leading to larger update magnitudes. They also show that this adaptive sampling introduces a positive bias, meaning the gradient update systematically favors trajectories with higher advantages, thus accelerating learning from high-quality signals.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The Shuffle-R1 framework was evaluated extensively across multiple benchmarks and model scales.

#### 4.1. Experimental Setup

- **Datasets:** Training was conducted on Geometry3K (2.1k samples) and a subset of MMK12 (2.1k samples) for limited resource evaluations. For larger-scale experiments, a 30k-sample training set was constructed by combining the full Geometry3K with 27k randomly selected samples from MM-Eureka.
- **Benchmarks:** Evaluation included in-domain tests (Geometry3K, MMK12) and out-of-domain visual reasoning benchmarks: MathVerse, MathVision, WeMath, MathVista (math reasoning), HallusionBench (visual perception), and ChartQA (chart understanding).
- **Models:** Qwen2.5-VL-3B/7B/32B-Instruct were used as base models.
- **Metrics:** Pass@1 accuracy was reported, averaged over 8 runs with a decoding temperature of 0.5 to reduce randomness. Gemini-2.0-Flash-001 was used for answer extraction and verification.
- **Hyperparameters:** Rollout batch size 512, update batch size 128, learning rate 1e-6. For PTS, 2N=16 rollouts were generated, N=8 pairs formed, and M=4 pairs (α=0.5) selected. For ABS, T=256 pairs per sub-sampling, with S=8 shuffle rounds.

#### 4.2. Comparison with Representative Algorithms

- **Performance on Limited Data (Geometry3K, K12):**
 - On Geometry3K (Table 1), Shuffle-R1 3B achieved 47.88% accuracy, outperforming GRPO by 5.2% and DAPO by 2.7%. The 7B model achieved 55.89%, with gains of 3.3% over GRPO and 1.4% over DAPO.
 - Similar gains were observed on MMK12 (Table 2), with the 3B model achieving 62.22% (+3.03% over DAPO) and the 7B model achieving 68.78% (+0.43% over DAPO).
 - Consistent improvements were noted across out-of-domain benchmarks (Math Avg., HallusionBench, ChartQA), indicating robustness and generalization. Shuffle-R1 also demonstrated superior performance compared to the latest GSPO.
- **Performance on Broader Benchmarks (MM-Eureka 30k):**
 - Trained on 30k samples for 150 steps, Shuffle-R1 7B (Table 3) showed significant accuracy gains over the base Qwen2.5-VL-7B.
 - It outperformed various open-source 7B RL competitors, including MM-Eureka with direct RL, VLAA-Thinker, MMR1-Math, and VL-Rethinker.
 - The model achieved competitive or superior performance compared to leading closed-source models such as GPT-4o and Claude-3.7-Sonnet on several benchmarks (e.g., MathVerse, MathVista, WeMath).
 - The 3B variant also demonstrated strong performance, occasionally outperforming several 7B models.

#### 4.3. Efficiency Analysis

- **Advantage Distribution:** Figure 5 illustrates that PTS effectively increases the proportion of large-magnitude advantages, mitigating Advantage Collapsing. ABS further optimizes batch composition by prioritizing these informative trajectories.
- **Training Dynamics:** Shuffle-R1 achieved higher training and validation accuracy and converged to comparable performance as GRPO in approximately half the training steps (Figure 6a,b).
- **Resource Utilization:** The framework effectively mitigated Rollout Silencing, maintaining a high token utilization rate throughout all training stages (Figure 6c).
- **Computational Cost:** Shuffle-R1 incurred a minimal computational overhead, increasing total GPU time by only 4% to 7.7% relative to GRPO. Crucially, to achieve the same accuracy as GRPO, Shuffle-R1 required roughly half the training steps and approximately 60% of the total wall-clock time (Figure 6d, 7).

#### 4.4. Ablation Study

- **Component Contribution (Table 4):**
 - PTS alone improved Geometry3K accuracy from 42.64% (GRPO baseline) to 46.21% (+3.57%).
 - Adding ABS further increased accuracy to 47.88% (+1.67%), demonstrating the complementary benefits of both modules. Similar trends were observed on out-of-domain tasks.
 - An experiment applying ABS to uniformly sampled pairs (without PTS) also showed notable improvement over the baseline, confirming ABS's individual effectiveness.
- **PTS Rationality (Table 5):** The proposed max-min pairing in PTS was superior to one-way positive sampling, one-way negative sampling, or unbiased random sampling. One-way sampling strategies even led to performance degradation.
- **ABS Rationality (Table 5):** Advantage-aware shuffling within ABS significantly outperformed unbiased shuffling (uniform weights) and static reordering, highlighting the importance of weighting trajectories by their advantages.
- **Hyperparameters (Figure 8, Table 12):**
 - **Sampling ratio (α) in PTS:** An α of 0.5 (selecting 8 out of 16 rollouts) provided a good balance between signal quality and computational efficiency, outperforming both lower (0.25) and higher (0.75) ratios, which suggested over-pruning or diminishing returns, respectively.
 - **Shuffle times (S) in ABS:** Performance improved with increasing S up to 8, but saturated or slightly declined beyond that, indicating that moderate resampling is beneficial.

#### 4.5. Extension Experimental Results

- **Scaling to 32B Model (Table 6):** Shuffle-R1 demonstrated scalability and consistent performance gains on the Qwen2.5-VL-32B model, further improving over GRPO.
- **Referring Expression Comprehension (Table 7):** The framework adapted well to soft-reward tasks, showing improved performance on RefCOCO/RefCOCOg/RefCOCO+ datasets compared to GRPO.
- **Language-only Reasoning (Table 8):** Early experiments on Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B/7B-Base models for text-only reasoning tasks (Math12K, AIME24, MATH500, GSM8K, GPQA, OlympiadBench) also showed significant improvements over GRPO, indicating the potential generalizability of Shuffle-R1 beyond MLLMs.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Shuffle-R1 framework addresses fundamental inefficiencies in the reinforcement learning fine-tuning of multimodal large language models by directly confronting the issues of "Advantage Collapsing" and "Rollout Silencing." By introducing Pairwise Trajectory Sampling and Advantage-based Batch Shuffle, the research demonstrates that dynamically restructuring trajectory sampling and batch composition can lead to substantial improvements in training efficiency and model performance.

The significance of this work lies in several key aspects:

1. **Enhanced Efficiency:** Shuffle-R1 achieves higher training and validation accuracy with fewer training steps and reduced wall-clock time compared to established baselines. This efficiency gain is crucial for the resource-intensive training of large models, making RL fine-tuning more accessible and practical.
2. **Improved Performance and Generalization:** The framework consistently outperforms strong RL baselines across various model scales and a diverse set of both in-domain and out-of-domain visual reasoning benchmarks. Its ability to compete with or surpass leading closed-source models on challenging tasks highlights its effectiveness in boosting the reasoning capabilities of MLLMs.
3. **Data-Centric Paradigm Shift:** The research underscores the importance of a data-centric approach in RL post-training. By demonstrating that *which* data the model updates on is as critical as *how* it updates, Shuffle-R1 advocates for moving beyond sole reliance on reward design or algorithm optimization towards adaptive data structuring. This perspective could inspire new research directions focused on active data curation, prioritization, and dynamic sampling strategies.
4. **Robustness and Generalizability:** The framework's consistent gains across different datasets, model scales (3B, 7B, 32B), and even different task types (math/visual reasoning, referring expression comprehension, language-only reasoning) indicate its robust design and broad applicability.

The potential impact of Shuffle-R1 extends to accelerating the development of more capable and efficient large language and multimodal models. By mitigating common RL training inefficiencies, it could facilitate faster experimentation, enable the fine-tuning of larger models on limited resources, and ultimately contribute to MLLMs that exhibit stronger, more generalized reasoning abilities across various domains. The findings encourage further exploration into dynamic and adaptive data management techniques within the broader field of reinforcement learning.
