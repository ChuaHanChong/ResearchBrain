# RLDX-1 Technical Report

> [!summary] Summary
> RLDX-1, developed by RLWRLD and KAIST, is a unified Vision-Language-Action (VLA) system for dexterous robotic manipulation that integrates motion awareness, long-term memory, and physical sensing. It demonstrates superior performance over existing frontier VLA models across diverse simulation benchmarks and real-world tasks, including complex humanoid operations and contact-rich scenarios, with a notable 97.8% success rate on LIBERO and significant improvements in functional capabilities.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, while demonstrating 'versatile intelligence', often lack crucial functional capabilities required for complex real-world dexterous manipulation tasks.
- Current VLAs struggle with dynamic environments, long-horizon sequential tasks, and contact-rich interactions, which demand motion awareness, long-term memory, and physical sensing, respectively.
- There is a scarcity of scalable, high-quality data for specialized and high-degree-of-freedom robotic manipulation scenarios, particularly for humanoid platforms.

## Method

- Developed **RLDX-1**, a unified VLA system featuring a ==Multi-Stream Action Transformer== (**MSAT**) architecture that integrates vision, language, proprioception, and physical signals to enable motion awareness, long-term memory, and physical sensing.
- Created a ==synthetic data generation pipeline== using image/video editing and VLM-based instruction synthesis to augment rare manipulation scenarios, complemented by robust video quality and motion-consistency filtering.
- Implemented a ==three-stage training== procedure (pre-training, mid-training, post-training with RL) to progressively specialize the policy, along with inference optimizations for real-time control on physical robots.

## Results

- RLDX-1 achieved a **97.8%** success rate on LIBERO and consistently outperformed frontier VLA models on diverse simulation benchmarks, including complex tasks like RoboCasa Kitchen (>**70%** success) and GR-1 Tabletop (**58.7%** success).
- Demonstrated superior performance in real-world humanoid tasks, achieving **100%** on seen and **75%** on unseen conveyor speeds (motion awareness), **91.7%** on object-in-box selection (long-term memory), and **97.2%** on card slide-and-pick (physical sensing).
- Inference optimizations, including static CUDA graph conversion and custom kernel design, yielded a **1.63x** speedup, reducing per-step latency from 71.2 ms to 43.7 ms on an NVIDIA RTX 5090, critical for real-time physical robot control.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A unified neural architecture (MSAT) can effectively integrate heterogeneous modalities and functional capabilities (motion awareness, long-term memory, physical sensing) to enhance robotic dexterity beyond general visual-language understanding.
> - High-fidelity synthetic data, coupled with rigorous filtering, provides a scalable method to address data scarcity for complex and rare manipulation scenarios, significantly improving policy performance.
> - A multi-stage training pipeline, including pre-training on broad data, mid-training with functional supervision, and post-training with RL using a VLM-derived critic, enables robust policy development and efficient adaptation to real-world tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of generalist robot policies capable of human-like dexterous manipulation in real-world environments represents a central objective in contemporary robotics research. Existing efforts have largely concentrated on developing "versatile intelligence" within Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. These models aim to achieve broad scene understanding, language-conditioned generalization, and robustness to perturbations by building upon pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs). Notable frameworks in this area include modular approaches that use VLMs for high-level planning alongside specialized models and low-level policies, and more recent end-to-end VLA models that unify visual perception, language reasoning, and action generation. These end-to-end VLAs typically either fine-tune VLMs to generate discretized action tokens or use dedicated action decoders with flow-matching transformers conditioned on VLM representations.

However, the authors identify a limitation in these existing VLA models: versatility alone is often insufficient for many complex real-world manipulation tasks. Such tasks frequently demand a broader spectrum of functional capabilities beyond general visual-language understanding. These include, but are not limited to, understanding dynamic environments (motion awareness), making decisions based on past interactions (long-term memory), and perceiving contact forces or subtle physical changes (physical sensing).

RLDX-1 positions itself within this landscape by addressing these identified gaps. Instead of solely focusing on versatile intelligence, this work proposes a unified VLA system that explicitly integrates capabilities for motion awareness, long-term memory, and physical sensing. This approach diverges from prior work that has often explored these functionalities in isolation. Furthermore, the work extends the application of synthetic data generation for robot learning beyond common tabletop pick-and-place scenarios to specialized humanoid hardware and complex real-world tasks, leveraging recent advancements in video generative models for data augmentation. The project aims to advance VLA models from primarily versatile intelligence towards practical, reliable deployment in complex, contact-rich, and dynamic real-world dexterous manipulation scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for RLDX-1 stems from the observation that while current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models demonstrate significant progress in "versatile intelligence"—encompassing broad scene understanding and language-conditioned generalization—they exhibit limitations in complex real-world dexterous manipulation. These limitations arise from the necessity for capabilities beyond static visual and linguistic understanding. Specifically, the authors identify three critical functional capabilities that are often lacking in existing VLAs and are crucial for human-like manipulation:

1. **Motion Awareness:** In dynamic environments, such as manipulating objects on a moving conveyor belt, current VLAs struggle because static visual observations do not adequately capture object trajectories or temporal dynamics. A robot needs to perceive and react to motion patterns to act appropriately.
2. **Long-Term Memory:** For sequential and long-horizon tasks, decisions must be grounded in prior interactions and historical information beyond short-term multi-frame observations. Tasks like the "shell game" or multi-step assembly require retaining knowledge of past events to infer the current task state accurately.
3. **Physical Sensing:** Many contact-rich manipulation tasks involve occlusions or subtle visual changes that are difficult to discern from visual input alone. Inferring contact forces, regulating grip strength, detecting incipient slip, or inserting a plug requires explicit physical signals such as tactile or torque feedback.

The key objective of RLDX-1 is to develop a general-purpose robotic policy for dexterous manipulation that overcomes these limitations by integrating these broader functional capabilities into a unified system. This involves several detailed objectives:

- **Architectural Unification:** To design a neural architecture, the Multi-Stream Action Transformer (MSAT), capable of integrating heterogeneous modalities (vision, language, proprioception, physical signals) and unifying diverse functional capabilities (motion awareness, long-term memory, physical sensing).
- **Data Augmentation for Specialized Scenarios:** To develop a synthetic data generation pipeline that augments rare dexterous manipulation scenarios that are difficult to collect at scale in the real world. This includes generating diverse scenes, tasks, and annotating actions with high fidelity.
- **Specialized Training Procedures:** To implement a three-stage training pipeline that progressively specializes the policy from broad multi-embodiment priors to embodiment-specific functional supervision, and finally to task-specific adaptation and refinement for real-world deployment.
- **Inference Optimization for Real-time Control:** To optimize the inference pipeline to enable real-time control on physical robots by minimizing latency, addressing both graph-level and kernel-level overheads.

By addressing these objectives, RLDX-1 aims to advance VLAs towards reliable performance in complex, dynamic, and contact-rich real-world dexterous manipulation scenarios, making a step towards truly human-like robotic capabilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The RLDX-1 system is constructed around four interconnected components: a neural architecture, a data generation pipeline, a training procedure, and an inference optimization strategy.

#### 3.1. Neural Architecture

RLDX-1 utilizes a **Multi-Stream Action Transformer (MSAT)** as its core architecture, extending the Multi-Modal Diffusion Transformer (MM-DiT) to action modeling. It comprises two main parts:

- **Vision-Language Model (VLM):**
 - **RLDX-1-VLM:** Based on Qwen3-VL 8B, fine-tuned on a robot-specific Visual Question Answering (VQA) dataset. This VQA dataset includes questions on spatial relationships, intermediate subtasks, and low-level actions to provide embodied grounding.
 - **Cognition Tokens:** Learnable query tokens appended to the VLM input, designed to extract action-relevant representations (cognition features) by attending to visual and linguistic contexts.
 - **Motion Awareness:** Integrated into the VLM to handle dynamic environments. It uses multi-frame video observations processed by a motion module that explicitly models temporal dynamics via space-time self-similarity (STSS). The LLM backbone efficiently captures temporal context by compressing past video frames into a single token in intermediate layers.
 - **Long-Term Memory:** An explicit memory module processes the current cognition features along with a queue of recent cognition features (from past H+1 timesteps) through a lightweight Transformer, enabling long-term temporal reasoning.

- **Action Model:**
 - Implemented as a flow-matching Diffusion Transformer (DiT), which learns a denoising velocity field over action trajectories. It predicts a chunk of H+1 future actions.
 - **Multi-Stream Action Transformer (MSAT):** Processes heterogeneous inputs (cognition features, proprioceptive state, physical signals) through dedicated streams (Cognition, Action, Physics) coupled by joint self-attention.
 - **Physical Sensing:** A decoupled Physics (P) stream processes physical signals (tactile, torque). An auxiliary objective trains this stream to predict future physical sensory signals, encouraging the model to internalize interaction dynamics. The P stream can be disabled when physical signals are unavailable.
 - **Further Design Choices:** Rotary positional embeddings (RoPE) are applied to the action stream, the flow-matching timestep is injected as an in-context token, and RMSNorm and SwiGLU activation functions are used in the Transformer blocks.

#### 3.2. Training Data

RLDX-1 leverages three complementary data sources:

- **Public Real-World Data:** A large-scale corpus including Open-X-Embodiment (OXE), DROID, Galaxea Open-World, Agibot World, Fourier ActionNet, and Humanoid Everyday datasets, covering single-arm, dual-arm, and humanoid platforms.
- **In-house Real-World Data:** Collected on two platforms:
 - Franka Research 3 (FR3): Augmented with an AnySkin tactile sensor and joint torque measurements.
 - ALLEX Humanoid: A high-DoF humanoid robot, with joint torques estimated from motor currents.
- **Synthetic Data:** Generated to augment rare manipulation scenarios:
 - **Data Generation:** Source demonstrations are diversified via scene and task augmentation. Scene augmentation uses image-to-image (I2I) editing and video-to-video (V2V) transfers to vary visual factors (table appearance, object identity/appearance, lighting, background). Task augmentation synthesizes new instructions using VLMs, either by factorized composition or skill-primitive-conditioned variations.
 - **Action Labeling:** An Inverse Dynamics Model (IDM) predicts action sequences for the generated videos.
 - **Data Filtering:**
 - **Video Quality Filtering:** Uses a VLM to assess instruction following and trajectory plausibility.
 - **Motion-Consistency Filtering:** Replays IDM-predicted actions in a simulator to generate a rollout video, which is then compared against the synthetic video using a learned consistency classifier to ensure action alignment.

#### 3.3. Training Procedure

A three-stage training pipeline progressively specializes the policy:

- **Pre-Training:** The base model is trained on the large-scale multi-embodiment dataset (including synthetic GR-1 data) to learn broad embodied action priors and temporal modeling capabilities. Embodiment-specific and embodiment-agnostic projection layers facilitate adaptation.
- **Mid-Training:** The pre-trained model is specialized for target embodiments (ALLEX, FR3) and expanded with functional capabilities (motion awareness, long-term memory, physical sensing). This stage uses in-house and synthetic data, which are the primary sources of supervision for the new modalities.
- **Post-Training:** Further refines the model for specific downstream tasks.
 - **Adaptive Data Collection:** Involves base data collection (balancing consistency and task diversity) and refinement data collection (iteratively targeting observed failure cases).
 - **Reinforcement Learning (RL):** Integrates with the RECAP framework. A VLM-derived text-based critic is introduced to predict values using the VLM's native number tokens, avoiding the need for new prediction heads and leveraging the VLM's internal knowledge for robust value estimation.

#### 3.4. Inference Strategy

To enable real-time control on physical robots, an inference optimization pipeline is employed:

- **Graph Capture Optimization:** Converts the model into a static CUDA Graph to eliminate kernel launch overhead, which can bottleneck performance in off-the-shelf execution stacks (e.g., PyTorch Eager, Torch Compile). This is achieved by precomputing configuration-dependent tensors and capturing the entire forward pass as a single graph.
- **Kernel Optimization:** Designs custom kernels that fuse critical operator groups (e.g., RMSNorm, RoPE, attention) to reduce unnecessary memory traffic and exploit on-chip memory reuse, thereby improving execution efficiency for short-prefill workloads.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of RLDX-1 encompasses diverse simulation benchmarks and real-world manipulation tasks across multiple robot embodiments, comparing its performance against frontier VLA models such as π0.5 and GR00T N1.6.

#### 4.1. Simulation Experiments

RLDX-1 consistently outperformed baselines across a broad suite of simulated robotic manipulation benchmarks:
- **Classical Benchmarks:** On LIBERO, RLDX-1 achieved a 97.8% success rate, indicating strong performance in single-arm manipulation. In SIMPLER Google-VM and WidowX, it reached 81.5% and 71.9% respectively, demonstrating effective zero-shot policy transfer under sim-to-real visual domain shifts.
- **Robustness Benchmarks:** RLDX-1 also showed robustness, achieving 86.7% on LIBERO-Plus and 77.4% on SIMPLER Google-VA, outperforming baselines which often experienced significant performance drops under perturbation.
- **Challenging Benchmarks:** On RoboCasa Kitchen, RLDX-1 exceeded 70% success, surpassing all baselines. On GR-1 Tabletop (humanoid manipulation), it achieved 58.7%, notably higher than most baselines below 50%. For RoboCasa365, RLDX-1 achieved an average success rate of 32.1%, significantly outperforming baselines (14.8% to 26.9%), particularly on complex composite tasks (19.0% on seen and 5.6% on unseen composite tasks). These results suggest that RLDX-1 maintains its advantage as tasks become more complex and require long-horizon behaviors.

#### 4.2. Real-World Experiments: OpenArm Humanoid

On the OpenArm humanoid benchmark, designed to evaluate versatility in humanoid manipulation, RLDX-1 achieved superior performance across all task settings:
- **Basic Manipulation and Instruction Following:** RLDX-1 reached 50% success on Basic Pick-and-Place and demonstrated stronger instruction-following capabilities, surpassing the strongest baseline π0.5 by 14.6% on Directional Pick-and-Place (Shelf) and 12.5% on (Dish Rack).
- **Generalization:** Gains persisted on Unseen Object (54.2% vs. π0.5's 37.5%), Unseen Task (54.2% vs. π0.5's 45.8%), and Object Grounding (87.5% vs. GR00T N1.6's 33.3%), indicating robust generalization to unseen variations and fine-grained object grounding.

#### 4.3. Real-World Experiments: ALLEX Humanoid

On the ALLEX humanoid, evaluating functional capabilities beyond general visual-language understanding:
- **Motion Awareness (Conveyor Pick-and-Place):** RLDX-1 maintained strong performance across both seen (100%) and unseen (75%) conveyor speeds, adapting its action tempo. Baselines struggled, with GR00T N1.6 averaging 50% and π0.5 averaging 29.2%.
- **Long-Term Memory (Object-in-Box Selection):** RLDX-1 achieved 91.7% success by accurately recalling past human demonstrations, whereas baselines performed near-randomly (GR00T N1.6: 29.2%, π0.5: 33.3%).
- **Physical Sensing (Card Slide-and-Pick, Pot-to-Cup Pouring):** RLDX-1 attained a near-perfect progress score of 97.2 on Card Slide-and-Pick, highlighting the benefit of joint torque feedback. In Pot-to-Cup Pouring, RLDX-1 achieved a 70.8 progress score, perceiving weight changes to complete the task smoothly, while baselines largely failed to complete the task.

#### 4.4. Real-World Experiments: Franka Research 3

On the Franka Research 3 platform, RLDX-1 further demonstrated its functional capabilities:
- **Motion Awareness (Spin Tracking, Pong Game):** RLDX-1 reached 97.9% on Spin Tracking (baselines below 33%) and 81.5% on Pong Game, indicating effective perception and response to complex motion patterns.
- **Long-Term Memory (Cup Swapping, Shell Game):** RLDX-1 showed substantial improvements, achieving 45.8% on Cup Swapping (baselines below 25%) and 91.7% on Shell Game (baselines around 50%).
- **Physical Sensing (Plug Insertion, Egg Pick-and-Place):** RLDX-1 achieved 33.3% success on Plug Insertion (baselines below 21%) and 61.1% on Egg Pick-and-Place (baselines below 46%), demonstrating that tactile and force/torque information improves performance in contact-rich manipulation tasks.

#### 4.5. Ablation and Analysis

- **VLM Design:** Selecting an intermediate VLM feature extraction layer (Layer 18) yielded optimal performance (60.9% success on RoboCasa Kitchen). Robot-specific VQA training contributed a 3.4% increase in success rate and led to more concentrated attention on robot and target objects.
- **Synthetic Data:** Incorporating synthetic data during pre-training progressively improved performance on the GR-1 Tabletop benchmark, with 100% synthetic data leading to a 9.1% increase (from 41.0% to 50.1%).
- **RL Application:** Post-training with RECAP reinforcement learning substantially reduced episode length and the number of attempts (approximately 3x reduction from behavior cloning) on the Light Bulb Twisting task, exceeding human teleoperation performance in efficiency and consistency.
- **Inference Optimization:** The combination of static graph conversion and kernel optimization achieved a 1.63x speedup, reducing the per-step latency of the all-modality RLDX-1 from 71.2 ms to 43.7 ms on an NVIDIA RTX 5090, which is critical for real-time robotic control.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The RLDX-1 technical report presents a comprehensive approach to advancing general-purpose robotic policies, extending the capabilities of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models beyond traditional versatility. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized in several key areas:

- **Integration of Functional Capabilities:** RLDX-1 demonstrates a unified framework that successfully integrates motion awareness, long-term memory, and physical sensing into a single end-to-end VLA. This integration addresses a critical limitation of previous VLA models, which often struggled with real-world tasks requiring these specific functional demands. This represents a step towards more capable and robust robots in dynamic, sequential, and contact-rich environments.
- **Performance Advancement Across Diverse Benchmarks:** The consistent outperformance of RLDX-1 against established frontier VLAs across a wide range of simulation and real-world benchmarks is notable. This includes complex humanoid manipulation tasks (e.g., GR-1 Tabletop, ALLEX humanoid tasks) and tasks requiring robustness to perturbations (e.g., LIBERO-Plus). These results indicate that the proposed architectural and methodological advancements lead to tangible improvements in robotic task execution.
- **Practical Real-World Deployment:** The system-level design choices implemented in RLDX-1, including the adaptive data collection protocol, the reinforcement learning refinement, and particularly the inference optimizations, contribute to making these advanced capabilities practical for real-world robotic deployment. The demonstrated speedup in inference latency is crucial for responsive and safe robot control.
- **Scalable Data Augmentation:** The developed synthetic data generation and rigorous filtering pipeline offers a method to address data scarcity in specialized manipulation scenarios, particularly for high-DoF humanoids. This approach provides a scalable complement to real robot data, enhancing policy performance without incurring extensive real-world collection costs.
- **Contribution to Embodied AI Research:** By focusing on "human-like dexterous manipulation," RLDX-1 contributes to the broader field of embodied AI, moving towards robots that can interact with the physical world in more nuanced and intelligent ways. The VLM-derived text-based critic for reinforcement learning also offers an avenue for efficient value estimation and adaptation in complex robotic tasks.

In summary, RLDX-1 represents a meaningful step towards general-purpose robot policies that can robustly combine versatile intelligence with essential functional capabilities. The demonstrated performance and comprehensive system design collectively contribute to the development of more reliable and capable robots for complex manipulation tasks in real-world settings.
