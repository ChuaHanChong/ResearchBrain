# RLDX-1 Technical Report

> [!summary] Summary
> RLDX-1, developed by RLWRLD and KAIST, is a unified Vision-Language-Action (VLA) system for dexterous robotic manipulation that integrates motion awareness, long-term memory, and physical sensing. It demonstrates superior performance over existing frontier VLA models across diverse simulation benchmarks and real-world tasks, including complex humanoid operations and contact-rich scenarios, with a notable 97.8% success rate on LIBERO and significant improvements in functional capabilities.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, while demonstrating 'versatile intelligence', often lack crucial functional capabilities required for complex real-world dexterous manipulation tasks.
- Current VLAs struggle with dynamic environments, long-horizon sequential tasks, and contact-rich interactions, which demand motion awareness, long-term memory, and physical sensing, respectively.
- There is a scarcity of scalable, high-quality data for specialized and high-degree-of-freedom robotic manipulation scenarios, particularly for humanoid platforms.

## Method

- Developed **RLDX-1**, a unified VLA system featuring a ==Multi-Stream Action Transformer== (**MSAT**) architecture that integrates vision, language, proprioception, and physical signals to enable motion awareness, long-term memory, and physical sensing.
- Created a ==synthetic data generation pipeline== using image/video editing and VLM-based instruction synthesis to augment rare manipulation scenarios, complemented by robust video quality and motion-consistency filtering.
- Implemented a ==three-stage training== procedure (pre-training, mid-training, post-training with RL) to progressively specialize the policy, along with inference optimizations for real-time control on physical robots.

## Results

- RLDX-1 achieved a **97.8%** success rate on LIBERO and consistently outperformed frontier VLA models on diverse simulation benchmarks, including complex tasks like RoboCasa Kitchen (>**70%** success) and GR-1 Tabletop (**58.7%** success).
- Demonstrated superior performance in real-world humanoid tasks, achieving **100%** on seen and **75%** on unseen conveyor speeds (motion awareness), **91.7%** on object-in-box selection (long-term memory), and **97.2%** on card slide-and-pick (physical sensing).
- Inference optimizations, including static CUDA graph conversion and custom kernel design, yielded a **1.63x** speedup, reducing per-step latency from 71.2 ms to 43.7 ms on an NVIDIA RTX 5090, critical for real-time physical robot control.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A unified neural architecture (MSAT) can effectively integrate heterogeneous modalities and functional capabilities (motion awareness, long-term memory, physical sensing) to enhance robotic dexterity beyond general visual-language understanding.
> - High-fidelity synthetic data, coupled with rigorous filtering, provides a scalable method to address data scarcity for complex and rare manipulation scenarios, significantly improving policy performance.
> - A multi-stage training pipeline, including pre-training on broad data, mid-training with functional supervision, and post-training with RL using a VLM-derived critic, enables robust policy development and efficient adaptation to real-world tasks.
