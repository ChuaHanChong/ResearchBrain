# EquiContact: A Hierarchical SE(3) Vision-to-Force Equivariant Policy for Spatially Generalizable Contact-rich Tasks

> [!summary] Summary
> EquiContact is a hierarchical, provably SE(3)-equivariant vision-to-force policy for contact-rich manipulation. A Diffusion-EDF planner estimates a global reference frame from point clouds, while a localized Geometric Compliant ACT reads a geometrically consistent error vector, wrist images and force-torque signals, emitting relative poses and admittance gains to a geometric admittance controller. Trained on one fixed configuration, it reaches 20/20 peg-in-hole success on unseen spatial configurations.

## Problem

- Action chunking transformers and diffusion policies are evaluated only under limited spatial variation, scaling near-linearly with demonstrations because they interpolate rather than generalize spatially.
- Explicitly equivariant neural networks buy sample efficiency but are specialized, computationally heavy, and awkward for real-time large-scale deployment against standard massively pretrained models.
- SE(3)-equivariant vision planners capture global structure yet lack the sub-millimeter placement precision that tight-tolerance assembly demands, and prior compliant policies use non-equivariant world-frame representations.

## Method

- **G-CompACT**, an ==ACT== variant observing only local signals: geometrically consistent error vector, end-effector-frame force-torque, and wrist RGB; it outputs relative pose plus admittance gains, making it ==left-invariant==.
- Language-grounded vision encoder: ==CLIP-ResNet50== features modulated by ==FiLM== layers conditioned on frozen CLIP text tokens, encouraging visual features invariant to global ==SE(3)== scene transformations.
- Hierarchical anchoring: ==Diff-EDF== supplies an equivariant reference frame from scene point clouds, ==geometric admittance control== executes the compliant wrench, yielding proven end-to-end SE(3) equivariance.

## Results

- On out-of-distribution flat platforms EquiContact scored **20/20** insertions and **19/20** on a 30-degree tilted platform, while world-frame CompACT scored **0/10**.
- Compliance is decisive: ACT without geometric admittance control succeeded only **1/5** due to safety-triggering contact forces, versus **18/20** with it; gain-outputting CompACT lowered z-direction interaction forces.
- Extreme transformations (**45, 90, 135** degrees) gave **9-10/10** peg-in-hole and full wiping success at 45 degrees, but screwing dropped to **0-4/10** and wiping failed beyond 45 degrees.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Anchoring a purely local policy on a globally estimated reference frame yields spatial generalization without specialized equivariant networks, a reusable divide-and-conquer design principle.
> - Choosing observation and action frames carefully can induce equivariance structurally; representation geometry substitutes for data scale in contact-rich imitation learning.
> - The invariance assumption is empirical and brittle: language grounding fails when prompts match background distractors, and near-singular configurations break the equivariance guarantee.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research presented in this paper is situated within the field of robot learning, particularly focusing on vision-based robotic manipulation. Recent advancements in imitation learning have expanded robot capabilities, moving from keyframe-based approaches to continuous action generation from vision inputs. Two dominant paradigms are emerging:
- **Large-scale Data-driven Policies:** This approach, inspired by large language models, aims to build generalizable policies by training on massive datasets. While effective for learning broad knowledge, these policies often exhibit limitations in spatial generalizability and tend to interpolate between seen demonstrations rather than inherently generalize to novel spatial configurations. This necessitates extensive data collection to achieve robust performance across varying task setups.
- **Symmetry and Equivariance-based Methods:** An alternative approach leverages symmetry, specifically equivariance, to enhance spatial generalizability and improve sample efficiency in training. These methods aim to reduce data requirements by embedding geometric priors into the policy architecture. However, specialized equivariant neural networks can be computationally intensive and less developed compared to standard neural network architectures, posing challenges for real-time and large-scale deployment.

This paper addresses a known limitation in contact-rich manipulation tasks, where high precision (e.g., sub-millimeter accuracy for peg-in-hole) and robust force interaction are critical. Prior work in equivariant control for tasks like peg-in-hole, while demonstrating promise, often assumed known target poses or lacked the precision required for tight-tolerance operations. Similarly, high-level vision planners, such as Diffusion-EDF (Diff-EDF), can estimate global structures but may not provide the sub-millimeter accuracy needed for precision contact tasks, necessitating an intermediate layer for real-time visual feedback and error correction.

EquiContact integrates elements from both data-driven and equivariant methodologies. It aims to develop a framework that achieves robust spatial generalization for contact-rich tasks from limited demonstrations by combining an equivariant high-level vision planner with a low-level compliant visuomotor policy designed for localized, invariant observations. This approach attempts to bridge the gap between global scene understanding and local precision control, offering a complementary perspective to large-scale, end-to-end visuomotor learning by providing structural guidelines for improving spatial generalizability through SE(3) equivariance.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a vision-based robotic policy for contact-rich manipulation tasks that exhibits robust spatial generalization across varying task configurations, particularly when trained from a limited number of demonstrations. The authors identify specific challenges that motivate their proposed framework:

1. **Limited Spatial Generalizability of Existing Policies:** Many state-of-the-art imitation learning policies, even those trained on substantial datasets, struggle to generalize effectively to new spatial arrangements of tasks. They often require large amounts of data to learn robust behaviors, suggesting an interpolative rather than a truly generalizable learning process.
2. **Precision Requirements in Contact-Rich Tasks:** Tasks such as peg-in-hole, screwing, and surface wiping demand sub-millimeter precision and careful management of interaction forces. High-level vision planners, while capable of estimating approximate target poses, typically lack the necessary accuracy for these fine-grained operations.
3. **Challenges with Pure Equivariant Methods:** While equivariant neural networks can enhance spatial generalizability and sample efficiency, they are often computationally demanding and more specialized, which can hinder their real-time and large-scale deployment compared to standard models.
4. **Integration of High-Level Planning and Low-Level Control:** A specific motivation arose from the observation that while SE(3)-equivariant vision models (like Diff-EDF) could provide initial target poses, their accuracy was insufficient for precision tasks. This highlighted the need for an intermediate layer that could refine these coarse estimates using real-time local feedback and handle compliant force interactions.

To address these motivations, the paper proposes EquiContact, a hierarchical policy designed with three key objectives:
- **Real-time Visual Feedback for Refinement:** To correct residual errors from high-level planners, the low-level policy must integrate real-time visual feedback.
- **Compliant Force-Based Interaction:** The policy needs to manage fine force-based interactions through compliant control to execute contact-rich tasks safely and effectively.
- **Provable SE(3)-Equivariance:** The entire pipeline, from perception to force control, should be provably SE(3)-equivariant to ensure robust generalization to arbitrary spatial transformations of the task setup.

The authors postulate three key principles for achieving spatially generalizable contact-rich policies: (1) left-invariant compliant control action (implemented via Geometric Admittance Control, GAC), (2) localized policies (emphasizing left invariance by operating on observations and actions defined in the end-effector frame), and (3) induced equivariance (achieved through structured observations and actions rather than explicitly equivariant neural networks). These principles collectively aim to enable a "divide-and-conquer" strategy: "anchoring a localized policy on a globally estimated reference frame."

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The EquiContact framework is a hierarchical policy designed to achieve SE(3) vision-to-force equivariance for contact-rich manipulation. It integrates a high-level vision planner with a low-level compliant visuomotor policy and a geometric admittance controller.

### 4. A. EquiContact Framework Overview

The framework operates on the principle of "anchoring a localized policy on a globally estimated reference frame."
- **High-Level Planner: Diffusion Equivariant Descriptor Field (Diff-EDF)**:
 - **Function:** Diff-EDF serves as the high-level vision module, responsible for estimating a coarse target reference frame `g_EDF` from scene point clouds.
 - **Input:** Scene point cloud (`O_scene`) and gripper point cloud (`O_grasp`).
 - **Output:** An estimated target pose `g_EDF ∈ SE(3)`.
 - **Property:** Diff-EDF is designed to be left-equivariant with respect to SE(3) transformations of the target object, meaning if the scene is transformed by `g_l`, the output `g_EDF` is also transformed by `g_l`. This ensures global consistency.
- **Low-Level Policy: Geometric Compliant Action Chunking Transformer (G-CompACT)**:
 - **Function:** G-CompACT is a compliant visuomotor policy built upon the Action Chunking Transformer (ACT). It refines the coarse reference frame from Diff-EDF and generates compliant motion commands based on local, real-time feedback.
 - **Key Design Principles:** To ensure spatial equivariance, G-CompACT incorporates:
 1. **Left-invariant policy:** The policy's output is independent of global transformations of the environment.
 2. **Policy representation in the end-effector body frame:** All observations and actions are defined relative to the end-effector.
 - **Observations (`o(k)`):** G-CompACT utilizes local, end-effector frame information:
 - **Geometrically Consistent Error Vector (GCEV) `e_G`:** A 6-dimensional error vector between the current end-effector pose `g` and the estimated reference frame `g_ref` (from Diff-EDF), defined in the end-effector frame. This vector is proven to be left-invariant (Lemma 1).
 - **Force-Torque (F/T) Sensor Readings `F_e`:** Measured directly in the end-effector frame, these readings capture contact behaviors and are left-invariant.
 - **Wrist-Mounted RGB Images `I_w`:** Captured by cameras attached to the robot's wrist.
 - **Visual Feature Processing (Assumption 1):** The RGB images `I_w` are processed by a vision encoder `μ_ϕ` (CLIP-ResNet50, fully retrained) to produce visual features `z`. To achieve approximate left-invariance for `z`, the vision encoder is modulated by a Feature-wise Linear Modulation (FiLM) layer conditioned on task descriptions (text tokens `t_text` from a frozen CLIP text encoder). The hypothesis is that language grounding helps extract object-identity-related features, which are invariant to global SE(3) transformations of the scene.
 - **Actions (`a(k)`):** G-CompACT outputs two types of actions:
 - **Relative Desired Pose `g_rel`:** A desired pose expressed relative to the current end-effector frame. The spatial frame desired pose `g_d` is then calculated as `g_d = g g_rel`.
 - **Admittance Gains (`K_p`, `K_R`):** Stiffness matrices for translational and rotational dynamics, provided to the GAC module.
 - **Provable Properties:**
 - **Proposition 1 (Left-invariance of G-CompACT):** Under the assumption of approximately left-invariant visual features, the G-CompACT policy `π_θ` is left-invariant in the end-effector frame.
 - **Corollary 1 (SE(3) Equivariance of G-CompACT):** The G-CompACT policy `π_θ` is SE(3)-equivariant when its output (desired pose and gains) is represented in the spatial frame.
- **Lowest-Level Controller: Geometric Admittance Control (GAC)**:
 - **Function:** GAC translates the desired poses and admittance gains from G-CompACT into compliant motion commands for the robot.
 - **Input:** Desired end-effector pose `g_d` and admittance gains (`K_p`, `K_R`).
 - **Mechanism:** It defines desired end-effector dynamics, including an elastic wrench `f_G` which is also proven to be left-invariant (Lemma 1). The desired pose command for the robot is computed in discrete time based on these dynamics.
 - **Overall Equivariance (Proposition 2):** When Diff-EDF is SE(3)-equivariant and Assumption 1 holds, the entire EquiContact policy `h_Θ` (from point clouds and RGB inputs to interaction forces) is SE(3)-equivariant when described relative to the spatial frame.

### 5. B. Extensions to Pick Tasks

The framework extends to picking tasks similarly. Diff-EDF provides pick reference frames, which anchor the G-CompACT. For picking, the G-CompACT is trained for a fixed, aligned grasping pose and does not utilize force-torque sensor values or output variable admittance gains.

### 6. C. Training and Inference

- **Training:** G-CompACT and Diff-EDF are trained separately. G-CompACT learns from expert teleoperated demonstrations on a fixed platform, with background visual distractors to promote robustness. Noise is intentionally added to the reference frame `g_ref` during training to make the policy robust to errors from Diff-EDF during inference. Diff-EDF is trained on a dataset of scene and grasp point clouds with corresponding target reference frames, collected with translational and rotational randomization.
- **Inference:** The system follows a ROS2-based pipeline. Diff-EDF first processes scene point clouds to generate pick-and-place reference frames. The robot moves to the vicinity of these frames, and G-CompACT then activates, using real-time local feedback. Task prompts (language) are averaged and fed to the policy as text tokens. Post-processing heuristics are applied to Diff-EDF outputs to refine pose estimates.

### 7. Main Findings and Results

The experimental section aims to validate the SE(3) vision-to-force equivariance of EquiContact, its applicability to various contact-rich tasks, and the effectiveness of its design choices.

### 8. A. Peg-in-Hole (PiH) Benchmark Results (RQ1 & RQ3)

- **Importance of Compliance:** Comparison of ACT with and without Geometric Admittance Control (GAC) demonstrated that compliance is a critical factor for success in contact-rich tasks. ACT without GAC had a significantly lower success rate (1/5 attempts) due to safety shutdowns caused by excessive contact forces, whereas ACT with GAC achieved 18/20 successes in in-distribution scenarios.
- **Benefits of Variable Compliance:** CompACT, which outputs task-adaptive admittance gains, maintained low interaction forces during insertion, particularly in the z-direction, compared to ACT with fixed GAC gains. Both achieved near-perfect success rates in in-distribution flat platform settings (19/20 and 18/20 respectively).
- **Demonstration of Equivariance for Spatial Generalization:**
 - CompACT, using global spatial frame observations and actions, failed to generalize to translationally unseen flat platforms (0/10 success rate).
 - EquiContact, designed with SE(3) vision-to-force equivariance, achieved a perfect success rate (20/20) on translationally unseen flat platforms.
 - Furthermore, EquiContact demonstrated robust generalization to fully SE(3) transformed tasks, achieving a near-perfect success rate (19/20) on a tilted platform (30° OOD). The single failure was attributed to a substantial error from Diff-EDF exceeding the training noise level.
- **Full Pick-and-Place Pipeline:** When evaluated on the full pick-and-place pipeline for PiH, EquiContact achieved a perfect success rate (20/20) on OOD flat platforms and a near-perfect rate (19/20) on tilted platforms (one placement failure).

### 9. B. Feasibility to Other Contact-Rich Tasks (RQ2)

To assess broader applicability, EquiContact was tested on screwing and surface wiping tasks, assuming known reference frames.
- The framework achieved consistent high success rates across both tasks: 10/10 for screwing and wiping on OOD flat platforms, and 9/10 for screwing and 10/10 for wiping on tilted platforms (30° OOD). This indicates the framework's feasibility for diverse contact-rich manipulation tasks.

### 10. C. Results on Extreme Transformation Cases (RQ3)

EquiContact's robustness was evaluated under extreme task transformations (e.g., large rotations of the entire setup).
- **PiH Task:** G-CompACT for PiH tasks demonstrated almost perfect success rates (9/10 to 10/10) across all tested extreme transformations (45° y-axis, 90° x-axis, 135° x-axis rotations). This suggests that a well-trained G-CompACT policy, anchored by a reference frame, can handle significant spatial shifts.
- **Surface Wiping Task:** The policy generalized well only to a 45° y-axis rotation (10/10 success). It failed completely in other extreme rotation cases (0/5). The failure mode involved the robot attempting to track background features (aluminum extrusion slots, black cables) rather than the intended black lines on the whiteboard. This was attributed to the specificity of the language prompts ("the black line") and the lack of prominent visual cues on the target.
- **Screwing Task:** Success rates for the screwing task were significantly lower (0/10 to 4/10) under extreme transformations, even with a relaxed success criterion. This was attributed to high vibrations in the experimental setup (platforms attached to cantilevered aluminum extrusions) under force exertion, which significantly impacted the precision required for screwing.

### 11. D. Vision Encoder Design Study (Appendix AIII-B)

A controlled study on vision encoder variants for PiH tasks validated the design choices for achieving left-invariant visual features (Assumption 1).
- A ResNet18-based encoder without language guidance (RN18) showed degraded performance under OOD rotation (6/10).
- A frozen, pre-trained CLIP-ResNet50 (CLIP-RN50-frozen) performed poorly even in-distribution (3/10), indicating a domain mismatch.
- Fine-tuning CLIP-ResNet50 with a low learning rate (CLIP-RN50-SB) achieved in-distribution success (10/10) but failed completely OOD (0/10), suggesting insufficient adaptation to large geometric shifts.
- Jointly fine-tuning CLIP-ResNet50 with the policy (proposed EquiContact design) recovered both in-distribution and OOD performance (10/10), demonstrating that strong encoder adaptation is crucial for wrist-camera generalization under task transformations.

### 12. Significance and Potential Impact

The EquiContact framework presents a method for learning vision-based robotic policies that demonstrate robust spatial generalization for contact-rich manipulation tasks, while operating from a comparatively small number of demonstrations. The core contributions and potential impacts are:

- **Advancing Spatial Generalization:** The paper provides a principled approach to achieve SE(3) equivariance from vision to force control without relying solely on explicitly equivariant neural networks. By combining an equivariant high-level planner with a localized, left-invariant low-level policy, the framework successfully generalizes to unseen spatial configurations and orientations, a known challenge for many robot learning methods.
- **Structural Design Guidelines:** The identified three key principles—left-invariant compliant control, localized policies, and induced equivariance—offer structural guidelines for developing robotic policies. This "anchoring localized policy on a globally estimated reference frame" paradigm proposes a structured, interpretable "divide-and-conquer" approach to complex manipulation problems.
- **Enhanced Robustness and Precision:** The integration of Geometric Admittance Control ensures compliant interaction, which is critical for success in contact-rich tasks, reducing interaction forces and preventing safety shutdowns. The hierarchical structure allows for coarse global planning to be refined by precise local feedback, balancing broad scene understanding with fine-grained execution.
- **Complementary to Large-Scale Learning:** This work provides insights that complement the trend of building generalist policies from massive datasets. Instead of relying solely on data volume, it offers architectural and mathematical guarantees for generalization, potentially leading to more sample-efficient learning in specific domains.
- **Feasibility Across Tasks:** The successful application to peg-in-hole, screwing, and surface wiping tasks indicates the framework's broad applicability to various contact-rich manipulation scenarios.

### 13. Limitations and Future Work

The authors acknowledge several limitations and areas for future research. Manipulator singularities, which can degrade pose tracking accuracy, were not fully addressed and required careful selection of testing scenarios. The quality and diversity of training datasets, particularly for complex tasks like screwing and surface wiping, were identified as potential areas for improvement. Extending the EquiContact framework to other visuomotor policies (e.g., Diffusion Policy) is also noted. Furthermore, exploring more advanced vision encoders for left-invariance, such as object-centric representations or newer vision-language models capable of inducing 3D equivariance from 2D images, could enhance the framework's robustness.
