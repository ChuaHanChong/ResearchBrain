# Paper2Poster: Towards Multimodal Poster Automation from Scientific Papers

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces the Paper2Poster benchmark and the PosterAgent framework for automatically generating academic posters from scientific papers. The framework effectively distills entire research papers into editable, visually coherent, and informative posters, achieving high content fidelity and substantial cost-efficiency.

## Problem

- Researchers face a laborious and skill-intensive challenge in creating effective academic posters, which demand complex content compression and visual design for a single-page format.
- Existing AI-driven content generation methods for academic purposes struggle with the unique demands of posters, such as long multimodal context, tight text-graphic interleaving, and stringent spatial constraints.
- There was no standardized benchmark or comprehensive evaluation suite for assessing the quality and content conveyance of automatically generated scientific posters.

## Method

- A new benchmark, =="Paper2Poster Benchmark,"== was established, including a dataset of 100 paper-poster pairs, a suite of evaluation metrics for visual quality and textual coherence, and the novel =="PaperQuiz"== for measuring knowledge conveyance.
- The =="PosterAgent" framework== was developed as a "top-down, visual-in-the-loop" multi-agent pipeline, featuring a Parser for global content organization, a Planner for local layout and content structuring, and a ==Painter-Commenter module== for iterative visual refinement.
- The Painter-Commenter loop integrates visual feedback from a VLM (Commenter) with a "Zoom-in strategy" and in-context prompts to iteratively refine panel content and layout, ensuring accuracy, readability, and visual balance.

## Results

- PosterAgent, including its open-source Qwen-based variant, consistently outperformed existing GPT-4o-driven multi-agent baselines across most metrics, achieving competitive visual similarity, strong textual coherence (PPL **8.31-8.81**), and higher VLM-as-Judge scores.
- The PosterAgent-Qwen variant demonstrated remarkable efficiency, using **87%** fewer tokens than the next best multi-agent baseline and costing approximately **$0.005** to transform a **22-page** paper into an editable '.pptx' poster.
- PaperQuiz was validated as a reliable metric, with VLM scores correlating with human evaluations, and its density-augmented penalty effectively highlighted the value of concise, well-designed context compression over raw information density.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - End-to-end generative models like GPT-4o excel at visual similarity but significantly struggle with generating readable and coherent embedded text in complex layouts, failing to effectively convey information.
> - The VLM-as-Judge evaluation indicated that "Engagement" is a primary aesthetic bottleneck for automated systems, suggesting human poster design prioritizes visual semantics and intuitive design for conveying meaning.
> - PosterAgent's multi-agent architecture, specifically its binary-tree layout strategy and iterative visual feedback, is crucial for handling the complex multimodal input and stringent single-page output constraints of poster generation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research addresses a critical yet underexplored area within automated scientific communication: the generation of academic posters. It builds upon and diverges from existing work in automated document summarization and visual design automation.

Historically, research in AI-driven content generation for academic purposes has seen significant advancements in areas like automated slide generation, with systems such as PPTAgent and D2S pioneering text-to-slide pipelines. These systems typically convert long-form text documents into multi-page presentations, distributing content across slides, each often focusing on a single message.

However, the authors correctly identify academic poster generation as a *substantially more challenging task*. Unlike slide decks, which can spread information, a poster demands the condensation of an entire paper into a *single, visually coherent page*. This necessitates:
1. **Longer Multimodal Context Handling**: Processing and summarizing thousands of words and numerous figures/tables from a multi-page paper.
2. **Tight Interleaving of Text and Graphics**: Conveying complex ideas at a glance requires sophisticated semantic alignment between textual summaries and visual elements.
3. **Stringent Spatial Constraints**: The single-page format imposes strict layout requirements to avoid text overflow, ensure legibility, and maintain aesthetic appeal.

Existing approaches in visual design automation (e.g., text-rich image generation via diffusion models, complex visual layouts for websites or slides) often fall short. Diffusion models struggle with high-fidelity, readable embedded text, while agentic workflows for slides, while effective, don't face the same single-page, high-density multimodal compression challenge. Moreover, general-purpose vision-language agents, despite advances in reasoning and tool use, often lack the domain-specific knowledge and accuracy required for professional tasks like scientific poster design.

"Paper2Poster" carves out a new niche by specifically tackling these unique challenges for posters. It distinguishes itself by:
- **Introducing the First Benchmark**: There was no systematic way to evaluate poster generation, making this paper a foundational contribution.
- **Developing a Holistic Evaluation Suite**: Beyond basic metrics, it includes "VLM-as-Judge" for fine-grained aesthetic/informational criteria and, notably, "PaperQuiz" to measure actual content conveyance.
- **Proposing a Specialized Multi-Agent Framework**: "PosterAgent" is designed with a "top-down, visual-in-the-loop" approach, explicitly integrating visual feedback—a crucial element missing in many LLM-only or VLM-only solutions that struggle with spatial reasoning.

In essence, this work pushes the boundaries of multimodal AI agents by tackling a problem that requires sophisticated long-context understanding, precise visual-textual alignment, and iterative spatial refinement, areas where current general-purpose models often falter. It establishes a new standard and a dedicated ecosystem for future research in scientific visual communication.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind "Paper2Poster" stems from the critical role of academic posters in scientific communication and the inherent difficulties in their creation. Academic posters serve as a vital bridge for rapid dissemination of research findings at conferences, where attendees have limited time to grasp complex ideas from full papers. However, generating an effective poster is a challenging and time-consuming task for researchers, demanding unique skills in content compression, visual design, and layout optimization.

The key objectives of this research are twofold:

1. **To establish a standardized and comprehensive benchmark for scientific poster generation**: Before this work, no dedicated benchmark or metric suite existed for this complex multimodal task. The authors aim to fill this gap by introducing the "Paper2Poster Benchmark," which includes:
 - A curated dataset of recent conference papers paired with author-designed posters.
 - A robust set of evaluation metrics covering visual quality, textual coherence, holistic assessment (via VLM-as-judge), and crucially, the poster's ability to convey core knowledge (via PaperQuiz).
 This benchmark is motivated by the need for systematic comparison and evaluation of automated poster generation systems, laying the groundwork for future advancements.

2. **To develop an effective and efficient AI agent framework for automated poster generation**: Recognizing the limitations of existing generative models (e.g., LLM-only or VLM-only approaches) in handling the multimodal, long-context, and layout-sensitive nature of poster creation, the authors propose "PosterAgent." The motivation for this system is to overcome these challenges by:
 - **Handling Long, Multimodal Context**: Processing entire research papers (20K+ tokens, interleaved text, figures, tables) efficiently.
 - **Ensuring Visual Coherence and Spatial Efficiency**: Creating a single-page output that is both aesthetically pleasing and easy to read, avoiding common issues like text overflow or disorganization.
 - **Integrating Visual Feedback**: Mimicking human poster design by iteratively refining the layout and content based on visual assessment, a crucial aspect for spatial reasoning.
 The ultimate goal is to provide a practical, scalable, and high-quality solution that can transform lengthy scientific papers into polished, editable academic posters with minimal human intervention.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of "Paper2Poster" is bifurcated into two main components: the **Paper2Poster Benchmark** for evaluation and the **PosterAgent** framework for generation.

#### 3.1. Paper2Poster Benchmark

The benchmark provides the necessary tools and metrics for systematically evaluating poster generation models.

- **Task Definition**: The goal is to automatically generate a single-page academic poster that faithfully conveys a scientific paper's core content in a visually coherent and spatially efficient format. This is defined as a "Long-Context Long-Horizon," "Interleaved Multimodal Inputs," and "Layout-Aware Multimodal Outputs" task.
- **Data Curation**: The "Paper2Poster dataset" comprises 100 paper-poster pairs from recent AI conferences (ICML, NeurIPS, ICLR 2022–2024). Papers are lengthy (average 22.6 pages, ~20K tokens, 22.6 figures), highlighting the compression challenge, as posters average 774 words and 8.7 figures. The dataset is stratified by publication year and venue to ensure diversity.
- **Evaluation Metrics**: A comprehensive suite of four dimensions:
 1. **Visual Quality**:
 - **Visual Similarity**: CLIP image embeddings compare generated posters to author-designed ground truths, capturing high-level visual-textual correspondence.
 - **Figure Relevance**: Measures average CLIP similarity between extracted figures and their corresponding text sections, ensuring contextual appropriateness.
 2. **Textual Coherence**: Quantified by **Perplexity (PPL)** using Llama-2-7b-hf on the entire poster text, assessing language fluency and local coherence.
 3. **Holistic Assessment (VLM-as-Judge)**: A VLM (e.g., GPT-4o) scores posters on six fine-grained criteria (Element Quality, Layout Balance, Engagement, Clarity, Content Completeness, Logical Flow) using a 1-5 scale. This provides image-centric feedback on both aesthetics and informativeness.
 4. **PaperQuiz**: This novel metric evaluates how effectively the poster conveys core knowledge.
 - An LLM (GPT-4o) acts as an "examiner," generating 100 multiple-choice questions per paper (50 verbatim, 50 interpretive).
 - Six different VLMs (representing "diverse readers" from students to professors) then answer these quizzes based *solely* on the poster image.
 - Scores are adjusted with a **density-augmented penalty** to reward concise, informative posters rather than overly verbose ones, mirroring the human preference for well-condensed content.

#### 3.2. PosterAgent Framework

PosterAgent is a "top-down, visual-in-the-loop" multi-agent pipeline designed to transform long scientific papers into structured, visually appealing posters. It consists of three main modules:

1. **Parser (Global Organization)**:
 - **Input**: Full paper PDF.
 - **Process**: Leverages tools like MARKER and DOCLING to convert the PDF into Markdown. An LLM then processes this Markdown to generate a structured, JSON-like outline.
 - **Output**: An "asset library" containing section-level text summaries and extracted figures/tables (with captions as keys). This performs coarse-grained compression.

2. **Planner (Local Organization)**:
 - **Asset Matching**: An LLM semantically aligns visual assets (figures/tables) with their most relevant text sections from the asset library, creating (section, figure) pairs.
 - **Layout Generation**: Adopts a **binary-tree layout strategy** to translate hierarchical content constraints into panel bounding boxes. This method estimates content length and maintains reading order and spatial balance, addressing the instability of direct numerical coordinate prediction by LLMs.
 - **Panel Iteration**: The Planner iterates over each section's synopsis, condensing it into concise, hierarchically structured bullet points suitable for poster panels. This sets up the content for the refinement loop.

3. **Painter–Commenter (Local Refinement Loop)**:
 - **Painter**: For each panel's asset pair (section, figure), an LLM distills the section synopsis into bullet points. A deterministic code generator then uses `python-pptx` to generate presentation code, which is executed to render a draft panel image.
 - **Commenter**: A VLM evaluates the rendered panel image. To mitigate VLM hallucination in visual design tasks, a **Zoom-in strategy** focuses attention on the panel region, and **in-context reference prompts** (examples of good/bad layouts) guide the VLM to provide targeted feedback (e.g., "overflow," "too blank," "good to go").
 - **Loop**: This Painter-Commenter loop iterates, with the Commenter's feedback informing the Painter's revisions, until the panel meets quality criteria or a maximum iteration count is reached. This ensures each panel is accurate, readable, and visually balanced.

The PosterAgent is designed to be fully open-source-compatible, with variants using Qwen-2.5-7B for LLM and Qwen-2.5-VL-7B for VLM, demonstrating its flexibility and efficiency.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The comprehensive evaluation using the Paper2Poster Benchmark yielded several key insights and demonstrated the superior performance of PosterAgent:

1. **Limitations of End-to-End Generative Models (GPT-4o)**:
 - While GPT-4o's direct image generation ("4o-Image") achieved high *visual similarity* (0.76), indicating it *looks* like a poster, it suffered from severe textual issues. It recorded the worst perplexity (77.13) and very poor PaperQuiz performance (overall raw accuracy 39.93, augmented score 100.36). This indicates that while it can generate visually *appealing* layouts, the embedded text is often noisy, incoherent, or unreadable, failing to convey actual information.
 - Its VLM-as-Judge scores were also low for "Information" (1.77 Avg), confirming the lack of content fidelity.
 - GPT-4o's HTML generation ("4o-HTML") yielded better textual coherence (PPL 9.86) and higher VLM-as-Judge aesthetic scores (3.36 Avg) among baselines, but still produced blog-like, text-dense outputs lacking visual engagement.

2. **Aesthetic Bottleneck: Reader Engagement**:
 - The VLM-as-Judge evaluation revealed that "Engagement" was the primary aesthetic bottleneck across all variants (scoring below 3). This suggests that human-designed posters convey meaning predominantly through visual semantics and intuitive design rather than just raw information density, a challenge for automated systems.
 - Human-designed (GT) posters, despite having relatively high PPL (11.26), show higher overall VLM-as-Judge scores, implying authors prioritize visual appeal and engagement over strict textual fluency on the poster itself.

3. **Reliability of PaperQuiz**:
 - PaperQuiz proved to be a reliable metric. VLM reader scores correlated closely with human evaluations, validating its use as an effective proxy for human judgment.
 - More capable VLMs (e.g., closed-source models) achieved higher scores on well-designed posters, indicating that stronger perceptual ability leads to better poster comprehension.
 - The density-augmented penalty was crucial: while the raw "Paper" PDF had the highest accuracy, the "GT Poster" performed best with the penalty, highlighting the value of concise, well-designed context compression.

4. **Superiority and Efficiency of PosterAgent**:
 - Both PosterAgent-4o (using GPT-4o for LLM/VLM) and PosterAgent-Qwen (using Qwen-2.5-7B and Qwen-2.5-VL-7B for LLM/VLM) consistently outperformed existing GPT-4o-driven multi-agent approaches (OWL-4o, PPTAgent-4o) across nearly all metrics.
 - PosterAgent achieved competitive visual similarity (0.75), high figure relevance (0.24), strong textual coherence (PPL 8.31-8.81), and significantly higher VLM-as-Judge scores (e.g., PosterAgent-4o avg 3.72 vs. PPTAgent-4o avg 2.11).
 - Crucially, PosterAgent-Qwen, a fully open-source solution, surpassed even GPT-4o-based baselines, demonstrating that effective design can mitigate limitations of less capable LLMs.
 - **Efficiency**: PosterAgent demonstrated remarkable token efficiency, using 87% fewer tokens than the next best multi-agent baseline (47.6K vs. 361.1K for OWL-4o). This translates to a significantly lower cost, transforming a 22-page paper into an editable '.pptx' poster for just $0.005 with the Qwen-based variant. Parallelization further reduced runtime by 40.7%.

5. **Qualitative Observations**: Qualitative comparisons confirmed that PosterAgent generates structurally coherent and readable posters, avoiding the garbled text of image-only models, the blog-like outputs of HTML-based agents, and the layout failures of template-filling systems like PPTAgent.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research marks a significant step forward in the automation of scientific communication and the development of intelligent AI agents. Its potential impact spans several key areas:

1. **Transforming Scientific Communication**: By automating the laborious and skill-intensive process of poster generation, Paper2Poster can democratize access to high-quality scientific dissemination. Researchers can focus more on their core work, while the system ensures their findings are presented effectively, enhancing knowledge transfer at conferences and beyond. The ability to generate an editable `.pptx` file is particularly valuable for practical adoption.

2. **Establishing a Foundational Benchmark**: The "Paper2Poster Benchmark" is the first of its kind for academic poster generation. This contribution is critical for guiding future research, enabling systematic comparison of new models, and pushing the state-of-the-art. The novel "PaperQuiz" metric, in particular, offers a robust, content-focused evaluation that directly assesses a poster's primary function: conveying knowledge.

3. **Advancing Multimodal AI Agents**: PosterAgent's "top-down, visual-in-the-loop" multi-agent architecture introduces a robust design pattern for complex multimodal tasks. Its iterative refinement process, guided by visual feedback and in-context learning, addresses fundamental challenges in spatial reasoning and text-visual alignment that have plagued previous generative models. This approach can inspire similar solutions for other complex visual design automation problems.

4. **Driving Efficiency and Accessibility in AI**: The remarkable token efficiency and low cost of the PosterAgent-Qwen variant ($0.005 per poster) demonstrate that high-quality, practical AI solutions can be achieved with open-source models, making advanced automation more accessible and sustainable. This finding encourages the development of resource-efficient AI systems.

5. **Charting Future Research Directions**: The paper explicitly outlines limitations and future work, such as parallelizing panel execution, integrating external knowledge (e.g., community feedback, institutional branding), and fostering human-AI collaboration. These directions provide clear pathways for extending the impact and capabilities of automated poster generation systems, moving towards more intelligent, adaptive, and personalized tools.

In conclusion, "Paper2Poster" not only provides a highly effective and efficient solution for a challenging real-world problem but also establishes a crucial benchmark and methodological framework that will undoubtedly stimulate further innovation in the fields of multimodal AI, intelligent agents, and automated scientific communication.
