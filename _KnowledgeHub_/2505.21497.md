# Paper2Poster: Towards Multimodal Poster Automation from Scientific Papers

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces the Paper2Poster benchmark and the PosterAgent framework for automatically generating academic posters from scientific papers. The framework effectively distills entire research papers into editable, visually coherent, and informative posters, achieving high content fidelity and substantial cost-efficiency.

## Problem

- Researchers face a laborious and skill-intensive challenge in creating effective academic posters, which demand complex content compression and visual design for a single-page format.
- Existing AI-driven content generation methods for academic purposes struggle with the unique demands of posters, such as long multimodal context, tight text-graphic interleaving, and stringent spatial constraints.
- There was no standardized benchmark or comprehensive evaluation suite for assessing the quality and content conveyance of automatically generated scientific posters.

## Method

- A new benchmark, =="Paper2Poster Benchmark,"== was established, including a dataset of 100 paper-poster pairs, a suite of evaluation metrics for visual quality and textual coherence, and the novel =="PaperQuiz"== for measuring knowledge conveyance.
- The =="PosterAgent" framework== was developed as a "top-down, visual-in-the-loop" multi-agent pipeline, featuring a Parser for global content organization, a Planner for local layout and content structuring, and a ==Painter-Commenter module== for iterative visual refinement.
- The Painter-Commenter loop integrates visual feedback from a VLM (Commenter) with a "Zoom-in strategy" and in-context prompts to iteratively refine panel content and layout, ensuring accuracy, readability, and visual balance.

## Results

- PosterAgent, including its open-source Qwen-based variant, consistently outperformed existing GPT-4o-driven multi-agent baselines across most metrics, achieving competitive visual similarity, strong textual coherence (PPL **8.31-8.81**), and higher VLM-as-Judge scores.
- The PosterAgent-Qwen variant demonstrated remarkable efficiency, using **87%** fewer tokens than the next best multi-agent baseline and costing approximately **$0.005** to transform a **22-page** paper into an editable '.pptx' poster.
- PaperQuiz was validated as a reliable metric, with VLM scores correlating with human evaluations, and its density-augmented penalty effectively highlighted the value of concise, well-designed context compression over raw information density.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - End-to-end generative models like GPT-4o excel at visual similarity but significantly struggle with generating readable and coherent embedded text in complex layouts, failing to effectively convey information.
> - The VLM-as-Judge evaluation indicated that "Engagement" is a primary aesthetic bottleneck for automated systems, suggesting human poster design prioritizes visual semantics and intuitive design for conveying meaning.
> - PosterAgent's multi-agent architecture, specifically its binary-tree layout strategy and iterative visual feedback, is crucial for handling the complex multimodal input and stringent single-page output constraints of poster generation.
