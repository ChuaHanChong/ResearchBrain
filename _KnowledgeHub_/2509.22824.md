# Critique-Coder: Enhancing Coder Models by Critique Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> CRITIQUE-CODER, developed by researchers at the University of Waterloo and Vector Institute, integrates a Critique Reinforcement Learning (CRL) paradigm with standard reinforcement learning to foster internal critique in large language models during training. The model achieved a 4.8-point accuracy increase on LiveCodeBench (v5) for the QWEN3-4B model and an average 6.1-point improvement on BIG-Bench Extra Hard logic reasoning tasks.

## Problem

- Standard reinforcement learning (RL) in large language models primarily optimizes for correct responses, often overlooking the development of internal critique and reflective reasoning abilities.
- Existing methods for teaching LLMs to critique typically rely on imitating teacher critiques or knowledge distillation, rather than integrating critique as a direct optimization objective within an RL framework.
- Large language models can struggle with robust self-correction or internal reflection, limiting their ability to refine solutions effectively in complex reasoning tasks.

## Method

- Introducing ==Critique Reinforcement Learning== (CRL), a novel RL paradigm where models are explicitly rewarded for generating critiques of question-solution pairs based on their alignment with ground-truth judgments.
- Developing CRITIQUE-CODER, a model trained using a hybrid framework that combines CRL with standard verifiable reward reinforcement learning (RLVR) through the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) algorithm.
- Constructing a specialized dataset that integrates 20% CRL data with 80% standard RL data, enabling joint learning of evaluative judgment and direct solution synthesis.

## Results

- CRITIQUE-CODER-**4B** achieved 59.0 accuracy on LiveCodeBench (v5), a 4.8-point increase over the base model, and outperformed DeepCoder-**14B** across benchmarks using **28%** fewer parameters.
- The model exhibited an average improvement of 6.1 points over the baseline on the BIG-Bench Extra Hard (BBEH) logic reasoning benchmark, showing strong transferability of learned skills.
- Ablation studies indicated that a **20%** proportion of CRL data in the hybrid training dataset yielded optimal performance, confirming CRL's role as a complement to standard RL.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly rewarding models for accurate critique generation within an RL framework significantly enhances their reasoning and problem-solving capabilities, surpassing models trained solely with standard RL.
> - The critical thinking and reasoning abilities acquired through Critique Reinforcement Learning (CRL) training demonstrate transferability, improving performance across both code generation and general logic reasoning benchmarks.
> - Critique Reinforcement Learning (CRL) functions most effectively as a complement to standard RL, with ablation studies suggesting an optimal integration ratio that balances critique-oriented learning and solution generation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of large language models (LLMs) has led to advancements in complex reasoning tasks, including code generation, mathematical problem-solving, and logical deduction. A significant factor in these advancements is the integration of reinforcement learning (RL) with chain-of-thought (CoT) prompting, which facilitates the iterative refinement of intermediate reasoning steps. In the domain of code generation, several studies have focused on scaling reasoning abilities by developing automated large-scale code data generation pipelines and applying RL with reward models and test case pass rewards. Examples include AceCoder, HardTests, and KodCoder. Additionally, SWE-RL has explored RL for real-world software engineering tasks using rule-based reward systems.

While standard verifiable reward reinforcement learning (RLVR) has demonstrated utility in enhancing problem-solving capabilities, it typically emphasizes generating correct responses and does not explicitly foster internal critique or reflective behavior towards existing solutions. A distinct line of research, including methods like Critique-Fine-Tuning (CFT) and Critique-Guided-Distillation (CGD), has emerged to explicitly teach LLMs how to critique. These approaches have indicated that instructing LLMs in critique generation can improve their reasoning abilities.

This work addresses the identified gap by proposing a novel training paradigm, Critique Reinforcement Learning (CRL), which integrates critique mechanisms directly into the RL framework. CRL aims to explicitly reward models for accurate reflection and judgment of solutions, thereby complementing the problem-solving focus of standard RL. This distinguishes it from previous critique-focused methods that might rely on imitating teacher critiques or distilling knowledge. By combining CRL with standard RL, the research seeks to develop models that not only solve problems but also exhibit enhanced critical thinking and reasoning abilities, which are transferable across various tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are:

1. **Introduce Critique Reinforcement Learning (CRL):** To propose a novel RL paradigm where the model is explicitly tasked with generating a critique for a given question-solution pair. The reward for this task is defined by the alignment of the model's binary judgment (True/False) with a ground-truth judgment. This aims to explicitly incentivize and develop critique abilities within the RL framework.
2. **Develop CRITIQUE-CODER:** To construct and train a model, CRITIQUE-CODER, that leverages a hybrid training framework combining CRL with standard verifiable reward reinforcement learning (RLVR). The goal is to unify the strengths of both paradigms: CRL for fostering critical thinking and reasoning, and RLVR for enhancing problem-solving performance.
3. **Evaluate Performance on Code Generation:** To assess whether CRITIQUE-CODER demonstrates superior performance compared to base models and models trained solely with standard RL on a variety of code generation benchmarks. This includes evaluating accuracy on tasks of varying difficulty and in multi-language contexts.
4. **Investigate Transferable Reasoning Ability:** To determine if the critique and reasoning abilities acquired through CRL training extend beyond code generation tasks. This objective involves evaluating CRITIQUE-CODER on general logic reasoning benchmarks to ascertain the generality of the learned reasoning skills.
5. **Analyze the Complementary Role of CRL:** To conduct ablation studies to understand the optimal integration of CRL with standard RL, specifically by varying the proportion of CRL data in the hybrid training dataset. This aims to identify if CRL acts as a complement or an alternative to RL.

The motivation for this work stems from the observation that standard RL, while effective for optimizing problem-solving, does not inherently encourage or reward internal critique and reflection in LLMs. Previous research on explicit critique teaching, such as CFT and CGD, has indicated that models can benefit from learning to critique. However, these methods often rely on imitating existing critique traces or distillation, rather than integrating critique as a direct optimization objective within an RL setting. By introducing CRL, the authors aim to address this limitation, allowing models to develop critical judgment skills through verifiable feedback, similar to how standard RL improves solution generation through execution-based feedback. This integration is hypothesized to lead to more robust and reflective reasoning capabilities, which could manifest as improved performance across both coding and general reasoning tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology for developing CRITIQUE-CODER centers on a hybrid training framework that combines standard Reinforcement Learning (RL) with the proposed Critique Reinforcement Learning (CRL). The training process leverages the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) algorithm and involves specific data construction and training procedures.

#### 3.1. Problem Definition and Optimization

- **Standard RL:** For a given question `q`, the policy `π_θ` samples `n` candidate solutions `{s_i}`. Each `s_i` is evaluated against annotated test cases `T` to compute a pass rate, which serves as the reward `R_rl,i`.
- **Critique Reinforcement Learning (CRL):** For a given annotated dataset `D = {([q_k; s_k], c*_k)}`, where `[q; s]` is a question-solution pair with a binary judgment label `c* ∈ {0, 1}` (True/False), the policy `π_θ` is trained to generate `n` predictions `{c_i}` indicating the correctness of `s` for `q`. The reward `R_crl,i` is derived from comparing `c_i` with `c*`.
- **Unified Optimization with GRPO:** Both reward signals, `R_rl,i` from RL and `R_crl,i` from CRL, are combined to update the policy parameters `θ` using the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) algorithm. GRPO is utilized for its reported stability and efficiency compared to PPO. It optimizes a clipped objective function that incorporates a KL divergence regularization term to prevent policy drift. In this hybrid setup, the advantage estimation (`A_i,t`) for GRPO is jointly shaped by task outcomes (for RL data) and critique guidance (for CRL data).

#### 3.2. Dataset Construction

The construction of the training dataset involves two main steps:

- **RL Dataset Selection and Filtering:** The base for the CRL dataset is derived from the human-seeded rStar-Coder dataset. This dataset initially contains numerous and often lengthy test cases. To enhance efficiency and consistency during RL training, the data is filtered by discarding test cases longer than 200 tokens and randomly sampling 30 test cases per problem. This process reduces the average test case input characters from 96,208 to 40 and the average number of test cases from 87 to 24, thereby shortening evaluation time. The filtered dataset, with a QWEN3-4B Pass@1 score of 43.72%, is considered to have a moderate difficulty level.
- **Critique Data Generation:** For each problem in the filtered dataset, a larger model, QWEN3-CODER-30B-A3B-INSTRUCT (which is noted to perform weaker than the QWEN3-4B (Thinking) model on this specific dataset), is prompted to generate candidate solutions. Code blocks from these outputs are extracted, and empty blocks are discarded. Each candidate solution is then executed against the filtered test cases, and its pass rate is computed. A solution is labeled `True` (correct) if its test pass rate exceeds 80% and `False` otherwise. This pragmatic threshold addresses issues with excessively long execution times or false negatives from timeouts. This process generates `([q; s], c*)` pairs for CRL training.

#### 3.3. Hybrid Data Integration

To prevent the model from biasing towards purely evaluative behaviors (from CRL) or solely task-oriented solution generation (from RL), a hybrid dataset is constructed. This dataset combines CRL and standard RL data, enabling the model to jointly learn evaluative judgment and direct solution synthesis. Specifically, 20% of the filtered rStar-Coder seed dataset is designated as CRL data, with the remaining 80% used as standard RL data. This specific ratio is determined through ablation studies to provide a balance between learning critique skills and maintaining solution generation capabilities.

#### 3.4. Training Procedure

The training procedure for CRITIQUE-CODER is outlined as follows:

1. **Initialization:** The policy model `π_θ` is initialized from a pretrained checkpoint.
2. **Iterative Training:** In each training step, a batch `B` is sampled from the hybrid dataset.
3. **Output Generation and Reward Calculation:**
 - **For CRL data:** Given a `([q; s], c*)` instance, the model samples `G` critique outputs `{o_i}`. The final judgment `c_i` is parsed from the `\conclusion{}` field. A reward `R_crl,i` of 1 is assigned if `c_i` matches the ground truth `c*`, and 0 otherwise. During the initial 16k context phase, CRL rewards are scaled by 0.8.
 - **For RL data:** Given a `(q, t)` instance, the model samples `G` solution outputs `{o_i}`. Code blocks `s_i` are extracted from outputs. Rewards `R_rl,i` are computed as the pass rate (K/N) on test cases `t`.
4. **Policy Update:** The computed rewards `R_i` (either `R_crl,i` or `R_rl,i` depending on data type) are transformed into advantage estimates `A_i,t`. These are then used to update the policy model `π_θ` via the GRPO algorithm.
5. **Context Lengthening:** Training follows a two-phase strategy. Initially, the maximum response length is set to 16k tokens. Once rewards stabilize, this is extended to 32k tokens to enable longer reasoning chains.

#### 3.5. Training Settings

Experiments were conducted on QWEN3-4B and QWEN3-8B models in "thinking mode." Hyperparameters included a batch size of 128, a learning rate of 1e-6, and 8 sampled outputs per prompt. An asymmetric clipping ratio (upper bound 0.3, lower bound 0.2) was used in GRPO for exploration and entropy stabilization. The model was trained for one epoch on the entire dataset, with the best checkpoint selected based on LiveCodeBench (v5) validation performance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides several findings regarding the performance and characteristics of CRITIQUE-CODER.

#### 4.1. Performance on Code Generation Benchmarks

CRITIQUE-CODER demonstrated consistent performance improvements across various code generation benchmarks when compared against baseline models (Qwen3-4B and Qwen3-8B in thinking mode) and models trained solely with standard RL.

- **Gains over Base Models:**
 - On QWEN3-4B, CRITIQUE-CODER achieved 59.0 accuracy on LiveCodeBench (v5), an increase of 4.8 points over the base model. This performance surpassed the QWEN3-8B baseline by 1.5 points.
 - On QWEN3-8B, CRITIQUE-CODER reached 35.6 points on Aider-Polyglot, showing a 7.2-point improvement over the baseline.
 - Overall average scores increased by 2.4 points for the 4B model and 3.5 points for the 8B model.

- **Advantages over RL-trained Models:**
 - When compared to models trained exclusively with standard RL on identical datasets and configurations, CRITIQUE-CODER consistently achieved superior results.
 - For QWEN3-4B, CRITIQUE-CODER outperformed Qwen3-4B-RL by 2.4 points on LiveCodeBench and by 1.5 points on the overall benchmark average.
 - For QWEN3-8B, it surpassed Qwen3-8B-RL by 2.7 points on BigCodeBench-Hard, contributing to an average gain of 1.7 points across benchmarks.
 - Qualitative analysis (Figure 4) indicated that CRL training resulted in longer reasoning traces within "think blocks" and a higher incorporation of explanatory comments in the generated code, suggesting enhanced deliberation and self-explanation.

- **Comparison with Frontier Models:**
 - CRITIQUE-CODER-4B outperformed DeepCoder-14B across all evaluated benchmarks, using significantly fewer parameters (28%).
 - CRITIQUE-CODER-8B remained competitive with models like DeepSeek-V2.5-238B and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-14B. It lagged behind GPT-o1 primarily on Aider-Polyglot, which the authors attributed to a lack of explicit optimization for non-Python languages.
 - On EvalPlus, CRITIQUE-CODER-4B achieved 86.5, closely trailing GPT-o1, with CRITIQUE-CODER-8B further improving to 87.7.

#### 4.2. Transferable Reasoning Ability

Evaluation on the BIG-Bench Extra Hard (BBEH) logic reasoning benchmark demonstrated that the critique and reasoning abilities fostered by CRL training were transferable beyond coding tasks.

- CRITIQUE-CODER-4B consistently outperformed both the baseline QWEN3-4B and the RL-trained variant across all four BBEH reasoning subtasks.
- An average improvement of 6.1 points over the base model was observed.
- Gains were particularly pronounced on tasks requiring multi-step logical consistency, such as Time Arithmetic (+8.0) and Zebra Puzzles (+7.5).
- CRITIQUE-CODER achieved an additional 4.0-point average gain over the RL-only model, and a 4.5-point lead on BoardgameQA.

#### 4.3. Test-Time Scaling

CRITIQUE-CODER-4B demonstrated further performance enhancements with sequence-based test-time scaling (budget forcing) on LiveCodeBench (v5).

- Increasing the computation budget for reasoning at inference time led to consistent, monotonic improvements.
- Starting from 59.0 accuracy without scaling (approx. 13k tokens), the model reached 62.0 accuracy after four iterations of scaling, an absolute gain of 3.0 points.
- Subsequent increases in reasoning budget yielded marginal but stable improvements, indicating diminishing returns.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study on CRL Data Proportion

An ablation study investigated the impact of varying the proportion of CRL data within the training set, while keeping total data volume and optimization settings constant.

- Introducing a moderate amount of CRL data (20%) consistently improved performance over the pure RL baseline (0% CRL). The 20% CRL configuration yielded the best overall average score (47.2 vs. 45.7 for RL-only).
- Increasing the CRL ratio to 50% showed competitive average performance but degraded on more challenging subsets like BigCodeBench-I (Hard).
- Training exclusively on CRL data (100% CRL) resulted in a significant performance drop across multiple benchmarks, achieving an average score (44.5) lower than the RL-only baseline.
- These results suggest that CRL is most effective as a complement to standard RL rather than a replacement, with a 20% CRL data ratio providing the most favorable trade-off between critique-oriented learning and solution generation.

#### 4.5. Limitations in Self-Critique

Despite the improvements in reasoning and critique-related capabilities during generation, CRITIQUE-CODER did not demonstrate reliable *post-hoc* self-critique when only the final answer was available.

- Attempts to implement critique-based parallel test-time scaling, where 10 candidate solutions were critiqued by the model (64 critiques per solution) and scored, did not lead to performance improvements on LiveCodeBench v5.
- This outcome aligns with previous research indicating that LLMs struggle to reliably evaluate or refine their final outputs without access to intermediate reasoning trajectories or external signals.
- The authors clarify that CRL's benefits stem from internalizing critique-based judgment *during training*, which enables more thorough reasoning before a final answer is produced, rather than allowing for retrospective self-correction on completed solutions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Critique Reinforcement Learning (CRL) and the development of CRITIQUE-CODER represent a contribution to the field of large language model (LLM) training, particularly for complex reasoning tasks like code generation.

The **significance** of this work lies in several key aspects:

- **Addressing a Gap in RL:** CRL directly addresses the limitation of standard reinforcement learning (RL) paradigms, which primarily focus on solution generation and do not explicitly incentivize internal critique or reflective reasoning. By incorporating a verifiable reward for accurate critique generation, CRL provides a mechanism to foster critical thinking within the RL framework.
- **Enhanced Reasoning Capabilities:** CRITIQUE-CODER's consistent outperformance against both baseline and RL-only models on multiple code generation benchmarks indicates that explicitly training LLMs to critique solutions can lead to superior problem-solving abilities. The qualitative observations of longer reasoning traces and increased code comments further support the notion that CRL elicits a more deliberate and reflective problem-solving process.
- **Transferability of Skills:** The demonstration that CRL-enhanced training improves performance on general logic reasoning tasks (BBEH benchmark) is significant. This suggests that the critique and reasoning abilities acquired through CRL are not domain-specific but rather represent a more general enhancement of cognitive capacities in LLMs, which can transfer to diverse intellectual tasks.
- **Complementary Training Paradigm:** The ablation study findings underscore the importance of integrating CRL as a complement to standard RL, rather than a replacement. This highlights a nuanced understanding of how different learning objectives can synergistically contribute to overall model performance. The identified optimal ratio of 20% CRL data provides practical guidance for future research and development in this area.
- **Efficiency for Smaller Models:** CRITIQUE-CODER-4B's ability to surpass larger, existing models like DeepCoder-14B with significantly fewer parameters suggests an efficiency benefit. This indicates that the CRL approach can potentially enable smaller models to achieve performance levels previously associated with larger parameter counts, which has implications for deployment and resource utilization.

The **potential impact** of this research is multifaceted:

- **Advancement in Code Generation:** The demonstrated improvements in code generation accuracy across various benchmarks can lead to more reliable and robust AI coding assistants, benefiting software development workflows. The enhanced reasoning and self-explanation capabilities could also make AI-generated code easier for human developers to understand and debug.
- **Broader LLM Reasoning:** The transferability of improved reasoning skills to logical deduction tasks suggests that CRL could be applied to a wider array of complex problem-solving domains beyond code, such as scientific discovery, mathematical theorem proving, or complex decision-making.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** This work establishes CRL as a novel and effective training paradigm. It opens avenues for future research into different reward functions for critique, alternative ways to integrate critique into RL, and exploring its application with various LLM architectures and tasks. The limitation identified regarding post-hoc self-critique also points towards important directions for future investigation into how LLMs can reliably evaluate and refine their final outputs.
- **Development of More Robust AI Agents:** By fostering a form of internal reflection, CRL contributes to the development of AI systems that are not only capable of generating solutions but also of critically evaluating them. This could lead to more robust, trustworthy, and less error-prone AI agents in various applications.

In conclusion, CRITIQUE-CODER, through the novel CRL paradigm, demonstrates a method for enhancing LLMs' reasoning and critique abilities that extends beyond initial task performance, offering transferable benefits across different domains.
