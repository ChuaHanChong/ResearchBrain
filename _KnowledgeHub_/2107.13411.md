# Predicting the Future from First Person (Egocentric) Vision: A Survey

> [!summary] Summary
> This survey consolidates research on predicting the future from first-person (egocentric) vision, proposing a unified terminology and systematically reviewing its applications, challenges, datasets, and methodologies. It identifies key research gaps, including the need for standardized benchmarks and more diverse datasets, to advance real-world applications.

## Problem

- A fragmented understanding of the rapidly evolving field of egocentric future prediction, with scattered knowledge across various papers.
- Lack of a comprehensive survey specifically focusing on future prediction from first-person vision, distinct from general egocentric vision or third-person future prediction.
- Inconsistent and ambiguous terminology used across different studies within the field, hindering clear communication and comparative research.

## Method

- Conducted a ==systematic literature review==, aggregating research from egocentric vision, machine learning, computer vision, and psychology.
- Proposed a ==unified taxonomy== for future prediction terms (e.g., ==Prediction==, ==Expectation==, ==Anticipation==) based on psychological literature.
- Systematically categorized applications, devices, challenges, datasets, and computational models relevant to egocentric future prediction.

## Results

- A principled taxonomy for future prediction terms was established to standardize vocabulary across the research community.
- **Nine** specific future prediction challenges were thoroughly categorized into Action, Region, and Trajectory prediction types, each mapped to a level of the proposed taxonomy.
- Identified significant gaps in task standardization for most challenges and highlighted the need for more diverse datasets to improve model generalization and real-world applicability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Egocentric videos provide a unique perspective valuable for understanding human perception and interaction, offering direct insights into wearer intentions.
> - Standardized benchmarks, such as those for short-term action anticipation on EPIC-KITCHENS, are crucial for driving research progress and systematic comparison, with their absence hindering other prediction tasks.
> - While datasets are growing in size and richness, they often lack critical modalities (e.g., depth, paired first/third-person views) and diverse real-world scenarios (e.g., industrial, multi-person interactions).

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This survey situates itself within the rapidly evolving field of egocentric (first-person) vision, a domain that has gained momentum due to the increasing availability of wearable cameras and large-scale egocentric datasets. The broader research landscape in computer vision is transitioning from merely understanding current visual information to predicting future events, a development with significant implications for human-centric applications.

Prior to this work, several surveys have addressed aspects of egocentric vision or future prediction generally. For instance, Betancourt et al. (2015) provided a comprehensive summary of first-person vision evolution up to that year. Bandini and Zariffa (2020) focused on hand analysis in egocentric vision, while Hamid et al. (2017) reviewed egocentric activity recognition in life-logging datasets, and Del Molino et al. (2016) studied egocentric video summarization. These previous surveys offered insights into specific facets of egocentric vision but did not specifically focus on the task of *future prediction*.

Concurrently, other surveys have explored future prediction but predominantly from a third-person perspective or without explicit emphasis on egocentric data. Examples include surveys on eye gaze in security applications (Katsini et al., 2020), general human action recognition and anticipation (Kong & Fu, 2018), and deep learning approaches for future prediction (Rasouli, 2020), the latter of which, while mentioning some egocentric scenes, primarily focused on third-person vision.

This survey addresses a gap in the literature by specifically aggregating and analyzing research dedicated to *predicting the future from egocentric vision*. It is presented as the first comprehensive survey to exclusively focus on this interdisciplinary area, aiming to consolidate the scattered knowledge and provide a structured overview of its applications, challenges, datasets, and methodologies. A particular contribution to the broader landscape is the proposal of a unified terminology for future prediction tasks, drawing from psychology literature, to mitigate ambiguities and discrepancies prevalent in the field.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this survey stems from the inherent value of egocentric videos in understanding human perception and interaction with the environment. Such data offers a direct perspective, often capturing the wearer's intentions and interactions with objects more clearly than third-person views. The ability to predict future human activities, trajectories, and object interactions from this unique viewpoint is identified as crucial for advancing various technological applications.

The key objectives of this survey are detailed as follows:

1. **Summarize Research Evolution:** To provide a comprehensive summary of the progression of studies concerning future prediction specifically within the context of egocentric vision. This involves tracking the development from foundational concepts to contemporary advancements.
2. **Overview of Key Aspects:** To offer an overview of the critical components defining this research area, including:
 - **Applications:** Identifying the real-world scenarios where egocentric future prediction methods can be applied, such as human-robot interaction, assistive technologies for daily living and industrial settings, entertainment (AR/VR), and autonomous driving.
 - **Devices:** Cataloging the wearable devices utilized for collecting egocentric data and implementing predictive systems, noting their capabilities and evolution.
 - **Existing Problems/Challenges:** Systematically outlining the technical and conceptual difficulties inherent in predicting the future from a first-person perspective.
 - **Commonly Used Datasets:** Reviewing and comparing the prominent datasets that facilitate research in this domain, considering their size, annotations, and suitability for various tasks.
 - **Models and Input Modalities:** Analyzing the diverse computational models and sensory input modalities (e.g., RGB, optical flow, gaze, depth) employed by researchers to address future prediction tasks.
3. **Propose Standardized Terminology:** To introduce a principled taxonomy for terms related to future prediction, such as "prediction," "anticipation," "expectation," "prospection," and "forecasting," grounded in psychological literature. This aims to establish a unified vocabulary to prevent misunderstandings and discrepancies within the research community.
4. **Highlight Impact and Future Directions:** To emphasize the potential significant impact of egocentric future prediction methods across various applications. Furthermore, a crucial objective is to identify and highlight areas requiring further research, specifically advocating for the standardization of tasks and the development of new datasets that consider real-world, complex scenarios, including those with an industrial focus.
5. **Identify Effective Techniques and Gaps:** To pinpoint the most effective techniques currently employed for solving future prediction tasks and concurrently highlight research areas that require further investigation and development.

In essence, the survey aims to provide a structured, in-depth reference for the egocentric future prediction field, fostering clarity, guiding future research, and accelerating the development of practical applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors adopted a systematic literature review methodology to synthesize existing research on future prediction from egocentric vision. Their approach involved several structured components:

1. **Literature Aggregation:** The survey primarily draws from the egocentric vision domain but also incorporates relevant papers from machine learning, computer vision, and psychology to provide a comprehensive perspective on the field.
2. **Categorization of Predictive Systems:** To provide a structured analysis, the authors proposed a series of parameters for classifying general predictive systems. These parameters include:
 - **Time-scale of Prediction:** Distinguishing between short-term and long-term predictions.
 - **Focusing Capabilities:** The ability of a system to selectively attend to relevant visual information.
 - **Generalization Capabilities:** The capacity of models to perform well on unseen events or inputs.
 - **Prediction Target:** Differentiating between predictions related to the camera wearer's actions and external environmental events.
 - **Internal State Predictions:** Whether the system predicts its own future internal state or directly outputs future events.
3. **Taxonomy for Future Prediction Terms:** A core methodological step was the proposal of a unified terminology based on psychological literature (Bubic et al., 2010). This taxonomy defines "Prediction" as the general concept, "Expectation" as the representation of predicted short-term events, "Anticipation" as the process of formulating short-term expectations, "Prospection" for distant future events, and "Forecasting" for modeling future events based on time-series data. This framework underpins the classification of tasks within the survey.
4. **Challenges Grouping:** The authors categorized future prediction challenges into three main clusters:
 - **Action Prediction:** Including Short-term Action Anticipation, Long-term Action Anticipation, and Long-term Future Action Captioning.
 - **Region Prediction:** Encompassing Next Active Object Prediction, Predicting Interaction Hotspots, and Future Gaze Prediction.
 - **Trajectory Prediction:** Covering Forecasting the Trajectory of the Camera Wearer, Forecasting the Trajectories of Other Persons or Objects, and Hands Trajectory Forecasting.
 - Two related challenges, Temporal Action Segmentation and Action Recognition, were also discussed due to their foundational role. Each challenge was mapped to a "level of prediction" from the proposed taxonomy.
5. **Device Review:** Modern first-person vision devices were categorized and analyzed based on their technical specifications (resolution, field of view), dataset usage, additional sensors (gaze, depth, IMU, GPS), computing capabilities, and high-level functionalities (AR/MR, localization).
6. **Dataset Analysis:** A detailed review of 20 relevant egocentric video datasets was conducted. These datasets were classified and compared using 17 attributes, including task, scripted/non-scripted nature, video duration, number of frames/videos/segments/classes, native environment usage, number of environments/subjects, presence of object/hand/audio annotations, scenario type, number of persons, trimmed/untrimmed status, and camera position. This provided insights into dataset characteristics and gaps.
7. **Model Review:** The authors comprehensively reviewed existing approaches for future prediction, categorizing them based on:
 - **Task(s) addressed:** Which specific prediction challenges the model tackles.
 - **Datasets used for testing:** Indicating the operational contexts.
 - **Input Modalities:** Such as RGB, optical flow, hand representations, object features, and gaze.
 - **Model Components:** Highlighting the use of 2D/3D CNNs, RNNs (LSTMs, GRUs), attention mechanisms (spatial, temporal, modality), heuristics, GANs, graph neural networks, and semantics modeling.
 - **Loss Functions:** Whether specific or generic.
 - **Training Approach:** End-to-end versus feature-based training.
 - **Prediction Scope:** Whether the model considers the time-scale, predicts own actions vs. external events, or predicts internal states.
 - A comparative table (Table 4) summarized these attributes for various methods.
8. **Performance Evaluation Discussion:** The survey discussed common evaluation protocols, emphasizing the need to account for prediction uncertainty (e.g., "best of many" criteria, top-k accuracy). It presented a snapshot of egocentric action anticipation results on the EPIC-KITCHENS-55 test set (Table 5) to illustrate the state-of-the-art performance and trends.

This systematic and detailed approach allowed the authors to present a structured overview of the field, identify key trends, highlight limitations, and propose directions for future research.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The survey yields several key findings across the analyzed aspects of egocentric future prediction:

1. **Terminology Standardization:** A significant result is the proposal of a principled taxonomy for future prediction terms (Prediction, Anticipation, Expectation, Prospection, Forecasting) derived from psychology. This aims to provide a unified vocabulary, addressing the prior lack of common agreement and facilitating clearer communication within the research community.
2. **Diverse Applications but Early Stage:** The report identifies a broad spectrum of potential applications for egocentric future prediction, including assistive technologies (daily life, industrial safety), human-robot interaction, AR/VR entertainment, and autonomous driving. However, it notes that many of these applications are still in their early or potential stages, suggesting room for further development.
3. **Evolving Device Landscape:** Modern egocentric vision devices are increasingly feature-rich, integrating multiple sensors (gaze, depth, IMU, GPS) and on-board computing capabilities. This evolution, spanning from action cameras to smart glasses and AR/MR devices, enables more sophisticated data collection and real-time assistive systems.
4. **Categorized Challenges and Benchmark Gaps:** Nine specific challenges related to future prediction from egocentric vision are thoroughly categorized into Action, Region, and Trajectory prediction. A crucial finding is the lack of standardized benchmarks for most of these challenges. Only short-term action anticipation, notably with the EPIC-KITCHENS dataset and its associated challenge, has a well-established benchmark, which has demonstrably spurred significant research and performance improvements. For tasks like egocentric trajectory forecasting and long-term action anticipation, standard benchmarks are largely absent, hindering systematic comparison and progress.
5. **Dataset Trends and Limitations:**
 - **Growth and Richness:** A clear trend of increasing dataset size and richer annotations (objects, hands, gaze) is observed. Untrimmed videos and unscripted activities captured from multiple subjects in native environments (e.g., EPIC-KITCHENS series) are gaining prominence and are particularly beneficial for studying natural future prediction.
 - **Modality Gaps:** Despite this growth, specific modalities and tasks remain underexplored. Few datasets offer paired first- and third-person videos, human location coordinates are often missing, and depth information is rarely included in egocentric datasets, limiting the study of crucial interactions and environmental understanding.
 - **Scenario Bias:** Current datasets predominantly focus on everyday activities (e.g., cooking), with industrial or specific health assistance scenarios being largely under-represented. Datasets for multi-person interactions are also scarce.
6. **Dominance of Deep Learning Models:** Deep learning paradigms, particularly 2DCNNs as feature extractors, combined with recurrent neural networks (RNNs like LSTMs/GRUs) or attention mechanisms, are dominant. Spatio-temporal 3DCNNs and Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) are also gaining traction, especially for generating future representations. The importance of multi-modal inputs (optical flow, hands, objects, gaze) is recognized, often fused using attention mechanisms. Emerging transformer-based architectures are noted as a promising direction, drawing from their success in broader video understanding.
7. **Evaluation Protocols:** Evaluation of future prediction models often accommodates the inherent uncertainty of future events by employing "best of many" criteria or top-k accuracy metrics. The consistent improvement observed on the EPIC-KITCHENS action anticipation benchmark (Table 5) highlights the value of established challenges for advancing the field. However, results also indicate a tendency for models to overfit to seen environments, underscoring the need for better generalization.

In summary, the survey consolidates the current state of egocentric future prediction, highlighting the progress driven by advanced devices and deep learning, while critically identifying significant gaps in task standardization, dataset diversity, and evaluation robustness that need to be addressed for the field to mature.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This survey holds significant implications for the advancement and practical application of egocentric future prediction.

#### 5.1. Advancement of the Research Field

The primary significance lies in its role as a foundational reference. By comprehensively aggregating and structuring research in egocentric future prediction, the survey provides a consolidated overview of a rapidly evolving, interdisciplinary field that was previously fragmented. The proposed taxonomy for future prediction terms is a critical contribution, aiming to standardize vocabulary and reduce ambiguity, thereby facilitating clearer communication and fostering more rigorous research design. The detailed analysis of challenges, datasets, and models offers a roadmap for newcomers and a benchmark for experienced researchers, enabling them to identify current strengths and pinpoint areas ripe for innovation.

#### 5.2. Enabling Real-World Applications

The report underscores the high practical utility of egocentric future prediction algorithms across numerous domains:
- **Assistive Technologies:** The ability to predict human intentions and activities proactively can revolutionize assistive devices. This includes enhancing safety in industrial settings by anticipating dangerous actions, providing non-explicit requested assistance to individuals with cognitive impairments, or guiding blind people by forecasting their trajectories and potential obstacles. Such systems can significantly improve quality of life and worker safety.
- **Human-Robot Interaction:** Predicting human movements and intentions from an egocentric viewpoint can make human-robot collaboration more fluid, safe, and intuitive. Robots could anticipate a user's next action or destination, avoiding collisions and providing timely support.
- **Augmented/Virtual Reality and Entertainment:** In AR/VR, anticipating where a user will look or interact can enable more natural user interfaces and improved rendering, leading to more immersive and responsive experiences.
- **Marketing and Behavioral Analysis:** Understanding customer behaviors and trajectories in environments like shopping centers could offer new insights for marketing strategies.

#### 5.3. Guiding Future Research and Development

A crucial impact of the survey is its clear identification of research gaps and future directions:
- **Standardization of Benchmarks:** The explicit call for unified benchmarks for tasks beyond short-term action anticipation (e.g., egocentric trajectory forecasting, long-term action anticipation) is vital. As demonstrated by EPIC-KITCHENS, established benchmarks catalyze concentrated research efforts, accelerate progress, and allow for robust comparisons.
- **Dataset Expansion and Diversity:** The emphasis on creating more diverse datasets is critical. This includes data from under-represented scenarios (industrial, multi-person interactions, health assistance), incorporating modalities like depth information, and collecting paired first- and third-person videos. Such datasets are essential for developing generalizable models applicable to complex real-world conditions.
- **Algorithmic Innovation:** The recommendation to focus on inference time for real-time applicability highlights a practical need for developing faster algorithms. Furthermore, exploring unsupervised and semi-supervised learning techniques is crucial for reducing reliance on extensive labeled datasets, which are costly and time-consuming to create.
- **Generalization Capabilities:** Addressing the observed overfitting to "seen" environments is paramount for transitioning research findings into reliable, deployable systems.

In conclusion, this survey is a significant contribution that organizes the landscape of egocentric future prediction, articulates its immense potential, and provides a structured agenda for future scientific and engineering endeavors, particularly in driving the field towards practical, real-world applications.
