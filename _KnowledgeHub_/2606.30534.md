# Orca: The World is in Your Mind

> [!summary] Summary
> The Orca project by Beijing Academy of Artificial Intelligence proposes "Next-State-Prediction" as a unified paradigm for general world foundation models, learning a latent world representation from multimodal data through both unconscious and conscious learning. The model demonstrates scalable learning behavior and improved performance in text generation, physically plausible image prediction, and emergent action generation across diverse downstream tasks with a frozen backbone.

## Problem

- Current AI foundation models are specialized for isolated prediction targets (e.g., next-token, next-frame, next-action), hindering the development of truly general intelligence.
- The absence of a unified framework to model the world's latent states and dynamic transitions across diverse modalities.
- Challenges in embodied AI due to data scarcity and the need for robust, generalizable world representations that can transfer to action tasks without extensive action-specific pre-training.

## Method

- **Orca** introduces ==Next-State-Prediction== as a unified paradigm, shifting from isolated predictions to modeling a ==unified world latent space== that abstracts state transitions.
- Employs an ==Encoder-Decoder architecture== with two complementary learning paradigms: =="unconscious learning"== for dense, natural state transitions from continuous video, and =="conscious learning"== for sparse, meaningful language-conditioned state transitions and VQA.
- Constructs a large-scale multimodal world-learning data inventory comprising 125,000 hours of video, 160 million event annotations, and 11.5 million VQA pairs to train the model.

## Results

- Orca-4B achieved an average score of **51.8** across four video understanding benchmarks (MVBench, TemporalBench, 3DSRBench, SWITCH), surpassing comparable VLM baselines like Qwen3.5-4B (**46.7**).
- In image prediction on PRICE-V0.1, Orca-4B+2 attained the highest average judge score of **59.8**, outperforming image generation baselines like FLUX.2 (**56.1**) in generating physically plausible state changes.
- For action generation on a real-world robot, Orca-4B achieved an overall average rule-based score of **32.4**, comparable to strong VLA baselines like \pi_0.5 (**29.4**) and outperforming V-JEPA 2.1 (**17.0**), demonstrating emergent action capabilities without action labels in pre-training.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The proposed "Next-State-Prediction" paradigm is scalable, with pre-training losses consistently decreasing and downstream performance improving as model size and data volume increase.
> - A stronger, more generalized world latent representation learned during pre-training significantly enhances diverse downstream capabilities (text, vision, action) even when the model backbone remains frozen.
> - The complementary "unconscious" and "conscious" learning objectives contribute synergistically, with unconscious learning proving vital for action generation (temporal dynamics) and conscious learning crucial for vision prediction (instruction adherence).

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper positions Orca within the evolving field of artificial intelligence, particularly in the development of foundation models. It argues for a fundamental shift in the modeling paradigm, moving beyond current specialized models that focus on "Next-Token-Prediction" (common in Large Language Models), "Next-Frame-Prediction" (seen in image and video generation), or "Next-Action-Prediction" (used in embodied AI). Instead, Orca proposes "Next-State-Prediction" as a unified approach to building general world foundation models.

**Relationship to Existing Paradigms:**

- **Self-Supervised Learning and Latent World Models:** Orca builds upon the concepts of Joint Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPA), such as I-JEPA and the V-JEPA series, which learn semantic representations rather than pixel-level reconstructions. While JEPA-style models focus on self-supervised visual representation learning, Orca broadens this to a general world-learning formulation. It aims to abstract latent world states from multimodal signals and model state transitions under both implicit dynamics and explicit semantic conditions.
- **Next-Token Prediction (Large Language Models and Multimodal LLMs):** Current LLMs like LLaMA 3.1 and Qwen3, and multimodal LLMs such as Gemini 3.1 and Emu3, primarily organize knowledge and reasoning through autoregressive language modeling. Orca differs by integrating language as an *explicit semantic condition* that guides state transitions, rather than solely as a prediction target. It leverages VQA for commonsense grounding and semantic understanding of the learned world representation.
- **Next-Frame Prediction (Image and Video Generation Models):** Models like FLUX.2 and Seedance 2.0 focus on synthesizing coherent and visually appealing frames. Orca, however, aims not to be a "painter" but to model target states that adhere to the physical constraints and interaction processes of the real world. Its objective in visual prediction is to ensure physical plausibility, scene consistency, and instruction adherence in interaction-conditioned state changes.
- **Next-Action Prediction (Vision-Language-Action and World Action Models):** VLA models (e.g., OpenVLA, π_0.5, GR00T) and world-action models (e.g., Motus, DreamZero) typically organize embodied learning around action prediction, policy learning, or joint video-action modeling. Orca adopts a "world-learning-first" philosophy. It learns how scenes and objects change through state-transition modeling *without relying on action labels during pre-training*. This design intends to provide a more generalized foundation by building a stronger world representation of temporal changes and physical interactions, which can then adapt more efficiently to embodied tasks with limited robot data.

In summary, Orca's contribution to the landscape is its attempt to unify different prediction tasks under a Next-State-Prediction paradigm, thereby fostering a more holistic understanding and modeling of the world's latent dynamics and explicit conditions across diverse modalities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of the Orca research is to initiate the development of a "general world foundation model." This overarching goal is driven by a vision of intelligence that transcends current task-specific AI models, aiming for a system that can continuously learn and self-evolve by internalizing physical laws, causal relationships, and dynamic evolution.

The core motivations and specific objectives are detailed as follows:

1. **Shift to Next-State-Prediction:** The fundamental motivation is to move away from isolated prediction targets (next-token, next-frame, next-action) that characterize many contemporary AI models. The authors argue that true general intelligence requires an ability to build and manipulate internal representations of "world states" and model "state transitions." This shift to Next-State-Prediction is presented as a more unified and robust paradigm for understanding, predicting, and acting upon the world, encompassing both implicit dynamics and explicit conditions.
2. **Learning a Unified World Latent Space:** Orca's immediate objective is to learn a unified world latent space. This space is intended to abstract and represent the complex dynamics of the world from diverse multimodal signals. In its initial instantiation, Orca focuses on visual and language signals, which are analogous to how humans perceive and understand the world, providing sensory input and causal explanations.
3. **Developing Complementary Learning Paradigms:** To effectively learn this world latent space, Orca aims to integrate two distinct, yet complementary, learning mechanisms:
 - **Unconscious learning:** This is motivated by the observation that humans learn much about the world through continuous, unsupervised observation of natural phenomena. Orca seeks to mimic this by capturing dense, natural state transitions from continuous video data without explicit labels, thereby internalizing fundamental physical laws and dynamics.
 - **Conscious learning:** This paradigm is motivated by the human ability to learn from explicit instructions, events, and task intentions. Orca aims to model sparse, meaningful state transitions guided by language-described events and VQA supervision, enabling the model to understand cause-and-effect relationships and goal-directed changes.
4. **Scalability and Efficacy of World Modeling:** A key objective is to empirically investigate two core hypotheses:
 - **Feasibility and Scalability:** To determine if the proposed learning paradigms are effective and can scale with increasing model size and data volume. This involves observing whether training losses consistently decrease under larger scales.
 - **Impact on Downstream Capabilities:** To ascertain whether a stronger, more generalized world latent representation, learned through pre-training, can lead to enhanced performance across diverse downstream tasks (text generation, image prediction, and embodied action generation), even when the core model backbone is frozen and only lightweight decoders are trained. This design choice highlights the intention to evaluate the *quality of the learned world representation* rather than task-specific fine-tuning.
5. **Construction of a Comprehensive World-Learning Data Inventory:** To support these ambitious learning objectives, a practical motivation was the creation of a large-scale, real-world data collection encompassing vast amounts of video data and event annotations, grounded in diverse interaction scenarios.

In essence, Orca is motivated by the pursuit of more general and human-like intelligence, proposing a novel approach to world modeling that prioritizes a unified understanding of state transitions across modalities, with an explicit focus on verifying the scalability and transferability of the learned world representation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Orca adopts an Encoder-Decoder architecture designed to learn a unified world latent space from multimodal inputs and expose this representation through various modality-specific readouts.

**Overall Architecture:**
- **Encoder:** The central component, responsible for learning the world latent space (S) from multimodal world signals (X). In this initial version, X primarily consists of visual signals (videos and images) and language signals (text). The latent space models state transitions S_{t+Δ} ~ p_Θ(S_{t+Δ} | S_t, z_t, c_t), where z_t represents implicit dynamics and c_t denotes explicit conditions.
- **Decoder:** A set of lightweight, modality-specific modules that read out information from the learned world latent space into formats like text, images, and actions. A key aspect is that the Orca Encoder backbone remains frozen during downstream post-training, with only these decoders being trainable, to evaluate the effectiveness of the learned latent space itself.

**Learning Paradigms for the Encoder:** Orca employs two complementary learning paradigms:

1. **Unconscious Learning:**
 - **Objective:** To capture dense, natural state transitions from continuous video observations. This process operates without explicit semantic conditions, simulating learning from observation alone. It aims to internalize natural dynamics, object motion, physical regularities, and scene evolution.
 - **Implementation:** Given a video frame `v_t`, the model (using a learnable `Query 1` vector) predicts the latent representation `v_l_{t+1}` of the next adjacent frame. The ground truth `v_l_{t+1}` is obtained from a frozen vision encoder. The supervision signal is derived from the model's own predictions against observed future states.

2. **Conscious Learning:**
 - **Objective:** To model sparse, meaningful state transitions under explicit semantic conditions provided by language. This paradigm aims to capture event-level changes, task intentions, and causal relationships.
 - **Implementation:**
 - **Event-conditioned state transition:** Given a frame `v_t` and a language description `e_{t+Δ}` of an adjacent (next or previous) event, the model (using `Query 1` and `Query 2` vectors) predicts the latent representation `v_l_{t+Δ}` of a random frame within the specified event. The language `e_{t+Δ}` guides the transition.
 - **VQA response generation:** To further ground conscious learning in world understanding, the model processes a video `V` and a related question `l_q`, generating a language answer `l_a` using its Language Modeling (LM) head. This provides a mechanism for common sense and interpretive reasoning.

**Pre-training Details:**
- **Backbone:** Orca utilizes a native pre-trained Vision-Language Model (VLM), specifically Qwen3.5, which is aligned in both language and vision spaces.
- **Learning Objectives (Loss Functions):** The pre-training loss `L` is a weighted sum of three components:
 - `L_obs`: Latent matching loss for observation-only state transition.
 - `L_evt`: Latent matching loss for event-conditioned state transition, averaging transitions to previous and next events. The latent matching uses a combination of L2 distance and cosine similarity (0.1 L2 + 0.9 Cosine).
 - `L_vqa`: Standard next-token prediction loss for VQA response generation.
 - The total loss is `L = 0.1 L_obs + 0.5 L_evt + 0.4 L_vqa`.
- **Query-Based Implementation:** Both state-transition objectives are implemented using learnable query vectors (`Query 1`, `Query 2`) that are trained from scratch. These queries extract specific hidden states from the VLM backbone, which are then passed through MLPs to predict the target latents.
- **Pre-training Data:** A large-scale "world-learning inventory data" was constructed:
 - **Video Data:** 125,000 hours of real-world videos covering ego-centric interaction, exo-centric manipulation, action-free robot execution, and natural dynamics. Used for `L_obs` and `L_evt`.
 - **Event Data:** 160 million event annotations, derived from video data through multi-level segmentation and language captions. Each event specifies a transition. Used for `L_evt`.
 - **VQA Data:** 11.5 million general VQA data pairs, constructed from language signals and video. Used for `L_vqa`.
 - For the current version, approximately one-tenth of the total video data was utilized.

**Downstream Post-training (Readouts):**
After pre-training, Orca's frozen backbone is connected to modality-specific decoders.
- **To Language (Text Generation):** Reuses the VLM backbone's LM head directly, without additional trainable modules.
- **To Vision (Image Prediction):** An MLP adaptor projects Orca's latent into the conditioning space of a frozen Stable Diffusion 3.5 (SD3.5) decoder. Only the MLP adaptor and LoRA parameters attached to SD3.5's attention projections are trainable.
- **To Action (Action Generation):** A DiT-based Action Expert (trained from scratch) takes Orca's latent (via an MLP adaptor), noisy action with time embedding, and robot proprioception as input. It is trained with a flow-matching loss to predict short-horizon action chunks.

**Infrastructure:**
The training infrastructure, built on FlagScale, includes several optimizations to handle the high memory and communication demands:
- **FSDP2 (Fully Sharded Data Parallel):** For flexible parameter sharding and memory management.
- **Activation Recomputation:** To reduce activation memory footprint by reconstructing intermediate states during backpropagation.
- **Chunked Cross-Entropy Loss:** To reduce peak memory usage during VLM's log-softmax computation.
- **Forward/Backward Pre-fetching:** To overlap FSDP all-gather communication with computation, reducing GPU idle time.
These optimizations resulted in a 4.4x acceleration compared to baseline setups.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of Orca focused on demonstrating the scalability of its learning paradigm and the effectiveness of its learned world latent space for diverse downstream tasks.

#### 4.1. Effectiveness and Scaling Behavior

- **Learning Paradigm Scalability (Question 1.1):** Experiments showed that Orca's total pre-training loss, calculated as a weighted sum of the three objectives, consistently decreased as both the model size (from 0.8B to 4B parameters) and the scale of pre-training data (up to approximately 7,000 hours of video) increased. The larger 4B model achieved lower objective loss than the 0.8B model, indicating that the learning paradigm is effective and benefits from increased capacity and data.
- **Latent Space Strength and Downstream Performance (Question 1.2):** Probing experiments, where Orca's backbone was frozen at different pre-training checkpoints, demonstrated a direct correlation between the scale of pre-training data and the performance on downstream tasks (text generation, image prediction, and action generation). As pre-training scaled up, all three downstream readouts showed consistent improvement. This result supports the hypothesis that a stronger world latent space, developed during pre-training, enhances downstream capabilities, even without task-specific fine-tuning of the backbone. Notably, this improvement in action generation occurred without the use of action-labeled data during pre-training, suggesting an emergent capability.

#### 4.2. Downstream Readout Analysis

Orca's performance was evaluated across three representative downstream tasks, with its backbone frozen and only lightweight decoders trained. No benchmark-specific training data or tuning was used for these evaluations.

- **4.2.1. Text Generation:**
 - **Benchmarks:** MVBench, TemporalBench, 3DSRBench, and SWITCH, covering general video understanding, fine-grained temporal dynamics, 3D spatial reasoning, and real-world tangible interface interactions.
 - **Results:** Orca-4B achieved an average score of 51.8 across these benchmarks, outperforming comparable-sized Vision-Language Models (e.g., Qwen3.5-4B at 46.7, Gemma 4-4B at 40.8) and larger-sized world models (e.g., Emu3 at 30.4, Emu3.5 at 29.8).
 - **Capability Dimensions:** A cross-benchmark analysis revealed Orca's specific strengths:
 - **State Transition:** Orca-4B showed a 12.27% improvement over Qwen3.5-4B (64.13% vs 51.86%).
 - **Commonsense Reasoning:** Orca-4B improved by 5.19% (62.95% vs 57.76%).
 - **Dynamic Motion:** Orca-4B improved by 8.52% (65.55% vs 57.03%).
 - **Spatial Relations:** Orca-4B showed a slight improvement of 0.57% (55.25% vs 54.68%).
 - **Analysis:** These results indicate that Orca's state-transition modeling significantly enhances abstract reasoning, understanding of causal temporal dynamics, physical state changes, and commonsense reasoning.

- **4.2.2. Image Prediction:**
 - **Benchmark:** PRICE-V0.1 (Prediction of Real-world Interactions with Constraints Evaluation), a novel real-world instruction-conditional image-to-image generation task evaluated by LLM judges. The focus was on predicting physically plausible state changes under instructions, rather than pixel-level reconstruction.
 - **Results:** Orca-4B+2 (Orca backbone + 2B parameter decoder) achieved the highest average judge score of 59.8, outperforming recent image generation baselines such as FLUX.2 [klein] (56.1), OmniGen2 (39.6), and FLUX.1-Kontext (40.9).
 - **Analysis:** Qualitatively, Orca's predictions demonstrated better preservation of robot morphology, scene and object consistency, contact relationships, and instruction following, avoiding common flaws like object hallucination or poor instruction adherence seen in baselines. This indicates that the learned world latent space effectively contains predictive information about future visual states under real-world interactions.

- **4.2.3. Action Generation:**
 - **Benchmark:** Five real-world manipulation tasks on a dual-arm wheeled humanoid robot, evaluated in Environment Out-of-Distribution (OOD) and Object OOD settings. Performance was measured using task-specific rule-based scores and dense trajectory-level diagnostics from PRM-as-a-Judge.
 - **Results:** Orca-4B achieved an overall average rule-based score of 32.4, outperforming V-JEPA 2.1 (17.0) and Qwen3.5 (10.5) (both using the same Action Expert trained from scratch, highlighting the latent's quality). Orca's performance was comparable to π_0.5 (29.4), a strong VLA baseline pre-trained on large-scale robot data.
 - **Metrics Analysis:** Orca demonstrated higher Milestone25% (M25) and Milestone50% (M50) completion, Success Rate (SR), MaxProcess in Failure (MaxP-F), Failure Near-Success Score (FNS), and Drawdown Recovery Ratio (DRR).
 - **Analysis:** These metrics suggest that Orca produces action trajectories that make more meaningful intermediate progress, suffer less from stagnation, and are more capable of recovering from execution errors. This indicates effective transfer of the learned world latent to action readout, even under limited robot data and OOD conditions.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

An ablation study on the three pre-training objectives (`L_obs`, `L_evt`, `L_vqa`) revealed their complementary contributions:
- The combined use of all three objectives (`✓✓✓`) yielded the most balanced performance across text, image, and action readouts (Average 48.0).
- `L_obs` (observation-only state transition) was particularly important for action generation, suggesting that dense natural dynamics from videos inform temporal changes and physical interactions relevant to robot control.
- `L_evt` (event-conditioned state transition) was crucial for vision readout, as it aligns language-described events with visual state transitions, enabling instruction-guided prediction.
- `L_vqa` (VQA response generation) maintained the language interface and strengthened the semantic grounding and common sense of the world latent space.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Orca project represents an initial step towards the development of general world foundation models, shifting the paradigm from modality-specific prediction to unified "next-state prediction." Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **A Unified Paradigm for General Intelligence:** By focusing on a unified world latent space and state-transition modeling, Orca proposes a framework that could lead to more integrated and general artificial intelligence. This approach aims to equip models with an intrinsic understanding of the world's dynamics, rather than merely performing specific tasks.
2. **Scalability and Transferability of World Knowledge:** The demonstrated scalability of Orca's learning paradigm with model size and data, coupled with the consistent improvement in diverse downstream tasks from a frozen backbone, suggests that a generalized world latent can be learned and effectively transferred. This implies that foundational world models could learn a rich internal representation that benefits various applications without extensive task-specific re-training.
3. **Efficiency in Embodied AI:** The emergent capability for action generation, even without explicit action labels in pre-training, is a significant finding. This could potentially alleviate the challenge of data scarcity in robotics by leveraging large-scale unlabeled video data to build robust world representations, thus improving generalization and recovery capabilities in real-world embodied tasks.
4. **Enhanced Multimodal Understanding:** Orca's ability to integrate unconscious learning (natural dynamics) and conscious learning (language-guided events) demonstrates a more comprehensive approach to multimodal understanding. This could lead to AI systems that not only perceive the world visually but also interpret it through causal and semantic reasoning.
5. **New Avenues for Research:** The paper identifies several limitations and proposes future research directions, inspiring the community towards:
 - **Broader Multimodal Integration:** Incorporating more sensory and physical signals (audio, tactile, force, proprioception) and extending to scientific domains to build a more complete world model.
 - **Native World-State Modeling:** Developing models that learn a unified world space directly from multi-source signals from scratch, rather than relying on pre-trained modality-specific encoders.
 - **Advanced Evaluation Systems:** Creating comprehensive evaluation frameworks for state prediction, intervention response, physical quantifiability, and counterfactual inference.
 - **Self-Evolutionary Learning Loops:** Enabling models to autonomously generate, evaluate, and filter interaction data for continuous self-improvement.
 - **Expanding Cognitive Boundaries:** Applying world models to complex systems in science, quantum mechanics, and cosmology to aid discovery and extend human cognitive reach.

In conclusion, Orca represents a foundational step in building models that aim to understand and interact with the world through a deeply integrated, latent state representation, offering a promising trajectory for the development of general-purpose AI.
