# OmniClone: Engineering a Robust, All-Rounder Whole-Body Humanoid Teleoperation System

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Beijing Institute of Technology and collaborators developed OmniClone, a robust and versatile whole-body humanoid teleoperation system, along with OmniBench, a new diagnostic evaluation suite. OmniClone achieved over 66% reduction in Mean Per-Joint Position Error compared to baselines on OmniBench and successfully collected high-quality demonstrations for training autonomous policies, achieving 85.71% success on a pick-and-place task.

## Problem

- Existing whole-body humanoid teleoperation systems lack robustness and task-agnostic versatility, limiting their practical deployment.
- Current evaluation methodologies rely on aggregate metrics that obscure specific failure modes and narrow specialization of models across distinct motion regimes.
- Existing systems frequently employ heterogeneous hardware and tightly coupled software, leading to labor-intensive, per-operator calibration requirements that impede scalability.

## Method

- Introduced OmniBench, the first diagnostic benchmark for humanoid full-body teleoperation, systematically assessing policy performance across varied workspaces, skill demands, and stratified difficulty levels using unseen motions.
- Developed OmniClone, a robust, versatile, and affordable teleoperation system with a Transformer-based full-body tracking policy, trained on a meticulously designed data recipe balancing diverse skills.
- Engineered an optimized infrastructure including subject-agnostic refined retargeting and robust queue-based data communication to mitigate real-world disturbances like morphological inconsistencies and network latency.

## Results

- OmniClone achieved superior tracking performance on OmniBench, reducing the Mean Per-Joint Position Error (MPJPE) by over 66% compared to state-of-the-art baselines like GMT and Twist2.
- Demonstrated robust real-time teleoperation with six diverse human operators (1.47 m to 1.94 m in height), enabling novice users to successfully complete complex loco-manipulation tasks within 5-7 practice trials.
- Collected high-quality, kinetically feasible demonstrations from OmniClone allowed a state-of-the-art VLA model (GR00T N1.6) to achieve 85.71% success on 'Pick-and-Place' and 80.00% on 'Squat to Pick-and-Place' after post-training with only 50 full-body demonstrations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Diagnostic benchmarks, such as OmniBench, are crucial for exposing specific skill imbalances and failure modes in teleoperation policies that aggregate metrics often mask.
> - The composition of training data, even subtle adjustments to a small fraction of it, significantly influences the policy's comprehensive coverage and balanced performance across dynamic and stable motion regimes.
> - A Transformer-based architecture consistently outperforms MLPs for whole-body teleoperation, demonstrating superior capacity for modeling long-range temporal dependencies and scaling with diverse skill datasets.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Humanoid robots are recognized as adaptable, general-purpose platforms capable of executing a range of complex tasks, either autonomously or through teleoperation. Whole-body teleoperation, which allows humans to remotely control humanoid robots, serves a dual role: it functions as a real-time operational interface and provides a scalable mechanism for collecting demonstrations to train autonomous learning systems. While frameworks for fixed-base manipulators and quadruped robots have achieved a certain level of maturity, comprehensive whole-body humanoid teleoperation continues to present significant challenges. The required operational scope for humanoids necessitates the integration of capabilities such as robustness, stability, dynamic agility, and precise control, which can often be conflicting.

Existing approaches in humanoid teleoperation have expanded the capabilities of these systems. However, these systems have been noted to exhibit limitations in terms of robustness and task-agnostic versatility, which restricts their practical deployment in real-world environments. The paper identifies two primary contributing factors to these limitations. First, current evaluation methodologies often rely on aggregate metrics that combine performance across distinct motion regimes. This practice can obscure specific failure modes and mask the narrow specialization of models, failing to provide diagnostic granularity. Second, existing teleoperation systems frequently employ heterogeneous hardware and software configurations that are tightly coupled to specific methods. This leads to labor-intensive calibration requirements for each new operator or motion capture setup, which impedes scalability and reproducibility. This work positions itself within this landscape by seeking to address these identified evaluation and system-level gaps, aiming to develop a teleoperation system that is robust, versatile, and accessible for practical use.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop OmniClone, a robust, versatile, and affordable whole-body humanoid teleoperation system capable of high-fidelity, multi-skill control. This overarching goal is motivated by the recognized limitations in current humanoid teleoperation systems, specifically their lack of robustness, versatility, and practical deployability for real-world scenarios.

The detailed objectives and motivations are as follows:

- **Addressing Evaluation Gaps through OmniBench:** A significant motivation was the absence of comprehensive, diagnostic evaluation benchmarks. Prior systems were often validated using aggregate metrics that conflated different motion regimes, thereby masking critical failure modes and the narrow specialization of models. This work aimed to introduce OmniBench, the first diagnostic benchmark for humanoid full-body teleoperation. OmniBench was designed to systematically assess policy performance across varied workspaces, skill demands (from high-dynamic agile motions to stable, precise quasi-static manipulation), and stratified difficulty levels using unseen motions. The motivation was to provide fine-grained diagnostics that could expose skill imbalances and bottlenecks, guiding subsequent policy and data improvements.

- **Engineering a Robust and Versatile Teleoperation System (OmniClone):** Building on insights from OmniBench, a core objective was to develop OmniClone, a teleoperation system that overcomes the narrow coverage of existing models. This involved:
 - **Developing a High-Capacity, Skill-Balanced Control Policy:** The motivation here was to move beyond policies that specialize in isolated skills. The objective was to create a transformer-based full-body tracking policy trained on a meticulously designed data recipe that balances diverse skills, from high agility to stable manipulation, ensuring comprehensive coverage.
 - **Integrating System-Level Mechanisms for Real-World Robustness:** Current systems struggle with real-world disturbances such as morphological inconsistencies (due to different MoCap hardware or operator anthropometric variability) and communication issues (fluctuations, latency). The motivation was to incorporate subject-agnostic refined retargeting and robust data communication mechanisms to mitigate these disturbances, ensuring consistent performance in practical deployment.

- **Ensuring Affordability and Accessibility:** A key motivation was to make whole-body humanoid teleoperation more accessible. The objective was to design OmniClone to be computationally efficient, requiring minimal data (approximately 30 hours of motion data) and a single consumer-grade GPU. This aims to reduce the barriers to entry for researchers and practitioners.

- **Achieving Control-Source-Agnostic Capability and Generalization:** The motivation was to develop a unified policy that is flexible enough to handle various control inputs. The objective was for OmniClone to support real-time teleoperation, generated motion playback, and Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models without requiring policy switching. Furthermore, the system needed to generalize reliably across human operators with a wide range of body proportions, demonstrating practical utility without extensive per-user calibration.

- **Validating Utility for Autonomous Learning:** Teleoperation serves as a critical tool for collecting demonstrations for imitation learning. A motivation was to demonstrate that OmniClone could collect high-quality, kinetically feasible data suitable for training downstream autonomous policies, thereby acting as a scalable engine for autonomous learning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employed a systematic, two-pronged approach, focusing on both diagnostic evaluation and system engineering, to develop the OmniClone teleoperation system.

**A. OmniBench Benchmark Construction**
The first step involved constructing OmniBench, a diagnostic evaluation suite for whole-body teleoperation policies.
- **Reference Motion Generation:** The benchmark utilizes the HY-Motion-1.0 dataset to generate a comprehensive set of reference motions. These motions are then retargeted to the humanoid robot's morphology using General Motion Retargeting (GMR). The goal was to ensure rigorous evaluation on motions not encountered during training, simulating real-world generalization challenges.
- **Motion Categorization and Stratification:** The evaluation set is structured across six functional categories: ‘Loco-manipulation,’ ‘Manipulation,’ ‘Squatting,’ ‘Walking,’ ‘Running,’ and ‘Jumping.’ Each category contains 60 episodes, with an average duration of 90 frames sampled at 30 Hz. To enable fine-grained diagnosis, these motions are further stratified by complexity (High, Medium, Low) and dynamic intensity (Fast, Medium, Slow). This detailed stratification allows for the identification of specific performance strengths and weaknesses across different skill types and difficulty levels.

**B. OmniClone Teleoperation System Architecture**
OmniClone is designed as a robust and versatile system, comprising a control policy and an optimized infrastructure.

#### 3.1. Transformer-Based Whole-Body Tracking Policy

- **Policy Architecture:** Recognizing the limitations of Multi-layer Perceptrons (MLPs) in capturing complex temporal dependencies, the system adopts a high-capacity Transformer architecture for both the teacher and student policies. This choice is based on the Transformer's ability to model long-range sequences and its scalability for learning from diverse, high-variability training environments. The policy maps observations (O) to actions (A), where A represents target joint angles.
- **Teacher Policy:** The teacher policy receives a comprehensive observation set (O_teacher) including full body orientations, angular velocities, the robot's full kinematic state (q, p), gravity vector (g), root angular velocity (ω_root), previous action (a_last), and reference motion joint states and body link kinematics (q_ref, p_ref). Observations are in the robot's local base frame for spatial invariance.
- **Student Policy:** For real-world deployment, the student policy's observations (O_student) exclude data difficult to estimate reliably on physical hardware, such as individual body link angular velocities. It includes the robot's kinematic state (q), gravity vector (g), root angular velocity (ω_root), previous action (a_last), and a buffer of future reference body states (p_ref_t:t+f).
- **Network Architecture and Reward:** Both teacher and student policies utilize a Transformer-based Actor-Critic architecture with linear input projection and MLPs output heads. The teacher policy uses a lightweight Transformer (embedding dimension 256, FFN dimension 512), while the student model has higher representational capacity (embedding dimension 512, FFN dimension 1024). The reward function encourages tracking fidelity and physical plausibility, with specific components for action rate, contact air time, joint acceleration, joint position/velocity/action limits, and tracking of torso, full-body, and end-effector states.
- **Training Data Recipe:** The policy is trained using a data recipe meticulously optimized through ablation studies guided by OmniBench diagnostics. The final recipe consists of 60% manipulation motions, with the remaining 40% balanced between dynamic maneuvers and stable locomotion. This data is aggregated from AMASS and LAFAN datasets and further augmented and heuristically filtered based on root state and whole-body joint energy.

#### 3.2. Optimized Whole-Body Teleoperation Infrastructure

- **Subject-Agnostic Refined Retargeting:** To counter morphological inconsistencies arising from different MoCap hardware configurations and anthropometric variations among human subjects, OmniClone implements a dynamic scaling factor. This factor is computed from the initial calibration frame and the humanoid's kinematics, then used to rescale raw MoCap data to match the humanoid's morphology. This method eliminates the need for manual, per-operator calibration and mitigates geometric discrepancies.
- **Robust Data Communication:** To address control signal fluctuations and network latency in real-world deployment, a queue-based data manager is employed. This manager buffers `f` frames of retargeted motion in a FIFO queue at a fixed update frequency. During signal interruptions, a zero-order hold strategy is used to maintain smooth execution. Communication between the retargeting server and policy server (supporting both remote and onboard configurations) utilizes UDP for efficiency, resulting in an end-to-end latency of approximately 80 ms.

**C. Implementation Details**
The policy training is conducted in Isaac Lab on a single NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU. The teacher policy converges in approximately 60 hours, over 33,000 Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) steps with 4,096 parallel environments. The student policy is then distilled from the teacher using DAgger in approximately 22 hours under the same configuration. The entire system requires about 80 GPU hours, emphasizing its affordability and computational efficiency on consumer hardware.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents detailed findings from both simulations on OmniBench and real-world experiments, demonstrating OmniClone's performance, robustness, and utility.

**A. Simulations on OmniBench**
- **Baseline Evaluation:** Initial evaluations of state-of-the-art (SOTA) methods (GMT and Twist2) on OmniBench revealed significant limitations. These baselines exhibited narrow specialization, achieving reasonable performance on manipulation tasks from a standard standing stance but showing considerable degradation in tasks demanding lower-body agility (e.g., deep squatting, low-altitude loco-manipulation) or highly dynamic maneuvers (e.g., jumping). The diagnostic granularity of OmniBench confirmed that aggregate metrics previously reported by these systems masked critical skill imbalances and failure modes in specific categories.
- **OmniClone Performance:** OmniClone achieved superior tracking performance across most tasks compared to GMT and Twist2, as quantified by both Success Rate (SR) and Mean Per-Joint Position Error (MPJPE). The system demonstrated a well-balanced performance profile across the 18 stratified categories of OmniBench, significantly improving overall tracking fidelity. Specifically, OmniClone reduced MPJPE by over 66% compared to the best performing baseline.
- **Ablation on Training Data Recipe:**
 - **OmniClone_Dynamic:** A policy trained exclusively on high-dynamic maneuvers and varying heights excelled at explosive actions like jumping but exhibited instability during precise manipulation due to a lack of robust stabilization strategies.
 - **OmniClone_Stable:** This variant, augmented with a larger proportion of stable locomotion and loco-manipulation data, showed improved performance on stability-oriented tasks such as squatting and loco-manipulation.
 - **OmniClone_Balance:** By further increasing the proportion of dynamic data, this version achieved a more balanced performance across both dynamic and stable regimes.
 - **OmniClone (Final):** The final OmniClone model was derived from OmniClone_Balance by rigorously filtering out biased sequences identified during real-world deployment. This optimization yielded the best overall trade-off in both SR and MPJPE across all categories. This ablation highlights that even subtle adjustments to the composition of a small fraction of the training data can significantly steer policy behavior and capabilities.
- **Ablation on Model Architecture:** A comparison between OmniClone (using a Transformer backbone) and OmniClone_MLP (using a standard MLP policy) demonstrated that the Transformer architecture consistently outperformed the MLP in both SR and MPJPE. This was attributed to the Transformer's capacity for modeling long-range temporal dependencies and its scalability with diverse skill datasets.

**B. Real-World Experiments**
- **Robustness and Reliability:**
 - **Real-time Teleoperation with Diverse Operators:** OmniClone was tested with six participants (ranging from 1.47 m to 1.94 m in height), most of whom were novice teleoperators. The system maintained consistent stability and task success during a composite loco-manipulation task (walk, stabilize, pick-and-place), despite substantial anthropometric differences. All novice users successfully completed the task within 5–7 practice trials, indicating effective generalization across varied body proportions and an intuitive user interface.
 - **Zero-Shot Generalized Motion Playback:** The system successfully tracked unseen synthetic motions generated by HY-Motion-1.0 (spanning stationary manipulation, loco-manipulation, and highly dynamic locomotion) with consistent fidelity. This validates OmniClone's control-source-agnostic nature, demonstrating reliable execution regardless of whether the reference motion originates from a real-time operator or a generative model.
 - **Qualitative Results:** Real-time teleoperation showcased OmniClone's ability to execute a broad spectrum of behaviors, including complex, long-horizon maneuvers like transporting coffee beans, retrieving tissue from a low cabinet, and serving tea without spillage, as well as highly dynamic motions such as running. All these behaviors were achieved with a single, unified policy without switching.
- **Data Utility for Autonomy:**
 - To validate the quality of demonstrations collected via OmniClone, the GR00T N1.6 Vision-Language-Action (VLA) foundation model was post-trained on only 50 full-body demonstrations collected by OmniClone.
 - The autonomous policy achieved a success rate of 85.71% on the 'Pick-and-Place' task and 80.00% on the 'Squat to Pick-and-Place' task, generalizing to minor object position variations. These results confirm that OmniClone produces clean, consistent, and kinetically feasible demonstrations, which are suitable for effective downstream policy learning even in low-data regimes. Notably, OmniClone served as both the data collection interface and the low-level controller during autonomous inference.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The OmniClone research contributes to the field of humanoid robotics by addressing critical gaps in whole-body teleoperation evaluation and system design.

- **Advancement in Teleoperation Robustness and Versatility:** OmniClone represents an advancement in whole-body humanoid teleoperation by providing a system that demonstrates robustness, versatility, and high-fidelity control across a broad range of motions and operators. Its ability to handle dynamic maneuvers, precise manipulation, and long-horizon tasks with a single unified policy marks a step towards more generalized humanoid control.

- **Diagnostic Evaluation for Policy Improvement:** The introduction of OmniBench is significant for the field as it provides the first comprehensive diagnostic benchmark. By disentangling policy performance across stratified motion categories and difficulty levels on unseen motions, OmniBench moves beyond coarse aggregate metrics. This granular analysis exposes specific failure modes and narrow specializations of existing systems, offering actionable insights for data curation and policy refinement in humanoid control. This diagnostic capability is crucial for identifying bottlenecks and guiding future research directions effectively.

- **Increased Accessibility and Affordability:** The design of OmniClone, requiring only approximately 30 hours of motion data and a single consumer-grade GPU for training, significantly lowers the computational and data barriers for developing advanced humanoid teleoperation systems. This affordability and accessibility can democratize research and development in humanoid robotics, enabling more institutions and researchers to engage with complex teleoperation challenges.

- **Enhanced Scalability for Autonomous Learning:** OmniClone's capacity to produce high-quality, kinetically feasible demonstrations, validated by its success in post-training a SOTA VLA model (GR00T N1.6) with limited data, positions it as a valuable tool for scalable imitation learning. By providing clean and consistent data, OmniClone facilitates the development of autonomous humanoid policies, potentially accelerating the transition from teleoperated control to fully autonomous operation in complex tasks.

- **Control-Source-Agnostic Platform:** The system's control-source-agnostic nature—seamlessly supporting real-time teleoperation, generated motion playback, and VLA models—expands its utility. This flexibility allows for diverse applications, from human-in-the-loop control to the exploration of generative motion models and complex autonomous task execution.

- **Potential for Broader Real-World Deployment:** By mitigating issues like morphological discrepancies and communication disturbances through subject-agnostic retargeting and robust data communication, OmniClone moves closer to reliable real-world deployment. Its demonstrated generalization across operators of varying body proportions further enhances its practical applicability in diverse operational settings, potentially accelerating the adoption of humanoids in industries requiring complex human-robot interaction.

Future work suggested by the authors includes scaling the data recipe for more extreme dynamic regimes, further extending OmniBench with standardized real-world task protocols, and leveraging the control-source-agnostic nature of OmniClone to integrate generative motion models with long-horizon autonomous execution in physical environments.
