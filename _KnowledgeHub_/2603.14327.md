# OmniClone: Engineering a Robust, All-Rounder Whole-Body Humanoid Teleoperation System

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Beijing Institute of Technology and collaborators developed OmniClone, a robust and versatile whole-body humanoid teleoperation system, along with OmniBench, a new diagnostic evaluation suite. OmniClone achieved over 66% reduction in Mean Per-Joint Position Error compared to baselines on OmniBench and successfully collected high-quality demonstrations for training autonomous policies, achieving 85.71% success on a pick-and-place task.

## Problem

- Existing whole-body humanoid teleoperation systems lack robustness and task-agnostic versatility, limiting their practical deployment.
- Current evaluation methodologies rely on aggregate metrics that obscure specific failure modes and narrow specialization of models across distinct motion regimes.
- Existing systems frequently employ heterogeneous hardware and tightly coupled software, leading to labor-intensive, per-operator calibration requirements that impede scalability.

## Method

- Introduced OmniBench, the first diagnostic benchmark for humanoid full-body teleoperation, systematically assessing policy performance across varied workspaces, skill demands, and stratified difficulty levels using unseen motions.
- Developed OmniClone, a robust, versatile, and affordable teleoperation system with a Transformer-based full-body tracking policy, trained on a meticulously designed data recipe balancing diverse skills.
- Engineered an optimized infrastructure including subject-agnostic refined retargeting and robust queue-based data communication to mitigate real-world disturbances like morphological inconsistencies and network latency.

## Results

- OmniClone achieved superior tracking performance on OmniBench, reducing the Mean Per-Joint Position Error (MPJPE) by over 66% compared to state-of-the-art baselines like GMT and Twist2.
- Demonstrated robust real-time teleoperation with six diverse human operators (1.47 m to 1.94 m in height), enabling novice users to successfully complete complex loco-manipulation tasks within 5-7 practice trials.
- Collected high-quality, kinetically feasible demonstrations from OmniClone allowed a state-of-the-art VLA model (GR00T N1.6) to achieve 85.71% success on 'Pick-and-Place' and 80.00% on 'Squat to Pick-and-Place' after post-training with only 50 full-body demonstrations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Diagnostic benchmarks, such as OmniBench, are crucial for exposing specific skill imbalances and failure modes in teleoperation policies that aggregate metrics often mask.
> - The composition of training data, even subtle adjustments to a small fraction of it, significantly influences the policy's comprehensive coverage and balanced performance across dynamic and stable motion regimes.
> - A Transformer-based architecture consistently outperforms MLPs for whole-body teleoperation, demonstrating superior capacity for modeling long-range temporal dependencies and scaling with diverse skill datasets.
