# Articulate AnyMesh: Open-Vocabulary 3D Articulated Objects Modeling

> [!summary] Summary
> Articulate AnyMesh introduces a three-stage automated pipeline that transforms any rigid 3D mesh into a functionally articulated object by leveraging Vision-Language Models and geometric reasoning. The framework enables open-vocabulary part segmentation and accurate articulation parameter estimation, demonstrating superior generalization compared to existing methods and enhancing robotic manipulation learning in simulation and real-world transfer.

## Problem

- Embodied AI and robotics require large quantities of diverse, realistic, and functionally meaningful 3D articulated object data, but collecting this data is challenging and expensive.
- Existing 3D asset generation methods predominantly create rigid objects or part-aware structures lacking explicit motion parameters.
- Prior articulated object creation methods are limited by data dependency on specific datasets (e.g., PartNet-Mobility) and struggle to generalize to novel, out-of-domain categories.

## Method

- A three-stage automated pipeline, **Articulate AnyMesh**, is proposed to convert any rigid 3D mesh into its articulated counterpart, applicable to diverse inputs like generated surfaces or real-world scans.
- Movable part segmentation and semantic labeling are performed in an open-vocabulary manner using ==Vision-Language Models (VLMs)== like GPT-4o, which are prompted with rendered object images to identify potential parts.
- Articulation parameters (joint type, axis, position, limits) are estimated by analyzing the geometry of 'connecting areas' between segmented parts and using ==geometry-aware visual prompting== with VLMs for joint classification and axis inference.

## Results

- The framework successfully generates a wide variety of articulated objects from diverse input meshes, including daily necessities, furniture, and tools, significantly expanding asset diversity.
- Achieved superior joint parameter estimation accuracy, with angle errors of **6.257** (in-domain) and **6.582** (out-of-domain) and position errors of **0.076** (in-domain) and **0.083** (out-of-domain) on PartNet-Mobility, outperforming state-of-the-art baselines, especially on unseen categories.
- Demonstrated utility in downstream robotic tasks, enabling successful real-to-sim-to-real transfer for motion planning and improving robotic manipulation policy learning success rates by effectively diversifying training data.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Common-sense knowledge embedded in Vision-Language Models, combined with geometric cues, can effectively infer complex articulation structures and parameters from generic 3D meshes without explicit articulation datasets.
> - The geometric properties of the contact regions between adjacent parts provide critical information for identifying joint types (revolute, prismatic) and accurately determining their axes and positions.
> - An open-vocabulary approach, driven by VLMs, significantly broadens the scope of articulated object generation, moving beyond category-specific methods to enable diverse asset creation for embodied AI.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits Into the Broader Research Landscape

The generation and manipulation of 3D articulated objects are critical but challenging problems in computer vision, computer graphics, and robotics. This work addresses a significant bottleneck in the rapidly expanding fields of embodied AI and robotics, which are increasingly reliant on large foundation models. These models are inherently "data-hungry," intensifying the need for vast quantities of diverse, realistic, and functionally meaningful data. While real-world data collection is laborious and expensive, simulated environments offer a scalable alternative, capable of providing rich ground truth information.

Existing research efforts in large-scale data collection for simulations have explored various aspects, including asset generation, scene generation, task design, and demonstration collection. However, a major challenge has persisted in generating diverse and realistic *articulated* objects, which are ubiquitous in everyday human environments. Prior attempts in 3D asset generation predominantly produce rigid objects or part-aware structures that lack explicit motion parameters. Consequently, a generated cabinet, for example, might appear visually correct but cannot be interactively opened or used to store items, severely limiting its utility for embodied AI tasks.

Methods specifically designed for articulated object creation typically suffer from two key limitations:
1. **Data Dependency**: They often require dense observations of an object across multiple joint states for reconstruction or rely on neural networks trained on limited, pre-defined datasets (e.g., PartNet-Mobility, which primarily covers furniture like cabinets, ovens, and dishwashers).
2. **Limited Generalizability**: This data dependency restricts their ability to generalize to novel categories or objects unseen during training, making "open-vocabulary" articulated object generation nearly impossible.

"Articulate AnyMesh" directly confronts these limitations by proposing a framework that circumvents the need for large, labeled articulated object datasets. Instead, it ingeniously leverages the emergent capabilities of modern Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and geometric reasoning to convert *any* rigid 3D mesh into its articulated counterpart. This approach marks a significant shift from data-driven learning on specific articulated categories to a more generalizable, knowledge-based paradigm. By enabling open-vocabulary generation, this work aims to unlock an unprecedented scale and diversity of articulated objects for simulation, thereby providing a crucial missing piece for the advancement of foundation models in embodied AI and robotics.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching motivation behind ARTICULATE ANYMESH stems from the critical need for large-scale, diverse, and functional 3D articulated objects to fuel the development of embodied AI and robotics. Large foundation models are demonstrating immense potential in these domains, but their performance is intrinsically linked to the quantity and quality of training data. The authors highlight that while simulation offers an efficient avenue for data generation, the scarcity of diverse *articulated* assets hinders the creation of realistic and generalizable robotic learning environments.

The key objectives of ARTICULATE ANYMESH are therefore:

1. **To develop an automated, open-vocabulary framework**: The primary goal is to create a pipeline that can convert any given rigid 3D mesh into a functionally articulated object, transcending the limitations of existing methods that are restricted to a narrow set of pre-defined categories.
2. **To capture accurate semantic structures and articulation parameters**: Beyond merely generating geometry, the framework aims to identify semantically meaningful parts, estimate their relative motion capabilities, and precisely determine articulation parameters such as joint types (e.g., revolute, prismatic), axes, and positions.
3. **To expand the coverage of 3D articulated object datasets**: By enabling open-vocabulary generation, the project seeks to significantly increase the diversity and scale of available articulated objects, moving beyond existing limited datasets like PartNet-Mobility. This enriched data can then support more robust and generalizable robotic skill learning.
4. **To facilitate robot manipulation skill acquisition and transfer**: A practical objective is to demonstrate that the generated articulated assets are not just static models but usable components within simulation environments, enabling robots to learn new manipulation skills that are transferable to real-world systems.
5. **To leverage prior knowledge from foundation models**: Instead of relying on specific articulated object data, the authors aim to tap into the vast common-sense knowledge embedded in visual and language foundation models, combined with generalizable geometric clues, to infer articulation structures.

Ultimately, ARTICULATE ANYMESH is motivated by the vision of a future where robots can interact with the full spectrum of everyday objects, and this requires a scalable, flexible method for generating interactive digital twins of such objects.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ARTICULATE ANYMESH is an automated, three-stage pipeline designed to convert any 3D mesh into its articulated counterpart, leveraging Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and geometric reasoning. The process is applicable to various input meshes, including hand-crafted models, text-to-3D generated surfaces, and real-world reconstructions.

#### 3.1. Movable Part Segmentation via VLM Assistant (Figure 2A)

The first step focuses on identifying and segmenting all potentially movable parts within the input 3D mesh and assigning semantic labels to them in an open-vocabulary manner.
- **Core Method**: The pipeline primarily adopts PartSlip++, a method known for open-vocabulary 3D part segmentation.
- **Process**: Given an input 3D object, PartSlip++ utilizes an open-vocabulary grounding model (like SAM) and a multi-modal foundation model on multiple 2D rendered images of the object. These models extract bounding boxes and segmentation masks for relevant semantic labels in 2D. The 2D information is then fused to produce the final 3D part segmentation.
- **VLM Integration**: Crucially, the list of part labels required for segmentation is not pre-defined but dynamically generated by feeding an image of the input mesh into a VLM (specifically, GPT-4o). This allows the system to identify movable parts based on general common-sense knowledge rather than predefined categories.

#### 3.2. Articulation Estimation via Geometry-aware Visual Prompting (Figure 2B)

This is the core innovation, aiming to extract articulation parameters (joint type, axis, position, limits) for each segmented movable part, again in an open-vocabulary fashion. Unlike traditional methods that rely on trained models or datasets, ARTICULATE ANYMESH leverages geometric cues and VLM intelligence.
- **Connecting Area**: The central idea is that joints inherently relate to the geometry of the "connecting area" between two adjacent parts. This area is defined by points on one part that are within a predefined distance threshold to the closest points on the neighboring part.
- **Joint Types**: The method focuses on the two most common joint types: revolute (hinge-like rotation) and prismatic (linear sliding).
 - **Revolute Joints**:
 - **Hinge Identification**: The connecting area between parts is divided into multiple clusters, whose centers serve as candidate points. These points are projected onto 2D rendered images of the mesh and labeled with numbers.
 - **VLM Prompting**: GPT-4o is visually prompted with this annotated image to select two or more points that define the hinge, thereby establishing the rotation axis. The rendering viewpoint is optimized to maximize part visibility. K-means clustering is used to generate candidate points.
 - **Special Cases**: For hidden hinges (e.g., knobs) or complex mechanisms where the hinge isn't on the surface, the rotation axis is assumed perpendicular to a plane fitted to the connecting area. Additionally, for deeply concealed mechanisms, the method samples additional candidate points from the part's surface using the l0-cut pursuit algorithm to ensure distribution across various geometric and textured regions.
 - **Prismatic Joints**:
 - **Categorization by VLM**: GPT-4o is prompted to classify prismatic joints into two functional categories: "Inward & Outward sliding joints" (e.g., drawers, push buttons) or "Surface sliding joints" (e.g., sliding windows).
 - **Direction Estimation**:
 - *Inward & Outward*: The translation direction is determined by the normal vector of a plane fitted to the connecting area.
 - *Surface Sliding*: A 2D rendered image is annotated with arrows, and GPT-4o is prompted to select the most appropriate sliding direction.

#### 3.3. Geometry and Texture Post-Processing (Optional) (Figure 2C)

This final stage addresses common imperfections in input meshes, especially generated or scanned surface meshes that may lack internal geometry, leading to holes or incomplete parts after segmentation.
- **Shape Completion**: The HoloPart model is used to repair holes and recover missing internal geometry for segmented mesh parts. For instance, a microwave door might have only its outer surface initially, but HoloPart would complete its internal cavity.
- **Texture Generation**: Meshy, an off-the-shelf 3D texture generation model, is employed to apply high-quality textures (e.g., wood, plastic, metal) to the completed parts, enhancing visual realism. This step makes the pipeline self-contained for generating visually complete articulated objects from surface meshes.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted by the authors demonstrate the effectiveness and broad applicability of ARTICULATE ANYMESH across several dimensions:

1. **Open-Vocabulary Articulated Object Generation**:
 - The framework successfully processes a wide range of rigid 3D meshes—from generated surface meshes (e.g., via Meshy) to hand-crafted assets (e.g., Objaverse) and real-world scans—and converts them into high-quality articulated objects.
 - Figure 1, 3, and 4 visually showcase the generation of diverse objects such as daily necessities, furniture, vehicles, tools (e.g., drill), and even fictional items. This significantly expands the scope beyond categories typically found in existing datasets.

2. **Superior Joint Parameter Estimation Accuracy**:
 - **Quantitative Comparison (Table 1)**: ARTICULATE ANYMESH was quantitatively compared against state-of-the-art articulated object modeling methods, URDFormer and Real2Code, on the PartNet-Mobility dataset.
 - **In-Domain (ID) Performance**: While URDFormer and Real2Code were trained on specific categories, ARTICULATE ANYMESH achieved comparable or slightly better performance on these in-domain objects (e.g., angle error: 6.257 for Ours vs. 13.674/7.950 for baselines; position error: 0.076 for Ours vs. 0.144/0.167).
 - **Out-of-Domain (OOD) Performance**: Crucially, ARTICULATE ANYMESH demonstrated vastly superior generalization to out-of-domain categories. Its angle error remained low (6.582), while baselines saw significant performance drops (33.864 for URDFormer, 24.035 for Real2Code). Similarly, position error was significantly lower for ARTICULATE ANYMESH (0.083 vs. 0.299/0.194). This highlights the framework's key advantage in open-vocabulary scenarios.
 - **Additional Baselines (Appendix A)**: Further experiments against ANCSH, OPD, OPDmulti, NAP, and CAGE consistently showed ARTICULATE ANYMESH outperforming them, especially in generalization to unseen categories.
 - **VLM Ablation (Appendix B, Table 5)**: An ablation study on different VLMs (GPT-4o, Claude 3.7 Sonnet, Gemini 2.0 Flash) showed that while GPT-4o performed slightly best, all tested VLMs enabled ARTICULATE ANYMESH to significantly outperform conventional baselines in both ID and OOD settings.

3. **Demonstrated Utility in Downstream Robotic Tasks**:
 - **Real-to-Sim-to-Real Transfer (Figure 5)**: The framework facilitated the creation of digital twins of real-world objects from 2DGS reconstructions. These digital twins, complete with articulated structures, allowed for successful motion planning and trajectory generation in simulation (e.g., pushing a drill trigger, opening a microwave door, rotating a steering wheel). The generated trajectories were then successfully transferred to real robotic systems, demonstrating accurate part segmentation and joint prediction.
 - **Policy Learning in Simulation (Table 2)**: By augmenting existing training datasets for robotic manipulation tasks (e.g., lifting a bucket, opening a laptop lid) with objects generated by ARTICULATE ANYMESH, the authors showed an increase in the success rate of learned policies. This indicates that the generated assets effectively diversify training data and enhance robot learning, particularly for generalizing to a wider variety of object instances.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ARTICULATE ANYMESH represents a significant advancement in 3D articulated object modeling, with far-reaching implications across computer vision, robotics, and embodied AI.

1. **Pioneering Open-Vocabulary Articulated Object Generation**: This work introduces the first automated framework capable of converting *any* rigid 3D mesh into an articulated object in an open-vocabulary manner. This addresses a critical unmet need in the field, moving beyond the limitations of category-specific or dataset-dependent approaches.
2. **Empowering Data-Hungry Foundation Models**: By enabling the large-scale generation of diverse, high-quality articulated objects for simulation, ARTICULATE ANYMESH provides a vital resource for training the next generation of data-intensive foundation models in robotics and embodied AI. This can accelerate research by providing richer, more varied training environments, leading to more robust and generalizable intelligent systems.
3. **Novel Integration of VLMs and Geometric Reasoning**: The paper demonstrates an innovative and highly effective approach to leveraging the common-sense knowledge and visual understanding capabilities of advanced Vision-Language Models (like GPT-4o) in conjunction with geometric cues. This "geometry-aware visual prompting" method allows for robust part segmentation and articulation parameter estimation without requiring extensive, pre-labeled articulated object datasets, showcasing a powerful paradigm for complex 3D reasoning.
4. **Enhancing Robotic Learning and Sim-to-Real Transfer**: The demonstrated ability to create functional digital twins of real-world objects and use them to improve robot manipulation policy learning in simulation, with successful transfer to real robotic systems, is a key contribution. This capability can drastically reduce the cost and time associated with training robots for interacting with complex objects, making embodied AI more practical and accessible.
5. **Broadening Applications in 3D Content Creation**: Beyond robotics, the framework has significant potential for 3D content creation industries, including virtual reality, gaming, and animation. Artists and developers could rapidly convert static 3D models into interactive, articulated assets, streamlining workflows and expanding creative possibilities.
6. **Laying Groundwork for Future Research**: The work also highlights limitations, such as the occasional inaccuracy of articulation parameters due to imperfect input meshes or VLM biases, and the handling of uncommon joint types. These limitations define clear and exciting directions for future research, particularly in improving the 3D reasoning capabilities of VLMs and integrating more physically grounded structural models.

In conclusion, ARTICULATE ANYMESH not only solves a long-standing challenge in 3D modeling but also provides a foundational tool that is poised to significantly impact the development of more intelligent, adaptable, and capable embodied AI systems.
