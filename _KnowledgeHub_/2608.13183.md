# A Controlled Study of Self-Supervised Image and Video Pretraining under Limited Resources

> [!summary] Summary
> A controlled study pretraining image and video self-supervised objectives under matched K700 data, a shared ViT-B/16 encoder plus temporal neck, and an 80k-update budget. Contrastive, masked-reconstruction, feature-prediction, and diffusion losses are compared standalone and jointly, then frozen and probed on classification, segmentation, depth, action recognition, tracking, and camera pose. DINOv2 wins overall; adding VideoMAE trades geometry for semantics.

## Problem

- Published self-supervised methods differ simultaneously in data scale, resolution, patch size, model size, batch, and update count, so reported gaps confound objective with training conditions.
- Whether objectives remain complementary once data and compute are matched is untested, since most studies train one recipe or compare released checkpoints of unequal budgets.
- Objective-specific architectures block controlled image-versus-video comparison, and action-accuracy-only evaluation hides how representations differ on dense, tracking, and geometric tasks.

## Method

- Shared image-only ==ViT== encoder applied framewise, with a ==RoPE-based temporal neck== doing bidirectional space-time attention over concatenated per-frame CLS and patch tokens for video losses.
- Matched pretraining protocol fixing K700 source, ViT-B/16, 224px frames, 8-frame clips, and 80k optimizer updates across ==DINOv2==, ==I/V-JEPA==, ==I/V-MAE==, and ==I/V-Diffusion== objectives.
- ==DINOv2==-anchored ==multi-task joint training==: image losses backpropagate through the encoder only, video losses through encoder plus neck, with losses summed each step.

## Results

- DINOv2 ranks first on all six image evaluations, beating second-place I-JEPA by **13.9** points ImageNet top-1 and **10.7** to **16.6** mIoU across segmentation.
- V-MAE wins four of five video tasks, trading **7.0** points on K700 for plus **45.5** rotation and plus **23.7** translation pose accuracy over DINOv2.
- Joint V-MAE plus DINOv2 improves eight of nine DINOv2 numbers (plus **3.3** SSV2, plus **36.4** rotation) but drops tracking **0.3** and pose far below standalone V-MAE.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Under short budgets, feature-space objectives dominate pixel reconstruction and denoising; how each update is spent matters more than adding extra supervision signals.
> - Semantic and geometric representation quality trade off; a single benchmark axis such as classification accuracy will mislead any ranking of pretraining objectives.
> - Objective compatibility, not extra pixel loss, drives gains: masked reconstruction complements contrastive training while diffusion denoising degrades it at equal compute.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Modern neural vision systems are increasingly designed to generalize across a diverse array of tasks and deployment environments, ranging from classification and retrieval to dense prediction, tracking, and multimodal reasoning. Visual foundation models (VFMs) serve as a cornerstone in this paradigm, aiming to learn transferable visual representations rather than specializing in a single downstream task. This approach is particularly relevant for multimodal systems, such as visual large language models, where a frozen visual encoder must provide robust representations for varied prompts and tasks.

Self-supervised learning (SSL) has emerged as a prominent paradigm for training VFMs. SSL leverages the visual data itself as a supervision signal, exploiting intrinsic properties like appearance, geometry, context, and temporal structure from large image and video collections. This circumvents the need for extensive manual annotations, which are costly and time-consuming to acquire. Consequently, SSL underpins many contemporary image and video foundation models.

However, a significant challenge in the current landscape of SSL-based VFMs is their typical reliance on massive datasets, protracted training schedules, and substantial computational resources. This raises questions about the scalability and sustainability of current pretraining approaches. Prior studies often lack a normalized comparison framework, with various SSL methods being pretrained under differing conditions, including diverse source datasets, model architectures, input resolutions, training schedules, and optimization budgets. This variability makes it difficult to ascertain whether observed performance differences stem from the inherent effectiveness of the learning objectives or from the disparate training environments. For instance, Table 1 in the paper illustrates this lack of normalization across representative methods like DINOv2, MAE, I-JEPA, VideoMAE, V-JEPA, and JiT, showing simultaneous variations in data size, input resolution, patch geometry, training phases, iteration count, batch size, model size, and EMA targets.

Another open question in the field concerns objective complementarity. While abundant resources might allow for continuous optimization of a single objective to yield strong representations (e.g., MAE showing no saturation after 1600 epochs), resource-limited scenarios might benefit from combining objectives that emphasize different aspects of visual structure. This includes the potential complementarity between semantic (e.g., contrastive methods like DINO) and reconstruction-based objectives (e.g., MAE), which may capture object-level semantics versus fine-grained spatial information, respectively. Similarly, combining image-based and video-based SSL is hypothesized to be advantageous, as video pretraining can contribute information related to motion, correspondence, viewpoint variation, and temporal consistency beyond what static images provide. This work directly addresses these gaps by conducting a controlled study to understand the behavior and complementarity of different SSL objectives under predefined resource constraints.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the increasing resource demands of state-of-the-art visual foundation models (VFMs) and the practical implications of obtaining effective models with fewer computational resources. The ability to achieve resource-efficient pretraining would enable faster experimentation, facilitate retraining of models following deployment distribution shifts, allow adaptation to private or site-specific datasets, and broaden the accessibility of foundation model development.

To contribute to this goal, the work sought to address three specific practical questions:

1. **Ranking of Modern SSL Objectives under Matched Conditions:** The study aimed to determine how contemporary SSL objectives compare in performance when confounding factors such as training data, model architecture, and computational budget are explicitly matched. This controlled comparison is considered essential for isolating the impact of the SSL objective itself on representation quality.

2. **Complementarity of Pixel-level SSL with Semantic Image Objectives:** The research investigated whether pixel-level SSL objectives, which typically encourage the retention of local spatial structure useful for dense prediction tasks, could complement a strong semantic image objective (e.g., DINOv2) within a fixed-resource regime. The hypothesis was that such a combination might produce representations that are robust for both semantic recognition and fine-grained tasks.

3. **Advantageous Combination of Image-based and Video-based SSL Objectives:** The study explored whether jointly optimizing image-based and video-based SSL objectives could lead to stronger representations than either type of objective alone, particularly under limited compute budgets. This was motivated by the observation that video pretraining often contributes information about motion and temporal consistency that is unavailable in static images, potentially enhancing the shared visual backbone.

In addressing these objectives, the authors made several contributions:

- They conducted a controlled comparison of contrastive-style, reconstruction-style, feature-prediction, and diffusion-style SSL objectives for both image and video pretraining. This comparison was performed under matched K700 source data, a unified architecture, and an 80k-update budget, establishing a benchmark for resource-efficient VFM development.
- They investigated objective complementarity by combining DINOv2 with both pixel-level image objectives and video SSL objectives, all within a fixed pretraining budget.
- They evaluated the resulting representations across a diverse suite of image and video tasks, including classification, semantic segmentation, monocular depth estimation, action recognition, tracking, and relative camera pose estimation, to assess the types of visual structure preserved by different SSL objectives.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this study focused on establishing a controlled environment to compare various SSL objectives, both individually and in combination, under identical resource constraints.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries: Self-Supervised Learning Objectives

The study compared several categories of SSL objectives:

- **DINOv2 (Contrastive Teacher-Student Training):** This objective involves a teacher-student framework where a student model learns to predict cluster assignments generated by an exponentially moving average (EMA) teacher from augmented views of an image. It incorporates both image-level and iBOT-style patch prediction, using Sinkhorn-Knopp assignments and a KoLeo regularizer.
- **I-JEPA and V-JEPA (Embedding Prediction):** These methods train a predictor to infer representations of masked target regions from visible context regions. An EMA target encoder provides stable target features. An L1 loss is applied in the representation space. I-JEPA operates on images, while V-JEPA extends this to spatiotemporal regions in video clips.
- **I-MAE and V-MAE (Masked Reconstruction):** These objectives involve masking a subset of image or video tokens and reconstructing their original RGB pixel values from the visible tokens. The encoder processes only visible tokens, and a decoder predicts the masked content using an L2 loss. I-MAE handles image tokens, and V-MAE handles spatiotemporal tube tokens in video clips.
- **I-Diffusion and V-Diffusion (Diffusion-style SSL):** These methods corrupt an image or video clip with Gaussian noise and train a denoiser to recover the clean signal across different noise levels. The denoiser predicts patch-space image or video values, applied in a velocity-normalized form. I-Diffusion works on images, and V-Diffusion on video clips.

#### 3.2. Model Architecture

A unified and simple architectural design was adopted to facilitate controlled comparisons and enable joint image-video pretraining.

- **Shared Image-Only Encoder:** All SSL methods utilize a common ViT-B/16 image-only encoder architecture ($E_\theta$). This encoder is applied either to individual images or framewise to all frames of a video clip with shared weights. This design ensures that the image SSL objectives directly supervise the core visual representation.
- **Pretrained Temporal Neck:** For video SSL objectives, a temporal neck ($N_\phi$) is added *after* the framewise image-only encoder. This neck models interactions across frames, taking patch and CLS tokens from the framewise encoder, adding temporal position embeddings, flattening tokens over space and time, and applying a RoPE-based transformer stack. This allows video objectives to add temporal supervision without replacing the visual backbone established by image objectives.
- **RoPE:** All attention layers in the encoder, temporal neck, and attention-based downstream heads employ rotary positional encodings (RoPE).

#### 3.3. Controlled Pretraining

A strict set of controls was implemented to ensure comparability:

- **Source Data:** All SSL runs exclusively used the Kinetics-700 (K700) dataset. Image objectives sampled individual frames from K700 videos, while video objectives sampled clips of consecutive frames.
- **Architecture:** The image-only encoder was a ViT-B/16. A consistent temporal neck architecture was used when present.
- **Input Resolution and Clip Length:** Image inputs were 224x224 pixels. Video clips consisted of 8 frames.
- **Compute Budget:** All pretraining runs were limited to an 80k optimizer-update schedule. This intentionally smaller budget, compared to typical state-of-the-art pretraining, allowed for extensive comparison of multiple objectives within a practical resource constraint.
- **Joint Training:** For combined objectives, a multi-task training setup was used. Image objectives attached to the image-encoder path, and video objectives attached to the framewise image encoder followed by the temporal neck. Losses from active objectives were summed. The image encoder received gradients from all active losses, while the temporal neck received gradients only from video SSL losses. The study focused on DINOv2-anchored combinations (DINOv2 + I-MAE, DINOv2 + I-Diffusion, DINOv2 + V-MAE) given DINOv2's strong semantic baseline.

#### 3.4. Downstream Evaluation Tasks

After pretraining, SSL objective-specific heads and decoders were removed, and the pretrained modules (image encoder and temporal neck, if applicable) were frozen. Only lightweight task-specific heads were trained for evaluation.

- **Image Tasks:** Evaluated tasks included image classification (ImageNet-1K), semantic segmentation (Pascal VOC, Cityscapes, ADE20K), and monocular depth estimation (NYUv2, KITTI). For these tasks, only the frozen image-only encoder was used, ensuring matched parameter counts and compute across different pretraining strategies.
- **Video Tasks:** Evaluated tasks included video classification (K700, Something-Something V2 - SSV2), point tracking (MOVi-F), and relative camera pose estimation (RE10K). These tasks were evaluated in two variants:
 - **Encoder-only variant:** Downstream heads received tokens directly from the framewise image-only encoder.
 - **Pretrained-neck variant:** Tokens were first processed by the frozen pretrained temporal neck ($N_\phi$) before being fed to the downstream heads. This allowed for assessing the impact of reusing temporal pretraining.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study's findings are presented across several experimental setups, focusing on the performance of individual and combined SSL objectives under controlled resource limitations.

#### 4.1. Performance of Individual SSL Objectives under Controlled Budget (Tables 3 & 4)

- **Image Tasks (Table 3):**
 - **Feature-space objectives excelled:** DINOv2 consistently delivered the strongest overall performance across all six image evaluation benchmarks (ImageNet-1K classification, Pascal VOC, Cityscapes, ADE20K segmentation, NYUv2, and KITTI depth estimation). I-JEPA ranked second across all these tasks. For example, DINOv2 achieved 62.2% Top-1 accuracy on ImageNet-1K, surpassing I-JEPA (48.3%) by 13.9 points, and showing superior mIoU scores for segmentation and lower RMSE for depth estimation.
 - **Image-based vs. Video-based objectives on image tasks:** Image-based SSL objectives generally outperformed their video-based counterparts on image tasks (e.g., I-MAE vs. V-MAE, I-JEPA vs. V-JEPA). Image-based methods outperformed video-based methods in 16 out of 18 direct comparisons on image tasks, suggesting that under a short, resource-limited schedule, video objectives do not automatically yield a stronger image encoder for static image understanding.

- **Video Tasks (Table 4):**
 - **Task-dependent specialization:** Performance leaders varied by task. DINOv2, an image-based objective, was strongest in appearance-driven video classification (59.8% Top-1 on K700). Conversely, V-MAE, a video-based masked reconstruction objective, exhibited superior performance on tasks requiring motion and geometric understanding, leading on SSV2 (51.3% Top-1), both RE10K camera pose metrics (90.7% rotation accuracy, 34.8% translation accuracy), and MOVi-F point tracking (71.4% Jaccard).
 - **Video SSL advantage on geometric tasks:** Video-based objectives consistently outperformed their image-based counterparts on the geometric tasks (camera pose estimation), showing gains in all six comparisons. This observation suggests that video pretraining specifically contributes to learning spatiotemporal and geometric representations. These results indicate a clear complementarity between DINOv2 (semantic strength) and V-MAE (spatiotemporal/geometric strength) under the common update schedule.

#### 4.2. Combining SSL Objectives for Dense Image Tasks (Table 5)

- **I-MAE as a Complement to DINOv2:** Adding the pixel-level masked reconstruction objective of I-MAE to DINOv2 pretraining resulted in modest but broad performance improvements. Specifically, it increased ImageNet-1K classification accuracy by 0.6 points, improved all three segmentation mIoU scores (by 0.4 to 0.7 points), and slightly decreased NYUv2 depth RMSE. Only KITTI depth estimation showed a minor adverse change. This suggests that I-MAE's focus on raw pixel targets can provide fine spatial features that complement DINOv2's semantic representations.
- **I-Diffusion did not complement DINOv2:** In contrast, adding I-Diffusion denoising generally led to performance degradation on ImageNet-1K, Pascal VOC, and NYUv2, indicating that objective compatibility and efficiency under resource constraints are crucial.

#### 4.3. Adding DINOv2 to Video MAE (Table 6)

- **Joint DINOv2+V-MAE bridges specialists:** Combining DINOv2 with V-MAE resulted in a model that outperformed both standalone DINOv2 and V-MAE across all six classification and segmentation evaluations (image and video). This included improvements of +0.5 on ImageNet-1K, up to +1.4 on segmentation datasets, and up to +3.3 on video classification (SSV2), relative to standalone DINOv2.
- **Tradeoff in geometric tasks:** While the joint training significantly enhanced semantic and recognition capabilities, it led to reduced performance on relative camera pose and tracking compared to standalone V-MAE. However, when compared to standalone DINOv2, the joint recipe retained nearly identical tracking performance while substantially improving pose estimation (+36.4 rotation accuracy, +10.7 translation accuracy). This outcome indicates that the joint DINOv2+V-MAE recipe can produce a single frozen representation that is strong across image semantics, video recognition, and pose, effectively turning two specialized models into a more versatile one.

#### 4.4. What Structure Is Learned Where? (Table 7)

- **Temporal neck for geometric-temporal structure:** For V-MAE, removing the pretrained temporal neck consistently reduced performance across all video tasks, highlighting the critical role of this module in learning important semantic and geometric structure.
- **Image encoder for semantics, temporal neck for geometry in joint training:** In the DINOv2+V-MAE combination, removing the temporal neck primarily impacted geometric tasks (camera pose, tracking), causing significant performance drops. Semantic tasks (video classification on K700 and SSV2), however, largely maintained their accuracy. This observation suggests that in the joint image-and-video training setup, the image encoder learns robust image- and video-level semantic structure, while the temporal neck remains essential for acquiring geometric structure. This offers an insight for future work on targeting objectives to specific architectural components.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This study provides empirical evidence regarding the efficacy and complementarity of various self-supervised learning (SSL) objectives for visual foundation model (VFM) pretraining, specifically under resource-limited conditions. Its findings have several significant implications and potential impacts:

- **Guiding Resource-Efficient VFM Development:** The work establishes a controlled benchmark for evaluating SSL objectives, allowing for direct comparison of their impact on representation quality and transfer performance when data, architecture, and computational budgets are matched. The finding that DINOv2 consistently provides the strongest overall performance under these constraints is a practical guide for researchers and practitioners aiming to develop effective VFMs with limited resources.
- **Understanding Objective Complementarity:** The demonstration that masked reconstruction (I-MAE) can complement DINOv2 for dense image tasks, and that joint image-video pretraining with DINOv2 and VideoMAE can significantly improve semantic image and video understanding, underscores the value of combining different types of supervision. This suggests that a multi-objective approach can be more effective than relying on a single objective when resources are scarce.
- **Highlighting Semantic-Geometric Tradeoffs:** The observed tradeoff where joint DINOv2+V-MAE pretraining improves semantic tasks but can degrade performance on geometric tasks (tracking, camera-pose estimation) compared to a purely video-centric objective (V-MAE) identifies a key challenge. This highlights the need for further research into improved methods that can better balance semantic, temporal, and geometric supervision within a unified framework without compromising any aspect.
- **Architectural Insights for Joint Training:** The analysis differentiating between the roles of the shared image encoder (learning strong semantic structure) and the temporal neck (crucial for geometric structure) during joint image-video training offers valuable architectural insights. This suggests that future joint training strategies could be optimized by selectively applying objectives to specific parts of the model architecture, potentially leading to more efficient and comprehensive representation learning.
- **Broader Accessibility and Faster Iteration:** By demonstrating that effective VFMs can be obtained with fewer resources through careful objective selection and complementary supervision, the research contributes to making foundation model development more accessible. This can enable faster experimentation, facilitate adaptation of models to specific or private datasets, and allow for quicker retraining in response to data distribution shifts, thereby accelerating progress in real-world applications of computer vision.

In conclusion, this study offers practical guidelines for navigating the complexities of SSL pretraining under compute constraints, paving the way for the development of more resource-efficient and versatile visual foundation models.
