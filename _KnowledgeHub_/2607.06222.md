# APVI-SLAM: Real-Time Acoustic-Pressure-Visual-Inertial Localization and Photorealistic Mapping System in Complex Underwater Environment

> [!summary] Summary
> APVI-SLAM presents a multi-sensor fusion system for robust localization and photorealistic 3D mapping in complex underwater environments. It achieves real-time performance and superior reconstruction quality by adaptively reweighting sensor data and using a quadtree-guided 3D Gaussian Splatting with water-medium modeling.

## Problem

- Traditional Visual-Inertial SLAM systems suffer from unstable pose estimation and divergence in underwater environments due to rapid light attenuation, suspended particles, and ocean disturbances.
- Existing multi-sensor fusion methods struggle with persistent visual feature loss, leading to time-consuming re-initialization processes and hindering real-time deployment.
- Current underwater mapping often produces sparse, non-photorealistic reconstructions and lacks efficient real-time methods for high-fidelity 3D scene representation, especially considering complex water-medium optical effects.

## Method

- A ==reliability-aware sensor fusion== localization module adaptively reweights **Visual-IMU (VIO)** and **DVL-IMU-Pressure (DIPO)** subsystem estimates based on a coarse-to-fine self-discrimination mechanism to manage visual degradation.
- A ==sliding-window freezing strategy== maintains trustworthy states during VIO degradation, enabling rapid recovery from visual dropouts without full re-initialization.
- A ==quadtree-guided 3D Gaussians== mapping module reconstructs photorealistic scenes in real-time by integrating high-confidence localization data and employing an online-optimized ==water-medium model== for accurate rendering.

## Results

- APVI-SLAM achieved an average position error of **0.22m** on the Tank public dataset, outperforming AQUA-SLAM by **26.7%**, and consistently demonstrated lower ATE and RPE values than baselines on a new simulation dataset, especially in hard sequences.
- The system delivered superior photorealistic reconstruction quality, achieving PSNR values of **31.233** (easy) and **32.390** (hard) on the simulation dataset, outperforming Photo-SLAM and MonoGS, and produced fine-grained maps in real coral reef environments (**29.05 PSNR**).
- Ablation studies confirmed the criticality of the multi-sensor fusion (M+I+D+P), which achieved **0.22m** average error, and showed the sliding-window freezing strategy enabled **100%** success rates in degraded environments, while quadtree-guided densification and water-medium modeling significantly improved mapping quality (e.g., **2.37 PSNR** gain in hard sequences).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dynamically reweighting and fusing complementary sensor data (camera, IMU, DVL, pressure) based on real-time reliability assessment is crucial for maintaining robust localization in highly dynamic and visually degraded underwater settings.
> - Preserving stable state information within a sliding window and using a 'freezing' strategy allows for rapid and efficient recovery of visual-inertial odometry after intermittent visual failures, avoiding costly full system re-initialization.
> - Combining an advanced scene representation like 3D Gaussian Splatting with a lightweight quadtree-guided densification and online-optimized water-medium modeling enables photorealistic and structurally consistent underwater mapping in real-time.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) is critical for autonomous robotics, enabling robots to navigate and understand their environments. Underwater SLAM, however, presents unique challenges due to the extreme properties of subsea environments. These challenges include rapid light attenuation, the presence of suspended particles, and ocean disturbances, which significantly degrade imaging quality and limit the effective sensing range for visual systems. Consequently, traditional Visual-Inertial (VI) SLAM systems often suffer from unstable pose estimation, estimator divergence, and compromised reconstruction quality in underwater settings.

Existing approaches to underwater SLAM have attempted to address these issues through multi-sensor fusion, integrating complementary measurements from sensors such as Inertial Measurement Units (IMUs), Doppler Velocity Logs (DVLs), and pressure gauges into factor graphs. While such integration can improve robustness, tightly-coupled multi-sensor estimators remain vulnerable to persistent visual feature loss. Complete visual dropouts often necessitate time-consuming re-initialization processes, including scale and covariance re-estimation, which can hinder real-time deployment. Furthermore, many current underwater mapping systems primarily rely on point-cloud representations, often using expensive stereo camera setups, and frequently lack the capability to reconstruct scenes with high photometric realism and structural consistency.

Recently, 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) has emerged as an efficient and photorealistic scene representation for high-fidelity reconstruction. However, applying incremental 3DGS methods to underwater environments introduces a domain shift due to complex water-medium optical effects. Some offline methods like SeaSplat and WaterSplat have incorporated water-medium models to address this, but they do not support real-time, incremental mapping. DUV-SLAM has advanced incremental underwater 3D Gaussian mapping using deep neural network-based geometry estimation, but its computational cost limits real-time deployment. Traditional feature-based front-ends also face limitations in low-texture underwater environments, leading to sparse geometry priors and reduced optimization capabilities.

APVI-SLAM positions itself within this landscape by seeking to overcome these limitations. It aims to develop a real-time, multi-sensor fusion SLAM system that provides both robust localization and photorealistic mapping in complex underwater conditions. The work contributes to the broader field by proposing mechanisms to enhance robustness during intermittent visual failure, enable rapid recovery from tracking failures, and efficiently reconstruct high-fidelity scenes with water-medium modeling, all while addressing the computational constraints for real-time operation. Additionally, it contributes a new dataset to facilitate benchmarking of underwater mapping performance, which is an identified gap in the current research.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of the APVI-SLAM research are:

1. **Develop a Real-Time, Multi-Sensor Fusion SLAM System:** The core objective is to create a system capable of operating in real-time within complex underwater environments. This system should integrate data from multiple sensors, including a monocular camera, IMU, DVL, and pressure sensor.
2. **Achieve Robust Underwater Localization:** The system aims to maintain accurate and stable pose estimation even under challenging conditions such as rapid light attenuation, suspended particles, and ocean disturbances, which typically cause severe visual feature degradation and estimator divergence in conventional visual-inertial systems. This involves addressing the vulnerability of tightly-coupled multi-sensor estimators to persistent visual feature loss.
3. **Enable Photorealistic Underwater Mapping:** A significant objective is to reconstruct underwater scenes with high photometric realism and structural consistency. This includes overcoming limitations of existing methods that often produce sparse or non-photorealistic maps and adapting advanced scene representation techniques like 3D Gaussian Splatting to the unique optical challenges of underwater environments, such as water-medium effects.
4. **Mitigate Visual Degradation and Tracking Failures:** The research seeks to develop strategies that prevent visual degradation from corrupting overall system estimation and to enable rapid recovery from complete visual dropouts, thereby avoiding time-consuming re-initialization processes.
5. **Address Computational Efficiency:** The system is designed for real-time deployment, which necessitates efficient algorithms for both localization and mapping, particularly for complex scene representations like 3D Gaussian Splatting, which can be computationally intensive.
6. **Contribute a Benchmark Dataset:** Recognizing the absence of comprehensive benchmarks for underwater mapping evaluation, the authors aim to contribute a new coral reef surveying dataset with synchronized multi-modality data, featuring various degradation scenarios to facilitate future research and comparative analysis.

The motivation for these objectives stems from several challenges inherent in underwater robotics and existing SLAM solutions:

- **Extreme Underwater Environments:** The severe physical conditions (light attenuation, turbidity, low texture) frequently lead to unstable pose estimation and poor map quality in standard VI-SLAM systems.
- **Vulnerability of Multi-Sensor Fusion:** While multi-sensor fusion improves robustness, intermittent or persistent visual failures can still compromise tightly-coupled systems, leading to estimator divergence or requiring costly re-initialization.
- **Limitations of Existing Mapping Approaches:** Many underwater mapping frameworks lack photometric realism, rely on expensive hardware (e.g., stereo cameras), or are too computationally intensive for real-time, incremental reconstruction in complex environments.
- **Lack of Suitable Benchmarks:** The absence of comprehensive datasets for underwater mapping, particularly with synchronized multi-modal data and ground truth for mapping performance, hinders the development and rigorous evaluation of new systems.
- **Demand for High-Fidelity Models:** Recovering photorealistic models is crucial for marine exploration, survey tasks, and scientific research, motivating the integration of advanced scene representation techniques.

By addressing these motivations, APVI-SLAM aims to advance the state-of-the-art in underwater SLAM, making autonomous underwater vehicles more capable and reliable for various marine applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

APVI-SLAM is structured into two main components: a **reliability-aware sensor fusion localization** module and a **quadtree-guided 3D Gaussians mapping** module. The system takes monocular RGB images, IMU, DVL, and pressure sensor measurements as inputs.

#### 3.1. Reliability-aware Sensor Fusion Localization

This module focuses on achieving robust pose estimation by adaptively reweighting sensor estimators, particularly to manage intermittent visual degradation.

#### 3.1.1. State Estimation and Sensor Subsystems

The system defines the robot's state at time *i* as `x_i = [R_i, p_i, v_i, b_g_i, b_a_i]`, comprising rotation, position, linear velocity in the body frame, and IMU gyroscope and accelerometer biases. Measurements from DVL, pressure, and camera are handled using prior extrinsic calibrations.

- **Visual-IMU Odometry (VIO) Subsystem:**
 - **Front-End:** Employs traditional optical flow tracking to address the scarcity and instability of visual features in underwater images, instead of descriptor-based feature matching.
 - **Weighted Information Matrix:** To account for unstable feature tracks due to water background, exposure, and camera noise, a weighted information matrix `Σ_V^-1 = (α * (n - n_min) / (n_max - n_min) + β) * I` is used. This dynamically scales the influence of feature points based on their tracking consistency (`n`, `n_min`, `n_max` are tracking counts/thresholds).
 - **Back-End:** Performs nonlinear factor graph optimization over a sliding window to jointly estimate historical and current states (`VIO_x_i`) and feature points. The cost function includes terms for IMU pre-integration constraints (`r_I`) and visual reprojection errors (`r_V`), weighted by their respective information matrices.
 - **Outputs:** The VIO subsystem outputs the current frame state, feature point tracking counts and stability, and the Hessian matrix (`VIO_H_i`) of the 6-DOF state, which serves as a measure of estimation confidence.

- **DVL-IMU-Pressure Odometry (DIPO) Subsystem:**
 - **Error-State Kalman Filter (ESKF):** This subsystem estimates the current state using an ESKF, leveraging direct measurements of linear velocity from DVL and depth from the pressure sensor.
 - **Motion Model:** Based on IMU forward propagation using angular velocity and linear acceleration.
 - **Observation Models:**
 - **DVL:** The DVL measurement equation (`z = v_i - v_m_i, R^T (p_i - p_i-1) - Δp_m_i-1,i`) incorporates observed linear velocity changes (`v_m_i`) and translation residuals (`Δp_m_i-1,i`).
 - **Pressure Sensor:** The pressure observation residual is constructed by estimating the vertical (z-axis) displacement from the depth computed by the pressure sensor.
 - **Outputs:** The DIPO subsystem outputs the current state (`DIPO_x_i`) and the inverse of the covariance matrix of the pose (`DIPO_H_i = P^-1_DIPO`), which indicates estimation confidence and is used for reweighting.

#### 3.1.2. Coarse-to-fine Self-discrimination Mechanism

This module evaluates the reliability of both VIO and DIPO subsystems to dynamically determine weights and fusion strategies.

- **VIO Stability Evaluation (Alg. 1):** A three-step coarse-to-fine strategy is applied:
 1. **Coarse Check:** Verifies if the number of front-end feature points (`n_fp`) exceeds 50 and if more than 20% (`n_sfp > 0.2 * n_fp`) are stably tracked (tracked across 10 consecutive frames). If not met, VIO is considered failed.
 2. **Medium Check:** Compares the Euclidean distance offset between `DIPO_x_i` and `VIO_x_i` (less than 30cm), z-direction deviation (less than 30cm), and Euler angle difference (less than 10 degrees).
 3. **Fine Check:** Analyzes the eigenvalues (`λ`) of the VIO Hessian matrix (`VIO_H_i`), obtained from the Levenberg-Marquardt optimization. Eigenvalues falling below analytically determined thresholds (based on a semicircle distribution model) indicate parsing degradation and VIO failure.
- **Fusion Strategy:** Based on this self-discrimination, the system dynamically reweights the VIO and DIPO estimators to obtain the fused state (`x_i`). If VIO fails, the DIPO state is used directly. Otherwise, a Kalman gain (`K`) is applied to combine `DIPO_x_i` and `VIO_x_i`, using the inverse of their respective Hessian/covariance matrices as noise parameters.
- **Freeze/Drop Flags:** The stable fused output is marked with a "freeze" flag, while unstable outputs receive a "drop" flag.

#### 3.1.3. Sliding-window Freezing Strategy

This strategy aims for efficient re-initialization of the VIO subsystem by leveraging prior information from the stable DIPO subsystem.

- **Maintaining Trustworthy States:** When VIO degrades (marked with a "freeze" flag), stable fusion outputs are retained within the sliding window and included in factor graph optimization. Unstable "drop" flag outputs are only used as odometry and excluded from the factor graph.
- **Fixed Keyframes:** Keyframes marked with "freeze" and their associated feature points are kept fixed in the optimization. This preserves scale and state information.
- **Rapid Recovery:** As visual degradation subsides, the factor graph quickly converges without full re-initialization because the prior state information has been maintained. The "freeze" state of historical keyframes is released once VIO recovers.

#### 3.2. Quadtree-guided 3D Gaussians Mapping

This module reconstructs photorealistic maps, selectively integrating localization data based on system state and feature reliability. Only when the localization module is in a stable tracking state are the current keyframe pose (`T_CW_i`) and high-confidence sparse point clouds (`{o}_s`) fed to the mapping system.

#### 3.2.1. 3D Gaussians Rendering based on Water-Medium Modeling

- **Gaussian Primitive Representation:** The 3D scene is represented by a set of anisotropic Gaussian primitives, each characterized by a mean (`o`), scaling (`s`), density (`σ`), spherical harmonic coefficients (`SH`), and covariance (`Σ`).
- **Projection and α-blending:** Each Gaussian is transformed to the current camera frame, projected to 2D, and its pixel radiance `C(u)` is computed via α-blending.
- **Water-Medium Model:** The final observed color `I'(u)` is obtained by applying a water-medium model (`I'(u) = C(u) * e^(-β_D * z(u)) + B_∞ * (1 - e^(-β_B * z(u)))`). This model accounts for distance-dependent attenuation (`β_D`), backscatter (`β_B`), and background color (`B_∞`), with depth `z(u)` derived from the Gaussian. Water-medium parameters are optimized online, with an increased learning rate during VIO failures to accelerate convergence.

#### 3.2.2. Lightweight Quadtree-guided Densification

This scheme addresses sparsity of feature points and fills in weakly-textured or turbid regions.

- **Quadtree Division:** The input image is divided into a three-level quadtree (16x16, 8x8, 6x6 blocks). Pixels within each block are assumed to share similar texture and depth characteristics.
- **Photometric Cost:** Depth hypotheses (128 values from 0.10–25m) for quadtree-selected pixels are evaluated using a photometric cost function `V(u, d)`, which measures image intensity consistency across a sequence of 10 keyframes.
- **Belief Propagation:** Belief propagation (`B_u_k(d) = I(u_k, d) + Σ_(s∈N(u_k)) (m_(s→u_k)(d))`) is applied, where `m_(s→u_k)` is a message passed between neighboring pixels in the quadtree block. The depth `d*` that minimizes the belief vector is chosen.
- **Depth Upsampling:** Missing regions are filled, and consistent depth maps are obtained through depth upsampling.
- **Gaussian Primitive Densification:** For regions not covered by the initial sparse point cloud (`{o}_s`), the top-left representative pixel of each quadtree block is projected into the world coordinate system using its estimated depth (`d*`) to generate a densified point set (`{o}_d`). The combined set of `{o}_s` and `{o}_d` forms the complete set of 3D Gaussians.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of APVI-SLAM was conducted on a public benchmark (Tank dataset), a newly developed simulation dataset, and a real-world coral reef surveying dataset.

#### 4.1. Experiment Setup

- **Datasets:**
 - **Tank Dataset:** Publicly available, multi-sensor data with marker-based ground truth, used for localization performance under adverse conditions. Lacks complexity for detailed mapping evaluation.
 - **Simulation Dataset:** Custom-built using UUV Simulator, comprising 6 sequences across Ship, Seabed, and Dam environments, with clear and degraded visibility scenarios. Used for comprehensive evaluation of localization and mapping.
 - **Coral Reef Surveying Dataset:** Collected using a customized BlueROV with a forward industrial camera, 6-axis IMU, Bar50 pressure sensor, and WaterlinkA50 DVL. Contains 8 sequences of low-texture, turbid, non-structural environments, used for real-world validation of mapping quality.
- **Metrics:**
 - **Mapping Quality:** PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS (lower is better for LPIPS).
 - **Localization Accuracy:** Average Absolute Trajectory Error (ATE) RMSE and Relative Pose Error (RPE) RMSE.
 - **Computational Resources:** Tracking FPS, Rendering FPS, GPU memory usage.
- **Baselines:**
 - **Classical SLAM:** VINS-Fusion, ORB-SLAM3.
 - **Underwater Multi-Sensor Fusion:** UVA, AQUA-SLAM (results from original papers for these two due to non-open-source or monocular limitations).
 - **Neural/Gaussian-based SLAM:** Go-SLAM, Photo-SLAM, MonoGS, VINGS.
- **Implementation:** C++ and CUDA, leveraging LibTorch, on an Intel i7-14700K CPU and NVIDIA RTX 4070 GPU.

#### 4.2. Evaluation on Tank Public Dataset

- **Localization Performance (Table I):** APVI-SLAM demonstrated competitive localization performance, achieving an average position error of 0.22m across various scenarios. Compared to AQUA-SLAM, a relevant underwater SLAM system, APVI-SLAM reduced the average position error by 26.7%. Its performance was notably superior in more complex and visually degraded scenarios, such as "Structure Hard" (0.24m vs. 0.50m for AQUA-SLAM) and "WholeTank Medium" (0.26m vs. 0.52m for AQUA-SLAM).
- **Mapping Quality (Fig. 4):** Qualitatively, APVI-SLAM produced high-fidelity scene reconstructions with sharp details. In contrast, MonoGS and Photo-SLAM exhibited significant blurring and artifacts.

#### 4.3. Evaluation on New Simulation Dataset

- **Localization and Mapping Performance (Table II):** APVI-SLAM consistently outperformed baselines across localization accuracy, mapping quality, and computational efficiency.
 - **Localization:** In both easy and hard sequences, APVI-SLAM achieved lower ATE and RPE values compared to all baselines. For instance, in easy sequences, its ATE was 5.699m, whereas ORB-SLAM3 had 11.820m and Photo-SLAM 7.146m. In hard sequences, APVI-SLAM maintained robust localization (ATE 5.937m), while most other baselines experienced severe performance degradation or outright failure (e.g., VINS-Fusion ATE 185.820m, Go-SLAM 413.222m).
 - **Mapping:** APVI-SLAM delivered higher PSNR, SSIM, and lower LPIPS values across all sequences, indicating superior photorealistic reconstruction quality. It achieved a PSNR of 31.233 in easy sequences and 32.390 in hard sequences, outperforming Photo-SLAM (28.990, 30.483) and MonoGS (27.010, 27.350).
 - **Computational Efficiency:** The system maintained high tracking FPS (100.00 FPS) and rendering FPS (874.09 to 1035.497 FPS), with reasonable GPU memory usage (4-6 GB), demonstrating real-time capability.
- **Qualitative Results (Fig. 5):** The method generated complete reconstructions with photorealistic and detailed textures, even under extreme degradation. Baselines often struggled, leaving missing regions or producing less detailed results.

#### 4.4. Mapping Evaluation of Real Coral Surveying Dataset

- **Robustness and Quality (Fig. 6):** In real-world field scenarios characterized by constrained illumination, limited visibility, and high sensor noise, APVI-SLAM demonstrated robustness. Photo-SLAM failed to track features and produced incomplete reconstructions. VINGS struggled with stable trajectory initialization. MonoGS, while producing a complete map, suffered from blurred texture (24.14 PSNR) due to localization inaccuracies. In contrast, APVI-SLAM delivered photorealistic reconstructions (29.05 PSNR) with fine-grained and spatially consistent details.

#### 4.5. Ablation Experiments

- **Sensor Settings (Table III):**
 - The Visual-Inertial (M+I) estimator failed under degraded (Medium and Hard) conditions.
 - The DVL-Inertial (I+D) configuration provided meter-level accuracy, highlighting the necessity of acoustic-inertial sensing in turbid water (Avg. error 0.38m).
 - Integrating pressure measurements (I+D+P) further refined the estimate (Avg. error 0.31m), offering critical vertical constraints.
 - The full configuration (M+I+D+P) consistently yielded the best localization performance (Avg. error 0.22m), demonstrating effective cross-modal complementary fusion.
- **Sliding-window Freezing Strategy (Table IV):**
 - APVI-SLAM achieved a 100% success rate across all sequences, indicating its ability to handle severe degradation without complete system failure.
 - Without the freezing strategy, the system experienced initialization failures in "Hard" environments.
 - The strategy significantly reduced re-initialization time, allowing rapid VIO recovery after visual features reappeared, outperforming the baseline that allowed VIO state to drift.
- **Quadtree-guided Densification and Water-Medium Modeling (Table V):**
 - Simple random densification improved mapping quality over gradient-based methods alone.
 - The lightweight quadtree-guided densification strategy further enhanced mapping efficiency, slightly reducing rendering time while improving quality (e.g., PSNR 30.96 vs. 30.50 in easy sequences without W-M).
 - Water-medium modeling had a notable impact, especially in challenging sequences, mitigating distance-dependent color shifts and yielding significant PSNR gains (e.g., 2.37 gain in hard sequences, comparing row 3 vs 4).
 - APVI-SLAM's mapping strategy outperformed SeaSplat (an offline method with state-of-the-art water modeling), showcasing the efficiency of the proposed incremental scheme.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of APVI-SLAM offers several significant contributions and potential impacts on underwater robotics and environmental mapping:

1. **Enhanced Robustness in Extreme Environments:** The core contribution is a multi-sensor fusion SLAM system that maintains high localization accuracy and mapping quality in challenging underwater conditions. The reliability-aware sensor fusion framework, with its coarse-to-fine self-discrimination and dynamic reweighting, directly addresses the persistent problem of visual degradation and estimator divergence. This enhances the operational reliability of autonomous underwater vehicles (AUVs) in complex and low-visibility environments.
2. **Improved Recovery from Visual Failures:** The proposed sliding-window freezing strategy is significant for mitigating the impact of complete visual dropouts. By preserving trustworthy state variables and enabling rapid VIO re-initialization, it reduces downtime and improves the continuity of operation, which is critical for long-duration underwater missions.
3. **Photorealistic and Efficient Underwater Mapping:** The integration of a quadtree-guided 3D Gaussians mapping scheme with efficient water-medium modeling allows for the reconstruction of photorealistic and structurally consistent underwater scenes in real-time. This capability moves beyond traditional sparse point clouds, providing richer and more detailed environmental representations. Such high-fidelity maps are valuable for marine exploration, habitat monitoring, archaeological surveys, and infrastructure inspection.
4. **Addressing a Benchmark Gap:** The contribution of a new coral reef surveying dataset with synchronized multi-modality data is significant for the broader research community. This dataset provides a much-needed benchmark for evaluating both localization and, crucially, mapping performance in real-world underwater applications, fostering further development and comparative analysis of new SLAM algorithms.
5. **Real-Time Performance:** The implementation in C++ and CUDA, demonstrating state-of-the-art performance at real-time speeds on various datasets, highlights the practical applicability of APVI-SLAM. This real-time capability is essential for autonomous navigation and on-the-fly decision-making for AUVs.

The potential impact of APVI-SLAM extends to various domains. In **marine science**, it can facilitate more accurate and detailed mapping of underwater ecosystems, such as coral reefs, supporting conservation efforts and ecological studies. For **offshore industries**, it can enable more efficient and safer inspection of subsea infrastructure like pipelines and cables. In **defense and security**, it could support autonomous underwater reconnaissance and mine countermeasure operations. By providing a more reliable and visually rich understanding of underwater environments, APVI-SLAM contributes to advancing the autonomy and utility of underwater robotic platforms. Future work on dynamic object handling, water-medium removal, and geometric structure enhancement suggests continued advancements for even broader applicability.
