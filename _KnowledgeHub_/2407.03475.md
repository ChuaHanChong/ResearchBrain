# How JEPA Avoids Noisy Features: The Implicit Bias of Deep Linear Self Distillation Networks

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Apple theoretically analyze why Joint Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPA) often outperform Masked Autoencoders (MAE) in self-supervised learning for perception tasks, demonstrating that JEPA implicitly prioritizes features predictive of target representations, whereas MAE focuses on features with high covariance. The analysis shows JEPA's beneficial bias strengthens with increasing network depth.

## Problem

- Empirical evidence shows JEPA-based self-supervised learning methods often perform better on downstream perception tasks than Masked Autoencoders (MAE).
- The underlying theoretical reasons for this performance discrepancy have not been clearly understood.
- A fundamental lack of clarity existed regarding the implicit biases of MAE and JEPA paradigms.

## Method

- The researchers at Apple employed a tractable model using ==deep linear== neural networks to analyze the learning dynamics of each paradigm.
- They derived analytical solutions for the gradient flow of both JEPA and MAE objective functions within this simplified model.
- The analysis characterized the learning dynamics in terms of data covariance matrix eigenvalues (λi\lambda_iλi​) and regression coefficients (ρi\rho_iρi​) between features and target outputs.

## Results

- Analytical solutions show JEPA's learning dynamics for each feature depend on both covariance (λi\lambda_iλi​) and regression coefficient (ρi\rho_iρi​) with the relationship λi1/Lρi1−1/L\lambda_i^{1/L} \rho_i^{1-1/L}λi1/L​ρi1−1/L​.
- MAE's learning dynamics are primarily governed solely by feature covariance (λi\lambda_iλi​).
- Numerical simulations and theoretical derivations confirm that as network depth increases, JEPA increasingly prioritizes features with high regression coefficients, while MAE continues to prioritize high-variance features, with JEPA's dynamics almost entirely dependent on ρi\rho_iρi​ in very deep networks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - JEPA objectives are implicitly biased towards learning "influential features" characterized by high regression coefficients (ρᵢ), which indicate predictive power.
> - MAE objectives are implicitly biased towards learning "highly covarying features" based on data covariance (λᵢ), which may include noisy or less informative signals.
> - The preference of JEPA for influential features becomes more pronounced and stronger as the network depth (L) increases, explaining its robustness to noisy details.
