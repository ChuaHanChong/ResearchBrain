# NExT-GPT: Any-to-Any Multimodal LLM

> [!summary] Summary
> NExT-GPT, from researchers at the National University of Singapore's NExT++ Research Center, introduces an end-to-end, general-purpose multimodal large language model capable of perceiving inputs and generating content across text, image, video, and audio modalities. The model leverages an LLM core for reasoning and achieves efficient training by fine-tuning only approximately 1% of total parameters, demonstrating enhanced performance in both multimodal understanding and generation tasks.

## Problem

- Most existing Multimodal Large Language Models (MM-LLMs) primarily focus on input-side multimodal understanding, lacking the ability to generate content in modalities other than text.
- Prior 'any-to-any' approaches often relied on pipeline-based systems (LLM combined with external tools), which suffer from information transfer noise, limited semantic understanding due to non-end-to-end training, and reliance on inference-only tools.
- There was a lack of an end-to-end, general-purpose MM-LLM that leverages an LLM's core reasoning capabilities while supporting arbitrary multimodal generation to mimic human-like interaction.

## Method

- A ==three-tier architecture== is proposed, comprising a multimodal encoding stage (using ==ImageBind== and a multi-stage grouping projection), an LLM core (Vicuna) for understanding and reasoning, and a multimodal generation stage (output projection layers and diffusion decoders like Stable Diffusion, Zeroscope, and AudioLDM).
- A lightweight ==multimodal alignment learning== strategy is employed, fine-tuning only the input and output projection layers and a small subset of LLM parameters (approximately 1% of total parameters), while freezing pre-trained encoders and decoders.
- ==Modality-switching Instruction Tuning (MosIT)== is introduced, utilizing a manually curated, high-quality 5,000-sample multi-turn dataset to train the model for complex cross-modal reasoning and dynamic modality switching in both input and output.

## Results

- NExT-GPT achieved competitive or superior performance in multimodal perception tasks, including image captioning, image question answering (e.g., **0.534 SPIDEr** on AudioCaps), and video/audio comprehension.
- The model demonstrated superior performance in multimodal generation, with a FID score of **10.07** for text-to-image on MS COCO, a FAD score of **1.68** for text-to-audio on AudioCaps, and a CLIPSIM score of **31.97** for text-to-video on MSRVTT.
- Human evaluations rated NExT-GPT significantly higher in "Instruction Following," "Rationality," and "Quality" compared to pipeline-based systems like Visual-ChatGPT and HuggingGPT, validating the benefits of end-to-end training.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The use of 'modality signal' tokens within the LLM's output effectively enables implicit, continuous communication and instruction to multimodal decoders, overcoming the limitations of discrete textual intermediaries in pipeline systems.
> - Lightweight fine-tuning, involving only approximately 1% of total parameters, is highly effective for aligning diverse pre-trained multimodal components with an LLM, making the approach computationally efficient and easily extensible.
> - The multi-stage grouping mechanism for input projection significantly improves the alignment between multimodal features and the LLM's semantic understanding, enhancing the model's perceptual capabilities across modalities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Artificial Intelligence Generated Content (AIGC) has seen unprecedented advancements, with Large Language Models (LLMs) like ChatGPT revolutionizing text generation and diffusion models making significant strides in visual content creation. Initially, LLMs focused on purely text-based understanding and reasoning. The research landscape then evolved to Multimodal Large Language Models (MM-LLMs), which integrate LLMs with capabilities to understand other modalities like images, videos, and audio.

Most existing MM-LLMs, such as BLIP-2, Flamingo, MiniGPT-4, Video-LLaMA, LLaVA, PandaGPT, and SpeechGPT, primarily focus on **input-side multimodal understanding**. They excel at processing multimodal inputs and generating textual responses, but they generally lack the ability to produce content in modalities other than text. While some recent works like Emu, DREAMLLM, GILL, and SEED have begun to explore multimodal generation, they are largely confined to generating interleaved texts and images, not a full "any-to-any" capability.

A few notable exceptions exist that attempt "any-to-any" conversion. CoDi (Tang et al., 2023) made strides in processing and generating arbitrary combinations of modalities in parallel. However, the authors argue that CoDi lacks the deep reasoning and decision-making prowess inherent to LLMs, as it does not have an LLM as its core. Other approaches, like Visual-ChatGPT (Wu et al., 2023) and HuggingGPT (Shen et al., 2023), combine LLMs with external, pre-trained tools. While offering a semblance of "any-to-any" functionality, these pipeline-based systems suffer from critical limitations:
1. **Information transfer noise:** Relying on discrete text for inter-module communication inevitably introduces noise and propagates errors.
2. **Limited semantic understanding:** The lack of end-to-end training across the entire system restricts the models' ability to interpret intricate and implicit user instructions effectively.
3. **Inference-only tools:** They leverage existing tools for inference without comprehensive end-to-end training, limiting their adaptability and robustness.

NExT-GPT positions itself directly to fill this critical gap. It aims to create an **end-to-end, general-purpose, any-to-any MM-LLM** that not only understands multimodal inputs but also generates responses in arbitrary combinations of modalities, all while leveraging the advanced reasoning capabilities of an LLM at its core. This approach seeks to combine the best of both worlds: the multimodal generation flexibility of models like CoDi with the deep semantic understanding and reasoning of LLMs, overcoming the limitations of pipeline-based systems through end-to-end optimization. The paper effectively contributes to the ongoing quest for human-level AI that can seamlessly perceive and communicate across diverse modalities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind NExT-GPT stems from the observation that human cognition and communication inherently involve seamless transitions between various modalities (text, image, video, audio). Current MM-LLMs largely fall short in this regard, predominantly focusing on input-side multimodal understanding without the corresponding ability to produce content in multiple modalities. The authors identify a "compelling need to construct an end-to-end MM-LLM of arbitrary modalities" to bridge this gap towards human-level AI.

The key objectives of the NExT-GPT research are explicitly outlined as follows:

1. **Develop an end-to-end general-purpose any-to-any MM-LLM:** To create a unified system, named NExT-GPT, capable of processing and generating content in any combination of text, image, video, and audio, along with semantic understanding and reasoning. This is the first time such an end-to-end, arbitrary-modality system is presented.
2. **Achieve lightweight and efficient alignment learning:** To enable the system to bridge the feature space between different modalities (encoders/decoders) and the LLM efficiently. This is achieved by fine-tuning only a minimal amount of parameters (approximately 1%) within specific projection layers, thus benefiting from low-cost training and facilitating easy expansion to new modalities in the future. This involves "LLM-centric alignment" at the encoding side and "instruction-following alignment" at the decoding side.
3. **Empower with complex cross-modal understanding and generation via Modality-switching Instruction Tuning (MosIT):** To equip NExT-GPT with human-like capabilities in complex cross-modal generation and reasoning, the authors introduce a novel instruction tuning approach called MosIT. To support this, they manually curate a high-quality, diverse dataset comprising 5,000 samples that cover intricate instructions across various modal combinations and multi-turn conversations, addressing the absence of such data in the community.

In essence, the motivation is to move beyond mere multimodal perception to truly versatile multimodal interaction, mirroring human communication, in a computationally efficient and extensible manner.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

NExT-GPT is designed as an end-to-end system comprising three interconnected tiers, leveraging existing high-performing pre-trained models to minimize training costs and maximize efficiency.

#### 3.1. Overall Architecture (Three Tiers)

1. **Multimodal Encoding Stage:** This tier is responsible for perceiving inputs from various modalities.
 - **Encoders:** NExT-GPT employs established encoders for different modalities. Notably, it leverages **ImageBind (Girdhar et al., 2023)** as a unified, high-performance encoder across six modalities (though NExT-GPT currently focuses on text, image, video, and audio). Using ImageBind spares the system from managing numerous heterogeneous modal encoders.
 - **Input Projection Layer:** The encoded representations from ImageBind are then projected into a language-like representation that the core LLM can comprehend. Instead of simple linear layers, NExT-GPT introduces a **multi-stage grouping mechanism** inspired by (Xu et al., 2022). This mechanism aggregates patch-level features into more semantic "concept tokens" through learnable queries and a Gumbel-softmax-based grouping block, aiming for better alignment with intricate textual token semantics.

2. **LLM Understanding and Reasoning Stage:** This forms the central processing unit of NExT-GPT.
 - **Large Language Model (LLM):** The core agent is an existing open-source LLM, specifically **Vicuna (7B-v0)**. The LLM receives the projected multimodal representations and performs semantic understanding, reasoning, and decision-making based on the input instructions.
 - **Output Generation:** The LLM generates two types of outputs: 1) direct textual responses, and 2) unique **'modality signal' tokens**. These signal tokens act as implicit instructions for the downstream decoding layers, dictating whether and what type of multimodal content (image, video, or audio) to generate.

3. **Multimodal Generation Stage:** This tier is responsible for producing multimodal content based on the LLM's instructions.
 - **Output Projection Layers:** Transformer-based projection layers map the LLM's modality signal token representations into a format understandable by the multimodal decoders.
 - **Diffusion Decoders:** NExT-GPT utilizes state-of-the-art latent conditioned diffusion models for content generation:
 - **Stable Diffusion (SD-v1.5)** for image synthesis.
 - **Zeroscope (v2-576w)** for video synthesis.
 - **AudioLDM (l-full)** for audio synthesis.
 - These decoders take the projected signal representations as conditional input to guide the denoising process and generate the corresponding content.

#### 3.2. Lightweight Multimodal Alignment Learning

A crucial aspect of NExT-GPT's efficiency is its lightweight training strategy. All multimodal encoders (e.g., ImageBind) and decoders (diffusion models) are **frozen**. Only a minimal set of parameters, specifically the input and output projection layers, and a small subset of LLM parameters (via LoRA), are updated during fine-tuning. This amounts to approximately **1%** of the total parameters (155M out of 12.275B), significantly reducing computational overhead.

The alignment learning consists of two phases:

1. **Encoding-side LLM-centric Multimodal Alignment:** This stage focuses on aligning the output of multimodal encoders with the LLM's input space.
 - **Task:** An "X-to-text" generation task is used, where 'X' refers to image, audio, or video. The frozen LLM is prompted to generate corresponding text descriptions (captions) given the multimodal input representation.
 - **Data:** Standard 'X-caption' datasets such as Webvid-2M (video-caption), CC3M (image-caption), and AudioCaps (audio-caption) are used.
 - **Mechanism:** The novel grouping mechanism (as described in 4.1) aggregates patch-level features into semantic concept tokens before feeding them to the LLM.

2. **Decoding-side Instruction-following Alignment:** This stage aligns the LLM's output signal tokens with the input requirements of the diffusion decoders.
 - **Special Tokens:** The LLM learns to output specific signal tokens like `[IMG_i]`, `[AUD_i]`, and `[VID_i]` to implicitly carry instructions for the diffusion models.
 - **Loss Function:** The training objective minimizes: 1) negative log-likelihood for signal token production, 2) L2-distance between projected signal token representations and the conditional text representations from diffusion models' text encoders (to bridge the gap, as diffusion models are text-conditioned), and 3) conditional latent denoising loss.

#### 3.3. Modality-switching Instruction Tuning (MosIT)

To equip NExT-GPT with human-like complex cross-modal reasoning and generation capabilities, the authors introduce a novel **Modality-switching Instruction Tuning (MosIT)** approach.

- **Motivation:** Existing instruction tuning (IT) datasets for MM-LLMs primarily focus on "Text+X → Text" scenarios (multimodal input, text output) and often lack multi-turn, dynamic modality switching. True human-machine interaction requires diverse and dynamic input/output modalities.
- **Approach:** LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) is applied to fine-tune a small subset of LLM parameters, along with the input and output projection layers. The objective includes cross-entropy for textual responses and generation loss for multimodal content, aligning modal signal tokens with gold multimodal caption representations.
- **MosIT Dataset:** A high-quality, manually curated dataset of 5,000 multi-turn dialogues (3-7 turns per conversation) is created. These dialogues involve sophisticated cross-modal understanding, reasoning, and content generation, with alternating modalities at both input and output sides. GPT-4 is leveraged for initial conversation generation based on templates and topics, followed by human inspection, filtering, and retrieval of best-matched external multimodal content (Youtube, Google, Flickr, AIGC tools like Stable-XL, Midjourney). This addresses the scarcity of such complex cross-modal IT data.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

NExT-GPT underwent extensive evaluation across various multimodal perception and generation tasks, demonstrating competitive and often superior performance.

#### 4.1. Multimodal Perception

- **Image Comprehension:** NExT-GPT showed strong performance in image captioning (NoCaps, Flickr 30K, COCO) and image question answering (VQAv2, VizWiz, OKVQA), consistently achieving significant improvements over baseline models like InstructBLIP, LLaVA, mPLUG-Owl, Emu, and DREAMLLM.
- **Video and Audio Comprehension:** The model excelled in video reasoning tasks (MSRVTT, MSVD-QA, MSRVTT-QA, NExTQA) and audio captioning (AudioCaps). It achieved enhanced results compared to CoDi, which the authors attribute to its direct text generation capability leveraging the LLM's inherent expertise. For example, on AudioCaps, NExT-GPT achieved a SPIDEr score of 0.534 and CIDEr of 0.807, outperforming CoDi (0.480, 0.789).

#### 4.2. Multimodal Generation

- **Text-to-Image/Audio/Video Generation:** NExT-GPT exhibited superior performance in generating images, audio, and video conditioned on text.
 - **Image:** Achieved a FID score of 10.07 (11.18 zero-shot) on MS COCO, outperforming or matching models like SD-1.5 (11.21) and CoDi (11.26).
 - **Audio:** Achieved a FAD score of 1.68 (1.74 zero-shot) on AudioCaps, outperforming AudioLDM-L (1.96) and CoDi (1.80).
 - **Video:** Achieved a CLIPSIM score of 31.97 (30.96 zero-shot) on MSRVTT, outperforming CoDi (28.90) and MakeVideo (30.49). Its zero-shot video generation capability was particularly notable.
- **Multi-modal Editing:** While not always showing superior performance compared to specialized editing models, NExT-GPT still demonstrated competitive results in text-conditioned image, video, and audio editing tasks.

#### 4.3. In-depth Analysis

- **Impact of Signal Token Numbers:** Experiments revealed that the optimal number of modality-specific signal tokens varied. Videos, due to their complexity, required the most (24 tokens for optimal FID), while images (4 tokens) and audio (8 tokens) achieved satisfactory generation with fewer. This highlights the nuanced control mechanism via these tokens.
- **Impact of Grouping Mechanism:** The proposed grouping mechanism for input projection significantly improved the model's perceptual capabilities across all three modalities compared to simpler linear projections or even Q-former-based approaches. This supports the idea that aggregating patch-level features into semantic concept tokens better aligns with the LLM's understanding of textual semantics.
- **Comparison: Pipeline vs. End-to-End MM-LLMs:**
 - **Human Evaluation:** Human evaluators rated NExT-GPT significantly higher in "Instruction Following," "Rationality," and "Quality" compared to pipeline-based systems like Visual-ChatGPT and HuggingGPT, and even a NExT-GPT variant that used captions as a messenger (NExT-GPT-caption). This validates the benefits of end-to-end training and implicit signal token communication over discrete textual instructions.
 - **Qualitative Examples:** The case studies clearly illustrated how pipeline systems struggle with complex instructions involving "numeration understanding" (e.g., specific counts of objects) and "visual-spatial relational semantics" (e.g., object positioning). NExT-GPT, by directly passing implicit modality signal token embeddings, naturally encodes and transmits these modality-specific features, overcoming limitations inherent to text-only intermediaries.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Analysis

Qualitative examples showcased NExT-GPT's ability to interpret and generate across diverse modalities and their combinations.
- **Implicit Intent:** NExT-GPT demonstrated proficiency in inferring implicit user intentions. For instance, when a user expressed a negative mood, NExT-GPT empathetically suggested and generated a cheerful puppy video without explicit instruction for video generation.
- **Complex Interactions:** The model could handle multi-turn conversations, understand complex queries (e.g., "unusual part of this video"), and generate appropriate multimodal responses (e.g., adding funny music, showing similar scenes, providing presentation tips with visual examples).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

NExT-GPT represents a significant step forward in the development of general-purpose multimodal AI agents, addressing a critical limitation in the current landscape of Large Language Models. Its primary significance lies in demonstrating the **promising possibility of building a unified AI agent capable of modeling universal modalities in an end-to-end manner.**

Key impacts and contributions include:

1. **Bridging the Modality Gap:** NExT-GPT is the first end-to-end, any-to-any MM-LLM that can both perceive inputs and generate outputs across text, image, video, and audio. This capability moves AI closer to human-like perception and communication, where information seamlessly flows between sensory inputs and expressive outputs.
2. **Computational Efficiency and Extensibility:** By leveraging existing well-trained encoders and decoders and fine-tuning only a minimal percentage (1%) of parameters in lightweight projection layers, NExT-GPT offers a highly efficient training paradigm. This low computational cost makes complex multimodal model development more accessible and facilitates convenient expansion to incorporate even more modalities (e.g., 3D vision, web pages) in future work.
3. **Enhanced Understanding and Control:** The introduction of "modality signal tokens" allows the LLM to implicitly dictate multimodal generation, overcoming the limitations of discrete textual instructions used in pipeline-based systems. This enables more accurate interpretation of intricate and implicit user intentions, leading to higher quality and more rational multimodal outputs, as demonstrated by human evaluations.
4. **Novel Instruction Tuning Paradigm:** The Modality-switching Instruction Tuning (MosIT) approach, coupled with its meticulously curated high-quality dataset, is a crucial contribution. It provides a benchmark and training methodology for developing MM-LLMs that can handle dynamic, multi-turn, cross-modal conversations, pushing the boundaries of interactive AI.
5. **Paving the Way for Human-like AI:** The ability of NExT-GPT to engage in complex, nuanced multimodal interactions—understanding context, inferring implicit intent, and generating diverse content—paves a clear path for future research towards more truly intelligent and human-like AI systems.

**Potential Limitations and Ethical Considerations (as noted by authors):**
The authors acknowledge that NExT-GPT, like other generative AI systems, may produce low-quality or hallucinated content, which could potentially be harmful. They emphasize the need for careful interpretation of results and adherence to licensing rules, explicitly prohibiting commercial use in the initial release. Data privacy is prioritized through anonymization and user consent. Furthermore, they are vigilant in minimizing bias in dataset collection to ensure fairness and representativeness.

Overall, NExT-GPT lays a strong foundation for future research in unified multimodal AI, emphasizing efficiency, versatility, and deeper semantic understanding to unlock new possibilities for human-AI interaction.
