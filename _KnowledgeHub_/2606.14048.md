# WAM4D: Fast 4D World Action Model via Spatial Register Tokens

> [!summary] Summary
> WAM4D enhances robot world action models by efficiently incorporating 4D geometric priors via training-time spatial register tokens, which are removed during inference to maintain computational efficiency. It achieved a 90% real-world sub-action success rate and an average 91.8% success rate in simulation across challenging manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Current World Action Models (WAMs) often rely on 2D representations, lacking the explicit 3D spatial and contact geometry needed for precise robotic manipulation.
- Integrating dense 4D geometric prediction into WAMs incurs high computational costs and latency, hindering real-time deployment for robot control.
- Explicit 4D prediction objectives can inadvertently refocus WAMs on geometric reconstruction rather than strengthening the causal link between video prediction and action generation.

## Method

- Introduce lightweight ==spatial register tokens== as future-depth readouts, acting as learnable geometry queries to distill geometric priors from a pretrained geometric head into the WAM backbone during training.
- Utilize a causal mixture attention mechanism to ensure geometric supervision strengthens causal video features without introducing non-causal shortcuts to the action predictor.
- Remove the entire geometric branch (registers and head) during deployment, enabling fast and efficient 2D observation-to-action inference without 4D prediction overhead.

## Results

- WAM4D achieved an average **91.8%** success rate across 50 simulation tasks on RoboTwin **2.0**, performing competitively with or exceeding established WAM baselines.
- In real-world robot manipulation, it demonstrated a **90%** overall sub-action success rate on AstriBot S1 across four challenging tasks, surpassing other WAM and VLA approaches.
- The model maintained efficient action generation latency of **525.43** ms and low VRAM usage (**9.71** GiB) during inference, comparable to or better than other complex WAMs.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Distilling 4D geometric priors via training-time spatial register tokens effectively enhances a WAM's spatial reasoning without requiring explicit 4D prediction during inference.
> - Spatial register tokens, particularly when introduced at intermediate layers of the transformer, are more effective for geometric distillation than direct VAE depth heads, improving both visual and geometric consistency.
> - A carefully designed causal mixture attention mechanism is crucial to ensure geometric supervision strengthens causal video features for action prediction without introducing non-causal dependencies or exposing auxiliary geometry tokens to the action predictor.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated within the field of embodied artificial intelligence, specifically focusing on robot control through advanced world models. World models have emerged as a foundational concept for embodied intelligence, enabling agents to predict environmental evolution under their interactions. Initially, progress in video generative models propelled the development of embodied world models that function as learned simulators for interactive environments, predicting future scene evolution. These models typically convert generated videos into actions using inverse dynamics models.

Concurrently, Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies have gained prominence for direct robot control, mapping visual observations and language instructions to actions. However, VLA policies often learn geometric and dynamic factors primarily from action supervision, which can limit their generalization capabilities, particularly for tasks requiring precise manipulation. While spatially grounded VLAs attempt to address this by incorporating 3D inputs or geometric foundation priors, the future scene evolution and robot dynamics largely remain implicit within the action prediction objective.

This context led to the development of World Action Models (WAMs), which represent an advancement by jointly modeling future observations and executable robot actions. WAMs leverage video generative foundation models to couple imagination and control, learning implicit transition models for scene and robot evolution. However, a significant limitation of most existing WAMs is their reliance on 2D video or latent spaces for representing future states. Such 2D rollouts, while visually plausible, often lack the precise 3D spatial constraints and occluded contact geometry essential for accurate robotic manipulation. Errors in contact geometry, which are hidden in 2D representations, can directly impede spatially precise tasks.

To address the limitations of 2D WAMs, recent 4D embodied world models have incorporated dense geometric information such as depth maps, surface normals, or point clouds into future prediction. These models enhance physical consistency and spatial reasoning. However, they frequently treat dense 4D geometry as an explicit inference target, necessitating costly geometric decoding or optimization during action inference, which increases deployment costs and latency. Furthermore, an explicit 4D prediction objective can inadvertently shift the WAM's focus towards geometric reconstruction rather than strengthening the causal link between video prediction and action generation.

WAM4D positions itself to resolve this trade-off. It aims to integrate 4D geometric priors into WAMs to improve spatial consistency for manipulation without introducing the computational overhead of dense 4D prediction during inference. The work seeks to provide spatially precise action generation while maintaining efficiency, thereby advancing the capabilities of robotic manipulation in complex, real-world environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind WAM4D stems from a critical challenge in current robotic world action models (WAMs): the trade-off between acquiring precise 3D spatial understanding for manipulation and maintaining efficient inference for real-time control.

**Problem Statement:**
Most existing WAMs operate in 2D video or latent spaces. While these models can generate visually plausible future rollouts, they inherently lack the explicit 3D spatial constraints and information about occluded contact geometry that are crucial for precise robotic manipulation tasks. A visually appealing 2D prediction might conceal inaccuracies in the underlying physical state, leading to failures in tasks requiring fine-grained interaction with objects.

While 4D embodied world models attempt to address this by explicitly predicting dense 3D structures and motion, this approach introduces several new challenges:
- **Increased Computational Cost:** Forcing WAMs to predict dense 4D representations necessitates costly geometric decoding or optimization steps during action inference. This directly impacts deployment latency, making such models less suitable for real-time robotic applications.
- **Shift in Objective:** Explicitly targeting 4D prediction can inadvertently shift the model's learning objective towards geometric reconstruction, potentially diluting its focus on the causal coupling between video prediction and action generation, which is fundamental to a WAM's utility in control.
- **Non-Causal Shortcuts:** Integrating geometric information directly into the policy inference path without careful design could create non-causal shortcuts, where the action prediction inappropriately leverages future geometric information during training, leading to poor generalization.

**Key Objectives of WAM4D:**
To address these issues, WAM4D sets out to achieve the following specific objectives:
1. **Integrate Geometric Priors Efficiently:** To transfer strong geometric foundation priors into a causal video-action transformer without requiring dense 4D representation prediction at inference time.
2. **Maintain Fast Action Inference:** To ensure that the incorporation of 4D spatial awareness does not impede the speed of causal action generation. This involves designing a mechanism where the geometric component can be removed during deployment.
3. **Enhance Spatial Consistency and Action Prediction:** To improve the spatial consistency of predicted futures and the overall success rate of action prediction, particularly in manipulation tasks that are sensitive to geometry and contact.
4. **Prevent Non-Causal Shortcuts:** To design the model architecture and attention mechanisms such that geometric supervision strengthens the causal video features without introducing non-causal dependencies or exposing auxiliary geometry tokens to the action predictor during inference.
5. **Validation on Challenging Tasks:** To demonstrate the effectiveness and robustness of the proposed approach on diverse and challenging simulated and real-world manipulation tasks.

By meeting these objectives, WAM4D aims to provide a solution that enhances the spatial reasoning capabilities of WAMs for precise manipulation while adhering to the practical constraints of efficient real-time robot control.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

WAM4D introduces a novel architecture and training strategy to integrate 4D geometric priors into a causal video-action model, designed for efficient inference. The core idea is to use lightweight spatial register tokens as training-time future-depth readouts, transferring pretrained geometric priors into the WAM backbone, and then removing the geometric branch for deployment.

#### 3.1. WAM4D Backbone

The model builds upon the causal video-action backbone of LingBot-VA. At each decision step `t`, it processes a language instruction `l`, a multi-view RGB history (`O_hist_t`), and a queue of historical actions (`a_t-L_a:t-1`). Future RGB frames (`O_fut_t`) and future actions (`a_t:t+H_a-1`) serve as prediction targets during training.

1. **Video Stream Processing:** A video VAE encodes the RGB sequence into history tokens (`Z_hist_t`) and clean future targets (`Z_fut_t`). During flow-matching training, noised versions of future video tokens (`Z̃_fut_t`) are fed to the backbone.
2. **Action Stream Processing:** A lightweight action embedding layer (`E_a`) embeds historical actions (`A_hist_t`) and noised future action states (`Ã_fut_t`).
3. **Joint Token Sequence:** These tokens are concatenated into a single video-action token sequence: `X^(0)_t = [Z_hist_t, Z̃_fut_t, A_hist_t, Ã_fut_t]`.
4. **Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) Backbone:** This sequence is processed by a MoT backbone (`VABlock_ℓ`) with a causal visibility mask (`M_VA`). Separate heads then predict flow targets for noised future video and action tokens.

#### 3.2. Spatial Register Distillation

This is the core contribution for geometric integration.
1. **Spatial Register Tokens:** Learnable geometry queries, denoted `R⋆`, are introduced. A shared register grid (`R⋆`) is replicated across future depth timesteps (`T_t`) and spatially aligned with the multi-view RGB mosaic. Each register corresponds to a spatial region in the input image. For a three-view mosaic (main and two wrist views), with a VAE spatial stride of 16 and WAM transformer's 2x2 latent grouping, each register corresponds to a 32x32 pixel cell. With 8 future depth frames, the default model uses 960 spatial register tokens.
2. **Depth Extraction Blocks:** At selected layers (`ℓ ∈ L_r`) of the MoT backbone (default: layers 12, 14, 16, 18), the registers are updated by "DepthBlock_ℓ". In these blocks, registers act as queries, attending to themselves and to valid history video tokens (`Z_hist,ℓ_t`) from the video-action backbone. RoPE encoding is applied based on target timestep and mosaic coordinates. This combines self-attention among registers with cross-attention from registers to history video features.
3. **Pretrained Geometric Head:** The updated register features are projected (`P_g`) to the input space of a pretrained geometric head (`G_φ`), which is initialized from Depth Anything V3. This head predicts the future depth sequence (`D̂_fut_t`).
4. **Training-Time Only:** Crucially, this entire register branch (registers, depth blocks, projection layer, and geometric head) is used *only during training*. The depth loss from this branch backpropagates into the shared video-action backbone, distilling geometric priors into the history video features.
5. **Lightweight Inference:** At deployment, the geometric head and register branch are entirely removed. The model reverts to a pure 2D observation-to-action interface, maintaining lightweight and fast inference.

#### 3.3. Causal Mixture Attention

A causal mixture attention mechanism is designed for the MoT backbone to manage visibility between different token types (video, action, spatial registers) and prevent non-causal information flow.
- **Text Instructions:** Visible to both video and action tokens via cross-attention.
- **Future Action Tokens:** Can attend to history video tokens, history action tokens, and their own noised future action tokens. This provides the necessary causal context for action prediction.
- **Masking:** To prevent non-causal shortcuts, future action tokens are masked from future video tokens and spatial registers. Similarly, spatial registers are isolated; they attend only to themselves and valid history video tokens, not to action tokens or future-video tokens. This ensures the depth objective shapes causal video features without exposing geometry tokens to the action predictor during training.

#### 3.4. Training Objective

The total loss `L` is a combination of three components:
- **Video Loss (`L_video`):** From the conditional flow matching objective of the base causal WAM, targeting future video latents.
- **Action Loss (`L_action`):** From the conditional flow matching objective, targeting future action chunks.
- **Depth Loss (`L_depth`):** A SmoothL1 loss supervising the predicted future depth sequence (`D̂_fut_t`) against ground-truth depth maps.
The final objective is `L = L_video + λ_act * L_action + λ_depth * L_depth`, with `λ_act = 1` and `λ_depth = 1`.

#### 3.5. Implementation Details

- **Frame Sampling:** Up to 17 frames are sampled, with a variable history context (1, 5, or 9 frames) and 8 future video-prediction target frames sampled every 4 collected steps.
- **Action Prediction:** Predicts absolute end-effector poses (3D position, quaternion, gripper state) for both arms (16-dimensional vector), normalized using percentile-based min-max normalization.
- **Geometric Readout:** Uses a pretrained DA3-GIANT-1.1 any-view DualDPT head, with linear adapters mapping WAM hidden states.
- **Inference Procedure:** At deployment, the geometry path is removed. The policy maintains an observation queue and executed-action history, autoregressively denoises future action chunks, and executes them, continuously receiving fresh observations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

WAM4D was evaluated extensively on the RoboTwin 2.0 simulation suite and real-world manipulation tasks using the AstriBot S1 robot.

#### 4.1. Simulation Results on RoboTwin 2.0

- **Action Prediction Performance:** WAM4D achieved a competitive average success rate of 91.8% across 50 tasks in both clean (93.8%) and randomized (89.9%) settings. This performance matches Fast-WAM and is comparable to other strong baselines like LingBot-VA (92.3%) and Motus (87.9%), while significantly outperforming VLA baselines (π0: 62.2%, π0.5: 79.8%).
- **Efficiency:** Despite incorporating 4D geometric priors, WAM4D maintained efficient inference, with an action generation latency of 525.43 ± 5.64 ms and peak VRAM usage of 9.71 GiB. This is notably faster than Motus (1516.30 ms) and LingBot-VA (843.57 ms), and competitive with Fast-WAM (425.53 ms), while using less VRAM than Motus, LingBot-VA, and Fast-WAM. The low latency is achieved by removing the geometry path during deployment.

#### 4.2. Real-World Results

- **Robust Manipulation:** On the AstriBot S1 platform, WAM4D achieved the highest overall sub-action success rate of 90% across four challenging manipulation tasks (plate lifting, bottle placement, pen cap removal, LEGO sorting).
- **Outperformance of Baselines:** WAM4D outperformed LingBot-VA (84%), Fast-WAM (80%), and π0.5 (74%) in these contact-rich, geometry-sensitive, and long-horizon tasks. Baselines exhibited issues such as trajectory drift (LingBot-VA) or a narrower effective workspace (π0.5 and Fast-WAM).

#### 4.3. Model Analysis and Ablations

Ablation studies were conducted on a 10-task subset of RoboTwin 2.0 to analyze design choices.
- **Spatial Register Attention Analysis:** Visualizations demonstrated that spatial registers effectively capture meaningful geometric cues. Object-related registers focused on objects across views, background registers on static regions, and gripper-related registers on the initial gripper pose, indicating the model's use of early action-history cues for future geometry prediction.
- **Spatial Register Design Ablation:**
 - **Depth Readout Interface:** Spatial registers significantly outperformed a direct VAE depth head, which, while improving RGB generation, yielded weaker geometry metrics. This suggests registers are more effective in shaping history video features for depth encoding.
 - **Register Placement:** "Middle-layer registers" (inserted at layers 12, 14, 16, 18 of the transformer) achieved the best balance between geometric distillation and visual feature preservation, leading to the strongest unidirectional control performance and overall geometry quality. Shallow, deep, and uniform placements were less effective.
 - **Visibility:** A "bidirectional" register variant (where the main video-action stream could read register features) achieved the highest success rate, but at the cost of increased computational complexity and degraded geometry metrics. Therefore, unidirectional registers were selected as the default for balancing performance and training cost.
- **Geometric Head Ablation:**
 - Initializing the geometric head with pretrained weights (from Depth Anything V3) and allowing it to adapt during training yielded the strongest generation quality, with superior video, depth, and point-cloud consistency metrics, and the highest success rates.
 - A randomly initialized depth head resulted in a clear drop in both video and geometry quality, indicating that strong geometric priors are essential.
 - A fixed pretrained geometric head performed better than random initialization but lacked the full adaptability of the trainable pretrained version.

#### 4.4. Qualitative 4D Rollout Visualization

WAM4D demonstrated an interpretable 4D rollout capability during analysis. Starting from a single initial frame, the model could autoregressively predict future RGB frames and corresponding depth maps. These RGB-D frames could then be back-projected into point clouds, visualizing the induced 4D scene evolution. This analytical capability is distinct from the lightweight deployment path.

#### 4.5. Failure Analysis

The paper noted a limitation in long autoregressive rollouts: without an explicit long-term memory, objects that become occluded or leave the visible context might be completed as different objects. However, this limitation primarily affects closed-loop visualization and does not compromise policy success rates during control, as the policy continuously receives fresh observations from the environment.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

WAM4D represents a notable advancement in world action models for robotic manipulation by effectively integrating 4D geometric priors without incurring the computational overhead typically associated with dense 4D prediction during inference. Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

1. **Bridging the 2D-3D Gap with Efficiency:** The primary impact is addressing the critical trade-off between gaining explicit 3D spatial understanding (essential for precise manipulation) and maintaining the inference speed required for real-time robot control. By using spatial register tokens for training-time distillation of geometric priors and subsequently removing the geometric branch for inference, WAM4D allows for robust geometric reasoning in the core world model without burdening the deployed policy. This design enables a "best of both worlds" scenario where complex geometric knowledge improves policy learning, but a lightweight policy is executed.

2. **Enhanced Robotic Manipulation Capabilities:** The superior performance in both simulated (RoboTwin 2.0) and real-world (AstriBot S1) manipulation tasks, particularly those involving precise contact, long horizons, and complex object interactions, indicates a step forward in robot control. The improved spatial consistency fostered by 4D geometric priors contributes to more reliable and accurate actions, which is vital for industrial applications, service robotics, and other domains requiring dexterous manipulation.

3. **Effective Integration of Foundation Models:** WAM4D demonstrates a practical strategy for leveraging large, pretrained geometric foundation models (like Depth Anything V3) to imbue world action models with sophisticated spatial awareness. This approach of "knowledge distillation" through a compact interface (spatial registers) provides a template for integrating diverse foundation models into embodied AI systems without requiring end-to-end retraining or complex multi-modal architectures during deployment.

4. **Causal and Robust Policy Learning:** The meticulously designed causal mixture attention mechanism ensures that geometric supervision strengthens the video features relevant for action prediction without introducing non-causal shortcuts. This contributes to more robust and generalizable policies by ensuring that the model learns appropriate cause-and-effect relationships between observations, actions, and scene evolution.

5. **Path Towards More Capable Embodied AI:** While the immediate focus is on manipulation, the principles demonstrated by WAM4D – efficient integration of multi-modal, high-fidelity world knowledge for improved control – could have broader implications for other embodied AI tasks. Systems requiring detailed physical interaction, object permanence, or complex spatial reasoning could benefit from similar mechanisms.

6. **Future Research Directions:** The acknowledged limitation regarding long-term object memory during autoregressive rollout presents a clear avenue for future work. Integrating persistent object memory or scene-state tracking while retaining the lightweight deployment path could further enhance the model's ability to reason about highly occluded or out-of-view objects over extended periods, pushing the boundaries of consistent 4D world modeling.
