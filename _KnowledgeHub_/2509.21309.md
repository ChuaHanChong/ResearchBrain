# NewtonGen: Physics-Consistent and Controllable Text-to-Video Generation via Neural Newtonian Dynamics

> [!summary] Summary
> NEWTONGEN introduces a framework for text-to-video generation that synthesizes videos with physical consistency and precise parameter control by integrating Neural Newtonian Dynamics (NND). The approach yields higher physical invariance and specific motion controllability across 12 distinct motion types compared to state-of-the-art baseline models.

## Problem

- Current text-to-video generation models struggle to produce physically plausible motion, often violating fundamental physical laws despite visual realism.
- State-of-the-art models lack an explicit understanding of underlying physical dynamics, limiting their generalization to out-of-distribution scenarios and precise control over physical parameters.
- Existing physics-aware generation methods either separate generation and simulation or implicitly learn physics, hindering strong conditional controllability.

## Method

- **NewtonGen** is a two-stage framework that integrates explicit physical principles directly into the video generation process to ensure physical consistency and precise parameter control.
- The Neural Newtonian Dynamics (NND) module is proposed, utilizing physics-informed neural ODEs (linear ODEs with a residual MLP) to learn diverse motion dynamics from 'physics-clean' simulated data.
- During inference, NND predicts future physical states from user-specified text prompts, which then condition a motion-controlled text-to-video generation model ('Go-with-the-Flow') to synthesize the final video frames.

## Results

- NewtonGen achieved significantly higher Physical Invariance Scores (PIS) across 12 distinct motion types (e.g., **0.9830** for PIS-v in Uniform Motion compared to Sora's **0.6548**), demonstrating superior physical consistency.
- The framework exhibited precise parameter controllability, with generated video trajectories and velocities faithfully matching user-specified physical conditions (e.g., initial location, speed).
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical role of the residual MLP in NND for modeling nonlinear dynamics and showed that NND can effectively learn from as few as 100 'physics-clean' videos, and even from real-world data, albeit with lower PIS scores.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly modeling underlying physics with Neural Newtonian Dynamics (NND) significantly enhances physical plausibility, allowing generated videos to adhere to classical mechanics.
> - The hybrid architecture of NND, combining linear ODEs with a residual MLP, effectively captures a wide range of complex linear and nonlinear dynamics within a unified framework.
> - NND can accurately infer underlying system dynamics from a relatively small amount of 'physics-clean' simulated data, reducing reliance on extensive, perfectly annotated physical datasets.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of text-to-video generation has seen substantial advancements with the emergence of large-scale probabilistic diffusion models since the early 2020s. Contemporary models can synthesize visually appealing and realistic video frames. However, a primary limitation in state-of-the-art text-to-video generation remains the consistent production of physically plausible motion and the ability to control specific physical parameters. Existing models often generate sequences that violate fundamental physical laws, such as objects moving in unnatural ways or exhibiting abrupt changes in velocity or direction. This limitation is attributed to current models learning motion distributions primarily from visual appearance data, lacking an explicit understanding of the underlying physical dynamics.

This work addresses this gap by integrating explicit physical principles into the video generation process, contrasting with approaches that rely solely on data scaling or implicit physical understanding derived from large models. The paper categorizes existing physics-aware generation methods into three types: "Generation then Physical Simulation," where generative models produce static content later animated by physical simulators; "Physical Simulation then Generation," where physics simulation guides generative models; and "Generation with Learned Physics Priors," which aims to integrate physical knowledge directly into the generative process. NewtonGen positions itself within the "Generation with Learned Physics Priors" paradigm, but distinguishes itself by grounding its physical prior in both explicit physics models and "physics-clean" data, aiming for stronger conditional controllability and improved out-of-distribution generalization compared to methods that rely on models implicitly learning physics from general video data. The Neural Newtonian Dynamics (NND) component is partly inspired by methods that learn physics from videos, often using encoder-decoder structures to estimate parameters of governing equations, but NND adopts an encoder-only architecture with general neural ordinary differential equations (ODEs) to capture diverse dynamics within a unified framework.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a text-to-video generation framework that can synthesize videos with physical consistency and precise parameter controllability. The motivation stems from observed limitations in current state-of-the-art video generation models. Despite their ability to produce visually high-quality frames, these models frequently fail to generate dynamic sequences that adhere to fundamental physical laws. Instances include objects defying gravity or exhibiting non-smooth, discontinuous motion. This issue is not merely a matter of scaling up data or model size, but rather a fundamental deficiency: current models learn motion distributions at the pixel or appearance level and lack an explicit understanding of the underlying physical laws that govern real-world motion. Consequently, they struggle to generalize to out-of-distribution scenarios and offer limited control over the specific physical parameters of generated motion.

To address these challenges, the authors propose NewtonGen, which explicitly incorporates physical laws into the learning process. The key contributions are twofold:
1. **Framework for Physics-Consistent and Controllable Text-to-Video Generation**: To introduce NewtonGen, a framework that integrates dynamics directly into the generation process. This aims to provide interpretable, "white-box" control over the generated motion, ensuring physical plausibility.
2. **Neural Newtonian Dynamics (NND)**: To propose NND, a module designed to model various dynamics using unified neural ordinary differential equations (ODEs). NND is intended to efficiently learn latent dynamics from a relatively small amount of "physics-clean" data.

By achieving these objectives, NewtonGen aims to bridge the gap between purely data-driven generative AI and the physical world, enabling the creation of videos where objects move in accordance with physical laws and their motion can be precisely dictated by user-defined parameters.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

NewtonGen is a two-stage framework designed to generate videos of foreground objects exhibiting classical mechanics, with controllable physical parameters, based on text prompts describing scene and initial conditions.

**Problem Definition**: The task is to generate videos of foreground objects whose motion adheres to classical mechanics, with controllable physical parameters, from scene prompts and specified initial conditions.

**Overall Framework**:
The framework consists of two main stages:
1. **Stage I: Neural Newtonian Dynamics (NND) Training**: A Neural Newtonian Dynamics (NND) module is trained on a dataset of "physics-clean" videos to learn the underlying motion dynamics and associated parameters.
2. **Stage II: Physics-Controllable Inference**: The trained NND module predicts future physical states from user-specified initial conditions (via text prompts). These predicted states, along with scene prompts, condition a motion-controlled text-to-video generation model to produce the final video.

#### 3.1. Neural Newtonian Dynamics (NND)

NND's core is a general physics-informed neural ODEs (Neural ODEs) model. It is designed to capture a wide range of dynamical behaviors.
- **Latent Physical States**: A 9-dimensional latent physical state vector `Z = [x, y, vx, vy, θ, ω, s, l, a]` is defined. This vector captures an object's position (x, y), velocity components (vx, vy), rotation (θ, ω for angle and angular velocity), and deformation/size changes (s, l for shortest/longest dimensions, a for projected area). This allows for modeling translation, rotation, deformation, and 3D motion effects.
- **Linear Physics-Informed Neural ODEs with a Residual MLP**: To accommodate diverse dynamics, the framework combines linear physics-informed neural ODEs with a residual multilayer perceptron (MLP). The linear ODEs are responsible for capturing dominant linear dynamics (e.g., free-fall), while the residual MLP models nonlinear and unknown components, enabling approximation of a wider range of physical behaviors. The ODE system for each state element `z` is formulated as `az ¨z + bz ˙z + cz z + dz + MLP(Z) = 0`. An ODE solver (odeint) is used to predict future physical states `Zt` from an initial state `Z0`.

#### 3.2. Training for Neural Newtonian Dynamics

- **Overall Training Pipeline**: NND is trained using an encoder-only architecture, which optimizes solely in the latent physical space, reducing computational cost. A Video Physical Encoder `Ephys` converts each video frame `It` into its corresponding physical state `Zt`. The NND takes the initial state `Z0` and frame timestamps to predict future states `^Zt`. The training loss is computed as the squared L2 norm between the encoder-extracted ground truth states `Zt` and the NND-predicted states `^Zt` over time.
- **Training Data**: To obtain "physics-clean" data, the authors developed a Python-based physics data simulator. This simulator renders videos with precise timestamps for various world settings, initial conditions, and dynamics types. This addresses the lack of high-quality, physics-annotated datasets.
- **Video Physical Encoder**: To extract physical state labels (`Z`) from videos, the SAM2 segmentation model is first used to obtain masks for dynamic regions. Morphological analysis of these masks yields centroid, area, long/short axes, and orientation. Velocities are computed from inter-frame centroid differences. These attributes are then uniformly quantized to form the 9-dimensional physical states `Z`.

#### 3.3. Inference for Physical-Controllable Text-to-Video Generation

During inference, physical dynamics reasoning (NND) is decoupled from video generation.
- **Physical State Prediction**: Based on user text prompts, initial physical state `Z0` and future time stamps are parsed and fed into the trained NND. NND then predicts the physical states for all subsequent frames.
- **Optical Flow Generation**: Using world setting information from the prompts (e.g., scene dimensions, object size, geometric shape), an approximate pixel-level optical flow for each frame is computed based on the predicted physical states. These flows are then downsampled to match the resolution of the video generator's latent space, forming a structured optical flow sequence.
- **Video Generation**: The motion-controlled video generation model "Go-with-the-Flow" (chosen for its general optical flow control capabilities, unlike models relying on trajectories/bounding boxes that struggle with deformation/rotation) is used as the base model. This model receives the structured optical flow sequence and the user's scene prompts to synthesize the final video frames.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluated NewtonGen's ability to generate physically-consistent and controllable videos across various dynamics.

#### 4.1. Implementation Details

- **Supported Motion Types**: NewtonGen was evaluated on 12 distinct motion types: uniform motion, acceleration, deceleration, parabolic motion, 3D motion, slope sliding, circular motion, rotation, parabolic motion with rotation, damped oscillation, size changing, and deformation. These motions typically occurred within 1-2 seconds with velocities up to 15 m/s.
- **NND Training**: NND was optimized using AdamW with a CosineAnnealingLR scheduler. For each motion type, 100 physics-clean videos were collected from the custom simulator. Training involved 20,000 epochs with a batch size of 64 on an NVIDIA A100 GPU, taking approximately 2 hours.
- **Metrics**:
 - **Physical Invariance Score (PIS)**: Adapted from PIS (Zhang et al., 2025a), this metric assesses physical plausibility by checking if expected invariants `C` (e.g., horizontal velocity in parabolic motion) are preserved. It is defined as `(1 + Cσ / (|Cμ| + ϵ))−1`, where `Cσ` is the standard deviation and `Cμ` is the mean of `C` over time. PIS ranges from 0 to 1, with 1 indicating perfect invariance. Specific `C` values were chosen for each motion type (e.g., `vx` for uniform motion, `ax` for uniform acceleration, `ω` for rotation, `Δl` for 3D motion and deformation, `Δr` for size changing). Reference PIS scores, computed from guidance masks, were provided as practical upper bounds.
 - **Background Consistency (BC)** and **Motion Smoothness (MS)**: Adopted from VBench (Huang et al., 2024) to assess scene consistency and motion quality.

#### 4.2. Comparisons with Other Methods

NewtonGen was compared against five baselines: Sora, Veo3, CogVideoX-5B, Wan2.2, and PhyT2V. These baselines represent state-of-the-art in both closed-source and open-source video generation, including physics-based methods. 24 prompts were used for each motion type.

- **General Comparisons (Qualitative)**: Figure 3 visually demonstrates that NewtonGen-generated video sequences exhibit higher physical consistency across all 12 motion types. The motions displayed smooth, realistic trajectories, 3D movement effects (e.g., object scale changing with distance), physically plausible self-rotation, smooth deformations, and natural size variations.
- **General Comparisons (Quantitative)**: Table 1 presents quantitative results. NewtonGen achieved significantly higher PIS scores compared to competing methods across different motion categories. For example, in Uniform Motion, NewtonGen scored 0.9830 for PIS-v, outperforming Sora (0.6548) and Veo3 (0.9784). In Parabolic Motion, it achieved 0.9803 for PIS-vx and 0.8189 for PIS-ay, considerably higher than baselines. For Circular Motion, NewtonGen's PIS-ω was 0.9788, while Sora's was 0.8393. NewtonGen also maintained competitive scores for BC and MS, indicating good background consistency and motion quality alongside physical plausibility. The results indicate that the baseline models struggle with maintaining physical invariants, often producing motions with higher standard deviations in expected physical quantities.
- **Parameter Controllability Comparisons**: Figure 4 illustrates NewtonGen's ability to interpret and reflect user-specified physical parameters (initial location, speed, etc.) in the generated videos. Unlike other models, NewtonGen consistently produced videos where the trajectories and velocities faithfully matched the input physical conditions, demonstrating precise parameter control. Figure 31 and 32 further exemplify how NewtonGen can generate diverse scenes with consistent motion given the same initial physical states, or corresponding motions for different initial physical states with the same scene description.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

The ablation study focused on the NND module:
- **Effect of MLP**: Removing the MLP from NND ("W/o MLP") led to a significant increase in normalized absolute error for predicting physical states, especially for nonlinear dynamics like circular motion (0.5388 vs. 0.0255), parabolic motion with rotation (0.7451 vs. 0.0064), and damped oscillation (0.2275 vs. 0.0425). This indicates the MLP's critical role in capturing complex, non-linear, and unknown dynamics.
- **Training Data Scale**: Experiments with 10, 100, and 500 physics-clean training videos ("Our-data10," "Our-data100," "Our-data500") showed that increasing data from 10 to 100 samples generally reduced the normalized absolute error in physical state prediction. However, increasing from 100 to 500 samples did not yield substantial further gains, and in some cases, the error slightly increased (e.g., Size changing: 0.1193 for 100 data vs. 0.1379 for 500 data). This suggests that NND can accurately infer underlying system dynamics from a relatively small, but "physics-clean," dataset.
- **Real-World Video Training**: NewtonGen (NND) was trained on real-world fall videos from PISABench. Figure 5 demonstrates that NND could effectively learn the underlying falling dynamics despite motion blur and lower data quality compared to simulated data. Videos generated using dynamics learned from real-world data showed plausible falling motions in new scenes, though with lower PIS scores (PIS-vx = 0.8485, PIS-ay = 0.6008) than those from simulated data (PIS-vx = 0.9803, PIS-ay = 0.8189). This indicates the feasibility of real-world training but highlights the practical advantages of simulated "physics-clean" data regarding quality and cost.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

NewtonGen represents a development in text-to-video generation by explicitly addressing the limitations of physical consistency and precise parameter controllability, which are prevalent in current data-driven models. By integrating Neural Newtonian Dynamics (NND), the framework introduces a method for injecting latent dynamical constraints directly into the video generation process, moving beyond appearance-level learning to incorporate an understanding of underlying physical laws. This approach holds the potential to narrow the gap between generative AI capabilities and the accurate representation of the physical world.

The significance of this work lies in several aspects:
- **Improved Physical Plausibility**: By leveraging NND, NewtonGen can generate videos where objects exhibit motions consistent with classical mechanics, avoiding common artifacts like unnatural trajectories or abrupt changes in velocity. This enhances the realism and utility of generated video content.
- **Enhanced Controllability**: The framework offers explicit, "white-box" control over physical parameters (e.g., initial position, velocity, rotation, size change). This level of precise control enables users to dictate specific motion characteristics, which is crucial for applications requiring exact physical behaviors.
- **Generalization to Diverse Dynamics**: NND, through its combination of linear ODEs and a residual MLP, demonstrates the capacity to model a variety of Newtonian motions, including complex scenarios like parabolic motion with rotation or damped oscillation, within a unified framework.
- **Efficiency in Learning Dynamics**: The ability of NND to learn accurate dynamics from a relatively small amount of "physics-clean" data, as shown in the ablation studies, reduces the reliance on vast, perfectly annotated physical datasets. The use of a physics simulator for data generation also offers a scalable and cost-effective approach to obtaining high-quality training data.
- **Foundation for World Models**: By incorporating physical understanding, NewtonGen contributes to the broader objective of developing generative AI models that can function as "world simulators," capable of predicting and interacting with realistic physical environments.

**Limitations and Future Work**: The authors acknowledge that the current framework, based on continuous dynamics, may be less effective for handling complex multi-object interactions such as collisions or coalescence. Future research incorporating event-based or discrete neural architectures is suggested to address these scenarios.

**Ethical Implications**: The authors also include an ethics statement, noting that while the model is designed for high-quality content and educational videos, misuse without proper labels or watermarks could lead to the production of fake videos and the spread of misinformation. This highlights the ongoing need for responsible development and deployment of generative AI technologies.
