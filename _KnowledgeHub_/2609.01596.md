# Facet-0: A Robotic Foundation Model for Contact-Rich Precise Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Facet-0 is a robotic foundation model for sub-millimetre assembly that treats contact as a predicted consequence of action. A PaliGemma backbone fuses wrist-wrench history with vision, language and kinematics, and flow matching jointly generates each action chunk plus its expected future wrench; a distributional Action-Wrench Critic and a bounded local actor then refine behaviour from deployment, reaching 82% mean success versus 15% for the best baseline.

## Problem

- Generalist vision-language-action policies learn motion alone; visually identical trajectories can end in clean insertion or damaging jam, so semantic breadth fails at the final millimetre.
- Force- and tactile-aware policies feed wrench in as an input or auxiliary target, never attaching anticipated contact to a proposed action that deployment value can rank.
- Large manipulation corpora prioritise behavioural breadth over synchronised six-axis wrench, while assembly datasets with force supervision remain far smaller, tens of hours rather than hundreds.

## Method

- **ManuFacet-1K**: a 1,000-hour force-synchronised corpus over three embodiments and multiple cells, with a seven-phase contact taxonomy reused for pretraining, reward decomposition and conditioning.
- Joint action-wrench ==flow matching== over a ==PaliGemma== semantic-contact representation: a ten-step wrench history fuses with vision-language and kinematic state, decoding 50-step action chunks plus anticipated wrench.
- A distributional ==Action-Wrench Critic== scores the joint proposal; contact-selective credit upweights decisive frames within each contact regime, and a bounded tanh actor adapts locally over the frozen representation.

## Results

- **82%** mean success across five sub-millimetre assembly tasks versus **15%** for the strongest baseline, at **0.5 mm** placement accuracy and **50 ms** command latency against **150 ms**.
- Ablated variants trace **16%** for alignment, **38%** with value-guided RL, **82%** with local adaptation; against matched AWR, intervention falls **47%** to **24%** and recovery rises **44%** to **81%**.
- Few-shot transfer to an unseen RAM module reaches **45%** versus **5%** for baselines while updating **6.6%** of parameters; removing all wrench roles drops RAM success **95%** to **45%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Feeding contact in as an observation is not enough; making wrench a prediction target and a value signal is what changes behaviour during contact.
> - Chained tasks sit at their weakest contact link, so a bounded correction at one limiting contact state buys disproportionate gains in completed assemblies.
> - Adapting behind a frozen representation inside an admissible action box keeps on-robot learning cheap and safe; individual ablations at 20 trials cannot be ranked.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of robotic foundation models has significantly advanced robot capabilities, particularly in generalist manipulation through Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies. These models, exemplified by architectures such as PaliGemma, Diffusion Policy, and OpenVLA, demonstrate broad competence in following instructions and generalizing across diverse tasks and embodiments. However, a notable challenge remains in contact-rich, sub-millimeter precision assembly tasks, where success hinges on subtle physical interactions, compliant behavior, and robustness to potential failures like jamming. Existing VLA models primarily focus on motion generation and often treat contact signals as observations rather than anticipated consequences of actions.

Prior research has attempted to address components of this challenge. Force-aware policies (e.g., ForceVLA, TA-VLA, Tactile-VLA, FoAR, ForceFlow) integrate contact feedback into learned policies, typically as an input or an auxiliary target within imitation learning frameworks. While these approaches acknowledge the importance of force, they generally do not elevate anticipated contact consequences to a primary object of prediction and valuation for action generation. Simultaneously, classical contact control methods (e.g., hybrid force-position control, impedance/admittance control) offer principled solutions for regulating interaction forces but often require extensive task-specific tuning, limiting their generalizability.

The field of learning from deployment experience (e.g., SERL, HIL-SERL, RL-100, RECAP, VLA-RL) has demonstrated how real-world interaction can refine robot behavior. These methods leverage diverse data, often for post-training or adapting frozen policies. However, their application to precision assembly with explicit contact prediction and valuation remains an area for focused development. Furthermore, while large-scale manipulation datasets (e.g., Open X-Embodiment, DROID, AgiBot World) provide behavioral breadth, they frequently lack the synchronized six-axis wrench supervision and detailed contact-phase annotations crucial for learning intricate contact dynamics. Smaller, assembly-specific datasets (e.g., REASSEMBLE, FMB) exist but do not offer the scale required for foundation model training.

Facet-0 aims to bridge these gaps by developing a robotic foundation model that retains the semantic breadth of generalist VLAs while explicitly anticipating, valuing, and adapting to the physical consequences of actions in precision assembly. It proposes a shift from merely observing contact to predicting the future wrench profile associated with proposed actions, using deployment experience to value these action-wrench proposals, and efficiently adapting to part-specific dynamics through a lightweight mechanism. This work contributes a novel architectural and learning framework, along with a large-scale, force-synchronized dataset (ManuFacet-1K), to enhance robot reliability in the challenging domain of contact-rich, high-precision manipulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind Facet-0 is to enable robotic foundation models to achieve dependable physical performance in real-world precision assembly tasks, characterized by sub-millimeter tolerances, delicate interactions, and the critical need for robustness against contact failures. The paper identifies a significant limitation in existing generalist Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies: while they excel at recognizing objects and following instructions across diverse scenarios, their competence often diminishes in the "final millimeter" of an assembly operation. In this critical phase, minor pose errors can lead to undesirable lateral forces, friction, or jamming, where visually similar motions can produce vastly different physical outcomes.

To address this, the research sets forth several key objectives:

1. **Anticipating Physical Consequences**: The work is motivated by the need for robots to predict the physical consequences of their proposed actions, specifically the wrist-wrench profile they are expected to induce. This moves beyond merely observing current contact forces (which are a result of past actions) to proactively modeling the interaction quality of future actions. The objective is to enable the policy to encode how its proposed motion will alter contact dynamics before execution.

2. **Valuing Interaction Outcomes**: Precision assembly demands distinguishing between effective, compliant interactions and costly, damaging ones. Even if two actions lead to similar geometric progress, their contact quality might differ significantly (e.g., a gentle alignment versus a forceful jam). Therefore, a key objective is to develop a mechanism, specifically a distributional Action–Wrench Critic, that can evaluate proposed actions based on their anticipated wrench profiles and assign value to desired contact outcomes, guiding policy improvement towards robust and gentle interactions.

3. **Efficient Adaptation to Part-Specific Dynamics**: Generalist models, while broad, may struggle with the unique contact dynamics of new or varying parts (e.g., differences in mass, stiffness, or latching mechanisms). The goal is to design a lightweight, bounded adaptation mechanism that can reuse the frozen, generalist representation for efficient, on-robot learning. This adaptation should focus on local, part-specific corrections without requiring extensive retraining of the entire foundation model, thus improving transferability and reducing deployment costs.

4. **Developing a Force-Synchronized Dataset**: To train and evaluate models for precision contact, a comprehensive, real-world dataset that integrates synchronized force/torque data with visual and language information is essential. A specific objective is to create ManuFacet-1K, a large-scale corpus annotated with contact phases, to provide the necessary data for learning nuanced interaction behaviors.

5. **Achieving High-Performance Precision Assembly**: The overarching objective is to integrate these components into a unified robotic foundation model, Facet-0, that can demonstrate substantially higher success rates, improved accuracy, and robustness in complex, contact-rich computer assembly tasks compared to existing generalist and force-aware baselines. This includes improving failure recovery and reducing the need for human intervention.

In summary, the motivation stems from the need to imbue generalist robotic foundation models with the ability to "feel" and anticipate contact, make informed decisions based on the quality of these predicted interactions, and adapt efficiently to varied physical environments, thereby transitioning from broad competence to dependable physical performance in challenging real-world assembly scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Facet-0 integrates multimodal representation learning, predictive interaction modeling, and value-guided reinforcement learning (RL) into a unified framework designed for contact-rich precision manipulation.

#### 3.1. The ManuFacet-1K Dataset

The foundation of Facet-0's training is ManuFacet-1K, a 1,000-hour force-synchronized corpus.
- **Composition**: It comprises demonstrations and closed-loop policy rollouts collected across multiple sites, covering four computer assembly tasks: CPU, RAM, Disk, and GPU installation. Tasks involve diverse contact geometries and force regimes.
- **Hardware**: Data was collected using torque-controlled arms with parallel grippers, wrist six-axis force/torque sensors, three RGB cameras, and joint proprioception. Kinesthetic teleoperation with force feedback was used for demonstrations.
- **State Representation**: Each frame records a 13-dimensional state, including end-effector pose (6D), gripper opening (1D), and measured wrist wrench (6D). Action resolution is 0.5 mm.
- **Annotation**: The corpus is extensively annotated with VLM-generated sub-task/instruction pairs and a seven-phase skill taxonomy (e.g., approach, align, insert, press). Five phases (align, insert, press, seat, fasten) are designated "critical" for precision, allowing for phase-aware reward decomposition and policy conditioning.
- **Curation**: Data undergoes timestamp-based multimodal synchronization, validity extraction, saliency-based action-chunk sampling, VLM-assisted supervision, and anomaly correction.

#### 3.2. Semantic–Contact Alignment

This module aims to connect task intent with anticipated physical consequences.
- **Multimodal Grounding**:
 - **Backbone**: A PaliGemma vision-language backbone is paired with a flow-matching action expert.
 - **Observation Processing**: At each control step $t$, the system processes three synchronized RGB views, a language instruction, robot kinematic state (end-effector pose $x_t$, gripper opening $g_t$), and a causal history of the last $K=10$ measured wrist wrenches ($W_{t-K+1:t}$).
 - **Representation Fusion**: Vision-language ($E_{VL}$), kinematic-state ($E_s$), and wrench-history ($E_w$) encoders process their respective inputs. A learned cross-modal fusion map ($F_{fuse}$) combines these into a **semantic–contact representation** ($h_c$).
 - **Joint Action–Wrench Proposal**: Conditioned on $h_c$, a flow-matching policy ($\pi_\theta$) samples a horizon-$H$ (H=50) joint proposal $Y_t = [A_{t:t+H-1}, \hat{W}_{t+1:t+H}]$. $A$ represents Cartesian-pose and gripper commands (executable actions), while $\hat{W}$ represents the predicted future wrist wrenches (anticipated contact consequences). Only $A$ is executed.
 - **Structured Attention**: A structured mask aligns the vision-language and action-wrench learning paths, ensuring a shared semantic prefix while preventing target leakage.
- **Predictive Interaction Learning**:
 - **Conditional Flow Matching**: The policy learns a velocity field $v_\theta$ by interpolating between clean data targets ($Y_{data}$, comprising executed actions and *measured subsequent wrenches*) and Gaussian noise.
 - **Loss Function ($L_{AW}$)**: Optimizes $v_\theta$ to predict the difference between noise and data, comprising an action MSE term and a weighted ($\lambda_{pre}=0.1$) wrench MSE term. This forces the policy to encode how its proposed motion changes contact.
 - **Semantic Objective ($L_{VQA}$)**: An answer-token cross-entropy loss for predicting sub-tasks and next instructions, optimized alternately with $L_{AW}$ to avoid scale heterogeneity.

#### 3.3. Value-Guided Policy Refinement

This module utilizes deployment rollouts to rank interactions by task utility and refine the policy.
- **Interaction Valuation**:
 - **Reward Formulation**: A VLM-generated judge ($\Omega$) segments rollouts into sub-goals and labels contact phases. A per-step reward ($r_t$) is defined, granting credit for sub-goal completion (scaled by time efficiency) and penalizing force violations based on normalized wrench against phase-specific envelopes.
 - **Action–Wrench Critic**: A distributional return model ($Z_\psi$) is trained to predict the return distribution of executing the action component of the joint action–wrench proposal $Y_t$. Its mean ($Q_\psi$) forms the Action–Wrench Critic. This critic is crucial for distinguishing between motions with similar geometric progress but different contact outcomes (e.g., clean insertion vs. jamming).
 - **Critic Training**: Uses temporal difference learning (distributional regression loss) on stored rollouts, with four auxiliary heads for near-future wrench, contact intensity, within-contact progress, and success ranking.
- **Contact-Selective Credit**:
 - **Credit Score**: A short-horizon, undiscounted credit score ($\delta^{(N)}_t$) is computed, measuring how a frame's progress outruns the critic's expectation. This helps focus on decisive interactions.
 - **Regime-Conditioned Selection**: Frames are split by contact regime (contact/free), and the top-ranked fraction within each regime is selected, ensuring contact-critical frames receive positive credit.
 - **Policy Refinement**: The generative policy's flow-matching loss ($L_{FM}$) is then weighted by contact intensity ($c_t$) and guided by a binary positive-interaction label ($b_t$), refining the policy in action–wrench space.

#### 3.4. Local Policy Adaptation

To accommodate part-specific dynamics, a lightweight adaptation mechanism is introduced.
- **FACET Token**: A frozen bottleneck encoder ($E_z$) compresses $h_c$ into a FACET token ($z_t$), which is passed to the local actor without backpropagating into the generalist backbone.
- **Local Actor Interface**: The actor receives $z_t$, kinematic state, measured wrench, and a frozen reference action ($\hat{a}_t$) from the global policy, all mapped to modality embeddings ($e_t$).
- **Lightweight Bounded Actor ($f_\eta$)**: This MLP has two heads:
 - An executable Cartesian action head ($a_t$): Produces a raw activation, which is then mapped into a task-specific bounded box $[a_{min}, a_{max}]$.
 - An auxiliary wrench prediction head ($\hat{w}_{t+1}$): Predicts the next-step wrench but is non-commanded.
- **Actor Training ($L_{actor}$)**: Uses a TD3-style actor-critic framework. The actor is optimized to maximize the value from one of the twin critics (which evaluate *only* the 7D executable action). It also incorporates a behavior cloning term for successful human intervention actions and a loss for supervising the auxiliary wrench prediction.
- **Safety**: An external safety operator ($S_{task}$) applies workspace and per-step motion limits to $a_t$ before execution, ensuring commands remain within safe boundaries. The critics regress on these safety-filtered commands.

This hierarchical approach, from global semantic-contact representation learning to value-guided refinement and local adaptation, enables Facet-0 to perform complex, contact-rich manipulation tasks with high precision and robustness.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of Facet-0 was conducted on five sub-millimeter computer-assembly tasks (RAM, CPU, Disk, GPU installation, and LEVER closure), comprising 23 sub-goals (14 contact-critical). Performance was measured against several baselines, including generalist VLA models ($\pi_{0.5}$, GR00T N1.7) and force-aware policies ($\pi_{0.5}$+F, TA-VLA, $\pi_{0.5}$+RECAP-style).

#### 4.1. Main Comparison and Component Gains

- **Overall Performance**: Facet-0 achieved a mean success rate of 82% across the five tasks, a substantial improvement over the strongest baseline, $\pi_{0.5}$+RECAP-style, which achieved 15% mean success. Per-task margins ranged from +45 points (LEVER) to +75 points (GPU) over the best baseline.
- **Contact-Critical Task Success**: Baselines generally performed adequately on free-space sub-goals (60-100% success for pick operations) but exhibited significant performance drops at contact-critical sub-goals (e.g., align, place), with success rates often below 35%. Facet-0 maintained high performance across these critical phases (e.g., RAM align at 95% vs. 10% for $\pi_{0.5}$).
- **Wrench as Input**: Adding wrench as an input token to $\pi_{0.5}$ ($\pi_{0.5}$+F) or using a force-aware VLA (TA-VLA) showed marginal improvement, remaining in the 9–16% success band. This indicates that merely sensing contact does not equate to acting effectively on its consequences.
- **Contribution of Facet-0 Components**:
 - **Semantic-Contact Alignment (Align)**: Achieved 16% mean success, establishing the joint action–wrench representation.
 - **Value-Guided Refinement (+RL)**: Increased mean success to 38% (+22 points), with gains concentrated on insertion tasks.
 - **Local Adaptation (Full Model)**: Further boosted mean success to 82% (+44 points), providing part-specific correction across all tasks, including the contact-only LEVER task.
- **Efficiency and Precision**: Facet-0 achieved 0.5 mm placement accuracy and a 50 ms command latency (compared to 150 ms for $\pi_{0.5}$). The system completed tasks approximately 40% faster than an expert teleoperator.

#### 4.2. Post-Training from Deployment Experience

- **Comparison with AWR**: When compared against Advantage-Weighted Regression (AWR) using matched deployment data and budget on the Disk assembly task, Facet-0's contact-selective post-training demonstrated significant advantages:
 - Increased success rate from 20% to 65%.
 - Reduced human intervention rate from 47% to 24%.
 - Improved failure recovery rate from 44% to 81%.
- **Mechanism**: This improvement is attributed to Facet-0's contact-selective credit mechanism, which ranks samples within interaction regimes, thereby retaining high-credit contact-recovery frames often overlooked by global advantage-weighting methods like AWR.

#### 4.3. Adaptation: Paradigms and Few-Shot Transfer

- **Adaptation Dynamics**: On-robot learning dynamics showed that while DSRL (latent-noise steering) remained at low success rates, both Facet-0's bounded local actor (TD3+BC) and an HG-DAgger BC variant achieved high success, with increasing autonomy over adaptation episodes.
- **Adaptation Paradigms**: Facet-0's bounded local adaptation mechanism, with its auxiliary wrench prediction, enabled robust recovery behaviors such as backing off, re-aligning, and reseating a module after an off-axis collision. It achieved 95% success on RAM insertion with 5% violations, outperforming position-only residual adaptation (80% success, 20% violations) and DSRL (10% success, 90% violations).
- **Few-Shot Transfer to Unseen Parts**: For an unseen RAM module with different physical properties, Facet-0 achieved 45% success with only 10 demonstrations and three hours of training, updating only 6.6% of its parameters. In contrast, the strongest baselines managed only 5% success, requiring 100% parameter updates. This highlights Facet-0's efficiency in adapting to local dynamic changes while preserving generalized knowledge.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies on Wrench Signal

- **Contact Role Ablation**: Removing all three contact roles of the wrench signal (predictive wrench, critic wrench, adaptation wrench) on RAM insertion drastically reduced success from 95% to 45% and increased peak off-axis force from 0.2x to 1.0x (normalized to the force-free baseline).
- **Individual Contributions**: While individual removals showed small (1-2 trials) drops in success, consistent trends in contact quality were observed. Removing the critic wrench or adaptation wrench led to higher peak off-axis forces (0.5x and 0.4x respectively) compared to removing only the predictive wrench (0.2x), suggesting complementary functions of the wrench signal in prediction, valuation, and adaptation.

These findings collectively indicate that Facet-0 successfully addresses the challenge of precision, contact-rich manipulation by integrating semantic understanding with explicit prediction, valuation, and adaptation of physical interaction consequences.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Facet-0 represents a notable advancement in robotic manipulation, particularly for tasks requiring high precision and intricate physical contact. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Bridging the Generalist-Precision Gap**: The work directly addresses a critical limitation of existing generalist robotic foundation models, which, despite broad semantic understanding, often lack the nuanced physical intelligence required for sub-millimeter precision assembly. By explicitly incorporating anticipated contact consequences into action planning and evaluation, Facet-0 enables robots to move beyond purely kinematic reasoning to achieve dependable physical performance in challenging real-world scenarios.

2. **Novel Paradigm for Robotic Foundation Models**: Facet-0 introduces a paradigm where the future physical interaction (e.g., wrist wrench) is jointly predicted with the robot's actions. This fundamentally shifts contact from being a reactive observation to a proactive, valued component of the action generation process, fostering a deeper understanding of physical interactions within the model. This could inspire future foundation models to integrate other physical modalities (e.g., acoustics, thermal) in a predictive manner.

3. **Enhanced Reliability and Robustness in Manufacturing**: The demonstrated improvement in success rates (82% vs. 15% for baselines) on complex, contact-rich assembly tasks, coupled with high placement accuracy (0.5 mm) and low command latency (50 ms), indicates a significant leap in robot reliability for industrial automation. This can lead to increased autonomy, reduced failure rates, and decreased human intervention in manufacturing processes, making automation feasible for more intricate assembly lines.

4. **Efficient and Scalable Adaptation**: The lightweight, bounded local adaptation mechanism is particularly impactful. It enables robots to quickly and efficiently adapt to new parts or changing environmental dynamics with minimal data (e.g., 10 demonstrations, 3 hours of training for an unseen RAM module) and without extensive retraining of the entire, large foundation model. This addresses the challenge of deploying generalist robots in diverse and evolving manufacturing settings, where part variations are common.

5. **Improved Learning from Real-World Experience**: The value-guided policy refinement, specifically the Action–Wrench Critic and contact-selective credit, allows the system to learn effectively from deployment rollouts, particularly from rare but critical contact recovery scenarios. This results in higher recovery rates and a reduction in the need for human intervention, making human-in-the-loop learning more efficient and sustainable.

6. **Contribution of a Specialized Dataset**: The release of ManuFacet-1K, a 1,000-hour force-synchronized corpus with detailed contact-phase annotations, provides a valuable resource for the robotics and machine learning communities. This dataset addresses a gap in large-scale data specifically tailored for learning intricate contact dynamics, facilitating further research in precision manipulation.

7. **Broader Applicability**: While validated on electronics assembly, the core principles of semantic-contact alignment, value-guided refinement, and bounded local adaptation are generalizable. They could potentially be applied to a wider array of contact-rich manipulation tasks, including delicate handling, fine motor skills in medical robotics, or human-robot collaboration where compliant and force-aware interactions are paramount.

In conclusion, Facet-0 represents a significant step towards enabling robots to achieve human-like dexterity and reliability in complex physical tasks. By merging semantic understanding with predictive physical intelligence, it lays a foundation for more capable and adaptable robotic systems, with immediate and long-term implications for automation in manufacturing and beyond.
