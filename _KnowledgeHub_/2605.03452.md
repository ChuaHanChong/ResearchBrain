# BifrostUMI: Bridging Robot-Free Demonstrations and Humanoid Whole-Body Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> BifrostUMI enables humanoid robots to acquire whole-body manipulation skills from robot-free human demonstrations using a portable VR system and a hierarchical control framework. The system processes sparse keypoint trajectories and wrist-view images to facilitate complex tasks like cluttered tabletop pick-and-place and under-table waste disposal on a Unitree G1 humanoid.

## Problem

- High cost, low efficiency, and limited hardware accessibility hinder large-scale data acquisition for training humanoid whole-body manipulation policies.
- Bridging the 'embodiment gap' between diverse human motion and robot-executable actions, especially for complex whole-body coordination, remains challenging.
- Existing robot-free humanoid data collection methods often rely on expensive tracking systems or retargeting processes tightly coupled with specific controllers.

## Method

- A portable, low-cost robot-free data collection system uses a PICO 4 VR setup and custom UMI-inspired grippers to capture synchronized human whole-body motion, wrist-view visuals, and gripper states.
- A hierarchical visuomotor control architecture integrates a high-level ==diffusion policy== that predicts sparse keypoint trajectories, a ==Spatial Keypoint Retargeting== (**SKR**) module, and a low-level ==whole-body controller==.
- The **SKR** module explicitly maps five human keypoint trajectories (pelvis, left/right TCPs, left/right feet) onto the humanoid robot, primarily scaling vertical distances to account for height differences while preserving other metric spatial relationships.

## Results

- The Unitree G1 humanoid robot successfully executed a cluttered tabletop pick-and-place task, demonstrating the end-to-end viability of transferring skills from robot-free human demonstrations to a physical humanoid.
- The robot successfully completed an under-table waste disposal task, showcasing its ability to generate coordinated whole-body movements including stepping, torso bending, and knee flexion from sparse keypoint commands.
- The framework enabled the humanoid robot to maintain balance and stability during complex object manipulation and significant body reconfigurations across different real-world scenarios.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Complex whole-body manipulation intent for humanoids can be effectively captured and transferred from robot-free human demonstrations using a sparse, five-keypoint representation.
> - A modular hierarchical control structure, which explicitly separates visuomotor prediction, kinematic retargeting, and dynamic control, offers an interpretable and robust pathway for skill transfer.
> - Scaling only the vertical distance (z-axis) from foot to pelvis, while preserving other relative spatial relationships among keypoints, effectively addresses morphological discrepancies between humans and humanoids.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of advanced visuomotor policies for humanoid robots relies significantly on the availability of high-quality, large-scale demonstration data. Traditionally, such data has been acquired through robot teleoperation, a method that involves human operators directly controlling the physical robot. While effective, this paradigm is often characterized by high costs, low operational efficiency, limited hardware accessibility, and demanding labor requirements, which collectively impede the scalability of data acquisition for whole-body humanoid learning.

In response to these challenges, robot-free human demonstration has emerged as an alternative approach. The Universal Manipulation Interface (UMI) series of works demonstrated the feasibility of using portable, low-cost devices to capture human demonstrations for training robotic arms, enabling efficient data collection in diverse environments. This UMI paradigm has been extended to various embodied platforms, including quadruped and aerial robots, and more recently to dual-arm mobile manipulation with frameworks like HOMMI.

However, applying this robot-free paradigm to full-body humanoid control presents unique challenges, primarily due to the complex morphology and whole-body coordination involved. Previous efforts, such as HuMI, explored UMI-style data collection for humanoids using Vive tracking systems. While demonstrating feasibility, such systems often entail higher hardware costs, require intricate initialization and calibration, and may feature retargeting processes tightly coupled with specific downstream whole-body controllers. Bridging the embodiment gap between diverse human motion and robot-executable actions remains a significant challenge for precise whole-body manipulation in robot-free scenarios.

BifrostUMI positions itself within this landscape by addressing the limitations of existing humanoid data collection methods. It aims to provide a portable, low-cost, and robot-free framework that supports whole-body humanoid control. By leveraging a more accessible VR system (PICO 4) and proposing an explicit keypoint-based retargeting method that separates kinematic mapping from low-level control, BifrostUMI seeks to overcome the cost, complexity, and embodiment gap issues prevalent in prior humanoid data acquisition and policy learning efforts. It differentiates itself by offering a modular and interpretable hierarchical control structure designed for efficient and scalable data acquisition for complex humanoid behaviors.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind BifrostUMI stems from the inherent difficulties associated with collecting extensive and high-quality demonstration data for humanoid whole-body visuomotor policies. Current data acquisition methods predominantly rely on robot teleoperation, which necessitates direct interaction with the physical humanoid robot. This approach is resource-intensive, requiring access to expensive hardware, trained operators, meticulous safety supervision, and significant time investment for repeated hardware operation. These factors collectively limit the scalability and efficiency of data collection, thereby hindering advancements in data-driven humanoid robot learning.

The overarching goal of this research is to develop a more efficient, portable, and cost-effective paradigm for acquiring humanoid manipulation demonstrations without requiring the physical robot to be present during data collection. Inspired by the success of robot-free human demonstration frameworks like UMI for robot arms, the authors seek to extend this concept to the more complex domain of humanoid whole-body manipulation.

The key objectives of the BifrostUMI framework are:

1. **Develop a Portable and Low-Cost Robot-Free Data Collection System:** To introduce a novel data acquisition system that utilizes widely available, lightweight VR devices and custom-designed UMI-inspired grippers. This system aims to capture human whole-body motion, wrist-mounted visual observations, and gripper states in a synchronized manner, effectively overcoming the hardware accessibility and cost barriers of traditional teleoperation.
2. **Propose an Explicit Keypoint-Based Motion Retargeting Method:** To design a Spatial Keypoint Retargeting (SKR) module that can precisely map sparse human keypoint trajectories onto a humanoid robot's morphology. This method is specifically intended to preserve the metric spatial relationships critical for task execution while addressing the morphological and scale discrepancies between human demonstrators and humanoid robots. The objective is to provide an explicit inverse-kinematics interface, decoupling motion transfer from the downstream low-level control.
3. **Design and Validate a Hierarchical Visuomotor Policy Architecture:** To construct a robust hierarchical control framework that integrates a high-level diffusion-based visuomotor policy, the proposed SKR module, and a low-level whole-body controller. This architecture aims to enable the seamless transfer of diverse and agile behaviors from natural human demonstrations to humanoid embodiments by segmenting visuomotor prediction, spatial motion retargeting, and stable whole-body execution into distinct, interpretable stages.
4. **Demonstrate Efficacy and Versatility in Real-World Humanoid Tasks:** To experimentally validate the entire BifrostUMI pipeline on a physical humanoid robot (Unitree G1) across multiple real-world manipulation scenarios. This objective involves demonstrating that robot-free human demonstrations, collected with the proposed system, can be effectively transformed into executable, coordinated whole-body behaviors for tasks requiring both localized manipulation and broader body reconfiguration.

Through these objectives, the research aims to establish a practical and scalable pathway for training humanoid robots to learn complex physical skills from natural human behavior, thereby accelerating the progress of embodied intelligence.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

BifrostUMI introduces a hierarchical visuomotor control structure designed to bridge robot-free human demonstrations with humanoid whole-body manipulation capabilities. This framework decomposes control into three main stages: a high-level policy, a Spatial Keypoint Retargeting (SKR) module, and a low-level whole-body controller.

**A. Robot-Free Data Collection System**

The data acquisition system is engineered for portability and efficiency, enabling the capture of human whole-body motion, wrist-view visual observations, and hand-level manipulation commands without requiring the physical robot.

- **Hardware Components:**
 - **VR-based Whole-Body Motion Capture:** A PICO 4 VR system, comprising a headset, handheld controllers, and three lower-body trackers (foot-mounted and waist-mounted), is utilized. This setup provides real-time 6-Degrees-of-Freedom (DoF) body motion estimates by fusing inertial, geomagnetic, and active infrared sensing, generating a standardized skeleton representation.
 - **Handheld Manipulation Interfaces:** Two custom-developed grippers, inspired by UMI-style systems, are employed. Each gripper integrates a fisheye camera, a motor-driven rack-and-pinion mechanism for active actuation, and a dedicated slot for a PICO controller.
- **Recorded Data Modalities:**
 - **Whole-Body Keypoint Poses:** The system captures 6-DoF poses of the PICO controllers, pelvis, left foot, and right foot from the PICO SDK via XRobotoolkit. These are stored as trajectories of five spatial keypoints: pelvis, left Tool Center Point (TCP), right TCP, left foot, and right foot.
 - **Lower-Body Proprioceptive States:** During data collection, the recorded human keypoints are passed through the SKR module to generate and store corresponding lower-body joint configurations for the robot. This serves two purposes: visualizing retargeted motion for demonstrator feedback and providing proprioceptive conditioning for the high-level policy.
 - **Wrist-View Images:** Synchronized visual data is recorded from the fisheye cameras integrated into the grippers.
 - **Gripper States:** Continuous gripper width measurements are obtained from the magnetic encoders of the gripper motors.

This system provides synchronized multimodal data, which is formatted for training the high-level diffusion policy, without exposing the physical robot to potential risks or demanding intensive operational resources.

**B. High Level: Diffusion Policy**

The high-level policy is an adaptation of Diffusion Policy, extended to operate on a sparse task-space representation for whole-body control.

- **Action Space:** At each decision step, the policy predicts a receding-horizon action chunk (H=48 steps) comprising the future poses of the five keypoints (pelvis, left/right TCPs, left/right feet) and two scalar gripper-width commands. Each keypoint pose is represented by 3D translation and a 6D rotation (continuous over SO(3)), resulting in a 47-dimensional action vector.
- **Action Chunk Preparation:** The supervision target is constructed from absolute keypoint trajectories. Future poses are re-expressed in each keypoint’s own frame at the query time to remove world-frame dependency and noise propagation. Translations and gripper widths are min-max normalized, while 6D rotation components remain unscaled.
- **Observation Space:** The policy is conditioned on a single frame of 224x224 left/right wrist-view RGB images and three historical frames of a 15-dimensional lower-body proprioceptive vector (12 leg joints, 3 waist joints). The lower-body vector is derived from SKR outputs during training and robot encoders during deployment.
- **Architecture:** DINOv2 is used to encode wrist-view images. These visual features are fused with the lower-body proprioceptive states and the diffusion step into a global condition, which is then input to the diffusion model for predicting action trajectories.
- **Inference:** The diffusion policy denoises an action chunk in a normalized, per-keypoint relative form. This is then inverse-normalized and converted to absolute SE(3) targets, which are subsequently passed to the SKR module.

**C. Bridge: Keypoint Retargeting System (Spatial Keypoint Retargeting - SKR)**

SKR serves as a critical interface, converting the high-level keypoint predictions into robot-native whole-body motion commands, explicitly addressing human-to-robot morphological discrepancies.

- **Principle:** Unlike methods that globally rescale motion, SKR operates on five task-relevant keypoints (pelvis, left/right TCPs, left/right feet) and primarily scales only the vertical distance (z-axis) from each foot keypoint to the pelvis to account for height differences between the human and robot. All other metric spatial relationships among keypoints are preserved.
- **Process:** During deployment, the system computes the current relative poses of the TCPs and feet with respect to the robot's pelvis frame using forward kinematics. Given the desired keypoint poses from the high-level policy, SKR formulates an inverse kinematics (IK) problem. This IK problem is solved using `mink`, a MuJoCo-based IK solver, to yield a 36-dimensional robot-native motion representation, including the root position, root orientation, and 29 joint positions. This motion command is then forwarded to the low-level controller.

**D. Low Level: Whole-Body Controller (WBC)**

The low-level controller, trained in MJLab, is responsible for executing the retargeted motion stably and dynamically on the humanoid robot.

- **Functionality:** The WBC tracks a short horizon of the robot-native full-body reference motion generated by SKR, consisting of the root pose and 29-DoF joint configurations, ensuring dynamic consistency and robustness.
- **Frequency:** The high-level policy operates at a lower frequency, while the WBC runs at 50 Hz. The motion chunks from SKR are resampled (linear interpolation for positions, spherical linear interpolation for orientations) to match the control frequency.
- **Observation Space:** The WBC's observation `o_t` comprises two parts:
 - **Proprioceptive Input (`o_prop_t`):** Includes current root height, projected gravity vector, IMU angular velocity, joint positions and velocities, and previous action, with temporal history for stability.
 - **Motion-Command Input (`o_cmd_t`):** Specifies desired full-body motion from the reference chunk across a temporal window (both future and historical frames). This includes relative root position and orientation, reference root height, projected gravity, and target joint configuration.
- **Action Space:** The controller outputs a 29-dimensional action, interpreted as a joint position residual around the robot's default posture. This is clipped, scaled, and translated into desired joint position commands (`q_des_t`), which are then sent to the robot as joint-space PD targets.
- **Execution:** A receding-horizon scheme is used, where the high-level policy periodically predicts motion chunks, SKR converts them, and the WBC continuously tracks the resampled trajectory, thereby decoupling task-level motion generation from real-time dynamic control.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy and versatility of BifrostUMI were evaluated through real-world experiments on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot across two distinct manipulation tasks. The experiments aimed to assess the effectiveness of the robot-free data collection and training framework, as well as the whole-body manipulation capabilities derived from a sparse five-keypoint representation.

**A. Effectiveness of the Robot-Free Data Collection and Training Framework: Cluttered Tabletop Pick-and-Place**

- **Task Description:** The humanoid robot was required to visually localize a piece of bread on a cluttered tabletop, grasp it, transfer it, and place it onto a target plate. This task was designed to test the end-to-end viability of transferring skills from robot-free human demonstrations to a physical humanoid robot operating in a complex environment.
- **Challenges Addressed:** The task presented challenges beyond typical arm-based manipulation, including bridging the embodiment gap between human and humanoid motion, and requiring the robot to maintain a dynamically feasible whole-body posture while coordinating arm, torso, and lower body movements. Visual localization in a cluttered scene with distractor objects further demanded robust visual processing.
- **Results:** The Unitree G1 robot successfully completed the entire manipulation sequence, encompassing object localization, grasping, transfer, and placement, as illustrated in Fig. 6(a).
 - **Visual Perception:** The robot demonstrated the ability to extract task-relevant visual cues from wrist-view observations despite the presence of multiple distractor objects, enabling accurate object localization.
 - **High-Level Policy Efficacy:** The high-level visuomotor policy effectively predicted meaningful future keypoint motions from the robot-free demonstrations, guiding the robot's hand toward the target object for grasping.
 - **Retargeting and Control:** The predicted sparse keypoint trajectories were successfully retargeted into executable whole-body robot motions by the SKR module. The low-level controller accurately tracked these motions, maintaining the robot's balance during object lifting and arm movements.
- **Conclusion:** This experiment qualitatively demonstrated that the BifrostUMI pipeline can effectively transform robot-free human demonstrations into deployable humanoid visuomotor policies. The successful execution validated the cooperative function of all stages within the framework: visuomotor prediction, spatial keypoint retargeting, and stable whole-body control.

**B. Whole-Body Manipulation Capability of BifrostUMI: Under-Table Waste Disposal**

- **Task Description:** This task involved the robot grasping a crumpled paper ball from a tabletop, stepping backward, bending its waist and knees to lower its body, reaching toward a waste bin placed underneath the table, and releasing the object into the bin. This scenario specifically aimed to evaluate the framework's capacity for generating coordinated motions across the arms, waist, and legs, rather than relying solely on arm manipulation.
- **Challenges Addressed:** The task demanded significant whole-body reconfiguration and coordination, including dynamic stepping, torso bending, and knee flexion, to access targets outside the immediate arm-only workspace. It tested the ability of the sparse keypoint representation to encode complex whole-body intent.
- **Results:** The robot successfully executed the full under-table waste-disposal sequence, as shown in Fig. 6(b).
 - **Grasping and Repositioning:** The robot localized and grasped the paper ball while maintaining an upright posture. It then performed a backward step to create necessary space, indicating the generation of dynamic body repositioning behaviors beyond static manipulation.
 - **Coordinated Whole-Body Motion:** The robot demonstrated coordinated movement across its arms, waist, and legs by bending its knees and torso to lower the end effector towards the waste bin. This required cooperative rather than independent limb movements.
 - **Stable Release:** The object was released into the bin while the robot maintained a stable support configuration throughout the whole-body maneuver.
- **Conclusion:** This qualitative result indicated that the sparse five-keypoint representation is capable of encoding not only hand-level manipulation intent but also the necessary whole-body posture changes required for tasks involving targets beyond the robot's immediate arm reach. The successful execution demonstrated that BifrostUMI can generate physically executable whole-body manipulation behaviors that integrate object interaction, stepping, torso bending, knee flexion, and balance maintenance in spatially constrained environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

BifrostUMI represents a methodological contribution to the field of humanoid robotics, particularly in addressing the challenges of data acquisition and skill transfer. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Enabling Scalable and Efficient Data Collection:** The framework directly addresses a fundamental bottleneck in humanoid learning by offering a portable, low-cost, and robot-free data collection system. By leveraging VR-UMI interfaces, it eliminates the need for expensive hardware, trained operators, and constant access to the physical robot during the demonstration phase. This enables the acquisition of larger, more diverse datasets for training visuomotor policies, which is crucial for improving the generalization and robustness of humanoid behaviors. This could accelerate research and development in humanoid robotics by making data more accessible.

- **Introducing a Modular and Interpretable Hierarchical Control Architecture:** BifrostUMI's three-stage hierarchical control structure—high-level diffusion policy, Spatial Keypoint Retargeting (SKR) module, and low-level whole-body controller—provides a clear and interpretable pathway from human intent to robot execution. This modularity offers several advantages:
 - **Decoupling of Concerns:** It explicitly separates visuomotor decision-making from embodiment-specific kinematic mapping and dynamic control, potentially making the system less sensitive to variations in robot morphology or low-level controller designs.
 - **Preservation of Task-Relevant Geometry:** The SKR module ensures that the metric spatial relationships of human demonstrations are preserved during retargeting, which is crucial for tasks requiring precise interaction with the environment. This is an improvement over general motion rescaling methods that may distort task-critical geometry.

- **Demonstrating Advanced Whole-Body Manipulation Capabilities:** The experimental results, particularly the under-table waste-disposal task, highlight the framework's ability to generate complex, coordinated whole-body behaviors. The sparse five-keypoint representation proved sufficient to encode not only detailed hand manipulation but also significant body reconfigurations involving the arms, waist, and legs. This suggests that a concise intermediate representation can effectively bridge human motion and complex humanoid whole-body control, extending capabilities beyond static, arm-only tasks.

- **Paving the Way for More Capable Humanoid Robots:** By demonstrating the feasibility of learning complex skills from natural human demonstrations in a scalable manner, BifrostUMI contributes to the development of more autonomous and versatile humanoid robots. The approach provides a practical path toward data-driven humanoid robots that can acquire and execute sophisticated physical skills directly from human observation.

- **Future Research Directions:** The work also lays the groundwork for future research, including scaling to larger and more diverse datasets, tackling longer-horizon tasks, enabling richer object interactions, and operating in less structured environments. Integrating additional sensory feedback (e.g., tactile/force), online correction mechanisms, and even larger-scale diverse demonstrations are identified as avenues for further enhancing robustness and generalization.

In summary, BifrostUMI addresses a significant practical bottleneck in humanoid robotics by making high-quality data collection more accessible and efficient. Its hierarchical and explicit approach to skill transfer, validated through demanding real-world tasks, contributes to advancing the state of the art in embodied intelligence and the practical deployment of humanoid robots.
