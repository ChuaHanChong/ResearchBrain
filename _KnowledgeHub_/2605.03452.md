# BifrostUMI: Bridging Robot-Free Demonstrations and Humanoid Whole-Body Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> BifrostUMI introduces a robot-free framework for efficiently collecting human whole-body demonstrations for humanoid manipulation, leveraging accessible VR hardware and a novel Spatial Keypoint Retargeting module. It enables a Unitree G1 humanoid to learn and execute complex behaviors like dynamic throwing and locomotion-manipulation, achieving up to a 61x speedup in data collection for loco-manipulation tasks compared to teleoperation.

## Problem

- Acquiring high-quality, whole-body demonstration data for humanoid robots is challenging, requiring intricate coordination of perception, locomotion, and manipulation.
- Traditional robot-in-the-loop teleoperation is costly, inefficient, demands skilled operators, and extensive safety supervision for large-scale data collection.
- Existing robot-free human demonstrations, including UMI-style methods, struggle to bridge the embodiment gap for precise whole-body humanoid tasks and can introduce hardware/calibration complexities.

## Method

- A portable, low-cost, robot-free data collection system utilizing PICO VR headsets and custom UMI-style grippers captures human whole-body motion, wrist-view images, and gripper states.
- A ==hierarchical visuomotor policy== framework, featuring a high-level ==diffusion policy==, predicts sparse spatial keypoint trajectories and gripper actions from observations.
- A **Spatial Keypoint Retargeting (SKR)** module explicitly bridges the human-robot morphological gap, converting human keypoint trajectories into feasible robot-native whole-body references via ==morphology-aware scaling== and ==constrained inverse kinematics==.

## Results

- The framework enabled a Unitree G1 humanoid to successfully perform complex real-world tasks, including dynamic ball throwing, under-table waste disposal, and coupled locomotion-manipulation for coffee delivery.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the Spatial Keypoint Retargeting (SKR) module and latency matching are critical, with SKR showing substantial improvements in success rates over generic motion retargeting.
- BifrostUMI achieved a data collection speedup of approximately **2.2x** for non-locomotion tasks and up to **61x** for loco-manipulation tasks compared to a teleoperation baseline (TWIST2), while also lowering the operational barrier for novice users.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Separating task-space planning, kinematic correspondence, and low-level execution through a dedicated Spatial Keypoint Retargeting interface significantly improves the transfer of natural human intent to deployable humanoid whole-body behaviors.
> - Morphology-aware anisotropic scaling within the SKR module is critical for robust and stable humanoid motion retargeting, especially for accurate recovery of lower-body movements.
> - Precise latency matching between sensory inputs, policy inference, and robot actuation is essential for successful execution of timing-sensitive dynamic manipulation tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The acquisition of high-quality demonstration data is a recognized challenge in humanoid robot skill learning, particularly for whole-body behaviors that necessitate the coordination of perception, locomotion, and manipulation. The existing methodologies for data collection largely fall into two categories: robot-in-the-loop teleoperation and robot-free human demonstrations.

**Robot-in-the-loop teleoperation** methods involve human operators directly controlling a physical robot to generate training trajectories. Systems such as TWIST2, CLONE, Touch Dreaming, Humanplus, Mobile-Television, and Omnih2o exemplify this approach for humanoid or whole-body manipulation. While these methods produce embodiment-consistent trajectories, they typically require access to expensive physical hardware, skilled operators, extensive safety supervision, and repeated hardware operation, which renders large-scale data collection costly and inefficient.

**Robot-free or human-centric demonstration collection** offers an alternative by leveraging human motion or sensory data. EgoHumanoid, for instance, explores egocentric human demonstration and human-to-humanoid transfer, but faces the inherent challenge of bridging the embodiment gap between human motion and robot-executable actions for precise whole-body tasks. A notable sub-category within robot-free methods is the **Universal Manipulation Interface (UMI)** series of works (e.g., UMI, OmniUMI, ActiveUMI, UMI-on-legs, UMI-on-air). These systems utilize portable handheld devices and wrist-centric visual observations to efficiently collect in-the-wild demonstrations for robot arms, and some have been extended to quadruped and aerial robots. HoMMI extended UMI-style collection to dual-arm mobile manipulation, but without targeting full-body humanoid control. HuMI specifically investigated UMI-style data collection for humanoids, incorporating body tracking and UMI-style grippers. However, HuMI’s Vive-based tracking system involved additional hardware costs and calibration complexity, and its retargeting procedure exhibited tight coupling with the downstream whole-body controller, potentially limiting its generality.

The development of **whole-body visuomotor policies** for humanoid robots also represents a significant research area. Approaches like TWIST2 and Touch Dreaming learn policies that directly predict robot-level actions (e.g., joint commands) from teleoperated demonstrations, offering an end-to-end perception-to-control interface. However, this formulation couples task-level reasoning with embodiment-specific control, making policy learning sensitive to robot morphology and controller design. HuMI, while predicting task-space keypoints, still integrated its inverse kinematics and retargeting with the learned controller.

BifrostUMI positions itself within this landscape by addressing the limitations of prior work. It aims to provide a portable, low-cost, robot-free framework for *humanoid whole-body data collection* that overcomes the inefficiencies of teleoperation and improves upon the portability and generality of previous UMI-style humanoid data collection systems. It specifically tackles the challenge of **transferring natural human whole-body intent to deployable humanoid whole-body behaviors** by proposing a hierarchical policy framework that explicitly separates task-space planning, kinematic correspondence, and low-level execution through a dedicated spatial keypoint retargeting interface.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the recognized difficulty and inefficiency in acquiring high-quality demonstration data for humanoid robot skill learning, particularly for complex whole-body behaviors.

**Motivation:**
1. **Challenges of Humanoid Whole-Body Skill Learning:** Humanoid robots require intricate coordination of perception, locomotion, and manipulation for whole-body skills. Learning these skills necessitates a substantial volume of diverse and high-quality training data.
2. **Limitations of Existing Data Collection Methods:**
 - **Robot Teleoperation:** The prevalent method, robot-in-the-loop teleoperation, demands direct access to expensive physical robots, requires highly skilled human operators, involves careful safety supervision, and is hardware-intensive. These factors make large-scale data collection costly, time-consuming, and inefficient, creating a bottleneck for advancing humanoid robot capabilities.
 - **Robot-Free Approaches (UMI-style):** While robot-free human demonstrations, inspired by UMI, have shown promise for robot arm manipulation by enabling efficient in-the-wild data collection, extending this paradigm to *humanoid whole-body manipulation* is not straightforward. Humanoids must coordinate manipulation with balance, posture adjustment, stepping, and morphology-dependent whole-body motion. Existing UMI-style systems have largely focused on robot arms or dual-arm mobile manipulation, or, in the case of HuMI, introduced hardware and calibration complexities and a less explicit retargeting process, failing to fully explore a portable, low-cost, robot-free pipeline for *deployable whole-body humanoid policies*.
3. **Need for Generalizable Whole-Body Policies:** Current end-to-end visuomotor policies often tightly couple task-level reasoning with embodiment-specific control, leading to policies that are sensitive to robot morphology and controller design, and potentially limiting their transferability and interpretability.

**Key Objectives:**
To address these motivations, the authors established the following key objectives for BifrostUMI:
1. **Develop a Portable and Low-Cost Robot-Free Data Collection System:** The first objective was to design and implement a system that could capture human whole-body intent using readily available, lightweight VR devices (e.g., PICO 4) and UMI-inspired handheld grippers, without requiring the physical humanoid robot to be present during data collection. This system aims to collect synchronized wrist-view images, gripper widths, and a compact spatial representation of human whole-body motion.
2. **Propose a Sparse Spatial Keypoint Retargeting Interface:** The second objective was to create an explicit interface that could effectively bridge the morphological gap between human demonstrations and humanoid robots. This Spatial Keypoint Retargeting (SKR) module needed to convert sparse human keypoint trajectories into feasible robot-native whole-body references while preserving the task-relevant spatial structure and accounting for differences in body proportions and joint limits.
3. **Design a Hierarchical Policy Framework for Whole-Body Skill Learning:** The third objective was to construct a hierarchical visuomotor architecture that could learn deployable humanoid whole-body skills from the collected robot-free demonstrations. This framework would involve a high-level policy predicting future keypoint motions and gripper actions, an SKR module converting these predictions into robot-native references, and a low-level whole-body controller executing the retargeted motion with balance and stability on the physical robot. This design aimed to separate task-space planning from embodiment-specific control.
4. **Empirically Validate the Framework in Real-World Scenarios:** The final objective was to demonstrate the effectiveness and efficiency of BifrostUMI on a physical humanoid robot across various real-world manipulation tasks, including single-arm, bimanual, dynamic, and locomotion-manipulation scenarios, and to compare its data collection efficiency against teleoperation-based methods.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

BifrostUMI employs a hierarchical framework for learning humanoid whole-body manipulation skills from robot-free human demonstrations. This framework comprises three main stages: a robot-free data collection system, a high-level diffusion policy, a Spatial Keypoint Retargeting (SKR) module, and a low-level whole-body controller.

**A. Robot-Free Data Collection System**
The system is designed to acquire human whole-body motion, local wrist-view visual observations, and gripper state information in a synchronized and robot-compatible format without the physical robot.

1. **Data-Collection Hardware:**
 - **VR-based Motion Capture:** A PICO-based setup (PICO 4 VR headset, handheld controllers, and three lower-body trackers mounted on feet and waist) provides real-time 6-DoF body-motion estimates. This system fuses inertial, geomagnetic, and active infrared sensing, generating an SMPL-format body representation for stable motion capture.
 - **Handheld Manipulation Interfaces:** Two self-developed, UMI-inspired grippers are used. Each integrates a fisheye camera for wrist-view visuals, a motor-driven rack-and-pinion gripper mechanism for grasping, and a dedicated slot for a PICO controller, ensuring stable grasping and synchronized data capture.
2. **Recorded Data Modalities:**
 - **Keypoint Poses:** The system records 6-DoF poses of the two handheld controllers (gripper TCPs), pelvis, left foot, and right foot from the PICO SDK and SMPL representation. This forms a compact five-keypoint representation. For lower-body-intensive tasks, left and right knee poses are optionally added, forming a seven-keypoint variant. These poses are transformed to align with robot keypoint frames.
 - **Lower-Body Proprioception:** During data collection, the Spatial Keypoint Retargeting (SKR) module is run online to convert human keypoint trajectories into robot-native motion references, specifically recording lower-body DoF positions (q_low). These serve as proprioceptive conditioning for the high-level policy and allow operators to visualize retargeted humanoid motion.
 - **Visual Observations:** Synchronized wrist-view images (fisheye cameras) are recorded.
 - **Gripper State:** Gripper width is measured via magnetic encoders on the motor drive.
3. **Latency Matching:** A procedure adapted from UMI is used to calibrate and compensate for relative delays among sensory inputs, policy inference, and robot control, particularly critical for timing-sensitive tasks.

**B. High-Level: Diffusion Policy**
A whole-body extension of Diffusion Policy is employed as the high-level policy, operating in a sparse task space.

1. **Action Space:** The policy predicts a receding-horizon action chunk (H=48) for the five (or seven) keypoints and two gripper widths. Each keypoint's 6-DoF pose is represented by a 3-D translation and a continuous 6-D rotation, yielding 47 (or 65) dimensions. Keypoint poses are expressed relative to the pelvis frame at the current query time. Gripper widths are absolute scalars.
2. **Observation Space:** The policy is conditioned on synchronized 224x224 left/right wrist-view RGB images (encoded by DINOv2) and a three-frame history of a 15-D lower-body proprioceptive vector (12 leg joints, 3 waist joints), obtained from online SKR during data collection and robot encoders during deployment.
3. **Inference:** At runtime, the diffusion policy denoises and inverse-normalizes a relative action chunk. Relative keypoint motions are decoded into pelvis-referenced targets using the current robot state (from forward kinematics) and then passed to SKR.

**C. Bridge: Spatial Keypoint Retargeting (SKR) System**
SKR is a morphology-aware pipeline designed to convert sparse human keypoint trajectories into feasible robot whole-body references, bridging the kinematic differences between human demonstrators and the robot.

1. **Keypoint Layout:** Supports a default five-keypoint layout (pelvis, L/R foot, L/R TCP) and an augmented seven-keypoint layout (adding L/R knee) for lower-body-intensive tasks.
2. **Morphology-Aware Adjustment:**
 - Human keypoint poses are transformed and aligned with the robot coordinate system.
 - A pelvis-local anisotropic adjustment is applied only to leg-related keypoints (foot and knee). The vertical displacement from the pelvis (d_z) is scaled by a factor λ_leg (set to 0.75 for Unitree G1) to compensate for human-robot leg-length differences, while x and y displacements remain unscaled. Other keypoints (pelvis, TCPs) are not scaled.
 - Adjusted keypoints are associated with semantic robot frames (e.g., pelvis, toe links) and calibrated offsets are applied.
3. **Constrained Inverse Kinematics (IK):** A weighted IK problem is solved to find the optimal robot joint configuration (q*t) that minimizes the pose error between desired keypoint targets and corresponding robot frames, subject to robot joint limits. It also includes a term for temporal consistency by minimizing deviation from the previous solution (q_t-1). A two-stage IK procedure is used, prioritizing foot support and orientation, then refining pelvis and TCP positions (and knee positions for the 7-keypoint variant).
4. **Output:** The IK solution provides robot root pose and joint references, which are then temporally resampled and sent to the low-level controller.

**D. Low-Level: Whole-Body Controller**
A learned motion-tracking controller, trained in MJLab, is used to execute the robot-native full-body references generated by SKR on the physical Unitree G1 robot.

1. **Input:** The controller receives a short-horizon reference motion chunk (M_ref) from SKR, including reference root position, root orientation, and joint configuration.
2. **Control Loop:** Running at 50 Hz, the controller takes the current proprioceptive state and the resampled reference window. It outputs a 29-dimensional residual joint-position action.
3. **Execution:** After clipping and scaling, the desired joint position is computed and sent to the robot through joint-space PD control, enabling stable humanoid execution while maintaining balance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of BifrostUMI on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot focused on three key areas: the effectiveness of the robot-free framework, its whole-body manipulation capabilities, and the efficiency of data collection.

**A. Effectiveness of Robot-Free Data Collection and Training Framework**
The framework's ability to transfer robot-free human demonstrations into deployable visuomotor policies was tested on three real-world tasks:
1. **Cluttered Tabletop Pick-and-Place:** The robot successfully localized bread among distractors, grasped it, and placed it onto a target plate, demonstrating wrist-view visual grounding and single-arm manipulation.
2. **Bimanual Vegetable Collection:** The robot sequentially used its right hand to place an eggplant and its left hand to place a cucumber, illustrating coordination of two arms with distinct object targets while maintaining a feasible whole-body posture.
3. **Dynamic Ball Throwing:** The robot performed a wind-up, released a ball, and hit a target, showcasing the framework's ability to handle precisely timed dynamic release and actions beyond slow end-effector reaching.

**Ablation Studies:**
- **Spatial Keypoint Retargeting (SKR):** Replacing SKR with a standard General Motion Retargeting (GMR) method in the pick-and-place and bimanual collection tasks resulted in a substantial reduction in success rates. This finding confirms that SKR's morphology-aware scale adaptation and preservation of spatial structure for task-relevant keypoints are critical for stable and reliable humanoid deployment.
- **Latency Matching:** Removing latency matching in the dynamic ball throwing task led to degraded performance. This indicates that precise synchronization among sensing, gripper state, and robot actuation is essential for timing-sensitive tasks.
The complete BifrostUMI pipeline consistently outperformed its ablated variants, validating the integrated operation of its components: the high-level policy predicts task-relevant keypoint trajectories, SKR converts human intent into spatially consistent humanoid motion, latency matching enables accurate dynamic execution, and the low-level controller tracks references while maintaining balance.

**B. Whole-Body Manipulation Capability of BifrostUMI**
The ability of BifrostUMI to coordinate whole-body behaviors, involving lower-body participation in manipulation, was evaluated on two challenging tasks:
1. **Under-Table Waste Disposal:** The robot successfully grasped a crumpled paper ball, stepped backward, bent its knees and torso, reached toward a waste bin underneath the table, and released the object. This demonstrated the framework's capacity to encode and execute whole-body posture changes for reaching targets outside the typical arm-only workspace.
2. **Walking Coffee Delivery:** The robot walked forward with a natural gait, came to a stable stop, and extended its arm to deliver a cup of coffee. This illustrated coupled locomotion-manipulation, where lower-body motion directly contributed to task completion rather than merely stabilizing the upper body.

**Knee-Keypoint Ablation:** For these lower-body-intensive tasks, the augmented seven-keypoint variant (including knee keypoints) was compared to the default five-keypoint configuration. The results showed that the addition of knee keypoints was critical for recovering correct lower-body motion, especially knee flexion and body lowering, which are essential when waist and leg involvement is substantial. Without these additional keypoints, the lower-body posture was underconstrained, leading to less reliable execution. These experiments suggest that BifrostUMI can generate physically executable humanoid skills encompassing object interaction, stepping, torso bending, knee flexion, and stable reaching.

**C. Efficiency of Robot-Free Data Collection**
The efficiency of BifrostUMI's robot-free data collection was compared against TWIST2, a teleoperation-based method, over 10-minute sessions with both novice and experienced operators.
- **Higher Throughput:** BifrostUMI consistently achieved a higher throughput of valid demonstrations across all tested tasks and operators. For non-locomotion tasks (bimanual collection, throw trash), it showed an average speedup of approximately 2.2x.
- **Significant Gain in Loco-Manipulation:** The most pronounced efficiency gain was observed in the walking coffee delivery task, where BifrostUMI achieved a substantially larger speedup (e.g., 61x for the novice operator, collecting 61 valid demonstrations compared to 1 with TWIST2). This highlights its particular effectiveness for loco-manipulation tasks, which are often cumbersome and prone to failure with direct teleoperation.
- **Reduced Operator Barrier:** The comparison between novice and experienced operators indicated that BifrostUMI lowered the operational barrier. Novice users achieved demonstration throughput close to that of experienced users across all tasks with BifrostUMI, whereas TWIST2 exhibited a larger expertise gap, especially in the walking coffee delivery task.
These results suggest that BifrostUMI improves data collection efficiency while simultaneously reducing the dependence on operator skill and extensive teleoperation training.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

BifrostUMI represents a methodological advancement in the field of humanoid robotics, particularly in the domain of skill acquisition through imitation learning. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Addressing the Data Bottleneck for Humanoids:** The research directly tackles one of the primary limitations in humanoid robot learning: the scarcity and high cost of high-quality, whole-body demonstration data. By providing a portable, low-cost, and robot-free data collection framework, BifrostUMI offers a scalable alternative to traditional teleoperation methods, which are constrained by hardware accessibility, operator expertise, and efficiency. This can significantly accelerate the collection of large, diverse datasets necessary for training more capable humanoid policies.
2. **Enabling Scalable Skill Learning:** The ability to collect demonstrations without the physical robot reduces the infrastructural and logistical overhead associated with robot experimentation. This democratizes access to data collection, allowing a broader range of researchers and potentially even non-experts to contribute to the dataset generation process. Such scalability is crucial for leveraging large-scale learning paradigms, similar to those seen in other areas of AI.
3. **Advancing Human-to-Humanoid Skill Transfer:** The introduction of the Spatial Keypoint Retargeting (SKR) module is a key technical contribution. By explicitly accounting for morphological differences between humans and robots through an anisotropic scaling mechanism and constrained inverse kinematics, SKR provides a robust and geometrically consistent bridge for transferring human intent to robot-native whole-body references. This structured approach, which separates task-level visuomotor reasoning from embodiment-specific control, enhances the interpretability and generalizability of learned policies.
4. **Facilitating Complex Whole-Body Behaviors:** The demonstrated success across a range of complex tasks—including single-arm manipulation, bimanual coordination, dynamic throwing, posture adaptation (e.g., under-table reaching), and locomotion-manipulation (e.g., walking coffee delivery)—indicates that BifrostUMI can generate highly coordinated whole-body behaviors. This extends the capabilities of humanoid robots beyond static manipulation to tasks requiring dynamic interaction, balance, and integrated movement, paving the way for more versatile and agile humanoids.
5. **Lowering the Barrier to Entry for Robot Control:** The observed efficiency gains and reduced dependence on operator expertise suggest that BifrostUMI could lower the entry barrier for programming and teaching robots. Simpler, more intuitive demonstration interfaces can allow more individuals to "teach" robots new skills, potentially broadening the applicability and adoption of humanoid robots in various real-world scenarios.

In summary, BifrostUMI offers a practical and scalable solution for humanoid robot skill learning, with the potential to significantly impact the development of more capable, versatile, and easily programmable humanoid robots by addressing the fundamental challenge of data acquisition.
