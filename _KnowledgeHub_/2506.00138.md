# Intrinsic Goals for Autonomous Agents: Model-Based Exploration in Virtual Zebrafish Predicts Ethological Behavior and Whole-Brain Dynamics

> [!summary] Summary
> A novel intrinsic motivation algorithm, 3M-Progress, enables embodied AI agents to replicate the naturalistic active-passive behavioral cycling of larval zebrafish and accurately predict whole-brain neural-glial dynamics, demonstrating high alignment with biological data. The model provides a goal-driven computational framework for understanding autonomous animal behavior and its underlying neural processes.

## Problem

- Current intrinsic motivation methods in reinforcement learning (RL) struggle with inconsistent exploration, the "noisy TV" problem, and failure to converge to stable exploratory policies in reward-free environments.
- Systems neuroscience lacks understanding of the cellular and circuit-level basis for naturalistic, task-independent autonomous behavior, especially regarding non-neuronal cells like astrocytes in whole-brain interactions.
- Prior embodied AI models in neuroscience often rely on imitation or supervised learning, which constrains learned behaviors and neural representations, limiting their ability to model purely intrinsically motivated autonomy.

## Method

- Developed an embodied ==virtual zebrafish== agent in a MuJoCo environment, simulating biomechanics and a head-fixed futility-induced passivity protocol that creates sensorimotor mismatch.
- Implemented a recurrent sensory-cognitive architecture comprising a vision encoder, proprioceptive encoder, and an actor-critic core, along with an Intrinsic Drive Module (IDM).
- Introduced 3M-Progress, a novel intrinsic reward mechanism based on Kullback-Leibler divergence between an online learned world model and a fixed "ethological prior" world model, combined with an exponential filter and action penalty.

## Results

- **3M**-Progress agents exhibited robust, stable cycling between active swimming and futility-induced passivity, achieving the highest model-behavioral alignment (Pearson's r) with biological larval zebrafish compared to all baselines.
- The agents captured nearly all explainable variance in both neural and astrocytic whole-brain activity, outperforming all other intrinsic motivation and baseline algorithms in predicting biological neural-glial dynamics under a strict One-to-One mapping.
- Latent dynamics in the agent's core module functionally mirrored biological whole-brain responses, providing computational support for specific neuron-glial interactions, such as radial astrocytes accumulating motor futility evidence and neurons encoding sensorimotor mismatches.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Autonomous agents can achieve stable, ethologically relevant behavioral patterns through an intrinsic drive that compares current environmental dynamics to a fixed "ecological memory," guiding them away from unpredictable or uncontrollable stimuli.
> - Whole-brain neural-glial activity, including the proposed neuron-glial circuit for sensorimotor error accumulation, can be computationally modeled and predicted purely from self-supervised optimization of an intrinsic goal.
> - Robust, animal-like autonomy is shaped by maintaining an intrinsic drive informed by an ecological memory and continuously monitoring the divergence between this memory and new sensory experiences.
