# Intrinsic Goals for Autonomous Agents: Model-Based Exploration in Virtual Zebrafish Predicts Ethological Behavior and Whole-Brain Dynamics

> [!summary] Summary
> A novel intrinsic motivation algorithm, 3M-Progress, enables embodied AI agents to replicate the naturalistic active-passive behavioral cycling of larval zebrafish and accurately predict whole-brain neural-glial dynamics, demonstrating high alignment with biological data. The model provides a goal-driven computational framework for understanding autonomous animal behavior and its underlying neural processes.

## Problem

- Current intrinsic motivation methods in reinforcement learning (RL) struggle with inconsistent exploration, the "noisy TV" problem, and failure to converge to stable exploratory policies in reward-free environments.
- Systems neuroscience lacks understanding of the cellular and circuit-level basis for naturalistic, task-independent autonomous behavior, especially regarding non-neuronal cells like astrocytes in whole-brain interactions.
- Prior embodied AI models in neuroscience often rely on imitation or supervised learning, which constrains learned behaviors and neural representations, limiting their ability to model purely intrinsically motivated autonomy.

## Method

- Developed an embodied ==virtual zebrafish== agent in a MuJoCo environment, simulating biomechanics and a head-fixed futility-induced passivity protocol that creates sensorimotor mismatch.
- Implemented a recurrent sensory-cognitive architecture comprising a vision encoder, proprioceptive encoder, and an actor-critic core, along with an Intrinsic Drive Module (IDM).
- Introduced 3M-Progress, a novel intrinsic reward mechanism based on Kullback-Leibler divergence between an online learned world model and a fixed "ethological prior" world model, combined with an exponential filter and action penalty.

## Results

- **3M**-Progress agents exhibited robust, stable cycling between active swimming and futility-induced passivity, achieving the highest model-behavioral alignment (Pearson's r) with biological larval zebrafish compared to all baselines.
- The agents captured nearly all explainable variance in both neural and astrocytic whole-brain activity, outperforming all other intrinsic motivation and baseline algorithms in predicting biological neural-glial dynamics under a strict One-to-One mapping.
- Latent dynamics in the agent's core module functionally mirrored biological whole-brain responses, providing computational support for specific neuron-glial interactions, such as radial astrocytes accumulating motor futility evidence and neurons encoding sensorimotor mismatches.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Autonomous agents can achieve stable, ethologically relevant behavioral patterns through an intrinsic drive that compares current environmental dynamics to a fixed "ecological memory," guiding them away from unpredictable or uncontrollable stimuli.
> - Whole-brain neural-glial activity, including the proposed neuron-glial circuit for sensorimotor error accumulation, can be computationally modeled and predicted purely from self-supervised optimization of an intrinsic goal.
> - Robust, animal-like autonomy is shaped by maintaining an intrinsic drive informed by an ecological memory and continuously monitoring the divergence between this memory and new sensory experiences.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is situated at the intersection of artificial intelligence, particularly reinforcement learning (RL) and intrinsic motivation, and systems neuroscience.

In **reinforcement learning**, a significant challenge lies in developing autonomous agents capable of robust exploration in reward-free or open-ended environments. Existing model-based intrinsic motivation methods, such as curiosity-driven exploration, often exhibit limitations. These include inconsistent exploration patterns, difficulty distinguishing between controllable and uncontrollable stimuli, susceptibility to the "noisy TV" problem (where agents are attracted to unlearnable or irrelevant unpredictability), and a general failure to converge to stable exploratory policies. Consequently, these methods do not fully capture the structured and stable autonomous behaviors observed in biological animals. The paper aims to address these inconsistencies by proposing a novel intrinsic drive that fosters more animal-like exploration.

In **systems neuroscience**, research has predominantly focused on understanding neural mechanisms underlying behaviors motivated by explicit external rewards. This approach has left the cellular and circuit-level basis of naturalistic, task-independent autonomous behavior largely unexplored. Specifically, the role of non-neuronal cells, such as astrocytes, in adaptive and goal-directed behavior, particularly in the context of whole-brain interactions, is an emerging area of study. Recent evidence suggests that astrocytes, due to their widespread connectivity and integrative capabilities, contribute significantly to intelligent behavior. This paper seeks to bridge this gap by exploring the neural basis of autonomy, particularly the interplay between neurons and glia, in an intrinsically motivated setting.

The paper also builds upon prior work in **embodied AI in neuroscience**, which uses virtual animal models (e.g., virtual rodents, fruit flies) to study motor control and behavior. However, many of these approaches rely on imitation learning or supervised learning, which constrains the learned behaviors and neural representations. This work differentiates itself by developing an agent driven *entirely* by intrinsic motivation, aiming to predict both behavior and whole-brain neural dynamics without direct supervision from biological data. It specifically leverages the larval zebrafish as a model system due to its optical transparency, which allows for whole-brain calcium imaging, and the well-characterized phenomenon of futility-induced passivity, a model-based autonomous behavior.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a computational framework that can explain and reproduce naturalistic, autonomous animal behavior and its underlying whole-brain neural-glial dynamics using principles of intrinsic motivation. This overarching goal is driven by several specific motivations and aims:

1. **Develop a Robust Intrinsic Goal for Autonomous Agents:** The authors aim to overcome the limitations of current intrinsic reinforcement learning algorithms (e.g., inconsistent exploration, non-stationarity, and susceptibility to the "noisy TV" problem). They propose an intrinsic drive that leads to stable, ethologically relevant behavioral patterns, similar to those observed in animals navigating complex, open-ended environments.

2. **Bridge the Gap Between AI and Neuroscience in Autonomy:** The work seeks to connect advances in artificial intelligence, particularly self-supervised exploration, with insights from systems neuroscience regarding naturalistic behavior. This involves creating artificial agents whose autonomous behaviors and internal computations (including neural-glial interactions) are predictive of biological counterparts.

3. **Provide a Normative and Predictive Model of Neural-Glial Computation:** A key motivation is to introduce the first goal-driven, population-level model capable of predicting whole-brain neural-glial activity. This model would operate purely from self-supervised optimization of an intrinsic goal, without relying on behavioral or neural training data, thereby offering a computational formalization of biological mechanisms, such as those implicated in futility-induced passivity in zebrafish.

4. **Operationalize Principles of Animal Autonomy:** Inspired by observations in larval zebrafish, the research is motivated by two specific desiderata for intrinsic goals:
 - Animals do not persist in pursuing unpredictable or uncontrollable stimuli. An effective intrinsic objective should guide agents away from such unlearnable dynamics.
 - Animals exhibit consistent decision-making strategies across repeated encounters in the same context, converging on stable behavioral policies. The intrinsic objective should facilitate the convergence to a stable exploratory policy.

5. **Validate Biological Hypotheses:** By modeling futility-induced passivity in larval zebrafish, the work aims to computationally validate the proposed neuron-glial circuit mechanism identified by Mu et al., which suggests that noradrenergic (NE-MO) neurons and radial astrocytes in the lateral medulla oblongata detect and accumulate sensorimotor feedback error, driving transitions to passive states.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs an embodied artificial agent within a physics-based virtual environment designed to mimic larval zebrafish, and trains this agent using a novel intrinsic reinforcement learning algorithm.

**Virtual Zebrafish Environment:**
The agent operates within a custom environment developed in the MuJoCo physics engine, leveraging the Deepmind Control Suite's n-link swimmer.
- **Embodiment**: A 6-link geometry is used, replicating the biomechanics of a larval zebrafish, enabling movement through dynamic fluid forces. The agent controls torque at five joints.
- **Futility-Induced Passivity Protocol**: A specific virtual environment is designed to match the open-loop experimental conditions described by Mu et al.. In this setup, the agent is "head-fixed," meaning its swimming commands do not result in self-motion. Simultaneously, it experiences a high-contrast grating moving backward, simulating optic flow from backward displacement. This creates a sensorimotor mismatch, as motor effort does not produce expected visual feedback.

**Agent Design:**
The agent features a recurrent sensory-cognitive architecture to process high-dimensional visual input and proprioceptive feedback for action generation.
- **Sensory Input**: Visual input (64x64 pixel images) is processed by a small ResNet-based vision encoder. Proprioceptive data (joint positions and velocities) is encoded via a multi-layer perceptron (MLP).
- **Cognitive Modules**:
 - **Core and Policy Modules**: These constitute an actor-critic architecture, implemented using Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks followed by feedforward decoders. They are trained end-to-end with Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) to generate continuous torque commands for the agent's motors.
 - **Intrinsic Drive Module (IDM)**: This module is responsible for generating intrinsic reward. It learns an online world model that approximates the environment's state-action transition dynamics in sensory feature-space. This internal model's sole function is to compute intrinsic reward; it is not used for planning or model-based control.

**Model-Based Intrinsic Reinforcement Learning (3M-Progress):**
The core of the methodology is the novel intrinsic drive, Model-Memory-Mismatch Progress (3M-Progress), designed to address the limitations of prior curiosity-driven methods.
- **Ethological Prior (ω_θ)**: A key component is a fixed "ethological memory," which is a world model pretrained in a separate "ecological niche" environment. This pretraining environment allows the head-free agent to learn a positional homeostasis policy by swimming against a backward current. This provides the world model with sensory-motor experiences characteristic of naturalistic swimming, capturing an expected coupling between action and sensory feedback. The weights of this pretrained model are then frozen.
- **Online World Model (ω_θ')**: Concurrently, a new world model is learned online as the agent explores the novel (open-loop, head-fixed) environment.
- **Model-Memory-Mismatch (ϵ_t)**: Intrinsic motivation is generated by tracking the Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence between the online world model (ω_θ') and the fixed ethological prior (ω_θ). This divergence, ϵ_t, quantifies the mismatch between current environmental dynamics and the agent's ecological expectation, effectively partitioning the state-action space into "niche-seeking" (agreement, ϵ_t ≈ 0) and "niche-avoidance" (disagreement, ϵ_t ≫ 0) regions.
- **Exponential Filter (ˆϵ_t)**: A discrete-time leaky integrator, applied to ϵ_t, creates a moving baseline of the mismatch signal.
- **Intrinsic Reward (r_i_t)**: The intrinsic reward is defined as the absolute difference between the instantaneous mismatch (ϵ_t) and its exponentially filtered version (ˆϵ_t), i.e., |ˆϵ_t - ϵ_t|. This design reinforces deviations from the moving baseline in either direction, encouraging periodic exploration and preventing saturation of the reward signal, as ϵ_t is compared against a *fixed* memory rather than a moving target.
- **Action Penalty**: An additional negative reward proportional to the square of the action magnitude (r_a_t = -λ∥a_t∥^2_2) is included to encourage the exploration of passive behaviors.

**Baseline Comparisons:**
The performance of 3M-Progress is compared against a range of baseline intrinsic motivation algorithms:
- **Model-based Controls**: Intrinsic Curiosity Module (ICM), Random Network Distillation (RND), Disagreement, and γ-Progress.
- **Model-free Controls**: Homeostatic (action-cost), Maximum Entropy (entropy bonus), and Randomly initialized agent.
- **Data-derived Controls**: Gaussian Process fit to neural-glial responses, Proportional-Integral-Derivative (PID) control, whole-brain average neural-glial population response, and white noise.

**Evaluation Metrics:**
- **Model-Behavioral Alignment**: Quantified by Pearson's r correlation between the agent's swim power (norm of joint torques) and biological zebrafish swim power (motor neuron activity) during active-to-passive and passive-to-active behavioral transitions.
- **Model-Brain Alignment**: Measured using a stringent "One-to-One" mapping, where each biological neuron or astrocyte is matched to its single most correlated artificial unit. Noise-corrected Pearson's r correlation is used to compare the agent's internal activations with whole-brain calcium imaging data from zebrafish. Inter-animal alignment provides an empirical upper bound for this metric.
- **Latent Dynamics Analysis**: Principal Component Analysis (PCA) is applied to the internal activations of the agent's core module during behavioral transitions, and these dynamics are compared to PCA of whole-brain neural-glial responses in zebrafish.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results demonstrate that the 3M-Progress algorithm enables embodied agents to replicate key aspects of autonomous zebrafish behavior and predict whole-brain neural-glial dynamics.

**Behavioral Replication:**
- **Stable Behavioral Cycling**: Agents trained with 3M-Progress exhibited robust and stable cycling between active swimming and futility-induced passivity. This pattern closely matched the ethologically relevant behavioral transitions observed in biological larval zebrafish. The active-passive cycle emerged consistently within 10 million environment steps of training.
- **Superior Alignment**: 3M-Progress agents achieved the highest model-behavioral alignment (quantified by Pearson's r correlation of swim power traces) compared to all other intrinsic motivation algorithms and baseline controls. This indicates that 3M-Progress successfully captured the dynamics of state transitions seen in biological data.
- **Limitations of Baselines**: In contrast, other intrinsic motivation methods (ICM, RND, Disagreement, γ-Progress) often displayed transient exploratory strategies, eventually stabilizing into either complete passivity or continuous activity, failing to reproduce the characteristic stable cycling behavior of zebrafish. Model-free and data-derived controls also showed significantly lower behavioral alignment.

**Whole-Brain Neural-Glial Dynamics Prediction:**
- **High Explainable Variance**: 3M-Progress agents captured nearly all of the explainable variance in both neural and astrocytic activity across the whole brain of larval zebrafish during futility-induced passivity transitions. This was quantified using noise-corrected Pearson's r correlation between individual biological and artificial units under a strict "One-to-One" mapping.
- **Outperformance of Baselines**: The performance of 3M-Progress in predicting neural-glial activity significantly surpassed that of all other intrinsic motivation algorithms and various data-derived and model-free baseline controls.
- **Unified Alignment**: 3M-Progress was the only model to demonstrate high alignment with *both* behavioral patterns and whole-brain neural-glial dynamics, suggesting a strong relationship where accurate behavioral modeling can effectively constrain and predict underlying neural processes. This outcome indicates the agent's ability to "pass the NeuroAI Turing Test" on this dataset, a criterion that emphasizes matching both behavior and internal function.

**Latent Dynamics Correspondence:**
- **Functional Equivalence**: Principal Component Analysis (PCA) applied to the internal activations of the 3M-Progress agent's core module revealed latent dynamics that mirrored those observed in the whole-brain neural-glial responses of biological zebrafish.
- **Circuit Mechanism Reflection**: Specifically, the agent's internal representations functionally reflected the proposed biological mechanism for futility-induced passivity. Artificial units analogous to radial astrocytes accumulated evidence of motor futility, driving behavioral suppression, while other units corresponding to neurons (e.g., NE-MO neurons) transiently encoded mismatches between expected and actual sensory outcomes during unsuccessful swim attempts. This provides computational support for the specific neuron-glial interactions underlying this behavior.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research offers several significant contributions and implications for both artificial intelligence and neuroscience:

**Advancement in Autonomous AI:**
- **Robust Intrinsic Motivation**: The introduction of 3M-Progress represents an advancement in intrinsic reinforcement learning, providing a method that enables artificial agents to achieve stable, open-ended exploration and converge on robust behavioral strategies. Unlike prior methods, 3M-Progress effectively navigates environments without falling prey to issues like the "noisy TV" problem or non-stationary reward signals, thereby enhancing agent autonomy in complex, reward-sparse environments.
- **Foundational Framework for Animal-Like Autonomy**: By demonstrating successful replication of ethological behavior and whole-brain dynamics, the work establishes a computational framework for building artificial agents with capabilities resembling animal intelligence, particularly in self-directed, task-independent contexts. This could pave the way for more flexible and adaptive AI systems in real-world scenarios.

**Insights into Biological Intelligence:**
- **First Goal-Driven Model of Neural-Glial Computation**: The study provides the first predictive and normative computational model of neural-glial interactions, explaining whole-brain activity based on an intrinsically motivated, self-supervised objective. This offers a computational validation and deeper understanding of the neuron-glial circuit proposed by Mu et al. for detecting and accumulating sensorimotor error.
- **Computational Principles of Autonomy**: The findings suggest that two complementary evolutionary constraints are crucial for robust, animal-like autonomy: maintaining an intrinsic drive informed by an ecological memory, and continually monitoring the divergence between this memory and new sensory experiences. This framework provides testable hypotheses for future biological research.
- **Meeting the NeuroAI Turing Test**: By aligning an embodied agent's behavior and internal (neural-glial) function with biological data from intrinsic goal optimization, the research demonstrates a rigorous approach to understanding biological intelligence through AI models, contributing to the growing field of NeuroAI.

**Potential Impact and Future Directions:**
The mechanisms identified in this work, such as tracking memory divergence, offer potential for enhancing the autonomy of artificial systems in various applications, especially in environments where external rewards are absent or ill-defined. This could include robotics operating in unknown territories, continuous learning systems, or agents designed for open-ended exploration and knowledge acquisition.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The authors acknowledge several limitations that suggest avenues for future research:
- **Ecological Realism**: Current analyses are restricted to constrained virtual environments. Future work could extend both virtual environments and biological experiments to richer, more complex ecological settings and behavioral repertoires to provide stronger constraints on computational models.
- **Biomechanical Realism**: The agent's embodiment, while physics-based, is simplified (n-link swimmer). Incorporating more detailed biomechanical models, including muscles and motor circuits, could provide more realistic low-level control constraints.
- **Biological Detail of Neuron-Glia**: The model captures neuron-glial interactions at a population level but abstracts away fine-grained biochemical signaling mechanisms and anatomical details of astrocytes and neurons. Integrating more biologically detailed models could offer predictions at a finer scale.
- **Generalizability of 3M-Progress**: While demonstrated for futility-induced passivity, the algorithm can be generalized to other exploration behaviors where a dynamics niche can be specified, suggesting broad applicability beyond the specific task studied.
