# In the Eye of the Beholder: Gaze and Actions in First Person Video

> [!summary] Summary
> This research from the University of Wisconsin-Madison and Georgia Institute of Technology introduces a probabilistic deep learning model that jointly estimates gaze and recognizes actions from first-person videos. It also presents the EGTEA Gaze+ dataset, which includes synchronized video, gaze tracking, hand masks, and fine-grained action annotations. The model achieves improved action recognition accuracy and competitive gaze estimation performance, demonstrating robustness to gaze uncertainty and generalizability to datasets without explicit gaze supervision.

## Problem

- Existing FPV research often modeled gaze and actions separately or required additional side information (e.g., hand masks) during testing.
- Previous FPV datasets lacked comprehensive multi-modal annotations, specifically synchronized gaze tracking data alongside fine-grained action labels.
- Gaze measurements inherently contain uncertainty and noise (e.g., saccades, eye-tracker errors) which can hinder models relying on deterministic gaze inputs.

## Method

- The authors created the "EGTEA Gaze+" dataset, a comprehensive First Person Vision (FPV) benchmark with synchronized video, binocular gaze tracking, pixel-level hand masks, and fine-grained action annotations.
- A novel deep learning model was developed that jointly estimates gaze and recognizes actions by treating gaze as a probabilistic variable to account for its inherent uncertainty.
- The model employs a variational learning approach with a Gumbel-Softmax reparameterization trick, allowing end-to-end training and robust integration of gaze into an attention mechanism.

## Results

- The probabilistic gaze model achieved an average of 55.03% mean class accuracy for action recognition on the EGTEA Gaze+ dataset, surpassing previous state-of-the-art FPV methods.
- The model demonstrated competitive gaze estimation performance (averaging 33.84% F1 score across splits) while simultaneously performing action recognition, comparable to models solely dedicated to gaze prediction.
- When applied to the large-scale EPIC-Kitchens dataset, the model achieved state-of-the-art performance among single-model entries (e.g., 38.75% Top1 action accuracy on s1 seen test set), demonstrating strong generalizability even without explicit gaze supervision.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Modeling gaze probabilistically significantly improves action recognition by robustly accounting for inherent uncertainty and noise in egocentric gaze measurements, outperforming deterministic gaze integration methods.
> - A comprehensive multi-modal dataset with synchronized gaze and action annotations provides crucial resources for advancing research in joint gaze estimation, action recognition, and hand segmentation in FPV.
> - The proposed attention mechanism can effectively learn to focus on action-relevant regions even without direct gaze supervision during training (using a uniform prior), demonstrating the model's adaptability and potential for broader application.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper contributes to the field of First Person Vision (FPV), also known as Egocentric Vision. FPV research focuses on analyzing visual data captured by wearable cameras, which record daily visual experiences from the wearer's perspective. This domain has seen increasing interest due to advancements in wearable camera technology, enabling large-scale data collection.

Existing FPV research encompasses various tasks, including action and activity recognition, gaze estimation and prediction, pose estimation, and video summarization. Prior work has demonstrated the utility of egocentric gaze for FPV action recognition, suggesting that "where we look" provides information about "what we do." However, a common limitation in previous approaches was the separate modeling of gaze and actions, or the requirement of additional side information (e.g., hand masks, object information) during testing. Furthermore, a significant challenge in integrating gaze data is the inherent uncertainty and noise associated with gaze measurements, such as saccades (rapid eye movements irrelevant to current actions) and measurement errors from eye-trackers.

This paper addresses these limitations by introducing a comprehensive FPV dataset that integrates video, gaze, hand masks, and action annotations. It also proposes a novel deep learning model designed for the joint estimation of gaze and recognition of actions, specifically accounting for the uncertainty in gaze measurements. By modeling gaze as a probabilistic variable and integrating it into an attention mechanism, the work seeks to develop a more robust and integrated understanding of egocentric perception and action, advancing beyond models that treat these components as separate or require external cues during inference. The application of this model to larger-scale FPV datasets, even without direct gaze supervision, positions it as a step towards generalizable FPV action recognition.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations guiding this research are:

- **Joint Gaze Estimation and Action Recognition:** The central objective is to develop a computational model capable of simultaneously inferring a person's actions and their point of gaze from head-worn camera video. The motivation stems from the hypothesis that gaze provides critical information about attention and interaction with objects, which in turn informs the understanding of ongoing actions. Observing examples where actions are easily recognizable even with only gaze-centered regions visible underscores the strong link between gaze and action.

- **Introduction of a Comprehensive FPV Dataset:** A significant motivation was the perceived lack of a sufficiently comprehensive benchmark dataset for FPV research. While existing FPV datasets contribute to action recognition, they often lack rich multi-modal annotations such as gaze tracking data and egocentric hand masks alongside fine-grained action labels. To address this, the authors aimed to create the "EGTEA Gaze+" dataset, providing a broad range of synchronized signals for a holistic study of attention and action in FPV. This dataset is intended to serve as a resource for the community to further research in FPV gaze estimation, hand segmentation, action recognition, and action detection.

- **Addressing Gaze Measurement Uncertainty:** A key challenge identified in previous work is the inherent uncertainty in egocentric gaze measurements. Gaze data can include saccades (rapid eye movements not related to visual input), fixations unrelated to the current action, and inherent eye-tracker errors. This noise and ambiguity can hinder the effectiveness of models that rely on deterministic gaze inputs. The motivation here is to develop a model that can robustly account for this uncertainty by treating gaze as a probabilistic variable, thereby preventing irrelevant gaze events from distracting the action recognition process.

- **Development of an End-to-End Deep Learning Model:** The authors sought to develop a deep neural network that integrates the probabilistic gaze model with an attention mechanism. This model should be capable of end-to-end training using both action labels and noisy gaze measurements, and crucially, should be able to infer both gaze and action from video input alone during testing, without requiring explicit side information. The motivation is to create a unified framework that leverages the strengths of deep learning for complex visual tasks while incorporating human cognitive processes like visual attention.

- **Demonstrating Model Generalizability:** A further objective was to demonstrate that the developed model architecture, initially trained on the comprehensive EGTEA Gaze+ dataset, could be effectively transferred and applied to other large-scale FPV datasets, such as EPIC-Kitchens, even when direct gaze supervision is unavailable during training. This showcases the model's robustness and potential for broader application in real-world FPV scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research comprises two main components: the creation of a new dataset and the development of a novel deep learning model.

#### 3.1. The EGTEA Gaze+ Dataset

The EGTEA Gaze+ dataset is an expansion of the previous GTEA Gaze+ dataset, designed to be a comprehensive benchmark for FPV research.

- **Data Collection:**
 - **Environment:** Videos were collected in a naturalistic kitchen environment at the Aware Home on the Georgia Tech campus.
 - **Participants & Tasks:** 32 participants performed 7 different meal preparation tasks across 86 unique sessions.
 - **Equipment:** Videos, audio, and binocular gaze tracking data were recorded using SMI eye-tracking glasses.
 - **Protocol:** Participants followed recipes, with eye-tracker calibration performed before and after each session. Sessions with low tracking quality were discarded.
 - **De-identification:** Recorded videos were manually screened to remove any frames that could reveal participant identity, primarily during glasses mounting/removal or from reflections. Removed frames were replaced with black images.
 - **Statistics:** The dataset totals 28 hours of 1280x920 resolution video at 24Hz. Gaze data was tracked at 30Hz. Gaze points were classified into fixations (53.6%), saccades (26.1%), unknown (17.0%), or untracked (3.3%).

- **Data Annotation:**
 - **Hand Mask Annotation (Hand14K):** Pixel-level annotations of the first person's hands were provided for sparsely sampled frames (one frame every 5 seconds). These were generated by a third-party company using polygons and converted into masks. The Hand14K dataset comprises 15,176 hand masks from 13,847 frames.
 - **Action Annotation:**
 - **Taxonomy:** Actions were defined by a combination of a single verb and a set of nouns (e.g., "take tomato," "turn on oven"). 19 verbs and 53 nouns formed 106 unique fine-grained action labels.
 - **Pipeline:** A multi-stage annotation pipeline was developed to ensure accuracy and efficiency:
 1. **Action Candidate Labeling:** Annotators used ELAN software to mark rough temporal onsets and offsets of potential actions.
 2. **Action Naming:** Cropped video clips of action candidates were presented via a web interface, where annotators assigned verbs from a pre-defined list and entered nouns. Clips were flagged for issues (no action, multiple actions, incomplete action).
 3. **Action Trimming:** A separate web interface allowed annotators to refine the exact temporal boundaries (onset/offset) of the labeled actions within padded clips.
 - **Post-Processing:** Labels were refined by removing short clips (<0.5 seconds), running spell checks, merging synonyms (e.g., fork/knife/spoon into "eating utensil"), and combining sub-categories into super-categories. The top 106 most frequent categories were retained.
 - **Statistics:** The dataset contains 10,325 action instances with an average duration of 4.2 seconds, exhibiting a long-tailed distribution (e.g., "read recipe" 752 times, "put (down) oil container" 32 times).
 - **Train/Test Splits:** Three non-overlapping splits were created for fair benchmarking, with approximately 80% of samples per category for training and the rest for testing.

#### 3.2. Joint Gaze and Action Model

The proposed model addresses joint gaze estimation and action recognition in FPV videos by modeling gaze probabilistically.

- **Core Idea: Probabilistic Gaze Modeling:** The participant's gaze, denoted `g`, is treated as a probabilistic variable to account for its inherent uncertainty. The overall goal is to predict action category `y` for an input video `x` by integrating over possible gaze states: `p(y|x) = ∫g p(y|g, x)p(g|x)dg`. This integral is approximated by modeling the posterior `p(g|x)` with a carefully designed `q_ψ(g|x)`, representing the distribution of a 2D attention map.

- **Stochastic Units and Attention Mechanism:**
 - **Gaze Distribution `q_ψ(g|x)`:** A deep neural network `q_ψ` outputs parameters `π` for a discrete 2D grid, defining a probability distribution `q(m,n)` for gaze at each position `(m,n)`. This `q` represents the latent attention map.
 - **Sampling Attention Map:** A gaze map `~g` is sampled from `q_π`.
 - **Attention-Guided Recognition:** The sampled `~g` is used to selectively aggregate visual features `φ(x)` (extracted by another network `φ`) through a weighted average pooling. These pooled features are then fed into a recognition network `f` (a linear classifier with softmax) to predict the action `y`. This is a soft attention mechanism allowing back-propagation, enabling top-down modulation of gaze from action labels.

- **Variational Learning:**
 - **Objective Function:** The model is trained by maximizing a lower bound of `log p(y|x)`, which corresponds to minimizing the loss function: `- ∑g log p(y|g, x) + KL[q(g|x)||p(g|x)]`. This combines a negative log-likelihood (cross-entropy for action recognition) with a Kullback–Leibler (KL) divergence term between the predicted gaze distribution `q(g|x)` and a prior gaze distribution `p(g|x)`.
 - **Learning with Egocentric Gaze (EGTEA Gaze+):** When human gaze measurements are available, `p(g|x)` models the noise pattern. For tracked fixation points, it's a 2D isotropic Gaussian. For saccades or missing gaze points, it's a 2D uniform distribution.
 - **Learning without Human Gaze (EPIC-Kitchens):** When gaze data is unavailable, `p(g|x)` is simplified to a 2D uniform prior `p(g)`, which acts as a regularizer.
 - **Reparameterization:** To enable end-to-end back-propagation through the sampling process, the Gumbel-Softmax trick is applied, allowing gradients to flow to the parameters `π`.

- **Approximate Inference:** During testing, instead of expensive multiple samples from `q` to average predictions, the model uses a deterministic approximation `f(E[g], x) ≈ E_g[f(g, x)]`, where `E[g]` is the expectation of the gaze map (i.e., `q_π`). This is justified by Jensen's Inequality, as `f` (a softmax linear classifier) is convex.

- **Network Architecture:**
 - **Backbone:** The model utilizes a two-stream I3D (Inflated 3D Convolutional Network) architecture, processing both RGB frames and optical flow maps.
 - **Fusion:** Streams are fused using element-wise summation at the end of the 4th convolutional block for gaze estimation and the 5th for action recognition.
 - **Output:** The model processes 24 frames at a temporal stride of 8, outputting a 7x7 spatial resolution gaze map (downsampled by 32x) and action scores.
 - **Implementation Details:** Video frames are downsampled to 320x256; optical flow computed using FlowNet V2. Random cropping, horizontal flip, and color jittering are used for augmentation. During testing, frames and their flipped versions are processed, and clip-level scores are averaged.
 - **Training Details:** SGD with momentum and weight decay. Initial weights are from Kinetics pre-trained I3D models. Batch size of 40 over 4 GPUs, learning rate of 0.032, decayed at epoch 40, trained for 80 epochs. Batch normalization and dropout (0.7) are used.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. Ablation Study on EGTEA Gaze+

- **Backbone Network (RGB vs. Flow):** Joint training of RGB and flow streams in an I3D network yielded the best performance (55.76% mean class accuracy on Split1), outperforming RGB (50.08%) and Flow (45.78%) streams individually. Flow-only performance was significantly lower than RGB, indicating challenges in capturing motion cues in FPV.
- **Probabilistic vs. Deterministic Modeling:** The probabilistic gaze model consistently outperformed a deterministic "Gaze MLE" variant. For action recognition, the probabilistic model with gaze supervision (57.20%) surpassed Gaze MLE (55.88%) by an average of 1.0% and the I3D baseline (55.76%) by 1.44%. For gaze estimation, it achieved 34.01% F1 score, significantly higher than Gaze MLE's 26.63%.
- **Training without Human Gaze:** When trained using a uniform prior (without human gaze supervision), the model (56.50%) still marginally outperformed the base I3D (55.76%) and Gaze MLE (55.88%) for action recognition, demonstrating that probabilistic attention aids learning even without explicit gaze labels. Gaze estimation performance, however, was significantly lower (11.63% F1) without gaze supervision.
- **Improvements over Conference Version:** Additional regularization (dropout) and longer training improved both baseline I3D and the proposed model, with I3D joint accuracy increasing from 49.79% to 53.55% and the probabilistic model from 53.30% to 57.20% on the first split.

#### 4.2. FPV Gaze Estimation on EGTEA Gaze+

- **Performance:** The proposed model achieved an F1 score of 34.01% (Split1), 31.79% (Split2), and 35.73% (Split3) for gaze estimation.
- **Comparison to Baselines:**
 - Deep models (e.g., Simple Gaze, Deep Gaze, Gaze MLE, Ours) significantly outperformed simpler baselines like Center Prior (10.65% F1) and EgoGaze (16.63% F1), which uses hand-crafted features.
 - Models employing KL loss (Deep Gaze, Ours) generally performed better than those using cross-entropy loss (Simple Gaze, Gaze MLE), suggesting KL divergence is more effective for balancing gaze and action losses.
 - The proposed model's gaze estimation performance was comparable to Deep Gaze (34.46% F1 on Split1) and slightly worse by an average of 0.4% across splits, despite Deep Gaze being solely focused on gaze estimation. This suggests the joint model maintains strong gaze estimation capabilities.

#### 4.3. FPV Action Recognition on EGTEA Gaze+

- **Performance:** The proposed model, supervised by human gaze (Ours w. gaze), achieved mean class accuracies of 57.20% (Split1), 53.75% (Split2), and 54.13% (Split3), averaging 55.03%. Even without gaze supervision (Ours wo. gaze), it achieved an average of 54.53%.
- **Comparison to Baselines:**
 - Deep models significantly surpassed traditional methods like EgoIDT+Gaze (39.13% average accuracy).
 - I3D (joint) (54.15% average) served as a strong baseline, outperforming prior deep FPV action recognition methods such as Ego-RNN-2S (50.53% average) and LSTA-2S (53.00% on Split1).
 - Naively integrating egocentric cues (I3D+Gaze, I3D+Center Prior, I3D+EgoConv) did not improve or even decreased performance, highlighting the need for proper integration.
 - The proposed model consistently outperformed most state-of-the-art methods, including Ego-RNN-2S and LSTA-2S, by a notable margin (e.g., 4.2% over LSTA-2S on Split1).
 - It outperformed Mutual Context-2S (55.70% on Split1) but was outperformed by Multitask (57.60% average), which used a stronger backbone, hand masks, and joint verb/noun classification.

- **Visualizations:** Qualitative results showed that the model's gaze outputs often attended to relevant foreground objects that the person was interacting with, which likely contributed to better action recognition. These visualizations also assisted in diagnosing recognition errors, such as confusion between similar objects or action verbs.

#### 4.4. Extension to EPIC-Kitchens Dataset

- **Performance:** Applying the model (Ours + CSN152) using only RGB frames and a uniform gaze prior on EPIC-Kitchens achieved competitive results:
 - Seen (s1) test set: Verb 68.51%/89.32%, Noun 49.96%/72.30%, Action 38.75%/59.00% (Top1/Top5).
 - Unseen (s2) test set: Verb 60.05%/81.97%, Noun 38.14%/63.81%, Action 27.35%/45.24% (Top1/Top5).
- **Comparison:** The model achieved state-of-the-art performance among single-model entries, outperforming methods that used optical flow, object detectors, or audio data (e.g., EPIC-Fusion, R(2+1)D). In the EPIC-Kitchens Challenge 2020, it ranked 2nd for unseen environments and 4th for seen environments.
- **Ablation on EPIC-Kitchens:** Replacing the I3D backbone with a pre-trained CSN152 network significantly boosted performance (e.g., +14.0% Top1 action accuracy on s1 compared to I3D-Res50). On top of the strong CSN152 baseline, the proposed model further improved results (e.g., +0.3% Top1 action accuracy on s1), consistent with EGTEA findings.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of First Person Vision (FPV) through the introduction of a comprehensive dataset and a novel deep learning model.

The **EGTEA Gaze+ dataset** provides a detailed benchmark, integrating high-resolution video, synchronized gaze tracking data, pixel-level hand masks, and fine-grained action annotations. This rich multi-modal resource is significant as it facilitates a more holistic study of attention and action in FPV, addressing the previous lack of datasets offering such a comprehensive set of signals. It is anticipated to be a valuable resource for advancing research in FPV gaze estimation, hand segmentation, action recognition, and activity understanding.

The proposed **deep learning model** introduces a probabilistic approach to jointly estimate gaze and recognize actions in FPV. By treating gaze as a stochastic variable and accounting for its inherent uncertainty through variational learning and a Gumbel-Softmax reparameterization, the model demonstrates robustness to noisy gaze measurements. The attention mechanism, guided by the predicted gaze distribution, enables the model to focus on action-relevant regions, thereby improving action recognition performance.

The **main findings** indicate that:
- Probabilistic modeling of gaze, even with noisy supervision, generally enhances action recognition performance compared to deterministic approaches.
- The model achieves competitive results in gaze estimation while concurrently performing action recognition, unlike methods solely dedicated to gaze prediction.
- It establishes new performance levels on the EGTEA Gaze+ dataset for FPV action recognition, surpassing many existing state-of-the-art deep models.
- Crucially, the model's architecture, when adapted to leverage advanced backbones (like CSN152) and trained with a simplified uniform gaze prior (in the absence of direct gaze supervision), achieves state-of-the-art results on the large-scale EPIC-Kitchens dataset. This demonstrates its generalizability and applicability to real-world FPV scenarios where explicit gaze tracking data might not be available during training or inference.

The **potential impact** of this work is multi-faceted. It offers a principled approach to integrate visual attention (gaze) into action recognition systems, making them potentially more robust and interpretable. By enabling joint inference of gaze and actions from video alone, it paves the way for advanced FPV applications such as context-aware wearable assistants, intuitive human-computer interaction, and fine-grained behavioral analysis. The insights gained into the relationship between egocentric gaze and actions, particularly the benefits of probabilistic modeling, can inform future research directions in computational understanding of human behavior and visual cognition. Future work could explore incorporating other cues like hand locations into more sophisticated gaze priors or attention mechanisms, further enhancing the model's capabilities.
