# In the Eye of the Beholder: Gaze and Actions in First Person Video

> [!summary] Summary
> This research from the University of Wisconsin-Madison and Georgia Institute of Technology introduces a probabilistic deep learning model that jointly estimates gaze and recognizes actions from first-person videos. It also presents the EGTEA Gaze+ dataset, which includes synchronized video, gaze tracking, hand masks, and fine-grained action annotations. The model achieves improved action recognition accuracy and competitive gaze estimation performance, demonstrating robustness to gaze uncertainty and generalizability to datasets without explicit gaze supervision.

## Problem

- Existing FPV research often modeled gaze and actions separately or required additional side information (e.g., hand masks) during testing.
- Previous FPV datasets lacked comprehensive multi-modal annotations, specifically synchronized gaze tracking data alongside fine-grained action labels.
- Gaze measurements inherently contain uncertainty and noise (e.g., saccades, eye-tracker errors) which can hinder models relying on deterministic gaze inputs.

## Method

- The authors created the "EGTEA Gaze+" dataset, a comprehensive First Person Vision (FPV) benchmark with synchronized video, binocular gaze tracking, pixel-level hand masks, and fine-grained action annotations.
- A novel deep learning model was developed that jointly estimates gaze and recognizes actions by treating gaze as a probabilistic variable to account for its inherent uncertainty.
- The model employs a variational learning approach with a Gumbel-Softmax reparameterization trick, allowing end-to-end training and robust integration of gaze into an attention mechanism.

## Results

- The probabilistic gaze model achieved an average of 55.03% mean class accuracy for action recognition on the EGTEA Gaze+ dataset, surpassing previous state-of-the-art FPV methods.
- The model demonstrated competitive gaze estimation performance (averaging 33.84% F1 score across splits) while simultaneously performing action recognition, comparable to models solely dedicated to gaze prediction.
- When applied to the large-scale EPIC-Kitchens dataset, the model achieved state-of-the-art performance among single-model entries (e.g., 38.75% Top1 action accuracy on s1 seen test set), demonstrating strong generalizability even without explicit gaze supervision.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Modeling gaze probabilistically significantly improves action recognition by robustly accounting for inherent uncertainty and noise in egocentric gaze measurements, outperforming deterministic gaze integration methods.
> - A comprehensive multi-modal dataset with synchronized gaze and action annotations provides crucial resources for advancing research in joint gaze estimation, action recognition, and hand segmentation in FPV.
> - The proposed attention mechanism can effectively learn to focus on action-relevant regions even without direct gaze supervision during training (using a uniform prior), demonstrating the model's adaptability and potential for broader application.
