# SoccerDiffusion: Toward Learning End-to-End Humanoid Robot Soccer from Gameplay Recordings

> [!summary] Summary
> SoccerDiffusion, developed by the University of Hamburg, introduces a transformer-based diffusion model that learns end-to-end control policies for humanoid robots directly from 15 hours of real-world RoboCup gameplay recordings. The model successfully replicates fundamental behaviors like walking, kicking, and fall recovery, achieving a 95% success rate in physical robot fall recovery tests and enabling real-time inference on embedded hardware through distillation.

## Problem

- Traditional robot control systems involve extensive manual engineering, leading to strong inductive biases and limited adaptive behavior in dynamic environments.
- Existing end-to-end learning methods for robotics often depend on labor-intensive, handcrafted demonstrations to bootstrap basic skills.
- Modeling complex, multimodal action distributions (where multiple valid actions exist for a given state) is challenging for conventional behavioral cloning approaches, often resulting in averaged, suboptimal policies.

## Method

- The authors developed **SoccerDiffusion**, a transformer-based diffusion model trained on 15 hours (340 GB) of real-world multi-modal gameplay recordings from RoboCup competitions.
- The approach leverages diffusion models to capture and generate complex, multimodal distributions of joint command trajectories, addressing the challenge of diverse possible actions.
- A distillation technique is applied to compress the multistep diffusion process into a single-step inference model, enabling real-time execution on resource-constrained embedded robot hardware.
- The system processes synchronized multi-modal sensor inputs (images, joint states, IMU data) to predict future joint command trajectories.

## Results

- The model achieved a **95%** overall success rate in fall recovery tests on a physical robot (Wolfgang-OP platform) and **100%** in the Webots simulator, demonstrating robust recovery from various fall directions.
- SoccerDiffusion successfully learned fundamental behaviors including stable bipedal walking, turning, kicking, head-scanning for localization, and dynamic stabilization from real-world gameplay data.
- The distilled single-step inference model exhibited no noticeable qualitative difference compared to the original multistep diffusion model, enabling real-time performance on target hardware.
- The model showed a significant bias towards stillness if no prior motion was present in its context window and did not acquire high-level tactical behaviors like intentional goal-kicking or strategic field positioning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Complex and dynamic humanoid robot behaviors can be effectively extracted from large volumes of naturally occurring observational gameplay data, reducing the need for handcrafted demonstrations.
> - Transformer-based diffusion models are capable of learning and representing complex, multimodal action distributions for robust control in dynamic robotic tasks.
> - Distillation allows computationally intensive deep learning models, like diffusion models, to operate in real-time on embedded robot platforms, bridging the gap between advanced AI and practical robotics.
> - Policies learned exclusively from real-world data exhibit strong sim-to-real transferability, performing robustly in simulation without additional tuning.
