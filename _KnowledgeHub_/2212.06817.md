# RT-1: Robotics Transformer for Real-World Control at Scale

> [!summary] Summary
> Google's RT-1 is a Transformer-based model trained on 130,000 real-world robotic demonstrations across 700 language-conditioned tasks, achieving a 76% success rate on unseen instructions and exhibiting strong robustness to distractors and novel environments. It effectively integrates diverse data sources, including simulation and data from other robot morphologies, with minimal performance degradation on original tasks.

## Problem

- Robot learning has historically relied on siloed, task-specific approaches, requiring bespoke datasets and models for each new skill or environment.
- Real-world robotic data collection is difficult, expensive, and time-consuming, hindering the development of generalist agents.
- Existing multi-task robot policies often struggle with robust generalization to novel tasks, objects, or environments and lack resilience to real-world clutter.

## Method

- The **Robotics Transformer 1 (RT-1)** is a ==35M parameter== ==Transformer-based policy== that takes image sequences and natural language instructions as input to output ==discretized actions==.
- The model was trained on a large-scale dataset of approximately 130,000 real-world human demonstrations across over 700 distinct ==language-conditioned tasks==, collected over 17 months using a fleet of 13 mobile manipulators.
- Key architectural components include an ==ImageNet-pretrained EfficientNet-B3== with ==FiLM== for early language fusion, a ==TokenLearner== to compress visual tokens, and a ==decoder-only Transformer==, with all 11 action dimensions discretized into ==256 bins== for ==multimodal action representation==.

## Results

- RT-1 achieved a **97%** success rate on over 200 training instructions and **76%** on novel, unseen instructions, outperforming baselines like Gato (**52%**) and BC-Z (**72%**).
- The model demonstrated superior robustness with an **83%** success rate on tasks with distractors (**36%** higher than BC-Z) and **59%** on tasks with new backgrounds.
- Integrating **518k** simulated trajectories improved performance on real-world tasks with simulated-only objects from **23%** to **87%**, and combining with data from different robot morphologies nearly doubled performance on a bin-picking task (**39%** vs. **22%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The "large general model" paradigm, successful in computer vision and natural language processing, is viable and highly beneficial for real-world robotics.
> - Data diversity across tasks, objects, and environments significantly improves generalization and robustness more than simply increasing data quantity.
> - A carefully designed Transformer architecture can effectively integrate and learn from highly heterogeneous data sources, including simulation and different robot morphologies, without degrading performance on original tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic learning has historically been characterized by siloed, task-specific approaches, requiring bespoke datasets and models for each new skill or environment. This contrasts sharply with the recent paradigm shift in computer vision (CV) and natural language processing (NLP), where large, general models (like GPT-3, BERT, ImageNet-pretrained models, CLIP, DALL-E) trained on massive, diverse datasets have demonstrated unprecedented generalization capabilities, often performing well on new tasks zero-shot or with minimal fine-tuning.

This paper argues that similar "large, general models pre-trained on broad, large datasets" are critically needed in robotics. The motivation is even stronger in robotics, where real-world data collection is inherently difficult, time-consuming, and expensive compared to collecting images or text from the internet. The goal is to move away from collecting "large task-specific datasets" for every new robot task.

Prior works have attempted multi-task robot policies, but often with limitations:
- **Gato (Reed et al., 2022):** A generalist agent, but its real-world robotic tasks were limited, primarily focusing on block stacking without extensive evaluation of generalization to new tasks or varied real-world settings. RT-1 directly compares itself to a scaled-down version of Gato.
- **Instruction Following Methods (Shridhar et al., 2021; 2022, Jang et al., 2021 - BC-Z):** While these methods incorporate language conditioning for task specification, they often focus on training tasks rather than robust generalization to novel tasks, objects, or environments, and may attain comparatively lower performance on new scenarios. BC-Z is another key baseline for RT-1.
- **Other Transformer-based policies:** Several works have explored Transformers for robotic control and language-conditioned tasks, but RT-1 aims to push the boundaries of *real-world* application, scale, and computational efficiency for practical deployment.

RT-1 positions itself as a significant step towards achieving truly generalist robotic agents by:
- **Demonstrating scalable model properties:** Showing that high-capacity Transformer-based architectures can indeed "absorb" diverse robotic data.
- **Focusing on real-world generalization:** Providing extensive empirical evidence on complex tasks, unseen objects, varied environments, and robustness to distractors, going beyond simulated or narrowly defined real-world settings.
- **Addressing real-time constraints:** Designing an architecture that is efficient enough for practical real-world robotic control (3 Hz inference).
- **Leveraging diverse data sources:** Showing the ability to integrate data from simulation and even different robot morphologies, a crucial step towards reducing the burden of de novo data collection for every new task or robot.

In essence, RT-1 aims to be robotics' answer to the large-scale pre-trained models that have revolutionized other AI domains, providing a concrete demonstration of their potential benefits in a domain where data scarcity and generalization are paramount challenges.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to develop and validate a general robot learning system capable of learning a wide variety of real-world tasks and generalizing effectively to novel scenarios. This objective is driven by several key motivations:

- **Bridging the Gap from AI Successes to Robotics:** The authors observe the transformative success of large, general models in fields like computer vision and natural language processing, where models trained on vast, diverse datasets exhibit impressive zero-shot or few-shot generalization. They are motivated to achieve a similar breakthrough in robotics.
- **Addressing Data Scarcity and Cost in Robotics:** Unlike other domains where data is abundant and easily collected, robotic data (especially real-world, high-quality demonstration data) is difficult, expensive, and time-consuming to acquire. The ability of a generalist model to transfer knowledge and generalize from limited task-specific data or to new tasks without any specific data is thus "even more critical in robotics."
- **Achieving Real-World Generalization and Robustness:** Existing multi-task robot policies often struggle with generalizing to novel tasks, objects, or environments, and lack robustness to real-world clutter or variations. A core motivation is to develop a model that performs well on a large number of training tasks while also exhibiting superior generalization to unseen instructions, distractors, and backgrounds.
- **Enabling Long-Horizon, Complex Tasks:** For robots to be truly useful in human environments, they must be able to perform sequences of actions to achieve complex goals. The motivation is to create a low-level policy that is robust and capable enough to serve as a strong foundation for higher-level planning systems, such as SayCan.
- **Building High-Capacity, Yet Efficient, Architectures:** Transformers are known for their high capacity, essential for absorbing diverse knowledge. However, their computational demands often hinder real-time control. A key objective is to design a Transformer-based architecture (RT-1) that balances high capacity with the computational efficiency required for deployment on real robots at practical control frequencies (e.g., 3 Hz).
- **Demonstrating Data Absorption Capabilities:** The authors aim to show that RT-1 can effectively "sponge up" experience and integrate data from highly heterogeneous sources, including simulated environments and even different robot morphologies, without performance degradation on original tasks.

In summary, the motivation is to unlock the potential of large-scale, generalist models for real-world robotics, moving beyond niche applications to truly capable and adaptable robotic systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RT-1's methodology integrates several advanced deep learning techniques and a massive, diverse real-world data collection effort to achieve its objectives.

#### 3.1. Data Collection Strategy

- **Scale and Diversity:** The cornerstone of RT-1 is its dataset, collected over 17 months using a fleet of 13 mobile manipulators (Everyday Robots). It comprises approximately 130,000 successful human demonstrations across over 700 distinct language-conditioned tasks.
- **Environment:** Data was collected in "robot classrooms" (office kitchen segments) designed for large-scale collection. Evaluation extended to two real office kitchens with varying lighting, backgrounds, and geometry to test generalization.
- **Task Specification:** Each demonstration is annotated with a natural language instruction (e.g., "pick apple from top drawer"). Tasks are grouped into "skills" (verbs like "pick," "open") and objects (nouns like "apple," "drawer") to ensure diversity.
- **Autonomous Data Collection Setup:** Robots autonomously approached stations and communicated instructions to human operators, who used VR remotes for teleoperation. A software module ensured balanced dataset sampling and scene randomization.

#### 3.2. RT-1 Model Architecture

RT-1 is a Transformer-based policy designed for efficiency and high capacity (35M parameters). It takes a short sequence of images and a natural language instruction as input and outputs discretized actions. The architecture (Figure 1a, 3) consists of:

- **Instruction and Image Tokenization (FiLM EfficientNet-B3):**
 - **Input:** A history of 6 images (300x300 resolution) and a natural language instruction.
 - **Image Processing:** Images are passed through an ImageNet-pretrained EfficientNet-B3, outputting a spatial feature map (9x9x512). This map is flattened into 81 visual tokens.
 - **Language Conditioning (FiLM):** The natural language instruction is first embedded using a Universal Sentence Encoder (pretrained). This embedding conditions the EfficientNet via identity-initialized FiLM (Feature-wise Linear Modulation) layers. This "early language fusion" allows the image encoder to extract task-relevant features from the beginning, improving performance and enabling the model to ignore distractors.
 - **Pre-training Advantage:** ImageNet pre-training provides general visual knowledge, significantly boosting generalization and robustness.

- **TokenLearner (Ryoo et al., 2021):**
 - **Purpose:** To compress the high number of visual tokens (81 from EfficientNet) into a much smaller, more manageable set for the subsequent Transformer.
 - **Mechanism:** An element-wise attention module learns to "soft-select" important image token combinations.
 - **Output:** Reduces 81 visual tokens per image to just 8 tokens. For a history of 6 images, this results in 48 total tokens (8 tokens/image * 6 images).

- **Transformer (Vaswani et al., 2017):**
 - **Type:** A decoder-only sequence model with 8 self-attention layers (19M parameters).
 - **Input:** The 48 vision-language tokens (with added position encoding) from the TokenLearner.
 - **Output:** Discretized action tokens.

- **Action Tokenization:**
 - **Discretization:** Each of the 11 action dimensions (7 for arm: x, y, z, roll, pitch, yaw, gripper opening; 3 for base: x, y, yaw; 1 discrete for mode: arm, base, terminate) is discretized into 256 bins.
 - **Benefit:** This allows the model to represent complex, potentially multi-modal action distributions, unlike continuous Gaussian outputs which assume a single mode.

#### 3.3. Learning and Inference

- **Loss Function:** Standard categorical cross-entropy objective with causal masking (behavioral cloning).
- **Inference Speed Optimization:** Crucial for real-time control (target 3 Hz, i.e., <100ms per action).
 - **TokenLearner:** Reduces the number of tokens the Transformer needs to process.
 - **Token Re-use:** Computes tokens once and reuses them for overlapping future inferences.
 - **Non-Autoregressive Actions:** Unlike some Transformer controllers, RT-1 does not auto-regressively condition on previous actions, which significantly speeds up inference without a performance hit.
- **Closed-Loop Control:** RT-1 performs closed-loop control, commanding actions at 3 Hz until a "terminate" action or time limit.

#### 3.4. Heterogeneous Data Integration

- **Simulation Data:** Explores mixing real demonstration data with simulated data containing novel objects.
- **Multi-Robot Data:** Investigates combining data from different robot morphologies (Everyday Robots mobile manipulators and Kuka IIWA data from QT-Opt). This involves transforming action spaces and relabeling tasks to "pick anything."

This comprehensive approach combines state-of-the-art neural architectures with extensive real-world data and efficiency considerations, specifically tailored for the demands of scalable robotic manipulation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted in the paper extensively evaluate RT-1's performance, generalization, robustness, and ability to absorb diverse data, comparing it against strong baselines (Gato and BC-Z/BC-Z XL). Over 3000 real-world trials were performed, making it one of the largest evaluations of a robot learning system to date.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance, Generalization, and Robustness (Table 2)

- **Seen Tasks:** RT-1 achieves an impressive 97% success rate on over 200 training instructions, significantly outperforming BC-Z (72%) and Gato (65%). This demonstrates its ability to learn a large and diverse set of skills.
- **Unseen Tasks Generalization:** RT-1 shows strong zero-shot generalization, succeeding in 76% of novel, unseen instructions (combinations of seen skills and objects). This is 24% higher than the next best baseline (Gato at 52%).
- **Robustness (Distractors & Backgrounds):** RT-1 exhibits superior robustness:
 - **Distractors:** 83% success rate, 36% higher than BC-Z (47%).
 - **Backgrounds:** 59% success rate, 18% higher than BC-Z (41%).
 This indicates RT-1's ability to focus on relevant objects and ignore irrelevant visual clutter or environmental changes.

#### 4.2. Generalization to Long-Horizon Robotic Scenarios (Table 3 & 6)

- **Realistic Scenarios:** RT-1 is evaluated on 15 complex, multi-step long-horizon tasks in real kitchen environments, using the SayCan framework. These scenarios progressively increase in generalization difficulty (L1: new layout/lighting; L2: new distractors; L3: drastically new settings/objects/locations).
- **Performance:** RT-1 achieves a 70% success rate across all realistic scenarios, far outperforming BC-Z XL (55%) and Gato (30%). Crucially, its manipulation performance (67% execution success) does not visibly drop when moving from Kitchen1 (similar to training) to Kitchen2 (new, more challenging environment), while baselines perform poorly (Gato 0%, BC-Z 13% in Kitchen2). This highlights RT-1's robust generalization for practical deployment.

#### 4.3. Incorporating Heterogeneous Data Sources (Table 4 & 5)

- **Simulation Data (Table 4):**
 - Adding 518k simulated trajectories (with objects unseen in real-world data) does not degrade RT-1's performance on original real tasks (90% vs. 92% for real-only).
 - It significantly *improves* performance on real-world tasks involving objects seen *only* in simulation (23% to 87%) and on unseen skills with simulated objects (7% to 33%). This demonstrates RT-1's impressive domain transfer and data absorption capabilities.
- **Data from Different Robots (Table 5):**
 - Mixing RT-1's Everyday Robots data with 209k Kuka IIWA bin-picking episodes results in only a minimal 2% performance drop on original tasks.
 - Crucially, this mixed-robot model nearly *doubles* performance on a "bin-picking eval" task on the Everyday Robot (39% vs. 22% for EDR-only model). This shows RT-1 can effectively learn from and transfer skills across different robot morphologies.

#### 4.4. Impact of Data Quantity and Diversity (Table 7)

- **Data Diversity is Key:** Ablations show that data diversity has a significantly higher impact on performance and generalization than data quantity.
 - Removing 25% of tasks (making data narrower) while keeping 97% of the data leads to a substantial drop in unseen task generalization (54% vs. 76% for full data) and robustness.
 - In contrast, reducing data quantity by nearly 50% (from 100% to 51%) while maintaining task diversity leads to a smaller drop in generalization (50% vs. 76%).
- This finding suggests that for scaling robot learning, prioritizing broad task coverage and varied scenarios is more beneficial than simply collecting more data for already-seen tasks.

#### 4.5. Model Ablations (Table 13)

- **Discretized Actions:** Switching to continuous Gaussian actions causes a significant performance decline (-29% on seen tasks), indicating that RT-1's per-dimension discretization is crucial for representing complex multi-modal action distributions.
- **ImageNet Pre-training:** Removing ImageNet pre-training dramatically reduces unseen task performance (-33%) and robustness, highlighting the importance of rich visual priors.
- **History:** Including a short image history primarily benefits generalization to distractors.
- **Transformer Component:** Removing the Transformer (e.g., using only EfficientNet features directly) leads to a uniform but small negative impact across performance metrics.
- **Auto-regressive Actions:** Surprisingly, auto-regressively conditioning on actions (as in Gato) did not benefit performance and significantly slowed inference (>2x), leading to its exclusion in the final RT-1 design.
- **Inference Speed:** RT-1 achieves 15ms inference time, enabling 3Hz control, significantly faster than a similarly sized Gato (129ms) but slower than BC-Z (5.3ms).

In summary, RT-1 sets a new benchmark for real-world robot generalization and robustness, demonstrating that large-scale, diverse data combined with a carefully designed, efficient Transformer architecture can unlock capabilities previously unseen in robotic systems.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of RT-1 represents a significant step forward for the field of robot learning, with several profound implications and potential impacts:

- **Paving the Way for Generalist Robotics:** RT-1 provides strong empirical evidence that the "large general model" paradigm, successful in CV and NLP, is viable and highly beneficial for real-world robotics. This marks a shift from narrow, task-specific robot skills towards more general, adaptable, and robust robot intelligence.
- **Reduced Data Dependency for New Tasks:** The impressive zero-shot generalization capabilities of RT-1 mean that future robotic systems might require significantly less task-specific data collection for new variations of known tasks or for novel combinations of existing skills and objects. This addresses a critical bottleneck in robot deployment.
- **Enhanced Robustness to Real-World Variability:** The model's demonstrated robustness to unseen backgrounds, distractors, and varying environments is crucial for moving robots out of controlled lab settings and into dynamic, unpredictable human environments like offices and homes. This is a key requirement for practical, deployable robots.
- **Enabling Complex, Long-Horizon Automation:** By providing a highly reliable and generalized low-level policy, RT-1 significantly enhances the capabilities of higher-level planning systems like SayCan. This allows for the execution of much longer and more complex sequences of tasks, opening up possibilities for more autonomous and intelligent robotic assistants.
- **Leveraging Heterogeneous Data Sources:** The ability of RT-1 to effectively absorb data from simulation and other robot morphologies is a game-changer. This means that valuable data, regardless of its origin, can contribute to improving a single, unified robot policy. This could lead to a virtuous cycle where data from diverse sources (simulated, human teleoperation, other robots, reinforcement learning) collectively improve a generalist model, accelerating progress in robot learning.
- **Blueprint for Scalable Robot Learning:** The paper offers a detailed "recipe" for achieving scalable robot learning, highlighting the critical roles of large, diverse datasets, high-capacity yet efficient architectures, and specific design choices like action tokenization and early language fusion. This provides a clear direction for future research and development in the field.
- **Accelerating Research via Open-Sourcing:** The authors' commitment to open-sourcing the RT-1 code is a major contribution to the research community. This will enable other researchers to build upon this work, replicate findings, and accelerate innovation in scalable robot learning.

**Limitations and Future Work (as stated by authors):**
- **Imitation Learning Constraints:** As an imitation learning method, RT-1 cannot surpass the performance of its human demonstrators or discover completely novel behaviors. Future work might integrate reinforcement learning to overcome this.
- **Limited Generalization to Novel Motions:** Generalization is currently limited to new combinations of *previously seen concepts*. The model cannot yet synthesize completely new motions it has not observed.
- **Dexterity of Tasks:** The current tasks, while diverse, are not highly dexterous. Extending to more intricate manipulation is a future challenge.
- **Further Robustness Improvement:** While robust, further improvements in robustness to backgrounds and environments (requiring even greater data diversity) are planned.
- **Scalable Attention and Memory:** Enhancing reaction speeds and context retention through more scalable attention mechanisms and memory structures is a future direction.
- **Non-Expert Data Collection:** Developing methods for non-experts to train robots and leverage model prompting could scale data collection even faster.

In conclusion, RT-1 is a landmark paper that empirically validates the scaling hypothesis for real-world robotics. It demonstrates that by meticulously addressing data collection at scale, designing efficient high-capacity models, and carefully integrating diverse information, robots can achieve unprecedented levels of generalizability and robustness, laying a strong foundation for the next generation of intelligent, adaptable robotic systems.
