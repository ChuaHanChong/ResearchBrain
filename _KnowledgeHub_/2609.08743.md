# FOCI Policy: Focus on Object-Centric Interactions for Relational Manipulation Policies

> [!summary] Summary
> FOCI Policy is an interaction-centric imitation learning framework for rigid relational manipulation. It automatically extracts short interaction segments from demonstrations via change-point detection, then predicts them as relative SE(3) motion between source and target objects using a two-stage GMM goal predictor plus trajectory decoder. From one demonstration and one camera it reaches 63.0% average success on ten RLBench tasks, beating object-centric and action-centric baselines.

## Problem

- Action-centric imitation policies learn direct observation-to-action mappings, so they need many demonstrations covering diverse object poses and scene configurations even for simple pick-and-place.
- Object-centric methods predicting static goal poses discard interaction dynamics, while dense motion-flow representations are difficult to learn and prone to drift during execution.
- Existing temporal abstractions extract keyframes from end-effector motion or contact heuristics, remaining action-centric and still tied to the observation-action pairs seen during training.

## Method

- Interaction segment extraction: ==PELT change-point detection== on a combined signal of gripper linear speed, angular speed and source-target distance isolates the constrained phase, with one global penalty beta=0.6.
- ==Object-centric SE(3) formulation==: a skill is the source object's pose in the target frame; actions are recovered deterministically by composing predicted relative poses with estimated target pose and grasp offset.
- Two-stage policy: ==PointNet++ encoders== plus ==cross-attention== over source and target clouds fused with CLIP language tokens, a ==GMM goal predictor==, then a ==trajectory decoder== conditioned on that goal.

## Results

- On eight RLBench relational tasks with one demonstration and one camera it hits **96-99%** on phone-on-base, stack-wine and meat-on-grill, beating Imagination Policy, SPOT, 3D Diffuser Actor and RVT2.
- Under COLOSSEUM all-perturbations it reaches **25.3%** versus **20.1%** for BridgeVLA and **21.4%** for ActiveVLA using **1%** of their data; RLBench-18 overall is **43.7%**, **88.2%** with ground-truth poses.
- Ablation: removing interaction segmentation drops average success from **69.6%** to **38.6%**; extracted segments cut cross-demonstration translation and rotation variance by roughly **73-79%** and **90-92%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Constrained interaction phases carry most task structure; discarding high-variance transport motion turns imitation into a low-variance learning target that generalizes from a single demonstration.
> - Relative SE(3) object framing buys embodiment and scene invariance and survives visual perturbation better than large VLAs, but cannot rescue tasks where pose estimation fails.
> - The bottleneck shifts from policy learning to perception: thin, symmetric or occluded geometry breaks canonicalization, and continuous-contact tasks resist clean temporal segmentation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic manipulation policies traditionally learn a direct mapping from observations to robot actions, a paradigm often referred to as "action-centric." While straightforward, these methods typically require extensive data collection across various object poses and scene configurations, limiting their data efficiency and generalization capabilities. This is particularly evident in tasks requiring precise interactions or exhibiting variability in initial conditions.

To address these limitations, a growing body of research has shifted towards "object-centric" policy learning. These approaches leverage intermediate object-level representations, such as goal poses or motion flows, to generate robot actions. While improving data efficiency and cross-embodiment generalization, existing object-centric representations have exhibited their own constraints. Some representations, such as static goal poses, may oversimplify complex interaction dynamics, failing to capture the full scope of constrained motion during manipulation. Conversely, dense motion flows, while detailed, can introduce high complexity, making them challenging to learn efficiently and prone to accumulation of errors over time.

FOCI POLICY situates itself within the object-centric paradigm but proposes a refinement: focusing on "object-object interactions" (OOIs) as a more compact and functionally relevant abstraction. The work observes that many rigid relational manipulation tasks are primarily governed by short, tightly constrained interaction phases. By isolating and modeling these critical interaction segments, FOCI POLICY aims to strike a balance, capturing essential dynamics without the redundant complexity associated with full trajectory or dense flow representations. This approach aligns with broader trends in robotics research seeking to improve few-demonstration imitation learning and leverage temporal abstraction for more robust and generalizable skill acquisition, while also drawing connections to task and motion planning by learning continuous interaction trajectories within skills.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of FOCI POLICY is to develop a robotic manipulation policy that demonstrates improved data efficiency and generalization capabilities, particularly for relational manipulation tasks. This objective is motivated by several identified limitations in current robotic policy learning paradigms:

1. **Data Inefficiency of Action-Centric Policies:** Traditional action-centric policies learn a direct mapping from observations to robot joint commands or end-effector poses. This necessitates a large volume of demonstrations to adequately cover the high-dimensional robot state space and various scene configurations, even for seemingly simple tasks. This data requirement is a significant bottleneck for real-world robotic deployment due to the high cost and time involved in data collection.
2. **Representational Limitations of Existing Object-Centric Policies:** While object-centric approaches improve data efficiency by focusing on object-level representations, they often fall into one of two categories:
 - **Oversimplified Representations:** Methods that predict static object goal poses may fail to capture the dynamic, constrained motion required during precise interactions (e.g., insertion, screwing). The precise path and forces during contact-rich phases are critical but not fully represented by a static goal.
 - **Excessively Dense Representations:** Conversely, approaches that model dense motion flows (e.g., point cloud motion) can introduce unnecessary complexity. Learning and accurately reproducing high-dimensional, continuous motion fields across an entire task can be difficult, susceptible to noise, and prone to drift, especially with limited data.
3. **Lack of Invariance and Generalization:** Many existing policies struggle to generalize across different initial scene configurations, object poses, or robot embodiments. They often implicitly learn scene-specific or robot-specific "muscle memory" rather than transferable skills.

Motivated by these challenges, the authors propose a core insight: **rigid relational manipulation tasks are predominantly governed by short, tightly constrained phases of object-object interaction.** During these "interaction segments," the relative motion between task-relevant objects is critical, while preceding or succeeding transport motions are often less constrained and highly variable.

Based on this insight, FOCI POLICY aims to achieve a two-fold abstraction:
1. **Temporal Abstraction:** Automatically extract compact "interaction segments" from demonstrations, focusing learning on the most functionally critical phases and discarding task-irrelevant variability from unconstrained transport motions.
2. **Spatial Invariance:** Represent these skills as relative SE(3) motion between task-relevant objects. This object-centric formulation aims to yield invariance to variations in scene configurations (e.g., absolute object positions) and robot embodiment, making the learned skills more transferable.

By addressing these points, the key objectives are to enable robust and data-efficient learning of manipulation policies from a minimal number of demonstrations (e.g., a single demonstration), leading to enhanced generalization across novel settings and robot platforms.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

FOCI POLICY is designed as an interaction-centric framework that distills manipulation skills into a compact abstraction by employing both temporal and spatial mechanisms.

The core idea is to isolate interaction segments (τrel) from demonstrations. These segments represent the motion of a "source object" (e.g., the object being manipulated or the gripper) relative to a "target object" (e.g., a reference object or container). Mathematically, given world-frame poses Tsrc(k) and Ttgt(k) for k waypoints, the relative motion is defined as τrel = {(Ttgt(k))⁻¹Tsrc(k)}n k=1. Gripper-object interactions (GOIs) are a special case where the gripper itself acts as the source object. Once an interaction segment τrel is predicted, executable robot actions (desired end-effector trajectories Tdes(k)) are recovered by composing the predicted relative poses with the estimated current target object pose and a grasp offset (Tofs) if applicable (Tdes(k) = Tofs * Trel(k) * Ttgt_estimated).

The overall problem is to learn a conditional policy p(τrel | Psrc, Ptgt, ℓ), where Psrc and Ptgt are point clouds of the source and target objects, and ℓ is a language instruction.

#### 3.1. Identification of Interaction Segments

A critical aspect of FOCI POLICY is the automatic identification of interaction segments from raw demonstrations. The premise is that relational manipulation tasks exhibit a consistent structure: an unconstrained transport phase followed by a tightly constrained interaction phase. The transition between these phases is detectable through changes in kinematic and spatial signals.

The method constructs a multivariate time series ˜S(t) = [˜v(t), ˜ω(t), ˜d(t)]. Here, ˜v(t) and ˜ω(t) represent the normalized linear and angular speeds of the gripper, respectively. ˜d(t) is the normalized distance between the centroids of the source and target objects. All signals are normalized over the entire demonstration horizon.

Interaction segment identification is formulated as a change-point detection (CPD) problem on ˜S(t). The goal time tg is determined by gripper state changes (e.g., closing) or the end of the demonstration. The start time ts is identified as the last change point detected before tg. The PELT algorithm is employed to find an optimal set of change points by minimizing a cost function that balances within-segment variation (measured by an RBF kernel) and the number of change points (controlled by a penalty factor β). A single global β (β=0.6) is calibrated offline across a development set of tasks. This approach enables automatic, task-agnostic extraction of functionally critical interaction intervals.

#### 3.2. Object-Centric Interaction Prediction

The core of FOCI POLICY is a conditional model that maps object-centric observations and language instructions to interaction segments.

- **Data Preparation:** From each demonstration, object poses (Tsrc, Ttgt) are estimated using a pose estimator (FoundationPose) based on object meshes and ground-truth masks. Point clouds are transformed into their canonical frames to achieve scene-invariance. The extracted interaction segments are then uniformly sampled into 'n' waypoints to form τrel. Training samples are (Psrc, Ptgt, ℓ, τrel).

- **Model Architecture:** The model consists of:
 - **Masked Geometric Encoding:** Point clouds Psrc and Ptgt are processed by shared PointNet++ encoders, which map local clusters to feature tokens. During training, a proportion (ϕ=0.4) of these tokens are randomly dropped to enhance robustness to partial observations.
 - **Multi-Modal Fusion:** Cross-attention is applied between the source and target point cloud feature tokens to capture interaction-relevant geometry. A pretrained CLIP-ViT32 text encoder generates language embeddings for ℓ. All feature tokens (source, target, language) are concatenated and fused via a Transformer encoder layer to produce a compact context representation H.
 - **Two-Stage Trajectory Predictor:** This design explicitly models the multi-modal nature of interactions:
 1. **Goal Prediction (Stage 1):** Conditioned on H, a goal predictor models the terminal interaction pose Trel(n) as a Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) with M=5 components. This allows for representing multiple valid interaction modes (e.g., different insertion directions). This stage is optimized via negative log-likelihood loss.
 2. **Trajectory Generation (Stage 2):** A trajectory decoder then generates the intermediate waypoints {Trel(k)}n-1 k=1, conditioned on both H and the sampled terminal goal pose Trel(n). This stage is optimized via a trajectory reconstruction loss, minimizing the L1 distance for translation and 6D rotation representations.

The final objective function is a weighted sum of the goal likelihood loss and the trajectory reconstruction loss. All models are trained using AdamW on a single NVIDIA RTX 2080 Ti GPU. For inference, the predicted trajectory is instantiated in the world frame using estimated object poses, and executed by a motion planner (cuRobo) to generate collision-free paths.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of FOCI POLICY addressed four research questions across various simulation and real-world benchmarks.

#### 4.1. RQ1: Data-Efficient Learning on Relational Manipulation Tasks

Experiments on 8 RLBench tasks with clear OOIs (e.g., insertion, articulated manipulation, placement under clutter) demonstrated that FOCI POLICY achieved strong performance with substantially less training data.
- **Performance:** FOCI POLICY (trained with 1 or 5 demonstrations) consistently outperformed prior object-centric (Imagination Policy, SPOT) and action-centric (3D Diffuser Actor, RVT-2) baselines, even when baselines used 5 demonstrations and multi-view observations while FOCI POLICY used a single camera.
- **Precision-Critical Tasks:** On tasks requiring high precision like Put-Roll and Put-Umbrella, FOCI POLICY showed a notable advantage, indicating the effectiveness of its interaction-centric representation for capturing constrained dynamics.
- **Efficiency:** The method required less than 0.5 hours of training time, further supporting its data-efficient nature. These results suggest that interaction-centric representations provide an effective inductive bias for relational manipulation tasks where OOIs are reliably extractable.

#### 4.2. RQ2: Robustness Under Severe Visual Distribution Shifts

Evaluation on 6 COLOSSEUM tasks, which introduce 12 categories of unseen visual perturbations (e.g., texture, lighting, distractors, camera pose changes), assessed the method's robustness.
- **Comparison with VLAs:** While state-of-the-art 3D VLA models (BridgeVLA, ActiveVLA) trained on 100 demonstrations achieved higher nominal performance, FOCI POLICY (trained on 1 demonstration) achieved over 60% of their absolute performance.
- **Outperformance in Severe Perturbations:** Under the most severe perturbation setting ("All Perturbations"), FOCI POLICY outperformed both VLA models (25.3% vs. 20.1%/21.4%). This suggests that the SE(3) object-centric representations offer a degree of robustness to severe visual distribution shifts, compensating for the significantly lower data volume.

#### 4.3. RQ3: Generalization Beyond Tasks with Clearly Structured OOIs

FOCI POLICY was benchmarked on the broader RLBench-18 suite, categorizing tasks by interaction segment grounding difficulty (easy-to-ground vs. hard-to-ground).
- **Easy-to-Ground Tasks:** On tasks with distinctive objects and identifiable contact events, FOCI POLICY achieved 79.6% success with a single demonstration, outperforming Imagination Policy.
- **Hard-to-Ground Tasks:** Performance dropped to 7.8% on tasks involving thin, symmetric, or heavily occluded objects. However, when provided with ground-truth object poses, success rates on these hard-to-ground tasks rose to 78.1%. This indicated that the primary bottleneck was reliable interaction grounding (object canonicalization and pose estimation) rather than the interaction-centric formulation itself.
- **Overall:** The method achieved an average success rate of 43.7% across RLBench-18 tasks in the one-demonstration setting, demonstrating effectiveness beyond tasks with explicitly clear OOIs, while highlighting the need for robust perception.

#### 4.4. RQ4: Effectiveness Under Real-World Perceptual Uncertainty and Execution Noise

FOCI POLICY was deployed on a Franka Emika Panda robot for five real-world tasks (e.g., tool use, precise insertion, pouring, articulated manipulation) with increasing pose variation.
- **Real-World Performance:** The method achieved over 53% task success across all tasks with a single demonstration, outperforming the Replay Policy baseline on all five tasks. The advantage was more pronounced in precision-critical tasks (Insert-Tube, Pour-Liquid).
- **Rapid Skill Acquisition:** The entire pipeline, from kinesthetic demonstration to real-robot deployment, was demonstrated to be achievable in under 10 minutes for a new skill.
- **Failure Analysis:** Analysis of real-world failures indicated that 54% were due to pose estimation errors, 27% to motion planning failures (e.g., unfavorable grasp configurations limiting feasible trajectories), and 19% to grasp instability. This reinforced the conclusion from RQ3 that perceptual reliability, particularly object pose estimation under challenging conditions (e.g., thin objects, occlusion, symmetry), is a key area for future improvement.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies

- **Interaction Segment Modeling:** Removing automatic segmentation or using fixed-length segments resulted in substantial performance drops, especially on precision-sensitive tasks (e.g., Put-Umbrella). This confirms the criticality of accurately identifying interaction segments.
- **Two-Stage Architecture:** The two-stage (GMM goal + trajectory decoder) architecture consistently outperformed a one-stage trajectory predictor, indicating the benefit of explicitly modeling the terminal goal for relational manipulation.
- **Pose Features and Masking:** Incorporating explicit pose features into the model offered limited additional benefit, suggesting that the object-centric formulation primarily relies on local geometric relationships from point clouds. Random masking of point cloud tokens improved robustness on most tasks, acting as a regularization technique.

#### 4.6. Additional Findings (from Appendix)

- **Cross-Demonstration Variability Reduction:** Interaction segments were shown to significantly reduce cross-demonstration translation and rotation variance (average 78.6%/90.5% for GOI, 73.4%/92.3% for OOI) compared to full trajectories, validating the temporal abstraction.
- **Generalizability and Robustness:** FOCI POLICY demonstrated generalization to unseen object instances within the same category (e.g., different cups), robustness to cluttered scenes and partial observations (leveraging object segmentation and pose estimation), and inherent support for obstacle avoidance through integration with external motion planners.
- **Cross-Embodiment Transfer:** The learned object-centric interaction representation enabled zero-shot transfer to different robot grippers, highlighting its independence from robot embodiment.
- **Robot-Free Demonstrations:** The method showed potential for learning from human video demonstrations by extracting hand poses, demonstrating a pathway to leveraging weak, robot-free data.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

FOCI POLICY contributes to the field of robotic manipulation by introducing an interaction-centric framework that addresses key challenges in data efficiency and generalization. The work demonstrates the effectiveness of abstracting manipulation skills through a two-fold approach: temporally, by automatically extracting compact interaction segments, and spatially, by representing skills as relative SE(3) motion between task-relevant objects.

The primary significance of this research lies in its empirical demonstration that, for rigid relational manipulation tasks, effective policies can be learned with substantially less training data—specifically, from a single demonstration in many cases—compared to prevalent action-centric or dense object-centric methods. This data efficiency has substantial implications for reducing the cost and time associated with deploying new skills on robots in real-world environments.

The proposed interaction-centric representation, focusing on the constrained phases of object-object interaction, offers several potential impacts:
- **Improved Generalization:** By modeling interactions relative to objects, FOCI POLICY naturally yields invariance to scene configurations and robot embodiment, making learned skills more transferable to novel environments and platforms. This is a crucial step towards more versatile and adaptable robots.
- **Enhanced Robustness:** The method's performance under severe visual perturbations and real-world uncertainties suggests that explicitly modeling object-object relationships provides a robust inductive bias, enabling generalization beyond the specific training distribution.
- **Bridging Perception and Action:** FOCI POLICY integrates advanced perception modules (object segmentation, pose estimation) with a learning framework to bridge the gap between raw sensory observations and executable robot actions. This highlights the importance of reliable perception for abstract, object-centric control.
- **Rapid Skill Acquisition:** The capability to learn and deploy new skills in under 10 minutes in real-world settings could accelerate the development and deployment of robotic applications, particularly in dynamic or customized manufacturing and service robotics contexts.

While the framework assumes that task-relevant interaction phases are temporally separable and geometrically identifiable, and its performance is sensitive to the reliability of upstream object segmentation and pose estimation modules, these limitations delineate clear avenues for future research. Future work focusing on reconstruction-based or zero-shot pose estimation, extending to deformable objects, and improving interaction mode coverage from limited demonstrations could further broaden the applicability and robustness of this interaction-centric paradigm. Overall, FOCI POLICY offers a valuable contribution to the development of more intelligent, adaptable, and deployable robotic manipulation systems.
