# World Model Robustness via Surprise Recognition

> [!summary] Summary
> Georgia Institute of Technology researchers developed a surprise-driven filtering framework that enhances the robustness of world model-based reinforcement learning agents by leveraging the world model's intrinsic measure of "surprise" to identify and mitigate the impact of noise. This approach maintains agent performance across various sensor failures and noise types, enabling smaller models to operate more reliably in dynamic real-world environments.

## Problem

- AI systems frequently encounter unpredictable noise, distractions, and out-of-distribution (OOD) observations in real-world environments, leading to unreliable or unsafe behaviors.
- Current robust training methods are limited by the impossibility of preparing for all possible OOD conditions, particularly for world model-based agents.
- A lack of effective online strategies to identify and adapt to compromised sensory inputs, without relying on redundant sensors or extensive pre-training on all noise types, hinders AI deployment in safety-critical domains.

## Method

- The framework quantifies "surprise" using ==Bayesian surprise== (KL divergence between posterior and prior latent states) to detect noisy or OOD observations.
- A multi-sensor strategy employs ==surprise-guided rejection sampling== to select uncorrupted input subsets and uses ==multi-representation dropout== training to prepare the world model for missing inputs.
- A single-sensor strategy utilizes a ==rejection sampling state machine==, switching to internal world model predictions when an observation is too corrupted to trust, and uses reconstruction loss for rejection scoring.

## Results

- The **Confident Representation** method achieved the highest robustness across various simulated sensor failures in CARLA and Safety Gymnasium, preserving performance relative to baselines.
- The multi-sensor selection method efficiently identified and "shut off" failed sensors with **O(n log n)** complexity, matching the performance of computationally intensive exhaustive searches.
- Single-sensor rejection sampling consistently achieved the highest scores in CARLA under heavy noise and improved generation quality by approximately **7%** in a robot pouring task with the diffusion-based **Cosmos** world model.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A world model's intrinsic Bayesian surprise measure provides a reliable, self-contained signal for identifying and mitigating the impact of unexpected noise or out-of-distribution data.
> - Adaptive strategies, like selectively rejecting corrupted sensor inputs or relying on internal model predictions, enable graceful degradation and maintain policy stability without needing external detectors.
> - Training world models with multi-representation dropout significantly enhances their robustness and ability to infer consistent latent dynamics under partial or corrupted observations.
