# World Model Robustness via Surprise Recognition

> [!summary] Summary
> Georgia Institute of Technology researchers developed a surprise-driven filtering framework that enhances the robustness of world model-based reinforcement learning agents by leveraging the world model's intrinsic measure of "surprise" to identify and mitigate the impact of noise. This approach maintains agent performance across various sensor failures and noise types, enabling smaller models to operate more reliably in dynamic real-world environments.

## Problem

- AI systems frequently encounter unpredictable noise, distractions, and out-of-distribution (OOD) observations in real-world environments, leading to unreliable or unsafe behaviors.
- Current robust training methods are limited by the impossibility of preparing for all possible OOD conditions, particularly for world model-based agents.
- A lack of effective online strategies to identify and adapt to compromised sensory inputs, without relying on redundant sensors or extensive pre-training on all noise types, hinders AI deployment in safety-critical domains.

## Method

- The framework quantifies "surprise" using ==Bayesian surprise== (KL divergence between posterior and prior latent states) to detect noisy or OOD observations.
- A multi-sensor strategy employs ==surprise-guided rejection sampling== to select uncorrupted input subsets and uses ==multi-representation dropout== training to prepare the world model for missing inputs.
- A single-sensor strategy utilizes a ==rejection sampling state machine==, switching to internal world model predictions when an observation is too corrupted to trust, and uses reconstruction loss for rejection scoring.

## Results

- The **Confident Representation** method achieved the highest robustness across various simulated sensor failures in CARLA and Safety Gymnasium, preserving performance relative to baselines.
- The multi-sensor selection method efficiently identified and "shut off" failed sensors with **O(n log n)** complexity, matching the performance of computationally intensive exhaustive searches.
- Single-sensor rejection sampling consistently achieved the highest scores in CARLA under heavy noise and improved generation quality by approximately **7%** in a robot pouring task with the diffusion-based **Cosmos** world model.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A world model's intrinsic Bayesian surprise measure provides a reliable, self-contained signal for identifying and mitigating the impact of unexpected noise or out-of-distribution data.
> - Adaptive strategies, like selectively rejecting corrupted sensor inputs or relying on internal model predictions, enable graceful degradation and maintain policy stability without needing external detectors.
> - Training world models with multi-representation dropout significantly enhances their robustness and ability to infer consistent latent dynamics under partial or corrupted observations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The deployment of Artificial Intelligence (AI) systems in real-world environments frequently encounters challenges associated with noise, distractions, environmental interference, transmission errors, and out-of-distribution (OOD) observations. These unpredictable elements can destabilize AI policies, leading to unreliable or unsafe behaviors. Current approaches to enhance the robustness of AI systems often involve robust training methods, which attempt to anticipate and mitigate various forms of noise. However, it is infeasible to prepare for all possible OOD conditions that an AI system might encounter in complex, dynamic environments.

Within the field of reinforcement learning (RL), particularly with world model-based agents, research has explored methods to improve resilience. Existing strategies include procedural generation and data augmentation, which aim to broaden an agent's experience but can incur high sample complexity. Other work focuses on identifying irrelevant observation components using foundation models or Siamese architectures, which may increase training time or require augmented datasets for specific OOD scenarios. Dropout techniques, where certain sensors or modalities are masked, have also been investigated, but these typically assume prior knowledge of which sensors have failed or do not provide clear strategies for online identification of corrupted inputs.

World models, which learn internal representations of environmental dynamics, have demonstrated some capacity for handling OOD scenarios. Specifically, the concept of "surprise" within a world model has been identified as a potential heuristic for detecting OOD observations. This paper builds upon this insight, positioning itself as a method to proactively utilize a world model's inherent surprise mechanism to robustly handle unknown sensor failures and noise without requiring redundant sensors or extensive pre-training on all possible noise types. It differentiates itself from traditional Fault Detection, Isolation, Recovery (FDIR) techniques by not relying on redundant sensors, but rather on distinct data representations from different sensors or a single sensor. The work aims to provide a more adaptive and online approach to maintaining policy stability when faced with unexpected data corruption.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the inherent vulnerability of AI systems to unexpected noise and out-of-distribution (OOD) conditions when operating in the real world. Such conditions, which can include sensor failures, environmental interference, or novel object classes, can render AI policies unpredictable, unreliable, or unsafe. Current robust training paradigms are limited by the impossibility of anticipating all potential forms of data corruption, leading to a critical need for AI systems that can degrade gracefully and make informed decisions even with compromised inputs.

The paper identifies two fundamental capabilities required for robust AI behavior in the presence of noise:
1. **Identification of Noise**: The ability to discern which parts of an observation are likely noise versus actionable information.
2. **Adaptive Decision-Making**: The capacity to make appropriate decisions despite a lack of complete or reliable actionable information.

Building on these motivations, the key objectives of this work are:

- **Leverage World Model Surprise**: To develop an algorithm that utilizes a world model's intrinsic measure of "surprise" as a signal to identify and mitigate the impact of noise in world model-based reinforcement learning agents. The hypothesis is that high surprise indicates OOD data or corruption.
- **Introduce Rejection Sampling Variants**: To design and implement both multi-representation and single-representation rejection sampling algorithms.
 - **Multi-representation rejection sampling** targets scenarios where an agent possesses multiple sensors or multiple representations of sensory data, enabling the system to identify and disable corrupted inputs from a subset of sensors.
 - **Single-representation rejection sampling** addresses situations where only a single sensor input is available, allowing the agent to determine if the observation is too corrupted to trust, and if so, to rely on its internal world model predictions instead.
- **Preserve Agent Performance under Noise**: To demonstrate that the proposed techniques can maintain agent performance, relative to baselines, under varying types and levels of noise. The goal is to show that agents can continue to operate effectively despite significant data corruption, rather than experiencing substantial performance degradation.
- **Enhance Stability of State-of-the-Art World Models**: To validate the robustness and generalizability of the proposed methods by applying them to world models with different underlying architectures, specifically DreamerV3 (VAE-based Recurrent State-Space Model) and Cosmos (diffusion-based video world model). This objective aims to demonstrate the broad applicability of the surprise-recognition concept across diverse world modeling paradigms.
- **Contribute to Safe AI Deployment**: Ultimately, the research aims to contribute towards building more stable and safe AI systems for real-world deployment, particularly in safety-critical domains like autonomous driving.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the proposed methodology is to leverage a world model's inherent "surprise" signal to identify and manage noisy or out-of-distribution (OOD) observations. The approach is bifurcated into strategies for multi-sensor and single-sensor settings, supported by a specialized training regimen and evaluated across diverse environments and world model architectures.

#### 3.1. Surprise Measure

The central concept of "surprise" is quantified using the Bayesian surprise measure, defined as the Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence between the world model's learned posterior distribution and its prior distribution of the next latent state: $KL[q_{\phi}(z_t | h_t, x_t) || p_{\phi}(\hat{z}_t | h_t)]$. This measure is hypothesized to correlate with unexpected or noisy observations.

#### 3.2. Multi-Sensor Representation Selection

This setting applies when an agent receives inputs from multiple distinct sensors (e.g., cameras, LiDAR) or processes a single sensor's data into multiple representations (e.g., pixel-level image, semantic map).

- **Surprise-Guided Multi-Representation Rejection Sampling**: At each time step, the agent samples a set of sensory observations. If any subset of these inputs exhibits abnormally high surprise, indicating potential corruption, the model avoids relying on the full fused observation. Instead, it selects a latent state prediction conditioned on a *subset* of uncorrupted inputs. This selection is formulated as an optimization problem to find the input subset that minimizes the Bayesian surprise. The chosen latent state is then used to select an action. Algorithm 2 details this process, which runs in O(n log n) computational cost during inference, where n is the number of sensors/representations.
- **Multi-Representation Dropout Training (Algorithm 1)**: To prepare the world model for missing or corrupted sensory inputs, a mask-based sensor dropout strategy is employed during training. This exposes the agent to random subspaces of the full representation space by randomly masking out some representations. This encourages the world model to learn robust latent distributions that are less sensitive to the absence of specific sensory components.

#### 3.3. Single Sensor Representation Selection

In scenarios where the agent only receives a single high-dimensional observation, the methodology categorizes inputs into three types: clean ($x_t$), lightly noisy ($x_{light}$), and heavily noisy ($x_{heavy}$).

- **Rejection Sampling State Machine**: The agent switches between two modes during inference:
 - **Predictive Mode**: If an observation is classified as $x_{heavy}$, the agent rejects it and instead relies on its internally predicted next state from the world model. This prevents corrupted input from destabilizing the policy.
 - **Ground-Truth Mode**: If an observation is classified as $x_t$ or $x_{light}$ (after optional denoising D(x\*)), the agent accepts it and uses it for decision-making.
- **Rejection Score**: A function $M(x^*)$ assigns a score to the observation, which is rejected if it exceeds a threshold $\tau$. For experiments, $M(\cdot)$ is defined as an expected reconstruction loss of the unconditioned observation, and $D(\cdot)$ is the predicted posterior representation generated by the world model's variational autoencoder component. The threshold $\tau$ is set empirically based on standard deviations from average reconstruction loss in a normal environment.
- **Context Alignment**: To manage potential drift when in predictive mode, the agent performs a "context reset" upon re-entering ground-truth mode, realigning its latent representation with the accepted observation. When an observation is rejected in ground-truth mode, the agent retains its most recent valid internal context as a residual to maintain coherent belief evolution.

#### 3.4. World Models and Environments

- **World Models**:
 - **DreamerV3**: A state-of-the-art Recurrent State-Space Model (RSSM) based on a Variational Autoencoder (VAE), used for most multi-sensor and single-sensor experiments.
 - **Cosmos Predict 2.5**: A diffusion-based video world model, used to verify the generalization of the single-sensor rejection sampling mechanism to pixel-space prediction models.
- **Environments**:
 - **CARLA**: An open-source urban driving simulator, used for self-driving tasks involving multiple realistic sensors (Lidar, camera, BEV, etc.).
 - **Safety Gymnasium**: A benchmark suite for evaluating safety-performance trade-offs in RL, involving robotic control and navigation tasks with cost signals for unsafe behaviors.
 - **Crafter**: A procedurally generated environment, briefly explored for semantic noise experiments.
- **Noise Types**: Various structured and unstructured OOD noises are injected into sensor data, including Gaussian, occlusion, glare, jitter, chromatic aberration (chrome), and latency, at varying intensities and proportions. The agent is never explicitly notified about the nature of the noise.

#### 3.5. Baselines for Comparison

- **Base**: World model agent trained normally without any robustness techniques.
- **Augmented**: Base model trained on a dataset augmented with Gaussian noise.
- **Random Masked Encoder (RME)**: A technique employing mask-based sensor dropout but without a mechanism to decide which sensors to mask.
- **Median Filtering (Filter)**: A common image processing technique for noise reduction.
- **Hybrid RSSM (HRSSM)**: Augments the RSSM framework to capture task-relevant dynamics while suppressing noise.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several key findings across different settings and world model architectures, demonstrating the effectiveness of surprise recognition for enhancing world model robustness.

#### 4.1. Multi-Sensor Representation Selection (DreamerV3 in CARLA and Safety Gymnasium)

- **Consistent Robustness**: The "Confident Representation" method (multi-sensor rejection sampling with dropout training) consistently achieved the highest level of robustness across various simulated sensor failures in both the Safety Gymnasium and CARLA environments.
- **Performance Preservation**: While noise typically degrades agent performance, Confident Representation preserved performance relative to baselines under varying types and levels of noise. In Safety Gymnasium, it maintained a competitive score-to-cost ratio, indicating safer task accomplishment in failure scenarios (Figure 3).
- **Identification of Faulty Sensors**: In CARLA, where up to five sensor failures were simulated, the Confident Representation method demonstrated the ability to identify and effectively "shut off" failed sensors during inference. As more sensors failed, the agent maintained high rewards by utilizing meaningful signals from uncorrupted representations (Figure 4).
- **Efficiency**: The O(n log n) surprise-guided sensor selection technique (Algorithm 2) achieved performance similar to an exhaustive 2^N subset search, despite significantly lower computational complexity (Figure 9). This indicates that the surprise signal provides a reliable ranking for sensor utility, making combinatorial search unnecessary.
- **Generalization Across Noise Types**: The method showed effective mitigation against diverse visual corruptions, including Chromatic Aberration, Jitter, Glare, Gaussian noise, and Occlusion (Figure 6).

#### 4.2. Single-Sensor Representation Selection (DreamerV3 in CARLA and Cosmos in Robot Pouring Task)

- **Improved Performance under Heavy Corruption**: In the CARLA Four-Lane task with a single sensor, the Rejection Sampling method consistently achieved the highest scores across all tested noise types. It maintained robustness as both the proportion and intensity of noise increased, notably avoiding the decline toward negative scores observed in other methods under high noise levels (Figure 6).
- **Robustness Across World Model Architectures**: The rejection-based filtering mechanism generalized effectively to the Cosmos Predict 2.5 diffusion-based video world model.
 - **Enhanced Generation Quality**: In the Robot Pouring Task, Rejection Sampling improved the average PAI quality score by approximately 7% overall when the input video was corrupted. It brought the performance of generations under various augmentation types to a similar, improved level (around 0.81), regardless of their initial degradation severity (Table 1). This suggests the ability to suppress artifact propagation even in Transformer-based video world models.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies and Further Insights

- **Representation Dropout Training**: Ablation studies confirmed that multi-representation dropout training (Algorithm 1) during the world model training phase significantly enhances the stability and robustness of the model. Dropout-trained agents achieved comparable or slightly higher final scores with smoother and more stable learning dynamics, showing reduced variance and improved ability to infer consistent latent dynamics under partial observations (Figure 16).
- **Alternative Rejection Scores and Denoising Methods**: Experiments with alternative rejection scores (Bayesian surprise, trained ResNet-18 classifier) and denoising methods (prior interpolation, Restormer) showed varying effectiveness (Tables 3 and 4). The chosen expected reconstruction loss and posterior representation for $M(x^*)$ and $D(x^*)$ in the main results generally demonstrated strong performance, reinforcing the choice of internal world model mechanisms.
- **Semantic Noise (Crafter)**: Preliminary experiments in the Crafter domain, exploring semantic noise (e.g., hidden enemies, novel colors), suggested that agents equipped with the multi-sensor selection algorithm could exhibit improved stability. This hints at the potential for applying the framework beyond purely sensory corruption to more abstract semantic shifts (Figure 12, 13).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research addresses a fundamental challenge in the reliable deployment of AI systems: maintaining performance and safety in the face of unpredictable, out-of-distribution (OOD) noise and sensor failures. The proposed surprise-driven filtering framework offers a novel and practical approach to enhancing the robustness of world model-based reinforcement learning agents.

The significance of this work stems from several aspects:

- **Enhanced Real-World Safety and Reliability**: By enabling AI systems to proactively identify and mitigate the impact of corrupted sensory inputs, the framework contributes to safer and more reliable operation in complex, dynamic real-world environments. This is particularly critical for safety-sensitive applications such as autonomous driving, where sensor failures can have severe consequences.
- **Leveraging Intrinsic World Model Uncertainty**: The method innovatively utilizes the world model's inherent measure of "surprise" (Bayesian surprise) as a self-contained mechanism for detecting anomalies. This eliminates the need for external detectors or extensive manual annotation of noise types, making the system more autonomous and adaptable to unforeseen forms of corruption.
- **Generalizability Across Architectures and Settings**: The demonstrated effectiveness across two distinct world model architectures (VAE-based DreamerV3 and diffusion-based Cosmos) and different operational settings (multi-sensor and single-sensor) suggests broad applicability. This indicates that the core concept of surprise recognition is not limited to specific model designs but can generalize to various forms of world modeling.
- **Computational Efficiency**: The O(n log n) complexity for multi-sensor selection (Algorithm 2) provides a computationally efficient alternative to exhaustive search, making it feasible for real-time deployment in systems with multiple sensory inputs.
- **Adaptive Behavior**: The ability of agents to adaptively switch between relying on observed data and their internal world model predictions, or to selectively disable corrupted sensors, represents a step towards more intelligent and resilient AI behavior. This adaptive capability allows for graceful degradation rather than catastrophic failure.
- **Foundation for Future Research**: The work opens several avenues for future research, including:
 - Further exploration into the properties of surprise signals for diverse OOD conditions.
 - Integration with more advanced denoising techniques and adaptive thresholding mechanisms.
 - Extension to handle more complex semantic noise and abstract domain shifts, as hinted by the preliminary Crafter results.
 - Investigation into optimizing the trade-off between conservatism (relying on internal predictions) and responsiveness (trusting denoised observations) in safety-critical scenarios.

Overall, this research provides a robust and generalizable framework for improving the resilience of AI systems, particularly those built on world models, against unexpected environmental and sensor challenges. Its impact lies in paving the way for more dependable and safer AI deployment across various real-world applications.
