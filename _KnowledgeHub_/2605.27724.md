# HumanoidMimicGen: Data Generation for Loco-Manipulation via Whole-Body Planning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from NVIDIA and The University of Texas at Austin developed HumanoidMimicGen, a method for generating large-scale synthetic data for humanoid loco-manipulation by adapting limited human demonstrations through whole-body planning. This approach enables training policies that achieve an average policy success rate of 0.89 on a new 9-task simulation benchmark and improve real-world humanoid performance by 20% through sim-and-real co-training.

## Problem

- Training advanced Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for humanoids is hindered by extreme data scarcity for complex, high-degrees-of-freedom loco-manipulation tasks, which are difficult and costly to collect via teleoperation.
- Existing automated data generation tools for robot manipulators are unsuitable for humanoids as they cannot account for whole-body dynamics, dynamic stability, and contact-rich interactions during simultaneous locomotion and manipulation.
- The lack of large, accessible humanoid loco-manipulation datasets limits systematic studies into how data quality, randomization, and policy learning architectures impact VLA model performance and generalization for humanoids.

## Method

- **HumanoidMimicGen** generates thousands of diverse loco-manipulation demonstrations for humanoids by adapting a small set of human-collected, object-centric whole-body skills to new states and object poses through structured ==skill planning==.
- The method uses a ==hierarchical hybrid action space==, integrating a reinforcement-learned locomotion controller (Homie) for base motion and balance with a joint-space controller for upper-body manipulation, enabling decoupled planning for dynamic and static stability.
- It incorporates motion noise (Gaussian perturbations to robot actions) and initialization randomization (perturbing initial robot and object poses) during data generation to enhance the robustness and generalization of trained policies.

## Results

- Policies trained on 1,000 HumanoidMimicGen demonstrations achieved an average Policy Success Rate (PSR) of **0.89** across nine diverse loco-manipulation tasks on the new G1 simulation benchmark, significantly outperforming baselines (e.g., **0.33** for an extended DexMimicGen+ baseline).
- Co-training with HumanoidMimicGen synthetic data boosted real-world humanoid performance by improving the average policy score from **0.51** (real-only training) to **0.71** (co-training) across four physical tasks.
- Ablation studies confirmed the importance of randomization, showing that removing motion noise or initialization randomization during data generation reduced the mean policy success rate from **0.89** to **0.49** and **0.51** respectively.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Both trajectory-level motion noise and state-level initialization randomization are critical components for generating robust training data, with ablations showing a significant reduction in policy success rates without them.
> - The hybrid control space, combining learned locomotion with classical whole-body inverse kinematics and motion planning, effectively addresses the challenges of high-dimensional, contact-rich humanoid loco-manipulation tasks.
> - High-quality synthetic data generated by HumanoidMimicGen can substantially augment limited real-world data, leading to notable improvements in policy performance on real humanoid robots through sim-and-real co-training.
