# HumanoidMimicGen: Data Generation for Loco-Manipulation via Whole-Body Planning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from NVIDIA and The University of Texas at Austin developed HumanoidMimicGen, a method for generating large-scale synthetic data for humanoid loco-manipulation by adapting limited human demonstrations through whole-body planning. This approach enables training policies that achieve an average policy success rate of 0.89 on a new 9-task simulation benchmark and improve real-world humanoid performance by 20% through sim-and-real co-training.

## Problem

- Training advanced Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for humanoids is hindered by extreme data scarcity for complex, high-degrees-of-freedom loco-manipulation tasks, which are difficult and costly to collect via teleoperation.
- Existing automated data generation tools for robot manipulators are unsuitable for humanoids as they cannot account for whole-body dynamics, dynamic stability, and contact-rich interactions during simultaneous locomotion and manipulation.
- The lack of large, accessible humanoid loco-manipulation datasets limits systematic studies into how data quality, randomization, and policy learning architectures impact VLA model performance and generalization for humanoids.

## Method

- **HumanoidMimicGen** generates thousands of diverse loco-manipulation demonstrations for humanoids by adapting a small set of human-collected, object-centric whole-body skills to new states and object poses through structured ==skill planning==.
- The method uses a ==hierarchical hybrid action space==, integrating a reinforcement-learned locomotion controller (Homie) for base motion and balance with a joint-space controller for upper-body manipulation, enabling decoupled planning for dynamic and static stability.
- It incorporates motion noise (Gaussian perturbations to robot actions) and initialization randomization (perturbing initial robot and object poses) during data generation to enhance the robustness and generalization of trained policies.

## Results

- Policies trained on 1,000 HumanoidMimicGen demonstrations achieved an average Policy Success Rate (PSR) of **0.89** across nine diverse loco-manipulation tasks on the new G1 simulation benchmark, significantly outperforming baselines (e.g., **0.33** for an extended DexMimicGen+ baseline).
- Co-training with HumanoidMimicGen synthetic data boosted real-world humanoid performance by improving the average policy score from **0.51** (real-only training) to **0.71** (co-training) across four physical tasks.
- Ablation studies confirmed the importance of randomization, showing that removing motion noise or initialization randomization during data generation reduced the mean policy success rate from **0.89** to **0.49** and **0.51** respectively.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Both trajectory-level motion noise and state-level initialization randomization are critical components for generating robust training data, with ablations showing a significant reduction in policy success rates without them.
> - The hybrid control space, combining learned locomotion with classical whole-body inverse kinematics and motion planning, effectively addresses the challenges of high-dimensional, contact-rich humanoid loco-manipulation tasks.
> - High-quality synthetic data generated by HumanoidMimicGen can substantially augment limited real-world data, leading to notable improvements in policy performance on real humanoid robots through sim-and-real co-training.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research addresses challenges within the field of robotic control, particularly concerning humanoid robots and Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. VLA models have demonstrated capabilities in robotic manipulation, primarily in stationary settings where the robot's base does not move. However, extending this paradigm to humanoid robots, which are designed to navigate and interact in human environments, presents significant difficulties.

One key challenge is the requirement for large-scale datasets to train capable VLA models. For stationary manipulators, collecting such datasets through teleoperation is already a demanding and time-intensive process. This difficulty is amplified for humanoids due to their high degrees of freedom (DoF) and the need for whole-body coordination to maintain balance during simultaneous locomotion and manipulation (loco-manipulation). Consequently, large-scale loco-manipulation datasets are not readily available, making VLA training for humanoids prohibitive.

Simulation and synthetic data generation have emerged as a method to overcome this data scarcity. Existing automated data generation tools, such as MimicGen, MoMaGen, and WBCMimicGen, have shown promise for generating datasets for fixed-base or mobile manipulators. However, these methods typically assume stable and independent end-effector control per limb and operate in task space, which does not directly translate to legged humanoids where dynamic stability and careful whole-body coordination are crucial. Task and Motion Planning (TAMP) algorithms have also been explored for data generation but often require explicit planning models that are challenging for contact-rich humanoid tasks.

Learning manipulation from demonstrations, often via Behavior Cloning (BC), is a prominent approach. While effective for robot manipulation, its success hinges on high-quality, large-scale datasets. Recent work has shown that synthetic data can augment or replace real-world demonstrations for training effective real-world policies.

Concurrently, research into humanoid loco-manipulation has a history in classical planning and control systems, focusing on integrating dynamic walking with whole-body motion planning, multi-modal planning, and multi-contact planning. More recent efforts, like Homie, HOVER, SONIC, and OmniRetarget, combine teleoperation with reinforcement learning to develop whole-body control frameworks. This paper builds on this foundation by applying imitation learning for policy training, thereby seeking to avoid the complexities of reward tuning inherent in reinforcement learning.

HumanoidMimicGen positions itself as a data generation method specifically tailored for the complexities of humanoid loco-manipulation, aiming to bridge the gap between existing data generation techniques for manipulators and the unique requirements of humanoids. It seeks to enable systematic studies into the impact of data quality and policy learning decisions on humanoid performance, an area where lack of data has previously limited comprehensive investigation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a method for generating extensive and high-quality synthetic data for humanoid legged loco-manipulation. This data is intended for training Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, thereby addressing the significant challenges associated with deploying such advanced robotic control paradigms on complex humanoid platforms.

The motivation for this work stems from several critical factors:

- **Potential of Humanoid Robots:** Humanoid robots possess a human-like form factor, which is anticipated to enable them to move and interact fluidly within environments designed for human use. Realizing this potential requires robust and generalizable control capabilities that can handle both locomotion and intricate manipulation tasks.
- **Data Scarcity for Humanoid Loco-Manipulation:** Training advanced VLA models necessitates large-scale datasets. For even stationary robotic manipulators, collecting such datasets via teleoperation is a demanding and costly endeavor, requiring substantial human time and effort. This challenge is compounded for humanoids, where the simultaneous demands of balance, dynamic stability, and high degrees of freedom during locomotion and manipulation make teleoperation and subsequent data collection significantly more difficult and resource-intensive. Current large-scale loco-manipulation datasets are largely unavailable, rendering the application of VLA training to humanoids prohibitive.
- **Limitations of Existing Data Generation Methods:** While automated data generation tools exist for robot manipulators, they are generally not suitable for humanoids. These existing methods often assume simplified action spaces or stable end-effector control, which do not account for the high-dimensional, composite action spaces of humanoids that involve intricate coordination across arms, legs, and torso for maintaining stability. A direct application of these methods to humanoids would be ineffective due to their inability to manage whole-body dynamics and contact-rich interactions.
- **Need for Systematic Studies:** The absence of large, accessible loco-manipulation datasets has hindered systematic investigations into how data quality, types of randomization, and policy learning architectures impact the performance of VLA models for humanoids. Such studies are crucial for optimizing policy training and understanding generalization capabilities.
- **Enhancing Real-World Deployment:** The ultimate goal is to enable the deployment of capable loco-manipulation policies on real-world humanoids. Synthetic data, if generated effectively, can supplement or even reduce the reliance on expensive real-world data collection, facilitating more sample-efficient sim-and-real co-training.

In light of these motivations, the specific objectives articulated by the authors include:

- **Developing HumanoidMimicGen:** To create a data generation method that synthesizes loco-manipulation demonstrations for humanoids by leveraging whole-body skill planning and adapting a small number of human demonstrations.
- **Establishing a Benchmark:** To introduce the G1 loco-manipulation benchmark, a nine-task simulation suite designed for evaluating humanoid loco-manipulation capabilities and enabling reproducible comparisons among different methods.
- **Analyzing Policy Learning:** To conduct experiments on this benchmark to systematically study how factors such as randomization strategies, different data-generation algorithms, and various model architectures influence policy success rates.
- **Demonstrating Sim-and-Real Co-training:** To provide real-world evidence that policies co-trained with data generated by HumanoidMimicGen, alongside real-world data, can outperform policies trained solely on real-world data.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

HumanoidMimicGen is designed to generate humanoid loco-manipulation data by adapting contact-rich whole-body skills from a limited number of source demonstrations to new states and object poses. This is achieved through a multi-faceted approach that integrates a hybrid control space, structured skill planning, whole-body motion planning, and data augmentation techniques.

**Hybrid Humanoid Action Space:**
The method employs a hierarchical hybrid action space for humanoid control. At the lowest level, all robot joints (legs, torso, arms, hands) are commanded via joint position control. A high-level action API provides two components:
1. **Upper Body Control:** Joint position commands for arms, hands, and torso. These are typically executed via a joint-space controller for manipulation.
2. **Lower Body Control:** Base motion commands for locomotion, specified as planar base velocity (ẋ, ẏ), yaw rate (θ̇), and desired torso height (z). This is managed by a reinforcement-learned locomotion controller, specifically the Homie controller, which translates these high-level commands into dynamically feasible leg joint positions, delegating balance, contact handling, and leg coordination. This decoupled approach factors planning into segments that require dynamic (locomotion) and static (manipulation) stability.

**Skill Planning:**
The process begins with a small set of human-collected source demonstrations, which are annotated with object-centric skill segments. Each skill is defined by an end-effector (e.g., left arm) and a reference object frame (e.g., a box). The target poses for the end-effector within a skill are adapted to new initial states by computing the relative pose to the object frame in the original demonstration and applying it to the object's current pose in the new scene.
To handle complex bimanual tasks, the system incorporates precedence order pairs (one skill must complete before another starts) and concurrent order pairs (two skills must start simultaneously). Coordination constraints are reduced to additional precedence constraints, forming a Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG) of skills. A greedy algorithm then selects sets of skills from this DAG that are free of uncompleted precedence constraints, allowing for concurrent execution of independent skills.

**Whole-Body Data Generation Procedure:**
HumanoidMimicGen iteratively plans and executes skill segments until all skills for a given task are completed:
1. **Skill Selection and Adaptation:** In each iteration, the system identifies the next set of skills (Ψᵢ) that can be executed based on the DAG. For each skill in Ψᵢ, it adapts the initial end-effector pose from the source demonstration to the current scene's object pose, yielding a target end-effector pose (T[e]).
2. **Whole-Body Inverse Kinematics (IK):** The method solves whole-body inverse kinematics to find a batch of full robot configurations (Q) that can achieve the target end-effector poses for all active skills concurrently. The IK solver prioritizes minimal L₀ distance from the current configuration, weighted by joint groups, to minimize unnecessary joint movement.
3. **Decomposition into Locomotion and Manipulation Subproblems:**
 - **Locomotion Planning:** For each candidate IK configuration (q''), an intermediate "switch configuration" (q') is computed (upper joints from current state, lower joints from q''). A locomotion trajectory (τ_l) is planned from the current robot configuration (q) to this switch configuration (q'). This plan is executed using the learned RL locomotion controller. If planning fails, the next candidate IK configuration from Q is attempted.
 - **Manipulation Planning:** After executing the locomotion plan, the actual achieved switch configuration (q') is used as the starting point. A manipulation trajectory (τ_m) is planned between q' and the target IK configuration (q''). This manipulation plan is then executed using the upper-body joint-space controller.
4. **Skill Trajectory Adaptation and Execution:** The entire skill demonstration trajectory, consisting of a sequence of end-effector actions, is adapted to the current scene's object pose. Whole-body IK is used iteratively to track these adapted end-effector target poses, generating a skill trajectory (τ_Ψᵢ) which is then executed via the manipulation controller. Hand joint positions are replayed directly.
5. **Collision Handling and GPU Acceleration:** The system leverages cuRobo for GPU-accelerated collision checking and batch inverse kinematics. Robot and object geometries are approximated using spheres. To handle contact-rich tasks where end-effectors are intentionally in collision with objects, the radii of spheres involved in contact are dynamically shrunk to render the states collision-free for the planner while respecting task constraints.
6. **Success Check:** After completing all skills in Ψᵢ, the current state is checked against the environment's success criteria.

**Motion and Initialization Randomization:**
To enhance policy robustness and generalization, two types of perturbations are applied during data generation:
1. **Motion Noise:** Gaussian noise is added to the robot's actions during execution (a' = a + ε), but the original expert action (a) is stored as the demonstration label. This exposes the policy to off-nominal states while maintaining the intended expert behavior.
2. **Initialization Randomization:** The robot's initial base pose and object poses are randomly perturbed around their nominal start states, subject to task feasibility constraints. This increases the diversity of starting conditions in the generated dataset.

**G1 Loco-Manipulation Benchmark:**
A new simulation benchmark built on robosuite and MuJoCo is introduced. This benchmark comprises nine diverse loco-manipulation tasks for a simulated G1 humanoid robot, designed to test base motion, whole-body coordination, object interaction complexity (single-arm, bimanual, whole-body), and execution horizon. Tasks have binary success conditions and feature randomized initial object and robot root poses.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings regarding the effectiveness of HumanoidMimicGen for data generation and its impact on humanoid loco-manipulation policy performance in both simulation and real-world settings.

**Simulation Benchmark Performance:**
- HumanoidMimicGen demonstrated a substantial improvement in policy success rates (PSR) on the G1 simulation benchmark. Policies trained on 1,000 demonstrations generated by HumanoidMimicGen achieved an average PSR of 0.89 across nine diverse tasks.
- This performance significantly surpassed baselines: policies trained on a single human demonstration (average PSR 0.26), on 100 human demonstrations (average PSR 0.48), and on data generated by an extended DexMimicGen+ baseline (average PSR 0.33).
- HumanoidMimicGen effectively addressed long-horizon loco-manipulation problems. For instance, it achieved a 1.00 PSR on "PushShelf Forward" (1230 steps) and 0.97 on "BoxLiftFloor" (900 steps), while DexMimicGen+ achieved 0.35 and 0.87, respectively, and source demonstrations alone resulted in 0.70 and 0.14. This indicates the method's ability to generate data for complex tasks requiring coordinated navigation and whole-body manipulation over extended periods.

**Analysis of Data Generation Design:**
- **Motion Noise:** Removing synthetic motion randomization during data generation led to a reduction in mean policy success rate from 0.89 to 0.49.
- **Initialization Randomization:** Fixing the initial robot base pose (removing initial-state diversity) resulted in a decrease in mean policy success rate from 0.89 to 0.51.
- These ablations indicate that both trajectory-level (motion noise) and state-level (initialization noise) randomization strategies are critical components that contribute to the robust performance of the trained policies.

**Effect of Different Policy Architectures:**
- When trained on datasets generated by HumanoidMimicGen (1,000 demonstrations), a fine-tuned Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model (GR00T N1.6 base model checkpoint) achieved the highest average PSR of 0.89.
- A flow-matching transformer policy (following AdaFlow) performed competitively with an average PSR of 0.86.
- A Diffusion Policy baseline yielded a lower average PSR of 0.51. This suggests that while HumanoidMimicGen can generate data suitable for various policy architectures, the VLA model showed superior performance in this benchmark.

**Real-World Evaluation:**
- Policies co-trained on HumanoidMimicGen-generated simulation data and a limited set of real-world teleoperated demonstrations consistently outperformed policies trained solely on real-world data.
- Across four real-world tasks ("ThrowBottle," "BoxToCart," "PickCanister," and "PickCanisterWithObstruction"), co-training improved the average policy score from 0.51 (real-only) to 0.71.
- Specific gains included 0.15 on ThrowBottle, 0.25 on BoxToCart, 0.25 on PickCanister, and 0.15 on PickCanisterWithObstruction. These results suggest that HumanoidMimicGen's synthetic data is effective in boosting real-world loco-manipulation performance for humanoid robots.

**Scaling Across Embodiments (Appendix F):**
- The method was also applied to a floating-base humanoid variant without algorithmic changes. Policies trained and evaluated on this variant showed similar average performance (0.90 PSR) compared to the legged G1 humanoid (0.89 PSR), suggesting the pipeline's applicability to different humanoid embodiments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The HumanoidMimicGen research contributes to the field of humanoid robotics by addressing fundamental challenges in data generation and policy learning for complex loco-manipulation tasks.

**Scalable Data Generation for Humanoids:** The primary significance lies in providing a scalable and automated method for generating large datasets for humanoid loco-manipulation. This directly addresses the critical bottleneck of data scarcity, which has historically hindered the training of sophisticated Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for humanoids. By amplifying a few human demonstrations into thousands of diverse trajectories, the method significantly reduces the reliance on laborious and expensive manual teleoperation.

**Enhanced Real-World Policy Performance:** The demonstrated improvements in real-world policy performance through sim-and-real co-training highlight the practical utility of HumanoidMimicGen. The ability to leverage synthetic data to increase policy success rates by a notable margin (20% average improvement) indicates that this approach can accelerate the development and deployment of more capable humanoid robots in real-world environments. This suggests a pathway for more sample-efficient learning in robotics.

**Enabling Systematic Research and Development:** The introduction of the G1 Loco-Manipulation Benchmark, coupled with the data generation methodology, provides a controlled environment for systematic experimentation. Researchers can now more effectively evaluate and compare different data generation strategies, policy architectures, and other design choices for humanoid control, fostering a deeper understanding of what factors contribute to robust performance.

**Advancement in Whole-Body Control and Planning:** The methodology effectively integrates a learned locomotion policy with classical whole-body inverse kinematics and motion planning. This hybrid approach demonstrates a viable strategy for handling the complex, high-dimensional, and contact-rich action spaces inherent in humanoid loco-manipulation, where dynamic stability must be maintained alongside precise manipulation.

**Reduced Engineering Effort:** By automating the generation of diverse training data, the method has the potential to reduce the significant engineering effort typically required for developing and refining humanoid control systems. This could lead to faster iteration cycles and more rapid progress in the capabilities of humanoid robots.

**Potential for Broader Generalization:** While the current work uses object-centric skills and fixed sequences, the underlying principles of whole-body planning and adaptation lay groundwork for future extensions. The integration of randomization strategies (motion and initialization noise) further contributes to policy generalization, making robots more robust to variations encountered in deployment.

In summary, HumanoidMimicGen represents a step forward in making advanced learning-based control paradigms more accessible and effective for humanoid robots, particularly in scenarios requiring complex, coordinated movement and interaction with their environment.
