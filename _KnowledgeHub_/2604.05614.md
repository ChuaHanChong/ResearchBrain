# Grounding Hierarchical Vision-Language-Action Models Through Explicit Language-Action Alignment

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework called Grounded Preference-based Language-Action Alignment (GPLA) enhances the transparency of hierarchical Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models by explicitly aligning intermediate natural language instructions with robot actions and visual observations. This approach, leveraging a contrastive grounding model and offline preference learning, reduces the need for costly human annotations while maintaining robust action execution and stable semantic coherence in generated language.

## Problem

- Existing hierarchical Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models often lack explicit alignment mechanisms between generated natural language sub-task descriptions and the robot's executed actions.
- Evaluation of hierarchical VLAs primarily focuses on task success rates, often neglecting the groundedness and consistency of intermediate language outputs with robot perceptions and actions.
- Developing hierarchical VLAs relies heavily on extensive and expensive human annotations for intermediate sub-task descriptions, limiting scalability.

## Method

- A **GPLA** (Grounded Preference-based Language-Action Alignment) framework is introduced, employing an ==iterative preference learning scheme (SimPO)== to refine a hierarchical VLA model.
- The framework uses a high-level VLM to decompose instructions and a low-level VLA to generate action trajectories, which are then evaluated by a ==grounding model==.
- A novel ==action-conditioned grounding model== is developed, which learns a ==shared embedding space== across vision, action, and language by modulating visual features with embedded actions via ==FiLM layers==.

## Results

- **GPLA** achieved trajectory generation performance comparable to supervised baselines (e.g., MSE **0.045** for GPLA Action-Conditioned vs. **0.043** for supervised SmolVLA).
- Generated low-level instructions, while having lower token-overlap metrics (BLEU), maintained stable semantic coherence as indicated by BERTScore, which remained around **0.98** across methods.
- The action-conditioned grounding model uniquely demonstrated multimodal fusion in its embedding space, showing integrated vision and language representations, unlike general-purpose contrastive models like CLIP or SigLIP 2.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Preference-based learning, using a grounding model, can effectively align language with actions in hierarchical VLAs, reducing reliance on expensive human annotations while maintaining performance.
> - Explicit grounding mechanisms improve robot transparency by ensuring generated natural language explanations are consistent with the robot's visual perceptions and physical actions.
> - An action-conditioned grounding model fosters a more unified, multi-modal embedding space where vision, language, and action are integrated, offering deeper insights into multimodal representations for embodied agents.
