# VipAct: Visual-Perception Enhancement via Specialized VLM Agent Collaboration and Tool-use

> [!summary] Summary
> The VipAct framework enhances fine-grained visual perception in large multimodal models (VLMs) by orchestrating specialized VLM agents and integrating high-precision vision expert models. This approach achieved state-of-the-art accuracy, such as 73.79% on the Blink benchmark with GPT-4o, consistently outperforming traditional prompting and visual programming methods on challenging tasks.

## Problem

- State-of-the-art Vision-Language Models (VLMs) struggle with fine-grained visual perception tasks requiring detailed pixel-level analysis.
- Prior visual programming methods for VLMs often lack generalizability, depend solely on text queries for code generation, and employ rigid tool selection.
- Text-based prompting strategies frequently lead to ungrounded reasoning or hallucinations in visual tasks, and visual prompting can adversely affect VLM perception by cluttering images.

## Method

- ==VipAct== introduces an autonomous VLM-based agent framework featuring an ==orchestrator agent== that receives direct image input for comprehensive task analysis and planning.
- The framework incorporates three ==specialized VLM-based agents== for focused image captioning, visual prompt description, and focused image comparison to provide targeted visual analysis.
- It integrates a suite of ==vision expert models== (e.g., depth estimation, object detection, segmentation) that return both textual and processed image data directly into the VLM's reasoning workflow for pixel-level precision.

## Results

- VipAct achieved state-of-the-art performance on all sub-tasks in the Blink and MMVP benchmarks, obtaining an average accuracy of **73.79%** on Blink with GPT-4o, significantly surpassing baseline methods.
- Ablation studies confirmed that multi-agent collaboration, direct visual input to the orchestrator, and the inclusion of specialized VLM agents and vision expert models are indispensable for the framework's performance.
- Closed-source VLMs like GPT-4o demonstrated robust performance with VipAct, while open-source models exhibited instruction-following limitations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Multi-agent collaboration is crucial for eliciting comprehensive System-2 reasoning in VLMs, enabling significantly more detailed image analysis compared to direct prompting.
> - Providing direct image input to the orchestrator agent is essential for grounding task planning and precise parameter selection for tools, leading to more accurate visual understanding.
> - Traditional text-based prompting and visual prompting methods do not consistently improve fine-grained visual perception with VLMs; they can lead to ungrounded reasoning or impaired perception.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

VipAct operates at the intersection of several rapidly evolving fields within AI, primarily **Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) / Vision-Language Models (VLMs)**, **Agentic AI**, and **Tool-use in AI systems**.

Recent advancements in VLMs like GPT-4o, Gemini, and Claude have demonstrated impressive capabilities in tasks that integrate textual and visual information. These models can perform various image-text benchmarks, from visual question answering (VQA) to web navigation. However, the paper correctly identifies a critical limitation: despite their general prowess, state-of-the-art VLMs consistently struggle with **fine-grained visual perception tasks**. These are tasks that require detailed, pixel-level analysis, such as detecting subtle object boundaries, counting numerous small objects accurately, or understanding intricate spatial relationships—tasks often trivial for humans but challenging for current VLMs. This "blindness" to fine details is a significant bottleneck for deploying VLMs in high-stakes applications like surgical robotics, autonomous driving, or even advanced image editing, where precision is paramount.

To address these limitations, prior research has explored several avenues:
- **Visual Programming Methods:** These approaches involve Large Language Models (LLMs) generating code that invokes specialized vision models, using their outputs as predictions. Examples include MM-REACT, ViperGPT, and VisProg. While effective for predefined tasks, VipAct argues that these methods often lack generalizability, depend solely on text queries for code generation, and employ rigid tool selection, limiting their adaptation to new or complex visual scenarios (e.g., visual prompts, multi-image inputs).
- **Prompting Strategies:** This includes textual prompting (e.g., Chain-of-Thought, Least-to-Most, Tree-of-Thought) to elicit "System-2" reasoning, and visual prompting (e.g., Set-of-Mark) which adds artifacts to images to guide VLMs. VipAct notes that the efficacy of textual prompting on fine-grained *visual* perception is underexplored, and visual prompting, while promising for some compositional reasoning, struggles with accurately perceiving prompts or improving performance in broader perception tasks due to potential clutter or misinterpretation.
- **LLM-based Agents:** The broader field of agentic AI, where LLMs are employed for planning, reasoning, and action taking, has seen significant growth. While many VLM-based agents focus on Web/GUI interaction or embodied robotics, VipAct identifies a gap in agents specifically designed for **natural image perception tasks**.

VipAct positions itself as a novel solution that bridges these gaps. It synergistically combines the strengths of VLM-based agents, multi-agent collaboration, and specialized vision expert models to overcome the inherent limitations of VLMs in fine-grained perception. By integrating these components, VipAct aims to provide a more flexible, generalizable, and precise framework for visual understanding, moving beyond the shortcomings of previous visual programming or prompting-only methods. Its emphasis on multi-agent collaboration to elicit more robust System-2 reasoning and the direct integration of visual outputs from expert models into the VLM's workflow are key innovations that distinguish it within the current landscape.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the VipAct framework are rooted in addressing the fundamental shortcomings of current Vision-Language Models (VLMs) when confronted with tasks requiring detailed visual analysis. Specifically, the authors aim to:

- **Enhance Fine-Grained Visual Perception in VLMs:** The core objective is to improve VLMs' ability to perform tasks that demand meticulous pixel-level analysis, such as identifying subtle features, accurately localizing small objects, or discerning intricate spatial relationships. Existing VLMs, despite their strong general vision-language understanding, demonstrably "struggle with fine-grained visual perception tasks that require detailed pixel-level analysis."
- **Elicit Comprehensive System-2 Reasoning:** While VLMs can perform basic visual reasoning, complex perception tasks often require iterative, step-by-step reasoning (System-2 thinking). The paper aims to develop a mechanism that can effectively elicit and ground this comprehensive reasoning process from VLMs, moving beyond superficial interpretations.
- **Overcome Limitations of Prior Approaches:** The authors are motivated to surmount the deficiencies of existing methods:
 - **Lack of Generalizability in Visual Programming:** Previous visual programming methods are often rigid and tied to predefined toolsets, making them inflexible for new tasks, especially those involving complex inputs like visual prompts or multiple images.
 - **Inconsistent Efficacy of Prompting Strategies:** Text-based prompting (e.g., CoT) frequently leads to "ungrounded" reasoning or hallucinations when applied to visual tasks, while visual prompting (e.g., Set-of-Mark) can sometimes impair perception by cluttering images or confusing models.
- **Integrate High-Precision Perceptual Information:** Recognizing that current VLMs may inherently lack the pixel-level precision of specialized computer vision models, a key motivation is to seamlessly integrate these "vision expert models" into the VLM's workflow. This allows the VLM to leverage high-fidelity perceptual data where its own capabilities might be insufficient.
- **Develop a Flexible and Extensible Framework:** The authors aim for VipAct to be an autonomous, modular system capable of handling diverse visual perception tasks with a single prompt template. This flexibility allows for the easy addition of new specialized agents or vision tools as needed, paving the way for more advanced and adaptable visual perception systems.

Ultimately, the motivation is to bridge the gap between human-like detailed visual understanding and the current capabilities of state-of-the-art VLMs, thereby enabling their deployment in critical real-world applications that demand precise visual comprehension.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VipAct addresses the challenges of fine-grained visual perception by proposing a VLM-based agent framework that orchestrates **multi-agent collaboration** and **vision expert model integration**. The framework is composed of three core interacting components: an orchestrator agent, specialized agents, and vision expert models.

#### 3.1. Orchestrator Agent

Serving as the central intelligence, the orchestrator agent is typically powered by a powerful VLM (e.g., GPT-4o). Its responsibilities include:

- **Task Requirement Analysis and Planning:** It begins by analyzing the user query and associated images to identify key elements, critical visual features, and criteria necessary to solve the problem. Based on this, it generates a detailed plan, outlining sequential steps to acquire the required information. Crucially, the orchestrator agent *receives direct image input*, allowing it to ground its planning in visual context, unlike prior methods that rely solely on textual descriptions of images.
- **Tool Selection and Coordination:** Following the plan, the orchestrator dynamically selects and invokes appropriate tools, which can be either specialized agents or external vision expert models. It manages the workflow and coordinates interactions between these components.
- **Evidence Summarization:** After tools and agents execute their tasks in separate environments, the orchestrator aggregates, compiles, and summarizes the collected evidence. It also includes mechanisms to resolve conflicting information or handle potential hallucinations from subordinate models, ensuring coherent and factual synthesis.
- **Final Answer Deduction:** Using the summarized evidence, the orchestrator applies its reasoning to deduce the final answer, potentially generating and executing Python code through an external interpreter for complex computations or logic. If a perfect answer isn't achievable, it selects the closest plausible option.

#### 3.2. Collaboration with Specialized Agents

VipAct incorporates three specialized VLM-based agents, each designed to handle specific types of visual analysis and provide detailed, task-relevant information to the orchestrator:

- **Focused Image Captioning:** This agent generates detailed descriptions of an image, with an optional "focus" argument to emphasize specific objects or elements relevant to the task. This allows the orchestrator to obtain precise, targeted textual analysis of visual content.
- **Visual Prompt Description:** Crucial for interpreting annotated images, this agent analyzes visual prompts (e.g., colored circles, bounding boxes, arrows, labels) within images, describing their locations, characteristics, and—most importantly—the specific regions or objects they target.
- **Focused Image Comparison:** Designed for multi-image inputs, this agent identifies similarities and differences between multiple images, also supporting an optional "focus" parameter for targeted comparative analysis. This is vital for tasks like change detection or multi-view reasoning.

These agents act as "experts" for specific VLM-interpretable tasks, providing richer, more focused VLM outputs than the orchestrator might generate on its own.

#### 3.3. Integration of Vision Expert Models

To overcome VLMs' inherent limitations in pixel-level precision, VipAct integrates a suite of specialized vision expert models. These models provide high-fidelity perceptual information, and uniquely, *return both textual data and processed images* directly into the orchestrator's reasoning workflow, making VipAct one of the pioneering agent frameworks to do so. The expert models include:

- **Visual Prompt Detector:** Identifies and localizes annotated elements (e.g., circles, bounding boxes) within images, providing their precise coordinates.
- **Depth Estimator:** Generates a grayscale depth image from an input image, providing crucial spatial information about relative object distances from the camera. This processed image is then available for direct VLM interpretation.
- **Object Detection:** Identifies and localizes objects, returning bounding box coordinates and object labels, along with a processed image highlighting detections.
- **Image Segmentation:** Provides precise delineation of image regions and object boundaries, returning segmentation masks and related textual information.
- **Embedding-based Similarity Computation:** Quantifies visual similarities between images using embedding models (e.g., CLIP) to generate similarity scores.

The orchestrator agent leverages the planning capabilities of a powerful VLM, enhanced by the targeted analysis from specialized VLM agents and the pixel-level precision from vision expert models. This synergistic combination allows VipAct to perform comprehensive reasoning, generate intermediate steps, and effectively handle complex fine-grained visual perception tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of VipAct on challenging visual perception benchmarks, **Blink** and **MMVP**, reveals several significant findings:

- **Superior Performance of VipAct:** VipAct consistently achieves state-of-the-art performance across all diverse sub-tasks in both the Blink and MMVP datasets. For instance, on the Blink benchmark with GPT-4o, VipAct achieved an average accuracy of 73.79%, significantly outperforming the next best baseline (CoT prompting at 65.85%). This demonstrates VipAct's effectiveness and strong generalization ability in enhancing fine-grained visual perception.
- **Ineffectiveness of Text-based Prompting:** Traditional text-based prompting methods (Zero-shot, CoT, Least-to-Most, Tree-of-Thought), which are often effective for LLMs in text-only tasks, do not consistently improve performance on fine-grained visual perception with VLMs. While some tasks saw minor improvements, others showed minimal or even negative impacts. This is attributed to the fact that reasoning steps generated by these methods are often "ungrounded in visual elements" and can lead to severe hallucinations in the visual domain.
- **Detrimental Effect of Visual Prompting (SoM):** The Set-of-Mark (SoM) visual prompting technique, which overlays labeled masks on images, was found to adversely affect VLM performance on most tasks in both datasets. The authors suggest that this is because cluttered masks can interfere with the VLM's perception of original objects or confuse it with existing visual prompts, indicating that its effectiveness in limited compositional reasoning tasks does not generalize well to broader perception.
- **Poor Generalization of Previous Visual Programming Methods:** Earlier visual programming frameworks like MM-ReAct, ViperGPT, and VisProg exhibited poor generalization, performing adequately only on simpler VQA-like tasks. They often failed on tasks requiring visual prompt understanding (e.g., depth estimation requiring locating red circles) or complex multi-image inputs. Their reliance solely on text queries for code generation limited their flexibility and adaptability.
- **Impact of Different VLMs:** While VipAct shows significant improvements with powerful closed-source VLMs (GPT-4o, Gemini-1.5-Pro, Claude-3.5-Sonnet), its application to open-source models (LLaVA-OneVision-7B, InternVL-2-Pro, Llama-3.2-90b-vision) revealed instruction-following limitations. Open-source models often failed to adhere to required output formats or exhibited biases, indicating that strong instruction-following capabilities are critical for VipAct's agentic framework to function optimally. However, even with these models, some benefits over raw zero-shot performance were observed in specific scenarios.

**Ablation Studies revealed critical components:**
- **Multi-agent Collaboration is Crucial:** Removing specialized agents and integrating their prompts directly into the orchestrator consistently degraded performance. Multi-agent collaboration enabled significantly more detailed image analysis (over 80% more generated tokens), demonstrating its role in eliciting comprehensive System-2 reasoning from diverse perspectives.
- **Direct Image Input for Orchestrator Agent is Essential:** When the orchestrator agent received only image paths (textual) instead of direct visual input, performance significantly declined. Direct visual access allows the orchestrator to ground its planning and decision-making in actual visual context, leading to more accurate task analysis and precise parameter selection for tools.
- **Specialized Agents and Vision Expert Models are Indispensable:** The removal of either specialized VLM agents or vision expert models led to noticeable performance drops. Specialized agents provide focused visual information without distraction, while vision expert models deliver pixel-level precision beyond current VLM capabilities, making each component vital for VipAct's overall success.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The VipAct framework represents a significant step forward in enhancing the visual perception capabilities of large multimodal models, addressing a critical bottleneck in their broader applicability. Its significance and potential impact span several dimensions:

- **Advancing VLM Capabilities for Fine-Grained Perception:** VipAct demonstrates a robust method for enabling VLMs to tackle complex, fine-grained visual perception tasks that were previously challenging. This moves VLMs closer to human-level visual understanding, particularly in scenarios demanding high precision and detailed analysis.
- **Flexible and Generalizable Agentic Framework:** By proposing an autonomous, modular, and extensible framework, VipAct offers a blueprint for building more capable visual AI systems. Its ability to handle diverse visual perception tasks using a single prompt template and flexibly integrate new tools and agents makes it highly adaptable to emerging challenges and new model advancements.
- **Insights into System-2 Reasoning and Multi-Agent Collaboration:** The ablation studies provide valuable empirical evidence on the importance of multi-agent collaboration in eliciting more detailed and grounded System-2 reasoning in VLMs. This contributes to the growing understanding of how to design effective agent architectures for complex problem-solving.
- **Highlighting the Role of Direct Visual Input in Planning:** The finding that direct image input for task planning is crucial for the orchestrator agent offers important guidance for future VLM and agentic system design. It underscores that relying solely on textual descriptions for planning can lead to suboptimal outcomes in visually-intensive tasks.
- **Identifying VLM Limitations for Future Research:** The detailed error analysis (failures in small object part perception, distinguishing closely positioned visual prompts, fine-grained spatial reasoning, misinterpretation of relative object positions, and object orientation) provides a clear roadmap for future research directions in VLM development. These insights can guide efforts to improve fundamental visual intelligence in foundation models.
- **Enabling High-Stakes Real-World Applications:** The enhanced fine-grained visual perception offered by VipAct has immediate and profound implications for critical applications such as:
 - **Surgical Robotics:** Where precise recognition of tissue boundaries, instruments, and anatomical structures is vital.
 - **Autonomous Driving:** Requiring accurate perception of road conditions, small obstacles, traffic signs, and pedestrian behavior.
 - **Advanced Image Editing and Design:** Enabling more intelligent and precise content understanding for creative tools, automating complex visual manipulation tasks.
 - **Scientific Image Analysis:** For fields like microscopy or astronomy, where minute details hold significant information.

In conclusion, VipAct provides a powerful, practical, and theoretically insightful framework that pushes the boundaries of VLM capabilities. By demonstrating how multi-agent collaboration and strategic tool-use can unlock advanced visual perception, it paves the way for more intelligent, precise, and widely applicable AI systems in visually rich domains.
