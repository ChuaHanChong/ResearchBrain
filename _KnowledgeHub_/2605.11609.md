# Anti-Self-Distillation for Reasoning RL via Pointwise Mutual Information

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Xiaohongshu Inc. and the Chinese Academy of Sciences introduced Anti-Self-Distillation (AntiSD), a method that reconfigures on-policy self-distillation to enhance reasoning in large language models by explicitly rewarding deliberative tokens. AntiSD demonstrated a 2-10x speedup in reaching baseline accuracy and improved final performance by 2.1-11.5 percentage points on math reasoning benchmarks, also showing gains on code reasoning tasks.

## Problem

- Reinforcement learning from verifiable rewards (RLVR) for reasoning tasks, particularly with large language models (LLMs), suffers from sparse, trajectory-level scalar rewards, making precise credit assignment to individual intermediate reasoning steps challenging.
- Existing on-policy self-distillation methods, despite their promise for self-improvement, perform inconsistently or modestly in complex mathematical reasoning problems, often failing to surpass strong baselines.
- Standard self-distillation introduces a "structural shortcut bias," where an oracle-like teacher (conditioned on a verified solution) over-rewards tokens directly implied by the solution ("shortcut tokens") while penalizing critical "deliberation tokens" necessary for multi-step search and exploration.

## Method

- The proposed Anti-Self-Distillation (**AntiSD**) method reconfigures the per-token signal by reversing its gradient direction, thereby rewarding deliberation tokens and penalizing shortcut tokens.
- **AntiSD** ascends Jensen-Shannon Divergence (JSD) instead of reverse KL, resulting in an asymmetrically bounded per-token advantage that counteracts the over-sampling of deliberation tokens while proportionally suppressing extreme shortcut tokens.
- An entropy-triggered gate is incorporated to monitor the teacher's per-token entropy, disabling the **AntiSD** term if entropy falls below a calibrated threshold to prevent policy degeneration and ensure stable training dynamics.

## Results

- AntiSD achieved a 2-**10x** speedup in reaching Group-Normalized Policy Optimization (GRPO) baseline accuracy and improved final mean accuracy by +2.1 to +**11.5** percentage points across five LLMs (Qwen3, Olmo-3) on the DAPO-Math-17k dataset.
- In contrast, default self-distillation consistently underperformed the GRPO baseline on every evaluated model, often by substantial margins (e.g., Qwen3-8B Avg: **30.6%** vs GRPO's **57.4%**).
- The method demonstrated generalizability to code reasoning, yielding improvements over the GRPO baseline on HumanEval+ (+1.2 points) and MBPP+ (+2.3 points), while also exhibiting stable training dynamics by maintaining teacher and actor entropy within a healthy range.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The per-token signal generated by standard self-distillation, where the student model acts as its own teacher conditioned on privileged information, can be formally characterized as conditional Pointwise Mutual Information (PMI).
> - This conditional PMI analysis reveals a "structural shortcut bias" within self-distillation, where the privileged context causes the self-teacher to inadvertently reinforce "shortcut tokens" and suppress crucial "deliberation tokens" that are essential for complex reasoning.
> - Counteracting this identified PMI bias by reversing the per-token reward polarity, utilizing JSD for its asymmetric bounding properties, and adding an entropy gate to stabilize training can significantly enhance language model reasoning capabilities and training efficiency.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a challenge within the field of reinforcement learning for reasoning tasks, particularly those involving large language models (LLMs). Reinforcement learning from verifiable rewards (RLVR) has emerged as a dominant paradigm for post-training progress in reasoning. However, RLVR typically provides sparse, trajectory-level scalar rewards, which complicates credit assignment to individual intermediate reasoning steps.

Two primary approaches have been developed to tackle this credit assignment problem:
1. **Process Reward Models (PRMs):** Training a separate model to score intermediate steps, often requiring human annotations or complex estimations.
2. **On-Policy Distillation (OPD):** Utilizing a stronger external teacher model to provide a token-level imitation signal.

On-policy self-distillation is a specialization of OPD where the student model acts as its own teacher. This teacher is conditioned on privileged information, such as a verified solution or environment feedback, during training. This approach aims to generate a token-level signal without requiring an external teacher or a separate PRM, offering a path towards self-improvement. While on-policy self-distillation has shown effectiveness in tasks like instruction-following and tool-use, its performance in more challenging mathematical reasoning problems has been inconsistent or modest. This paper investigates the reasons for these inconsistencies and proposes a modification to enhance its efficacy in such domains.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to improve the reasoning capabilities of language models, specifically in mathematical reasoning, by addressing the limitations of existing on-policy self-distillation methods. The authors identified a persistent issue where standard self-distillation often fails to outperform strong baselines, such as Group-Normalized Policy Optimization (GRPO), on math reasoning benchmarks.

The motivation for this work stems from a detailed analysis of the per-token signal generated by default self-distillation. This analysis revealed a structural bias introduced by the privileged context provided to the self-teacher. When the teacher model is conditioned on a verified solution, it effectively becomes an "oracle." This oracle teacher tends to exhibit high confidence in tokens that are directly implied by the known solution, such as structural connectives or verifiable claims (termed "shortcut tokens"). Conversely, it shows low confidence in "deliberation tokens" (e.g., "Wait," "Let," "Maybe") that represent multi-step search, exploration, or re-examination of alternatives, as these are unnecessary when the solution is already known.

Standard self-distillation, by descending a divergence (e.g., KL divergence) between the student and this oracle teacher, pulls the student towards the teacher's distribution. This process unintentionally reinforces shortcut tokens and penalizes deliberation tokens. This bias can lead to responses that shorten as training progresses, a phenomenon previously observed in self-distillation, but which the authors recast not as benign compression but as the suppression of critical deliberative steps necessary for complex reasoning.

Given this identified "structural shortcut bias" and the observation that the distribution of these biased tokens is often asymmetric (with deliberation tokens being over-sampled in student rollouts), the core motivation was to design a self-distillation mechanism that counteracts these issues. The aim was to foster rather than suppress deliberative reasoning and leverage the rich information contained in the privileged context effectively and robustly.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research involves an analytical diagnosis of standard self-distillation, followed by the proposal and empirical validation of a modified approach called Anti-Self-Distillation (AntiSD).

**Preliminaries:**
The base setup uses an autoregressive language model $\pi_\theta$ that generates trajectories given a problem $x$. RLVR provides a scalar reward $R(x, y)$ for the final answer. Following GRPO, a group of rollouts is sampled per prompt, and a group-normalized sequence-level advantage $A_{\text{seq}}$ is used as the policy-gradient signal.
Standard on-policy self-distillation augments the GRPO objective with a per-token signal. The same network $\pi_\theta$ acts as both student $\pi_S(\cdot | x, y_{<t})$ and teacher $\pi_T(\cdot | x, c, y_{<t})$, where $c$ is the privileged context (verified solution, environment feedback). The standard self-distillation loss is typically a per-token KL divergence $D_{KL}(\pi_S || \text{sg}[\pi_T])$, where sg[$\cdot$] denotes stop-gradient. This loss generates a per-token contribution $\delta_t$, which is additively combined with $A_{\text{seq}}$ to form the total advantage $A_{i,t} = A_{\text{seq},i} + \lambda \cdot \delta_t$.

**Per-token Reward as Conditional PMI (Diagnosis):**
The authors define $s_t := \log \pi_S(y_t | x, y_{<t})$, $t_t := \log \pi_T(y_t | x, y_{<t})$, and $u_t := t_t - s_t$. Through a score-function identity, they show that the per-token contribution from the KL divergence loss (when descending) can be expressed as $\delta_t = +u_t$. Critically, under the self-distillation setup where student and teacher share parameters, $u_t$ is identified as the conditional Pointwise Mutual Information (PMI):
$u_t = \log \frac{\pi_\theta(y_t | x, c, y_{<t})}{\pi_\theta(y_t | x, y_{<t})} = \text{PMI}(y_t; c | x, y_{<t})$.
This means the sign of $u_t$ indicates whether the privileged context $c$ raises ($u_t > 0$) or lowers ($u_t < 0$) the probability of the token $y_t$. The default self-distillation rewards tokens whose probability is increased by $c$ (shortcut tokens) and penalizes those whose probability is decreased (deliberation tokens). The analysis also observed an asymmetric distribution, with deliberation tokens (where $u_t < 0$) being over-sampled in the batch.

**Anti-Self-Distillation (AntiSD):**
To counteract the identified issues, AntiSD incorporates three main components:
1. **Reversed Gradient Direction:** Addressing the "wrong polarity" (O1), AntiSD reverses the gradient direction. Instead of descending a divergence, it ascends one. This fundamentally flips the sign of the per-token reward, so deliberation tokens are rewarded and shortcut tokens are penalized.
2. **Jensen-Shannon Divergence (JSD) Ascent:** To address the "asymmetric distribution" (O2) of $u_t$, AntiSD ascends Jensen-Shannon Divergence instead of reverse KL. The authors derive that ascending JSD results in a per-token advantage $A^{\text{AntiSD}}_t = -\varphi(u_t)$, where $\varphi(u) := \frac{1}{2}(\text{softplus}(u) - \log 2)$. This specific shape is asymmetrically bounded: it is capped on the negative side (deliberation side), which counterbalances the heavy-tailed oversampling of deliberation tokens, while remaining linear on the positive side (shortcut side), allowing for proportional suppression of extreme shortcut tokens.
3. **Entropy-Triggered Gate:** Since pure ascent is not self-terminating and could lead to policy drift, an entropy-triggered gate is introduced. This gate monitors the teacher's per-token entropy ($H$). If $H$ falls below a calibrated threshold $\tau_{\text{down}}$ (indicating the teacher has become overconfident and its log-ratio $u_t$ degenerates), the AntiSD term is disabled ($\lambda=0$). It is re-enabled only when $H$ recovers to a predefined $H_{\text{warm}}$ baseline, preventing chatter. The threshold $\tau_{\text{down}}$ is auto-calibrated from initial warmup steps.

**Experimental Setup:**
The methods were evaluated across five LLMs from the Qwen3 and Olmo-3 families (4B to 30B parameters) on the DAPO-Math-17k dataset. Training consisted of 200 on-policy steps. Four conditions were compared: un-trained base, +GRPO, +SD (default self-distillation), and +AntiSD. Evaluation was performed using avg@32 on AIME 2024/2025/2026 and HMMT 2025, and avg@4 on MinervaMath. The privileged context $c$ comprised a verified solution (sampled from a correct rollout or dataset) and binary correctness feedback. AntiSD's gate was auto-calibrated during the first 5 warmup steps. The approach was also tested on code reasoning benchmarks (HumanEval+, MBPP+).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrated several consistent patterns across different models and tasks:

**Performance on Math Reasoning:**
- **Speedup:** AntiSD consistently reached GRPO's accuracy in a fraction of the training steps, showing a speedup of 2–10x across all five models. Smaller models with weaker GRPO baselines exhibited larger speedups (e.g., Qwen3-4B-IT-2507 at 10x, Olmo3-7B-IT at 9.5x). This early ignition is attributed to the per-token reward being informative from the outset, reducing reliance on sparse trajectory-level rewards.
- **Final Accuracy:** AntiSD's final mean accuracy surpassed GRPO's on every evaluated model, with improvements ranging from +2.1 to +11.5 points. The largest gains were observed on weaker baselines. This gain is consistent with the hypothesis that optimizing for deliberation tokens enables the model to solve problems previously unreachable by GRPO's sparse signal.
- **Default Self-Distillation Underperformance:** In contrast, default self-distillation (+SD) consistently underperformed the GRPO baseline on every model, often by substantial margins (e.g., Qwen3-8B Avg: 30.6% vs GRPO's 57.4%).
- **Pass@k Analysis:** Pass@k plots on HMMT 2025 indicated that AntiSD's performance advantage over GRPO was sustained across varying $k$ values. This suggests that AntiSD genuinely solved problems GRPO could not, even with multiple attempts, and maintained rollout diversity rather than merely concentrating probability mass on already-correct rollouts.

**Performance on Code Reasoning:**
- AntiSD generalized to code reasoning, demonstrating improvements over the GRPO baseline on HumanEval+ (+1.2 points) and MBPP+ (+2.3 points) using Qwen3-8B. While smaller than gains on math, these results indicate transferability of the per-token mechanism.

**Training Dynamics:**
- AntiSD exhibited early ignition, with truncation-corrected train reward climbing rapidly (e.g., from ~0.5 to ~0.95 within ~30 steps on Qwen3-8B and Qwen3-4B-IT-2507), a regime GRPO reached much later, and SD often never reached.
- Default self-distillation showed varied failure modes: for some models (e.g., Qwen3-4B-IT-2507), teacher and actor entropy collapsed, leading to over-confident, shortcut-oriented responses and eventual run termination. For others (e.g., Olmo3-7B-IT), entropy inflated, indicating a drift away from useful tokens. AntiSD, conversely, maintained teacher and actor entropy within a stable middle band across models.

**Ablation Studies:**
- **No-teacher:** Removing the teacher completely (i.e., making the per-token signal solely dependent on the student's log-probability) led to a self-reinforcement collapse within approximately 70 steps on all tested models. This finding emphasizes that AntiSD's effectiveness relies on the conditional information derived from the privileged context, not just generic log-probability shaping.
- **No-gate:** Disabling the entropy-triggered gate resulted in model-dependent outcomes. On Qwen models, it led to faster initial ignition but subsequent collapse as teacher entropy approached zero (around step 90). On Olmo3-7B-IT, the run survived and even performed well, suggesting the gate acts as a robust stabilization mechanism, particularly for models prone to early entropy collapse.
- **Component Sensitivity:**
 - **Divergence Choice:** Using reverse KL ascent instead of JSD ascent resulted in a significant performance drop (e.g., Qwen3-8B Avg from 65.7 to 30.6), confirming the importance of JSD's asymmetric bounding.
 - **Threshold Sensitivity:** The canonical gate deactivation threshold of 0.93 $H_{\text{warm}}$ was found to transfer well across models, even if not strictly optimal for every single model.
 - **Composition:** Additive composition of the AntiSD advantage with GRPO outperformed multiplicative composition, as multiplicative scaling could diminish the deliberation push when GRPO's signal was weak.

**Beyond GRPO Saturation:**
- Applying AntiSD as a refinement on top of a GRPO-saturated checkpoint demonstrated that AntiSD's advantages are complementary to GRPO. On Qwen3-8B, continual AntiSD (50 steps post-GRPO saturation) nearly matched the accuracy of AntiSD trained from scratch, achieving its peak in 6x fewer steps than the from-base AntiSD. This indicates that the deliberation-token reward signal remains informative even when GRPO's trajectory-level reward has saturated.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research offers several points of significance and potential impact:

- **Addressing a Fundamental Problem:** The work provides a detailed analysis of why on-policy self-distillation, despite its theoretical appeal, has shown inconsistent performance in complex reasoning tasks like mathematics. By identifying the "structural shortcut bias" as conditional pointwise mutual information, the authors offer a theoretical understanding of the mechanism behind the observed failures.
- **Scalable Self-Improvement:** AntiSD opens a path towards more scalable self-improvement for LLMs. By enabling a model to bootstrap its own reasoning capabilities through its training signal, it reduces the reliance on external strong teachers or extensive human annotations for process-level rewards.
- **Improved Efficiency and Performance:** AntiSD significantly improves training efficiency, allowing models to reach comparable or higher accuracy in 2 to 10 times fewer training steps than GRPO. It also boosts final accuracy by a substantial margin (up to 11.5 points) over GRPO and largely outperforms default self-distillation. This suggests practical benefits for training reasoning-capable LLMs.
- **Robustness and Stability:** The design choices in AntiSD, particularly the JSD ascent for bounded advantages and the entropy-triggered gate, contribute to more stable training dynamics, preventing the collapse modes observed in standard self-distillation. This robustness is crucial for reliable deployment and further development of self-distillation techniques.
- **Broader Applicability:** While primarily evaluated on math reasoning, the demonstrated generalization to code reasoning suggests that the core principles of correcting the PMI bias and promoting deliberation could extend to other complex reasoning domains.
- **Future Research Directions:** The characterization of the per-token signal as conditional PMI offers a framework for designing future training-free process reward mechanisms and understanding how privileged information shapes model learning. Extensions to multi-turn agentic settings, broader coding benchmarks, and richer privileged context formats (e.g., process-level critiques) are natural next steps, potentially enhancing agent capabilities in interactive environments.

The authors acknowledge that the conditional-PMI account is a local characterization and that understanding the long-horizon dynamics of the full AntiSD update is an ongoing question. They also highlight the dual-use nature of advancements in LLM reasoning, emphasizing that AntiSD does not introduce new high-risk model artifacts but rather enhances the capabilities of existing models.
