# Latent Cluster Analysis for Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> LAVLA is an interpretability framework that clusters layer-wise embeddings from a VLA model, covering the vision-language backbone's final layer and every cross-attention layer of GR00T N1.5's diffusion action decoder. A cross-attention-derived weighting reweights visuolinguistic tokens before clustering, and an external VLM labels clusters with human-readable concepts. Weighted clustering raised Silhouette scores by 62.1%, and clusters disentangle spatiotemporal and kinematic structure.

## Problem

- Vision-language-action models generalize well across embodiments and tasks, but their internal representations stay opaque, leaving out-of-distribution failures and unsafe behaviour hard to anticipate or diagnose.
- Prior mechanistic work on these models clusters feed-forward activations projected into token space and never probes the action decoder, so the diffusion denoising path remains unexamined.
- Sparse autoencoders, the dominant interpretability tool, need large training corpora and adapt slowly, which suits language models but not the fast-moving, data-scarce robotics setting.

## Method

- **LAVLA** collects embeddings from the backbone's final layer and all cross-attention layers of the ==diffusion transformer==, stored per denoising timestep, then groups them with ==agglomerative hierarchical clustering== under Euclidean average linkage.
- A ==cross-attention embedding-weighting module== averages the attention matrix over target tokens and heads, softmax-normalises the per-source impact scores, and Hadamard-multiplies them into the vision-language token sequence.
- A ==concept-generation pipeline== takes the five samples nearest each centroid, prompts an external large VLM to synthesise one label from command text plus decoded frames, and scores it with ==CLIP similarity==.

## Results

- Weighting lifted Silhouette from **0.0837** to **0.1357** without dimensionality reduction (**62.1%** gain) and from **0.1324** to **0.1642** with it (**24.0%**), while Davies-Bouldin improved roughly **8%** in both settings.
- Cluster counts collapsed from **15,703** unweighted to **3,835** weighted, and to **7.15** under combined PCA plus weighting, with counts falling steadily toward later denoising steps as latents grew structured.
- Action-decoder clusters showed roughly **20%** larger mean intra-cluster Wasserstein distance for arm-joint displacement than backbone clusters; concept CLIP similarity stayed near **0.21-0.23** across all configurations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Attention weights already encode which context tokens matter, so reusing them as feature amplifiers cleans up noisy latents cheaply and should transfer to sparse-autoencoder-style probes too.
> - Clustering is a viable low-data alternative to autoencoder probing for fast-moving robotics, though high-dimensional embeddings need reduction or weighting to avoid singleton-dominated partitions.
> - Automatic concept labelling saturates when datasets have low visual diversity and short clips, so similarity scores should not be read as evidence of concept quality.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represent an emerging area within robotics research, recognized for their capability to ground language instructions and visual perceptions into robot actions. These models are typically trained on large-scale datasets encompassing diverse robotic experiences, which contributes to their generalization capabilities across various environments, tasks, and robot embodiments. Recent works such as those by Kim et al. (2025) and NVIDIA et al. (2025) exemplify the progress in VLA performance.

Despite advancements in VLA model performance, there is a recognized limitation in the understanding of their internal reasoning processes, as highlighted by Häon et al. (2025). This lack of interpretability is a significant concern in robotics, where the potential for unanticipated out-of-distribution scenarios and the consequences of unverified behavior can be substantial. Improving insights into how these models encode multimodal information is considered essential for building user trust and for identifying the sources of reasoning failures (Ma et al., 2025; Zang et al., 2025).

Existing methods for latent analysis in foundation models include Sparse AutoEncoders (SAEs), which aim to extract sparse, interpretable features from high-dimensional embeddings (Huben et al., 2024). While SAEs have been applied to large language models (LLMs) and extended to VLAs and VLMs (Grant et al., 2026; Zhang et al., 2025; Pach et al., 2025), they generally require extensive training data, which can limit their adaptability in fast-paced robotics development. An alternative approach involves clustering techniques, which group similar embeddings without the need for large training datasets, making them potentially more suitable for robotic applications (Fel et al., 2023; Hawasly et al., 2024).

The current work distinguishes itself by specifically focusing on the interpretability of the action decoder within VLA models. While Häon et al. (2025) explored clustering of feed-forward network activations in VLA models, their analysis did not extensively probe the action decoder's internal representations. This paper aims to address this gap by providing a framework for analyzing the latent space of the action decoding process, thereby extending the mechanistic interpretability of VLAs to their action generation components.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the interpretability of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models by providing a framework for analyzing their internal representations, with a particular focus on the action decoder.

The specific objectives outlined in the paper include:

- **Proposal of LAVLA:** To introduce LAVLA, a novel cluster-based framework designed for the latent cluster analysis of VLA model embeddings.
- **Layer-wise Study:** To apply the LAVLA framework to conduct a layer-wise investigation of a state-of-the-art VLA model, specifically the NVIDIA GR00T N1.5 model.
- **Characterization of Latent Space:** To characterize the latent space of the action decoder during the action diffusion process.
- **Introduction of Embedding-Weighting:** To develop and integrate a cross-attention-based embedding-weighting module. This module is intended to amplify features from relevant visuolinguistic tokens and suppress less informative ones within the cross-attention layers of the action decoder, thereby guiding the action denoising process.
- **Concept Extraction:** To establish a methodology for extracting human-interpretable concepts from the identified latent clusters, thereby linking internal model representations to semantic descriptions.
- **Analysis of Feature Disentanglement:** To investigate how latent clusters progressively disentangle spatiotemporal and kinematic features across different layers and diffusion timesteps.

The motivations underpinning these objectives stem from several factors:

- **Lack of Interpretability:** Despite their growing popularity and generalization capabilities in robotics, the internal reasoning and representations of VLA models are not well understood. This opacity limits their trustworthiness and reliability.
- **Safety in Robotics:** In robotic applications, particularly those involving real-world deployment, the range of possible out-of-distribution scenarios is vast, and unverified or inexplicable behavior can lead to severe consequences. Understanding the model's inner workings is deemed crucial for safety and failure identification.
- **User Trust:** Gaining insight into how VLAs encode and process multimodal information (vision and language) to generate actions is essential for improving user trust in autonomous robotic systems.
- **Limitations of Existing Methods:** While methods like Sparse AutoEncoders (SAEs) exist for latent analysis, they often demand extensive training data, which can hinder their rapid application in the evolving field of robotics. Clustering techniques offer a more adaptive alternative.
- **Research Gap:** Previous interpretability research on VLAs has not adequately focused on the action decoder, which is responsible for generating the final robot actions. This work aims to address this specific gap.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces the Latent Cluster Analysis for Vision-Language-Action Models (LAVLA) framework, designed to analyze the internal representations of VLA models. The methodology involves several steps: embedding collection, clustering with a novel weighting mechanism, quantitative cluster evaluation, and concept generation.

#### 3.1. Target Model and Data

The study focuses on **NVIDIA's GR00T N1.5 model**, a state-of-the-art 3-billion-parameter VLA. This model comprises an Eagle VLM backbone for processing vision and language inputs into multimodal representations, and an action decoder head. The action decoder is a Diffusion Transformer (DiT) that utilizes alternating self-attention and cross-attention blocks to denoise trajectories into continuous action vectors. It receives noisy action tokens and encoded robot state, while the fused vision-language embeddings from the Eagle backbone are integrated specifically through the cross-attention layers. The action decoder performs four iterative denoising steps.

For experimental data, a subset of the **Open-X-Embodiment dataset** (O’Neill et al., 2024) was utilized. This subset consists of 24,000 observation-action pairs collected from 24 distinct tasks, featuring a GR1 humanoid robot performing tabletop manipulation. Samples within the dataset are offset by 10 frames to ensure an even distribution across different episodes.

#### 3.2. Embedding Collection

During forward passes of the GR00T N1.5 model, embeddings were collected from two key modules:

- The **12th layer of the Eagle VLM backbone**: This represents the final VLM layer before visuolinguistic embeddings are passed to the action decoder.
- All **intermediate cross-attention layers of the DiT action decoder head**: Given that the action decoder performs four denoising steps, embeddings from each layer were stored along with their corresponding diffusion timestep (t=0 to t=3).

#### 3.3. Clustering

After embedding collection, a layer-wise clustering approach was applied.

##### 3.3.1. Clustering Algorithm

**Agglomerative hierarchical clustering** was chosen for its performance, particularly in early stages of analysis, over alternatives like DBSCAN and k-means. This algorithm initiates by treating each sample as an individual cluster and then iteratively merges the closest clusters based on a predefined distance threshold and average linkage, continuing until no further merges are possible. Euclidean distance was used as the metric for proximity.

##### 3.3.2. Cross-Attention-Based Embedding-Weighting

A novel component of LAVLA is the **cross-attention-based embedding-weighting module**. This module is motivated by the observation that action decoder embeddings are initially noisy and become semantically defined only in later layers. The goal is to measure and leverage the focus of cross-attention layers on visuolinguistic tokens from the VLM backbone, thereby amplifying important features and reducing the impact of noise.

The weighting process involves:
1. **Extracting Attention Weights:** The attention weight matrix, α (dimensions: action token sequence length × visuolanguage token sequence length), is extracted directly from the cross-attention layers. This matrix reflects the attention scores where the query originates from action tokens (from the previous self-attention layer) and keys are projected from the VLM backbone embeddings.
2. **Calculating Impact Scores:** The weights in α are averaged for each source token across all target tokens to obtain a vector of impact scores, M.
3. **Normalizing Weights:** After reducing across all heads in multi-head attention by taking the mean, a softmax function is applied to M to derive a normalized weight vector, W.
4. **Applying Weights:** A Hadamard product (element-wise multiplication) is performed between the normalized weight vector W and the original visuolinguistic tokens S_VL, resulting in weighted tokens S'_VL. These weighted embeddings are specific to each cross-attention layer and diffusion timestep, enabling an analysis of token importance throughout the action denoising.

##### 3.3.3. Dimensionality Reduction

Optionally, **PCA (Principal Component Analysis)** was applied for dimensionality reduction, reducing the embedding dimension to 100 per embedding. When used in conjunction with embedding-weighting, PCA was applied after the weighting step.

#### 3.4. Cluster Evaluation

The quality and consistency of the identified clusters were assessed using multiple metrics:

- **Quantitative Cluster Metrics:**
 - **Silhouette Coefficient (Rousseeuw, 1987):** Measures how similar an object is to its own cluster compared to other clusters (higher values indicate better separation).
 - **Caliński-Harabasz (CH) Index (Caliński and Harabasz, 1974):** Evaluates the ratio of between-cluster variance to within-cluster variance (higher values indicate better-defined clusters).
 - **Davies-Bouldin (DB) Index (Davies and Bouldin, 1979):** Computes the average similarity measure of each cluster with its most similar cluster (lower values indicate better clustering).
 These metrics were applied layer-wise (VLM Backbone, and cross-attention layers CA0 to CA14) and across diffusion timesteps.
- **Cluster Consistency Metrics:**
 - **Overlap Coefficient (OC) and Jaccard Index (Verma and Aggarwal, 2020):** These metrics were calculated between all possible pairs of clusters from different layers and diffusion steps to track the evolution and stability of cluster assignments.
 - **Cluster Alignment:** To address label switching (Stephens, 2000) that can occur when clusters are computed independently across layers/timesteps, an alignment step using the **Hungarian algorithm (Kuhn, 1955)** for optimal bipartite matching was employed. This ensured consistent cluster identifiers for comparative analysis.

#### 3.5. Concept Generation

To enhance interpretability, a pipeline was developed to assign human-interpretable concepts to each cluster:

1. **Sample Selection:** For each cluster, the top-n (n=5, due to computational constraints) samples nearest to the cluster centroid were selected as representative.
2. **VLM-Assisted Labeling:** An external VLM (LLaVA 72B) was prompted to generate a short sentence summarizing each selected sample (comprising the textual command and 10 video frames). These individual summaries were then passed back to the VLM to synthesize a single, concise concept label for the entire cluster.
3. **Concept Evaluation:** The generated concepts were evaluated using the **CLIPSIM metric (Liu et al., 2024b)**, which measures the CLIP similarity between the final concept label and the single frames of each cluster sample.

Experiments were limited to the 10 most populous clusters per layer/cluster technique pair to manage computational resources.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The analysis of the GR00T N1.5 model using the LAVLA framework yielded several findings regarding latent space organization, clustering performance, and concept interpretability.

#### 4.1. Clustering Performance

The introduction of the cross-attention-based embedding-weighting mechanism consistently led to improvements in cluster quality.
- **Silhouette Score:** Increased by 62.1% without PCA and 24.0% with PCA when weighting was applied.
- **Davies-Bouldin (DB) Index:** Improved by approximately 8% in both scenarios (with and without PCA) with the weighting mechanism.
- **Caliński-Harabasz (CH) Index:** Showed improvement only when PCA was also applied, suggesting that in unweighted scenarios, a high number of single-sample clusters might inflate the score.

These improvements are attributed to the weighting mechanism's ability to focus on semantically meaningful backbone embeddings throughout the action diffusion process, even when action tokens are initially noisy.

#### 4.2. Latent Space Structure and Coherence

- **Dimensionality and Cluster Count:** The application of agglomerative clustering on high-dimensional, unreduced embeddings typically resulted in a large number of clusters, with only a few containing more than a minimal number of samples. This highlights the "curse of dimensionality." PCA dimensionality reduction significantly reduced the total number of extracted clusters.
- **Progression of Structure:** Latent representations exhibited increased structural coherence at deeper architectural layers and later diffusion timesteps. As the diffusion process neared completion, the latent space became more organized, leading to a reduction in the number of clusters. This suggests a consolidation of disparate embeddings into cohesive groups over time.
- **Effect of Weighting:** The organizing trend of the latent space was less prominent when the embedding-weighting mechanism was employed. This is likely because the weighted backbone embeddings direct samples towards specific clusters early in the process, reducing the need for extensive denoising iterations to resolve a structured latent space. Early cross-attention layers, which receive barely processed noisy action tokens, appeared unstructured without weighting, but showed improved organization with the weighting mechanism.

#### 4.3. Cluster Consistency

- **Stability Across Timesteps:** Cluster assignments demonstrated stability across different diffusion timesteps within the same layer. This consistency was particularly evident closer to the final outputs, a likely consequence of the diffusion process reducing noise over time.
- **Repeating Patterns:** The analysis of Overlap Coefficient and Jaccard Index revealed repeating patterns in consistency heatmaps, suggesting that individual layers might selectively focus on specific features (e.g., color representations vs. object-centric features) from the backbone embeddings. This indicates a specialized role for different layers in processing the visuolinguistic context.

#### 4.4. Latent Space Organization: Feature Disentanglement

The analysis suggested a progressive disentanglement of features within the latent space of the action decoder.

- **Temporal Characteristics:** The action diffusion head exhibited higher temporal separability among clusters compared to the VLM backbone. An increased mean within the top clusters suggested that the model discriminates between generalizable states encountered at specific intervals within episodes, indicating developing spatiotemporal awareness or a structured spatial representation of the task environment.
- **Kinematic Features:** Clusters demonstrated a separation of body part usage. A notable increase (approximately 20%) in mean intra-cluster Wasserstein distance was observed for arm joints (left and right) in the action decoder layers compared to the VLM backbone, indicating a clearer differentiation of arm movements. A similar, though less prominent, trend was observed for waist movements.

#### 4.5. Concept Generation Results

- **Concept Faithfulness:** PCA-based and weighted clustering variants generally yielded slightly more faithful concepts, with PCA consistently improving performance across layers as measured by CLIPSIM scores. However, overall CLIPSIM scores between the generated concepts and video frames remained in a narrow range (approximately 0.20-0.23).
- **Limitations and Feasibility:** The limited visual diversity and short duration of video clips in robotic manipulation datasets likely imposed an upper bound on achievable CLIPSIM scores. Consequently, the extracted concepts were often semantically similar, frequently revolving around basic actions like "picking up objects and placing them into a container." Despite these limitations, the experiment demonstrated the feasibility of constructing human-interpretable concepts from latent clusters. The persistence or evolution of concepts across layers, rather than being fixed, also suggests that the denoising process contributes to refining the conceptual understanding of macro tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The LAVLA framework and its application contribute to the field of robotic learning by advancing the interpretability of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models.

- **Enhanced Interpretability of VLAs:** LAVLA provides a systematic framework for analyzing the latent spaces of VLAs, particularly focusing on the action decoder, which is crucial for understanding how these models translate high-level commands and perceptions into low-level actions. By making these latent spaces more transparent, it addresses a key challenge in VLA research.
- **Improved Understanding of Action Denoising:** The embedding-weighting mechanism illuminates the connection between the VLM backbone's feature extraction and the action decoder's iterative denoising process. This mechanism has been demonstrated to significantly enhance representational clarity, especially in the early, noisy stages of action generation.
- **Insights into Feature Disentanglement:** The analysis reveals that VLA models progressively disentangle complex features. Specifically, latent clusters in the action decoder demonstrate a clear separation of spatiotemporal and kinematic features, with representations becoming more refined in middle layers and stabilizing towards the output. This provides insights into how the cross-attention mechanism effectively integrates visuolinguistic context.
- **Foundation for Trustworthy Robotic Systems:** By offering deeper insights into the internal reasoning of language-driven robotic systems, LAVLA moves towards increasing their trustworthiness. An enhanced understanding of VLA behavior can facilitate the identification of reasoning failures, predict potential out-of-distribution issues, and improve the overall safety and reliability of VLA deployment in real-world scenarios, where unverified actions can have significant consequences.
- **Guidance for Model Development:** The findings, such as the layer-wise consistency of cluster assignments and the differential focus of cross-attention layers on specific features, can inform future VLA model design and training strategies, potentially leading to more robust and interpretable architectures.
- **Generalizability of Methodology:** The authors hypothesize that the embedding-weighting mechanism could be beneficial for other interpretability frameworks, such as Sparse AutoEncoders (SAEs), suggesting a broader applicability of the developed techniques.
- **Ethical Implications:** By enhancing understanding of VLA inner workings, the framework supports the detection of biases and promotes responsible AI development in robotics. It uses publicly available data and models, posing no inherent risk to data privacy.
