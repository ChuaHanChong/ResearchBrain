# Guided Motion Diffusion for Controllable Human Motion Synthesis

> [!summary] Summary
> Guided Motion Diffusion (GMD) introduces a framework for human motion synthesis that integrates precise spatial constraints like trajectories, keyframes, and obstacle avoidance with natural language descriptions, achieving an FID score of 0.212 on HumanML3D and demonstrating improved coherence and adherence to spatial guidance over prior diffusion models.

## Problem

- State-of-the-art diffusion models for human motion synthesis struggle to incorporate precise spatial constraints like trajectories, keyframes, or obstacle avoidance.
- Motion incoherence, such as "foot skating," arises because the global trajectory, a small part of the high-dimensional motion representation, is often overlooked during denoising.
- Temporally sparse spatial guidance signals (e.g., individual keyframes) are frequently ignored by diffusion models, which treat them as noise.

## Method

- Introduces "==Emphasis Projection==" which explicitly increases the relative importance of spatial (trajectory-related) information within the motion representation.
- Develops "Dense Signal Propagation" that uses the DPM's denoiser to spread the influence of sparse spatial guidance, transforming it into a dense signal across the sequence.
- Proposes a two-stage pipeline: first, a dedicated trajectory DPM generates a dense global trajectory from sparse constraints, then a main motion DPM synthesizes detailed local poses conditioned on this trajectory.

## Results

- Achieved an FID score of **0.212** on the HumanML3D dataset for text-to-motion generation, outperforming state-of-the-art methods like MDM (**0.556**) and MLD (**0.473**).
- Emphasis projection reduced the "Foot skating ratio" to **0.128**, indicating significantly improved coherence between local poses and global trajectories compared to baselines (**0.262**).
- The complete GMD framework, incorporating dense signal propagation, effectively guides motion to sparse keyframe locations, reducing "Location error" to **0.020** compared to **0.049** for single-stage baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Addressing the imbalance in dimensionality between local pose and global trajectory information is crucial for achieving coherent motion synthesis in DPMs.
> - The DPM's own denoiser can be leveraged to effectively propagate sparse guidance signals densely across a sequence, enhancing adherence to constraints.
> - Using an "epsilon"-predicting diffusion model, particularly for components heavily reliant on classifier guidance, helps mitigate the model's inherent bias and improve controllability at late denoising steps.
