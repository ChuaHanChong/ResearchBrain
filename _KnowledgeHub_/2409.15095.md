# Whole-Body Teleoperation for Mobile Manipulation at Zero Added Cost

> [!summary] Summary
> MoMa-Teleop introduces a method for whole-body teleoperation of mobile manipulators that uses standard, low-cost interfaces by leveraging a pre-trained reinforcement learning agent to handle complex base and arm coordination. This approach leads to faster task completion, safer operation, and generates high-quality demonstration data that improves the generalization capabilities of learned robotic skills.

## Problem

- Teleoperating mobile manipulators is challenging due to their high degrees of freedom, leading to high cognitive load and slow task completion with traditional interfaces.
- Existing whole-body teleoperation solutions often require expensive, specialized hardware (e.g., exoskeletons, high-end motion capture systems) or impose restrictive operator workspaces.
- Collecting high-quality, consistent demonstration data for mobile manipulation via teleoperation is difficult, hindering the efficiency and generalization of robot learning from demonstration.

## Method

- **MoMa-Teleop** presents a modular ==teleoperation framework== that decouples human end-effector commands from the robot's whole-body coordination.
- It leverages a ==pre-trained reinforcement learning (RL) agent== (N²M²) to translate inferred end-effector motion plans into kinematically feasible and ==collision-aware== mobile base and arm movements.
- The system is compatible with standard, low-cost input devices like joysticks and allows for safe hand guidance, requiring no specialized hardware or restricted operator workspace.

## Results

- MoMa-Teleop achieved significantly faster task completion times (up to **45% faster**) compared to traditional joystick or hand guidance methods for complex tasks on two different mobile manipulators.
- The system enables safe kinesthetic teaching for mobile manipulators by dynamically avoiding collisions with the human operator and environment, leveraging the underlying reinforcement learning agent.
- Data collected using MoMa-Teleop, when used with an 'EE + N²M²' imitation learning policy, enabled skill acquisition from as few as **five demonstrations** and generalized successfully to novel environments with unseen obstacles or object heights.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Delegating complex mobile base and arm coordination to a pre-trained reinforcement learning agent enables human operators to focus solely on task-relevant end-effector motions.
> - Cost-effective, standard input devices can effectively control high-DoF mobile manipulators by inferring operator intent and offloading whole-body kinematics and collision avoidance to a learned policy.
> - Decoupling end-effector policies from base control during imitation learning significantly improves generalization of learned skills to novel environmental configurations, such as new obstacles or object positions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics is rapidly advancing towards intelligent systems capable of performing complex tasks in unstructured human environments, such as households. A critical bottleneck in achieving this goal is the efficient generation of diverse and high-quality robot demonstration data, which is essential for training modern robotic foundation models and teaching complex behaviors through imitation learning.

While significant progress has been made in teleoperation and kinesthetic teaching for static manipulators (e.g., industrial arms), extending these methods to **mobile manipulators** introduces substantial challenges. Mobile manipulators possess a large number of degrees of freedom (DoF) – combining a mobile base with a multi-jointed arm – making their control inherently complex and often overwhelming for human operators using standard input devices like joysticks or keyboards. The cognitive load associated with coordinating simultaneous base and arm movements is immense.

Existing solutions in the broader research landscape attempt to address this complexity through various means:
- **Specialized Hardware and Avatars:** Approaches like exoskeletons, or sophisticated avatars, aim to provide intuitive, whole-body control by mapping human movements directly to the robot. However, these are typically very expensive, highly specialized, and often robot-specific. They also suffer from the "embodiment mismatch" problem, where the human's morphology or kinematic limits do not align with the robot's, leading to awkward or inefficient movements.
- **Motion Tracking Systems:** Systems employing motion capture, RGB-D cameras, or VR interfaces,– offer more intuitive human-robot interfaces. While some alleviate the need for expensive exoskeletons, they still require specialized hardware setups (cameras, lighthouses) and restrict the operator to a "tracked workspace." This spatial confinement prevents the operator from moving freely with the robot over extended tasks, forcing them to operate from a distance, which can hinder precision and real-time feedback. They also still grapple with embodiment mismatch and can suffer from precision issues.
- **Traditional Teleoperation with Mode Switching:** Common joystick or keyboard interfaces for mobile manipulators often resort to mode-switching (e.g., between base control and arm control) using shoulder buttons. This sequential control significantly increases task completion time and cognitive burden, as it prevents simultaneous, continuous whole-body motions.
- **Kinesthetic Teaching:** Physically guiding the robot's joints avoids embodiment mismatch and can provide rich contact force data. While effective for static arms, it is generally impractical for heavy mobile platforms. Existing kinesthetic approaches for mobile manipulators, often lack environmental awareness, making them prone to collisions with obstacles or even the human operator.

This paper positions MoMa-Teleop as a breakthrough that addresses the core limitations of existing methods. By decoupling end-effector control from base control and delegating the latter to a pre-trained reinforcement learning agent, the authors propose a "zero added cost" solution that is robot-agnostic, requires no specialized hardware or workspace restrictions, and enables continuous, collision-aware whole-body teleoperation. This approach directly tackles the challenges of high DoF, cognitive load, and data quality for imitation learning, aiming to generate superior demonstration data that generalizes well.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of "Whole-Body Teleoperation for Mobile Manipulation at Zero Added Cost" is to develop a novel and highly accessible method for operating mobile manipulators that overcomes the inherent complexity and cost associated with existing teleoperation systems.

The key objectives can be summarized as follows:
- **Simplify Mobile Manipulator Operation:** Reduce the cognitive load on the human operator by allowing them to focus solely on the task-relevant end-effector motions.
- **Achieve Whole-Body Teleoperation with Zero Added Cost:** Enable full coordination of the mobile base and manipulator arm using standard, readily available interfaces (e.g., joysticks, hand guidance) without requiring expensive specialized hardware, avatars, or complex tracking systems.
- **Eliminate Workspace Restrictions:** Allow the human operator to move freely with the robot over spatially extended tasks, rather than being confined to a tracked area.
- **Generate High-Quality Demonstration Data:** Produce diverse, continuous, and kinematically feasible whole-body motion data that is suitable for efficient imitation learning.
- **Facilitate Generalization in Imitation Learning:** Develop a framework that enables learned skills to transfer to unseen settings (e.g., new obstacles, changed object positions) from a minimal number of demonstrations.

The primary motivations driving these objectives stem from the current bottlenecks in robot learning and deployment:
- **Data Collection Bottleneck:** The efficient collection of high-quality, diverse demonstration data is paramount for training robust robotic policies, especially in the context of emerging robotic foundation models. Current teleoperation methods for mobile manipulators are either too cumbersome, expensive, or produce suboptimal data due to embodiment mismatch or control limitations.
- **High Degrees of Freedom (DoF) Challenge:** Mobile manipulators have many DoF, making their simultaneous control by a human operator extremely difficult and prone to errors. This leads to slow task completion and high cognitive burden.
- **Cost and Accessibility Barriers:** Expensive and specialized teleoperation hardware (exoskeletons, high-end motion capture) limits the widespread adoption and accessibility of advanced teleoperation for research and practical applications.
- **Safety Concerns in Kinesthetic Teaching:** While intuitive, physically guiding a mobile manipulator without intelligent collision avoidance is risky for both the robot and the human.
- **Lack of Generalization in Learned Skills:** Directly imitating raw whole-body motions often leads to policies that are brittle and fail to generalize to minor changes in the environment, requiring extensive data collection for every new scenario. The authors aim to address this by leveraging a learned base agent for robust execution.

By addressing these motivations, MoMa-Teleop seeks to democratize mobile manipulation control and accelerate the development of autonomous robotic capabilities through efficient, high-quality data generation and generalizable skill learning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MoMa-Teleop employs a novel modular architecture that decouples the operator's input from the complex whole-body coordination of a mobile manipulator. The core idea is to infer the operator's task-relevant end-effector motions and delegate the intricate base and arm joint movements to a pre-trained reinforcement learning (RL) agent.

The system comprises three main components:
1. **User Interface:** Gathers 6-DoF signals from various standard input devices.
2. **Inference Module:** Translates these signals into a short-term end-effector motion plan.
3. **RL Base Agent:** Converts the end-effector motion plan into whole-body commands, ensuring kinematic feasibility and obstacle avoidance.

**A. Background: Learning Feasible Base Motions (N²M² Agent)**
The foundation of MoMa-Teleop is the reuse of the authors' previously developed N²M² (Navigation for Mobile Manipulation) reinforcement learning agent. This agent is pre-trained to effectively decouple end-effector motions from the remaining joint movements of the mobile manipulator.
- **Input to Agent:** The agent receives a desired end-effector velocity vector ($\vec{v}_{ee}$) and a more distant 6-DoF end-effector subgoal (g) in the robot's base frame. This "look-ahead" capability allows the agent to anticipate the operator's longer-term intentions.
- **Agent's Output:** Based on these inputs, the RL agent generates velocities for the robot's base ($\vec{v}_b$) and torso ($v_{torso}$). Inverse kinematics (IK) is then used to compute the required joint velocities for the arm.
- **Key Capabilities:** The N²M² agent learns to regularize the end-effector speed, dynamically avoid obstacles (using a local occupancy map from LiDAR), and generalize to unseen end-effector motions and dynamic environments. This pre-trained intelligence is crucial for alleviating the human's cognitive burden and ensuring safe, compliant motion.

**B. Interfaces**
MoMa-Teleop is designed to be compatible with readily available, low-cost interfaces, ensuring "zero added cost" and no workspace restrictions for the operator:
- **Joystick:** A standard Dualshock3 joystick is used. The 6-DoF end-effector control (translation and orientation) is comfortably mapped to the joystick's sticks and shoulder buttons. A dedicated button allows switching to a high-precision mode, which restricts the base agent's velocity scaling. Gripper control is also included. This significantly reduces the button complexity compared to traditional multi-mode joystick teleoperation.
- **Hand Guidance (Kinesthetic Teaching):** The operator physically guides the robot's end-effector. Changes in the end-effector's pose (translation and orientation) are tracked as motion signals. A deadman switch on the end-effector ensures safety. Crucially, the underlying N²M² base agent's obstacle avoidance capabilities are leveraged to detect the human operator (via LiDAR) and other obstacles, enabling safe, collision-free kinesthetic teaching. This is particularly valuable for collecting contact force data for imitation learning.
- **Arbitrary 6-DoF Input Modalities:** The modular design ensures compatibility with any device capable of generating 6-DoF input signals, such as VR controllers or SpaceMouses, without needing specific implementations.

**C. Inferring End-Effector Motions**
The raw 6-DoF signals (translational velocity $\vec{v}_{signal}$ and orientation change $q_{signal}$) from the interfaces are transformed into a future end-effector motion plan ($m_{ee}$). This plan is a sequence of 6D end-effector poses extrapolated over a certain distance (up to 1.5m) at a fixed resolution (0.1m).
- **Joystick Input Processing:** Raw joystick inputs are mapped to translation and rotation commands. The translational velocity vector is normalized to match the RL agent's training resolution, and angular velocities are scaled. The high-precision mode reduces the planning horizon and constrains the agent's velocity scaling.
- **Hand Guidance Input Processing:** End-effector pose history is recorded and smoothed using a weighted sum of first differences for translation and a weighted average of quaternions for orientation. These smoothed signals are then re-normalized to match the agent's expected input range. This process ensures robust signal generation despite potential human noise.
- **Functional Form:** A linear dynamic system is used to extrapolate the inferred signals into the future end-effector motion ($m_{ee}$). This provides a smooth and predictable trajectory for the RL agent to follow, enabling it to make anticipatory decisions. The plan length adapts to the magnitude of the velocity signal.

**D. RL Base Agent**
The generated end-effector motion plan ($m_{ee}$) is continuously fed to the pre-trained N²M² base agent. The agent is active only when the human operator provides input and immediately pauses when the signal stops, ensuring responsiveness and safety. This system allows for continuous, fluid whole-body motions, avoiding the jerky, sequential movements of traditional teleoperation.

**E. Imitation Learning Integration**
The paper demonstrates the utility of the collected data for imitation learning, employing TAPAS-GMM, a state-of-the-art method based on Gaussian Mixture Models. Two imitation policies are explored:
- **Whole-Body Imitation:** This policy directly learns and jointly fits the GMM to both the recorded end-effector and base poses. During inference, it uses inverse kinematics to generate full whole-body joint commands.
- **EE + N²M² Policy:** This policy is the paper's key innovation for generalization. It only models the gripper action and end-effector poses from the collected data. The learned end-effector motions are then fed into the *reused* N²M² base agent, which handles the conversion to whole-body motions. This separation allows the learned skill to be task-centric (end-effector motions) while the base agent handles environment-specific challenges (kinematic feasibility, obstacle avoidance).

The overall methodology aims to create a highly efficient, safe, and cost-effective teleoperation system that not only simplifies human control but also serves as a powerful tool for generating data for advanced robot learning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents extensive real-world experiments on two different mobile manipulators—an **HSR robot** (8-DoF) and an **FMM robot** (11-DoF)—across a diverse range of 8 tasks, including pick-and-place, microwave operation, door opening (inward/outward), cleaning, and opening various cabinets/toolboxes. The results consistently demonstrate the superiority of MoMa-Teleop in terms of efficiency, robustness, and data quality for imitation learning.

**A. Teleoperation Evaluation:**
- **Comparative Performance:** MoMa-Teleop was compared against several baselines:
 - **HSR:** Traditional Joystick teleoperation, Vision Tracking, VR Tracking.
 - **FMM:** Static Hand Guidance + Joystick, Vision Tracking, VR Tracking.
- **Task Completion Time:** MoMa-Teleop achieved significantly faster task completion times across most tasks. For expert users, it was faster than traditional joystick/hand guidance on 6 out of 8 tasks. For instance, on the HSR, MoMa-Teleop completed "Microwave" ~14% faster and "Toolbox" ~34% faster than traditional Joystick. On the FMM, "Door Outwards" was completed ~45% faster and "Clean Table" ~10% faster than Hand Guidance + Joystick. This demonstrates its efficiency and fluidity of control.
- **Success Rate:** MoMa-Teleop consistently achieved high success rates (95% average across tasks and robots), comparable to traditional joystick/hand guidance (100% and 90% respectively).
- **Limitations of Baselines:**
 - **Tracking Approaches (Vision/VR):** While sometimes faster for simple pick-and-place, they proved imprecise and unreliable for tasks requiring high precision, larger movements, or operating in confined spaces. They suffered from limited operator workspace, difficulties with distant operation, and embodiment mismatch, leading to frequent safety limit violations and emergency stops. Consequently, they were not evaluated on more complex tasks due to hardware safety concerns.
 - **Traditional Joystick/Hand Guidance:** These methods were robust in success but significantly slower due to the need for frequent mode-switching (joystick) or repeated base repositioning (hand guidance), breaking the flow of complex tasks (e.g., opening a door while moving).
- **Safety with Kinesthetic Teaching:** The base agent's dynamic obstacle avoidance was confirmed to ensure safe operation during hand guidance, proactively avoiding collisions with the human operator and the environment.

**B. User Study:**
- A user study with six participants on the FMM robot for "Door Outwards" and "Fridge P&P" tasks confirmed the findings for expert users.
- MoMa-Teleop reduced average task completion time for new users by almost 40% compared to static hand guidance. This highlights its ease of use and ability to reduce cognitive load even for novice operators.

**C. Imitation Learning from Collected Data:**
- **Data Quality:** Data collected using MoMa-Teleop consistently yielded smooth and consistent whole-body motions. In contrast, data from traditional hand guidance, especially for tasks requiring large base movements (e.g., "Door Outwards"), often led to inconsistent trajectories due to varying base repositioning strategies by the operator. This inconsistency made it difficult for imitation learning models to capture the underlying task structure, requiring more data or prior operator planning.
- **Learning Efficiency:** The high quality and consistency of MoMa-Teleop's data enabled efficient learning from as few as five demonstrations. The combined data collection and model fitting took less than ten minutes.
- **Generalization to New Contexts:**
 - The **"EE + N²M²" policy**, which models only end-effector motions and reuses the learned base agent, demonstrated excellent generalization. It successfully adapted to unseen scenarios such as an object placed at a different height (e.g., "Open Drawer High") and new obstacles in the environment (e.g., "Clean Table" with varying obstacle positions), with no performance drop. This is because the end-effector motions are task-centric, and the pre-trained base agent dynamically handles kinematic feasibility and obstacle avoidance in novel contexts.
 - The **"Whole-Body Imitation" policy**, which jointly models end-effector and base motions, failed to generalize to these new scenarios. The tight coupling of base and arm motions in this model meant that changes in the environment (like a new object height or obstacle) broke the learned movement patterns, leading to collisions or failure to reach target poses.

In summary, MoMa-Teleop consistently outperforms existing teleoperation methods in terms of efficiency and user experience. Crucially, it generates superior demonstration data that, when combined with a modular imitation learning approach, enables rapid and generalizable skill acquisition for mobile manipulation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Whole-Body Teleoperation for Mobile Manipulation at Zero Added Cost" paper presents a significant advancement in robotic control and learning, with profound implications across several domains:

**A. Democratization and Accessibility of Mobile Manipulation:**
- **Low Cost and Accessibility:** By enabling whole-body teleoperation with standard, readily available interfaces like joysticks or simple hand guidance, MoMa-Teleop eliminates the prohibitive costs and complex setups associated with specialized hardware (exoskeletons, high-end motion capture, VR systems). This significantly lowers the barrier to entry for research groups, educators, and potentially even end-users interested in mobile manipulation, making advanced robot control more accessible.
- **Reduced Cognitive Load:** The delegation of complex base and arm coordination to an intelligent agent frees the operator to focus solely on the end-effector's task-relevant movements. This reduction in cognitive load makes robot operation more intuitive, less tiring, and accessible to a wider range of users, including those without extensive robotics experience.

**B. Enhanced Efficiency and Safety in Teleoperation:**
- **Faster Task Completion:** The experimental results demonstrate a significant reduction in task completion times, both for expert and novel users. This efficiency is crucial for rapid prototyping of robot behaviors, real-world deployment, and
 data collection at scale.
- **Continuous Whole-Body Motions:** Unlike traditional methods requiring mode-switching, MoMa-Teleop enables continuous and fluid whole-body motions. This allows for seamless execution of complex, spatially extended tasks (e.g., opening a door while driving through it) without interruptions for base repositioning or re-grasping.
- **Safe Kinesthetic Teaching:** The integration of the base agent's dynamic obstacle avoidance capabilities makes kinesthetic teaching of mobile manipulators safe for the first time. The robot can proactively avoid collisions with the human demonstrator, enabling the collection of valuable contact force data for delicate manipulation tasks.

**C. Advancing Robot Learning from Demonstration:**
- **High-Quality Data Generation:** MoMa-Teleop provides a robust and consistent method for generating high-quality, kinematically feasible whole-body demonstration data. This data is free from embodiment mismatch, ensuring that the collected motions accurately reflect the robot's capabilities. The consistency of the generated data, regardless of operator proficiency, addresses a major challenge in robot learning.
- **Efficient Few-Shot Learning:** The quality and structure of the collected data, combined with the "EE + N²M²" imitation learning policy, enable rapid skill acquisition from as few as five demonstrations. This dramatically reduces the data requirements for teaching new mobile manipulation tasks, accelerating the development of robotic skills.
- **Improved Generalization:** The paper's most significant contribution to imitation learning is demonstrating how decoupling the end-effector policy from the base policy (by reusing the pre-trained N²M² agent) leads to remarkable generalization. Learned end-effector skills can directly transfer to unseen environments with new obstacles or varied object positions, as the base agent dynamically handles the robot's interaction with the environment. This paradigm offers a pathway towards more robust and adaptable robotic intelligence.

**D. Foundation for Future Research and Applications:**
- **Scaling Up Robot Learning:** By simplifying data collection and improving data quality, MoMa-Teleop can facilitate the creation of large-scale mobile manipulation datasets, which are essential for training next-generation robotic foundation models.
- **Human-Robot Collaboration:** The intuitive control and inherent safety features make MoMa-Teleop a promising tool for developing more effective human-robot collaboration paradigms in various settings, from industrial assistance to household robotics.
- **Open-Source Contribution:** The decision to make the code publicly available is highly significant. It will enable other researchers to reproduce the results, build upon this work, and accelerate progress in mobile manipulation and robot learning, fostering a collaborative research ecosystem.

In conclusion, MoMa-Teleop represents a pivotal step towards making intelligent mobile manipulation practical and widely accessible. By ingeniously combining human intuition with learned robotic intelligence, it addresses critical challenges in control, data generation, and skill generalization, paving the way for more capable and autonomous robots in the real world.
