# EMPIRE: Explicit Manipulation Planning as a Learnable Intermediate Representation for Egocentric Hand-Motion Forecasting

> [!summary] Summary
> EMPIRE forecasts five-second bimanual hand motion from a single egocentric RGB frame by inserting an explicit manipulation plan as a learnable intermediate. Stage I fine-tunes a PaliGemma-2 VLM to emit ordered per-hand sub-actions from RGB, monocular depth, caption and camera FoV; Stage II freezes it and trains a flow-matching DiT on the plan's hidden states, reaching 84.53 mm MPJPE.

## Problem

- Prior VLM-based forecasters like VITRA and Being-H0 map observations directly to future motion, leaving the intermediate manipulation process that governs hand-object interaction only implicitly supervised.
- End-to-end joint optimization lets motion-generation gradients overwrite manipulation-aware VLM representations, so the generator conditions on a feature space that keeps shifting during training.
- General-purpose VLMs are unreliable at metric distance and 3D spatial reasoning, and existing egocentric hand datasets lack per-hand plan supervision aligned to long-horizon forecasting windows.

## Method

- Stage I 'Learn to Plan': a ==PaliGemma-2-3B== planner takes a multimodal prefix of ==SigLIP== RGB, ==Depth-Anything-3== depth, caption and FoV tokens, and autoregressively decodes ordered per-hand sub-action plans.
- Stage II 'Learn to Act': the planner is frozen and a 182M ==DiT-B== action expert cross-attends to plan-span hidden states plus the current hand state, trained by ==flow matching==.
- **EMPIRE-651K** dataset: ==EgoDex== skeletons fitted to ==MANO==, split into 650,910 five-second windows over 111 tasks, with ==Qwen2.5-VL== captions and plans filtered by ==Qwen3== consistency labeling.

## Results

- On **6,836** held-out windows EMPIRE reaches **84.53 mm** MPJPE and **38.97 mm** finger-relative error, cutting VITRA's MPJPE **19.8%** and matching Being-H0-14B at **83.5x** faster inference (**1.0 s**/window).
- Ablations: adding the plan drops MPJPE from **124.52** to **102.26 mm**, concatenated depth to **95.42 mm**; freezing the VLM beats a trainable two-stage variant, **94.67** versus **118.44 mm**.
- Planning helps **89/111** tasks, averaging **22.3 mm** gain that scales with difficulty (**4.6 mm** easy, **49.7 mm** hard); training costs **71** versus **116 GPU-hours**, **38.8%** less.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An explicit, human-readable intermediate plan buys more than extra parameters: a 3B planner-actor matched a 15B autoregressive model on accuracy and beat it on finger detail.
> - Freezing the semantic backbone after plan learning is a performance gain, not just a compute saving, because low-level motion gradients otherwise erode pretrained interaction-aware structure.
> - Condition downstream modules on predicted, not ground-truth, intermediates to close the train-deploy gap; and expect structured guidance to pay off mainly on hard, unseen, long-horizon tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

This work operates within the domain of egocentric hand-motion forecasting, a foundational component for intelligent interactive systems with applications in robot learning, virtual and augmented reality, and human-robot collaboration. Recent advancements in this field have leveraged large-scale egocentric datasets and Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to predict dexterous hand movements from egocentric observations.

Existing approaches primarily follow two paradigms:
1. **Diffusion-based methods** (e.g., VITRA) which generate continuous hand motion conditioned on VLM representations.
2. **Autoregressive models** (e.g., Being-H0) which jointly model multimodal observations and discretized future hand motion in a unified sequence.

Both paradigms typically share a common design: directly mapping VLM representations to future hand motion without explicitly modeling the intermediate manipulation process that drives hand-object interactions. This indirect approach leads to two identified limitations:
1. **Implicit supervision of manipulation planning:** Models infer manipulation planning from final trajectory or motion-token sequences. This implicit learning can lead to locally plausible but globally inconsistent motions, especially in long-horizon tasks where early planning errors can accumulate.
2. **Coupled optimization of semantic learning and motion generation:** Existing methods often jointly optimize the VLM backbone and the motion generation model. This coupling can cause motion-generation gradients to interfere with pre-learned manipulation-aware semantic representations, potentially weakening the VLM's ability to provide stable guidance for scene understanding and interaction anticipation.

EMPIRE is presented as a framework designed to address these limitations by introducing an *explicit manipulation plan* as a learnable intermediate representation and employing a *decoupled two-stage optimization* strategy. This positions EMPIRE as an evolution in egocentric hand-motion forecasting, aiming to provide more stable, interpretable, and accurate long-horizon predictions by directly modeling the manipulation process.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the accuracy and efficiency of forecasting dexterous bimanual hand motions from egocentric observations, particularly for long-horizon tasks involving complex hand-object interactions and fine-grained finger articulation.

This objective is motivated by two identified limitations in existing VLM-based hand-motion forecasting methods:

1. **Addressing Implicit Manipulation Planning:** Current methods map visual-language observations directly to future hand motions, implicitly inferring the underlying manipulation process. The motivation for EMPIRE is that accurate and consistent long-horizon forecasting requires an explicit understanding of the sequence of manipulation decisions (e.g., object interaction, hand coordination, task progression). Without an explicit representation of the manipulation process, generated motions may be locally plausible but deviate from the intended interaction progression over time. EMPIRE aims to provide this explicit representation through a structured, motion-oriented manipulation plan, thereby offering stable, interpretable guidance for motion generation.

2. **Overcoming Coupled Optimization:** Existing approaches often jointly optimize the VLM backbone (responsible for semantic understanding) and the motion generation model. This coupled optimization can lead to several issues:
 - Motion-generation gradients may continually update the VLM's semantic representation, forcing the generator to adapt to a dynamically changing feature space.
 - Optimizing directly for spatial motion objectives might overwrite or degrade the manipulation-aware structure acquired during VLM pretraining, thereby weakening the representation crucial for scene understanding and anticipating interactions.
 EMPIRE's motivation is to decouple these learning processes. By freezing the VLM during motion generation, the framework aims to preserve the integrity of manipulation-aware representations, ensuring stable semantic conditioning for the motion generator.

A supporting objective, crucial for enabling the proposed explicit planning framework, is the construction of a large-scale, bimanual hand-motion forecasting dataset, EMPIRE-651K. This dataset augments existing egocentric video data with explicit, per-hand manipulation plans, providing the necessary supervision for learning the intermediate planning representation.

### 3. Methodology and approach

EMPIRE operates as a two-stage "plan-then-act" pipeline, comprising a Planner and an Actor.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The task is defined as predicting a future two-hand trajectory $A = (a^{(1)}, \ldots, a^{(T)})$ given an egocentric observation $O = (I, c, f)$ (RGB image, coarse caption, camera FoV) and the current two-hand state $s_0$. Instead of directly learning $p(A | O, s_0)$, EMPIRE introduces a structured motion plan $P$ as an intermediate representation, decomposing the problem into $p(A | O, s_0) = \sum_P p_{\theta}(A | H_P, s_0) p_{\phi}(P | O)$. Here, $p_{\phi}$ is the Planner and $p_{\theta}$ is the Actor, with $H_P$ representing the hidden states of the Planner's tokens.

#### 3.2. Stage I: Learn to Plan (Planner)

- **Model:** A PaliGemma-2 VLM is used as the Planner.
- **Input:** Multimodal context is constructed into a prefix $Z$. This includes:
 - RGB tokens from a frozen SigLIP encoder for semantic appearance.
 - Inferred monocular depth features from Depth Anything3 via a trainable Depth Mapper for geometric context.
 - Caption tokens.
 - FoV token from a trainable FoV Encoder for camera configuration.
- **Output:** The Planner predicts an explicit, motion-oriented plan $P$. This plan decomposes the coarse action caption into temporally ordered, hand-specific sub-actions (e.g., reaching, stabilizing, grasping, lifting, placing). The hidden states $H_{plan}$ corresponding to the generated plan span serve as the planning interface.
- **Training:** The VLM is fine-tuned for one epoch. The visual (SigLIP) and depth (Depth Anything3) encoders remain frozen, while the VLM's decoder and lightweight modality adapters (Depth Mapper, FoV Encoder) are optimized. The objective is autoregressive next-token prediction over the plan, using a language modeling loss $L_{plan}$.

#### 3.3. Stage II: Learn to Act (Actor)

- **Model:** A flow-matching Diffusion Transformer (DiT) serves as the Actor.
- **Input:**
 - The current two-hand state $s_0$, encoded by $E_{state}$ to $Z_{state}$.
 - A manipulation representation $Z_{plan}$, derived from the hidden states $H_{plan}$ of the plan $\hat{P}$ predicted by the *frozen* Stage-I Planner for each training window. $H_{plan}$ is mapped to the Actor's conditioning space via a lightweight projector $G_{\psi}$.
- **Process:** The DiT treats noisy motion tokens as queries, cross-attends to $Z_{plan}$ as keys and values, and uses $Z_{state}$ to condition on the initial pose.
- **Output:** The Actor synthesizes future two-hand motion $A$.
- **Training:** The Stage-I Planner is initialized with its learned parameters and *frozen* during Stage-II training. This prevents motion generation gradients from altering the learned planning representation. The DiT-based Actor and its condition projector $G_{\psi}$ are trained using a flow-matching objective $L_{act}$. The Actor predicts the velocity field from a linear interpolation $A_{\tau}$ (between Gaussian noise $\epsilon$ and ground-truth trajectory $A^{\star}$) back to $A^{\star} - \epsilon$. Critically, Stage II is trained on plans *predicted by Stage I* rather than ground-truth plans, aligning with the inference-time conditions and reducing the train-deployment gap.

#### 3.4. Deployment: Plan Then Act

During inference, the frozen Stage-I Planner first predicts the structured motion plan $\hat{P}$ online. The hidden states $H_{plan}$ of these generated plan tokens are extracted. The Stage-II Actor then conditions on $H_{plan}$ (via $G_{\psi}$) and the current hand state $s_0$ to generate the future motion.

#### 3.5. Dataset: EMPIRE-651K Construction

To support the method, EMPIRE-651K was constructed from the EgoDex dataset, comprising 650,910 training windows and 6,836 test windows across 111 manipulation tasks.
- **Skeleton-to-MANO conversion:** Raw ARKit 3D hand joint annotations from EgoDex were converted into MANO-based hand representations, optimizing for a 15-dimensional PCA pose and preserving wrist SE(3) transformations, ensuring temporally consistent two-hand motion targets.
- **Temporal window construction:** EgoDex recordings were divided into task-specific episodes and then into non-overlapping five-second windows (150 frames at 30 FPS, resampled to 60 frames at 12 FPS) for forecasting.
- **Caption and motion-plan annotation:** A two-stage VLM-assisted pipeline was used:
 1. **Caption generation:** Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct generated concise captions describing the dominant manipulation intent for each five-second segment.
 2. **Manipulation plan generation:** Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct, conditioned on the video segment and generated caption, produced structured plans decomposing interactions into temporally ordered, hand-specific sub-actions.
 3. **Annotation filtering:** Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct-2507 evaluated consistency between captions and plans; inconsistent samples were removed.
- **Human Annotation Audit:** A human audit of 100 randomly sampled training windows confirmed an overall annotation quality of 4.62/5 and an 84.0% usable-pair rate, indicating the reliability of the generated annotations.

### 4. Main findings and results

The research presented several findings regarding EMPIRE's performance, efficiency, and the contribution of its core components.

#### 4.1. Forecasting Accuracy

- EMPIRE achieved an MPJPE (Mean Per Joint Position Error) of 84.53 mm on the EMPIRE-651K test set. This represents an improvement compared to a re-implemented VITRA baseline (105.37 mm MPJPE), reducing the error by 19.8%.
- When compared to larger Being-H0 models (1B, 8B, 14B), EMPIRE demonstrated comparable or superior overall MPJPE, achieving 84.53 mm while Being-H0-14B (a much larger model) obtained 84.90 mm.
- Notably, EMPIRE exhibited a Finger-relative MPJPE of 38.97 mm, which was substantially lower than Being-H0-14B's 51.65 mm, indicating improved accuracy in fine-grained finger articulation. While Being-H0-14B had a slightly better Wrist MPJPE (66.54 mm vs. EMPIRE's 73.25 mm), EMPIRE's overall performance was competitive.
- Qualitative comparisons suggested that EMPIRE maintained more stable and accurate hand-object interactions and finger articulations over long horizons compared to baselines.

#### 4.2. Training and Inference Efficiency

- **Training:** The two-stage training strategy of EMPIRE required 71 hours on 8 A40 GPUs, which was a 38.8% reduction in optimization time compared to the 116 hours required for the single-stage VITRA re-implementation. This efficiency gain was attributed to decoupling the expensive VLM backbone optimization from the lightweight DiT motion generator training.
- **Inference:** EMPIRE processed each forecasting window in 1.0 second. This was significantly faster than Being-H0 models, which ranged from 15.6 seconds (1B) to 83.5 seconds (14B) per window. EMPIRE's efficiency stems from generating a compact manipulation plan once, whose hidden states then condition multiple parallel DiT samples, avoiding the numerous sequential token generations required by autoregressive models like Being-H0.

#### 4.3. Impact of Explicit Manipulation Planning

- **Core Contribution:** Introducing the explicit motion plan significantly reduced MPJPE. In an ablation study on a 26-task subset, adding the motion plan reduced overall MPJPE from 124.52 mm (caption-only VITRA re-implementation) to 102.26 mm.
- **Generalization:** The improvement was particularly pronounced on unseen tasks, where the error decreased from 129.73 mm to 104.80 mm, suggesting that explicit plans provide transferable temporal guidance beyond purely semantic information.
- **Task Difficulty:** The benefit of explicit planning increased with task complexity. MPJPE reduction grew from 4.6 mm on easy tasks to 49.7 mm on hard tasks. Planning improved 89 out of 111 tasks, achieving an average MPJPE reduction of 22.3 mm.

#### 4.4. Contribution of Monocular Depth Features

- Direct concatenation of monocular depth features into the multimodal prefix improved performance, reducing overall MPJPE to 95.42 mm in ablation studies. This was more effective than using depth features via cross-attention. The depth-augmented model primarily benefited unseen tasks.

#### 4.5. Decoupled Optimization Strategy

- Ablations confirmed the advantage of isolating motion gradients. Freezing the VLM after Stage I and optimizing only the motion generator reduced MPJPE to 94.67 mm, representing a 20.1% improvement compared to allowing motion gradients to update the VLM (118.44 mm MPJPE). This supports the hypothesis that stable semantic representations are crucial for motion generation.

#### 4.6. DiT Capacity

- The DiT-B (182M parameters) model for the Actor achieved the best balance between motion modeling capability and generalization. Smaller models (DiT-M) lacked capacity, while larger models (DiT-L) showed degraded generalization on unseen tasks, suggesting potential overfitting.

#### 4.7. Training Stage II with Predicted Plans

- Training the Stage II Actor with plans *predicted by the Stage I Planner* (as done during deployment) consistently improved performance compared to training with *ground-truth plans*. For the full 111 tasks, this strategy led to an MPJPE of 84.53 mm versus 90.22 mm, validating the approach of matching the inference-time conditioning distribution.

### 5. Significance and potential impact

The EMPIRE framework introduces a novel paradigm for egocentric hand-motion forecasting by explicitly integrating manipulation planning as a learnable intermediate representation. This approach has several significant implications and potential impacts:

1. **Enhanced Forecasting Accuracy and Robustness:** By explicitly modeling the manipulation process, EMPIRE achieves state-of-the-art accuracy, particularly in fine-grained finger articulation and for long-horizon tasks. The improvements are notable across tasks of varying difficulty and demonstrate better generalization to unseen scenarios, suggesting a more robust and transferable understanding of manipulation dynamics.

2. **Improved Efficiency in Training and Inference:** The two-stage, decoupled optimization strategy allows for more efficient training by freezing the computationally intensive VLM during the motion generation stage. Furthermore, EMPIRE's inference process is significantly faster than previous autoregressive models, making it more practical for real-time applications in intelligent interactive systems.

3. **Introduces an Interpretable Intermediate Representation:** The explicit manipulation plan offers an interpretable layer between high-level vision-language understanding and low-level motion synthesis. This interpretability can facilitate debugging, understanding model behavior, and potentially enabling human oversight or intervention in complex robotic tasks.

4. **Novel Dataset for Research:** The creation of EMPIRE-651K, a large-scale bimanual hand-motion forecasting dataset augmented with explicit manipulation plans, provides a valuable resource for the research community. This dataset enables further investigation into explicit planning, long-horizon motion forecasting, and multimodal learning for dexterous manipulation.

5. **Advancements in Embodied AI and Robotics:** The success of EMPIRE's plan-then-act paradigm suggests that integrating explicit high-level planning with decoupled low-level execution can be a powerful strategy for developing more capable and generalizable embodied AI systems. This could lead to more sophisticated robot learning agents capable of understanding and executing complex human-like dexterous manipulations in various environments, contributing to progress in areas such as human-robot collaboration, surgical robotics, and assistive technologies.

6. **Guidance for Future Model Architectures:** The findings regarding decoupled optimization and the benefits of a stable VLM representation provide architectural guidance for future multimodal models in complex sequential decision-making tasks, emphasizing the importance of dedicated modules for high-level reasoning and low-level control.
