# A Unifying View of Coverage in Linear Off-Policy Evaluation

> [!summary] Summary
> A new scale-invariant parameter, feature-dynamics coverage (C^ϕ), is developed for linear Off-Policy Evaluation (OPE) under realizability, offering finite-sample error bounds for LSTDQ. This parameter integrates disparate coverage concepts from tabular settings, state abstractions, and Bellman-complete function approximation into a single theoretical framework.

## Problem

- The theoretical understanding of coverage in linear Off-Policy Evaluation (OPE), especially when only the target value function is linearly realizable, remained fragmented and unclear.
- Existing candidate coverage parameters for LSTDQ, such as the inverse of the minimum singular value of matrix A, lacked scale invariance, a clear off-policy characterization, and failed to unify with established coverage notions from other RL settings.
- Error bounds for LSTDQ often presented complex, obscure conditions that were difficult to interpret as meaningful measures of coverage.

## Method

- LSTDQ was re-framed as an ==Instrumental Variable== (IV) problem, enabling the extension of linear regression insights to the discounted, off-policy setting.
- A novel finite-sample analysis of LSTDQ was conducted, leading to error bounds explicitly dependent on a new, interpretable coverage parameter, feature-dynamics coverage (C^

_π).
- The new parameter was rigorously defined and characterized to address the shortcomings of previous coverage candidates, demonstrating its scale invariance and off-policy interpretability.

## Results

- New finite-sample error bounds for LSTDQ's value estimation were provided, showing an error dependency of O(V_max/(1 − γ) * sqrt(C^

_π * log(1/δ)/n)) for population coverage and O(V_max/(1 − γ) * sqrt(hat{C}^

_π * (d + log(1/δ))/n)) for empirical coverage.
- It was demonstrated that feature-dynamics coverage (C^

_π) reduces to χ^2-divergence in the tabular setting, recovers aggregated concentrability for state abstractions, and simplifies to standard linear coverage under Bellman-complete function approximation.
- The new theoretical bounds are quantitatively sharper than existing guarantees from previous works, such as Perdomo et al. (2023), and provide dimension-free leading terms in certain scenarios.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Interpreting LSTDQ as an Instrumental Variable problem provides a powerful analytical framework for deriving finite-sample guarantees in discounted, off-policy contexts.
> - The introduced feature-dynamics coverage (C^

_π) is intrinsically scale-invariant and offers an intuitive measure of how well the data's feature evolution covers the target policy's needs.
> - C^

_π serves as a unifying concept, recovering established notions of coverage, such as χ^2-divergence in tabular settings, aggregated concentrability under state abstractions, and standard linear coverage under Bellman-completeness.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Off-policy evaluation (OPE) is a foundational task in reinforcement learning (RL) that involves estimating the performance of a target policy using data collected from a different behavior policy. A central challenge in OPE, and indeed in much of RL theory, is distribution shift, which is mathematically characterized by "coverage parameters" in finite-sample guarantees. These parameters quantify the degree to which data contains information relevant to the target policy. The concept of coverage is not confined to OPE, appearing in offline policy learning, online RL, and even considerations for large language models.

While a unified theoretical understanding of coverage has been emerging across various RL settings (e.g., policy evaluation vs. optimization, offline vs. online, tabular vs. function approximation), this paper identifies a significant gap in the literature concerning linear OPE. Specifically, when the target value function is linearly realizable in a given feature map—a minimal yet fundamental assumption—the understanding of coverage is described as lacking and fragmented. Existing analyses for algorithms like LSTDQ (Least-Squares Temporal Difference for Q-functions), a canonical method for this setting, often yield error bounds with obscure conditions or rely on coverage definitions that possess undesirable properties, such as a lack of scale invariance or clear off-policy characterization. Moreover, these definitions have not been successfully unified with more established notions of coverage from other settings (e.g., Bellman-complete function approximation or state abstractions).

This work positions itself to address this gap by providing a novel analysis of LSTDQ that introduces a new, interpretable coverage parameter capable of unifying previous disparate understandings within linear OPE.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a clearer and more unified theoretical foundation for coverage in linear off-policy evaluation, particularly under the minimal assumption that only the target value function is linearly realizable by the features.

The motivation stems from several limitations observed in the existing literature regarding coverage in this fundamental setting:

- **Fragmented Understanding:** While coverage is broadly recognized as crucial for OPE, its characterization in linear OPE settings, especially for algorithms like LSTDQ, is not well-understood. Error bounds often appear with complex conditions that are difficult to interpret as coverage measures.
- **Issues with Existing Coverage Candidates:** The most natural candidate for a coverage parameter in LSTDQ, the inverse of the minimum singular value of the key matrix `A` (i.e., `1/σ_min(A)`), has several deficiencies:
 - **Lack of Scale Invariance:** Its value arbitrarily changes with feature rescaling, which is problematic for a fundamental property like coverage.
 - **Lack of Off-Policy Characterization:** It does not provide an intuitive measure of how well the data distribution covers the target policy's needs in an off-policy context, and its behavior is only well-understood in restrictive on-policy scenarios.
 - **Lack of Unification:** It fails to recover established coverage parameters from related settings, such as aggregated concentrability for state abstractions or standard linear coverage under Bellman-completeness, indicating a disconnect in theoretical understanding.

To overcome these issues, the authors aim to:

- Provide a novel finite-sample analysis of LSTDQ.
- Develop error bounds that explicitly depend on a new, more interpretable coverage parameter.
- Demonstrate that this new parameter addresses the identified deficiencies, offering scale invariance, intuitive off-policy characterization, and the ability to unify various notions of coverage previously considered distinct or fragmented within the linear OPE landscape.

By achieving these objectives, the research seeks to consolidate and clarify the theoretical understanding of coverage, which is critical for the robustness and applicability of off-policy evaluation methods.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research focuses on analyzing LSTDQ (Least-Squares Temporal Difference for Q-functions), a canonical algorithm for linear OPE when the target value function is linearly realizable. The methodology involves:

1. **Re-framing LSTDQ from an Instrumental-Variable (IV) View:** The paper observes that LSTDQ can be interpreted as solving an Instrumental Variable problem. In this view, `Z = ϕ(s, a)` serves as the instrumental variable to address "error-in-the-variable" issues arising from using `X = ϕ(s, a) - γϕ(s', a')` to predict rewards `Y = r`, where the random realization of `ϕ(s', a')` introduces noise. This perspective is crucial for extending insights from linear regression to the discounted, off-policy setting.

2. **Finite-Sample Analysis for $\gamma = 0$ (Contextual Bandits):** The analysis begins with the simpler case of $\gamma=0$, which corresponds to a contextual bandit problem with linear rewards. Here, LSTDQ reduces to linear regression. For estimating the target policy's value `J(π)`, which involves evaluating `ϕ_0^T θ_lstd` (where `ϕ_0` is the expected initial feature vector), the analysis demonstrates that the error can be bounded by `∥ϕ_0∥_Σ^{−1}`, reflecting how well the target initial features are covered by the data covariance `Σ`. This "directional" bound is noted to be dimension-free in its main term, unlike general function-estimation bounds.

3. **Extension to $\gamma > 0$ using IV Literature:** Leveraging the IV interpretation, the paper extends the finite-sample analysis from the $\gamma=0$ case to the general $\gamma > 0$ setting. This involves adapting concentration bounds from the IV literature to derive error guarantees for LSTDQ.

4. **Derivation of New Coverage Parameter:** Through this analysis, a novel coverage parameter is naturally identified: **feature-dynamics coverage ($C^\phi_\pi$)**.
 - **Population Version ($C^\phi_\pi$):** Defined as `(1 − γ)^2 ϕ_0^T A^{−1} Σ A^{−T} ϕ_0`, where `A = Σ − γΣ_cr`, `Σ = E_μD[ϕ(s, a)ϕ(s, a)^T]`, `Σ_cr = E_μD[ϕ(s, a)ϕ(s', a')^T]`, and `ϕ_0 = E_{s_0∼μ_0,a_0∼π}[ϕ(s_0, a_0)]`.
 - **Empirical Version ($\hat{C}^\phi_\pi$):** Defined analogously using empirical estimates of `A` and `Σ`.

5. **Formulation of Error Bounds:** The methodology yields two main types of finite-sample error bounds for `|J_Q_lstd(π) − J(π)|`:
 - **Population Coverage Bound (Theorem 1):** `O(V_max/(1 − γ) * sqrt(C^\phi_π * log(1/δ)/n) + o(sqrt(1/n)))`. This bound has a lower-order term that depends on `d` and `1/σ_min(A)`.
 - **Empirical Coverage Bound (Theorem 2):** `O(V_max/(1 − γ) * sqrt(hat{C}^\phi_π * (d + log(1/δ))/n))`. This bound depends on the empirical coverage parameter and does not have lower-order terms but includes a `d` dependence in the leading term. A further variation (Theorem 4) is introduced that removes `1/σ_min(A)` dependence in the population bound at the cost of `1/λ_min(Σ)` dependence and requiring a loss-minimization form of LSTDQ.

6. **Interpretation and Unification:** A significant part of the methodology involves interpreting $C^\phi_\pi$ and demonstrating its unifying properties by:
 - Showing its reduction to $\chi^2$-divergence in the tabular setting.
 - Interpreting it as feature coverage in a linear dynamical system defined by `B_π = (Σ^{−1}Σ_cr)^T`.
 - Formally demonstrating how it recovers aggregated concentrability under state abstraction features and standard linear coverage ($C^\text{lin}_\pi$) under Bellman-completeness, thus unifying previously distinct notions.
 - Comparing its quantitative sharpness to existing bounds, such as those by Perdomo et al. (2023).

The analysis leverages matrix and vector Bernstein inequalities for concentration bounds, coupled with covering arguments for handling function estimation and general parameter spaces.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The core contribution of this work is the introduction and characterization of **feature-dynamics coverage ($C^\phi_\pi$)** as a unified coverage parameter for linear Off-Policy Evaluation (OPE) under the minimal assumption of realizability.

1. **Novel Finite-Sample Guarantees for LSTDQ:**
 - The paper provides new high-probability finite-sample error bounds for LSTDQ's performance in estimating `J(π)`.
 - **Theorem 1 (Population Coverage Bound):** Under certain sample size conditions, the evaluation error is bounded by `O(V_max/(1 − γ) * sqrt(C^\phi_π * log(1/δ)/n))` with an additional lower-order `o(sqrt(1/n))` term that may depend on `d` and `1/σ_min(A)`.
 - **Theorem 2 (Empirical Coverage Bound):** A tighter bound explicitly uses an empirical estimate of the coverage parameter, $\hat{C}^\phi_\pi$, and is `O(V_max/(1 − γ) * sqrt(hat{C}^\phi_π * (d + log(1/δ))/n))`. This bound directly reflects the observed data properties and does not have lower-order terms, though its leading term includes `d` dependence.
 - These bounds are established to be tight, recovering known dimension-free results for contextual bandits (the $\gamma=0$ case) up to lower-order terms.

2. **Introduction of Feature-Dynamics Coverage ($C^\phi_\pi$):**
 - The parameter is defined as `C^\phi_π := (1 − γ)^2 ϕ_0^T A^{−1} Σ A^{−T} ϕ_0`, where `ϕ_0` is the expected initial feature vector, `Σ` is the feature covariance, and `A = Σ − γΣ_cr` (with `Σ_cr` being the cross-feature covariance).
 - It naturally inherits **scale invariance**, as the `ϕ_0` term and the matrix inversion appropriately normalize feature scaling, addressing a major shortcoming of prior candidate parameters like `1/σ_min(A)`.
 - It provides an explicit **off-policy characterization** that is interpretable, as detailed below.

3. **Unified Interpretation of Coverage Parameters:** This is a central finding, showing how $C^\phi_\pi$ connects previously disparate notions of coverage:
 - **Tabular Setting:** In the tabular case (where features are one-hot encodings of states and actions), $C^\phi_\pi$ simplifies to `E_μD[(μ_π/μ_D)^2]`, which is the $\chi^2$-divergence between the target policy's discounted occupancy `μ_π` and the data distribution `μ_D`. This aligns with established $\chi^2$-type coverage measures in tabular OPE.
 - **General Interpretation (Proposition 1):** $C^\phi_\pi$ is shown to be equivalent to `(μ^\phi_π)^T Σ^{−1} μ^\phi_π`, where `μ^\phi_π` is a "feature occupancy" vector. This `μ^\phi_π` is derived from a deterministic linear dynamical system `μ^\phi_{π,t+1} = B_π μ^\phi_{π,t}` with initial state `μ^\phi_{π,0} = ϕ_0`, where `B_π = (Σ^{−1}Σ_cr)^T`. This interpretation posits coverage as how well the "feature evolution" under `B_π` is covered by the data.
 - **Aggregated Concentrability (Proposition 4):** When features correspond to state abstractions and the target policy is consistent with this abstraction, $C^\phi_\pi$ recovers the $\chi^2$ version of aggregated concentrability. This result clarifies the theoretical basis of coverage in state abstraction methods which use compressed dynamics.
 - **Standard Linear Coverage under Bellman-Completeness (Proposition 5):** When the function class `F_ϕ` is Bellman-complete (a stronger assumption than just realizability), $C^\phi_\pi$ is shown to simplify to `(ϕ_π)^T Σ^{−1} ϕ_π`, which is the established notion of linear coverage ($C^\text{lin}_\pi$). This demonstrates that error propagation through the true MDP dynamics (as captured by `ϕ_π`) is a special case of propagation through the feature-induced compressed dynamics. This finding resolves a long-standing disconnect in the literature.
 - **Novel On-Policy Condition (Proposition 2):** The paper introduces a new, weaker on-policy condition for tractability: if `ϕ` includes a bias term and `E_μD[ϕ(s, a)] = E_μD[ϕ(s', π)] = ϕ_0`, then $C^\phi_\pi \le 1$.

4. **Sharper Bounds than Previous Works:**
 - A quantitative comparison (Proposition 3) shows that $C^\phi_\pi$ can lead to sharper return-estimation guarantees than those of Perdomo et al. (2023), particularly due to its sensitivity to `ϕ_0`'s alignment with the spectral properties of the matrix `(I − γΣ^{−1/2}Σ_crΣ^{−1/2})`.
 - Connections to Minimax Weight Learning (MWL) and Bellman Residual Minimization (BRM) are drawn, showing that LSTDQ (or its equivalent forms) with the new coverage parameter matches or improves upon existing guarantees for these algorithms, particularly by offering dimension-free leading terms in some cases.

5. **Function Estimation Guarantees (Theorem 3):** Beyond return estimation, the methodology also provides function estimation guarantees, bounding the expected squared error `E_ν[(Q_π(s, a) − hat{Q}_lstd(s, a))^2]` with a coverage parameter `C^\phi_{π,fn}` that extends $C^\phi_\pi$ to arbitrary evaluation distributions `ν`.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research provides a more coherent and robust theoretical framework for understanding off-policy evaluation, particularly in the foundational setting of linear function approximation with realizability. Its significance and potential impact are multifold:

1. **Unified Theoretical Foundation:** By introducing feature-dynamics coverage ($C^\phi_\pi$) and demonstrating its ability to recover established coverage concepts under different assumptions (tabular, state abstractions, Bellman-completeness), the paper unifies fragmented theoretical understandings of coverage in linear OPE. This unification simplifies the landscape of RL theory and facilitates cross-comparison of algorithms and settings.
2. **Clarification of Minimal Assumption Setting:** The work specifically addresses the challenging "minimal assumption" setting where only realizability is assumed, offering clear and interpretable coverage conditions where previous analyses were obscure. This is crucial for developing robust algorithms that do not rely on strong, often unrealistic, assumptions like Bellman-completeness.
3. **Improved Understanding of LSTDQ:** The detailed finite-sample analysis of LSTDQ provides sharper statistical guarantees and a deeper understanding of its behavior and limitations. This clarity can inform the development of improved LSTD variants or regularization techniques, especially when dealing with potentially problematic matrices (e.g., near-singular `A`).
4. **Practical Implications for Algorithm Design:** A clearer understanding of coverage parameters directly informs the design of more reliable and provably efficient OPE algorithms. Researchers can now better identify data conditions under which linear OPE is tractable and design algorithms that explicitly account for feature-dynamics coverage.
5. **Broader Impact on RL Theory:** The concept of "feature-dynamics" (the deterministic linear dynamical system induced by features `B_π`) could serve as a new lens for analyzing other RL problems beyond OPE. The method of using an Instrumental Variable view for analysis also suggests promising avenues for tackling similar challenges in diverse RL contexts, particularly in offline and batch settings where distribution shift is prevalent. The discussion on "dimension-free" bounds also has implications for understanding the sample efficiency of RL algorithms.
6. **Guidance for Offline Reinforcement Learning:** The paper's insights are particularly relevant for offline RL, where accurate OPE is essential for policy selection and model validation. By providing a more precise characterization of coverage, this work aids in predicting algorithm performance and identifying when offline data is sufficient for reliable learning.

In summary, this research advances the fundamental understanding of OPE by introducing a unifying coverage parameter, clarifying previously ambiguous theoretical aspects, and offering a robust framework that can guide future theoretical and algorithmic developments in reinforcement learning.
