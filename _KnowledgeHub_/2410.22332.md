# Local Policies Enable Zero-shot Long-horizon Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> ManipGen tackles sim2real manipulation with local policies: skills that observe and act only in a region around the target object, making them invariant to absolute robot/object pose, skill order, and scene layout. Thousands of single-object RL experts are distilled into five generalist visuomotor skills, then chained by a VLM planner and neural motion planner, reaching 97% in Robosuite and 76% zero-shot across 50 real long-horizon tasks.

## Problem

- Real robot data collection needs years and 100K-1M trajectories, yet resulting policies still generalize only within centimeters of trained object poses for complex tasks.
- Prior sim2real manipulation work trains and tests on the same task, avoiding the harder problem of generating every scene and task configuration needed for open-world transfer.
- Sense-plan-act pipelines break on contact-rich interaction and state-estimation error, while VLM-to-trajectory methods handle only short-horizon, top-down tasks and need task-specific primitives.

## Method

- ==Local policies==: a policy class initialized within 8cm of the target, observing clamped 30cm wrist depth plus initial segmentation, acting in relative delta-pose, granting pose/skill-order/scene invariance.
- Two-phase scale-up: 3.5K+ single-object ==PPO== experts with privileged state including 16 farthest-point-sampled shape keypoints and reward-component observations, then multi-task ==DAgger== distillation using a replay buffer interpolating on/off-policy.
- Deployment stack: **GPT-4o** emits (object, skill) tuple plans, ==Grounded SAM== estimates target poses, ==Neural MP== plans collision-free reaches, and five local skills (pick, place, grasp handle, open, close) execute contact.

## Results

- Robosuite zero-shot average **97%** versus PSL **96%**, TAMP **86%**, SayCan **80%**, DRQ-v2 **14%**, despite baselines training on the evaluation tasks.
- Across 50 real long-horizon tasks (2-8 stages) **ManipGen** hits **76%** success and **4.3/4.8** stages, beating SayCan **40%**, VoxPoser **16%**, LLMTrajGen **14%**, OpenVLA **0%**.
- Ablations: locality matters most, **97.4%** at 8cm initialization falling to **72.5%** at 64cm; FurnitureBench insertion **80%** versus Transic **45%** with no real-world data.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Restricting a policy's observation and action frame to the interaction region buys pose, ordering and scene invariance for free, shrinking the distribution simulation must cover.
> - Long-horizon competence can come from composition rather than long-horizon training: foundation-model planning plus motion planning glues short, robust skills into 8-stage behaviors.
> - Locality has a cost ceiling: performance degrades sharply as the initialization region grows, and system failures shift to open-set detection and motion planning errors.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper "Local Policies Enable Zero-shot Long-horizon Manipulation" addresses one of the grand challenges in robotics: creating truly generalist robots capable of performing complex, multi-stage tasks in unstructured, unseen environments without requiring extensive prior demonstrations or task-specific engineering. This work sits at the intersection of several rapidly evolving fields: **sim-to-real (sim2real) transfer, long-horizon task planning, and the application of large foundation models (such as Vision-Language Models - VLMs) in robotics.**

Traditionally, robotic manipulation has relied on either **Sense-Plan-Act (SPA)** paradigms, which are capable of generalization but demand precise state estimation and often significant manual engineering, especially for contact-rich interactions, or **data-driven learning**, which struggles with the high cost and limited scalability of real-world data collection, leading to poor generalization outside of training distributions.

Sim2real transfer emerged as a promising solution to scale data generation by training policies in vast, procedurally generated simulated environments. While successful in locomotion and some industrial manipulation tasks, prior sim2real efforts often focused on single skills or required training and testing on the *same* task in simulation, limiting their generalization to truly novel, open-world scenarios. The core bottlenecks for extending sim2real to open-world manipulation include accurately simulating complex contact dynamics, generating diverse enough scene and task configurations, and acquiring long-horizon behaviors that demand intractable amounts of data if learned end-to-end.

In parallel, the rise of **foundation models** in vision and language has sparked immense interest in their application to robotics. Approaches like SayCan and TidyBot leverage Large Language Models (LLMs) or VLMs for high-level task planning over a predefined library of skills. Others, such as LLMTrajGen and CoPa, attempt direct generation of robot trajectories from VLMs, though often limited to shorter horizons. While some methods like PSL and Boss use LLMs to accelerate RL training for long-horizon tasks, they still require training on the test task.

This paper distinguishes itself by uniquely combining these threads to overcome the limitations of each:
- It leverages **sim2real** for data scalability, but critically, it *does not* require real-world data for the target tasks, enabling zero-shot transfer.
- It addresses the challenge of generalizing to *any* task from text instruction by proposing **"local policies"** – a novel policy class designed to be invariant to absolute robot and object pose, skill ordering, and global scene configuration. This modularity allows skills learned in simple, randomized local simulated environments to be composed for complex, long-horizon tasks.
- It orchestrates these local policies with **off-the-shelf foundation models** (GPT-4o for VLM task decomposition, Grounded SAM for object perception, Neural MP for collision-free motion planning). This high-level reasoning and planning capability, combined with robust low-level skills, offers a powerful alternative to manually engineered systems or end-to-end learning that struggles with generalization.

By demonstrating state-of-the-art zero-shot performance on diverse, multi-stage real-world manipulation tasks, ManipGen pushes the boundaries of robotic generalization. It stands out by showing that a library of generalist skills, trained solely in simulation with a focus on *locality*, can effectively transfer to the real world and handle significant variation in objects, poses, and scene configurations, surpassing methods that rely on task-specific real-world data or simpler planning paradigms.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to develop **generalist robot systems that can plan, reason, and interact with the world like humans**, specifically tackling complex, long-horizon manipulation problems in unstructured environments. The authors envision a robot capable of everyday tasks like cleaning or organizing shelves without requiring extensive task-specific engineering or demonstrations.

This ambitious goal is motivated by several critical challenges in current robotics:

1. **Data Scarcity for Generalization:** Unlike vision and language, where massive internet-scale datasets have enabled generalist foundation models, collecting large-scale robotic manipulation data (100K-1M trajectories) is extremely labor-intensive and costly, often taking years. This data scarcity severely limits the generalization capabilities of learned robot policies, frequently restricting them to minor pose variations or previously seen objects.

2. **Bottlenecks in Sim-to-Real Transfer for Open-World Manipulation:** While sim2real has proven effective for specific skills (like locomotion or industrial assembly), extending it to open-world, general-purpose manipulation introduces new hurdles:
 - **Accurate Simulation of Complex Contacts:** Simulating realistic contact dynamics, crucial for manipulation, remains a significant challenge, despite progress with techniques like domain randomization.
 - **Generating Realistic Task Distributions:** Creating simulated environments that encompass all possible scene and task configurations to ensure broad generalization is practically intractable.
 - **Acquiring Long-Horizon Behaviors:** Learning multi-stage, sequential tasks directly requires an immense amount of data, making it prohibitively expensive even in simulation as the task horizon grows.

To overcome the latter two sim2real bottlenecks, the authors introduce a core insight: **the "locality of interaction"**. Many manipulation tasks can be simplified into two phases: achieving a pose near a target object, then performing a localized manipulation. This insight forms the basis for their novel policy class.

Based on these motivations, the **key objectives** of the ManipGen framework are:

- **Introduce a Novel Policy Class: Local Policies.** These policies are designed to operate on relative observations and actions within a localized region around the object of interest. By design, they are:
 - **Absolute Pose Invariant:** They reason about relative poses between the robot and objects, rather than global coordinates.
 - **Skill Order Invariant:** They can be chained flexibly by motion planning, as their initiation and termination sets are well-defined.
 - **Scene Configuration Invariant:** They observe only the local interaction region, making them robust to surrounding clutter or changes in the global scene.
- **Develop Techniques for Large-Scale Simulation Training:** To ensure broad object-level generalization, the approach involves training thousands of these local policies across diverse simulated objects and environments.
- **Seamless Integration with Foundation Models:** To enable zero-shot, long-horizon task completion from natural language instructions, the trained local policies must be effectively combined with existing generalist vision-language models for high-level task decomposition and motion planners for collision-avoidant navigation.
- **Demonstrate State-of-the-Art Zero-shot Performance:** Validate the entire system's ability to solve a vast set of long-horizon manipulation tasks in the real world, generalizing to unseen objects, poses, receptacles, and skill orders, outperforming current state-of-the-art methods.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The ManipGen approach is a hierarchical system designed for zero-shot long-horizon manipulation, combining the strengths of foundation models for high-level reasoning with robust, locally-trained policies for low-level interaction. It comprises three main components: a hierarchical framework, a two-phase training process for local policies, and a deployment strategy integrating various generalist models.

**A. Framework Decomposition:**
ManipGen hierarchically decomposes a given language goal `g` into a sequence of executable skills.
1. **High-Level Planning (VLM):** A pre-trained Vision-Language Model (VLM), specifically **GPT-4o**, acts as the high-level policy `p_theta`, decomposing the global task `g` into a sequence of language sub-goals `g_k`. These sub-goals are structured as `(object, skill)` tuples (e.g., "(handle, open)", "(rice, pick)").
2. **Mid-Level Motion Planning (π_reach):** For each sub-goal `g_k`, a motion planner `π_reach` is responsible for moving the robot's end-effector (EE) from its current configuration to a target configuration `X_targ,k` near the object of interest. This involves:
 - **Language-Conditioned Pose Estimation:** **Grounded SAM** is used to segment the target object from scene observations, and its 3D points are averaged to determine the object's position. Orientation is estimated heuristically based on the task context (e.g., pointing downwards for open-space picking) or by sampling poses and performing collision checks for tight spaces or articulated objects. GPT-4o is also used to refine object prompts for Grounding Dino (part of Grounded SAM) to ensure correct object detection.
 - **Collision-Avoidant Trajectory Generation:** **Neural MP**, a fast neural motion planner, takes the estimated `X_targ,k` and current scene point cloud to generate a collision-avoidant trajectory `τ_reach` in an open-loop fashion.
3. **Low-Level Local Policies (π_loc):** Once the robot's end-effector is within an initialization region `s_init` (defined as being within a small `ϵ` distance of `X_targ,k`), a skill-specific local policy `π_loc` takes over for contact-rich interaction. These policies are designed to be invariant to absolute robot and object poses, skill order, and global scene configuration.

**B. Training Local Policies for Sim2Real Manipulation:**
Local policies are trained exclusively in simulation using a two-phase approach:

1. **Phase 1: Acquire Object-Specific RL Experts at Scale:**
 - **Environment & Objects:** Thousands of state-based RL policies are trained using **PPO** in a GPU-parallelized simulation (Isaac Gym). For pick/place, 3.5K objects from UnidexGrasp are used. For articulated manipulation (grasp handle, open, close), 2.6K handles from PartNet are used, attached to procedurally generated cuboid doors/drawers.
 - **Data Generation & Randomization:** To ensure robustness and generalization, diverse training data is generated with extensive randomization:
 - Random spawning of clutter objects and obstacles.
 - Randomized initial robot poses (in a half-sphere around the target object/handle, pointing towards it).
 - Randomization of articulated object properties (size, shape, position, orientation, joint range, friction, damping coefficients).
 - Collection of valid pre-grasp poses (antipodal sampling) and rest poses to guide policy learning.
 - **Observations (Privileged):** RL experts receive privileged information to accelerate learning, including end-effector pose/velocity, object pose, low-dimensional object shape representation via Farthest Point Sampling (FPS) keypoints, and individual reward components.
 - **Actions:** Policies predict delta pose targets for a Task Space Impedance (TSI) controller, which has shown good sim2real transfer for precise tasks.
 - **Rewards:** Skill-specific reward functions are carefully designed to encourage desired behaviors (e.g., minimizing distance to grasp poses for picking, reaching target joint angles for opening) while penalizing undesirable actions (e.g., excessive gripper rotation, large contact forces).

2. **Phase 2: Learn Generalists via Large-Scale Distillation:**
 - **Multitask Online Imitation Learning (Modified DAgger):** The high-performing state-based RL experts are distilled into generalist visuomotor policies using a novel variant of DAgger. This variant incorporates a replay buffer of size K*B (K=100, B=32 empirically found best) to smoothly blend on-policy and off-policy data, addressing instability issues in multi-task distillation.
 - **Observation Space Design for Locality (Vision-based):** For real-world deployment, policies use local observations:
 - **Wrist Camera Depth Maps:** Chosen for their locality and proven sim2real transfer. Depth values are clamped (max 30cm) and normalized.
 - **Initial Context:** The initial local depth map (`O_0_loc_depth`) and a segmentation mask of the target object (`O_0_loc_seg`) are included at every step to provide context about the overall object shape, which might be obscured in close-up views.
 - **Relative Proprioception:** Absolute proprioception is transformed into observations relative to the first time-step, further enforcing locality and improving transfer. Velocity information is also included.
 - **Augmentations for Sim2Real Robustness:** To make policies robust to real-world sensor noise and artifacts, training data is augmented with:
 - **Correlated Depth Noise:** To model edge artifacts and noisiness along object edges.
 - **Random Irregular Holes:** To simulate randomly placed zero-depth pixels.
 - **Random Camera Cropping:** To improve visuomotor learning performance.

**C. Zero-shot Text-Conditioned Manipulation (Deployment):**
The deployed ManipGen system operates in a loop:
1. **Scene Tagging:** An initial phase uses the VLM to list present objects, and Grounded SAM generates corresponding segmentation masks. The VLM then refines its understanding of the scene by interpreting these tags.
2. **Task Planning:** GPT-4o, prompted with the overall task, scene images, and descriptions of available skills, generates a plan as a sequence of `(object, skill)` tuples.
3. **Execution Loop:** For each stage in the plan:
 - **Pose Estimation:** Grounded SAM and heuristics determine the target pose `X_targ,k` for the current `(object, skill)`.
 - **Motion Planning:** Neural MP plans a collision-avoidant path to reach `X_targ,k`.
 - **Local Policy Execution:** Once the robot is at `X_targ,k`, the appropriate pre-trained local policy (`π_loc`) is activated. It takes wrist-camera depth, initial context, and relative proprioception as input, and outputs delta pose targets for the robot's TSI controller. The policy runs for a fixed, skill-specific duration (max 8s).
 - The gripper is then actuated (open/close) as appropriate, and the system proceeds to the next stage.

**Hardware Setup:** A Franka Panda robot arm with UMI gripper and wrist-mounted Intel Realsense d405 camera (for local observations) is used. Four calibrated Intel Realsense d455 cameras provide global scene views for the VLM and motion planner.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of ManipGen spans extensive simulation benchmarks and challenging real-world long-horizon manipulation tasks, demonstrating its superior zero-shot generalization capabilities.

**A. Simulation Comparisons and Analysis:**

1. **Robosuite Benchmark Results:** ManipGen was zero-shot transferred from Isaac Gym to the Robosuite benchmark tasks (e.g., PickPlace, up to 4 stages). It achieved an average success rate of **97.33%**, outperforming or matching state-of-the-art methods like PSL (95.83%), DRQ-v2, RAPS, TAMP, and SayCan. This is significant because the baselines often use task-specific data or privileged state information, whereas ManipGen transfers its policies without any task-specific training data from Robosuite, validating its strong generalization from large-scale sim training.

2. **ManipGen Analysis and Ablations (Single-object pick policies on 5 objects):**
 - **Observation Space Design:** Including key-point observations (for object shape perception) was the most impactful improvement, raising success from 94.33% to 97.44%. Incorporating success/reward observations also showed benefits.
 - **Level of Locality:** The core principle of locality was validated. ManipGen, initialized within 8cm of the target object, achieved 97.44% success. Performance drastically diminished with increasing initialization distance (e.g., 72.52% for 64cm), confirming that policies benefit from a tightly defined local interaction region.
 - **DAgger Distillation:**
 - **Vision Observation Components:** Including velocity information, the first observation frame, and using relative proprioception were all crucial for robust visuomotor policy learning, with their removal significantly hurting performance (e.g., from 94.3% to 89.92% for removing velocity info).
 - **DAgger Buffer Size:** A buffer size of 100 (smoothly trading off on-policy and off-policy) yielded the best multitask training performance (85% success averaged across 100 held-out objects), outperforming purely on-policy (78%) or off-policy (75%) variants.

**B. Real World Evaluation:**

1. **FurnitureBench Results:** ManipGen was deployed on single-stage FurnitureBench tasks (Stabilize, Reach and Grasp, Insert), which involve contact-rich and precise motions. ManipGen achieved an average success rate of **90%**, outperforming various direct-transfer, imitation learning, and even human-in-the-loop methods (like Transic, which uses real-world correction data) without requiring *any* real-world training data. Notably, for the "Insert" task, ManipGen achieved 80% success, significantly surpassing Transic's 45%. This demonstrates ManipGen's superior sim2real transfer capabilities for challenging manipulation.

2. **Zero-shot Long-horizon Manipulation (Novel Tasks):** The most compelling evaluation involved 5 novel, diverse long-horizon tasks (Cook, Replace, CabinetStore, DrawerStore, Tidy) with up to 8 stages, unseen objects, receptacles, and randomized scene configurations.
 - **Overall Performance:** ManipGen achieved an impressive **76% zero-shot success rate** across 50 real-world trials, completing an average of 4.3 out of 4.8 stages per task.
 - **Baseline Comparison:** ManipGen significantly outperformed all state-of-the-art text-conditioned manipulation baselines:
 - **OpenVLA:** Failed to solve any task (0% success), even with few-shot data, struggling with generalization to held-out objects and poses.
 - **LLMTrajGen:** Achieved 14% success, excelling at simple pick-and-place but failing with obstacle avoidance or articulated object manipulation due to limitations in prompt coverage.
 - **VoxPoser:** Achieved 16% success, similar to LLMTrajGen, struggling with precision and complex tasks.
 - **SayCan:** The strongest baseline, achieving 40% success, leveraging VLM planning but struggling with contact-rich control or sub-optimal initial poses where its pre-defined primitives broke down.
 - **Generalization Capabilities:** ManipGen successfully avoided obstacles and manipulated unseen objects in arbitrary poses and configurations.
 - **Failure Cases:** The primary failure modes observed were:
 - **Vision failures:** Open-set detection models (Grounded Dino) sometimes misidentified the target object.
 - **Imperfect motion planning:** Resulting in collisions with the environment or dropped objects.
 - **Local policies failing from sub-optimal initial poses:** Though designed for robustness, extremely challenging initial conditions could still lead to failures.
 - **Task Difficulty:** Longer and more complex tasks like "DrawerStore" and "Tidy" naturally presented greater challenges for all methods, including ManipGen, resulting in lower success rates (60% for ManipGen).

In summary, the results conclusively demonstrate that ManipGen's combination of local policies, large-scale sim training, and integration with foundation models enables unprecedented zero-shot generalization to complex, long-horizon manipulation tasks in the real world, significantly outperforming existing methods.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in "Local Policies Enable Zero-shot Long-horizon Manipulation" represents a significant step forward in the quest for generalist robotic systems. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enabling True Zero-Shot Generalization:** The most impactful contribution is the demonstration of highly capable zero-shot transfer from simulation to reality for complex, multi-stage manipulation tasks. Unlike previous approaches that often require real-world data or fine-tuning for specific tasks, ManipGen's policies, trained *solely in simulation*, generalize to entirely unseen objects, poses, scene configurations, and task sequences. This dramatically reduces the prohibitive cost and time associated with data collection in the real world, paving the way for faster deployment of robots in novel environments.

2. **Novelty and Efficacy of Local Policies:** The introduction of "local policies" is a key technical innovation. By designing policies that are invariant to absolute robot and object pose, skill ordering, and global scene configuration, the authors have found a powerful way to modularize robotic skills. This locality enables:
 - **Robustness:** Policies are less sensitive to global scene changes or minor calibration errors.
 - **Composability:** Simple, well-defined local skills can be chained by higher-level planners to solve arbitrarily complex long-horizon tasks.
 - **Efficient Training:** Training can focus on the local interaction, simplifying scene generation in simulation and allowing for broad object-level randomization.

3. **Effective Integration of Foundation Models and Robotics:** The work successfully demonstrates a practical and high-performing pipeline that synergistically combines the high-level reasoning capabilities of large Vision-Language Models (like GPT-4o and Grounded SAM) with robust low-level control policies and motion planners. This integration shows how symbolic planning (from VLMs) can be effectively grounded in continuous, contact-rich robot control, bridging a crucial gap in embodied AI. This modular approach is more interpretable and potentially more robust than end-to-end black-box learning for complex tasks.

4. **Scalability in Robotic Learning:** By leveraging GPU-parallelized simulation environments (Isaac Gym) for large-scale training of local policies (thousands of experts and objects), the approach directly addresses the data scalability challenge. The refined DAgger strategy for distilling these experts into generalist visuomotor policies further ensures that this simulation-generated diversity translates into real-world performance.

5. **Accelerating Robot Development and Deployment:** The ability to develop and evaluate manipulation capabilities in simulation, with strong zero-shot transfer to reality, can significantly accelerate the research and development cycle for robotic applications. This means faster iteration, lower hardware costs during development, and potentially quicker deployment of robots into new domains, from logistics and manufacturing to domestic assistance.

6. **Paving the Way for Truly Generalist Robots:** ManipGen moves beyond laboratory-confined tasks or industrial automation and demonstrates progress towards robots that can adapt to unstructured, dynamic human environments. The capacity to clean a table, organize a shelf, or put items away into drawers with diverse, unseen objects and configurations is a testament to progress towards general-purpose manipulation for everyday human needs.

While the paper acknowledges remaining challenges (vision failures, motion planning imperfections, and limitations of local policies under extreme initial conditions), ManipGen provides a robust and innovative framework that lays crucial groundwork for the future development of highly autonomous, adaptable, and generalist robot systems.
