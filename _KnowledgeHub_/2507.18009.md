# GRR-CoCa: Leveraging LLM Mechanisms in Multimodal Model Architectures

> [!summary] Summary
> GRR-CoCa, developed at Rice University, integrates modern architectural features from Large Language Models (LLMs) into the CoCa multimodal model, specifically updating its Vision Transformer (ViT) encoder and textual decoders. This enhanced model demonstrated consistent performance improvements in contrastive and generative image-text tasks, indicating that LLM mechanisms can benefit multimodal architectures without substantially increasing parameters.

## Problem

- Leading foundational multimodal models, such as CoCa, have architectural designs that lag behind the advancements seen in contemporary Large Language Models (LLMs).
- Architectural enhancements like GEGLUs, RMSNorm, and RoPe, proven effective in LLMs, have not been widely adopted in multimodal vision-language models, particularly within their visual encoders.
- There is a need to enhance the Vision Transformer (ViT) encoder in multimodal models to produce more feature-rich image latent representations for improved multimodal tasks.

## Method

- Developed GRR-CoCa, an enhanced version of the ==CoCa== model, by systematically integrating ==GEGLUs==, ==RMSNorm==, and ==RoPe== into its textual decoders and Vision Transformer (ViT) encoder.
- Established a 'Baseline CoCa' model for comparison, which incorporated the modern LLM architectures only into its textual decoders, isolating the impact of modifications to the visual encoder.
- Rigorously benchmarked GRR-CoCa against Baseline CoCa using a standard pretraining-to-fine-tuning workflow on the CC12M dataset for pretraining and MSCOCO, ROCO, and Flickr30K for fine-tuning, evaluating across contrastive, generative, and combined CoCa loss metrics.

## Results

- During pretraining on the CC12M dataset, GRR-CoCa achieved a **2**7.25%** improvement in contrastive loss** and a **3.**71%** reduction in perplexity** compared to Baseline CoCa.
- GRR-CoCa consistently outperformed Baseline CoCa during fine-tuning across MSCOCO, ROCO, and Flickr30K datasets, showing average improvements of **1**3.66%** in contrastive loss**, **5.**18%** in perplexity**, and **5.**55%** in CoCa loss**.
- These performance gains were achieved with GRR-CoCa having only a marginal **0.**17%** increase in trainable parameters** (394.5M vs 393.8M) compared to Baseline CoCa.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Architectural mechanisms from LLMs (GEGLUs, RMSNorm, RoPe) can be effectively applied to multimodal models, including their visual encoders, leading to performance gains.
> - Integrating RoPe throughout the Vision Transformer blocks likely helps preserve positional information more effectively in deeper layers, improving spatial understanding.
> - GEGLUs and RMSNorm contribute to faster learning, better generalization, and more refined manipulation of embeddings by filtering noise and magnifying relevant features.
