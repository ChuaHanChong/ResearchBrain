# GRR-CoCa: Leveraging LLM Mechanisms in Multimodal Model Architectures

> [!summary] Summary
> GRR-CoCa, developed at Rice University, integrates modern architectural features from Large Language Models (LLMs) into the CoCa multimodal model, specifically updating its Vision Transformer (ViT) encoder and textual decoders. This enhanced model demonstrated consistent performance improvements in contrastive and generative image-text tasks, indicating that LLM mechanisms can benefit multimodal architectures without substantially increasing parameters.

## Problem

- Leading foundational multimodal models, such as CoCa, have architectural designs that lag behind the advancements seen in contemporary Large Language Models (LLMs).
- Architectural enhancements like GEGLUs, RMSNorm, and RoPe, proven effective in LLMs, have not been widely adopted in multimodal vision-language models, particularly within their visual encoders.
- There is a need to enhance the Vision Transformer (ViT) encoder in multimodal models to produce more feature-rich image latent representations for improved multimodal tasks.

## Method

- Developed GRR-CoCa, an enhanced version of the ==CoCa== model, by systematically integrating ==GEGLUs==, ==RMSNorm==, and ==RoPe== into its textual decoders and Vision Transformer (ViT) encoder.
- Established a 'Baseline CoCa' model for comparison, which incorporated the modern LLM architectures only into its textual decoders, isolating the impact of modifications to the visual encoder.
- Rigorously benchmarked GRR-CoCa against Baseline CoCa using a standard pretraining-to-fine-tuning workflow on the CC12M dataset for pretraining and MSCOCO, ROCO, and Flickr30K for fine-tuning, evaluating across contrastive, generative, and combined CoCa loss metrics.

## Results

- During pretraining on the CC12M dataset, GRR-CoCa achieved a **2**7.25%** improvement in contrastive loss** and a **3.**71%** reduction in perplexity** compared to Baseline CoCa.
- GRR-CoCa consistently outperformed Baseline CoCa during fine-tuning across MSCOCO, ROCO, and Flickr30K datasets, showing average improvements of **1**3.66%** in contrastive loss**, **5.**18%** in perplexity**, and **5.**55%** in CoCa loss**.
- These performance gains were achieved with GRR-CoCa having only a marginal **0.**17%** increase in trainable parameters** (394.5M vs 393.8M) compared to Baseline CoCa.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Architectural mechanisms from LLMs (GEGLUs, RMSNorm, RoPe) can be effectively applied to multimodal models, including their visual encoders, leading to performance gains.
> - Integrating RoPe throughout the Vision Transformer blocks likely helps preserve positional information more effectively in deeper layers, improving spatial understanding.
> - GEGLUs and RMSNorm contribute to faster learning, better generalization, and more refined manipulation of embeddings by filtering noise and magnifying relevant features.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of several rapidly evolving fields within artificial intelligence: **Large Language Models (LLMs)**, **Vision Transformers (ViTs)**, and **Multimodal Models**, particularly those integrating vision and language.

The advent of the Transformer architecture by Vaswani et al. (2017) revolutionized Natural Language Processing (NLP), leading to the development of highly capable LLMs like Meta's Llama series (Dubey et al.), GPT-4 (Achiam et al.), and DeepSeek-V3 (Liu et al.). These LLMs have demonstrated remarkable abilities in complex language tasks, from code generation to advanced reasoning. Simultaneously, the Transformer architecture has been successfully adapted for computer vision, notably with the Vision Transformer (ViT) by Alexey (2020), which achieved state-of-the-art performance in image classification.

The growing trend is towards **multimodal models** that can process and integrate information from various data streams, such as text and images. Early pioneers in this space include CLIP (Radford et al.) and ALIGN (Jia et al.), which leveraged large-scale contrastive pretraining on image-text pairs to learn robust joint representations. These models showed that self-supervised learning on multimodal data significantly improved performance on both multimodal and unimodal downstream tasks.

Building upon these foundations, the **Contrastive Captioners (CoCa)** model, introduced by Yu et al. (2022), emerged as a state-of-the-art multimodal architecture. CoCa distinguishes itself by employing a dual-task pretraining objective that combines contrastive learning (aligning image and text embeddings) with generative captioning (producing text from images). This allows CoCa to excel in image-text retrieval and image captioning, while its visual encoder also achieves top performance on tasks like ImageNet classification.

Despite CoCa's strong performance, the authors identify a critical gap: while CoCa was novel at its inception, the rapid pace of innovation in LLM architectures has led to architectural improvements that have not yet been widely adopted in multimodal models like CoCa. Modern LLMs, such as those in the Llama series, incorporate architectural enhancements like Gaussian Error Gated Linear Units (GEGLUs), Root Mean Squared Normalization (RMSNorm), and Rotary Positional Embeddings (RoPe). These mechanisms have been empirically shown to improve model efficiency, expressiveness, and performance in unimodal language and vision tasks.

This work directly addresses this identified gap. By systematically integrating these modern LLM architectural mechanisms into the CoCa framework – specifically within its textual decoders and, more critically, its Vision Transformer encoder – the authors aim to "update" a leading multimodal model. This positions GRR-CoCa as a significant contribution to the ongoing effort to enhance the capabilities and efficiency of multimodal AI, leveraging lessons learned from the cutting edge of unimodal LLM development. The research seeks to demonstrate that these architectural refinements, which have proven beneficial in LLMs, can yield similar, if not greater, advantages when applied to complex multimodal architectures.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind the GRR-CoCa research stems from a perceived architectural stagnation in even state-of-the-art multimodal models, such as CoCa, when compared to the rapid advancements seen in contemporary Large Language Models (LLMs). The authors explicitly state that leading foundational multimodal model architectures "frequently lag behind the architectural sophistication of contemporary LLMs."

This observation leads to several key motivations:

- **Leveraging Proven LLM Innovations:** Recent literature has consistently demonstrated that architectural modifications like Gaussian error gated linear units (GEGLUs), Root Mean Squared Normalization (RMSNorms), and Rotary Positional Embeddings (RoPe) significantly improve model performance in LLMs. These improvements include better perplexity scores, enhanced expressiveness, increased efficiency, and improved generalization. The researchers are motivated to investigate if these benefits translate to the multimodal domain.
- **Updating CoCa's Architecture:** The CoCa model, while a state-of-the-art Contrastive Captioner, was introduced in 2022. Since then, LLM architectures have evolved considerably. The authors see a "critical need for the older SOTA CoCa model to be updated" to incorporate these newer, more advanced architectural features.
- **Enhancing Visual Encoder Capabilities:** Beyond textual decoders, the Vision Transformer (ViT) used as CoCa's visual encoder also lags behind in incorporating these modern LLM architectures. Previous work suggests RoPe improves ViTs for image classification. The motivation extends to improving the ViT's ability to produce "more feature-rich image latent representations" that can be better leveraged by downstream multimodal tasks.

Based on these motivations, the study sets out with clear objectives:

1. **Develop GRR-CoCa:** To propose and implement an enhanced version of the CoCa model, named GRR-CoCa. This model would systematically integrate GEGLUs, RMSNorm, and RoPe into its textual decoders (unimodal and multimodal) and crucially, into its Vision Transformer (ViT) encoder.
2. **Establish a Baseline for Comparison:** To create a "Baseline CoCa" model as a control. This baseline would incorporate the modern LLM architectures (GEGLUs, RMSNorm, RoPe) only into its textual decoders, while retaining the original ViT encoder architecture of CoCa. This allows for a direct comparison to isolate the impact of the visual encoder modifications in GRR-CoCa.
3. **Benchmark Performance and Generalization:** To rigorously benchmark GRR-CoCa against Baseline CoCa using a standard pretraining-to-fine-tuning workflow. This involves evaluating performance on the pretraining dataset (CC12M) and a diverse set of fine-tuning datasets (MSCOCO, ROCO, Flickr30K) across contrastive, generative, and combined CoCa loss metrics. The goal is to quantify the performance improvements and assess GRR-CoCa's capacity to fit data and generalize across different vision-language domains, including domain transfer (ROCO).
4. **Quantify Impact of Architectural Modifications:** To explicitly quantify the "significant improvements in performance that can be gained in multimodal transformer models by architectural modifications with almost no increase in model parameter size." This objective emphasizes the efficiency and practical utility of the proposed enhancements.

In essence, the research aims to demonstrate that directly applying architectural innovations from unimodal LLMs to multimodal models can yield substantial performance gains, thereby providing a pathway for developing more robust, efficient, and higher-performing next-generation vision-language foundation models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of this research is centered on a comparative study between two variants of the Contrastive Captioners (CoCa) model: Baseline CoCa and the proposed GRR-CoCa. Both models follow a standard pretraining and fine-tuning workflow, evaluated on a diverse set of image-captioning datasets.

**Model Architectures:**
The core of the methodology lies in the architectural modifications:
- **Baseline CoCa:** This model serves as the control. Its textual decoders (unimodal and multimodal) are updated to include GEGLUs, RMSNorm, and RoPe, aligning them with modern LLM architectures. However, its Vision Transformer (ViT) encoder retains the original CoCa architecture, utilizing traditional feedforward layers, LayerNorm, and absolute positional encoding.
- **GRR-CoCa:** This is the proposed model. It incorporates the same modern architectural improvements (GEGLUs, RMSNorm, and RoPe) not only in its textual decoders but also in its Vision Transformer (ViT) encoder. This ensures architectural consistency across modalities and is hypothesized to yield superior performance.

**Specific Architectural Modifications:**
The paper details three key architectural modifications, each inspired by modern LLM designs:
1. **Gaussian Error Gated Linear Units (GEGLUs):** These replace the standard feedforward networks. Instead of a single linear transformation followed by an activation, GEGLUs use two parallel linear transformations, one of which is activated (e.g., with GELU), followed by a Hadamard product. This gating mechanism is designed to enhance model expressiveness. To maintain comparable model sizes, the hidden layer scaling factor for GEGLUs was reduced from 4x (for non-GEGLU) to 2.7x, resulting in only a marginal 0.17% increase in total trainable parameters for GRR-CoCa compared to Baseline CoCa.
2. **Root Mean Squared Normalization (RMSNorm):** This replaces the LayerNorm layers. RMSNorm simplifies LayerNorm by eliminating the need to compute the mean statistic and removing the recentering parameter. This reduces computational overhead while often improving performance, particularly in pre-normalization contexts.
3. **Rotary Positional Embedding (RoPe):** This replaces the absolute positional encoding. RoPe applies a set of non-trainable linear transformations (rotations) to query and key embeddings based on their positional index. Unlike absolute positional encoding which is added upfront, RoPe is integrated into each ViT block, allowing positional information to be preserved deeper into the network.

**Datasets and Preprocessing:**
- **Pretraining:** The Conceptual Captions 12 Million (CC12M) dataset was used, containing over 10 million image-caption pairs. This large dataset simulates the proprietary JFT-300M dataset used by the original CoCa model. A 95%/5% train/validation split was applied.
- **Fine-tuning:** Three diverse datasets were used to assess generalization:
 - **Microsoft Common Objects in Context (MSCOCO):** A widely used dataset for object recognition and image captioning, similar in distribution to CC12M.
 - **Radiology Objects in COntext (ROCO) Version 2:** A medical image-captioning dataset, representing a significant domain shift from general-purpose datasets, testing transfer learning capabilities.
 - **Flickr 30K:** A common benchmark dataset with high-quality, hand-annotated captions.
- **Data Preprocessing:** Captions were tokenized using a modified T5 base tokenizer, prepended with a "beginning of sequence" (BOS) token, appended with an "end of sequence" (EOS) token, and padded. Images were resized to 288x288 pixels, converted to RGB, scaled to, and normalized using ImageNet channel statistics.

**Loss Functions and Evaluation Metrics:**
The models were trained simultaneously on two tasks, combined into a CoCa loss function:
- **Contrastive Loss (L_Con):** Maximizes similarity between matching image-text embeddings and dissimilarity between non-matching pairs within a batch. Lower values indicate better alignment.
- **Generative Captioning Loss (L_Cap):** Uses autoregressive causal language modeling to predict the next token in a caption given previous tokens and the image context. Evaluated by **Perplexity**, the exponential of the average cross-entropy loss per token.
- **CoCa Loss (L_CoCa):** A weighted sum of L_Con and L_Cap. Weights were set to λ_Con = 2, λ_Cap = 1 during pretraining (emphasizing contrastive learning for robust representation) and λ_Con = 1, λ_Cap = 2 during fine-tuning (emphasizing generative captioning for task-specific performance).

**Training Techniques:**
- **Hardware:** Training was performed on four Tesla L40S GPUs using PyTorch.
- **Batch Size:** A total simulated batch size of 768 samples per step (48 per GPU * 4 GPUs * 4 gradient accumulation steps).
- **Optimizer:** AdamW with default betas and a weight decay of 0.001.
- **Learning Rate Schedule:** A linear warm-up (2,000 steps) during pretraining, followed by a Cosine Annealing with Warm Restart (CAWR) scheduler. Learning rate ranges were 1e-4 to 1e-6 for pretraining and 1e-5 to 1e-7 for fine-tuning.
- **Stability:** Gradient clipping (max norm of 1) was applied.
- **Early Stopping and Soft Reset:** For fine-tuning, a validation loss monitoring early stopper was used with a patience of 9 epochs. A "soft reset" mechanism was implemented: if validation loss didn't improve for 3 epochs, the model reverted to its best state, and the learning rate range was reduced by a factor of 100 before resuming training. This ensured convergence by dynamically adjusting the learning rate.

This comprehensive methodology allows for a direct, controlled comparison of GRR-CoCa against a strong baseline, thoroughly evaluating the impact of the architectural innovations across various training stages and dataset characteristics.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results consistently demonstrate the superior performance of GRR-CoCa over Baseline CoCa across all evaluated metrics and datasets, validating the hypothesis that incorporating modern LLM architectural mechanisms into multimodal models, particularly their visual encoders, yields significant benefits.

**Pretraining Performance on CC12M Validation Set:**
After 21 epochs of pretraining on the CC12M dataset, GRR-CoCa exhibited notable improvements:
- **Contrastive Loss:** Achieved a loss of 0.2626, a substantial **27.25% improvement** compared to Baseline CoCa's 0.3610. This indicates a much stronger alignment between image and text latent representations.
- **Perplexity:** Improved to 12.5151, a **3.71% reduction** from Baseline CoCa's 12.9976. Lower perplexity signifies better generative text quality.
- **CoCa Loss:** Registered 3.0516, representing a **7.15% improvement** over Baseline CoCa's 3.2864. This overall loss, combining both contrastive and generative objectives, confirms GRR-CoCa's superior learning capacity.

The significant improvement in contrastive loss during pretraining is particularly noteworthy. The authors suggest this is because the contrastive task might be "easier for the network to model" compared to the complex interdependencies required for generative captioning. Despite this, perplexity also improved, indicating benefits for both tasks.

**Fine-Tuning Performance Across Diverse Datasets:**
Following pretraining, both models were fine-tuned to convergence on three distinct datasets. GRR-CoCa consistently outperformed Baseline CoCa on these downstream tasks as well.

- **MSCOCO Validation Set:**
 - CoCa Loss: -6.10% improvement (4.4090 vs 4.6955)
 - Perplexity: -5.75% improvement (5.1523 vs 5.4669)
 - Contrastive Loss: -12.92% improvement (1.1296 vs 1.2971)
- **ROCO Validation Set (Medical Domain):**
 - CoCa Loss: -2.69% improvement (6.8708 vs 7.0609)
 - Perplexity: -2.40% improvement (11.7377 vs 12.0258)
 - Contrastive Loss: -6.80% improvement (1.9479 vs 2.0900)
 - *Observation:* While improvements were smaller on ROCO, this dataset represents a more challenging domain transfer, and GRR-CoCa still demonstrated superior generalization.
- **Flickr30K Validation Set:**
 - CoCa Loss: -7.86% improvement (5.0111 vs 5.4383)
 - Perplexity: -7.39% improvement (7.3686 vs 7.9568)
 - Contrastive Loss: -21.25% improvement (1.0164 vs 1.2908)

**Overall Average Fine-Tuning Improvements (across MSCOCO, ROCO, Flickr30K):**
- **Contrastive Loss:** 13.66% average improvement.
- **Perplexity:** 5.18% average improvement.
- **CoCa Loss:** 5.55% average improvement.

**Efficiency and Learning Capacity:**
The results were achieved with GRR-CoCa having only 0.17% more trainable parameters than Baseline CoCa (394,548,926 vs 393,863,270). This underscores the efficiency of the architectural modifications. The consistent outperformance across all metrics and datasets, despite similar parameter counts and training regimes, indicates that GRR-CoCa's modified ViT architecture possesses a "higher capacity to learn training data and generalize to unseen validation data." The "linear warm-up" and "learning rate reduction with soft reset" schedulers further optimized training time and ensured maximal performance extraction from both models.

In summary, the findings robustly support the paper's central claim: integrating architectural advancements from LLMs, specifically GEGLUs, RMSNorms, and RoPe, into the Vision Transformer encoder of a multimodal model like CoCa leads to significant and consistent performance gains in both contrastive and generative tasks, without substantially increasing model complexity.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in "GRR-CoCa: Leveraging LLM Mechanisms in Multimodal Model Architectures" carries significant implications for the development of future multimodal AI models. The findings go beyond a simple demonstration of improved performance; they offer practical guidance and foundational insights into optimizing complex deep learning architectures.

**Demonstrated Efficacy and Practical Guidance:**
The most direct impact of this work is the empirical validation that specific architectural enhancements (GEGLUs, RoPe, and RMSNorms), widely adopted and proven beneficial in Large Language Models (LLMs), can be seamlessly and effectively integrated into Vision Transformer (ViT)-based visual encoders of multimodal models. This addresses a critical architectural gap identified by the authors. By quantifying consistent improvements in both contrastive and generative captioning tasks across diverse datasets, the study provides concrete evidence for the utility of these mechanisms. The average improvements in CoCa losses (5.95%), perplexities (4.81%), and contrastive losses (17.05%) over multiple datasets are compelling.

This work serves as crucial "practical guidance for designing next-generation foundational models." It suggests that future multimodal architectures should consider adopting these elements from the outset, rather than solely focusing on scaling existing, potentially outdated, designs. The fact that these gains were achieved with "almost no increase in model parameter size" (GRR-CoCa had only 0.17% more parameters) is particularly significant. It implies that performance enhancements can be realized through architectural refinement, leading to more efficient models that are faster to train, incur lower computational costs, and achieve better accuracy without necessarily requiring massive increases in model scale.

**Deeper Learned Representations and Generalization:**
The consistent performance gains across varied datasets, including the challenging domain transfer to medical images (ROCO), indicate that GRR-CoCa learns "more feature-rich latent representations of images." This implies a deeper, more nuanced understanding of visual information, which in turn benefits both the alignment with textual data (contrastive learning) and the generation of descriptive captions. The improved generalization capacity is crucial for real-world applications where models must perform well on unseen data distributions.

The discussion section provides theoretical insights into why these specific mechanisms contribute to performance:
- **RoPe:** Its integration into each ViT block likely helps preserve positional information more effectively in deeper layers, preventing the attenuation seen with absolute positional encoding. This allows the model to maintain spatial understanding throughout its processing.
- **GEGLUs:** By creating an "information bottleneck" and gating information flow, they may enable more refined manipulation of embeddings, potentially filtering noise and magnifying relevant features, leading to faster learning and better generalization.
- **RMSNorm:** Its simplification over LayerNorm, specifically by removing the recentering parameter, suggests that the gamma scaling parameter is more critical, leading to less noise modeling and improved overall generalization.

**Broader Impact on AI Development:**
The findings resonate beyond just image captioning. Multimodal models, as the paper notes, are versatile. Their pretrained components (visual encoders or textual decoders) can be fine-tuned for a wide array of single-modality tasks. Therefore, improvements to the foundational architecture of such models can cascade into better performance for numerous downstream applications in both computer vision and natural language processing. This could lead to more robust image recognition, improved visual question answering, more accurate cross-modal retrieval, and ultimately, more capable AI systems that bridge modalities.

**Future Directions:**
The paper outlines promising avenues for future research:
- **Scaling:** The most immediate next step is to test GRR-CoCa's architecture at much larger scales, using multi-billion parameter models and datasets like LAION-5B or JFT 3B, similar to the original CoCa's training. This would confirm whether the observed benefits hold true and potentially amplify with scale.
- **Task Generalization:** Investigating the adaptability of these modifications to other vision-language tasks (e.g., visual question answering, visual entailment, cross-modal retrieval) will be crucial to ascertain whether the improvements are specific to image captioning or generalize to the broader spectrum of tasks requiring nuanced visual-textual understanding.

In conclusion, GRR-CoCa offers a compelling demonstration of how strategic architectural cross-pollination between LLMs and multimodal models can yield significant performance gains. This work provides a clear roadmap for designing more robust, efficient, and higher-performing foundational models, pushing the boundaries of what integrated AI systems can achieve.
