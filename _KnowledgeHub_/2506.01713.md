# SRPO: Enhancing Multimodal LLM Reasoning via Reflection-Aware Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> SRPO enhances multimodal large language models by integrating explicit self-reflection and self-correction capabilities through a two-stage training framework. The approach achieves state-of-the-art performance among open-source models, scoring 78.5% on MathVista with SRPO-32B, and showing competitive results against leading closed-source models across diverse reasoning benchmarks.

## Problem

- Multimodal LLMs (MLLMs) struggle with complex reasoning and self-correction, often producing redundant or erroneous steps, unlike unimodal LLMs.
- Existing reflection methods for MLLMs are often superficial, failing to generate meaningful feedback or truly revise reasoning due to limitations set during initial pre-training.
- Traditional supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning methods for MLLMs activate existing knowledge but do not foster genuine acquisition of new cognitive behaviors or extend reasoning beyond pre-training limits.

## Method

- SRPO introduces a two-stage training framework combining ==reflection-oriented== supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and reflection-aware reinforcement learning (RL).
- The SFT stage injects foundational self-reflection knowledge by using a curated dataset of 10,000 multimodal samples that teach the model to revise initial responses using generated reflections.
- The RL stage refines the model's abilities using an enhanced Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with a novel reward function that explicitly incentivizes proper reflection formatting, concise yet informative reflections, and the effectiveness of reflection in correcting or preserving answers.

## Results

- SRPO-**32B** achieved state-of-the-art performance among open-source MLLMs, scoring **78.5%** on MathVista and consistently outperforming other open-source reasoning models across diverse mathematical benchmarks.
- The framework consistently surpassed existing open-source MLLMs on general reasoning benchmarks (e.g., MMMU-Pro, MMMU, EMMA) and challenging cross-disciplinary tasks (e.g., +5.1 points on Physics over MM-Eureka-**7B**).
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical importance of both the reflection-oriented SFT for cold-start initialization and the reflection-aware RL components, particularly the effectiveness reward (IeffI_{eff}Ieff), for robust performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly integrating reflection-oriented SFT and a reflection-aware RL reward mechanism is crucial for enabling genuine self-reflection and self-correction in MLLMs, extending their cognitive boundaries.
> - A sophisticated reward function that directly evaluates the *effectiveness* of reflection (e.g., correcting errors, preserving correct reasoning) and controls for brevity is vital for fostering meaningful self-improvement.
> - Structured reflection patterns (e.g., 'initial solution → reflection → refined solution') provide substantial benefits over simpler iterative thinking, leading to significant improvements in reasoning quality.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of large language models (LLMs) has seen rapid advancements, with multimodal LLMs (MLLMs) demonstrating impressive capabilities in interpreting scientific figures, geometric reasoning, and complex image-text comprehension tasks. However, the paper identifies a significant gap: while unimodal, text-based LLMs have made strides in complex reasoning through explicit self-reflection and self-correction, MLLMs still largely struggle with these capabilities. Existing reflection methods for MLLMs are often simplistic, failing to generate meaningful or instructive feedback because the core reasoning ability and knowledge limits are primarily fixed during initial pre-training.

Current approaches to enhancing MLLM reasoning often involve transferring reinforcement learning (RL) techniques from text-based models or employing supervised fine-tuning (SFT) with Chain-of-Thought (CoT) data. Yet, these methods frequently encounter limitations. For instance, token-level Markov processes during generation can lead to redundant or erroneous reasoning steps, sometimes even resulting in performance inferior to simpler, "fast-thinking" models. Prior research has indicated that RL typically improves reasoning by activating decision-making within fixed structures rather than truly acquiring new knowledge or behaviors, suggesting that external interventions are needed to surpass pre-training limitations.

SRPO directly addresses this critical challenge by proposing a novel framework that integrates explicit reflective mechanisms into *both* the supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and reinforcement learning (RL) stages. Unlike previous work that might focus solely on extended CoT supervision or direct prompting for reflection, SRPO systematically injects reflection knowledge and incentivizes effective self-correction. This two-stage approach aims to move beyond simply activating existing reasoning capabilities, instead fostering genuine self-reflection and self-correction to extend MLLMs' intrinsic reasoning boundaries. It builds upon and extends frameworks like Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), which has shown promise in enhancing complex reasoning, by specifically tailoring it for multimodal contexts and introducing reflection-aware reward mechanisms. By explicitly promoting self-reflection, SRPO seeks to improve logical coherence, eliminate unnecessary steps, and enhance overall reasoning quality in MLLMs, closing the gap with their unimodal counterparts.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to significantly enhance the reasoning capabilities of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), particularly for complex problems that demand explicit self-reflection and self-correction. The authors aim to overcome the limitations where existing MLLMs, despite their advancements, still struggle to generate meaningful, instructive feedback and effectively revise their reasoning, often due to constraints established during initial pre-training.

The core motivations behind SRPO are:

- **Address MLLM Reasoning Limitations:** MLLMs frequently produce redundant, repetitive, or erroneous reasoning steps due to their token-level Markov generation process, hindering their performance on complex tasks.
- **Improve Self-Reflection Quality:** Existing reflection methods in MLLMs are often superficial and ineffective. The authors are motivated by cognitive science principles, which emphasize that robust human reasoning involves active self-reflection and iterative self-correction.
- **Surpass Pre-training Constraints:** Traditional RL methods for LLMs often activate decision-making within fixed pre-trained knowledge structures, rather than enabling the acquisition of genuinely new cognitive behaviors or knowledge. The goal is to design a method that allows MLLMs to move beyond these inherent limitations.
- **Foster Self-Correction:** Beyond just reasoning, the authors aim to build MLLMs that can autonomously evaluate their own responses, identify errors, and iteratively revise them through reflective reasoning.
- **Incentivize Meaningful Reflection:** To counter the tendency of models to generate verbose or redundant reflections for higher rewards without real gains, the work aims to create a reward mechanism that explicitly encourages concise, task-oriented, and cognitively meaningful reflection.
- **Demonstrate Generalizability:** The research seeks to show that reflection-enhanced training can improve MLLM performance not only in specific domains (e.g., mathematical reasoning) but also across diverse, cross-disciplinary reasoning tasks.

In essence, the authors are motivated to develop a robust and effective framework that instills true self-reflective and self-corrective capabilities in MLLMs, thereby improving their reasoning accuracy and the quality of their explanations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SRPO (multimodal Self-Reflection enhanced reasoning with Group Relative Policy Optimization) is a novel two-stage reflective training framework designed to promote explicit self-reflection and self-correction in MLLMs.

#### 3.1. Reflection-oriented Cold-start Initialization (Stage 1)

This stage focuses on building foundational self-reflective reasoning capabilities within the policy model before reinforcement learning.

- **Self-Reflection SFT Data Construction:**
 - **Motivation:** Recognizing that RL primarily refines existing knowledge, this stage aims to explicitly *inject* reflection knowledge into the MLLM. High-quality SFT can expand the cognitive boundaries established during pre-training.
 - **Data Curation:** A subset of 10,000 multimodal reasoning samples is curated from large-scale datasets like LLaVA-CoT, Mulberry, and MathV360K, covering diverse domains.
 - **Two Complementary Strategies for Reflection Generation:**
 1. **Refinement of Correct CoTs:** For initial responses that are correct but potentially verbose or less optimal, an advanced MLLM (GPT-o4-mini) generates reflections that streamline or improve clarity.
 2. **Revision of Incorrect CoTs:** For initial responses with errors, GPT-o4-mini generates reflections that diagnose flaws and guide error correction, leading to a revised, correct answer.
 - **Output Structure:** Each final sample comprises three components: the initial policy model response, the generated self-reflection, and the ground truth answer. This dataset teaches the policy model how to reflect effectively.
- **Cold-start Initialization of Policy Model:** The constructed reflection-aware dataset is used to supervised fine-tune the initial MLLM (policy model $\pi_{initial}$). The training objective is to teach the model to revise its initial response ($a_1$) towards the ground truth ($a_2$) by utilizing the generated reflection ($\langle reflection \rangle$). This process equips the model with self-correction capabilities and aligns it with correct reasoning trajectories.

#### 3.2. Reflection-aware Reinforcement Learning (Stage 2)

This stage further refines the model's self-reflection and reasoning abilities using an enhanced RL framework.

- **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO):**
 - SRPO is built upon GRPO, an RL algorithm known for optimizing policy gradients using reward losses, distinct from token-level SFT losses.
 - GRPO promotes exploration by comparing generated responses within sampled groups, leveraging an intra-group advantage estimation rather than a traditional critic model, making it computationally efficient.
- **Enhanced GRPO with Reflection-Aware Rewards (SRPO):**
 - A novel reward function $R_{total} = R_{task} + R_{reflection}$ is introduced to incentivize both reasoning ability and effective self-reflection. The expected response format is: First Solution → Reflection → Second Refined Solution.
 - **Task Reward ($R_{task}$):** Combines:
 - **Format Reward ($R_{format}$):** +0.5 if the reasoning is enclosed in `<think>...</think>` tags.
 - **Accuracy Reward ($R_{accuracy}$):** +0.5 if the *first solution* matches the ground truth. This helps improve initial reasoning.
 - **Reflection Reward ($R_{reflection}$):** This is the core innovation for reflection awareness, comprising:
 - **Reflection Format Indicator ($I_{ref}$):** +0.25 if reflection segments are properly enclosed with `<reflection>...</reflection>` tags.
 - **Reflective Brevity Reward ($f_{len}(L_{response})$):** An exponential reward function that peaks at a `T_target` (optimal response length) and decays smoothly. This encourages concise, informative reflections, penalizing verbosity without hard cutoffs. `T_target` is set to 2x the length of the initial `think` segment, and `T_max` to 2.5x.
 - **Effectiveness Indicator ($I_{eff}$):** Directly evaluates the impact of reflection on the *final answer*:
 - +0.25 if reflection *preserves* a correct answer.
 - +0.5 if reflection *corrects* a wrong answer.
 - 0 if reflection fails to correct a wrong answer.
 - -0.25 if reflection *misconducts* a right answer into a wrong one.
 This crucial component encourages reflection as a tool for genuine improvement, discouraging superficial or harmful reflections.
- **Advantages over Standard GRPO:** SRPO's explicit structured reflection formatting ($I_{ref}$), smooth length reward ($f_{len}$), and direct effectiveness reward ($I_{eff}$) foster deeper self-correction, improve sample efficiency, and mitigate reward gaming behaviors (e.g., verbose or empty reflections), leading to superior performance in complex reasoning tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research conducted comprehensive experiments using Qwen-2.5-VL-7B and Qwen-2.5-VL-32B models across a wide array of multimodal reasoning benchmarks, demonstrating SRPO's significant effectiveness.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Performance

- **Multimodal Mathematical Reasoning (Table 1):** SRPO achieved state-of-the-art performance among open-source MLLMs and highly competitive results even against leading closed-source models.
 - On MathVista, SRPO-32B scored 78.5%, trailing only GPT-o1 (73.9%) among closed-source, but significantly outperforming other open-source reasoning models (e.g., VL-Rethinker-7B at 74.9%, MM-Eureka-7B at 73.0%).
 - Similar strong performance was observed across MathVerse, MathVision, OlympiadBench, and WeMath, with SRPO consistently demonstrating clear advantages over other open-source reasoning models.
- **Multimodal General Reasoning (Table 1):** SRPO-7B and SRPO-32B consistently outperformed existing open-source MLLMs on general-domain benchmarks like MMMU-Pro, MMMU, and EMMA. SRPO-32B even exceeded Gemini2-flash by 4.6 points on the EMMA benchmark. This highlights the broad generalizability of reflection-enhanced training.
- **Cross-disciplinary Reasoning (Table 2):** SRPO showed superior performance in physics, chemistry, and biology tasks within the MMK12 benchmark, surpassing models like MM-Eureka-7B (by 5.1 points on Physics) and OpenVLThinker-7B (by 9.7 points on Chemistry). This indicates that the integrated reflection-enhanced SFT and RL significantly improve generalization to unseen reasoning domains.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies (Table 3, 4, 5)

- **RL Training Data Size:** SRPO's performance improved with more data, but even with limited data (5K samples), it significantly outperformed Qwen-2.5-VL-7B and standard GRPO (e.g., +7.1 points on Physics over GRPO). This suggests that reflection enhancement efficiently boosts reasoning even with less data.
- **Effectiveness of Self-Reflection Components:**
 - Removing **Self-Reflection SFT** caused a notable performance drop, yet still maintained an advantage over standard GRPO, indicating its importance for cold-start initialization.
 - Eliminating **Self-Reflection RL** yielded minimal improvements over the base Qwen-2.5-VL-7B, confirming that SFT alone is insufficient and explicit incentivization during RL is crucial.
- **Effectiveness of Reflection-Aware RL Components:**
 - Omitting the **Effectiveness Reward ($I_{eff}$)** significantly degraded performance (average drop from 53.5 to 52.0), underscoring the critical role of directly rewarding effective reflection.
 - Removing the **Length Reward ($f_{len}$)** also led to a performance decline, suggesting that controlling verbosity is important for accurate reasoning.
- **Reflective vs. Two-Step Thinking:** A comparison showed that GRPO + 2-Step Thinking (consecutive `<think>` steps) offered no significant gains over vanilla GRPO. In contrast, SRPO's explicit reflection pattern (`think` → `reflection` → `rethink`) substantially improved reasoning, validating the utility of structured reflection.
- **Disentangling Reflection Format vs. Teacher Distillation (Table 5):** Experiments showed that adding explicit reflection segments in SFT (Reflection-SFT) yielded a +1.3 average gain over plain CoT SFT, indicating that the structured reflection format, not just teacher distillation, contributes to better reasoning. When both models underwent RL, SRPO achieved an additional +2.1 gain by aligning rewards with reflection effectiveness, emphasizing the benefit of the reflection-aware reward design.

#### 4.3. Qualitative Analysis (Figure 1, 3)

- **Self-Reflection in RL Training:** SRPO explicitly guides the model to effectively self-reflect on its initial reasoning, correcting wrong steps and providing concise revisions, leading to refined final answers. GRPO-generated samples lacked such explicit reflective corrections.
- **Self-Reflection in Test Cases:** During inference, SRPO-trained models exhibited clear self-reflection patterns, actively refining or correcting flawed reasoning and answers. Models without self-reflection rarely adjusted incorrect reasoning, leading to persistent errors.

#### 4.4. Further Analysis (Figure 4, 5)

- **RL Training Dynamics:** SRPO converged faster, consistently generated longer responses (due to reflection and revision), and showed more stable policy updates (lower, smoother ratio clip upper curve) compared to GRPO and SRPO w/o self-reflection SFT.
- **Generality with Alternative RL Methods:** Integrating SRPO's self-reflection strategy with PPO and DAPO algorithms consistently improved their performance, highlighting the generalizability of the approach. SRPO (GRPO-based) slightly surpassed reflection-enhanced PPO, suggesting GRPO's group-based advantage estimation combined with reflection-oriented rewards is particularly effective.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The SRPO framework represents a significant advancement in enhancing the reasoning capabilities of multimodal large language models (MLLMs). By systematically integrating reflection-oriented training into both supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and reinforcement learning (RL) stages, the authors have successfully equipped MLLMs with explicit self-reflection and self-correction behaviors that transcend the inherent limitations often set during initial pre-training.

**Key Contributions and Significance:**

- **Novel Reflection-Oriented Training Paradigm:** SRPO introduces a two-stage approach that not only leverages high-quality, reflection-focused datasets generated by advanced MLLMs but also employs a sophisticated, reflection-aware reward mechanism within an RL framework. This structured approach for imbuing self-reflection is a notable departure from previous, more simplistic methods.
- **Superior Reasoning Performance:** The consistent outperformance of SRPO against state-of-the-art open-source MLLMs and its competitive standing with leading closed-source models across diverse mathematical, general, and cross-disciplinary reasoning benchmarks underscores its effectiveness. This demonstrates that explicit reflection-oriented training can genuinely extend MLLMs' cognitive boundaries.
- **Enhanced Reflection Quality and Conciseness:** The carefully designed reward function, particularly the effectiveness indicator ($I_{eff}$) and brevity reward ($f_{len}$), is critical. It moves beyond merely generating verbose text, instead incentivizing meaningful, concise, and functionally beneficial self-reflection that either corrects errors or refines correct reasoning.
- **Improved Generalization:** The strong performance across various domains, including challenging cross-disciplinary tasks, suggests that SRPO fosters a more robust and transferable reasoning capability, moving beyond rote learning to more adaptable problem-solving.
- **Practical Applicability:** The method leverages open-source base models (Qwen-2.5-VL-7B and -32B) and demonstrates efficiency gains even with limited RL data, making it a viable approach for researchers and practitioners.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Advanced AI Assistants:** MLLMs trained with SRPO could power more reliable and accurate AI assistants capable of deeper understanding and problem-solving in complex domains like scientific research, engineering, and education. This could lead to more trustworthy AI tools for interpreting scientific data, solving complex equations, or providing detailed explanations.
- **Scientific Discovery and Education:** Improved multimodal reasoning can accelerate scientific discovery by enabling AI to analyze and interpret complex visual data (e.g., charts, diagrams, medical images) more effectively. In education, it could lead to AI tutors that not only provide answers but also guide students through reflective problem-solving processes.
- **Decision-Making Support:** In fields requiring critical analysis of visual and textual information, such as finance, logistics, or defense, SRPO-enhanced MLLMs could offer more robust decision support by identifying potential flaws in reasoning and proposing corrections.

**Limitations and Societal Implications:**

The authors acknowledge limitations, primarily that SRPO was evaluated on dense MLLMs at 7B and 32B scales, with no exploration of Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) or diffusion LM architectures, or larger-scale commercial reasoning datasets. Future work could explore these avenues.

From a societal perspective, while the advancements offer clear positive contributions, improved reasoning capabilities also carry potential risks. MLLMs, if not carefully trained or if reflecting on biased data, could generate more convincing yet inaccurate or biased content. This necessitates careful curation of training datasets and explicit efforts to mitigate biases. Additionally, without equitable access, advanced multimodal reasoning models could inadvertently reinforce existing societal inequalities. The authors rightly emphasize the importance of responsible and transparent use of these enhanced frameworks to ensure positive societal outcomes.
