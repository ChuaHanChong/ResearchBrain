# AnyTouch: Learning Unified Static-Dynamic Representation across Multiple Visuo-tactile Sensors

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Renmin University of China and collaborators developed `AnyTouch`, a framework that learns unified static and dynamic representations across multiple visuo-tactile sensors. The system leverages a newly collected, aligned `TacQuad` dataset, resulting in superior performance in tactile perception tasks and robust generalization to previously unseen sensors.

## Problem

- Low standardization across diverse visuo-tactile sensors, leading to fragmented data and limited generalizability of sensor-specific models.
- A lack of explicitly aligned multi-sensor data, hindering effective knowledge transfer and unified representation learning.
- Existing models struggled to capture both static (e.g., texture, material) and dynamic (e.g., sliding, pressure changes) aspects of tactile perception comprehensively.

## Method

- Created `TacQuad`, a comprehensive dataset with aligned multi-modal (touch, vision, language) and multi-sensor data from four distinct visuo-tactile sensors, collected using fine-grained and coarse-grained strategies.
- Designed the `AnyTouch` framework with a multi-level architecture for unified static-dynamic perception, combining masked modeling for pixel-level detail with multi-modal alignment and cross-sensor matching for semantic features.
- Introduced a universal sensor token strategy to enable generalization to unseen sensors by progressively replacing sensor-specific tokens with a universal one during training.

## Results

- `AnyTouch` consistently outperformed state-of-the-art single-sensor and multi-sensor baselines on various static perception tasks (e.g., material, hardness classification, grasp prediction).
- The framework demonstrated strong generalization to unseen sensors, achieving superior performance on material classification tasks on datasets like ObjectFolder 1.0 (TACTO) and ObjectFolder 2.0 (Taxim).
- It achieved the lowest mean error in a challenging real-world fine-grained pouring task using a GelSight Mini sensor, highlighting the benefits of unified static-dynamic representations for complex manipulation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly aligned multi-sensor, multi-modal data is crucial for bridging the gap between diverse tactile sensors and learning truly unified representations.
> - A multi-level training approach, from pixel-level reconstruction to semantic-level multi-modal alignment and cross-sensor matching, effectively fosters a sensor-agnostic representation space.
> - The use of a universal sensor token enables models to generalize effectively to entirely new sensor types not present during training, enhancing real-world applicability.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic tactile perception is rapidly advancing, aiming to imbue robots with human-like sensing capabilities for delicate and complex manipulation tasks. Visuo-tactile sensors, which capture contact deformations through visual means, have emerged as a prominent sub-area due to their high resolution. However, the research landscape for tactile sensing, particularly with visuo-tactile sensors, is fragmented by a fundamental challenge: **low standardization**.

Historically, diverse tactile sensors have been developed (e.g., Liu et al., 2022; Maiolino et al., 2013; Yuan et al., 2017), each with unique characteristics and data formats. This leads to sensor-specific data collection and model training, which severely limits the scale and diversity of data available for any single sensor. Consequently, models often exhibit suboptimal perception capabilities and lack generalizability across different sensor types. Previous works have successfully applied visuo-tactile sensors to tasks like grasping (Xu et al., 2024) and insertion (Li et al., 2014), but these efforts often remain confined to the specific sensor they were trained on.

Recent attempts have started to explore multi-sensor data collaboration to enhance cross-sensor knowledge transfer (Yang et al., 2024; Zhao et al., 2024). However, these efforts have been hampered by a critical missing piece: the lack of *aligned* multi-sensor data. Without explicit alignment, integrating disparate sensor data into a unified representation space remains challenging. While Rodriguez et al. (2024) introduced a dual-sensor paired dataset, its scope was limited in terms of sensor variety, collected objects, and crucially, it overlooked the benefits of paired multi-modal data (e.g., vision and language alongside touch) for enhancing transferability and comprehensive perception.

In the broader context of representation learning, techniques like masked autoencoders (MAE) (He et al., 2022; Tong et al., 2022) and multi-modal aligning (Yang et al., 2024; Cheng et al., 2024), inspired by successes in vision-language pre-training (Radford et al., 2021), have been applied to tactile data. These methods have improved single-sensor tactile models by learning features from tactile images or aligning them with other modalities. However, none have effectively addressed the holistic challenge of creating a truly *unified multi-sensor representation* that is adaptable to a wide array of tasks (both static and dynamic) while bridging the significant gap between *multiple* diverse visuo-tactile sensors and leveraging comprehensive multi-modal information.

`AnyTouch` directly addresses these limitations by providing both an explicitly aligned multi-sensor, multi-modal dataset (`TacQuad`) and a novel framework designed to learn unified static-dynamic representations. By integrating various sensors and modalities and drawing inspiration from human tactile perception (which encompasses both static and dynamic aspects), this work aims to move beyond sensor-specific models towards a more generalizable and powerful tactile AI system, thereby standardizing the learning paradigm for visuo-tactile perception.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this research stems from the inherent difficulties posed by the low standardization of visuo-tactile sensors in robotics. Each sensor type often possesses distinct data characteristics, leading to a fragmented ecosystem where data collected for one sensor is largely incompatible or difficult to transfer to another. This severely limits the scale and diversity of training data for any single sensor, resulting in models with suboptimal perception capabilities and poor generalization.

The overarching goal of this work is to establish a powerful and generalizable tactile perception system for robots, one that can precisely understand and manipulate objects, akin to human tactile abilities. To achieve this, the authors identify a critical need: **learning unified multi-sensor representations**. Such representations would integrate disparate sensors, enabling effective knowledge transfer between them and overcoming the data scarcity problem.

The key objectives that drive the `AnyTouch` project can be summarized as follows:

1. **Develop a Comprehensive, Aligned Multi-Sensor Dataset:** To explicitly bridge the data gap between various visuo-tactile sensors, a crucial objective was to collect `TacQuad`, an aligned multi-modal, multi-sensor tactile dataset. This dataset is designed to provide diverse data from representative public, self-made, and force-field sensors, along with corresponding visual images and textual descriptions, to facilitate explicit integration and semantic understanding.
2. **Learn Unified Static-Dynamic Representations:** Recognizing that human tactile perception is a dynamic interplay of static properties (e.g., texture, material) and dynamic changes (e.g., sliding, pressure variation), a core objective is to learn unified multi-sensor representations that capture both static and dynamic tactile information. This aims to create a perception system adaptable to a wide array of tasks requiring different types of tactile understanding.
3. **Enhance Comprehensive Perceptual Abilities and Cross-Sensor Transferability:** The research aims to design a learning framework (`AnyTouch`) that not only extracts fine-grained pixel-level tactile details but also understands semantic-level, sensor-agnostic features. This dual focus is intended to lead to models with robust perceptual capabilities that can generalize effectively across different sensor types, including those unseen during training.
4. **Promote Knowledge Transfer to Unseen Sensors:** A specific objective is to enable seamless knowledge transfer from seen sensors to unseen ones, thereby maximizing the utility of collected multi-sensor data and reducing the need for extensive data collection for every new sensor. This is addressed through novel architectural and training strategies.

By addressing these objectives, the authors aim to move beyond sensor-specific limitations and establish a more standardized and versatile paradigm for visuo-tactile perception in robotics.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The `AnyTouch` framework tackles the challenge of learning unified static-dynamic representations across multiple visuo-tactile sensors through a comprehensive approach that involves novel data collection and a multi-level learning architecture.

#### 3.1. Aligned Multi-Modal Multi-Sensor Tactile Dataset (TacQuad)

The foundation of `AnyTouch` is the novel `TacQuad` dataset, meticulously designed to overcome the lack of aligned multi-sensor data. It features:
- **Sensor Diversity:** Data from four distinct visuo-tactile sensors: GelSight Mini (publicly available), DIGIT (publicly available), DuraGel (self-made), and Tac3D (a force field sensor). This selection ensures representation across different sensor technologies and data outputs (tactile images and deformation force fields).
- **Dual-Strategy Data Collection:**
 - **Fine-grained spatio-temporal aligned data:** Collected using a calibration platform where sensors are precisely controlled to press the same position on the same object with identical speed and depth. This ensures high-accuracy spatio-temporal alignment for 17,524 contact frames across 25 objects.
 - **Coarse-grained spatial aligned data:** Acquired through handheld collection, where sensors sequentially touch the same location on an object, often with twisting motions to simulate dynamic human touch. This method allows for larger-scale data collection (55,082 frames from 99 objects, including indoor and outdoor scenes) while maintaining spatial alignment.
- **Multi-Modality:** Each tactile frame is paired with a visual image of the object and detailed tactile attribute descriptions generated (and manually corrected) using GPT-4o. This multi-modal pairing serves as a crucial bridge to mitigate sensor variability and enables the model to learn semantic-level tactile attributes.

#### 3.2. AnyTouch Learning Framework

The `AnyTouch` framework is built upon a multi-level architecture designed to learn unified static-dynamic representations from both pixel-level details and semantic-level sensor-agnostic features.

- **Unified Input Format for Static and Dynamic Perception:**
 The framework unifies the processing of static tactile images and dynamic tactile video clips. A static image `I` is treated as a single-frame video and replicated `F` times to form a 4-D tensor $X^T \in R^{F \times H \times W \times 3}$. This allows a shared patch projection layer to convert both images and videos into spatio-temporal tokens ($z \in R^{N \times d}$), enhancing the model's ability to comprehend both types of information.

- **Multi-Level Training Stages:**

 - **Stage 1: Masked Modeling for Pixel-Level Details:**
 This stage focuses on capturing fine-grained tactile deformations and temporal dynamics. Inspired by Masked Autoencoders (MAE), the framework randomly masks portions of both tactile images and videos. A decoder then reconstructs the masked pixels.
 - **Loss Functions:** Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss is used for static image reconstruction ($L_{S_{rec}}$) and dynamic video reconstruction ($L_{D_{rec}}$).
 - **Next Frame Prediction:** To further enhance understanding of continuous deformation, an additional MSE loss ($L_{D_{pred}}$) is introduced to predict the next frame in a dynamic video sequence.

 - **Stage 2: Semantic-Level Features via Multi-Modal Alignment and Cross-Sensor Matching:**
 After learning pixel-level details, this stage aims to understand higher-level semantic properties and reduce the gap between sensors.
 - **Modality-Missing-Aware Multi-Modal Aligning:** Leverages the paired touch-vision-language data. The text modality is used as an anchor to align touch, vision, and text representations, as it offers consistent descriptions of tactile attributes across datasets. The method is designed to be compatible with missing modalities by maximizing the use of available paired data within each batch for contrastive learning ($L_{align}$). GPT-4o is used to expand text modality pairings across various datasets.
 - **Cross-Sensor Matching:** A novel task that explicitly compels the model to cluster representations of the same tactile information from different sensors. The model is trained to determine if two tactile inputs originate from the *same position on the same object* across different sensors. Positive pairs are from the same object/position but different sensors; negative pairs are from different objects/positions. A Binary Cross Entropy Loss ($L_{match}$) guides this matching, fostering sensor-agnostic feature extraction and a unified representation space.

- **Universal Sensor Token:**
 To integrate sensor-specific information and maximize knowledge transfer, especially to unseen sensors, the framework introduces learnable sensor-specific tokens ($s_k$) and a universal sensor token ($s_u$). During training, the sensor-specific token $s_k$ for a given input is randomly replaced by the universal token $s_u$ with a linearly increasing probability ($p_u$). During inference, only the universal sensor token $s_u$ is used for data from new sensors, ensuring the model can generalize by leveraging integrated knowledge.

- **Training Paradigm:**
 The framework employs a sequential, multi-level training process. Stage 1 (masked modeling) is completed first, followed by Stage 2 (multi-modal aligning and cross-sensor matching), allowing the model to progressively learn from fine-grained to semantic-level features.

This comprehensive methodology, combining novel data collection with a multi-level learning architecture and specific strategies for cross-sensor generalization, forms the core of `AnyTouch`.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted provide strong evidence for the effectiveness of `AnyTouch` in achieving unified static-dynamic multi-sensor representations and enhancing transferability across diverse tactile tasks and sensors.

#### 4.1. Sensor Transferability Analysis (Q1)

- **Pre-training is Crucial:** Initial experiments demonstrate that pre-training with tactile data, even from a single sensor, significantly boosts performance on downstream tasks across all sensors compared to models without tactile pre-training (e.g., CLIP). This highlights the generalizability of learned tactile features.
- **Benefits of Multi-Sensor Integration:** Sequentially integrating data from multiple sensors (GelSight, GelSlim, DIGIT, GelSight Mini) consistently improves performance on *unseen datasets* (both from seen and unseen sensors). This indicates effective knowledge transfer across different hardware. The improvements were more pronounced for truly unseen sensors, confirming the value of multi-sensor pre-training for generalization.
- **Task-Specific Trade-offs:** An interesting observation was a slight performance drop in certain *seen dataset* tasks (e.g., TAG material classification) when more diverse sensor data was integrated. This is attributed to the reduced proportion of the specific target dataset within the larger pre-training corpus, suggesting a balance between broad generalization and domain-specific fine-tuning. Differences in hardware characteristics between sensors also influenced the extent of positive transfer.

#### 4.2. Unified Multi-Sensor Representation Space (Q2)

- **Visual Confirmation of Unified Space:** Using t-SNE visualizations on `TacQuad`'s fine-grained subset, the study qualitatively shows the evolution of the representation space:
 - **CLIP:** Without tactile exposure, CLIP clusters samples primarily by sensor type, failing to recognize common tactile information across different sensors.
 - **Masked Modeling Only:** Representations become more centralized *within* each sensor, as the focus is on pixel-level, sensor-dependent features. This is not ideal for cross-sensor generalization.
 - **Multi-Modal Aligning Added:** Representations from different sensors begin to blend and cluster by the *objects* they represent, indicating the emergence of cross-sensor generalization.
 - **Cross-Sensor Matching Added:** The representations from various sensors fully mix into a shared multi-sensor space, clustering distinctly by the *object's tactile information*. This visually confirms `AnyTouch`'s ability to extract sensor-agnostic features and form a truly unified representation space.

#### 4.3. Static Perception on Seen Sensors (Q3)

- `AnyTouch` consistently outperforms state-of-the-art single-sensor models (VIT-LENS-2, TLV-Link, OmniBind) and the multi-sensor `UniTouch` model (even when `UniTouch` was trained on the target data) on tasks like material, hardness, roughness classification, and grasp prediction (datasets like TAG and Feel). This demonstrates the superior static perception capabilities and the benefit of `AnyTouch`'s unified representations on sensors seen during training. The integration of dynamic perception and more multi-sensor data generally led to the best overall performance in various tasks.

#### 4.4. Static Perception on Unseen Sensors (Q3)

- `AnyTouch` exhibits strong generalization capabilities to *unseen sensors*. It outperforms `UniTouch` on datasets like ObjectFolder 1.0 (TACTO) and ObjectFolder 2.0 (Taxim) in material classification. This is a crucial finding, indicating that `AnyTouch`'s sensor-agnostic features are indeed transferable, allowing it to interpret tactile data from entirely new sensor types without direct exposure during training. The model trained on the full dataset achieved the highest performance on ObjectFolder 1.0, reinforcing the effectiveness of learning sensor-agnostic semantic features.

#### 4.5. Real-World Dynamic Perception (Q3)

- In the challenging real-world fine-grained pouring task using GelSight Mini, `AnyTouch` significantly improves performance:
 - A static-only `AnyTouch` model performs comparably to `T3`, a strong multi-sensor baseline trained on a larger dataset (3M vs. `AnyTouch`'s 2.48M). This underscores the efficiency and effectiveness of `AnyTouch`'s learned representations even without explicit dynamic modules.
 - `AnyTouch` with integrated dynamic perception capabilities achieves the *best performance* (lowest mean error), demonstrating the critical importance of learning unified static-dynamic representations for complex real-world manipulation tasks. CLIP, lacking tactile perception, struggles significantly.

#### 4.6. Cross-Sensor Generation (Appendix A.10)

- Further experiments in the appendix reveal that `AnyTouch` excels in cross-sensor generation. It outperforms `T3` in generating DuraGel images from GelSight Mini inputs and reconstructing force fields from the unseen Tac3D sensor based on DIGIT/GelSight Mini data. This highlights the high quality and versatility of the unified representations, capable of not just understanding but also translating tactile information between different sensors.

In summary, the results convincingly demonstrate that `AnyTouch` successfully creates a unified multi-sensor representation space, enables strong cross-sensor transferability, and delivers superior performance in both static and dynamic tactile perception tasks, including challenging real-world scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The `AnyTouch` research represents a significant advancement in the field of robotic tactile perception, with several profound implications and potential impacts:

1. **Bridging the Standardization Gap:** The most critical contribution is directly addressing the long-standing challenge of low standardization in visuo-tactile sensors. By successfully learning unified representations across diverse sensors, `AnyTouch` moves the field closer to a plug-and-play future for tactile sensing, where new sensors can be integrated into robotic systems with minimal re-training and data collection.

2. **Novel Data Resource for the Community:** The introduction of `TacQuad`, an explicitly aligned multi-modal, multi-sensor tactile dataset, is a monumental step. It provides a unique and much-needed resource for researchers to train and evaluate models for cross-sensor transfer and multi-modal integration. The dual data collection strategy (fine-grained and coarse-grained) is innovative in balancing precision with scale, fostering more robust research. Its open availability will undoubtedly accelerate progress in the field.

3. **Unified Static-Dynamic Perception Paradigm:** `AnyTouch`'s design, inspired by human tactile perception of both static properties and dynamic changes, establishes a novel and comprehensive learning paradigm. This unified approach makes robots more versatile, enabling them to tackle a broader spectrum of manipulation tasks that require both instantaneous contact understanding and continuous monitoring of interactions.

4. **Enhanced Cross-Sensor Transferability:** The framework's ability to extract sensor-agnostic features, validated by superior performance on unseen sensors and in cross-sensor generation tasks, is transformative. This reduces the immense burden of sensor-specific data collection and model training, allowing for faster deployment of tactile AI in new robotic platforms and applications. The universal sensor token strategy is a clever mechanism for facilitating this generalization.

5. **Multi-Level Feature Learning:** The multi-level architecture, which progresses from pixel-level detail learning (via masked modeling) to semantic-level understanding (via multi-modal alignment and cross-sensor matching), offers a robust and comprehensive approach to tactile feature extraction. This ensures that the learned representations are rich enough for both fine-grained analysis and high-level conceptual understanding.

6. **Advancing Real-World Robotics:** The demonstrated success in a complex real-world task like fine-grained pouring underscores the practical utility of `AnyTouch`. By enabling robots to perform such tasks with enhanced tactile intelligence, the work contributes directly to the development of more dexterous, adaptable, and autonomous robotic systems for manufacturing, healthcare, service industries, and beyond.

7. **Foundation for Future Research:** By open-sourcing the dataset, code, and model, the authors are setting a new standard and providing a strong foundation for future research. This will likely inspire further exploration into multi-sensor fusion, more diverse tactile modalities, advanced representation learning techniques, and expanded applications in robotic manipulation and human-robot interaction. The work also opens avenues for investigating how large language models can further enhance tactile reasoning by providing richer semantic context.

In conclusion, `AnyTouch` pushes the boundaries of robotic tactile perception by offering a principled and effective solution to the long-standing problem of sensor heterogeneity. Its contributions lay crucial groundwork for realizing truly generalizable and human-like tactile intelligence in robotic systems.
