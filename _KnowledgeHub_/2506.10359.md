# Demonstrating Multi-Suction Item Picking at Scale via Multi-Modal Learning of Pick Success

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Amazon Robotics developed a multi-modal deep learning system for predicting the success of robotic picks using multi-suction grippers on diverse, open-set items. Their approach, based on in-domain pretraining and fine-tuning a MultiMAE architecture, achieved 90.6 AUC for pick success, outperforming previous expert-engineered methods by 5 percentage points and demonstrating significant reductions in mispicks and human interventions in real-world warehouse deployments.

## Problem

- Robotic item picking from unstructured piles presents a challenge, particularly for an open set of diverse items in high-throughput industrial environments.
- Previous deep learning approaches for package picking had been outperformed by shallow models utilizing human-engineered features.
- High throughput demands solutions that are robust to clutter and occlusion while minimizing costly errors like mispicks and items requiring human intervention (amnesty).

## Method

- Develops a multi-modal deep learning approach leveraging ==MultiMAE== as a visual encoder to process RGB, depth, and semantic segmentation images.
- Implements a two-stage training process: ==self-supervised pretraining== on large-scale, in-domain industrial data followed by ==supervised fine-tuning== for pick success prediction.
- Integrates specific robotic pick features (e.g., coordinates, suction pistons) into the visual model using a ==cross-attention mechanism== for contextual understanding.

## Results

- Achieved **90.6 AUC** on largely unoccluded item picks, outperforming a shallow model with expert features (**85.1 AUC**) by over **5 percentage points**.
- Demonstrated robust performance on partially occluded items and generalized to package picking, surpassing baselines in both scenarios.
- Real-world test deployments showed the potential to reduce mispicks by **38%** and amnesty cases by **41%**, indicating substantial operational efficiency gains.
- Ablation studies confirmed that multi-modal pretraining, in-domain data, encoder fine-tuning, and cross-attention for pick feature integration are critical for performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Multi-modal, in-domain pretraining on large real-world datasets drastically improves performance, outperforming generic pretraining or no pretraining.
> - Deep visual architectures can automatically learn representations that surpass expert-engineered features for complex open-set item picking tasks.
> - Integrating specific pick features and location information via cross-attention and dedicated input modalities is crucial for accurate pick success prediction.
> - Fine-tuning the entire visual encoder (not just freezing it) during the downstream task is essential for optimal performance, allowing task-specific adaptation of features.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses the critical and challenging task of **robotic item picking from unstructured piles**, especially in the context of high-throughput industrial environments like warehouses. This problem has been a long-standing challenge in robotics, typically approached through:

- **Traditional Model-Based Reasoning:** Early methods often relied on geometric reasoning, planning, and optimization, requiring accurate models of objects and their properties (e.g., force closure). While robust for known objects, these methods struggle with the "open set of items" and cluttered, unstructured scenes prevalent in real-world logistics.
- **Learning-Based Approaches:** Over the past decade, data-driven methods have gained prominence, particularly since events like the Amazon Picking Challenge. These approaches learn grasping policies directly from data, offering better generalization to unknown objects and cluttered environments.
 - **Gripper Types:** Learning-based methods have been explored for various end-effectors, including parallel/pinch grippers and suction-based grippers. This paper specifically focuses on **multi-suction grippers**, which are favored in warehouse settings for their simplicity and robustness in handling heavy or irregular items.
 - **Data Domain:** Much prior learning-based work is limited to closed sets of items or relies heavily on training in simulation. This paper distinguishes itself by focusing on **open-set item picking** using **large-scale, real-world industrial data**, which is much more challenging due to the immense diversity of items and configurations.
 - **Representation Learning:** The paper directly builds upon and contrasts with recent work on learning visual representations for robotic tasks.
 - **Shallow Models vs. Deep Learning:** A particularly important piece of prior work by Li et al., also from Amazon Robotics, demonstrated that a shallow model with human-engineered features outperformed a deep learning model for package picking. This paper directly confronts that finding, aiming to show that with proper architectural choices and training strategies, deep learning can indeed surpass expert-engineered features for the more complex task of open-set item picking.
 - **Generic vs. In-Domain Pretraining:** Many state-of-the-art visual encoders (e.g., MAE, DINO, MVP) are pretrained on large generic datasets like ImageNet and then often used as frozen feature extractors for downstream tasks. This paper critically investigates the effectiveness of **in-domain pretraining** and **finetuning** on proprietary industrial data, demonstrating its significant benefits over generic pretraining or frozen encoders.
 - **Multi-Modal Learning:** Recognizing that robotic work cells provide diverse sensor data (RGB, depth, semantics), the paper integrates advancements in multi-modal, multi-task models like MultiMAE. This is a novel application of such models to large-scale pick success prediction, addressing the hypothesis that combining modalities can yield richer representations.

In summary, this work pushes the boundaries of robotic picking by demonstrating a highly effective, data-driven deep learning solution for a notoriously difficult problem: picking an open set of diverse items from clutter in a high-throughput industrial setting. It challenges previous findings on the superiority of shallow models and highlights the crucial role of in-domain, multi-modal representation learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind this research are:

- **To demonstrate the effectiveness of autonomously learning robotic operations from sparsely-labeled, real-world data at industrial scale.** The core premise is that leveraging vast amounts of real-world interaction data from deployed systems, even with sparse labeling (e.g., just pick success/failure), can lead to superior robotic solutions.
- **To achieve improved performance in multi-suction robot picking.** Specifically, the goal is to enhance the prediction of pick success for candidate robotic picks, which is crucial for maximizing throughput and minimizing costly errors in warehouse automation.
- **To address the challenge of picking diverse, open-set items from unstructured piles.** Real-world warehouses deal with millions of unique items in arbitrary configurations. The solution must generalize well beyond a closed set of known items and handle significant clutter and occlusion.
- **To overcome the limitations of prior deep learning approaches in this domain.** The paper is explicitly motivated by the finding from previous work that a shallow model with expert-engineered features outperformed deep learning for package picking. A key objective is to show that modern deep visual architectures can learn effective representations *automatically* that surpass such expert features, thereby automating and simplifying solution development.
- **To exploit the availability of multi-modal sensory data.** Robotic work cells provide RGB, depth, and semantic information. The motivation is to investigate how state-of-the-art multi-modal visual encoders can learn expressive representations by combining these diverse inputs.
- **To validate the benefits of in-domain pretraining and finetuning.** Contrary to trends of using generic, frozen encoders, the authors aim to demonstrate that pretraining and fine-tuning on large amounts of *domain-specific* data yield significant performance gains.
- **To reduce the reliance on human expert feature engineering.** By learning representations automatically, the goal is to make the development and deployment of picking solutions more scalable and less dependent on specialized human knowledge.

In essence, the research aims to develop a robust, high-performance, and scalable learning-based system for autonomous item picking in real-world industrial environments, by effectively leveraging multi-modal data and advanced deep learning architectures.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology revolves around a **multi-modal visual encoder** and a two-stage training process: self-supervised pretraining followed by fine-tuning for pick success prediction.

**A. Datasets:**
The work utilizes large-scale, real-world industrial datasets from Amazon's operations:
- **Standard Dataset:** 343K multi-modal images for pretraining (RGB, depth, semantic segmentation) and 275K training examples for fine-tuning, with pick candidate features and success/failure labels. This dataset focuses on largely unoccluded item picks. The success-to-failure ratio is highly imbalanced (11:1).
- **Random Dataset:** A smaller dataset (8.4K training examples) where picks were generated randomly, leading to more instances of partially occluded items. This tests robustness to different item configurations.
- **Package Picking Dataset:** 100K training examples for picking packages (boxes, envelopes), which have less appearance variety than open-set items. This tests generalizability to different object domains.

**B. Preliminary Evaluation of Visual Encoders:**
The authors first confirm the prior finding that a shallow model with expert features outperforms generic pretrained deep visual encoders (e.g., ViT with MAE, DINO, MVP pretraining on ImageNet) when trained on unimodal (RGB) input. This motivates their proposed improvements.

**C. Demonstrated Strategy (MultiMAE-based Pick Success Prediction):**
The core approach (illustrated in Fig. 1) consists of:
1. **Multi-Modal Visual Encoder:** Utilizes **MultiMAE (Multi-modal Multi-task Masked Autoencoder)** as the visual backbone. MultiMAE can process multiple input modalities (RGB, depth, semantic segmentation) and learn expressive representations. Each modality is processed into image tokens with different input adapters.
 - **Input Modalities:** The default approach uses RGB, depth, and a "pick location image" during finetuning/inference. Semantic segmentation is primarily used during pretraining (and optionally during finetuning).
 - **Local Crops:** Input images are cropped to a local bounding box around the target item's segmentation mask (with padding), found to be more effective than global scene images.
2. **Pick Feature Integration:** Instead of simple mean pooling of visual tokens, the model incorporates **pick features** (X, Y, Z coordinates, activated suction pistons, end-effector orientation) using a **cross-attention mechanism**. This allows the model to learn relationships between the encoded visual scene and specific pick candidate information, enabling a weighted summation of visual tokens based on the pick features. An explicit "pick location image" (a 2D image marking the pick point) is also optionally used as an additional visual input.
3. **Pick Success Prediction Head:** The combined representation (weighted sum of visual tokens + projected pick features) is fed into a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) for binary classification (pick success/failure).

**D. Two-Stage Training Process:**
1. **Pretraining (MultiModal Autoencoder):**
 - **Self-supervised:** MultiMAE is pretrained on 343K examples from the standard dataset in a self-supervised manner.
 - **Objective:** The model learns to reconstruct randomly masked portions of input RGB, depth, and semantic segmentation images. This forces the model to learn correlations across modalities and robust representations of the scene.
 - **Initialization:** MultiMAE is initialized with weights from a publicly available version pretrained on ImageNet and then further pretrained for 800 epochs on the in-domain data.
 - **Semantic Segmentation:** Semantic inputs (9 classes) are downsampled (56x56) to reduce computation.
2. **Fine-tuning for Pick Success Prediction:**
 - **Supervised:** The entire model (MultiMAE encoder + cross-attention + prediction head) is fine-tuned on the 275K training examples with annotated pick success/failure.
 - **Objective:** Binary cross-entropy loss (weighted to account for class imbalance) to predict pick success.
 - **Encoder Update:** Crucially, the MultiMAE encoder weights are **updated (finetuned)** during this stage, not frozen, which the authors show is vital for performance.
 - **Data Augmentation:** Random shifts and padding applied to local crops during training to improve robustness.

**E. Evaluation Metrics:**
The primary metric is **Area Under the Receiver Operating Characteristic Curve (ROC AUC)**, which is suitable for imbalanced datasets and assesses performance across various decision thresholds.

This systematic approach, combining advanced multi-modal deep learning with large-scale in-domain data and a two-stage training strategy, aims to learn highly effective representations for complex robotic manipulation tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents a comprehensive experimental evaluation, demonstrating the superior performance of their proposed multi-modal learning approach.

**A. Performance on Largely Unoccluded Item Picks (Standard Dataset):**
- The **demonstrated MultiMAE approach** achieved a **90.6 AUC**, significantly outperforming both:
 - A **shallow model with expert-engineered features (AUC ~85.1)**, by about 5 percentage points. This is a crucial finding, showing that automated deep learning can indeed surpass human-engineered features at scale.
 - A **unimodal MAE baseline (AUC 79.1)** and a learn-from-scratch **PointTransformerV3 (PTv3) model (AUC 84.6)**, highlighting the benefits of multi-modal and in-domain pretraining.

**B. Performance on Partially Occluded Item Picks (Random Dataset):**
- The demonstrated approach consistently **outperformed the shallow model** across various training and inference settings (e.g., 86.28 AUC vs. 82.92 AUC when finetuned on random dataset, and 88.06 AUC vs. 85.16 AUC when finetuned on both standard and random datasets).
- This demonstrates the **robustness and generalization capability** of the learned representations to scenes with different item configurations, including challenging partial occlusions.

**C. Performance on Package Picking Dataset:**
- Even on a different domain (package picking), the demonstrated approach (88.28 AUC) **outperformed the shallow model (86.50 AUC)** and other baselines. This indicates the generalizability of the methodology to different types of manipulable objects.

**D. Key Takeaways from Ablation Studies:**
The extensive ablation studies provide critical insights into the proposed method's components:
1. **Effect of Visual Modalities:** Pretraining with more modalities (RGB, Depth, Semantics) significantly boosts performance, even if only RGB is used during finetuning (90.05 AUC vs. 87.35 AUC for RGB only pretrain). Using both RGB and Depth during finetuning yields the best performance (90.60 AUC).
2. **Effect of In-Domain Pretraining:** In-domain multi-modal pretraining (90.60 AUC) drastically outperforms no pretraining (80.44 AUC) or pretraining on generic datasets like ImageNet (84.43 AUC for R-D-S generic). This is a strong argument for the value of large-scale domain-specific data.
3. **Effect of Local Crop Sizes:** Using a local crop centered around the target item (with padding of 50 pixels yielding 90.60 AUC) is superior to using the global scene image (89.32 AUC). This focuses the model's attention on relevant visual information.
4. **Different Ways to Incorporate Pick Features:**
 - **Cross-attention** significantly improves performance by learning to weight visual tokens based on pick features (88.55 AUC vs. 85.76 AUC for global images with mean pooling). Visualizations confirm that cross-attention helps the model focus on the target item and pick point.
 - Explicitly providing a **pick location image** as an additional input modality further boosts performance, particularly with local crops (90.60 AUC).
 - The effects of cross-attention, local crop, and pick location image are partly overlapping but cumulatively improve performance.
5. **Effect of Finetuning the Visual Encoder:** Fine-tuning the MultiMAE encoder weights during the pick success prediction stage (90.60 AUC) significantly outperforms freezing the encoder weights (87.89 AUC), indicating the importance of task-specific adaptation for the backbone.
6. **Effect of Data Augmentation:** Even with large datasets, random shifts and padding in local crops provide a marginal but consistent performance improvement (90.60 AUC vs. 90.40 AUC).
7. **Effect of Pretraining Epochs and Data Ratio:**
 - Pretraining for longer epochs (800 epochs achieved best performance) continues to improve performance, although gains diminish after 200 epochs.
 - Crucially, even **1% of the in-domain pretraining data (3.4K examples) provides significant benefit (87.90 AUC)**, already outperforming the shallow model and generic pretrained models, highlighting the high sample efficiency of in-domain pretraining.

**E. Impact of the Demonstrated Approach (Real-World Deployment):**
- **Confusion Matrix Analysis:** The demonstrated approach reduced the false positive rate by 9% and the false negative rate by 1% compared to the shallow model.
- **Real-World Test Deployment:** In a one-week test deployment involving 17K pick attempts, the approach demonstrated the potential to reduce **multipicks by 2%, mispicks by 38%, and amnesty (items requiring human intervention) by 41%**. These are highly impactful reductions for large-scale industrial operations, translating to significant cost savings and increased efficiency.

In summary, the results overwhelmingly support the proposed multi-modal, in-domain pretraining and finetuning strategy, showing it to be superior to existing baselines, robust across diverse conditions, and demonstrably impactful in real-world industrial settings.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The significance and potential impact of this research are substantial, particularly within the domain of robotic automation and logistics:

- **Advancement in Robotic Picking Capabilities:** The paper demonstrates a state-of-the-art solution that achieves significantly higher pick success prediction accuracy for an open set of items in highly unstructured environments. This directly translates to more reliable and efficient autonomous manipulation in warehouses.
- **Reduction in Operational Costs:** The real-world deployment results showing a 38% reduction in mispicks and 41% reduction in amnesty are exceptionally impactful. Mispicks (incorrectly picked items) and amnesty (items that robots cannot pick, requiring human intervention) are major cost drivers in fulfillment centers due to lost inventory, re-sorting, and labor. These reductions lead to substantial cost savings and increased throughput at scale.
- **Shift from Expert-Engineered Features to Automated Learning:** The most significant finding is the ability of deep learning to automatically learn robust visual representations that surpass the performance of human-engineered features. This is a pivotal step towards more autonomous system development, reducing the need for laborious and specialized expert knowledge in feature design. This paradigm shift can accelerate the deployment of robotic solutions across various tasks.
- **Validation of In-Domain Multi-Modal Learning:** The research strongly advocates for the power of self-supervised pretraining on large quantities of *domain-specific, multi-modal data*. This challenges the common practice of relying solely on generic pretraining datasets and frozen encoders, suggesting that for high-stakes industrial applications, investing in large-scale in-domain data collection and training is highly rewarding.
- **Foundation for Future Robotic AI:** By establishing a robust method for pick success prediction, this work lays the groundwork for more complex robotic behaviors. The learned multi-modal representations could be leveraged for other downstream tasks like damage prediction, targeted picking, or even learning pick generation strategies themselves, as mentioned in future directions.
- **Scalability and Robustness:** The method's demonstrated performance across diverse item types (open-set), occlusion levels, and even different object domains (items vs. packages) underscores its practical scalability and robustness, which are paramount for industrial adoption.
- **Practical Insights for ML Practitioners:** The extensive ablation studies offer valuable insights for machine learning engineers and researchers working on real-world robotics, detailing the importance of specific architectural choices (cross-attention), input modalities (local crops, pick location images), and training strategies (finetuning the encoder, data augmentation, pretraining duration and data ratio).

**Limitations (as stated by authors):**
The paper acknowledges several limitations:
1. **Reliance on Heuristic Pick Generator:** The success prediction model evaluates candidate picks generated by a *heuristic* approach. Developing a learning-based pick generator is a future direction.
2. **Specific Robotic Embodiment:** The experiments are focused on a single robotic arm with a multi-suction end-effector, limiting immediate generalization to multi-arm settings or other gripper types (e.g., pinch, soft grippers).
3. **Industrial Warehouse Setting:** The results are specific to the challenging open-set item picking in an industrial warehouse environment and are not tested in other contexts like households or hospitals.

Despite these limitations, the paper marks a significant step forward in industrial robotic manipulation, demonstrating the immense potential of large-scale, data-driven, multi-modal deep learning for solving complex, real-world problems with tangible economic impact.
