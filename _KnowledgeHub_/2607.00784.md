# LeVLJEPA: End-to-End Vision-Language Pretraining Without Negatives

> [!summary] Summary
> LeVLJEPA presents a non-contrastive end-to-end vision-language pretraining method that forgoes negative pairs, yielding visual encoders with enhanced dense semantic features. The approach improves performance on semantic segmentation and as a frozen backbone for vision-language models, also demonstrating batch-size invariance.

## Problem

- Vision-language pretraining (VLP) predominantly relies on contrastive objectives, which often necessitate negative pairs, temperature parameters, and large batch sizes for effective training.
- Existing contrastive VLP methods primarily optimize for global image-text alignment, leading to sub-optimal dense (per-token) visual features that are crucial for modern multimodal applications.
- Applications like visual instruction tuning, dense prediction, and language-conditioned robotics increasingly require high-quality, spatially localized visual features, which current VLP approaches do not prioritize.

## Method

- **LeVLJEPA** introduces a fully ==non-contrastive objective== that combines ==cross-modal prediction== with ==stop-gradients== and per-modality ==Sketched Isotropic Gaussian Regularization (SIGReg)== to prevent collapse.
- The method employs a ==Vision Transformer== for images and a ==GPT-2== model for text, with small ==MLP predictors== enabling cross-modal embedding prediction.
- It eliminates the need for negative pairs, temperature parameters, momentum encoders, and teacher-student schedules, simplifying the VLP training process.

## Results

- LeVLJEPA consistently outperforms contrastive baselines (InfoNCE, SigLIP) on semantic segmentation, achieving **23.15%** mIoU on ADE20K and **31.10%** mIoU on COCO-Stuff.
- As a frozen visual backbone for Vision-Language Models, LeVLJEPA yields the highest downstream accuracy across GQA, VQAv2, and POPE benchmarks, improving VQAv2 by up to **4.8 points** with Qwen-1.5B.
- The method demonstrates stability, robustness to batch size (maintaining performance from **1024** to **4096**), and improved background robustness on ImageNet-9 compared to contrastive approaches, while matching their performance on linear probing of global features.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Effective end-to-end vision-language pretraining is viable without contrastive negative pairs, challenging the prevailing paradigm in VLP research.
> - Non-contrastive VLP can produce visual encoders with stronger dense semantic features, which are more suitable for applications requiring fine-grained visual understanding.
> - Zero-shot classification performance (global features) can be decoupled from, and even inversely related to, the quality of dense visual features required for Vision-Language Model (VLM) backbones.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

This research positions itself within the evolving landscape of self-supervised learning (SSL), specifically at the intersection of computer vision and natural language processing. In vision-only SSL, there has been a notable paradigm shift from contrastive learning methods, such as SimCLR, towards non-contrastive objectives, including DINO, I-JEPA, and LeJEPA. These non-contrastive approaches have demonstrated the ability to learn robust visual representations without relying on negative pairs, temperature parameters, or complex momentum encoders.

In contrast, vision-language pretraining (VLP) has historically remained predominantly reliant on contrastive objectives. Established methods like CLIP and SigLIP utilize contrastive image-text alignment to learn joint embeddings, where matched image-caption pairs are pulled together, and unmatched pairs are pushed apart. This approach has proven effective for tasks like zero-shot image classification and retrieval.

However, the role of vision-language encoders in downstream applications has broadened. They are increasingly deployed not primarily as zero-shot classifiers, which consume a single pooled embedding, but as frozen visual backbones within larger multimodal systems. Examples include visual instruction-tuned models (e.g., LLaVA), dense prediction methods (e.g., DenseCLIP), and language-conditioned robotics systems (e.g., CLIPort, Voltron). In these contexts, the downstream system often attends over the full grid of patch tokens emitted by the vision encoder, rather than a single pooled image vector. Consequently, the quality of these dense, per-token visual features has become a critical factor for downstream performance. Existing contrastive VLP objectives primarily optimize for global cross-modal alignment, supervising per-token features only as a byproduct.

LeVLJEPA aims to bridge this methodological gap by introducing a fully non-contrastive end-to-end VLP method. It seeks to bring the advancements from non-contrastive vision-only SSL to the vision-language domain, hypothesizing that such an approach can yield visual encoders that are more suitable for applications requiring high-quality dense semantic features. The work directly addresses the question of whether VLP can be effective without contrastive negatives and explores the implications for the types of visual features learned.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop and validate an end-to-end vision-language pretraining method that operates entirely without contrastive negative pairs. This involves addressing the fundamental question of whether it is possible to achieve robust and useful vision-language representations when foregoing the negative sampling mechanism that characterizes dominant VLP approaches.

The motivation for pursuing this objective stems from several practical and theoretical considerations:

- **Reliance on Dense Semantic Features**: A core motivation is the shifting landscape of vision-language model deployment. While traditional VLP focuses on tasks like zero-shot classification and retrieval that benefit from a single, globally aligned image embedding, modern applications increasingly rely on the fine-grained, spatially localized information contained within the full sequence of patch tokens. For instance, visual instruction-tuned models, dense prediction systems, and language-conditioned robotics operate by consuming these dense visual features. The authors hypothesize that optimizing directly for global embedding alignment (as contrastive methods do) may not be optimal for learning high-quality dense features.

- **Limitations of Contrastive Objectives for Dense Features**: Contrastive VLP methods primarily supervise a pooled, global image embedding for cross-modal alignment. The quality of individual patch tokens, which are crucial for dense prediction tasks and fine-grained visual understanding, is only indirectly influenced. The authors suggest that a direct optimization strategy for these per-token features is needed.

- **Simplification of Training**: Contrastive VLP objectives often involve complexities such as a learned temperature parameter, careful management of negative samples (often requiring large batch sizes), momentum encoders, or teacher-student schedules. A non-contrastive approach could potentially offer a simpler, more stable, and less infrastructure-dependent training procedure, especially concerning batch size limitations.

- **Bridging the Gap with Vision-Only SSL**: The success of non-contrastive methods in vision-only self-supervised learning (e.g., DINO, I-JEPA, LeJEPA) suggests that these principles might be transferable to the multimodal domain. The work aims to investigate if the theoretical and empirical benefits of non-contrastive learning, such as preventing collapse through distributional regularization, can be effectively applied to align images and text.

- **Exploring Alternative Alignment Mechanisms**: The paper seeks to demonstrate that effective image-text correspondence can emerge through alternative mechanisms, specifically cross-modal prediction with stop-gradients and per-modality distributional regularization, without explicitly pushing unmatched pairs apart. This explores whether "negative-pair discrimination" is a necessary precondition or merely one pathway to useful vision-language representations.

In summary, the key objective is to pioneer a non-contrastive VLP method that can learn strong, particularly dense, semantic features for visual encoders, thereby better serving the needs of contemporary vision-language models and dense prediction systems, while also simplifying the training process.

### 3. Methodology and approach

LeVLJEPA is presented as the first fully non-contrastive end-to-end vision-language pretraining method. Its approach builds upon the LeJEPA framework, which focuses on joint-embedding predictive architectures with explicit distributional regularization.

**Core Principles:**
The method seeks to learn image-text alignment by combining three elements:
1. **Cross-modal Prediction**: An image embedding predicts a target text embedding, and a text embedding predicts a target image embedding.
2. **Stop-Gradient Targets**: Crucially, the target embeddings in the prediction step are detached via a stop-gradient operation. This ensures that gradients flow only through the predictor and its own encoder, not back into the encoder producing the target. This asymmetry is inspired by vision-only SSL methods like SimSiam and BYOL.
3. **Per-modality Distributional Regularization**: Each modality's embeddings (vision and text) are independently regularized to encourage an isotropic Gaussian marginal distribution. This is achieved using Sketched Isotropic Gaussian Regularization (SIGReg) from LeJEPA.

**Architectural Components:**
- **Vision Encoder ($f_\theta$)**: A Vision Transformer (ViT-B/16) that maps images to embeddings. It produces both a pooled CLS token embedding and a grid of patch tokens.
- **Text Encoder ($g_\phi$)**: A GPT-2 model that maps captions to embeddings, typically using the final-token hidden state.
- **Projection Heads**: After each encoder, a one-layer MLP projection maps the output to a shared embedding space of dimension $d$. This projection is applied before calculating the SIGReg loss to circumvent issues with final Layer Normalization in the backbones.
- **Cross-modal Predictors ($h_v$, $h_t$)**: Separate small MLPs (depth-4, width 2048 with BatchNorm, GELU, and 10% dropout) operating on top of the projected embeddings. $h_v$ predicts text embeddings from image embeddings, and $h_t$ predicts image embeddings from text embeddings.

**Training Objective:**
The full LeVLJEPA objective combines a cross-modal Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss with the per-modality SIGReg terms:
$L_{LeVLJEPA} = (1 - λ_v - λ_t) L_{cross} + λ_v SIGReg(Z_v) + λ_t SIGReg(Z_t)$

Where:
- $L_{cross} = \frac{1}{B} \sum_{i=1}^{B} [\|h_v(z_{v_i}) - sg(z_{t_i})\|^2_2 + \|h_t(z_{t_i}) - sg(z_{v_i})\|^2_2]$
 - $z_{v_i}$ and $z_{t_i}$ are the image and text embeddings for the $i$-th sample in a batch of size $B$.
 - $sg(\cdot)$ denotes the stop-gradient operation, detaching the target from the computation graph.
- $SIGReg(Z_v)$ and $SIGReg(Z_t)$ apply the Sketched Isotropic Gaussian Regularization independently to the batch of vision embeddings $Z_v$ and text embeddings $Z_t$, respectively. This regularization projects embeddings onto random 1D directions and applies a characteristic-function-based normality test to prevent collapse and ensure high-rank representations.
- $λ_v$ and $λ_t$ are trade-off hyperparameters for the SIGReg terms.

**Key Design Choices and Eliminated Components:**
- **No Negative Pairs**: Unlike CLIP or SigLIP, LeVLJEPA does not explicitly use negative pairs.
- **No Temperature Parameter**: The objective does not include a temperature scalar common in InfoNCE loss.
- **No Momentum Encoder**: There are no momentum-averaged target networks as seen in BYOL or DINO.
- **No Teacher-Student Schedule**: The method does not rely on a separate teacher network or a student-teacher distillation process.
- **Batch Size Invariance**: The objective design inherently removes batch-level terms, making it less sensitive to batch size compared to contrastive methods.

**Experimental Protocol:**
- **Two-stage Protocol**: Hyperparameter tuning and objective ablations were conducted on CC12M (12M samples). The selected configuration was then fixed and used for evaluation on Datacomp-L (92M samples).
- **Baselines**: Compared against InfoNCE (CLIP-style) and SigLIP, using established OpenCLIP hyperparameters for fair comparison.
- **Matched Compute**: All methods were trained on the same ViT-B/16 backbone, same data pool (∼92M samples from Datacomp-L due to link rot), and for a matched total of 819M samples seen.
- **Evaluation Metrics**:
 - **Global Features**: Zero-shot transfer (ImageNet, Places365, Aircraft, Pets) using cosine similarity, linear probing on frozen CLS tokens (same benchmarks), and background robustness (ImageNet-9).
 - **Dense Features**: Linear semantic segmentation (ADE20K, COCO-Stuff) on frozen patch tokens, and performance as a frozen visual backbone for Vision-Language Models (GQA, VQAv2, POPE) using Llama-1B and Qwen-1.5B LLMs with a lightweight MLP bridge.

**Ablation Studies**: Demonstrated that direct symmetric MSE collapses, SIGReg alone is insufficient, and predictors without SIGReg are unstable. The full LeVLJEPA objective (predictors + stop-gradient + SIGReg) is necessary for stable and performant training.

### 4. Main findings and results

The research paper presents several findings regarding LeVLJEPA's performance compared to contrastive baselines across various evaluation protocols, emphasizing the distinction between global and dense feature quality.

**Training Stability and Batch Size Invariance:**
- LeVLJEPA demonstrates stable training from initialization, with the effective rank of both vision and text embeddings steadily increasing and SIGReg terms converging.
- The method exhibits robustness to architectural choices, such as predictor depth and width, and to the SIGReg trade-off weights, as long as minimum thresholds are met.
- LeVLJEPA's performance on zero-shot and linear probing metrics remains consistent across varying batch sizes (e.g., 1024, 2048, 4096) at matched compute. This indicates batch-size invariance, a notable distinction from contrastive objectives whose performance often scales with batch size.

**Global Representation Quality:**
- **Zero-Shot Alignment**: While LeVLJEPA learns a non-trivial cross-modal alignment without negative pairs, it generally trails contrastive baselines (InfoNCE and SigLIP) on zero-shot classification benchmarks (e.g., ImageNet, Places365, Aircraft, Pets) when trained on Datacomp-L. For instance, on ImageNet, LeVLJEPA achieved 42.45% compared to SigLIP's 50.78%. This is attributed to contrastive methods directly optimizing for the matched-vs-unmatched discrimination that zero-shot transfer measures. However, at a smaller scale (CC12M), LeVLJEPA was competitive, sometimes exceeding CLIP.
- **Linear Probing (Global Features)**: On linear probing of the frozen CLS token, which assesses the discriminative power of the global visual feature independently of cross-modal alignment, LeVLJEPA performs on par with InfoNCE and SigLIP across all evaluated datasets at the Datacomp-L scale. For example, on ImageNet, LeVLJEPA scored 65.42%, very close to SigLIP's 66.34% and InfoNCE's 65.75%. This indicates that its global visual features are equally discriminative.
- **Background Robustness**: LeVLJEPA achieved the highest accuracy and exhibited the smallest degradation on the ImageNet-9 background robustness challenge. It showed better performance on Original, Mixed-Same, and Mixed-Rand splits, with smaller accuracy drops compared to contrastive baselines when backgrounds were shifted. This suggests LeVLJEPA learns a more object-focused and less context-dependent global representation.

**Dense Feature Quality (Primary Contribution):**
- **Semantic Segmentation**: When evaluating dense prediction capabilities by training a single linear layer on frozen patch tokens, LeVLJEPA consistently outperforms both InfoNCE and SigLIP. It achieved 23.15% mIoU on ADE20K and 31.10% mIoU on COCO-Stuff, exceeding the stronger contrastive baseline by 2.25 and 2.08 mIoU respectively. This indicates superior semantic and spatial structure within its per-token features.
- **Vision-Language Model (VLM) Backbone Performance**: As a frozen visual backbone for vision-language models, LeVLJEPA consistently delivers the highest downstream accuracy across multiple benchmarks and two distinct language model families (Llama-1B and Qwen-1.5B), with only a lightweight MLP bridge trained.
 - **GQA (Compositional Question Answering)**: LeVLJEPA outperformed the best contrastive baseline by 1.9 points with Llama-1B and 1.5 points with Qwen-1.5B.
 - **VQAv2 (General Visual Question Answering)**: LeVLJEPA showed its largest margin, exceeding the strongest contrastive backbone by 2.6 points with Llama-1B and 4.8 points with Qwen-1.5B.
 - **POPE (Object Hallucination)**: LeVLJEPA achieved the highest accuracy and, notably, the most balanced answer distribution (yes-rate closest to 50%), indicating reduced hallucination tendencies, especially with Qwen-1.5B where it also had the highest F1 score.
- **Consistency Across Language Models**: The observed performance ordering of pretrained encoders (LeVLJEPA > InfoNCE > SigLIP) remained consistent across both Llama-1B and Qwen-1.5B, suggesting that the benefits are attributable to the learned visual features rather than interactions with a specific LLM architecture.
- **Dissociation of Zero-Shot and Backbone Quality**: The results highlight that zero-shot accuracy is not necessarily indicative of, and can even be inversely related to, the quality of visual features for VLM backbones. For instance, SigLIP was the strongest zero-shot model but the weakest VLM backbone among the three, whereas LeVLJEPA was the weakest zero-shot but strongest VLM backbone.

### 5. Significance and potential impact

The development of LeVLJEPA marks a significant step in the field of vision-language pretraining, primarily by demonstrating the viability and benefits of a fully non-contrastive approach.

**Challenging the Dominant Paradigm**: The most immediate significance is its empirical validation that end-to-end vision-language pretraining can be achieved effectively without relying on contrastive negative pairs. This challenges the long-standing dominance of contrastive objectives in VLP, suggesting that negative-pair discrimination is one effective pathway but not an indispensable prerequisite for learning useful cross-modal representations.

**Emphasis on Dense Semantic Features**: The work provides compelling evidence that non-contrastive VLP, specifically LeVLJEPA, yields vision encoders with "markedly stronger dense semantic features" compared to contrastive methods. This is crucial because modern applications of vision-language models, such as visual instruction tuning, dense prediction, and language-conditioned control systems, increasingly consume the full grid of patch tokens rather than just a single pooled embedding. By outperforming contrastive baselines in semantic segmentation and as a frozen VLM backbone across multiple tasks and language models, LeVLJEPA demonstrates its superior utility for these downstream uses.

**Implications for Model Design and Training Efficiency**: LeVLJEPA's simplified training recipe, which foregoes negative pairs, temperature parameters, momentum encoders, or teacher-student schedules, could lead to more straightforward and potentially more stable large-scale pretraining. Its demonstrated batch-size invariance also reduces a critical constraint that has tied contrastive performance to the availability of extensive computing infrastructure for large batch sizes. This could make VLP more accessible and efficient.

**Reconsideration of Evaluation Protocols**: A key insight from this research is the observed dissociation between zero-shot classification performance (a common VLP benchmark) and the quality of dense visual features required for VLM backbones. The finding that high zero-shot accuracy does not necessarily translate to strong dense features, and can even be inversely related, highlights a potential mismatch between current VLP evaluation practices and the actual needs of how these encoders are deployed. This calls for a re-evaluation of standard benchmarks to prioritize token-level feature quality over global alignment metrics for assessing VLP models, particularly for applications where fine-grained visual understanding is critical.

**Future Research Directions**: The work opens several avenues for future research. Investigating how to combine the dense-feature advantages of non-contrastive pretraining with competitive zero-shot alignment within a single objective is a natural next step. Furthermore, confirming that these advantages persist and scale effectively to larger model architectures and even larger datasets beyond Datacomp-L is important for broader adoption. The results also implicitly motivate the development of new non-contrastive architectures designed explicitly for multimodal understanding beyond simple image-text pairing.

In essence, LeVLJEPA contributes to a paradigm shift in VLP, advocating for a vision-centric formulation that prioritizes the semantic richness of dense visual features. This alignment with the demands of modern multimodal AI systems positions the work to significantly impact the design, training, and evaluation of future vision-language models.
