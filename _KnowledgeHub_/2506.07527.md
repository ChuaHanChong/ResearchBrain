# Learning What Reinforcement Learning Can't: Interleaved Online Fine-Tuning for Hardest Questions

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Peking University developed ReLIFT, an interleaved online fine-tuning strategy that combines reinforcement learning with targeted supervised fine-tuning to overcome limitations in LLM reasoning. ReLIFT achieved a state-of-the-art 52.6% overall accuracy on math reasoning and out-of-distribution benchmarks while using fewer resources and demonstrating better generalization.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) primarily reinforces existing behaviors and knowledge, struggling to induce genuinely novel reasoning abilities and often leading to performance plateaus on complex tasks.
- Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) can incorporate new knowledge but relies heavily on substantial, high-quality demonstration data and often exhibits limited generalization to out-of-distribution scenarios.
- Existing hybrid approaches often involve computationally intensive, multi-stage training or require large pre-collected datasets, limiting their efficiency and adaptability.

## Method

- The **ReLIFT** (Reinforcement Learning Interleaved with Online Fine-Tuning) framework dynamically integrates ==Group Relative== Policy Optimization (GRPO) with targeted SFT.
- During RL training, the system identifies "hardest" questions (those with zero accuracy) online and collects high-quality Chain-of-Thought (CoT) solutions for these specific problems.
- These solutions are used for interleaved, entropy-regularized SFT, applied with an adaptive frequency, to specifically address emergent model weaknesses and foster novel reasoning capabilities.

## Results

- ReLIFT achieved a new state-of-the-art overall accuracy of **52.6%** on a suite of six benchmarks (AIME, AMC, OlympiadBench, MATH500, MMLU-Pro), outperforming all baselines including previous RLVR and combined SFT/RL methods.
- The framework required significantly less training time (**52 x** 8 GPU hours) and fewer demonstration samples (**8K**) compared to several combined SFT/RL methods, while also generating more concise solutions.
- ReLIFT consistently yielded substantial performance improvements across different model scales (Qwen2.5-Math-**1.5B**, Qwen2.5-Math-**7B**, Llama3.1-**8B**), demonstrating broad applicability and enhanced strategic planning behaviors.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An empirical analysis revealed complementary strengths: RL refines existing skills on easier problems, while SFT is crucial for acquiring new knowledge to solve the most challenging, previously unanswerable questions.
> - Dynamically identifying and fine-tuning on online-collected "hardest" questions, coupled with entropy regularization, allows LLMs to learn beyond their initial capabilities without stifling exploration.
> - ReLIFT's adaptive scheduling and targeted data selection lead to a resource-efficient training paradigm, balancing RL's exploration with SFT's knowledge acquisition effectively.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Recent advancements in large language model (LLM) reasoning have prominently featured Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) as a key technique. Models such as OpenAI o-series, DeepSeek-R1, Gemini 2.5, and Kimi k-series have demonstrated enhanced reasoning capabilities, often by generating extensive Chains-of-Thought (CoT) responses. RLVR assigns rewards based on direct matches to ground-truth solutions or successful unit tests, enabling scalability without extensive demonstration data. Policy optimization algorithms like Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) and Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) have been instrumental in this development.

However, existing literature suggests limitations in RLVR. Studies indicate that RLVR primarily reinforces pre-existing behaviors and knowledge rather than fostering genuinely novel reasoning abilities, potentially leading to performance plateaus on complex tasks and suppressed exploration. For instance, RLVR-trained models might underperform base models at higher pass@k values, implying limited expansion of reasoning capabilities. This limitation is attributed to RL’s on-policy nature, which optimizes paths the model already deems likely to yield rewards.

Conversely, supervised fine-tuning (SFT) has demonstrated its capacity to incorporate new knowledge and reasoning patterns through high-quality demonstration data. SFT can achieve superior reasoning performance, particularly in smaller LLMs, but its effectiveness is contingent on the availability of substantial, high-quality data and often results in limited generalization to out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios. RL-based approaches, in contrast, tend to exhibit greater robustness to OOD conditions.

The current work addresses the complementary strengths and weaknesses of RL and SFT. Previous hybrid approaches often involve an initial SFT phase followed by RL, but these methods are still limited by the SFT data quality and the subsequent RL phase's confinement to the SFT-trained model's knowledge. Approaches like LUFFY attempt to integrate off-policy reasoning traces but require pre-collected data and probability-sharpening techniques. This paper aims to develop a more adaptive and efficient hybrid strategy that leverages the strengths of both paradigms to overcome their individual limitations and enable LLMs to learn beyond their initial capabilities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel training strategy that effectively combines Reinforcement Learning (RL) and Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) to improve large language model (LLM) reasoning, enhance out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization, reduce dependence on expensive demonstration data, and achieve capabilities beyond the current cognitive constraints of base models.

The motivation for this work stems from observed limitations in current state-of-the-art LLM training paradigms:
1. **Limitations of RLVR:** Despite its success in enhancing reasoning, RLVR has been found to be insufficient in inducing capabilities that genuinely exceed the inherent limitations of the base model. Research suggests that RL primarily optimizes based on existing knowledge, reinforcing known reasoning paths rather than acquiring novel skills. This on-policy learning approach can lead to models converging on narrow behavioral patterns, stifling exploration, and plateauing on challenging reasoning tasks.
2. **Limitations of SFT:** While SFT is effective at incorporating new knowledge and reasoning patterns by leveraging high-quality demonstration data, its performance is highly dependent on the availability of such data. Furthermore, SFT-trained models often exhibit limited generalization when applied to out-of-distribution scenarios.
3. **Complementary Strengths:** An initial analysis conducted by the authors revealed that RL excels at improving performance on questions within the model’s original capabilities (e.g., "Easy" and "Medium" difficulty), while SFT is more effective at enabling progress on questions that are beyond the current scope of the model (i.e., "Hardest" questions). This suggests a natural complementarity, where RL refines existing skills and SFT facilitates the acquisition of new knowledge.
4. **Addressing Training Inefficiencies:** Existing hybrid approaches often involve pre-collected datasets or fixed two-stage training (SFT then RL), which can be computationally intensive and resource-demanding, particularly concerning demonstration data and GPU hours. The motivation includes developing a resource-efficient paradigm that dynamically addresses model weaknesses as they emerge during training.

Therefore, the key motivation is to design an adaptive, interleaved training strategy that leverages these complementary strengths. By identifying questions where RL struggles and selectively applying SFT with targeted, high-quality solutions, the authors aim to push LLM reasoning capabilities beyond what either method can achieve in isolation, leading to more robust, generalizable, and resource-efficient reasoning models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology is structured around an initial analysis of RL and SFT training dynamics, followed by the proposal and implementation of the ReLIFT (Reinforcement Learning Interleaved with Online Fine-Tuning) framework.

#### 3.1. RL vs. SFT: Training Dynamics Across Question Difficulty Levels

The research began with an empirical analysis of how accuracy changes across different question difficulties during independent RL and SFT training.
- **Model and Dataset:** A Qwen2.5-Math-7B model was trained using both SFT and RL on a subset of the Open-R1-math-220K dataset for 120 steps, with checkpoints saved every 30 steps.
- **Difficulty Categorization:** Validation questions (1,000 samples) were categorized into four difficulty levels:
 - **Easy:** Average accuracy (acc(q)) ≥ 0.75 (6-8 correct out of 8 attempts).
 - **Medium:** 0.375 ≤ acc(q) ≤ 0.625 (3-5 correct out of 8).
 - **Hard:** 0.125 ≤ acc(q) ≤ 0.25 (1-2 correct out of 8).
 - **Hardest:** acc(q) = 0 (0 correct out of 8), representing questions beyond current capabilities.
- **Observations:**
 - **Accuracy:** RL demonstrated superior performance on Easy and Medium questions, while SFT showed better results on Hardest questions. SFT sometimes degraded performance on simpler questions, which RL did not.
 - **Response Length:** SFT generated longer responses across all difficulties, aiming to mimic target long responses. RL initially produced shorter responses, with lengths increasing for Medium and Hard questions over time, suggesting exploration. For Hardest questions, RL's response length remained stable, implying a cessation of exploration.
 - **Accuracy Evolution:** Tracking initial and final accuracy showed SFT enabling more Hardest questions to be solved, while RL struggled to improve performance on these most challenging items.
- **Conclusion:** This analysis established the complementary strengths: RL refines existing skills, while SFT acquires new knowledge for difficult problems. This motivated the development of a hybrid strategy.

#### 3.2. Reinforcement Learning Interleaved with Online Fine-Tuning (ReLIFT)

ReLIFT is a training strategy that dynamically integrates RL and SFT.
- **Overview:** The model is primarily trained using RL. During this process, particularly challenging examples are identified. For these "hardest" questions, high-quality Chain-of-Thought (CoT) solutions are collected, filtered, and then used for targeted SFT. This interleaving is adaptive, allowing the model to learn from its weaknesses.

- **Reinforcement Learning while Collecting Hard Questions:**
 - **RL Algorithm:** Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) is employed as the main RL algorithm, leveraging reward scores from multiple sampled solutions to estimate advantage without a separate value model. The GRPO objective is defined by Equation 1.
 - **Hard Question Identification:** During the GRPO rollout phase, for each question `q`, a set of solutions `o_i` is generated, and the accuracy `acc(q)` is computed. Questions for which `acc(q) = 0` are identified as "particularly challenging."
 - **High-Quality CoT Collection:** For these challenging questions, high-quality CoT solutions `s` are obtained. These can be collected in advance, sourced from a stronger reasoning model (e.g., DeepSeek-R1), or provided by human annotators.
 - **Data Filtering and Buffering:** The collected `(q, s)` pairs are filtered to ensure `extract(s) = a` (where `a` is the ground-truth answer). These verified pairs are then added to a `Buffer_hardest`, which in turn populates a primary fine-tuning buffer, `Buffer_FT`. This collection process occurs online during training.

- **Interleaved Online Fine-Tuning for Hardest Questions:**
 - **SFT Trigger:** When the size of `Buffer_FT` reaches a predefined threshold `M`, a batch of `M` `(q, s)` pairs is sampled from the buffer.
 - **Fine-tuning Objective:** A standard cross-entropy loss (Equation 3) is applied to these hard examples.
 - **Entropy Regularization:** To prevent SFT from overly constraining the model's exploratory behavior, an entropy regularization term `αL_Entropy(θ)` is added to the loss function, forming the full SFT objective (Equation 4). `α` is a hyperparameter.
 - **Adaptive Frequency:** The frequency of this interleaved SFT is adaptive. Early in training, SFT is applied more frequently to accelerate the learning of new patterns, while later, as the model improves, RL is prioritized. This online approach reduces the need for large, pre-collected CoT datasets.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. Experimental Setup

- **Dataset:** Training used OpenR1-Math-46k-8192, a filtered subset of OpenR1-Math-220k with high-quality demonstrations from DeepSeek-R1.
- **Evaluation Benchmarks:**
 - **Mathematical Reasoning:** AIME 2024, AIME 2025, AMC, OlympiadBench, MATH500. Evaluation metrics were `avg@32` for AIME/AMC and `avg@8` for OlympiadBench/MATH500.
 - **Out-of-Distribution (OOD):** MMLU-Pro (main), ARC-Challenge, GPQA-Diamond, MMLU-Redux, and HumanEval (additional OOD in Appendix G).
- **Baselines:** Qwen2.5-Math-7B (base model), SFT, RL, previous RLVR methods (SimpleRL-Zero, OpenReasoner-Zero, PRIME-Zero, Oat-Zero), and methods combining SFT and RL (RL w/ SFT loss, SFT then RL (v1/v2), LUFFY).
- **Implementation:** All models were based on Qwen2.5-Math-7B for main experiments, with extensions to Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B, Qwen2.5-7B, and Llama3.1-8B. Training was conducted on 8 x A800 GPUs.

#### 4.2. State-of-the-Art Performance with Qwen2.5-Math-7B

- **Overall Accuracy:** ReLIFT achieved a new state-of-the-art overall accuracy of 52.6% on the primary suite of six benchmarks (five math reasoning, one OOD), outperforming all baselines.
- **Benchmark Performance:** ReLIFT consistently ranked first or second on every individual benchmark, indicating both high reasoning performance and strong generalization capabilities.
- **Comparison to Combined Methods:** ReLIFT surpassed all other methods combining RL and SFT (RL w/ SFT loss, SFT then RL, LUFFY).

#### 4.3. Resource Requirements

- **Efficiency:** ReLIFT required less training time (52 x 8 GPU hours) and fewer demonstration samples (8K) compared to several combined SFT/RL methods (e.g., RL w/ SFT loss: 113.5 x 8 GPU hours, 46K demos; SFT then RL v1: 57 x 8 GPU hours, 46K demos; LUFFY: 73 x 8 GPU hours, 46K demos). This indicates both effectiveness and efficiency.
- **Conciseness:** ReLIFT generated more concise solutions on average (3502 tokens) compared to SFT (5533 tokens), RL w/ SFT loss (5508 tokens), SFT then RL (v1: 3845 tokens, v2: 4534 tokens), and LUFFY (3808 tokens).

#### 4.4. Training Dynamics

- **Rewards:** ReLIFT consistently achieved higher rewards than pure RL, suggesting more effective optimization towards the target objective.
- **Response Lengths:** ReLIFT showed a gradual increase in response length during training, indicative of continued ability to address difficult problems, contrasting with RL's tendency to generate shorter responses, which may signal reduced capacity for challenging questions.
- **Entropy:** ReLIFT maintained relatively high and fluctuating entropy, surpassing RL. This indicated sustained exploration, facilitating discovery of novel solutions and ongoing performance improvements, while RL's entropy steadily declined.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies and Analysis

- **Scheduling and Data Selection:**
 - `ReLIFT(all)` (alternating FT after each RL step on the same batch) led to rapid performance collapse (23.8% accuracy), attributed to conflicting optimization objectives.
 - `ReLIFT(uniform)` (fixed FT intervals) and `ReLIFT(random)` (random non-hardest questions for FT) resulted in lower accuracy (49.1% and 45.5% respectively) and longer responses compared to ReLIFT.
 - These results emphasize that dynamic scheduling and targeted fine-tuning on the most challenging questions are crucial for ReLIFT's effectiveness.
- **Entropy Coefficient (α):** An `α` value of 0.0001 for the entropy regularization term in the SFT loss was found to be optimal, maximizing accuracy. Deviations in `α` significantly decreased performance, highlighting its importance for blending SFT and RL effectively.
- **Buffer Size and Difficulty Threshold (Appendix H):** Extreme settings for the FT buffer threshold (M) and difficulty threshold (Q) (which filters questions with acc(q) <= Q) led to suboptimal results. For instance, a very small buffer (M=32) or a relaxed filtering condition (Q=1/4) combined with a small M led to performance degradation, suggesting overly frequent SFT updates without stability. Optimal performance was observed with configurations like (M=64, Q=0) or (M=128, Q=1/8), indicating a balance between data selection and fine-tuning capacity. The `Q=0` condition was recommended for its minimal demonstration data requirement.

#### 4.6. Extension to More Models

- **Generalizability:** ReLIFT consistently yielded substantial improvements across smaller (Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B), weaker (Qwen2.5-7B), and architecturally different (Llama3.1-8B) base models, surpassing both SFT and RL. For example, on Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B, ReLIFT achieved 36.5% overall accuracy compared to SFT's 32.6% and RL's 34.2%. This demonstrated the broad applicability of the method.

#### 4.7. Reasoning Behaviors (Appendix E)

- **Keyword Analysis:** Analysis of response keywords on AIME25 revealed distinct behaviors:
 - **Summarizing:** SFT models showed higher usage of summary keywords.
 - **Rethinking:** ReLIFT models used fewer rethinking/re-evaluation keywords, suggesting higher efficiency and confidence.
 - **Planning:** ReLIFT models exhibited a substantial increase in planning keywords, indicating a more robust and sophisticated strategic planning approach.

#### 4.8. Case Study (Appendix F)

- An example from AIME25 Problem 3 illustrated that ReLIFT produced a more concise and correct solution compared to RL (which failed to include one possible combination) and SFT (which required excessive analysis).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of ReLIFT (Reinforcement Learning Interleaved with Online Fine-Tuning) represents a significant advancement in the training paradigms for large language models, particularly for complex reasoning tasks. The work systematically analyzes and empirically demonstrates the complementary strengths of RL and SFT, establishing that RL is effective for refining existing skills on problems within a model's current capabilities, while SFT is crucial for acquiring new knowledge to tackle problems beyond those capabilities.

The primary significance of ReLIFT lies in its ability to leverage these complementary strengths through an adaptive, interleaved training strategy. By dynamically identifying "hardest" questions online and performing targeted SFT with high-quality solutions, ReLIFT addresses emergent model weaknesses without sacrificing overall performance or efficiency. This approach leads to several potential impacts:

1. **Enhanced Reasoning Capabilities:** ReLIFT achieves state-of-the-art performance on challenging mathematical reasoning benchmarks and demonstrates superior generalization to out-of-distribution tasks. This indicates that the method effectively enables LLMs to learn beyond the inherent limitations of their base models, fostering a more robust and capable reasoning ability.
2. **Resource Efficiency:** The method significantly reduces the requirement for extensive pre-collected demonstration data and demands fewer GPU training hours compared to many existing hybrid and RLVR baselines. This efficiency makes developing high-performing reasoning models more accessible and scalable, particularly for resource-constrained research efforts.
3. **Improved Generalization:** By combining the explorative nature of RL with the knowledge acquisition capability of SFT, ReLIFT yields models with stronger out-of-distribution generalization, as evidenced by consistent performance across diverse OOD benchmarks. This is crucial for deploying LLMs in varied real-world applications where novel problems are common.
4. **Adaptive Learning Process:** The online nature of identifying and collecting high-quality solutions for hard questions, coupled with the adaptive frequency of SFT, allows the model to continuously learn from its own mistakes and evolve its capabilities. This dynamic learning process is a departure from static, two-stage training approaches.
5. **More Concise and Strategic Responses:** ReLIFT-trained models generate more concise solutions while exhibiting a higher propensity for strategic planning behaviors compared to models trained with pure RL or SFT. This suggests an improvement not just in correctness but also in the efficiency and quality of the reasoning process.
6. **Broad Applicability:** The consistent improvements observed across different model scales and architectures (from 1.5B to 8B parameters, and across Qwen and Llama families) underscore the broad applicability and robustness of the ReLIFT framework.

In conclusion, ReLIFT offers a powerful and resource-efficient paradigm for developing advanced reasoning LLMs. Its approach of intelligently combining RL and SFT to target specific learning gaps represents a step forward in pushing the boundaries of what reinforcement learning can achieve for LLM reasoning. Future work will likely focus on further scaling ReLIFT to larger models and refining the coordination strategies between SFT and RL.
