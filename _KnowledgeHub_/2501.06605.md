# RoboHorizon: An LLM-Assisted Multi-View World Model for Long-Horizon Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> RoboHorizon introduces an LLM-assisted multi-view world model, integrating a Recognize-Sense-Plan-Act (RSPA) pipeline for long-horizon robotic manipulation, achieving significant success rate improvements on complex multi-stage tasks by automating dense reward generation and enhancing visual perception through keyframe-aware multi-view representation learning.

## Problem

- Achieving efficient and reliable control for long-horizon robotic manipulation is challenging due to complex representation learning, sparse rewards, and the need for extensive manual engineering.
- Existing language and visual encoders struggle to capture the intricate hierarchical structure and inter-dependencies of multi-stage sub-tasks, limiting the robot's comprehension of task dynamics.
- Reinforcement learning policies for long-horizon tasks suffer from sparse reward signals, leading to unstable and ineffective policy development.

## Method

- Introduces the ==Recognize-Sense-Plan-Act (RSPA)== pipeline, extending the traditional Sense-Plan-Act framework by incorporating LLM-assisted task recognition to break down complex tasks.
- Utilizes pre-trained Large Language Models (LLMs) to automatically generate structured, dense reward functions for multi-stage sub-tasks based on natural language instructions, addressing the sparse reward problem.
- Employs a Key-Horizon Multi-View Masked Autoencoder (KMV-MAE) for enhanced multi-view representation learning, which integrates keyframe discovery to focus on critical interaction periods and improve multi-stage perception.

## Results

- RoboHorizon achieved an average success rate of **75.58%** on short-horizon tasks, representing a **25.35%** improvement over the best state-of-the-art baseline (MV-MWM).
- For long-horizon tasks, it demonstrated an average success rate of **45.47%**, an impressive **29.23%** improvement compared to MV-MWM, highlighting its particular strength in multi-stage scenarios.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both LLM-assisted reward generation and key-horizon multi-view representation learning are crucial and complementary, with varying impacts depending on whether semantic understanding (dispersed objects) or fine-grained visual perception (dense objects) is more critical.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Stable execution of long-horizon tasks in model-based visual RL is achieved by combining accurate task understanding (via LLMs) with robust perception of multi-stage interactions (via key-horizon multi-view representations).
> - Automating dense, staged reward generation with LLMs significantly reduces the manual engineering burden and improves policy learning stability compared to sparse or manually-defined rewards.
> - Focusing visual representation learning on 'key-horizons'—critical interaction periods identified by keyframes—captures essential temporal dynamics and cross-view correlations for complex, multi-stage manipulations more effectively than general multi-view approaches.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation is striving for general-purpose robots capable of performing complex, multi-stage tasks in real-world environments. However, achieving efficient and reliable control for **long-horizon robotic manipulation** remains a significant challenge. This difficulty stems from several interconnected problems:
1. **Complex Representation Learning**: Extracting meaningful features from high-dimensional visual inputs that capture the hierarchical structure and dependencies inherent in multi-stage tasks.
2. **Policy Learning with Sparse Rewards**: Long-horizon tasks often provide feedback only at the ultimate completion, making it difficult for reinforcement learning (RL) agents to learn effective policies due to sparse reward signals.
3. **Handling Complex Visual Features**: Real-world environments are visually rich and dynamic, requiring robust perception systems that can identify critical objects and interactions across multiple stages.
4. **Extensive Manual Engineering**: Traditional approaches like Task and Motion Planning (TAMP) or skill-chaining often demand considerable human intervention in defining sub-tasks, state machines, and reward functions.

Historically, robotic control has been built upon the "Sense-Plan-Act" (SPA) pipeline, involving environment perception, task planning with dynamic models, and low-level action execution. While model-based visual reinforcement learning (RL) has shown promise in this context, it frequently falters when confronted with the sparse rewards and intricate visual demands of long-horizon tasks. Methods leveraging multi-view data have improved visual manipulation but are often limited to short-horizon scenarios or fail to capture the temporal progression of multi-stage interactions effectively.

In parallel, Large Language Models (LLMs) have emerged as powerful tools in robotics, demonstrating capabilities in step-by-step planning, goal-oriented dialogue, and even direct reward generation. However, simply using LLMs for direct reward signals often overlooks the multi-stage, hierarchical nature of long-horizon tasks.

RoboHorizon positions itself at the intersection of these challenges and emerging solutions. It extends the traditional SPA pipeline into **Recognize-Sense-Plan-Act (RSPA)**, specifically designed to address the unique complexities of long-horizon tasks. By integrating LLMs, it tackles the sparse reward problem by automating the generation of dense, structured reward functions that account for task stages. By introducing keyframe-aware multi-view representation learning, it enhances the robot's ability to 'sense' critical interactions across time, filling a crucial gap where existing multi-view methods fall short for long-horizon scenarios. This work directly contributes to making model-based visual RL more robust and applicable to truly complex, real-world robotic tasks, reducing the reliance on laborious manual engineering and pushing towards more autonomous and adaptable robotic systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **develop an efficient and stable control system for long-horizon robotic manipulation tasks**, which are characterized by multiple, sequentially dependent sub-task phases and often suffer from sparse reward signals.

The authors are motivated by two key limitations of existing approaches:
1. **Inadequate Task Understanding and Representation Learning for Long-Horizon Tasks**: Current language and visual encoders struggle to capture the intricate hierarchical structure and inter-dependencies of multi-stage sub-tasks. This limits the robot's ability to fully comprehend task dynamics and environmental context over extended periods.
2. **Challenges with Sparse Rewards**: Reinforcement Learning (RL) policies, which are crucial for learning control in complex environments, are heavily reliant on rational and dense reward structures. In long-horizon tasks, environmental feedback is typically sparse, leading to unstable and ineffective policy development.

To overcome these challenges, the authors propose a core insight: stable execution of long-horizon tasks in model-based visual RL hinges on enabling robots to:
- **Accurately understand tasks**: By leveraging pre-trained LLMs to generate structured, dense rewards that align with multi-stage sub-tasks.
- **Perceive multi-stage interactions**: By utilizing multi-view camera data combined with keyframe discovery to learn robust, key-horizon-aware visual representations.
- **Learn stable control policies**: Through a well-defined, structured reward system that facilitates efficient planning and reliable action execution.

Therefore, the specific objectives of RoboHorizon are to:
- Introduce the **Recognize-Sense-Plan-Act (RSPA)** pipeline, extending the traditional SPA framework to incorporate LLM-assisted task recognition.
- Develop **RoboHorizon**, an LLM-assisted multi-view world model, tailored for long-horizon manipulation.
- Utilize pre-trained LLMs to **generate dense reward structures** for multi-stage sub-tasks based on natural language instructions, improving task recognition.
- Integrate **keyframe discovery into a multi-view masked autoencoder (MAE) architecture** (KMV-MAE) to enhance the robot’s ability to "sense" critical task sequences and strengthen multi-stage perception.
- Construct a **robotic world model** that leverages these dense rewards and multi-view representations for efficient long-horizon task planning.
- Enable the robot to **reliably act** through reinforcement learning algorithms guided by the world model's imagined trajectories.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of RoboHorizon is the novel **Recognize-Sense-Plan-Act (RSPA) pipeline**, which systematically breaks down complex long-horizon tasks into manageable, interconnected stages. Each stage is implemented with specific architectural components:

#### 3.1. Recognize: LLM-assisted Reward Generation

This stage addresses the sparse reward problem by using pre-trained Large Language Models (LLMs) to automatically generate dense reward functions. The process is divided into two sub-stages:
1. **Multi-stage Plan Descriptor**: Given a natural language task description (e.g., "take shoes out of box"), an LLM (specifically GPT-4o was found most reliable) interprets and expands this into a detailed, step-by-step language description of the required robot motions. This leverages the LLM's internal knowledge of motion planning.
2. **Dense Reward Generator**: Another LLM converts these detailed motion descriptions into executable reward code. This is treated as a coding task, with prompts guiding the LLM to generate dense stepwise rewards for individual actions and larger intermediate rewards for completing sub-task stages. This reward code is integrated directly into the environment interface, providing continuous and structured feedback. This is a crucial distinction from prior LLM-based reward generation which often lacked multi-stage awareness.

#### 3.2. Sense: Key-Horizon Multi-View Representation Learning

To enable the robot to effectively perceive multi-stage interactions, the paper introduces the **Key-Horizon Multi-View Masked Autoencoder (KMV-MAE)**. This module builds upon existing multi-view MAE architectures but integrates keyframe discovery to focus on critical interaction periods.
1. **Keyframe Discovery**: This method identifies significant moments (keyframes) in multi-view demonstrations. Keyframes are detected when joint velocities are near-zero and gripper states are unchanged, signaling the completion of a sub-action or transition between stages. This effectively segments long, continuous demonstrations into "key-horizons" – variable-length sequences between adjacent keyframes.
2. **View&Tube Masking and Reconstruction**: Instead of processing entire long trajectories, KMV-MAE focuses on these extracted key-horizons. It applies a novel masking strategy: for each frame within a key-horizon, three of the four camera views are entirely masked, while 95% of patches in the remaining view are masked. Across the key-horizon, the unmasked viewpoint follows a "tube masking" strategy, maintaining temporal consistency. A Vision Transformer (ViT) encoder processes the unmasked features, and a ViT decoder reconstructs the missing pixel patches and predicts rewards. This joint reconstruction and reward prediction objective helps the model learn fine-grained visual features, cross-view correlations, and temporal dynamics crucial for multi-stage tasks.

#### 3.3. Plan: RoboHorizon World Model

This stage leverages the learned representations and dense reward structures to build a robust world model for long-horizon task planning. RoboHorizon is implemented as a variant of the Recurrent State Space Model (RSSM).
- **Components**: It consists of an **Encoder** (extracts latent states from previous states, actions, and current KMV-MAE representations), a **Decoder** (reconstructs KMV-MAE representations and predicts rewards from latent states), and a **Dynamics Model** (predicts future latent states without direct access to visual inputs, enabling imagination).
- **Training**: The world model parameters are optimized by minimizing the negative variational lower bound, encouraging the model to accurately predict future states and rewards based on learned latent dynamics.

#### 3.4. Act: Control Policy Learning

Finally, this stage enables the robot to execute actions effectively using reinforcement learning.
- **Actor-Critic Framework**: The system adopts an actor-critic framework, similar to DreamerV2. A stochastic actor learns to generate actions, and a deterministic critic estimates future values.
- **Policy Optimization**: The policy is trained to maximize predicted future values by backpropagating gradients through the RoboHorizon world model. Crucially, the prediction horizon for imagination is set to match the length of individual key-horizons, aligning with the segmented nature of the tasks.
- **Behavior Cloning**: An auxiliary behavior cloning loss is integrated, which encourages the agent to mimic expert actions from demonstrations, providing a strong initial policy and improving stability during learning, especially in the early stages or for complex maneuvers.
- **Overall Objective**: The entire RoboHorizon framework is optimized by minimizing a combined loss function that incorporates the KMV-MAE reconstruction and reward prediction loss, the world model's variational lower bound, and the actor-critic losses (including behavior cloning).

**Experimental Setup**: The methodology is evaluated on two representative benchmarks, RLBench and FurnitureBench, known for their sparse rewards and multi-camera settings. Experiments use RGB observations only, without proprioceptive or depth information. 50-100 expert demonstrations are provided per camera view for each task, and a multi-view observation/single-view control paradigm is used.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of RoboHorizon, conducted on a diverse set of tasks from RLBench (4 short-horizon, 6 long-horizon) and FurnitureBench (3 long-horizon assembly tasks), yields compelling results:

#### 4.1. Superior Performance Against SPA-driven Baselines

- **Overall Outperformance**: RoboHorizon consistently outperforms all five state-of-the-art SPA-driven model-based visual RL baselines (TCN+WM, CLIP+WM, MAE+WM, MWM, and MV-MWM). This validates the effectiveness of the proposed RSPA pipeline in handling complex manipulation tasks.
- **Significant Improvement on Short-Horizon Tasks**: On the 4 short-horizon RLBench tasks, RoboHorizon achieved an average success rate of **75.58%**, representing a substantial **25.35% improvement** over the best baseline, MV-MWM (50.23%).
- **Even Greater Gains on Long-Horizon Tasks**: The advantage was even more pronounced for long-horizon tasks. Across the 6 long-horizon RLBench tasks and 3 FurnitureBench assembly tasks, RoboHorizon achieved an average success rate of **45.47%**, an impressive **29.23% improvement** compared to MV-MWM (16.23%). This highlights RoboHorizon's particular strength in tackling multi-stage, complex scenarios where other methods struggle due to sparse rewards and intricate interactions.
- The baselines, relying on manually defined rewards, showed inconsistent performance across tasks, indicating the fragility of such reward structures, especially for increasing task complexity.

#### 4.2. Impact of LLM-Generated Stepwise Rewards on Baselines

- When SPA-driven baselines were augmented with LLM-generated *stepwise* rewards (without the multi-stage awareness of RoboHorizon's full "Recognize" component), they did show some performance improvement compared to their manually rewarded counterparts. This confirms that well-designed dense rewards, even if only stepwise, can aid robotic understanding.
- However, these enhanced baselines still failed to match the performance of the full RoboHorizon framework, especially in long-horizon tasks. This finding underscores the critical importance of RoboHorizon's comprehensive LLM-assisted reward generation, which considers *staged* rewards for each sub-task phase, and the overall RSPA pipeline's integration of perception and planning modules.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study of Key Design Components

The ablation study investigated the individual contributions of RoboHorizon's two main innovations: LLM-assisted reward generation ("Recognize") and key-horizon multi-view representation learning ("Sense").
- **"RoboHorizon w/o LLM" (manual rewards)**: Performance dropped significantly, particularly in tasks where interpreting dispersed objects or complex sequences was crucial, demonstrating that LLM-assisted reward generation plays a vital role in task recognition and guiding the policy.
- **"RoboHorizon w/o key" (MV-MWM-like perception)**: Performance also suffered, especially in tasks with densely packed objects or intricate contact-rich interactions. This indicates that the key-horizon multi-view representation learning is indispensable for accurately sensing environmental interactions, particularly when visual features are cluttered or difficult to discern.
- **Differential Impact**: The study revealed a nuanced impact: LLM-assisted reward generation was more impactful in tasks with dispersed objects (where semantic understanding of goals is key), while key-horizon multi-view representation learning was more critical in tasks with densely distributed objects (where fine-grained visual perception of interaction is paramount). This confirms that both components are essential and complement each other for robust long-horizon manipulation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

RoboHorizon represents a significant advancement in the field of long-horizon robotic manipulation, addressing several long-standing challenges and contributing to the development of more autonomous and capable robotic systems.

#### 5.1. Novel Framework for Long-Horizon Tasks

The introduction of the **Recognize-Sense-Plan-Act (RSPA) pipeline** is a fundamental contribution. By explicitly adding an LLM-driven "Recognize" stage to the traditional Sense-Plan-Act paradigm, the framework provides a more structured and intelligent approach to task comprehension. This paradigm shift paves the way for robots to interpret complex natural language instructions and translate them into actionable, multi-stage plans with dense, task-aligned reward structures.

#### 5.2. Bridging Language and Perception for Robotic Control

RoboHorizon effectively integrates the symbolic reasoning capabilities of Large Language Models with the rich sensory processing of visual world models. The LLM-assisted reward generation automates the tedious and often brittle process of manual reward engineering, significantly reducing development overhead and enabling more robust learning in sparse reward settings. This is crucial for scaling robotic capabilities to a wider array of real-world scenarios.

#### 5.3. Enhanced Multi-View Visual Representation Learning

The **Key-Horizon Multi-View Masked Autoencoder (KMV-MAE)** significantly improves how robots "sense" their environment during long-horizon tasks. By focusing on keyframes and the interactions within "key-horizons," the model learns more salient and temporally relevant visual representations. This addresses the limitations of prior multi-view methods that often failed to capture the sequential dependencies and critical interaction points necessary for complex, multi-stage manipulations.

#### 5.4. State-of-the-Art Performance in Complex Scenarios

The empirical results demonstrate that RoboHorizon sets a new state-of-the-art in success rates, particularly in challenging long-horizon tasks on benchmarks like RLBench and FurnitureBench. This validates the effectiveness of the integrated RSPA pipeline and its components, showcasing a tangible leap towards robust long-duration robotic autonomy. The consistent outperformance, especially for tasks involving multiple objects and sequential actions, suggests that RoboHorizon is more resilient to the compounding errors often seen in long-horizon planning.

#### 5.5. Potential for General-Purpose Robotics

By enabling more reliable execution of multi-stage tasks with less manual intervention, RoboHorizon moves closer to the vision of general-purpose robotic manipulators. Such robots could eventually perform complex household chores, warehouse logistics, or assembly tasks with minimal human programming, driving progress in automation and intelligent agents.

#### 5.6. Future Directions

The authors wisely identify critical avenues for future research:
- **Human-in-the-Loop Reward Generation**: Incorporating human feedback into the LLM-assisted reward generation process could create a closed-loop system, enhancing the framework's adaptability and robustness across an even wider range of tasks and unforeseen scenarios.
- **Sim-to-Real Transfer**: Applying the RSPA pipeline and RoboHorizon model to real-world robotic manipulation tasks is the ultimate test. This would involve addressing practical challenges like sensor noise, latency, and calibration, pushing the framework's generalizability beyond simulation.

In summary, RoboHorizon presents a coherent, integrated solution that leverages the strengths of LLMs and advanced visual learning to make substantial progress in long-horizon robotic manipulation, promising a future with more capable and adaptable robotic assistants.
