# EchoRL: Reinforcement Learning via Rollout Echoing

> [!summary] Summary
> EchoRL addresses the "advantage degeneration" issue in Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) for large language models by extracting latent learning signals from successful but reward-degenerated reasoning paths. It consistently boosts reasoning performance across various benchmarks and LLM backbones by up to 5.61% on in-distribution and 5.04% on out-of-distribution tasks for state-of-the-art RLVR methods, introducing negligible computational overhead.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) for Large Language Models (LLMs) suffers from "advantage degeneration."
- This occurs when all sampled rollouts for a given prompt achieve identical verified success, causing the standard deviation of rewards and thus the policy gradient to vanish.
- The issue caps training performance and underutilizes valuable computational resources spent on generating qualitatively different but equally successful reasoning paths.

## Method

- A "==rollout echoing==" procedure is introduced, comprising EchoClip Mining and **EchoRL** Update, to extract and leverage latent learning signals from successful rollouts.
- EchoClip Mining identifies the "hardest" correct reasoning step (based on maximum step-level entropy) within successful rollouts and extracts its prefix as an "EchoClip."
- The EchoRL Update integrates an auxiliary negative log-likelihood loss for these EchoClips into the main RLVR objective, providing a stable gradient signal even during advantage degeneration.

## Results

- EchoRL consistently improved the performance of state-of-the-art RLVR methods (GRPO, DAPO, LUFFY, UFT) by up to **5.61%** on in-distribution and **5.04%** on out-of-distribution benchmarks.
- The method successfully mitigated entropy collapse during training, maintaining significantly higher step-level entropy and enabling continuous performance growth in contrast to standard RLVR methods that stagnate.
- EchoRL operates with negligible computational overhead, introducing virtually no additional latency or memory usage compared to baseline RLVR frameworks due to its integrated optimization strategy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Even when all rollouts for a prompt are equally successful, they contain qualitatively different reasoning paths, and valuable "usable learning signals" systematically cluster around high-entropy decision points.
> - Step-level entropy serves as a reliable proxy for critical learning signals, evidenced by expert trajectories exhibiting higher entropy and ablation studies showing that removing high-entropy steps significantly degrades accuracy.
> - Reinforcing these high-entropy critical segments (EchoClips) through an auxiliary loss provides a dense, non-vanishing gradient that sustains learning and prevents entropy collapse, allowing for continuous performance improvement.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research contributes to the field of large language model (LLM) post-training, specifically focusing on Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) to enhance reasoning capabilities. LLMs have demonstrated advancements across various high-stakes applications, including mathematical reasoning and code generation. Post-training, particularly through RL, is considered a crucial technique for refining these reasoning behaviors. RLVR methods are especially appealing for improving reasoning in domains where outcomes can be automatically verified, providing scalable and reliable supervision signals.

A prominent framework within RLVR is Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), which optimizes policy based on group-normalized advantages derived from a set of sampled rollouts. However, a known challenge in RLVR methods, including GRPO and its variants, is the phenomenon referred to as "advantage degeneration." As LLMs improve, an increasing number of prompts result in all sampled rollouts achieving verified success. This lack of variation in rewards within a group causes the standard deviation of rewards to approach zero, consequently making the group-relative advantages for each rollout also zero. This degeneration leads to vanishing policy gradients, hindering further model optimization and capping training performance.

Prior approaches to address this issue typically involve either filtering out advantage-degenerated prompts (e.g., rejection sampling, dynamic rollout budget allocation), which reduces data efficiency and resource utilization, or incorporating "golden trajectories" from external expert models, which introduces dependence on expensive external knowledge. This paper positions EchoRL as an alternative solution that aims to extract and utilize latent learning signals from advantage-degenerated rollouts themselves, thereby avoiding data discarding and reliance on external experts. It seeks to provide a "plug-and-play" module compatible with existing RLVR frameworks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to address the issue of "advantage degeneration" in Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) for Large Language Models (LLMs). This phenomenon occurs when an LLM becomes proficient enough that all sampled rollouts for a given prompt achieve successful, and thus identical, verifiable rewards. Under the standard GRPO framework, which relies on within-group reward standardization, this leads to a zero standard deviation of rewards, making all advantages zero and causing the policy gradient to vanish. Consequently, training progress stalls, and the model's performance improvement becomes marginal, despite the computational resources spent generating these successful rollouts.

The motivation behind EchoRL stems from the observation that even when all rollouts yield identical success rewards, they may still embody qualitatively different reasoning paths. For instance, a problem might be solvable via a brute-force algebraic method (low-entropy, routine) or through a more elegant, insightful algorithmic trick (high-entropy, innovative). Existing RLVR methods, being blind to the quality of reasoning paths and solely focused on final answer correctness, treat these qualitatively different successful paths identically, resulting in a lost opportunity for learning. The authors argue that these rollouts, even if advantage-degenerated, still contain valuable "usable learning signals" that are currently overlooked.

The specific objectives are:
1. **Identify and characterize usable learning signals:** The authors hypothesize that valuable learning signals systematically cluster around high-entropy decision points in successful reasoning trajectories. This is motivated by an empirical diagnostic contrasting expert golden trajectories with self-generated verified rollouts, revealing higher entropy in expert paths. An ablation study is conducted to confirm that high step-level entropy marks the most critical parts of reasoning trajectories.
2. **Develop a mechanism to extract these signals:** Based on the entropy analysis, the objective is to devise a lightweight module, termed "EchoClip Mining," that can identify and extract critical trajectory segments (EchoClips) from verified-success rollouts, specifically those containing high step-level entropy.
3. **Integrate these signals into the RL objective:** The extracted EchoClips must be effectively fed back into the learning process. The goal is to introduce an auxiliary supervision signal into the RL objective that can provide non-vanishing gradients, even when the primary RLVR signal degenerates to zero. This "EchoRL Update" should be a plug-and-play module compatible with existing RLVR algorithms.
4. **Demonstrate consistent performance improvement with minimal overhead:** The final objective is to experimentally validate that EchoRL consistently enhances reasoning capabilities across diverse benchmarks, LLM backbones, and RLVR methods, while introducing negligible computational or memory overhead. This would address the efficiency concerns of prior methods that discard data or require external experts.

In summary, the core motivation is to unlock the latent learning potential within advantage-degenerated rollouts by identifying and leveraging the "hardest" correct reasoning steps, thereby sustaining continuous and effective post-training for LLMs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of EchoRL is designed to mine and exploit usable learning signals from advantage-degenerated prompts through a two-phase procedure termed "rollout echoing": (1) EchoClip Mining and (2) EchoRL Update.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries: Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) and Advantage Degeneration

The paper first establishes the context by reviewing GRPO, a common RLVR framework. For a given prompt, GRPO samples a group of *N* rollouts, each receiving a verifiable reward *r<sub>i</sub>*. Group-relative advantages (ˆ*A<sub>i</sub>*) are then calculated by normalizing *r<sub>i</sub>* against the mean (μ*<sub>r</sub>*) and standard deviation (σ*<sub>r</sub>*) of rewards within the group. The GRPO loss function (J<sub>GRPO</sub>) incorporates these advantages, weighted by importance ratios and a KL regularization term.

Advantage degeneration is formally defined as the condition where σ*<sub>r</sub>*(q) = 0 for a given prompt *q*. When this occurs, all advantages ˆ*A<sub>i</sub>* become zero, leading to a vanishing policy gradient and halting learning progress from that prompt.

#### 3.2. Entropy-Based Motivation

EchoRL is motivated by an empirical analysis of entropy patterns in reasoning trajectories. The authors observe that:
- **Golden trajectories have higher entropy:** External expert-generated "golden trajectories" exhibit systematically higher entropy distributions compared to self-generated verified-success rollouts, suggesting that valuable supervision often co-occurs with points of high predictive uncertainty.
- **High-entropy steps are critical:** A case study demonstrates that even among equally rewarded successful rollouts, some paths (e.g., using a "log-derivative trick") exhibit substantially higher step-level entropy than routine algebraic solutions. An ablation study confirms that removing high-entropy steps from reasoning trajectories causes significant accuracy degradation at lower removal ratios compared to low-entropy or random step removal. This validates that high step-level entropy serves as a reliable proxy for critical, usable learning signals.

To mitigate noise from single-token fluctuations, the approach aggregates token-level entropy within each reasoning step by averaging, yielding a smoother "step-level entropy" (¯*H*(*s<sub>j</sub>*)).

#### 3.3. EchoClip Mining

This phase aims to extract the "hardest" correct reasoning clip from verified-success rollouts.
1. **Filter successful rollouts:** For a given prompt *q*, the model first samples a group of rollouts. From these, a subset *V* is filtered, containing only verified-success rollouts (where *r*(o) = 1).
2. **Segment into steps:** Each verified rollout *o* ∈ *V* is decomposed into a sequence of discrete reasoning steps (*s<sub>1</sub>*, *s<sub>2</sub>*..., *s<sub>M</sub>*), using natural language delimiters (e.g., newline characters `\n`).
3. **Calculate step-level entropy:** For each step *s<sub>j</sub>*, the step-level entropy ¯*H*(*s<sub>j</sub>*) is computed by averaging the policy's token-level entropy *H<sub>θ</sub>*(*x*) for all tokens *x* within *s<sub>j</sub>*.
4. **Identify the most critical step:** The single most critical step (*s*<sup>⋆</sup>) is identified as the step with the maximum entropy across all steps from all verified rollouts for the current prompt.
5. **Extract the EchoClip:** The unique EchoClip (*o<sub>echo</sub>*) is defined as the prefix of the parent rollout (*o*<sup>⋆</sup>) containing *s*<sup>⋆</sup>, ending precisely at *s*<sup>⋆</sup>. This clip represents the model's successful navigation through its most uncertain decision point among all successful attempts.

#### 3.4. EchoRL Update

The mined EchoClip is then used to augment the standard RLVR objective.
1. **Auxiliary Loss (J<sub>EchoRL</sub>):** An auxiliary loss term is introduced, defined as the negative log-likelihood of the EchoClip tokens under the current policy (J<sub>EchoRL</sub>(θ) = -1/|*o<sub>echo</sub>*| Σ log π<sub>θ</sub>((o<sub>echo</sub>)*<sub>t</sub>* | *q*, (*o<sub>echo</sub>*)*<sub><t</sub>*)). This term encourages the model to assign higher probabilities to the tokens within the EchoClip, reinforcing the observed successful reasoning path.
2. **Integrated Objective:** This auxiliary term is integrated into the total objective function (J(θ) = J<sub>RLVR</sub>(θ) + λJ<sub>EchoRL</sub>(θ)), where λ is a hyperparameter that balances the standard RL signal with the auxiliary supervision.
3. **Mitigation of Advantage Degeneration:** When advantage degeneration occurs (i.e., σ*<sub>r</sub>* → 0, causing the GRPO gradient to vanish), J<sub>EchoRL</sub> remains active for verified rollouts. This provides a stable, dense gradient signal that reinforces the successful resolution of high-uncertainty steps captured by the EchoClip, thereby sustaining training progress.

#### 3.5. Experimental Setup

The approach is evaluated across a broad set of experiments:
- **Benchmarks:** 10 benchmarks, including 6 in-distribution math tasks (AIME 2024/2025, AMC, MATH-500, Minerva, OlympiadBench) and 3 out-of-distribution tasks (ARC-c, GPQA-Diamond, MMLU-Pro), plus the multimodal spatial reasoning benchmark Geometry3K.
- **Baselines:** EchoRL is integrated into four representative RLVR methods: GRPO, DAPO, LUFFY, and UFT. Comparisons are also made against SFT, SFT-KL, and other zero-shot RLVR methods.
- **Models:** 5 LLM backbones ranging from 1.5B to 8B parameters (Qwen2.5, LLaMA-3.1 families, and Qwen2.5-VL for multimodal).
- **Parameters:** A rollout batch size of 128, update batch size of 64, 8 responses sampled per on-policy question, and a default λ of 0.001.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of EchoRL yielded several consistent findings across various LLM backbones, benchmarks, and integration with existing RLVR methods.

#### 4.1. Consistent Performance Improvements (RQ1)

- **Universal Booster:** EchoRL consistently improved the performance of several representative RLVR methods, including GRPO, DAPO, LUFFY, and UFT. These gains were observed across different model families (Qwen, LLaMA) and scales (from 1.5B to 8B parameters).
- **Broad Applicability:** The performance enhancements extended from standard in-distribution mathematical reasoning tasks to out-of-distribution generalization benchmarks (ARC-c, GPQA-Diamond, MMLU-Pro), and even to multimodal spatial reasoning (Geometry3K).
- **Quantitative Gains:** EchoRL improved state-of-the-art RLVR methods by up to 5.61% on in-distribution benchmarks and 5.04% on out-of-distribution benchmarks for the Qwen2.5-Math-7B model (Table 1). Similar trends were observed for other model scales and families (Figure 5, Table 2). For example, GRPO+EchoRL on Qwen2.5-Math-7B achieved an average in-distribution performance of 47.0% compared to GRPO's 44.5%, and an out-of-distribution average of 58.4% compared to 55.6%. DAPO+EchoRL showed even larger gains, reaching 49.9% (ID) and 60.9% (OOD) versus DAPO's 47.9% and 57.6%.

#### 4.2. Benefits Scale with Model Size

- **Larger Models, Greater Impact:** A clear trend was identified where the relative improvement offered by EchoRL increased with the model size (e.g., from 1.5B to 8B parameters). This is attributed to larger LLMs encountering advantage degeneration earlier in training or on easier tasks, making EchoRL's mechanism of utilizing verified-success rollouts more impactful in these scenarios.

#### 4.3. Mitigation of Entropy Collapse and Sustained Learning

- **Sustained Exploration:** Analysis of training dynamics (Figure 4, Right) showed that while standard GRPO exhibited rapid decay in step-level entropy—indicative of premature convergence to a narrow set of reasoning paths—EchoRL maintained significantly higher levels of step-level entropy throughout training.
- **Continuous Performance Growth:** This sustained entropy correlated with continuous performance growth, suggesting that the auxiliary EchoClip supervision prevented the optimizer from stagnating at local optima and provided a persistent learning signal even when the primary reward signal degenerated.

#### 4.4. Negligible Computational Overhead (RQ2)

- **"Free Lunch" Upgrade:** Empirical profiling confirmed that EchoRL introduced virtually no additional latency or memory usage compared to standard GRPO. This efficiency stems from its unified optimization strategy, merging the auxiliary EchoClip loss with the primary objective and executing a single backward pass.
- **Lightweight Operations:** The additional computations for EchoClip (step-level entropy calculation via reduction over logits, linear-time segment identification) are minimal, leveraging data already computed by vanilla GRPO. Figure 9 demonstrates that the "Actor Update Time" for EchoRL closely tracks that of GRPO, with statistically indistinguishable curves.

#### 4.5. Framework Analysis and Robustness (RQ3)

- **Hyperparameter Sensitivity (λ):** An ablation study on the auxiliary loss coefficient λ (Figure 6, Left) indicated that λ = 0.001 was an optimal balance. Larger values (e.g., λ ≥ 0.1) degraded performance by overpowering the main RL signal, while the chosen default value consistently improved stability.
- **Clip/Step Selection Strategy:** Comparing different auxiliary target definitions (Figure 8, Middle) showed that EchoClip (selecting the prefix ending at the highest-entropy step) yielded the strongest ID/OOD gains, outperforming using the full verified rollout or the lowest-entropy rollout clip. This reinforces the importance of focusing supervision on pivotal uncertain decision points.
- **Training Schedule:** Ablation on the timing of EchoRL activation (Figure 8, Right) suggested that full-training performs best for GRPO, while for DAPO, activating EchoRL during mid-to-late training (steps 300–500) could be slightly more effective, aligning with the intuition that later stages contain more high-quality verified-success rollouts. However, to avoid introducing additional hyperparameters, full-training was adopted as the default.
- **Statistical Significance:** Rigorous sensitivity analysis across three independent runs (Table 3, Figure 6, Right) demonstrated that EchoRL's improvements were statistically significant, with non-overlapping 95% confidence intervals, confirming robustness against random fluctuations.
- **No Length/Stylistic Inflation:** EchoRL did not systematically increase response length (Figure 7), indicating that performance gains were not due to trivial effects like generating longer answers.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The significance of EchoRL lies in its effective solution to a fundamental limitation in current Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) frameworks for Large Language Models (LLMs): advantage degeneration. This phenomenon, where policy gradients vanish as models become proficient, has been a bottleneck, leading to stalled training progress and underutilization of computational resources.

**Key areas of significance include:**

- **Enhanced Training Efficiency and Performance:** EchoRL allows for continuous learning and performance improvement even when traditional RLVR signals collapse. By extracting "usable learning signals" from advantage-degenerated rollouts, it prevents the stagnation of training, leading to higher final reasoning capabilities. This directly addresses the efficiency concern of discarding valuable data or relying on expensive external expert knowledge.
- **Resource-Friendly Approach:** A notable impact is that EchoRL achieves these gains with negligible computational overhead. As a lightweight, "plug-and-play" module, it integrates seamlessly into existing RLVR pipelines without requiring additional infrastructure like external reward models or separate backward passes. This makes it a practical and accessible enhancement for LLM post-training.
- **Improved Model Robustness and Exploration:** By sustaining a healthy level of step-level entropy throughout training, EchoRL helps models avoid premature convergence to narrow reasoning paths. This encourages continued exploration of diverse and potentially more optimal solution strategies, leading to more robust and versatile reasoning abilities.
- **Broad Applicability:** The demonstrated efficacy across various LLM backbones (Qwen, LLaMA), model scales (1.5B to 8B parameters), and diverse reasoning domains (mathematics, general science, multimodal spatial reasoning) suggests that EchoRL is a generalizable solution. Its compatibility with different RLVR methods (GRPO, DAPO, LUFFY, UFT) positions it as a widely applicable enhancement.
- **New Paradigm for Signal Extraction:** EchoRL introduces an entropy-based method for identifying and leveraging critical reasoning steps within successful but otherwise "uninformative" trajectories. This provides a novel perspective on how to extract fine-grained supervisory signals from self-generated data, moving beyond simple reward scores.

**Potential impact:**

- **Accelerated LLM Development:** Researchers and practitioners can leverage EchoRL to push the performance boundaries of LLMs in reasoning tasks, making post-training more effective and less prone to early saturation.
- **Reduced Training Costs:** By maximizing the utility of generated rollouts, EchoRL can indirectly contribute to reducing the overall cost of training high-performing reasoning LLMs, as fewer rollouts might be "wasted."
- **Improved Generative Quality:** By reinforcing sophisticated reasoning paths, EchoRL could lead to LLMs that not only provide correct answers but also articulate higher-quality, more insightful, or efficient reasoning processes.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The methodology of using step-level entropy to identify critical learning signals could inspire further research into fine-grained analysis of LLM reasoning processes and novel ways to guide learning.

In conclusion, EchoRL offers a technically sound and empirically validated solution to a critical problem in LLM post-training. Its ability to enhance performance and efficiency without additional resource burden represents a practical advancement for the development and deployment of advanced language models. The specific techniques introduced do not inherently present new risks beyond those already associated with existing large language model technologies.
