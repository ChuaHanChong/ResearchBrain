# GenHOI: Contact-Aware Humanoid-Object Interaction by Imitating Generated Videos without Task-Specific Training

> [!summary] Summary
> GenHOI lets a humanoid perform object-interaction tasks zero-shot by imitating one generated video. It renders a digital-twin first frame from onboard RGB-D, prompts a video diffusion model, extracts hand-object contact constraints from the video, and refines the recovered motion via geometry-guided terminal trajectory optimization tracked closed-loop. Average success reaches 76.7% across four objects with under two minutes of skill acquisition.

## Problem

- Prior HOI methods need per-task reward design, demonstrations, and policy training, costing over an hour per object and tying behavior to fixed reference trajectories.
- Video-imitation work targets fixed-base manipulators with simple contacts, leaving whole-body loco-manipulation, which couples dynamic balance with bimanual contact on complex geometry, largely unaddressed.
- Existing video-to-trajectory pipelines assume metric consistency between generated 2D video and the real scene, rely on external cameras, and cannot retarget to unseen robot-object poses.

## Method

- **GenHOI**'s ==real-to-sim video generation==: onboard RGB-D 6D pose estimation rebuilds the robot-object scene in ==MuJoCo==, and the rendered first frame plus language command conditions ==Seedance 2.0== to synthesize a 5-second interaction video.
- ==Contact-aware geometric constraint extraction==: a VLM picks the terminal contact key frame, monocular depth is metric-rescaled via the twin, and hand keypoints snap or ray-cast onto the object mesh.
- ==Geometry-guided trajectory optimization==: ==constrained nonlinear least-squares== refines root pose, waist pitch, and 14 arm joints toward contact targets with an inward virtual-spring bias, then ==quintic smoothstep== propagates the correction over 3 seconds.

## Results

- Across box, chair, table, and cylinder tasks GenHOI reaches **76.7%** average success and **0.22** m hand-contact error, versus **11.7%** and **0.75** m for the ExoActor video-tracking baseline.
- Ablations confirm each part: removing contact detection drops success to **41.7%**, dropping the inward bias gives **43.3%**, and removing trajectory smoothing gives **28.3%**.
- Skill acquisition takes **1** min **51** s versus about **70** min for policy training; at an out-of-distribution **3.0** m box distance GenHOI scores **8/10** while both baselines score **0/10**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A digital twin supplies the metric scale monocular video lacks: contact points grounded on a known mesh convert visual cues into executable, physically consistent targets.
> - Decoupling one reference motion from the current object pose buys generalization that trajectory-tracking policies lack; re-solving constraints online beats retraining when scenes shift.
> - Pipeline quality is capped by its weakest generative link: most failures came from video generation artifacts, so stronger generators and trackers gate progress.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Humanoid-Object Interaction (HOI) is a critical capability for deploying humanoid robots in human-centered environments. This task presents challenges due to the necessity of coordinating whole-body stability with intricate physical interaction across diverse objects.

Current approaches to HOI primarily fall into two categories:
1. **Direct Trajectory Mimicry:** Methods that directly mimic a single reference trajectory. These often exhibit limited generalization, as behaviors are tied to specific reference motions and may fail when robot-object relative poses, object geometry, or contact affordances deviate from the training distribution.
2. **Task-Specific Policy Training:** Approaches that learn task policies, often regularized by motion-distribution priors (e.g., motion-prior models, AMP-based discriminators) or reinforcement learning. These methods typically require extensive pre-collected motion data, task-specific demonstrations, meticulous reward design, and considerable policy training for each new task or object category, making them time-consuming and resource-intensive.

Recently, significant progress in video generation models, such as Sora and Seedance, has demonstrated their capacity to synthesize videos with increasing visual fidelity and physical plausibility. This development offers a potential alternative to traditional model-based or data-driven HOI methods, suggesting a more generalizable pathway for action planning.

Efforts to leverage generated videos for robot manipulation can be broadly categorized into:
- **Auxiliary Tools for Policy Learning:** In this paradigm, video generation models are used to augment training data or learn video-based representations. While these approaches can enhance generalization, they still necessitate task-specific policy training and real robot interaction data.
- **Direct Translation to Robot Behaviors:** This emerging direction aims to translate generated videos directly into robot actions, bypassing additional policy learning. Prior work in this area has largely focused on fixed-base manipulators, which operate within constrained workspaces and involve comparatively simpler robot-object interactions. Extending this direct imitation to humanoid loco-manipulation introduces substantial complexities, including coordinating whole-body locomotion with bimanual manipulation and establishing stable contacts with geometrically diverse objects. Furthermore, prior methods often overlook or inadequately address spatial and metric inconsistencies between 2D generated videos and the real environment, leading to potential inaccuracies in contact localization and motion execution. Existing paradigms also frequently tie behaviors to the specific scene configuration depicted in the video, limiting generalization to unseen object poses.

The GenHOI framework positions itself by addressing these limitations. It explores a novel paradigm for humanoid loco-manipulation by directly imitating single generated videos without task-specific training or physical demonstration data. The work specifically targets the challenges of metric inconsistency and generalization to unseen robot-object relative poses in the context of whole-body humanoid interaction. It distinguishes itself by integrating contact-aware geometric constraints derived from the generated video to guide trajectory optimization, thereby aligning the trajectory with the real robot workspace and enabling generalization across different robot-object relative poses through an updated optimization process.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable humanoid robots to perform diverse object-interaction tasks in a zero-shot manner, by directly imitating a single generated video, without requiring task-specific policy training or physical demonstration data. This overarching goal is motivated by several identified challenges and limitations of existing HOI methods:

1. **Addressing the Challenge of Humanoid-Object Interaction (HOI):** HOI is inherently complex for humanoid robots due to the tight coupling between maintaining dynamic balance and interacting stably with various objects. Robots must simultaneously coordinate whole-body motion, contact locations, and interaction forces, which presents a significant control problem.
2. **Overcoming Limitations of Task-Specific Training:** Existing HOI methods often entail time-consuming and resource-intensive task-specific policy training or rely on rigid trajectory replay. This dependence limits their ability to adapt to novel interaction scenarios, including variations in robot-object relative pose, object geometry, or contact affordances beyond the training distribution. The authors aim to bypass this intensive training requirement.
3. **Leveraging Advances in Video Generation Models:** Recent progress in video generation models (e.g., Sora, Seedance) has shown their capability to synthesize videos that visually and dynamically resemble the real world. The motivation is to explore if these models' generalization capabilities can be harnessed to plan future actions for humanoid loco-manipulation tasks, offering a potentially more scalable and generalizable alternative to traditional approaches.
4. **Mitigating Spatial and Metric Inconsistencies:** Direct translation of generated 2D videos into robot behaviors for humanoid loco-manipulation introduces challenges related to spatial and metric inconsistencies. The 2D nature of generated videos can lead to depth ambiguity, occlusion, and perspective effects, resulting in inaccurate contact localization and motion execution errors in the real 3D environment. A key motivation is to develop a mechanism to resolve these discrepancies and ensure physical plausibility.
5. **Achieving Generalization to Unseen Scenarios:** Previous methods often tie robot behaviors to the specific scene configurations depicted in demonstrations or generated videos, limiting their generalization to unseen object poses or different robot-object relative poses. The researchers are motivated to develop a framework that allows a single reference trajectory to be adapted to novel configurations.
6. **Enabling Autonomous Operation with Onboard Sensing:** Prior work relying on generated videos often uses manually captured images from external cameras as input. A motivation for GenHOI is to enable autonomous operation by generating interaction videos based solely on onboard robot sensing, thereby facilitating closed-loop deployment.

In summary, the core motivation is to develop a simple, efficient, and generalizable framework for humanoid loco-manipulation that capitalizes on the progress in video generation while explicitly addressing the challenges of physical consistency and adaptability to novel environments and interaction parameters.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

GenHOI is structured into four sequential modules: real-to-sim video generation, contact-aware geometric constraint extraction, geometry-guided trajectory optimization, and closed-loop trajectory tracking.

#### 3.1. A. Real-to-Sim Video Generation

This module aims to generate a task-oriented interaction video using only onboard robot sensing.
1. **Scene Reconstruction:** The robot first estimates the 6D pose of the target object relative to itself using onboard RGB-D observations (e.g., via foundationPose or AprilTag), assuming the object mesh is available.
2. **Simulation Rendering:** The robot and the estimated object are then reconstructed and rendered within a MuJoCo simulation environment. Virtual cameras are strategically placed to obtain third-person views of the robot-object scene.
3. **Video Synthesis:** A simulator-rendered first-frame image, along with a language command describing the desired task, conditions a video diffusion model (Seedance 2.0) to synthesize a 5-second interaction video from a fixed viewpoint.
4. **3D Pose Recovery:** The generated video's N frames are processed to recover the robot's 3D pose sequence. This involves estimating 3D human poses using GVHMR and then retargeting them to the humanoid robot using GMR, yielding a raw robot 3D pose trajectory comprising root poses and joint angles.

#### 3.2. B. Contact-Aware Geometric Constraint Extraction

This module addresses the inaccuracies inherent in 3D pose recovery from 2D videos by identifying critical contact events and estimating hand-object contact regions, which are then encoded as geometric constraints.
1. **Key Frame Selection:** To identify the moment of initial physical interaction, frames from the last 3 seconds of the generated video are uniformly sampled and concatenated into a composite image. A Vision-Language Model (VLM), Doubao-Seed-2.0, is then queried with a prompt to identify the earliest frame where the humanoid's hands are fully in contact with the object. This is designated as the terminal interaction frame.
2. **Metric Depth Recovery:** Monocular depth for the selected key frame is estimated using Depth Anything. The predicted depth, which is scale-ambiguous, is then metrically scaled using the digital-twin setup where camera parameters and the object pose in the camera frame are known.
3. **3D Hand Position Estimation and Refinement:**
 - VitPose extracts 2D keypoints for both hands, which are then lifted to coarse 3D positions using the restored metric depth.
 - These coarse positions are refined to align with the object mesh:
 - If a hand is visible and not occluded by the object, its position is refined to the nearest point on the object mesh.
 - If a hand is occluded, a ray is cast from the camera center through the 3D hand position, and its last intersection with the object mesh is used as the contact position. Hand visibility is determined by the color region within the hand mask.
 - The hand orientations are retained from the recovered humanoid motion at the terminal interaction frame. These refined hand positions and orientations define the contact-aware geometric constraints.

#### 3.3. C. Geometry-Guided Trajectory Optimization

This module refines the raw 3D pose trajectory to achieve physically consistent robot-object interaction, resolve scale ambiguity, and generalize to new object poses.
1. **Lightweight Terminal Refinement:** Instead of optimizing the entire trajectory (which is computationally intensive for full-body humanoids), a subset of the terminal full-body state is optimized. This subset includes the robot's root position (x, y, z), root yaw angle (ψ), waist pitch angle (φ), and upper-body arm joint angles (q).
2. **Forward Kinematics:** The world-frame poses of the left and right hands are computed from the optimized terminal state using forward kinematics.
3. **Compliance-Aware Inward Bias:** For bimanual object holding, a heuristic inward bias is introduced. The target hand positions are slightly displaced towards each other along the inter-hand direction. This "virtual compression" aims to induce a nominal holding tendency, acting as a compliance prior for stable interaction.
4. **Constrained Nonlinear Least-Squares Problem:** The terminal refinement is formulated as an optimization problem minimizing a geometric residual. The residual comprises 6D pose errors between the current and adjusted target hand poses, regularized by the deviation from the raw terminal state. The optimization is subject to box constraints on root height, root yaw, waist pitch, and individual joint limits.
5. **Trajectory Smoothing:** The optimized terminal state correction is smoothly propagated to the preceding K (e.g., 90) frames using a quintic smoothstep function. This ensures a gradual transition to the optimized configuration, preventing abrupt motion changes and improving tracking stability.

#### 3.4. D. Closed-Loop Trajectory Tracking

The final module executes the optimized trajectory on the real humanoid robot.
1. **Real-time Localization:** The robot uses its onboard LiDAR to estimate its current global pose during execution, providing feedback for trajectory tracking.
2. **Decoupled Tracking:** A closed-loop tracking controller, based on the open-source Sonic general-purpose motion controller, is used. It decouples tracking into:
 - **Lower Body:** Tracks the optimized global root trajectory in Cartesian space (x, y, z translation).
 - **Upper Body:** Tracks the optimized joint trajectory, including the waist pitch angle and arm joint angles. This decoupled strategy maintains stable locomotion while enabling the upper body to sustain the desired hand-object interaction.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The GenHOI framework was evaluated through extensive simulation and real-world experiments on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot across diverse object-interaction tasks.

#### 4.1. A. Comparison with Baselines

The study compared GenHOI against ExoActor (a direct video-to-trajectory tracking method), a task-specific policy learning method (HDMI), and several ablation variants (W/o Cont. Det., W/o Traj. Smooth., W/o Inward Bias).

1. **Success Rate and Contact Accuracy (Table II, Fig. 3):**
 - GenHOI achieved an average success rate of 76.7% and an average hand-contact point error of 0.22 m across box grasping, asymmetric bimanual chair carrying, table lifting, and cylindrical-object enveloping tasks.
 - ExoActor showed significant spatial deviations and low success rates (11.7% average) due to scale ambiguity from monocular videos, resulting in large contact errors (0.75 m average).
 - Ablation variants demonstrated the necessity of each module:
 - **W/o Cont. Det.:** Improved over ExoActor (41.7% success, 0.27 m error) but lacked explicit contact optimization for stable interaction.
 - **W/o Traj. Smooth.:** Led to abrupt motion changes and degraded tracking accuracy (28.3% success, 0.37 m error).
 - **W/o Inward Bias:** Achieved geometric alignment but lacked the compressive force prior for stable holding (43.3% success, 0.23 m error).
 - Failure analysis revealed that root-position tracking errors and their propagation to hand trajectories were common causes of failure, increasing hand-contact point error and leading to collisions or unstable grasping.

2. **Learning Time (Table III):**
 - GenHOI required significantly less time to acquire skills (1 min 51 s for box carrying) compared to task-specific policy learning methods like HDMI (approximately 70 minutes). This highlights the efficiency of the zero-shot imitation paradigm.

3. **Generalization Performance (Table IV, Fig. 4):**
 - GenHOI demonstrated strong generalization to out-of-distribution (OOD) object poses. For box carrying, it consistently achieved high success rates (e.g., 80% at -1.0 m and +1.5 m OOD distances relative to the reference 1.5 m), outperforming HDMI and ExoActor.
 - This generalization ability is attributed to GenHOI's online trajectory refinement conditioned on the observed object pose, allowing it to adapt a single reference trajectory to varying robot-object configurations. HDMI's performance degraded significantly outside its training distribution.

#### 4.2. B. Key Factors of Video Generation for Loco-Manipulation

The study investigated the influence of video generation models and camera viewpoints on performance.

1. **Video Generation Model (Fig. 5):**
 - Stronger video generation models (Seedance 2.0) yielded higher generation success rates (up to 90% for box/chair tasks) by producing videos with better prompt adherence, consistent object geometry, and physically plausible interactions.
 - Weaker models (Wan 2.2, Seedance 1.0) frequently suffered from object deformation and camera viewpoint drift, leading to invalid task-oriented videos, especially for complex interactions like table and cylinder manipulation.

2. **First-Frame Camera Azimuth (Fig. 6):**
 - Viewpoints where both the robot and object were clearly visible maintained consistent high success rates.
 - A noticeable drop in success rate occurred at azimuth angles of 150° and 180° due to the robot substantially occluding the target object in the generated videos, leading to unreliable hand detection and contact-point estimation.

#### 4.3. C. Key Modules Evaluation

1. **Key Frame Selection (Table V):**
 - VLM-based key frame selection (Doubao-Seed-2.0 and GPT-5.5) achieved high accuracy (95.0% and 96.7% average, respectively) across different tasks, reliably localizing the initial hand-object contact moment. The main performance drop was observed in the table task due to larger object geometry and partial occlusions.

2. **Contact Point Detection (Fig. 7):**
 - The proposed refinement step effectively recovered reliable hand-object contact locations, even when hands were partially occluded or weakly visible in the key frame. This contributed to accurate object-consistent target hand positions.

#### 4.4. D. Real-World Experiments

Real-world deployments on the Unitree G1 robot demonstrated the framework's capabilities.

1. **Interaction with Different Objects (Fig. 8):** GenHOI successfully performed loco-manipulation tasks for a box, chair, table, and cylinder. It generated distinct interaction strategies tailored to object geometries and contact configurations, such as symmetric bimanual contacts for a box, asymmetric contacts for a chair, lifting from below for a table, and an enveloping motion for a cylinder.

2. **Generalization to OOD Object Positions (Fig. 9):** Using a single generated video, the robot continuously performed box-grasping tasks at varying planar positions (1.5 m, 2.5 m, 3.5 m) and heights (0.3 m, 0.6 m, 0.8 m). Online trajectory optimization enabled adaptation to these different OOD configurations, demonstrating the framework's steerable interaction capability.

#### 4.5. E. Analysis of Failure Cases (Fig. 10)

Out of 50 repeated trials for box grasping, 34 were successful.
- The primary source of failure was identified as video generation issues (e.g., camera-viewpoint drift, object deformation, hallucination).
- Errors in keyframe extraction and contact detection were also contributing factors.
- A smaller number of failures occurred during robot trajectory tracking, attributed to limitations in the low-level humanoid controller's tracking accuracy.
- The findings suggest that the quality of generated videos and the robustness of the underlying humanoid controller are key bottlenecks for direct video imitation in HOI.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The GenHOI framework represents a step towards enabling humanoid robots to perform complex object-interaction tasks with increased efficiency and generalization.

1. **Zero-Shot Learning for HOI:** The work introduces a paradigm that allows humanoid robots to acquire diverse interaction skills by directly imitating a single generated video, without the need for task-specific policy training or extensive physical demonstration data. This significantly reduces the time and computational resources typically required for humanoid loco-manipulation tasks.
2. **Enhanced Generalization:** By incorporating contact-aware geometric constraints and geometry-guided trajectory optimization, the framework enables a single generated reference motion to generalize effectively to out-of-distribution (OOD) object poses and relative robot-object configurations. This addresses a critical limitation of previous methods that often struggle with novel scenarios beyond their training distribution.
3. **Physically Grounded Interaction:** The methodology explicitly accounts for spatial and metric inconsistencies between 2D video data and the 3D physical world. The extraction and integration of contact-aware geometric constraints convert visual interaction cues into physically grounded optimization priors, leading to more stable and accurate real-world interactions.
4. **Autonomous Operation:** The "real-to-sim" video generation module, which leverages onboard robot sensing (RGB-D, LiDAR) to reconstruct the scene and generate a first-frame image, enables autonomous operation without reliance on external cameras or manual input for video generation.
5. **Scalable Action Priors:** This research highlights the potential of large-scale generative video models as scalable action priors for robotics. By leveraging the increasingly sophisticated visual and dynamic fidelity of these models, robots can derive diverse and complex behaviors without explicit programming or data collection for each new task.
6. **Insights into Video Generation for Robotics:** The systematic evaluation of different video generation models and camera viewpoints provides insights into the factors that influence the quality and utility of generated videos for downstream robotic tasks, informing future development in controllable video synthesis for robotics.

While the current framework has limitations, such as the assumption of accurate object meshes and the use of coarse grippers, it lays foundational work for future advancements. Potential future impact includes the integration of online 3D reconstruction, shape completion, and dexterous hands, which could further expand the scalability and precision of humanoid-object interaction in unstructured environments. The work contributes to the broader goal of developing more versatile, autonomous, and adaptable humanoid robots capable of operating effectively in human-centric spaces.
