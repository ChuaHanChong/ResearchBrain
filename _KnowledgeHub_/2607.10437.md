# Interleaved POMDP Planning for Multi-Object Search in Unknown Multi-Room Household Environments

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Georgia Institute of Technology, The Hong Kong University of Science and Technology, and MIT developed Inter-POMDP, an interleaved POMDP planning algorithm enabling robots to efficiently search for multiple objects in unknown, multi-room household environments. The system reduced collision counts by up to 63%, navigation steps by up to 35%, and detection counts by up to 32% compared to baseline methods in simulated and real-world experiments.

## Problem

- Traditional multi-object search in robotics faces computational intractability when using POMDPs in large, unknown, and cluttered multi-room household environments due to exponential growth of belief space.
- Existing learning-based or Large Foundation Model (LFM) methods often lack efficient exploration, struggle with generalization, or ignore crucial geometric constraints and fine-grained object correlations.
- Current hierarchical POMDP approaches for object search typically operate sequentially, failing to integrate low-level geometric feedback into high-level semantic decisions, leading to suboptimal global plans.

## Method

- **Inter-POMDP** employs a two-level ==hierarchical POMDP== planning framework for multi-object search, separating abstract semantic reasoning from fine-grained geometric navigation.
- A High-Level ==POUCT (Partially Observable UCT)== leverages LLM-inferred object-object and object-furniture semantic relations to select promising search regions.
- A Low-Level Motion Planner uses a ==particle belief== over obstacle states to plan safe navigation paths, and its accumulated navigation costs are propagated back to initialize the high-level ==UCT== values, enabling interleaved planning.

## Results

- In simulations, Inter-POMDP reduced collision counts by up to **63%**, aggregated navigation steps by up to **35%**, and detection counts by up to **32%** compared to baseline methods.
- The system consistently achieved **zero collisions** for subsequent objects in simulated environments and demonstrated improved generalizability across diverse scenes.
- Real-world deployment on a mobile manipulator confirmed the practical applicability, demonstrating efficient target localization and robust navigation in an unknown, multi-room environment.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Interleaved planning, where low-level geometric navigation costs directly inform and bias high-level semantic search decisions, significantly improves the balance between planning quality and efficiency.
> - Leveraging Large Language Models (LLMs) to infer object-object and object-furniture semantic relations provides powerful, adaptive priors for high-level search, enhancing generalizability.
> - Maintaining separate belief representations (histogram for high-level semantic areas, particle for low-level geometric obstacles) and updating them interactively allows for robust hedging against both types of uncertainty.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work addresses the classical problem of object search within the field of household robotics, specifically focusing on scenarios in unknown, multi-room, and cluttered environments. Traditional approaches to object search often assume deterministic settings, which are insufficient for real-world applications characterized by extensive uncertainty regarding object locations, environmental layouts, and unobserved obstacles.

Partially Observable Markov Decision Processes (POMDPs) offer a principled framework for sequential decision-making under uncertainty. However, POMDPs are known to be computationally intractable in large domains due to the exponential growth of belief space dimensionality with the number of possible states and objects. This intractability makes long-horizon planning particularly challenging in large, partially observed environments.

Existing methods in object search and related fields include:
- **Learning-based methods**: These approaches train robots through repeated interactions. However, they often suffer from inefficient exploration, a lack of direct modeling of semantic and geometric scene information, and poor generalizability due to extensive data requirements and challenges in sim-to-real transfer.
- **Large Foundation Model (LFM) methods**: These leverage natural language and visual inputs for navigation and target localization, often using semantic priors from models like LLMs. While beneficial for high-level reasoning, many LFM methods tend to ignore geometric constraints, past planning experience, or rely on coarse, room-level hierarchies, thereby missing fine-grained object correlations and physical navigation challenges.
- **Probabilistic planning methods (POMDPs)**: While principled for uncertainty, these methods face computational scaling issues. Some employ sparse belief sampling and Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) but can be overly greedy or lack transparency. Many existing POMDP-based object search systems either use naive POMDP solvers without adapting to problem structure, ignore navigation uncertainty, lack continuous exploitation of semantic information, assume object independence, or do not incorporate low-level feedback into high-level decisions. For instance, some hierarchical POMDP approaches operate purely sequentially, where high-level decisions do not account for the real-world costs or feasibility of low-level execution.

This research aims to bridge the gap between the theoretical robustness of POMDPs and the practical demands of real-world household robotics by developing an interleaved, hierarchical planning framework. It specifically extends prior work, such as that addressing MDP settings with known dynamics in pre-partitioned environments, by tackling the complexities of POMDP planning with unknown transition models (due to unobserved obstacles) and integrating abstract semantic reasoning for efficient search in multi-room environments. The work positions itself as a method that systematically combines semantic and geometric reasoning to manage the dual challenges of planning quality and computational efficiency in complex, partially observable domains.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable autonomous household robots to efficiently and safely perform multi-object search in unknown, multi-room, and cluttered household environments. This overarching goal is driven by several specific motivations and aims to address key challenges inherent in such tasks:

- **Addressing POMDP Intractability in Large Domains**: The core motivation is to overcome the computational intractability of traditional POMDPs when applied to large-scale environments with numerous unknown objects and obstacles. The belief space in such scenarios grows exponentially, making exact or even approximate planning impractical for long horizons and complex state-action spaces.
- **Balancing Planning Quality and Efficiency**: The research seeks to design a planning framework that can effectively balance the need for high-quality, robust plans (e.g., minimizing collisions and ensuring detection) with the computational efficiency required for real-time operation in dynamic, uncertain environments. This involves tackling the depth of search trees and the branching factors arising from a vast state-action space.
- **Managing Extensive Uncertainty**: Real-world household environments present extensive uncertainty, including unknown object locations, the dynamic nature of cluttered spaces, and the presence of unobserved obstacles. The objective is to develop a system that can reason about and plan effectively under these conditions.
- **Integrating High-Level Semantics with Low-Level Geometry**: A key motivation is to move beyond approaches that only consider distance-based reachability or rely solely on high-level semantic priors. The work aims to systematically incorporate informative object-level and object-furniture relations, derived from semantic understanding, into the low-level navigation costs. This integration is critical because target objects can also serve as obstacles, and cluttered navigational spaces demand planning that accounts for physical-world uncertainties at a fine-grained level.
- **Developing Robust Navigation in Cluttered Spaces**: The arbitrary arrangements of furniture and objects in household environments make obstacle-free navigation a significant challenge. The research is motivated by the need to integrate grounded navigation information into the belief update process to make planning more robust against physical uncertainties, rather than simply replanning upon failure or relying on abstract action re-selection strategies.
- **Principled Interleaved Planning**: Existing hierarchical POMDP approaches often suffer from a lack of interaction or operate sequentially, leading to suboptimal global plans because high-level decisions do not account for the cost or feasibility of low-level execution. The objective is to design a novel interleaved mechanism where feedback from low-level geometric reasoning can inform and bias high-level semantic decisions, iteratively refining the global plan.

In summary, the research is motivated by the limitations of current object search methods in handling the scale, uncertainty, and complexity of real-world household environments, with the explicit aim of developing a POMDP planning algorithm that achieves a principled balance between planning quality and efficiency through a hierarchical and interactively informed approach.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose Inter-POMDP, a novel interleaved POMDP planning algorithm designed to address multi-object search in unknown, multi-room household environments. The methodology is structured around a two-level planning hierarchy with principled communication between the levels.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The environment includes known furniture items, unknown target objects, unknown landmark objects (contextual cues), and unknown obstacles on the floor. The robot possesses a 2D occupancy grid map ($M_o$) encoding walls and furniture, but target objects, landmark objects, and obstacles are initially unknown and discovered incrementally.
- **State**: The joint state $s_t = \{s_r_t, S_{O_{tar}}, S_{O_{obj}}, S_{O_{obs}}\}$ includes the robot's known position and orientation ($s_r_t$) and the unknown grid coordinates and semantic attributes of objects and obstacles.
- **Belief**: The robot maintains a belief $b_t(s_t) = Pr(s_t | h_t)$ over the joint state.
- **Hierarchical Decomposition**:
 - **High-Level Action Space ($A_h$)**: Abstract actions: Navigate (move between regions), Search (move locally), Sense (object detection), Declare (terminate mission).
 - **Low-Level Action Space ($A_l$)**: Primitive actions (move ahead, turn left, turn right) for Navigate/Search, and camera operation for Sense.
- **Transition Model ($T$)**: Navigate actions involve trajectory-level uncertainty due to unknown obstacles.
- **Observation Model ($H$)**: Upon Sense, $z_t$ includes detected states within the robot's fan-shaped camera FOV.
- **Reward Model ($R$)**: Rewards for successful/failed mission termination, costs for navigation steps and collisions.
- **Object Relations**: Semantic priors are defined as object-object ($C_{oo}$) and object-furniture ($C_{of}$) conditional probabilities, guiding the high-level planner.
- **Enhanced Maps**:
 - **Semantic Map ($M_s$)**: Abstracts grid cells to furniture, incrementally built online using object relations, informing high-level search.
 - **Topological Map ($M_p = \langle N, E, W \rangle$)**: A sparse graph of reachable positions. Nodes ($N$) are sampled from object/obstacle beliefs, edges ($E$) ensure connectivity, and weights ($W$) encode navigation costs influenced by unknown obstacles, updated online for low-level planning.

#### 3.2. Structural Planning Hierarchy

The core strategy is to decompose the intractable joint POMDP into two interacting levels to mitigate the curse of dimensionality:
1. **High-Level POUCT for Generalized Object Localization**:
 - **Purpose**: Operates on the semantic map ($M_s$) to identify promising search locations by selecting which furniture or region to visit next.
 - **LLM Relation Inference**: Instead of pre-modeling, Large Language Models (LLMs), specifically GPT-4o, infer object-object ($C_{oo}$) and object-furniture ($C_{of}$) relations using structured prompts. This leverages commonsense knowledge and incorporates semantic attributes (location, category, usage). A memory map ($M_m$) is maintained to accumulate environment-specific knowledge.
 - **Histogram Belief Representation**: The high-level belief is represented as a histogram over discretized furniture-level locations, allowing for Bayesian inference. Two beliefs are maintained: a primary belief ($b$) from POMDP observations and a refined belief ($b'$) conditioned on the LLM-inferred semantic relations. These relations help narrow the search region as landmark objects are discovered.
 - **Action Selection**: The high-level POUCT selects semantic subtasks based on $M_s$, $b'$, and the relation models.
2. **Low-Level Motion Planner for Uncertainty-aware Navigation**:
 - **Purpose**: Executes the high-level subtasks by determining safe navigation paths, reasoning over fine-grained geometric uncertainties from unknown obstacles.
 - **Particle Belief Representation**: A POMCP planner maintains a particle belief over obstacle states ($s_{obs_k}$). This belief is updated via a low-level transition model ($T_l$) and observation model, with each particle representing a hypothesis about local obstacle configurations, enabling collision risk assessment.
 - **Topological Map Construction**: The planner constructs and refines the topological map ($M_p$). Nodes are reachable positions sampled from beliefs over targets and obstacles, and edge weights reflect navigation costs influenced by discovered and hypothesized obstacles.
 - **Navigation Cost as Domain Knowledge**: As the robot explores, it accumulates planning experience (traversals, observations, collisions), yielding increasingly reliable navigation cost estimates. These costs form the domain knowledge transmitted to the high-level planner.

#### 3.3. Interleaved Communication with Domain Knowledge

The key innovation is the interleaved mechanism, which allows bi-directional information flow between the planners:
1. **High-level to Low-level**: The high-level POUCT issues semantic subtasks (e.g., "Navigate to kitchen table") to the low-level motion planner, narrowing its geometric search space.
2. **Low-level to High-level (Domain Knowledge Propagation)**: Low-level navigation costs (computed via A* path planning on $M_o$ and refined topological map $M_p$) are propagated back to the high-level POUCT.
3. **Domain Knowledge Augmented UCT**: In the high-level POUCT, the standard UCT (Upper Confidence Bound 1 applied to Trees) action value $Q(s, a)$ is not initialized to zero. Instead, it is initialized with $Q_{init}(s, a)$, which encodes the accumulated navigation costs from the low-level geometric reasoning. This biases the high-level tree search towards states that are both semantically promising and geometrically feasible, accelerating convergence to high-quality plans without altering MCTS asymptotic guarantees. The rollout policy also incorporates this domain knowledge for informed action selection.

This iterative co-refinement process ensures that the overall policy optimally hedges against both semantic uncertainty in object locations and geometric uncertainty in navigation, leading to more robust and efficient planning than purely sequential or non-interacting hierarchical approaches.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The Inter-POMDP algorithm was evaluated against two baseline methods, CSG-TL and COSPOMDP, through both simulation and real-world experiments. The evaluation focused on three grounded metrics: collision counts, navigation steps, and detection counts.

#### 4.1. Experimental Setup

- **Simulation Environment**: ProcTHOR scenes (train 1, train 8, train 13), each featuring 8-12 rooms with numerous objects and furniture. A mobile robot with an onboard camera was used.
- **Real-World Environment**: A RealMan RMC-DA 7DOF dual-arm mobile robot equipped with an STM32 chassis and Nvidia Jetson AGX Orin compute platform, deployed in an 8.71m × 7.22m multi-room environment. YOLO11 was used for object detection.
- **LLM Integration**: GPT-4o was utilized for LLM relation inference, providing semantic priors.
- **Relation Model Training**: A GATv2Conv backbone was employed for training the relation model, using a dataset of 1500 ProcTHOR object/furniture relations.
- **Trials**: 15 trials were conducted across the three ProcTHOR scenes, with each trial requiring the robot to find 3 target objects. Results were reported as mean ± standard deviation across 5 trials per mission.

#### 4.2. Simulation Results

The simulation experiments demonstrated that Inter-POMDP consistently outperformed the baselines across all evaluated metrics.
- **Collision Avoidance**: Inter-POMDP achieved a significant reduction in collision counts. It consistently reached zero collisions for the second and third objects across all scenes, exhibiting a clear decreasing pattern (1 → 0 → 0). In contrast, baselines showed higher collision counts without a systematic reduction. The method reduced collision counts by up to 63% compared to baselines. This improvement was attributed to the low-level particle-based beliefs for geometric nuance capture and the interleaved feedback mechanism, enabling the robot to incrementally build accurate geometric understanding.
- **Navigation Efficiency**: While initial navigation steps for the first object were comparable across methods, Inter-POMDP showed substantial improvements for subsequent objects. For instance, in "train 1," Inter-POMDP found the third object in 12 ± 1 steps, compared to 34 ± 3 for CSG-TL and 26 ± 2 for COSPOMDP. In "train 13," the contrast was more pronounced, with Inter-POMDP requiring 14 ± 1 steps versus 80 ± 2 (CSG-TL) and 166 ± 5 (COSPOMDP), representing reductions of over 90% and 80% respectively. Overall, Inter-POMDP achieved the lowest aggregated navigation steps, reducing them by up to 35% compared to baselines. The superior performance was attributed to the interleaved mechanism, which translates low-level navigation knowledge into more informed high-level subtask selections. Baselines' limitations included CSG-TL's reliance on off-the-shelf navigation and room-level abstraction, and COSPOMDP's less generalizable correlation model and lack of low-level feedback for fine-grained planning.
- **Sensing Economy**: Inter-POMDP consistently required fewer detection activations. By the third object, it typically needed only a single detection across all scenes, while CSG-TL required 2, and COSPOMDP ranged from 1 to 4 detections. This indicates that Inter-POMDP guided the robot to more precisely targeted locations, reducing unnecessary exploratory sensing, which is beneficial for real-world deployments. The method reduced detection counts by up to 32% compared to baselines.
- **Cross-Scene Generalizability**: Inter-POMDP demonstrated consistent performance across the diverse ProcTHOR scenes, leveraging LLMs for stable semantic reasoning and the interleaved geometric feedback. Baselines, particularly those with learned correlation models, exhibited higher variance in performance across different environments.

#### 4.3. Real-World Experiments

A real-world deployment on a mobile manipulator tasked with finding breakfast items (cup, apple, fork) in an unknown multi-room environment confirmed the practical applicability of Inter-POMDP. The robot successfully maintained its dual-layer mapping process, with the high-level POUCT updating the semantic map ($M_s$) and the low-level motion planner updating the topological map ($M_p$) from LiDAR observations. The simultaneous update of histogram and particle beliefs, coupled with the propagation of navigation costs from the low-level to the high-level planner, enabled efficient target localization and robust navigation in a physical setting.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of Inter-POMDP presents several significant contributions to the fields of robotics and artificial intelligence, with potential impacts on the deployment of autonomous systems in complex, uncertain environments.

- **Addressing the POMDP Intractability Challenge**: The research offers a method to manage the computational intractability of POMDPs in large-scale, partially observable, multi-object search problems. By effectively decomposing the problem into interacting high-level semantic reasoning and low-level geometric navigation, the framework enables planning that would otherwise be infeasible for traditional POMDP solvers.
- **Enhanced Performance in Robotic Search**: The demonstrated reductions in collision counts (up to 63%), navigation steps (up to 35%), and detection counts (up to 32%) signify a substantial improvement in the safety, efficiency, and resource utilization of robotic search missions. This directly translates to more reliable and practical autonomous household robots capable of operating in real-world conditions.
- **Principled Integration of Semantic and Geometric Information**: The interleaved planning mechanism, which allows low-level navigation costs to inform high-level semantic decisions, represents a notable advancement over prior hierarchical methods that often lack such bi-directional communication. This principled integration leads to globally more informed plans, as the high-level planner considers the practical execution costs associated with its semantic choices.
- **Robustness and Generalizability through LLM Integration**: The incorporation of LLMs for inferring semantic object and furniture relations provides a powerful mechanism for leveraging commonsense knowledge as search priors. Combined with the adaptive geometric reasoning, this approach contributes to the system's robustness and generalizability across diverse, unknown environments without requiring extensive scene-specific tuning or pre-training. This is particularly impactful for deployment in varied and unstructured household settings.
- **Advancement in Embodied AI for Household Robotics**: The work contributes to the development of more capable and autonomous embodied AI agents. By enabling robots to effectively search for multiple objects in complex, unknown environments while mitigating uncertainty and avoiding obstacles, it moves closer to realizing truly intelligent household assistants.
- **Foundation for Future Research**: The paper outlines several promising avenues for future work, including extending the framework to 3D environments, integrating object manipulation capabilities in cluttered workspaces, and modeling continuous actions using advanced POMDP techniques like POMCPOW with learned samplers. These directions suggest that Inter-POMDP could serve as a foundational step for more sophisticated and comprehensive robotic task execution in domestic and similar environments.

In summary, this research provides a robust and efficient solution for a challenging problem in robotics, pushing the boundaries of what is achievable in partially observable, multi-object search, and laying groundwork for more capable autonomous systems.
