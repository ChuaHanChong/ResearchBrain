# Self-Corrective Task Planning by Inverse Prompting with Large Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> InversePrompt, developed by researchers at Chung-Ang University and Korea University, introduces a self-corrective task planning approach that enhances the reliability and interpretability of Large Language Model (LLM)-generated robot task plans. This method utilizes a novel 'inverse prompting' strategy to enable LLMs to identify and rectify their own errors through a multi-step, logic-grounded validation process, achieving up to 30% higher success rates in benchmark scenarios and enabling more robust real-world robot task completion.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) often generate plausible-sounding but inaccurate or infeasible task plans for robots, which can lead to unsafe operations or task failures.
- Existing external validation methods for LLM-generated robot plans are limited by reliance on predefined rules, requiring extensive human effort, or incurring high computational costs.
- Prior LLM-based self-correction methods for robot planning often suffer from 'naïve' or 'single-step' reasoning, providing vague feedback that fails to guide effective re-planning for complex tasks.

## Method

- InversePrompt introduces an iterative self-correction framework where an LLM generates an initial task plan and then validates each step in a loop.
- The core innovation is 'inverse prompting,' where the LLM is prompted to generate the inverse action for a given step, apply it to the resulting state, and compare the outcome to the original state.
- If a discrepancy is found between the inverted state and the original state, the difference is explicitly used as detailed, multi-step feedback to guide the LLM's re-planning process.

## Results

- InversePrompt consistently achieved higher success rates in benchmark environments, averaging **78%** with GPT-4o-mini and **89%** with Gemini-**1.5**-Flash, representing up to a **30%** improvement over baseline 'No Validator' methods.
- The method demonstrated higher accuracy in the self-correction process, with up to **88%** true positives and true negatives for Gemini-**1.5**-Flash, indicating more reliable error detection compared to standard self-correction.
- Successfully validated in real-world Blocksworld and Cooking scenarios with a Franka Emika Panda robot arm, enabling the robot to autonomously correct infeasible plans and complete tasks where other self-correction methods failed due to vague feedback or undetected errors.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Verifying an action's logical consistency by applying its inverse and checking if the original state is restored proves effective for detecting planning errors.
> - Explicitly quantifying the difference between the inverted state and the original state provides precise, grounded, and actionable feedback for the Large Language Model during re-planning.
> - A multi-step, structured reasoning process, like inverse prompting, significantly improves the LLM's ability to generate accurate and interpretable self-correction feedback.
