# Learning a Unified Policy for Position and Force Control in Legged Loco-Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at BIGAI and UniTree Robotics developed a unified policy for legged robots to control both position and contact force without relying on external force sensors. This policy enhanced imitation learning by generating force-aware data, leading to a 39.5% improvement in success rates for contact-rich manipulation tasks on quadrupedal and humanoid robots.

## Problem

- Legged robots struggle with complex, contact-rich loco-manipulation due to their intricate kinematics and the need for precise force control.
- The absence of direct force-sensing hardware on many legged robots limits their capacity for compliant and nuanced physical interaction.
- Traditional imitation learning methods rely on datasets lacking contact force information, hindering performance in tasks requiring physical interaction.

## Method

- A unified force-position control policy is developed using reinforcement learning in simulation, leveraging an ==impedance control== formulation for both end-effector and base.
- A learned state estimator is integrated into the policy to predict external contact forces from proprioceptive states, thus eliminating the need for physical force sensors.
- The learned policy is used to create a novel "==force-aware==" data collection pipeline, enriching imitation learning datasets with estimated contact forces for improved downstream performance.

## Results

- The unified policy accurately tracks end-effector position (error within **0.1m** in simulation) and applies commanded forces (average error within **10N** in real-world experiments).
- Force-aware imitation learning achieved approximately **39.5%** higher success rates on challenging real-world contact-rich tasks like wiping and cabinet opening compared to position-only baselines.
- The policy demonstrated strong generalizability, supporting diverse manipulation behaviors and performing effectively on both quadrupedal (Unitree B2-Z1) and humanoid (Unitree G1) robot platforms.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A single learned policy can effectively manage both position and force control for legged robots, enabling a wide range of compliant and interactive behaviors without explicit force sensors.
> - Contact forces can be robustly estimated from a robot's proprioceptive states alone, offering a hardware-agnostic solution for force awareness in legged loco-manipulation.
> - Augmenting imitation learning datasets with estimated contact force information significantly boosts the success rate of policies in complex, contact-rich real-world manipulation tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics, especially for legged systems, has seen rapid advancements in locomotion and manipulation. However, the integration of these two capabilities, termed "loco-manipulation," presents significant challenges, particularly when robots interact physically with their environment. This paper addresses several key gaps and trends in this evolving landscape:

- **Bridging the Gap Between Locomotion and Manipulation:** Traditional robotics often separates locomotion (movement) from manipulation (object interaction). Recent works, particularly those leveraging reinforcement learning (RL) and whole-body control (WBC), have begun to integrate these for more complex tasks. This paper contributes directly to this trend by developing a unified policy that seamlessly manages both, enhancing the robot's ability to navigate and interact simultaneously.

- **Addressing Contact-Rich Interactions:** Many real-world manipulation tasks involve contact with the environment (e.g., pushing, wiping, opening doors). Accurately modeling and controlling contact forces is crucial for these tasks, ensuring both effective task execution and robot safety (e.g., avoiding damage to the robot or environment, applying appropriate force). Current visuomotor policies often focus on position control, limiting their effectiveness in such scenarios. This work directly tackles this by explicitly integrating force control capabilities.

- **Overcoming the Limitation of Force Sensors:** A significant bottleneck in force-controlled robotics is the reliance on expensive and fragile force-sensing hardware (e.g., force-torque sensors at end-effectors). This paper presents a novel approach by *learning to estimate contact forces from proprioceptive states*, effectively eliminating the need for external force sensors. This aligns with a broader trend in robotics to achieve sophisticated control with simpler, more robust, and cost-effective hardware. Previous works have explored indirect force estimation, but this paper's RL-based approach for unified force-position control in legged robots, *without* force sensors, is a significant advancement.

- **Enhancing Imitation Learning (IL):** Imitation learning is a powerful paradigm for teaching robots complex skills from demonstrations. However, existing large-scale datasets for IL often consist solely of robot trajectories (position commands), lacking critical contact force information. This limitation becomes apparent in contact-rich tasks where visual cues alone may be insufficient. This paper leverages its learned force-estimation policy to create "force-aware" demonstration data, which then significantly improves the performance of downstream imitation learning policies. This highlights a crucial direction for making IL more effective for complex, physical interaction tasks. This contribution aligns with recent efforts to enrich robot learning datasets with more diverse modalities (e.g., tactile sensing), but uniquely provides estimated force information without requiring additional hardware.

- **Generalizability Across Embodiments:** The validation of the proposed policy on both quadrupedal and humanoid robots (Unitree B2-Z1 and G1) demonstrates a commitment to generalizable solutions. This is a critical aspect of modern robot learning, moving beyond single-robot, single-task solutions towards more versatile and adaptable agents. The ability to generalize across different robot morphologies, especially those with vastly different kinematics and control challenges, speaks to the robustness of the learning framework.

In essence, this work integrates advancements in reinforcement learning, whole-body control, and imitation learning to address a fundamental challenge in robotic interaction: achieving robust, compliant, and force-aware loco-manipulation without relying on dedicated force-sensing hardware. It pushes the boundaries of what learned policies can achieve in complex, contact-rich environments and paves the way for more practical and adaptable robotic systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this research stems from the inherent difficulties in controlling legged robots, especially in scenarios requiring physical interaction with the environment. Loco-manipulation tasks are often contact-rich, demanding a precise interplay between robot position and applied forces. The authors identify several critical gaps and needs:

1. **Complexity of Legged Manipulators:** Legged robots possess complex kinematic structures, making their control inherently challenging. This complexity is compounded when manipulation tasks require interaction with the environment.
2. **Absence of Force Sensing:** Many legged robots lack direct force-sensing hardware (e.g., force-torque sensors at end-effectors), which is vital for accurate modeling of contact forces and achieving compliant behaviors. This absence severely limits their applicability in tasks that require nuanced physical interaction.
3. **Limitations of Existing Visuomotor Policies:** Current learning-based policies for loco-manipulation often focus primarily on position control, overlooking the crucial aspect of force control. This "position-only" paradigm restricts their effectiveness in contact-rich tasks where force feedback is indispensable.
4. **Deficiencies in Imitation Learning Datasets:** Trajectory-based imitation learning, while powerful, often relies on datasets that record only robot movements, omitting critical contact information. This lack of force awareness leads to suboptimal performance in contact-rich tasks.

In light of these challenges, the authors set out to achieve the following key objectives:

1. **Develop a Unified Force-Position Control Policy:** The overarching goal is to propose the *first unified policy* for legged robots that can jointly model and control both contact force and robot position. This policy aims to operate effectively *without* relying on explicit force sensors.
2. **Enable Diverse Loco-Manipulation Behaviors:** The learned policy should be versatile enough to support a wide spectrum of manipulation behaviors under varying force and position inputs. This includes fundamental capabilities such as position tracking, direct force application, force tracking, and compliant interactions with the environment.
3. **Enhance Imitation Learning with Force-Aware Data:** A significant objective is to leverage the developed unified policy to create a novel "force-aware" data collection pipeline for imitation learning. By incorporating estimated contact forces into demonstration data, the goal is to significantly improve the performance of downstream imitation learning policies in contact-rich manipulation tasks.
4. **Validate Cross-Embodiment Generalizability:** To demonstrate the robustness and adaptability of their approach, the authors aim to validate the learned policy on different legged robot platforms, specifically a quadrupedal manipulator (Unitree B2-Z1) and a humanoid robot (Unitree G1), across a diverse range of challenging scenarios.

In essence, the research is motivated by the need for more intelligent, adaptable, and robust legged robots capable of seamless and compliant physical interaction, even without specialized force-sensing hardware, and aims to address this need through an innovative reinforcement learning framework and its application to data collection for imitation learning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of this research revolves around learning a unified policy for force and position control using reinforcement learning (RL) in simulation, followed by its application to enhance imitation learning in real-world scenarios.

#### 3.1. Unified Formulation for Force and Position Control

The authors build their control framework on an impedance control formulation:
$F = K(x - x_{des}) + D(\dot{x} - \dot{x}_{des}) + M(\ddot{x} - \ddot{x}_{des})$
For manipulation tasks, especially for the end-effector (EE), they simplify this to a static model: $F = K(x - x_{des})$, given that EE movements are typically slow. The net force F on the end-effector is decomposed into the active force command ($F_{cmd}$), the passive reaction force from the environment ($F_{react}$), and external disturbances ($F_{ext}$). This leads to the desired target position $x_{target}$ for the end-effector:
$x_{target} = x_{cmd} + \frac{F_{ext} + (F_{cmd} - F_{react})}{K}$
This formulation allows the policy to derive various control behaviors:
- **Position Control:** When no external forces or commands, $x_{target} = x_{cmd}$.
- **Force Control:** End-effector moves until $F_{react}$ matches $F_{cmd}$, driven by the force compensation term.
- **Impedance Control:** Robot adjusts position compliantly to external forces, tracking $x_{cmd} + \frac{F_{ext}}{K}$.
- **Hybrid Control:** Combines position tracking in one direction with force control in another.

For other robot body parts, specifically the robot base, the formulation is adapted for velocity control due to its focus on locomotion rather than precise position. Given base velocity ($v^{cmd}_{base}$) and force commands ($F^{cmd}_{base}$), along with external disturbances ($F^{ext}_{base}$), the target base velocity is derived:
$v^{target}_{base} = v^{cmd}_{base} + \frac{F^{ext}_{base} + (F^{cmd}_{base} - F^{react}_{base})}{D}$
This allows for compliant locomotion where base velocity compensates for external forces. The paper initially treats end-effector and base independently, leaving integrated multi-contact derivation for future work.

#### 3.2. Learning a Unified Force-Position Control Policy

The policy is trained using **Reinforcement Learning (RL)** in the **Isaac Gym** parallel simulator.
- **Observations ($o_t$):** Includes robot's base orientation and angular velocity, joint positions and velocities, previous action, feet clock timings, and crucially, a **command vector ($c^{cmd}_t$)**. This command vector is comprehensive, encompassing base velocity, end-effector position, end-effector force, and base force commands.
- **Actions ($a_t$):** Residuals added to a predefined default pose, which are then converted into joint position targets for a low-level PD controller.
- **Policy Architecture:** Comprises three modules:
 1. **Observation Encoder:** Processes a history of observations ($H=32$ time steps) to produce a latent feature.
 2. **State Estimator:** Takes the latent feature and predicts the robot's current state, including the *external force* ($F = F_{ext} + F_{react}$), end-effector position, and base velocity. This module is critical as it enables force awareness without direct sensors.
 3. **Actor:** Generates the control action $a_t$.
- **Force Simulation Strategy:** To learn robust force-position control, the policy is exposed to diverse scenarios. This is achieved by randomly sampling various combinations of position, velocity, and force commands, as well as external disturbance forces. The sampled forces are applied in a structured manner: linearly ramped up, held constant, and then ramped down, creating varied contact conditions.
- **Two-Stage Training Procedure:**
 1. **Stage 1:** Focuses on fundamental whole-body reaching and locomotion skills.
 2. **Stage 2:** Introduces random force commands and external disturbances to train the policy for contact-rich interactions. This staged approach was empirically found to be more stable.
- **Policy Learning Rewards:** The policy is supervised by a reward function that incentivizes accurate tracking of the target end-effector position ($x^{target}_{ee}$) and base velocity ($v^{target}_{base}$), calculated using the unified formulations (Eqs. 2 & 4). An **MSE loss** is also applied to improve the accuracy of the state estimator's predictions for robot state and external force. Additional rewards for safety, smoothness, and gait stability are included.
- **Domain Randomization:** Extensive domain randomization (e.g., varying friction, body mass, CoM, motor strength, payload, external pushes) is applied during training to improve sim-to-real transfer and robustness.

#### 3.3. Force-aware Imitation Learning

This learned unified policy is then leveraged to address a critical limitation in existing imitation learning pipelines: the lack of contact force information.
- **Data Collection Pipeline:** The learned force-position policy acts as the base teleoperation policy. During human teleoperation, not only are position and force commands passed to the robot, but the policy's embedded **contact force estimator** is used to *simultaneously collect estimated end-effector contact forces* alongside joint states, base states, control commands, and RGB images from onboard cameras.
- **Training a Diffusion-Based IL Policy:** The collected force-aware data is then used to train a high-level diffusion-based imitation learning policy. This IL policy takes robot states, *estimated forces*, and image observations as input. Its output consists of both force and end-effector position commands, which are then fed to the low-level learned force-position policy.
- **Key Advantage:** By incorporating the estimated force, the IL policy gains crucial contact information, enabling more accurate and robust object interaction and force application compared to traditional vision-only or position-only imitation learning approaches.

This comprehensive methodology, spanning advanced RL training in simulation to novel data collection for real-world imitation learning, provides a robust framework for contact-rich loco-manipulation tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several compelling findings validating the effectiveness and versatility of the proposed unified force-position policy across simulation and real-world experiments on different robot embodiments.

#### 4.1. Force and Position Command Tracking

- **Position Tracking (Simulation):** When only position commands are given, the end-effector tracking error remains mostly within 0.1m, with the state estimator's position estimation error being even lower, within 0.05m. This demonstrates the policy's proficiency in basic position control.
- **Force Control (Simulation & Real World):**
 - In simulation, when the policy receives force commands matching applied external forces, position tracking error slightly increases but stays within 0.1m, indicating effective force-aware behavior and compensation.
 - In real-world experiments, the robot demonstrates the ability to apply commanded forces ranging from 0 N to 60 N. Measurements with a dynamometer show average errors within 10 N across five different end-effector positions. Force estimation also shows errors between 5-10 N. While minor sim-to-real discrepancies are noted, particularly along the Y-axis, the overall accuracy is deemed sufficient for the targeted manipulation tasks.

#### 4.2. Force-aware Imitation Learning Performance

This is a critical contribution, highlighting the practical utility of the learned force estimator. The policy was evaluated on four challenging real-world tasks requiring hybrid force-position control and force sensing:
- **Tasks:** Wipe-blackboard, open-cabinet, close-cabinet, and open-drawer-occlusion. These tasks were chosen specifically for their contact-rich nature and the need for accurate force application.
- **Significant Improvement:** The proposed force-aware imitation learning approach achieved **approximately ~39.5% higher success rates** compared to a baseline policy that relied solely on position control and lacked force information.
- **Task-Specific Insights:**
 - **Wipe-blackboard:** The force-aware policy successfully maintained consistent contact pressure, preventing insufficient wiping or excessive force that could damage the surface or robot. The position-only baseline often failed due to unstable contact.
 - **Open/Close-cabinet:** These tasks involved a push-to-open mechanism with a very narrow 3mm stroke, difficult for vision alone to detect. The force estimator accurately sensed the required contact force, enabling reliable activation and significantly improving success rates.
 - **Open-drawer-occlusion:** This task involved visual occlusion during interaction. The baseline policy, relying solely on visual cues, saw its success rate drop to 0.3. In contrast, the force-aware method leveraged force sensing to detect contact under occlusion, boosting the success rate to 0.76 and underscoring the critical role of force estimation when visual information is compromised.

#### 4.3. Diverse Basic Manipulation Policies

The unified policy inherently supports various control behaviors without additional training:
- **Force Control:** Demonstrated by the robot successfully counteracting gravity to support a 2.5kg dumbbell using a 25N upward force command. Without the command, the end-effector drops.
- **Force Tracking:** The end-effector tracks a zero-force command, meaning it moves in response to external forces and remains in the displaced position after the force is removed, instead of returning to an original target.
- **Impedance Control:** The robot exhibited compliant behavior in response to external disturbances (e.g., human-robot tug-of-war and arm-wrestling), adjusting its whole-body posture and resisting deviations from a target position proportional to the displacement.

#### 4.4. Cross-Embodiment Performance

The policy's versatility was validated on two distinct robot platforms:
- **Unitree B2-Z1 (quadrupedal manipulator) and Unitree G1 (humanoid robot):** This demonstrates the generalizability of the learning framework.
- **Locomotion with Force Compensation:** For locomotion tasks, the robot base velocity is adjusted to compensate for external forces. The humanoid robot, for instance, could halt and lean to maintain balance when the compensated velocity opposed its command. Similarly, the quadruped robot could walk forward when kicked, even with zero force and velocity commands, showcasing robust compliant locomotion.

In summary, the results convincingly demonstrate that the unified force-position policy, learned without direct force sensors, is highly effective in diverse contact-rich loco-manipulation tasks, capable of tracking both position and force commands, enabling complex compliant behaviors, and significantly enhancing imitation learning performance in challenging real-world scenarios across different robot types.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research marks a significant advancement in the field of embodied AI and robotics, particularly for legged systems performing complex interactions. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Pioneering Unified Force-Position Control Without Sensors:** The most notable contribution is the development of the *first unified policy* for legged robots that simultaneously models and controls both force and position, *critically, without requiring external force-sensing hardware*. This is a paradigm shift, as it enables robots to achieve sophisticated, contact-rich behaviors using only proprioceptive information and learned estimation, reducing hardware complexity and cost.
- **Enhanced Robot Autonomy in Contact-Rich Environments:** By integrating force awareness, the policy empowers legged robots to perform tasks that were previously challenging or impossible for position-only controllers. This includes compliant interactions, precise force application, and robust handling of unexpected contacts, making robots more capable and reliable in unstructured, real-world environments. Tasks like wiping, opening tricky mechanisms, and interacting under occlusion become feasible and more robust.
- **Advancement in Imitation Learning (IL) Data Collection:** The proposed "force-aware" data collection pipeline is a crucial innovation for the IL community. By leveraging the learned force estimator during teleoperation, it enriches demonstration datasets with vital contact information that is typically absent. This directly addresses a major limitation of current trajectory-only datasets, leading to significantly improved performance in contact-rich IL tasks. This could accelerate the development of more generalist robot policies for complex physical interactions.
- **Improved Sim-to-Real Transfer and Generalizability:** The robust training methodology, including extensive domain randomization and a two-stage curriculum, contributes to the policy's successful transfer from simulation to diverse real-world robot platforms (quadruped and humanoid). This cross-embodiment validation highlights the general applicability of the framework, paving the way for more versatile and adaptable robotic solutions.
- **Foundation for Future Complex Skills:** The unified policy provides a strong foundation for developing even more advanced loco-manipulation skills. The ability to estimate and control forces opens up avenues for dynamic tasks, multi-contact interactions, and more nuanced human-robot collaboration where compliant and force-sensitive behaviors are paramount. The acknowledged limitations, such as improving high-frequency force estimation and extending to multi-point force control, clearly outline exciting future research directions built upon this work.
- **Democratization of Advanced Robotics:** By reducing the reliance on specialized and often expensive force sensors, this research makes advanced force-controlled manipulation more accessible to a broader range of robotic platforms and researchers. This could accelerate innovation in areas like home robotics, logistics, and hazardous environment exploration where cost and robustness are key considerations.

In conclusion, this research is highly significant for pushing the boundaries of learned control for legged robots. It provides a robust, sensor-agnostic solution for unified force and position control, which not only enhances current robot capabilities but also offers a powerful tool for accelerating the development of future, more intelligent and interactive robotic systems through force-aware imitation learning.
