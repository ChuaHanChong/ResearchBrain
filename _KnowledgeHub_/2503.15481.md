# Learning to Play Piano in the Real World

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from TU Dresden's LASR Lab present the first learning-based system capable of playing the piano using a real-world dexterous robotic hand. This work employs an iterative Sim2Real2Sim workflow combined with domain randomization to transfer policies trained in simulation, achieving an average F1-score of 0.881 on five simple songs during real-world execution.

## Problem

- The challenge of transferring learned dexterous manipulation policies from simulation to physical robots due to the "Sim2Real gap."
- Previous robotic piano playing efforts either used hand-designed controllers for real robots or confined learning-based approaches to simulation.
- Developing robots capable of human-level manipulation involving precise, adaptable, and contact-rich hand movements for complex tasks.

## Method

- A robotic system comprising a Ufactory xArm7 and an ==Allegro Hand== with custom 3D-printed fingertips, operating on an M-Audio MIDI keyboard.
- An iterative Sim2Real2Sim workflow that refines simulation parameters using real-world data to minimize discrepancies before policy training and deployment.
- Incorporation of domain randomization during simulation training to enhance policy robustness against variations and reduce the Sim2Real gap.

## Results

- The system achieved an average F1-score of **0.881** when playing five simple songs (e.g., "Happy Birthday," "Ode To Joy") on a real piano, with individual song F1-scores ranging from **0.821** to **0.919**.
- Policies trained with simulated key fences exhibited a smaller Sim2Real gap (**6.8%**) and better real-world performance (F1-score of **0.881**) compared to policies trained without fences (**11.2%** gap, F1-score of **0.840**).
- In simulation, policies achieved an average F1-score of **0.946**, demonstrating the agent's ability to learn the songs within the optimized virtual environment.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The inclusion of narrow "fences" between piano keys in simulation environments improves training efficiency and reduces the Sim2Real gap by making the simulated physics more closely resemble real-world contact constraints.
> - A specific fingertip collision penalty in the reward function accelerates the learning process by guiding the agent towards physically plausible interactions, though it does not alter the final learned strategy.
> - The combination of iterative Sim2Real2Sim and domain randomization is an effective strategy for successfully deploying complex, learned dexterous skills, like piano playing, from simulation onto physical robots.
