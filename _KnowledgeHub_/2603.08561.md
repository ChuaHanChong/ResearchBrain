# RetroAgent: From Solving to Evolving via Retrospective Dual Intrinsic Feedback

> [!summary] Summary
> RETROAGENT is an online reinforcement learning framework that enables large language model agents to continuously adapt and evolve by introducing a retrospective dual intrinsic feedback mechanism. It achieves new state-of-the-art performance across four agentic benchmarks, demonstrating faster convergence and improved test-time adaptation compared to existing methods.

## Problem

- Large Language Model (LLM) agents trained with standard Reinforcement Learning often converge to suboptimal policies due to over-exploitation of early successes and limited exploration.
- Accumulated experience during interactions is typically stored implicitly within LLM parameters, hindering explicit retrieval and reuse for guiding future decisions.
- Existing methods for improving exploration or explicit experience reuse are largely separate, lacking a cohesive framework for continuous adaptation.

## Method

- The **RETROAGENT** framework introduces a ==hindsight self-reflection== mechanism that generates ==dual intrinsic feedback== after each episode: numerical and language-based.
- ==Intrinsic numerical feedback== (capability-evolution reward) quantifies incremental subtask progress relative to historical baselines, encouraging beneficial exploration.
- ==Intrinsic language feedback== distills actionable lessons from trajectories into a memory buffer, retrieved using a ==Similarity & Utility-Aware UCB (SimUtil-UCB)== strategy balancing semantic relevance, historical utility, and exploration coverage.

## Results

- **RETROAGENT** achieved new state-of-the-art performance across ALFWorld (**+18.3%**), WebShop (**+15.4%**), Sokoban (**+27.1%**), and MineSweeper (**+8.9%**) over the GRPO baseline.
- The framework demonstrated rapid test-time adaptation and strong out-of-distribution generalization, maintaining robustness on tasks exceeding training difficulty.
- It showed accelerated training convergence, reaching baseline peak performance **46%** (in-context) and **32%** (RL-trained) faster, while successfully generalizing across different model architectures and scales.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating dual intrinsic feedback (numerical for exploration, language for experience reuse) significantly outperforms single feedback types, demonstrating their complementary nature for a comprehensive learning signal.
> - A self-reflection capability, particularly when jointly optimized with the decision-making policy, enhances an LLM agent's ability to assess progress and distill high-quality, actionable lessons.
> - A balanced memory retrieval strategy that actively explores under-utilized but potentially valuable lessons, while considering semantic similarity and historical utility, is crucial for effective experience reuse without hindering diverse exploration.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of large language model (LLM) agents for complex interactive environments, often trained using reinforcement learning (RL), constitutes a significant area of current research. Existing RL frameworks for LLM agents primarily focus on optimizing extrinsic task-success rewards, which can lead to agents that excel at one-off task completion but may exhibit limitations in continuous adaptation across multiple interactions. This success-driven optimization paradigm presents two primary issues:
1. **Premature Convergence:** Agents may over-exploit early successes, potentially converging to suboptimal policies rather than exploring alternative, more robust strategies.
2. **Implicit Experience Storage:** Useful experience gained from interactions is typically encoded implicitly within model parameters, hindering its explicit retrieval, inspection, and reuse for future decision-making or policy improvement. This can impede learning, particularly in scenarios with sparse or context-dependent relevant experience.

Current research efforts to address these limitations generally fall into two categories:
- **Exploration-Oriented Methods:** These approaches, such as meta-RL with cross-episode training or uncertainty-modulated rewards, aim to broaden the search space. However, they typically do not explicitly preserve or reuse experience, limiting the long-term role of past interactions in policy evolution.
- **Memory-Augmented Methods:** These methods involve storing raw trajectories, distilled skills, rules, or lessons in memory buffers. While they facilitate access to past information, memory is often used as passive context augmentation. Retrieval is frequently governed by fixed similarity metrics, independent of the downstream utility of the retrieved information, and remains decoupled from the core policy optimization. This can lead to brittleness if memory is noisy or unavailable, and over-reliance on retrieved experience may suppress further exploration.

RetroAgent is positioned within this landscape as an approach that attempts to bridge the gap between these two categories. It seeks to integrate exploration with explicit experience reuse by introducing a mechanism for agents to generate intrinsic signals from their own trajectories. This aims to allow experience to guide policy evolution and be internalized over time, rather than solely relying on external memory or sparse extrinsic feedback. The work draws inspiration from human retrospective self-improvement to enable agents to evolve across episodes, thus moving beyond isolated task solving.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central question motivating this research is whether an agent can effectively couple exploration with explicit experience reuse such that past experience guides policy evolution and is gradually internalized, rather than being solely supplied by an external memory buffer. More broadly, the authors investigate if agents can exploit intrinsic signals from their own trajectories to achieve continuous evolution under sparse external supervision.

The motivation for addressing this question stems from several identified limitations of standard reinforcement learning (RL) in the context of large language model (LLM) agents:

- **Limitations of Extrinsic Reward Optimization:** Conventional RL predominantly optimizes for extrinsic task-success rewards. While effective for specific task completion, this approach often encourages agents to find a successful action sequence and terminate, rather than fostering continuous adaptation and improvement across diverse and repeated interactions. This "one-off task solving" paradigm can lead to policies that are not robust or adaptable in dynamic environments.

- **Risk of Premature Convergence to Suboptimal Policies:** By primarily optimizing for immediate success, agents may rapidly converge on early successful strategies. This can lead to an over-exploitation of known good solutions, preventing the agent from exploring alternative paths that might yield more optimal or generalized policies in the long run. The lack of incentive for continued exploration beyond initial success can trap the agent in local optima.

- **Inefficient Experience Utilization:** In existing frameworks, the valuable knowledge and lessons derived from past interactions—both successful and unsuccessful—are often stored only implicitly within the model's parameters. This implicit encoding makes it challenging for the agent to explicitly retrieve, inspect, or effectively reuse this experience in future, potentially similar, decision-making scenarios. This limitation can slow down the learning process and weaken the agent's ability to generalize to new or slightly varied contexts due to the difficulty in accessing relevant prior knowledge.

- **Incomplete Solutions from Existing Approaches:** Current efforts to overcome these issues often address only part of the problem. Exploration-focused methods broaden search but do not explicitly preserve reusable experience. Memory-augmented methods store experience but typically treat it as passive context, with retrieval mechanisms (e.g., fixed similarity metrics) that may not consider the utility of the memory for future actions or are decoupled from the policy optimization process. This can result in brittle agents or an over-reliance on external memory, hindering genuine internalization of learning.

To address these motivations, RetroAgent sets forth several key objectives:

1. **Establish an Online RL Framework for Continuous Policy Evolution:** To move beyond isolated task solving, the research aims to introduce an online RL framework where agents are trained to evolve their capabilities across episodes in complex interactive environments.
2. **Design Dual Intrinsic Feedback:** The framework seeks to augment extrinsic task-success rewards with a novel, hindsight-generated dual intrinsic feedback mechanism. This feedback is designed to provide richer, more nuanced learning signals:
 - **Intrinsic Numerical Feedback:** To encourage beneficial exploration, this feedback quantifies incremental subtask progress relative to prior attempts, rewarding promising behaviors even if they do not immediately lead to full task success.
 - **Intrinsic Language Feedback:** To facilitate efficient experiential learning, this feedback distills actionable lessons from both successful and failed trajectories into a reusable textual memory.
3. **Propose a Utility-Aware Memory Retrieval Strategy:** To effectively leverage the accumulated linguistic lessons, an advanced retrieval strategy, Similarity & Utility-Aware Upper Confidence Bound (SimUtil-UCB), is proposed. This strategy is designed to balance semantic relevance, historical utility, and an exploration bonus for under-used lessons.
4. **Demonstrate State-of-the-Art Performance and Generalization:** A primary objective is to evaluate RetroAgent's performance against strong baselines across multiple challenging agentic benchmarks to demonstrate its effectiveness in improving success rates, test-time adaptation, and out-of-distribution generalization.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RetroAgent introduces an online reinforcement learning framework that leverages a hindsight self-reflection mechanism to enhance exploration and experiential learning, particularly in sparse extrinsic reward settings.

**Problem Formulation:**
The agent-environment interaction is modeled as a Markov Decision Process (MDP). The agent's policy $\pi_\theta(\cdot | s_t)$ selects actions $a_t$ given state $s_t$, yielding reward $r_{t+1}$ and next state $s_{t+1}$. In LLM agent settings, $s_t$ is the history of observations and actions. Standard RL optimizes for expected discounted extrinsic return ($J_{Standard}(\theta)$). RetroAgent augments this with an intrinsic reward ($R_{int}$), leading to a composite objective ($J_{RetroAgent}(\theta)$). Extrinsic rewards ($R_{ext}$) are typically sparse terminal rewards, which are uniformly redistributed across time steps for credit assignment. The agent's actions are influenced by a mixture of its base policy and a memory-augmented policy, incorporating retrieved lessons.

**Hindsight Self-Reflection Mechanism:**
At the conclusion of each episode, a reflection function, $z = f_{reflect}(\tau)$, analyzes the trajectory $\tau$ to produce a reflection tuple $(ϕ_{(x,τ)}, c, l)$:
- $ϕ_{(x,τ)}$: A scalar potential score in $[0,1]$ estimating subtask completion. This is used for intrinsic numerical feedback.
- $c$: A binary prediction of the episode's success or failure.
- $l$: A natural-language lesson distilled from the trajectory, stored in a memory buffer.

Two variants of this mechanism are proposed:

1. **In-Context Variant:** Employs pairwise induction, where the reflection function $f_{reflect}(\tau_{ref}, I_{ext}, \tau)$ uses a contrastive reference trajectory $\tau_{ref}$ (with a different outcome) and a binary outcome indicator $I_{ext}$ to improve the quality of generated potential scores and lessons.
2. **RL-Trained Variant:** The agent's self-reflection capability is jointly optimized with its decision-making policy. A reflection policy $\phi_\theta$ generates the reflection tuple. A reflection reward $R_{reflect}:= R_{ext,(i)} \cdot \mathbf{1}\{c = I_{ext}\}$ is introduced, penalizing inaccurate self-assessments. This variant modifies the overall objective to include a self-reflection term weighted by $\lambda_{reflect}$.

**Intrinsic Numerical Feedback for Directed Exploration:**
This mechanism addresses sparse extrinsic rewards by providing a progress-sensitive signal.
- For each task $x$, a historical baseline $\Phi_x^{(k)}$ (maximum group-mean extrinsic success rate) is maintained.
- The intrinsic reward $R_{int,k}(\tau)$ for a trajectory $\tau$ at iteration $k$ is calculated as the rectified gain over this baseline: $R_{int,k}(\tau) = [\phi_{(x,\tau),k} - \Phi_x^{(k)}]_+$.
- This reward is positive only if the reflected potential score of the current trajectory exceeds the agent's established capability for that task. This encourages capability improvement rather than undirected novelty.
- The augmented reward for policy optimization is $R_{aug,k}(\tau) = R_{ext}(\tau) + R_{int,k}(\tau)$.
- Theoretical justification is provided in Proposition 2 (intrinsic reward favors valuable exploration) and Proposition 3 (monotone exploration pressure).

**Intrinsic Language Feedback via Reflection Memory:**
To provide reusable guidance, RetroAgent maintains a retrieval-augmented reflection memory. Each memory entry $m_i = (x_i, l_i, \tau_i, u_i, n_i, d_i)$ contains the task, lesson, source trajectory, empirical utility $u_i$, retrieval count $n_i$, and extrinsic outcome $d_i$.

- **SimUtil-UCB Retrieval Strategy:** Given a current task $x$, SimUtil-UCB scores each memory entry $m_i$ using a combination of factors:
 1. **Semantic Relevance ($s_{rel}$):** Measured by cosine similarity between the current task embedding $E(x)$ and the memory's task embedding $v_i = E(x_i)$. Entries below a threshold (0.4) are discarded.
 2. **Empirical Utility ($u_i$):** Initialized to 0.5 and updated via Exponential Moving Average (EMA) using a smoothing factor $\beta_{util}$ whenever the memory is retrieved and leads to a task success score $\hat{u}_t$.
 3. **UCB Exploration Bonus:** To prevent over-exploitation of frequently used memories, an Upper Confidence Bound (UCB) term $\kappa \sqrt{\ln N_M / \max(n_i, 1)}$ is added, where $N_M$ is the total number of retrievals and $n_i$ is the individual memory's retrieval count.
- The final retrieval score is $S(m_i | x, M) = \alpha s_{rel}(x, x_i) + (1-\alpha) u_{UCB}^{(i)}$, where $\alpha$ balances relevance and utility. Top-k lessons are concatenated to augment the task instruction. Retrieval counts $n_i$ are incremented.
- Theoretical justification is provided in Definition 4 (memory-informative feedback), Definition 5 (memory-conditioned transfer eluder dimension), Theorem 6 (memory-guided complexity reduction), and Proposition 7 (SimUtil-UCB retrieval behavior).

**Policy Optimization with Dual Intrinsic Feedback:**
RetroAgent is implemented with GRPO (Group-Relative Policy Optimization), adapted for dual intrinsic feedback.

- **Trajectory Generation:** For each task $x$, $N$ trajectories are generated. Half (N/2) are sampled from the base policy $\pi_{\theta_{old}}(\cdot | x)$, and the other half from the memory-augmented policy $\pi_{\theta_{old}}(\cdot | f_{memory}(x, M))$. This balance allows for both independent exploration and memory-guided learning.
- **Decision-Making Objective:** The policy is optimized to maximize $J_{Decision-Making}(\theta)$, a GRPO-style objective using composite returns $G^{(i)} = \sum \gamma^t (R_{ext,(i)} + R_{int,(i)})$. Advantages $\hat{A}^{(i)}$ are calculated relative to the group mean, normalized by standard deviation. A clipped surrogate loss and KL divergence regularization term (towards a reference policy $\pi_{ref}$) are used.
- **Self-Reflection Objective (for RL-Trained Variant):** For this variant, a separate objective $J_{Self-Reflection}(\theta)$ is used to train the reflection policy $\phi_\theta$ via REINFORCE, maximizing the expected reflection reward $R_{reflect}$. The total objective becomes $J_{RetroAgent}(\theta) = J_{Decision-Making}(\theta) + \lambda_{reflect} \cdot J_{Self-Reflection}(\theta)$.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

RetroAgent was evaluated against various baselines on four agentic benchmarks: ALFWorld, WebShop, Sokoban, and MineSweeper, using Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct models.

**State-of-the-Art Performance:**
- RetroAgent consistently achieved new state-of-the-art performance across all four benchmarks.
- It outperformed the GRPO baseline by significant margins: +14.4% on ALFWorld, +12.0% on WebShop, +21.4% on Sokoban, and +8.6% on MineSweeper (success rates).
- On WebShop, RetroAgent surpassed other strong baselines like GiGPO and SkillRL by +6.1% to +6.2%.

**Effectiveness of Dual Intrinsic Feedback:**
- The integration of hindsight-generated intrinsic feedback (both numerical and language) led to more effective policy learning compared to extrinsic-only RL.
- RetroAgent's performance gains over memory-augmented methods (MemRL, EvolveR, SimpleMem+GRPO) and exploration-guided RL (GRPO w/ EMPG) indicated that combining progress-aware exploration with explicit experience reuse is more effective than either strategy in isolation.

**Distilled Lessons vs. Raw Trajectories:**
- RetroAgent, which distills lessons from trajectories, significantly outperformed EvolveR, which reuses raw trajectories. For example, on WebShop, RetroAgent achieved 78.9–82.3% success rates compared to EvolveR's 17.6%. This suggests that concise, transferable lessons generated through retrospective self-reflection are more beneficial than raw interaction histories.

**RL-Trained Self-Reflection:**
- Jointly optimizing the self-reflection policy with the decision policy (RL-Trained Reflection variant) further improved performance, achieving higher success rates (e.g., 95.6% on ALFWorld vs. 91.7% for In-Context Reflection). This indicates that the ability to generate reflective feedback can itself be learned and optimized.

**Test-Time Adaptation and Generalization:**
- **Rapid Test-Time Adaptation:** RetroAgent demonstrated strong test-time adaptation, achieving near-perfect discovery rates (solving a task within k attempts) in both in-distribution (WebShop) and out-of-distribution (ALFWorld) settings, often within three attempts. It consistently outperformed the Meta-RL baseline LaMer.
- **Internalization of Feedback:** Ablation studies showed that removing memory retrieval during test time resulted in only minor performance drops in Discovery@1 and Discovery@2, and no change in Discovery@3. This suggests that the dual intrinsic feedback mechanisms effectively aid in internalizing learned knowledge into the policy parameters during training, reducing reliance on explicit memory access at inference.
- **Robustness to Challenging Tasks:** RetroAgent maintained its superior performance on harder-than-training instances of MineSweeper, demonstrating robustness to increased task difficulty.

**Analysis of In-Context Self-Reflection:**
- **Pairwise Induction Superiority:** Comparing single-trajectory vs. pairwise-trajectory induction for self-reflection, pairwise induction yielded more accurate subtask completion scores (better aligned with oracle estimates) and higher-quality language lessons (lower hallucination rates, higher estimated utility). This translated to better downstream policy optimization.
- **Preserving Unguided Exploration:** Using memory augmentation for only half of the sampled trajectories ("half-group memory augmentation") outperformed full-group augmentation. This indicates that maintaining some unguided exploration is crucial to prevent premature convergence and encourage diversity in learned strategies.

**Effect of Intrinsic Numerical Feedback:**
- The addition of capability-evolution rewards (intrinsic numerical feedback) on top of discounted returns further improved GRPO's performance (e.g., from 74.7% to 79.7% success rate on WebShop). These self-reflection-based potential scores provided richer shaping signals than binary extrinsic outcomes alone.

**Effect of Intrinsic Language Feedback:**
- The SimUtil-UCB retrieval strategy was crucial for effective intrinsic language feedback. While similarity-only or similarity-utility (without UCB) retrieval sometimes degraded performance, SimUtil-UCB consistently improved it. The UCB exploration bonus effectively diversified memory usage, preventing over-reliance on a small subset of memories.

**Effect of Combining Dual Intrinsic Feedback:**
- Combining both intrinsic numerical and language feedback generally led to superior agentic reasoning. However, in some cases (e.g., in-context variant), the simultaneous exploration signals from both channels exhibited minor interference during action selection, suggesting potential challenges in multi-objective balancing.
- The RL-trained self-reflection mechanism maintained higher reflection accuracy throughout training compared to the in-context variant, indicating its capacity for sustained quality.

**Impact on Exploration:**
- All intrinsic-feedback variants resulted in higher Vendi Scores (a measure of diversity) for successful trajectories compared to the GRPO baseline, demonstrating that intrinsic feedback promotes more diverse and useful exploration.

**Training Efficiency:**
- While RetroAgent had a higher total training cost than GRPO, it reached GRPO's peak performance significantly faster (reducing time by 46% for in-context and 32% for RL-trained variants). This indicates accelerated convergence due to intrinsic feedback.

**Sensitivity and Generalization:**
- **Hyperparameter Sensitivity:** Prioritizing utility in memory retrieval (lower $\alpha$) and increasing the weight of the self-reflection objective ($\lambda_{reflect}$) positively impacted performance.
- **Architectural Generalization:** RetroAgent consistently achieved state-of-the-art results when evaluated with Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, demonstrating its generality across different model architectures.
- **Model Scaling:** RetroAgent showed consistent outperformance of baselines at both 7B and 14B Qwen2.5-Instruct scales, with modest performance gains from scaling up, suggesting larger models can better encode experiential knowledge.

**Qualitative Analysis:**
- Qualitative examination revealed that during training, RetroAgent evolved from making incorrect decisions with low confidence (e.g., at step 65 on WebShop) to making correct, confident decisions guided by relevant retrieved lessons (e.g., at step 150), illustrating the effectiveness of policy evolution.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

RetroAgent introduces a novel online reinforcement learning framework that addresses several limitations in the training of large language model (LLM) agents, particularly concerning continuous adaptation and efficient experience utilization. The significance and potential impact of this work can be summarized as follows:

- **Paradigm Shift for LLM Agents:** The framework proposes a shift from a "one-off task solving" paradigm to one of "continuous policy evolution." By integrating hindsight self-reflection and dual intrinsic feedback, RetroAgent encourages agents to learn and adapt across episodes rather than merely completing isolated tasks. This aligns with a broader vision of developing more autonomous and continuously improving AI agents.

- **Enhanced Exploration and Learning Efficiency:** The dual intrinsic feedback mechanism is designed to provide richer learning signals than sparse extrinsic rewards alone.
 - **Directed Exploration:** Intrinsic Numerical Feedback, by rewarding incremental subtask progress relative to a dynamic baseline, guides agents towards beneficial exploration, mitigating premature convergence to suboptimal policies. This allows agents to learn from partial successes and promising failures.
 - **Efficient Experiential Learning:** Intrinsic Language Feedback distills complex interaction trajectories into concise, reusable textual lessons. This explicit form of experience, coupled with the SimUtil-UCB retrieval strategy, enables agents to learn from and apply past knowledge more effectively, reducing the need for redundant exploration.

- **Improved Adaptability and Generalization:** The empirical results demonstrate that RetroAgent achieves state-of-the-art performance across diverse and challenging agentic benchmarks, including out-of-distribution scenarios. Its strong test-time adaptation capabilities, where agents rapidly improve performance over a few attempts, indicate a robust ability to generalize and adapt to new tasks or environments. The observed internalization of feedback into policy parameters further suggests that the learned knowledge is deeply integrated, reducing runtime dependencies on external memory.

- **Principled Memory Management:** The SimUtil-UCB retrieval strategy represents a principled approach to managing and leveraging experiential memory. By balancing semantic relevance with empirical utility and incorporating an exploration bonus, it ensures that memory retrieval is both effective and diverse, preventing over-reliance on a limited set of memories and promoting broader learning.

- **Advancement in Agent Autonomy:** The RL-trained self-reflection variant suggests that not only can agents learn from their reflections, but they can also learn to reflect more effectively. This co-evolution of decision-making and self-reflection capabilities represents a step towards more autonomous and self-improving agents.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Foundational for Future Agent Research:** RetroAgent's framework provides a foundation for developing more intelligent and adaptive LLM agents that can operate effectively in complex, dynamic, and open-ended environments. It offers a blueprint for integrating cognitive processes like reflection and learning from experience into AI systems.
- **Broader Application Domains:** The principles of dual intrinsic feedback and utility-aware memory retrieval could be extended to various domains beyond the simulated environments tested, including robotics, interactive content generation, and intelligent assistants, where continuous learning and adaptation from sparse feedback are crucial.
- **Addressing AI Safety and Robustness:** By fostering more robust exploration and learning from diverse experiences, RetroAgent might contribute to building agents that are less brittle and more resilient to unexpected situations, which has implications for AI safety and trustworthiness.

**Future Directions:**
The authors identify future work that includes developing more effective multi-objective optimization strategies for jointly training self-reflection and decision-making policies. Extending RetroAgent to multi-agent and open-ended settings is also suggested, which could further broaden its applicability and impact.
