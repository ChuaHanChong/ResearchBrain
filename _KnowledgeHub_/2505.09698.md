# ManipBench: Benchmarking Vision-Language Models for Low-Level Robot Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> ManipBench introduces a new open-source, multiple-choice question (MCQ) based benchmark to evaluate Vision-Language Models' (VLMs) low-level physical reasoning for diverse robotic manipulation tasks. The benchmark demonstrates a strong positive correlation between VLM performance on its MCQs and success rates in real-world robot experiments, validating its utility as a diagnostic tool.

## Problem

- Existing VLM benchmarks for robotics often focus on high-level planning or are computationally expensive due to full trajectory rollouts, lacking efficient evaluation for low-level physical reasoning.
- A standardized and comprehensive benchmark was needed to systematically evaluate general-purpose VLMs for precise, action-centric decisions across diverse and challenging manipulation tasks, including deformable and dynamic objects.
- The community lacked a clear understanding of the intrinsic capabilities and limitations of VLMs in generating low-level trajectories and understanding object-object interactions for robot manipulation.

## Method

- Developed **ManipBench**, an open-source benchmark with 12,617 multiple-choice questions (MCQs) designed for efficient evaluation of VLMs' low-level physical reasoning in robotics.
- Sourced questions from public datasets (DROID, Bridge), an in-house fabric manipulation setup, and various simulated environments to cover a broad spectrum of tasks (e.g., pick-and-place, articulated, deformable, tool, dynamic manipulation).
- ==Generated MCQs== around selecting appropriate interaction keypoints or trajectories, using adapted MOKA-style pipelines for real-world data and direct keypoint plotting for simulation, often incorporating Chain-of-Thought prompting.

## Results

- Gemini-**2.5**-pro consistently emerged as the top-performing VLM across many categories, demonstrating superior accuracy and robustness in tasks like fabric manipulation and public robotic datasets.
- ManipBench showed a strong positive correlation (Pearson's coefficient of **0.889**, p=**0.003**) between VLM performance on MCQs and success rates in real-world robot experiments, particularly for fabric manipulation tasks (Pearson's **0.950**, p=**0.001**).
- Tasks involving pick-and-place were handled comparatively well by VLMs (avg. accuracy **0.525**), while articulated object manipulation (**0.396**) and dynamic manipulation (**0.357**) proved more challenging.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A substantial performance gap exists between even the best VLMs and human-level understanding in low-level manipulation, with significant variation across tasks and model families.
> - Scaling laws were observed, where larger open-source VLM variants generally outperformed smaller ones, though some plateauing suggests diminishing returns beyond certain model sizes.
> - VLMs struggle more with reasoning about precise object placement (ending tile prediction) compared to initial object grasping (picking point prediction), highlighting a specific area for improvement.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancements in Vision-Language Models (VLMs), exemplified by models like GPT-4 and Gemini, have significantly impacted artificial intelligence, including robotics. These models excel at commonsense reasoning and have primarily been leveraged in robotics as high-level planners, generating task sequences or code for robots to execute. However, the unique challenges of robotic manipulation, which demand precise, low-level reasoning about physical interactions, object properties, and exact movements, often present a bottleneck. Traditional approaches require extensive, task-specific real-world data collection or rely on simulations that face a "sim-to-real" gap, hindering the scalability and generalization of robotic agents.

Recent research has begun exploring the application of these "foundation" models to generate low-level trajectories, aiming to bypass the need for large-scale, task-specific data. This direction promises faster deployment and improved generalization in open-world settings. However, the community has lacked a standardized and comprehensive benchmark to systematically evaluate how well general-purpose VLMs (i.e., those not specifically fine-tuned for robotics) can perform this crucial low-level reasoning for diverse manipulation tasks.

Existing benchmarks for VLMs in robotics, such as NEWTON, PhysBench, Octopi, MultiNet, and VLABench, address various aspects of physical or embodied AI reasoning. However, as highlighted by the authors in Table 1, these benchmarks often have limitations. Some focus on high-level reasoning (e.g., object properties), others rely on full trajectory rollouts (which are computationally expensive for widespread VLM evaluation), and many lack task diversity, particularly in challenging areas like deformable or dynamic object manipulation. For instance, PhysBench evaluates spatial reasoning but not direct action prediction, while MultiNet uses MSE for trajectory prediction, which may not capture multimodal performance nuances. VLABench assumes a skill library, which ManipBench does not. Crucially, many prior efforts don't adequately focus on *low-level manipulation reasoning* (i.e., precise action-centric decisions) or systematic *task factorization* to diagnose specific VLM weaknesses.

ManipBench directly addresses this gap by proposing a novel, open-source, and efficient multiple-choice question (MCQ) based benchmark. It specifically targets the low-level physical reasoning capabilities of VLMs across a wide array of manipulation tasks, including object-object interactions, articulated object manipulation, deformable object manipulation (a strong focus), tool use, and dynamic manipulation. By evaluating general VLMs rather than only robotics-specific Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models (like RT-2 or Octo), ManipBench helps understand their intrinsic capabilities and potential impact when integrated into larger robotic systems. This work complements existing efforts by providing a diagnostic tool to pinpoint VLM limitations in realistic manipulation scenarios, thereby guiding future research in both VLM development and robotic foundation models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind ManipBench stems from the lack of a clear, common, and efficient benchmark to evaluate the low-level reasoning capabilities of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) in robotics. While VLMs have demonstrated impressive high-level planning abilities, their aptitude for making precise decisions about robot movements, understanding object-object interactions, and handling complex scenarios like deformable object manipulation remains largely underexplored and unquantified in a standardized manner. Specifically, the authors pose critical questions:
- Which foundation model is optimal for a "VLM agent" in diverse manipulation tasks?
- How well do VLMs perform in the granular, low-level reasoning required for practical manipulation?

To address these questions, the **key objectives** of this work are:

1. **Develop a Novel Benchmark**: To create ManipBench, an open-source, MCQ-based benchmark capable of evaluating VLMs' reasoning for low-level robotic manipulation. This benchmark is designed to be efficient, bypassing the need for computationally expensive trajectory rollouts.
2. **Ensure Task Diversity**: To encompass a broad spectrum of manipulation tasks, including pick-and-place, articulated object manipulation, deformable object manipulation, tool manipulation, and dynamic manipulation, providing a comprehensive assessment.
3. **Evaluate Diverse VLM Families**: To extensively test a wide range of representative VLMs (both closed-source and open-source, including different model sizes) to identify their strengths, weaknesses, and general performance trends across various dimensions of low-level reasoning.
4. **Validate Real-World Relevance**: To conduct real-world robot experiments that demonstrate a significant correlation between a VLM's performance on the ManipBench MCQs and its effectiveness in selecting actual robot actions for unseen manipulation tasks, thereby validating the benchmark's utility as a reliable proxy.
5. **Identify Performance Gaps**: To highlight the current limitations of VLMs in low-level manipulation understanding and pinpoint areas where further research and innovation are most needed to bridge the gap between model capabilities and human-level understanding.

In essence, ManipBench aims to be a diagnostic tool that fosters a deeper understanding of VLM capabilities in a critical and challenging domain of robotics, ultimately facilitating the development of more generalist and capable robotic agents.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose **ManipBench**, a novel open-source benchmark comprising **12,617 multiple-choice questions (MCQs)** designed to evaluate the low-level physical reasoning capabilities of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) in robotic manipulation. The core approach revolves around an efficient MCQ-based evaluation, which significantly reduces computational overhead compared to full trajectory rollouts.

#### 3.1. Data Sources

The benchmark's questions are derived from three distinct sources to ensure diversity and realism:
- **Public Robotic Manipulation Datasets**: Data from **DROID** (articulated and pick-and-place tasks) and **Bridge** datasets are utilized. For DROID, the authors use a human-annotated subset for gripper positions, and further fine-tune GroundingDINO to detect grippers and extract accurate picking and placing points for both datasets, addressing empirical discrepancies found in raw DROID data.
- **In-house Fabric Manipulation Setup**: Recognizing the practical importance of deformable object manipulation, the authors manually curate data from a real-world workstation featuring a robot arm, camera, fabrics, and solid objects. This data is used to formulate questions across **ten distinct dimensions** (e.g., Task Planning Understanding, Fabric State Understanding, Inverse Dynamics Understanding, Fabric-Solid Body Interaction Understanding, Temporal Understanding of Action Sequence), each assessing a fundamental aspect of fabric manipulation reasoning.
- **Simulation Data**: To cover tasks that are difficult or cumbersome to execute in the real world, simulated environments are employed. This includes:
 - **SimplerEnv** for pick-and-place (e.g., Place Carrot) and articulated object manipulation (e.g., Close Drawer).
 - **SoftGym** for deformable object manipulation (e.g., Straighten Rope).
 - **RLBench** for tool manipulation (e.g., Sweep Object).
 - A custom **IsaacSim** environment for dynamic manipulation (e.g., Ball Shooting).
 Demonstrations are generated using pre-trained policies or manual annotations, with ground-truth keypoint information readily available.

#### 3.2. Question Generation

The MCQs are primarily designed around selecting appropriate interaction keypoints (e.g., picking, placing, pushing points) or trajectories.
- **Preprocessing for Real-World Data**: A **MOKA-style pipeline** is adapted. This involves:
 1. Prompting GPT-4o to identify the key object from an observation image and task description.
 2. Using Grounded SAM to segment all objects and sample points (center and contour).
 3. Annotating the original image with sampled points and a grid overlay (typically 5x5).
 4. Using ground-truth keypoints to generate the correct MCQ option, ensuring it lies on the key object.
 Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting is incorporated to assist VLMs.
- **MCQs from Public Datasets**: Two types of questions are generated:
 - **Type 1 (Q1)**: VLMs choose the best matching trajectory (picking keypoint + start/end tiles) from four candidates, covering 6120 questions.
 - **Type 2 (Q2)**: VLMs first choose a picking point, then select the appropriate ending tile (given the ground-truth picking point), covering 3060 questions. "Question augmentation" is used to create multiple versions from the same episode.
- **MCQs from In-house Fabric Setup**: 2662 questions are manually curated across the ten dimensions. Options are distinct from the correct answer, and neighboring grid cells are excluded to avoid ambiguity.
- **MCQs from Simulation**: Ground-truth keypoint information is directly used to plot points or colored arrows (for dynamic tasks) on images, forming four-choice questions without a grid overlay.

#### 3.3. Evaluation Plan

- **Model Selection**: 33 representative VLMs from 10 families are evaluated, including closed-source models (GPT-4.1, GPT-4o, GPT-4o-mini, Gemini-2.5-pro, Gemini-2.0-flash, Gemini-1.5-pro, Gemini-1.5-flash, and o1) and various open-source models (GLM-4V, InternVL2, InternVL2.5, Qwen-VL, Qwen2-VL, Qwen2.5-VL, LLaVA-NeXT, Llama3.2-Vision), often including size-based variants.
- **Metrics**: Percentage accuracy of correctly answering MCQs.
- **Baselines**: Random guessing (25% for 4 choices, empirically estimated for Q2). Human performance (36 volunteers answering subsets of 50-70 questions per type) serves as an upper bound.
- **Real-World Validation**: A crucial step involves physical robot experiments using a UR5 arm and Robotiq gripper. Seven novel, unseen manipulation tasks (e.g., Color Specific p&p, Fit Apple, Move Fabric Easy/Intermediate/Hard) are designed. VLMs, using a similar keypoint selection pipeline as for MCQs, choose robot actions. The correlation between ManipBench performance and real-world success rates is then statistically analyzed.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive evaluation of 33 Vision-Language Models (VLMs) on ManipBench yielded several key insights into their low-level robot manipulation reasoning capabilities:

- **Significant Performance Variation and Room for Improvement**: VLM performance varied significantly across different tasks and model families. While most models generally outperformed random guessing, there remains a substantial gap between even the best VLMs and human-level understanding, highlighting considerable room for future innovation. Human accuracy consistently approached or exceeded 80-90% on most tasks, contrasting with VLM performance.

- **Top Performing Models (Closed-Source)**:
 - **Gemini-2.5-pro** consistently emerged as the top performer across many categories, achieving the best accuracy on Q1 questions from public robotic manipulation datasets (Bridge, DROID articulated, DROID pick-and-place) and demonstrating superior understanding in fabric manipulation dimensions. It also showed high robustness across task categories.
 - **o1** (another closed-source model) was a close contender, achieving the best performance in DROID pick-and-place Q2 questions and performing strongly in fabric manipulation tasks.

- **Open-Source VLM Trends**:
 - Among open-source models, **InternVL2.5-38B** and **InternVL2.5-78B** generally demonstrated the best performance, particularly on the public robotic manipulation datasets.
 - A clear trend of **scaling laws** was observed (further detailed in Appendix I): larger model variants within the same family (e.g., InternVL2.5, Qwen2.5-VL) generally outperformed their smaller counterparts. However, some plateauing behavior was noted for InternVL, suggesting diminishing returns beyond certain model sizes.
 - Smaller open-source VLMs (under 2 billion parameters) often performed similarly to or even worse than random guessing, indicating that adequate model capacity is critical for complex physical reasoning.

- **Task Difficulty Breakdown**:
 - VLMs handled **pick-and-place tasks** comparatively well, with an average accuracy of 0.525.
 - **Articulated object manipulation** and **dynamic manipulation** proved more challenging, achieving mean accuracies of 0.396 and 0.357, respectively, across all models.
 - In **fabric manipulation**, tasks related to "Task Planning Understanding" were relatively easier for VLMs, while dimensions like "Fabric-Fabric Interaction Understanding" and "Inverse Dynamics Understanding" posed greater challenges.

- **Robustness Across Task Categories**:
 - **Gemini-2.5-pro** was identified as the most robust closed-source model, exhibiting a low coefficient of variation (CV) of 0.089 across task categories.
 - **Qwen2.5-VL-32B** was the most robust open-source model (CV of 0.085), even outperforming its larger variant (Qwen2.5-VL-72B) in consistency, despite a slightly lower average accuracy.

- **Performance Transfer to Real-World Robot Experiments (Crucial Validation)**:
 - A cornerstone finding is the **strong positive correlation** between VLM performance on ManipBench MCQs and their success rate in selecting actions for unseen real-world robot manipulation tasks.
 - Statistical analysis revealed a Pearson's coefficient of 0.889 (p=0.003), a Spearman's coefficient of 0.850 (p=0.007), and a Kendall's Tau of 0.691 (p=0.018). These statistically significant correlations strongly validate ManipBench as a reliable proxy for evaluating VLMs in embodied robotic settings.
 - Specifically, the fabric manipulation questions showed the strongest correlation with real-world success rates (Pearson's 0.950, p=0.001), indicating the benchmark's particular utility for this challenging domain.

- **Error Mode Analysis (Appendix F)**: An analysis of Type 2 questions revealed that VLMs exhibited significantly lower accuracy in predicting the *ending tile* (placement) compared to predicting the *picking point*. This suggests that reasoning about precise object placement, which often involves more complex spatial and physical interactions, is a more difficult skill for current VLMs than initial object grasping.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ManipBench makes several significant contributions and has substantial potential impact on the fields of Vision-Language Models and Robotics:

- **Addresses a Critical Gap in VLM Evaluation**: The most significant contribution of ManipBench is filling a crucial void in VLM evaluation. By specifically focusing on **low-level manipulation reasoning** with an efficient MCQ-based design, it provides a much-needed standardized tool to diagnose VLM capabilities for precise robot actions, a dimension largely overlooked or inefficiently assessed by prior benchmarks. This allows for rapid and scalable evaluation of a wide array of models without requiring complex and time-consuming physical robot deployments or full trajectory rollouts for every test.

- **Comprehensive Diagnostic Tool**: The benchmark's diverse task categories (pick-and-place, articulated, deformable, tool, dynamic manipulation) and the fine-grained dimensions for fabric manipulation provide a comprehensive diagnostic framework. This enables researchers to pinpoint specific strengths and weaknesses of different VLM architectures, model sizes, and training methodologies. For instance, the finding that placement reasoning (ending tile prediction) is harder than picking point prediction offers a clear direction for VLM improvement.

- **Validates Benchmark Reliability for Embodied AI**: The strong statistical correlation between VLM performance on ManipBench MCQs and their success in real-world robot manipulation tasks is a critical validation. This demonstrates that ManipBench is not just an academic exercise but a **reliable proxy** for assessing VLM utility in actual embodied robotics. This real-world grounding enhances the benchmark's credibility and encourages its adoption by practitioners and researchers alike.

- **Guides Future VLM Development**: By clearly highlighting the substantial gap between current VLM capabilities and human-level understanding in low-level manipulation, ManipBench provides a clear call to action for the VLM research community. It serves as a challenging benchmark for developing next-generation models that possess more robust physical commonsense, spatial reasoning, and fine-grained interaction understanding. The identified difficulties in articulated, dynamic, and complex deformable object manipulation suggest fertile ground for architectural innovations, improved training data, and better grounding mechanisms for VLMs.

- **Fosters Open Science and Collaboration**: As an open-source benchmark with a dedicated website, ManipBench encourages community participation. Researchers can easily evaluate new VLM models against a common standard, contribute new tasks or data, and collaborate on advancing the state-of-the-art. The detailed analysis of scaling laws also provides empirical guidance for resource allocation in VLM development, indicating when brute force scaling might plateau and when architectural innovation is more likely to yield significant gains.

- **Enables Informed Decision-Making in Robotics**: For roboticists, ManipBench offers valuable insights into selecting optimal foundation models for VLM-driven robot agents. Understanding which general-purpose VLMs inherently possess better low-level reasoning can inform choices for integration into robotic foundation models (Vision-Language-Action models) or for downstream fine-tuning, potentially accelerating the deployment of generalist robots capable of performing diverse tasks in unstructured environments.

In conclusion, ManipBench is a timely and impactful contribution that provides a robust, efficient, and real-world validated benchmark for the critical area of low-level robot manipulation reasoning in Vision-Language Models. Its findings not only advance our understanding of current VLM capabilities but also provide a clear roadmap for future research, pushing the boundaries of what embodied AI can achieve.
