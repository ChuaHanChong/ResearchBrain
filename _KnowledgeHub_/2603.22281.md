# ThinkJEPA: Empowering Latent World Models with Large Vision-Language Reasoning Model

> [!summary] Summary
> ThinkJEPA integrates a Vision-Language Model to semantically guide a JEPA-style latent world model, improving representation quality and achieving up to 14% lower Average Displacement Error (ADE) and 15% lower Final Displacement Error (FDE) in hand-manipulation trajectory prediction compared to V-JEPA baselines.

## Problem

- JEPA-style latent world models exhibit limited temporal context for prediction and weak semantic grounding, hindering their ability to capture long-horizon semantics and align with general world knowledge.
- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) face computational constraints leading to sparse frame sampling, making them inadequate for modeling fine-grained dynamics, and their language-output bottleneck can lose fine-grained visual details.
- Adapting VLMs for domain-specific prediction tasks can lead to data regime mismatches and potential catastrophic forgetting of general knowledge.

## Method

- **ThinkJEPA** integrates a VLM 'thinker' branch with a ==JEPA-style latent world model==, combining dense dynamics modeling from JEPA with long-horizon semantic reasoning from the VLM within a unified framework.
- It employs a ==dual-temporal perception== design, using dense frame sampling for the JEPA branch to capture fine-grained motion and sparse sampling for the VLM branch to provide long-range contextual guidance.
- A ==hierarchical pyramid representation extraction== module aggregates multi-layer VLM features, which are then injected as layer-wise, sample-specific guidance into the JEPA predictor via ==Feature-wise Linear Modulation (FiLM)==.

## Results

- ThinkJEPA achieved an Average Displacement Error (ADE) of **0.061** and Final Displacement Error (FDE) of **0.056** on the EgoDex dataset, outperforming VLM-only, V-JEPA-only, and **six** established trajectory prediction baselines.
- The framework consistently demonstrated **improved** latent forecasting quality, indicated by **lower** L2, SmoothL1, and cosine distances between predicted and target latents.
- Under recursive rollout for long horizons (up to H=**32**), ThinkJEPA maintained the **best performance**, showing **reduced error accumulation** and superior stability compared to baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating semantic guidance from a VLM significantly enhances the quality of learned representations and boosts downstream task performance in latent world models.
> - A dual-temporal sampling strategy, coupled with hierarchical pyramid extraction of VLM features, effectively reconciles fine-grained dynamics with long-horizon semantic reasoning.
> - VLM semantic conditioning proves critical for stabilizing iterative predictions and mitigating error accumulation during long-horizon recursive rollouts.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research in "ThinkJEPA" operates at the intersection of latent world models, self-supervised representation learning, and large vision-language models (VLMs). World models are designed to learn predictive abstractions of environments, facilitating forecasting, planning, and control. Latent world models, such as those employing a Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) paradigm (e.g., V-JEPA2), focus on predicting future states within a learned representation space rather than generating high-fidelity pixels. This approach prioritizes computational efficiency and the capture of higher-level structures like dynamics and physical constraints over appearance details.

Despite advancements in JEPA-style models, they typically rely on short, densely sampled observation windows. This can limit their temporal context, biasing predictions towards local, low-level extrapolation and hindering the capture of long-horizon semantics or event-level cues. Additionally, their latent spaces, often learned through self-supervised visual objectives, lack alignment with open-vocabulary concepts and general world knowledge, which restricts their generalization beyond specific domains.

Conversely, Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have demonstrated strong capabilities in high-level video understanding and multimodal reasoning, leveraging large-scale pretraining and multimodal alignment. When applied to uniformly sampled frames over longer durations, VLMs can provide extensive temporal context, recognize entities and their attributes, and draw upon general world knowledge. However, VLMs are not ideal as standalone dense predictors due to several factors: their quadratic attention costs necessitate sparse sampling, limiting their ability to model high-frequency, fine-grained dynamics essential for physical interaction. Furthermore, their language-output bottleneck compresses fine-grained visual information into language-compatible representations, which can be semantically effective but physically inconsistent for accurate forecasting. Finally, adapting VLMs to smaller, domain-specific action-conditioned datasets often leads to data regime mismatches and potential catastrophic forgetting of general knowledge.

This work addresses these complementary strengths and weaknesses by proposing a framework that integrates VLM semantic guidance with JEPA-style dense latent prediction. It seeks to combine the fine-grained dynamics modeling of JEPA with the long-horizon semantic understanding and general knowledge of VLMs, thereby enhancing the robustness and generalization of future latent forecasting. This approach distinguishes itself from prior multimodal fusion methods that either use JEPA features as VLM inputs for understanding or predominantly shift the output interface towards language generation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a latent world modeling framework that overcomes the limitations of existing JEPA-style models and Vision-Language Models (VLMs) when applied to forecasting future world states.

**Motivation stems from two key limitations of current approaches:**

1. **Limited Temporal Perspective and Semantic Grounding in JEPA-style Latent World Models:**
 - **Short Observation Window:** Current JEPA models typically use a short window of densely sampled frames for prediction. While dense sampling captures fine-grained motion, it restricts the temporal context available to the model. This can lead to predictions biased towards local dynamics, making it difficult to capture longer-horizon semantics and event-level cues crucial for robust forecasting.
 - **Weak Semantic Grounding:** The latent spaces in these models are usually learned through self-supervised visual representation tasks (e.g., masked reconstruction). This process primarily yields motion-sensitive features but provides limited alignment with open-vocabulary concepts and compositional knowledge. Consequently, these predictors may understand "how" things move but lack a deeper understanding of "what" the entities are, their attributes, or relevant relations, thus limiting generalization to broader or novel domains.

2. **Impracticality of VLMs as Standalone Dense Predictors:**
 - **Compute-Driven Sparsity:** Video VLMs, constrained by quadratic attention costs and GPU memory, typically process only a small number of uniformly sampled frames. While this design provides long-horizon context, it makes them unsuitable for modeling high-frames-per-second, fine-grained dynamics critical for tasks involving physical interaction and manipulation.
 - **Language-Output Bottleneck:** Most VLM pipelines are designed to produce language outputs (e.g., captions, rationales). This involves progressive transformation of visual information towards language generation objectives, which can compress fine-grained spatial details and continuous interaction states (like contact or precise trajectories) into language-compatible representations. Such representations are effective for semantic recognition but often inadequate for accurate physical forecasting, leading to potentially physically inconsistent language-based plans.
 - **Data Regime Mismatch:** Deploying VLMs for specific prediction or control tasks often requires adaptation to relatively small, domain-specific datasets. Naive fine-tuning in such scenarios can degrade the VLM's general knowledge and semantic capabilities due to catastrophic forgetting.

To address these issues, the authors propose a framework where VLMs serve as "thinkers" providing semantic and knowledge guidance, rather than acting as standalone dense predictors. The core idea is to integrate a VLM-thinker branch into a JEPA-style latent world model. This unified framework aims to combine the strengths of both: dense-frame dynamics modeling for fine-grained motion cues from the JEPA branch, and long-horizon semantic guidance and general knowledge from the VLM thinker branch.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ThinkJEPA proposes a VLM-guided JEPA-style latent world modeling framework designed to combine dense-frame dynamics with long-horizon semantic guidance.

#### 3.1. Problem Definition

The objective is to forecast future latent representations from an observed video clip `v`, specifically focusing on 3D hand trajectory prediction. A JEPA-style latent world modeling paradigm is adopted, where a visual backbone encodes video frames into latent tokens, and a transformer predictor forecasts future latent tokens from past observations. This prediction is further conditioned on cached features from a VLM, acting as a "thinker" for knowledge-rich guidance. For long videos exceeding a single forward pass, a recursive rollout strategy is employed, where previously predicted latents serve as inputs for subsequent predictions.

#### 3.2. Dual-Temporal Perception Field Sampling Architecture

ThinkJEPA introduces a dual-temporal perception-field design to reconcile the need for dense temporal signals for dynamics forecasting and long-horizon context for semantic understanding.
- **Large Temporal Perception Field (VLM-Thinker Branch):** For the VLM, a uniformly sampled clip `v_u` is used, comprising `N_u` frames spanning the entire video. This sparse sampling enables the VLM (Qwen3-VL (Thinking) in this implementation) to access long-range context and event-level semantics under limited computational constraints, providing semantic guidance and general knowledge. Intermediate representations from the VLM are cached.
- **Dense Frame Sampling (JEPA Branch):** For the JEPA branch, a densely sampled clip `v_d` is constructed from a short observation window of `N_d` frames. This dense sampling captures high-frequency motion and fine-grained dynamics crucial for accurate latent forecasting. The V-JEPA backbone encodes `v_d` into past latent tokens `F_past`.

This dual sampling strategy ensures that the VLM provides long-range context, while the JEPA branch handles fine-grained dynamics, addressing their respective limitations.

#### 3.3. JEPA-style Latent Tokenization and Forecasting

The V-JEPA visual backbone encodes `v_d` into per-frame spatial tokens `F`. A masked-token transformer predictor then forecasts future latent tokens `F_fut` from `F_past`. This predictor operates in an internal dimension `D_p` and projects its outputs back to the backbone latent space. For predictions beyond the single clip length, a recursive rollout strategy is used: `F_fut` from the current step becomes `F_past` for the next, allowing arbitrary long-horizon forecasts, albeit susceptible to error accumulation.

#### 3.4. VLM Thinker: Hierarchical Pyramid Representation Extraction

The VLM is utilized for guidance rather than direct prediction. ThinkJEPA injects VLM guidance into the JEPA predictor in a layer-wise manner. The predictor forecasts `F_fut` conditioned on `F_past(v_d)`, VLM-derived guidance features `ϕ(v_u)`, and an optional text prompt `p`.

A key innovation is the **hierarchical pyramid representation extraction module**. This module aggregates multi-depth VLM signals, rather than relying solely on final-layer features. It combines:
- Visual tokens from the VLM's visual encoder (ViT tokenizer).
- Intermediate hidden states from selected language model layers.

These multi-depth features are pooled and projected into the predictor's space, forming the guidance features `ϕ(v_u)`. This approach leverages the VLM's progressive reasoning, capturing both low-level visual cues (from early layers) and high-level semantic reasoning traces (from deeper layers), which are often more suitable for guiding latent forecasting than terminal, language-generation-oriented features.

**Layer-wise Guidance:** The extracted `ϕ(v_u)` is injected into the JEPA predictor using **Feature-wise Linear Modulation (FiLM)**. For each predictor block `ℓ`, the guidance generates modulation parameters `(γ_ℓ, β_ℓ)`, which modulate the block input `z` as `FiLM(z; γ_ℓ, β_ℓ) = γ_ℓ ⊙ z + β_ℓ`. This provides sample-specific conditioning, integrating semantic and knowledge cues without requiring the VLM to perform dense prediction directly.

**Joint Prediction for Downstream Regression:** For trajectory regression, the predicted future latent tokens are concatenated with past teacher latents to form a full-length token sequence, which is then fed into a lightweight temporal trajectory regression head to produce the target trajectories.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate that ThinkJEPA consistently outperforms various baselines across four diverse video forecasting benchmarks: EgoDex, EgoExo4D, BAIR Robot Pushing, and Physion.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Comparison (Table 1)

- **EgoDex and EgoExo4D (Trajectory Forecasting):** ThinkJEPA significantly improved trajectory prediction metrics (ADE, FDE, Acc) and latent forecasting quality (FD, SL1, CD) compared to both V-JEPA Predictor (JEPA-only) and Qwen3-VL Thinking (VLM-only). For example, on EgoDex, ThinkJEPA achieved an ADE of 0.061 and FDE of 0.056, substantially lower than V-JEPA Predictor (0.071 ADE, 0.066 FDE) and Qwen3-VL Thinking (0.142 ADE, 0.144 FDE). This indicates that VLM guidance enhances semantic grounding while preserving dense dynamics.

#### 4.2. Task-specific Trajectory Comparison (Table 2)

- **EgoDex (Trajectory Prediction Baselines):** ThinkJEPA achieved the lowest ADE (0.0610) and FDE (0.0560) among all compared methods, including decoder-only and encoder-decoder predictors combined with Behavior Cloning, DDPM, and Flow Matching objectives. This suggests that VLM-guided latent forecasting provides a stronger trajectory representation than direct trajectory prediction methods.

#### 4.3. BAIR Robot Pushing (Action-Conditioned Latent Rollout, Table 3)

- ThinkJEPA improved over the V-JEPA Predictor on all latent metrics (FD, SL1, CD) for three-step autoregressive rollout in frozen V-JEPA latent space, reducing FD from 68.711 to 67.049. It also outperformed DINO-WM and a persistence baseline, supporting its effectiveness for action-conditioned latent rollout in robotic interaction.

#### 4.4. Physion (Action-Free Physical Scene Forecasting, Table 4)

- ThinkJEPA achieved the best Accuracy (0.754) and AUC (0.828) for object-contact prediction, surpassing the V-JEPA Predictor (0.741 Acc, 0.821 AUC). This result supports the benefit of ThinkJEPA for action-free physical-scene forecasting.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies (Tables 5, 6)

- **VLM Token Sources:** Ablations revealed that single-source VLM guidance (e.g., encoder tokens only or autoregressive (AR) tokens only) was insufficient and could degrade performance. ThinkJEPA's gain was attributed to combining both visual grounding (encoder tokens) and generation-side semantic guidance (AR tokens) through pyramid representation extraction.
- **VLM Layer Selection:** The hierarchical all-layer pyramid variant consistently achieved the strongest overall performance compared to using only last-layer or mid-layer VLM guidance. This supports the hypothesis that aggregating multi-depth VLM representations better preserves visual reasoning cues.

#### 4.6. Long-Horizon Recursive Rollout (Table 7)

- ThinkJEPA demonstrated the best performance across all tested horizons (4, 8, 16, 32 frames), significantly outperforming Qwen3-VL Thinking (which degraded sharply) and stabilizing error accumulation better than the V-JEPA Predictor. This suggests that VLM-guided latent forecasting helps stabilize iterative prediction while maintaining dense dynamics modeling.

#### 4.7. Qualitative Results (Figure 2)

- Visualizations of predicted hand trajectories showed that ThinkJEPA produced smoother, more plausible, and more accurate trajectories with better temporal consistency and joint alignment. The V-JEPA baseline often exhibited temporally collapsed predictions, while the VLM-only baseline sometimes produced hallucinations (e.g., non-existent hands).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "ThinkJEPA" offers a notable advancement in latent world modeling by effectively integrating semantic reasoning from large Vision-Language Models (VLMs) with the fine-grained dynamics modeling of JEPA-style architectures.

**Significance of Findings:**
- The core contribution is the successful development and validation of a dual-temporal pathway framework. This design reconciles the contrasting needs for dense temporal observations in dynamics prediction and sparse, long-horizon context in semantic understanding, which has been a challenge for previous single-pathway models.
- The introduction of a hierarchical pyramid representation extraction module for VLMs is significant. It demonstrates that leveraging multi-depth VLM features, rather than relying solely on terminal or single-layer representations, provides richer and more effective guidance for latent predictors. This addresses the "language-output bottleneck" by allowing the JEPA predictor to benefit from the VLM’s internal visual reasoning process.
- The consistent outperformance of ThinkJEPA across diverse benchmarks (egocentric trajectory prediction, robotic latent rollout, physical-scene forecasting) suggests that VLM-guided JEPA-style latent forecasting creates a more robust and generalizable interface for downstream world model prediction tasks. This demonstrates that combining the strengths of both paradigms can yield synergistic benefits that neither can achieve in isolation.
- The ablation studies underscore the importance of the specific architectural choices, particularly the dual-temporal sampling and the aggregation of multi-depth VLM signals, validating the design decisions. The improved performance in long-horizon recursive rollout further highlights the framework's ability to maintain coherence and accuracy over extended temporal sequences, which is crucial for real-world applications.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Enhanced World Models:** ThinkJEPA offers a blueprint for building more semantically grounded and robust world models. By incorporating general knowledge and high-level reasoning from VLMs, these models could better understand complex interactions and predict future states more accurately, even in novel or varied scenarios where purely visual cues might be ambiguous or insufficient.
- **Advancements in Embodied AI and Robotics:** Improved latent forecasting capabilities can directly benefit embodied AI agents and robotic systems. More accurate prediction of future states, especially in dynamic environments with human interaction, could lead to better planning, control, and decision-making for tasks like dexterous manipulation, navigation, and human-robot collaboration.
- **Improved Video Understanding:** Beyond robotic control, the framework could contribute to more sophisticated video understanding systems, enabling them to not only recognize actions but also predict their consequences with greater semantic and physical accuracy.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** This work opens new avenues for research, particularly in exploring how to scale VLM guidance mechanisms for even longer and more diverse videos, and how to extend this predictive framework to closed-loop planning and broader agentic tasks.

**Limitations and Future Directions (as identified by the authors):**
- **Focus on Predictive Latent Modeling:** The current work primarily focuses on predictive latent modeling and does not fully explore closed-loop planning or policy deployment. Extending ThinkJEPA to agentic settings where world models are used for planning and decision-making is a critical future step.
- **Generalization Challenges:** Current latent world models, including V-JEPA2 and DINO-WM, still face challenges in generalizing across highly diverse scenarios. Stronger VLM thinkers with broader world knowledge could further enhance this aspect.
- **Scalability to Longer/Diverse Videos:** Scaling ThinkJEPA to handle much longer and more diverse videos may require more efficient VLM feature caching and more adaptive temporal guidance mechanisms.

**Broader Impacts:**
The authors acknowledge potential positive impacts in improving video understanding, embodied AI, and robotic learning by addressing the lack of high-level semantic grounding in existing latent world models. However, they also highlight societal risks associated with deploying predictive world models in decision-making or robotics systems without sufficient validation, such as incorrect forecasts, biased data, or privacy concerns related to egocentric video data. Mitigations include careful dataset governance, privacy-preserving data handling, uncertainty estimation, human oversight, and restricted deployment in safety-critical settings. The authors view ThinkJEPA as a step towards more semantically grounded and potentially safer world models, emphasizing that it is not intended as a standalone decision-making system.
