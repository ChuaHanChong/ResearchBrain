# In-Context VLA: Endowing Vision-Language-Action Models with Language via In-Context Post-Training and Agentic Tool Use

> [!summary] Summary
> The VLA-Talker framework enhances Vision-Language-Action models by enabling them to consume externally acquired, grounded language, rather than generating free-form textual reasoning. It achieves state-of-the-art success rates, such as 97.4% on LIBERO, while significantly improving inference efficiency, running approximately 4.6 times faster than generative Chain-of-Thought methods.

## Problem

- Standard VLA models trained via behavior cloning exhibit opaque instruction interpretation, struggling with paraphrased instructions and passive observation processing.
- Generative Chain-of-Thought (CoT) for VLAs suffers from a 'grounding gap' (rationales lack new evidence), 'objective interference' (language generation loss dominates), and substantial inference latency.
- Existing approaches do not effectively equip VLA models with genuine language competence to interpret diverse linguistic expressions for the same underlying geometric facts.

## Method

- **VLA-Talker** utilizes an ==agentic tool-use loop== that queries external perception modules (e.g., open-vocabulary detectors, depth estimators) to acquire precise, grounded spatial evidence.
- This acquired evidence is converted into diverse, paraphrased natural language descriptions and injected as ==read-only context== into the VLA model's prompt, with supervision applied only to action tokens.
- ==Trajectory-level reinforcement learning (GRPO)== further fine-tunes the policy, aligning tool invocation and action timing with sparse, task-level success rewards.

## Results

- Achieved state-of-the-art average success rates in simulation (e.g., **97.4%** on LIBERO, **59.5%** on RoboCasa-GR1) and on a real-world AgiBot G1 platform (**58.1%** single-task, **45.0%** multi-task).
- Demonstrated superior inference efficiency, with a per-decision latency of **78 ms** (**12.8 Hz**), which is approximately **4.6 times faster** than generative CoT methods (359 ms/decision, 2.8 Hz).
- Showcased enhanced data efficiency, outperforming behavior cloning with half the demonstration budget, and improved generalization to unseen objects, distractors, and diverse linguistic phrasings.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - For low-level robot control, enabling VLA models to consume externally acquired and grounded language is more effective and efficient than requiring them to generate free-form reasoning.
> - Decoupling evidence acquisition from its use and supervising only action tokens eliminates objective interference and the grounding gap prevalent in generative CoT approaches.
> - Training with diverse, paraphrased spatial descriptions in the context allows the policy to build robust language competence, leading to better generalization across linguistic variations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have emerged as a prominent approach for developing generalist robot policies, typically by fine-tuning pre-trained vision-language backbones to generate low-level action tokens. A common training paradigm for these models is behavior cloning, where the policy imitates expert action sequences conditioned on an image and a fixed instruction. While simple and data-efficient, behavior cloning has known limitations, including opaque understanding of instructions (leading to fragility with paraphrasing) and passive observation processing (inability to actively seek missing information).

In response to these limitations, the broader research community has explored incorporating explicit reasoning, such notably Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting, into VLA models. CoT-based methods involve the model first generating a textual (or visual) rationale before predicting actions, aiming to improve temporal planning and high-level task performance. However, existing work on free-form generative CoT for VLAs has identified several challenges, such as the rationale lacking new evidence (grounding gap), the language generation objective interfering with low-level action control, and substantial inference latency.

This paper positions itself by proposing an alternative approach to enhance VLA language competence. Instead of the model generating free-form reasoning, it argues for the necessity of VLA models to *consume* externally acquired, grounded language. This perspective draws a parallel to human operators who acquire specific facts ("the mug is to the left of the plate") and then act, rather than verbalizing extensive paragraphs before each motion. The work integrates with the growing field of tool-augmented and agentic VLAs, which delegate specific perceptual or reasoning tasks to external modules to improve robustness and generalization. By addressing the identified shortcomings of generative CoT and existing behavior cloning methods, this research aims to provide a more effective and efficient framework for language-conditioned robot manipulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to enhance Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models with genuine language competence, specifically by enabling them to effectively *consume* grounded language rather than primarily *generating* free-form textual reasoning. This objective is motivated by several identified limitations of current VLA training paradigms:

1. **Limitations of Standard Behavior Cloning:**
 - **Opaque Instruction Interpretation:** Existing VLA models, when trained solely via behavior cloning, treat natural language instructions as opaque conditioning strings. This results in models memorizing specific phrases rather than understanding the underlying intent, causing performance degradation when instructions are paraphrased or unseen synonyms for objects are used.
 - **Passive Observation:** The policy consumes observations in a single feed-forward pass, preventing it from actively seeking out missing information. The model is forced to make inferences or guesses even when crucial information might be readily available through targeted perception.

2. **Identified Failures of Generative Chain-of-Thought (CoT) for VLAs:** While CoT was hypothesized to improve reasoning, the authors empirically and analytically identify three coupled failures in free-form generative CoT for low-level control:
 - **Grounding Gap:** The textual rationales generated by CoT methods often derive from the same static visual features already available to the action head. Consequently, the rationale provides no new perceptual evidence and can actively mislead the action head if it hallucinates incorrect information (e.g., misidentifying an object's location).
 - **Objective Interference:** The training objective for generative CoT typically combines supervision for language tokens (the rationale) and action tokens. Because language rationales can comprise hundreds of tokens compared to a few action tokens, the language generation objective often dominates the loss. This imbalance can push the policy to become a fluent narrator at the expense of precise and accurate action execution.
 - **Latency and Drift:** Autoregressive generation of potentially hundreds of rationale tokens per decision introduces significant latency, breaking the closed-loop timing necessary for responsive robot manipulation. Furthermore, any errors or inaccuracies in the early parts of the long autoregressive rationale prefix can propagate and contaminate the subsequent action suffix.

To address these motivations, the authors propose a fundamental shift in perspective: a VLA model does not primarily need the ability to *generate* language; rather, it needs the ability to *consume* grounded language. This leads to the following key objectives for the VLA-Talker framework:

- **Decouple Evidence Acquisition from Evidence Use:** Separate the process of gathering task-relevant information from the policy's use of that information to formulate actions.
- **Acquire Grounded Evidence Agentically:** Utilize external, specialized perception tools (e.g., open-vocabulary object detectors, depth estimators, vision-language models) in an agentic loop to actively acquire precise, grounded spatial and relational information.
- **Inject Evidence as Read-Only Context:** Integrate this acquired, structured evidence into the VLA policy's input prompt as a read-only context, without requiring the policy to generate it.
- **Train for Diverse Language Interpretation:** Enable the policy to interpret a wide variety of linguistic expressions for the same underlying geometric facts by rendering diverse, paraphrased spatial descriptions. This aims to build genuine language competence robust to novel phrasing.
- **Align Tool Use with Task Success:** Employ trajectory-level reinforcement learning to align when the policy invokes its perception tools with actual task success, bridging the gap between per-token imitation and overall task outcomes.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VLA-Talker is a framework designed to imbue VLA models with language competence by enabling them to consume grounded language. It consists of two primary components: an agentic tool-use loop for acquiring grounded spatial evidence, and an in-context post-training scheme that injects this evidence as read-only context, followed by a trajectory-level reinforcement learning stage.

#### 3.1. Problem Setup and Notation

The policy observes an RGB image ($o_t$), proprioception ($s_t$), and a fixed natural-language instruction ($\ell$). It emits an action chunk ($a_{t:t+H}$) consisting of 7-DoF end-effector commands. Unlike standard behavior cloning which optimizes $L_{BC}(\theta) = -\sum_{k=0}^{H-1} \log \pi_\theta (a_{t+k} | o_t, s_t, \ell, a_{t:t+k})$, VLA-Talker extends the observation with an evidence context ($c_t$). The training objective becomes $L(\theta) = -\sum_{k=0}^{H-1} \log \pi_\theta (a_{t+k} | o_t, s_t, \ell, c_t, a_{t:t+k})$. Crucially, no token of $c_t$ contributes to the loss.

#### 3.2. Agentic Tool Use for Grounded Evidence Acquisition

At designated keyframes, the policy delegates information gathering to a suite of perception tools. This agentic loop aims to acquire image-space locations and relative depths of the gripper and task-relevant objects.

- **Depth and Relative Geometry:** A monocular depth estimator (e.g., DepthAnything) generates a normalized depth map. This allows for sampling relative depths at gripper and object pixels, enabling the agent to infer height ordering (e.g., "object above gripper").
- **Object Localization (Open-Vocabulary + Fallback):**
 - Target and destination object names are extracted from the instruction.
 - An open-vocabulary detector (e.g., GroundingDino) localizes each object, providing a pixel centroid.
 - In cases of detector uncertainty or failure, an agentic fallback queries a Vision-Language Model (VLM) locator (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-7B) for approximate coordinates or qualitative spatial descriptions. This detector-then-VLM cascade ensures robust object localization.
- **Gripper Localization:** The end-effector's world position (from proprioception) is projected into image space using known camera intrinsics and extrinsics, yielding an exact gripper pixel location without requiring learning.
- **Evidence Tuple:** The tool loop outputs a structured evidence tuple ($c_{raw_t}$) containing gripper and object coordinates, depths, and explicit gripper-object and object-object relations.

#### 3.3. Caption Rendering

To prevent the policy from overfitting to specific linguistic templates, the raw evidence tuple ($c_{raw_t}$) is converted into diverse, paraphrased natural language descriptions. These descriptions are wrapped in `<spatial>...</spatial>` tags and vary along several axes:
- **Modality of Reference:** Absolute coordinates, relative offsets, or qualitative descriptions.
- **Referential Frame:** Egocentric (from gripper), allocentric (from camera/table), or object-relative.
- **Lexical and Syntactic Paraphrase:** Varying synonyms for objects, spatial prepositions, verb phrases, and sentence structure.
- **Depth Verbalization:** Numeric differences, comparative terms, or actionable hints.
- **Verbosity and Granularity:** Short sentences or longer multi-sentence captions.
- **Evidence-Conditioned Content:** The detail and precision of the description reflect the reliability of the underlying evidence (e.g., coordinate-rich for detector success, descriptive for VLM fallback).
A "round-trip consistency filtering" mechanism parses rendered sentences back to verify their agreement with the original evidence, discarding unfaithful realizations to maintain grounding.

#### 3.4. In-Context Post-Training

The rendered context ($c_t$) is injected into the VLM alongside the task instruction and image. The crucial aspect is the supervision mask: the loss is applied *only* to the action tokens and the preceding separator, while all instruction and `<spatial>` tokens are masked out. This ensures the model learns to *condition* on $c_t$ without learning to *produce* it, eliminating objective interference and autoregressive generation latency. Evidence injection occurs only on keyframes (initial, gripper state changes, periodic checks) to optimize efficiency, otherwise $c_t$ remains empty.

#### 3.5. Trajectory-Level RL for Causal Alignment

As a final stage, the VLA-Talker policy is further fine-tuned using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) to align complete perception-reasoning-action trajectories with sparse, task-level success rewards.
- **Trajectory and Reward:** Rollouts produce trajectories combining reasoning/decision steps, perception tool calls, returned evidence, and executed action chunks. The reward is sparse, assigned for verifiable task success ($I_{success}$) and a small format regularization for valid tool calls ($I_{format}$).
- **Group-Relative Advantage:** Advantage is estimated by normalizing trajectory rewards within a group of M sampled trajectories, reducing variance in sparse-reward settings.
- **Objective:** GRPO optimizes a clipped surrogate objective with a KL anchor to the initial in-context policy ($\pi_{ref}$). Only action tokens contribute to the importance ratio, ensuring RL refines *when and how to act* on evidence, not how to generate text.

#### 3.6. Implementation Details

The system initializes from OpenVLA-OFT weights. Tools include GroundingDino (open-vocabulary detector), DepthAnything (depth estimator), and Qwen2.5-VL-7B (VLM locator fallback). Training is two-stage: supervised in-context cold-start followed by GRPO, with specific batch sizes, learning rates, and AdamW optimizer.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The VLA-Talker framework demonstrates consistent performance improvements and efficiencies across various benchmarks and real-world scenarios.

1. **State-of-the-Art Performance in Simulation:**
 - **LIBERO:** Achieved a new state-of-the-art average success rate of 97.4%, outperforming generative CoT (Gen-CoT) and other VLA baselines by significant margins.
 - **RoboCasa-GR1:** Achieved the best average success rate of 59.5% on a challenging large-scale kitchen manipulation benchmark, demonstrating superiority over Gen-CoT and other top methods.
 - **SimplerEnv:** Achieved the highest average success rate of 72.4% on a WidowX manipulation suite designed to stress generalization, with a notable margin over Gen-CoT.

2. **Superior Inference Efficiency:** VLA-Talker significantly reduced inference latency compared to CoT-based methods. It achieved a per-decision latency of 78 ms (12.8 Hz control frequency), which is approximately 4.6 times faster than Gen-CoT (359 ms/decision, 2.8 Hz). This efficiency is attributed to avoiding autoregressive rationale generation during inference.

3. **Benefits of Grounded Evidence Injection and Action-Only Supervision:**
 - Ablation studies confirmed that the primary benefit stems not merely from having evidence, but from *injecting* it as read-only context and supervising *only* the action tokens. This configuration consistently yielded higher accuracy compared to generating the evidence or injecting it while still supervising the text tokens.
 - This finding validates the hypothesis that decoupling language consumption from generation eliminates objective interference and leads to more robust action policies.

4. **Enhanced Data Efficiency:** VLA-Talker exhibited improved data efficiency across varying demonstration budgets. With only 25 demonstrations per task, it surpassed the performance of a Behavior Cloning (BC) baseline trained on 50 demonstrations. Gen-CoT consistently performed below BC at all data budgets.

5. **Complementary Training Stages:**
 - The in-context post-training stage alone (SFT-only) significantly boosted the backbone model's performance (e.g., from 90.4% to 95.6% on LIBERO), confirming that grounded evidence consumption is the main driver of initial gains.
 - The subsequent trajectory-level RL (GRPO) stage further improved performance (e.g., an additional +1.8% on LIBERO), indicating its role in aligning tool invocation and action timing with task-level outcomes. GRPO applied without the in-context initialization was found to be unstable.

6. **Improved Generalization and Robustness:**
 - **Unseen Objects and Distractors:** VLA-Talker degraded gracefully when faced with unseen object categories or visual distractors, whereas BC and Gen-CoT showed significant performance drops. This is because VLA-Talker's agentic tool loop resolves target identity explicitly.
 - **Robustness to Phrasing:** The diverse caption rendering approach enabled VLA-Talker to maintain high performance with paraphrased instructions and unseen object synonyms, suggesting genuine language interpretation rather than surface-form memorization.
 - **Statistical Significance:** VLA-Talker demonstrated lower variance across multiple random seeds compared to baselines, indicating increased training stability and reliability.

7. **Real-World Robot Deployment:**
 - Evaluations on an AgiBot G1 humanoid platform across eight tabletop manipulation tasks confirmed VLA-Talker's effectiveness in the physical world.
 - It achieved the best average success rates in both single-task (58.1%) and multi-task (45.0%) regimes, outperforming plain behavior cloning and the matched-evidence generative CoT variant by substantial margins. The improvements were particularly pronounced in fine-grained, small-object manipulation tasks.

8. **Error Analysis:** Manual error decomposition revealed that VLA-Talker's dominant residual failure mode was control precision in contact-rich placements, while perception errors were rare. In contrast, Gen-CoT was primarily limited by grounding errors, where fluent but ungrounded rationales led to incorrect actions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The VLA-Talker framework presents a significant contribution to the field of embodied AI and robot learning, with several potential impacts:

1. **Paradigm Shift in VLA Language Competence:** The work fundamentally re-evaluates how VLA models should integrate language, advocating for *grounded language understanding* over *generative language reasoning* for low-level control. This challenges the prevailing Chain-of-Thought approach in VLAs and offers a robust alternative. The implication is that for real-time, precise robotic actions, explicit, external grounding is more beneficial than internal, potentially ungrounded, reasoning generation.

2. **Enhanced Performance and Efficiency for Robotic Systems:** By achieving state-of-the-art success rates across diverse simulation and real-world benchmarks, coupled with a substantial 4.6x inference speedup over generative CoT methods, VLA-Talker offers a highly practical and effective solution for developing generalist robot policies. The improved efficiency directly translates to higher control frequencies, which is critical for dynamic and responsive robot manipulation tasks.

3. **Improved Robustness and Generalization:** The framework's ability to generalize to unseen objects, distractors, and diverse linguistic phrasings represents a crucial step towards more adaptable and robust robot agents. This is achieved through the modular design that leverages specialized, open-vocabulary perception tools for explicit grounding, making the policy less susceptible to visual or linguistic variations present in real-world environments.

4. **Increased Data Efficiency for Robot Learning:** Demonstrating that VLA-Talker can achieve superior performance with fewer expert demonstrations (e.g., surpassing BC with half the data) is highly impactful. Data collection for robot learning is often time-consuming and expensive, so methods that can reduce data requirements accelerate the development and deployment of new robotic skills.

5. **Foundation for More Intelligent Embodied Agents:** The modular, agentic architecture, which actively queries external tools for information, contributes to the development of more intelligent and information-seeking embodied agents. This design allows for easier integration of advanced perception or reasoning modules and provides a pathway for agents to dynamically acquire necessary information, akin to human cognitive processes.

6. **Guidance for Future Research:** The identified dominant residual failure mode (control precision in contact-rich tasks) provides clear direction for future research. This suggests that combining VLA-Talker's grounded evidence injection with more advanced low-level control representations or techniques (e.g., finer action chunking, visual servoing) could further close the performance gap. Additionally, improving the heuristic keyframe gating to a learned, adaptive tool invocation strategy is a promising avenue.

In summary, VLA-Talker advances the state of the art in VLA models by providing a framework that is both highly performant and efficient, laying groundwork for more capable, robust, and data-efficient robot learning systems in complex, real-world scenarios.
