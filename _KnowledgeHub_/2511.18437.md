# Perceptual-Evidence Anchored Reinforced Learning for Multimodal Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> PEARL (Perceptual-Evidence Anchored Reinforced Learning) introduces a dual-branch, perception-reasoning framework to ground multimodal reasoning in verifiable visual evidence, addressing the limitation of existing RL methods that neglect upstream visual perception. The approach significantly reduces both perception and reasoning errors, achieving consistent performance gains of up to 9.7% on benchmarks like MathVerse while also proving more computationally efficient than prior methods.

## Problem

- Existing Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) methods for Vision-Language Models (VLMs) primarily verify textual output, overlooking the accuracy of visual perception.
- This oversight causes visual hallucinations, reward hacking, and ungrounded reasoning, limiting performance and trustworthiness.
- Previous attempts to integrate perceptual grounding faced issues such as noisy reward signals from free-form text or high computational costs.

## Method

- **PEARL** constructs a "==perception checklist==" for each instance, comprising simple, verifiable sub-questions derived using operational guidelines and targeting specific visual skills.
- It implements a "Fidelity Gate (Early-Stop Filtering)" to halt reasoning rollouts and policy updates for instances where visual perception fails, preventing training on flawed visual understanding.
- A synergistic optimization scheme integrates perceptual rewards directly into the reasoning advantage and uses a dual-objective formulation to balance perception and reasoning gradient updates.

## Results

- PEARL concurrently and substantially reduced both perception and reasoning errors, overcoming the limitations of standard RLVR methods that only address reasoning errors.
- The approach consistently outperformed supervised and RLVR baselines, achieving up to **9.7%** absolute improvement on MathVerse and significant gains across six multimodal reasoning benchmarks.
- It demonstrated robust scalability, improving performance on larger models like Qwen2.5-VL-**32B** and preventing degradation on advanced models such as Qwen3-VL-**8B** where standard RLVR failed.
- PEARL exhibited lower computational overhead compared to other RL methods due to its early-stop filtering strategy and soft online filtering, leading to more efficient training.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Outcome-based RLVR significantly reduces reasoning errors in VLMs but largely fails to address underlying perception errors, leading to "spurious reasoning chains" and a performance ceiling.
> - Directly anchoring reasoning to verifiable perceptual evidence, through structured perception checklists and fidelity gates, is essential for truly grounded and reliable multimodal intelligence.
> - The design of perceptual supervision, prioritizing task-relevant, concise, and verifiable probes, is more effective than simply increasing the volume or density of general visual descriptions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of multimodal reasoning, Vision-Language Models (VLMs), and Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR). The broader landscape has seen significant advancements in Large Language Models (LLMs) through RLVR, which optimizes their reasoning capabilities by providing rewards based on the correctness of their textual outputs. This success has naturally extended to VLMs, aiming to imbue them with similar reasoning prowess over visual and textual inputs.

However, the paper identifies a critical and pervasive flaw in the application of "vanilla" RLVR to VLMs: it typically verifies only the *final textual output*, critically neglecting the fidelity of the *upstream visual perception*. This oversight leads to several significant problems:
1. **Visual Hallucinations:** Models generate outputs that are ungrounded in the actual visual evidence.
2. **Reward Hacking:** Models learn to exploit spurious correlations or generate logically plausible reasoning steps that ultimately lead to a correct answer, even if their foundational visual understanding is flawed.
3. **Unreliable Reasoning:** Reasoning built upon misinterpretations of visual facts is inherently unreliable, posing a "hard ceiling" on performance and trustworthiness.

Existing research has attempted to address the challenges of perceptual grounding in VLMs through various approaches:
- **Interactive mechanisms:** Some models are designed to interact directly with visual input (e.g., selecting regions, drawing, highlighting). While this encourages better visual attention, these methods often require extensive, carefully curated training data, limiting their generalizability and scalability.
- **Implicit alignment:** Other works try to implicitly align perception and reasoning, for example, by decoupling the two via an intermediate image description stage or by probing models under altered visual states (e.g., masked input). These "condition-based" methods aim to ensure reasoning consistency but may lack explicit, strong supervisory signals for perception.
- **Describe-then-reason paradigms:** These approaches encourage models to generate structured image descriptions or captions prior to reasoning, with rewards derived from external reward models or heuristic scoring functions. However, the paper argues that such reward signals are often noisy and ambiguous, as quantifying the "correctness" of free-form text is challenging and prone to "perception reward hacking." Moreover, relying on additional reward models or LLM-based judges introduces substantial computational and implementation overhead.

The current paper, "Perceptual-Evidence Anchored Reinforced Learning (PEARL)," directly confronts these limitations. It proposes a novel RL paradigm that explicitly integrates verifiable perceptual feedback into the training loop, thereby preventing the entanglement of flawed perception and downstream reasoning. By forging a robust, verifiable link between "seeing" and "thinking," PEARL aims to address a fundamental bottleneck in multimodal reasoning, pushing beyond the limitations of outcome-only RLVR and noisy perception-aware methods. It represents a significant step towards more reliable and truly grounded multimodal AI systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from a critical, empirically validated flaw in existing Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) methods when applied to Vision-Language Models (VLMs). The authors observe that while RLVR successfully enhances the *textual reasoning* capabilities of LLMs and VLMs, it often does so by verifying only the *final textual output*, completely neglecting the fidelity of the underlying visual perception.

To empirically support this claim, the authors conduct a preliminary diagnostic analysis. They finetune a leading VLM (Qwen2.5-VL) using a representative outcome-based RLVR method, GRPO, and analyze its failure modes on the WeMath benchmark. The results are stark: standard GRPO training significantly reduces *reasoning errors* (flawed logic, incorrect calculations) but leaves *perception errors* (misinterpreting visual facts, diagram elements) almost unchanged (as shown in Figure 2). This creates a scenario where the model learns to generate plausible reasoning steps that lead to a correct answer, but these steps are often based on fundamentally flawed visual understanding. This phenomenon, which the authors term "spurious reasoning chains," highlights a "critical bottleneck" that imposes a "hard ceiling on both performance and reliability" for multimodal reasoning models.

Given this fundamental limitation, the **key objectives** of this research are:
1. **Explicitly Cultivate Perceptual Competence:** Develop a mechanism to directly reinforce the model's ability to accurately extract and understand visual information.
2. **Anchor Reasoning in Verified Visual Evidence:** Ensure that the reasoning process is firmly grounded in correct visual perception, preventing reward hacking and ungrounded outputs.
3. **Develop a Synergistic RL Paradigm:** Create a unified framework where perception and reasoning capabilities are learned and optimized concurrently and dependently.
4. **Demonstrate Effectiveness and Generalizability:** Validate that the proposed approach leads to substantial improvements on challenging multimodal reasoning benchmarks and can generalize across different datasets and model scales.

In essence, the paper aims to compel VLMs to "learn how to reason correctly only when it has proven it can see correctly," thereby paving the way for more reliable and capable multimodal reasoning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, **PEARL (Perceptual-Evidence Anchored Reinforced Learning)**, introduces a dual-branch, perception-reasoning synergistic RL paradigm. It is designed to strengthen multimodal reasoning by explicitly anchoring it to verified visual evidence. PEARL builds upon existing RLVR frameworks like GRPO and DAPO but extends them to incorporate perceptual fidelity.

The core components of PEARL's methodology are:

#### 3.1. Perception Checklist Construction

Unlike methods relying on noisy, free-form image descriptions, PEARL generates a "perception checklist" for each reasoning instance. These checklists consist of a series of simple, factual, perception-oriented sub-questions with verifiable answers. This approach ensures high-quality, easily verifiable supervision directly relevant to the reasoning task.
The checklist construction operates along two primary dimensions:
- **Content Derivation:** "Operation guidelines" instruct a VLM (GPT-4.1 is used) to generate questions based on four patterns:
 1. **Direct Extraction:** Surfacing explicit facts from the image or question.
 2. **Pattern Summary:** Isolating salient regions or structural cues.
 3. **Derived Calculation:** Requiring a one-step inference based on visual patterns.
 4. **Inverse from Answer:** Using the original answer as a constraint to recover latent information.
- **Targeted Perceptual Skill:** Each sub-question probes a specific low-level skill, such as identifying objects, reading trends, counting quantities, or recognizing geometric arrangements.
This dual-dimension design ensures the generated sub-questions are logically tied to the original task, serving as a verifiable "perception checklist" with concise, high-quality, and verifiable answers (e.g., a number or label), suitable for RLVR training.

#### 3.2. Perception and Reasoning Rollouts

PEARL employs distinct rollouts for perception and reasoning.
- **Perception-Oriented Rollouts:** For a given image and its primary reasoning question, the VLM is first prompted with a compact perception prompt that serializes all perception checklist probes. Multiple outputs are generated, and a rule-based verifier assigns probe-level rewards for each prediction. These are averaged to compute $\bar{R}_p$, the mean perceptual reward, which quantifies the model's visual perception on the image.
- **Reasoning-Oriented Rollouts:** These follow the standard RLVR procedure (e.g., GRPO), where the VLM generates step-by-step reasoning and a final answer for the primary reasoning question, receiving a reasoning reward $R_r$.

Crucially, PEARL introduces a **Fidelity Gate (Early-Stop Filtering)**: if $\bar{R}_p = 0$ (indicating a perception failure), the reasoning rollouts for that instance are *halted*. The policy update for the main reasoning task is only permitted if $\bar{R}_p > 0$ ("perception-passes" instance). This mechanism directly combats reward hacking by preventing the model from reinforcing spurious reasoning chains built on faulty perception, while also reducing training costs.

#### 3.3. Synergistic Optimization

PEARL integrates perceptual feedback directly into the policy optimization:
- **Perception-Reweight Reasoning:** The mean perceptual reward $\bar{R}_p$ is used as a soft reliability prior to modulate the reasoning advantage $\hat{A}_r$. Specifically, $\hat{A}_r \leftarrow \hat{A}_r \cdot \min(\bar{R}_p, 0.5)$. This mechanism amplifies the gradients for perception-anchored reasoning and suppresses updates that are inconsistent with the visual evidence, biasing the optimization towards perceptually consistent solutions.
- **Soft Online Filtering:** Extending DAPO's filtering, PEARL retains a sample group if $\bar{R}_r \notin \lor \bar{R}_p \notin$. This ensures that instances with a meaningful signal from *either* the reasoning or perception path contribute to learning, preventing loss of valuable perceptual gradients in cases where reasoning rewards might have saturated.
- **Dual-Objective Formulation:** The final objective combines the GRPO objective for reasoning with a GRPO objective for perception: $J_{dual}(\theta) = J_{GRPO}(\theta; \hat{A}_r) + \lambda J_{GRPO}(\theta; \hat{A}_p)$. The hyperparameter $\lambda$ (set to 0.1) controls the relative contribution of the perception path to the overall gradient update.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

PEARL is implemented on the EasyR1 codebase, using the AdamW optimizer. It employs Qwen2.5-VL-3B, Qwen2.5-VL-7B, Qwen2.5-VL-32B, and Qwen3-VL-8B as base models, trained on the ViRL39K dataset for main experiments, and additionally on MMK12 and Geo3K for generalization studies. Evaluation is performed on six challenging multimodal reasoning benchmarks (MathVista MINI, MathVision, MathVerse Vision-only, DynaMath, WeMath, LogicVista) and several general VQA benchmarks. GPT-4.1 is utilized for constructing the initial perception checklist.

This comprehensive methodology ensures that PEARL not only improves reasoning but grounds it firmly in verifiable visual understanding, addressing a critical gap in current multimodal AI.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted demonstrate PEARL's significant effectiveness and versatility across various multimodal reasoning tasks and model architectures.

#### 4.1. Addressing the Fundamental Flaw

The diagnostic analysis (Figure 2) serves as a crucial foundation, empirically validating the paper's core hypothesis. Standard outcome-based GRPO training effectively reduces *reasoning errors* but fails to mitigate *perception errors*, leading to "spurious reasoning chains." In stark contrast, PEARL achieves concurrent and substantial reductions in *both* perception and reasoning errors, successfully breaking the performance ceiling imposed by faulty visual understanding. This is a primary validation of PEARL's design principle.

#### 4.2. Superior Performance on Multimodal Reasoning Benchmarks

(Table 1)
PEARL consistently outperforms all supervised and RLVR baselines, including GRPO, DAPO, PAPO variants, and Vision-SR1, across six challenging multimodal reasoning benchmarks (MathVerse, MathVision, MathVista, LogicVista, WeMath, DynaMath).
- **Consistent Gains:** PEARL achieves the highest average accuracy across both Qwen2.5-VL-3B (39.8%) and Qwen2.5-VL-7B (47.9%) backbones.
- **Significant Improvements:** For instance, on MathVerse, PEARL demonstrates a +9.7% absolute improvement over the base model and +6.6% over GRPO for the 7B model. Similar substantial gains are observed across other datasets.
- **Targeted Effectiveness:** PEARL yields marked improvements on datasets like WeMath (geometry and diagram-centric) and MathVision (competition-level math problems), indicating its ability to better exploit visual structure and unlock advanced reasoning potential by ensuring perceptual correctness.
- **Enhanced Robustness:** On DynaMath, designed to assess reasoning robustness, PEARL attains competitive or superior results, suggesting that its perception-gated filtering mechanism effectively suppresses brittle, hallucination-prone behaviors.
- **Uniformity of Gains:** Compared to other perception-aware methods, PEARL delivers more uniform gains across all benchmarks and backbones, highlighting the reliable transferability of its probe-based design.

#### 4.3. Generalization of Perception Probe Construction

(Table 2)
The study on the generalization of perception probes confirms that PEARL's methodology is not an artifact of specific datasets.
- When applied to MMK12 and Geo3K datasets, PEARL consistently outperforms the "Reasoning Rewarding" (standard GRPO) baseline across both 3B and 7B models. This validates that PEARL's probes and perception-shaping updates act as a generalizable inductive bias for multimodal reasoning.
- "Perception Rewarding" (training only on probes) also yields non-trivial gains over the supervised baseline, demonstrating that the probes provide a meaningful and transferable supervisory signal, even if not directly solving complex reasoning tasks.

#### 4.4. Ablation on Perception QA Design

(Table 3)
An ablation study comparing the "QA-Anchored Checklist" (PEARL's approach) with a "Caption-Augmented Dense Checklist" (more numerous, richer probes derived from detailed descriptions) reveals a key insight:
- The QA-anchored checklist achieves a higher average score (47.9) than the dense checklist (45.5). This indicates that the alignment and fidelity of perceptual supervision (i.e., task-relevant, concise, verifiable probes) matter more than simply increasing the quantity or coverage of probes.

#### 4.5. Ablation on PEARL Components

(Table 4)
A step-wise ablation study starting from the GRPO baseline progressively reveals the contribution of each PEARL component:
- Adding the **Perception Checklist** alone provides clear average gains, especially on geometry- and vision-intensive benchmarks.
- Incorporating **Soft Online Filtering and removing KL regularization** further improves overall performance.
- Finally, the full PEARL method, including **Perception-Reweight and Early Stop** as gating mechanisms, achieves the best performance across all benchmarks.

#### 4.6. Training Cost Comparison

(Figure 4, Figure 6)
Despite being a dual-path framework, PEARL introduces *lower* computational overhead compared to DAPO. This efficiency gain is attributed to the early-stop filtering strategy and soft online filtering, which reduce unnecessary reasoning rollouts and token consumption, making PEARL practical and cost-effective.

#### 4.7. Statistical Dependency of Reasoning on Perception Probes

(Figure 5)
A post-hoc correlation analysis shows a strong positive correlation between perception success (as measured by PEARL's probes) and reasoning success. Samples with high perception scores exhibit significantly higher reasoning accuracy. This confirms that the generated probes are not irrelevant noise but are precisely coupled to the downstream reasoning tasks, serving as valid indicators of necessary visual understanding.

#### 4.8. Generalization Beyond Reasoning Benchmarks

(Table 8)
PEARL's benefits extend beyond complex reasoning tasks to general and perception-centric VQA benchmarks (e.g., MMMU, AI2d, RealWorldQA, BLINK, CountBench, InfoVQA). It achieves significant improvements on these benchmarks (+6% on MMMU_dev, +9.4% on AI2d), suggesting that the perception-aware reward mechanism enhances overall visual understanding.

#### 4.9. Scalability to Larger and Stronger Models

(Table 6, Table 7)
PEARL demonstrates robust scalability:
- On Qwen2.5-VL-32B, PEARL continues to offer strong and consistent improvements over both the base model and GRPO.
- Crucially, on the highly capable Qwen3-VL-8B model, where standard GRPO training *degrades* performance, PEARL *improves* it (+3% on MathVerse, +5.1% on WeMath). This highlights PEARL's ability to provide a critical supervisory signal even for advanced models, especially for granular visual details that might be implicitly perceived by humans but not explicitly captured in typical text-image datasets.

In summary, the main findings consistently support that PEARL effectively addresses the foundational problem of ungrounded reasoning in VLMs, leading to more accurate, reliable, robust, and efficient multimodal reasoning across various scales and tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "Perceptual-Evidence Anchored Reinforced Learning for Multimodal Reasoning" holds significant implications for the development of more robust and trustworthy Vision-Language Models. Its potential impact spans several key areas:

1. **Fundamental Advancement in Multimodal AI:** PEARL addresses a critical and previously overlooked flaw in the application of Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) to VLMs: the neglect of upstream visual perception. By explicitly anchoring reasoning to verifiable visual evidence, PEARL offers a paradigm shift from outcome-only reward signals to a "perception-reasoning synergistic RL paradigm." This is a foundational step towards genuinely grounded intelligence, where models truly "see" before they "think."

2. **Enhanced Reliability and Trustworthiness:** The demonstrated reduction in both perception and reasoning errors, coupled with the early-stop filtering mechanism that prevents learning from flawed premises, leads to more reliable and robust multimodal reasoning. This is crucial for real-world applications where ungrounded outputs and hallucinations can have severe consequences, such as in medical imaging analysis, autonomous driving, or complex scientific problem-solving. By providing a verifiable link between visual input and reasoning output, PEARL increases the trustworthiness of VLM predictions.

3. **Improved Generalization and Robustness:** The consistent performance gains across diverse, challenging multimodal reasoning benchmarks (MathVerse, WeMath, DynaMath) and general VQA tasks, along with successful generalization to unseen datasets and larger models, indicates that PEARL instills a more fundamental and transferable understanding. This makes VLMs less prone to spurious correlations and more adaptable to novel scenarios.

4. **Efficiency in RL Training:** Counterintuitively, despite being a dual-path framework, PEARL introduces lower computational overhead than comparable RL methods like DAPO. The early-stop filtering strategy and soft online filtering efficiently prune unpromising reasoning paths, leading to faster and more economical training. This practical advantage makes PEARL more accessible for large-scale deployment and research.

5. **Insights into Data Curation and Model Training:** The findings, particularly with advanced models like Qwen3-VL-8B, highlight a critical challenge: highly granular visual details (e.g., spatial relationships, subtle object attributes) are often implicitly understood by humans but rarely explicitly documented in text-image datasets. PEARL's ability to improve such models where standard RLVR fails suggests the necessity of perception-aware training to imbue models with these crucial, often implicit, visual competencies. This could guide future efforts in data annotation and synthetic data generation for VLMs.

6. **Scalability and Applicability to Advanced Models:** PEARL's demonstrated scalability to larger model architectures (Qwen2.5-VL-32B) and its ability to improve even highly capable foundation models (Qwen3-VL-8B) is a significant achievement. It shows that even state-of-the-art models can benefit from explicit perceptual grounding, suggesting that current training regimes for these models might still be missing crucial perceptual signals.

In conclusion, PEARL offers a principled and effective solution to a long-standing challenge in multimodal AI. By ensuring that reasoning is firmly anchored in verifiable visual evidence, it contributes significantly towards building more intelligent, reliable, and interpretable VLMs, pushing the frontier of truly grounded artificial intelligence. This work serves as an important step towards developing AI systems that not only generate correct answers but also derive them from a correct understanding of the world.
