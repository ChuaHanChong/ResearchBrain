# Text-Aware Diffusion for Policy Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> TADPoLe (Text-Aware Diffusion for Policy Learning) introduces a method for zero-shot policy learning by using frozen, pre-trained text-conditioned diffusion models to generate dense reward signals. The approach enables agents to learn complex and natural behaviors, outperforming existing language-conditioned baselines in simulated Humanoid, Dog, and Meta-World environments without requiring manual reward engineering or expert demonstrations.

## Problem

- Manually designing precise reward functions for diverse and intricate behaviors in reinforcement learning is often intractable and unscalable.
- Existing language-conditioned policy learning methods typically rely on costly expert demonstrations or require fine-tuning generative models on environment-specific data, limiting zero-shot generalization.
- Traditional RL approaches struggle to produce behaviors that are not only goal-achieving but also qualitatively natural or human-perceptive.

## Method

- **TADPoLe** introduces a framework that uses ==frozen, pre-trained text-to-image or text-to-video diffusion models== to directly compute ==dense, zero-shot reward signals== for reinforcement learning policies.
- The reward signal is derived from the diffusion model's ability to ==predict noise==, measuring both the ==alignment== of an observed frame/sequence with a text prompt and its ==naturalness (reconstruction quality)== through ==conditional and unconditional noise predictions==.
- Rewards are normalized using a ==`symlog` transformation== to ensure robust performance across different tasks and environments, and the approach is agnostic to the underlying RL algorithm, demonstrating its compatibility with ==TD-MPC==.

## Results

- TADPoLe enables zero-shot learning of novel behaviors (e.g., "a person doing splits") with higher human evaluation agreement for text-alignment (e.g., **84%** for splits) and naturalness (**62.5-87.5%** preference) compared to baselines.
- Video-TADPoLe significantly outperforms baselines in continuous locomotion tasks (e.g., "a person walking" achieving **145.60** cumulative ground-truth reward vs. **25.51** for ViCLIP-RM) by fostering more coherent and natural motions.
- The method achieves higher average success rates on diverse Meta-World robotic manipulation tasks (**42.2%** vs. **20.8%** for VLM-RM) and successfully supervises policy learning in synthetic visual environments without fine-tuning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Pre-trained, frozen text-conditioned diffusion models, trained on internet-scale data, encode rich implicit priors about naturalness and text-visual alignment.
> - These diffusion models can be used to automatically compute dense, zero-shot reward signals by evaluating how likely rendered observations are given a text prompt, eliminating the need for manual reward engineering or expert demonstrations.
> - Formulating reward signals based on the difference between conditional and unconditional noise predictions (alignment) and the ability to reconstruct original noise (naturalness) allows for guiding policies towards both goal achievement and qualitatively natural behaviors.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of reinforcement learning (RL) has achieved remarkable successes in training agents for complex tasks. However, a significant bottleneck remains the manual design of reward functions. Crafting appropriate rewards for novel or nuanced behaviors is often intractable and unscalable, especially when moving beyond simple objective functions like reaching a target or maintaining balance. This challenge is particularly acute in applications such as character animation or versatile robotic manipulation, where a vast array of behaviors is desired.

To address this, researchers have explored methods to specify desired behaviors more flexibly, with natural language emerging as a powerful interface. Prior work in language-conditioned policy learning can be broadly categorized:
- **Demonstration-based approaches:** Methods like LangLfP, Text-Conditioned Decision Transformer, and Hiveformer learn policies from datasets of trajectories annotated with natural language. While effective, they are limited by the need for extensive, often human-generated, labeled demonstrations, which are costly to acquire and lack zero-shot generalization to truly novel behaviors.
- **Generative Model-based Planning/Reward:** Some works treat sequential decision-making as a video generation problem. UniPi and VLP train video diffusion models *ad-hoc* on target environments to produce future visual plans or goals, which are then converted to actions. Similarly, VIPER and Diffusion Reward utilize video prediction models' likelihoods as reward signals, but also require *in-domain* expert videos for training these models. This reliance on environment-specific or task-specific demonstrations limits their versatility and zero-shot capabilities.
- **Vision-Language Model (VLM)-based Rewards:** Approaches like Mahmoudieh et al. and VLM-RM leverage VLM embeddings (e.g., CLIP) to compute reward signals from text descriptions and pixel observations. While enabling zero-shot language conditioning, these methods primarily focus on static image-text alignment and may not fully capture notions of naturalness or complex temporal dynamics inherent in behaviors.

TADPoLe distinguishes itself by making a critical conceptual leap: it proposes to directly use **pre-trained, frozen, domain-agnostic text-conditioned diffusion models** to compute *dense zero-shot reward signals*. This bypasses the need for manual reward engineering, expert demonstrations, or fine-tuning generative models on environment-specific data. The core insight is that diffusion models, trained on internet-scale image/video datasets, encode rich priors about "naturalness" and text-visual alignment. By evaluating an agent's rendered observations against these priors, the diffusion model effectively acts as a dynamic critic, guiding policy learning.

Inspired by DreamFusion, which uses 2D diffusion models to supervise 3D model learning by critiquing rendered views for spatial consistency, TADPoLe extends this concept to the temporal domain for policy learning. It views a policy as an implicit video generator, where the environment handles physics and temporal continuity. This novel approach positions TADPoLe at the forefront of combining large-scale generative models with reinforcement learning to enable highly flexible, zero-shot behavior specification.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a framework, TADPoLe, that enables **zero-shot policy learning** for complex and novel behaviors specified by natural language. This aims to overcome the significant limitations of traditional reinforcement learning, where:

1. **Reward Function Design is Intractable:** Manually crafting precise reward functions for diverse and intricate behaviors (e.g., "a person doing splits" or "a dog chasing its tail") becomes prohibitively complex and time-consuming. This severely limits the scalability of RL to real-world applications requiring a vast repertoire of skills.
2. **Lack of Generalization:** Existing methods often rely on costly expert demonstrations or require training task-specific visual models, hindering their ability to generalize to entirely new tasks or environments without additional data.

TADPoLe is motivated by the hypothesis that **large-scale pre-trained generative models, specifically text-conditioned diffusion models, encode rich implicit priors** about both text-visual alignment and a notion of "naturalness" derived from their internet-scale training data. By leveraging these frozen models, the authors propose to:

- Automatically generate **dense reward signals** directly from natural language prompts, eliminating the need for ad-hoc reward function design.
- Guide policies not only to achieve text-aligned goals but also to exhibit **qualitatively natural behaviors**, as the diffusion model implicitly encapsulates natural visual and motion priors.
- Demonstrate that this approach can enable policy learning **zero-shot**, meaning without any ground-truth rewards or expert demonstrations specific to the target environment or task.

In essence, the goal is to "distill" the vast knowledge embedded in foundation generative models into reinforcement learning policies, thereby unlocking unprecedented flexibility and scalability in behavior specification.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

TADPoLe introduces a novel reward computation mechanism for policy learning, leveraging frozen, pre-trained text-conditioned diffusion models. The approach is divided into two main variants: TADPoLe (using text-to-image models) and Video-TADPoLe (using text-to-video models).

**Core Principle:**
A reinforcement learning policy interacting with an environment can be conceptualized as an agent-centric implicit video representation. The policy's actions lead to state transitions, which are then rendered into visual observations (frames). A text-conditioned diffusion model can then "critique" these generated frames/sequences by evaluating how likely they are to be produced given a specific text prompt, thereby providing a dense reward signal.

#### 3.1. Text-Aware Diffusion for Policy Learning (TADPoLe)

This variant uses a text-to-image diffusion model (e.g., StableDiffusion 2.1) to compute rewards from single, immediate subsequent frames.
- **Reward Computation:** At each timestep *t*, an action `a_t` leads to a new state `s_t+1` and a rendered observation `o_t+1`.
 1. A Gaussian source noise vector `ϵ₀` is sampled and used to corrupt `o_t+1`, producing a noisy observation `˜o_t+1`.
 2. The *frozen, pre-trained text-to-image diffusion model* predicts the source noise `ϵ` in two ways:
 - An unconditional prediction: `ˆϵ_ϕ(˜o_t+1; t_noise)`
 - A conditional prediction (conditioned on the text prompt `y`): `ˆϵ_ϕ(˜o_t+1; t_noise, y)`
- **Reward Terms:**
 - **Alignment Reward (`r_align`):** `∥ˆϵ_ϕ(˜o_t+1; t_noise, y) - ˆϵ_ϕ(˜o_t+1; t_noise)∥₂²`. This term measures the difference between conditional and unconditional noise predictions. The intuition is that if `o_t+1` is well-aligned with `y`, the conditional prediction will significantly differ from the unconditional one (which aims for the "closest clean mode"), indicating the model "worked harder" to align with the text. Maximizing this term encourages text-alignment.
 - **Reconstruction Reward (`r_rec`):** `∥ˆϵ_ϕ(˜o_t+1; t_noise) - ϵ₀∥₂² - ∥ˆϵ_ϕ(˜o_t+1; t_noise, y) - ϵ₀∥₂²`. This term promotes "naturalness." If the diffusion model (especially the conditional one) can accurately predict the exact noise that was applied, it implies the original image `o_t+1` is natural and consistent with the text prompt's priors. Maximizing this means the conditional prediction reconstructs `o_t+1` better than the unconditional one, indicating text-alignment and naturalness.
- **Final Reward Signal:** The two terms are combined with tunable weights (`w₁`, `w₂`) and a `symlog` transformation: `r_t = symlog(w₁ * r_align) + symlog(w₂ * r_rec)`. The `symlog` function is crucial for normalizing the raw reward signals and enabling hyperparameter reusability across different models and environments.
- **Noise Level (`t_noise`):** The level of noise corruption is critical. Too low, and the model struggles to differentiate text-alignment; too high, and it struggles to find visual features. A middle range (e.g., `U(400, 500)`) is found to be optimal.

#### 3.2. Video-TADPoLe

To address limitations of static image evaluation (e.g., understanding speed or direction), Video-TADPoLe extends the concept to use a text-to-video diffusion model (e.g., AnimatedDiff v2).
- **Reward Computation:** Rewards are calculated over sliding windows of consecutive frames `o_[i+1:j+1]`. The noise `ϵ₀` is applied to the entire video segment.
- **Generalized Reward Terms:** `r_align_[i:j]` and `r_rec_[i:j]` are computed similarly, but applied to the sequence of noisy frames and utilizing the space-time U-Net of the video diffusion model.
- **Context Window:** For a desired context window size `n`, the reward `r_t` at timestep `t` is an average over all context windows that involve `o_t+1`. This allows the reward to consider temporal consistency and motion priors. A higher noise level is generally preferred for video models due to their ability to exploit information across frames.

#### 3.3. Reinforcement Learning Framework

TADPoLe is agnostic to the underlying RL algorithm. For experiments, the authors utilize **TD-MPC**, a model-predictive control algorithm, fixing its hyperparameters across all tasks for fair comparison. Policies are trained for a fixed number of steps (e.g., 2M for Humanoid/Dog, 700K for Meta-World).

#### 3.4. Baselines and Evaluation

The method is compared against other text-to-reward approaches: VLM-RM (CLIP-based), LIV (CLIP-based, finetuned on demonstrations), and Text2Reward (LLM-generated reward functions). Evaluation includes quantitative metrics (cumulative ground-truth rewards where available, success rates for Meta-World) and qualitative human evaluations for text-alignment and naturalness of learned behaviors.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

TADPoLe demonstrates significant effectiveness across diverse environments and tasks, showcasing its zero-shot capabilities and ability to distill natural priors.

#### 4.1. Goal Achievement (Humanoid and Dog Environments)

- **Quantitative Performance:** For the "a person standing" task in Humanoid, TADPoLe achieves competitive cumulative ground-truth reward (254.43 ± 8.76) compared to an agent trained on ground-truth reward (287.68 ± 4.64) and significantly outperforms VLM-RM (247.05 ± 16.90), LIV (11.27), and Text2Reward (10.50).
- **Novel Zero-Shot Behaviors:** Crucially, TADPoLe consistently learns novel behaviors specified by natural language (e.g., "a person in lotus position," "a person doing splits," "a person kneeling," "a dog standing") where baselines (VLM-RM, LIV, Text2Reward) frequently fail to produce text-aligned results according to human evaluation. For instance, TADPoLe achieves 60% agreement for "lotus position" and 84% for "doing splits," while VLM-RM achieves 52% and 64% respectively, and other baselines are much lower or 0%.
- **Naturalness:** Human evaluation consistently favors TADPoLe for producing more natural-looking motions and poses compared to VLM-RM, with preference rates ranging from 62.5% to 87.5% for various poses.
- **Prompt Sensitivity:** TADPoLe exhibits sensitivity to subtle variations in prompts (e.g., "a person standing with hands above head" vs. "hands on hips"), learning distinct, fine-grained behaviors.

#### 4.2. Continuous Locomotion (Humanoid and Dog Environments)

- **Video-TADPoLe Superiority:** For tasks like "a person walking" and "a dog walking," Video-TADPoLe significantly outperforms all baselines. For "a person walking," it achieves 145.60 ± 48.20 cumulative ground-truth reward, vastly exceeding ViCLIP-RM (25.51 ± 40.45), LIV (0.65), and Text2Reward (3.35). Similar trends are observed for "a dog walking."
- **Coherent Motion:** While ViCLIP-RM learns to take steps, it often does so with unnatural side-stepping or fixed poses. Video-TADPoLe learns more natural, step-taking motions for both Humanoid and Dog agents.
- **Naturalness Preference:** Human evaluation confirms a higher preference for the naturalness of policies learned by Video-TADPoLe compared to ViCLIP-RM (84% for human walking, 76% for dog walking).
- **Reward Signal Coherence:** Training curves show a positive correlation between Video-TADPoLe's computed reward and the ground-truth reward during training, indicating that the diffusion-derived signal is a coherent proxy for desired performance.

#### 4.3. Robotic Manipulation (Meta-World Environment)

- **Outperformance over VLM-RM:** On a diverse suite of Meta-World tasks, TADPoLe achieves significantly higher average success rates (42.2%) compared to VLM-RM (20.8%).
- **Zero-shot Transfer:** Notably, TADPoLe successfully supervises policy learning in the synthetic visual environment of Meta-World without any fine-tuning of the pre-trained diffusion model, despite the visual dissimilarity to internet-scale images.
- **Problem Solving:** TADPoLe can successfully solve tasks like "Door Open" (40% success rate), which VLM-RM and even sparse-reward-only baselines fail to address.

#### 4.4. Normalization Study

- The `symlog` transformation for reward normalization is found to be the most performant and robust strategy. It consistently yields the best empirical results across different robotic configurations, visual environments, and tasks while allowing for the reuse of the same hyperparameters (`w₁`, `w₂`). Other normalization techniques (direct scaling, symexp, min-max, standardization) lead to reduced or inconsistent policy learning performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

TADPoLe represents a significant step forward in reinforcement learning by offering a scalable and flexible paradigm for behavior specification. Its main significance lies in:

1. **True Zero-Shot Policy Learning:** By leveraging pre-trained, frozen, domain-agnostic text-conditioned diffusion models, TADPoLe eliminates the need for manual reward engineering, expert demonstrations, or environment-specific fine-tuning of generative models. This capability is crucial for scaling RL to real-world applications where obtaining task-specific data is difficult or impossible.
2. **Distilling Natural Priors:** The method effectively "distills" the rich visual and motion priors learned by large-scale generative models from vast internet data into RL policies. This results in agents that not only achieve specified goals but also exhibit behaviors that are qualitatively more natural and human-perceptive, a crucial aspect for believable character animation and human-robot interaction.
3. **Versatility Across Domains:** The successful application across diverse environments (Humanoid, Dog, Meta-World) and tasks (goal-achievement, continuous locomotion, robotic manipulation) demonstrates TADPoLe's broad applicability and robustness. Its ability to supervise policies even in synthetic visual environments without explicit adaptation is a testament to the power of general-purpose visual models.
4. **Novel Reward Function Design:** The innovative formulation of reward signals based on noise prediction differences and reconstruction capabilities of diffusion models provides a powerful new direction for automatically constructing dense, multimodal rewards. This could inspire further research into novel ways to extract useful supervisory signals from complex generative models.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Accelerated RL Development:** Researchers and practitioners can prototype and train agents for new behaviors much faster, without the bottleneck of reward engineering.
- **More Natural and Intuitive Agents:** Agents trained with TADPoLe could lead to more engaging and believable virtual characters and more acceptable and predictable robotic movements in human environments.
- **Bridging Generative AI and RL:** This work further cements the synergy between large-scale generative models and reinforcement learning, opening avenues for future research into using other foundation models (e.g., large language models directly) for more complex, abstract, or long-horizon tasks.
- **Democratization of RL:** By reducing the technical barrier of reward engineering, TADPoLe could make advanced RL techniques more accessible to a wider range of users and applications.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The authors acknowledge several limitations that also point to interesting future research directions:
- **Fine-grained Control over Prompts:** Explicitly controlling the influence of individual words within a prompt remains challenging. Future work could explore methods for more granular semantic control over the generated behavior.
- **Stochasticity and Stability:** The reliance on repeatedly sampling Gaussian noise vectors introduces stochasticity, which can lead to variance in policy behavior and convergence across runs. Investigating methods to control and improve the stability of convergence to a consistent policy is an important area.
- **Multi-Camera Views:** Currently, TADPoLe uses a single camera view. Incorporating multiple camera views could provide richer observational data for reward computation, potentially leading to more robust and accurate behavior learning, especially in complex 3D environments.

Overall, TADPoLe represents a significant contribution towards making reinforcement learning more adaptable, scalable, and intuitive by harnessing the rich implicit knowledge embedded in state-of-the-art generative models.
