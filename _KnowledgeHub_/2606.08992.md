# SpaceVLN: A Zero-Shot Vision-and-Language Navigation Agent with Online Spatial Cognitive Memory and Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> SpaceVLN introduces a zero-shot vision-and-language navigation agent that integrates online spatial cognitive memory and task-guided spatial reasoning within a stagewise closed-loop framework. This agent achieves new state-of-the-art zero-shot performance on multiple benchmarks, including R2R-CE (53.3% SR) and RxR-CE (48.9% SR), and demonstrates robust navigation on a physical robot with a 48% success rate.

## Problem

- Existing zero-shot vision-and-language navigation (VLN) agents struggle with global spatial reasoning and often get lost in complex continuous environments due to reliance on local observations and linear history.
- Current VLN methods often necessitate costly pre-exploration for map building or extensive task-specific training data, which limits their adaptability and generalizability to novel environments.
- Bridging the gap between natural language instructions and an agent's understanding of complex 3D spatial structures in continuous environments remains a challenge.

## Method

- A stagewise ==closed-loop== navigation framework that decouples planning (generating verifiable space-landmark stages) from execution (local control), incorporating VLM output validation and action-space constraints.
- Online Spatial Cognitive Memory, comprising a Hierarchical Spatial Memory (dynamic Spatial Waypoint graph from real-time RGB-D observations) and Local Landmark Memory (subtask-centric landmark grounding).
- Task-Guided Spatial Reasoning (Spatial-CoT) through schema-guided chain-of-thought variants for both the planner and executor, integrating spatial memory into foundation model decision-making.

## Results

- Achieved new zero-shot state-of-the-art in Vision-and-Language Navigation on R2R-CE (**53.3%** Success Rate) and RxR-CE (**48.9%** Success Rate), surpassing prior methods with notable relative gains.
- Established new state-of-the-art for Object-Goal Navigation on HM3D-OVON (**51.6%** Success Rate, **33.1%** SPL), demonstrating cross-task effectiveness.
- Successfully deployed SpaceVLN on a real robot, achieving a **48%** success rate in diverse indoor settings, which outperformed baselines like Open-Nav (**34%**) and NaVid (**30%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating online, hierarchical Spatial Cognitive Memory, which abstracts explored regions into Spatial Waypoints and graphs, fundamentally enhances an agent's global spatial understanding for zero-shot navigation.
> - Decoupling the navigation process into distinct planning and execution stages, guided by task-specific Chain-of-Thought reasoning (Spatial-CoT), effectively grounds large vision-language models in dynamic physical environments.
> - Zero-shot, training-free agents can achieve state-of-the-art performance, even surpassing some supervised methods, by strategically augmenting foundation models with structured spatial cognition and reasoning mechanisms.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) aims to enable embodied agents to follow natural-language instructions within 3D environments. Early VLN benchmarks, such as R2R and RxR, primarily focused on navigation within discrete viewpoint graphs. More recently, the field has shifted towards VLN in continuous environments (VLN-CE), which more closely approximates real-world deployment and necessitates advanced spatial understanding and low-level control in metric space.

Existing navigation strategies exhibit several limitations that SpaceVLN attempts to address:
- **Pre-built map-based navigators:** These methods often provide robust spatial context but typically require costly and time-consuming pre-exploration or static offline memory to generate scene representations.
- **Training-based methods:** Approaches relying on imitation or reinforcement learning often incorporate pre-training, physical priors, or waypoint prediction. However, they frequently demand large amounts of task-specific navigation data, learned waypoint modules, or supervised policies, limiting their generalizability to novel, unseen environments. Many also focus on local action or waypoint prediction without maintaining a comprehensive global spatial state.
- **Foundation model navigators:** The emergence of large language models (LLMs) and vision-language models (VLMs) has enabled zero-shot navigation, eliminating the need for task-specific policy training. While these models can perform high-level reasoning and have been augmented with features like progress estimation, graph constraints, and history analysis (e.g., NavGPT, Open-Nav, EvoNav), their reasoning remains largely tied to local observations and linear historical data. This reliance limits their spatial cognition, making them susceptible to getting lost in complex, multi-space navigation tasks where a deeper understanding of 3D spatial structure is required. Research, such as OpenEQA and Thinking in Space, has indicated that VLMs often exhibit local spatial awareness but weak global spatial reasoning.

SpaceVLN positions itself within this landscape by focusing on enhancing the spatial cognition and reasoning abilities of zero-shot, training-free agents. It moves beyond the limitations of local-cue reliance and the requirements of pre-mapping or extensive training by building an online, hierarchical spatial memory and integrating task-guided spatial reasoning. This approach aims to provide foundation models with richer, dynamic spatial context, enabling them to make more informed navigation decisions in complex continuous environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust, zero-shot embodied navigation agent, named SpaceVLN, capable of accurately following natural-language instructions in previously unseen 3D continuous environments without requiring any task-specific policy training.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges observed in current VLN systems:
- **Bridging the gap between instructions and spatial understanding:** In continuous environments, an agent cannot rely solely on local observations to determine its next action. Instead, it must develop an understanding of the broader spatial structure—including explored regions, traversed paths, landmarks, and their interrelations—to effectively ground language instructions and progress toward a goal. Existing methods often fall short in developing this intrinsic spatial awareness.
- **Addressing limitations of current zero-shot approaches:** While foundation models have opened avenues for zero-shot navigation, many existing navigators tend to depend heavily on local visual cues and linear historical data for reasoning. This can lead to agents getting disoriented in environments with multiple interconnected spaces or when instructions require complex spatial understanding, such as "turn left at the end of the hallway and wait by the red sofa in the living room." The motivation is to imbue these agents with a stronger, more abstract understanding of space.
- **Reducing reliance on costly pre-exploration or training:** Traditional map-based navigation systems often necessitate extensive pre-exploration to build detailed scene representations. Similarly, many high-performing VLN agents rely on large datasets for task-specific training, which limits their adaptability and deployment in new scenarios. A key motivation is to overcome these resource-intensive prerequisites, making navigation agents more versatile and deployment-ready.
- **Enhancing the reasoning capabilities of foundation models:** Although foundation models possess strong high-level reasoning abilities, they typically lack direct mechanisms for incorporating physical affordances, motion feasibility, or real-time environmental feedback into their decision-making. The paper is motivated to provide these models with a structured, online spatial context to improve their grounding in the physical world and enable more effective task-guided planning.
- **Developing a unified framework for diverse navigation tasks:** The authors aim to create a single framework that can handle different types of embodied navigation tasks, specifically Vision-and-Language Navigation and Object-Goal Navigation, under a unified zero-shot setting. This would demonstrate the generalizability of the proposed spatial cognition and reasoning mechanisms.

In essence, the motivation is to create a more spatially intelligent, adaptive, and efficient zero-shot navigation agent that can interpret and execute complex language instructions by building and utilizing an online understanding of its environment's spatial layout.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SpaceVLN operates as a stagewise closed-loop navigation framework, integrating online Spatial Cognitive Memory and Task-Guided Spatial Reasoning. It is designed to function in a zero-shot manner, without task-specific policy training.

#### 3.1. Stagewise Closed-Loop Navigation Framework

The framework coordinates planning and execution by breaking down navigation into verifiable space–landmark stages.
- **Planner:** At each planning step *k*, the planner receives the navigation task (*q*), current observations (a 12-view panoramic look-around *O*<sub>12</sub><sup>*k*</sup>), and the current Spatial Cognitive Memory (*M*<sup>*k*</sup>). It uses these inputs to construct a task-conditioned space–landmark anchor chain (*C*<sup>*k*</sup>), where each anchor (*v*<sup>*k*</sup><sub>*i*</sub>) pairs a spatial state (e.g., room, connector, floor transition) with an identifying cue (e.g., landmark, object). The planner identifies the current verified anchor and the immediate next unverified anchor to set as the stage target. It then generates a stage-level subtask (*σ*<sup>*k*</sup>), which includes the current and next anchors, the full anchor chain, a selected panoramic direction (*δ*<sup>*k*</sup>), a stage instruction (*u*<sup>*k*</sup>) for the executor, a subtask landmark (*ℓ*<sup>*k*</sup>), and a completion flag (*z*<sup>*k*</sup>).
- **Executor:** The executor is responsible for realizing the current subtask (*σ*<sup>*k*</sup>). It uses First-Person View (FPV) observations (*I*<sup>*t*</sup>), obstacle perception (*Ω*<sup>*t*</sup>), and subtask landmark perception (*Λ*<sup>*t*</sup>) to issue primitive actions (*a*<sup>*t*</sup>). The executor focuses on local control towards the next anchor, guided by the subtask landmark and traversability cues. Control is returned to the planner upon destination arrival, task completion, exhaustion of the stage-specific budget, or detection of an unresolvable blockage. The executor provides feedback (*F*<sup>*k*</sup>) summarizing the execution outcome and landmark status for the planner's verification and replanning in the subsequent cycle.
- **Control Reliability:** SpaceVLN incorporates mechanisms for control reliability, including validation of VLM outputs for correct parsing and logical consistency. Action-space constraints prevent unsafe or ineffective movements (e.g., limiting consecutive turns, masking forward movement if blocked). Stage and episode termination criteria are robust, relying on landmark-grounded arrival evidence and predefined budgets.

#### 3.2. Spatial Cognitive Memory

This component organizes online perception into a relational spatial state, crucial for spatial cognition. It consists of two main parts:
- **Hierarchical Spatial Memory (*W*<sub>*t*</sub>):** This stores the Spatial Waypoint graph (*G*<sub>*t*</sub>) and the executed Spatial Waypoint chain (*P*<sub>*t*</sub>).
 - **Spatial Waypoints:** These are persistent units abstracting explored regions. Each waypoint (*w*<sub>*i*</sub>) comprises a metric pose (*x*<sub>*i*</sub>), floor identifier (*f*<sub>*i*</sub>), region label (*r*<sub>*i*</sub>), and local landmark cues (*Λ*<sub>*i*</sub>). Spatial Waypoints are merged when their region labels and metric supports are consistent, while connectors (like doorways) and floor transitions prevent inappropriate merging across distinct spaces.
 - **Spatial Waypoint Graph (*G*<sub>*t*</sub>):** This graph connects Spatial Waypoints, with edges capturing region membership and traversability. The edge set combines map-induced reachability (inferred from the metric map) and chain-induced adjacency (preserving the executed route).
 - **Executed Spatial Waypoint Chain (*P*<sub>*t*</sub>):** This is a sequential record of the Spatial Waypoints visited, providing a history of the agent's path.
 - **Tiled Metric RGB-D World Map:** An underlying layer that projects RGB-D observations into an online map, recording explored free space, obstacles, agent poses, and trajectory history. This map is used to derive and update the Spatial Waypoint graph.
 - **Planner Serialization:** Before each planning step, the Hierarchical Spatial Memory is serialized into an enhanced egocentric spatial representation (*Ψ*<sup>*k*</sup>) for the VLM. This representation is agent-centered, includes an adjacency list of waypoints, and a rendered map.
- **Local Landmark Memory (*L*<sub>*t*</sub>):** This memory is subtask-centric, providing detailed local landmark information for execution.
 - **Grounding and Segmentation:** When a subtask landmark (*ℓ*<sup>*k*</sup>) is identified by the planner, SpaceVLN uses open-vocabulary grounding and segmentation (GroundingDINO and RepViT-SAM) to detect instances of this landmark in the current FPV observations.
 - **Ranked Landmark Pool:** Detections are projected with depth, fused by category, metric proximity, and confidence, then filtered to form a ranked pool (*B*<sup>(*k*)</sup><sub>*t*</sub>) of relevant candidates. This pool provides distance, bearing, confidence, visibility, and arrival status for each landmark.
 - **Special Handling:** Opening-like targets (e.g., doorways) are identified by category and depth-profile cues, with arrival evaluated using type-specific thresholds.

#### 3.3. Task-Guided Spatial Reasoning (Spatial-CoT)

SpaceVLN employs two schema-guided Chain-of-Thought (CoT) variants to integrate spatial memory into foundation model reasoning:
- **Planner Spatial-CoT:** This operates at planning boundaries. It processes the task, 12-view observations, enhanced egocentric spatial representation (*Ψ*<sup>*k*</sup>), and previous-stage feedback. It uses a five-field schema (observation analysis, perception grounded in Spatial Cognitive Memory, progress estimation, next-stage planning, and plan summarization) to localize task progress within the anchor chain and generate a structured stage-level subtask (*σ*<sup>*k*</sup>). This ensures that the VLM's output is parseable and aligned with the navigation framework.
- **Executor Spatial-CoT:** This operates at a higher frequency for local control. It receives the current subtask (*σ*<sup>*k*</sup>), FPV observation (*I*<sup>*t*</sup>), obstacle state (*Ω*<sup>*t*</sup>), and Local Landmark Memory (*Λ*<sup>*t*</sup>). Its four-field schema (front-view content, landmark validation, stage progress, and obstacle-aware action generation) guides the generation of admissible primitive actions (*a*<sup>*t*</sup>), focusing on local traversability and subtask progress.

By decoupling the reasoning into planner and executor components and providing rich, structured spatial context, SpaceVLN aims to enable robust zero-shot navigation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

SpaceVLN underwent extensive evaluation across multiple benchmarks and real-robot deployment, demonstrating its effectiveness in zero-shot embodied navigation.

#### 4.1. Vision-and-Language Navigation Performance

- **R2R-CE and RxR-CE Val-Unseen:** SpaceVLN established new zero-shot state-of-the-art (SOTA) performance.
 - On R2R-CE, it achieved a Success Rate (SR) of 53.3, surpassing the previous SOTA (GTA) by a relative gain of 9.2%.
 - On RxR-CE, it achieved an SR of 48.9, exceeding GTA by a relative gain of 5.8%.
 - Notably, SpaceVLN, despite being training-free, outperformed several supervised learning and pre-exploration baselines, indicating the strength of its spatial memory and reasoning.
- **GN-Bench:** For this benchmark featuring complex instructions, SpaceVLN achieved a training-free SR of 39.3, outperforming many training-based baselines and establishing the best zero-shot performance.

#### 4.2. Object-Goal Navigation Performance

- **HM3D-OVON Val-Unseen:** SpaceVLN achieved a new SOTA with an SR of 51.6 and SPL of 33.1, surpassing the previous SOTA (DRIVE-Nav) with a relative SR gain of 2.8%. This result confirms the method's applicability and effectiveness across different types of navigation tasks.

#### 4.3. Real-Robot Deployment

- SpaceVLN was deployed on a TX-Q1 differential-drive robot in 50 custom indoor navigation episodes across various environments (Hall, Office, Office–Hall).
- It achieved an overall SR of 48%, outperforming baselines such as Open-Nav (34%) and NaVid (30%).
- The largest gains were observed in the cross-area Office–Hall setting, where SpaceVLN obtained an SR of 43%, suggesting its particular effectiveness in complex multi-space navigation scenarios by maintaining task progress as a spatial state.

#### 4.4. Ablation Analysis

An ablation study on R2R-CE was conducted to assess the contribution of SpaceVLN's key components:
- **Spatial Cognitive Memory:**
 - Removing both Hierarchical Spatial Memory (HSM) and Local Landmark Memory (L-LM) led to a significant drop in SR from 53.3 to 37.3 and an increase in average steps, indicating increased disorientation.
 - HSM was identified as the dominant contributor; its removal alone resulted in a 14.4-point drop in SR, underscoring the importance of global Spatial Waypoint topology for progress localization.
 - Removing L-LM decreased SR to 42.9, highlighting its role in local landmark grounding and tracking.
- **Task-Guided Spatial Reasoning and Stagewise Control:**
 - Removing Planner Spatial-CoT (P-SCoT), Executor Spatial-CoT (E-SCoT), and Stagewise Control (SC) together caused SR to drop from 53.3 to 38.3 and SPL from 34.5 to 21.9, while increasing average steps. This suggests that the schema-guided Spatial-CoT mechanisms clarify progress and next-stage prediction, and SC enhances efficiency through stage verification and replanning.
 - P-SCoT had a larger impact on SR (8.5 points drop) compared to E-SCoT (6.2 points drop), emphasizing the planner's critical role in spatial verification and next-stage anchor selection.

#### 4.5. Runtime and Model Comparison

- SpaceVLN demonstrated competitive online efficiency, with a lower average runtime per episode on R2R-CE compared to other foundation model navigators. For instance, SpaceVLN with Qwen3.5-Plus/Flash took 4.3 minutes per episode, compared to 14.7 minutes for Open-Nav (Gemini-2.5-Pro).
- The choice of backend foundation model affected performance, with Qwen3.5-Plus/Flash achieving the best SR and fewest steps, suggesting that SpaceVLN benefits from VLMs with strong multimodal spatial reasoning capabilities.

The findings collectively indicate that Spatial Cognitive Memory and Task-Guided Spatial Reasoning, within a stagewise closed-loop framework, significantly enhance zero-shot navigation performance and generalizability in continuous and complex environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in SpaceVLN carries several significant implications for the field of embodied AI and robotics:

- **Advancement in Zero-Shot Embodied Navigation:** SpaceVLN establishes new benchmarks for zero-shot Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) and Object-Goal Navigation (OVON). Its ability to achieve state-of-the-art performance without task-specific policy training or costly pre-exploration represents a notable step forward in the development of highly adaptable and generalizable navigation agents. This reduces the barriers to deploying robotic systems in novel environments, where extensive prior mapping or training data collection is impractical.

- **Enhanced Spatial Intelligence for AI Agents:** The core contributions of online Spatial Cognitive Memory and Task-Guided Spatial Reasoning highlight the importance of structured, dynamic spatial understanding beyond local observations and linear histories. By enabling agents to progressively abstract explored regions into Spatial Waypoints and reason over hierarchical spatial relationships, SpaceVLN provides a framework for imbuing AI agents with stronger spatial cognition. This approach addresses a fundamental bottleneck in embodied foundation models, which often struggle with global spatial reasoning despite local perceptual capabilities.

- **Unified Framework for Diverse Navigation Tasks:** The design of SpaceVLN, utilizing a stagewise closed-loop framework with verifiable space–landmark stages, allows it to address both instruction-following VLN and target-seeking OVON within a single, unified architecture. This versatility demonstrates the generalizability of its underlying principles, suggesting a path towards more comprehensive and flexible embodied AI systems capable of handling a broader range of real-world tasks.

- **Practical Applicability and Real-World Transferability:** The successful deployment and evaluation of SpaceVLN on a physical robot validate its robustness and transferability from simulated environments to the real world. This is a critical factor for the practical adoption of research in robotics. The performance gains observed in real-robot experiments, particularly in complex, multi-space scenarios, indicate the potential for SpaceVLN-like architectures to power autonomous robots in various indoor settings.

- **Foundation for Future Research:** The research identifies Spatial Cognitive Memory and Task-Guided Spatial Reasoning as a practical foundation for developing more capable embodied navigation agents. While SpaceVLN demonstrates significant progress, its limitations (e.g., sensitivity to noisy labels, long-horizon geometric planning, efficiency of foundation model calls) also open clear avenues for future research. This work provides a strong base upon which subsequent efforts can build to further refine online spatial grounding, improve control robustness, and optimize computational efficiency for real-time deployment.
