# Mantis: A Versatile Vision-Language-Action Model with Disentangled Visual Foresight

> [!summary] Summary
> Mantis is a vision-language-action (VLA) model for robotic control that integrates a disentangled visual foresight (DVF) head and an adaptive temporal ensemble (ATE). The model achieved a 96.7% average success rate on the LIBERO benchmark and demonstrated improved generalization to out-of-domain instructions in real-world scenarios compared to existing VLA models.

## Problem

- VLA models often underutilize their capacity due to the mismatch between high-dimensional visual inputs and sparse, low-dimensional action signals.
- Existing visual foresight methods either incur high training costs and risk hallucinations with direct pixel prediction or suffer from information bottlenecks when compressing visual states.
- Many VLA models degrade their pre-trained language comprehension and reasoning capabilities during robot-specific fine-tuning, limiting generalization to novel instructions.

## Method

- **Mantis** introduces a ==Disentangled Visual Foresight== (DVF) head that is separate from the VLA backbone, using ==latent-action queries== and a residual connection to capture inter-frame dynamics without burdening the main model.
- A three-stage progressive training recipe is employed, which includes language-supervised mix training to explicitly preserve the Vision-Language Model backbone's semantic understanding and reasoning capabilities.
- An Adaptive Temporal Ensemble (ATE) is developed for inference, dynamically adjusting ensemble strength based on target and dynamic visual patches to improve computational efficiency without sacrificing motion stability.

## Results

- Mantis achieved an average success rate of **96.7%** on the LIBERO benchmark, surpassing leading vision-augmented and non-vision-augmented VLA baselines and demonstrating faster convergence.
- In real-world experiments, Mantis showed superior generalization to out-of-domain instructions (e.g., 8.25 successful executions out of 10 for complex OOD tasks) compared to state-of-the-art open-source VLA models.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the DVF head is critical for action learning, and the ATE reduced inference calls by nearly **50%** while maintaining comparable success rates on the LIBERO benchmark.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling visual foresight prediction from the main VLA backbone allows for efficient extraction of 'latent actions' (inter-frame dynamics), providing dense guidance for explicit action learning.
> - Explicitly retaining language supervision during robot-specific training is crucial for robust instruction following and generalization to out-of-domain commands that require world knowledge or intent inference.
> - Dynamically activating temporal ensemble based on task-relevant visual changes can significantly improve inference efficiency for real-world robotic deployment while maintaining high performance.
