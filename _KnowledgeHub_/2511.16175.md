# Mantis: A Versatile Vision-Language-Action Model with Disentangled Visual Foresight

> [!summary] Summary
> Mantis is a vision-language-action (VLA) model for robotic control that integrates a disentangled visual foresight (DVF) head and an adaptive temporal ensemble (ATE). The model achieved a 96.7% average success rate on the LIBERO benchmark and demonstrated improved generalization to out-of-domain instructions in real-world scenarios compared to existing VLA models.

## Problem

- VLA models often underutilize their capacity due to the mismatch between high-dimensional visual inputs and sparse, low-dimensional action signals.
- Existing visual foresight methods either incur high training costs and risk hallucinations with direct pixel prediction or suffer from information bottlenecks when compressing visual states.
- Many VLA models degrade their pre-trained language comprehension and reasoning capabilities during robot-specific fine-tuning, limiting generalization to novel instructions.

## Method

- **Mantis** introduces a ==Disentangled Visual Foresight== (DVF) head that is separate from the VLA backbone, using ==latent-action queries== and a residual connection to capture inter-frame dynamics without burdening the main model.
- A three-stage progressive training recipe is employed, which includes language-supervised mix training to explicitly preserve the Vision-Language Model backbone's semantic understanding and reasoning capabilities.
- An Adaptive Temporal Ensemble (ATE) is developed for inference, dynamically adjusting ensemble strength based on target and dynamic visual patches to improve computational efficiency without sacrificing motion stability.

## Results

- Mantis achieved an average success rate of **96.7%** on the LIBERO benchmark, surpassing leading vision-augmented and non-vision-augmented VLA baselines and demonstrating faster convergence.
- In real-world experiments, Mantis showed superior generalization to out-of-domain instructions (e.g., 8.25 successful executions out of 10 for complex OOD tasks) compared to state-of-the-art open-source VLA models.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the DVF head is critical for action learning, and the ATE reduced inference calls by nearly **50%** while maintaining comparable success rates on the LIBERO benchmark.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling visual foresight prediction from the main VLA backbone allows for efficient extraction of 'latent actions' (inter-frame dynamics), providing dense guidance for explicit action learning.
> - Explicitly retaining language supervision during robot-specific training is crucial for robust instruction following and generalization to out-of-domain commands that require world knowledge or intent inference.
> - Dynamically activating temporal ensemble based on task-relevant visual changes can significantly improve inference efficiency for real-world robotic deployment while maintaining high performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Mantis is situated at the forefront of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, a rapidly evolving field aiming to enable robots to understand human instructions, perceive their environment, and execute complex tasks. This work specifically addresses several critical challenges within the VLA paradigm:

**Evolution of VLA Models:** VLA models represent a significant advancement from traditional robotic control, leveraging the powerful understanding and reasoning capabilities of pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to translate natural language instructions and visual observations into executable robotic actions. Early VLA models, such as RT-1 and RT-2, demonstrated the potential of large-scale pre-training for general robot control. However, these models often faced limitations due to the inherent mismatch between high-dimensional visual sensory inputs and sparse, low-dimensional action signals, leading to underutilized model capacity and constrained performance.

**Challenges in Vision-Augmented Action Learning:** To mitigate the issue of sparse action signals, researchers have explored "vision-augmented action learning," which involves predicting future visual states or other dense visual cues. However, existing approaches present their own set of problems:
- **Direct Visual Foresight:** Approaches that directly predict high-dimensional future visual states (e.g., UnifiedVLA, CoT-VLA) can distribute the model's capacity away from action prediction, incur high training costs, and slow down convergence. They also risk the model associating physical motion with irrelevant visual changes (e.g., lighting) leading to hallucinations.
- **Compressed Visual States (Track Guidance):** Methods that compress visual states into more compact supervisory signals, such as keypoint tracks (e.g., ATM), often suffer from information bottlenecks. The compression can diminish fine-grained motion details, and the accuracy of extracted tracks can be limited, leading to higher action prediction errors.
- **Latent Action Supervision:** Some methods learn discrete latent actions from inter-frame differences (e.g., UniVLA, MOTO), but these often require training an additional quantization model, increasing computational complexity.
- **Neglect of Language Supervision:** Crucially, many existing VLA models, especially after robot-specific fine-tuning, tend to overwrite the rich vision-text alignments acquired during pre-training. This degradation compromises their comprehension, reasoning, and instruction-following capabilities, making generalization to novel instructions difficult.

Mantis enters this landscape by proposing a novel framework that seeks to overcome these limitations by **disentangling visual foresight prediction** from the main VLA backbone and **explicitly preserving language supervision**. This disentangled approach aims to provide the benefits of dense visual signals without the drawbacks of direct high-dimensional prediction or information bottlenecks, while simultaneously ensuring robust language understanding and generalization.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the development of Mantis are clearly articulated:

1. **Address the Mismatch between High-Dimensional Visual Inputs and Sparse Action Signals:** Current VLA models struggle to effectively utilize their large representational capacity due to the low-dimensional nature of action supervisions. Mantis aims to provide a dense, complementary supervisory signal without overburdening the VLA backbone.
2. **Overcome the Limitations of Existing Visual Foresight Methods:**
 - **Reduce Training Cost and Improve Convergence:** Direct prediction of high-dimensional visual states is computationally expensive and slows convergence. Mantis seeks a more efficient way to incorporate visual foresight.
 - **Avoid Information Bottlenecks:** Compressing visual states into compact signals can lose crucial fine-grained motion information. Mantis aims to extract meaningful dynamics without excessive compression.
 - **Prevent Distraction and Hallucinations:** Direct pixel-level forecasting can distract the model with irrelevant visual details or lead to spurious correlations between motion and visual appearance.
3. **Preserve and Enhance Language Comprehension and Reasoning:** Existing VLA models often degrade their semantic understanding and reasoning capabilities during robot-specific training due to the neglect of explicit language supervision. A core motivation for Mantis is to maintain and leverage these capabilities for robust instruction-following and generalization, particularly to out-of-domain tasks.
4. **Boost Explicit Action Learning:** By capturing "latent actions" through disentangled visual foresight, Mantis aims to provide more targeted and effective guidance for the explicit prediction of robot actions.
5. **Improve Inference Efficiency without Sacrificing Performance:** While temporal ensemble methods enhance motion stability, they incur high computational overhead. Mantis seeks to introduce an adaptive strategy to balance efficiency and stability.
6. **Contribute to Open-Source Robotics:** The release of code and weights underscores the objective of fostering collaborative research and development in the VLA community.

In essence, Mantis is motivated by the desire to build a more versatile, robust, and efficient VLA model that intelligently integrates visual foresight and language understanding for superior robotic manipulation across diverse and complex tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Mantis introduces a novel framework built upon a Vision-Language-Action (VLA) backbone, augmented with a Disentangled Visual Foresight (DVF) head and an Adaptive Temporal Ensemble (ATE) for efficient inference.

#### 3.1. Model Architecture Overview

The Mantis architecture comprises:
- **Backbone Model (P):** Utilizes Qwen2.5-VL, a robust VLM known for its understanding and reasoning capabilities, capable of handling flexible input resolutions for primary and wrist cameras.
- **DVF Head (D):** Responsible for predicting future visual states, it employs Sana (a Diffusion Transformer, DiT) known for efficient text-to-image generation.
- **Action Head (π):** A DiT-based component that translates aggregated information into an n-step action trajectory.
- **Connector (C):** Bridges the backbone's output space with the DVF head's input space, comprising transformer encoder layers.
- **Trainable Queries:** Includes `[LAT]` (latent-action queries) for capturing inter-frame dynamics, `[ACT]` (action queries) for action extraction, and `[GAP]` (multi-gap queries) for multi-interval future frame generation.

#### 3.2. Disentangled Visual Foresight (DVF)

The core innovation is the DVF, which decouples visual foresight from the VLA backbone.
- **Decoupling Mechanism:** Instead of the VLA backbone directly predicting future pixels, it generates an intermediate representation (`h_t`) that, along with the current visual state (`o_t`), is fed into a separate DVF head (Sana). This prevents the backbone from being burdened with redundant visual information.
- **Latent-Action Queries (`[LAT]`):** A crucial element. By feeding the current visual state (`o_t`) to the DVF head via a residual connection, the `[LAT]` queries are trained to capture *inter-frame dynamics* – the changes between frames – rather than reconstructing entire future frames. These dynamics are interpreted as "latent actions," which are visual manifestations of explicit robot motions, thereby providing targeted guidance for action prediction.
- **Multi-Gap Queries (`[GAP]`):** Introduced to allow the DVF to predict future frames at varying time step intervals, supporting denser visual predictions and diverse downstream tasks.

#### 3.3. Progressive Training Recipe

To manage the complexity of fusing vision, language, and action modalities and ensure stable optimization, Mantis employs a three-stage progressive training recipe:
1. **Stage 1: Multiple Gap Vision Training:** The model is initially trained on large-scale human manipulation videos (SSV2 dataset) to predict future frames. The DVF head and `[LAT]` and `[GAP]` queries are unfrozen, while the VLM backbone remains frozen to preserve its pre-trained language representations. This stage focuses on learning general manipulation skills and inferring latent actions from visual dynamics.
2. **Stage 2: Vision-Action Joint Training:** Robot demonstration data (DROID dataset) is introduced. The time step gap is fixed to match the action chunk size. The model is optimized jointly for DVF (diffusion loss) and action prediction (diffusion loss for the action head). `[ACT]` queries are unfrozen, while the backbone remains frozen. This stage aligns visual foresight with explicit robot actions.
3. **Stage 3: Language Supervised Mix Training:** The VLM backbone is unfrozen, and the model is jointly trained on a diverse collection of 38 multimodal datasets along with robot demonstration data. A cross-entropy loss (`L_lang`) is applied to language outputs, explicitly preserving the backbone’s semantic understanding and reasoning capabilities. This stage ensures robust multimodal understanding and instruction following.

#### 3.4. Adaptive Temporal Ensemble (ATE)

For inference, Mantis leverages a standard Temporal Ensemble (TE) for motion stability. To address TE's computational inefficiency, ATE is introduced:
- **Dynamic Ensemble Strength:** ATE dynamically adjusts the ensemble strength based on the motion stability required at each timestep.
- **Patch-Based Analysis:** It identifies two sets of input vision patches:
 - **Target Patches:** Most relevant to the language instruction, identified by high text-to-vision attention scores.
 - **Dynamic Patches:** Exhibit significant visual changes, identified by low cosine similarity between current and previous image patches.
- **Overlap-Based Activation:** When there is an overlap between target and dynamic patches (indicating fine-grained manipulation like grasping), the Temporal Ensemble is activated. Otherwise, it is disabled to improve computational efficiency. This yields the Mantis-ATE variant, reducing inference calls without compromising performance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

Mantis demonstrates significant advancements across simulation and real-world robotic tasks, supported by comprehensive evaluations and ablation studies.

#### 4.1. Simulation Experiments (LIBERO Benchmark)

- **Superior Performance:** Mantis achieves a remarkable 96.7% average success rate on the challenging LIBERO benchmark, outperforming leading vision-augmented and non-vision-augmented baselines, including UniVLA (95.2%), UnifiedVLA (95.5%), DreamVLA (92.6%), and F1 (95.7%). It shows superior performance in 3 out of 4 task suites (Spatial, Object, Long).
- **Faster Convergence:** Compared to traditional visual foresight methods like UnifiedVLA, Mantis exhibits significantly faster convergence speed during fine-tuning. It converges at a pace comparable to non-vision-augmented (OpenVLA) and latent action supervision (UniVLA) methods, highlighting the efficiency gained by disentangling foresight prediction from action learning.
- **Effectiveness of DVF:** The superior performance generally observed in vision-augmented methods over non-vision-augmented ones corroborates the value of dense visual states. Mantis's leading performance specifically validates the efficacy of its DVF in providing concise and instructive look-ahead cues.

#### 4.2. Real-World Experiments

- **Robust Instruction Following and Generalization:** Evaluated on an Agilex robot platform across three scenarios with in-domain (ID) and out-of-domain (OOD) instructions, Mantis consistently outperforms $\pi_{0.5}$, a state-of-the-art open-source VLA model.
 - Mantis shows strong instruction-following capabilities on ID tasks (e.g., average 8.25 successful executions out of 10 for Scenario 1 ID).
 - Crucially, Mantis demonstrates superior generalization to OOD instructions (e.g., average 8.25 for Scenario 1 OOD, compared to 3.5 for $\pi_{0.5}$), which often require world knowledge (e.g., "Taylor Swift"), basic arithmetic logic (e.g., "3+5"), or human intent detection (e.g., "I'm hungry. Can you help?").
- **Efficacy of Language Supervision:** The significant gap in OOD performance between Mantis and $\pi_{0.5}$ (and Mantis-LU in ablation) underscores that Mantis's language supervision during training effectively preserves the backbone's understanding and reasoning capabilities, which are critical for robust generalization.
- **Generated Future Frames:** Visualizations confirm that the DVF head generates future frame sequences that closely mirror ground truth final states, substantiating its efficacy in refining action prediction.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **ATE Analysis:** Mantis-ATE reduces inference counts by nearly 50% while maintaining comparable success rates on the LIBERO benchmark. This demonstrates a substantial improvement in computational efficiency for real-time deployment without sacrificing performance.
- **DVF Ablations:**
 - `pretrained-DVF` (Mantis's full DVF with pretraining) achieves the highest success rate (96.2%), followed by `vanilla-DVF` (95.7%).
 - `flawed-DVF` (without the residual connection) performs worse (94.4%), confirming that the residual connection is essential for DVF to effectively capture latent actions.
 - `no-DVF` (only action head) performs the worst (91.3%), validating that DVF indeed facilitates action learning.
 - These results collectively confirm the benefits of DVF, the importance of the residual connection for latent action capture, and the enhancement from video pretraining.
- **Language Supervision Ablations:** Comparison on VQA and multimodal benchmarks (MME, OCRBench, RealWorldQA) shows Mantis retains strong understanding capabilities, with only a marginal drop compared to the original Qwen2.5-VL backbone. A language-unsupervised variant (Mantis-LU) in real-world settings shows poor OOD performance, further emphasizing the criticality of language supervision for instruction generalization.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Mantis represents a notable advancement in the field of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, offering several significant contributions and potential impacts:

#### 5.1. Contributions to the Field

- **Novel Disentangled Visual Foresight (DVF):** Mantis introduces a paradigm shift in how visual foresight is integrated into VLA models. By decoupling future frame prediction from the main backbone and leveraging meta queries with a residual connection, it efficiently extracts "latent actions" – the inter-frame dynamics crucial for action learning – without the computational overhead or information bottlenecks of prior approaches. This design allows the VLA backbone to focus on understanding and reasoning.
- **Preservation of Language Understanding and Reasoning:** Through its progressive training recipe and explicit language supervision, Mantis effectively mitigates the degradation of VLM capabilities often observed during robot-specific fine-tuning. This ensures that the robot can robustly interpret, reason about, and generalize from diverse natural language instructions, including complex, out-of-domain commands that require world knowledge or inferring human intent.
- **Enhanced Performance and Efficiency:** Mantis achieves state-of-the-art performance on challenging benchmarks like LIBERO, with faster convergence speeds. Furthermore, the Adaptive Temporal Ensemble (ATE) strategy significantly improves inference efficiency by dynamically adjusting motion stability, making Mantis more practical for real-world robotic deployments.
- **Strong Generalization in Real-World Scenarios:** The real-world experiments on the Agilex platform convincingly demonstrate Mantis's superior ability to follow both in-domain and out-of-domain instructions, a critical step towards truly versatile and intelligent robotic assistants.
- **Open-Source Contribution:** The release of code and weights fosters transparency, reproducibility, and accelerates further research and development within the open-source robotics community.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

The advancements presented by Mantis have the potential to significantly impact several areas:
- **More Capable and Adaptable Robots:** Robots powered by Mantis-like VLA models could operate more effectively in complex, unstructured environments, performing a wider array of tasks with greater autonomy and adaptability to human commands. This could accelerate the deployment of robots in logistics, manufacturing, healthcare, and domestic settings.
- **Bridging the Gap between Language and Embodied AI:** By robustly connecting human language instructions to complex physical actions, Mantis moves closer to seamless human-robot interaction, making robots easier to program and more intuitive to use for non-experts.
- **New Directions for VLA Research:** The disentangled foresight approach may inspire future research into more efficient and effective ways to incorporate dense visual supervision, potentially leading to architectures that scale better with larger models and datasets. The emphasis on preserving language capabilities also sets a higher bar for future VLA model development.
- **Foundation for General-Purpose Robots:** Mantis contributes to the long-term vision of developing general-purpose robots that can learn and adapt to entirely new tasks and environments with minimal human intervention, relying on their comprehensive multimodal understanding.

#### 5.3. Limitations and Future Work

The authors acknowledge a few limitations and outline future work:
- **Missing Robot State Inputs:** Minor motion rollbacks observed in real-world scenarios are attributed to the lack of robot state inputs (e.g., proprioception), which are crucial for precise control.
- **Inference Speed Optimization:** While ATE improves efficiency, further optimization of inference speed remains an area for improvement.
- **Integration of Richer Inputs:** Future work plans to integrate richer sensory inputs, such as 3D point clouds, to provide a more comprehensive understanding of the environment and potentially improve task execution.

Overall, Mantis is a well-designed and empirically validated VLA model that addresses fundamental challenges in the field, offering a promising path toward more intelligent, adaptable, and efficient robotic systems.
