# Transformer-based World Models Are Happy With 100k Interactions

> [!summary] Summary
> TWM builds a sample-efficient world model by feeding latent states, actions, and rewards autoregressively into a Transformer-XL, which predicts next latent state, reward, and discount. A separate VAE encodes observations. An actor-critic trained purely in latent imagination, needing no transformer at inference, reaches 0.956 human-normalized mean on Atari 100k, beating prior model-free and model-based methods.

## Problem

- Deep RL agents need hundreds of millions of environment interactions, while humans reach comparable skill with under 1% of that data, blocking real-world use.
- Recurrent world models compress history into a single state, so past latents are seen only indirectly, limiting the dependencies the dynamics model can represent.
- Existing transformer world models omit past rewards from the input and require running the full model at inference, raising deployment cost during real-environment rollouts.

## Method

- Autoregressive ==Transformer-XL== dynamics model consumes modality-specific embeddings of latent states, actions, and fed-back rewards, emitting hidden states that drive reward, discount, and next-latent MLP predictors.
- Balanced ==cross-entropy loss== disentangles ==DreamerV2=='s balanced KL into explicit entropy regularizer and consistency cross-entropy terms, letting their relative weights be tuned independently.
- Thresholded entropy loss hinges on normalized policy entropy below a threshold; balanced dataset sampling softmaxes negative visitation counts with temperature to oversample recent experience.

## Results

- On Atari 100k, TWM reaches **0.956** human-normalized mean and **0.505** median, versus **0.616/0.396** for SPR, **0.465/0.313** for DrQ, and **0.332/0.134** for SimPLe.
- TWM improves all four aggregate metrics (median, IQM, mean, optimality gap) with **95%** bootstrap intervals over 5 runs per game, topping games like Boxing (**77.5**) and UpNDown (**15981.7**).
- Ablations show balanced sampling with temperature **20** beats uniform sampling and lowers dynamics loss, and removing reward feedback markedly degrades scores on games such as Boxing and Gopher.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Feeding a generative model its own predicted rewards gives it observable evidence of sampled noise, letting it condition on its past outputs rather than drift.
> - Restricting the policy to latent states keeps the transformer training-only, so heavy sequence models can buy sample efficiency without paying inference cost.
> - In low-data regimes a slowly growing replay buffer biases uniform sampling toward stale early data; visitation-count-based reweighting is a cheap overfitting fix.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

Reinforcement learning has achieved remarkable successes in complex decision-making tasks, from mastering Atari games to solving strategic board games. However, a fundamental limitation persists: the extreme data hunger of deep reinforcement learning algorithms. While humans can quickly learn new tasks with minimal experience, typical RL algorithms require hundreds of millions of environmental interactions, making them impractical for real-world applications where data collection is expensive, time-consuming, or potentially dangerous.

This research, conducted by Jan Robine, Marc Höftmann, Tobias Uelwer, and Stefan Harmeling from the Technical University of Dortmund, addresses this critical challenge by introducing a Transformer-based World Model (TWM) that achieves state-of-the-art sample efficiency on the challenging Atari 100k benchmark. The work represents a significant advancement in the "learning in imagination" paradigm of model-based reinforcement learning, where agents learn environment dynamics and then train policies using synthetic experience generated by the learned world model.

### 2. Problem Statement and Motivation

The core challenge in reinforcement learning is the sample efficiency problem. Traditional deep RL algorithms require vast amounts of interaction data, often millions or billions of steps, to achieve competent performance. This data requirement stems from the need to explore the environment, understand its dynamics, and learn effective policies through trial and error.

The Atari 100k benchmark specifically tests this limitation by constraining algorithms to learn effective policies using only 100,000 environmental interactions (equivalent to about 2 hours of human gameplay). This constraint mimics real-world scenarios where extensive data collection is prohibitive, such as robotics applications, autonomous driving, or medical treatment optimization.

Previous approaches to improving sample efficiency have followed two main directions:
- **Model-free methods** that better utilize limited data through techniques like data augmentation or auxiliary objectives
- **Model-based methods** that learn environment dynamics and use these models for planning or generating synthetic training data

This work firmly positions itself in the model-based camp, specifically in the "learning in imagination" framework pioneered by algorithms like Dreamer and DreamerV2.

### 3. Transformer-Based World Model Architecture

The TWM consists of two main architectural components that operate without shared parameters:

#### 3.1. Observation Model (Variational Autoencoder)

The observation model handles the encoding and decoding of high-dimensional visual observations:
- **Encoder**: A convolutional neural network that transforms image observations `o_t` into compact, stochastic, discrete latent states `z_t`
- **Decoder**: Reconstructs observations from latent states, providing a reconstruction loss signal
- **Latent Representation**: Following DreamerV2, latent states are vectors of 32 categorical variables, each with 32 categories

#### 3.2. Autoregressive Dynamics Model

The dynamics model predicts future states based on historical context:
- **Aggregation Model**: The core component implemented as a causally masked Transformer-XL that computes deterministic hidden states `h_t`
- **Input Sequence**: Linear embeddings of sequences of latent states `z_t`, actions `a_t`, and crucially, rewards `r_t`
- **Predictors**: Multi-layer perceptrons that use the hidden state to predict rewards, discount factors, and next latent states

The key innovation lies in the explicit inclusion of reward feedback in the transformer input. This allows the world model to react to its own predictions, particularly important when rewards are sampled stochastically.

### 4. Training Methodology and Key Innovations

#### 4.1. Loss Functions

The world model employs self-supervised learning with carefully designed loss functions:

**Observation Model Loss**:
```
L_Obs = -log p(o_t|z_t) - H(p(z_t|o_t)) + CrossEntropy(p(z_t|o_t), p(ẑ_t|h_t-1))
```

**Dynamics Model Loss**:
```
L_Dyn = CrossEntropy(p(z_t+1|o_t+1), p(ẑ_t+1|h_t)) - log p(r_t|h_t) - log p(γ_t|h_t)
```

#### 4.2. Policy Learning in Imagination

The policy is trained using Advantage Actor-Critic on imagined trajectories:
- The policy takes only latent states as input, ensuring computational efficiency during deployment
- A novel thresholded entropy loss `L_Ent = max(0, Γ - H(π)/ln(m))` stabilizes exploration across different games
- Generalized Advantage Estimation uses discount factors predicted by the world model

#### 4.3. Balanced Dataset Sampling

To prevent overfitting to early experiences, the method introduces a novel sampling procedure that tracks visitation counts and converts them to sampling probabilities:
```
p_t = softmax(-v_t/τ)
```
This prioritizes recent experiences, ensuring the world model remains relevant to current data.

### 5. Experimental Results and Performance

The TWM was evaluated on the Atari 100k benchmark against strong baselines including SimPLe (model-based), and model-free methods DER, CURL, DrQ, and SPR. The evaluation follows rigorous statistical practices with 95% stratified bootstrap confidence intervals.

#### 5.1. Quantitative Results

TWM achieved superior performance across all aggregate metrics:
- **Median human-normalized score**: 0.505 (highest among all methods)
- **Interquartile Mean (IQM)**: 0.75 (significant improvement over previous best)
- **Mean**: 0.956 (approaching optimal performance)
- **Optimality Gap**: 0.044 (closest to zero, indicating near-optimal performance)

#### 5.2. Ablation Studies

Comprehensive ablations validated the importance of key components:
- **Balanced dataset sampling** showed significant improvements in multiple games
- **Reward feedback to transformer** led to substantial performance gains
- **Thresholded entropy loss** stabilized policy training across different environments
- **History length** of 16 steps proved beneficial over shorter contexts

### 6. Analysis of World Model Capabilities

#### 6.1. Meaningful Imagination

The world model demonstrates the ability to generate coherent, meaningful trajectories. Visualizations show accurate prediction of:
- Object movements and interactions
- Reward dynamics in response to actions
- Complex multi-step scenarios across different game environments

#### 6.2. Attention Mechanisms

Analysis of the transformer's attention patterns reveals sophisticated temporal reasoning:
- The model attends to current actions and recent states as expected
- Crucially, it also attends to past rewards, confirming the benefit of reward feedback
- Different games exhibit distinct attention patterns, suggesting adaptive learning

#### 6.3. Generalization Capabilities

Despite being trained on sequences of length 16, the world model successfully generalizes to much longer imagined trajectories (up to 230 steps), accurately predicting complex environment transitions across multiple game "rooms" or scenarios.

### 7. Significance and Impact

This work establishes several important contributions to the field of sample-efficient reinforcement learning:

#### 7.1. Methodological Advances

- **Transformer Integration**: Successfully demonstrates the superiority of transformer architectures over traditional RNNs for world modeling
- **Reward Feedback**: Proves the value of explicitly feeding predicted rewards back into the dynamics model
- **Balanced Sampling**: Introduces an effective strategy for handling growing datasets in online learning scenarios

#### 7.2. Practical Impact

- **New State-of-the-Art**: Achieves the best reported performance on the Atari 100k benchmark
- **Computational Efficiency**: Maintains efficient policy deployment by decoupling the transformer from inference-time action selection
- **Real-World Applicability**: Significantly reduces the data requirements for RL, making it more viable for applications where data collection is expensive or risky

#### 7.3. Future Directions

The work opens several avenues for future research:
- Extension to more complex 3D environments and continuous control tasks
- Investigation of different transformer variants and their suitability for world modeling
- Development of more sophisticated reward prediction and integration mechanisms
- Exploration of curriculum learning strategies for world models

The TWM represents a significant step toward making deep reinforcement learning practical for real-world applications by dramatically reducing the amount of environmental interaction required to learn effective policies. By achieving human-level sample efficiency on challenging benchmarks, this work brings us closer to RL systems that can learn as efficiently as humans do.
