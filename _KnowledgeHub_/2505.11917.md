# OneTwoVLA: A Unified Vision-Language-Action Model with Adaptive Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Tsinghua University and Shanghai Qi Zhi Institute present OneTwoVLA, a unified vision-language-action model for robotic control that adaptively switches between explicit reasoning and direct action generation. This approach demonstrates improved success rates in long-horizon tasks, robust error recovery, natural human-robot interaction, and enhanced visual grounding through the use of synthetic, reasoning-centric vision-language data.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models struggle with complex reasoning tasks, such as long-horizon planning, error recovery, and generalization to novel scenarios.
- Dual-system robotics frameworks, which separate high-level reasoning from low-level action, suffer from limited mutual awareness, inference latency, and inefficient decoupling of learning.
- The scarcity and high cost of human-labeled robot data hinder the development of VLA models with strong reasoning and generalization capabilities.

## Method

- **OneTwoVLA** integrates high-level ==cognitive reasoning== and low-level physical action generation within a single neural network architecture.
- An adaptive inference mechanism allows the model to dynamically switch between a 'reasoning mode' (generating textual reasoning) and an 'acting mode' (generating actions), triggered by special decision tokens like [BOR] and [BOA].
- A scalable, automated pipeline was developed to synthesize high-quality, embodied reasoning-centric vision-language data, enabling co-training with real robot data to enhance generalization and reasoning.

## Results

- OneTwoVLA achieved an average **87%** success rate on challenging long-horizon tasks, outperforming baseline VLA models by **30%** and dual-system approaches by **24%**.
- The model successfully recovered from **80%** of error occurrences, compared to approximately 57-**58%** for baseline methods, by autonomously detecting errors and reasoning about corrective strategies.
- OneTwoVLA demonstrated **100%** success in subtask-level human-robot interaction and **72.5%** success in novel interaction scenarios, including proactive clarification and dynamic plan adjustments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Unifying reasoning and acting within a single, adaptively switching model allows for synergistic learning and context-aware behavior, overcoming limitations of decoupled systems.
> - Adaptive inference balances computational efficiency by invoking explicit reasoning only at critical junctures, while maintaining responsive action generation.
> - Synthetically generated, reasoning-rich vision-language data can effectively augment real robot data, transferring web knowledge and improving a robot's ability to generalize to novel tasks and objects.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

#### 1.1. Current State of Robot Learning

The field of robot learning is rapidly advancing towards creating general-purpose robots capable of performing diverse and complex tasks in unstructured environments. A significant paradigm in this pursuit is the development of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. These models, often initialized from pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs), learn to translate visual observations and natural language instructions directly into robot actions. Recent VLAs have shown impressive capabilities in handling a wide range of manipulation tasks, leveraging large robot datasets.

However, a key challenge in VLA development lies in endowing robots with sophisticated reasoning abilities. Traditional VLAs, while adept at generating actions, often exhibit limited reasoning, struggling with long-horizon tasks, error recovery, human-robot interaction (HRI), and generalization to novel objects or instructions outside their training distribution.

#### 1.2. Limitations of Existing Approaches

Many existing approaches to integrate reasoning and acting in robotics draw inspiration from Kahneman's dual-system framework, which posits two modes of thinking: System One (fast, intuitive, automatic) and System Two (slow, deliberative, logical). In robotics, this translates to dual-system frameworks where a high-level reasoning component (System Two), often a large language model (LLM) or VLM, generates intermediate plans or instructions, and a low-level policy (System One), such as a VLA, translates these into physical actions.

The paper highlights several critical limitations of these dual-system approaches:
1. **Limited Mutual Awareness:** System Two may generate plans or instructions that System One cannot physically execute, leading to a disconnect between reasoning and acting capabilities.
2. **Latency Issues:** High-level reasoning models, especially large foundation models, can incur significant inference latency. This delay can lead to outdated or irrelevant guidance in dynamic real-world environments, hindering agile execution and recovery.
3. **Inefficiency:** Constantly invoking a high-level reasoning system at every step, as some models do, can be computationally expensive and time-consuming, impacting real-time performance.
4. **Lack of Unified Learning:** Decoupling reasoning and acting prevents the synergistic learning where actions inform reasoning and reasoning guides actions, a characteristic of human intelligence.

OneTwoVLA directly addresses these limitations by proposing a single, unified model that learns both reasoning and acting, and crucially, adaptively switches between these modes. This approach aligns with a broader trend in AI research towards unifying diverse capabilities within single, powerful models (e.g., multimodal foundation models), which is proving to be a highly effective strategy for advancing general AI. The paper also tackles the data challenge by introducing a scalable pipeline for synthesizing embodied reasoning-centric vision-language data, overcoming the limitations of costly human-labeled robot data and domain-shifted web data.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a general-purpose robot capable of both synergistic reasoning and acting within a single, unified model architecture. Specifically, OneTwoVLA aims to achieve:

1. **Unified Reasoning and Acting:** To integrate high-level cognitive reasoning (System Two equivalent) and low-level physical action generation (System One equivalent) within one neural network. This contrasts with prevalent dual-system architectures that explicitly separate these functions.
2. **Adaptive Mode Switching:** To enable the unified model to intelligently determine *when* to engage in explicit natural language reasoning (e.g., at critical task junctures, upon errors, or during human interaction) and *when* to directly generate actions based on its most recent reasoning. This adaptive behavior seeks to balance computational efficiency with the need for thoughtful deliberation.
3. **Enhanced Reasoning and Generalization:** To significantly improve the model's ability to plan for long-horizon tasks, detect and recover from errors, interact naturally with humans, and perform robust visual grounding, especially in novel or unseen scenarios.
4. **Scalable Data Provision:** To overcome the bottleneck of scarce and expensive human-annotated robot data by developing a scalable pipeline for synthesizing high-quality, embodied reasoning-centric vision-language data. This synthetic data is crucial for co-training with real robot data, thereby unlocking stronger reasoning and generalization capabilities.

The motivation behind OneTwoVLA stems from the observed shortcomings of dual-system robotics. The authors contend that the explicit decoupling of reasoning and acting limits mutual awareness between systems, introduces latency, and impedes holistic learning. By proposing a unified, adaptively reasoning model, the authors seek to imbue robots with a more human-like "physical intelligence" where reasoning and acting are flexibly interleaved and mutually informative, ultimately paving the way for more capable and robust generalist robots.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

OneTwoVLA's methodology is characterized by a unified model architecture, an adaptive inference mechanism, and a dual-strategy for data acquisition—curated robot data and scalable synthetic vision-language data.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation and Adaptive Inference

The core of OneTwoVLA is a robotic control policy ($\pi_\theta$) that can autonomously decide at each timestep whether to operate in "reasoning mode" or "acting mode."
- **Reasoning Mode:** The policy takes multi-camera image observations ($I_{1:n}^t$), reference images from the latest reasoning timestep ($I_{1:n}^{ref}$), the language instruction ($\ell$), and the latest reasoning content ($R$) as input. It then generates updated textual reasoning content ($\hat{R}$).
- **Acting Mode:** In addition to the above inputs, the policy also incorporates the robot's proprioceptive state ($s_t$) and generates an action chunk ($A_t$).

**Adaptive Inference (Algorithm 1):** The model employs two special decision tokens: [BOR] (Beginning of Reasoning) and [BOA] (Beginning of Action). Based on the current visual, language, and reasoning context, the model first predicts either [BOR] or [BOA].
- If [BOR] is predicted, the model enters reasoning mode, generates textual reasoning until an end-of-sentence token, and updates $R$.
- If [BOA] is predicted, the model directly enters acting mode and generates an action chunk.
This adaptive framework allows the model to primarily operate in efficient acting mode, invoking reasoning only at critical junctures (e.g., subtask completion, error detection, human interaction), thereby maintaining high inference efficiency while providing informative reasoning when needed.

#### 3.2. Model Architecture

OneTwoVLA is designed to be general and leverages an existing strong VLA model, $\pi_0$ (Black et al., 2024), as its base.
- **Vision-Language Model:** The vision-language component of $\pi_0$ is adapted to auto-regressively generate textual reasoning during inference. It is supervised using a cross-entropy loss during training for both decision tokens and reasoning content.
- **Action Expert:** The action expert architecture from $\pi_0$ is inherited to model complex continuous action distributions, trained using a flow matching loss.
Input modifications include using both current and reference images (for observation history and mitigating outdated reasoning content) and incorporating historical proprioceptive states (for smoother actions).

#### 3.3. Robot Data Curation with Embodied Reasoning

To enable synergistic reasoning and acting, the authors introduce a novel robot data format that augments traditional observation-action pairs with explicit reasoning information.
- **Demonstration Segmentation:** Human-expert demonstration trajectories are segmented into "reasoning intervals" and "acting intervals."
- **Reasoning Intervals:** These capture key steps requiring deliberation (subtask completion, error detection, human interaction). They are annotated with rich textual reasoning content, and the model is trained to predict [BOR] and update $R$.
- **Acting Intervals:** The model primarily learns to predict [BOA] and actions based on observations and the latest $R$. Training for decision tokens in these intervals helps address class imbalance.
- **Embodied Reasoning Content:** Each reasoning output comprises four components:
 1. **Scene Description:** Detailed spatial and attribute information of task-relevant objects.
 2. **High-Level Plan:** An outline of sequential steps for task completion.
 3. **Historical Summary:** Concise progress tracking.
 4. **Next Step:** Immediate instruction for the robot.
This comprehensive content encourages visual understanding, high-level planning, and progress tracking. Error detection/recovery and human-robot interaction capabilities are specifically integrated by collecting and labeling recovery-focused data and annotating interaction contexts within demonstrations.
- **Automated Annotation Pipeline:** A two-stage, fully automated pipeline uses Gemini 2.5 Pro to:
 1. **Interval Annotation:** Identify reasoning intervals after subtask completion based on a predefined high-level plan.
 2. **Reasoning Content Generation:** From a midpoint frame of each interval, generate the scene description, high-level plan, historical summary, and next step. This pipeline achieved high quality, with human annotators deeming over 80% of annotations correct.

#### 3.4. Scalable Synthesis of Vision-Language Data

To enhance generalization and reasoning, the authors developed a scalable, fully automated pipeline to synthesize high-quality, embodied reasoning-centric vision-language (VL) data:
1. **Textual Descriptions:** Gemini 2.5 Pro generates diverse textual descriptions of tabletop layouts with common household items.
2. **Image Synthesis:** FLUX.1-dev generates high-quality images from these descriptions. Images are augmented with fisheye distortion and composited robot grippers to resemble real robot observations.
3. **Task Instruction and Reasoning Generation:** Gemini 2.5 Pro generates task instructions and corresponding reasoning content for each synthetic image. These tasks fall into:
 - **Visual Grounding Tasks:** Instructions implicitly refer to objects (spatial, attributes, semantic features), and reasoning explicitly identifies the object and its location.
 - **Long-Horizon Tasks:** Instructions describe multi-step objectives, and reasoning provides step-by-step plans, sometimes incorporating HRI elements.
This pipeline generated 16,000 data samples, significantly augmenting the robot training data and helping activate web knowledge within the pre-trained VLM.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

OneTwoVLA's effectiveness is validated through extensive real-world experiments across four key capabilities, demonstrating superior performance compared to a flat VLA (π₀) and a dual-system baseline.

#### 4.1. Long-Horizon Task Planning and Generalization

- **Performance:** OneTwoVLA achieved an average success rate of 87% across three challenging long-horizon tasks (Tomato-Egg, Hotpot, Cocktail), outperforming π₀ by 30% and the dual-system approach by 24%. It consistently generated correct plans, tracked progress, and produced precise actions.
- **Baseline Limitations:** π₀ often lost track of its current step due to a lack of explicit reasoning, while the dual-system approach suffered from a lack of mutual awareness (System Two issuing infeasible commands) and latency, leading to outdated guidance and out-of-distribution states for System One.
- **Generalizable Planning (OneTwoVLA-VL):** Co-training with the synthetic vision-language data significantly enhanced generalization. OneTwoVLA-VL achieved a 70% success rate on novel tasks requiring deep commonsense reasoning (e.g., "Get Icy Cola" requiring opening a fridge, "Tool Use" for out-of-reach objects), whereas OneTwoVLA (without VL co-training) and π₀ showed minimal capabilities. This highlights the transfer of knowledge from VL data to robot control.

#### 4.2. Error Detection and Recovery

OneTwoVLA demonstrated a strong ability to detect errors in real-time, reason about corrective strategies, and execute recovery actions.
- **Quantitative Results:** It successfully recovered from 80% of error occurrences across Hotpot and Tomato-Egg tasks, significantly better than π₀ (57.1%) and the dual-system approach (58.3%).
- **Mechanism:** When a failure occurred (e.g., failed grasp, dropped object), OneTwoVLA autonomously entered reasoning mode, determined a corrective strategy (e.g., retract, reposition, retry grasp with increased firmness), and then executed the recovery. The dual-system often failed to respond promptly due to latency, entering unrecoverable states.

#### 4.3. Natural Human-Robot Interaction

OneTwoVLA excelled in natural human-robot interaction (HRI) due to its adaptive nature and explicit reasoning process.
- **Quantitative Results:** It achieved a 100% success rate in 20 subtask-level interactions on Hotpot and Cocktail tasks, compared to the dual-system's 65%.
- **Adaptive Interaction:** OneTwoVLA seamlessly handled human interventions (e.g., changing cocktail ingredients mid-task) and proactively sought clarification when faced with ambiguity ("Which cocktail would you like?"). Its reasoning process allowed it to immediately adjust plans and actions.
- **Generalizable HRI:** With VL data co-training, OneTwoVLA-VL achieved a 72.5% success rate on 20 novel interaction scenarios, demonstrating proactive reasoning (e.g., asking "Which plate of meat would you like?" when two meat plates are present) and dynamic adaptation to unseen interruptions.

#### 4.4. Enhanced Visual Grounding

OneTwoVLA significantly improved visual grounding, the ability to link language instructions to specific objects in the visual world.
- **Single-Environment (Single-Env):** OneTwoVLA achieved 78% success, vastly outperforming π₀ (5%) in tasks requiring understanding of spatial relationships, object attributes, and semantic features. This performance gap indicates that explicit reasoning helps the model genuinely understand the visual world rather than merely overfitting actions. The action MSE for π₀ was 62% higher than OneTwoVLA's.
- **Open-World Generalization (OneTwoVLA-VL):** In diverse, unseen environments with novel objects, OneTwoVLA-VL achieved a 73% success rate, significantly outperforming OneTwoVLA (8%) and π₀ (3%). This demonstrates the effectiveness of reasoning-centric VL co-training in transferring commonsense knowledge and activating web knowledge within the pre-trained VLM, enabling generalization even to objects unseen in any training data.

#### 4.5. Other Notable Findings (from Appendix)

- **Multi-Modal Actions:** OneTwoVLA can produce multi-modal actions. When instructed to "Grasp cube" with two identical cubes, it adaptively reasoned to grasp either the left or right cube, exhibiting balanced selection, unlike π₀ which consistently chose only right-side objects. This suggests that the probabilistic nature of its reasoning output can lead to diverse action choices.
- **Reasoning-Compliant Actions:** Actions generated by OneTwoVLA consistently align with its reasoning, even if the reasoning itself is incorrect. This unified cognition-behavior suggests that improving reasoning accuracy would directly lead to more appropriate actions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

#### 5.1. Contributions to General-Purpose Robotics

OneTwoVLA represents a significant step towards developing more intelligent and capable general-purpose robots by effectively addressing the limitations of decoupled reasoning-acting systems. Its core contributions and potential impact are:

1. **Unified, Synergistic Intelligence:** By integrating reasoning and acting into a single, adaptively switching model, OneTwoVLA brings robots closer to human-like physical intelligence. This unified approach fosters a deeper, more context-aware understanding, where reasoning guides actions, and actions provide feedback for subsequent deliberation.
2. **Enhanced Robustness and Adaptability:** The model's ability to adaptively reason, detect errors, and recover in real-time makes robots more robust to unexpected situations and dynamic environments. This is crucial for deploying robots outside controlled laboratory settings.
3. **Natural Human-Robot Collaboration:** OneTwoVLA's capability for natural human-robot interaction, including seeking clarification and responding to dynamic interruptions, is pivotal for seamless integration of robots into human-centric environments, enabling more intuitive and effective collaboration.
4. **Superior Generalization:** The scalable pipeline for synthesizing embodied reasoning-centric vision-language data, combined with co-training, significantly boosts the model's generalization abilities. This allows robots to understand novel instructions, identify unseen objects, and plan for tasks beyond their explicit training data, a key requirement for open-world deployment.
5. **Efficiency and Performance Balance:** The adaptive inference mechanism ensures that reasoning is invoked only when necessary, balancing the computational cost of complex reasoning with the need for efficient action generation, making the system practical for real-world scenarios.

This work contributes substantially to the ongoing paradigm shift towards generalist AI in robotics, providing a more holistic and integrated framework for robot intelligence.

#### 5.2. Limitations and Future Directions

The authors candidly acknowledge several limitations and outline promising avenues for future research:

1. **Heuristic Reasoning Triggers:** OneTwoVLA currently relies on hand-selected heuristics to determine when to reason. Future work could explore reinforcement learning (RL) based training to learn more optimal and natural reasoning triggers, moving beyond supervised fine-tuning.
2. **Reasoning Latency:** Although adaptive, reasoning still requires the robot to pause for a few seconds when complex deliberation is needed. Developing asynchronous architectures that allow simultaneous reasoning and action generation could further enhance efficiency and fluidity.
3. **Action Inference Efficiency:** As the unified model scales, the inference time for actions might become a bottleneck. Integrating advanced inference techniques from large language model (LLM) literature could accelerate action generation.
4. **Data Source Diversity:** Due to resource constraints, the study focused on high-quality synthetic vision-language data. Future research could investigate the impact of even more diverse sources of vision-language data on VLA reasoning capabilities, potentially including web-scale, less curated datasets.
5. **Multi-Modal Action Ambiguity:** While OneTwoVLA shows multi-modal action capability, explicitly clarifying reasoning for ambiguous instructions (e.g., "grasp left cube") might indicate that the multi-modality isn't fully emergent for truly ambiguous cases, or that the system defaults to reasoning when unsure. Further research could explore how to balance explicit reasoning with inherent multi-modal action generation in ambiguous scenarios.

This research presents a compelling advancement in embodied AI, proposing a unified model that intelligently interweaves reasoning and acting. OneTwoVLA's demonstrated capabilities in complex tasks, error recovery, human interaction, and generalization underscore its potential to pave the way for a new generation of more autonomous and adaptable robots.
