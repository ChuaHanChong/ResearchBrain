# Exploring Data Scaling Trends and Effects in Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at ByteDance Seed developed data-centric strategies for Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) to mitigate reward hacking and improve response diversity in large language models. Their approach, combining a hybrid reward system, intelligent prompt selection, and early-stage task prioritization, improved overall performance by 1.4 points on challenging test sets and led to specific gains of up to 3.9 points in STEM tasks.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) trained with Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) often suffer from "reward hacking," where they optimize for reward scores without truly improving performance or aligning with human preferences.
- RLHF training can lead to a deterioration of model response diversity, resulting in less varied and creative outputs as the model prematurely converges on coarse distinctions.
- Simply increasing the quantity of RLHF training data does not reliably improve performance, indicating data-driven bottlenecks beyond scale.

## Method

- Implemented a hybrid reward system combining objective ==Reasoning Task Verifiers== (RTVs) and a Generative Reward Model (GenRM) with ground truth, alongside a conventional Bradley-Terry model.
- Developed Pre-PPO, a prompt selection method, to identify and prioritize challenging training prompts with lower initial reward scores that are less susceptible to reward hacking.
- Applied strategic task prioritization during early RLHF training, focusing initially on mathematical and coding tasks which benefit from robust ground-truth assessments.

## Results

- The proposed data-centric approach consistently outperformed baselines, achieving a 1.4-point overall improvement on challenging test sets and up to 3.9-point gains in STEM tasks.
- Strategies scaled positively to larger models (**150B** parameters) and enhanced generalization, particularly on more difficult and out-of-distribution prompts.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both Pre-PPO (sustaining learning beyond baseline plateau) and early task emphasis (accelerating learning in coding/math) contributed to the gains.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reward models vary in susceptibility to hacking; Reasoning Task Verifiers (RTVs) are highly resistant, while Generative Reward Models (GenRM) with ground truth are robust, and traditional preference models are more vulnerable.
> - Effective Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) training requires prioritizing data quality and strategic data utilization over mere quantity, as increasing low-quality data can degrade performance.
> - Learning fine-grained distinctions first, particularly from tasks with clear ground truth, helps mitigate the negative impact of prematurely optimizing for coarse-grained patterns from less robust reward signals.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) has emerged as a cornerstone technique for aligning large language models (LLMs) with human values, preferences, and instructions. Pioneering models like OpenAI's ChatGPT/GPT-4, Anthropic's Claude, and Google's Gemini all heavily rely on RLHF (specifically, Proximal Policy Optimization, PPO, or its variants) to generate responses that are helpful, harmless, and honest.

The existing research landscape in RLHF has largely concentrated on two main fronts:
1. **Algorithmic Advancements:** Many studies aim to improve the efficiency and performance of RLHF algorithms. Examples include efforts to reduce computational overhead by modifying PPO (e.g., dropping the critic model as in) or by filtering noisy samples for more efficient training.
2. **Reward Model Improvements:** A significant body of work focuses on enhancing the capability of the reward model itself to mitigate the notorious "reward hacking" problem, where the model learns to exploit flaws in the reward function rather than achieving the intended objective. This is crucial because a mis-specified or inaccurate reward model directly leads to suboptimal or even harmful LLM behavior.

However, the authors identify a critical gap in this landscape: "the critical role of prompt-data construction and its scalability has received comparatively less attention." While some work has explored data selection for DPO or identifying key training prompts from a complete set, there has been a lack of systematic analysis on how to *select and structure training prompts* to substantially improve performance in PPO-based RLHF, particularly concerning data scaling.

This paper directly addresses this overlooked area. Instead of solely focusing on algorithmic modifications or general reward model improvements, Shen et al. investigate the *data-driven bottlenecks* in RLHF performance scaling. They recognize that simply increasing the *quantity* of training prompts does not necessarily translate into better performance, especially when issues like reward hacking and diminished response diversity are prevalent. Their contribution lies in proposing novel data construction methods and training strategies that complement existing algorithmic advancements, providing a more holistic approach to improving RLHF effectiveness. By focusing on *data quality* and *strategic data utilization*, this work offers practical methodologies that can enhance the alignment and capabilities of LLMs in a robust and scalable manner.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to systematically explore and mitigate the data-driven bottlenecks that impede the performance scaling of Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) for Large Language Models.

The authors are primarily motivated by two persistent challenges observed during RLHF training:

1. **Reward Hacking:** The phenomenon where LLMs learn to generate responses that artificially inflate their reward scores without genuinely improving task performance or aligning with true human preferences. This leads to an initial performance rise followed by a decline, as the model "hacks" the reward system rather than learning the desired behavior. The paper explicitly notes that this issue permeates various tasks, including mathematics, creative writing, and instruction following.
2. **Deterioration of Model Response Diversity:** As RLHF training progresses, the model's response entropy often continuously declines, leading to a reduction in the variety and creativity of its outputs. This lack of diversity not only limits the model's adaptability across diverse tasks and contexts but also suggests that the model might be prematurely converging on coarse-grained distinctions, overlooking valuable fine-grained nuances in responses. The authors acknowledge that existing mitigation strategies like iterated RLHF or RL from pre-trained models have not fully resolved these issues.

Recognizing that simple expansion of training data quantity does not guarantee improved RLHF performance (as demonstrated by their initial experiments with increased prompt datasets that failed to yield gains), the core motivation shifts from "more data" to "smarter data." The paper aims to develop a *proactive strategy* through careful data construction and optimized training methods to enhance RLHF performance *before* these two pervasive problems significantly hinder model improvement. This approach seeks to maximize the benefits of RLHF by addressing its fundamental data-related limitations.

To achieve this, the authors set out to:
- Design a robust reward system resistant to reward hacking by combining reasoning task verifiers (RTV) and a generative reward model (GenRM) that can leverage ground truth.
- Propose a novel prompt selection method, Pre-PPO, to identify and prioritize challenging training prompts that are less prone to reward hacking and contain rich fine-grained response variations.
- Investigate the impact of prioritizing specific task types, particularly mathematical and coding tasks, during the early phases of RLHF training, given their inherent fine-grained distinctions and clearly defined ground truths.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a standard RLHF pipeline but introduces novel data construction and training strategies to address the identified bottlenecks. The overall framework consists of three main stages:

1. **Initial Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT):** A pre-trained language model is initially fine-tuned on human-written demonstrations to establish foundational instruction-following capabilities.

2. **Reward Model Training:** This crucial stage involves preparing and training three distinct types of reward models to provide comprehensive and robust feedback:
 - **Bradley-Terry (BT) Reward Model:** This is a conventional reward model trained on pairwise comparison data. It learns to infer underlying reward scores by optimizing parameters via maximum likelihood estimation from human preference judgments.
 - **Generative Reward Model (GenRM):** This model is trained using Pairwise Reward Modeling (pairRM). Critically, it operates in two modes:
 - **GenRM with Ground Truth (GT):** For reasoning tasks (e.g., math, logical reasoning), explicit ground-truth answers are collected and used as a reliable reference for comparison. This provides a strong, objective signal.
 - **GenRM without Ground Truth (SFT Best-of-N):** For other task types (e.g., creative writing), the trained BT Reward Model is used to select the "best" outcome from *N* candidate samples generated by the supervised fine-tuned (SFT) model. This serves as a proxy for a preferred response when direct ground truth is unavailable.
 - **Reasoning Task Verifiers (RTV):** These are specialized, often deterministic, verifiers designed to directly validate the correctness of model responses for specific tasks. Examples include code sandboxes for programming tasks, which can execute and evaluate code outputs objectively. RTVs are inherently highly resistant to reward hacking due to their clear, verifiable criteria.

3. **Reinforcement Learning Optimization (PPO):** The language model is iteratively optimized using PPO, guided by a hybrid reward signal from a combination of GenRM and RTV. The novelty here lies in the *strategic curation and scheduling of training prompts*:

 - **Pre-PPO for Training Prompts Selection:**
 - **Initial Observation:** Simply adding more prompts (e.g., 5 million new prompts to 1 million original) did not improve RLHF performance; instead, it often led to decline due to reward hacking in high-scoring new prompts. Manual inspection revealed that ~90% of newly collected prompts, despite receiving high reward scores from the GenRM, exhibited reward hacking.
 - **Pre-PPO Strategy:** The authors designed an algorithm, "Pre-PPO," to explicitly identify and select prompts with *lower* reward model scores from an initial PPO run. The rationale is that these "low-scoring" prompts are inherently more challenging for the model to learn from and, crucially, are *less susceptible to reward hacking*. Before selection, reward model scores are normalized within each task domain to account for distinct distributions. Only the bottom 10% of prompts based on their scores are selected and combined with the original dataset for subsequent RL training. This focuses training on "hard" but "clean" examples.

 - **Early-stage RLHF: Prioritizing Mathematical and Coding Tasks:**
 - **Initial Observation:** Test scores for coding and math tasks steadily improved throughout initial training, likely because these tasks are assessed by RTV and GenRM with ground-truth references, making them more resistant to reward hacking. They naturally encode fine-grained distinctions.
 - **Prioritization Strategy:** The RLHF model is explicitly trained *first* on a higher proportion of mathematical and coding prompts during the early stages. Specifically, training begins exclusively on coding prompts, gradually introduces mathematical prompts, and then transitions to the complete mixed-domain training dataset. This leverages the robustness and fine-grained nature of these tasks to build a strong foundation, leading to accelerated learning and superior performance in these specialized areas and potentially general capabilities.

By integrating these data-centric strategies—a robust, hybrid reward system, intelligent prompt selection, and strategic task prioritization—the authors aim to proactively steer RLHF training towards more stable learning, higher quality outputs, and better generalization, effectively overcoming the identified data-scaling bottlenecks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments, conducted on two model sizes (25B and 150B parameters) and evaluated using two versions of a comprehensive test set (V1.0 and more challenging V2.0), yield several significant findings:

1. **Overall Performance Improvement:** The proposed "Data Scale" approach (combining Pre-PPO and early prioritization of math/coding tasks) consistently and significantly outperforms the baseline PPO method. On TestSet V1.0, a noticeable improvement of +1.1 points was observed, and on the more challenging TestSet V2.0, the gain increased to +1.4 points overall, demonstrating robustness and generalizability. Human evaluations further corroborated these improvements across various abilities, showing an overall gain of +3.2 points.

2. **Strong Generalization on Challenging Tasks:** The larger performance gains observed on TestSet V2.0 (which contains substantially more challenging prompts) indicate that the proposed approach leads to models with robust generalization capabilities, particularly on harder and potentially out-of-distribution tasks.

3. **Significant Task-Specific Gains:** The method notably enhanced performance in mathematics-intensive (STEM) and coding tasks, with improvements of +3.9 points in STEM (Large, V2.0) and +3.2 points in coding (Large, V1.0). This validates the effectiveness of strategically prioritizing these tasks during early training phases. Human annotators reported noticeable improvements in logical coherence, informational density for STEM, and accuracy/richness for coding.

4. **Ablation Studies:**
 - **Pre-PPO Effectiveness:** The Pre-PPO method showed sustained performance improvement beyond the 2000-step mark, whereas the baseline plateaued. This suggests that the selected low-score, challenging prompts are more resistant to "hacking," fostering continued and effective learning.
 - **Early Training Emphasis Effectiveness:** Prioritizing mathematical and coding tasks significantly improved overall RLHF performance, particularly for coding and STEM tasks. For coding, performance surpassed the baseline plateau as early as 1000 training steps, demonstrating accelerated learning.

5. **Positive Scaling Trends with Model Size:** The effectiveness of both Pre-PPO and early emphasis strategies scaled positively to the larger 150B model, achieving significantly better performance than the baseline. This suggests the methods are applicable and beneficial across different model scales.

6. **Counter-Intuitive Data Scaling in Pre-PPO:** A crucial finding was that *increasing the quantity* of newly collected training data within the Pre-PPO strategy (from 10% to 20% or 50% of the lowest-scoring prompts) led to a *degradation* in overall RLHF performance. This strongly suggests that "high-quality training prompts are scarce in real-world settings" and that "simply scaling data quantity does not guarantee improvement," emphasizing the need for quality over quantity.

7. **Reward Hacking Susceptibility Analysis:**
 - **RTV:** Exhibited the strongest resistance to reward hacking, with test scores continuously improving throughout training.
 - **GenRM with Ground Truth:** Showed consistent improvements up to approximately 2800 training steps before potential decline.
 - **BT Reward Model / GenRM without Ground Truth (SFT Best-of-N):** Most susceptible to hacking, with improvements only up to about 2100 steps, after which test scores declined. This finding directly supports the authors' hybrid reward system design.

8. **Early Acquisition of Fine-Grained Differences:**
 - Analysis of "edit distance" (granularity of response differences) revealed that GenRM with GT and RTV were sensitive to *fine-grained distinctions*, while GenRM without GT primarily captured *coarse-grained differences*.
 - The Pre-PPO strategy, by implicitly or explicitly filtering certain types of prompts, and the early emphasis on math/coding tasks (which have fine-grained distinctions and ground truths), guides the model to capture fine-grained distinctions first. This is hypothesized to mitigate the negative impact of prematurely learning coarse-grained patterns from tasks supervised by less robust reward models.

9. **Impact on Response Diversity:** While overall response entropy typically declines in RLHF, the proposed method resulted in *higher* response entropy for creative/cosplay tasks (supervised by GenRM without GT) but *lower* entropy for math, coding, and reasoning tasks (supervised by GenRM with GT or RTV). This suggests that removing coarse-grained patterns from the former tasks alleviates diversity decline, while enabling focused learning of precise, fine-grained responses in the latter, enhancing data-scaling effectiveness.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research offers significant contributions to the field of large language model alignment, with both theoretical and practical implications:

1. **Addresses Critical RLHF Bottlenecks:** The paper directly tackles two pervasive and challenging problems in RLHF: reward hacking and the deterioration of response diversity. By providing actionable strategies to mitigate these issues, it enhances the reliability and effectiveness of RLHF, which is foundational for building safe, helpful, and honest LLMs.

2. **Highlights Data-Centric Strategies:** In a field often dominated by algorithmic innovations, this work powerfully underscores the "importance of careful data construction." It demonstrates that simply scaling up data quantity is insufficient; instead, *strategic data quality, selection, and scheduling* are paramount. This shifts the research focus towards a more data-centric approach for RLHF, likely inspiring further investigations into intelligent data curation and augmentation techniques.

3. **Enhances Model Performance and Generalization:** The consistent and significant performance improvements observed across different model sizes and challenging evaluation sets validate the proposed methods. The ability to generalize well, especially to difficult out-of-distribution tasks, is crucial for the real-world utility and robustness of LLMs.

4. **Provides Actionable Methodologies for Practitioners:** The paper offers concrete, implementable strategies for LLM developers:
 - **Hybrid Reward Systems:** Encourages the use of a combination of reward models (RTV for verifiable tasks, GenRM with ground truth where available) to build more hack-resistant feedback loops.
 - **Intelligent Prompt Selection (Pre-PPO):** Advocates for selecting "challenging but less hackable" prompts for training, moving beyond naive data expansion.
 - **Strategic Task Prioritization:** Suggests an effective training curriculum by emphasizing tasks with clear ground truths and fine-grained distinctions (like math and coding) in early training phases.

5. **Deepens Understanding of RLHF Dynamics:** The detailed analysis of reward hacking susceptibility across different reward models and the distinction between fine-grained and coarse-grained learning shed light on the underlying mechanisms of RLHF. The hypothesis that learning coarse-grained patterns prematurely can negatively impact scalability is a crucial insight that can guide future research into curriculum learning for LLMs.

6. **Informs Future Research Directions:** The paper opens several intriguing avenues for future work, including:
 - Optimizing hyperparameter adjustments when scaling from small to large models.
 - Exploring synthetic prompt generation directly from LLMs as a more promising alternative to manual data collection.
 - Connecting the findings on "fine-grained control" to advanced techniques like Chain-of-Thought (CoT) and Constitutional AI (CAI), particularly for training generative reward models to be more sensitive to subtle linguistic nuances and preferences.

In conclusion, this research not only offers practical solutions for immediate challenges in RLHF but also provides fundamental insights into the dynamics of data scaling and learning in alignment processes. Its emphasis on data quality and strategic utilization is a vital step towards developing more reliable, versatile, and high-performing large language models for a wide array of applications.
