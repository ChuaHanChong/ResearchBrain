## Problem

- Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) often exhibit "superficial safety alignment" (SSA), where they produce safe final outputs but harbor unethical or harmful internal reasoning processes.
- This SSA vulnerability allows for potential information leakage or misuse, particularly when confronted with adversarial or "jailbreak" attacks.
- Current safety alignment techniques primarily focus on output-level constraints or parameter modifications, frequently failing to explicitly intervene in or align the intermediate reasoning steps of LRMs.

## Method

- CRAFT employs a two-phase framework: Latent Contrastive Learning for Reasoning (LCLR) structures the latent space of reasoning traces to semantically encode safety information.
- LCLR enforces an ordered geometry where unsafe, rethink, and safe reasoning traces occupy progressively aligned regions using a margin-based triplet loss and calibrates latent representations to probabilistic safety scores.
- Reinforcement over Reasoning Latents (R₂L) utilizes Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with a multi-component reward function, including latent semantic, textual safety, and a crucial latent-textual consistency reward, to align latent reasoning trajectories and synchronize internal safety judgments with external outputs.

## Results

- CRAFT consistently outperformed six state-of-the-art baselines on safety benchmarks, achieving an average 82.1% improvement in reasoning-level safety and 89.6% in final-response safety over base models, and specifically 18.1% and 38.3% gains over the strongest baselines.
- The framework not only preserved but enhanced general reasoning performance, showing an average 8.0% improvement on mathematical and code generation tasks, unlike baselines which often incurred performance reductions.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical contribution of all CRAFT components, particularly the latent-textual consistency reward and the LCLR phase, to the observed safety improvements and robustness against advanced jailbreak attacks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Initial analysis revealed that safe, unsafe, and 'rethink' reasoning traces occupy geometrically distinct regions in the model's latent space, providing a foundation for targeted alignment.
> - Directly manipulating and structuring the latent space of reasoning traces, coupled with a reward that ensures consistency between internal latent safety and external textual safety, is effective in preventing superficial safety alignment.
> - Integrating contrastive learning for latent space structuring with reinforcement learning for policy optimization allows for a robust, reasoning-level safety alignment that also preserves or enhances model utility.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a challenge in the safety alignment of Large Reasoning Models (LRMs), specifically the phenomenon of "superficial safety alignment" (SSA). SSA occurs when an LRM generates a safe refusal as its final output, but its internal reasoning process contains unethical or harmful information. This vulnerability allows for potential information leakage or misuse, particularly under adversarial or "jailbreak" attacks.

The broader research landscape concerning Large Language Models (LLMs) and LRMs has seen significant efforts in safety alignment. These efforts generally fall into several categories:
- **LLM Alignment Techniques:** Standard approaches like Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF), Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), and Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) aim to reduce unsafe generations. More recent studies explore lightweight interventions such as dynamic model editing, parameter modifications, or test-time decoding adjustments to suppress unsafe outputs. These methods primarily target the model's parameters, decoding behavior, or final textual outputs.
- **Reasoning Defense:** With the advent of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting, the focus has expanded to safeguarding the internal reasoning process itself. Existing reasoning defenses can be categorized into:
 - **Training-time alignment:** Methods that curate safety-oriented CoT trajectories or optimize explicit safety-aware reasoning objectives (e.g., STAR-1, RealSafe-R1, SafeChain, Deliberative Alignment).
 - **Inference-time defenses:** Approaches that increase computational resources at test time or steer CoT trajectories during generation (e.g., decoding-time control, early priming, process-level intervention).
 - **Guard models:** Systems that analyze intermediate reasoning traces to detect hidden risks before the final output (e.g., ReasoningShield, ThinkGuard, GuardReasoner).

This research differentiates itself by proactively aligning the model at the reasoning level. Unlike previous methods that primarily focus on output-level constraints, parameter tuning, or post-hoc analysis of reasoning traces, this work directly shapes the latent geometry of reasoning traces. It aims to discourage unsafe internal trajectories during the generation process itself by integrating contrastive learning and reinforcement learning over hidden representations. This direct intervention at the latent state level positions it as a novel approach to mitigate SSA and improve robustness against jailbreak attacks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an alignment framework, named CRAFT (Contrastive Reasoning Alignment: Reinforcement Learning from Hidden Representations), that enhances the robustness of LRMs against jailbreak attacks by preventing superficial safety alignment (SSA).

The detailed motivations for this work are as follows:
- **Addressing Superficial Safety Alignment (SSA):** A significant motivation is the observed phenomenon where LRMs, despite being aligned with standard safety protocols, can still produce unsafe internal reasoning processes even when their final responses are safe refusals. This failure mode represents a critical vulnerability, as harmful reasoning content could potentially be exploited or leak, undermining the perceived safety of the model. The example of a model internally reasoning about how to construct a discriminatory letter while outwardly refusing the request illustrates this issue.
- **Limitations of Current Alignment Strategies:** Existing safety alignment techniques for LRMs (such as RLHF, DPO, or SFT) primarily focus on aligning the final outputs or modifying the model's parameters. These methods often do not explicitly intervene in or align the intermediate reasoning steps. This oversight allows unsafe internal states to persist and potentially lead to SSA. The paper argues for a more profound alignment that extends beyond the output layer into the model's internal thought processes.
- **Leveraging Intrinsic Model Structure:** The researchers identified that different categories of reasoning traces—specifically safe, unsafe, and "rethink" (intermediate/ambiguous) traces—occupy geometrically distinct regions within the model's latent representation space. This observation, demonstrated through PCA projections of hidden states, motivated the hypothesis that directly manipulating and structuring this latent space could be an effective strategy for safety alignment. This suggests an opportunity to steer the model's internal reasoning trajectories towards safer regions.
- **Need for Robust, Reasoning-Level Safety:** To achieve genuinely robust safety, LRMs need to not only produce safe final responses but also conduct their internal reasoning in a safety-aware manner. This requires an alignment mechanism that operates directly on the intermediate reasoning dynamics, guiding them away from harmful pathways from within.
- **Integration of Complementary Machine Learning Techniques:** The motivation extends to combining contrastive representation learning, which is effective at structuring latent spaces based on semantic similarity and dissimilarity, with reinforcement learning, which is adept at optimizing policies based on defined reward functions. This synergistic integration aims to create a framework that can both geometrically structure the latent space for safety and then actively steer the model's generation process within this structured space.
- **Theoretical Rigor:** A further motivation is to provide a theoretical basis demonstrating that the proposed approach can effectively eliminate superficially aligned policies. By incorporating a latent-textual consistency reward, the aim is to mathematically show that such policies are ruled out as local optima, ensuring a more fundamental and robust safety alignment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

CRAFT employs a two-phase framework: Latent Contrastive Learning for Reasoning (LCLR) and Reinforcement over Reasoning Latents (R₂L).

#### 3.1. Latent Contrastive Learning for Reasoning (LCLR)

LCLR is designed to structure the latent space of reasoning traces to semantically encode safety information before reinforcement learning commences. It enforces an ordered geometry where unsafe, rethink, and safe reasoning traces occupy progressively aligned regions.
1. **Hidden Representation Extraction:** For an input prompt $x$ and a generated reasoning trace $\tau = (y_1, \dots, y_T)$, the hidden representation $h$ of the final reasoning token is extracted. This $h$ is then projected to a normalized latent hypersphere vector $z = f_\omega(h) \in \mathbb{R}^k$.
2. **Semantic Labeling:** Each reasoning trace is associated with a semantic label from $\{Unsafe, Rethink, Safe\}$. These labels are derived from model self-evaluation and validated by Llama-Guard, following the R2D-R1 framework. "Rethink" is treated as an intermediate state between safe and unsafe.
3. **Learnable Prototypes:** Class-wise prototypes $\mu_c$ are maintained for each category ($c \in \{\text{Unsafe, Rethink, Safe}\}$) and updated using exponential moving average (EMA) to ensure training stability.
4. **LCLR Objective:** The total LCLR objective ($L_{LCLR}$) is a linear combination of three components:
 - **Structured Geometric Alignment ($L_{proto}$):** A margin-based triplet loss, augmented with a rethink-anchoring term. This loss ensures that safe traces cluster around $\mu_{safe}$, unsafe traces diverge towards $\mu_{unsafe}$, and rethink traces are positioned intermediately around $\mu_{rethink}$. The objective function includes a margin hyperparameter ($\eta$) for separability and a weight ($\gamma_{rt}$) for rethink trace alignment.
 - **Instance-Level Invariance ($L_{inst}$):** This component employs an InfoNCE objective, drawing inspiration from SimCLR. It treats different augmented views of the same reasoning trace (generated via token dropout or paraphrasing) as positive pairs, while all other samples in the batch are considered negatives. This helps ensure that the latent representations are invariant to superficial textual variations, focusing on semantic content.
 - **Latent Safety Calibration ($L_{cal}$):** This loss calibrates the latent representations to probabilistic safety scores. A safety scorer $g_\psi: \mathbb{R}^k \rightarrow$ is trained to map latent vectors to soft labels ($y_{soft} \in \{0, 0.5, 1\}$). The objective combines Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE) with Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence distillation from a frozen textual safety verifier ($p_{text}$). This alignment ensures that smooth transitions in the latent space correspond to calibrated, monotonic changes in safety probability.

#### 3.2. Reinforcement over Reasoning Latents (R₂L)

R₂L is a reinforcement learning framework that uses Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) to align latent reasoning trajectories towards safety and maintain consistency between internal reasoning and final outputs. It introduces a comprehensive reward function:
1. **Latent Semantic Rewards ($R_{ls}$):** This reward explicitly aligns the model's internal reasoning traces by measuring the geometric distance of the projected hidden state $z$ within the reasoning trajectory relative to the learned prototypes ($\mu_{safe}$, $\mu_{unsafe}$, $\mu_{rethink}$). It encourages the model's internal monologue to remain within a safe manifold, using cosine similarity and a tightness coefficient ($\alpha$).
2. **Textual Safety Reward ($R_{txt}$):** This component ensures the final generated response $y$ adheres to safety policies. It is derived from an external discriminator (e.g., StrongReject score) that provides a probability $P(S|y)$ of the text being safe. The reward is formulated to provide a symmetric and zero-centered gradient signal, $2 \cdot P(S|y) - 1$, penalizing unsafe outputs.
3. **Latent-Textual Consistency Reward ($R_{cons}$):** To prevent representation-output mismatch (SSA), this reward synchronizes the model's internal latent safety judgment with its external textual safety. It is defined as $1 - |p_z - P(S|y)|$, where $p_z$ is the safety probability predicted from the latent space by a safety head $\psi$. Maximizing this reward encourages a unified safety representation across internal and external behaviors.
4. **Overall Reward and Optimization:** The total reward $R_{total} = w_{lat}R_{ls} + w_{txt}R_{txt} + w_{cons}R_{cons}$ is optimized using the GRPO algorithm. The weights ($w_{lat}$, $w_{txt}$, $w_{cons}$) control the relative contributions of each reward component.

#### 3.3. Theoretical Analysis

The paper includes a theoretical analysis (Proposition 5.1) demonstrating that the latent-textual consistency reward ($R_{cons}$) eliminates policies exhibiting SSA. Under assumptions of Lipschitz continuity for the projection and safety heads, local controllability of policies, GRPO convergence to local optima, and a fixed textual evaluator, the analysis proves that policies with significant internal-external safety mismatch cannot be locally optimal stationary policies. This implies that GRPO will steer the model away from SSA states.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of CRAFT involved two strong reasoning models (Qwen3-4B-Thinking and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B) on multiple safety benchmarks and general reasoning tasks.

- **Initial Latent Space Structure:** Prior to alignment, PCA projections of hidden states revealed a clear geometric separation between safe and unsafe reasoning traces in LRMs. Rethink traces consistently occupied an intermediate region, indicating transitional states. Quantitative analysis confirmed that the centroid of rethink traces lay between safe and unsafe centroids along the principal safety axis (projection coefficient $\alpha$ between 0.53 and 0.83 across models).
- **Improved Superficial Safety Alignment (SSA) Robustness:**
 - CRAFT consistently outperformed six state-of-the-art reasoning-based safety alignment baselines (SafeChain, RealSafe, STAR, SafeKey, IPO, ReasoningShield) on the JailbreakBench and StrongReject benchmarks.
 - It achieved an average improvement of 82.1% in reasoning-level safety and 89.6% in final-response safety compared to the base models.
 - CRAFT delivered a 18.1% improvement in reasoning-trace safety and a 38.3% gain in final-response safety compared to the strongest baselines.
- **Preservation and Enhancement of Reasoning Performance:**
 - CRAFT incurred the smallest overall performance reduction compared to baselines on mathematical reasoning benchmarks (AIME24, Minerva, MATH-500) and a code generation benchmark (LiveCodeBench).
 - Notably, CRAFT demonstrated an average performance *improvement* of 8.0% over the base models in these tasks. This was attributed to the reasoning-centric SFT or GRPO training, which exposed models to high-quality reasoning trajectories, leading to generalization benefits.
- **Robustness Against Advanced Jailbreak Attacks:**
 - Evaluations against aggressive jailbreak methods like GPTFuzzer and AutoDAN showed that CRAFT achieved substantial safety gains. It improved reasoning-trace safety by 72.1% and final-response safety by 85.9% under these challenging conditions.
- **Contribution of Individual Modules (Ablation Study):**
 - All components of CRAFT were found to contribute materially to its effectiveness.
 - Removing the latent-textual consistency reward ($R_{cons}$) led to the largest degradation, increasing the average jailbreak score by 27.2%.
 - Removing the latent semantic rewards ($R_{ls}$) resulted in a 25.4% degradation.
 - Removing the textual safety reward ($R_{txt}$) led to an 18.0% degradation.
 - Removing the entire LCLR phase, which is foundational for $R_{cons}$ and $R_{ls}$, caused the most severe performance drop, reducing overall safety by 32.4%.
- **Impact on Latent Geometry:**
 - Post-training with CRAFT, PCA visualizations indicated a clearer separation between safe and unsafe reasoning traces.
 - Rethink traces shifted closer to the safe region and exhibited reduced dispersion, consistent with the framework's design to guide ambiguous reasoning states towards safer representations.
- **Independent Evaluator Validation:**
 - CRAFT's safety improvements were further validated using LlamaGuard, an independent safety classifier, confirming that the observed gains were not specific to the training reward function but represented genuine safety alignment.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presents CRAFT as a significant development in the field of large reasoning model safety alignment, offering several potential impacts:

- **Enhanced Model Trustworthiness:** By directly addressing superficial safety alignment (SSA), CRAFT contributes to making LRMs more genuinely trustworthy. It helps prevent scenarios where models outwardly refuse harmful requests but internally engage in unethical reasoning, thereby reducing the risk of hidden information leakage or exploitation. This moves beyond surface-level safety to a deeper, more robust form of alignment.
- **Novel Alignment Paradigm:** The methodology of aligning safety at the latent space level by integrating contrastive learning with reinforcement learning introduces a novel paradigm. This approach extends alignment efforts beyond traditional output-level or parameter-based interventions, opening new avenues for research into controlling and shaping complex model behaviors.
- **Improved Robustness against Adversarial Attacks:** The demonstrated effectiveness of CRAFT against state-of-the-art and aggressive jailbreak attacks, including GPTFuzzer and AutoDAN, suggests that it can significantly enhance the resilience of LRMs in real-world deployment scenarios. This robustness is critical for deploying AI safely in sensitive applications.
- **Balancing Safety and Utility:** CRAFT achieves substantial safety improvements while not only preserving but, in some cases, enhancing the model's core reasoning capabilities in mathematics and code generation. This capability to improve safety without incurring a "safety tax" on performance is crucial for the practical adoption and utility of aligned models.
- **Theoretical Contributions:** The theoretical analysis provides a deeper understanding of why latent-textual consistency helps eliminate SSA, offering a formal basis for the empirical successes. This theoretical backing can guide future research in designing more principled and effective alignment strategies.
- **Generalizability of Latent Structure Insights:** The consistent observation of distinct latent regions for safe, unsafe, and rethink traces across different LRM architectures indicates that this underlying structure may be a general property of reasoning models. This insight could facilitate the development of more universal safety alignment techniques.
- **Ethical AI Development:** The framework is explicitly positioned as a defensive alignment tool, aimed at mitigating harmful information leakage and model misuse. Its successful implementation could contribute to the development of more ethical and responsible AI systems by making their internal processes more aligned with safety guidelines.
- **Future Research Directions:** The work also highlights avenues for future research, such as developing more computationally efficient optimization strategies, exploring adaptive safety signals, and investigating cross-family transferability of latent safety components. These directions could further extend the impact and applicability of latent-space alignment.
