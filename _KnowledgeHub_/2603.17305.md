# Contrastive Reasoning Alignment: Reinforcement Learning from Hidden Representations

> [!summary] Summary
> CRAFT, a framework developed by researchers from Northwestern University, University of Michigan, and Illinois Institute of Technology, mitigates Superficial Safety Alignment in Large Reasoning Models by using contrastive learning to structure hidden representations and reinforcement learning to align internal reasoning. The method improved reasoning-level safety by 79.0% and final-response safety by 87.7% on jailbreak benchmarks, while also boosting reasoning performance by 4.7%.

## Problem

- Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) often exhibit Superficial Safety Alignment (SSA), where final outputs are safe but internal reasoning contains harmful content.
- Existing safety alignment methods primarily focus on output-level alignment, leaving internal reasoning traces vulnerable to expressing harmful or exploitable content.
- Unsafe internal states increase the model's attack surface, allowing adversaries to potentially exploit these hidden vulnerabilities.

## Method

- CRAFT integrates Latent Contrastive Learning for Reasoning (LCLR) to geometrically structure the latent space of reasoning traces, learning prototypes for 'Unsafe', 'Rethink', and 'Safe' categories.
- LCLR enforces a structured geometry in the latent space through a margin-based triplet loss and ensures instance-level invariance and latent safety calibration.
- Reinforcement over Reasoning Latents (R2L) uses Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with a multi-component reward, including latent semantic rewards and a crucial latent-textual consistency reward, to align internal reasoning with external safety.

## Results

- CRAFT achieved an average **79.0%** improvement in reasoning-level safety and **87.7%** improvement in final-response safety on jailbreak benchmarks compared to base models.
- The framework not only preserved but also enhanced reasoning performance, showing an average **4.7%** improvement across mathematical reasoning and code generation tasks.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the Latent-Textual Consistency Reward and Latent Semantic Rewards were critical components, with their removal leading to significant safety degradation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Directly optimizing objectives over a model's hidden state space can proactively guide internal reasoning dynamics towards safety, rather than just filtering final outputs.
> - Ensuring consistency between a model's internal latent-space judgments and its external textual safety evaluations is critical to prevent Superficial Safety Alignment.
> - Structuring the latent space with semantic categories allows for controllable shifts in reasoning trajectories, enabling reinforcement learning to steer the model towards safer internal states.
