# Revisiting LRP: Positional Attribution as the Missing Ingredient for Transformer Explainability

> [!summary] Summary
> Tel Aviv University researchers develop Positional-Aware LRP (PA-LRP), a framework for Transformer explainability that explicitly attributes relevance to positional encoding components alongside token embeddings, addressing a fundamental flaw in existing Layer-wise Relevance Propagation methods that violate conservation properties by ignoring positional information, achieving consistent performance improvements across NLP (7-51% AU-MSE reduction on IMDB classification, 3-8% gains on LLaMA-3 zero-shot tasks) and vision tasks (4-point average improvement on ImageNet perturbation tests, 2-3% gains in segmentation metrics) by introducing specialized LRP rules for various positional encoding types (learnable, sinusoidal, RoPE, ALiBi) and reformulating the input space as position-token pairs with per-layer positional sinks, with empirical analysis revealing that positional relevance constitutes 9-22% of total model relevance in vision transformers, demonstrating that structural and spatial cues carry distinct explanatory value complementary to semantic attributions.

## Problem

- Existing LRP methods for Transformers ignore positional encoding during relevance propagation, violating the conservation property and losing significant relevance information (9-22% of total relevance)
- Current explainability approaches fail to attribute the distinct structural and spatial information encoded in positional components, leading to incomplete and potentially unfaithful explanations
- State-of-the-art methods like AttnLRP produce fragmented attribution maps that miss holistic object-level patterns captured by positional encoding

## Method

- Reformulate input space as ==position-token pairs== with separate "sinks" for positional relevance at each layer to prevent information loss during backward propagation
- Design specialized ==LRP rules== for different positional encoding types: standard LRP-ε for input-level PE (learnable/sinusoidal), matrix multiplication rules for attention-level PE (RoPE), and addition rules for bias-based PE (ALiBi)
- Aggregate final relevance by summing positive contributions from both semantic embeddings and all per-layer ==positional components== using multi-sink approach

## Results

- Consistent improvements across diverse tasks: **14.5-51%** AU-MSE reduction on IMDB classification with LLaMA models, **3.2%** AUAC and **7.7%** AU-MSE gains on zero-shot question answering
- Superior segmentation performance on ImageNet with **1.8-3%** improvements in pixel accuracy and mIoU across DeiT models, demonstrating better object boundary detection
- Empirical validation of theoretical claims showing positional relevance constitutes **9.22-22.39%** of total model relevance in vision transformers, confirming conservation property violations in existing methods

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Positional encoding carries substantial and unique relevance signal that complements semantic attributions, with "PE Only" explanations often matching full AttnLRP baseline performance
> - Positional relevance captures broader spatial and structural patterns (entire objects, sequential relationships) while semantic relevance focuses on specific discriminative features
> - Conservation property violation in existing methods represents a significant theoretical flaw that impacts explanation faithfulness, particularly when positional cues are dominant

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Explainable AI (XAI) is a critical frontier in deep learning, driven by the need to understand, trust, and debug increasingly complex "black-box" models. Transformers, with their massive scale and intricate self-attention mechanisms, pose unique challenges for interpretability, despite their dominance across various domains like Natural Language Processing (NLP) and computer vision.

Historically, XAI for Transformers began with methods leveraging attention maps to quantify token contributions. However, these were later shown to be potentially misleading and not always correlative with true model behavior. This led to the emergence of more robust model-specific techniques. Layer-wise Relevance Propagation (LRP) stands out as a promising approach due to its ability to propagate relevance scores backward through a network, adhering to principles like conservation and stability. Early LRP-based methods for Transformers and subsequent refinements, such as those by Ali et al. (for LayerNorm and attention layers) and Achtibat et al. (AttnLRP, considered state-of-the-art), significantly advanced the field.

This paper identifies and addresses a crucial, yet overlooked, gap in this existing body of LRP-based Transformer explainability research: the explicit attribution of positional encoding (PE). Positional encoding is fundamental to how Transformers process sequential and spatial information, distinguishing them from permutation-invariant architectures. Whether it's input-level PE (e.g., Learnable, Sinusoidal) or attention-level PE (e.g., Rotary PE (RoPE), ALiBi), this component imbues the model with crucial structural understanding.

The authors argue that by ignoring PE during relevance propagation, existing LRP methods for Transformers:
1. **Violate the conservation property:** The total relevance is not preserved from output to input, as a significant portion is lost when traversing PE layers.
2. **Lose unique relevance:** Positional relevance often carries distinct structural and spatial meaning, which is crucial for faithful explanations, especially in tasks dependent on order or relative positioning.
3. **Produce unfaithful explanations:** Without attributing PE, explanations can be incomplete or misleading, particularly when positional cues are highly influential in a model's decision-making.

By proposing Positional-Aware LRP (PA-LRP), this work directly extends and corrects the deficiencies of current state-of-the-art LRP methods for Transformers. It aims to provide more comprehensive and faithful explanations by treating positional information as a first-class citizen in the attribution process. This contribution is particularly timely given the widespread adoption of RoPE in modern large language models (LLMs) such as LLaMA, Pythia, and Gemma, making the developed techniques immediately relevant to understanding these powerful models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation behind this research stems from a critical oversight in existing Layer-wise Relevance Propagation (LRP) methods for Transformer models. While LRP has emerged as a leading approach for explaining deep neural networks, its application to Transformers has consistently neglected the propagation of relevance through positional encoding (PE) layers. This omission, as highlighted by the authors, is not merely a minor detail but a fundamental flaw that compromises the integrity and completeness of the explanations generated.

The paper articulates several key problems arising from this oversight:
- **Violation of the Conservation Property:** LRP's theoretical grounding relies on the conservation principle, ensuring that the total relevance at the output of a layer is preserved when propagated to its inputs. The authors rigorously demonstrate (Lemma 3.1) that ignoring PE in input-level Transformers leads to a violation of this property, meaning a portion of the model's relevance is effectively "lost" during the back-propagation process.
- **Loss of Unique Positional Relevance:** Positional encoding is explicitly designed to inject crucial sequential and spatial information into Transformers. By not attributing relevance to these components, current methods discard a unique and important type of relevance associated with structural and positional features. This leads to fragmented and less informative heatmaps, as visually demonstrated in Figure 1, where the proposed method captures more of the object's holistic relevance compared to the baseline.
- **Unfaithful Explanations:** As shown in Lemma 3.3, in simplified scenarios where positional features are critical, existing LRP methods without PE awareness produce unfaithful explanations, indicating that their ability to accurately represent feature importance is compromised.

Driven by these identified limitations, the paper sets out the following key objectives:
1. **Reformulate the Input Space:** The primary objective is to redefine the input space for Transformer explainability. Instead of solely focusing on token or patch embeddings, the new formulation treats the input as "position-token pairs," explicitly introducing positional features as distinct entities for attribution. This includes defining per-layer "sinks" for positional relevance to ensure proper absorption and prevent loss of information.
2. **Develop Specialized LRP Rules for Positional Encoding:** Design theoretically-grounded LRP rules specifically tailored for various positional encoding methods prevalent in modern Transformers. This includes learnable PE, sinusoidal PE, Rotary PE (RoPE), and ALiBi, ensuring broad applicability. These rules are designed to facilitate stable and faithful propagation of relevance through these components.
3. **Restore the Conservation Property:** Through the newly designed rules, the objective is to ensure that the conservation property of LRP is upheld for Transformers by correctly accounting for the relevance contributed by positional information (Lemma 3.2).
4. **Enhance Explainability Faithfulness:** By integrating positional attribution, the goal is to produce more faithful, comprehensive, and holistic explanations that accurately reflect how both semantic and positional cues influence a Transformer's predictions.
5. **Demonstrate Superiority Empirically:** Validate the proposed method (PA-LRP) against the state-of-the-art (AttnLRP) through extensive experiments across diverse tasks (NLP classification, zero-shot QA, next token prediction, vision classification), model types (fine-tuned, foundation models like LLaMA 3), and model sizes.
6. **Facilitate Adoption:** Provide a publicly available, open-source implementation of the method to encourage its adoption and further research within the community.

In essence, the paper aims to correct a fundamental oversight in Transformer XAI, ensuring that the critical role of positional information in model decision-making is fully and faithfully attributed, leading to more trustworthy and insightful explanations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of this paper's methodology lies in introducing **Positional-Aware LRP (PA-LRP)**, a novel approach designed to overcome the limitations of existing LRP methods for Transformers by explicitly accounting for positional encoding (PE). The approach involves two fundamental modifications: a reformulation of the input space and the introduction of specialized LRP rules for PE.

#### 3.1. Reformulating the Input Space

Traditional Transformer explainability defines the input space solely as embedded tokens ($E_i$). PA-LRP redefines this as a set of **position-token pairs**, denoted as $(E_i, (P_{i,1}, P_{i,2}, \dots, P_{i,K}))$. Here, $E_i$ represents the semantic embedding for token $i$, and $P_{i,k}$ represents the per-layer positional embedding for token $i$ at layer $k$. This formulation introduces separate "sinks" for positional relevance at each layer, ensuring that positional attributions are not lost or overshadowed, and that information from various layers is properly absorbed.

#### 3.2. Custom LRP Rules for Positional Encoding

The paper proposes specific LRP rules for different types of PEs, ensuring theoretical grounding and stability.

- **Input-Level PE (e.g., Learnable PE, Sinusoidal PE):**
 - **Learnable PE:** For combined input representation $z_i = P'_i + E_i$, relevance $R(z_i)$ is propagated to the positional component $P'_i$ using the standard LRP-$\epsilon$ rule for addition: $R(P'_i) = P'_i \cdot \frac{R(z_i)}{P'_i + E_i + \epsilon}$.
 - **Sinusoidal PE:** Since these PEs are fixed (non-trainable), the authors suggest reparameterizing the positional embedding matrix (similar to $P'$) with the sine and cosine values. This avoids complex gradient propagation through non-linear functions while still allowing the system to attribute relevance to these fixed positional signals.

- **Attention-Level PE (e.g., Rotary PE (RoPE), ALiBi):**
 - **RoPE:** This PE modifies query (Q) and key (K) matrices using position-dependent rotation matrices $R_{i,k}$. PA-LRP flattens $R_{i,k}$ into a vector $P_{i,k}$ to serve as a relevance sink. Relevance is propagated from the modified queries $\tilde{Q}$ and keys $\tilde{K}$ (which receive relevance from the attention mechanism) back to the rotation matrices $R_{i,k}$ using the LRP rule for matrix multiplication (adopted from AttnLRP), then summed as $R(R_{i,k}) = \frac{1}{2}R(\tilde{Q}_i) + \frac{1}{2}R(\tilde{K}_i)$.
 - **ALiBi:** Similar to RoPE, ALiBi modifies attention scores by adding a linear bias $P_{i,j} = m(|i-j|)$. Relevance from the adjusted attention scores $R(A'_{i,j})$ is propagated to $P_{i,j}$ using the LRP-$\epsilon$ rule. Then, relevance from $R(P_{i,j})$ is propagated to the positional indices $i$ and $j$, treating $m$ as a constant.

#### 3.3. Relevance Aggregation

The final relevance for each token $i$ ($R_i$) is computed by aggregating the positive contributions from both the semantic embedding ($E_i$) and all per-layer positional embeddings ($P_{i,k}$). This is done by summing positive values following a ReLU-like function: $R_i = \sum_{d \in [D]} E_i[d]^+ + \sum_{k \in [K]} \sum_{d' \in [D']} P_{i,k}[d']^+$. This "multi-sink" approach is crucial for preventing the loss of positional relevance due to negative contributions in later layers.

#### 3.4. Overall Framework and Efficiency

PA-LRP integrates these new PE-aware rules with the existing state-of-the-art AttnLRP rules for non-positional components of the Transformer. The method does not require any changes to the Transformer model itself; instead, it proposes an "equivalent forward path" for the purpose of defining accurate backward propagation rules. This design choice maintains computational efficiency comparable to a single backward pass, making it practical for large models.

#### 3.5. Theoretical Analysis

The methodology is supported by theoretical proofs (detailed in Appendix E) demonstrating that the proposed LRP rules:
- **Satisfy the conservation property:** Lemma 3.2 proves that PA-LRP rules ensure relevance conservation for attention-level PEs, contrasting with existing methods (Lemma 3.1).
- **Improve faithfulness:** Lemma 3.3 illustrates how current methods fail to provide faithful explanations when positional features are dominant, implying PA-LRP's advantage.

#### 3.6. Evaluation

The effectiveness of PA-LRP is evaluated through quantitative and qualitative experiments across NLP and vision domains using various metrics:
- **Perturbation Tests (NLP & Vision):** Measure how model predictions change as tokens/pixels are masked based on relevance scores (positive: highest relevance first; negative: lowest relevance first). Metrics: AUAC (Area Under Activation Curve, higher is better) and AU-MSE (Area Under MSE, lower is better).
- **Segmentation Tests (Vision):** Assess the quality of attributed masks in distinguishing foreground from background using metrics like Pixel Accuracy and Mean Intersection-over-Union (mIoU).
- **Baselines and Ablation Studies:** Comparison against AttnLRP (SoTA) and ablation variants like "PE Only" (isolating positional relevance) and "w/o Multi-Sink" (evaluating the aggregation strategy).

This comprehensive methodological approach ensures that PA-LRP is not only theoretically sound but also empirically validated across diverse and challenging Transformer applications.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted across both NLP and vision domains, involving various Transformer models and tasks, consistently demonstrate the significant improvements offered by Positional-Aware LRP (PA-LRP) over existing state-of-the-art methods.

#### 4.1. Qualitative Analysis (Vision)

Visualizations in Figure 3 (and Appendix C) reveal striking differences:
- **Effectiveness of PE-associated relevance:** The "PE Only" ablation variant, which attributes relevance solely to positional components, produces explainability maps that are surprisingly on par with the full AttnLRP baseline. This crucial finding empirically validates the authors' initial hypothesis: positional encoding carries a substantial and often overlooked signal, emphasizing the importance of dedicated PE-aware rules.
- **Uniqueness of PE-associated relevance:** The "PE Only" maps show distinct characteristics, capturing broader spatial and structural patterns (e.g., distributing relevance across an entire object like a snake or bird). This contrasts with the often sparser, highly selective patterns of the AttnLRP baseline, which tends to focus on specific "semantic" patches. This suggests that positional relevance captures higher-level structural concepts.
- **Importance of Balancing:** The most compelling finding is that the combined approach of PA-LRP (labeled "Ours") yields the most robust and comprehensive explanations. By integrating both semantic (from AttnLRP) and positional (from PA-LRP's new rules) relevance, it produces a richer and more faithful attribution map, indicating that these two types of relevance are complementary.

#### 4.2. Quantitative Analysis (NLP)

- **Finetuned Models (IMDB Classification - Table 1):** PA-LRP consistently outperforms AttnLRP across different LLaMa-2 variants (7B, Quantized, Tiny-LLaMa). Notably, PA-LRP achieves significant reductions in AU-MSE (lower is better for error) for generation scenarios: 14.5% for LLaMa 2-7B, 10.6% for LLaMa 2-7B Quantized, and a remarkable 51.41% for Tiny-LLaMa. This indicates that PA-LRP leads to explanations that more accurately pinpoint features critical for prediction.
- **Zero-Shot Models (LLaMa 3-8B - Table 3):** In zero-shot settings, PA-LRP again demonstrates superiority. For Multiple-Choice Question Answering (MCQA), it improves AUAC by approximately 3.2% and AU-MSE by 7.7%. For Next Token Prediction (NTP), improvements are around 0.5% for AUAC and 3% for AU-MSE. The authors note that the smaller improvement in NTP might be due to the longer texts in the Wikipedia dataset, making shifts in relevance distributions less critical.
- **Ablation Study (Table 2):** The "PE Only" variant's performance, often comparable to or even better than AttnLRP, reinforces the notion that positional relevance is a significant, distinct signal. Furthermore, ablating the "multi-sink" approach (which draws only positive contributions across layers) showed a 7% drop in performance, validating its importance for maintaining positional relevance.

#### 4.3. Quantitative Analysis (Vision)

- **Perturbation Tests (ImageNet, DeiT Variants - Table 4):** PA-LRP outperforms AttnLRP, particularly in negative perturbation tests (where less relevant elements are masked first), showing an average improvement of 3.97 points across three DeiT model sizes. This indicates better stability of predictions when unimportant features are removed. While PA-LRP occasionally lags slightly in positive perturbation (by at most 1.2 points), its overall performance is strong.
- **Segmentation Tests (ImageNet, DeiT Variants - Table 5):** PA-LRP consistently achieves higher Pixel Accuracy and Mean Intersection-over-Union (mIoU) compared to AttnLRP across all DeiT sizes. For the Tiny DeiT model, PA-LRP significantly improves Pixel Accuracy from 74.815% to 76.613% and mIoU from 52.850% to 55.920%. These results confirm PA-LRP's superior ability to delineate foreground objects from the background, reflecting more accurate and holistic spatial attributions.

#### 4.4. Conservation Percentage (Appendix F, Figure 8)

Empirical measurements show that the "PE Only" relevance constitutes a significant portion (9.22% to 22.39%) of the total relevance in DeiT models. This directly validates Lemma 3.1, demonstrating that disregarding positional embeddings indeed leads to a non-negligible violation of the conservation property in existing LRP methods.

In summary, the results compellingly demonstrate that PA-LRP not only theoretically addresses a fundamental gap in Transformer explainability but also empirically outperforms existing methods across a wide range of tasks and models, providing more faithful and robust explanations by properly attributing positional information.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in "Revisiting LRP: Positional Attribution as the Missing Ingredient for Transformer Explainability" carries significant implications for the field of Explainable AI, particularly in the context of Transformer models.

**Addressing a Critical Oversight:** The most immediate impact is the rectification of a fundamental oversight in existing LRP-based Transformer explainability methods. By formally recognizing and attributing relevance to positional encoding (PE), the paper fills a crucial gap that has, until now, led to incomplete and potentially misleading explanations. This is particularly relevant as modern Transformers, including large language models, heavily rely on various forms of PE (e.g., RoPE, ALiBi) to capture sequential and spatial relationships.

**Enhancing Explanation Faithfulness and Trust:** By providing a more comprehensive and faithful attribution of model decisions, PA-LRP improves the trustworthiness of AI systems. Users and developers can gain deeper insights into *why* a Transformer made a particular prediction, understanding not just the semantic contributions but also how positional or structural cues influenced the outcome. This holistic view is vital for critical applications where interpretability is paramount.

**Improved Model Understanding and Debugging:** The ability to disentangle semantic and positional relevance offers a powerful diagnostic tool. Researchers can better characterize which concepts are primarily driven by positional information (e.g., object boundaries, relative word order) versus semantic content. This deeper understanding can aid in debugging model failures, identifying biases related to spatial or sequential patterns, and guiding future model design. For instance, if a vision Transformer consistently misclassifies objects based on their placement, PA-LRP could highlight the undue influence of positional features.

**Broad Applicability and Practicality:** The proposed PA-LRP rules are designed to be compatible with various common PE methods (Learnable, Sinusoidal, Rotary, ALiBi), ensuring broad applicability across different Transformer architectures (e.g., LLaMA, DeiT). The method's computational efficiency, comparable to a single backward pass, makes it practical for integration into existing deep learning pipelines without significant overhead, even for large foundation models. The release of open-source code further facilitates its adoption and encourages community-driven extensions.

**Strengthening Theoretical Foundations:** The work provides theoretical backing for the conservation property of LRP when PE is considered, demonstrating that ignoring PE leads to a violation. By formally addressing this, the paper strengthens the theoretical rigor of LRP applications to Transformer architectures, paving the way for more robust and reliable interpretability methods.

**Catalyst for Future Research:** While PA-LRP successfully addresses positional attribution, the authors themselves acknowledge a limitation: they focus on new PE rules but do not systematically revisit or redesign existing LRP rules for other Transformer components (e.g., the self-attention mechanism itself). This implicitly opens new avenues for research, encouraging the community to further refine LRP rules across the entire Transformer architecture, potentially leading to even more precise and stable explanations.

In conclusion, this paper delivers a significant step forward in Transformer explainability. By meticulously identifying and rectifying the overlooked role of positional encoding in LRP, it provides a more complete, faithful, and theoretically sound framework for understanding these dominant deep learning models, thereby enhancing transparency, trust, and the overall utility of AI.
