# LLaVA-CoT: Let Vision Language Models Reason Step-by-Step

> [!summary] Summary
> LLaVA-CoT, from a collaboration including Peking University and Tsinghua University, introduces a Vision-Language Model capable of autonomous multistage reasoning, enhanced by a novel test-time self-correction mechanism named Stage-wise Retracing Search (SWIRES). It improves reasoning-intensive tasks by 5.8% over its base model and demonstrates competitive performance against larger multimodal models.

## Problem

- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) struggle with systematic and structured reasoning for complex visual question-answering (VQA) tasks, unlike Large Language Models (LLMs).
- Existing VLM Chain-of-Thought (CoT) implementations frequently produce errors, inconsistencies, or hallucinations because their reasoning processes are often not sufficiently systematic or structured.
- VLMs tend to initiate responses without proper problem organization, deviate from logical paths, or prematurely state conclusions, leading to flawed reasoning trajectories.

## Method

- The model employs an ==autonomous multistage reasoning== approach, decomposing answer generation into four distinct, explicitly tagged stages: Summary, Caption, Reasoning, and Conclusion.
- A specialized ==LLaVA-CoT-100k dataset== was created using GPT-4o to generate detailed, structured reasoning annotations for a diverse set of VQA pairs, then used for supervised fine-tuning of the Llama-3.2-11B-Vision-Instruct model.
- ==Stage-wise Retracing Search (SWIRES)==, a novel test-time scaling method, dynamically assesses the quality of intermediate reasoning steps and can "retrace" to earlier stages to regenerate better outputs, enhancing self-correction during inference.

## Results

- LLaVA-CoT achieved a **5.8%** average benchmark score increase over its Llama-3.2-11B-Vision-Instruct base model across six multimodal reasoning benchmarks.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both the multistage format and explicit structured tags within the LLaVA-CoT-100k dataset are essential for the observed performance gains in reasoning-intensive tasks.
- SWIRES consistently outperformed other test-time scaling strategies, exhibiting a stronger scaling effect with increased computation and enabling the **11B** LLaVA-CoT to achieve competitive performance against larger and closed-source state-of-the-art multimodal models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly structured reasoning, guided by distinct stages and tags, is crucial for imbuing VLMs with systematic, in-depth thinking abilities.
> - A high-quality, multistage reasoning dataset (LLaVA-CoT-100k) is essential for training VLMs to adopt and effectively utilize structured thought processes.
> - Dynamic, stage-aligned self-correction during inference, as implemented by SWIRES, significantly improves VLM robustness and accuracy by recovering from suboptimal intermediate steps, outperforming traditional test-time scaling methods.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research presented in LLaVA-CoT addresses a critical challenge in the rapidly evolving field of Vision-Language Models (VLMs): the struggle to perform systematic and structured reasoning, especially for complex visual question-answering (VQA) tasks. While Large Language Models (LLMs) have demonstrated remarkable advancements in reasoning capabilities, VLMs, which integrate both language and vision, often fall short when tasks demand logical, multi-step thought processes.

Historically, early open-source VLMs primarily relied on direct prediction, generating concise answers without explicitly showcasing a structured reasoning path. This direct-response paradigm limits their effectiveness in scenarios requiring in-depth analysis. The introduction of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning marked a significant improvement, encouraging LLMs to break down complex problems into step-by-step solutions. This approach has been extended to VLMs, with models attempting to generate CoT explanations. However, existing VLM CoT implementations frequently suffer from issues like errors, inconsistencies, or "hallucinated" outputs because their reasoning processes are often not sufficiently systematic or structured. Models might initiate responses without proper problem organization, deviate from logical paths, or prematurely state conclusions before adequately justifying them, leading to flawed reasoning trajectories.

LLaVA-CoT positions itself as a significant advancement by addressing these limitations head-on. It moves beyond conventional CoT prompting by proposing an *autonomous multistage reasoning* approach that is inherently both systematic and structured. Unlike prior works that might apply CoT superficially, LLaVA-CoT forces the model into distinct, explicitly tagged reasoning stages (Summary, Caption, Reasoning, Conclusion), ensuring a clear and organized thought process. This structured approach aims to mitigate the common pitfalls of unguided CoT, where models often generate erroneous intermediate steps.

Furthermore, the paper innovates in the domain of test-time scaling. Existing test-time scaling methods like majority voting, best-of-N search, or standard beam search often operate at fixed granularities or struggle with effectively correcting errors introduced in early stages. LLaVA-CoT introduces **Stage-wise Retracing Search (SWIRES)**, a novel approach that dynamically assesses the quality of intermediate reasoning steps and, if necessary, "retraces" to earlier stages to regenerate better outputs. This self-correction mechanism represents a more adaptive and robust way to improve VLM performance during inference, moving beyond simple selection from multiple independent generations. By focusing on deep, systematic reasoning and a sophisticated test-time scaling strategy, LLaVA-CoT seeks to push the boundaries of multimodal AI towards more reliable, human-like cognitive capabilities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind LLaVA-CoT stems from the observation that despite substantial progress in Large Language Models (LLMs) demonstrating strong systematic and in-depth reasoning, Vision-Language Models (VLMs) lag in this regard. Current VLMs often struggle with complex visual question-answering tasks that necessitate structured and systematic reasoning. Even when employing Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting, VLMs frequently produce errors or hallucinated outputs, primarily because their reasoning processes lack sufficient structure and discipline. They tend to initiate responses without adequately organizing the problem, deviate from logical reasoning pathways, or prematurely present conclusions, which then propagates errors through subsequent steps.

Given these challenges, the key objectives of this research are:

1. **Develop an Autonomous Multistage Reasoning VLM:** To introduce LLaVA-CoT, a large VLM capable of independently engaging in a predefined sequence of reasoning stages: summarization, visual interpretation, logical reasoning, and conclusion generation. This aims to imbue VLMs with a more robust and systematic reasoning framework.
2. **Achieve Marked Improvements in Reasoning-Intensive Tasks:** By implementing this structured approach, the goal is to significantly enhance LLaVA-CoT's performance on VQA tasks that demand complex logical thinking and systematic analysis.
3. **Construct a Structured Reasoning Dataset:** To create the LLaVA-CoT-100k dataset, which integrates diverse visual question-answering sources with explicit, structured reasoning annotations. This dataset is crucial for supervised fine-tuning the model to adopt the desired multistage reasoning process.
4. **Propose an Effective Test-Time Scaling Method:** To introduce Stage-wise Retracing Search (SWIRES), a novel test-time scaling technique that not only enhances the model's reasoning capabilities but also incorporates an error-correction mechanism by allowing the model to retrace and regenerate suboptimal intermediate steps.
5. **Demonstrate Superior Performance:** To empirically show that LLaVA-CoT, with its structured reasoning and test-time scaling, can outperform its base model and even surpass the capabilities of larger or closed-source state-of-the-art multimodal models on a range of reasoning benchmarks.

In essence, the paper is motivated by the need for VLMs to evolve beyond superficial CoT prompting towards a truly structured and self-correcting reasoning paradigm, thereby unlocking deeper cognitive abilities for multimodal understanding.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

LLaVA-CoT's methodology is built upon two principal pillars: enhancing reasoning capability through structured thinking during training and optimizing inference with a novel test-time scaling mechanism.

#### 3.1. Enhancing Reasoning Capability Through Structured Thinking (Training Phase)

The core idea during the training phase is to instill a systematic and in-depth reasoning ability into a VLM. This is achieved by decomposing the answer generation process into four distinct, self-initiated reasoning stages, each serving a specific purpose:

- **Summary Stage:** The model begins by interpreting the question at a high level, outlining its approach and the primary aspects of the problem to be addressed.
- **Caption Stage:** It then provides a concise overview of the visual elements in the image that are directly relevant to the question, ensuring a grounded understanding of the multimodal input.
- **Reasoning Stage:** Building on the summary and visual interpretation, the model conducts a structured, logical, step-by-step analysis to derive a preliminary answer.
- **Conclusion Stage:** Finally, LLaVA-CoT synthesizes a coherent answer based on the preceding reasoning. This output is the direct response to the user, with its conciseness or detail adapted to the user's requirements.

To ensure the model adheres to this structured process, special, dedicated tags (e.g., `<SUMMARY>...</SUMMARY>`) are used to mark the beginning and end of each stage. These tags guide the model to seamlessly transition between stages without external prompting after training.

**Data Preparation and Model Training:**
To train LLaVA-CoT, a specialized dataset called **LLaVA-CoT-100k** was constructed. Since most existing VQA datasets lack the detailed reasoning processes required, this new dataset integrates approximately 99,000 image-QA pairs from a diverse range of widely used VQA sources, including general-purpose datasets (ShareGPT4V, ChartQA, A-OKVQA, DocVQA, PISC, CLEVR) and science-targeted datasets (GeoQA+, AI2D, ScienceQA, CLEVR-Math).

Crucially, the detailed reasoning processes (Summary, Caption, Reasoning, Conclusion) for these QA pairs were generated using **GPT-4o**. A carefully designed prompt guided GPT-4o to produce responses strictly in the four-stage format, ensuring the final conclusion matched the original dataset's correct answer. A subsequent verification step filtered out non-compliant or inconsistent generations, ensuring high-quality structured annotations.

The **Llama-3.2-11B-Vision-Instruct** model was chosen as the base VLM. Full parameter supervised fine-tuning (SFT) was performed on this base model using the LLaVA-CoT-100k dataset. The training was conducted on a single node equipped with 8 H100 GPUs, with specific hyperparameters detailed in the supplementary material (e.g., learning rate, epochs, batch size, mixed precision).

#### 3.2. Test-Time Scaling via Stage-wise Retracing (SWIRES)

After training, LLaVA-CoT's reasoning capability is further enhanced during inference through a novel test-time scaling method called **Stage-wise Retracing Search (SWIRES)**. This method addresses the limitations of traditional beam search by introducing dynamic, stage-aligned search granularity and a self-correction mechanism.

- **Stage-wise Beam Search (Precursor to SWIRES):**
 - Unlike conventional beam search that operates at fixed intervals (e.g., after a set number of tokens), stage-wise beam search aligns its search steps with the completion of each reasoning stage.
 - At each stage, the model generates `M` candidate responses and selects the top `N` most promising ones based on a reward model. These `N` responses then branch out to generate candidates for the next stage.
 - **Limitation:** This can still suffer from local optima; an early-stage error might not be recoverable in later stages.

- **Stage-wise Retracing Search (SWIRES):**
 - SWIRES builds upon stage-wise beam search by integrating a **retracing mechanism**.
 - At each reasoning stage, after generating `M` candidate responses, a reward model (InternLM-XComposer2.5-Reward) evaluates their quality.
 - If *none* of the `M` candidates surpass a predefined reward threshold (calculated based on the mean and standard deviation of reward scores), it signals that the output from the *previous* stage might be suboptimal.
 - In such cases, the algorithm retains the current stage's candidates but *retraces* to the previous stage to regenerate its response. These newly generated previous-stage responses are then used to generate new candidates for the current stage.
 - If any of these new candidates meet the threshold, the search for the current stage concludes, the top `N` candidates are selected, and the process moves to the next stage. Otherwise, generated responses are added to a candidate pool, and retracing continues up to a maximum of `C` times.
 - Empirically, retracing is applied starting from the caption stage, as the summary stage typically produces high-quality outputs. This adaptive retracing allows LLaVA-CoT to dynamically correct errors, significantly improving its robustness and accuracy during complex inference tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of LLaVA-CoT across a range of multimodal reasoning benchmarks demonstrates its effectiveness and superior performance.

#### 4.1. Post-Training Performance and Ablation Studies

- **Overall Performance:** LLaVA-CoT, trained with only 100k samples, achieved a significant average benchmark score increase of **5.8%** over its base model, Llama-3.2-11B-Vision-Instruct, across six multimodal reasoning benchmarks (MMStar, MMBench, MMVet, MathVista, AI2D, HallusionBench). This validates the fundamental effectiveness of the structured reasoning approach.

- **Ablation Study: Effectiveness of LLaVA-CoT-100k Dataset:**
 - A model trained directly on original Q&A pairs (without structured CoT) from the LLaVA-CoT-100k sources showed some general improvement but performed significantly worse than LLaVA-CoT, particularly on benchmarks like MMVet which require detailed responses. This underscores that the **multistage format** and explicit reasoning annotations within LLaVA-CoT-100k are crucial for enhancing reasoning, not just the quantity of data.

- **Ablation Study: Importance of Structured Tags:**
 - Removing the explicit structured tags (e.g., `<SUMMARY>...</SUMMARY>`) from the LLaVA-CoT-100k dataset during training resulted in a substantial drop in performance. This finding highlights that these tags are essential for guiding the model to adhere to the structured reasoning process and leverage it effectively.

- **Performance Gains in Reasoning-Intensive Areas:**
 - An analysis of performance across different skill areas on the MMStar benchmark revealed that LLaVA-CoT primarily yields improvements in tasks requiring systematic reasoning, such as **instance reasoning, logical reasoning, mathematics, and science & technology**. Gains in coarse and fine-grained perception were comparatively smaller, confirming that the method effectively boosts the model's higher-level reasoning capabilities.

#### 4.2. Test-Time Scaling Results

- **Comparison of Scaling Strategies:** Under comparable computational budgets (measured by test time), the proposed SWIRES method consistently outperformed stage-wise beam search, which in turn outperformed the best-of-N search strategy. This indicates the superior efficacy of SWIRES in utilizing computational resources for better inference.

- **Scaling Trend Analysis:** SWIRES demonstrated the strongest scaling effect as computation time increased. While stage-wise beam search and best-of-N search plateaued (or even slightly declined for best-of-N) around 10,000 seconds on MMStar, SWIRES continued to show performance improvements. This highlights its robust scalability and enhanced error correction capabilities. With scaling, LLaVA-CoT improved its average benchmark score from 62.4% to **65.5%**.

#### 4.3. Comparison to State-of-the-Art (SOTA) VLMs

- LLaVA-CoT (an 11B model, when equipped with SWIRES) achieved competitive and often superior performance against many larger open-source models (e.g., Llama-3.2-90B-Vision-Instruct, Deepseek-VL2 27B, Qwen2-VL-7B) and even some closed-source models (GPT-4o-mini, Gemini-1.5-pro).
- Notably, LLaVA-CoT achieved these results despite being fine-tuned from Llama-3.2-11B-Vision-Instruct, which was the weakest performer among the evaluated base models. This underscores that the architectural and methodological innovations of LLaVA-CoT, rather than simply model size, are responsible for its strong reasoning performance, especially on tasks filtered for reasoning-intensity (MMStar-R, MMBench-R, MMVet-R).

#### 4.4. Qualitative Insights

Qualitative examples (Figures 2, 7, 8) clearly illustrate LLaVA-CoT's ability to generate coherent, step-by-step reasoning, correctly interpreting visual information and performing calculations, in stark contrast to the base model's often flawed, generalized, or hallucinatory responses. The effect of test-time scaling in correcting intermediate reasoning errors is also visually demonstrated, providing clear evidence of its practical benefit.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of LLaVA-CoT marks a notable advancement in the field of Vision-Language Models (VLMs), particularly in their ability to perform complex, systematic reasoning. The work's significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Enhanced Multimodal Reasoning:** LLaVA-CoT significantly bridges the gap between the strong reasoning capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs) and the visual understanding of VLMs. By enabling autonomous, multistage reasoning with explicit structural guidance, it allows VLMs to tackle complex visual question-answering (VQA) tasks more effectively and reliably than previous approaches, which often suffered from unstructured reasoning or hallucination.

- **Novel Methodological Contributions:** The paper introduces two key innovations:
 - **Structured Thinking:** The decomposition of reasoning into distinct, tagged stages (Summary, Caption, Reasoning, Conclusion) provides a robust framework for guiding VLM thought processes, addressing a fundamental limitation of less-structured Chain-of-Thought (CoT) methods.
 - **Stage-wise Retracing Search (SWIRES):** This novel test-time scaling method offers a dynamic and self-correcting inference mechanism. By allowing the model to retrace and regenerate suboptimal intermediate outputs, SWIRES enhances performance reliability and error correction, moving beyond passive selection of the best-of-N generations. This approach demonstrates superior scalability with increasing computational resources compared to existing methods.

- **Efficiency and Performance:** Achieving state-of-the-art or highly competitive performance against larger and even closed-source models, despite using a comparatively smaller 11B base model and a modest 100k training dataset (LLaVA-CoT-100k), highlights the efficiency and effectiveness of the proposed methodology. This suggests that focused architectural and training innovations can yield substantial gains without necessarily requiring massive scaling of model parameters or data.

- **Broader Applicability:** The success of LLaVA-CoT across diverse reasoning benchmarks, including general VQA, mathematical reasoning, scientific VQA, and hallucination control, indicates its broad applicability. This versatility makes it a promising candidate for various real-world applications requiring detailed multimodal understanding.

- **Open Science and Future Research:** The public release of the code, dataset, and pre-trained weights promotes transparency, reproducibility, and accelerates further research in multimodal reasoning. The authors acknowledge limitations, such as occasional failures with overly complex images, suggesting clear avenues for future work, including the potential application of reinforcement learning to refine complex multimodal reasoning.

In conclusion, LLaVA-CoT represents a crucial step towards developing more intelligent and trustworthy multimodal AI systems. By enabling VLMs to reason step-by-step in a systematic and self-correcting manner, it moves closer to human-like cognitive abilities, thereby unlocking new possibilities for human-AI interaction and problem-solving in visually rich environments.
