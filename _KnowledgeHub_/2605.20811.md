# Demo-JEPA: Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture for One-shot Cross-Embodiment Imitation

> [!summary] Summary
> Demo-JEPA introduces a framework for one-shot cross-embodiment imitation learning by inferring target-compatible latent goals from visual demonstrations using a Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture. The approach allows robots to transfer skills across diverse morphologies, achieving a 0.36 success rate in zero-shot simulated tasks and 0.25 in real-world scenarios, outperforming concatenation baselines.

## Problem

- The "embodiment gap" in robotic imitation learning, stemming from physical, kinematic, and action space mismatches between demonstrators and learning robots, hinders skill transfer.
- Traditional action-centric imitation learning methods are data-intensive, requiring extensive multi-embodiment data or explicit action alignment, which limits generalization for heterogeneous agents.
- Pixel-level predictive models in imitation from observation often capture task-irrelevant visual details, leading to computational expense, compounding errors, and poor generalization under significant embodiment variations.

## Method

- Formulates cross-embodiment imitation as latent goal-conditioned planning, where the target robot infers a target-compatible future latent goal from a source visual demonstration.
- Utilizes **Demo-JEPA**, an architecture featuring an action-conditioned ==world model== for target dynamics and a 'Dreamer Predictor' that translates source visual demonstrations into target-compatible future latent states.
- Employs a three-stage training process: pretraining a target world model, training the Dreamer Predictor with a latent reconstruction loss, and co-training the world model's dynamics predictor to align its rollouts with the Dreamer's predicted goals.

## Results

- Demo-JEPA demonstrated superior zero-shot generalization, achieving a 0.36 success rate in simulation and 0.25 in real-world unseen task configurations, compared to 0.**04/0**.00 for VPP and 0.**03/0**.05 for XSkill.
- The Dreamer Predictor successfully converts heterogeneous demonstrations into target-compatible latent goals, with Demo-JEPA's performance closely approaching that of an oracle model (0.45 vs. 0.55 in sim and 0.55 vs. 0.58 in real-world for Cross-Embodiment Bridging).
- Ablation studies confirmed the importance of 3D convolutional fusion for preserving structured temporal information, and a scaling study showed that task diversity is more critical than per-task episode volume for learning transferable latent mappings.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling the intent of a demonstration from its embodiment-specific execution, by interpreting demonstrations as future latent goals, enables more robust cross-embodiment skill transfer.
> - Joint Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPA) effectively learn semantically meaningful latent representations that abstract away nuisance visual variations and morphological differences, facilitating generalization.
> - An adaptive goal updating mechanism, which advances the current subgoal only upon successful achievement, stabilizes long-horizon tasks and prevents premature temporal progression in planning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic imitation learning is a field focused on enabling robots to acquire skills by observing human or other robot demonstrations. A fundamental challenge in this area is the "embodiment gap," which arises from physical, kinematic, and action space mismatches between the demonstrator and the learning robot. This gap significantly hinders the direct transfer of skills.

Previous methodologies to address the embodiment gap often rely on action-level alignment. These include Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, large-scale multi-embodiment co-training, the use of shared or aligned action spaces, explicit action correspondences, or heuristic action retargeting techniques. While these approaches can be effective in specific contexts, they face limitations when dealing with highly heterogeneous agents, where identical high-level behaviors require vastly different low-level motor commands. Such methods typically demand substantial multi-embodiment data and may struggle to generalize across diverse morphologies.

Another line of research, imitation from observation, focuses on recovering executable control policies from visual-only demonstrations without explicit action labels. Approaches like Visual Foresight and latent world models learn action-conditioned dynamics to predict future observations and optimize actions towards desired visual outcomes. However, these often operate in pixel space, leading to computational expense, compounding errors over long horizons, and a tendency to capture visually irrelevant details rather than abstract, task-relevant structures. This pixel-centric focus can limit generalization under significant embodiment variations.

This paper positions itself as a departure from action-centric paradigms by proposing an objective-centric view of demonstrations. It leverages advancements in world models and latent goal planning, specifically building upon Joint Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPA). JEPA-based methods learn robust, semantically meaningful latent representations by predicting future latent states rather than reconstructing pixels. This allows them to abstract away nuisance visual variations and embodiment-specific noise, making them suitable for cross-embodiment transfer. The work aims to bridge the embodiment gap by inferring demonstration intent as a future latent goal, thus avoiding the explicit action alignment bottleneck.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a framework for cross-embodiment imitation learning that effectively decouples the intent of a demonstration from its embodiment-specific execution. The core motivation stems from the limitations of traditional action-centric imitation learning methods when faced with the embodiment gap.

Specific objectives and motivations include:

- **Addressing the Embodiment Gap without Action Alignment:** The central bottleneck in cross-embodiment imitation is the disparity in physical and kinematic characteristics, as well as action spaces, between different robotic systems or human demonstrators. Existing methods often attempt to align actions directly, which is inherently restrictive and data-intensive for heterogeneous agents. The authors aim to circumvent this by reformulating demonstrations as implicit specifications of desired future states (goals) rather than explicit sequences of motor commands.
- **Decoupling Intent from Execution:** The research is motivated by the observation that humans can infer the underlying purpose of an observed behavior and adapt it to their own bodies, regardless of morphological differences. This suggests that the "what" (the desired outcome or intent) is more crucial than the "how" (the specific motor execution). The framework seeks to enable robots to similarly infer the demonstrator's intent and realize it using their own unique actuation capabilities.
- **Leveraging Predictive Latent Spaces for Robust Abstraction:** Traditional pixel-level predictive models often allocate representational capacity to task-irrelevant visual details (e.g., background, lighting, specific hardware appearance). This limits their ability to generalize across varying embodiments. The motivation behind using a Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) is its capacity to learn abstract, predictive representations of world structure. By operating in such a latent space, the system can preserve high-level behavioral intent while abstracting away low-level perceptual and morphological discrepancies, making it robust for cross-embodiment transfer.
- **Enabling Latent Goal-Conditioned Planning:** The work proposes to formulate cross-embodiment imitation as latent goal-conditioned planning. This means the target agent infers a target-compatible future latent trajectory (subgoals) from a source visual demonstration and then uses its own learned forward dynamics model to plan actions that achieve these subgoals. This approach eliminates the need for shared action spaces, explicit action correspondences, or heuristic retargeting.
- **Reducing Data and Engineering Requirements:** A practical motivation is to reduce the costly need for cross-embodiment action annotation or extensive data engineering. Demo-JEPA is designed to operate with only offline visual demonstrations from the source embodiment and the target agent's own interaction experience, making it more flexible and scalable.
- **Demonstrating Robust Generalization:** A key objective is to show that the proposed framework can achieve robust generalization capabilities, not only matching in-domain planners but also excelling in zero-shot tasks and under significantly different embodiment configurations where prior methods typically fail.

In summary, the research aims to fundamentally shift the paradigm of cross-embodiment imitation from action reproduction to intent inference and latent goal-conditioned planning, offering a more generalizable and data-efficient solution.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Demo-JEPA formulates cross-embodiment imitation as latent goal-conditioned planning within a learned world model. The methodology involves a three-stage training procedure and an adaptive inference pipeline.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The setting involves a source embodiment (E_s) providing a visual demonstration trajectory ($\tau_s = \{o_s_k\}$) and a target embodiment (E_t) interacting with the environment through observations ($o_t_k$), states ($s_t_k$), and actions ($a_t_k$). The core challenge is that E_s and E_t may have disparate morphology, kinematics, action spaces, and visual appearance.
At each time step, Demo-JEPA takes the current target observation $o_t_k$ and a pair of source frames ($o_s_k$, $o_s_{k+n}$) from $\tau_s$ as input. Instead of directly predicting actions, it first infers a target-compatible latent goal $z_{t,goal}$ representing the intended future state. An action-conditioned world model then generates actions by optimizing rollouts that match this latent goal.

#### 3.2. Demo-JEPA Architecture

The framework is built around two main components: an Action-conditioned World Model and a Dreamer Predictor.

- **Action-conditioned World Model:** This component utilizes VJEPA2.1 to model the target embodiment's dynamics in a latent space.
 - **JEPA Encoder E(·):** Maps raw visual observations $o_t_k$ into latent states $z_k = E(o_t_k)$.
 - **Dynamics Predictor F_wm(·):** Models future latent transitions conditioned on the current latent state, robot state, and action: $\hat{z}_{k+1} = F_{wm}(z_k, s_t_k, a_t_k)$. It can recursively predict states over a horizon.
 - **Action Optimization:** Given a latent goal $z_{t,goal}$, actions $a_{t,k:k+H-1}$ are obtained by minimizing the latent distance $d(F_{wm}(z_k, s_t_k, a), z_{t,goal})$. This optimization is performed using the Cross-Entropy Method (CEM), an iterative sampling-based approach to find optimal action sequences.

- **Dreamer Predictor:** This embodiment-aware latent predictor translates source visual demonstrations into target-compatible future latent states within the shared JEPA representation space.
 - **Input:** Current target observation $o_t_k$ and a source frame pair ($o_s_k$, $o_s_{k+n}$).
 - **Shared Encoder:** Maps all observations ($o_t_k$, $o_s_k$, $o_s_{k+n}$) into latent representations ($z_t_k$, $z_s_k$, $z_s_{k+n}$).
 - **Cross-Attention Modules:** Two cross-attention modules capture:
 - **Cross-embodiment correspondence:** $f_{emb} = \text{Attn}(Q=z_t_k, K=z_s_k, V=z_s_k)$.
 - **Temporal evolution of demonstrated behavior:** $f_{mot} = \text{Attn}(Q=z_s_{k+n}, K=z_s_k, V=z_s_k)$.
 - **3D Convolutional Fusion (ϕ):** Aggregates $z_t_k$, $f_{emb}$, and $f_{mot}$ into a fused feature representation $f_{fused} = \phi(z_t_k \oplus f_{emb} \oplus f_{mot})$.
 - **Transformer Predictor (T):** Decodes $f_{fused}$ into the final latent goal $\hat{z}_{t,goal} = T(f_{fused})$. This goal serves as the objective for the action-conditioned world model.

#### 3.3. Implementation Details (Training Procedure)

The training of Demo-JEPA proceeds in three stages:

- **Stage 0: Pretrained Target World Model:** An action-conditioned world model (VJEPA2.1) for the target embodiment is pretrained using its own interaction data. This stage is external to the core contribution of Demo-JEPA and its details are provided in the appendix.

- **Stage 1: Latent Goal Predictor Training:**
 - **Objective:** To train the Dreamer Predictor ($P_\theta$) to map source-embodiment transitions into target-compatible latent goals.
 - **Data:** Action-free cross-embodiment demonstrations (paired visual trajectories from source and target).
 - **Loss Function:** A latent reconstruction loss, $L_{pred} = \|\hat{z}_{goal} - E(o_{t,k+n})\|^2_2$, where $\hat{z}_{goal}$ is the predicted latent goal and $E(o_{t,k+n})$ is the ground-truth future target latent state.
 - **Temporal Perturbation:** To improve generalization, the source frame pair is sampled with a random temporal shift $\delta \sim U(-r, r)$ (i.e., $(o_{s,k+\delta}, o_{s,k+n})$). This exposes the predictor to diverse latent transitions.
 - **Optimization:** Only the Dreamer Predictor is optimized in this stage.

- **Stage 2: Action Co-Training:**
 - **Objective:** To adapt the world model's dynamics predictor ($F_{wm}$) to align its latent rollouts with the goal distribution produced by the (now frozen) Dreamer Predictor.
 - **Loss Function:** A planning loss, $L_{plan} = \|F_{wm}(z_{t,k}, s_{t,k}, a_{t,k:k+n-1}) - \hat{z}_{goal}\|^2_2$, where $F_{wm}$ predicts a future latent state based on an action sequence, and $\hat{z}_{goal}$ is inferred by the frozen Dreamer Predictor.
 - **Optimization:** Only the dynamics predictor $F_{wm}$ is unfrozen and optimized to minimize $L_{plan}$.

#### 3.4. Inference with Adaptive Goal Updating

During inference, the source demonstration $\tau_s$ acts as a sequence of reference segments.

- **Initial Goal Inference:** An initial latent goal $\hat{z}_{goal}$ is inferred by the Dreamer Predictor based on the current target observation and an initial source reference pair ($o_{s,i}$, $o_{s,i+\Delta}$).
- **CEM Planning:** The CEM planner optimizes target actions $a^*$ towards $\hat{z}_{goal}$ using the world model.
- **Execution and Observation:** The first action $a^*_0$ is executed, and a new target observation $o_{t,next}$ is obtained.
- **Adaptive Goal Advancement:** The system computes the latent discrepancy $D_k = d(z_{t,next}, \hat{z}_{goal})$ between the newly observed target state and the intended goal.
 - If $D_k < \epsilon$ (threshold), the current subgoal is considered reached, and the system advances to the next source reference pair ($o_{s,i+1}$, $o_{s,i+1+\Delta}$) and recomputes $\hat{z}_{goal}$.
 - If $D_k \ge \epsilon$, the current goal remains active, the Dreamer Predictor is bypassed, and the planner continues optimizing actions towards the unreached goal. This mechanism stabilizes long-horizon tasks by preventing premature temporal advancement.

#### 3.5. Experimental Setup

Experiments were conducted in simulation (RLBench, Sawyer arm as source, Franka arm as target) and in the real world (UR5e arm as source, Franka arm as target). Six real-world manipulation tasks were defined. Comparative baselines included VPP and XSkill. Training data involved Franka execution trajectories for the world model and paired visual trajectories for the Dreamer Predictor (retargeted end-effector poses for simulation, GTCC-aligned features for real-world). Evaluation covered three suites: behavior grounding (familiar conditions), cross-embodiment bridging (unseen actions), and zero-shot generalization (unseen configurations).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of Demo-JEPA was conducted in both simulated (RLBench) and real-world robotic manipulation tasks across three distinct evaluation suites designed to assess generalization under increasing distribution shifts.

#### 4.1. Simulation Results

- **Behavior Grounding:** VPP exhibited the highest average success rate (0.47), indicating its effectiveness in scenarios closely aligned with its in-domain training data. Demo-JEPA achieved an average of 0.31, while XSkill was at 0.39.
- **Cross-Embodiment Bridging:** As distribution shift increased, Demo-JEPA demonstrated stronger performance, achieving an average success rate of 0.45. This outperformed VPP (0.28) and XSkill (0.17) significantly, suggesting its capacity to infer target-compatible latent goals beyond direct action supervision.
- **Zero-Shot Generalization:** Under the most challenging conditions (unseen task configurations), Demo-JEPA maintained a success rate of 0.36, whereas VPP and XSkill achieved only 0.04 and 0.03, respectively. This highlights the robustness of Demo-JEPA's latent goal inference for cross-embodiment transfer to novel tasks.

#### 4.2. Real-World Results

- **Behavior Grounding:** Similar to simulation, VPP performed best in behavior grounding with an average success rate of 0.65, followed by Demo-DP (0.65) and Demo-JEPA (0.43).
- **Cross-Embodiment Bridging:** Demo-JEPA again showed superior performance under stronger distribution shifts, achieving the best average success rate of 0.55. VPP (0.53) and XSkill (0.40) performed comparatively lower.
- **Zero-Shot Generalization:** Demo-JEPA achieved a success rate of 0.25, outperforming VPP (0.00) and XSkill (0.05). These results confirm that interpreting cross-embodiment demonstrations as latent goals is more effective than action sequences or skill prototypes for bridging the embodiment gap in physical robots.

#### 4.3. Analysis

- **Goal Reference Comparison with V-JEPA 2.1:**
 - A "naive" V-JEPA 2.1 approach, which directly uses future latent states from source demonstrations as goals, consistently failed across all tasks. This indicates that V-JEPA 2.1's latents are not directly transferable across embodiments without adaptation.
 - An "oracle" V-JEPA 2.1, which used ground-truth future target-embodiment trajectories for planning, achieved high success rates (0.55 in sim, 0.58 in real-world for Cross-Embodiment Bridging).
 - Demo-JEPA, using only source demonstrations and current target observations, closely approached the oracle's performance (0.45 in sim, 0.55 in real-world for Cross-Embodiment Bridging). This validates that the Dreamer Predictor successfully converts heterogeneous demonstrations into target-compatible latent goals.

- **Ablations (w/o Conv3D):**
 - Replacing the Conv3D-based fusion module with mean pooling led to a performance decrease, particularly for complex motions and in real-world scenarios. For example, in simulation, "Change Channel" and "Close Box" showed noticeable drops without Conv3D, and in the real world, "Remove Plate" significantly degraded.
 - This suggests that Conv3D's explicit spatiotemporal modeling is crucial for preserving structured temporal information necessary for complex, articulated, or noisy real-world behaviors, as permutation-invariant averaging can be insufficient.

- **Imitation Learning Extension (Demo-DP vs. Demo-JEPA):**
 - "Demo-DP," which conditioned a Diffusion Policy (DP) head on the Dreamer Predictor's output, consistently improved over standard DP in both simulation and real-world settings, demonstrating that predictive latent goals are strong conditioning signals.
 - Demo-DP outperformed VPP and XSkill in zero-shot generalization.
 - Comparing Demo-DP with Demo-JEPA revealed that while Demo-DP excelled in behavior grounding (where DP acts as a strong local action expert), Demo-JEPA was superior in zero-shot generalization. This indicates that planner-based execution (as in Demo-JEPA) is more robust under severe distribution shifts compared to diffusion policy execution.

- **Scaling Study:**
 - An investigation into data and task scaling in simulation indicated that **task diversity is more important than per-task episode volume** for the Dreamer Predictor's performance.
 - At both 20% and 50% data scales, reducing task categories ("task scaling") resulted in lower success rates compared to reducing episode count per task ("data scaling").
 - This suggests that exposure to a broader range of task semantics is the primary driver for learning transferable source-to-target latent mappings, while increasing intra-task trajectories provides incremental improvements in predictor robustness.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work presents a significant contribution to robotic imitation learning by addressing the critical embodiment gap. Its primary significance lies in proposing and validating a novel approach that reframes cross-embodiment imitation as latent goal-conditioned planning, thereby decoupling demonstration intent from specific execution.

The key aspects of its significance and potential impact include:

- **Enhanced Generalization:** Demo-JEPA demonstrates robust zero-shot generalization capabilities across unseen tasks and embodiment configurations, a persistent challenge for prior methods. By inferring abstract latent goals rather than relying on direct action alignment, the framework enables robots to perform tasks learned from diverse demonstrators, including humans or robots with different morphologies and action spaces.
- **Reduced Data Dependency and Cost:** The methodology avoids the need for explicit action-level correspondences, shared action spaces, or manually designed retargeting rules. It primarily relies on visual demonstrations from the source and the target agent's own interaction experience, potentially reducing the need for costly cross-embodiment action annotation and large-scale multi-embodiment co-training datasets. This can make robot skill acquisition more accessible and efficient.
- **Leveraging Predictive Latent Representations:** The adoption of a JEPA-based world model is impactful as it highlights the utility of learning semantically meaningful and robust latent representations. These representations abstract away task-irrelevant visual details and morphological differences, focusing instead on the underlying predictive dynamics crucial for task transfer. This reinforces the value of predictive self-supervised learning for embodied AI.
- **Flexible Imitation for Heterogeneous Agents:** By focusing on "what" the demonstrator intends rather than "how" they execute it, Demo-JEPA provides a more flexible paradigm for imitation. This flexibility is crucial for deploying robots in varied environments and tasks, where a single demonstrator might need to teach skills to multiple, morphologically diverse robots.
- **Implications for Embodied AI:** This research contributes to the broader goal of scalable embodied AI systems. The ability to infer and adapt goals across different embodiments can lead to more versatile robots capable of learning from a wider range of sources, potentially accelerating the development of automation, assistive robotics, and advanced training platforms.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The authors acknowledge several limitations. The performance is currently constrained by modeling bottlenecks in existing action-conditioned world models, which may limit efficacy in highly complex or ultra-precision tasks. Additionally, the current pipeline still necessitates temporal or progress-aware alignment during the training phase, even if action-level alignment is avoided. Future research will focus on enhancing the fidelity of world models to handle more complex dynamics and developing fully unaligned learning strategies to further reduce data preparation overhead.

**Broader Impact:**
While aiming to improve robot capabilities, the development of increasingly capable robotic systems necessitates consideration of potential risks, such as unintended behavior transfer, reliability issues, and safety in real-world deployments. The authors state that the work has been evaluated in controlled simulation and laboratory settings, and additional safeguards and rigorous validation would be essential before deployment in safety-critical applications.
