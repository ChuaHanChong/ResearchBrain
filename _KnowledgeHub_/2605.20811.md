# Demo-JEPA: Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture for One-shot Cross-Embodiment Imitation

> [!summary] Summary
> Demo-JEPA introduces a framework for one-shot cross-embodiment imitation learning by inferring target-compatible latent goals from visual demonstrations using a Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture. The approach allows robots to transfer skills across diverse morphologies, achieving a 0.36 success rate in zero-shot simulated tasks and 0.25 in real-world scenarios, outperforming concatenation baselines.

## Problem

- The "embodiment gap" in robotic imitation learning, stemming from physical, kinematic, and action space mismatches between demonstrators and learning robots, hinders skill transfer.
- Traditional action-centric imitation learning methods are data-intensive, requiring extensive multi-embodiment data or explicit action alignment, which limits generalization for heterogeneous agents.
- Pixel-level predictive models in imitation from observation often capture task-irrelevant visual details, leading to computational expense, compounding errors, and poor generalization under significant embodiment variations.

## Method

- Formulates cross-embodiment imitation as latent goal-conditioned planning, where the target robot infers a target-compatible future latent goal from a source visual demonstration.
- Utilizes **Demo-JEPA**, an architecture featuring an action-conditioned ==world model== for target dynamics and a 'Dreamer Predictor' that translates source visual demonstrations into target-compatible future latent states.
- Employs a three-stage training process: pretraining a target world model, training the Dreamer Predictor with a latent reconstruction loss, and co-training the world model's dynamics predictor to align its rollouts with the Dreamer's predicted goals.

## Results

- Demo-JEPA demonstrated superior zero-shot generalization, achieving a 0.36 success rate in simulation and 0.25 in real-world unseen task configurations, compared to 0.**04/0**.00 for VPP and 0.**03/0**.05 for XSkill.
- The Dreamer Predictor successfully converts heterogeneous demonstrations into target-compatible latent goals, with Demo-JEPA's performance closely approaching that of an oracle model (0.45 vs. 0.55 in sim and 0.55 vs. 0.58 in real-world for Cross-Embodiment Bridging).
- Ablation studies confirmed the importance of 3D convolutional fusion for preserving structured temporal information, and a scaling study showed that task diversity is more critical than per-task episode volume for learning transferable latent mappings.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling the intent of a demonstration from its embodiment-specific execution, by interpreting demonstrations as future latent goals, enables more robust cross-embodiment skill transfer.
> - Joint Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPA) effectively learn semantically meaningful latent representations that abstract away nuisance visual variations and morphological differences, facilitating generalization.
> - An adaptive goal updating mechanism, which advances the current subgoal only upon successful achievement, stabilizes long-horizon tasks and prevents premature temporal progression in planning.
