# RoboDuet: Learning a Cooperative Policy for Whole-body Legged Loco-Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> RoboDuet introduces a cooperative two-policy reinforcement learning framework for legged mobile manipulators, achieving robust whole-body coordination and accurate 6D end-effector pose tracking. The framework demonstrates zero-shot transferability across different quadruped robots, significantly expanding the operational workspace and improving task success rates in both simulated and real-world environments.

## Problem

- Effectively controlling high-DoF legged robots with manipulators for true whole-body coordination and precise 6D end-effector pose tracking remains challenging.
- Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) approaches often struggle with multi-task learning interference and training inefficiency when attempting to control both locomotion and manipulation with a single policy.
- Existing methods, including control-based techniques and learning-based high-level command generation, frequently lead to limited manipulation workspaces, a lack of coordination, or poor adaptability to unknown environments.

## Method

- A ==cooperative==, ==two-policy== framework consisting of a 'Loco Policy' (for quadruped locomotion and base posture adjustment) and an 'Arm Policy' (for 6D end-effector pose tracking and guiding the Loco Policy's posture).
- A two-stage training strategy: first, the Loco Policy is trained for robust locomotion with a fixed arm; then, both policies are jointly trained for coordinated whole-body loco-manipulation.
- The Arm Policy provides guidance signals (desired pitch/roll) to the Loco Policy, enabling the quadruped to actively adjust its base posture to expand the arm's workspace and assist in precise end-effector tracking.

## Results

- RoboDuet achieved a **14.83%** improvement in solvability and expanded the operational workspace by **67%** (from **51.36 m³** to **85.79 m³**) compared to Floating Base + Inverse Kinematics in simulation.
- Real-world experiments demonstrated a **23%** higher success rate in object transfer tasks (39/60 vs 32/60 for the best baseline) and reduced self-collision rates from **50%** to **5%** when tracking challenging 6D poses.
- The policy exhibited strong zero-shot transfer capabilities, successfully deploying from a Unitree Go1 to Go2 (**14.7%** heavier) and A1 robots in both simulation and real-world scenarios, maintaining high survival rates and robust 6D pose tracking.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling whole-body control into cooperative locomotion and arm policies significantly improves training efficiency and stability compared to a monolithic policy, while mitigating learning interference.
> - A two-stage training process, grounding the robot in robust locomotion before integrating manipulation, enhances overall system robustness and survival rates against external disturbances.
> - Allowing the arm policy to guide the base posture (pitch and roll) is crucial for expanding the manipulation workspace and achieving challenging 6D end-effector poses that are otherwise unreachable or cause self-collisions.
