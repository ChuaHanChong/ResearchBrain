# RoboDuet: Learning a Cooperative Policy for Whole-body Legged Loco-Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> RoboDuet introduces a cooperative two-policy reinforcement learning framework for legged mobile manipulators, achieving robust whole-body coordination and accurate 6D end-effector pose tracking. The framework demonstrates zero-shot transferability across different quadruped robots, significantly expanding the operational workspace and improving task success rates in both simulated and real-world environments.

## Problem

- Effectively controlling high-DoF legged robots with manipulators for true whole-body coordination and precise 6D end-effector pose tracking remains challenging.
- Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) approaches often struggle with multi-task learning interference and training inefficiency when attempting to control both locomotion and manipulation with a single policy.
- Existing methods, including control-based techniques and learning-based high-level command generation, frequently lead to limited manipulation workspaces, a lack of coordination, or poor adaptability to unknown environments.

## Method

- A ==cooperative==, ==two-policy== framework consisting of a 'Loco Policy' (for quadruped locomotion and base posture adjustment) and an 'Arm Policy' (for 6D end-effector pose tracking and guiding the Loco Policy's posture).
- A two-stage training strategy: first, the Loco Policy is trained for robust locomotion with a fixed arm; then, both policies are jointly trained for coordinated whole-body loco-manipulation.
- The Arm Policy provides guidance signals (desired pitch/roll) to the Loco Policy, enabling the quadruped to actively adjust its base posture to expand the arm's workspace and assist in precise end-effector tracking.

## Results

- RoboDuet achieved a **14.83%** improvement in solvability and expanded the operational workspace by **67%** (from **51.36 m³** to **85.79 m³**) compared to Floating Base + Inverse Kinematics in simulation.
- Real-world experiments demonstrated a **23%** higher success rate in object transfer tasks (39/60 vs 32/60 for the best baseline) and reduced self-collision rates from **50%** to **5%** when tracking challenging 6D poses.
- The policy exhibited strong zero-shot transfer capabilities, successfully deploying from a Unitree Go1 to Go2 (**14.7%** heavier) and A1 robots in both simulation and real-world scenarios, maintaining high survival rates and robust 6D pose tracking.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling whole-body control into cooperative locomotion and arm policies significantly improves training efficiency and stability compared to a monolithic policy, while mitigating learning interference.
> - A two-stage training process, grounding the robot in robust locomotion before integrating manipulation, enhances overall system robustness and survival rates against external disturbances.
> - Allowing the arm policy to guide the base posture (pitch and roll) is crucial for expanding the manipulation workspace and achieving challenging 6D end-effector poses that are otherwise unreachable or cause self-collisions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of mobile robotics is rapidly advancing, with a growing demand for robots capable of operating in complex and unstructured environments. While wheeled robots have demonstrated capabilities, their limitations on diverse terrains and in adjusting base postures have driven interest towards legged robots. Equipping legged robots with manipulators (loco-manipulation) promises enhanced versatility, allowing them to overcome terrain constraints and significantly expand the manipulation workspace. However, effectively controlling such systems, involving a high number of degrees of freedom (DoFs) for both locomotion and manipulation, to achieve genuine whole-body coordination and precise 6D end-effector pose tracking, remains a substantial challenge.

Existing approaches to legged loco-manipulation generally fall into three categories:
1. **Control-based techniques (e.g., Model Predictive Control - MPC):** These methods explicitly consider the system's kinematic and dynamic properties. While suitable for scenarios with dense contact forces, they are often labor-intensive to engineer and may lack adaptability or robustness in unknown environments. Examples include Sleiman et al. and Ferrolho et al..
2. **Learning algorithms generating high-level commands:** These approaches use learning to provide commands for either the legged robot or the arm, which are then translated into low-level joint control via built-in controllers or inverse kinematics (IK). While successful in grasping (e.g., GAMMA, GeFF), they often separate the arm and quadruped control strategies, leading to a lack of effective coordination, constrained workspaces, and potential for infeasible IK solutions. Yokoyama et al. and Liu et al. also explore similar paradigms.
3. **Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) for whole-body control:** This emerging area aims to train a unified policy to control the entire system. Early efforts integrated model-based manipulators with RL-based locomotion policies, focusing on disturbance resistance rather than whole-body coordination. More recent work, like Fu et al., attempted a unified policy for locomotion and manipulation but was limited to end-effector *position* tracking, not full 6D pose tracking. Subsequent efforts extended to 6D pose tracking but faced data collection constraints or lacked simultaneous mobility and manipulation capabilities.

RoboDuet directly addresses the critical gap in DRL-based whole-body control: achieving simultaneous, accurate 6D end-effector pose tracking with seamless whole-body coordination, while also ensuring robust locomotion and generalization. By proposing a novel cooperative, two-policy framework, RoboDuet seeks to overcome the limitations of unified policies (difficulty in multi-task learning, poor training efficiency) and decoupled systems (limited workspace, lack of coordination), pushing the state-of-the-art towards more adaptable, robust, and generalizable legged loco-manipulation systems. The work distinguishes itself by emphasizing the "duet-like" interaction between policies and demonstrating zero-shot transfer capabilities, which are crucial for practical real-world deployment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the RoboDuet framework are:

1. **Achieve Effective Whole-Body Coordination for Loco-Manipulation:** The overarching goal is to fully leverage the capabilities of a quadruped robot equipped with a robotic arm, requiring control of all DoFs. The motivation stems from the limitations of prior work that either decoupled locomotion and manipulation or struggled with true whole-body coordination, leading to restricted arm workspaces and suboptimal performance in dynamic tasks.
2. **Enable Large-Range 6D End-Effector Pose Tracking:** A key deficiency in many existing whole-body control frameworks is the inability to achieve accurate 6D (position and orientation) pose tracking for the end-effector. This capability is crucial for precise manipulation tasks. RoboDuet aims to provide this precision across a wide operational range.
3. **Enhance Training Efficiency and Stability:** Training a complex whole-body loco-manipulation policy is challenging. The authors are motivated to simplify this problem and improve the training process's stability, arguing that a two-stage training strategy with cooperative policies can lead to more agile and robust agents.
4. **Facilitate Zero-Shot Transfer Across Robot Platforms:** A significant barrier to deploying learned policies in the real world is the need for retraining when hardware changes. RoboDuet aims to develop policies that exhibit zero-shot transfer capabilities across quadruped robots with similar morphology and physical dimensions, thus reducing deployment costs and increasing adaptability.
5. **Improve Robustness to External Disturbances:** Legged robots need to maintain stability while manipulating objects, especially in dynamic environments or when facing external forces. The framework seeks to enhance the system's resistance to such perturbations, a critical aspect for real-world reliability.

In summary, RoboDuet is motivated by the need for more capable, adaptable, and robust legged mobile manipulators. It aims to achieve this by introducing a novel cooperative, two-policy architecture and a staged training approach that specifically addresses the challenges of whole-body control, 6D pose tracking, and hardware generalization.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RoboDuet introduces a novel framework for whole-body legged loco-manipulation, built upon two cooperative policies and a two-stage training strategy.

**A. Cooperative Policy for Whole-body Control**
The core idea is to decouple the complex whole-body control problem into two interacting policies:
1. **Loco Policy (π_loco):** This policy is responsible for the quadruped's locomotion. Its goal is to follow target commands for linear velocities (v_cmd_x,y), angular yaw velocity (ω_cmd_yaw), and crucially, desired pitch and roll angles (ϕ_cmd_pitch, ϕ_cmd_roll) of the base. The observation includes leg states, base states, target commands, clock time, and the last leg action. The output (a_leg) represents joint position offsets for the leg motors.
2. **Arm Policy (π_arm):** This policy focuses on accurate 6D end-effector pose tracking. Its observation comprises arm states, target end-effector pose (χ_t), base states, and the last arm action. The arm policy's actions consist of two parts:
 - **Joint position offsets (a_arm_J):** Six actions for the arm's joint actuators, tracked by a PD controller.
 - **Guidance signals (a_arm_G):** Two actions (a_arm_G_p, a_arm_G_r) that *replace* the ϕ_cmd_pitch and ϕ_cmd_roll commands of the loco policy. This is the key cooperative mechanism: the arm policy *guides* the locomotion policy's posture adjustments to expand the manipulation workspace and assist in tracking the end-effector pose.

This "duet-like" performance means the loco policy adjusts its posture based on the arm's guidance, while the arm benefits from the expanded workspace provided by the loco policy's movements. Essential information is shared between policies, but their distinct focuses are maintained to mitigate learning interference.

**B. Two-stage Training**
To ensure robust loco-manipulation, RoboDuet employs a two-stage training process:

1. **Stage 1: Robust Locomotion Capability:**
 - **Focus:** Training the loco policy to achieve stable locomotion.
 - **Setup:** The robotic arm joints are fixed at default positions to stabilize the center of mass and inertia. The arm policy is inactive.
 - **Rewards:** Primarily based on the blind locomotion algorithm, but with modifications:
 - Fixed gait parameters (e.g., trotting gait timing offsets) for faster convergence.
 - Clock time adjusted for stationary standing to reduce jitter.
 - Body height tracking reward removed to allow adaptive height adjustment.
 - An **energy reward** (r_loco_energy) is introduced to penalize high motor torque/velocity, mitigating motor overheating caused by the arm's payload.

2. **Stage 2: Coordinated Whole-Body Loco-Manipulation:**
 - **Focus:** Training the arm policy in coordination with the (reused and adapted) loco policy.
 - **Activation:** The arm policy is activated, and all arm joints are operational.
 - **Target Pose:** 6D target end-effector pose (position in spherical coordinates, orientation using Euler angles converted to included angles) is used as policy input.
 - **Rewards:**
 - **Target pose tracking reward (r_χ_t):** An exponential reward penalizing errors in both position (l, p, y) and orientation (α, β, γ) of the end-effector. This reward is shared by both policies.
 - **Reward Adjustment (Transition from Stage 1 to 2):** To seamlessly integrate manipulation without compromising locomotion priors:
 - The Raibert heuristic footswing tracking reward (r_loco_s_cmd) is replaced with a hip joint constraint reward (r_loco_hip) to allow more adaptive foot placements.
 - Velocity tracking rewards (r_v_cmd_x,y, r_ω_cmd_yaw) are scaled down to half their original values.
 - New rewards for the arm: `r_arm_smooth` (for smoother arm motion) and `r_arm_guide` (to promote control of body orientation via the arm policy's guidance signals).
 - **Policy Synchronization:** The arm and loco policies are trained simultaneously with these adjusted rewards.

**C. Training Details**
- **Simulator:** IsaacGym with 4096 parallel agents.
- **Algorithm:** Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) with an asymmetric actor-critic framework.
- **Network Architecture:** Multilayer Perceptrons (MLPs) with ELU activations. Cooperative policy uses three layers (512, 256, 128 neurons); unified policy doubles neurons due to merged feature extractors.
- **Hardware:** NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPUs.

This structured approach allows RoboDuet to build upon robust locomotion foundations before introducing the complexities of manipulation, with the cooperative policy facilitating the integration of these two challenging control problems.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

RoboDuet's effectiveness was evaluated through extensive simulation and real-world experiments, compared against various baselines and through ablation studies.

**A. Simulation Experiments**

1. **Whole-body Control:**
 - **Comparison:** RoboDuet vs. Floating Base (FB)+IK (Inverse Kinematics).
 - **Results:** RoboDuet demonstrated a **14.83% improvement in solvability** (collision-free target pose reaching) and significantly enhanced the operational **workspace** (85.79 m³ for RoboDuet vs. 51.36 m³ for FB+IK in still mode). This highlights the superior ability of whole-body control to overcome structural limitations and expand the arm's reach.

2. **Ablation Study (Key Components: Two-stage Training and Cooperative Mechanism):**
 - **Configurations:** Unified (single policy, single stage), Two-Stage (single policy, two stages), Cooperated (two cooperative policies, single stage), and RoboDuet (two cooperative policies, two stages).
 - **Key Findings:**
 - **Survival Rate (Robustness to Disturbances):** Two-Stage improved survival rates by 8.31% over Unified in stationary standing (87.35% vs 95.66%), and even more dramatically in move mode (93.03% vs 99.09%), demonstrating the value of Stage 1's focus on robust locomotion. RoboDuet achieved the highest survival rates (98.20% still, 99.96% move).
 - **End-effector Pose Tracking:** Cooperated and RoboDuet consistently outperformed Unified and Two-Stage across almost all position and orientation tracking metrics. Cooperated achieved slightly better position tracking in still mode, but RoboDuet maintained comparable tracking performance while offering superior robustness and a larger operational workspace during motion (87.05 m³ for RoboDuet vs. 86.71 m³ for Cooperated in move mode).
 - **Conclusion:** The ablation confirms that both the two-stage training (for robustness and gait stability) and the cooperative mechanism (for improved tracking accuracy and workspace) are essential for RoboDuet's overall superior performance.

3. **Zero-shot Transfer:**
 - **Test:** Policy trained on Unitree Go1+ARX5 transferred to Unitree Go2+ARX5 and Unitree A1+ARX5.
 - **Results:** In stationary standing, Go2+ARX and A1+ARX achieved workspaces and survival rates very close to the original Go1+ARX system (e.g., Go2+ARX workspace 0.8552 m³ vs Go1+ARX 0.8579 m³). In "move" mode, there was a slight performance degradation for the new platforms, but they still maintained a high survival rate of nearly 93% under external perturbations. This demonstrates strong generalization across morphologically and dimensionally similar robots.

**B. Real-world Experiments**

1. **Extreme 6D Pose Tracking:**
 - **Comparison:** FB+IK vs. RoboDuet.
 - **Tasks:** Tracking five challenging target poses, including those near the ground (intersecting legs) and significantly elevated/pathological, often beyond training ranges.
 - **Results:** FB+IK failed to resolve poses 2, 4, and 5, with an overall **60% IK failure rate** and **50% self-collision rate**. RoboDuet, in contrast, maintained stability across all poses, achieved stable approximations for physically unreachable targets, and experienced only a **5% self-collision rate**. This highlights RoboDuet's robustness, solution stability, and ability to avoid self-collisions during dynamic arm movements.

2. **Whole-body Control (Object Transfer Tasks):**
 - **Comparison:** FB+IK, Adaptive Floating Base (AFB)+ID (built-in controllers), and RoboDuet.
 - **Tasks:** Transferring a doll between varying heights (0 cm to 100 cm).
 - **Results:**
 - FB+IK showed poor performance (10/60 total success), struggling with self-collisions, especially for ground-level objects.
 - AFB+ID performed better (32/60 total success) by adaptively adjusting body posture, but still struggled with objects directly in front of the head or at maximum reach.
 - RoboDuet achieved the highest success rate (39/60 total success), representing a **23% improvement over AFB+ID** in challenging loco-manipulation tasks. It effectively adjusted body posture, for instance, inclining the body to reduce vertical distance for high grasps, demonstrating superior solution stability and coordination.

3. **Zero-shot Transfer (Real-world):**
 - **Test:** Policy trained on Unitree Go1+ARX5 directly deployed on Unitree Go2+ARX5 (14.7% mass increase).
 - **Results:** RoboDuet demonstrated robust whole-body control and consistent 6D pose tracking on the heavier Go2 platform while executing the same trajectories. This confirmed the real-world generalizability and hardware agnosticism of the learned policy.

In summary, RoboDuet consistently outperforms baselines in both simulation and real-world scenarios, demonstrating superior whole-body control, accurate 6D end-effector pose tracking, enhanced robustness, and significant zero-shot transfer capabilities across different robot embodiments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The RoboDuet framework represents a significant advancement in the field of legged loco-manipulation, addressing several long-standing challenges and opening new avenues for robotic applications. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enhanced Dexterity and Versatility for Mobile Manipulation:** By achieving accurate 6D end-effector pose tracking alongside whole-body coordination, RoboDuet empowers legged robots to perform complex manipulation tasks that were previously difficult or impossible. This expands the operational capabilities of mobile manipulators, allowing them to grasp, lift, and place objects with precision across a larger workspace and in diverse, unstructured environments like homes, factories, or disaster zones.
2. **Robustness and Stability in Dynamic Environments:** The framework's ability to resist external disturbances and maintain stability during complex loco-manipulation tasks is critical for real-world deployment. The two-stage training, which first grounds the robot in robust locomotion, ensures that the addition of manipulation capabilities does not compromise fundamental stability, making the robots more reliable and safe for human interaction.
3. **Improved Training Paradigm for Complex Robotics Tasks:** The cooperative, two-policy architecture, combined with a two-stage training strategy, offers a more efficient and stable approach to learning whole-body control. This decoupling of locomotion and manipulation tasks simplifies the learning problem, overcoming the inherent difficulties of training monolithic, high-dimensional policies. This methodology could inspire similar approaches for other complex, multi-modal robotics challenges.
4. **Significant Progress in Generalization and Zero-shot Transfer:** The demonstrated zero-shot transfer capabilities across different quadruped robot models (Go1 to Go2 and A1), both in simulation and real-world, are highly impactful. This dramatically reduces the need for extensive retraining when hardware is upgraded or replaced, lowering development costs and accelerating deployment cycles. It moves the field closer to truly "hardware-agnostic" learned policies.
5. **Foundation for Future Autonomous Systems:** RoboDuet focuses on low-level whole-body control, which is a fundamental building block for fully autonomous mobile manipulation. Its robust and generalizable policies can serve as a strong foundation for integration with higher-level planners, perception systems, and advanced collision detection algorithms, paving the way for more intelligent and self-sufficient robots capable of tackling real-world problems.
6. **Contribution to Open Science:** By providing open-source code and additional videos, the authors facilitate further research and development in this domain. This commitment to transparency and reproducibility is invaluable for the scientific community, allowing other researchers to build upon and extend RoboDuet's capabilities.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The authors acknowledge several limitations that also point to future research directions:
- The current method focuses on low-level whole-body control and needs integration with high-level planners and advanced collision detection for fully autonomous task execution.
- While capable of handling terrains like slopes and gravel for fixed target poses, it has yet to achieve loco-manipulation on more complex terrains such as stairs. Expanding its environmental adaptability will be a key area for enhancement.

Despite these limitations, RoboDuet establishes a new benchmark for learning robust, coordinated, and generalizable whole-body legged loco-manipulation, offering substantial practical implications for the deployment of versatile mobile robots.
