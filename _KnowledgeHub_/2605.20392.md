# VBT-MPC: Vision-Based Tactile MPC for Contour Following

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of Alicante and Worcester Polytechnic Institute developed VBT-MPC, a control framework for robotic contour following using a marker-less Vision-Based Tactile Sensor (VBTS). The system achieves sub-millimeter contour tracking accuracy (e.g., orthogonal distance error below 0.51 mm RMSE for rigid objects) at 50 Hz, consistently outperforming traditional visual-tactile servoing methods by directly regulating tactile contour features and maintaining contact across diverse geometries.

## Problem

- Existing Image-Based Tactile Servoing (IBTS) methods for contour following often rely on additional, complex force-regulation controllers.
- Many Vision-Based Tactile Sensor (VBTS) control strategies require labor-intensive, supervised pose estimation models that may not generalize well to new objects.
- Current VBTS-based methods commonly express control objectives in end-effector pose or forces, rather than directly utilizing high-resolution contour features from marker-less tactile images, limiting responsiveness and accuracy.

## Method

- A Vision-Based Tactile ==Model Predictive Control== (**VBT-MPC**) framework was developed to directly regulate contour features extracted from a marker-less VBTS in an eye-in-hand configuration.
- The MPC controller maximizes forward velocity while enforcing critical constraints on contact maintenance and sensor field of view, operating without explicit pose estimation models or cascaded force controllers.
- A robust Neural Network and Line Fitting (NNaLF) pipeline, combining a Unet++ for segmentation and weighted least-squares line fitting, provides high-rate (50 Hz) contour features, augmented by an Extended Kalman Filter (EKF).

## Results

- The NNaLF contour extraction pipeline achieved superior accuracy (**10.4** px position RMSE, **0.034** rad orientation RMSE) and runtime (**28.84** ms) compared to baselines, demonstrating reliable multi-contact handling.
- VBT-MPC consistently reduced settling time by approximately **1.0** second in simulation compared to a decoupled controller and successfully tracked complex 3D-printed contours (e.g., S-shaped, hexagonal) where baselines failed due to oscillations or contact loss.
- In real-world experiments, VBT-MPC achieved sub-millimeter orthogonal distance errors (e.g., < **0.51** mm RMSE for rigid objects) on diverse objects while maintaining real-time performance (**20.53**-**20.74** ms computational time for 50 Hz control loop).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Direct control of contour features in the tactile image space via MPC simplifies the control architecture and enhances responsiveness compared to pose-based or cascaded force control approaches.
> - A combined neural network-based segmentation and line fitting approach (NNaLF) effectively extracts accurate and fast contour features from marker-less VBTS images, crucial for real-time control.
> - Explicitly incorporating contact maintenance and sensor field-of-view constraints within the MPC formulation enables stable and continuous contour tracking, even across complex geometries and under disturbances.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic manipulation tasks often necessitate tactile sensing for precise interaction with the environment, particularly in applications such as surface inspection. Such tasks demand capabilities like maintaining continuous contact and accurately tracking object contours. Traditional vision-only systems frequently lack the refined interaction information that touch sensing provides, such as contact forces, pressure, and texture.

Vision-Based Tactile Sensors (VBTS) have emerged as a significant technology in this field. These sensors offer high spatial resolution and are comparatively cost-effective when contrasted with other tactile array technologies (resistive, capacitive, magnetic, or piezoelectric). VBTS capture pixel-level contact deformations of a compliant elastomer, allowing the extraction of high-level features like contact position, local curvature, or contours. These features are crucial for active perception and control tasks. VBTS can be broadly categorized into marker-based, which track internal markers, and marker-less, which infer contact from surface appearance changes.

Control strategies for tactile robotics are often classified as Image-Based Tactile Servoing (IBTS) or Pose-Based Tactile Servoing (PBTS). PBTS relies on learned models to map tactile images to local poses, subsequently regulating the sensor's pose. This typically requires extensive supervised training with ground truth poses and estimation models. In contrast, IBTS defines the control objective directly within the feature space of tactile images, offering conceptual simplicity and independence from pose-estimation models. However, existing IBTS approaches for tasks like contour following frequently necessitate additional force-regulation controllers, which increases the overall complexity of the control system.

Previous research using tactile arrays (not specifically VBTS) has explored control schemes based on features extracted from pressure distributions. These methods, such as those by Li et al. and Kappassov et al., typically project pressure-derived descriptors into the robot's operational space. While innovative, these approaches do not directly exploit contour-based features within the tactile image domain.

With the advancement of VBTS, several studies have investigated contour perception and pose estimation. Lepora et al. used deep convolutional neural networks to detect contours in marker-based VBTS images for PBTS applications. Lloyd and Lepora proposed a contact pose estimator combining a convolutional neural network with Bayesian filtering for PBTS. Aquilina et al. extended this to 2D contour following in dynamic environments using a shear controller. These VBTS-based methods predominantly follow PBTS formulations, where deep neural networks estimate contact pose from marker-based tactile images before control is applied. The control objective in these cases is commonly expressed in terms of end-effector pose or contact forces, rather than directly within the tactile image domain.

This work positions itself to address a gap in the existing literature by proposing a control framework that performs contour tracking directly in the tactile image space using contour features extracted from a *marker-less* VBTS. Crucially, it does so within an IBTS framework using Model Predictive Control (MPC), thereby eliminating the need for explicit pose estimation models or cascaded force controllers, which differentiates it from both traditional tactile array methods and many existing VBTS-based control strategies.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the challenges inherent in robust and precise robotic contour following using tactile sensing. While tactile sensors offer critical information beyond vision, current control strategies face limitations:

1. **Complexity of Existing Systems:** Many existing tactile servoing methods, particularly Image-Based Tactile Servoing (IBTS) for contour following, often rely on additional force-regulation controllers to maintain stable interaction. This adds layers of complexity to the overall control architecture, increasing implementation effort and potential points of failure.
2. **Reliance on Pose Estimation:** A significant portion of Vision-Based Tactile Sensor (VBTS) control, especially Pose-Based Tactile Servoing (PBTS), involves supervised training with ground truth poses and subsequent pose estimation models. This process can be labor-intensive, requires extensive datasets, and may not generalize well to novel objects or environments.
3. **Indirect Control in Tactile Space:** Current VBTS-based methods commonly express control objectives in terms of end-effector pose or contact forces, rather than directly utilizing contour features available in the tactile image domain. This indirect approach can limit the responsiveness and accuracy of contour tracking, especially for high-resolution tactile information.
4. **Underutilization of Marker-less VBTS Capabilities:** While marker-less VBTS offer high-resolution pixel-level contact information, their integration into direct contour-following control schemes without complex intermediaries (like pose estimators or cascaded force controllers) is an area requiring further development.

Given these motivations, the key objectives of this research are defined as follows:

- **Develop a Vision-Based Tactile Model Predictive Control (VBT-MPC) framework:** The core objective is to design a control framework that enables robotic manipulators to follow contours using a marker-less VBTS in an eye-in-hand configuration. This framework is designed to operate directly in the contour features space, bypassing the need for separate pose-estimation modules or complex force-control architectures.
- **Regulate Contour Features Directly:** The controller aims to regulate contour features extracted directly from marker-less tactile images, ensuring contact maintenance, keeping tactile features within the sensor's field of view (FoV), and adhering to input constraints.
- **Maximize Forward Velocity under Constraints:** A specific objective of the VBT-MPC framework is to maximize the robot's forward velocity along the contour while simultaneously enforcing constraints related to touch maintenance and the perception requirements of tactile feature extraction.
- **Establish a Robust Contour Feature Extraction Pipeline:** To support the VBT-MPC, the research aims to develop and integrate a reliable contour feature extraction pipeline. This pipeline combines a segmentation neural network (Unet++), line fitting techniques, and an Extended Kalman Filter (EKF) to estimate and provide over-sampled contour features from VBTS measurements. This pipeline's performance is to be quantitatively evaluated against alternative contour extraction baselines.
- **Validate the Proposed Framework Experimentally:** The research seeks to rigorously validate the VBT-MPC framework through both simulation and real-world experiments. This involves comparing its contour tracking performance against adapted visual-servoing strategies (classical and decoupled approaches) on objects with diverse geometries and materials.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology centers on a Vision-Based Tactile Model Predictive Control (VBT-MPC) framework designed for robotic contour following. This framework integrates a marker-less Vision-Based Tactile Sensor (VBTS) in an eye-in-hand configuration with an MPC controller that operates directly on contour features.

**A. Tactile Features Model**
The system is modeled using three coordinate frames: robot base (B), camera (C), and image (I). The VBTS provides raw tactile features, $\mathbf{x}_{\text{P}}^{\text{C}} \in \mathbb{R}^3$, which are projected onto the image frame as $\mathbf{s}_{\text{P}}^{\text{I}} \in \mathbb{R}^3$ using a projection map $\text{P}(\cdot)$ and the VBTS intrinsic calibration matrix $\mathbf{K}$. The dynamics of these image features are defined by $\dot{\mathbf{s}}_{\text{P}}^{\text{I}} = \mathbf{J}_{\text{P}}(\mathbf{s}_{\text{P}}^{\text{I}}) \mathbf{\upsilon}^{\text{C}}$, where $\mathbf{J}_{\text{P}}$ is the interaction matrix and $\mathbf{\upsilon}^{\text{C}}$ is the end-effector twist.

To formulate contour following, an additional contour feature vector, $\mathbf{\xi}^{\text{I}} \in \mathbb{R}^4$, is introduced. This vector is derived from two raw tactile features, $\mathbf{s}_{\text{P}1}^{\text{I}}$ and $\mathbf{s}_{\text{P}2}^{\text{I}}$, via a nonlinear operator $\text{E}(\cdot)$. The dynamics of the contour features are $\dot{\mathbf{\xi}}^{\text{I}} = \mathbf{J}_{\text{E}}(\mathbf{s}_{\text{P}1}^{\text{I}}, \mathbf{s}_{\text{P}2}^{\text{I}}) \mathbf{\upsilon}^{\text{C}}$, with $\mathbf{J}_{\text{E}}$ as the corresponding Jacobian. The contour feature vector $\mathbf{\xi}^{\text{I}}$ comprises four parameters:
- $r$: Orthogonal distance of the detected contour in the tactile image plane (mm).
- $\beta$: Orientation of the detected contour in the tactile image plane (rad).
- $\alpha$: Tangential orientation of the sensor with respect to the local contact surface (rad).
- $\delta$: Local surface deformation, estimated from the reconstructed tactile depth image (mm).
The full system dynamics are then compactly represented as $\dot{\mathbf{x}} = f(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{u})$, where $\mathbf{x} = [\mathbf{s}_{\text{P}1}^{\text{I}} \mathbf{s}_{\text{P}2}^{\text{I}} \mathbf{\xi}^{\text{I}}]^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{10}$ and $\mathbf{u} = \mathbf{\upsilon}^{\text{C}} \in \mathbb{R}^6$ (end-effector twist).

**B. Vision-Based Tactile MPC (VBT-MPC)**
The VBT-MPC framework formulates the contour-following problem as an optimization problem over a finite prediction horizon $N$. The objective is to minimize a cost function while satisfying system dynamics and various constraints.
- **Cost Function:** The cost function includes terms for both contour feature error and twist error.
 - Contour error: $\mathbf{\xi}_{\text{e}}^{\text{I}} = \mathbf{\xi}_{\text{d}}^{\text{I}} - \mathbf{\xi}^{\text{I}}$, where $\mathbf{\xi}_{\text{d}}^{\text{I}}$ are the desired contour features.
 - Twist error: $\mathbf{u}_{\text{e}} = \mathbf{u}_{\text{d}} - \mathbf{u}$, where $\mathbf{u}_{\text{d}}$ is the desired end-effector twist.
 - A dynamic desired forward linear velocity component, $v_{x\text{d}}$, is introduced: $v_{x\text{d}} = v_{x,\text{max}} / (1 + \|\mathbf{\xi}_{\text{e}}^{\text{I}}\|^2_W)$. This enables the robot to move rapidly along low-curvature sections and slow down in regions with larger deviations.
 - The running and terminal cost functions are defined as quadratic forms: $\ell(\mathbf{\xi}_{\text{e}}^{\text{I}}) = \|\mathbf{\xi}_{\text{e}}^{\text{I}}\|^2_Q$ and $\ell_u(\mathbf{u}_{\text{e}}) = \|\mathbf{u}_{\text{e}}\|^2_R$, with positive definite weighting matrices $Q$ and $R$.
- **System Dynamics:** The continuous-time system dynamics are approximated using a discrete-time representation obtained via the fourth-order Runge-Kutta method, $\mathbf{x}_{k+1} = f_{\text{RK4}}(f(\mathbf{x}_k, \mathbf{u}_k), dt)$.
- **Constraints:** Constraints are crucial for maintaining physical interaction and operational limits. These include:
 - **Contact Maintenance and Perception:** Enforced by setting upper and lower bounds on the visual-tactile features, $\mathbf{x}_{\text{min}} \le \mathbf{x} \le \mathbf{x}_{\text{max}}$. Specifically, the deformation parameter $\delta$ is constrained within experimentally identified bounds (0.0182 m to 0.022 m) to prevent contact loss or excessive sensor deformation. This also ensures tactile features remain within the VBTS's FoV.
 - **Control Input Limits:** Standard bounds on the end-effector twist, $\mathbf{u}_{\text{min}} \le \mathbf{u} \le \mathbf{u}_{\text{max}}$.
The VBT-MPC operates under the assumptions that a single dominant contour feature is extractable, contact is maintained over the prediction horizon, and the object is rigid or semi-rigid enough to generate detectable tactile features.

**C. Tactile Contour Extraction and Extended Kalman Filter (EKF)**
A pipeline called Neural Network and Line Fitting (NNaLF) is developed to extract contour-based tactile features:
- **Segmentation:** A Unet++ encoder-decoder neural network (with a Resnet18 backbone) processes raw RGB tactile images to identify contact regions.
- **Line Fitting:** The segmented contact region's main axis is estimated by fitting a straight line using a weighted least-squares algorithm. This line is then parameterized to yield the tactile features ($\mathbf{s}_{\text{P}1}^{\text{I}}$, $\mathbf{s}_{\text{P}2}^{\text{I}}$, $\mathbf{\xi}^{\text{I}}$).
To provide contour features at a higher rate (50 Hz) than the GelSight Mini sensor's native rate (15 Hz), an Extended Kalman Filter (EKF) is employed. The tactile points $\mathbf{s}_{\text{P}1}^{\text{I}}$ and $\mathbf{s}_{\text{P}2}^{\text{I}}$ are used as measurements, while the process model maps robot joint velocities $\dot{\mathbf{q}}$ to tactile and contour features, incorporating the robot manipulator's Jacobian, $\mathbf{J}_{\text{r}}(\mathbf{q})$.

**D. Baseline Methods**
For comparison, the VBT-MPC framework is evaluated against:
- **Visual-Tactile Servoing (Classical IBVS):** A direct adaptation of classical IBVS, using the contour feature model. It computes $\mathbf{\upsilon}^{\text{C}} = \mathbf{J}_{\text{E}}^\dagger (\mathbf{\xi}_{\text{e}}^{\text{I}}) + (\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{J}_{\text{E}}^\dagger \mathbf{J}_{\text{E}})\mathbf{\lambda}$. The null-space component $\mathbf{\lambda}$ maximizes forward velocity. This method is known for limitations such as "camera retreat," which can lead to loss of contact.
- **Decoupled Visual-Tactile Servoing:** This controller addresses the limitations of classical IBVS by decoupling the end-effector twist components. It defines separate control laws for regulating the contour features (orthogonal distance $r$, deformation $\delta$, and tangential orientation $\alpha$) and for maintaining the desired orientation ($\beta$). Null-space vectors are used to maximize forward velocity and ensure continuous contact by regulating local deformation and tangential alignment. This represents an advanced visual servoing approach adapted for VBTS.
- **Tactile Contour Extraction Baselines:** For evaluating the NNaLF pipeline, three additional methods are implemented: Edge and Line Fitting (EaLF), Edge and Ellipse Fitting (EaEF), and Corner and Ellipse Fitting (CaEF). These methods use different image processing techniques (skeletonization, Canny edge detection, Shi-Tomasi corners) combined with line or ellipse fitting to extract contour features.

**E. Experimental Setup**
Experiments are conducted in both a ROS-based simulation environment and on a real robotic platform.
- **Robot:** A 7-DoF Kinova Gen3 manipulator.
- **Sensor:** A GelSight Mini VBTS (15 fps, 320x240 px RGB), capable of reconstructing depth maps via photometric stereoscopy, mounted in an eye-in-hand configuration.
- **Control Loop:** Controllers operate at 50 Hz, matching the EKF update rate.
- **MPC Solver:** The VBT-MPC problem is solved in real-time using ACADOS and CasADi with sequential quadratic programming, employing a 0.5-second prediction horizon.
- **Test Objects:** Experiments involve 3D-printed contours (S-shaped, hexagonal, square) and various real-world objects with diverse geometries and stiffness properties (e.g., shoe last, plastic banana, plate, boot zipper, shoe seam).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents detailed evaluations of the proposed VBT-MPC framework, including the tactile contour extraction pipeline and the controller's performance in both simulation and real-world scenarios.

**A. Evaluation of Approaches to Extract Tactile Contours**
A custom dataset of approximately 8,000 tactile images from GelSight Mini sensors, featuring various curved and polygonal edges as well as multi-contact cases, was created and manually labeled for ground truth evaluation. Four methods for tactile contour extraction were compared: EaLF, EaEF, CaEF, and the proposed NNaLF.
- **Accuracy:** NNaLF demonstrated superior accuracy, achieving the lowest Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) for both position (10.4 px $\pm$ 7.9) and orientation (0.034 rad $\pm$ 0.025). Its discrepancy rate (pixel error percentage) was also the lowest at 9.15%.
- **Consistency:** NNaLF exhibited the lowest dispersion in position error and a more symmetric error distribution, indicating greater consistency compared to baseline methods.
- **Runtime:** NNaLF was the fastest, with an average runtime of 28.84 ms $\pm$ 7.53, making it suitable for real-time control.
- **Multi-Contact Handling:** A key advantage of NNaLF was its ability to detect the target contour in multi-contact scenarios, a capability that non-learning-based methods lacked.

**B. Tactile Servoing in Simulation**
The controllers were evaluated on their ability to maintain contact and achieve a desired forward velocity (0.5 cm/s) along a straight edge.
- **Classical Tactile Visual Servoing:** This approach failed due to the "camera retreat" limitation, leading to a rapid loss of contact and inability to follow the contour.
- **Decoupled Controller:** Successfully converged to the desired feature values and achieved the target forward velocity while maintaining contact.
- **VBT-MPC:** Outperformed the decoupled controller by reducing the settling time by approximately 1.0 second, indicating improved responsiveness and efficiency for straight-edge tracking.

**C. Tactile Servoing in Real-World**

1. **Experiments on 3D-Printed Contours:**
 - **S-shaped Contour:** The decoupled controller exhibited noticeable oscillations and deviations, particularly in initial straight sections, with significant variability across trials. Two out of five trials failed due to contact loss caused by these oscillations. In contrast, VBT-MPC generated consistent, quasi-overlapping trajectories across all five trials, successfully tracking the S-shaped contour. VBT-MPC achieved sub-millimeter contour tracking (RMSE for $r_e < 4.8 \times 10^{-4}$ m and $\delta_e < 2.0 \times 10^{-4}$ m) with small orientation errors. The forward velocity closely matched the desired 1.0 cm/s with low dispersion.
 - **External Disturbances:** When external disturbances were applied, the decoupled controller responded with larger, persistent oscillations. VBT-MPC, however, showed only a minor transient deviation and quickly realigned with the intended trajectory, demonstrating superior robustness.
 - **Hexagonal Contour:** The decoupled controller failed at the first corner and could not continue tracking. VBT-MPC successfully completed the entire hexagonal trajectory, maintaining consistent tracking through abrupt corner transitions, highlighting the effectiveness of its contact and FoV constraints.

2. **Experiments on Real-World Objects:** VBT-MPC was tested on various real-world objects (shoe last, plastic banana, plate, boot zipper, shoe seam) with different geometries and stiffness. The desired forward velocities ranged from 0.2 cm/s to 0.5 cm/s.
 - **Contour Following Accuracy:**
 - The orthogonal distance error ($r_e$) remained below 2 mm for all objects. Rigid and regular objects like the plate and boot zipper exhibited the smallest $r_e$ (around 0.40-0.51 mm RMSE).
 - Local surface deformation error ($\delta_e$) was generally small, ranging from $4.58 \times 10^{-5}$ m to $1.55 \times 10^{-3}$ m (for the banana).
 - Tangential orientation error ($\alpha_e$) was highly consistent across all objects, around $2.7 \times 10^{-2}$ rad, indicating reliable alignment.
 - Local curvature error ($\beta_e$) showed more variability, being highest for the banana ($0.106$ rad) due to its asymmetrical shape.
 - **Real-time Performance:** The mean computational time for the control loop remained close to the nominal 20 ms for 50 Hz operation (ranging from 20.53 ms to 20.74 ms), confirming the real-time feasibility of the VBT-MPC implementation.
 - **Generalizability and Limitations:** The results suggest that VBT-MPC can maintain contact and follow a variety of real-world contours. Its performance, however, showed a dependence on local geometry and contact conditions, with some deterioration observed when contours were less distinguishable or when local changes in contour direction made feature extraction less reliable.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant contribution to the field of robotic manipulation and tactile sensing, offering a novel and effective approach to contour following.

- **Advancement in Tactile Servoing:** The proposed VBT-MPC framework advances Image-Based Tactile Servoing (IBTS) by operating directly in contour feature space using a marker-less Vision-Based Tactile Sensor (VBTS). This departs from traditional Pose-Based Tactile Servoing (PBTS) that relies on pose estimation models, and from IBTS methods that often require cascaded force control. This direct, feature-space control strategy simplifies the control architecture and potentially enhances responsiveness.
- **Improved Robustness and Accuracy:** Experimental results in both simulation and real-world scenarios demonstrate that VBT-MPC provides superior contour tracking performance compared to classical and decoupled visual-tactile servoing methods. It exhibits greater stability, effectively handles external disturbances, and successfully navigates complex geometries, including sharp corners, where other methods failed. This translates to higher reliability in robotic manipulation tasks.
- **Enhanced Autonomous Capabilities:** By enabling robots to accurately and robustly follow contours with tactile feedback, this work contributes to the development of more autonomous and capable robotic systems. Such capabilities are essential for complex tasks in manufacturing, quality inspection, and sensitive object handling, where precise interaction and surface exploration are paramount.
- **Practical Real-time Implementation:** The real-time feasibility of the VBT-MPC, supported by its efficient implementation using ACADOS and CasADi and consistent control loop execution times, indicates its potential for practical deployment in industrial and service robotics applications.
- **Enabling Technology for Marker-less VBTS:** The developed Neural Network and Line Fitting (NNaLF) pipeline for robust and fast contour feature extraction from marker-less VBTS images is a key enabling component. Its demonstrated accuracy and speed can facilitate wider adoption and more sophisticated use of marker-less tactile sensors.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The framework lays a foundation for future extensions. The authors indicate plans to incorporate explicit normal-force regulation, potentially through learning-based models from VBTS images, and to extend the method to handle moving rigid objects. These advancements would further enhance the versatility and robustness of tactile-guided robotic systems in dynamic and interactive environments.

In summary, this work offers a robust, accurate, and computationally efficient method for robotic contour following, overcoming several limitations of existing tactile servoing approaches and paving the way for more sophisticated and reliable tactile interactions in robotics.
