# ABot-Claw: A Foundation for Persistent, Cooperative, and Self-Evolving Robotic Agents

> [!summary] Summary
> ABot-Claw, developed by Alibaba Group's AMAP CV Lab, extends the OpenClaw runtime to enable persistent, cooperative, and self-evolving robotic agents by integrating a unified embodiment interface, a visual-centric cross-embodiment multimodal memory, and a critic-based closed-loop feedback mechanism. The system demonstrated effective coordination of heterogeneous robots and robust execution of complex, long-horizon tasks across manipulation, navigation, and human-robot interaction scenarios.

## Problem

- Existing embodied intelligent systems, often based on reactive System 1 paradigms, lack explicit models of task states, environmental topology, and long-term memory, limiting their performance in complex, long-horizon tasks.
- Current agent frameworks that incorporate System 2 cognitive functions frequently operate in closed, simulated environments or with predefined toolkits, lacking direct control over the underlying operating system and autonomous adaptation to novel actions or unexpected environmental changes.
- Real-world robotic deployments face challenges in uniformly organizing and controlling heterogeneous robot embodiments, maintaining persistent spatiotemporal context over extended periods, and robustly executing plans in the presence of perception noise and environmental uncertainties.

## Method

- A ==decoupled architectural design== spans an ==OpenClaw layer== (decision-making), a ==Shared Service layer== (perception/memory/evaluation), and a ==Robot Embodiment layer== (hardware execution), unified by a ==unified ROS-based interface== enabling ==cross-embodiment collaboration== across a dynamic robot pool.
- A ==visual-centric cross-embodiment multimodal memory== stores image-semantic embeddings, keyframes, object-centric data, and place anchors in a shared space, facilitating persistent context retention and grounded retrieval across different robots and over time, complete with a navigable return protocol for action-ready outputs.
- A ==closed-loop feedback mechanism==, powered by a ==generalist reward model (critic)==, evaluates task progress online, enabling completion detection, local corrections, and replanning under execution uncertainty, thereby enhancing system robustness and adaptability.

## Results

- A robotic arm successfully performed interactive object search under partial observability and semantic manipulation with ambiguous instructions, demonstrating online synthesis of search behaviors and self-correction upon grasp failure.
- A humanoid robot executed mobile manipulation for object delivery and recovered from robot-side failures by leveraging shared memory for cross-embodiment task reassignment, inspecting a non-responsive quadruped robot.
- A quadruped robot was successfully coordinated for visitor reception and guidance, showcasing effective embodiment selection based on task structure and sustained mobile behaviors over an extended spatial range.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A decoupled architectural design effectively allows for independent evolution and integration of high-level reasoning, shared services, and hardware-specific execution layers, which is crucial for scalable and maintainable real-world robotic systems.
> - Maintaining a shared, visual-centric, multimodal memory across heterogeneous robotic agents is key to achieving persistent environmental understanding and enabling long-horizon, context-aware actions, moving beyond instantaneous perception.
> - Integrating a critic-based closed-loop feedback mechanism provides robotic agents with the ability to adapt to real-world uncertainties, perform online self-correction, and strategically replan, significantly enhancing task robustness and adaptability.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of embodied intelligent systems is actively developing architectures that bridge high-level reasoning with low-level physical execution, often drawing inspiration from cognitive science's System 1 (fast, reactive) and System 2 (slow, deliberative) paradigm. Current System 1 approaches, such as Vision-Language-Action (VLA), Vision-Language Navigation (VLN), and World Action Model (WAM), provide strong perception and reactive responses, demonstrating generalization in local tasks. However, these systems are typically open-loop, lacking explicit modeling of task states, environmental topology, or long-term memory. Consequently, they encounter difficulties in complex, long-horizon tasks requiring continuous monitoring, dynamic adaptation, and coherent context maintenance.

To address these limitations, researchers have introduced agent frameworks with advanced cognitive functions, aiming to implement System 2 capabilities. These frameworks support goal-driven reasoning through task decomposition, memory management, and self-reflection, coordinating System 1 modules for concrete actions. While this hierarchical integration improves behavioral organization, most existing agents operate within "closed sandboxes." They rely on pre-registered toolkits and predefined functions, often failing to autonomously generate solutions for novel actions or adapt to unexpected environmental changes. Furthermore, these agents frequently lack direct operating system control, hindering their ability to interact with real-world systems, such as reading/writing files or invoking local applications, thereby remaining disembodied.

OpenClaw emerged as a solution to provide a self-hosted, local agent runtime with full system privileges, enabling shell command execution, GUI application control, and cross-platform communication. It also supports natural language-driven dynamic skill evolution, allowing agents to generate, validate, and deploy code autonomously. While OpenClaw provides a foundation for execution, it lacks a high-level control architecture for complex, long-duration, multi-device collaborative embodied tasks. Its reliance on textual logs can fragment multimodal information, and it lacks robust mechanisms for long-term state tracking and closed-loop feedback, making it vulnerable to failures. Moreover, integrating heterogeneous devices often requires tight-coupled configurations, limiting dynamic collaboration.

ABot-Claw is positioned as an embodied extension of OpenClaw, aiming to fill these specific gaps. It seeks to integrate a unified embodiment interface, a visual-centric cross-embodiment multimodal memory, and a critic-based closed-loop feedback mechanism. By doing so, ABot-Claw intends to provide the necessary high-level control architecture, persistent context retention, and robust execution monitoring that OpenClaw currently lacks, thereby advancing towards truly autonomous, general-purpose robotic agents capable of continuous learning and evolution in open, dynamic environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop a robust, flexible, and scalable embodied intelligence framework, named ABot-Claw, that extends the capabilities of the OpenClaw runtime for real-world robotic execution. This framework aims to bridge the substantial gap between high-level reasoning and low-level physical execution in open-world environments, enabling robotic agents that are persistent, cooperative, and capable of self-evolution.

The motivation for ABot-Claw arises from several challenges identified in current embodied intelligent systems:

- **Limitation of Open-Loop Systems:** Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, while effective for perception and reactive responses, operate in an open-loop manner. This design inherently limits their performance in long-horizon tasks, as they lack explicit modeling of task states, environmental topology, and the ability to retain context over time. The absence of continuous monitoring and dynamic adaptation makes them prone to cascading failures and prevents consistent decision-making across extended periods.
- **Constrained System 2 Agents:** While frameworks incorporating System 2 cognitive mechanisms improve planning and reasoning, they typically operate within closed environments or "sandboxes." These systems often rely on predefined toolkits and function sets, limiting their ability to handle novel actions or adapt to unforeseen environmental changes. A more fundamental issue is their lack of direct control over the underlying operating system, which restricts meaningful interaction with the physical world, rendering them disembodied in practice.
- **Incomplete Embodiment in OpenClaw:** OpenClaw provides a runtime with full system privileges, enabling agents to execute shell commands, control GUI applications, and evolve skills dynamically through natural language. This addresses the "disembodied" problem. However, OpenClaw, in its original form, lacks a high-level control architecture necessary for complex, long-duration, multi-robot embodied tasks. It does not clearly delineate roles between System 1 and System 2, struggles with sustained task decomposition and monitoring, fragments multimodal information (visual, linguistic, state) into isolated logs, and lacks mechanisms for long-term state tracking and closed-loop feedback. Furthermore, its approach to integrating heterogeneous devices limits dynamic collaboration and parallel execution.

To address these motivations, ABot-Claw sets out to integrate three core technical innovations:
1. **Unified Embodiment Interface and Dynamic Multi-Agent Scheduling:** To enable coordination of heterogeneous robots with diverse capabilities and actuation interfaces. This aims to facilitate capability-aware routing, parallel execution, and cross-embodiment collaboration.
2. **Visual-Centric Cross-Embodiment Multimodal Memory:** To provide persistent context retention and grounded retrieval across time, space, and robot embodiments. This memory system is intended to store object observations, place anchors, keyframes, and semantic visual representations, overcoming the limitations of text-only logs or purely geometric maps.
3. **Critic-Based Closed-Loop Feedback Mechanism:** To ensure robust execution in uncertain real-world environments. This mechanism, instantiated with a generalist reward model, is designed for online progress evaluation, local correction, and dynamic replanning when faced with execution uncertainty or failures.

By integrating these components, ABot-Claw aims to create an embodied intelligence framework capable of continuous learning, adaptation, and evolution in complex and dynamic environments, representing a step toward truly autonomous, general-purpose robotic systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ABot-Claw is designed as an extension of the OpenClaw runtime, building upon its foundation for high-level software operation and task orchestration. The system adopts a decoupled architecture comprising three main layers: the OpenClaw Layer, the Shared Service Layer, and the Robot Embodiment Layer, with bidirectional communication between them. This modularity is intended to enhance maintainability, scalability, and allow independent evolution of components.

The core methodological innovations of ABot-Claw are:

#### 3.1. Heterogeneous Embodiment Integration

ABot-Claw implements a "one-runtime, multiple-bodies" design where a single decision-making runtime coordinates a distributed pool of diverse robot embodiments.
- **Unified Embodiment Interface:** To abstract away the differences in low-level robot interfaces (e.g., manipulators, mobile platforms, humanoids), a unified interface is introduced. ROS-based adapters map native robot functions into a shared set of callable skills, such as navigation, observation, inspection, and manipulation primitives. This allows the high-level agent to issue intent-level actions through a common interface, decoupling planning from hardware specifics.
- **Centralized Runtime with Multi-body Execution:** The ABot-Claw runtime maintains a dynamic pool of connected physical devices, tracking their availability, task progress, and status. This enables the decomposition of user requests into subtasks that can be dispatched across multiple robots for parallel execution.
- **Task Routing and Cross-Embodiment Collaboration:** Robots operate as cooperative nodes sharing a global task objective and environmental memory. A task allocation strategy considers capability, location, load, and priority to route tasks and facilitate dynamic reassignment or handoffs between embodiments (e.g., a mobile robot delivering an object to a workstation for a fixed manipulator to precisely place).

#### 3.2. Visual-Centric Cross-Embodiment Multimodal Memory

This component addresses the need for persistent spatiotemporal context, aggregating observations from different robots into a shared, queryable, and action-ready world context.
- **Memory Entities:** The memory is organized into four types of entities:
 - **Visual Memory:** Stores scene-level observations with semantic and spatiotemporal context, using visual embeddings from a vision-language encoder for open-vocabulary retrieval. Sparse keyframes, associated with timestamps and poses, are also stored for efficient trajectory summarization and viewpoint revisiting.
 - **Object-Centric Memory:** Continuous visual detectors identify salient objects, storing their category labels, observation timestamps, source robot identifiers, and 3D poses for tasks requiring object-level grounding.
 - **Place-Anchor Memory:** Semantically meaningful locations (e.g., "kitchen") are registered, either automatically or via user annotation, to discretize space into language-friendly nodes for planning.
- **Retrieval Mechanisms:**
 - **Image-Semantic Memory:** Utilizes latent-space cross-modal retrieval, mapping visual observations and textual queries into a shared embedding space for nearest-neighbor search.
 - **Object-Centric and Place-Anchor Memory:** Employs structured retrieval based on discrete metadata (category, robot, time window, spatial constraints), which can be combined with semantic retrieval.
- **Navigable Return Protocol:** Retrieval results are normalized into a unified action-ready representation, including semantic category, confidence score, visual evidence, and a stable 3D pose in the global coordinate frame. This allows downstream controllers to directly consume the output for navigation or manipulation.
- **Shared Memory:** All connected robots contribute to and consume from the same memory space, reducing redundant exploration and accumulating environmental context across embodiments and time.

#### 3.3. Closed-Loop Feedback with a Generalist Critic

To improve robustness against real-world uncertainty, ABot-Claw incorporates an explicit critic module for online progress evaluation and intervention.
- **State Evaluation:** A generalist reward model is used as the critic, providing a scalar progress signal based on the task instruction and the current observation. This signal reflects how well the current state aligns with the intended goal.
- **Adaptive Correction and Strategy Switching:** The critic signal informs three runtime decisions:
 - **Completion:** If the score exceeds a threshold, the subtask is marked complete.
 - **Local Refinement:** If the score indicates improvement but is below the threshold, local adjustments (e.g., pose, viewpoint, repeating actions) are attempted.
 - **Replanning:** If the score stagnates or drops significantly, a new memory query, revised plan, or different embodiment assignment is triggered.
- **Closing the Execution Loop:** Execution traces and critic scores are logged, feeding back into the system as structured experience to inform future decision-making.

#### 3.4. End-to-End Execution Flow

The overall execution involves: (1) **Instruction Grounding and Embodiment Selection** based on capabilities and context; (2) **Memory-Assisted Action Generation** using stored place anchors, objects, or keyframes; (3) **Execution and Progress Evaluation** by the selected robot and the critic module; and (4) **Update, Refinement, or Replanning** based on feedback, closing the loop between environment grounding, physical execution, and high-level task organization.

#### 3.5. Engineering Implementation

The decoupled architecture ensures each layer evolves independently through stable interfaces:
- **OpenClaw Layer:** Handles human-robot interaction, high-level decision-making, task parsing, capability-driven scheduling, and outputs executable Python scripts. It manages task decomposition, resource allocation, and system-level coordination, triggering failure handling and replanning.
- **Shared Service Layer:** Hosts computationally intensive and reusable modules (e.g., object detection, grasp perception, spatial memory, task evaluation) as independent services accessible via unified interfaces. This layer acts as a platform for integrating advanced models (VLA, VLN, WAM) and centralizes resource management.
- **Robot Layer:** Maps high-level action semantics to hardware-specific commands using ROS-based interfaces. It encapsulates hardware-dependent components (drivers, sensors, control) and maintains platform-specific modules, while shared modules define unified representations for communication, thus isolating embodiment heterogeneity.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The effectiveness and generality of the ABot-Claw system were demonstrated through experiments conducted on three heterogeneous robotic platforms: the Unitree G1 humanoid robot, the Unitree Go2 quadruped robot, and the Agilex Piper robotic arm. All tasks were specified using natural language, without reliance on predefined command templates or structured APIs. The demonstrations highlight the system's ability to interpret instructions, select embodiments and skills, and coordinate perception, planning, and control across various scenarios.

#### 4.1. Robotic Arm Demonstration

- **Interactive Search under Partial Observability:** When instructed to pick up a bottle that was initially outside its field of view, the robotic arm did not terminate. Instead, ABot-Claw identified the observational uncertainty and engaged the user for clarification. Upon user confirmation, the system synthesized a search procedure involving viewpoint adjustments and repeated perception. The manipulator successfully expanded its workspace, detected the target, and executed the grasp, demonstrating online synthesis of search behaviors based on task context, user feedback, and visual observations.
- **Manipulation under Ambiguous Instructions:** The system was tasked to pick "something sour" from a table and place it on a plate. ABot-Claw perceived the scene, enumerated candidate objects, and performed semantic reasoning to identify the orange as the most appropriate item. It then generated and executed the manipulation program. The demonstration also illustrated robustness to execution failures; when an initial grasp failed, the monitoring module detected the issue and triggered replanning, leading to a successful reattempt. This shows the system's capability to ground high-level, ambiguous language into concrete actions and self-correct during execution.

#### 4.2. Humanoid Robot Demonstration

- **Mobile Manipulation for Object Delivery:** A long-horizon task involved delivering a coffee bottle to a person in Room 207. The humanoid robot successfully grounded the object reference from its current visual observation and performed a grasping action. After verifying the grasp, it used place-anchor memory to navigate to Room 207. Upon arrival, the robot detected the recipient and completed the task via a handover action. This demonstration showcased coordinated perception, manipulation, navigation, and human-robot interaction within a single execution loop.
- **Cross-Embodiment Task Reassignment for Robot Inspection:** When a user requested the status of a quadruped robot, direct communication failed due to service unavailability. ABot-Claw responded by querying its shared memory to retrieve the quadruped's last known location. It then reassigned the inspection task to the humanoid robot. The humanoid navigated to the location, observed the quadruped's fallen state, and reported its status. This example highlights the system's ability to recover from robot-side failures by leveraging shared memory and dynamically reassigning tasks across available embodiments.

#### 4.3. Quadruped Robot Demonstration

- **Quadruped-Guided Visitor Reception:** This task involved meeting a visitor at the elevator and escorting them to a meeting room. ABot-Claw selected the quadruped robot, prioritizing its mobility and guiding capabilities. The quadruped was dispatched to the elevator, where it identified the visitor. It then initiated a guidance behavior, leading the visitor along a planned route to the meeting room while the runtime monitored progress. This demonstration indicates that embodiment selection can be conditioned on task requirements and that the runtime can coordinate reception-style behaviors on a mobile legged platform.

Overall, the results demonstrate that ABot-Claw autonomously executes complex manipulation and search tasks, exhibits robust performance under partial observability, successfully grounds ambiguous semantic instructions into executable actions, and manages robot-side failures through cross-embodiment reassignment. The system's ability to coordinate diverse robot platforms and integrate various functionalities into a unified framework was validated across these heterogeneous environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ABot-Claw represents a significant step in the development of embodied intelligent systems by addressing several critical limitations of existing robotic agents. Its contributions carry the potential for broad impact across various applications of robotics.

**Bridging the High-Level Reasoning and Low-Level Execution Gap:** The framework directly tackles the challenge of connecting abstract natural language instructions and complex cognitive planning with concrete physical actions in dynamic, open-world environments. By integrating a high-level control architecture, ABot-Claw enables more coherent and persistent task execution than traditional open-loop VLA systems.

**Enabling Persistent, Cooperative, and Self-Evolving Robotic Agents:**
- **Persistence:** The visual-centric multimodal memory allows agents to retain and retrieve context across time, space, and different robot embodiments. This overcomes the fragmented knowledge problem of reactive agents, enabling robots to build a lasting understanding of their environment and tasks.
- **Cooperation:** The unified embodiment interface and dynamic multi-agent scheduling facilitate the coordination of heterogeneous robots. This enables task decomposition, parallel execution, and sophisticated cross-embodiment collaboration and handoffs, allowing complex tasks to be solved more efficiently or by leveraging complementary robot capabilities.
- **Self-Evolving:** The critic-based closed-loop feedback mechanism, combined with logging of execution traces and critic scores, provides a foundation for online supervision and self-correction. This internal feedback loop supports continuous adaptation and learning, moving towards agents that can progressively improve their performance and capabilities.

**Scalable and Modular Infrastructure:** The decoupled architecture, consisting of the OpenClaw, Shared Service, and Robot Layers, enhances system maintainability, scalability, and long-term evolution. This modularity allows for independent development, deployment, and upgrades of components (e.g., new perception models, control algorithms, or robot platforms) without requiring extensive modifications to the core system. This is crucial for developing general-purpose robotic systems that can adapt to changing technologies and environments.

**Robustness in Real-World Settings:** The closed-loop feedback mechanism, powered by a generalist reward model, significantly enhances the system's robustness to real-world uncertainties such as perception noise, environmental changes, and execution failures. The ability to perform online progress evaluation, local correction, and dynamic replanning allows ABot-Claw to operate more reliably in unpredictable environments compared to systems relying solely on open-loop plans.

**Advancement Towards General-Purpose Robotics:** By integrating these capabilities, ABot-Claw moves towards the realization of truly autonomous and general-purpose robotic systems. Such systems would not be limited to predefined tasks or static environments but could continually learn, adapt, and operate effectively across a wide range of complex, dynamic scenarios.

**Potential Impact and Applications:** The implications of ABot-Claw extend to various domains, including:
- **Logistics and Warehousing:** Coordinated execution by diverse robots for picking, packing, and transportation.
- **Assisted Living and Healthcare:** Humanoid robots for personal assistance, delivery, and monitoring, with support from other robot types.
- **Industrial Automation:** Flexible manufacturing and inspection tasks requiring adaptive manipulation and mobile capabilities.
- **Public Services:** Visitor reception, guidance, and environmental monitoring in complex buildings.
- **Exploration and Disaster Response:** Multi-robot teams for mapping, search, and rescue in hazardous or unknown environments.

In summary, ABot-Claw provides a comprehensive framework that addresses fundamental limitations in embodied AI, offering a practical foundation for developing robotic agents that are more intelligent, adaptable, and capable of operating in the complexities of the real world.
