# Neural Brain: A Neuroscience-inspired Framework for Embodied Agents

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Nanyang Technological University and collaborators propose a neuroscience-inspired "Neural Brain" framework for embodied agents, integrating multimodal active sensing, perception-cognition-action loops, neuroplasticity-driven memory, and energy-efficient neuromorphic hardware. This work defines the core components and architecture for building adaptable, real-world interactive AI systems.

## Problem

- Current AI systems, including advanced language models, are largely 'disembodied,' lacking the capacity for physical interaction and grounding in the real world.
- Autonomous agents require human-like adaptability and flexible control to navigate and manipulate complex, unstructured environments.
- A well-established architecture or theoretical foundation for constructing a comprehensive central intelligence system (Neural Brain) for embodied agents from a neuroscience perspective is currently lacking.

## Method

- Proposed the ==Neural Brain== framework, a biologically inspired computational architecture for embodied agents, drawing parallels with the human brain's functions.
- Defined ==4 fundamental components==: ==multimodal active sensing==, a ==perception-cognition-action integration== function, ==neuroplasticity-driven memory== storage and updating, and energy-efficient ==neuromorphic hardware/software co-design==.
- Conducted a comprehensive literature review to analyze the state-of-the-art in AI agents against the proposed framework, identifying existing gaps and future research directions.

## Results

- Formalized the **Neural Brain** as a computational framework for embodied agents, providing a novel blueprint that integrates **4** interconnected core components with detailed design principles.
- Conducted an extensive gap analysis, critically identifying limitations in current AI capabilities across multimodal sensing, PCA integration, memory systems, and hardware/software in comparison to human intelligence.
- Outlined a strategic, multi-stage roadmap for future research, categorizing short-term (**1-3 years**), mid-term (**5 years**), and long-term (**10+ years**) goals towards generalizable, autonomous agents capable of human-level intelligence.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Achieving human-level intelligence in embodied agents necessitates a unified, brain-inspired architecture that tightly integrates sensing, cognition, action, and memory processes.
> - Real-world adaptability for agents requires active sensing strategies, continuous and closed-loop perception-cognition-action cycles, and dynamic, neuroplasticity-driven memory systems capable of consolidation and adaptation.
> - Energy-efficient neuromorphic hardware and co-designed software, leveraging principles like sparse activation and event-driven computation, are crucial for scalable and sustainable deployment of complex embodied AI.
