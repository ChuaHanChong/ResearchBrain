# ViVa: A Video-Generative Value Model for Robot Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> ViVa, a video-generative value model for robot reinforcement learning developed by GigaAI researchers, repurposes a pretrained video generator to provide temporally sensitive and reliable value estimates for complex robotic tasks. The model achieved a 73% success rate on a real-world box assembly task, outperforming VLM-based baselines at 58%, and demonstrated robust generalization to novel objects like pants folding.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models face difficulties in real-world deployment for long-horizon robotic tasks due to partial observability and delayed feedback.
- Current VLM-based value functions, primarily trained on static image-text data, struggle to capture dynamic temporal evolution during robotic interactions, leading to unreliable value estimations.
- These VLM-based approaches often exhibit limited generalization to novel objects, relying instead on memorized visual patterns.

## Method

- **ViVa** reformulates ==value learning as a future prediction problem==, leveraging a pretrained ==video diffusion Transformer== to jointly predict a scalar value for the current state and future proprioceptive states.
- The model integrates current multi-view RGB images and proprioceptive states into a ==unified latent space==, and its denoiser is trained to reconstruct noisy future proprioception and value latents.
- The value target is derived from ==normalized episode success labels==, ensuring a consistent margin between successful and failed outcomes.

## Results

- **ViVa** achieved a **73%** success rate in the real-world box assembly task, surpassing VLM-based value functions (**58%**) and imitation-only policies (**42-53%**) within the RECAP framework.
- Qualitative analysis demonstrated ViVa's value estimates were smooth, accurately tracked task progress, and sensitively detected fine-grained manipulation errors (e.g., misalignments) in tasks like box assembly, shirt folding, and toilet paper organization.
- The model exhibited robust generalization to novel objects, successfully detecting task milestones during pants folding, whereas VLM-based methods showed erratic and unreliable value trends.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Video generative models, optimized for spatiotemporal evolution, are inherently more suitable for value estimation in robot reinforcement learning compared to VLMs trained on static data, as value learning is fundamentally an anticipation problem.
> - Grounding value estimation in anticipated embodiment dynamics, through joint prediction of future proprioceptive states, yields more reliable and sensitive value signals for policy improvement.
> - Leveraging spatiotemporal priors from large-scale video corpora enables robust generalization to novel objects and environments, overcoming limitations of methods reliant on memorized visual patterns.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of robots capable of perceiving, reasoning, and acting autonomously in physical environments represents a fundamental challenge in embodied artificial intelligence. Recent advancements in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have significantly improved robot manipulation capabilities by leveraging large-scale pretraining on diverse datasets. These models, such as 𝜋\*0.6 and Gigabrain-0, enable general-purpose manipulation.

Despite these strides, deploying robots in real-world settings faces obstacles related to partial observability and delayed feedback, where the consequences of actions may only become apparent over extended periods. This necessitates mechanisms for robots to understand if their current interactions are progressing towards successful task completion. In reinforcement learning (RL), this capability is formalized by the value function, which estimates expected future outcomes and provides a critical signal for policy improvement. Frameworks like RECAP, used in conjunction with VLA models, rely heavily on accurate value functions for advantage estimation and policy refinement, indicating that the quality of the value model directly influences learning performance.

Previous research has explored integrating Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for value estimation. Approaches such as GVL and TopReward query VLMs to assess task progress, sometimes framing value prediction as classification or temporal ordering. However, these VLM-based methods inherit a limitation: VLMs are typically trained on static image–text data, which are effective for semantic understanding of individual scenes but do not inherently model how scenes evolve over time or the dynamics of physical interactions. This characteristic restricts their ability to provide reliable value estimation for the continuous, temporally extended nature of robotic tasks.

This work introduces ViVa, which addresses this limitation by proposing a shift from static image-based models to video generative models as the foundation for value estimation. The premise is that value estimation is fundamentally a problem of anticipating future outcomes, and video generative models are explicitly designed to capture temporal evolution and scene dynamics. By repurposing a pretrained video generator, ViVa aims to intrinsically couple value prediction with foresight and anticipated embodiment dynamics, offering a more robust approach to value function learning in robotics.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the identified limitations of existing value models in facilitating real-world robot deployment for complex, long-horizon tasks. The core objectives are detailed as follows:

1. **Address Challenges of Real-World Robotics**: Robotic interaction in the physical world is characterized by partial observability, where the robot's full state is not always known, and delayed feedback, where the effects of actions are not immediately apparent. These factors make it difficult for robots to learn to connect present behavior with future outcomes and reliably assess their progress toward task completion. The paper aims to develop a value model that can overcome these challenges.

2. **Improve Reliability of Value Estimation**: Current VLM-based value models, while leveraging large-scale pretraining, struggle to accurately represent how environments dynamically transform through robotic interactions. This is because VLMs are typically trained on static image-text data, making them adept at capturing "what is present" but less so at "how it changes over time." This mismatch leads to unreliable value signals, particularly in tasks requiring an understanding of continuous temporal progression and physical contact. The objective is to enhance the reliability of these signals.

3. **Ground Value Estimation in Anticipated Dynamics**: The central hypothesis is that accurate value estimation is inherently a problem of anticipating future states and interactions. Video generative models, unlike discriminative VLM-based models, are explicitly optimized to learn and predict temporal evolution from large-scale video corpora. The research is motivated to harness these spatiotemporal priors by reformulating value learning as a future prediction problem.

4. **Develop a Video-Generative Value Model (ViVa)**: The key objective is to propose and implement ViVa, a novel architecture that repurposes a pretrained video generator to jointly predict two crucial elements:
 - **Future proprioception**: To force the model to internalize the robot’s own dynamics, which is vital for tasks requiring precise limb coordination and provides an implicit measure of motion.
 - **A scalar value**: Representing task progress, intrinsically coupling it with the model's capacity for foresight.
 This joint prediction aims to ground value estimation in anticipated embodiment dynamics beyond static visual snapshots.

5. **Provide Superior Learning Signals for Policy Optimization**: By generating more reliable value signals that accurately track task progress and detect execution errors, ViVa aims to facilitate more effective advantage computation and, consequently, improved policy optimization within reinforcement learning frameworks like RECAP. This should lead to enhanced performance and faster learning in robotic manipulation tasks.

6. **Demonstrate Robustness and Generalization**: The research seeks to validate ViVa's performance across various long-horizon manipulation tasks, including challenging scenarios involving deformable objects and multi-stage sequences. A specific objective is to demonstrate sensitivity to fine-grained execution errors and robust generalization capabilities to novel objects, indicating a more transferable representation of task dynamics.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ViVa addresses the limitations of VLM-based value models by repurposing a pretrained video generator, Wan2.2, to jointly predict future proprioception and a scalar value, thereby grounding value estimation in anticipated embodiment dynamics. The methodology involves several key components:

**Problem Formulation:**
Robotic manipulation is formalized as a Markov Decision Process (MDP). At each timestep $t$, the robot receives an observation $x_t$, comprising multi-view RGB images ($o_t$) and proprioception ($q_t$). The objective is to learn a value function $V_{\pi}(x_t)$ that estimates the expected future return under a policy $\pi$.

**Overall Architecture:**
ViVa is built upon Wan2.2, a pretrained video diffusion Transformer designed for generating future frames from initial images and text. The core architecture of Wan2.2 is preserved, but its input and output modalities are extended through a latent injection mechanism.

- **Latent Encoding of Modalities:**
 - All input and output modalities are uniformly mapped into latent frames of shape ($H'$, $W'$, $C'$).
 - **Images ($o_i^t$)**: Each camera view is independently compressed into a latent frame ($z_{o_i^t}$) using a pretrained spatiotemporal Variational Autoencoder (VAE).
 - **Proprioception ($q_t$)**: Low-dimensional proprioceptive states are first normalized to $[-1, 1]$. They are then embedded into a latent frame ($z_{q_t}$) by repeating their elements to match the latent frame size ($H'W'C'$) and reshaping.
 - **Scalar Value ($v_t$)**: The scalar value (representing task progress) is also normalized to $[-1, 1]$. It is embedded into a latent frame ($z_{v_t}$) by broadcasting the normalized value to every element of the latent frame.

- **Latent Sequence During Training:**
 A fixed-length sequence of latent frames is assembled, comprising both conditioning and target frames for a prediction horizon $K$.
 - **Clean Conditioning Frames**: These serve as the input context and include a zero-initialized blank placeholder ($z_{blank}$), the current proprioceptive state ($z_{q_t}$), and multi-view current images ($z_{o_1^t}, z_{o_2^t}, z_{o_3^t}$).
 - **Noisy Target Frames**: These are the outputs that the model learns to predict and consist of the future proprioceptive state ($z_{q_{t+K}}$) and the current scalar value ($z_{v_t}$). These target frames are corrupted with Gaussian noise at a randomly sampled level $\sigma$.
 - The denoiser $D_\theta$ within the diffusion Transformer is trained to recover the clean target frames from their noisy versions, conditioned on the clean prefix.

- **Latent Sequence During Inference:**
 At inference time, only the conditioning frames are available. The current observations (images and proprioception) are encoded into their respective latent frames to form the prefix. The model then performs reverse diffusion to generate the target frames: $\hat{z}_{q_{t+K}}$ (predicted future proprioception) and $\hat{z}_{v_t}$ (predicted current value).
 - **Value Prediction ($\hat{v}_t$)**: Obtained by averaging all elements of $\hat{z}_{v_t}$ and then rescaling the result from $[-1, 1]$ back to the original range $$.
 - **Future Proprioception Recovery ($\hat{q}_{t+K}$)**: The inverse of the repeat-padding injection procedure is applied: $\hat{z}_{q_{t+K}}$ is flattened, split into chunks corresponding to the original proprioception dimension, each chunk is averaged, and the result is rescaled.

- **Training Objective:**
 ViVa adopts the flow matching formulation, consistent with Wan2.2. For a clean latent frame $z_0$ (either $z_{q_{t+K}}$ or $z_{v_t}$), and a Gaussian noise latent $z_1 \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$, a linear interpolation path $z_\tau = (1-\tau)z_0 + \tau z_1$ is constructed. The model $v_\theta(z_\tau; \tau, c)$ is trained to predict the constant velocity $z_1 - z_0$ along this path. The total objective is a weighted combination of losses for future proprioception ($\mathcal{L}_{prop}$) and value prediction ($\mathcal{L}_{val}$), with $\lambda_{prop}$ and $\lambda_{val}$ as their respective weights.
 - Notably, initial experiments attempting to jointly predict future visual latents degraded value estimation accuracy, which the authors hypothesize is due to the inherent difficulty mismatch between high-dimensional visual generation and the simpler structure of value prediction.

**Reward Definition and Value Training:**
To provide a consistent and outcome-aware supervision signal, a specific reward definition is employed:
- Each training episode is annotated with a binary success label.
- For an episode of length $T$, the step-wise reward $r_t$ is defined as:
 - $r_t = 1/T$ if $t < T$ (progress for successful episodes).
 - $r_t = 0$ if $t = T$ and success (no additional reward at successful terminal state).
 - $r_t = 1$ if $t = T$ and failure (terminal penalty for failed episodes).
- This definition leads to a cumulative return $G_t = \sum_{k=t}^T r_k$ that provides discriminative supervision:
 - $G_t = (T-t)/T$ for successful episodes (values in $[0, 1)$).
 - $G_t = (T-t)/T + 1$ for failed episodes (values in $[1, 2)$).
- This structure ensures a constant margin of 1.0 between successful and failed outcomes at any temporal stage, resolving ambiguity between task progress and failure. $G_t$ serves as the clean target for the value latent $z_{v_t}$ in the flow matching objective.

The integration of future proprioceptive prediction compels the model to internalize the robot's physical dynamics, which complements visual cues and enhances value estimation by capturing both task-level integrity and embodied evolution.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents extensive evaluations across three long-horizon manipulation tasks: shirt folding, box packaging, and paper roll organization, along with an out-of-domain generalization task (pants folding) and real-robot experiments.

**Evaluation Tasks and Metrics:**
The tasks require dual-arm coordination and multi-stage manipulation of deformable objects. Evaluation metrics include Pearson and Spearman correlation for overall value quality, and event-level metrics: Milestone Sensitivity (MS), Error Sensitivity (ES), Milestone Detection Rate (MDR), and Error Detection Rate (ErrDR). MS and ES measure the directional correctness and magnitude of value changes at key events, while MDR and ErrDR quantify the proportion of correctly detected events.

**Value Model Evaluation (In-Domain Tasks):**
- **Quantitative Comparison (Table 1)**: ViVa demonstrates strong global correlation with ground truth task progress. While sparse-sampled VLM-based methods (GVL, TopReward) show low or negative correlations, dense-supervised approaches (𝜋\*0.6, ViVa) achieve significantly higher correlations. ViVa attains Pearson correlations of 0.984 on shirt folding and 0.952 on paper roll organization, outperforming 𝜋\*0.6 on these two tasks, while 𝜋\*0.6 shows a slightly higher correlation on box packaging.
- **Fine-grained Event-level Metrics (Tables 2 & 3)**: ViVa consistently outperforms 𝜋\*0.6 in capturing fine-grained event responses.
 - On **shirt folding**, 𝜋\*0.6 exhibits negative MS (-0.266) and ES (-0.299), indicating that its value predictions move in the incorrect direction at key task milestones and errors. In contrast, ViVa achieves positive MS (0.095) and ES (0.012), alongside dramatically higher MDR (0.990 vs. 0.407) and ErrDR (1.000 vs. 0.500), signifying near-perfect detection of both milestones and errors.
 - Similar trends are observed across **box packaging** and **paper roll organization**, where ViVa generally shows higher ES and ErrDR, indicating superior error awareness.
- **Trajectory Visualization (Figure 6 & 5)**: Qualitative analysis of value trajectories confirms ViVa's sensitivity. It produces clear value increases at task milestones and distinct decreases at execution errors, indicating precise progress awareness. This contrasts with the noisy or muted responses from VLM-based models.

**Object Generalization (Out-of-Domain Task: Pants Folding):**
- **Quantitative (Table 4)**: On the unseen pants folding task, ViVa consistently outperforms 𝜋\*0.6 across all metrics. Its MS more than doubles (0.037 vs. 0.016), and MDR improves by over 60% (0.643 vs. 0.397), demonstrating stronger generalization capabilities.
- **Trajectory Visualization (Figure 6)**: ViVa maintains its ability to respond to milestone events on the novel task, suggesting that its learned spatiotemporal priors provide a more transferable representation compared to VLM-based methods.

**Real-Robot Experiments:**
- **Success Rates (Table 5)**: Evaluated on real robots across the three main tasks. RECAP (ViVa) significantly outperforms both imitation learning baselines (𝜋0.5, Gigabrain-0) and RECAP (VLM). RECAP (ViVa) achieves an average success rate of 80.0%, compared to RECAP (VLM)'s 63.3%, indicating that improved value estimation translates directly to better policy performance.
- **Rollout-wise Performance Evolution (Figure 7)**: ViVa demonstrates faster and more substantial improvement across successive rollout rounds. For example, on shirt folding, ViVa's success rate jumps from 50% to 80% after one rollout round, while the VLM-based approach shows less improvement.

**Ablation Studies:**
- **Effect of Video Generator Backbone (Tables 6, 7, 8)**: Replacing the VLM backbone with a video generator (even without proprioceptive input/output) consistently leads to performance gains, particularly in milestone sensitivity and detection rates. This highlights the superior foundation provided by video generative priors.
- **Effect of Input and Future State (Tables 6, 7, 8)**: Incorporating current proprioceptive state as input and, crucially, predicting future proprioceptive state further enhances ViVa's performance. The full ViVa architecture consistently achieves the best results, especially in improving error sensitivity and detection, by grounding value in anticipated embodiment dynamics.
- **Effect of Pretrained Weights (Tables 9, 10, 11)**: Removing pretrained weights from the video generator backbone causes significant degradation across all metrics. This confirms that the spatiotemporal priors learned from large-scale video pretraining are indispensable for robust value estimation and error detection.

**Hyperparameter Analysis:**
- **Loss Weight ($\lambda_{prop}$) (Figure 8)**: An optimal balance is found at $\lambda_{prop} = 0.5$, which provides robust performance across tasks, ensuring sufficient proprioception modeling for error awareness without over-prioritizing it.
- **Prediction Horizon ($K$) (Figure 9)**: A prediction horizon of $K = 50$ timesteps is determined to be optimal, balancing the need for sufficient temporal context with avoiding noise from overly long horizons.

**Efficiency Comparison (Table 12):**
ViVa demonstrates competitive computational efficiency. It trains in 4 GPU·days and achieves an inference time of 0.18 seconds per frame. This represents a 1.5x faster training time compared to the VLM-based baseline (6 GPU·days, 0.32 s/frame), indicating that the enhanced predictive capabilities are achieved with minimal additional overhead.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of ViVa represents a notable advancement in value function estimation for robot reinforcement learning. By reformulating value learning as a future anticipation problem and leveraging video generative models, this work addresses a key limitation of prior VLM-based approaches, which struggled with capturing dynamic temporal evolution and physical interactions.

The significance of ViVa stems from several aspects:

- **Improved Value Signal Quality**: ViVa demonstrates the ability to generate more reliable, sensitive, and accurate value signals. This is critical for reinforcement learning, as the quality of the value function directly influences the effectiveness of policy optimization. The high Event Response Scores and Event Detection Rates, particularly for error detection, indicate that robots using ViVa can better discern progression towards a goal and identify when an action has led to a detrimental state.
- **Enhanced Real-World Robot Performance**: The real-robot experiments confirm that ViVa's superior value estimation translates into tangible performance improvements. The RECAP (ViVa) system achieved higher success rates and faster learning convergence across challenging manipulation tasks compared to VLM-based alternatives and imitation learning baselines. This suggests a direct path for integrating video-generative value models into practical robotic systems.
- **Stronger Generalization Capabilities**: The demonstrated ability of ViVa to generalize to an out-of-domain task (pants folding) without specific training for that object highlights the robustness of the spatiotemporal priors learned by the video generator. This indicates that grounding value in anticipated dynamics provides a more transferable representation of task context.
- **Novel Paradigm for Value Estimation**: This research establishes video generative models as a viable and effective foundation for value estimation in robotics. It shifts the focus from static semantic understanding to dynamic temporal prediction, opening new avenues for incorporating rich spatiotemporal information into robot learning.
- **Embodiment-Aware Prediction**: The joint prediction of future proprioception, alongside the scalar value, forces the model to internalize the robot's own dynamics. This embodiment-aware prediction provides a complementary signal to visual cues, making the value estimation more comprehensive and sensitive to the robot's physical state and potential interaction errors.

The potential impact of ViVa is substantial. By providing robots with a more accurate and temporally grounded understanding of task progress and potential failures, it could lead to the development of more robust, adaptive, and autonomous robotic systems. Future work could explore scaling the pretraining of such value models to even larger datasets, potentially unlocking stronger cross-task generalization and further broadening their applicability in diverse and complex real-world scenarios.
