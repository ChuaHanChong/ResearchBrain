# SUREFlow: State-space Uncertainty-aware REsidual Flow Matching for Robust Robot Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> SUREFlow, a framework from Kyungpook National University, introduces a meta-reinforcement learning approach that integrates a Mamba-based state-space model with uncertainty-aware residual flow matching for robust robot manipulation. This method achieves an average success rate of 92.5% on the LIBERO benchmark, outperforming existing transformer and Mamba-based policies, and attains long-horizon robustness comparable to models with significantly more parameters (7 billion vs. 179.1 million).

## Problem

- Generative policies for robot manipulation often suffer from instability and error accumulation during long-horizon rollouts due to compounding velocity errors.
- Existing generative policies typically lack explicit mechanisms to model input-dependent uncertainty, which reduces their robustness in handling complex, real-world manipulation tasks.
- Many current vision-language-action (VLA) models rely on computationally intensive transformer-based architectures, leading to high overhead and scalability challenges.

## Method

- **SUREFlow** integrates a ==Mamba==-based ==state-space model== for efficient sequence modeling, offering linear computational complexity instead of quadratic.
- It employs an **Uncertainty-aware Residual Flow (URFlow)** module that jointly predicts ==action velocities== and input-dependent ==residual uncertainty==, enabling explicit uncertainty modeling.
- The framework incorporates an internal, ==closed-loop refinement== mechanism during inference, using predicted uncertainty to selectively correct unreliable action dimensions without external feedback.

## Results

- SUREFlow achieved an average success rate of **92.5%** on the LIBERO benchmark, surpassing transformer-based Octo by **17.4%** and Mamba-based MaIL by **34.2%**.
- On the LIBERO-PRO benchmark, SUREFlow, with **179.1 million** parameters, demonstrated long-horizon robustness comparable to OpenVLA (**7 billion** parameters) and π0.5 (**3 billion** parameters), with a normalized success rate of **0.49**.
- Real-robot experiments on a color-matching pick-and-insert task yielded an **88.5%** success rate, confirming the practical applicability and robustness against real-world perception noise and pose variations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Modeling heteroscedastic uncertainty in the flow velocity field allows the policy to quantify prediction confidence for each action dimension, differentiating reliable from unreliable predictions.
> - Internal action refinement based on predicted uncertainty can effectively mitigate compounding integration errors and enhance stability during long-horizon rollouts without relying on online environment feedback.
> - Leveraging state-space models like Mamba provides a computationally efficient backbone that achieves competitive robustness with significantly fewer parameters compared to large transformer-based VLA models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robot manipulation has seen advancements through generative vision-language-action (VLA) policies, which leverage multi-view visual observations and natural language instructions to enable generalizable robotic skills. Traditional approaches often rely on direct action regression, resulting in deterministic predictions that may struggle with multimodal behaviors observed in real-world tasks. This limitation has led to increased interest in generative policy learning methods, such as diffusion-based and flow-based policies, which model conditional action distributions. These generative methods offer stochastic action generation and improved expressiveness, contributing to enhanced robustness and flexibility compared to deterministic baselines.

However, existing continuous generative policies typically operate at a single temporal scale and may lack explicit temporal abstraction. Stochasticity introduced during action generation can accumulate over extended action sequences, potentially leading to unstable execution and inconsistent behaviors during long-horizon rollouts. Furthermore, these methods often incur high computational costs due to iterative sampling processes and frequently rely on transformer-based architectures, which present quadratic computational complexity with respect to sequence length, thereby increasing the overhead for large-scale models.

Concurrently, state-space models (SSMs) like Mamba have emerged as efficient alternatives to Transformer backbones, offering linear time complexity and robust temporal modeling capabilities. While Mamba has demonstrated success in language and vision domains, its application in generative robot control, particularly for addressing issues of instability and uncertainty, has been limited. Current generative manipulation policies often lack explicit mechanisms to quantify or correct uncertain action predictions during inference, which can hinder their robustness in complex manipulation tasks when executed in an open-loop manner.

This research addresses the aforementioned gaps by proposing a framework that integrates an efficient state-space model with explicit uncertainty awareness into a generative flow-matching policy. It aims to develop a robust and computationally efficient solution for robot manipulation that can operate stably over long horizons, distinguishing itself from approaches that primarily rely on large, computationally intensive backbones or lack mechanisms for internal error correction during inference.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are to enhance the robustness and stability of robot manipulation policies, particularly in scenarios involving noise, partial observability, and stochastic initial conditions, while maintaining computational efficiency. The specific motivations and objectives are detailed as follows:

- **Addressing Instability in Generative Policies:** Existing generative policies, including diffusion and flow-based methods, often exhibit instability during extended rollouts. This instability stems from the accumulation of small velocity errors over time, degrading execution reliability. A key objective is to mitigate these compounding errors.
- **Incorporating Explicit Uncertainty Modeling:** Current generative policies typically assume homoscedastic (uniform) residuals and do not explicitly model input-dependent uncertainty within the action generation process. This limitation reduces their robustness, as they cannot differentiate between reliable and unreliable action dimensions. The research aims to introduce explicit, input-dependent uncertainty modeling to quantify prediction confidence alongside action velocities.
- **Enabling Selective Action Refinement without External Feedback:** A major motivation is to develop a mechanism for internal, closed-loop refinement of action sequences. By predicting uncertainty, the policy can selectively refine unreliable action dimensions during inference without requiring online environment feedback or external action correction. This is intended to stabilize iterative flow integration.
- **Leveraging Efficient Sequence Modeling Architectures:** Many current VLA models rely on large, transformer-based backbones, leading to substantial computational overhead and scalability challenges. An objective is to utilize a computationally efficient architecture, specifically a Mamba-based state-space model, to enable scalable and efficient generative control without necessitating billion-parameter backbones.
- **Improving Long-Horizon Robustness:** The research aims for the proposed policy to achieve long-horizon robustness comparable to, or exceeding, large-scale VLA models, but with a significantly lighter architecture. This involves ensuring stability and consistency of execution over extended action sequences.
- **Integrating Context-Aware Action Refinement:** The policy aims to enhance temporal conditioning and contextual reasoning by incorporating a mechanism that allows action representations to selectively aggregate relevant contextual information from multimodal observations before velocity prediction.

In summary, the motivation is to overcome the limitations of existing generative VLA policies concerning instability, lack of explicit uncertainty handling, and high computational costs, by proposing a unified framework that combines efficient state-space modeling with uncertainty-aware residual flow matching and internal action refinement.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, SUREFlow, is an end-to-end VLA framework that integrates uncertainty-aware residual flow matching within a Mamba-based state-space backbone. The approach formulates action generation as a continuous-time flow matching problem and jointly predicts action velocities and input-dependent residual uncertainty.

**A. Problem Formulation: Conditional Flow Matching**
The policy learns a conditional mapping $\pi(a | o, g)$ from multimodal observations ($o$, including multi-view RGB images and robot proprioceptive states) and a natural language task instruction ($g$) to a continuous action sequence ($a$). The task instruction $g$ is encoded into a fixed task embedding $e$.

SUREFlow formulates policy learning as a conditional flow matching problem. Instead of direct action regression, the objective is to learn a time-dependent vector field $v_t$ that generates a probability path $p_t(x)$. This path transforms a simple prior distribution $p_1$ (Gaussian noise, $z \sim N(0, I)$) into the expert action distribution $p_0$ (ground truth action $a \sim p_0$). The conditional probability trajectory $x_t$ for time $t \in$ is defined as a linear interpolation: $x_t = (1 - t)a + tz$. The analytic target velocity field for this transformation is $v_t(x_t) = z - a$. The policy learns to predict $\hat{v}_t = f_{\theta}(x_t, s, t)$, where $s = \psi(o_t, e)$ represents the fused multimodal conditioning representation. SUREFlow treats the discrepancy between $\hat{v}_t$ and $v_t(x_t)$ as a residual flow matching problem, hypothesizing that this residual contains information about task complexity and data uncertainty.

**B. Overall Architecture: SUREFlow**
The SUREFlow architecture (Fig. 2) processes multimodal inputs, encodes them into a unified token representation, and feeds them to a Mamba backbone. This backbone is conditioned on continuous flow time. A "Memory-Guided Action Decoder (MGAD)" module is optionally used for temporal conditioning and contextual reasoning. The policy then predicts an action velocity field and an uncertainty estimate, forming the "Uncertainty-aware Residual Flow (URFlow)" module. URFlow constructs flow-matching and uncertainty-weighted losses for training and performs closed-loop refinement during inference.

**C. Encoding and Conditioning**
1. **Input Encoding:**
 - **Visual Observations:** Two RGB images ($I^{(1)}_t$, $I^{(2)}_t$) from agent view and eye-in-hand view are processed independently by convolutional visual encoders (ResNet-18) to yield visual embeddings ($z^{(k)}_t \in \mathbb{R}^d$).
 - **Proprioceptive States:** Robot joint and gripper states ($s_t$) are linearly projected to a proprioceptive token ($z^{(s)}_t$).
 - **Language Instruction:** Natural language instruction $g$ is encoded by a pretrained language encoder (CLIP ViT-B/32) into a fixed task embedding $e$.
 - **Flow Time Embedding:** A learnable time embedding $\phi(t) \in \mathbb{R}^d$ captures the continuous flow time variable $t \in$.
2. **Unified Token Sequence:** All encoded components, including the current action token $x_t$, are combined into a unified token sequence $X_t = \{\phi(t), e, z^{(1)}_t, z^{(2)}_t, z^{(s)}_t, x_t\}$, which serves as input to the sequence modeling backbone.
3. **Flow Conditioned Modulation (FCM):** Beyond concatenation, FCM injects temporal conditioning into intermediate feature representations. It uses the time embedding $\phi(t)$ to generate feature-wise scale ($\gamma$) and shift ($\beta$) parameters via linear projections, which then modulate feature sequences $U$: $FCM(U, \phi(t)) = U \odot (1 + \gamma) + \beta$. All modulation parameters are initialized to zero. FCM is applied to embedded state, action, task tokens, and decoded action features.

**D. Sequence Modeling and Action Generation**
1. **Mamba Backbone:** The unified token sequence $X_t$ is processed by a stack of $L$ Mamba layers. Each layer implements a selective State-space Model (SSM), providing efficient modeling of long-range temporal dependencies with linear computational complexity.
2. **Memory-Guided Action Decoder (MGAD):** This optional module enhances context-action interaction. The action token ($h^{(a)}_t$) from the final Mamba layer output ($H^{(L)}_t$) acts as a learnable query. It performs a lightweight, single-step cross-attention to the other tokens in $H^{(L)}_t$ (visual, proprioceptive, task) to produce a refined action token $\tilde{h}^{(a)}_t$.
3. **Velocity and Uncertainty Prediction:** The refined action representation $\tilde{h}^{(a)}_t$ (or $h^{(a)}_t$ if MGAD is not used) is mapped by two independent linear projection heads to predict the action velocity $\hat{v}_t$ and its log-variance $\hat{s}_t$.

**E. Uncertainty-aware Residual Flow (URFlow)**
URFlow explicitly models heteroscedastic uncertainty in the predicted flow velocity field, enabling selective refinement.
1. **Residual Flow Definition:** The policy $f_{\theta}$ predicts $\hat{v}_t$ and $\hat{s}_t$. The residual flow is defined as $r_t = \hat{v}_t - v_t(x_t)$, where $v_t(x_t)$ is the analytic target velocity.
2. **Training Objective:** The model optimizes a combined loss: a standard flow matching loss ($L_{FM} = E_{a,z,t}[\Vert r_t \Vert_2^2]$) and an uncertainty-aware residual loss ($L_u = \Vert r_t \Vert_2^2 / \exp(\hat{s}_t) + \hat{s}_t$). The total loss is $L = L_{FM} + \lambda_u L_u$, where $\lambda_u$ weights the uncertainty term. The uncertainty loss penalizes residuals under the predicted variance, encouraging the model to correctly estimate its confidence.
3. **Inference Time Refinement:** During inference, after generating an initial action sequence $a$, URFlow performs a deterministic refinement. At $t=0$, the policy predicts $\hat{v}_0$ and $\hat{s}_0$. An uncertainty mask $M$ is created, identifying action dimensions $i$ where $\hat{s}_{0,i} > \tau$ (a predefined threshold). For dimensions in $M$, the action is refined iteratively for $K$ steps: $a_i \leftarrow a_i - \eta \hat{v}_{0,i}$, where $\eta$ is a fixed step size. This internal refinement mechanism operates without online environment feedback.

**F. Implementation Details**
The models are implemented in PyTorch, trained with AdamW for 300 epochs (batch size 256). 70 flow integration steps are used during inference. The Mamba backbone has 5 layers with a hidden dimension of 256. MGAD uses 10 learnable action queries with a single-layer, 4-head cross-attention decoder. ResNet-18 is used for visual encoders, and CLIP ViT-B/32 for language encoding. URFlow parameters are $\lambda_u = 0.001$, $\tau = 0.9$, and 3 refinement steps $K$.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents experimental results across various benchmarks and analyses to address the proposed research questions regarding error reduction, stability under extended rollouts, long-horizon robustness, and uncertainty calibration.

**A. Comparison with State-of-the-Art Methods**
(Addresses RQ1: Can URFlow reduce compounding integration errors? And RQ2: Does URFlow matching improve stability and success under extended rollout horizons?)

- **LIBERO Benchmark:** SUREFlow achieved an average success rate (SR) of 92.5%, outperforming transformer-based models like Octo by 17.4% and Mamba-based methods such as MaIL by 34.2%. This performance indicates that the proposed URFlow-based uncertainty-aware residual modeling and closed-loop refinement contribute to performance gains beyond what a Mamba backbone alone offers.
- **Meta-World Benchmark:** On Meta-World, which assesses diverse manipulation skills across varying difficulty levels, SUREFlow attained an average SR of 88.32%. This result surpassed DP3 (74.4%), Diffusion Policy (37.6%), Mamba Policy (76.8%), and TinyVLA-H (31.6%). The nearly 78% improvement over Diffusion Policy suggests the efficiency and stability of SUREFlow's action generation, particularly for more complex tasks.
- **Interpretation:** The consistent improvements over various baselines, including those with Mamba backbones, indicate that URFlow effectively reduces compounding integration errors and enhances stability for generative flow-based control across different task complexities and rollout lengths.

**B. Robustness Under Distribution Shift and Efficiency**
(Addresses RQ3: Can URFlow achieve long-horizon robustness comparable to large-scale VLA models, without relying on billion-parameter backbones?)

- **LIBERO-PRO Benchmark:** SUREFlow achieved an average normalized SR of 0.49 with a model size of 179.1 million parameters. This performance is competitive with larger VLA models such as OpenVLA (7 billion parameters, SR 0.52) and $\pi_{0.5}$ (3 billion parameters, SR 0.53). SUREFlow also outperformed $\pi_0$ (3 billion parameters, SR 0.44).
- **Interpretation:** These results suggest that SUREFlow's architecture, combining the Mamba backbone's linear-time sequence modeling with URFlow's uncertainty-guided refinement, can achieve long-horizon robustness comparable to models significantly larger in scale. This indicates that the method effectively compensates for model size through its uncertainty-aware mechanisms, reducing the reliance on billion-parameter backbones.

**C. Stability and Uncertainty Calibration Analysis**
(Addresses RQ2, RQ3, and RQ4: How does uncertainty calibration interact with selective refinement?)

- **Long-Horizon Rollout Stability:** Analysis of performance across increasing rollout horizons (Fig. 4a) showed that SUREFlow with URFlow enabled exhibited slower performance degradation and maintained greater stability compared to the variant without URFlow. This supports the claim that URFlow improves stability and success under extended rollout horizons (RQ2) and contributes to long-horizon robustness without larger models (RQ3).
- **Refinement Activation and Success Heatmap:** Experiments with varying uncertainty thresholds ($\tau$) for refinement (Fig. 4b, 4c) demonstrated that lower thresholds triggered more frequent residual updates, while higher thresholds led to more selective refinement. A moderate $\tau$ maintained stable performance across longer horizons, indicating a balance between refinement frequency and stability, directly addressing RQ4.
- **Effect of MGAD on Uncertainty Calibration:** The inclusion of the Memory-Guided Action Decoder (MGAD) was observed to lead to faster convergence and a more stable uncertainty range during training (Fig. 4d). Models without MGAD exhibited lower $\hat{s}_{\text{min}}$ values, suggesting potentially overconfident predictions. MGAD contributed to better uncertainty calibration by moderating extreme values.

**D. Ablation Study**

- **Component Analysis:** Individual contributions of FCM, MGAD, and URFlow were evaluated. URFlow provided the largest single-module improvement (0.81 SR) compared to FCM (0.72 SR) and MGAD (0.77 SR). The full SUREFlow model, integrating all three components, achieved the highest SR of 0.92. These improvements were achieved with minor changes in parameter count and FLOPs, suggesting efficiency benefits beyond brute-force scaling.
- **Sensitivity Analysis of URFlow:** The uncertainty weighting parameter $\lambda_u$ and the number of refinement steps $K$ were analyzed. A properly calibrated $\lambda_u = 0.001$ yielded the best SR of 0.92, while excessive weighting degraded performance. For refinement steps, 3 steps achieved the optimal SR of 0.92, outperforming 2 or 5 steps, indicating the importance of balanced refinement.

**E. Real Robot Experiment**

- **Task:** SUREFlow was evaluated on a real-world tabletop color-matching pick-and-insert task using a Franka Emika arm. The task required visual discrimination, grasping, and accurate spatial alignment.
- **Performance:** SUREFlow achieved an average SR of 88.5% over 20 rollouts per color, consistently completing the task.
- **Interpretation:** These real-world results suggest that uncertainty-aware residual refinement enhances robustness against real-world perception noise and minor pose variations, supporting the practical applicability of SUREFlow.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces SUREFlow, a state-space uncertainty-aware residual flow matching framework, which represents a contribution to the field of robot manipulation. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key aspects:

- **Enhanced Robustness in Generative Policies:** The core contribution of the Uncertainty-aware Residual Flow (URFlow) module is its ability to explicitly model input-dependent uncertainty and perform selective action refinement. This directly addresses a limitation of many existing generative policies, which often struggle with error accumulation during long, iterative rollouts. By stabilizing execution and mitigating compounding errors without relying on environment feedback, SUREFlow enhances the reliability of robot manipulation in dynamic and noisy environments.
- **Computational Efficiency and Scalability:** The integration of a Mamba-based backbone provides a significant advantage in computational efficiency. Mamba's linear time complexity for sequence modeling offers a scalable alternative to the quadratically complex Transformer architectures common in large Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. This enables SUREFlow to achieve performance comparable to much larger, billion-parameter VLAs with a substantially lighter architecture (179.1M parameters), making it more accessible for deployment in resource-constrained robotic systems.
- **Novelty in Uncertainty-Aware Generative Control:** The approach of unifying conditional flow matching with uncertainty-aware residual refinement within a state-space generative policy is presented as a novel direction. It combines the expressive power of generative models with a principled way to manage and correct prediction uncertainties, which has not been widely explored in this specific context.
- **Improved Long-Horizon Task Execution:** The demonstrated ability of SUREFlow to maintain stability and high success rates over extended rollout horizons, as shown in the LIBERO Long and LIBERO-PRO benchmarks, suggests its potential for more complex, multi-step manipulation tasks that are common in real-world applications.
- **Practical Applicability:** The successful deployment and high success rate in a real-robot color-matching and insertion task underscore the practical effectiveness of SUREFlow. This suggests its capability to handle real-world perception noise and minor variations, paving the way for more reliable autonomous robot operation outside of simulation.
- **Guidance for Future Research:** The detailed ablation studies and sensitivity analyses regarding Flow Conditioned Modulation (FCM), Memory-Guided Action Decoder (MGAD), uncertainty weighting, and refinement steps offer insights for optimizing generative manipulation policies. This provides a foundation for future research in designing efficient, robust, and uncertainty-aware control frameworks for robotics.

In conclusion, SUREFlow offers a promising direction for developing robust, efficient, and scalable generative policies for robot manipulation, addressing critical challenges in reliability and computational cost that are pertinent for the broader advancement of autonomous robotic systems.
