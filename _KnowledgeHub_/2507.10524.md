# Mixture-of-Recursions: Learning Dynamic Recursive Depths for Adaptive Token-Level Computation

> [!summary] Summary
> Mixture-of-Recursions (MoR) introduces a unified framework for language models that combines parameter efficiency, adaptive computation, and efficient KV caching. It achieves strong performance, outperforming vanilla Transformers with nearly 50% fewer parameters and reducing training FLOPs by 25%, while increasing inference throughput by up to 2.06x.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) face high computational and memory costs during training and deployment, hindering accessibility and sustainability.
- Existing approaches to efficiency, such as parameter sharing and adaptive computation, often operate in isolation or introduce practical hurdles like complex training procedures.
- Traditional Recursive Transformers use fixed recursion depths for all tokens, ignoring varying computational needs, while KV caches consume vast GPU memory during inference.

## Method

- ==Mixture-of-Recursions (MoR)== integrates ==dynamic, token-level computation== within a parameter-shared architecture, leveraging the =="Middle-Cycle" strategy== for efficient weight reuse.
- Lightweight routers, either ==Expert-choice== (selecting top-k tokens for continued processing) or ==Token-choice== (assigning fixed recursion depth per token), enable adaptive computational allocation.
- Two KV caching strategies, Recursion-wise (selective storage) and Recursive KV Sharing (single computation and reuse), optimize memory usage during inference.

## Results

- MoR models achieve lower validation NLL (**2.7511**) and higher average few-shot accuracy (**43.1%**) compared to Vanilla Transformers (NLL **2.7824**, accuracy **42.3%**), utilizing nearly **50%** fewer parameters under equivalent training FLOPs.
- Processing the same amount of data (**20B** tokens), MoR reduces training FLOPs by **25%**, leading to a **19%** reduction in training time and **25%** less peak memory usage.
- Inference throughput is boosted by up to **2.06x** over vanilla baselines when MoR is combined with continuous depth-wise batching.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The unification of parameter efficiency, adaptive computation, and efficient KV caching within a single framework establishes a new Pareto frontier for LLM efficiency.
> - Dynamic, token-level routing allows models to adaptively allocate computational resources, enabling deeper "thinking" on complex tokens while optimizing overall compute.
> - MoR exhibits compute-optimal scaling behavior that prioritizes increases in model capacity (parameter count) over extending training length under isoFLOPs constraints.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of large language models (LLMs) has witnessed unprecedented advancements in capabilities, from few-shot generalization to complex reasoning, largely driven by scaling model size to hundreds of billions of parameters (e.g., Brown et al., 2020; OpenAI, 2023; Llama Team, 2024; Gemini Team, 2024). However, this scaling comes at a significant cost: immense computational and memory demands during both training and deployment, making these models expensive and challenging to utilize outside hyperscale data centers (Patterson et al., 2021).

In response, the research community has actively pursued "efficient" model designs along several axes:
1. **Parameter Efficiency/Sharing:** This involves reducing the total number of unique model weights, often through techniques like layer tying or reusing parameters across different parts of the network. Recursive Transformers (e.g., Dehghani et al., 2018; Bae et al., 2024) are a prime example, where a shared block of layers is repeatedly applied.
2. **Adaptive Computation:** This paradigm aims to dynamically allocate computational resources, spending more compute only when and where it is truly needed. Examples include early-exiting methods, which allow "easy" tokens or samples to exit the network early (Elbayad et al., 2020; Schuster et al., 2022), and Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) or Mixture-of-Depths (MoD) architectures (Shazeer et al., 2017; Fedus et al., 2022; Raposo et al., 2024), which route tokens to a subset of experts or layers.
3. **Efficient Key-Value (KV) Caching:** During autoregressive decoding, KV caches store intermediate attention computations, preventing redundant work. However, these caches can consume vast amounts of GPU memory, especially for long contexts and large batches. Research in this area focuses on compression, quantization, or sharing strategies (e.g., Shazeer, 2019; Ge et al., 2023; Brandon et al., 2024).

The critical gap that "Mixture-of-Recursions" (MoR) addresses is the lack of an architecture that effectively unifies *both* parameter efficiency and adaptive computation simultaneously, in a practically deployable manner. While Recursive Transformers offer inherent weight sharing, most prior attempts at dynamic recursion have faced practical hurdles, such as requiring specialized training procedures or efficient deployment challenges, often defaulting to fixed-depth computation. MoR aims to overcome these limitations by integrating dynamic token-level recursion *within* a parameter-shared architecture, and further enhancing it with selective KV caching.

Furthermore, this work touches upon the emerging field of "latent reasoning," where LLMs perform iterative internal computations ("thinking") within their hidden states before generating an output (Goyal et al., 2023; Geiping et al., 2025). MoR contributes to this by enabling adaptive thinking depth on a per-token basis, dynamically allocating internal deliberation based on token complexity.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce **Mixture-of-Recursions (MoR)**, a unified framework for language models that seamlessly integrates parameter efficiency, adaptive computation, and efficient KV caching. This framework aims to achieve large-model quality without incurring the prohibitive computational and memory costs typically associated with such models.

The motivation for MoR stems from several critical challenges in the current LLM landscape:

- **Computational and Memory Demands:** The impressive capabilities of large-scale Transformers come with a steep price in terms of training FLOPs and memory footprint, making them inaccessible for many researchers and expensive for widespread deployment.
- **Unifying Efficiency Paradigms:** While significant progress has been made independently in parameter sharing (e.g., Recursive Transformers) and adaptive computation (e.g., early-exiting, MoE/MoD), a single architecture that elegantly combines both without introducing significant practical overhead has been missing.
- **Limitations of Prior Recursive Models:** Existing Recursive Transformers, despite their parameter efficiency, often employ a fixed recursion depth for all tokens, neglecting the varying computational needs of different input elements. Attempts to introduce adaptivity often require complex, post-hoc training procedures or present deployment challenges, hindering their practical utility.
- **KV Cache Bottleneck:** The growing size of KV caches for long contexts and large batches constitutes a major memory and I/O bottleneck during inference. Solutions are needed that are integrated into the model architecture itself, rather than being post-hoc optimizations.

To address these motivations, MoR sets out to achieve the following specific goals:

1. **Dynamic Token-Level Thinking:** Enable the model to dynamically assign a specific recursion depth to each individual token based on its perceived difficulty, thereby directing computational effort precisely where it's most needed.
2. **End-to-End Trainability:** Develop lightweight routers that can be trained end-to-end with the main model, avoiding the need for complex, separate training phases often associated with adaptive computation methods.
3. **Memory-Efficient KV Caching:** Implement KV caching strategies that leverage the dynamic recursion to selectively store and retrieve key-value pairs, thereby reducing memory traffic and improving throughput.
4. **Superior Performance and Throughput:** Demonstrate that MoR can achieve lower validation perplexity and higher few-shot accuracy compared to vanilla Transformers and existing recursive baselines, even with significantly fewer parameters and higher inference throughput, under comparable computational budgets.
5. **Scalability:** Show that the benefits of MoR extend across different model scales, making it a viable and efficient alternative for pretraining and deployment of increasingly large models.

In essence, MoR's motivation is to provide an efficient and adaptive pathway for LLMs to "think" deeper on complex inputs while remaining lean in terms of parameters and memory, making high-quality LLMs more accessible and sustainable.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Mixture-of-Recursions (MoR) builds upon the foundation of Recursive Transformers to introduce dynamic, token-level computation and optimized KV caching. The core methodology involves two main components: a routing mechanism and KV caching strategies, integrated within a parameter-shared architecture.

#### 3.1. Preliminary: Recursive Transformers and Parameter-Sharing Strategies

MoR starts with the concept of Recursive Transformers, which reduce parameter count by reusing a shared stack of layers (a "recursion block") multiple times, effectively increasing the network's depth without growing its unique parameter size. The paper investigates four parameter-sharing strategies:
- **Cycle:** Recursion blocks reuse layers cyclically (e.g., `[(0, 1, 2), (0, 1, 2), (0, 1, 2)]`).
- **Sequence:** Each recursion block reuses the same layer consecutively before moving to the next (e.g., `[(0, 0, 0), (1, 1, 1), (2, 2, 2)]`).
- **Middle-Cycle:** Preserves unique parameters for the first and last layers while sharing weights among intermediate layers cyclically.
- **Middle-Sequence:** Similar to Middle-Cycle but with sequential sharing for intermediate layers.

Through ablation studies, the authors identify **Middle-Cycle** as the most effective parameter-sharing strategy, consistently achieving the lowest validation loss and highest accuracy. This strategy is therefore adopted for all MoR and Recursive Transformer models in the main experiments.

#### 3.2. Mixture-of-Recursions Components

**Routing Strategies:** MoR introduces lightweight routers that dynamically assign token-specific recursion depths.
- **Expert-choice Routing:**
 - **Mechanism:** Inspired by Mixture-of-Depths (MoD), each recursion step acts as an "expert" and selects a subset of top-𝑘 tokens to continue processing. Tokens not selected "exit" from that recursion step.
 - **Implementation:** A router, typically a linear layer or MLP, computes a scalar score 𝑔𝑟𝑡 for each token 𝑡 at recursion step 𝑟. The top-𝑘 tokens, based on these scores, proceed.
 - **Causality Mitigation:** To prevent information leakage (routers needing future token information for top-𝑘 selection), the authors use an "auxiliary loss" that applies binary cross-entropy to the main router. This helps the router learn to push selected token scores towards 1 and unselected towards 0 during training, making its inference-time decisions reliable without future context.
 - **Load Balancing:** This strategy naturally ensures a static compute budget by capping the number of tokens processed at each depth.
- **Token-choice Routing:**
 - **Mechanism:** Each token's recursion depth is determined *once* at the beginning (after the first non-shared layer). A router assigns each token to one of 𝑁𝑟 recursion depths (experts) via top-1 gating.
 - **Implementation:** The router computes scores for each possible recursion depth, and the token is assigned to the depth with the highest score.
 - **Load Balancing Mitigation:** To prevent experts from being under- or overloaded, techniques like "balancing loss" (regularizing for uniform token distribution across experts) or "loss-free" router biasing (dynamically adjusting per-expert bias terms) are employed.

**KV Caching Strategies:** MoR proposes two strategies to optimize memory usage related to Key-Value (KV) caches:
- **Recursion-wise KV Caching:**
 - **Mechanism:** KV pairs are cached selectively. Only tokens that are routed to a specific recursion step store their KV entries at that level. Attention operations are then restricted to these locally cached tokens.
 - **Benefits:** Reduces KV cache size and memory I/O by only storing what's actively needed at each recursion depth.
- **Recursive KV Sharing:**
 - **Mechanism:** KV pairs are computed and cached *only once* at the initial (first) recursion step and then reused across all subsequent recursion steps.
 - **Benefits:** Maximizes memory savings and eliminates recomputation/prefill at deeper recursive steps.
 - **Trade-offs:** Can introduce distribution mismatches as deeper recursions use KV representations from the first step, potentially impacting performance if routing is highly precise.

#### 3.3. Training and Evaluation

- **Model Architecture:** Models are pretrained from scratch using a Llama-based Transformer architecture, specifically leveraging configurations from open-source SmolLM models.
- **Dataset:** Deduplicated subset of the FineWeb-Edu dataset (220 billion tokens).
- **Training Infrastructure:** Four H100 or A100 GPUs.
- **Learning Rate Schedule:** Trapezoid schedule for isoFLOPs analysis, cosine annealing for other experiments.
- **Evaluation:**
 - **Validation Loss:** Negative log-likelihood (NLL) on the FineWeb-Edu validation set.
 - **Few-shot Accuracy:** Evaluated on six benchmarks: LAMBADA (LD), HellaSwag (HS), PIQA (PQ), WinoGrande (WG), ARC (Easy and Challenge), and MMLU.
 - **Inference Throughput:** Measured using a continuous depth-wise batching inference system, comparing tokens/second under fixed and maximum batch sizes.

This comprehensive methodology allows MoR to systematically combine parameter sharing, adaptive computation, and KV cache optimization within a unified framework, addressing the efficiency challenges of modern LLMs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on MoR models, ranging from 135M to 1.7B parameters, demonstrate significant improvements across various metrics compared to vanilla Transformers and existing recursive baselines.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance and Efficiency

- **New Pareto Frontier:** MoR consistently establishes a new Pareto frontier for LLM efficiency.
 - **Equal Training FLOPs (16.5e18 FLOPs):** An MoR model with an expert-choice router and two recursions (167M parameters) achieves a lower validation NLL (2.7511) and higher average few-shot accuracy (43.1%) than the Vanilla Transformer (315M parameters, NLL 2.7824, accuracy 42.3%). This is achieved despite using nearly **50% fewer parameters**.
 - **Equal Training Data (20B tokens):** MoR (167M parameters, 𝑁𝑟=2) processes the same amount of data with **25% fewer training FLOPs** than the vanilla baseline, leading to a 19% reduction in training time and a 25% cut in peak memory usage. This translates to lower validation loss and higher accuracy.
- **Scalability:** MoR consistently outperforms recursive baselines across all model sizes (135M, 360M, 730M, 1.7B parameters) and compute budgets (2e18, 5e18, 16.5e18 FLOPs). For models larger than 360M parameters, MoR not only matches but often exceeds the performance of Vanilla Transformers, particularly under low and mid-range compute budgets, while using significantly fewer parameters (approximately one-third for 𝑁𝑟=3).

#### 4.2. Impact of Routing and Caching Strategies

- **Routing Strategy Comparison:** Expert-choice routing generally yields superior performance (e.g., 42.6% avg. accuracy for MoR 𝑁𝑟=3 with expert-choice) compared to token-choice routing (40.0% avg. accuracy for MoR 𝑁𝑟=3 with token-choice). This indicates that the fine-grained, adaptive token selection at each recursion step in expert-choice routing is more effective.
- **KV Caching Strategy Comparison:** Recursion-wise KV caching typically performs better than recursive KV sharing for expert-choice routing under fixed token settings. This suggests that allowing attention to use KV pairs specific to the current recursion depth (even with a smaller cache) is more beneficial for model quality. However, recursive sharing offers maximal memory savings and could be advantageous for token-choice routing by providing full context to compensate for potentially weaker routing decisions.

#### 4.3. Inference Throughput Evaluation

- **Significant Speedups:** MoR models, when combined with continuous depth-wise batching, dramatically boost inference throughput. All MoR variants outperform the vanilla baseline, which uses continuous sequence-wise batching.
- **Depth-dependent Gains:** Increasing recursion depth in MoR leads to more tokens exiting early and further reduces KV cache usage, directly translating to higher throughput. For instance, MoR-4 achieves up to a **2.06× speedup** over the vanilla baseline. While there might be a slight performance degradation at higher recursion depths (due to fewer unique parameters), the substantial throughput gain often makes it a favorable trade-off for practical deployment.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Parameter Sharing:** "Middle-Cycle" strategy consistently delivers the lowest validation NLL and highest average few-shot accuracy among all tested parameter-sharing strategies (Cycle, Sequence, Middle-Cycle, Middle-Sequence), proving its effectiveness and thus being adopted as the default for MoR.
- **Expert-choice Routing Design:** Using an **auxiliary loss** (to enforce binary routing decisions) with a **sigmoid activation** function and a **simple linear router architecture** yields the best performance. This configuration results in near-perfect separation of router outputs for selected and unselected tokens.
- **Token-choice Routing Design:** Employing a **balancing loss** (with a softmax activation and MLP router) is crucial for stable and accurate routing, significantly reducing load imbalance (MaxVio) across experts without compromising performance.
- **KV Sharing Robustness:** KV sharing is found to be relatively robust in Recursive Transformers, with shared parameter blocks exhibiting highly consistent KV magnitude patterns and high cosine similarity, justifying its use with only a slight performance drop.

#### 4.5. Compute-optimal and Test-time Scaling Analysis

- **Compute-Optimal Scaling:** MoR exhibits a distinct compute-optimal scaling behavior, favoring increases in model capacity (parameter count) over extending training length when operating under isoFLOPs constraints. This implies MoR is "less data-hungry" and benefits more from larger base shared blocks.
- **Adaptive Routing Behavior:** The learned router effectively allocates recursion depth based on token predictability. Simpler tokens (e.g., common word endings, punctuation closings) require fewer recursion steps, while more complex or semantically significant tokens require deeper processing.
- **Test-time Scaling:** MoR enables test-time scaling, where increasing the maximum allowed recursion depth during inference leads to improved log-likelihood. This demonstrates that deeper recursion allows for further refinement of token representations and "thought processes," enhancing generation quality.

In summary, MoR successfully unifies critical efficiency paradigms, delivering state-of-the-art performance with substantial reductions in computational and memory overhead, making large language models more accessible and efficient.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Mixture-of-Recursions" (MoR) framework presents a highly significant advancement in the development of efficient large language models, offering profound potential impacts across research and practical deployment.

#### 5.1. Bridging the Efficiency Gap for LLMs

The most direct and significant impact of MoR is its success in unifying parameter efficiency, adaptive computation, and efficient KV caching within a single, coherent architecture. This addresses a critical unmet need in the LLM landscape, where impressive capabilities have been coupled with prohibitive resource demands. By demonstrating that large-model quality can be achieved with significantly fewer unique parameters and FLOPs, MoR democratizes access to advanced LLM technology, making it viable for a broader range of researchers and organizations.

#### 5.2. Economic and Environmental Benefits

The substantial reductions in training FLOPs (25% less for equal data) and memory usage (25% less peak memory), coupled with significant inference throughput gains (up to 2.06× speedup), have considerable economic and environmental implications. Lower FLOPs translate directly to reduced energy consumption and carbon emissions during training, aligning with growing concerns about the environmental footprint of AI. For deployment, increased throughput and lower memory footprint mean reduced operational costs, enabling more users to be served per GPU, or allowing larger batch sizes for higher efficiency.

#### 5.3. Enhanced Adaptive Latent Reasoning

MoR's ability to dynamically adjust recursion depth on a per-token basis fundamentally advances the concept of "latent reasoning." Instead of fixed-depth internal deliberation, MoR models can "think" deeper or shallower as needed, optimizing the internal thought process based on the complexity of each token. This adaptive reasoning could lead to:
- **More Efficient Reasoning:** Allocating computational resources more intelligently for complex reasoning tasks.
- **Improved Interpretability:** The varying recursion depths might offer insights into which parts of an input require more cognitive effort from the model.
- **Foundational for Future AI:** This adaptive vertical thinking complements existing work on generating continuous thoughts along the sequence axis, laying groundwork for more sophisticated and efficient internal reasoning mechanisms in future AI systems.

#### 5.4. Practical Deployment Advantages

The improvements in inference throughput, driven by continuous depth-wise batching and reduced KV cache sizes, make MoR highly attractive for real-world deployment scenarios. For applications requiring low-latency responses or processing long input sequences, MoR offers a tangible competitive advantage. Its end-to-end trainability for routers further simplifies the development pipeline, avoiding complex post-hoc optimization stages.

#### 5.5. Future Research Directions

The work opens several exciting avenues for future research:
- **Reasoning MoR Models:** Applying MoR to actual reasoning datasets to dynamically adjust recursion depth based on the complexity of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) chains could significantly enhance reasoning accuracy and efficiency.
- **Scaling to Larger Models:** Validating MoR's performance at even larger scales (beyond 1.7B parameters) is crucial, potentially by combining it with techniques like depth-specific LoRA or Mixture-of-Experts to further boost capacity and efficiency.
- **Adaptive Capacity Control:** Developing more sophisticated routing mechanisms that allow for flexible adjustment of top-k values during inference without performance degradation is an important next step.
- **Compatibility with Sparse Algorithms:** Integrating MoR with structured sparsity techniques (e.g., pruning, quantization) could lead to even greater efficiency gains, dynamically optimizing computation at token, layer, and parameter levels.
- **Multimodal Expansion:** The modality-agnostic nature of the recursion block suggests MoR's applicability to vision, speech, and unified multimodal transformers, promising memory efficiencies and throughput gains for processing long multimodal sequences.

In conclusion, Mixture-of-Recursions is a seminal contribution that effectively tackles the escalating costs of LLMs by harmoniously integrating multiple efficiency paradigms. Its impact will likely be felt in more sustainable AI development, broader accessibility of advanced models, and a new generation of adaptively intelligent systems.
