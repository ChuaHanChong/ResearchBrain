# Vision-Language Procedural Reasoning for Context-Aware Reward Modeling of Robotic Endovascular Guidewire Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Tongji University, The Chinese University of Hong Kong, and Shanghai Operation Robot Co., Ltd. developed a Vision-Language Procedural Reasoning (VL-PR) framework for autonomous endovascular guidewire navigation. This system utilizes a Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM) to infer real-time navigation contexts, dynamically adjusting a reinforcement learning agent's reward function, which led to 100% success rates in coronary and renal interventions on a physical phantom and reduced navigation steps by 48.4% in coronary scenarios.

## Problem

- Endovascular interventions are technically demanding due to complex vascular anatomies and limited real-time feedback, requiring a substantial learning curve for clinicians.
- Existing robotic systems for these procedures often function in leader-follower modes, requiring continuous manual intervention and limiting procedural standardization and scalability.
- Current reinforcement learning (RL) methods for autonomous navigation rely on static reward functions, failing to adapt to the heterogeneous, phase-dependent demands of real-world endovascular procedures.

## Method

- A **Vision-Language Procedural Reasoning (VL-PR)** framework was developed, integrating a ==Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM)== with a ==reinforcement learning (RL)== agent.
- The MLLM acts as a ==procedural reasoning module==, interpreting multimodal observations (visuals, guidewire pose, vessel geometry) to infer high-level navigation contexts (e.g., 'cruise', 'bifurcation', 'retraction').
- These inferred procedural insights from the MLLM dynamically adjust the weights of a ==multi-component reward function== for the RL agent, enabling ==context-aware reward adaptation== for a single, unified control policy trained with ==Soft Actor-Critic (SAC)==.

## Results

- The MLLM achieved high accuracy in classifying procedural states (e.g., **92.3%** for 'Cruise', **91.2%** for 'Bifurcation', **94.0%** for 'Final align'), significantly outperforming a rule-based baseline.
- The VL-PR framework achieved **100%** success rates in coronary (**30/30** trials) and renal (**30/30** trials) interventions and **97%** in carotid interventions (**29/30** trials) on a physical robotic platform, matching or exceeding expert manual operation and outperforming all baseline RL algorithms.
- It demonstrated enhanced navigational efficiency, reducing steps by **48.4%** in coronary interventions (**41.6 ± 2.1** steps) compared to standard SAC (**80.6 ± 3.5** steps), and showed overall stronger robustness across diverse vascular scenarios.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating high-level semantic understanding from MLLMs into the RL reward mechanism, rather than for direct action generation, provides crucial contextual awareness for complex long-horizon tasks.
> - Dynamically adapting reward functions based on the inferred procedural context allows a single RL policy to effectively balance competing objectives (e.g., progress vs. safety) across different navigation phases.
> - The framework effectively bridges high-level procedural reasoning with low-level motion control, leading to more robust and efficient robotic endovascular navigation than static reward approaches.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Endovascular interventions are a common therapeutic approach for cardiovascular and cerebrovascular diseases, involving the precise navigation of guidewires and catheters through complex and often tortuous vascular anatomies. Despite their clinical effectiveness, these procedures are technically demanding due to the intricate vessel geometries and limited real-time visual feedback. This complexity contributes to a substantial learning curve for clinicians and a scarcity of experienced operators.

Robotic platforms have emerged as a technological advancement designed to enhance manipulative dexterity, improve procedural stability, and mitigate operator fatigue and radiation exposure. However, many existing robotic systems primarily function in leader-follower architectures, requiring continuous low-level manual intervention from the clinician. This dependency limits the potential for procedural standardization and scalability, particularly in highly complex anatomical scenarios.

The broader research landscape in medical robotics is increasingly focused on achieving task-level autonomy, moving beyond teleoperation to automate discrete segments of surgical procedures. Reinforcement learning (RL) has been identified as a promising framework for this goal, capable of mapping high-dimensional sensory inputs to precise actuation commands for steering flexible instruments. However, a limitation of many current RL-based methods for autonomous endovascular navigation is their reliance on single-policy architectures optimized against static reward functions throughout the entire procedure. Such static reward structures often fail to address the heterogeneous and phase-dependent demands inherent in real-world navigation. For instance, different control priorities are required during rapid progression in straight vessel segments compared to careful steering at bifurcations. While adaptive or phase-aware learning strategies have been explored in other surgical domains (e.g., surgical gesture segmentation), their application to procedural reward adaptation in autonomous endovascular guidewire navigation remains underexplored. The absence of mechanisms to dynamically shift optimization priorities while maintaining a unified task goal limits the robustness and efficiency of these systems in long-horizon tasks.

Against this background, Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) offer a framework for procedural reasoning by integrating visual observations with linguistic domain knowledge. MLLMs have been applied successfully in various surgical robotics contexts, including perception, planning, and decision support (e.g., surgical action planning and language-guided assistance). Endovascular intervention presents a suitable environment for vision-language procedural reasoning due to its long task durations, diverse anatomical regions, and context-dependent safety requirements. The need for a high-level understanding of anatomy and interaction risks to recover from operational difficulties further motivates the use of MLLMs to identify navigation contexts. This integration of MLLM-driven procedural reasoning with RL is a step towards enabling context-aware reward adaptation, potentially leading to more robust and intelligent navigation of flexible instruments. This paper seeks to bridge the gap between high-level semantic understanding and low-level motion control in autonomous endovascular navigation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are to address the limitations of existing autonomous guidewire navigation methods, specifically their reliance on static reward functions and the lack of explicit procedural reasoning, which hinder context-awareness and adaptability.

The key objectives and their underlying motivations are as follows:

1. **Develop a Vision-Language Procedural Reasoning (VL-PR) Framework:**
 - **Objective:** Establish a framework that integrates a Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM) as a procedural reasoning module. This module is designed to interpret real-time visual observations and infer high-level navigation contexts during robotic endovascular guidewire navigation.
 - **Motivation:** Current autonomous navigation methods often operate without explicit high-level contextual understanding of the task progression or the anatomical environment. This lack of procedural reasoning leads to policies that struggle with the dynamic and varied demands of real-world endovascular procedures, where distinct operational modes are required across different anatomical phases (e.g., straight vessel segments versus bifurcations). The MLLM aims to provide this crucial high-level semantic understanding, mirroring the contextual awareness of an experienced clinician.

2. **Enable Context-Aware Reward Adaptation:**
 - **Objective:** Utilize the inferred procedural insights from the MLLM to dynamically adjust the importance of different reward components. This approach enables context-aware reward adaptation, rather than generating low-level control commands directly from the MLLM.
 - **Motivation:** Existing RL-based autonomous navigation systems often rely on static reward functions that apply uniformly across the entire procedural continuum. Such static formulations fail to account for the heterogeneous and phase-dependent demands of endovascular navigation, where competing objectives (e.g., rapid progress versus safety at critical junctions) must be prioritized differently. Dynamically adapting the reward function allows a single policy to resolve these trade-offs and handle complex transitions more effectively, while still working towards a consistent global task goal. This aims to improve convergence efficiency and learning stability in long-duration, multi-stage tasks.

3. **Enhance Task Reliability and Navigational Efficiency:**
 - **Objective:** Demonstrate that the proposed VL-PR framework, by enabling context-aware reward adaptation, leads to enhanced task reliability and streamlined navigational efficiency on a physical robotic platform across diverse vascular scenarios.
 - **Motivation:** The limitations of static-reward methods result in suboptimal performance, including lower success rates and less efficient navigation trajectories. By incorporating high-level contextual reasoning, the research intends to improve the robustness of the navigation policy, reduce the number of steps required to reach targets, and increase the overall success rate of complex endovascular procedures. This directly addresses the need for scalable and reliable solutions for advanced robotic endovascular interventions.

In summary, the core motivation is to overcome the limitations of current autonomous endovascular navigation by introducing intelligent procedural reasoning to guide reinforcement learning, thereby creating a more adaptive, robust, and efficient robotic system that can emulate the context-aware decision-making of expert interventionalists.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research proposes a Vision-Language Procedural Reasoning (VL-PR) framework for autonomous guidewire navigation. This framework integrates a Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM) with a reinforcement learning (RL) agent, where the MLLM provides high-level contextual understanding to modulate the RL reward function.

**A. Problem Formulation and Modeling**
Autonomous guidewire navigation is formalized as a sequential decision-making problem within a Markov Decision Process (MDP) tuple `M = <S, A, P, r, γ>`.
- **State (s_t):** At each time step `t`, the state `s_t` comprises `s_env_t` (local vascular environment, e.g., geometry) and `s_pose_t` (guidewire tip pose).
- **Action (a_t):** A continuous control action `a_t` is selected, representing translational and rotational actuation of the guidewire.
- **Objective:** The goal is to optimize a policy `π_θ(a_t | s_t)` that maximizes the expected cumulative return, `J(π_θ)`, derived from a reward function `r`.
The reward function `r` is designed to incorporate multiple, potentially competing objectives, such as progress towards the target, maintenance of safe vessel wall clearance, and guidewire motion stabilization. The need to balance these objectives, which vary in priority across different navigation phases, motivates the use of procedural reasoning and context-aware reward adaptation.

**B. Procedural Reasoning via Multimodal Large Language Model**
A task-specific procedural reasoning module, based on an MLLM, is introduced to provide high-level contextual understanding.
- **Multimodal Observation (o_t):** At each context-update step, the MLLM receives `o_t = {I_t, s_pose_t, g_goal_t, s_geo_t}`. This includes local vascular observations (`I_t`), guidewire tip configuration (`s_pose_t`), intervention target (`g_goal_t`), and local vessel geometry/topology cues (`s_geo_t`) like centerline direction, curvature, and bifurcation structures.
- **Procedural States (G):** A discrete procedural state space `G = {cruise, bifurcation, retraction, final_align}` is defined. Each state corresponds to a distinct navigation regime with specific control priorities. For example, 'retraction' indicates safety-aware withdrawal behavior.
- **MLLM Processing:** The MLLM processes `o_t` to produce a latent representation `z_t`. A prediction head then computes a probability distribution `p_ψ(g_t | o_t)` over the discrete procedural states using a Softmax function.
- **MLLM Training:** The reasoning module is adapted using a dataset of labeled pairs `(o_t, g*_t)`, where `g*_t` are expert-defined and operator-verified procedural labels. The training objective `L_MLLM` combines three loss terms:
 - **Categorical Cross-Entropy Loss (L_proc):** Supervises the prediction of `g*_t`.
 - **Temporal Consistency Regularization (L_temp):** Encourages coherent predictions over time, minimizing `∥z_(t+1) - z_t∥^2`.
 - **Entropy Regularization (L_ent):** Prevents overconfident predictions in ambiguous regions.
- **Role in RL:** During RL and real-world deployment, the MLLM's parameters are fixed. It does not generate direct control actions but instead provides inferred procedural insights `p_ψ(g_t | o_t)`, which modulate the policy through context-aware reward adaptation.

**C. Context-Aware Reward Adaptation and Reinforcement Learning**
The MLLM's inferred procedural context is integrated into the RL framework by modulating the optimization objective, while maintaining a single, unified control policy.
- **Reward Components (Φ):** The instantaneous reward feedback is decomposed into four components, represented as a 2x2 matrix `Φ(s_t, a_t, s_(t+1))`:
 - `ϕ_prog`: Centerline progress (positive).
 - `ϕ_safe`: Vessel wall contact safety (negative penalty for `τ_t`, accumulated contact time).
 - `ϕ_dev`: Deviation from the vessel centerline (negative).
 - `ϕ_bend`: Guidewire bending stability (negative penalty for `κ^2_t`, local curvature).
- **State-Dependent Weight Matrix (W_g):** A 2x2 weight matrix `W_g(ξ_t)` dynamically adjusts the importance of `Φ` components based on the current procedural state `g ∈ G` and a scalar risk indicator `ξ_t`. `ξ_t` is a risk measure derived from accumulated wall contact, centerline deviation, and guidewire curvature.
- **Weight Adaptation for Specific States:**
 - **Cruise:** Emphasizes efficient advancement while increasing safety penalties with risk.
 - **Bifurcation:** Prioritizes geometric alignment and steerability, with increasing penalties for deviation and bending as risk grows.
 - **Retraction:** Suppresses progress reward to facilitate withdrawal actions, emphasizing safety and configuration restoration.
 - **Final Align:** Shifts priority towards geometric precision and stable positioning near the target.
- **Context-Aware Reward Calculation (r_t):** The final reward `r_t` is computed as a weighted sum of the Frobenius inner products of `W_g(ξ_t)` and `Φ(s_t, a_t, s_(t+1))`, where the weights are given by the MLLM's predicted procedural distribution `p_ψ(g_t | o_t)`.
- **Policy Learning:** Policy optimization is performed using the Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) algorithm, a maximum-entropy RL framework, with `r_t` as the reward signal.

**D. Experimental Setup and Implementation**
- **Physical Platform:** ALLVAS™ robotic system (Shanghai Operation Robot Co., Ltd.) with dual coordinated arms and adaptive grippers, on an anthropomorphic vascular phantom (coronary, carotid, renal anatomies) with emulated physiological flow. An overhead camera captures images mimicking fluoroscopy.
- **Simulation Environment:** A high-fidelity, physics-based simulation based on the SOFA framework, serving as a digital twin for policy training.
- **MLLM Details:** Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct backbone, fine-tuned via 4-bit QLoRA on a dataset of vascular observations, guidewire tip configurations, vessel geometry, and expert-derived semantic labels. MLLM parameters are frozen during RL training.
- **RL Details:** SAC framework with standard hyperparameters. A success-biased replay strategy is employed to mitigate reward sparsity.
- **Perception Pipeline:** For real-world deployment, a visual perception pipeline processes monocular camera frames (simulating X-ray). YOLOv5 detects guidewire tip and target, and a U-Net segments the guidewire geometry to provide real-time state observations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental validation was conducted on a physical robotic platform across diverse vascular phantom scenarios (coronary, carotid, renal) and quantitatively compared the proposed VL-PR framework against established continuous-control RL baselines (PPO, DDPG, TD3, standard SAC) and expert manual operation.

**A. Procedural State Classification Accuracy**
The MLLM's ability to classify procedural states was evaluated against a rule-based baseline.
- **MLLM Performance:** The MLLM demonstrated high accuracy in classifying procedural states: 92.3% for 'Cruise', 91.2% for 'Bifurcation', 93.1% for 'Retraction', and 94.0% for 'Final align'.
- **Comparison to Baseline:** This significantly outperformed the rule-based baseline, which achieved classification accuracies ranging from 22.0% to 27.7% across the states. This indicates the MLLM's effectiveness in providing accurate high-level contextual understanding from multimodal observations.

**B. Cross-Anatomy Evaluation and Procedural Navigation Performance**
The core performance of the VL-PR framework in autonomous guidewire navigation was assessed based on success rate and navigational efficiency (number of steps).
- **Enhanced Success Rate:** The proposed VL-PR framework consistently achieved higher success rates across all tested vascular regions compared to all baseline RL algorithms.
 - **Coronary Intervention:** 100% success rate (30/30 trials), significantly higher than standard SAC (70%, 21/30) and other baselines.
 - **Renal Intervention:** 100% success rate (30/30 trials), compared to standard SAC (73%, 22/30).
 - **Carotid Intervention:** 97% success rate (29/30 trials), matching expert manual operation (97%, 29/30) and outperforming all RL baselines (e.g., standard SAC at 73%, 22/30).
- **Streamlined Navigational Efficiency:** The VL-PR framework also demonstrated improved efficiency, requiring fewer navigation steps to complete the tasks.
 - **Coronary Intervention:** Averaged 41.6 ± 2.1 steps, a 48.4% reduction compared to standard SAC (80.6 ± 3.5 steps).
 - **Carotid Intervention:** Averaged 49.3 ± 2.4 steps, comparable to standard SAC (46.4 ± 3.0 steps) but with a much higher success rate.
 - **Renal Intervention:** Averaged 30.8 ± 1.8 steps, an improvement over standard SAC (33.2 ± 2.6 steps).
- **Ablation Study (w/o MLLM):** An ablation study confirmed the contribution of the procedural reasoning module. When the MLLM-based reasoning module was removed (i.e., using a static reward function as in conventional RL), performance declined notably:
 - Success rates decreased to a range of 73% to 77% across the tasks.
 - The number of navigation steps increased significantly (e.g., 78.5 ± 3.6 steps for Coronary intervention compared to 41.6 ± 2.1 with MLLM).
This finding indicates that context-aware reward adaptation is critical for resolving competing objectives and enabling reliable navigation in complex branching vascular structures.

**C. Robustness:** The results suggest that the reasoning-guided policy generates more direct navigation behaviors and maintains stronger robustness compared to conventional RL policies, particularly in challenging scenarios like the Carotid artery intervention.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework that integrates high-level procedural reasoning with reinforcement learning for autonomous endovascular guidewire navigation, addressing several limitations of existing approaches and offering multiple areas of potential impact.

**A. Addressing Core Limitations in Autonomous Navigation:**
The primary significance lies in explicitly tackling the challenges posed by static reward functions and the absence of explicit procedural reasoning in autonomous robotic endovascular interventions. By introducing a Vision-Language Procedural Reasoning (VL-PR) framework, the paper demonstrates a method to incorporate high-level contextual understanding into the control loop. This allows the system to differentiate between various navigation phases (e.g., cruising, bifurcation, retraction) and dynamically adjust its control priorities accordingly.

**B. Enhanced Performance and Reliability:**
The quantitative results demonstrate substantial improvements in task reliability and navigational efficiency. Achieving 100% success rates in coronary and renal interventions and 97% in carotid interventions—matching or exceeding expert manual operation—highlights the framework's robustness. The significant reduction in navigation steps compared to conventional RL methods implies more direct and efficient control, which could translate to shorter procedure times and reduced cumulative risk in clinical settings. The ablation study further corroborates that the context-aware reward adaptation, enabled by procedural reasoning, is critical for this performance enhancement.

**C. Advancing Robotic Autonomy in Endovascular Procedures:**
This work contributes to the broader goal of achieving task-level autonomy in robotic-assisted surgery. By enabling a single policy to adapt its optimization focus across different navigation phases while maintaining a consistent global task goal, the framework moves beyond teleoperation or discrete phase-specific policies. This approach is more scalable and adaptable to the inherent variability and complexity of patient-specific anatomies and multi-task procedures.

**D. Improved Safety and Precision:**
The dynamic adaptation of reward components based on inferred procedural context allows for a more nuanced balance between objectives like traversal efficiency and safety-critical precision. For example, prioritizing safety at bifurcations or enabling corrective retraction actions under risk demonstrates a more intelligent and clinically aligned approach to guidewire manipulation, potentially reducing the incidence of vessel damage or complications.

**E. Bridge Between Perception, Cognition, and Control:**
The integration of an MLLM to synthesize visual and linguistic domain knowledge for procedural reasoning represents a step towards bridging the gap between high-level cognitive understanding and low-level motion control in medical robotics. This allows the robotic system to interpret complex real-time scenarios in a manner conceptually similar to human clinicians, influencing its decision-making and actions.

**F. Foundation for Future Research and Clinical Translation:**
The framework establishes a foundation for several future research directions. Extending procedural reasoning to richer structured representations, improving sim-to-real generalization, integrating formal safety constraints, and evaluating in more clinically realistic scenarios (e.g., patient-specific anatomies, multi-instrument coordination) are outlined as next steps. Successful progression in these areas could ultimately lead to the clinical translation of more trustworthy and fully autonomous endovascular robotic interventions, potentially democratizing access to complex procedures, standardizing care, and improving patient outcomes.
