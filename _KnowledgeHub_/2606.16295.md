# VisualClaw: A Real-Time, Personalized Agent for the Physical World

> [!summary] Summary
> VisualClaw is a multimodal agent framework that utilizes an edge-side cascaded encoding gate for efficient video processing and a self-evolving skill and memory bank for continual adaptation. It introduces VisualClawArena, a new benchmark for evaluating agents in tool-using, workspace-centric scenarios, demonstrating improved accuracy while significantly reducing VLM API costs by an average of 98.1% compared to full-frame video input.

## Problem

- High latency and computational cost associated with processing dense video streams and lengthy prompts in Vision Language Models (VLMs) hinder real-time deployment on edge devices.
- VLM-based agents operate with static scaffolds, lacking mechanisms for continual adaptation, personalization, and learning from failures.
- Existing video-QA benchmarks are insufficient for evaluating agents in dynamic, interactive, tool-using, and workspace-centric tasks that require visual evidence and executable actions.

## Method

- The **VisualClaw** framework integrates a per-frame ==Cascaded Encoding Gate== (G) for real-time video filtering at the edge, reducing data sent to cloud VLMs.
- A per-question Skill Bank with hybrid hot/cold injection manages agent prompts efficiently, minimizing token costs, while per-session Memory-Augmented Meta-Evolution enables self-adaptive learning from failures.
- VisualClawArena, a new multimodal agentic benchmark, was curated to simulate real-world computer-use scenarios requiring visual grounding, tool use, and persistent workspace state for comprehensive evaluation.

## Results

- VisualClaw reduced VLM API costs by an average of **98.1%** (up to **99.3%**) compared to full-frame video, while achieving an average **+3.85%** accuracy boost over the Plain baseline on static video-QA tasks.
- The self-evolution stack consistently improved performance on the VisualClawArena benchmark, yielding +**2.92** points over the baseline for Codex and +**3.16** points for Claude Code, with more pronounced benefits on Medium and Hard scenarios.
- The Cascaded Encoding Gate (cascade-fill) outperformed uniform frame selection at a matched budget on short-clip benchmarks (e.g., **+1.80%** on NextQA), indicating the importance of scene-change keyframes.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Real-time, efficient multimodal agent deployment on edge devices is feasible by intelligently filtering redundant video frames at the source and dynamically managing prompt context.
> - Agents can continually learn and adapt from their failures and experiences without retraining core VLM weights, through an external, self-evolving skill and memory bank.
> - A new evaluation paradigm is necessary to assess complex, interactive, and tool-using capabilities, as traditional video-QA benchmarks are inadequate for real-world agentic behavior.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research addresses critical deployment challenges for Vision Language Models (VLMs) as general-purpose interfaces for complex multimodal tasks. Within the existing landscape, VLMs have demonstrated proficiency in tasks like video question-answering (video-QA) benchmarks when provided with full visual information and long prompts. However, the paper identifies three significant gaps in their practical deployment: high latency and cost associated with processing dense video streams and lengthy prompts, the static nature of agent scaffolds post-deployment, and the inadequacy of standard video-QA benchmarks to assess agents in tool-using, workspace-centric scenarios that require visual evidence.

Current research efforts have predominantly tackled these issues in isolation. For efficiency, frame-selection systems aim to compress or subsample video inputs, yet typically assume offline access to full clips and do not address the escalating prompt-side costs from growing skill banks. In the realm of adaptation, skill libraries for Large Language Model (LLM) agents distill reusable behavioral rules from past failures, enhancing model accuracy without weight updates. These systems, however, are primarily text-only, leading to token bottlenecks when expanded to multimodal contexts. Memory systems for agents also exist, such as MemGPT and Generative Agents, but often do not integrate with multimodal learning or skill evolution. For evaluation, existing video-QA benchmarks focus on one-shot answering, failing to capture an agent's ability to operate within dynamic workspaces, reconcile multimodal evidence, or perform executable actions.

VisualClaw positions itself by integrating solutions for all three gaps. It introduces an edge-side, online, CPU-only cascade for efficient video processing, a self-evolving multimodal skill and memory bank for adaptive learning, and a novel benchmark, VisualClawArena, designed for agentic evaluation in tool-using environments. This work builds upon concepts from related projects like VisionClaw by adding capabilities for self-evolution, prompt-cost management, and comprehensive agentic evaluation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are to overcome the aforementioned deployment challenges for Vision Language Models (VLMs) and to enable their effective use as real-time, personalized agents in physical world applications. These objectives are driven by three core motivations:

**a. Addressing High Latency and Cost in Multimodal Processing:**
- **Motivation:** Contemporary VLMs typically incur significant latency and computational costs when processing dense streams of video frames and lengthy prompts. This is particularly problematic for real-time applications such as wearable AI glasses or cloud assistants, where continuous, instantaneous processing of visual input is required. Full-frame video upload is economically and computationally prohibitive for long sessions or edge devices with limited bandwidth and processing power.
- **Objective:** Develop a method to substantially reduce the data transfer volume and prompt token count, thereby cutting down API costs and latency, without sacrificing the quality of visual information critical for task completion. This involves intelligent filtering of redundant video frames and efficient management of the prompt context.

**b. Enabling Continual Adaptation and Personalization for Agents:**
- **Motivation:** Standard VLM-based agents operate with a static scaffold after deployment. They do not possess mechanisms to learn from their own failures or adapt to new, unforeseen scenarios and user-specific preferences over time. This limits their utility as personalized assistants that can improve with experience. Updating VLM weights is often infeasible due to computational cost and the proprietary nature of frontier models.
- **Objective:** Introduce a self-evolving framework that allows the agent to continually learn and refine its skills and memories from accumulated experiences, especially from failures. This adaptation should occur without requiring updates to the underlying VLM's frozen weights, making it practical for deployment. The framework should facilitate personalization and maintain a manageable skill bank size.

**c. Bridging the Gap in Multimodal Agentic Evaluation:**
- **Motivation:** Existing video-QA benchmarks primarily evaluate one-shot question-answering capabilities and do not adequately test whether an agent can effectively use visual evidence within interactive, tool-using workspaces. Real-world tasks often involve inspecting files, reconciling conflicting information from video and text, editing workspace artifacts, and passing executable checks, none of which are typically assessed by current benchmarks.
- **Objective:** Curate a novel, comprehensive multimodal agentic benchmark that simulates real-world computer-use scenarios. This benchmark, VisualClawArena, aims to validate an agent's ability to integrate visual perception with language understanding, memory, and tool-use in dynamic, multi-step tasks requiring executable checks and persistent workspace state.

Through these objectives, the research aims to pave the way for more efficient, adaptive, and capable multimodal AI agents that can function effectively in diverse real-world environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The VisualClaw framework is designed as a self-evolving multimodal agent, built upon two core principles: hybrid encoding for efficient edge deployment and self-evolving skills and memories for adaptive agents. Additionally, the paper introduces a new benchmark, VisualClawArena, to evaluate these capabilities in agentic tasks.

**a. VisualClaw Framework for Efficient and Adaptive Deployment:**
VisualClaw operates as a three-timescale system, integrating components for visual encoding, skill injection, and continuous evolution.

- **Per-frame: Cascaded Encoding Gate (G)**
 - This component operates on the edge, filtering streaming video frames in real-time to reduce the data sent to the cloud VLM API. It makes a per-frame decision (MAJOR, MINOR, SKIP) without needing future frames.
 - **Perceptual Hash (dHash):** The first stage performs Hamming-distance based deduplication against a rolling buffer to remove bit-exact or near-exact duplicates, addressing camera shake or stationary periods.
 - **Lightweight CPU Encoder:** Frames that pass the dHash stage are processed by a 128-dimensional CPU-only encoder that computes a scene vector based on HSV histogram, luminance, edge density, and texture. This avoids heavy deep network or GPU computation on the edge.
 - **Adaptive Change Gate:** The encoded frame is compared against a rolling reference. An adaptive change gate emits a MAJOR verdict if the change crosses a high threshold (frame forwarded to VLM), a MINOR verdict for a lower threshold (updates rolling reference but not forwarded), or a SKIP verdict if below both. Temporally-decaying thresholds ensure sensitivity to both rapid and slow scene changes.

- **Per-question: Skill Bank with Hybrid Hot/Cold Injection**
 - This mechanism manages the agent's textual skill bank (S) efficiently to minimize prompt costs.
 - **Skill Structure:** Each skill `s_j` is a markdown card containing a name, a one-line description (for retrieval), a procedural body, and an anti-pattern section. The bank is initialized with `K_seed` cross-cutting visual-reasoning patterns.
 - **Hybrid Injection:** For each incoming question `q`, a sentence-transformer embedding ranks the entire skill bank `S` against `q`. The `top-k` most relevant skills (`S_hot`) have their full text inlined into the system prompt. The remaining skills (`S_cold`) are exposed as a compact catalogue of names and descriptions, with their full bodies fetchable on demand if the VLM determines a need. This approach bounds the per-question prompt cost by `k` rather than the growing size of `|S|`.

- **Per-session: Memory-Augmented Meta-Evolution**
 - This component enables the agent to learn from failures and update its skill bank over time.
 - **Memory Store (M_v):** Correctly-solved examples are stored as dense embeddings in `M_v`, and only high-confidence matches are retrieved.
 - **LLM Evolver (E):** When a predefined number of failures (`N_evo`) accumulate, an offline LLM evolver `E` (e.g., Claude Haiku) analyzes these failures along with relevant memories retrieved from `M_v` to propose new skills (`∆S`).
 - **Memory-to-Evolver Channels:** Two variants are explored for feeding retrieved memory (`R`) into the evolver:
 - **Concatenation (Cat.):** `R` is directly appended as auxiliary context to the standard skill-evolution prompt `P_0(S, D_fail)`.
 - **Guided (Guide):** A lightweight prefix `P_guide(R)` is added, instructing the evolver to specifically abstract reusable skills from the memory and avoid scenario-specific details, thereby conditioning `P_0(S, D_fail)`.
 - **Bank Hygiene Filters:** To maintain skill bank quality and prevent unbounded growth or redundancy:
 - **F1 (Token-Jaccard Dedup):** Rejects new skills in `∆S` if their names heavily overlap with existing skills in `S` (Jaccard similarity `≥ 0.5`).
 - **F2 (Per-skill Utility Tracker):** Periodically prunes skills whose accuracy or hit rate falls below the bank's average.

**b. VisualClawArena: A Multimodal Agentic Benchmark:**
To address the evaluation gap, VisualClawArena was curated to assess multimodal agents in tool-using, workspace-based tasks.

- **Benchmark Construction:**
 - **Sources:** Built from 200 scenarios using videos from existing datasets (100 Indoor/VSI, 50 EgoSchema, 50 QVHighlights).
 - **Workspace Components:** Each scenario includes a short video, background facts, documents (e.g., `AGENTS.md` for task goals, `IDENTITY.md` for agent role, `USER.md` for stakeholder request), chat/audio traces, dynamic updates, and executable checkers (`questions.json`, `check_*.py`).
 - **Timestamp-grounded Visual Facts:** Gemini 3.1 Pro is used to mark video timestamps supporting critical visual facts, ensuring questions are grounded in visible evidence.
 - **Multi-modal Tasks:** Scenarios involve multiple-choice visual checks and executable tasks (e.g., editing files, reconciling video/text contradictions).

- **Five-Stage Curation Pipeline:**
 1. **Candidate Generation and Workspace Construction:** Initial setup of scenarios and their associated workspaces.
 2. **Validation:** Checks for valid JSON schema, correct citation formats, explicit declaration of required evidence modalities (with at least 40% video-required steps), and successful execution of gold solutions.
 3. **Text-only Leakage Filtering:** Each step is tested with and without video. Steps solvable purely from text (where clip provides no additional benefit or even regresses performance) are flagged and repaired to ensure visual grounding.
 4. **Multimodal Criteria-based Selection:** Surviving candidates are ranked using a heuristic score that prioritizes visual-required steps, cases where the clip actively helps, penalizes text leakage, and rewards cross-modal steps.
 5. **Health Checks:** Final verification ensuring all video clips are present, scenarios have at least 15 valid steps, and all steps have a leakage label.

- **Bridge to Multimodal Agentic Backends:**
 - For evaluation, a clean computer workspace is initialized for each evolve step. Document/data updates are applied, keyframes staged as local image files, and retrieved skills/memory prepended to the agent prompt.
 - Agents (e.g., Codex, Claude Code) interact with the workspace, inspecting files and keyframes, editing outputs, and running lightweight checks.
 - Scoring happens externally, checking the final workspace state or parsing answers. Failures are routed back into the memory and skill-evolution loop.

This comprehensive methodology aims to create a robust and adaptive multimodal agent capable of operating efficiently in dynamic, real-world scenarios, evaluated against a realistic and challenging benchmark.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents detailed findings across both static video-QA benchmarks and the novel VisualClawArena, demonstrating the efficacy of VisualClaw's hybrid encoding and self-evolution mechanisms in terms of accuracy and efficiency.

**a. Accuracy and Analysis on Static Video-QA Tasks:**
- **Performance Gains:** VisualClaw, particularly the (Guide) variant (FullEvo with guided memory-to-evolver context), consistently improved accuracy. It achieved an average performance boost of +3.85% over the Plain baseline and a peak of +15.80% on EgoSchema using Gemini 3 Flash. Gemini 3 Flash showed significant lifts on egocentric benchmarks (EgoSchema +15.80%, EgoPlan-Bench +4.23%).
- **Component Ablation:** The initial skill bank (Seed) alone provided an average +6.10% lift for Gemini's Plain baseline, indicating its importance in addressing common VLM failure modes. While simple evolvers (+Evolve, +SkillMemCat, +Cat.) offered gains, the (Guide) variant proved most stable and effective, lifting even the stronger GPT-5.2 backbone by +1.27% on average.
- **Validation on Offline Baseline:** When FullEvo (Guide) was applied to the stronger Uniform-8 offline method, it delivered an additional +3.50% to +13.00% accuracy improvement, peaking at +13.0% on EgoSchema (from 60.6% to 73.6%). This suggests that the memory-driven skill evolution is beneficial regardless of the initial frame selection method.
- **Cascade-fill vs. Uniform-8:** At a matched K=8 frame budget, cascade-fill (cascade-selected keyframes padded with uniform fillers) outperformed pure uniform-8 on short-clip benchmarks (e.g., NextQA +1.80%, EgoPlan-Bench +3.58%). This indicates that scene-change keyframes selected by the cascade carry more salient information than evenly-spaced ones.
- **Guided Memory Evolution for Static QA:** The (Guide) variant generally surpassed the (Cat.) variant in static video-QA (average +1.24% margin, peak +3.80% on EgoSchema/Gemini). This suggests that abstracting reusable skills from memory via guided instructions is more effective for single-pass VLM decisions than concatenating raw examples, which can become narrow context.
- **EgoSchema Leaderboard:** VisualClaw's streaming FullEvo (Guide) achieved 68.40% on EgoSchema (Gemini 3 Flash), surpassing VideoAgent (60.20%). The offline Uniform-8+FullEvo (Guide) configuration reached 73.60%, outperforming Gemini 1.5 Pro (72.20%) and LLoVi (GPT-4o) (67.60%) using a smaller, cheaper VLM backbone.

**b. Results and Analysis on VisualClawArena:**
- **Agentic FullEvo Transfer:** The self-evolution stack consistently improved performance across both tool-using agent backends. With Codex, VisualClaw (Cat.) achieved 54.27% macro accuracy, a +2.92 point improvement over VisualClaw w/o FullEvo and +4.02 points over Uniform-8. For Claude Code, VisualClaw (Cat.) reached 52.16% macro accuracy, improving by +3.16 points over w/o FullEvo and +8.17 points over Uniform-8.
- **FullEvo (Cat.) for Complex Workflows:** Unlike static video-QA, the simple memory-skill concatenation (Cat.) variant showed slightly better performance than (Guide) in VisualClawArena (e.g., Claude Code +1.39%). This suggests that agentic backends, which process information through multi-step operations like file reads and tool calls, can effectively utilize longer, raw memory context as concrete procedural traces.
- **Temporal Trends:** Within-scenario accuracy generally increased from early to late stages. VisualClaw (Cat.) for Codex was strongest in the mid and late thirds (56.19%, 57.38%), and for Claude Code, it led across all three thirds. This indicates that the FullEvo mechanism becomes more beneficial as the agent accumulates local trajectory context to convert retrieved failures into concrete decisions.
- **Difficulty-Dependent Gains:** FullEvo's benefits were more pronounced on Medium and Hard scenarios. While showing slight reductions on Easy scenarios, VisualClaw (Cat.) improved Medium scenarios (e.g., Codex +0.38, Claude Code +2.60) and significantly improved Hard scenarios (e.g., Codex +5.38, Claude Code +5.33), suggesting it provides valuable procedural bias for complex, cross-modal tasks.

**c. Efficiency Results and Analysis:**
- **API Cost Reduction (Video-QA):** The cascade-driven video encoding dramatically cut API costs. Compared to Full-frame @1 fps, VisualClaw reduced frames sent by 2–340x, leading to a cost reduction of -34.4% to -99.3% per benchmark (average -98.1%, peak -99.3% on V-MME long). Against the Uniform-8 + FullEvo baseline, it still reduced cost by -15.2% to -41.3% (average -25.9%), while maintaining competitive accuracy. The saving widened with clip duration.
- **Cost Reduction (VisualClawArena):** For Claude Code, Cascade-8 was 9.5% cheaper than Uniform-8, primarily due to sending fewer frames and consequently triggering fewer failure-driven evolution calls. This indicates that cascade selection reduces visual context, leading to fewer downstream failures and thus lowering both answer-time and evolution-time budgets.

These findings collectively demonstrate that VisualClaw effectively addresses the three identified deployment gaps, offering substantial improvements in efficiency and adaptability for multimodal agents across diverse tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents VisualClaw, a self-evolving multimodal agent, and VisualClawArena, a new benchmark, with implications for the development and deployment of advanced AI systems.

**a. Enabling Real-Time Edge Deployment:** The cascaded encoding gate represents a significant step towards deploying sophisticated multimodal agents on resource-constrained edge devices like AI glasses. By drastically reducing the number of video frames sent to cloud-based VLMs (an average of 98.1% cost reduction compared to full-frame upload), it makes real-time, continuous perception economically and technically feasible for streaming applications. This addresses a major bottleneck for ubiquitous AI assistants, allowing for longer operational periods on mobile data plans.

**b. Fostering Adaptive and Personalized AI:** The self-evolving skill and memory banks allow agents to learn and improve from experience, particularly from failures, without requiring expensive and frequent updates to the underlying VLM's weights. This mechanism facilitates personalization, enabling agents to tailor their behavior and knowledge to specific users or environments over time. Such adaptability is crucial for agents operating in dynamic real-world scenarios, where unforeseen situations and evolving user needs are common.

**c. Advancing Multimodal Agentic Capabilities:** By integrating efficient perception with a dynamic reasoning scaffold, VisualClaw demonstrates enhanced capabilities for agents beyond traditional question-answering. Its performance on VisualClawArena, a benchmark designed for tool-using, workspace-centric tasks, indicates that the framework can empower agents to perform complex operations involving visual grounding, document interaction, and executable actions. This pushes the frontier of AI from mere information retrieval to active problem-solving within interactive environments.

**d. Addressing Benchmark Gaps in Agentic AI:** The introduction of VisualClawArena is a substantial contribution to the field of AI evaluation. By curating a benchmark that demands multimodal reasoning, tool use, and persistent workspace state, it provides a more realistic and challenging environment for assessing agent intelligence. This benchmark encourages the development of agents that can reconcile diverse forms of evidence (video, text, dynamic updates) and perform actionable steps, moving beyond the limitations of static, one-shot QA evaluations.

**e. Cost-Effectiveness and Practicality:** The demonstrated ability of VisualClaw to boost accuracy while simultaneously reducing API costs by a considerable margin makes advanced multimodal agents more practical and economically viable for widespread adoption. This efficiency gain, coupled with the adaptive learning capabilities, suggests a pathway for creating powerful AI assistants that are both performant and sustainable in real-world deployment.

In summary, VisualClaw and VisualClawArena collectively advance the state of multimodal agents by tackling critical deployment barriers, fostering continuous learning, and providing a rigorous evaluation framework for interactive, real-world tasks.
