# emg2tendon: From sEMG Signals to Tendon Control in Musculoskeletal Hands

> [!summary] Summary
> A new large-scale emg2tendon dataset, mapping sEMG signals to tendon control for anthropomorphic hands, was computationally derived from the emg2pose dataset using the MyoSuite MyoHand model. A Conditional Latent Diffusion Model (CLDM) predicts these tendon control signals from sEMG with an RMSE of 0.201, leading to improved hand pose estimation of 11.9° ± 1.3° mean angular error when combined with physics-based simulation.

## Problem

- Dexterous control of tendon-driven robotic hands is challenging due to the lack of direct mapping between visual pose data and tendon control signals.
- Existing sEMG-to-pose mapping datasets do not provide direct tendon control signals, which are crucial for biomechanically accurate control of anthropomorphic tendon-driven robots.
- Traditional vision-based control methods for robotic hands suffer from occlusions, limited field of view, and environmental constraints in real-world applications.

## Method

- Created **emg2tendon**, the first large-scale dataset extending emg2pose by computationally deriving 39 tendon control signals from hand pose data using the ==MyoSuite MyoHand== musculoskeletal model.
- Developed a ==Conditional Latent Diffusion Model (CLDM)== specifically designed for robust regression of high-dimensional time-series data, learning the complex mapping from sEMG to tendon control signals.
- Established a comprehensive benchmarking framework to evaluate direct sEMG-to-pose, sEMG-to-tendon control, and tendon control-to-pose mappings, including a ==physics-based simulation== path.

## Results

- The proposed CLDM achieved the lowest RMSE of **0.201** and MAE of **0.139** for predicting tendon control signals from sEMG, outperforming baselines.
- For direct sEMG-to-pose mapping, CLDM yielded a mean angular error of **11.3° ± 1.0°**, a **7.4%** reduction compared to the TDS baseline.
- The two-step approach (sEMG 	Tendon Control Signals 	Simulated Pose) with CLDM and MyoSuite MyoHand achieved the lowest overall mean angular error of **11.9° ± 1.3°**, outperforming direct sEMG-to-pose methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Tendon control signals serve as a valuable and biomechanically consistent intermediate representation for translating sEMG into dexterous hand movements, especially for tendon-driven robots.
> - Diffusion models, specifically CLDM, are effective at learning complex, high-dimensional, and temporally dependent mappings from sEMG signals to continuous tendon control, outperforming traditional regression baselines.
> - Integrating learned tendon control signals with a physics-based musculoskeletal simulation (e.g., MyoSuite MyoHand) further enhances the accuracy and realism of hand pose estimation from sEMG by incorporating physical constraints.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of dexterous robotic hands, particularly those driven by tendons, presents significant challenges in control policy learning. Traditional methods for robotic control often rely on visual pose tracking (e.g., motion capture, depth sensors), which are susceptible to issues such as occlusions, limited field of view, and environmental constraints in real-world applications. These limitations hinder continuous and robust operation.

Surface electromyography (sEMG) sensors offer an alternative by directly capturing neuromuscular signals, thereby overcoming some of the practical limitations of vision-based systems. Existing research has explored mapping sEMG signals to hand poses (EMG-to-pose regression). For instance, studies by Liu et al. and SensingDynamics demonstrated sEMG decoding for hand pose, though often on limited datasets with a small number of participants and short recording durations. The `emg2pose` dataset represents a larger-scale effort, providing a comprehensive mapping between sEMG recordings and hand joint poses.

However, tendon-driven robotic hands, which mimic the human musculoskeletal system, operate differently from joint-actuated robots. They lack a direct, one-to-one mapping between motion capture data (representing joint angles) and the underlying tendon control signals. This inherent difference makes learning control policies for tendon-driven systems particularly complex and computationally expensive. While musculoskeletal models (e.g., OpenSim) have been developed to simulate muscle and joint kinematics, their high computational demands and limited support for contact forces have restricted their widespread application in data-driven control. The `MyoHand` model within the `MyoSuite` framework has emerged as a more computationally efficient and contact-rich simulation tool for tendon-driven manipulation.

Despite advancements in sEMG-to-pose mapping and musculoskeletal modeling, a critical gap remains: the absence of large-scale datasets directly linking sEMG signals to tendon control signals for anthropomorphic robotic hands. Current approaches often infer movement through direct joint angle regression, which may not be biologically consistent for tendon-driven systems. Advances in imitation learning, particularly with diffusion-based models, have shown promise for efficient policy learning, offering a potential avenue for addressing the complexities of high-dimensional control spaces in biomechanical systems.

This work addresses this gap by introducing the first large-scale dataset, `emg2tendon`, specifically designed to map sEMG signals to tendon control signals. It extends the existing `emg2pose` dataset by computationally deriving tendon control signals using the `MyoSuite MyoHand` model, thereby providing a biologically consistent intermediary for controlling tendon-driven robotic hands. The research also introduces a novel diffusion-based regression model to learn this complex mapping, positioning itself at the intersection of sEMG-based control, musculoskeletal modeling, and advanced machine learning for robotic manipulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to advance the field of EMG-to-tendon control models for anthropomorphic hand control. This overarching goal is driven by several detailed motivations and specific objectives:

**Motivation for Tendon-Driven Robotic Hands:**
- **Enhanced Dexterity and Adaptability:** Tendon-driven robotic hands are modeled after the human hand, which exhibits exceptional dexterity, compliance, and adaptability crucial for complex manipulation tasks in various domains, including manufacturing and surgical procedures. They offer a higher level of control compared to many conventional robotic designs.
- **Biomechanical Accuracy and Compliance:** Unlike non-anthropomorphic designs, tendon-driven hands provide greater biomechanical accuracy by mimicking biological muscle-tendon dynamics. This compliance is essential for safe and adaptive interaction with objects and environments, promoting human-like movement.
- **Facilitating Human-Robot Interaction:** A closer mapping between human and robotic hand mechanics (both in design and control) enhances the efficacy of teleoperation, imitation learning, and adaptive control strategies, fostering more intuitive human-robot interaction.

**Motivation for Using sEMG Signals:**
- **Overcoming Vision-Based Limitations:** Traditional vision-based pose tracking methods (e.g., motion capture, depth sensors) face inherent limitations such as occlusions, restricted field of view, and susceptibility to environmental changes. These render them impractical for continuous real-world applications.
- **Robust and Inexpensive Alternative:** Wrist-worn sEMG sensors offer a robust, non-invasive, and relatively inexpensive alternative by directly capturing the electrical potentials generated by muscle activations, providing a direct link to neuromuscular intent.
- **Enabling Natural and Responsive Operation:** Mapping these neuromuscular signals to robotic hand control is envisioned to facilitate more natural and responsive operation, which has substantial implications for advanced prosthetics, teleoperated systems, and human-robot collaboration.

**Motivation for EMG-to-Tendon Control (over EMG-to-Pose):**
- **Addressing Control Policy Challenges:** For tendon-driven systems, there is no direct one-to-one correspondence between motion capture data (representing joint angles) and the internal tendon control signals. This disconnect makes learning control policies complex and expensive.
- **Biologically Consistent Estimation:** Rather than directly inferring joint angles, the paper proposes inferring movement through a musculoskeletal hand model that operates based on tendon-driven actuation. This approach ensures biologically consistent pose estimation and provides a stable framework for learning and predicting control signals that are directly relevant to tendon-actuated robots.
- **Enabling Scalable and Accurate Control:** By establishing a clear mapping from sEMG to tendon control, the research aims to lay the groundwork for scalable and accurate control strategies for dexterous robotic manipulation.

**Specific Contributions and Objectives:**
- **Creation of the First Large-Scale EMG-to-Tendon Control Dataset:** The primary objective is to develop and release `emg2tendon`, an extension of the `emg2pose` dataset, which includes derived tendon control signals. This dataset encompasses 193 subjects, 370 hours of data, and 29 stages of diverse gestures, representing a significant resource for the community.
- **Development of a Comprehensive Benchmarking Framework:** The authors aim to establish an extensive framework for evaluating models that perform EMG-to-tendon control, tendon control-to-pose, and direct EMG-to-pose mappings.
- **Introduction of a Novel Conditional Latent Diffusion Model (CLDM):** A key objective is to propose and evaluate a diffusion-based regression model designed to predict musculoskeletal hand control signals from sEMG recordings with high accuracy.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on extending an existing large-scale sEMG-to-pose dataset to include tendon control signals, and then developing and benchmarking models to learn this novel mapping.

**Dataset Generation: `emg2tendon`**

1. **Foundation: `emg2pose` Dataset:**
 - The `emg2tendon` dataset is built upon the `emg2pose` dataset, which comprises sEMG recordings and corresponding hand joint poses.
 - **Data Collection:** `emg2pose` was collected using a 16-channel bipolar sEMG-RD wristband (2 kHz sampling) and a 26-camera motion capture system (60 Hz, with 19 markers per hand).
 - **Scale:** It includes data from 193 participants, accumulating 370 hours across 751 sessions, covering 29 distinct gesture categories.
 - **Pose Data Processing:** Motion capture data was processed using an inverse dynamics solver to reconstruct hand joint angles. Due to occlusions, 12.7% of frames required interpolation to match the sEMG sampling rate.

2. **Derivation of Tendon Control Signals (Pose to Tendon):**
 - **Challenge:** Direct capture of tendon movement in vivo or generation of real-world EMG-to-tendon datasets is highly challenging.
 - **Solution:** The inverse dynamics of the `MyoSuite MyoHand` musculoskeletal model were utilized to compute tendon control signals corresponding to the existing pose data in `emg2pose`.
 - **MyoHand Model Integration:** The `emg2pose` dataset contains 20 hand joints, while the `MyoHand` model has 23. All 20 `emg2pose` finger joints were mapped to `MyoHand`, excluding three wrist-related joints (pro-supination, radial-ulnar deviation, flexion-extension) not explicitly modeled in `emg2pose`. This mapping ensures compatibility with the biomechanical model.
 - **Muscle Actuator Dynamics (MuJoCo):** The paper details the underlying MuJoCo-based muscle-tendon actuator dynamics.
 - **Actuator Length:** Defined as the sum of biological tendon length ($L_T$) and muscle length ($L_M$).
 - **Scaled Length and Velocity:** Formulas for scaled muscle length ($L$) and velocity ($V$) are provided, considering optimal resting muscle length ($L_0$).
 - **Force-Length-Velocity (FLV) Function:** Active and passive forces are modeled by $F_{LV}(L, V, act) = F_L(L) \cdot F_V(V) \cdot act + F_P(L)$, where `act` is the activation level.
 - **Total Muscle Force:** Calculated as actuator force = $-F_{LV}(L, V, act) \cdot F_0$, where $F_0$ is the peak active force.
 - **Activation State:** Governed by a first-order nonlinear filter (Eq. 6) dependent on the control signal (`ctrl`) and activation/deactivation time constants ($\tau_{act}, \tau_{deact}$).
 - **Computation of Control Signals:** The control signal `ctrl` is determined by solving a muscle actuator equation (Eq. 9), which is reformulated as a quadratic program (QP) (Eq. 11) using a sigmoid function approximation for efficient optimization.
 - **Output:** This process yields 39 tendon control signals for the `MyoHand` model.
 - **Data Processing:**
 - The Inverse Kinematics (IK) solver failed in 21 sessions due to occlusions and infeasible joint configurations.
 - To accelerate computation, a distributed setup of 15 Apple Mac Mini devices (M1, M2, M3 chips) was used, processing 25,254 sessions in 46 hours.
 - Pose recordings were downsampled from 2 kHz to 500 Hz for the IK solver, and the resulting tendon control signals were then upsampled back to 2 kHz to match the original sEMG data, ensuring synchronization.

**Modeling and Experiments**

The authors establish a benchmarking framework and introduce a novel model for prediction tasks.

1. **Baseline Models:** Three existing regression models from the `emg2pose` literature were used as baselines:
 - Time-Depth Separable Convolution (TDS) Network
 - NeuroPose
 - SensingDynamics

2. **Proposed Model: Conditional Latent Diffusion Model (CLDM):**
 - **Architecture:** CLDM employs a three-stage training process for time-series data.
 1. **Autoencoder Training:** Two autoencoders with Kullback-Leibler (KL) regularization are trained to compress sEMG (input) and tendon control signals (output) into latent vector representations.
 2. **Diffusion Model Training:** The frozen encoder of the output autoencoder generates latent vectors, which are then processed by a diffusion model. This model learns to reverse a process of gradual noise addition, conditioned on the latent encoding of the sEMG input.
 3. **Decoding:** The final output from the diffusion model is decoded using the decoder of the output autoencoder to reconstruct the predicted tendon control signals.
 - **Inference:** During inference, a random noise sample is generated from a learned Gaussian distribution, conditioned by the latent vector from the input sEMG autoencoder, and passed through the reverse diffusion process before decoding.
 - **Components:** Autoencoders feature ResNet Blocks, Attention Blocks, and Downsampling/Upsampling Blocks. The diffusion process utilizes a UNet-based architecture.
 - **Hyperparameters:** Autoencoders use a 16-channel latent space with a four-layer encoder-decoder and attention. CLDMs employ a UNet with a sample size of 4000, 16 extra conditioning channels, and downsampling across four blocks. A linear noise schedule with 1000 timesteps ($\beta_{start}=0.0001, \beta_{end}=0.02$) and Adam optimizer ($10^{-4}$ learning rate) with a Reduce-on-Plateau scheduler were used.

3. **Evaluation Tasks:** Models were trained and evaluated across five distinct tasks:
 - **Pose Regression from EMG:** Direct prediction of joint poses from sEMG signals (using baselines and CLDM).
 - **Tendon Control Signals Regression from EMG:** Prediction of tendon control signals from sEMG recordings (using baselines and CLDM).
 - **Pose Regression from Tendon Control Signals:** Prediction of joint poses from derived tendon control signals (using baselines and CLDM).
 - **Two-Step Pose Estimation (EMG → Tendon Control Signals → Pose):** Combining the learned mappings from the second and third tasks.
 - **Two-Step Pose Estimation with Physics-Based Simulation (EMG → Tendon Control Signals → Simulated Pose):** Predicted tendon control signals from sEMG are applied to the `MyoSuite MyoHand` model to generate pose estimations through simulation, incorporating biomechanical constraints.

4. **Metrics:** Angular error (degrees) was used for pose regression, while Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE) and Mean Absolute Error (MAE) were reported for tendon control signal prediction.

5. **Computational Resources:** All models were trained on a single NVIDIA GH200 system equipped with a 96GB H100 GPU, 64-core ARM64 CPU, and 463.9GB RAM.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation demonstrated that the Conditional Latent Diffusion Model (CLDM) generally outperformed the three baseline models (TDS, NeuroPose, and SensingDynamics) across various prediction tasks.

**Tendon Control Signals Regression from sEMG Signals (Table II):**
- CLDM achieved the lowest errors in predicting tendon control signals from sEMG recordings, with an RMSE of 0.201 and an MAE of 0.139 across users.
- The baseline models yielded higher errors: NeuroPose reported RMSE 0.226 and MAE 0.155, while TDS reported RMSE 0.215 and MAE 0.150.
- These results indicate CLDM's improved capability in capturing the complex patterns of muscle activation to predict fine-grained tendon control signals.

**Pose Regression from sEMG Signals (Direct EMG-to-Pose, Table I):**
- For direct mapping from sEMG to hand pose, CLDM again achieved the lowest mean angular error, measuring 11.3° ± 1.0° across users.
- This performance surpassed TDS (12.2° ± 1.3°), NeuroPose (13.2° ± 1.1°), and SensingDynamics (15.5° ± 1.4°).
- The diffusion-based model exhibited an error reduction of 7.4% compared to TDS and 14.3% compared to NeuroPose, suggesting benefits from its temporal smoothing and uncertainty modeling capabilities.

**Pose Regression from Tendon Control Signals (Tendon-to-Pose, Table I):**
- When predicting joint angles solely from tendon control signals, CLDM continued to demonstrate superior performance with a mean angular error of 10.8° ± 0.9° across users.
- This was lower than TDS (11.1° ± 1.1°), NeuroPose (12.4° ± 1.3°), and SensingDynamics (14.6° ± 1.2°), indicating a more accurate learned mapping from tendon control to pose.

**Two-Step Pose Estimation (EMG → Tendon Control Signals → Pose, Table I):**
- The approach where sEMG signals were first mapped to tendon control signals, and then these signals were used to predict pose, resulted in a mean angular error of 12.6° ± 1.2° for CLDM.
- This error was lower than the direct EMG-to-Pose regression achieved by the TDS baseline (14.5° ± 1.4°), suggesting that tendon control signals serve as a beneficial intermediate representation for improving pose estimation accuracy.

**Two-Step Pose Estimation with Physics-Based Simulation (EMG → Tendon Control Signals → Simulated Pose, Table I):**
- Integrating the predicted tendon control signals with the `MyoSuite MyoHand` physics-based simulation model yielded the lowest overall mean angular error among all methods evaluated in this category.
- CLDM achieved 11.9° ± 1.3° in this setup, representing a 5.6% improvement over TDS (13.6° ± 1.3°) and a 7.6% improvement over NeuroPose (14.4° ± 1.1°).
- This finding indicates that combining learned tendon control signals with biomechanical simulation, which incorporates physical constraints, contributes to more realistic and accurate pose estimation by mitigating errors from purely data-driven models.

In summary, the CLDM-based models consistently achieved the lowest angular errors for pose estimation and RMSE/MAE for tendon control signal prediction across all tasks and test sets (user, stage, and combined user/stage generalization settings). This indicates the effectiveness of diffusion models in learning the complex relationships between sEMG signals and tendon control. The results also highlight the utility of tendon control signals as a meaningful intermediary for improved pose estimation, particularly when leveraged within a physics-informed simulation environment.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a notable advancement in the field of dexterous robotic manipulation and human-robot interfaces, particularly concerning tendon-driven systems.

**Novel Dataset for Tendon Control:** The introduction of `emg2tendon` represents a significant contribution by creating the first large-scale dataset specifically for mapping sEMG signals to tendon control signals. This dataset, extending the `emg2pose` dataset, provides a critical resource for training and evaluating models for tendon-driven robotic hands, addressing a previously identified data gap. Its extensive scale (193 subjects, 370 hours) supports the development of models that can generalize across diverse users and gestures.

**Enabling Advanced Robotic Hand Control:** By establishing a computational pipeline to derive and predict tendon control signals from sEMG, the work lays foundational groundwork for more naturalistic, responsive, and biomechanically consistent control of anthropomorphic, tendon-driven robotic hands. This approach aligns with the biological mechanisms of human hand movement, potentially leading to robotic systems that operate with greater dexterity and compliance.

**Improved Hand Pose Estimation:** The findings demonstrate that utilizing tendon control signals as an intermediary representation can enhance the accuracy of hand pose estimation from sEMG, especially when integrated with physics-based simulation. This suggests a more robust and biologically plausible pathway for interpreting human neuromuscular intent, potentially overcoming some limitations of direct sEMG-to-pose mapping.

**Advancements in Prosthetics and Teleoperation:** The ability to accurately and robustly translate sEMG signals into specific tendon control commands has direct implications for the development of next-generation prosthetic hands. Such prosthetics could offer users more intuitive and fine-grained control, leading to improved functionality and quality of life. Similarly, this technology could enhance teleoperation systems by allowing operators to control dexterous robots with a more natural and direct mapping of their own hand movements and muscle activations.

**New Modeling Paradigm for Physiological Signals:** The successful application of Conditional Latent Diffusion Models (CLDM) to predict continuous tendon control signals from sEMG demonstrates the efficacy of diffusion-based approaches for learning complex, high-dimensional mappings in physiological data. This represents a promising modeling paradigm that can capture intricate temporal dependencies and manage uncertainties inherent in biological signals, potentially surpassing the capabilities of traditional regression methods.

**Future Research and Practical Applications:** The paper outlines clear directions for future work, including validating the approach on real tendon-driven robotic hands, exploring reinforcement learning techniques to further enhance control signal prediction, and extending the dataset to include hand-object interactions. These steps are crucial for translating this research from a synthetic environment to practical, real-world dexterous manipulation tasks, further bridging the gap between human neuromuscular control and advanced robotic capabilities. While acknowledging limitations such as reliance on inverse dynamics and a simplified musculoskeletal model, this work marks a significant step towards achieving biologically inspired and highly capable robotic hand control.
