# HoPE: Hybrid of Position Embedding for Long Context Vision-Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Carnegie Mellon University and Xiaohongshu Inc. developed HoPE, a hybrid position embedding approach for Vision-Language Models that theoretically and empirically improves long video understanding. HoPE integrates a theoretically-backed frequency allocation strategy and dynamic temporal scaling, achieving up to 22.23% higher accuracy on the V-NIAH benchmark compared to prior methods.

## Problem

- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) experience performance degradation when processing long video contexts due to the difficulty of modeling intricate spatial-temporal dependencies across extended sequences.
- Existing 1D Rotary Position Embedding (RoPE), designed for text, distorts spatial-temporal locality when directly applied to complex 3D visual data like flattened video patches.
- Current multimodal RoPE extensions rely on heuristic frequency allocation strategies, lacking theoretical guarantees for reliably preserving semantic similarities over long contexts.

## Method

- Proposed a ==Hybrid Frequency Allocation (HFA) strategy== that assigns higher frequencies to spatial components and critically sets the lowest frequencies for the temporal component to zero, maximizing the 'semantic preference' guarantee.
- Introduced a ==Dynamic Temporal Scaling (DTS) mechanism== that trains VLMs with a multi-scale set of temporal scaling factors to enhance robustness to varying video speeds and enable flexible inference.
- Conducted a theoretical analysis demonstrating that ==setting temporal frequencies to zero within RoPE== provides the strongest foundation for preserving semantic similarity over long temporal distances.

## Results

- HoPE consistently outperformed vanilla RoPE and other multimodal RoPE variants (M-RoPE, VideoRoPE) across long video understanding benchmarks, with improvements up to **4.05%** for the 7B model at 32k context length.
- Achieved superior extrapolation capabilities on the V-NIAH (Visual Needle-In-A-Haystack) benchmark, demonstrating a **22.23%** higher accuracy (**63.56%** vs **52.00%**) compared to the best baseline.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both the Hybrid Frequency Allocation (HFA) and Dynamic Temporal Scaling (DTS) components individually contributed to HoPE's improved performance and robustness.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Directly applying 1D RoPE to flattened video tokens distorts their inherent 3D spatial-temporal locality.
> - Existing multimodal RoPE frequency allocation strategies are unreliable for maintaining 'semantic preference' over long contexts, as even low frequencies can cause arbitrary rotations that obscure semantic similarities.
> - Setting temporal frequencies to zero in a hybrid RoPE design maximizes the semantic preference guarantee, offering a theoretically sound method to preserve long-range semantic understanding in videos.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) has witnessed rapid advancements, enabling machines to understand and generate content across visual and textual modalities. These models have achieved remarkable success in diverse tasks such as visual question answering, image captioning, and cross-modal retrieval. However, a significant limitation of current VLMs is their degradation in performance when confronted with **long-context scenarios**, particularly long videos. This issue stems from the difficulty of effectively modeling intricate spatial-temporal dependencies across extended sequences of visual tokens.

This problem mirrors challenges previously faced in Large Language Models (LLMs) regarding length generalization, where **Rotary Position Embedding (RoPE)** emerged as a dominant solution. RoPE effectively encodes relative positional information, crucial for transformer-based architectures to scale to longer text sequences. However, directly applying 1D RoPE, designed for sequential text, to complex 3D (time, height, width) visual data like videos is suboptimal. It fails to capture the inherent spatial-temporal structure, treating flattened video patches as mere sequential tokens, thereby distorting locality.

Several attempts have been made to extend 1D RoPE for multimodal inputs, primarily by allocating different frequency ranges within RoPE to encode 3D positional information. Examples include M-RoPE, which assigns higher frequencies to temporal information, and VideoRoPE, which assigns lower frequencies to temporal components, driven by empirical observations about "attention sink" phenomena. However, these existing approaches largely rely on **heuristics** for frequency allocation, lacking a deep theoretical understanding of how these choices impact long-context capabilities and the preservation of semantic similarities. Furthermore, these methods often employ fixed or unidirectional temporal scaling mechanisms, which are inadequate for the diverse speeds and information densities found in real-world videos.

HoPE addresses these critical gaps by providing the first theoretical analysis of multimodal RoPE frequency allocation and proposing a novel, theoretically-grounded approach. It aims to fundamentally improve VLMs' ability to handle long videos, pushing the boundaries of multimodal length generalization beyond existing heuristic methods. By tackling the core issue of unreliable semantic modeling in extended contexts and introducing adaptive temporal scaling, HoPE positions itself as a significant advancement in making VLMs more robust and practical for real-world, long-duration multimodal content.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the HoPE paper are multifaceted, stemming from identified limitations in current VLM capabilities for long-context understanding:

1. **Overcome Performance Degradation in Long-Context VLMs:** The most critical motivation is to address the significant performance drop VLMs experience when processing long videos. This limitation is a major hurdle for real-world deployment, where input lengths frequently exceed pre-trained context windows. The authors highlight that VLMs struggle even with basic tasks like object counting and temporal localization in extended video contexts.

2. **Provide Theoretical Understanding for Multimodal RoPE Design:** Current multimodal RoPE extensions rely heavily on heuristic frequency allocation strategies. A key objective is to move beyond these heuristics by conducting an in-depth theoretical analysis of how different frequency allocation strategies impact VLMs' long-context capabilities. This includes understanding the ideal properties a multimodal RoPE should possess.

3. **Ensure Reliable Semantic Modeling Over Extended Contexts:** The analysis reveals that existing multimodal RoPE variants fail to reliably capture semantic similarities over long distances. The paper aims to propose a new frequency allocation strategy that guarantees a stronger preservation of "semantic preference" – the ability of the attention mechanism to prioritize semantically similar tokens regardless of their relative distance.

4. **Develop a Flexible Temporal Scaling Mechanism:** Current methods either ignore temporal scaling for visual tokens or apply a fixed, unidirectional scaling factor. This is suboptimal given the wide variance in video speeds and information densities in real-world scenarios. The authors aim to develop a dynamic mechanism that can accommodate both compression and expansion of temporal indices, making VLMs more robust and adaptable.

In essence, the paper seeks to advance the fundamental understanding of positional encoding for multimodal data and translate these theoretical insights into a practical solution (HoPE) that significantly enhances the ability of VLMs to process and comprehend lengthy visual inputs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

HoPE addresses the identified challenges through two primary components: a **Hybrid Frequency Allocation (HFA) strategy** and a **Dynamic Temporal Scaling (DTS) mechanism**. These components are developed based on a comprehensive theoretical analysis of multimodal RoPE variants.

#### 3.1. Theoretical Foundation

The paper begins with an in-depth theoretical analysis (Section 3) to outline ideal properties for multimodal RoPE and expose limitations of existing methods:
- **Vanilla RoPE Limitations:** The authors formally prove (Proposition 3.1) that directly applying 1D RoPE to flattened video tokens distorts spatial-temporal locality. A relative distance of 1 in spatial or temporal dimensions becomes `W` or `HW` after flattening, where `W` and `H` are frame dimensions, causing a mismatch between encoding and 3D structure.
- **Unreliable Semantic Modeling in Current Multimodal RoPEs:** The paper introduces the concept of "Semantic Preference" (Definition 3.1), which dictates that attention should favor semantically similar tokens irrespective of distance. Theorem 3.1 rigorously demonstrates that existing frequency allocation strategies (e.g., highest frequencies for temporal like M-RoPE, or lowest frequencies for temporal like VideoRoPE) are unreliable. With sufficiently long contexts, even the lowest frequencies can cause arbitrary rotations, violating semantic preference and making it difficult for the model to identify semantically related tokens over long distances.
- **Need for Flexible Temporal Indexing:** The analysis also highlights that current fixed temporal indexing (e.g., same stride for text/video or a single scaling factor) overlooks varying information densities in multimodal inputs and lacks robustness to diverse video speeds.

#### 3.2. Hybrid Frequency Allocation (HFA) Strategy

To address the unreliability of existing frequency allocation strategies, HoPE proposes a novel HFA strategy:
- **Spatial Encoding:** Higher frequencies, which are more sensitive to positional differences and better at capturing local features, are allocated to spatial components (x, y) in an interleaved manner. This aligns with findings that high frequencies are crucial for local feature sensitivity.
- **Temporal Encoding (Zero Frequencies):** Crucially, HoPE departs from existing methods by directly setting the lowest frequencies allocated to the temporal component (t) to zero. This effectively transforms the temporal component of RoPE into a No Positional Encoding (NoPE) approach for these dimensions.
- **Theoretical Guarantee:** Lemma 4.1 formalizes the condition for maintaining semantic preference, showing that the sum of cosine terms across all dimensions must remain non-negative. Building on this, Theorem 4.1 proves that setting temporal frequencies to zero maximizes this semantic preference guarantee, providing the strongest theoretical foundation for long-range semantic modeling compared to any other frequency allocation strategy. By making the temporal rotation an identity operation for certain dimensions, HoPE ensures that these dimensions do not contribute to potentially destructive rotations over long temporal distances, thereby preserving semantic similarity.

#### 3.3. Dynamic Temporal Scaling (DTS) Mechanism

To enhance robustness to varying video speeds and enable flexible inference, HoPE introduces a DTS mechanism:
- **Multi-scale Learning during Training:** A predefined set of scaling factors Γ (e.g., {0.5, 0.75, 1, 1.25, 1.5}), including both compression (γ < 1) and expansion (γ > 1), is used. During training, a scaling factor γ is randomly selected from Γ for each video. This exposes the VLM to temporal relationships at multiple scales, fostering robustness to diverse video paces.
- **Flexible Inference:** During inference, the scaling factor can be flexibly selected from Γ to accommodate videos of different lengths and speeds. This contrasts with fixed, unidirectional scaling in prior works, offering practical adaptability. The position indices for visual tokens are adjusted by `L_t + γ(l - L_t)`, where `L_t` is text length and `l - L_t` is the frame index relative to the video start. This ensures that the overall temporal position is influenced by the scaling factor.

By combining HFA's theoretically-backed approach to preserving semantic similarity with DTS's practical flexibility, HoPE aims to provide a robust and effective solution for long-context video understanding.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of HoPE demonstrates its consistent superiority over existing RoPE variants across various long video understanding and retrieval tasks, using Qwen2-1.5B and Qwen2-7B as backbone models.

#### 4.1. Performance on Long Video Understanding

- **Consistent Outperformance:** HoPE consistently outperforms vanilla RoPE, M-RoPE, and VideoRoPE across three benchmarks (MLVU, LongVideoBench, Video-MME) and diverse context lengths (8k, 16k, 32k, 64k). For instance, with the 7B model at 32k context length, HoPE surpassed vanilla RoPE by 2.82% on MLVU, 4.05% on LongVideoBench, and 1.45% on Video-MME.
- **Scalability with Backbone Size:** HoPE's effectiveness scales with the backbone LLM size. Performance gains are more pronounced when moving from the 2B to the 7B model, suggesting that HoPE provides more significant benefits for larger, more capable base models.
- **Challenges at Extreme Lengths:** While HoPE consistently performs best, the performance of all methods, including HoPE, degrades substantially at extremely long context lengths (e.g., 64k on Video-MME), highlighting that extrapolation to very high multiples of training length remains a significant challenge. However, HoPE still maintains the most robust performance under these extreme conditions.

#### 4.2. Performance on Long Video Retrieval

- **Superior Extrapolation:** On the V-NIAH (Visual Needle-In-A-Haystack) benchmark, HoPE demonstrates significantly better extrapolation capabilities compared to M-RoPE and VideoRoPE. The visual results (Figure 3) clearly show HoPE maintaining higher accuracy across various frame depths and number of frames, especially beyond the 8k training context length.
- **Quantitative Improvement:** Quantitatively, HoPE achieved an average accuracy of 63.56% on V-NIAH, surpassing the best baseline (VideoRoPE at 52.00%) by a substantial margin of 22.23%. This confirms HoPE's effectiveness in tasks requiring precise temporal localization within extremely long videos.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

Ablation experiments confirm the individual contributions of HoPE's components:
- **3D Structure is Crucial:** Integrating a 3D structure into vanilla RoPE significantly improves performance, validating Proposition 3.1 that 1D RoPE distorts spatial-temporal locality.
- **HFA Enhances Semantic Modeling:** Building upon the 3D structure, the Hybrid Frequency Allocation (HFA) strategy further boosts long-range semantic modeling, achieving an average improvement of 1.69% across context lengths on Video-MME.
- **DTS Improves Robustness:** The Dynamic Temporal Scaling (DTS) mechanism provides additional performance gains, confirming its role in enhancing VLMs' robustness to varying video speeds and facilitating flexible inference.

#### 4.4. Impact of Test-time Scaling Factor Selection

Experiments on test-time scaling factor selection reveal task-specific optimal strategies:
- **Long Video Retrieval Prefers Smaller Scaling Factors:** For tasks like V-NIAH, smaller scaling factors (e.g., γ = 0.75) yielded better results. This is attributed to the extreme length of these videos (1-hour, 3000 frames/432k tokens), where smaller factors prevent spatial position indices from becoming excessively large, thereby better preserving semantic preference over vast temporal distances.
- **Long Video Understanding Benefits from Larger Scaling Factors:** For video understanding benchmarks with context lengths closer to the training length (16k–32k), larger scaling factors (e.g., γ = 1.5) generally performed better. The authors hypothesize a trade-off: while smaller factors maintain semantic preference, larger factors are beneficial for preserving crucial spatial details, which are more critical for complex understanding tasks when temporal distances are not excessively large.

Overall, HoPE consistently demonstrates superior performance and improved length generalization, supported by both its novel architectural design and robust dynamic scaling.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of HoPE represents a significant step forward in the field of Vision-Language Models, particularly for applications involving long-context video understanding. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enabling Robust Long-Context Video Understanding:** HoPE directly addresses a critical limitation of current VLMs—their inability to effectively process and comprehend long videos. By improving length generalization, HoPE paves the way for VLMs to be deployed in real-world scenarios that demand understanding of extended temporal dynamics, such as movie analysis, long-form content summarization, surveillance footage interpretation, and comprehensive video search and retrieval.

2. **Bridging Theoretical Gaps in Multimodal Positional Encoding:** The paper provides the first theoretical analysis of how different frequency allocation strategies in multimodal RoPEs impact long-context capabilities. This foundational work offers crucial insights for the design and analysis of future multimodal positional embeddings, moving the field beyond heuristic-driven approaches. The formal proof that setting temporal frequencies to zero maximizes the "semantic preference guarantee" is a notable theoretical contribution.

3. **Enhanced Reliability and Flexibility:** HoPE's hybrid frequency allocation strategy provides a stronger theoretical guarantee for reliable semantic modeling over arbitrary long contexts. Coupled with its dynamic temporal scaling mechanism, it offers unprecedented flexibility during inference to accommodate videos of diverse lengths and speeds. This adaptability makes VLMs more practical and robust for varied real-world video content, which often presents inconsistencies in pace and information density.

4. **Improved Performance Across Tasks:** The consistent outperformance of HoPE across multiple long video understanding and retrieval benchmarks, with substantial gains over existing methods (e.g., 22.23% on V-NIAH), validates its practical effectiveness. These improvements are critical for applications requiring precise temporal localization and comprehensive content understanding in lengthy videos.

5. **Catalyst for Future Research:** By demonstrating the importance of theoretically-guided design for positional embeddings in multimodal contexts, HoPE is likely to inspire further research into optimal frequency allocation, adaptive scaling mechanisms, and the interplay between position encoding and attention mechanisms in various multimodal architectures.

While the authors acknowledge limitations, such as the performance degradation at extremely long context lengths (64k tokens) and the current evaluation limited to 2B and 7B backbone models, the algorithmic benefits demonstrated by HoPE are clear. Training with larger models and more long-context data could further amplify these gains. Overall, HoPE provides a robust and theoretically sound solution to a major challenge in multimodal AI, promising to unlock new capabilities for VLMs in diverse real-world applications involving video content.
