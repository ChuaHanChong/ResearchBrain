# Shop-R1: Rewarding LLMs to Simulate Human Behavior in Online Shopping via Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Michigan State University, Amazon, Northeastern University, University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign, and Northwestern University developed Shop-R1, a reinforcement learning framework that significantly enhances large language models' ability to simulate human online shopping behavior. This framework achieved a 65% relative improvement in exact-action accuracy compared to supervised fine-tuning alone, reaching 27.72% on a real-world shopping dataset.

## Problem

- Large language models (LLMs) often struggle to accurately simulate nuanced, non-deterministic human behavior in interactive web environments like online shopping.
- Existing methods, such as zero-shot prompting or Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) with synthetic rationales, are limited in personalization, adaptability, and the quality of generated reasoning.
- Traditional reinforcement learning (RL) for web agents faces challenges with sparse binary rewards, leading to inefficient learning and vulnerability to reward hacking for complex, multi-component actions.

## Method

- The approach initializes the model with ==Supervised Fine-Tuning== (SFT) using LLM-generated rationales to provide a strong behavioral prior.
- It decomposes the simulation task into distinct sub-tasks for rationale generation and action prediction, each guided by tailored reinforcement learning reward signals.
- A hybrid reward scheme is implemented, featuring a self-certainty score for rationale generation and a hierarchical reward for action prediction that includes difficulty-aware scaling (DARS) to encourage complex, high-entropy actions.

## Results

- The Shop-R1 framework achieved **27.72%** exact-action accuracy on the SHOP-CART dataset, representing a relative improvement of over **65%** compared to supervised fine-tuning alone.
- It simultaneously boosted action-type accuracy to **36.40%** and action-type F1 to **31.28%**, indicating enhanced fidelity in both high-level intent and fine-grained action component prediction.
- Ablation studies confirmed that each component—SFT warm-start, format reward, self-certainty reward, and hierarchical difficulty-scaled action rewards—is necessary for preventing reward hacking and ensuring robust performance across diverse action types.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An SFT warm-start is crucial for stable RL training, providing essential linguistic priors and structural knowledge for generating complex, long-text arguments.
> - A combined reward system featuring a self-certainty score for rationales and a hierarchical, difficulty-aware reward for actions effectively provides fine-grained feedback, stabilizes RL, and mitigates reward hacking.
> - Providing explicit simplified HTML context from previously visited pages is critical for generating precise, fine-grained action arguments beyond merely inferring coarse action types.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is situated within the expanding field of leveraging Large Language Models (LLMs) for simulating human behavior, particularly in interactive digital environments. Prior research has established LLMs' capabilities in planning, reasoning, and decision-making, extending these to web-based environments for generating user-like action sequences. Applications span e-commerce, education, and social computing.

Previous approaches to simulating human behavior in web environments typically include:
1. **Zero-shot prompting:** LLMs are given textual instructions to imitate user types and output action sequences. While simple, this method often lacks the personalization and adaptability required for high-fidelity behavior modeling.
2. **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) with synthetic rationales:** Recent work has augmented training data with LLM-synthesized rationales, creating `⟨context, action, rationale⟩` triplets for SFT. This approach aims to improve behavioral accuracy and reasoning coherence. However, its effectiveness is constrained by the quality and diversity of the rationales generated by the initial LLM.

The broader landscape also includes Reinforcement Learning (RL) for LLM alignment, notably Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) and Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), which utilize human preferences. Additionally, Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) applies to tasks with deterministic correctness criteria, such as code generation. While RL offers benefits like fine-grained control and adaptability to sparse feedback, its application to *simulation-oriented* human behavior modeling in web environments, particularly online shopping, has been less explored compared to task-completion settings where the goal is explicitly defined.

This paper addresses these gaps by introducing an RL framework specifically designed for *simulating* realistic human shopping behavior, rather than merely completing a task. It aims to overcome the limitations of rationale quality in SFT and the scarcity of ground-truth rationales in natural settings by incorporating self-supervised and hierarchical reward mechanisms within an RL paradigm.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the reasoning ability of LLMs to simulate real human behavior in online shopping environments using a novel reinforcement learning framework called Shop-R1.

The motivations for this objective stem from several limitations observed in existing approaches:

1. **Inadequacy of Current LLM Agents in Human-like Behavior:** Despite advancements, current LLM agents often fail to produce behaviors that accurately align with real human users in web environments. They may generate logically sound actions for task completion but lack the nuanced, non-deterministic, and context-dependent characteristics of human decision-making in complex settings like online shopping.
2. **Limitations of Zero-Shot Prompting:** While straightforward, zero-shot prompting lacks the personalization and adaptability necessary for high-fidelity behavior modeling, leading to generic and often unrealistic simulations.
3. **Bounded Quality of SFT with Synthetic Rationales:** Approaches that use LLM-synthesized rationales for supervised fine-tuning are inherently limited by the reasoning capabilities and diversity of the LLM used for data generation. The quality of the synthetic rationales constrains the model's ultimate performance, making it difficult to surpass the "teacher" model.
4. **Challenges in Acquiring Ground-Truth Rationales:** Collecting explicit ground-truth rationales from real users is difficult. Such self-reported rationales may also omit implicit or unconscious decision factors, hindering the training of models that can capture the full spectrum of human reasoning.
5. **Inefficiency of Sparse Binary Rewards:** Traditional RL setups often use sparse binary rewards, which can lead to inefficient learning, especially in complex environments where successful actions are rare. Agents may struggle to escape "no-reward" plateaus, making the training process unstable and slow.
6. **Vulnerability to Reward Hacking:** With simple reward structures, agents can exploit the reward function by repeatedly performing easy actions (e.g., "terminate") to gain points, leading to degenerate policies that do not reflect diverse human behaviors.
7. **Need for Decomposed and Fine-Grained Feedback:** Human shopping behavior involves both high-level reasoning (rationales) and detailed actions. Existing methods often treat these monolithically or lack fine-grained feedback for components of complex actions.

To address these motivations, Shop-R1 aims to:
- Decompose the human behavior simulation task into rationale generation and action prediction, allowing for tailored reward signals for each.
- Leverage internal model signals for rationale generation, offering a self-supervised approach to guide reasoning in the absence of ground-truth rationales.
- Introduce a hierarchical reward structure for action prediction that provides partial credit, densifying the reward landscape and promoting more robust learning.
- Implement difficulty-aware reward scaling to mitigate reward hacking and incentivize the model to learn more complex, realistic actions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of Shop-R1 is structured around a reinforcement learning framework, built upon an initial supervised fine-tuning (SFT) phase, to simulate human online shopping behavior.

**Problem Formulation:**
The task is defined as predicting the next rationale ($r_t$) and action ($a_t$) given the cumulative web context ($c_{1...t}$) and action history ($a_{1...t-1}$). The web context is provided as a simplified HTML structure. The action space consists of three primary types: 'type_and_submit' (for text input and submission), 'click' (for interactive elements), and 'terminate' (to end a session). The goal is to learn the distributional tendencies of real user decision-making, acknowledging its non-deterministic nature.

**Cold Start with Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):**
The model is first initialized through SFT using annotated trajectories. Each rationale in this training data is automatically generated by Claude 3.5 Sonnet. This SFT phase serves as a "cold start" for the subsequent RL, grounding the model in realistic patterns of rationale and action. The SFT objective maximizes the likelihood of ground-truth rationale-action pairs, conditioned on the cumulative context and action history. This step is crucial for stability, sample efficiency, and for teaching the model to produce high-quality, structured text outputs, which are difficult to acquire solely through RL due to sparse rewards.

**Shop-R1 Reinforcement Learning Framework:**
Shop-R1 decomposes each prediction step into two sub-tasks: rationale generation and action prediction, each guided by distinct reward signals. The overall objective is to maximize a combined reward signal, regularized by a Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence to a fixed reference policy ($\pi_{ref}$), which typically is the SFT-trained model.
$max_{\pi_\theta} E_{r,a \sim \pi_\theta(q)} [v(a) + \alpha s(r) + -\beta KL(\pi_\theta(r, a | q) \parallel \pi_{ref}(r, a | q))]$
where $v(a)$ is the action prediction reward, $s(r)$ is the self-certainty reward for rationale generation, and $\alpha$, $\beta$ are hyperparameters.

The reward scheme incorporates four distinct components:

1. **Format Reward:** This is a binary reward that encourages the model to generate responses in a structured JSON format (containing 'rationale' and 'action' keys). A non-zero reward (0.5) is given only if the output conforms to the expected format, ensuring parseability for downstream evaluation and reward computation. Otherwise, a zero reward is applied.

2. **Rationale Generation (Self-Certainty Reward):** To address the difficulty of obtaining ground-truth rationales, a self-certainty score is employed. This score quantifies the model's confidence in its generated rationale by computing the average Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence between the model's predicted vocabulary distribution and a uniform distribution, over the tokens in the generated rationale. Higher KL divergence values indicate greater certainty and consistency in the model's reasoning, contributing to training stability without external supervision.

3. **Action Prediction (Hierarchical Reward Scheme with Difficulty-Aware Scaling - DARS):** This component replaces sparse binary signals with a hierarchical reward structure to provide more nuanced feedback, stabilize training, and discourage reward hacking.
 - **Coarse-grained Reward (Action Type):** A base reward (0.3) is granted for correctly predicting the high-level action type (e.g., 'click', 'type_and_submit', 'terminate').
 - **Fine-grained Reward (Sub-action Attributes and Values):**
 - For actions like 'click' and 'type_and_submit', additional rewards are given for the correctness of sub-action attributes (e.g., `name` attribute of a button) and long-text values (e.g., search query `text`).
 - Discrete sub-action attributes (e.g., `name` presence) are evaluated with a partial reward (e.g., 0.2 for `click`, 0.1 for `type_and_submit`).
 - Free-form text values (e.g., `name` value for 'click', `text` value for 'type_and_submit') are rewarded based on their ROUGE-L similarity to the ground truth, but only if the similarity exceeds a preset threshold (e.g., 0.75).
 - **Difficulty-Aware Reward Scaling (DARS):** A DARS factor (default 1000) is applied to rewards for correctly predicting long-text sub-components. This amplification incentivizes the model to tackle more complex, high-entropy actions (like generating search queries or specific button names) rather than repeatedly selecting simpler actions like 'terminate' for easy points, thereby preventing reward hacking. The 'terminate' action has no sub-actions and thus only earns the coarse-level type reward.

**Training Details:**
- **Dataset:** SHOP-CART dataset (52,137 real-world shopping sessions) enhanced with Claude 3.5 Sonnet-generated rationales for SFT. Observations are simplified HTML.
- **Model:** Qwen-2.5-3B-Instruct is the base LLM, with experiments also conducted on 1.5B and 0.5B variants.
- **Algorithm:** Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) is used for RL.
- **Infrastructure:** NVIDIA A100 GPUs with Fully Sharded Data Parallelism (FSDP).
- **Hyperparameters:** Max context length 32k tokens, sampling temperature 0.6. SFT: 4 epochs, learning rate 2 × 10⁻⁵. RL: 500 steps, learning rate 1 × 10⁻⁷. DARS factor 1000, $\alpha = 0.005$, $\beta = 0.001$.

**Evaluation Metrics:**
Performance is assessed using:
- **Exact match accuracy:** A prediction is correct only if every relevant component (action type, all sub-action attributes, and text values) precisely matches the ground truth.
- **Action type accuracy:** Correctness of the high-level action type alone.
- **Action type F1 score:** A class-balanced metric for action type prediction.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of the Shop-R1 framework in simulating human online shopping behavior.

**Performance Comparison with Baselines (Table 2):**
- **Zero-shot prompting** yielded minimal exact-action accuracy (0.32%), indicating that generic instruction tuning alone is insufficient for long-horizon web behavior simulation.
- **RL with sparse binary rewards (from scratch)** achieved low exact-match accuracy (1.01%) and type accuracy (6.17%), highlighting the challenge of sparse feedback without prior grounding.
- **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)** alone significantly improved performance, reaching 16.76% exact-match accuracy and 22.25% action type accuracy. This confirmed the importance of dense, teacher-forced trajectories for learning structural knowledge and the format of long-text fields.
- **SFT + RL (Binary)** showed mixed results: exact-match accuracy slightly decreased to 16.55%, while type-level F1 improved to 28.07%. This suggested that while the agent learned to better guess coarse intent, it struggled to accurately reproduce the fine-grained, long-text values required for an exact match, due to the limitations of a brittle binary reward.
- **Shop-R1 (Ours)** achieved a substantial improvement, reaching 27.72% exact-action accuracy, representing a relative improvement of over 65% compared to SFT. It also boosted action-type accuracy to 36.40% and F1 to 31.28%. This simultaneous improvement across both coarse and fine-grained metrics indicated that Shop-R1 not only identified the correct intent more frequently but also reproduced long-text values with higher fidelity.

**Performance by Action Type (Table 3):**
- Analysis by action type ('click', 'type_and_submit', 'terminate') revealed that zero-shot prompting exhibited a clear "intent–content" split, where models could guess the general action type (e.g., 38.7% type accuracy for 'click') but almost never named the exact UI target (0.58% exact accuracy).
- SFT improved performance, but gains remained uneven, particularly for high-entropy arguments like search queries, suggesting that teacher-forcing alone did not provide sufficient credit assignment for such cases.
- Shop-R1 demonstrated higher exact-match accuracy across all action types (e.g., 7.39% for 'click', 7.53% for 'type_and_submit', 81.84% for 'terminate' for 3B model), indicating that the model learned to identify correct widgets and query text in addition to the correct action type. This highlighted the role of dense and structured feedback in overcoming the "no-reward" plateau and making reward hacking less economical.

**Ablation Study and Analysis:**
- **Model Size (Table 2 & 3):** Larger backbones (3B) exhibited stronger out-of-the-box priors. While SFT benefited all model sizes, Shop-R1 enabled each backbone to reach its best operating point. The 3B model showed balanced improvements across complex action types, whereas smaller models (0.5B) achieved high aggregate exact-match accuracy primarily by over-predicting the 'terminate' action (97.07% exact), demonstrating that scaling augments the ability to handle long-text, high-entropy actions.
- **Sampling Temperature (Figure 2):** Shop-R1 demonstrated robustness to sampling temperature in the 0.6–0.8 range. Action-type accuracy remained stable, while F1 scores declined with increasing temperature. Exact-match accuracy peaked at a moderate temperature (0.7), suggesting that a slight degree of stochasticity could help the model escape local maxima and generate full long-text arguments that greedy decoding might miss.
- **Training Component Contributions (Table 4):**
 - **Removing SFT warm-start** severely crippled the agent (4.63% exact accuracy), underscoring the necessity of a supervised prior for learning the structure of long-text arguments.
 - **Omitting the format reward** led to a drastic drop in accuracy (2.87% exact accuracy), as unparseable JSON outputs yielded zero credit, starving the learning process of gradient signals.
 - **Ablating the self-certainty (rationale) reward** reduced exact-match accuracy by 0.8%, while coarse intent prediction remained strong, indicating its role in refining long-text precision.
 - **Disabling difficulty-aware reward scaling (DARS) or reverting to a binary action reward** caused a reward-hacking pattern, where the model achieved comparable exact accuracy but its type-level F1 degraded significantly (11–12%), as it gravitated towards the easy, high-reward 'terminate' action.
 - The full Shop-R1 configuration, combining all components, achieved the best balance, demonstrating that each element (SFT, format reward, self-certainty reward, and hierarchical difficulty-scaled action rewards) is necessary for robust performance.
- **Whole-session vs. Latest-step Context (Table 5):** Removing the simplified HTML of previously visited pages from the action history reduced exact-match accuracy by nearly 50% (from 27.72% to 14.74%). This highlighted that while coarse action-type inference might still be possible from dialogue traces, providing concrete UI context is critical for generating fine-grained arguments (e.g., precise button labels or query strings) necessary for high-fidelity simulation.

**Generalizability (Appendix E):**
Experiments on the public OPeRA dataset showed consistent performance trends, confirming the generalizability of Shop-R1. Zero-shot prompting performed poorly, SFT provided substantial improvements, and Shop-R1 (SFT + RL) yielded the largest gains, particularly in exact-action accuracy.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Shop-R1 marks a development in the application of reinforcement learning to the complex domain of human behavior simulation in web-based environments. Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

1. **Advancement in Human Behavior Simulation:** Shop-R1 is noted as the first framework to integrate RL into simulation-oriented human behavior modeling in web-shopping. By specifically addressing the simulation aspect rather than just task completion, it contributes to developing agents that mimic human interaction patterns more accurately, including the non-deterministic and nuanced aspects.
2. **Addressing Limitations of Prior LLM-based Approaches:** The framework effectively mitigates key challenges of existing methods, such as the quality limitations of LLM-generated rationales for SFT and the inefficiency of sparse binary rewards in RL. This provides a more robust and effective training paradigm for LLM agents in interactive settings.
3. **Novel Hybrid Reward Design:** The proposed hybrid reward scheme, which combines a self-certainty signal for rationale generation with a hierarchical, difficulty-aware reward scheme for action prediction, offers a template for designing richer feedback mechanisms in complex, multi-component generation tasks. This design promotes stable learning, provides fine-grained credit assignment, and discourages reward hacking.
4. **Improved Fidelity and Interpretability:** By enabling LLMs to generate both rationales and actions with higher fidelity, Shop-R1 contributes to more interpretable and believable simulations. The explicit rationale generation stage, guided by self-certainty, allows for insights into the model's "thought process" leading to an action.
5. **Practical Applications in E-commerce and UX Research:** The ability to accurately simulate human online shopping behavior has several practical implications:
 - **Automated A/B Testing:** Generating realistic virtual user cohorts for testing website design changes or new features without requiring extensive human user panels.
 - **Personalized Recommendations:** Better understanding user behavior patterns to refine recommender systems.
 - **User Experience (UX) Research:** Identifying potential pain points or common user flows on a website by simulating diverse user behaviors.
 - **Synthetic Data Generation:** Creating large-scale, high-quality synthetic interaction data for training other models or for market research.
6. **Generalizability Across Web GUI Interactions:** The design of the action space and reward structure, which reflects general interaction patterns in Web GUIs (click, type_and_submit, terminate), suggests that the framework could be adapted and generalized to other simulation-oriented tasks involving various web-based graphical user interfaces, beyond just online shopping.
7. **Guidance for LLM Training:** The ablation studies provide insights into the importance of various training components, such as the necessity of SFT for linguistic priors, format rewards for parseability, rationale rewards for precision, and hierarchical/scaled rewards for preventing degenerate policies. These findings inform future research and development in training sophisticated LLM agents for interactive environments.

Overall, Shop-R1 presents a reinforcement learning approach that improves the capacity of LLMs to simulate complex, human-like behavior in online shopping, with potential broader implications for AI agents interacting with dynamic web environments.
