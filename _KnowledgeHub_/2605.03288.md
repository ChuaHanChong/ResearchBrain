# Neural Control: Adjoint Learning Through Equilibrium Constraints

> [!summary] Summary
> The "Neural Control" framework from the University of Michigan and UCLA addresses control challenges in physical systems with implicit equilibrium constraints, providing an adjoint-based method and receding-horizon control to efficiently manipulate multi-stable deformable objects. This approach achieved up to 127 times faster optimization and 6 orders of magnitude lower loss compared to gradient-free methods in simulations, also demonstrating robust qualitative sim-to-real transfer for robotic manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Controlling physical systems where states are implicitly defined by equilibrium conditions, making direct gradient computation complex and computationally intensive for long horizons.
- The high computational cost and numerical fragility of backpropagating through iterative implicit equilibrium solvers (e.g., Newton-CG) over extended control sequences.
- Managing multi-stability and path dependence, where identical control inputs can lead to different stable configurations based on actuation history, complicating robust long-horizon planning.

## Method

- Developed an adjoint-based method for computing trajectory-dependent gradients that differentiates equilibrium conditions, bypassing the need to unroll internal iterations of iterative solvers.
- Introduced a Receding-Horizon Continuation (RHC) scheme to segment control tasks, re-anchoring optimization to realized equilibria, which enhances robustness against multi-stability and path dependence.
- Employed a neural network controller, optimized using these efficient adjoint gradients within the RHC loop, to generate control signals for system boundary conditions.

## Results

- The Adjoint+RHC method demonstrated significant efficiency, being 19 to 127 times faster and achieving 3 to 6 orders of magnitude lower final loss than gradient-free baselines (SPSA, CEM) in numerical simulations.
- It consistently achieved better performance across tasks including point-to-point targeting, trajectory tracking, and multi-stable shape formation, reliably selecting specified equilibrium branches.
- Qualitative real-world experiments with a 6-DoF robotic arm manipulating an elastic strip validated the framework's practical applicability and robust sim-to-real transfer for compliant object manipulation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Adjoint-based gradients, derived from implicit differentiation of equilibrium conditions, offer a memory-efficient and computationally tractable way to optimize implicitly-defined physical systems.
> - The 'frozen-tangent' approximation used in the adjoint method is empirically sufficient for achieving first-order accurate gradients, balancing theoretical rigor with practical efficiency.
> - Combining adjoint-based gradient computation with a receding-horizon approach is crucial for reliable control in multi-stable physical systems, preventing error accumulation and unintended state transitions over long control horizons.
