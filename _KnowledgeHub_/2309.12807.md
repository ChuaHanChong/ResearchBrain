# Teacher-Student Reinforcement Learning for Mapless Navigation using a Planetary Space Rover

> [!summary] Summary
> A two-stage reinforcement learning framework was developed for mapless navigation of planetary rovers, addressing the reality gap by training a student policy on noisy, offline data derived from a high-performing teacher policy. This approach from Aalborg University yielded enhanced robustness to sensor noise and improved zero-shot sim-to-real transferability on a physical rover.

## Problem

- Traditional navigation methods for planetary rovers can be overly conservative, hindering efficient exploration in complex, unknown extraterrestrial terrains.
- Reinforcement Learning (RL) policies trained in simulation often face a "reality gap," where performance degrades when transferred to physical robots due to discrepancies like real-world sensor noise and environmental unpredictability.
- Developing robust mapless navigation for high-stakes space missions requires strategies resilient to the inherent noise and imperfections of real-world sensor data.

## Method

- A two-stage deep ==reinforcement learning== approach was implemented, where a teacher policy is first trained online in a GPU-accelerated physics simulation (NVIDIA Isaac Sim) with noise-free, privileged observations.
- An offline dataset of observations and teacher actions is collected and then augmented with a specific noise model to simulate real-world sensor imperfections.
- A student policy is subsequently trained in a supervised manner, imitating the teacher's actions but learning from the noisy offline data, using a Recurrent Neural Network (GRU) with an attention mechanism to handle noisy inputs.

## Results

- In noisy simulations, the student policy achieved success rates of **90%** and **63%** on two challenging terrains (T1(n) and T2(n), respectively), substantially outperforming the teacher's **77%** and **54%**, and a domain-randomized teacher's **66%** and **43%**.
- During zero-shot sim-to-real transfer on a physical rover, the student agent consistently reached goals more rapidly and exhibited fewer oscillations in its action outputs compared to the teacher agent.
- The student policy demonstrated improved noise resilience and more consistent behavioral performance in physical deployment, aligning its real-world behavior more closely with its simulated performance than the teacher policy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Training the initial teacher policy without domain randomization led to better convergence rates in simulation, suggesting that robustness is best introduced in a subsequent learning stage.
> - Explicitly training the student policy on data with applied noise significantly improved its robustness and performance in noisy simulated environments, outperforming both the noise-naïve teacher and a teacher trained with direct domain randomization.
> - The student's GRU-based architecture, utilizing an attention gate, effectively reconstructed latent representations from noisy inputs, enabling it to learn resilient behaviors mimicking the teacher's optimal actions.
