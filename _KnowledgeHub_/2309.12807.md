# Teacher-Student Reinforcement Learning for Mapless Navigation using a Planetary Space Rover

> [!summary] Summary
> A two-stage reinforcement learning framework was developed for mapless navigation of planetary rovers, addressing the reality gap by training a student policy on noisy, offline data derived from a high-performing teacher policy. This approach from Aalborg University yielded enhanced robustness to sensor noise and improved zero-shot sim-to-real transferability on a physical rover.

## Problem

- Traditional navigation methods for planetary rovers can be overly conservative, hindering efficient exploration in complex, unknown extraterrestrial terrains.
- Reinforcement Learning (RL) policies trained in simulation often face a "reality gap," where performance degrades when transferred to physical robots due to discrepancies like real-world sensor noise and environmental unpredictability.
- Developing robust mapless navigation for high-stakes space missions requires strategies resilient to the inherent noise and imperfections of real-world sensor data.

## Method

- A two-stage deep ==reinforcement learning== approach was implemented, where a teacher policy is first trained online in a GPU-accelerated physics simulation (NVIDIA Isaac Sim) with noise-free, privileged observations.
- An offline dataset of observations and teacher actions is collected and then augmented with a specific noise model to simulate real-world sensor imperfections.
- A student policy is subsequently trained in a supervised manner, imitating the teacher's actions but learning from the noisy offline data, using a Recurrent Neural Network (GRU) with an attention mechanism to handle noisy inputs.

## Results

- In noisy simulations, the student policy achieved success rates of **90%** and **63%** on two challenging terrains (T1(n) and T2(n), respectively), substantially outperforming the teacher's **77%** and **54%**, and a domain-randomized teacher's **66%** and **43%**.
- During zero-shot sim-to-real transfer on a physical rover, the student agent consistently reached goals more rapidly and exhibited fewer oscillations in its action outputs compared to the teacher agent.
- The student policy demonstrated improved noise resilience and more consistent behavioral performance in physical deployment, aligning its real-world behavior more closely with its simulated performance than the teacher policy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Training the initial teacher policy without domain randomization led to better convergence rates in simulation, suggesting that robustness is best introduced in a subsequent learning stage.
> - Explicitly training the student policy on data with applied noise significantly improved its robustness and performance in noisy simulated environments, outperforming both the noise-naïve teacher and a teacher trained with direct domain randomization.
> - The student's GRU-based architecture, utilizing an attention gate, effectively reconstructed latent representations from noisy inputs, enabling it to learn resilient behaviors mimicking the teacher's optimal actions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of autonomous navigation capabilities for planetary space rovers is a critical area of research, driven by the increasing ambition of future space missions. Traditional methods, such as NASA's Approximate Clearance Evaluation (ACE) and Probabilistic ACE (p-ACE), have been instrumental but are noted for their potential conservatism in complex terrains. This necessitates the exploration of alternative approaches that can offer greater adaptability and derive optimal navigation strategies in unknown and unstructured environments.

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has emerged as a promising paradigm for robotic autonomy due to its ability to learn complex behaviors through interaction. However, a significant challenge in applying RL to real-world robotics, particularly in high-stakes contexts like space exploration, is the "reality gap." This refers to the discrepancy between simulated training environments and physical systems, leading to performance degradation when policies trained in simulation are transferred to real robots. The reality gap is exacerbated in planetary navigation by unpredictable terrains, varying environmental factors, and the inherent noise present in real-world sensor data.

Prior research has applied RL to autonomous vehicles in structured environments and even for tasks like grasping on planetary surfaces, often relying on 3D observations. However, many of these studies have not fully addressed the complexities of sim-to-real transfer. Techniques to mitigate the reality gap include domain randomization, adversarial RL, and transfer learning, each with its own advantages and limitations. Domain randomization, for instance, introduces noise into simulation inputs to enhance adaptability, while transfer learning involves continued training on the physical system, which is impractical for extraterrestrial deployments due to risk and resource constraints.

Mapless navigation, which relies solely on local 3D observations without constructing global maps or requiring external localization systems like GPS, is particularly relevant for planetary rovers operating in unfamiliar environments with limited data sources. This approach minimizes computational demands and emphasizes real-time decision-making.

This paper positions itself within this landscape by introducing a novel two-stage RL approach designed to specifically address the reality gap for mapless navigation on planetary rovers. By leveraging a teacher-student policy learning paradigm, inspired by the "learning by cheating" methodology, the work aims to bridge the gap between policies trained in ideal simulated conditions and those requiring robustness to real-world uncertainties and noise. It explores the application of this specific sim-to-real transfer technique, which has been prevalent in control frameworks, to the relatively unexplored domain of planetary mapless navigation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the increasing demand for enhanced navigation autonomy in future planetary space missions. Achieving mission objectives efficiently and safely in complex extraterrestrial environments necessitates rovers capable of sophisticated autonomous navigation. Existing navigation systems, such as NASA’s ACE, while innovative, can be overly conservative, hindering progress in challenging terrains. This limitation underscores the need for more adaptable and robust navigation strategies.

Reinforcement learning (RL) is recognized for its potential to develop adaptive and optimal control strategies for robots. However, its practical application is hampered by the "reality gap"—the difficulty in transferring policies trained in simulations to real-world scenarios. This gap is particularly pronounced for mapless navigation on Mars and Moon-like terrains, where unpredictable geological features, varying illumination, and environmental factors contribute to high levels of data noise and simulation inaccuracies. Effective navigation in such contexts requires a method that is specifically designed to be robust to these real-world complexities and sensor data imperfections.

The key objectives of this work are articulated as follows:

1. **Develop a Two-Stage Deep Reinforcement Learning Approach:** The central objective is to design and implement a novel two-stage end-to-end deep reinforcement learning framework for mapless navigation. This framework is specifically tailored for planetary rovers operating in challenging, Martian-like terrains.
2. **Mitigate the Reality Gap:** A core objective is to reduce the reality gap, thereby improving the sim-to-real transferability of navigation policies. This is addressed through a teacher-student paradigm, where a teacher policy trained in simulation guides a student policy, which is trained to be robust against real-world uncertainties.
3. **Craft Robust Navigation Policies:** The goal is to develop navigation policies capable of handling diverse and complex terrains, including various obstacles such as holes, valleys, and rocks of different sizes. These policies should exhibit resilience to noise and unexpected environmental variations.
4. **Leverage GPU-Accelerated Simulation:** To facilitate efficient training and comprehensive exploration of agent capabilities, the research aims to utilize a parallelized, physics-based 3D simulation environment. This includes introducing a GPU-accelerated environment within NVIDIA Isaac Sim specifically configured for planetary settings.
5. **Utilize a Teacher-Student Model with Offline Noisy Data:** The methodology seeks to employ a teacher-student model where the teacher, trained with optimal (privileged) data, guides a student. The student is then trained offline on noisy data derived from the teacher's behavior, aiming to mimic the teacher's proficiency while gaining robustness to real-world noise.
6. **Validate Sim-to-Real Performance:** A crucial objective is to validate the practical applicability of the developed policies through zero-shot sim-to-real transfer. This involves testing the policies on a custom-designed physical rover that mimics the kinematics of NASA's Perseverance rover. The validation aims to demonstrate improved behavioral performance, heightened noise resilience, and more effective transfer compared to policies without this two-stage approach.

In summary, the motivation is to overcome the limitations of current planetary navigation systems and the reality gap in RL, while the objectives focus on developing and validating a robust, autonomous, and transferrable navigation solution for future space exploration missions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces a two-stage reinforcement learning approach for mapless navigation of planetary rovers, primarily designed to enhance sim-to-real transferability and robustness to noise. The methodology incorporates a teacher-student framework, offline data generation, and end-to-end learning within a procedurally generated physics simulation environment.

**Simulation Environment:**
The primary simulation environment is NVIDIA Isaac Sim 2022.1.1, which provides a GPU-accelerated physics engine. The terrains are procedurally generated using a NASA algorithm, incorporating features like small bumps, larger hills with smooth slopes, and various rock models. Collision detection is implemented using ray-tracing, distinguishing between climbable and non-climbable rocks. Rovers are spawned in a fixed grid with randomized orientations, and goal points are placed randomly on the perimeter of a 9-meter radius circle around the rover. The simulation runs 512 rovers in parallel at 60 Hz, with a controller frequency of 5 Hz, using the Isaac Gym API and the `skrl` RL-framework for Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO).

**Physical Rover Setup:**
A custom-designed physical rover, kinematically mirroring NASA’s Perseverance rover, is used for real-world testing. It has a footprint of 1.03 × 1.05 meters and weighs 40 kg. It is equipped with an NVIDIA Orin for data processing, DB59 motors for steering and driving, a ZED 2i camera for heightmap extraction, and an Intel RealSense T265 camera for positional tracking (relative distance and heading to the target goal). The ZED 2i camera's point cloud is converted into a grid to extract a heightmap, simulating the sensor input used in the training.

**Action and Observation Space:**
- **Action Space:** Defined by linear velocity ($v_{lin}$) and angular velocity ($v_{ang}$), controlling the rover via Ackermann steering. A "turn-on-the-spot" mode is enabled if the ratio between velocities is below 0.15 for maneuvering in constrained environments.
- **Observation Space ($o_{teacher}$):** Comprises proprioceptive and exteroceptive inputs.
 - **Proprioceptive Input ($o_p$):** Euclidean distance to target, angle between rover and target, and previous actions.
 - **Exteroceptive Input ($o_e$):** A dense heightmap (5 cm sample distance) and a sparse heightmap (15 cm sample distance), covering areas up to 4 meters from the rover.

**Composite Reward Function:**
A reward function is designed to encourage reaching the target without collisions, combining several terms:
- $r_d$: Rewards decreasing distance to the target.
- $r_a$: Penalizes oscillations in actions.
- $r_v$: Penalizes driving backward ($v_{lin} < 0$).
- $r_h$: Penalizes large angles (greater than 115 degrees) between the rover's heading and the target.
- Collision penalty ($\omega_c$): Not explicitly detailed in the reward function equation but mentioned as a PPO hyperparameter.

**Teacher-Student Framework:**

1. **Teacher Policy Training (Online):**
 - The teacher policy is trained online using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) within the Isaac Sim environment.
 - It receives privileged information in the form of noise-free observations.
 - **Network Architecture:** A Gaussian policy model. It uses two separate linear layer encoders (each two layers of size with LeakyReLU activation) for the dense and sparse heightmap inputs, generating latent representations ($l_d$, $l_s$). These are concatenated with the proprioceptive input and fed into a three-layer MLP (size with LeakyReLU) to output actions.
 - Hyperparameters for PPO (e.g., learning rate, KL threshold, discount factor) and reward weights are specified.

2. **Data Collection for Student Policy:**
 - After the teacher policy is trained, it is run in inference mode within the simulation.
 - Observations (proprioceptive and exteroceptive) and the inferred actions are logged from 512 rovers over 1500 timesteps. This process generates an offline dataset that is initially devoid of noise.

3. **Student Policy Training (Offline):**
 - The student policy is trained in a supervised manner, aiming to imitate the teacher's actions but using noisy input data.
 - **Noise Model:** A noise model, inspired by Miki et al., is applied to the exteroceptive input (heightmaps) *before* it is fed to the student policy. This model has three modes: small deviations ($\rho = 0.6$), small deviations with an offset ($\rho = 0.3$), and large deviations ($\rho = 0.1$). Additionally, a percentage of heightmap points is set to zero to simulate missing information (e.g., occlusions, reflections). The noise parameters (standard deviation, offset, zeroed percentage) are specified.
 - **Observation Space ($o_{student}$):** Noisy exteroceptive input combined with clean proprioceptive input.
 - **Network Architecture:** A deterministic policy modeled as a time sequence using a Recurrent Neural Network (RNN), specifically a multilayer Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU). The GRU, along with an attention gate mechanism, acts as a "belief encoder" to reconstruct ground truth-like latent representations ($l_d$, $l_s$) from the noisy input, leveraging previous hidden states. This reconstructed belief state and proprioceptive input are then fed into a three-layer MLP (size) to generate actions.
 - **Optimization:** The student policy is optimized using the Mean Squared Error (squared L2 norm) for its policy loss, comparing its actions to those of the teacher.

This two-stage approach allows the teacher to learn optimal behaviors in a clean environment, and the student to learn to mimic these behaviors while becoming robust to the sensor noise and uncertainties characteristic of real-world deployment.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted in both simulation and on a physical rover to evaluate the performance of the teacher and student agents and assess the sim-to-real applicability of the proposed approach.

**A. Simulation Results**

1. **Convergence Rate Comparison:**
 - An initial test compared the convergence rate of training a policy with and without domain randomization for the teacher agent.
 - The results indicated that the policy trained *without* domain randomization exhibited a better convergence rate.
 - Policies trained *with* domain randomization converged to a suboptimal policy, providing a justification for the two-stage teacher-student approach where the teacher is trained in a less noisy environment initially.

2. **Teacher vs. Student Performance (Inference Mode):**
 - The teacher and student agents were simulated in inference mode across two types of terrains: T1 (with large rocks) and T2 (more challenging, with large and small climbable rocks).
 - Performance was evaluated based on the percentage of rovers reaching the goal and the average duration of successful episodes.
 - Gaussian noise (standard deviation of 0.1) was added to the heightmap, and 10% of points were zeroed in "noisy" tests (T1(n), T2(n)) to assess robustness.

 - **Without Noise (T1, T2):**
 - The teacher policy demonstrated slightly superior performance in both T1 (92.6% success rate, 36.9s avg duration) and T2 (87% success rate, 34.0s avg duration) compared to the student policy (T1: 91.4%, 40.9s; T2: 85%, 45.9s). This suggests that in ideal, noise-free conditions, the directly trained teacher is marginally more efficient.

 - **With Noise (T1(n), T2(n)):**
 - The teacher's performance significantly deteriorated with the introduction of noise. For T1(n), its success rate dropped to 77% (67.0s avg duration), and for T2(n), it further decreased to 54% (69.5s avg duration). This indicates the teacher policy's sensitivity to noisy observations.
 - The student agent, however, exhibited improved performance in the presence of noise. For T1(n), it achieved a 90% success rate (42.8s avg duration), and for T2(n), it reached 63% (42.7s avg duration).
 - This demonstrates that the student agent, explicitly trained with noisy data, was more robust and outperformed the teacher in noisy simulation environments, especially in the more challenging terrain T2(n).
 - An agent labeled "Teacher noise" (presumably a teacher trained with domain randomization, as indicated by Figure 6) showed notably lower success rates across all noisy terrains (66% for T1(n) and 43% for T2(n)), further supporting the efficacy of the teacher-student approach over direct domain randomization for the teacher.

**B. Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer**

- **Initial Challenges with Obstacles:** Both agents initially faced difficulties navigating obstacles directly in front of them on the physical rover, leading to unsuccessful tests in terrains with complex obstacles. The agents performed better when obstacles were slightly to the side.
- **Performance in Flat Environment with Noise:** To isolate the impact of sensor noise, both agents were tested in a flat physical environment without obstacles. Five tests were conducted for each agent.
- **Student Superiority:**
 - The student agent consistently reached the goal more rapidly than the teacher agent.
 - Crucially, the student exhibited fewer oscillations in its action outputs (linear and angular velocity) when approaching the goal. This was attributed to the student's training to mitigate the noise inherent in real-life sensor data, which the teacher policy struggled to manage.
- **Alignment with Simulation:** The student agent's behavior in real-world scenarios more closely mirrored its simulated performance compared to the teacher agent. The pronounced oscillations observed in the teacher's real-world actions due to noise were substantially diminished by the student's method.
- **Conclusion on Sim-to-Real:** The findings from the zero-shot sim-to-real transfer indicated that the proposed two-stage learning strategy effectively enhanced the transferability of the agent. The student policy demonstrated heightened noise resilience and more consistent behavioral performance in physical deployment.

Overall, the results suggest that while a teacher policy trained in ideal simulation conditions performs marginally better without noise, the student policy, trained with deliberately introduced noise to mimic real-world conditions, is significantly more robust and achieves improved performance and stability during zero-shot sim-to-real transfer.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces a two-stage reinforcement learning paradigm that addresses the critical challenge of the "reality gap" in deploying autonomous planetary rovers. The findings have several implications for the field of robotic autonomy and space exploration.

**Mitigation of the Reality Gap:** The primary significance of this work lies in its successful demonstration of a method to effectively bridge the reality gap. By training a student policy on noisy, offline data collected from a high-performing teacher policy, the authors show that policies can be made more robust to real-world sensor noise and environmental uncertainties. This is a foundational step for transferring complex learned behaviors from simulation to physical systems, especially in scenarios where extensive real-world training or fine-tuning is impractical or risky.

**Enhanced Autonomous Navigation for Space Rovers:** The developed methodology has the potential to significantly enhance the autonomous navigation capabilities of future planetary rovers. By enabling robust mapless navigation in challenging, unpredictable terrains, rovers could operate more independently, reducing reliance on remote human intervention and accelerating mission timelines. This could lead to more ambitious and scientifically productive exploration missions.

**Robustness to Real-World Noise:** The student policy's demonstrated resilience to noise, both in simulations and on the physical rover, is a notable contribution. Planetary environments are characterized by varying lighting conditions, dust, and imperfect sensor readings, all of which contribute to noisy data. A navigation policy that can effectively filter or account for this noise is critical for reliable operation in extraterrestrial settings.

**Efficient Policy Development:** The use of a GPU-accelerated simulation environment (NVIDIA Isaac Sim) with parallelized training allows for efficient data collection and rapid iteration during the teacher policy's development. Subsequently, the offline training of the student policy on this collected data, combined with a noise model, offers a computationally efficient way to develop robust policies without requiring extensive and time-consuming real-world experiments. This reduces the cost and risk associated with robotic development for space applications.

**Broader Applicability of Teacher-Student Learning:** While applied to planetary navigation, the teacher-student framework, particularly with the "learning by cheating" inspiration and the integration of specific noise models, could have broader applicability to other robotic domains. Any application facing significant sim-to-real transfer challenges due to sensor noise, dynamic inaccuracies, or environmental variability could potentially benefit from this two-stage learning approach.

**Foundation for Future Research:** This work establishes a foundation for several future research directions. These include investigating the impact of diverse and dynamic noise types on teacher observations, optimizing noise parameters for even greater robustness, and refining the student network's architecture for enhanced performance. Crucially, the emphasis on further aligning simulation dynamics with real-world rover behavior highlights a continuous effort toward increasing the fidelity and reliability of autonomous systems for space exploration.

In summary, this research contributes to the advancement of autonomous robotics by offering a practical and effective method to overcome the reality gap, thereby paving the way for more resilient and capable planetary rovers.
