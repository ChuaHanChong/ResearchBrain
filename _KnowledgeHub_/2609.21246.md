# VLA-Scope: Shift-Aware Failure Prediction for Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> VLA-Scope is a two-stage failure predictor for frozen VLA policies under distribution shift. An OOD Characterizer uses pooled initial image and language embeddings to detect OOD inputs and classify seven shift types; a shared logistic regression then fuses the predicted type, 14 action-prefix features, and a PCA-compressed cumulative hidden-state average. On OpenVLA with LIBERO-Spatial it reaches 0.8497 failure ROC-AUC at 60 actions, beating ActProbe and SAFE.

## Problem

- Binary OOD detectors flag unfamiliar inputs but neither identify the shift type nor predict execution failure, yet VLA policies often still succeed under out-of-distribution conditions.
- Runtime failure monitors rely either on action behavior (ActProbe, FIPER, Sentinel) or internal policy representations (SAFE), leaving their complementarity under input shifts unexamined.
- Task-driven OOD methods tie shift to policy performance bounds but do not separate characterizing the initial input shift from predicting an individual rollout's eventual outcome.

## Method

- **OOD Characterizer**: mean-pools image and text token embeddings of the initial input into an 8192-dimensional vector; two class-balanced ==logistic regressions== detect ID/OOD (gate at 0.5) and classify seven shift types.
- Action-prefix features: 14 statistics over decoded commands, per-axis translation and rotation mean magnitudes and adjacent-step changes, plus mean gripper command and gripper switching rate, updated every step.
- **Failure Risk Predictor**: cumulatively averages final-layer last-prompt-token hidden states from step 10, compresses via ==PCA== to 32 dimensions, and fuses them with one-hot type and action features in a shared ==logistic regression==.

## Results

- OOD Characterizer reaches **0.9454** detection ROC-AUC (**84.57%** TPR, **6.20%** FPR) and **91.00%** shift-type accuracy under leave-one-task-out cross-validation, versus **0.8176** for an ID-only cosine nearest-neighbor baseline.
- On all **1,400** OOD rollouts, failure ROC-AUC at 60 actions is **0.8497**, beating ActProbe **0.8132**, SAFE-MLP **0.8055**, SAFE-LSTM **0.7414**, with lower CPU latency (**0.893 ms**); margin over ActProbe shrinks to **0.0074** at 30 actions.
- Ablation: removing cumulative representation drops step-60 ROC-AUC to **0.7906**, removing action features to **0.8344**; cumulative averaging cuts mean step-to-step risk change from **3.52** to **0.70** percentage points.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - OOD is not failure: shift detection should be decoupled from outcome prediction, since policies often succeed under shift and every shift category mixes successes with failures.
> - Action statistics and internal representations are complementary failure signals; cheap linear fusion of both can beat heavier recurrent or MLP probes built on either alone.
> - Temporal aggregation of hidden states stabilizes risk without output smoothing, but alarming on any threshold crossing still flags half of successful rollouts; alarm rules need care.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Overview

A robot instructed to "put the black bowl on the plate" may encounter a changed object layout and still complete a secure grasp, or it may encounter visual noise and produce repeated corrections, oscillating translation, and repeated gripper switching. These are different events: an unfamiliar input and a failing execution. VLA-Scope addresses the gap between them—how to estimate, from a partial rollout alone, whether the robot is heading toward failure—without changing the frozen policy or waiting for the failure to become visually obvious.

Left: failure rates across seven OOD categories—Camera 77.5%, Robot 67.5%, Noise 52.0%, Language 40.0%, Light 31.5%, Background 28.5%, and Layout 27.5%—from 200 rollouts per category. Upper right: a Layout example labeled "Object secured" and "Low risk," with a smooth translation trace and a gripper command that switches and then remains steady. Lower right: a Noise example labeled "Repeated corrections" and "High risk," with an oscillatory translation trace, repeated gripper switching, robot-motion corrections, and an inset labeled "Occluded camera view." Every category contains both successful and failed rollouts, so category-level rates provide context but do not determine an individual outcome.

The monitor works in two stages. At initialization it characterizes the input as in-distribution or out-of-distribution (OOD) and assigns one of seven shift categories. During execution it accumulates an internal execution representation, combines it with action-prefix statistics and the predicted shift category, and updates a failure-risk score. The strongest result: at step 60, the full monitor reaches ROC-AUC 0.8497 on all 1,400 OOD rollouts under ten-task leave-one-task-out cross-validation with a frozen OpenVLA policy—versus 0.7906 without the cumulative representation. ROC-AUC is the area under the receiver-operating-characteristic curve; here it measures how well scores rank eventual failures above eventual successes across thresholds.

### 2. From "shifted" to "at risk"

Top: observation and instruction enter a VLA, which emits actions and drives execution. Lower left, Module 1 "OOD Characterizer": pools image and text tokens into an initial representation, outputs ID/OOD and OOD type, and enables risk scoring only for OOD inputs. Lower right, Module 2 "Failure Risk Predictor": begins at $k \ge 10$; holds a fixed seven-dimensional predicted-type one-hot; derives 14-dimensional action features from magnitude, adjacent change, and gripper mean and switching rate; cumulatively averages 4,096-dimensional execution-step representations from steps 10 through $k$, reduces them to 32 dimensions with standardization and PCA; concatenates all inputs to 53 dimensions; and applies shared logistic regression. The plotted risk curve is explicitly schematic.

At initialization, VLA-Scope mean-pools valid image tokens and valid text tokens separately into two 4,096-dimensional vectors, concatenated to 8,192 dimensions. One logistic classifier estimates ID versus OOD at a shared threshold of 0.5; only scores above it enable the risk predictor. The predicted shift category is converted to a fixed seven-dimensional one-hot vector and is not updated later, so this module characterizes shifts present at initialization, not newly emerging shifts.

During execution, VLA-Scope derives 14 action features from the prefix: six mean absolute translation and rotation magnitudes, six mean adjacent-command changes, mean gripper command, and gripper switching rate. In parallel, it retains the 4,096-dimensional final-layer hidden vector at the last prompt-token position that generated each action, averages vectors from step 10 through the current step, standardizes them, and compresses them to 32 dimensions with PCA. The shared logistic regression receives all 53 inputs and updates a score from step 10 onward without changing OpenVLA's actions.

### 3. What the held-out rollouts show

The evaluation uses a frozen OpenVLA policy on ten LIBERO-Spatial tasks. Each task contributes 50 ID cases and 20 OOD cases per category, yielding 1,400 OOD rollouts for risk evaluation containing 751 eventual successes and 649 failures. Ten-fold leave-one-group-out cross-validation holds out one entire task per fold. The OOD characterizer reaches ROC-AUC 0.9454, TPR 84.57%, and FPR 6.20%, with category accuracy 91.00%; however, category detection TPR ranges from 100% for Camera to 61% for Layout, so category recognition is not equally reliable across perturbations.

On all 1,400 OOD rollouts, the failure-risk ROC-AUC rises from 0.6852 at step 10 to 0.7675 at step 30 and 0.8497 at step 60; the step-60 thresholded operating point has TPR 70.72%, FPR 12.52%, and F1 76.37%. At the same checkpoint, VLA-Scope scores 0.8497 versus ActProbe 0.8132, SAFE-MLP 0.8055, and SAFE-LSTM 0.7414, under the authors' OpenVLA/LIBERO-Spatial retraining protocol; these comparisons do not establish universal superiority across policies, robots, or datasets.

The ablation isolates which stream matters more: removing the cumulative representation falls to 0.7906; removing action features falls to 0.8344. Both streams contribute, but the accumulated execution representation produces the larger numerical drop. The paper also reports higher median translation change in failures than successes at step 60 (0.1005 versus 0.0743), while cautioning that action features may capture consequences of a failed grasp or repeated corrections rather than physical causes.

### 4. Context needs execution evidence

Shift type supplies useful context, while accumulated internal execution representations and action behavior refine the ranking as the rollout unfolds. The 0.8497 result is an offline diagnostic evaluation on the sampled OpenVLA/LIBERO-Spatial protocol; it does not demonstrate an intervention policy or proof of improved physical task success. At threshold $d(z_0) > 0.5$, the OOD gate covers 1,184 cases and excludes 216—including 45 eventual failures—that receive no risk score; an ID decision is not a prediction of task success.
