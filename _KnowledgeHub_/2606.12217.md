# Making Foresight Actionable: Repurposing Representation Alignment in World Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> The research introduces Action-Grounded Representation Alignment (AGRA) to overcome the "action-grounding gap" in World Action Models (WAMs), where visually plausible predictions fail to guarantee accurate robot actions. AGRA regularizes the world-action interface by aligning a video diffusion model's intermediate features with spatially coherent semantic representations from a frozen foundation visual encoder, significantly boosting real-world manipulation success rates from 34% to 80% and enhancing out-of-distribution generalization.

## Problem

- World Action Models (WAMs) frequently generate visually plausible future predictions but produce inaccurate low-level control actions, indicating an "action-grounding gap."
- Representations learned by video diffusion models, optimized for pixel reconstruction, do not inherently align with the requirements for generating precise robotic actions.
- Action decoders in WAMs often distribute attention across task-irrelevant regions, rather than focusing on critical interaction areas, leading to erroneous action predictions.

## Method

- Introduced ==Action-Grounded Representation Alignment== (**AGRA**), an auxiliary objective to regularize the interface between the World Action Model's visual features and the action decoder.
- AGRA aligns intermediate hidden states from selected layers of the video diffusion model with spatially coherent semantic features from a frozen foundation visual encoder like DINOv2.
- The alignment uses a negative cosine similarity loss between projected world model hidden states and the DINOv2 target features, thereby making the world model's representations more "action-grounded."

## Results

- AGRA achieved an **80%** success rate in in-distribution real-world manipulation tasks, a substantial improvement over the baseline WAM's **34%**.
- The method consistently improved out-of-distribution generalization by 27-**32%** across semantic, instance-level, and attribute variations.
- Analysis of action-head attention and causal interventions confirmed that AGRA precisely localized the action decoder's attention to critical hand-object interaction regions and reduced sensitivity to task-irrelevant information.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Features optimized for visual reconstruction in video diffusion models are not intrinsically organized for robust, low-level action control, leading to an "action-grounding gap" in World Action Models.
> - Aligning a world model's intermediate features with self-supervised visual encoders (e.g., DINOv2) can improve the actionability of representations by enhancing spatial coherence and focusing the action decoder on relevant regions.
> - The optimal alignment for semantic grounding often occurs in shallower layers of the generative model, allowing deeper layers to model motion and fine-grained dynamics effectively.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robot manipulation is increasingly moving towards generalist robot policies that can robustly interact with the physical world by connecting visual perception, instructions, and continuous control. A prominent paradigm within this field involves equipping policies with predictive models of the world, often referred to as World Action Models (WAMs). WAMs typically leverage advancements in video generation models, particularly video diffusion models, to forecast future scene evolution, which then guides action generation. These models are attractive because large video models can encode rich spatiotemporal structures and provide features relevant to motion and task progress in robotic manipulation.

Existing approaches to integrating video prediction into robot control include explicit generation-and-control paradigms, where world models forecast pixel-level future frames under actions or instructions, followed by methods like Model-Predictive Control (MPC) or inverse dynamics models (IDMs). More recent trends explore implicit future representations for action prediction, conditioning policies on latent representations of world models that encode plausible future dynamics. Notable examples include LingbotVA and DreamZero, which jointly model video and action and demonstrate generalization capabilities. However, a common characteristic of these world model-based policies is their direct use of predictive features as control inputs without systematically investigating their suitability for downstream action control.

This paper addresses a specific challenge within this landscape: the "action-grounding gap." While video diffusion models excel at pixel reconstruction, the representations they learn for generating plausible visual futures do not inherently align with the requirements for generating accurate, low-level robotic actions. This contrasts with self-supervised visual encoders (e.g., DINOv2), which are known for learning spatially coherent and semantically grounded features. The work draws inspiration from representation alignment techniques, such as REPA, which have been used to improve the semantic quality and training efficiency of generative diffusion models by aligning their hidden states with features from foundation visual encoders. Unlike previous applications of representation alignment primarily focused on improving generative modeling, this research repurposes this technique specifically for action grounding in robot policy learning. It aims to bridge the identified gap by regularizing the interface between the world model's visual features and the action decoder, thereby making the world model's representations more directly actionable for robotic control.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to enhance the reliability and robustness of robot manipulation policies that utilize World Action Models (WAMs) by addressing an observed discrepancy between visual foresight and actionable control. The motivation stems from empirical observations that WAMs, despite generating visually plausible and task-consistent future predictions, frequently produce inaccurate control actions. This phenomenon indicates a fundamental "action-grounding gap" in their internal representations.

To diagnose this failure mode, the authors conducted specific analyses:
1. **Action-Head Attention Analysis**: This involved examining the cross-attention maps within the action decoder (Action DiT). The findings indicated that the action decoder often failed to concentrate its attention on task-relevant interaction regions, such as the contact areas between a robot's hand and an object. Instead, attention was observed to diffuse across task-irrelevant areas, like static background elements or non-interacting parts of the robot.
2. **Causal Interventions on Hidden States**: To establish a causal link between specific regions of the world model's hidden states and the predicted actions, token-level causal interventions were performed. By perturbing hidden states in various spatial locations (e.g., zeroing out or replacing with mean values), the researchers measured the resulting impact on the predicted action. This analysis revealed that action outputs could be significantly sensitive to perturbations in task-irrelevant background regions, rather than being primarily driven by the critical hand-object interaction zones.
3. **Feature Space Visualization**: The study also contrasted the feature space structure of the world model (Cosmos-Predict-2.5) with that of a state-of-the-art self-supervised visual encoder (DINOv2) using Principal Component Analysis (PCA). This visualization demonstrated that the world model's features, being optimized for pixel reconstruction, were more entangled with low-level appearance details (e.g., texture, color, background clutter). In contrast, DINOv2 features exhibited a more spatially coherent and semantically organized structure, where functionally similar regions formed consistent structures, making them potentially more suitable for action decoding.

These diagnostic analyses motivated the hypothesis that hidden states optimized primarily for visual reconstruction are not inherently organized in a format that is spatially stable and functionally useful for low-level action control. This "representation mismatch" causes the action decoder to attend to spurious or irrelevant regions, leading to erroneous action predictions and reduced robustness.

The primary objective, therefore, was to bridge this action-grounding gap. The proposed solution, Action-Grounded Representation Alignment (AGRA), aims to regularize the world-action interface by aligning intermediate visual features from the video diffusion model with the spatially coherent semantic representations provided by a frozen foundation visual encoder. This alignment is intended to make the world model's features more "action-grounded," thereby focusing the action decoder on critical interaction regions, improving object localization and affordance understanding, and enhancing the policy's robustness to task-irrelevant perturbations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology revolves around modifying a baseline World Action Model (WAM) with a novel Action-Grounded Representation Alignment (AGRA) objective.

#### 3.1. Baseline World Action Model Architecture

The baseline WAM employs a dual Diffusion Transformer (DiT) architecture.
- **Video DiT**: A Video DiT, initialized from Cosmos-Predict-2.5-2B, acts as the world model. It predicts future visual latents conditioned on the current RGB observation, a language instruction, and the robot's proprioceptive state. It is trained using a flow-matching objective to predict the velocity field of noisy video latents. Intermediate hidden states from selected layers of the Video DiT are exposed to the Action DiT.
- **Action DiT**: An Action DiT, based on Gr00T-N1, generates a continuous action chunk from noise. It is conditioned on the predictive visual representations from the Video DiT and the robot's proprioceptive state. It employs alternating self-attention over action/state tokens and cross-attention to the world model's guidance features. This branch is also optimized with a flow-matching objective.
- **Multi-Layer Bridge Strategy**: To connect the two DiTs, a multi-layer bridge is used. For each of the Action DiT's cross-attention blocks, a specific Video DiT layer's hidden states are uniformly selected across the depth of the Video DiT. These selected hidden states are then projected to match the Action DiT's feature dimension and fed into its cross-attention mechanism.
- **Inference Strategy**: During inference, the Video DiT is evaluated once at a fixed high-noise level (τ_cond_v = 1) to extract predictive features. The Action DiT then performs iterative denoising in action space, conditioned on these features, to generate the action chunk efficiently.

#### 3.2. Diagnosing the Action-Grounding Gap

Before proposing AGRA, the authors performed several diagnostic analyses:
- **Action-Head Attention Analysis**: Cross-attention maps within the Action DiT were visualized. The attention weights were averaged over action tokens and attention heads, then projected back to the video latent shape to identify regions of focus for the action decoder.
- **Causal Intervention for Hidden-State Actionability**: Token-level causal interventions were applied to the world model's hidden states. Interventions (e.g., zero or mean value replacement) were made to individual spatial tokens, and the Euclidean distance of the resulting action deviation from the baseline action was measured. This quantified the causal influence of different spatial regions on the predicted action, with heatmaps indicating high-impact areas.
- **Reconstruction-Optimized Features Analysis**: Principal Component Analysis (PCA) was applied to visualize the feature spaces of Cosmos-Predict-2.5 and DINOv2. This aimed to compare how each model organizes semantic and functional information spatially.

#### 3.3. Action-Grounded Representation Alignment (AGRA)

AGRA is introduced as an objective to regularize the world-action interface:
- **DINOv2 Target Construction**: For each latent frame of the predicted video, the first corresponding RGB frame is used as a semantic reference. Patch-level features are extracted from a frozen DINOv2 encoder. These DINOv2 features are then spatially interpolated and temporally concatenated to match the spatial and temporal dimensions of the world model's hidden states.
- **Alignment Objective**: A set of world model layers (S_agra) is chosen for alignment. For each selected layer, the corresponding video hidden state is projected into the DINOv2 feature space via a dedicated projector network. The AGRA loss (L_AGRA) minimizes the negative cosine similarity between these projected world model hidden states and the aligned DINOv2 target features. This auxiliary loss is applied during training and does not replace the primary video prediction or action flow matching objectives; it constrains the intermediate world model features supplied to the action decoder.
- **Final Training Objective**: The total training objective combines the baseline WAM loss (L_WAM, which includes video and action flow-matching losses) with the AGRA loss, controlled by a weighting hyperparameter (λ_agra).

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

- **Robot and Tasks**: Experiments were conducted on the IRON-R01-1.11 humanoid robot for real-world manipulation tasks: Pick-and-Place and Open-Steamer-Transfer-Bun.
- **Evaluation Scenarios**: Evaluation included In-Distribution (ID) settings and three Out-of-Distribution (OOD) generalization settings: Semantic Generalization, Instance-Level Generalization, and Attribute Generalization.
- **Compared Variants**: The study compared the baseline WAM with several AGRA variants, including different alignment layers (e.g., AGRA-DinoL8, AGRA-DinoL15, AGRA-DinoL4/8/12), different foundation encoders (AGRA-SiglipL8), and architectural modifications (AGRA-BridgeL8). An additional comparison explored the benefit of EgoDex human data for cross-embodiment transfer. Simulation experiments on the RoboCasa GR1 benchmark were also performed.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides both quantitative and qualitative evidence supporting the effectiveness of AGRA in improving robot manipulation performance and addressing the action-grounding gap.

#### 4.1. AGRA Improves Action-Grounded Control

- **In-Distribution Performance**: AGRA achieved an 80% success rate in in-distribution real-world manipulation tasks, a substantial improvement over the baseline WAM's 34%.
- **Out-of-Distribution Generalization**: AGRA consistently demonstrated stronger robustness in OOD scenarios, improving performance by 27% (Semantic Generalization), 32% (Instance-Level Generalization), and 32% (Attribute Generalization) compared to the baseline WAM.

#### 4.2. Action-Head Attention Analysis

- **Qualitative Observations**: Visualizations of cross-attention maps showed that the baseline WAM often allocated attention to task-irrelevant regions (e.g., static left hand, table background), even when generating plausible future videos. In contrast, AGRA directed the action decoder's attention more precisely to the task-critical hand-object interaction regions.
- **Quantitative Metrics**: AGRA improved both "Attention in Mask Ratio" (fraction of attention mass within ground-truth interaction regions, higher is better) and "Centroid Error" (distance between attention centroid and annotated region center, lower is better). These results quantitatively confirmed that AGRA enabled more accurate attention localization.

#### 4.3. Causal Intervention for Hidden-State Actionability

- **Quantitative Metric**: The "Matched Sensitivity Ratio" (R = ∆(I)/∆(B), where ∆(I) is action deviation from perturbing interaction regions and ∆(B) is from background regions) was introduced. A higher R indicates greater actionability of interaction regions. AGRA consistently achieved higher R values across different intervention types (Mean, Zero, Shuffle, Swap w/ Comp), indicating that its hidden states are more action-grounded.
- **Qualitative Observations**: Action-sensitivity heatmaps showed that AGRA concentrated high-impact regions around hand-object contact areas, while the baseline WAM exhibited more diffuse or background-sensitive actionability. This suggested improved robustness to task-irrelevant information.

#### 4.4. Real-World Execution Cases

- AGRA led to more stable physical execution, producing more accurate grasps and better utilization of object affordances (e.g., accurately targeting the center of an elongated object like a banana and executing tighter finger closure). The baseline WAM frequently suffered from localization errors, missing target objects due to spatial deviations.

#### 4.5. Further Analysis and Ablations

- **Optimal Layer for Alignment**: Aligning the 8th layer of Cosmos (approximately 1/3 of the network depth) yielded better real-world performance than aligning deeper layers (e.g., 15th layer). This suggested that shallow layers are suitable for semantic alignment, freeing deeper layers to model motion and fine-grained spatiotemporal dynamics. Aligning multiple layers simultaneously or using hierarchical alignment did not further improve performance.
- **Choice of Visual Representation**: DINOv2 as the alignment target (AGRA-DinoL8) outperformed SigLIP (AGRA-SiglipL8). DINOv2 features demonstrated more object-centric and spatially coherent structures with clearer boundaries, which was attributed to better grasping accuracy and execution stability. SigLIP's language-aligned features did not translate to improved semantic generalization in the tested tasks due to execution-level failures.
- **Sufficiency of Semantic Information**: The AGRA-BridgeL8 variant, which used only the aligned 8th layer feature for all action cross-attention layers, performed poorly, including a 0% success rate on the complex Open-Steamer-Transfer-Bun task. This indicated that an aligned semantic representation alone is insufficient; multi-level predictive features are essential for capturing spatial, geometric, and dynamic details required for precise manipulation.
- **Cross-Embodiment Generalization**: AGRA substantially benefited from the inclusion of EgoDex human demonstration data, especially in OOD scenarios, whereas the baseline WAM showed little improvement. This suggests that AGRA, by anchoring Cosmos hidden states to visually coherent features, extracts more invariant object-centric and interaction-relevant structures that transfer better across embodiments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work identifies and characterizes a critical limitation in World Action Models (WAMs): the "action-grounding gap," where plausible visual future predictions do not guarantee the generation of accurate control actions. By diagnosing this gap through attention analysis and causal interventions, the research highlights that representations optimized for pixel reconstruction are not inherently organized in a manner conducive to robust robot control. This diagnosis itself is a significant contribution, providing a deeper understanding of the internal workings and failure modes of current embodied AI systems.

The proposed solution, Action-Grounded Representation Alignment (AGRA), offers a method to address this gap. By repurposing representation alignment techniques to regularize the world-action interface, AGRA demonstrates that aligning intermediate visual diffusion features with spatially coherent semantic representations from foundation visual encoders (like DINOv2) can significantly improve robot manipulation performance.

The potential impact of AGRA is multifaceted:
1. **Enhanced Robot Robustness and Generalization**: The most direct impact is the substantial improvement in both in-distribution performance and out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization across semantic, instance-level, and attribute variations. This directly contributes to more reliable and deployable robot policies in real-world, less-controlled environments, where unseen objects, altered backgrounds, and varying attributes are common.
2. **Improved Action Interpretability and Precision**: By forcing the action decoder to attend more accurately to task-critical hand-object interaction regions and making action decisions less sensitive to task-irrelevant background clutter, AGRA leads to more precise object localization and better affordance understanding. This can result in more stable grasps and finer motor control, crucial for complex manipulation tasks.
3. **Better Utilization of Foundation Models**: The work demonstrates a novel way to leverage the strengths of foundation visual encoders (e.g., DINOv2) within the context of generative world models for robotic control. It suggests a synergistic approach where the generative capabilities of video diffusion models are complemented by the semantic coherence of self-supervised visual features, moving beyond simply using predictive features as control inputs.
4. **Facilitating Cross-Embodiment Transfer**: The finding that AGRA benefits substantially from diverse human demonstration data (EgoDex) suggests its potential to unlock the utility of large-scale, heterogeneous datasets for robot learning. By reducing dependence on embodiment-specific cues, AGRA helps extract more invariant manipulation-relevant structures, paving the way for more generalist robot policies that can learn from a broader range of visual and interactive experiences.

While the current evaluation is limited to specific manipulation tasks, the principles established by AGRA could be extended to a wider array of robotic tasks and embodiments. The work also opens avenues for future research into quantitatively characterizing the video-action mismatch and exploring how different semantic alignment targets (e.g., language-aligned features from SigLIP) could be more effectively integrated for tasks requiring richer semantic reasoning. Overall, AGRA represents a step towards making visual foresight in WAMs more directly and reliably actionable for practical robot control.
