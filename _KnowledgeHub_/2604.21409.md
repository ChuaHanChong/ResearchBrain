# S1-VL: Scientific Multimodal Reasoning Model with Thinking-with-Images

> [!summary] Summary
> S1-VL, a 32B-parameter multimodal model from ScienceOne AI, integrates structured scientific reasoning with dynamic image interaction via a Python sandbox, enabling it to adaptively process complex visual information. The model achieved state-of-the-art results on Thinking-with-Images benchmarks and outperformed larger models on several scientific reasoning tasks, attributed to its high-quality data and progressive training.

## Problem

- Existing Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) process visual inputs statically, limiting their effectiveness for scientific tasks requiring fine-grained visual reasoning and iterative inspection.
- Current 'Thinking-with-Images' approaches often suffer from low-quality training data, including redundant operations, uninformative intermediate images, or inconsistent reasoning trajectories.
- Many prior models support only a single reasoning mode, insufficient for the diverse nature of scientific problems.

## Method

- Developed **S1-VL**, a 32B-parameter model based on ==Qwen3-VL-32B-Thinking==, supporting a ==dual-mode reasoning system==: Scientific Reasoning and Thinking-with-Images.
- Implemented a ==Python code sandbox (AIO Sandbox)== for the Thinking-with-Images mode, allowing dynamic image manipulation (cropping, zooming, annotation) and multi-turn iterative reasoning.
- Established a ==four-stage progressive training pipeline==, incorporating extensive multi-dimensional data quality filtering, adaptive data routing, and ==SAPO-based reinforcement learning== with specialized reward functions.

## Results

- S1-VL-32B achieved SOTA across all five Thinking-with-Images benchmarks, with HRBench-4K improving from **85.00 → 91.38** and HRBench-8K from **85.10 → 93.50**.
- The model surpassed larger models, including **Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B-Thinking**, on multiple scientific reasoning benchmarks like MMMU, MathVision, and Physics.
- Ablation studies confirmed that data quality filtering and adaptive data routing are critical for mitigating performance degradation in both scientific reasoning and tool-use tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A dual-mode reasoning approach, combining structured natural language reasoning with dynamic visual interaction, is crucial for effectively tackling diverse scientific problems.
> - Systematic multi-dimensional data filtering and adaptive data routing are essential for mitigating noise and improving the quality of 'Thinking-with-Images' training data, leading to more robust tool-use behavior.
> - Progressive training, including supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning with tailored multi-task reward functions, significantly enhances both scientific reasoning and interactive visual capabilities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have demonstrated progress in general visual understanding and text-centric reasoning. Existing MLLMs typically process visual inputs as static encodings, which limits their effectiveness in scientific domains where tasks often require fine-grained visual reasoning, iterative inspection, and dynamic manipulation of images. Scientific problems are characterized by high information density, critical local details, and the necessity for step-by-step analysis that frequently involves revisiting or altering visual evidence.

To address the limitations of static visual understanding, the "Thinking-with-Images" paradigm has emerged. This approach enables models to actively interact with images through operations like cropping, zooming, and annotation, interleaving visual perception with reasoning steps. Previous work in this area, such as OpenAI's o3, Thyme, V-Thinker, and Skywork-R1V4, has explored various implementations of this paradigm. However, these models often suffer from limitations: some support only a single reasoning mode (either purely textual or indiscriminately tool-invoking), and many encounter issues with the quality of training data, including redundant operations, uninformative intermediate images, or inconsistent reasoning trajectories.

S1-VL positions itself within this landscape by aiming to overcome these limitations. It seeks to provide a multimodal reasoning model specifically tailored for scientific domains that integrates two complementary reasoning paradigms and addresses training data quality issues through systematic filtering and adaptive data routing. The work builds upon recent advancements in MLLMs (e.g., Qwen3-VL) and reinforcement learning techniques (e.g., SAPO) to enhance fine-grained scientific visual reasoning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop a multimodal reasoning model, S1-VL, that effectively addresses complex scientific problems requiring both structured reasoning and dynamic visual interaction. This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations and specific objectives:

- **Bridging the Gap in Scientific Multimodal Reasoning:** Existing MLLMs often struggle with the fine-grained visual details and high information density inherent in scientific images (e.g., high-resolution charts, microscopic images, geometry diagrams). The static treatment of visual input in many models is a fundamental limitation for scientific tasks demanding repeated visual examination and iterative analysis. S1-VL aims to provide a more suitable model for these demanding scenarios.

- **Integrating Complementary Reasoning Paradigms:** The paper identifies that a single reasoning mode is insufficient for the diverse nature of scientific problems.
 - **Scientific Reasoning:** For a broad range of standard scientific problems (mathematics, physics, chemistry, astronomy, geography, biology), the objective is to enable structured, multi-step natural language reasoning that jointly analyzes textual and visual information. This mode emphasizes end-to-end logical reasoning, complex calculations, and symbolic derivations without active image manipulation.
 - **Thinking-with-Images:** For scientific problems requiring fine-grained observation and localized analysis, the objective is to allow the model to actively invoke Python code for image operations (cropping, zooming, contrast enhancement, annotations) within a sandbox environment. This mode transforms passive perception into an active "visual manipulation + reasoning" loop, enhancing the model's ability to utilize detailed visual information. The motivation is to overcome resolution and detail sensitivity issues prevalent in high-resolution scientific imagery.

- **Addressing Training Data Quality Limitations:** A significant motivation is to mitigate the prevalence of low-quality visual operations in existing Thinking-with-Images datasets. These issues include redundant operations, uninformative intermediate images, formatting errors, and logically inconsistent trajectories, which can hinder effective model training. The objective is to develop systematic methods for filtering and curating training data to ensure that the model learns when and how to appropriately invoke image operations.

- **Developing a Robust and Adaptive Training Strategy:** The paper aims to create a progressive training pipeline that systematically builds both strong scientific reasoning and effective image interaction abilities. This includes an objective to enable the model to adaptively judge the necessity of image operations, rather than applying them indiscriminately.

- **Establishing a State-of-the-Art Baseline:** The ultimate objective is for S1-VL to demonstrate competitive or superior performance against existing proprietary and open-source models on a comprehensive set of scientific reasoning and Thinking-with-Images benchmarks, particularly at the 32B parameter scale.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

S1-VL is built upon a Qwen3-VL-32B-Thinking base model and incorporates a dual-mode reasoning system, a specialized data construction pipeline, and a four-stage progressive training regimen.

#### 3.1. Base Model

The base architecture for S1-VL is **Qwen3-VL-32B-Thinking**. This foundation model is chosen for its native support for interleaved image-text contexts, strong performance on multimodal benchmarks, and existing capabilities in chain-of-thought reasoning, making it suitable for scientific and mathematical reasoning tasks.

#### 3.2. Reasoning Modes

S1-VL supports two distinct reasoning paradigms:

- **Scientific Reasoning Mode:** In this mode, the model addresses problems using structured natural language reasoning. The input image is encoded once as a static visual representation, and all subsequent reasoning is performed based on this fixed input alongside textual context. This mode is the default for problems where dynamic visual operations are not deemed necessary.
- **Thinking-with-Images Mode:** This mode enables the model to actively manipulate visual content during reasoning. It generates Python code for image processing, executes this code in a sandbox, obtains intermediate visual results (e.g., cropped images), and iteratively refines its reasoning based on the updated visual evidence.

#### 3.3. Python Code Sandbox and Multi-Turn Inference

The Thinking-with-Images capability is implemented through the **AIO Sandbox (All-in-One Agent Sandbox)**. This unified, cloud-native sandboxing environment provides a stateful Python execution environment (Jupyter kernel) and file system access. The model generates Python code for operations like cropping, zooming, contrast adjustment, or drawing auxiliary graphics. The execution output (e.g., intermediate image paths, computed results) is returned to the model, which then continues reasoning in a multi-turn, iterative loop. This process repeats for a maximum of 8 turns until a final answer is produced or the turn limit is reached. A consistent prompt template specifies the tool's functionality, invocation format, and allowed operations, augmented with image metadata (path, dimensions) for coordinate-based operations.

#### 3.4. Data Construction

Two parallel data processing pipelines are established for Scientific Reasoning and Thinking-with-Images paradigms, both following an iterative distillation framework.

#### 3.4.1. Scientific Reasoning Data Distillation

- **Data Collection and Self-Distillation:** Scientific multimodal question-answering data is collected across six disciplines (mathematics, physics, chemistry, astronomy, geography, biology) from public datasets, textbooks, and competition repositories. An initial model is trained on this seed data, then used for self-distillation to generate chain-of-thought trajectories. General-purpose multimodal data is mixed to maintain broad visual understanding, resulting in the **Scientific-Reasoning-SFT dataset** (685K instances).
- **Curriculum Learning and Difficulty Stratification:** Hard samples (pass rate < 0.4 over 10 sampling attempts) are identified for the reinforcement learning (RL) stage, forming the **Scientific-Reasoning-RL dataset** (20K instances). Additionally, 60K correct reasoning trajectories from these hard samples form the **Curriculum-Learning-SFT dataset**.
- **Multi-Dimensional Trajectory Filtering:** Correct trajectories are filtered to remove low-quality samples based on six criteria: meaningless wait token detection, short phrase repetition, short phrase multi-line anomalies, numeric sequence repetition, think format compliance, and semantic similarity filtering.

#### 3.4.2. Thinking-with-Images Data Construction

- **Data Sources:**
 - **Open-Source Data Adaptation:** Multi-turn tool-use trajectories from Thyme and V-Thinker are adapted to S1-VL's format, reconstructing responses, injecting intermediate images, and unifying dialogue structure.
 - **In-House High-Value Task Data:** Proprietary data is created for fine-grained image inspection (high-resolution charts, densely annotated images) and mathematical problems benefiting from auxiliary visual operations (drawing lines, marking points). These tasks often have verifiable answers, aiding automated quality assessment.
- **Multi-Dimensional Quality Filtering:** A six-dimensional framework addresses issues in existing Thinking-with-Images datasets:
 - **Format Validation:** Checks Python code block compliance and parsability.
 - **Reasoning-Answer Consistency Validation:** Ensures logical consistency between reasoning and final answer.
 - **Intermediate Image Validity Validation:** Validates generated intermediate images (e.g., not blank or solid-color).
 - **Image-Text Consistency Validation:** Assesses semantic alignment between intermediate images and reasoning text.
 - **Key Information Validity Validation:** Verifies if the final image contains core visual evidence for the solution.
 - **Cross-Turn Redundancy Detection:** Measures repetitiveness of visual operations across turns.
- **Adaptive Data Routing:** Samples where visual operations provide minimal information gain are identified. Their image operation steps and intermediate images are removed, and the remaining textual reasoning trajectory is converted into **Reasoning-mode training data**. This strategy aims to teach the model when image manipulation is truly necessary.
- After filtering, the **Thinking-with-Images-SFT dataset** (72K instances) is created. A temporary model performs rollouts on this data, and samples with consistently incorrect answers are removed. 10K instances are downsampled to form the **Thinking-with-Images-RL dataset**.

#### 3.5. Training Pipeline

S1-VL-32B is trained using a four-stage progressive pipeline, building on Qwen3-VL-32B-Thinking.

- **Stage 1: Scientific Reasoning SFT:** Full-parameter supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on the Scientific-Reasoning-SFT dataset (685K instances), including mixed general-purpose multimodal data, to establish a foundation in scientific visual understanding and reasoning.
- **Stage 2: Thinking-with-Images SFT:** Continues SFT, introducing Thinking-with-Images capabilities. It combines 60K high-quality hard scientific reasoning trajectories (Curriculum-Learning-SFT) with 72K filtered Thinking-with-Images instances. A **Last-Two-Turn Loss strategy** is applied, computing loss only on the last tool-use turn and the final answer turn to focus learning on more informative parts of the trajectory.
- **Stage 3: Scientific Reasoning RL:** Reinforcement learning (RL) using the **SAPO algorithm** on hard scientific problems from the Scientific-Reasoning-RL dataset (20K instances). A **multi-task scientific reward function** is designed, combining format checking, rule-based verification, and LLM-based semantic judgment, with task-conditioned weights. This stage aims to improve performance on challenging scientific reasoning tasks.
- **Stage 4: Thinking-with-Images RL:** Further enhances Thinking-with-Images capability through SAPO-based RL. A dedicated multi-turn tool-calling rollout pipeline is used. A composite **Thinking-with-Images reward function** is employed, comprising format reward, answer correctness reward, consistency reward (think-answer consistency, crop correctness), tool-calling efficiency reward (penalizing excessive calls), and a tool usage bonus. A "reward hacking" issue, where the model avoided tool use, was identified and resolved by adjusting the r_tool metric and adding an explicit r_bonus.

#### 3.6. Training Hyperparameters

Training is conducted using the ms-swift framework on a GPU cluster with NVIDIA A100 GPUs. DeepSpeed ZeRO-3 and sequence parallelism are utilized for memory optimization and distributed training efficiency. Learning rates are 1e-5 for SFT stages and 1e-6 for RL stages, with a global batch size of 64 for SFT and 256 for RL, and a max sequence length of 32768.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

S1-VL-32B, specifically the S1-VL-32B-RL checkpoint (after all four training stages), was evaluated on 13 benchmarks across scientific reasoning and Thinking-with-Images paradigms.

#### 4.1. Scientific Reasoning Results

S1-VL-32B demonstrated consistent improvements over its base model, Qwen3-VL-32B-Thinking, across all eight scientific reasoning benchmarks.
- **Outperformance against larger models:** S1-VL-32B surpassed Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B-Thinking (a model with approximately 7 times more parameters) on MMMU, MathVision, Physics, VRSBench, and GMAI.
- **Competitive with proprietary models:** On Physics, S1-VL-32B achieved 54.35, outperforming GPT-5 (48.34) by 6.01 points and Gemini 2.5 Pro (40.00) by 14.35 points. It also exceeded GPT-5 and Gemini 2.5 Pro on VRSBench.
- **Comparison with other scientific MLLMs:** S1-VL-32B achieved superior results on MMMU, MathVision, Physics, and VRSBench compared to Intern-S1 (235B+6B), another scientific multimodal reasoning model with a significantly larger backbone.
These results suggest that a targeted scientific training pipeline, including reinforcement learning, can compensate for differences in parameter scale.

#### 4.2. Thinking-with-Images Results

S1-VL-32B-RL achieved state-of-the-art performance across all five Thinking-with-Images benchmarks.
- **Significant gains over baselines:** S1-VL-32B-SFT (after supervised fine-tuning) already outperformed prior specialist models like Skywork-R1V4-30B and proprietary models such as Gemini 2.5 Pro on all five benchmarks.
- **Impact of RL:** Further improvements were observed from S1-VL-32B-SFT to S1-VL-32B-RL. For example, HRBench-4K improved from 85.00 to 91.38, and HRBench-8K from 85.10 to 93.50, surpassing the previous best results (Gemini 2.5 Pro) by 9.10 and 11.00 points, respectively. On V*, S1-VL-32B-RL achieved 92.70, exceeding Skywork-R1V4-30B (88.00) by 4.70 points.
This consistent improvement from SFT to RL validates the effectiveness of the composite reward design and the SAPO algorithm in the Thinking-with-Images context.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

Ablation experiments investigated the impact of the training strategy and data construction pipeline.

- **Impact of Training Strategy:**
 - **Scientific Reasoning RL (Stage 3):** Skipping Stage 3 led to a substantial performance drop on scientific reasoning benchmarks (e.g., GMAI from 62.13 to 59.63, Galaxy-10 from 51.52 to 42.45), indicating its role in tackling challenging problems beyond SFT capabilities and providing positive transfer to visual tool-use.
 - **Thinking-with-Images RL (Stage 4):** Skipping Stage 4 preserved scientific reasoning performance but resulted in a significant decline on Thinking-with-Images benchmarks (e.g., HRBench-4K from 91.38 to 86.40, MME-RW-CN from 77.70 to 73.60). This confirmed that Stage 4's RL, guided by a carefully designed reward, is crucial for robust and effective visual tool use.

- **Impact of Data Quality:**
 - **Thinking-with-Images Data Filtering:** Removing the six-dimensional quality filtering pipeline for Thinking-with-Images data led to consistent performance degradation across all benchmarks (e.g., HRBench-4K from 85.00 to 80.25, GMAI from 58.40 to 55.17), indicating the detrimental effect of noisy training data on both tool-use and general reasoning capabilities.
 - **Adaptive Data Routing:** Removing the adaptive data routing mechanism, which converts low visual information gain samples to reasoning-only data, caused the most severe degradation on scientific reasoning benchmarks (e.g., GMAI from 58.40 to 51.35, Galaxy-10 from 42.45 to 34.73). This highlighted that indiscriminate Thinking-with-Images training can lead to inappropriate tool invocations, interrupting coherent reasoning.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Case Studies

Qualitative analysis provided insights into S1-VL-32B's behavior.
- **Thinking-with-Images Successes:** The model demonstrated the ability to perform precise local inspection and geometric measurements in diverse scientific domains (radiology, remote sensing, crystallography) by autonomously cropping and zooming into regions of interest and integrating intermediate visual evidence.
- **Scientific Reasoning Successes:** The model exhibited strong capabilities in pure structured reasoning for complex scientific problems (multi-image mechanics, number-strip algebra, galaxy morphology classification) by producing rigorous chain-of-thought derivations and systematic visual feature analysis without external tool invocation.
- **Thinking-with-Images Failures:** Limitations included imprecise spatial grounding, sometimes requiring a corrective second turn. Another failure mode was spurious success, where the model produced a correct answer based on language priors despite cropping an irrelevant region, indicating a lack of genuine visual grounding in some cases.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

S1-VL represents an advancement in multimodal reasoning for scientific domains. Its primary significance lies in the development of a model that natively supports two complementary reasoning paradigms: structured Scientific Reasoning and dynamic Thinking-with-Images. This dual-mode capability allows the model to adapt its approach based on the problem's requirements, addressing a limitation of prior MLLMs that often rely on static visual inputs or indiscriminate tool invocation.

The methodological contributions, particularly the **multi-dimensional quality filtering** and **adaptive data routing strategies** for Thinking-with-Images data, are significant. These methods mitigate issues of redundant, ineffective, or erroneous visual operations in training data, which can hinder the learning of effective tool-use behaviors. By ensuring higher data quality and teaching the model *when* to invoke visual tools, S1-VL develops a more robust and efficient interactive reasoning process.

The **four-stage progressive training pipeline**, incorporating both supervised fine-tuning and SAPO-based reinforcement learning stages with carefully designed multi-task reward functions, systematically builds complex capabilities. The ablation studies confirm the distinct contributions of each stage and data component, validating the principled design of the approach.

The reported state-of-the-art performance of S1-VL-32B on Thinking-with-Images benchmarks and its competitive results, even outperforming larger models, on several scientific reasoning benchmarks, demonstrate the effectiveness of its integrated design. This suggests that a well-architected training pipeline and high-quality data can effectively compensate for differences in model scale.

The potential impact of S1-VL is multi-faceted:
- **Advancing Scientific AI:** S1-VL could contribute to the development of more capable AI assistants for scientific research, education, and problem-solving, particularly in fields requiring detailed image analysis, such as medicine, materials science, astronomy, and engineering.
- **Improved Human-AI Collaboration:** The interpretability afforded by step-by-step reasoning and explicit image manipulation could foster better understanding and trust in AI systems for scientific applications.
- **Setting New Benchmarks:** The release of model weights provides a strong baseline for future research in scientific multimodal reasoning and interactive visual AI.

Future work on addressing limitations like precise coordinate grounding, robust code generation, and fine-grained interpretation of specialized imagery, as well as exploring long-context, multi-image interleaved inputs and integration with broader agent frameworks, could further extend the impact of this research.
