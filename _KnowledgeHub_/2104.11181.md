# H2O: Two Hands Manipulating Objects for First Person Interaction Recognition

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from ETH Zurich, Microsoft, and Samsung AI Center introduce H2O, the first unified, markerless 3D egocentric dataset featuring two hands manipulating objects, complete with precise 3D hand poses, 6D object poses, and interaction labels. A novel Topology-Aware Graph Convolutional Network (TA-GCN) is proposed, achieving 79.25% accuracy in egocentric interaction recognition by adaptively learning inter- and intra-dependencies between hands and objects.

## Problem

- Existing egocentric action recognition datasets lack rich 3D annotations, especially for complex two-hand object manipulation scenarios.
- Prior 3D interaction datasets primarily focus on single-hand manipulations or rely on intrusive motion capture systems, limiting realism and generalizability to markerless real-world settings.
- A unified framework was needed to simultaneously estimate 3D poses of two hands and 6D poses of manipulated objects from egocentric RGB images for comprehensive interaction understanding.

## Method

- The H2O dataset was created using a semi-automatic multi-view RGB-D annotation pipeline, providing 571,645 frames with markerless 3D ground truth for two hands, 6D object poses, and 36 interaction classes.
- A single-shot network was developed for joint 3D hand and 6D object pose estimation directly from egocentric RGB images.
- A Topology-Aware Graph Convolutional Network (TA-GCN) was introduced, which adaptively learns spatiotemporal inter- and intra-dependencies between hands and objects for robust interaction recognition.

## Results

- The H2O dataset demonstrates high annotation precision with an approximate 1 cm error for both hand and object 3D annotations, enabling sophisticated contact modeling.
- The proposed TA-GCN achieved a state-of-the-art accuracy of 79.25% for egocentric interaction recognition on the H2O dataset, outperforming strong baselines like SlowFast (77.69%) and I3D (75.21%).
- Ablation studies confirmed that combining left hand, right hand, and object poses, and explicitly modeling their interconnections through the learned graph topology, is critical for robust interaction recognition.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - High-fidelity 3D ground truth, especially for two-hand interactions, is crucial for advancing egocentric interaction recognition beyond the limitations of 2D analyses.
> - Explicitly modeling interdependencies between two hands and manipulated objects via an adaptive graph convolutional network significantly enhances recognition accuracy.
> - A carefully designed semi-automatic annotation pipeline can effectively generate precise 3D ground-truth for complex multi-entity interactions in egocentric views.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses critical challenges in the evolving fields of video understanding, action recognition, and 3D human-object interaction. Historically, action recognition has seen significant progress, especially from third-person viewpoints, with algorithms reliably recognizing actions in various settings. However, egocentric (first-person) action recognition, where the camera is worn by the subject, has lagged due to inherent challenges like fast camera motion, large self-occlusions, background clutter, and most importantly, a lack of large and diverse datasets with rich 3D annotations.

Existing first-person interaction datasets typically provide only 2D features (e.g., bounding boxes, hand segmentation) and often focus on single-hand manipulations. While some pioneering works introduced 3D annotations for single-hand object manipulation (e.g., HOnnotate, ObMan) or used intrusive motion capture systems (e.g., FPHA, GRAB), they faced limitations:
- **Single-hand bias:** Most real-world interactions involve two hands manipulating objects, a scenario largely unaddressed by existing 3D datasets.
- **2D vs. 3D reasoning:** Reliance on 2D features is insufficient for the intricate nature of hand-object interactions, where 3D context is crucial for resolving occlusions and understanding fine-grained movements.
- **Intrusive capture:** Motion capture systems, while accurate, often use markers that contaminate RGB images, limiting their generalizability to markerless, real-world scenarios. They also require controlled environments.
- **Synthetic data limitations:** Purely synthetic datasets, while offering diverse annotations, often struggle with realism and generalization to real images, and frequently lack temporal context.

H2O directly tackles these limitations by introducing the first unified, markerless 3D dataset for egocentric interaction recognition that features *two hands* manipulating objects. This makes it a significant leap forward, bridging the gap between general action recognition and detailed 3D human-object interaction understanding in first-person perspectives. The dataset provides an unprecedented level of detail, including synchronized multi-view RGB-D images, ground-truth 3D poses for both hands, 6D object poses, camera poses, object meshes, scene point clouds, and comprehensive interaction labels.

Furthermore, the paper doesn't just present a dataset but also proposes a novel method for joint 3D hand-object pose estimation and interaction recognition, pushing the state-of-the-art in this specific, challenging domain. By explicitly modeling inter- and intra-dependencies between hands and objects in 3D using a topology-aware graph convolutional network (TA-GCN), the work contributes to the methodological advancements in structured prediction for complex human-centric scenes. This positions H2O as a foundational benchmark and methodological framework that will likely spur further research in augmented reality, robotics, and advanced surveillance applications, where a deep understanding of human-object interactions is paramount.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to advance egocentric interaction recognition by enabling a more comprehensive and detailed understanding of how two hands manipulate objects in 3D. This overarching goal is broken down into several key contributions:

1. **Creation of a Unified, Richly Annotated Egocentric Dataset:** The central motivation is to overcome the scarcity of suitable datasets for first-person action recognition. The authors aim to create "H2O" (2 Hands and Objects), the first benchmark providing markerless 3D annotations for two hands and 6D object poses, alongside interaction labels, synchronized multi-view RGB-D images, camera poses, object meshes, and scene point clouds. This unprecedented level of detail is crucial for robust 3D reasoning.
2. **Development of a Semi-Automatic Annotation Pipeline:** To achieve high-fidelity 3D annotations efficiently and at scale, the authors propose a practical semi-automatic pipeline. This addresses the challenge of accurately labeling complex 3D hand-object interactions in real-world scenarios, avoiding the limitations of intrusive motion capture systems.
3. **Introduction of a Unified Framework for Joint Pose Estimation and Interaction Recognition:** The paper aims to propose the first method capable of simultaneously estimating the 3D pose of two hands and the 6D pose of manipulated objects directly from RGB images. This is motivated by the need for practical, markerless solutions that can infer comprehensive scene interpretations from commodity sensors.
4. **Novel Approach for 3D Interaction Recognition using Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs):** A key objective is to develop a method that explicitly models the complex spatial and temporal interdependencies within and between two hands and manipulated objects. By learning the topology of a graph convolutional network, the authors seek to leverage the geometric structure of hand-object interactions for more accurate interaction recognition.

The motivation for these objectives stems from the significant potential applications of egocentric interaction recognition in fields such as:
- **Augmented Reality (AR):** Enabling AR systems to understand user interactions with virtual and real objects more intuitively.
- **Robotics:** Providing robots with a deeper understanding of human intentions and actions during collaborative tasks.
- **Surveillance:** Improving anomaly detection and activity understanding in first-person camera feeds.
- **Human-Computer Interaction:** Creating more natural and intelligent interfaces by understanding fine-grained manipulations.

By addressing the data gap and providing sophisticated 3D reasoning capabilities, the research aims to unlock new possibilities for these applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology is divided into two main parts: the creation of the H2O dataset and the proposed method for 3D hand-object interaction recognition.

#### 3.1. H2O Dataset Creation (Annotation Method)

The H2O dataset is curated using a semi-automatic pipeline designed for accuracy and scale, leveraging multi-view RGB-D cameras to capture complex two-hand manipulations.

1. **Capture Setup:**
 - **Hardware:** Five Azure Kinect RGB-D cameras are used, synchronized via physical cables to minimize lag. Four are static, and one is helmet-mounted for egocentric views.
 - **Environments:** Data is acquired in three diverse indoor settings (hall, office, kitchen) with various backgrounds.
 - **Participants & Objects:** Four subjects perform actions with eight different objects, resulting in 571,645 synchronized RGB-D frames.

2. **Camera Calibration:**
 - Intrinsic parameters are factory-calibrated. Extrinsic parameters are determined using IR reflective spheres, which are easily detectable and allow for a portable setup.
 - The 3D locations of spheres are used to solve for camera poses via PnP (Perspective-n-Point) algorithm.
 - Head-mounted camera poses are smoothed using Kalman filtering to reduce jitter.

3. **Object Pose Annotation:**
 - **3D Model Reconstruction:** Each object is scanned using a hand-held Kinect, and RGB-D frames are fed into BADSLAM to reconstruct a textured 3D mesh model.
 - **6D Pose Estimation:** An object mask predictor (Mask R-CNN) is trained using projected 3D models. These masks, along with RGB-D images, are fed into DenseFusion to estimate 6D object pose for each camera view. The prediction with highest confidence is selected and further refined via ICP (Iterative Closest Point) by merging point clouds from multiple depth images.

4. **Hand Pose Annotation:**
 - **MANO Model:** The widely used MANO parametric hand model (which factorizes hand shape and pose) is employed.
 - **Optimization-based Tracking:** Hand poses are tracked by minimizing a comprehensive loss function (Eq. 1) at each frame. This loss includes multiple terms:
 - `Ls`: Silhouette-based error, merging hand masks (from OpenPose and GrabCut) with object masks.
 - `L2D` & `L3D`: 2D and 3D joint errors, comparing MANO joints to OpenPose estimates.
 - `Lphy`: Physical constraint regularization, penalizing interpenetration between hand and objects.
 - `La`: Joint angle limit regularization, penalizing unrealistic joint angles.
 - `Lm`: 3D mesh surface error, penalizing distance between hand point cloud (from merged multi-view depth images) and the MANO surface.
 - `Lp`: Pose prior regularization, based on a multivariate Gaussian distribution of hand poses from the MANO dataset.
 - **Semi-automation:** After the automated pipeline, frames are manually inspected to identify and remove inaccurate poses, which are then corrected via temporal smoothing and interpolation using Kalman filtering.

5. **Temporal Action Annotation:**
 - Actions are manually annotated as verb-noun pairs (e.g., "grab book").
 - 11 verb classes and 8 noun classes combine to form 36 distinct action classes.

#### 3.2. Recognizing 3D Hand-Object Interactions

The proposed method consists of a pose prediction module and an interaction recognition module.

1. **Pose Prediction:**
 - **Architecture:** Builds upon, using a YOLOv2 backbone.
 - **Output:** Instead of a 2D grid, it produces a 3D grid, where each cell is associated with vectors for left hand, right hand, and object pose, along with confidence values.
 - **Parameterization:** Hand poses are represented by 3D joint coordinates, and object poses by 3D bounding box corner points (which allow 6D pose computation).
 - **Loss Function:** Minimizes pose prediction error and confidence error (Eq. 4 & 5).
 - **Processing:** Low-confidence predictions are pruned; high-confidence ones are selected.

2. **Interaction Recognition (Topology-Aware Graph Convolutional Network - TA-GCN):**
 - **Multi-Graph Structure:** The method parameterizes the left hand skeleton, right hand skeleton, and object bounding box as individual graphs, then combines them into a unified multi-graph.
 - **Spatiotemporal GCN:** Extends standard ST-GCN and 2s-AGCN to model both spatial and temporal information, specifically focusing on inter- and intra-dependencies.
 - **Adaptive Adjacency Matrices (Eq. 7):**
 - `A_j,intra`: Fixed adjacency matrices for intra-graph dependencies (e.g., within a single hand).
 - `A_j,inter`: Fixed adjacency matrices for static inter-related dependencies (e.g., between symmetric hand parts and object center).
 - `T_j,inter`: *Parameterized* adjacency matrix that adaptively learns inter-graph dependencies between hands and objects.
 - `T_j,intra`: *Parameterized* adjacency matrix that adaptively learns intra-graph dependencies within single entities.
 - `S_j`: An additional data-dependent term that learns a unique graph for each sample by measuring vertex similarity in an embedding space (Eq. 8).
 - **Learning Graph Topology:** Crucially, `T_j,inter` and `T_j,intra` are jointly optimized with other network parameters, allowing the model to learn the most relevant graph topology and edge weights directly from the data.
 - **Input:** The predicted 3D poses of hands and objects from the pose prediction module are fed as input feature maps to the TA-GCN.
 - **Output:** The TA-GCN, composed of 10 stacked blocks, predicts the interaction class.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several key findings, validating both the quality of the H2O dataset and the effectiveness of the proposed recognition method.

#### 4.1. Dataset Analysis and Verification

- **Annotation Accuracy:** Quantitative verification on a random subset of 500 images across 5 camera views shows high precision. The error for both hands and objects is approximately 1 cm, comparable to or exceeding other datasets despite the challenges of two-hand manipulation and mutual occlusions.
- **Contact Modelling:** H2O's precise hand and object pose annotations, combined with meshes, facilitate sophisticated contact modeling. The ability to generate contact hotspots on hand and object surfaces demonstrates its utility for understanding physical interactions.
- **Dataset Statistics:** The dataset includes 571,645 RGB-D frames from 4 participants performing 36 distinct action classes in 3 environments. Action instances are well-distributed, and the length of action clips varies, indicating diversity. Notably, two hands are used in 57.8% of the dataset, highlighting its focus on complex interactions.

#### 4.2. Experimental Results on Pose Estimation and Interaction Recognition

1. **Joint 3D Hand and 6D Object Pose Estimation:**
 - The proposed single-shot network reliably predicts the 3D pose of two hands and the 6D pose of manipulated objects from RGB images.
 - It establishes a strong baseline, demonstrating low error margins for hand (3D PCK) and object (2D reprojection, ADD) pose estimation (Fig. 7).
 - Qualitative examples (Fig. 5) show the method's ability to handle challenging occlusions.
 - While specifically designed for two hands, the method's individual left/right hand and object pose estimation performance is competitive with, and in some cases surpasses, existing single-hand object pose estimation methods like Hasson and H+O (Table 4, left side).

2. **3D Interaction Recognition:**
 - **Impact of Input Modalities:** An ablation study (Table 3a) demonstrates the critical importance of combined input. While individual hand or object poses provide some recognition capability, combining right hand, left hand, and object poses (`ALL`) significantly boosts interaction recognition accuracy to 79.25%, highlighting their complementary nature.
 - **Importance of Inter-dependencies:** Modeling the relationships between different entities is crucial. Explicitly learning interconnections between left hand and right hand (`LEFT HAND-RIGHT HAND`) and between hands and objects (`HANDS-OBJECT`) progressively improves accuracy. Combining all interconnections (`ALL INTERCONNECTIONS`) yields the best result (79.25%), validating the multi-graph approach (Table 3b).
 - **TA-GCN Component Analysis:** An ablation of the TA-GCN's components (Table 3c) shows that all proposed elements contribute to its effectiveness. The fully adaptive TA-GCN achieves the highest accuracy, outperforming baselines and variants where specific learned adjacency matrices (`T_j,inter`, `T_j,intra`) or static matrices (`A_j,inter`, `A_j,intra`) are removed. The addition of the data-dependent term `S_j` also contributes to the final performance.
 - **State-of-the-Art Comparison:** The proposed TA-GCN achieves state-of-the-art accuracy for egocentric interaction recognition on the H2O dataset. It outperforms strong image-based models (C2D, I3D, SlowFast) and pose-based methods (H+O, ST-GCN) that use its pose predictions. The TA-GCN achieved a test accuracy of 79.25%, significantly higher than the closest competitors (SlowFast at 77.69%, I3D at 75.21%, ST-GCN at 73.86%). This demonstrates the effectiveness of leveraging 3D pose predictions and the novel topology-aware graph learning.
 - **Learned Graph Connections:** Visualization of learned graph connections (Fig. 8) illustrates that the model attributes more weight to fingertips and DIP joints (distal interphalangeal joints) that are frequently involved in manipulation, and that hand-object connections are prioritized over hand-hand connections during object interaction.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The H2O dataset and the accompanying methodology represent a significant advancement in the fields of egocentric vision, 3D human-object interaction, and action recognition. Its potential impact is multi-faceted:

1. **Groundbreaking Dataset:** H2O is the first benchmark of its kind to offer markerless 3D annotations of *two hands manipulating objects* from an egocentric perspective, along with multi-view RGB-D data and comprehensive interaction labels. This addresses a critical data gap that has hindered progress in understanding complex first-person interactions. Its public release will serve as a vital resource for the research community.
2. **Enabling 3D Reasoning:** By providing rich 3D ground-truth for both hands and objects, H2O moves beyond traditional 2D analyses, facilitating deeper 3D reasoning about human actions. This is crucial for applications requiring a precise understanding of spatial relationships and manipulations.
3. **Establishing Strong Baselines:** The paper introduces the first method for jointly recognizing the 3D poses of two hands and manipulated objects from a single RGB image. This, coupled with the novel TA-GCN for interaction recognition, establishes powerful baselines that significantly outperform previous state-of-the-art approaches. These baselines provide a foundation for future research and innovation.
4. **Advancing Robotics and AR/VR:** The ability to accurately perceive and understand two-hand object manipulations from a first-person perspective is transformative for robotics (e.g., human-robot collaboration, robot learning from demonstration) and mixed reality applications (e.g., natural interaction with virtual objects, context-aware AR experiences).
5. **Bridging Research Gaps:** H2O helps bridge the gap between distinct research communities—3D hand pose estimation, 6D object pose estimation, hand-object interaction, and egocentric action recognition. The unified dataset and framework foster interdisciplinary research and allow for the development of holistic scene understanding systems.
6. **Methodological Innovation:** The proposed TA-GCN with its adaptive, learned graph topology represents a novel approach to modeling complex interdependencies in human-object interactions. This methodological contribution can be generalized to other structured prediction tasks in computer vision.

In conclusion, H2O provides both the necessary high-quality data and advanced analytical tools to unlock the next generation of intelligent systems capable of truly understanding human interactions with their environment from a first-person perspective, promising substantial impacts across various technological domains.
