# Graphormer-Guided Task Planning: Beyond Static Rules with LLM Safety Perception

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from UCSB and UESTC developed a framework that integrates large language models (LLMs) with Graphormers to provide robots with real-time, structured safety perception in dynamic environments. This approach enables adaptive task replanning in response to emergent risks, achieving high hazard detection and mitigation rates in simulated household tasks within AI2-THOR.

## Problem

- Current LLM-based robot planners lack structured risk perception, failing to detect high-risk physical interactions in real-world environments.
- Traditional static rule-based safety mechanisms are rigid, not scalable, and cannot adapt to unforeseen hazards in open-world scenarios.
- Robotic frameworks struggle with task adaptability, lacking the capacity to dynamically modify execution plans in response to emergent safety threats.

## Method

- A spatio-semantic safety graph is dynamically constructed, using an LLM to semantically annotate interactions and combine these with spatial proximity for quantitative danger scores.
- A Graphormer model is pretrained on a synthetic risk-aware dataset to detect high-risk interactions within the graph, using focal loss to address data imbalance.
- When a high-risk interaction is detected by the Graphormer, the LLM is queried to generate a revised, safety-aware task plan, enabling real-time adaptive safety compliance.

## Results

- The Graphormer achieved a **91.39%** recall for hazard detection, reliably identifying over **90%** of hazardous edges while filtering out approximately **70%** of false positives.
- In task planning, the framework demonstrated a **100%** safety notice rate and 95-**100%** risk handling success across simple, intermediate, and complex tasks, alongside high task success rates (up to **100%**).
- The proposed method consistently outperformed LLM-only, prompted LLM, and LLM + Static Rules baselines in detecting and mitigating dynamic risks, ensuring safer task execution.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating Graphormers provides LLM-driven robots with robust, real-time structured safety perception that goes beyond static rules.
> - A spatio-semantic safety graph, combined with LLM-generated risk annotations, offers an interpretable and adaptive representation of environmental hazards.
> - The framework successfully bridges the gap between flexible high-level LLM planning and robust, real-time safety compliance by enabling online hazard detection and adaptive task refinement.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The integration of Large Language Models (LLMs) into robotic task planning has emerged as a transformative area, promising unprecedented levels of reasoning, generalization, and natural language interaction for robots. LLMs have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in generating high-level action sequences and leveraging world knowledge for complex, long-horizon tasks, finding applications in domestic robotics, multi-agent collaboration, and interactive task execution.

However, the broader research landscape reveals significant limitations in current LLM-driven robotic systems, particularly concerning safety. Existing methods largely fall into several categories, each with its own drawbacks when it comes to ensuring safe task execution in dynamic, open-world environments:

- **LLM-Only Planning:** While offering flexibility, LLMs often struggle with inconsistencies, unpredictability, and a fundamental lack of structured risk perception. They primarily operate on semantic reasoning and excel at generating feasible task sequences based on prompts, but lack the intrinsic mechanism to detect or adapt to unforeseen physical hazards or dynamic changes in the environment, making them unsuitable for safety-critical applications. Their reliance on prompt engineering further complicates the robust encoding of safety constraints.
- **Rule-Based Safety Mechanisms (e.g., LTL):** Traditional safety approaches, often utilizing Linear Temporal Logic (LTL), provide formal guarantees and enforce predefined constraints. They are effective in controlled settings where all possible safety conditions can be exhaustively enumerated. However, their rigidity and poor adaptability make them impractical for open-world scenarios where unforeseen hazards can emerge unpredictably. They lack spatial perception and cannot infer risks beyond their explicitly defined rules.
- **Reinforcement Learning (RL):** RL-based approaches can optimize decision-making in controlled environments but typically require extensive training data and struggle with generalization to novel, low-latency scenarios where real-time hazard adaptation is crucial.
- **Vision-Language Models (VLMs) with LLMs:** While VLMs enhance environmental awareness and perceptual understanding for LLM-based planning, they largely focus on task feasibility rather than structured safety reasoning. They often rely on human-in-the-loop supervision, not autonomous risk adaptation.
- **Graph-Based Models for Spatial Reasoning:** Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) have shown significant promise in structured spatial data processing and risk modeling within autonomous systems (e.g., autonomous driving for trajectory prediction, pedestrian intent estimation, interaction-aware motion planning). These models excel at encoding agent-object dependencies and modeling spatial-temporal interactions, but their integration with LLM-driven task planning for real-time, adaptive safety perception has remained largely underexplored.

This paper positions itself directly in this gap. It aims to overcome the "lack of structured risk perception" in LLMs, the "static rule-based safety mechanisms," and the "limited task adaptability" of current approaches. By proposing a framework that synergistically combines LLM-based decision-making with Graphormer-enhanced structured safety modeling, the authors aim to bridge the divide between flexible high-level planning and robust, real-time safety compliance, pushing beyond the limitations of purely semantic or static rule-based systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this research stems from the critical need for robots to operate safely and adaptively in complex, human-populated environments. The authors vividly illustrate this with a household robot failing to notice a child approaching a hot stove, highlighting a pervasive limitation in current robotic task planning frameworks.

The key objectives and motivations are:

1. **Address the Lack of Structured Risk Perception in LLM-Based Planners:** Current LLM-based systems primarily rely on semantic reasoning and fail to incorporate spatially structured risk modeling. This prevents them from detecting high-risk physical interactions, such as a child nearing a hazardous object, which is crucial for real-world safety. The objective is to imbue LLM-driven planners with a mechanism for understanding and perceiving structured risks in their environment.
2. **Overcome the Rigidity of Static Rule-Based Safety Mechanisms:** Traditional safety approaches, based on predefined rules or formal logic like LTL, are rigid, difficult to scale, and cannot cope with the unpredictability of open-world environments. They require exhaustive enumeration of all possible safety conditions, which is infeasible. The motivation is to move beyond these static constraints towards a more flexible, context-aware, and adaptive safety framework.
3. **Enhance Task Adaptability to Safety Emergencies:** Existing robotic frameworks often generate task sequences in a feedforward manner, lacking the capacity to dynamically modify their execution plans in response to emergent safety threats. The objective is to enable robots to perform low-latency hazard adaptation and task refinement in real-time, ensuring continuous safety compliance.
4. **Integrate LLM Reasoning with Structured Safety Modeling:** The overarching goal is to synthesize the strengths of LLMs (flexible, generalizable task reasoning) with the capabilities of graph neural networks (structured, spatial-temporal interaction modeling) to create a robust framework for risk-aware task planning. This integration aims to enable online hazard detection and adaptive task refinement that goes beyond static rules.

In essence, the research seeks to empower robots with a proactive safety consciousness, allowing them to not only execute tasks efficiently but also to continuously monitor, perceive, and adapt to potential hazards in their dynamic surroundings, ensuring secure operation in safety-critical applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology, "Graphormer-enhanced risk-aware task planning," integrates structured safety perception with LLM-driven decision-making. The core idea is to dynamically construct a spatio-semantic safety graph that captures real-time spatial and contextual risk factors, enabling online hazard detection and adaptive task refinement.

The approach consists of the following key components:

1. **Problem Formulation:** The framework aims to take an initial task plan `T = {a1..., an}` and adaptively update it by constructing a spatio-semantic graph `G = (V, E)`. `V` represents environmental entities (objects, humans, robots), and `E` encodes risk-aware and spatial relationships. The agent must update `T` in response to high-risk edges `e_ij ∈ E`.

2. **Graph-Based Risk Representation:**
 - **LLM for Risk Annotation:** An LLM is used to semantically annotate interactions between entities. Given a scene with entities `{v1..., vm}`, the LLM generates structured risk annotations, `r(vi, vj) = LLM(vi, vj)`. This output includes a `danger_level` (low, medium, high) and an `llm_reason` for explainable hazard assessments. This allows for qualitative risk assessment.
 - **Synthetic Risk-Aware Dataset Construction:** A dataset is created by embedding these LLM-generated risk relationships into the AI2-THOR environment. Randomized human-agent interactions (e.g., children, adults, pets near hazardous objects) are introduced to generate diverse safety-critical scenarios.
 - **Danger Score Calculation:** A quantitative danger score `S(vi, vj)` is assigned to each interaction, combining the LLM-inferred risk level `r(vi, vj)` with spatial proximity `SP(vi, vj)`. Spatial proximity is calculated as `1/distance` if the distance is below a threshold `DT`, otherwise `1/DT`, preventing divergence for very close objects. This formulation ensures that both the type of interaction and physical closeness contribute to the overall risk assessment.

3. **Graphormer Pretraining for Risk Detection:**
 - **Model Choice:** A Graphormer model is employed and trained to predict high-risk interactions based on the structured safety graph. Graphormer is a Transformer-based model designed to handle graph structures, allowing it to effectively capture complex relationships.
 - **Mechanism:** The Graphormer processes the graph `G` (with node features `X`, adjacency matrix `A`, and edge features `E`) to compute learned risk embeddings `H`. It uses multi-head attention where attention weights `α_ij` incorporate both structural and semantic information, specifically using edge encodings (`b_ij`) derived from `E`. This enables the model to emphasize high-risk interactions through an attention-weighted graph representation.
 - **Addressing Data Imbalance:** Recognizing that hazardous edges are rare, a focal loss is used during training. This loss function assigns higher weights to rare (hazardous) and hard-to-classify examples, preventing the model from being overwhelmed by the majority class (safe interactions) and ensuring robust detection of critical risks.

4. **Risk-Aware Task Replanning:**
 - **Continuous Evaluation:** At runtime, the trained Graphormer continuously monitors the environment, updating the safety graph and identifying high-risk edges.
 - **Replanning Trigger:** When a high-risk interaction `e_ij` is detected (e.g., above a certain risk threshold), a replanning mechanism is invoked.
 - **LLM-Based Plan Revision:** The LLM is queried with the current task `T` and the detected hazardous graph `G` to generate a revised task plan `T'`. This revised plan explicitly incorporates safety-aware modifications to mitigate the identified risk (e.g., "Ensure child is in a safe location" before "Secure knife in a designated area"). This closed-loop process allows for adaptive safety compliance and proactive hazard avoidance.

The overall approach combines the global reasoning and natural language capabilities of LLMs with the structured, localized, and real-time risk perception strengths of Graphormer, enabling a flexible and scalable solution for safe robotic task planning in dynamic environments.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The authors conducted extensive experiments in the AI2-THOR environment to validate their framework, comparing it against several baselines.

**A. Risk Detection Experiments**
- **Environment and Dataset:** Experiments were carried out in 120 diverse AI2-THOR household environments (kitchens, living rooms, bedrooms, bathrooms). To simulate safety-critical scenarios, human agent nodes (e.g., children, adults, pets) were manually introduced near hazardous objects in half of the scenes. The dataset was split into 90 training, 15 validation, and 15 test samples.
- **Evaluation Metrics and Data Imbalance:** Due to severe class imbalance (hazardous edges constituting only 1% of total edges), Recall was prioritized over accuracy to ensure critical risks were not overlooked.
- **Comparison with Alternative Methods:**
 - **Random Guess Baseline:** Performed no better than chance, with near-zero precision.
 - **Rule-Based System:** Limited by manual specification and lack of adaptability, unable to comprehensively cover unforeseen indoor hazards.
 - **Our Model (Graphormer):** Achieved a strong balance between hazard detection and practical alert filtering. At a decision threshold of 0.21, the model demonstrated:
 - **Recall: 91.39%** (successfully identifying over 90% of hazardous edges).
 - **Precision: 29.27%** (filtering out about 70% of false positives). This indicates that while there are false positives, the crucial safety-critical hazards are reliably detected.

**B. LLM-Guided Risk-Aware Task Planning Experiments**
- **Tasks and Complexity:** Five household activities, categorized into simple (spatial reorganization), intermediate (object manipulation), and complex (cooking) tasks, were used. Each task was designed to inherently involve high-risk actions without proper intervention.
- **Baselines for Task Planning:**
 - **LLM-Only (No Risk Awareness):** LLM generates plans from scene descriptions without explicit safety perception.
 - **Prompted LLM (Risk-Aware Prompting):** LLM receives a general safety instruction but no structured environmental guidelines.
 - **LLM + Static Rules (LTL):** LLM receives signals from an LTL module with manually predefined rules for all hazards.
- **Evaluation Metrics:**
 - **Task Success Rate (TSR):** Percentage of completed tasks.
 - **Safety Notice Rate (SNR):** Percentage of scenarios where a high-risk situation is noticed.
 - **Risk Handling Success (RHS):** Percentage of cases where the system correctly identified and mitigated a hazard.
- **Key Results (Table II):**
 - **LLM-Only and Prompted LLM:** Showed high TSR (up to 100% for simple tasks) but consistently **0% SNR and RHS** (except for 10% RHS in complex LLM-Only, likely incidental). This confirms their inability to perceive or react to dynamic risks, even with safety prompts.
 - **LLM + LTL:** Achieved **100% SNR** for all tasks due to explicit rule-based detection. However, its rigidity led to **lower RHS** (90%, 80%, 40% for simple, intermediate, complex) and **reduced TSR** (95%, 90%, 60%). This highlights LTL's limitation in handling unforeseen or complex real-world hazards, leading to task failures despite noticing "some" risks.
 - **LLM + Graphormer (Ours):** Demonstrated **superior performance** across all metrics and complexities:
 - **TSR:** 100% (simple), 97.5% (intermediate), 92.5% (complex) – maintaining high task efficiency.
 - **SNR:** **100%** across all tasks – confirming robust hazard detection.
 - **RHS:** **100%** (simple), **95%** (intermediate), **95%** (complex) – showcasing exceptional ability to identify and successfully mitigate hazards.
- **Task Execution Times (Appendix):** The Graphormer's risk detection and graph building process is efficient (approximately 0.17 seconds). The primary bottleneck is the LLM's latency for receiving safety notices and generating revised task sequences (several seconds). This indicates that the safety perception module itself is fast, and future work can focus on optimizing LLM response times.

The results conclusively demonstrate that the Graphormer-enhanced framework significantly outperforms baselines in unstructured risk scenarios. It effectively addresses dynamic risk relationships, enabling proactive hazard detection and adaptive task planning for safer execution in complex, continuous environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Graphormer-Guided Task Planning: Beyond Static Rules with LLM Safety Perception" framework represents a significant advancement in the field of intelligent robotics, particularly in ensuring the safe deployment of autonomous agents in human-centric environments.

**Significance:**
- **Bridging the Safety Gap:** This work successfully addresses a critical limitation of current LLM-driven robotic systems: the lack of structured, adaptive safety perception. By integrating the flexible reasoning of LLMs with the robust, real-time risk assessment capabilities of Graphormer, the framework provides a crucial bridge between task efficiency and operational safety.
- **Adaptive Safety Compliance:** Unlike rigid, rule-based systems that struggle with unforeseen hazards, the proposed method introduces a context-aware risk perception module that continuously refines safety predictions based on real-time task execution. This enables genuinely adaptive safety compliance, moving beyond the brittle nature of static rules.
- **Structured Risk Modeling:** The introduction of a spatio-semantic safety graph, coupled with LLM-generated explainable risk annotations, provides a structured and interpretable representation of environmental hazards. This is a fundamental step towards building more transparent and trustworthy autonomous systems.
- **Superior Performance:** The experimental results clearly demonstrate the framework's superior performance in risk detection accuracy, safety notice rate, and successful hazard mitigation, particularly in dynamic and complex scenarios within AI2-THOR. It consistently outperforms both LLM-only and static rule-based baselines in ensuring task completion while prioritizing safety.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Safer Human-Robot Interaction and Coexistence:** The ability of robots to perceive and adapt to real-time safety threats, especially involving humans (e.g., children), is paramount for their widespread adoption in homes, hospitals, and public spaces. This research lays a foundation for building robots that can more safely interact with and operate alongside humans, fostering greater trust and acceptance.
- **Robust Autonomous Systems:** By enabling adaptive task modification in response to emergent risks, the framework contributes to the development of more robust and resilient autonomous systems. These systems can maintain high task success rates even when confronted with unpredictable environmental changes or unexpected events, reducing the likelihood of failures or hazardous situations.
- **New Benchmarks for Safety-Critical AI:** The methodology, particularly the use of LLMs for risk annotation and the creation of a synthetic risk-aware dataset within AI2-THOR, can inspire and facilitate the development of new benchmarks for evaluating safety perception and adaptive planning in robotics. The authors' intention to release a systematically curated dataset further supports this.
- **Foundation for Predictive Safety:** The framework's current focus on instantaneous risk perception sets the stage for future advancements in predictive safety. By extending to temporal frameworks, robots could not only recognize current hazards but also anticipate and prevent future ones, leading to even more proactive and intelligent safety measures.
- **Democratization of Complex Safety Systems:** By leveraging the generalizability of LLMs and graph neural networks, this approach potentially reduces the burden of manual rule engineering, making advanced safety systems more accessible and scalable for diverse robotic applications.

In conclusion, this work significantly contributes to the vision of truly intelligent and safe robots. By demonstrating how LLM-driven reasoning can be effectively guided by a structured, real-time safety perception module, the authors pave the way for a future where autonomous agents can perform complex tasks with unprecedented levels of awareness, adaptability, and, critically, safety.
