# Graphormer-Guided Task Planning: Beyond Static Rules with LLM Safety Perception

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from UCSB and UESTC developed a framework that integrates large language models (LLMs) with Graphormers to provide robots with real-time, structured safety perception in dynamic environments. This approach enables adaptive task replanning in response to emergent risks, achieving high hazard detection and mitigation rates in simulated household tasks within AI2-THOR.

## Problem

- Current LLM-based robot planners lack structured risk perception, failing to detect high-risk physical interactions in real-world environments.
- Traditional static rule-based safety mechanisms are rigid, not scalable, and cannot adapt to unforeseen hazards in open-world scenarios.
- Robotic frameworks struggle with task adaptability, lacking the capacity to dynamically modify execution plans in response to emergent safety threats.

## Method

- A spatio-semantic safety graph is dynamically constructed, using an LLM to semantically annotate interactions and combine these with spatial proximity for quantitative danger scores.
- A Graphormer model is pretrained on a synthetic risk-aware dataset to detect high-risk interactions within the graph, using focal loss to address data imbalance.
- When a high-risk interaction is detected by the Graphormer, the LLM is queried to generate a revised, safety-aware task plan, enabling real-time adaptive safety compliance.

## Results

- The Graphormer achieved a **91.39%** recall for hazard detection, reliably identifying over **90%** of hazardous edges while filtering out approximately **70%** of false positives.
- In task planning, the framework demonstrated a **100%** safety notice rate and 95-**100%** risk handling success across simple, intermediate, and complex tasks, alongside high task success rates (up to **100%**).
- The proposed method consistently outperformed LLM-only, prompted LLM, and LLM + Static Rules baselines in detecting and mitigating dynamic risks, ensuring safer task execution.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating Graphormers provides LLM-driven robots with robust, real-time structured safety perception that goes beyond static rules.
> - A spatio-semantic safety graph, combined with LLM-generated risk annotations, offers an interpretable and adaptive representation of environmental hazards.
> - The framework successfully bridges the gap between flexible high-level LLM planning and robust, real-time safety compliance by enabling online hazard detection and adaptive task refinement.
