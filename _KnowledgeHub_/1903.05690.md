# Putting Humans in a Scene: Learning Affordance in 3D Indoor Environments

> [!summary] Summary
> This work predicts 3D human affordances in indoor scenes: what poses a scene permits and where. A fully automatic pose synthesizer fuses 2D pose semantics from sitcom videos with SUNCG voxel geometry to generate 1.5M ground-truth 3D poses, then a jointly trained where/what conditional-VAE pair with a geometry-aware discriminator generates poses from one RGB, RGB-D, or depth image, beating prior art on semantic and geometry scores.

## Problem

- Explicit physics-simulation affordance modeling infers forces and contacts for specific sitting styles, but generalizes poorly to other pose types and needs complete 3D scene geometry.
- Data-driven affordance learning from sitcom video frames yields only about 20K manually collected examples with 2D poses, lacking the 3D geometry needed to respect scene physics.
- Instance-placement methods insert objects or pedestrians into 2D images, requiring user templates or given locations, and never check physical feasibility in the 3D scene.

## Method

- Automatic 3D pose synthesizer: a heat-map location network plus adapted 2D pose generator produce poses, mapped to 3D voxels via sampled human-height depth estimation and camera matrices.
- Affordance adjustment via 3D correlation enforces a free-space constraint (no body-object intersection, contact parts masked) and a support constraint (Gaussian-kernel surface detection, maximum-response placement).
- Generative model factorizes pose distribution into ==where== and ==what== conditional ==VAEs==, linked differentiably through ==DSNT== pelvis coordinates, trained with MSE, KL, and world-coordinate geometry losses plus a ==geometry-aware discriminator== on depth heat maps.

## Results

- From a single RGB image the model scores **91.69** semantic and **66.40** geometry, exceeding the baseline's **72.53** and **23.25** by **19.47%** and **19.02%**; depth input raises geometry to **72.11**.
- Ablations: removing joint training cuts semantic score **8.06%** (**91.69** to **83.34**), removing the geometry loss drops geometry score **4.59%**, and a single monolithic VAE reaches only **76.23** / **52.94**.
- At given locations the what module scores **78.43** geometry versus **56.29** for the prior method (**+6.66%** at best modality); in a **400**-vote user study its poses were indistinguishable from ground truth.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Factorizing a hard joint distribution into location and appearance sub-models linked by a differentiable variable beats one monolithic generator over a huge combined solution space.
> - Semantic plausibility learned from 2D video and physical feasibility enforced in 3D voxels are complementary; fusing both auto-generates million-scale supervision no annotator could produce.
> - A discriminator conditioned on scene depth, used only at training time, lets an RGB-only model hallucinate 3D geometry without needing depth sensors at inference.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of visual understanding encompasses various aspects, including scene understanding and the interpretation of human-scene interactions. Traditionally, research in this area has focused on the semantic recognition of objects and scenes. However, the concept of "affordances," introduced by J.J. Gibson, offers a complementary perspective by defining opportunities for interaction that an environment provides for an agent.

Early approaches to inferring affordances often involved explicit modeling of physical interactions and contacts between humans and 3D scenes, frequently utilizing simulations. While these methods could model specific interactions, such as sitting styles, they often encountered challenges in generalization to a wider variety of poses and interactions.

More recent efforts have explored data-driven approaches to affordance inference. For example, some methods have collected human-scene interaction data from 2D video frames to train deep learning models. While these methods have demonstrated an ability to generate semantically plausible human poses in alignment with scene images, a notable limitation has been the lack of 3D geometric information about the scenes. This deficiency often results in generated poses that violate physical feasibility, such as a human appearing to float or intersect with objects in a 3D environment. The data collection process for these methods has also sometimes required manual effort, leading to limited dataset sizes.

Concurrently, research in instance placement in scenes, which involves inserting objects or agents into feasible locations, shares similarities with affordance prediction. However, existing instance placement methods frequently operate in 2D image spaces or require user-provided templates, and typically do not prioritize the physical feasibility of inserted instances within a 3D scene.

This paper addresses the gap in the research landscape by focusing on generating 3D human poses that are not only semantically plausible but also physically feasible within 3D indoor environments. It seeks to overcome the limitations of prior data-driven methods by integrating both semantic knowledge from 2D pose distributions and explicit 3D geometric constraints. The work aims to achieve this without requiring extensive manual annotation for 3D human-scene interaction datasets, which are labor-intensive to create. By synthesizing a large-scale 3D affordance dataset and developing a generative model, this research contributes a method for predicting complete 3D human poses from various scene inputs, thereby advancing the capabilities of human-centric scene understanding.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a computational model capable of predicting human affordances in 3D indoor scenes. Specifically, the authors aim to predict what human poses are afforded by a given indoor environment, such as the suitable positions and gestures for sitting on a chair or standing on the floor.

This overarching objective is motivated by several detailed considerations:

- **Semantic Plausibility:** The model should generate human poses that align with natural human behaviors and typical interactions observed in indoor environments. For instance, a human should be generated sitting on a chair rather than standing on it. Achieving this requires an understanding of the semantic context of a scene and the typical actions associated with its objects.
- **Physical Feasibility in 3D:** A significant motivation is to overcome a limitation of previous data-driven methods, which often produce poses that are not physically viable in a 3D space. The generated 3D human poses must adhere to physical rules, meaning they should not collide with objects (e.g., walls, furniture) and must be properly supported by surfaces within the scene (e.g., sitting on a sofa, standing on the floor). This necessitates incorporating 3D geometric understanding into the model.
- **Data Scarcity for 3D Human-Scene Interactions:** Learning a robust model for 3D affordance prediction requires a large-scale dataset of human poses precisely aligned with 3D indoor scene geometries. Manually collecting and annotating such a dataset is a tedious and time-consuming task, posing a significant bottleneck for data-driven approaches. This motivates the development of an automatic method for synthesizing such data.
- **Generalization Beyond Explicit Modeling:** While explicit modeling of physical interactions can ensure feasibility, these methods often struggle with generalization to diverse poses and scene configurations. The authors are motivated to develop a data-driven approach that can infer affordances without relying on rigid, pre-defined physical simulations, thereby improving generalizability.
- **Prediction from Diverse Scene Inputs:** The objective includes developing a model that can generate 3D human poses from common scene representations, specifically a single RGB image, an RGB-D image, or a depth map. This aims to make the affordance prediction practical and applicable across various input modalities.
- **Setting a New Benchmark:** By creating a large-scale, physically consistent dataset and a performant generative model, the authors intend to establish a new benchmark for human-centric affordance prediction on comprehensive 3D scene datasets like SUNCG.

In summary, the core motivation is to bridge the gap between semantic understanding of human-scene interactions and their physical realization in 3D, enabled by an automated data synthesis pipeline that addresses the challenge of creating large, 3D-annotated datasets.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology comprises a two-stage pipeline: first, a fully-automatic 3D pose synthesizer to generate a large-scale "ground-truth" dataset, and second, an end-to-end 3D affordance generative model that uses this dataset to predict human poses in novel scenes.

#### 3.1. 3D Pose Synthesis

This stage aims to automatically create a large dataset of 3D human poses that are both semantically plausible and physically feasible within 3D indoor scenes.

1. **Affordance Prediction in 2D Scene Images:**
 - The process begins by leveraging semantic knowledge from the Sitcom dataset, which contains 2D human pose samples from TV shows.
 - A 2D human pose prediction model is trained on this dataset. This model is designed to predict feasible locations for human poses (represented by pelvis joint coordinates) and the most likely pose class (e.g., sitting, standing) at those locations. To address sparse 2D pose annotations, annotations are augmented from single points to local square patches. Pose classes are represented by 31-dimensional binary vectors, including a background class.
 - The trained model is then adapted to images from the SUNCG dataset using domain adaptation, specifically by matching second-order statistics of image features. This allows the model to generate 2D poses that are semantically consistent with indoor environments, even if the SUNCG dataset lacks explicit pose annotations.
 - To enable 3D pose generation, 2D poses from the Sitcom dataset are mapped to 3D poses from the Human3.6M dataset by finding nearest neighbors in a projected 2D space. The 2D pose prediction model is thus extended to generate 3D poses at sampled locations.

2. **Mapping Poses into 3D Scenes:**
 - For the 2D poses generated on SUNCG images, their 3D depths are unknown. The method estimates these depths by sampling human heights from Gaussian distributions (e.g., N(1.65, 0.1) for standing) and using camera parameters (intrinsic and extrinsic matrices) to project the 2D pose onto a specific depth plane in the 3D world. The depth of the pelvis joint is estimated, and other joint depths are derived from their offsets.
 - Each joint is then mapped into the 3D world coordinate system using the estimated depth and camera parameters.

3. **Affordance Constraint in the 3D World (Pose Adjustment):**
 - Since the initial 3D mapping relies primarily on 2D information, the resulting 3D poses may not be physically feasible (e.g., colliding with objects or floating).
 - **Free Space Constraint:** A 3D correlation operation is performed between the voxelized 3D pose and a binarized voxel representation of the scene (free space as zero, occupied as one). Areas where the correlation result is below a threshold indicate no intersection. Body parts expected to contact objects (e.g., thighs/pelvis for sitting, feet for standing) are masked out during this check.
 - **Support Constraint:** To ensure poses are supported, a two-step 3D correlation is used. First, a 3D Gaussian kernel is correlated with a voxel representation of affordable objects to detect surface voxels. Second, the 3D pose is correlated with these detected object surfaces to find locations with maximum contact. The pose is then adjusted to the location with the highest support correlation score, ensuring it is properly supported.
 - Poses that do not satisfy these constraints within a local adjustment region are discarded.
 - This fully automatic synthesizer produces approximately 1.5 million refined "ground-truth" 3D poses across 13,774 SUNCG scenes.

#### 3.2. 3D Affordance Generative Model

This stage develops a deep generative model to predict 3D human poses given a single scene image (RGB, RGB-D, or depth map). The model decomposes the complex task into two jointly learned sub-tasks:

1. **The Where Module: Pose Locations Prediction:**
 - A Variational Autoencoder (VAE) is employed to predict the plausible 3D location (pelvis joint coordinates (x, y) and depth (d)) and the most likely pose class (p_c) within a given scene image (I).
 - **Structure:** An 18-layer ResNet extracts image features, which are concatenated with location and pose class features. These are fed into fully connected layers to predict the mean (μ) and variance (σ) for a latent variable distribution (Q). The decoder samples from Q and, using scene context features, predicts {x, y, d, p_c}. A Differentiable Spatial to Numerical Transform (DSNT) layer converts a predicted heatmap into continuous coordinates.
 - **Objectives:** The module is trained with three losses:
 - Euclidean distance (L_mse) on predicted pelvis coordinates, depth, and pose class.
 - Kullback-Leibler divergence (L_kld) between the latent distribution Q and a standard normal distribution.
 - Geometry loss (L_geo), which minimizes the Euclidean distance between ground truth and predicted pelvis coordinates in the world coordinate system, using camera parameters.

2. **The What Module: Pose Gestures Prediction:**
 - A second VAE takes the pelvis joint coordinates (x, y), depth (d), and pose class (p_c) from the 'where' module, along with the scene image (I), to predict the 3D coordinates and depth for all 17 joints of the human pose (p).
 - **Structure and Objectives:** This module shares a similar VAE structure and objective functions (L_mse, L_kld, L_geo) as the 'where' module, but its output is the full 3D pose (all joint coordinates and depths).
 - The pose class (p_c) is represented by the normalized center pose of its corresponding cluster, capturing similarities between pose typologies.

3. **Joint Training and Geometry-Aware Discriminator:**
 - The 'where' and 'what' modules are jointly trained, with the pose location serving as a differentiable link, allowing mutual benefit from learning.
 - **Geometry-Aware Discriminator:** To encourage the model to hallucinate 3D geometry from 2D inputs and generate physically correct poses, a discriminator is introduced.
 - This discriminator is trained to distinguish between geometrically feasible (real, from the synthesizer) and unfeasible (fake, from the generator) pose-scene pairs.
 - A pre-trained CNN converts the discrete joint coordinates and depth into a "depth heat map," which is fed alongside the scene depth image to the discriminator.
 - The discriminator uses an adversarial loss (L_adv), similar to GANs, to regularize the generator. This discriminator is only used during training.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings regarding the proposed 3D pose synthesizer and the 3D affordance generative model.

#### 4.1. Dataset Synthesis and Evaluation Metrics

The automatic 3D pose synthesizer successfully generated approximately 1.5 million physically feasible and semantically plausible 3D poses across 13,774 SUNCG indoor scenes. This addresses the challenge of creating large-scale 3D human-scene interaction datasets.

To evaluate the generated poses, two main quantitative metrics were introduced:
- **Semantic Score:** Measured by a trained "pose authenticity classifier" (achieving 86% accuracy), which determines if a generated pose is plausible and looks like a natural human pose in the scene context. Higher scores indicate better semantic plausibility.
- **Geometry Score:** Measured by checking if a generated 3D pose satisfies both the free space constraint (no intersection with objects) and the support constraint (properly supported by surfaces) within the scene's voxel representation. Higher scores indicate better physical feasibility.
Additionally, a **user study** was conducted to obtain human judgment on the visual reasonableness of generated poses.

#### 4.2. 3D Affordance Prediction

The proposed model consistently demonstrated superior performance compared to a baseline method adapted from Wang et al., which primarily focuses on 2D pose prediction.

- **Overall Performance (RGB Input):** Even when relying solely on a single RGB image, the proposed method achieved a semantic score 19.47% higher and a geometry score 19.02% higher than the baseline model. This indicates the model's ability to understand both scene context and geometry.
- **User Study Validation:** In a user study, poses generated by the proposed method were deemed more reasonable than those from the baseline and were statistically indistinguishable from ground-truth poses.
- **Impact of Depth Information:** Incorporating depth information into the input (RGB-D or depth map only) further improved the geometry score, demonstrating the value of explicit 3D information for physical feasibility. For instance, the RGB-D model achieved a geometry score of 71.17%, and the depth-only model achieved 72.11% (compared to 66.40% for RGB). Qualitative examples illustrate this, where a sitting pose might float above a sofa with RGB input but aligns correctly with RGB-D or depth input.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies provided insights into the contribution of different components of the proposed model:

- **Decomposed vs. Single Model:** A single VAE attempting to model joint distributions of pose locations and gestures (semantic score 76.23, geometry score 52.94 for RGB) performed significantly worse than the proposed decomposed 'where' and 'what' modules (semantic 91.69, geometry 66.40 for RGB). This supports the factorization of the problem.
- **Joint Training:** Removing joint training of the 'where' and 'what' modules led to a substantial reduction in semantic score (8.06% drop for RGB input), indicating its importance for consistent semantic understanding. While geometry score might appear higher in some cases without joint training, this was attributed to unnaturally small poses that incidentally avoided collisions.
- **Adversarial Training (Geometry-Aware Discriminator):** The geometry-aware discriminator was effective in encouraging the model to generate poses that better adhere to geometric rules, resulting in higher geometry scores.
- **Geometry Loss:** The inclusion of a geometry loss, which minimizes the Euclidean distance between predicted and ground-truth poses in world coordinates, contributed to generating poses consistent with 3D world geometry, improving the geometry score by 4.59% (for RGB input).

#### 4.4. Comparison with State-of-the-Art (What Module)

Focusing solely on the 'what' module (pose generation at given locations) for a fair comparison with the baseline, the proposed model demonstrated a 6.66% higher geometry score (78.43% vs. 56.29% for RGB input). This indicates its superior ability to generate physically valid poses even when the location is provided. Furthermore, the model showed the ability to generate different pose scales and actions at the same (x, y) pixel location by interpolating depth values, demonstrating an understanding of 3D scene geometry.

#### 4.5. Failure Cases

Identified failure cases fell into two categories:
- **Semantic failures:** The model sometimes failed to predict socially acceptable poses due to incorrect semantic understanding of the scene (e.g., sitting on a cabinet).
- **Geometric failures:** Poses occasionally violated geometric rules, such as colliding with objects or floating, indicating a failure in 3D geometry hallucination from 2D information. These failures highlight challenges in comprehensive object functionality understanding and robust 3D reasoning from limited input.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research makes several contributions to the field of visual understanding and human-scene interaction, with potential impacts across various applications.

- **Overcoming Data Scarcity:** The introduction of a fully-automatic 3D human pose synthesizer is a significant contribution. It addresses the critical challenge of acquiring large-scale, detailed 3D human-scene interaction data, which has traditionally been a bottleneck due to the labor-intensive nature of manual annotation. By synthesizing millions of "ground-truth" poses, the work enables data-driven approaches to 3D affordance learning.
- **Bridging the 2D-3D Gap in Affordance Prediction:** The proposed end-to-end generative model effectively integrates semantic understanding (from 2D observations) with physical feasibility (from 3D geometry). This allows for the generation of human poses that are not only contextually appropriate but also geometrically sound, overcoming a key limitation of previous 2D-only data-driven methods.
- **Robust Generative Model:** The decomposed architecture (where and what modules) and the incorporation of a geometry-aware discriminator result in a robust model capable of predicting both plausible locations and gestures of human poses from various single-image inputs (RGB, RGB-D, depth). This demonstrates an advanced capability in hallucinating 3D geometry from 2D visual cues.
- **Establishing a New Benchmark:** The methodology, including the data synthesis process and the evaluation metrics (semantic and geometry scores, user studies), establishes a framework for future research in large-scale human-centric affordance prediction on 3D scene datasets.
- **Potential Applications:** The ability to accurately predict plausible and feasible human poses in 3D indoor environments has broad implications:
 - **Robotics:** Enhancing human-robot interaction by allowing robots to anticipate and understand human actions and spatial needs, facilitating safer and more intuitive collaboration. It could also aid in robot task planning by identifying functional regions for human activity.
 - **Virtual Reality and Augmented Reality:** Creating more immersive and realistic virtual environments by populating them with contextually appropriate and physically correct virtual agents.
 - **Computer Graphics and Animation:** Automating the placement and posing of characters in synthetic scenes, reducing manual effort for animators and content creators.
 - **Assistive Technologies:** Designing intelligent systems that can understand human intentions and environmental affordances to provide better support or recommendations.
 - **Advanced Scene Understanding:** Contributing to a deeper, human-centric understanding of scenes, moving beyond object recognition to inferring functional properties and interaction possibilities.
- **Directions for Future Research:** The identified failure cases regarding semantic understanding and robust 3D geometry hallucination from 2D inputs present clear avenues for future work, aiming to improve the reasoning capabilities of such models.

In conclusion, this research significantly advances the capabilities of predicting human affordances in 3D environments, driven by an innovative automated data generation pipeline and a robust, multi-faceted generative model.
