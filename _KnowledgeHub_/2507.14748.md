# Skill Learning via Policy Diversity Yields Identifiable Representations for Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers provided the first theoretical identifiability guarantee for learned features in reinforcement learning, demonstrating that methods like Contrastive Successor Features (CSF) can recover ground-truth environmental features up to a linear transformation. Empirical validation showed high R² scores in diverse environments and highlighted the importance of skill diversity for this identifiability.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning (RL) agents face challenges with sparse rewards, inefficient exploration, and the need for complex reward function engineering.
- Self-supervised skill discovery methods (MISL) exhibit inconsistent performance, lacking clear theoretical explanations for why certain architectures or mutual information parametrizations are more effective.
- A theoretical gap existed in providing identifiability guarantees for learned representations in RL, particularly in partially observable environments.

## Method

- The research models the Reinforcement Learning problem as a ==Data Generating Process== (DGP) to apply identifiability theory from nonlinear Independent Component Analysis (ICA).
- It theoretically analyzes Contrastive Successor Features (CSF), demonstrating its alignment with conditions required for identifiable representation learning.
- The approach leverages specific mutual information objectives (I(s, s'; z)) and an inner product parametrization for the critic to learn representations that are linearly identifiable.

## Results

- CSF achieved high R² scores (up to **0.99**) for learned features and feature differences in MuJoCo and DeepMind Control environments, empirically validating linear identifiability.
- Sufficient skill diversity, particularly continuous sampling from the skill distribution, was demonstrated as crucial for high identifiability (R² scores) and increased state coverage.
- The dimensionality of the learned latent space directly impacts identifiability, with dimensions lower than the true state space acting as an information bottleneck, and a latent space equal to state dimensionality often maximizing zero-shot task transfer.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Under specific conditions, Contrastive Successor Features (CSF) provably recover the environment's ground-truth features up to a linear transformation.
> - Optimizing the mutual information I(s, s'; z) (between consecutive states and skill) enforces a "locality" constraint, yielding a more meaningful and identifiable latent space geometry compared to I(s; z).
> - Maximum entropy skill-conditioned policies do not promote the necessary skill diversity for identifiability, as they break the unique dependence between state transitions and skills.
