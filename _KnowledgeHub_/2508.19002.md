# HuBE: Cross-Embodiment Human-like Behavior Execution for Humanoid Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from The Hong Kong Polytechnic University and Eastern Institute of Technology developed HuBE, a bi-level closed-loop framework for humanoid robots that generates behaviors exhibiting both kinematic similarity to human motion and contextual appropriateness. The system achieves millimeter-level accuracy when transferring behaviors across diverse robot morphologies and demonstrates improved computational efficiency compared to existing methods.

## Problem

- Existing robot motion generation methods often prioritize kinematic similarity to human movement but overlook contextual appropriateness, leading to awkward or socially unacceptable robot actions.
- Current human motion datasets lack the fine-grained semantic annotations needed to link specific contextual situations with appropriate behaviors, hindering the learning of nuanced human-like actions.
- Many motion generation techniques struggle with cross-embodiment adaptability, making it challenging to transfer learned behaviors to humanoid robots with different morphologies without extensive re-adaptation.

## Method

- A bi-level closed-loop **HuBE** framework that integrates contextual information and robot-specific constraints into the motion generation and execution process.
- ==The HPose== dataset, which augments existing human motion data with LLM-generated, fine-grained situational annotations and bone scaling for cross-embodiment compatibility, paired with a hindsight training strategy for broader motion semantics.
- A multi-constrained Closed-loop Inverse Kinematics (CLIK) solver built on Pinocchio to accurately retarget generated poses to robot joint space, preserving human-likeness across different robot anatomies.

## Results

- The framework achieved millimeter-level accuracy in bone-size stability and generation errors when adapting to different robot skeletal systems (e.g., GR1, TALOS, G1) after training with the augmented HPose dataset.
- HuBE outperformed state-of-the-art baselines (siMLPe, HumanPlus) in behavioral appropriateness metrics (lower FMD and MM-Dist, higher R-Precision) and achieved superior Human Similarity (H-S) while maintaining acceptable End-effector Accuracy (E-A).
- The system demonstrated high computational efficiency, executing in 0.0207s, which is significantly faster than a pure IK algorithm (0.3618s), contributing to more responsive human-robot interaction.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly integrating fine-grained, language-based situational contexts significantly enhances both the kinematic similarity and contextual appropriateness of generated robot behaviors.
> - A bone scaling-based data augmentation strategy is effective for achieving millimeter-level cross-embodiment compatibility, enabling a single policy to generalize across heterogeneous humanoid robot designs.
> - Hindsight training, by sampling future poses as targets, substantially enriches the training dataset and improves the model's ability to generate diverse and contextually appropriate human-like motions.
