# Beyond Attention or Similarity: Maximizing Conditional Diversity for Token Pruning in MLLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Peking University and ByteDance propose CDPruner, a training-free token pruning method for Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) that dynamically selects visual tokens by maximizing their conditional diversity, leading to over 94% token reduction while preserving more than 94% of original accuracy on various benchmarks and achieving up to a 10x reduction in FLOPs.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) generate a high number of visual tokens (hundreds to thousands) from image/video inputs, leading to prohibitive inference costs due to the quadratic complexity of attention mechanisms.
- Existing attention-based token pruning methods often retain redundant visual tokens and can be incompatible with efficient attention implementations like FlashAttention.
- Existing similarity-based pruning methods fail to consider the user's instruction, leading to static pruning that may discard visual information relevant to the specific query.

## Method

- Introduces ==CDPruner==, a training-free and model-agnostic visual token pruning method that maximizes ==conditional diversity==.
- Calculates a ==conditional kernel matrix== by weighting visual token feature similarities with their relevance to the input text instruction.
- Leverages ==Determinantal Point Processes (DPPs)== with a greedy algorithm to select a diverse subset of visual tokens that are most relevant to the given instruction.

## Results

- CDPruner maintains **94.3%** of original accuracy on LLaVA-1.5 even with a **94.4%** reduction in visual tokens (from **576** to **32**), outperforming other methods by up to **3%**.
- Achieves up to a **10x** reduction in FLOPs, **6.6x** faster prefill latency, **1.3x** faster decode latency, and over **88%** KV cache reduction for MLLMs like LLaVA-NeXT-7B.
- Demonstrates strong generalizability and superior performance across diverse MLLM architectures (LLaVA-1.5, LLaVA-NeXT, LLaVA-Video, Qwen2.5-VL, InternVL3) and various vision-language tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Jointly maximizing feature diversity and instruction relevance is crucial for effective visual token pruning, leading to dynamic and context-aware selection.
> - Determinantal Point Processes (DPPs) are an effective mathematical framework for modeling and selecting diverse subsets of visual tokens, outperforming traditional similarity-based methods.
> - A training-free and model-agnostic pruning approach can significantly reduce MLLM inference costs while maintaining high performance and compatibility with various architectures and optimizations like FlashAttention.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancement of large language models (LLMs) has naturally led to the development of multimodal large language models (MLLMs), which extend LLMs' reasoning capabilities to include other modalities, primarily images and videos. MLLMs achieve this by encoding visual inputs into a sequence of "visual tokens" that can be processed by the language model. However, a significant challenge arises from the disparity in token lengths: visual inputs often generate hundreds or even thousands of tokens, dwarfing the length of textual prompts. Given that attention mechanisms, a core component of these models, scale quadratically with input token length, this excessive number of visual tokens results in prohibitively high inference costs, making MLLMs impractical for real-time or resource-constrained applications.

The broader research landscape for addressing this issue can be categorized into several approaches:

- **Pre-fusion/Architectural Modifications:** Some methods aim to compress visual tokens through vision-text pre-fusion techniques or require significant architectural modifications and additional training. While effective, these approaches often introduce extra computational costs during training and lack flexibility.
- **Training-Free Token Pruning:** A more practical and flexible approach is training-free token pruning, which aims to remove redundant visual tokens during inference. This category is further divided:
 - **Attention-based Pruning:** These methods identify "important" visual tokens based on their attention scores within the language model. Examples include FastV, PyramidDrop, and SparseVLM. A key limitation, as highlighted by this paper, is that they tend to retain numerous duplicate tokens because high attention scores don't necessarily imply diversity or non-redundancy. Furthermore, reliance on internal attention scores can make them incompatible with optimized attention implementations like FlashAttention, which hide intermediate attention scores. They can also suffer from "attention shift," compromising pruning accuracy.
 - **Similarity-based Pruning:** These methods remove redundant parts based on feature similarity among visual tokens, often pruning tokens *before* they enter the language model. Examples include DART and DivPrune. While addressing some issues of attention-based methods (like incompatibility), they largely neglect the "instruction relevance"—the context provided by the user's query. This leads to static pruning results that don't adapt to the specific question, potentially discarding visual information crucial for answering the current instruction.
 - **Vision-based Pruning (Hybrid):** Some methods, like LLaVA-Prumerge, TRIM, and VisionZip, fall into a hybrid category, leveraging visual information (e.g., attention within the visual encoder) sometimes combined with text encoder information for pruning. They also face challenges in dynamic pruning and often rely on specific visual encoder architectures.

This paper, "Beyond Attention or Similarity," directly positions itself as an advancement in training-free visual token pruning. It critically assesses the limitations of both pure attention-based and pure similarity-based methods. By proposing **Conditional Diversity (CDPruner)**, the authors aim to overcome these limitations by dynamically adjusting pruning based on the user instruction while simultaneously maximizing the diversity of the retained visual information. This novel approach seeks to combine the strengths of both previous categories, offering a more effective and robust solution for MLLM inference acceleration.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation behind this research stems from the significant computational burden imposed by the high number of visual tokens in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). As MLLMs scale in capabilities (e.g., higher resolution inputs, video processing), the number of visual tokens can grow exponentially, leading to prohibitive inference costs due to the quadratic complexity of attention mechanisms. This makes MLLMs impractical for deployment in latency-sensitive or resource-constrained environments.

The key objectives of this work are to:

1. **Reduce MLLM Inference Cost:** The primary goal is to significantly decrease the computational overhead associated with processing visual inputs in MLLMs, enabling faster inference and lower resource consumption (FLOPs, CUDA latency, GPU memory, KV cache).
2. **Overcome Limitations of Existing Pruning Methods:**
 - **Address Redundancy in Attention-based Pruning:** Previous attention-based methods often retain duplicate visual tokens, leading to inefficient compression despite identifying "important" regions. The objective is to achieve more effective compression by minimizing redundancy.
 - **Incorporate Instruction Relevance in Similarity-based Pruning:** Existing similarity-based methods fail to consider the user's instruction or query during pruning, resulting in static and potentially suboptimal pruning decisions that may discard question-relevant visual information. The motivation is to enable dynamic pruning that adapts to the specific instruction.
3. **Develop a Novel Pruning Mechanism: Conditional Diversity:** Propose a new approach that goes "beyond attention or similarity" by maximizing the conditional diversity of the retained tokens. This involves simultaneously considering feature similarity among visual tokens and their relevance to the input instruction.
4. **Ensure Practicality and Generalizability:** Design the proposed method, CDPruner, to be:
 - **Training-free:** No additional training or fine-tuning is required, making it easy to integrate.
 - **Model-agnostic:** Compatible with various MLLM architectures and not reliant on specific visual encoders or language models.
 - **Plug-and-play:** Easy to apply to existing MLLMs without significant architectural modifications.
5. **Achieve State-of-the-Art Performance:** Demonstrate that the proposed CDPruner can achieve superior performance compared to existing token pruning methods across diverse vision-language and video understanding benchmarks, even at high reduction ratios.

In essence, the authors are motivated to develop a smart, efficient, and versatile visual token pruning strategy that preserves essential information relevant to a given user query while aggressively discarding redundant visual data, thereby making powerful MLLMs more accessible and deployable.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the proposed method, **CDPruner**, is a novel visual token pruning strategy that maximizes "conditional diversity" among the retained visual tokens. It is designed to be training-free and model-agnostic. The approach proceeds in three main steps:

1. **Visual Token Extraction and Representation:**
 - MLLMs typically convert an input image `X_v` into a sequence of visual tokens `H_v` using a vision encoder `f_v` and a multimodal projector `g` (i.e., `H_v = g(f_v(X_v))`). These visual tokens `H_v ∈ R^(n×d)` represent `n` visual patches, each with `d` dimensions.
 - The goal is to select a subset `~H_v*` of `m` tokens (`m < n`) that optimally represents the image and is relevant to the instruction.

2. **Defining Conditional Similarity (Feature Similarity + Instruction Relevance):**
 - **Feature Similarity (L):** The method first calculates the pairwise cosine similarity between all visual tokens `H_i_v` and `H_j_v` to form a kernel matrix `L ∈ R^(n×n)`. This `L` captures the intrinsic redundancy or diversity based purely on visual features.
 `L_ij = (H_i_v ⋅ H_j_v) / (||H_i_v|| ⋅ ||H_j_v||)`
 - **Instruction Relevance (r):** To make the pruning dynamic and instruction-aware, CDPruner incorporates the relevance of each visual token to the input text instruction `X_q`. This is achieved by:
 - Extracting a text embedding `_H_q` from the user instruction (e.g., averaging token embeddings from the language model or using a paired text encoder like CLIP's text encoder).
 - Calculating the cosine similarity `r_i` between each visual token `H_i_v` and the instruction embedding `_H_q`.
 - Normalizing these relevance scores `r` to a range of 0 to 1, resulting in `~r`.
 - **Conditional Kernel Matrix (`~L`):** The feature similarity matrix `L` is then modulated by the normalized instruction relevance scores `~r` to form a new conditional kernel matrix:
 `~L = diag (~r) ⋅ L ⋅ diag (~r)`
 This formulation effectively weights the similarity between visual tokens by their individual relevance to the instruction. Tokens highly relevant to the instruction will have their similarities amplified, making them more likely to be selected in a diverse subset.

3. **Maximizing Conditional Diversity with Determinantal Point Process (DPP):**
 - **DPP Background:** Determinantal Point Processes (DPPs) are a mathematical tool widely used for modeling diversity. They assign a probability to a subset of items that is proportional to the determinant of a submatrix of a positive semi-definite (PSD) kernel matrix. A larger determinant implies a more diverse and representative subset.
 - **Problem Reformulation:** The visual token pruning problem is reformulated as finding the subset `S*` of `m` tokens that maximizes `det(~L_S)`, where `~L_S` is the principal submatrix of the conditional kernel `~L` corresponding to `S`.
 - **Greedy MAP Inference:** While exact MAP inference for DPP is NP-hard, the authors utilize a fast greedy algorithm that provides a `(1 - 1/e)` approximation. This algorithm iteratively selects the token that, when added to the current subset, maximally increases the determinant (or, equivalently, `log(d_i^2)` where `d_i^2` is related to the conditional variance). The overall time complexity of this greedy algorithm is `O(nm^2)`, which is negligible (less than 10ms per sample) when `m << n`.

**Key Advantages of the Approach:**

- **Joint Consideration:** CDPruner uniquely combines feature diversity and instruction relevance, allowing for dynamic and intelligent pruning.
- **Training-Free & Model-Agnostic:** It operates directly on extracted visual and text embeddings, requiring no model retraining or architectural modifications. This makes it highly portable across different MLLMs (LLaVA, Qwen2.5-VL, InternVL3, etc.) and compatible with efficient attention implementations (like FlashAttention) as it doesn't rely on attention scores.
- **Global Diversity:** Unlike methods like Max-Min Diversity Problem (MMDP), DPP emphasizes global diversity, leading to more balanced and representative subsets of tokens.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted by the authors demonstrate the superior effectiveness and efficiency of CDPruner across various MLLM architectures and multimodal benchmarks.

#### 4.1. Performance on LLaVA-1.5 (Image Understanding)

- **State-of-the-Art:** CDPruner consistently outperforms existing attention-based, attention&similarity-based, and similarity-based pruning methods.
- **High Reduction Ratios:**
 - **77.8% reduction (128 tokens retained):** CDPruner maintains 99.0% of original accuracy, surpassing VisionZip by 1.4%.
 - **88.9% reduction (64 tokens retained):** CDPruner retains 97.0% of original accuracy, outperforming VisionZip by 2.6% and DivPrune by 2.3%. Attention-based methods show significant degradation (over 25% performance drop), highlighting their failure at high compression.
 - **94.4% reduction (32 tokens retained):** CDPruner still maintains 94.3% of original accuracy, significantly outperforming DivPrune (the best similarity-based method) by 3%.
- **Hallucination Mitigation:** On the POPE benchmark (designed to evaluate visual hallucination), CDPruner sometimes even exceeds the performance of the unpruned LLaVA-1.5, suggesting that appropriate pruning might help mitigate hallucination in MLLMs.
- **Limitations:** Performance advantage is limited on benchmarks like VizWiz where questions lack informative context for conditional guidance.

#### 4.2. Performance on LLaVA-NeXT (High-Resolution Inputs)

- **Strong Effectiveness:** Applied to LLaVA-NeXT (2,880 tokens initially), CDPruner maintains excellent performance:
 - **77.8% reduction (640 tokens retained):** Performance comparable to or slightly better than the original LLaVA-NeXT (100.1% of original accuracy), indicating high visual redundancy in high-resolution inputs.
 - **88.9% reduction (320 tokens retained):** 98.0% of original performance maintained, outperforming DivPrune by 2%.
 - **94.4% reduction (160 tokens retained):** 96.0% of original performance maintained, outperforming DivPrune by 3.1%.

#### 4.3. Performance on LLaVA-Video (Video Understanding)

- **Effectiveness in Video Scenarios:** On LLaVA-Video (over 10,000 tokens for 64 frames), CDPruner continues to show strong results:
 - **62.1% reduction (64 tokens per frame retained):** 98.6% of original performance, outperforming PDrop by 1.5%.
 - **81.1% reduction (32 tokens per frame retained):** 95% of original performance, significantly exceeding DivPrune's 93%.
 - **90.5% reduction (16 tokens per frame retained):** 89.7% of original performance, a substantial 10% improvement over SparseVLM, while text-based methods show severe degradation.

#### 4.4. Performance on Advanced Architectures (Qwen2.5-VL and InternVL3)

- **Generalizability:** CDPruner demonstrates strong generalizability on state-of-the-art open-source MLLMs.
- **Qwen2.5-VL (1,296 tokens initially):** Even when visual tokens are already compressed within the model's projector, CDPruner consistently outperforms FastV and DivPrune. At 90.1% reduction (128 tokens retained), CDPruner maintains 85.2% of original performance, significantly higher than DivPrune's 79.9%.
- **InternVL3-8B (1,280 tokens initially):** At 90.0% reduction (128 tokens retained), CDPruner retains 83.9% of original performance, 3% higher than FastV, demonstrating its adaptability to advanced MLLM architectures. Notably, DivPrune shows significant performance drop on InternVL3.

#### 4.5. Efficiency Analysis

- **Significant Resource Reduction (LLaVA-NeXT-7B, 320 tokens retained from 2,880):**
 - **FLOPs:** Achieves nearly a 10x reduction (95% reduction).
 - **CUDA Latency:** Reduces prefill time by 6.6x and decode time by 1.3x.
 - **GPU Memory:** Reduces by 17% (e.g., from 16.7GB to 13.8GB).
 - **KV Cache:** Reduces by over 88% (e.g., from 1440MB to 160MB).
- **Best Efficiency and Performance:** CDPruner consistently achieves the best efficiency while maintaining the highest performance compared to other pruning methods.

#### 4.6. Ablation Study

- **Benefit of DPP:** A variant without instruction relevance (DPPruner) still outperforms DivPrune, confirming that global diversity modeling via DPP is more effective than MMDP.
- **Benefit of Conditional Diversity:** Incorporating instruction relevance (CDPruner) yields additional performance gains, validating the joint modeling of feature similarity and instruction relevance.
- **Balance Factor (θ):** Tuning a balance factor between diversity and relevance can further improve performance, but even without explicit tuning (default setting used in main experiments), CDPruner performs strongly.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in "Beyond Attention or Similarity: Maximizing Conditional Diversity for Token Pruning in MLLMs" offers significant contributions to the field of efficient multimodal AI, with considerable potential impact on the practical deployment and accessibility of MLLMs.

#### 5.1. Core Significance

- **Novel Pruning Paradigm:** CDPruner introduces "conditional diversity" as a new, powerful paradigm for visual token pruning. By explicitly combining feature similarity and instruction relevance within a Determinantal Point Process (DPP) framework, it overcomes the inherent limitations of previous attention-based (redundancy) and similarity-based (lack of instruction awareness) methods. This hybrid approach represents a crucial step forward in intelligent token selection.
- **Robust Performance at High Compression:** The consistent state-of-the-art results across various MLLM architectures (LLaVA series, Qwen2.5-VL, InternVL3) and diverse benchmarks (VQA, GQA, POPE, MME, video tasks) demonstrate CDPruner's robustness. Its ability to maintain high accuracy even with drastic visual token reductions (e.g., 90-95%) is particularly significant, pushing the boundaries of what's achievable with training-free methods.
- **Efficiency Gains for Practical Deployment:** The substantial reductions in FLOPs (up to 10x), CUDA latency (up to 6.6x prefill, 1.4x decode), KV cache, and GPU memory are highly impactful. These efficiency improvements directly translate to:
 - **Lower Inference Costs:** Reducing the operational expenses for running MLLMs in cloud or data center environments.
 - **Faster Response Times:** Enabling MLLMs to be used in real-time or low-latency applications.
 - **Broader Accessibility:** Making powerful MLLMs viable on resource-constrained edge devices (e.g., mobile phones, embedded systems) that traditionally lack the computational power for such large models.

#### 5.2. Practical Advantages and Implications

- **Training-Free and Plug-and-Play:** The method's training-free nature eliminates the need for expensive and time-consuming retraining processes, making it immediately deployable to existing MLLMs. Its model-agnostic and plug-and-play design ensures wide compatibility and ease of integration.
- **Compatibility with Modern Optimizations:** Unlike attention-based methods that can interfere with efficient attention implementations (like FlashAttention), CDPruner's independence from attention scores ensures full compatibility, allowing it to benefit from ongoing hardware and software optimizations.
- **Potential for Hallucination Mitigation:** The observation that CDPruner can improve performance on hallucination benchmarks (like POPE) suggests an intriguing avenue for future research. By retaining more relevant and diverse information, the model might be less prone to generating factually incorrect or ungrounded responses, a critical issue in current MLLMs.

#### 5.3. Limitations and Future Work (as identified by authors)

- **Open-Source MLLM Dependency:** CDPruner currently requires access to the internal visual tokens, limiting its application to black-box models (e.g., ChatGPT, Gemini). Future work could explore proxy methods or model-specific adaptations.
- **Sensitivity in Advanced Architectures:** While effective, the paper notes that advanced MLLMs (Qwen2.5-VL, InternVL3) show a more noticeable performance drop after pruning compared to LLaVA. This is attributed to potential pre-existing visual token compression within their projectors. Future research could focus on optimizing pruning strategies specifically for these pre-compressed architectures.

#### 5.4. Broader Impacts

The work has a generally positive broader impact by making advanced AI technology more efficient and accessible. By reducing the computational footprint of MLLMs, it can democratize their use, foster innovation in various application domains, and reduce the energy consumption associated with large-scale AI inference. The authors explicitly state that their work does not mitigate potential misuse of MLLMs by malicious actors, which is a standard disclosure acknowledging the ethical considerations of general-purpose AI technologies.

In conclusion, CDPruner represents a significant step towards more efficient and practical MLLMs. Its novel approach to conditional diversity, combined with its training-free and model-agnostic nature, positions it as a highly valuable contribution to the ongoing efforts to democratize and deploy large multimodal AI models.
