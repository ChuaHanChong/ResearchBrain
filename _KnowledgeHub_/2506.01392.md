# Sparse Imagination for Efficient Visual World Model Planning

> [!summary] Summary
> A method called Sparse Imagination enhances the efficiency of visual world model planning by processing only randomly sampled subsets of visual tokens, leading to substantial reductions in planning time (e.g., 52.6% faster in PushT) while preserving or improving control performance in robotic manipulation and navigation tasks.

## Problem

- Transformer-based visual world models leverage numerous visual tokens for rich spatial understanding, but incur high quadratic computational costs during planning.
- The intensive computational burden from processing many tokens makes real-time deployment of these powerful models prohibitive in resource-constrained environments like robotics.
- Existing token efficiency methods primarily focus on training-time or static reduction, not dynamic inference-time planning for model predictive control.

## Method

- A ==sparsely trained== vision-based world model is developed using a fixed pre-trained image encoder and a transformer-based dynamics model.
- A randomized grouped attention strategy is employed during training, encouraging the model to operate robustly on arbitrary token subsets and promoting adaptability.
- During Model Predictive Control (MPC) planning, **Sparse Imagination** dynamically samples a random subset of visual tokens at each iteration, processing only these selected tokens for future state predictions.

## Results

- Achieved significant reductions in planning time (e.g., **52.6%** speedup in PushT) while maintaining or improving task success rates comparable to full-patch baselines across various environments.
- Demonstrated superior performance over single CLS-token baselines in tasks requiring fine-grained spatial reasoning, such as deformable object manipulation.
- Integrated with VLA models for real-world robotic tasks, it increased success rates (e.g., PickPlace from **60%** to **80%**) and reduced planning latency, making real-time control more feasible.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - ViT patch tokens exhibit sufficient redundancy such that accurate planning can be achieved with only a sparse, randomly sampled subset of tokens.
> - Simple random token sampling during planning often outperforms more complex, importance-based token selection strategies due to its unbiased coverage.
> - Static token importance methods can suffer from a "blind spot" problem, where ignoring specific regions leads to planning failures when task-critical information appears in those areas.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

World models have emerged as a significant paradigm in decision-making for complex environments, enabling agents to mentally simulate future states and formulate informed policies without extensive real-world trial-and-error. Early advancements in this field transitioned from computationally intensive pixel-level predictions to more efficient latent dynamics models, such as PlaNet and Dreamer, which compress entire images into lower-dimensional vectors. However, these compact representations can sometimes sacrifice the fine-grained spatial information crucial for high-precision manipulation tasks.

A recent trend in visual control tasks leverages powerful pre-trained self-supervised vision models, particularly Vision Transformers (ViTs), to derive rich, high-level visual features. Approaches like DINO-WM, for instance, utilize ViT patch tokens (e.g., DINO features) as state representations, allowing for zero-shot planning and improved generalization across diverse tasks by retaining rich spatial information. While effective, the use of numerous visual tokens in transformer-based world models leads to a significant computational burden, characterized by a quadratic scaling of self-attention with the number of tokens. This computational cost is particularly restrictive in real-time applications and resource-constrained environments, such as robotics, where numerous simulated rollouts are required for planning.

The challenge of ViT token efficiency has been a recognized area of research within computer vision, with various methods proposed to reduce computational cost. These include learned or attention-based token selection, token merging strategies, and training-time dropout mechanisms. These methods generally aim to exploit the observed redundancy in ViT representations to achieve efficiency gains. This paper fits into this broader landscape by addressing the specific problem of computational efficiency in *inference-time visual world model planning*, proposing a novel application of token reduction tailored to the dynamic and iterative nature of model predictive control.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the computational efficiency of visual world model planning, particularly in contexts where resources are constrained, such as robotic applications. The authors identify a crucial challenge: while recent advancements in visual world models leverage numerous high-level visual features (e.g., ViT patch tokens) to achieve robust generalization and detailed spatial understanding, the quadratic computational cost associated with processing these tokens in transformer-based architectures during extensive simulated rollouts becomes prohibitive. This computational burden impedes the deployment of these powerful models in real-time scenarios.

The central question addressed by this work is: "Can we retain the advantages of detailed visual world models while enhancing computational efficiency for planning?" The motivation stems from the insight that ViT-based representations, despite their richness, often exhibit redundancy. This redundancy suggests that not all patch tokens are equally critical or necessary for effective planning at every instant.

Therefore, the key motivations include:
- **Reducing Computational Burden**: Mitigate the prohibitive quadratic computational cost incurred by transformer-based visual world models during planning, especially for the numerous simulated rollouts required by Model Predictive Control (MPC).
- **Enabling Real-time Deployment**: Accelerate world model inference to make it feasible for real-time robotic control and other resource-constrained applications.
- **Maintaining Control Fidelity**: Achieve efficiency gains without significantly sacrificing task performance or the high control fidelity enabled by detailed visual representations.
- **Leveraging Representation Redundancy**: Exploit the inherent redundancy in ViT patch tokens by strategically processing only a sparse subset.

By addressing these points, the authors aim to develop a general technique for efficient visual planning that can be applied across various tasks, from simple trajectory optimization to complex real-world robotic manipulation with Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, "Sparse Imagination," integrates a sparsely trained vision-based world model with a randomized grouped attention strategy to enable efficient planning through selective processing of visual tokens.

#### 3.1. World Model Architecture

The world model comprises two main components:
1. **Pre-trained Image Encoder**: A fixed, pre-trained image encoder, such as DINO-ViT-S/16, extracts visual latent patch tokens from raw image observations $o_t$. This encoder remains frozen during training to provide stable visual representations. For an image $o_t \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times 3}$, it produces visual features $z_t \in \mathbb{R}^{H_p \times W_p \times D}$, where $N = H_p \times W_p$ is the total number of visual tokens.
2. **Transformer-based World Model**: This module, denoted $f_\theta$, is structured as a causal transformer decoder. It predicts future latent tokens $z_{t+1}$ based on a temporal context of historical visual tokens $z_{t-h:t}$ and actions $a_{t-h:t}$. Actions and proprioceptive information (if available) are linearly projected and concatenated to the visual tokens along the feature dimension. The model learns dynamics within the pre-trained DINO feature space.

**World Model Training**: The world model is trained by minimizing the Mean Squared Error (MSE) prediction loss between the predicted and ground-truth future visual tokens: $L_{wm} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N ||\hat{z}_{t+1,i} - z_{t+1,i}||^2$. An optional, detached decoder can be trained for visualization purposes.

#### 3.2. Randomized Grouped Attention Strategy

To enable the world model to effectively process sparse subsets of tokens at inference time, a randomized grouped attention strategy is employed during training. This strategy partitions visual tokens from each frame into two random groups. Attention masks are applied within the transformer layers, restricting token interactions exclusively to those within the same spatial group while maintaining temporal consistency. This mechanism encourages the model to learn to operate robustly on arbitrary token subsets, promoting adaptability across varying levels of sparsity and improving its generalization capabilities.

#### 3.3. Sparse Imagination for Model Predictive Control (MPC)

The core innovation, Sparse Imagination, is applied during the MPC planning phase. MPC involves iteratively rolling out candidate action sequences in the learned world model from the current state to predict future states.
- **Planning Mechanism**: For goal-conditioned tasks, candidate sequences are evaluated using the MSE loss between the predicted future state features and the goal state features: $L_{mpc} = ||\hat{z}_{t+H} - z_g||^2$. The Cross-Entropy Method (CEM) or pre-trained policies are used to sample and optimize action sequences.
- **Token Dropout**: At each MPC iteration, a new random token dropout mask is dynamically generated. This mask is defined by a user-chosen dropout fraction $p \in [0, 1)$, which determines the proportion of tokens to discard. Consequently, only $(1-p)N$ randomly sampled tokens are processed during forward prediction in the latent rollout.
- **Efficiency and Robustness**: This approach significantly reduces the computational cost, as attention layers, the primary source of burden, scale quadratically with the number of tokens. The authors state that the CEM planner's inherent robustness allows it to recover from minor misdirections caused by dropping key features in a single planning step, as new dropout patterns are sampled at each iteration, ensuring unbiased coverage over time. The dropout ratio $p$ serves as a tunable parameter to balance computational speed and task accuracy.

#### 3.4. Planning Setups

The framework is evaluated across three distinct planning schemes to assess its general applicability:
1. **MPC-CEM**: Used for tasks like Pointmaze, Wall, PushT, and Block Pushing. This involves replanning at each step in a receding-horizon manner, using CEM to optimize action sequences.
2. **CEM (Open-loop)**: Applied to computationally intensive deformable object simulations (Granular, Rope). This optimizes a single, fixed action sequence without replanning to manage high processing costs.
3. **Policy-Guided Planning**: Employed for complex, long-horizon tasks (LIBERO, Meta-World, LeRobot). Here, candidate action sequences are sampled from a pre-trained policy, such as Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models (e.g., SmolVLA), and the planner evaluates these candidates using the sparse world model.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents a comprehensive evaluation of Sparse Imagination across various simulated and real-world environments, detailing its performance and efficiency benefits.

#### 4.1. Test-Time Trajectory Optimization

Experiments on environments like Pointmaze, Wall, PushT, Granular, Rope, and Block Pushing using MPC-CEM or CEM demonstrate that Sparse Imagination maintains task performance while substantially reducing planning time.
- **Performance vs. Efficiency**: With moderate token dropout ratios (10-50%), the method preserves task performance comparable to the Full-Patch baseline (which uses all tokens). For instance, in PushT, a 50% dropout ratio reduced planning time by 52.6% (from 173s to 82s per iteration) without sacrificing performance. Similar efficiency gains were observed across other environments (Table 2).
- **Comparison to Baselines**: The CLS-Token world model, while faster, showed a significant performance drop in spatially demanding tasks (e.g., Granular: 20.0% success for CLS vs. 85.0% for Sparse Imagination with 30% drop; Rope: 36.7% vs. 76.7% with 40% drop), indicating its inability to retain critical spatial information. Sparse Imagination consistently surpassed CLS-Token performance while offering substantial speedups over Full-Patch (Table 1).

#### 4.2. Towards Complex and Real-World Tasks: VLA-Guided Planning

The framework was integrated with Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models (e.g., SmolVLA) for complex, long-horizon tasks, including LIBERO-10, Meta-World, and real-world robotic manipulation tasks (PickPlace and Drawer on a LeRobot SO-101 arm).
- **Simulation Results**: On LIBERO-10, a 50% token drop boosted the base VLA's success rate from 29% to 33%, matching the computationally expensive Full-Patch planner but completing episodes nearly twice as fast (29.7s vs. 53.4s). In Meta-World, the method showed comparable performance to the Full-Patch planner with reduced computational overhead (Table 3).
- **Real-world LeRobot Results**: For real-world PickPlace and Drawer tasks, a 50% token drop significantly increased success rates from 60% (VLA-only) to 80% and 70% respectively, matching the Full-Patch planner. This also reduced planner latency from 19.1s to 10.4s (PickPlace) and 14.0s to 10.6s (Drawer) per episode. Leveraging asynchronous inference, this performance was achieved with minimal overhead compared to VLA-only baselines, demonstrating practical applicability for real-time robotics (Figure 5).

#### 4.3. Token Reduction Methods

A comparative analysis against various sophisticated token-selection strategies (learning-based pruning, attention-based pruning, cluster & merging) revealed a counter-intuitive outcome.
- **Random Sampling Effectiveness**: Despite their complexity, none of the advanced methods (LTRP, STAR, ATC, Attention-Encoder, Attention-WM) consistently or significantly outperformed simple random sampling (Table 4). This suggests that the overhead of these complex methods often outweighs their marginal gains in this context.

#### 4.4. Ablation and Analysis

- **Architectural Generalization**: Sparse Imagination demonstrated effective generalization across different ViT encoders, including MoCo-v3 ViT-S, MAE ViT-B, and DINOv3 ViT-S, matching or outperforming their Full-Patch baselines (Section 5.1).
- **Robustness to Token Sparsity (Grouped Attention)**: Training with randomized grouped attention was shown to be crucial, significantly reducing prediction errors on token subsets compared to a standard full-attention model (e.g., 0.016 vs. 0.036 normalized L2 error for 50% drop), leading to improved planning performance (Figure 4).
- **Information Sufficiency of Sparse Token Subsets**: Analysis using normalized Hilbert-Schmidt Independence Criterion (nHSIC) and an attentive probing module indicated that even a small, random fraction of tokens retains substantial ground-truth state information, often more than a global CLS token, suggesting that task-relevant information is broadly distributed across patches (Figures 7, 8).
- **Explaining Random Sampling Success: The "Blind Spot" Problem**:
 - **Phenomenon**: Sophisticated importance-based sampling methods often underperformed random sampling.
 - **Diagnosis**: The "blind spot" problem. Static importance metrics, by design, ignore regions deemed unimportant. In dynamic planning, if an object of interest moves into such a "blind spot," the planner cannot differentiate between candidate actions, leading to planning collapse (Figures 9, 20). This was starkly demonstrated in a vision-only Wall planning experiment, where Attention-Encoder's success rate collapsed to 21.7% while random sampling remained robust at 58.3%.
 - **Robustness of Random Sampling**: Its success is attributed to unbiased coverage. Quantitative analysis using "cluster retention entropy" showed random sampling achieves significantly more uniform coverage (2.37 bits) than Attention-Encoder (1.91 bits). Furthermore, planning with tokens deemed *least* important by Attention-Encoder still yielded a strong 63.7% success rate (vs. 79.2% with most important), supporting the idea that task-relevant information is highly redundant and distributed across tokens.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Sparse Imagination presents a significant contribution to the field of visual world model planning by addressing the critical challenge of computational efficiency. The work establishes that processing only a sparse, randomly sampled subset of visual tokens can dramatically reduce computational complexity during planning without substantial loss of task performance.

The **significance** of this work lies in several key areas:
- **Enabling Real-time Robotics**: By achieving substantial planning speedups (e.g., up to ~50% reduction in planning time for moderate dropout) while maintaining high control fidelity, Sparse Imagination makes the deployment of powerful, high-dimensional visual world models feasible for real-time robotic control and other resource-constrained applications. This directly addresses a major bottleneck in bridging advanced AI models with practical robotic systems.
- **General Technique**: The approach is demonstrated to be a general technique applicable across a wide spectrum of tasks, from simple test-time trajectory optimization (MPC-CEM) to complex, long-horizon real-world robotic tasks guided by state-of-the-art Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. Its compatibility with various pre-trained vision encoders (DINO, MoCo-v3, MAE) further highlights its versatility.
- **Challenging Conventional Wisdom**: The finding that simple random sampling performs competitively or even superiorly to more complex, importance-based token selection methods is counter-intuitive and impactful. The identified "blind spot" problem provides a compelling explanation, suggesting that for dynamic planning, unbiased coverage often trumps static importance scores.
- **Practical Baseline**: This research establishes random dropout as a simple, robust, and highly practical baseline for efficient visual world model planning. Its near-zero overhead makes it an appealing choice for real-world applications where complexity and computational resources are critical considerations. The efficiency gains from this approach can be reallocated to improve other aspects of planning, such as widening the action search space or extending the history of observations.

**Potential Impact**:
The method has the potential to accelerate the adoption and deployment of advanced visual world models in domains beyond robotics, including autonomous driving, virtual reality, and other interactive AI systems where real-time decision-making is paramount. By making visual planning more computationally accessible, it could foster further research into increasingly complex and fine-grained control tasks that were previously computationally prohibitive.

**Limitations**:
The paper acknowledges two primary limitations:
1. **Dependency on Visual Representations**: The efficacy of Sparse Imagination inherently relies on the quality of the underlying visual representations provided by the pre-trained encoder.
2. **Task-Dependent Optimal Sparsity**: While a moderate dropout ratio (e.g., 50%) is shown to be robust across benchmarks, the ideal sparsity level is likely task-dependent. The paper explores a preliminary uncertainty-aware adaptive mechanism (Appendix B.6) but notes that developing a more sophisticated adaptive method for adjusting the dropout ratio remains a promising avenue for future research.
