# Reasoning Language Models: A Blueprint

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at ETH Zurich's Scalable Parallel Computing Laboratory propose a comprehensive, modular blueprint for Reasoning Language Models (RLMs), accompanied by the open-source x1 framework, to demystify complex architectures and unify existing explicit and implicit RLM schemes. The work provides practical insights for RLM construction and outlines scalable deployment strategies, aiming to democratize access to advanced reasoning capabilities.

## Problem

- State-of-the-art Reasoning Language Models (RLMs) are prohibitively expensive and largely proprietary, limiting accessibility and transparency.
- The complex, opaque architectures of existing RLMs hinder academic scrutiny, replicability, and broader innovation.
- A growing "rich AI" and "poor AI" divide creates an accessibility barrier to advanced reasoning capabilities, stifling progress.

## Method

- A comprehensive, modular blueprint is introduced, structuring RLMs into ==reasoning schemes, operators, models, and pipelines==, detailed with mathematical and algorithmic specifications.
- The ==x1 open-source framework== is provided as a practical implementation, enabling rapid prototyping and experimentation with RLM architectures.
- The blueprint demonstrates versatility by unifying diverse RLM schemes and structured prompting techniques under a single coherent framework.

## Results

- The blueprint successfully unifies diverse existing RLM schemes, including MCTS-based models, implicit RLMs like QwQ, and structured prompting techniques such as CoT, ToT, and GoT.
- Practical insights for RLM construction include the superior signal of process-based evaluation, a two-phase training strategy, and x1-framework analysis showing token probability distributions (variance, entropy) can inform adaptive reasoning during inference.
- Robust benchmarking of RLMs requires significantly larger sample sizes (e.g., over 500 total, 200 per category) to account for increased output variability and ensure statistical reliability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Process-Based Evaluation, which assesses the entire reasoning structure step-by-step, yields superior training signals compared to Outcome-Based Evaluation.
> - A two-phase training approach (SFT followed by RL) is effective for building robust RLMs, combining foundational understanding with adaptive fine-tuning.
> - Training on familiar data distributions, particularly for specific token usage like eois, improves model performance and stability.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The advent of Reasoning Language Models (RLMs), also known as Large Reasoning Models (LRMs), represents a significant leap beyond traditional Large Language Models (LLMs). While LLMs like GPT-4 and LLaMA have revolutionized AI with their ability to understand and generate human language, their reasoning capabilities often align with "System 1 Thinking"—fast, intuitive, and pattern-matching. RLMs, exemplified by OpenAI's o1 and o3, DeepSeek-R1, and Alibaba's QwQ, extend LLMs with sophisticated reasoning mechanisms, enabling "System 2 Thinking"—deliberate, iterative problem-solving, and enhanced contextual understanding. This evolution moves AI closer to Artificial General Intelligence (AGI) by allowing models to extrapolate beyond their training data, rather than merely interpolating within it.

However, this breakthrough comes with substantial challenges. State-of-the-art RLMs are typically high-cost and proprietary, creating an accessibility barrier that the authors term the "rich AI" and "poor AI" divide. This disparity threatens to stifle innovation and exacerbate systemic inequities, as businesses and individuals without access to these advanced systems face a growing disadvantage. The technical foundations of these models, often combining reinforcement learning (RL), search heuristics (like Monte Carlo Tree Search, MCTS, or Beam Search), and various supervision schemes, remain opaque and complex, further limiting their widespread adoption and development.

This paper directly addresses these issues by proposing a comprehensive, modular blueprint for RLM construction. It aims to demystify their architectures and democratize advanced reasoning capabilities, fostering broader experimentation and innovation. The work is positioned at the convergence of three critical threads:
1. **Large Language Models (LLMs):** Serving as the knowledge repository, albeit primarily for System 1 thinking.
2. **Reinforcement Learning (RL):** Providing frameworks for decision-making, exploration, and learning optimal strategies through trial and error, as demonstrated by systems like AlphaZero.
3. **High-Performance Computing (HPC):** Offering the necessary computational power and scalability for training and deploying these large and complex models, overcoming the limitations of Moore's Law through parallelism and heterogeneous computing.

By unifying these pillars, the paper seeks to provide a clear, actionable guide for building RLMs, pushing the boundaries of AI's problem-solving capabilities while making them more accessible and equitable.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observed limitations and challenges associated with current state-of-the-art Reasoning Language Models (RLMs). The authors identify three critical issues:
1. **High Costs:** Developing and deploying advanced RLMs is prohibitively expensive, making them inaccessible to many researchers and organizations.
2. **Proprietary Nature:** Many leading RLMs are proprietary "black boxes" (e.g., OpenAI's o1 and o3, Alibaba's QwQ), lacking transparency in their training and architectural details. This opacity hinders academic scrutiny, replicability, and further innovation.
3. **Complex Architectures:** RLMs integrate diverse, intricate components, including reinforcement learning, search heuristics, and various LLM-based sub-models (policy, value, reward models) with novel loss functions and fine-tuning schemes. The intricate interplay of these components is poorly understood, posing significant challenges for design and implementation.

To address these challenges, the paper sets forth several key objectives:

- **Propose a Comprehensive Blueprint:** The core objective is to introduce a detailed, modular framework that organizes the fundamental building blocks of RLMs. This blueprint aims to crystalize and standardize the components, moving from high-level overviews to low-level implementation details.
- **Provide Multi-Perspective Specifications:** To maximize clarity and comprehensiveness, the blueprint is presented through architectural diagrams, detailed mathematical formulations, and in-depth algorithmic specifications, making it directly actionable for developers.
- **Demonstrate Versatility and Unification:** The authors aim to prove the blueprint's broad applicability by showing how existing prominent RLM schemes (e.g., LLaMA-Berry, QwQ, Journey Learning, Graph of Thoughts) fit within it as special cases, thus unifying disparate approaches under a single coherent framework.
- **Introduce a Practical Implementation Framework (x1):** To illustrate the blueprint's utility and facilitate rapid prototyping, a modular and user-friendly implementation called `x1` is provided. This framework is designed to simplify experimentation with new RLM architectures, covering training, inference, and synthetic data generation.
- **Derive Key Design Insights:** Through a literature review and analysis using `x1`, the paper aims to provide practical insights for building effective RLMs, such as the benefits of multi-phase training and the importance of familiar training distributions.
- **Discuss Scalable Deployment:** The work also seeks to outline how RLMs can be efficiently deployed in modern cloud and HPC environments, integrating with the broader LLM ecosystem.

Ultimately, the overarching motivation is to **democratize access to advanced RLMs**, lower the barriers to their design and experimentation, and mitigate the growing gap between "rich AI" and "poor AI," fostering innovation across research, industry, and education.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core methodology of this paper is the development and validation of a **comprehensive RLM blueprint**, complemented by a practical open-source implementation.

#### 3.1. The RLM Blueprint: A Modular Framework

The blueprint is designed as a toolbox of interconnected components, structured into four main categories:

- **Reasoning Scheme:** This defines *what* the RLM reasons about and *how* it structures its thought process.
 - **Reasoning Step:** The fundamental unit of thought, which can be fine-grained (single token) or coarse-grained (e.g., a sentence, a logical argument, or even an entire chain of thought). This choice impacts computational cost and interpretability.
 - **Reasoning Structure:** How reasoning steps are connected. Common forms include:
 - **Chains:** Linear sequences (e.g., Chain-of-Thought).
 - **Trees:** Hierarchical branching for exploring multiple possibilities (e.g., MCTS-based models, Tree of Thoughts).
 - **Graphs:** Arbitrary dependencies between steps (e.g., Graph of Thoughts).
 - **Nesting:** A novel concept where a node within one structure can contain another complete reasoning structure (e.g., a tree node containing a CoT chain).
 - **Reasoning Strategy:** How the reasoning structure evolves to solve a task. Examples include Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS), Beam Search, and various Ensemble Methods (e.g., Best-of-N, Tree Ensemble/Forest). The paper emphasizes distinguishing this from token-level decoding strategies.

- **Operators:** These are the actions that modify or interact with the reasoning structure to advance the problem-solving process.
 - **Structure Operators:** Change the reasoning structure itself.
 - `Generate`: Adds new reasoning steps (e.g., by a policy model in MCTS).
 - `Aggregate`: Combines multiple steps or paths (e.g., in Graph of Thoughts).
 - `Prune`: Removes suboptimal steps.
 - `Restructure`: Applies arbitrary transformations (e.g., linearizing a tree in Journey Learning).
 - **Traversal Operators:** Define how the process navigates the structure.
 - `Select`: Chooses which steps to explore next (e.g., based on heuristic scores like PUCT in MCTS).
 - `Backtrack`: Returns to a previous step to explore alternative paths (e.g., observed in QwQ's output).
 - **Update Operators:** Enhance parts of the structure without altering its topology.
 - `Update`: Propagates evaluation scores (e.g., backpropagation in MCTS).
 - `Refine`: Modifies the content of individual nodes for improvement (e.g., self-critique, summarization).
 - **Evaluate Operators:** Assess parts of the structure without modifying it, producing a value.
 - `Terminal Evaluation`: Checks the final solution against ground truth.
 - `Intermediate Evaluation`: Estimates quality of non-terminal steps (e.g., using a reward model or value estimator).
 - **Test-Time Compute (TTC):** The blueprint explicitly integrates TTC by allowing dynamic allocation of computational resources during inference via specific operators (e.g., `Generate` more steps, `Refine` multiple times, `Select` multiple paths, combined with `Aggregate`).

- **Models:** Neural networks that implement various operators, primarily `Policy Models` (for `Generate` and `Select`) and `Value Models` (for `Evaluate`).
 - **Reward Models (RM):** Predict immediate rewards for state transitions.
 - **Value Models (VM):** Predict expected cumulative future rewards. Differentiated into `V-Value Models` (state evaluation) and `Q-Value Models` (state-action pair evaluation).
 - **Outcome-Based Reward Models (ORM):** Evaluate only the final outcome (sparse signal).
 - **Process-Based Reward Models (PRM):** Evaluate step-by-step intermediate quality (dense signal).
 - **Outcome-Driven Process-Based Reward Models (O-PRM):** A newly formalized class combining outcome signals with process-based objectives, often relying on automated simulations to generate step-wise labels.
 - **Training Paradigms:** Methods for optimizing models, including Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), Rejection Sampling, and RL-based methods like Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) and Direct Preference Optimization (DPO).
 - **Training Data Scope:** Defines what information a single training sample contains (OBS for input/output, PBS for full reasoning traces, and a proposed Trace-Based Supervision (TBS) capturing operator application sequences).

- **Pipelines:** Detailed specifications orchestrating the interaction between reasoning schemes, operators, and models to achieve specific objectives.
 - `Inference Pipeline`: For generating responses to user requests.
 - `Training Pipeline`: For updating models.
 - `Data Generation Pipeline`: For creating synthetic training data.

#### 3.2. The x1 Framework: A Practical Implementation

To demonstrate the blueprint's utility, the authors introduce `x1`, a modular and user-friendly open-source framework.
- **Scheme:** `x1` implements a tree reasoning structure with MCTS as the primary strategy.
- **Operators:** It utilizes `Generate` (with Diverse Beam Search or high-temperature sampling for diversity), `Select` (using PUCT for exploration/exploitation balance), `Backpropagation` (for updating q-values), and `Evaluate` (both value estimation and terminality evaluation against ground truth).
- **Models & Training:** Both policy and value models in `x1` are fine-tuned LLMs (e.g., Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct).
 - **Policy Model Training:** Fine-tuned to generate individual reasoning steps, using a novel "end of intermediate step" (`eois`) token alongside the standard "end of sequence" (`eos`) token for clear step delimitation.
 - **Value Model Training:** A Q-Value Model is trained to estimate the sum of expected discounted future rewards for sequences of reasoning steps. It uses data from completed MCTS searches, initializing q-values with true rewards from an external verifier during training (sparse reward setting).
 - **Two-Phase Training:** `x1` adopts a two-phase training approach:
 - **Phase 1 (Initialization):** Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) on high-quality reasoning sequences to prepare policy and value models.
 - **Phase 2 (Reinforcement Learning):** Iterative refinement through MCTS rollouts, collecting data in a replay buffer, and then fine-tuning the policy (e.g., with PPO, DPO, or SFT on high-value paths) and continually updating the Q-value model to track backpropagated values.
- **Scalability & Efficiency:** `x1` is designed for multi-GPU/node scaling, featuring decoupled value and policy servers for independent scaling, batch processing, KV caching, and parallel child node expansion.
- **Blueprint for Efficient Scaling:** The paper provides a blueprint for deploying `x1` on AI HPC systems and clouds, detailing considerations for deployment (VMs, containers, Kubernetes, Ray), data storage (object storage, in-memory caches), communication (high-performance network fabrics), parallelism (data, model, pipeline, elastic scaling), and advanced techniques like elastic compute (spot instances, serverless functions) and parameter-efficient resource sharing (LoRA).

#### 3.3. Mathematical and Algorithmic Formalization

The blueprint is underpinned by a rigorous mathematical framework, integrating Markov Decision Processes (MDPs) with the MCTS algorithm (Appendix A). This formalization clarifies how reasoning steps, state transitions, actions, rewards, and policies are modeled. Detailed algorithmic specifications for the MCTS process (Algorithm 1), policy model SFT (Algorithm 2), Q-value model training (Algorithm 3), and the two-phase RL training (Algorithm 4-7) are provided in the appendices, ensuring clear implementation guidelines.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper's "findings" are primarily qualitative and structural, demonstrating the comprehensiveness and utility of its proposed blueprint rather than presenting new state-of-the-art quantitative performance results.

- **Blueprint's Versatility and Unifying Potential:** A key finding is the successful demonstration that a wide array of existing RLM schemes, both explicit and implicit, as well as structured prompting techniques, can be mapped onto and expressed by the proposed blueprint (Table 1). This includes:
 - **Explicit RLMs:** MCTS-based designs like TS-LLM, AlphaLLM, MCTS-DPO, LLaMA-Berry, and Marco-o1, which typically use explicit tree structures, policy, and value models. The blueprint effectively captures their use of `Generate`, `Evaluate`, and `Update` operators, and how some generalize with `Refine` or `Restructure` (e.g., Journey Learning's linearization).
 - **Implicit RLMs:** Models like QwQ, where reasoning structure is embedded within model weights. The blueprint shows how their implicit operations (generation, backtracking, summarization, critique) align with the defined operators.
 - **Structured Prompting Schemes:** Paradigms like Chain-of-Thought (CoT), Tree of Thoughts (ToT), and Graph of Thoughts (GoT) are also shown to fit the blueprint, illustrating how they leverage explicit reasoning structures and specific operators (e.g., GoT's `Aggregate` and `Restructure` guided by an external controller). This mapping validates the blueprint's ability to serve as a unifying taxonomy for reasoning-augmented LLMs.

- **Key Insights for Effective RLM Construction:** Drawing from literature and their `x1` framework development, the authors present several practical insights:
 - **Process-Based Evaluation is Superior:** Assessing the entire reasoning structure step-by-step (Process-Based Evaluation) provides a richer and more reliable signal for models compared to evaluating only the final outcome (Outcome-Based Evaluation).
 - **Two-Phase Training Strategy:** A distinct separation of Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning (RL) phases is effective. SFT builds a foundational understanding of reasoning patterns, while RL fine-tunes for complex, adaptive conditions, leading to more robust RLMs.
 - **Train on Familiar Distributions:** Models perform better and stabilize faster when trained on carefully curated, familiar data distributions, particularly for `eois` token usage and mathematical problem-solving.
 - **Caution with Prompting LLMs for Critique:** Relying solely on prompting LLMs to self-critique or evaluate their outputs often leads to instability and inconsistent improvements. Structured training approaches and dedicated operator design are more effective for self-improvement capabilities.

- **Analysis of Token Probability Distributions:** The `x1` framework enables detailed analysis of token-level uncertainty.
 - **Relevance:** Token probability distributions provide insights into the model's confidence and diversity of next-step candidates, informing decisions on when to expand reasoning or apply other operators.
 - **Metrics:** Variance, Entropy, VarEntropy, and Gini coefficient are identified as key metrics to characterize distribution shapes (flat vs. skewed), guiding adaptive reasoning strategies (e.g., forking paths when probabilities are flat vs. deterministic selection when one token dominates).
 - **Examples:** Illustrated cases show how these metrics highlight regions of high uncertainty or contention, suggesting where additional clarification or refinement steps might be beneficial.

- **Benchmarking Considerations:** Experience with `x1` highlighted challenges in benchmarking RLMs due to increased output variability.
 - **Sample Size Requirements:** To ensure statistically robust and fair comparisons, benchmarks need significantly large sample sizes (at least 500 total, with a minimum of 200 samples per category) to achieve low error variability (single-digit percentage range).
 - **Bias Mitigation:** Adequate sample sizes and the use of templated benchmarks (where available) are crucial to mitigate biases from selective reporting or data contamination, especially for complex RLM setups that exhibit greater variability than simpler LLMs.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work carries significant implications for the future of AI research and application, particularly in advanced reasoning capabilities.

- **Demystification and Democratization of RLMs:** By providing a comprehensive, modular blueprint and a practical open-source implementation (`x1`), the paper profoundly demystifies the complex architectures of state-of-the-art RLMs. This transparency lowers the barrier to entry for researchers and practitioners, enabling wider participation in RLM design, training, and deployment. This directly addresses the critical "rich AI" versus "poor AI" divide, making advanced reasoning capabilities more accessible and equitable across the AI ecosystem.

- **Fostering Innovation and Standardization:** The blueprint serves as a common language and standardized framework, facilitating easier comparison, analysis, and extension of existing RLM designs. This structured approach can accelerate innovation by providing a clear conceptual space for exploring novel combinations of reasoning structures, strategies, and operators. Researchers can build upon well-defined components, reducing redundancy and focusing on truly novel contributions.

- **Foundational for Future Research Directions:** The paper explicitly outlines several promising avenues for future work, directly enabled by its blueprint:
 - **Trace-Based Supervision (TBS):** Extending Process-Based Supervision by incorporating detailed traces of operator application sequences. This could train more powerful implicit RLMs, allowing them to internalize the explicit reasoning dynamics observed in structured search processes, leading to more flexible and efficient reasoning.
 - **New Model Classes:** The formal identification of `Outcome-Driven Process-Based Reward Models (O-PRMs)` bridges the gap between outcome-based and process-based evaluation, offering more granular feedback during training without extensive manual labeling. This could lead to more efficient and scalable training of reward and value models.
 - **Novel Operators and Architectures:** The extensive toolbox of operators can inspire new reasoning strategies, such as advanced tree-based searches, multi-step refinement processes, or hybrid search algorithms that adapt dynamically to task complexity. The concept of nested reasoning structures also opens doors for tackling multi-layered problems requiring deep, structured reasoning.
 - **Probabilistic Insights for Strategy Design:** The analysis of token probability distributions offers actionable insights for optimizing reasoning strategies (e.g., dynamically adjusting exploration based on model confidence), leading to more effective generation and decision-making.

- **Scalable and Efficient Deployment:** The blueprint's modular design inherently supports scalable cloud and HPC deployments. By detailing best practices for distributed computing (decoupling, batching, parallelism, elastic compute), the work provides a roadmap for efficiently leveraging massive computational resources. This is crucial for real-world applications, ensuring that RLMs can move beyond research labs into production environments cost-effectively.

In essence, "Reasoning Language Models: A Blueprint" is not merely a survey; it is a foundational work that provides both a theoretical framework and practical tools. Its significance lies in its potential to democratize, standardize, and accelerate the development of advanced reasoning AI, making these transformative capabilities more broadly available and fostering a new wave of innovation in intelligent systems.
