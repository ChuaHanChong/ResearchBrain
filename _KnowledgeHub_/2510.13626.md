# LIBERO-Plus: In-depth Robustness Analysis of Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Fudan University and collaborators developed LIBERO-Plus, a diagnostic benchmark that systematically evaluates the robustness of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models across seven perturbation dimensions. This framework reveals that VLA models exhibit brittle generalization despite achieving high performance on traditional benchmarks.

## Problem

- VLA models achieve high success rates on standard benchmarks in controlled simulations, but these metrics often conceal deficiencies in real-world applicability.
- Current evaluation methodologies focus on aggregate success rates under static, ideal conditions, failing to capture policy stability and reliability under realistic environmental and task variations.
- Models struggle to maintain performance with minor variations, exhibiting interpolation rather than true generalization to diverse, dynamic environments.

## Method

- Introduces **LIBERO-Plus**, a comprehensive and fine-grained ==robustness evaluation framework== for VLA models implemented in a simulated environment.
- Evaluates models across ==seven distinct perturbation dimensions== (e.g., objects layout, camera viewpoints, language instructions, sensor noise) and ==21 sub-dimensions==, stratified into five difficulty levels.
- Utilizes ==automated task generation== to create large, diverse training and test samples, enabling systematic ==vulnerability analysis== and the construction of a ==generalization-oriented training dataset==.

## Results

- VLA models demonstrate 'brittle generalization,' with performance drops from over **95%** to below **30%** under modest camera viewpoint or robot initial state perturbations.
- Models exhibit 'language ignorance,' often failing to follow modified instructions and relying on visual context, with success rates dropping to nearly **0%** in goal replacement tasks.
- Fine-tuning models on the diverse, generalized dataset constructed for LIBERO-Plus substantially enhances robustness, with the post-trained OpenVLA-OFT achieving **79.6%** overall success and **92.8%** camera view robustness, significantly outperforming baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current VLA models often rely on superficial cues like positional memorization or frequently ignore language instructions, rather than demonstrating deep multimodal understanding.
> - A 'negative compositionality gap' indicates that performance degradation from multiple simultaneous perturbations is often greater than the sum of individual effects, exposing critical entanglements in learned representations.
> - The inclusion of a first-person wrist camera significantly enhances robustness, particularly to illumination changes, by providing close-range geometric and contact cues that are invariant to lighting.
