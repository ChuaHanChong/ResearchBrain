# DynaWM: Dynamics-Aware Distillation with World Model and Momentum Targets for Smooth Locomotion over Continuous Stairs

> [!summary] Summary
> DynaWM enhances bipedal-wheeled robot locomotion over continuous stairs by integrating a dynamics-aware world model to regularize the teacher encoder and a momentum target encoder for stable knowledge distillation. This approach leads to improved terrain adaptability and motion smoothness, achieving high success rates on challenging stair configurations in both simulation and real-world environments.

## Problem

- Existing teacher-student distillation frameworks suffer from weakened dynamics-aware representations, as teacher encoders often disregard broader geometric information beyond immediate rewards.
- During concurrent training, student encoders frequently experience dimensional collapse due to non-stationary teacher updates, leading to instability and loss of essential terrain features.
- The learned latent space in current methods is often opaque, limiting interpretability and preventing clear verification of how critical terrain properties like height are encoded.

## Method

- A world model is introduced to regularize the teacher encoder by enforcing forward-dynamics awareness through a state prediction loss, compelling it to capture comprehensive geometric and dynamical terrain information.
- A ==momentum target encoder==, inspired by BYOL, provides stable distillation targets for the student encoder through exponential moving average updates, preventing dimensional collapse during knowledge transfer.
- An augmented reward function incorporates terms for joint range, wheelbase distance, base position bounds, and leg balance to promote more stable and energy-efficient locomotion on stairs.

## Results

- DynaWM's teacher encoder achieved a PC-Correlation of **0.96** and Linear Probing R^2 of **0.94** for terrain height, significantly outperforming baselines and ablated versions in representation quality.
- The framework demonstrated superior motion smoothness (lower EsmoothE_{smooth}Esmooth and EaccE_{acc}Eacc) compared to the CTS baseline across various stair configurations, indicating more fluent and less jerky movement.
- In real-world experiments, DynaWM achieved **100%** success rates on previously unseen irregular stairs (5/5 trials) and continuous stairs (13 steps of 20cm height, 30cm width), exhibiting robust adaptability to out-of-distribution terrains.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The world model successfully compels the teacher encoder to learn structured, dynamics-aware representations of terrain, as evidenced by clear stratification of terrain height in PCA visualizations.
> - Utilizing a momentum target encoder effectively stabilizes knowledge transfer, allowing the student encoder to robustly acquire and replicate the teacher's terrain-aware manifold without dimensional collapse.
> - The proposed framework provides a transparent latent space, enabling both qualitative and quantitative verification of how terrain geometry is hierarchically encoded through metrics like PC-Correlation and Linear Probing R^2.
