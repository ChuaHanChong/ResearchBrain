# Off-Policy Safe Reinforcement Learning with Constrained Optimistic Exploration

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Delft University of Technology developed Constrained Optimistic eXploration Q-learning (COX-Q), an off-policy safe reinforcement learning algorithm. It combines cost-constrained optimistic exploration with conservative distributional value learning to achieve high sample efficiency, maintain robust safety performance, and effectively regulate data collection costs across various benchmarks, including autonomous driving scenarios.

## Problem

- Off-policy safe reinforcement learning (RL) methods often produce policies that violate safety constraints due to underestimation bias in cumulative cost values.
- Traditional off-policy exploration strategies are cost-unaware, leading agents to explore unsafe regions and incur excessive costs during training data collection.
- Existing on-policy safe RL approaches, while effective, suffer from lower sample efficiency, making them less practical for real-world applications where data collection is costly.

## Method

- Constrained Optimistic eXploration (**COX**) introduces ==Policy-MGDA== to resolve gradient conflicts between reward and cost objectives in the action space, combined with an adaptive step length to control data collection costs.
- Distributional Value Learning with Truncated Quantile Critics (TQC) utilizes TQC to learn robust return and cost distributions, mitigating underestimation bias in costs and providing stable gradients.
- CVaR-based Uncertainty Quantification estimates pessimistic cost lower bounds and optimistic reward upper bounds using Conditional Value at Risk (CVaR) from quantile critics to guide the policy update and exploration.

## Results

- COX-Q achieved superior sample efficiency and consistently kept test costs significantly below the predefined budget in Safe Velocity benchmark tasks, outperforming both on-policy and off-policy baselines.
- The algorithm effectively regulated training data collection costs, maintaining them near or below the safety budget throughout training across various tasks, preventing excessive cost accumulation.
- In complex SMARTS autonomous driving environments, COX-Q exhibited superior safety performance, notably reducing collision and rule violation events during both deployment and training, without causing increased timeouts.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating explicit gradient conflict resolution (Policy-MGDA) with adaptive step length control during exploration can effectively regulate data collection costs and enhance safety in off-policy safe RL.
> - Conservative distributional value learning via Truncated Quantile Critics (TQC) is effective in mitigating cost underestimation bias, leading to more reliable and safer policy learning.
> - The impact of gradient conflict resolution is most pronounced in environments where reward and cost objectives frequently conflict during exploration; in sparse-cost environments, addressing cumulative cost underestimation bias remains a primary challenge.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses challenges within the field of safe reinforcement learning (RL), particularly in the context of off-policy methods. Safe RL is motivated by the need for autonomous agents to operate reliably and without causing harm in real-world applications, such as robotics and autonomous driving. The problem is commonly formulated as a Constrained Markov Decision Process (CMDP), where the agent seeks to maximize cumulative reward while adhering to a cumulative safety cost constraint.

Many existing safe RL methods are on-policy, relying on direct enforcement of constraints during policy updates through techniques like adjusted gradients or trust regions. While these methods, such as CPO, RCPO, and FOCOPS, have demonstrated empirical performance, they typically suffer from lower sample efficiency because they require new data collection for each policy update. This limitation is significant for real-world applications where data collection can be costly, time-consuming, or involve human interaction.

Off-policy RL methods, characterized by their use of experience replay and active exploration, offer higher sample efficiency. However, their application to safe RL introduces specific difficulties. These include: 1) underestimation bias in cumulative cost values, which can lead to the deployment of unsafe policies; and 2) a lack of inherent cost constraints in their exploration strategies, potentially guiding agents into risky regions and leading to uncontrolled data collection costs. This often results in off-policy methods struggling to satisfy safety constraints during both training and deployment, as noted in benchmarks like OmniSafe. This paper positions itself within this gap, aiming to develop an off-policy safe RL algorithm that maintains high data efficiency while ensuring robust constraint satisfaction through both controlled exploration and reliable value learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an off-policy safe reinforcement learning algorithm that overcomes the limitations of existing off-policy approaches, specifically concerning data efficiency and robust constraint satisfaction during both data collection and deployment. The authors identify two main challenges that motivate their work:

1. **Underestimation bias in cumulative cost:** Off-policy RL methods often produce policies that violate safety constraints due to an inherent tendency to underestimate cumulative costs. This bias can lead agents to perceive risky actions as safer than they are, resulting in unsafe policies when deployed. The goal is to develop a robust cost value learning mechanism that mitigates this underestimation.

2. **Cost-agnostic exploration:** Traditional off-policy exploration strategies are primarily designed to maximize reward and are typically cost-unaware. This can cause agents to explore unsafe regions of the environment, leading to unacceptable costs during data collection and potentially hindering the learning of truly safe policies. The motivation is to integrate a cost-constrained exploration strategy that actively considers safety limits during data collection, preventing the agent from incurring excessive costs during training.

To address these challenges, the authors propose Constrained Optimistic eXplploration Q-learning (COX-Q). Their objectives for COX-Q include:
- Integrating a novel cost-bounded optimistic exploration strategy that resolves gradient conflicts between reward and cost objectives in the action space.
- Developing an adaptive trust region mechanism to control the cost incurred during training data collection.
- Adopting truncated quantile critics to stabilize cost value learning and quantify epistemic uncertainty, which can then guide exploration.

Ultimately, the research aims to demonstrate that COX-Q can achieve high sample efficiency, competitive test safety performance, and controlled data collection costs, making it suitable for safety-critical applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Constrained Optimistic eXploration Q-learning (COX-Q), is an off-policy primal-dual safe RL algorithm that integrates a cost-constrained optimistic exploration strategy with conservative distributional value learning. The approach is built upon the Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) framework and uses a CMDP formulation.

**Problem Formulation:**
The agent operates within a CMDP defined by (S, A, r, c, p, p₀, γ, d), where `S` is the state space, `A` is the action space, `r` is the reward, `c` is the non-negative cost, `p` is the transition probability, `p₀` is the initial state distribution, `γ` is the discount factor, and `d` is the cost threshold. The objective is to maximize the expected cumulative reward `E[Q_r^π(s, a)]` subject to the constraint that the expected cumulative cost `E[Q_c^π(s, a)]` remains below `d`. A primal-dual approach is used, where the policy `π` and a Lagrangian multiplier `λ` are iteratively updated to solve `max E[Q_r^π - λ(Q_c^π - d)]` and `min_λ>0 λ × (d - E[Q_c^π])`, respectively.

**Cost-Constrained Optimistic Exploration (COX):**
COX extends the single-objective Optimistic Actor-Critic (OAC) to multi-objective safe RL. It determines an effective exploration direction and a safe exploration step length.

1. **Policy-MGDA for Exploration Gradient Conflict Resolution:**
 - The method computes optimistic upper bounds for reward (`Q_r^UB`) and pessimistic lower bounds for cost (`Q_c^LB`), as well as the mean cost (`Q_c^mean`), from an ensemble of critics.
 - Gradients are calculated in the action space: `g_r = ∇_a Q_r^UB`, `g_c = ∇_a Q_c^LB`, `g_m = ∇_a Q_c^mean`. The dual form encourages higher reward and lower cost.
 - In safe regions (`Q_c^π(s, a) ≤ d`), exploration prioritizes reward, so `g* = g_r`.
 - In unsafe regions, a naive sum `g_r - λg_c` can lead to conflicts where one objective (e.g., reward) dominates, potentially increasing cost. To resolve this, the Multiple Gradient Descent Algorithm (MGDA) is adapted to the action space (Policy-MGDA).
 - Policy-MGDA aims to find an exploration direction `g*` that improves both reward (`⟨g_r, g*⟩_ΣT ≥ 0`) and reduces cost (`⟨-g_c, g*⟩_ΣT ≥ 0`). It defines a gradient space `K` (a hyper-cone) where both conditions hold and then finds `g*` as the projection of `g_raw = g_r - λg_c` onto `K`. This ensures that exploration simultaneously attempts to increase reward and decrease cost, even when the gradients are conflicting.

2. **Adaptive Step Length for Exploration Cost Control:**
 - Given the exploration gradient `g*`, an adaptive step length `η*` is determined to control the data collection cost.
 - The method explicitly bounds the cost expectation by adjusting `η`. A hinge function `ϕ(η) = [η⟨g_m, g*⟩_ΣT - (d - Q_c^mean)]+` is used to quantify the non-negative violation of the mean cost.
 - A bi-level optimization problem is formulated to maximize `η*` such that `ϕ(η*)` is minimized or zero within the original trust region (`η`). This ensures that if a full exploration step would exceed the cost limit, `η*` is adjusted to remain within the safety budget.
 - To prevent oscillatory behavior when `g*` is near zero, the maximum step length `η` (derived from a KL-divergence threshold `δ`) is adaptively adjusted based on the near-on-policy average cost in a recent replay buffer. This mechanism fine-tunes the aggressiveness of exploration based on the observed training cost.

**Distributional Value Learning and Uncertainty Quantification:**
To address sparsity of reward/cost and reduce variance, COX-Q adopts Truncated Quantile Critics (TQC).
- Each critic learns the return/cost distribution using evenly distributed quantiles.
- TQC mixes and sorts quantiles from all critics, then truncates the top `k_r` atoms for reward (to mitigate overestimation) and the bottom `k_c` atoms for cost (to mitigate underestimation and enforce conservatism).
- This approach provides low-variance gradients for stable learning and flexible bias control.
- Epistemic uncertainty is quantified using Conditional Value at Risk (CVaR). For cost, a lower bound (`Q_c^LB`) is estimated by averaging the `α` highest quantiles (most pessimistic estimates) across multiple critics, adjusted by a factor `β_c` of the standard deviation. For reward, an upper bound (`Q_r^UB`) is computed by averaging all quantiles, adjusted by `β_r` of the standard deviation. These bounds and their gradients are then used in the COX exploration strategy and the actor update.

The full COX-Q algorithm is implemented based on SAC and incorporates the Augmented Lagrangian Method (ALM) for enhanced constraint violation penalty, similar to CAL and ORAC. An “on-policy” conversion formula is also used to convert the episode cost limit to a Q-value limit for off-policy methods.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of COX-Q was evaluated across three distinct safe RL benchmarks: Safe Velocity, Safe Navigation, and SMARTS safe autonomous driving. Comparisons were made against various off-policy and on-policy baselines.

**Safe Velocity Benchmark:**
- **Sample Efficiency:** COX-Q demonstrated superior sample efficiency compared to both on-policy (CUP, RCPO, PPOSimmerPID, CPPOPID) and off-policy (SACLag-UCB, CAL, WCSAC, ORAC) baselines. It achieved high cumulative returns and nearly zero test costs faster.
- **Safety Performance:** It consistently maintained test costs significantly below the predefined budget, a characteristic often challenging for off-policy methods.
- **Controlled Data Collection Cost:** COX-Q effectively regulated data collection costs, keeping them near or below the budget throughout training, particularly evident in the smooth and constrained training cost profiles across tasks like hopper, walker2d, and ant. In contrast, other methods incurred higher training costs due to unregulated exploration.

**Safe Navigation Benchmark:**
- **Performance with Sparse Costs:** For these tasks, characterized by sparse goal-reaching rewards and costs, COX-Q adapted by not applying truncation but instead using CVaR-based upper bounds of cost for actor updates, similar to Worst-Case SAC.
- **Return and Safety:** COX-Q achieved returns that were on-par with or higher than baselines, especially in more challenging tasks such as CarButton1, CarButton2, and PointPush1. Both training and test costs converged below the set limit.
- **Cost Estimation Bias:** The estimation bias of COX-Q consistently converged to zero during training, indicating improved cost value learning through mixed quantiles, whereas baselines exhibited either over-conservative or unstable bias.

**Ablation Studies (Safe Velocity and Safe Navigation):**
- **TQC Contribution:** Ablation studies showed that the Truncated Quantile Critics (TQC) primarily contributed to improved returns across all tasks.
- **Exploration Strategy Impact:** In Safe Velocity, the COX-Q exploration strategy (Policy-MGDA + Adaptive Step Length) significantly influenced training costs, effectively controlling them below the budget compared to TQC-only or TQC + ORAC-style exploration.
- **Gradient Conflict in Sparse Environments:** In Safe Navigation, the differences between COX-Q and ORAC-style exploration were minimal. Analysis revealed that gradient conflicts between reward and cost objectives occurred infrequently (below 10%, often below 2% in early training stages) in these sparse-cost environments. This suggested that for such tasks, the underestimation bias in cumulative cost (due to sparse signals) was a more significant bottleneck than the exploration mechanism itself.

**SMARTS Safe Autonomous Driving Benchmark:**
- **Safety in Complex Environments:** Evaluated against CPPOPID, SACLag, CAL, and TQC-ORAC in complex interactive scenarios (Overtaking, Intersection, T-junction) with a near-zero cost limit (0.01).
- **Superior Safety Performance:** COX-Q achieved the best safety performance in tests, significantly reducing collision, off-road, and rule violation events without increasing timeouts (overly conservative driving).
- **Controlled Training Unsafe Events:** Compared to TQC-ORAC, COX-Q substantially reduced the number of unsafe events during data collection in all three scenarios. This indicates that resolving conflicting gradients to simultaneously reduce cost and improve reward contributes to a more effective and safer training process. The safety performance in overtaking was generally lower across all methods, attributed to the instantaneous lane change model used in SMARTS.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The proposed COX-Q algorithm represents a contribution to the field of safe reinforcement learning, particularly for off-policy methods. Its significance and potential impact stem from its ability to address critical challenges in deploying RL in safety-critical applications.

1. **Enhanced Data Efficiency and Safety:** By combining cost-constrained optimistic exploration with conservative distributional value learning, COX-Q achieves high sample efficiency while maintaining robust safety performance during both data collection and deployment. This is crucial for real-world scenarios where data collection is expensive or risky, making off-policy methods more practical.
2. **Controlled Exploration Costs:** The novel cost-constrained exploration strategy, including Policy-MGDA for gradient conflict resolution and adaptive step length adjustment, ensures that the agent's exploration during training adheres to predefined safety budgets. This prevents excessive cost accumulation in the real environment, a significant barrier for current off-policy safe RL methods.
3. **Reliable Value Learning:** The use of Truncated Quantile Critics (TQC) and CVaR-based uncertainty quantification provides more stable and accurate estimates of cost and reward distributions. This mitigates the common problem of underestimation bias in cost values, which can otherwise lead to unsafe policies. The method's ability to converge cost estimation bias to zero is a notable aspect for ensuring trustworthy policy learning.
4. **Applicability to Complex Systems:** Demonstrated success in complex environments like autonomous driving (SMARTS) with large neural networks, indicates the scalability and robustness of COX-Q. This expands its potential for real-world applications beyond simplified robotics tasks.

The work highlights that while COX-Q offers substantial benefits when exploration gradient conflicts are significant, future work could focus on improving its performance in sparse-cost tasks. Addressing the reliability of quantified epistemic uncertainty and leveraging techniques like Hindsight Experience Replay (HER) or prioritized experience replay for sparse cost signals are identified as potential avenues for further impact. Overall, COX-Q offers a promising framework for developing safe and efficient RL agents for a range of safety-critical decision-making problems.
