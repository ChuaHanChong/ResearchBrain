# Off-Policy Safe Reinforcement Learning with Constrained Optimistic Exploration

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Delft University of Technology developed Constrained Optimistic eXploration Q-learning (COX-Q), an off-policy safe reinforcement learning algorithm. It combines cost-constrained optimistic exploration with conservative distributional value learning to achieve high sample efficiency, maintain robust safety performance, and effectively regulate data collection costs across various benchmarks, including autonomous driving scenarios.

## Problem

- Off-policy safe reinforcement learning (RL) methods often produce policies that violate safety constraints due to underestimation bias in cumulative cost values.
- Traditional off-policy exploration strategies are cost-unaware, leading agents to explore unsafe regions and incur excessive costs during training data collection.
- Existing on-policy safe RL approaches, while effective, suffer from lower sample efficiency, making them less practical for real-world applications where data collection is costly.

## Method

- Constrained Optimistic eXploration (**COX**) introduces ==Policy-MGDA== to resolve gradient conflicts between reward and cost objectives in the action space, combined with an adaptive step length to control data collection costs.
- Distributional Value Learning with Truncated Quantile Critics (TQC) utilizes TQC to learn robust return and cost distributions, mitigating underestimation bias in costs and providing stable gradients.
- CVaR-based Uncertainty Quantification estimates pessimistic cost lower bounds and optimistic reward upper bounds using Conditional Value at Risk (CVaR) from quantile critics to guide the policy update and exploration.

## Results

- COX-Q achieved superior sample efficiency and consistently kept test costs significantly below the predefined budget in Safe Velocity benchmark tasks, outperforming both on-policy and off-policy baselines.
- The algorithm effectively regulated training data collection costs, maintaining them near or below the safety budget throughout training across various tasks, preventing excessive cost accumulation.
- In complex SMARTS autonomous driving environments, COX-Q exhibited superior safety performance, notably reducing collision and rule violation events during both deployment and training, without causing increased timeouts.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating explicit gradient conflict resolution (Policy-MGDA) with adaptive step length control during exploration can effectively regulate data collection costs and enhance safety in off-policy safe RL.
> - Conservative distributional value learning via Truncated Quantile Critics (TQC) is effective in mitigating cost underestimation bias, leading to more reliable and safer policy learning.
> - The impact of gradient conflict resolution is most pronounced in environments where reward and cost objectives frequently conflict during exploration; in sparse-cost environments, addressing cumulative cost underestimation bias remains a primary challenge.
