# Agentic Reasoning and Tool Integration for LLMs via Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> The ARTIST framework from Microsoft Research unifies agentic reasoning, dynamic tool integration, and reinforcement learning within large language models, allowing them to autonomously learn when and how to use external tools. This approach achieves superior performance on complex mathematical reasoning and multi-turn function calling tasks, outperforming state-of-the-art baselines and even larger frontier models while exhibiting emergent self-correction capabilities.

## Problem

- Large language models (LLMs) primarily rely on static pre-trained knowledge and text-only reasoning, leading to inaccuracies or limitations in handling dynamic, time-sensitive, or knowledge-intensive tasks.
- Existing tool integration methods often depend on labor-intensive hand-crafted prompts or extensive supervised fine-tuning, limiting their scalability, generalization, and robustness in complex, multi-turn scenarios.
- Current reinforcement learning (RL) approaches for LLMs largely focus on refining internal reasoning or output style, without explicitly integrating dynamic external tool use as a first-class operation within the RL loop.

## Method

- ==ARTIST (Agentic Reasoning and Tool Integration in Self-Improving Transformers)== is a unified framework that interleaves the LLM's text-based thinking, structured tool queries, and processing of tool outputs within a dynamic reasoning trajectory.
- It employs ==Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)== adapted with a ==loss masking strategy== to focus optimization on the agent's decision-making and reasoning for tool invocation.
- A ==composite reward system== guides the learning process, providing feedback based on final answer correctness, adherence to prompt format, and the fraction of successful tool calls, eliminating the need for step-level supervision.

## Results

- ARTIST achieves the highest Pass@1 accuracy across challenging math benchmarks, with Qwen2.5-14B-ARTIST showing up to **+22.0%** absolute improvement over base models on AMC and outperforming GPT-4o on all math benchmarks (e.g., **+8.9%** on Olympiad, **+7.6%** on AIME).
- On multi-turn function calling benchmarks (τ-bench, BFCL v3), ARTIST substantially improves performance, more than **doubling** the base model's accuracy on τ-bench Airline (**0.260** vs. **0.120**).
- ARTIST exhibits emergent agentic behaviors such as self-refinement, self-correction (diagnosing and recovering from tool errors), and self-reflection, leading to more interpretable and robust problem-solving strategies without explicit supervision.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Unifying agentic reasoning, dynamic tool integration, and outcome-based reinforcement learning allows large language models to autonomously learn when, how, and which tools to use.
> - Applying a loss masking strategy during RL training, focusing gradients only on agent-generated tokens (thinking and tool calls), is crucial for learning effective tool invocation strategies, rather than just imitating outputs.
> - A composite reward mechanism, incorporating answer correctness, adherence to structured prompt templates, and successful tool execution, enables the model to develop robust, context-aware, and self-correcting behaviors.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of large language models has seen remarkable progress, enabling capabilities from complex text generation to intricate reasoning. However, a significant limitation persists: LLMs primarily rely on their pre-trained static knowledge and text-only reasoning. This restricts their ability to handle dynamic, time-sensitive, or knowledge-intensive tasks, often leading to inaccuracies or "hallucinations." The broader research landscape has thus evolved along several interconnected pathways to address these challenges:

- **Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Reasoning and Prompting Approaches:** Techniques like CoT prompting ([Wei et al., 2023], [Kojima et al., 2023]) revolutionized LLM reasoning by eliciting explicit intermediate steps, thereby improving accuracy and interpretability. Variants like "Plan-and-Solve Prompting" ([Wang et al., 2023]) further refine this. While effective for leveraging internal knowledge, CoT alone falls short when external information or precise computation is needed ([Turpin et al., 2023]).
- **Tool-Based Reasoning and Integration:** To overcome the limitations of static knowledge, researchers began integrating external tools. Approaches like PAL ([Gao et al., 2023]) allow LLMs to generate and execute code, while frameworks such as ART ([Paranjape et al., 2023]) and ToRA ([Gou et al., 2024]) train models to invoke tools. Toolformer ([Schick et al., 2023]) demonstrated self-supervised tool use. While promising, many of these methods rely on labor-intensive hand-crafted prompts or require extensive supervised fine-tuning (SFT) with curated data, limiting their scalability and generalization, especially in dynamic, multi-turn scenarios. Tool use is often decoupled from the core reasoning process during training, leading to brittle behaviors.
- **Reinforcement Learning (RL) for Reasoning and Alignment:** RL, particularly Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) ([Ouyang et al., 2022]), has proven effective for aligning LLM outputs with human preferences and enhancing reasoning abilities ([Shao et al., 2024]). Algorithms like Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) ([Schulman et al., 2017]) and Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) ([Rafailov et al., 2024]) have been instrumental. More recently, Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) ([Shao et al., 2024]) has emerged as a particularly efficient RL algorithm for LLMs, especially in mathematical reasoning ([DeepSeek-AI et al., 2025]). These RL methods primarily focus on refining the model's internal reasoning or output style, without explicitly integrating dynamic external tool use as a first-class citizen in the RL loop.

**ARTIST's Unique Position:** ARTIST addresses a critical gap at the intersection of these three research areas. Most prior work either focuses on tool use in isolation (often decoupled from the reasoning process) or on RL for internal reasoning without direct emphasis on external action. ARTIST unifies agentic reasoning, dynamic tool integration, and reinforcement learning within a single framework. By enabling LLMs to learn *when*, *how*, and *which* tools to invoke within complex, multi-turn reasoning chains, it moves beyond static prompting or isolated tool calls. It empowers LLMs to interact with environments and leverage external resources as an integral part of their decision-making process, learning robust strategies from outcome-based rewards without requiring step-level supervision. This positions ARTIST as a significant step towards creating truly autonomous, adaptable, and generalizable LLM agents capable of complex, real-world problem-solving.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objectives of the ARTIST framework are driven by the fundamental limitations of current LLMs, even those enhanced with basic reasoning or tool-calling capabilities:

- **Overcome Static Knowledge and Text-Only Reasoning:** Traditional LLMs are limited by their reliance on static internal knowledge, which can be outdated, incomplete, or insufficient for precise computation or domain-specific queries. This often leads to factual inaccuracies or "hallucinations."
- **Address Challenges in Complex, Domain-Specific, or Open-Ended Problems:** Purely text-based reasoning struggles with tasks requiring dynamic, multi-step reasoning, adaptive decision-making, or interaction with external tools and environments (e.g., complex mathematics, programmatic data analysis, web navigation).
- **Improve Scalability and Robustness of Tool Integration:** Existing tool-integration strategies often depend on labor-intensive hand-crafted prompts or fixed heuristics, which are brittle and do not generalize well to complex or unseen scenarios. Supervised fine-tuning for tool use is limited by the availability of curated data and struggles to adapt to novel tasks or recover from tool failures.
- **Enable Autonomous and Adaptive Tool Use:** A key motivation is to enable LLMs to autonomously decide *when*, *how*, and *which* external tools (e.g., code interpreters, web search, APIs) or environments (e.g., web browsers, operating systems) to invoke. This dynamic coordination of reasoning, tool use, and environment interaction is crucial for real-world problem-solving.
- **Leverage Outcome-Based Reinforcement Learning:** The authors aim to achieve this autonomous tool use by employing reinforcement learning guided *only* by outcome-based reward signals, eliminating the need for burdensome step-level supervision or intermediate tool-call supervision. This approach is intended to foster robust, generalizable, and self-correcting tool-use behaviors.

In essence, ARTIST seeks to push LLMs beyond being mere "language models" into becoming truly "agentic" systems capable of intelligent, adaptive interaction with the outside world to solve complex problems reliably.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ARTIST (Agentic Reasoning and Tool Integration in Self-Improving Transformers) is a unified framework designed to empower LLMs with agentic reasoning, dynamic tool integration, and reinforcement learning.

#### 3.1. Core Architecture and Interleaved Reasoning

The central idea of ARTIST is to treat tool usage and environment interaction as first-class operations deeply integrated into the model's reasoning trajectory. As illustrated in Figure 1, the policy model (LLM) dynamically decides when, how, and which tools to invoke. The reasoning process alternates between:
- **Text-based thinking (`<think>...</think>`):** The model articulates its internal thought process.
- **Tool queries (`<tool_name>...</tool_name>`):** The model generates structured calls to external tools (e.g., `<python>...</python>`, `<search>...</search>`, `<browser>...</browser>`).
- **Tool outputs (`<output>...</output>` or `<tool_result>...</tool_result>`):** The results from tool execution or environment interaction are returned to the model and incorporated back into the reasoning chain.
- **Final Answer (`<answer>...</answer>`):** The ultimate solution to the task.
This iterative process enables the model to explore diverse reasoning and tool-use trajectories, allowing for self-correction and iterative refinement based on external feedback.

#### 3.2. Reinforcement Learning Algorithm (GRPO)

ARTIST employs **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)** ([Shao et al., 2024]), an RL algorithm known for its sample efficiency and stability, particularly with outcome-based rewards. GRPO is a variant of PPO that eliminates the need for a separate value function (critic) by estimating the baseline from a group of sampled responses.

- **GRPO Adaptation:** In ARTIST, rollouts capture agentic interactions. To ensure the model learns effective *invocation strategies* rather than merely imitating deterministic tool outputs, a crucial **loss masking strategy** is applied. Tokens from tool outputs are masked during loss computation, ensuring gradients are propagated only through model-generated tokens (thinking steps and tool-call formulations). This focuses optimization on the agent's decision-making and reasoning.
- **Training Procedure (Algorithm 1):**
 1. For each training iteration, a batch of tasks is processed.
 2. For each task, a group of `G` rollouts is sampled from the current (or old) policy.
 3. Each rollout involves the interleaved generation of thinking, tool queries, and processing of tool outputs until an answer is produced.
 4. An outcome-based reward `R(y_i)` is computed for each completed rollout.
 5. Groupwise advantages are calculated based on these rewards.
 6. Loss masking is applied.
 7. The GRPO loss is computed, and the policy model's parameters are updated.

#### 3.3. Reward Design

A composite reward mechanism guides the learning process, providing feedback beyond just final answer correctness:

- **Answer Reward:** A binary reward (e.g., 2 for correct, 0 for incorrect) for generating the correct final answer within the designated tags. This incentivizes task completion.
- **Format Reward:** Encourages adherence to the structured prompt template. It has two components:
 - **Relaxed:** Partial credit for the presence of required tag pairs.
 - **Strict:** Additional reward for correct ordering and structure of tags (`<think> -> <tool_name> -> <output> -> <answer>`). This promotes interpretable reasoning.
- **Tool Execution Reward:** Defined as the fraction of successful tool calls (`Tool_success / Tool_total`). This incentivizes the model to formulate syntactically correct and executable tool queries, enhancing robustness.

#### 3.4. Prompt Templates

ARTIST uses structured prompt templates that guide the model in organizing its outputs into explicit segments for thinking, tool querying, and tool output. These templates are customized for different domains (e.g., a `<python>` tag for math reasoning, `<tool>` for function calling, with corresponding output tags).

This comprehensive methodology allows ARTIST to learn robust, adaptive, and context-aware strategies for complex, multi-step tasks by tightly coupling internal reasoning with dynamic external interactions, all driven by an efficient reinforcement learning process.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

ARTIST consistently outperforms state-of-the-art baselines across challenging benchmarks in complex mathematical reasoning and multi-turn function calling, demonstrating significant improvements in accuracy and the emergence of advanced agentic behaviors.

#### 4.1. Complex Mathematical Reasoning (Pass@1 Accuracy)

- **Overall Performance:** ARTIST, particularly with the Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct model, achieves the highest Pass@1 accuracy across all four math benchmarks (MATH-500, AIME, AMC, Olympiad Bench).
- **Impact of Complexity:** The most substantial improvements are observed on highly complex tasks like AMC, AIME, and Olympiad.
 - On AMC, Qwen2.5-14B-ARTIST achieves 0.55, an absolute improvement of +22.0% over the base Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct (0.33) and +14.0% over the prompt-based tool baseline (0.410).
 - Similar significant gains are seen on AIME (+6.2% for 14B) and Olympiad (+18.0% for 14B), highlighting that dynamic tool integration and agentic reasoning are critical for difficult problems.
- **Comparison to Baselines:**
 - **Base LLMs + Prompted Tools:** While prompt-based tool integration offers some improvement over base LLMs, ARTIST consistently outperforms it (e.g., +14.0% on AMC for 14B). This indicates that explicit RL-based agentic training is crucial for effective tool invocation, beyond just providing access.
 - **Open-Source Tool-Augmented LLMs (e.g., ToRA, NuminaMath-TIR):** ARTIST achieves substantial improvements (up to +35.9% over PAL on Olympiad), demonstrating that its RL-driven agentic approach leads to more effective and adaptive tool use than prior tool-finetuned methods.
 - **Frontier LLMs (e.g., GPT-4o):** Remarkably, ARTIST (even at 7B scale) outperforms GPT-4o on all math benchmarks (e.g., +8.9% on Olympiad, +7.6% on AIME). This suggests that agentic RL with tool integration can bridge and even surpass the performance gap with much larger proprietary models.

##### 4.1.1. Metrics Analysis for Math Reasoning (ARTIST vs. Base-Prompt+Tools)

- **Reward Score (Solution Quality):** ARTIST dramatically improves solution quality. On AMC, average reward score nearly triples (0.9 to 2.8); on AIME, it quadruples (0.4 to 1.7). This indicates ARTIST generates more correct, structured, and complete solutions.
- **Number of Tool Calls (External Tool Utilization):** ARTIST learns to use external tools far more frequently and strategically on harder datasets (e.g., 0.3 to >3 tool calls on AIME). For easier problems (MATH-500), it *reduces* unnecessary tool usage, demonstrating adaptive, context-sensitive tool invocation.
- **Response Length (Reasoning Depth):** ARTIST generates substantially longer and more detailed responses, especially on AIME (up to twice the length), reflecting deeper, multi-step reasoning.

#### 4.2. Multi-Turn Function Calling (Pass@1 Accuracy)

- **Overall Performance:** ARTIST consistently delivers substantial improvements over base and prompt-based LLMs on τ-bench (Airline, Retail) and BFCL v3 (Missing Function, Missing Parameters, Long Context subsets).
- **Significant Gains:** On τ-bench, ARTIST more than doubles the base model's performance (e.g., 0.260 vs. 0.120 on Airline). On BFCL v3 Long Context, ARTIST achieves +9.0% improvement over the base model.
- **Comparison to Baselines:**
 - **Base LLMs + Reasoning via Prompt:** ARTIST shows clear gains, particularly on the hardest tasks like τ-bench Airline (+14.0%) and BFCL v3 Long Context (+7.5%), reinforcing that explicit RL training is crucial for robust multi-turn tool use.
 - **Frontier and Open-Source LLMs:** While GPT-4o generally leads, ARTIST narrows the gap and outperforms some much larger open-source models (e.g., surpasses Llama-3-70B on BFCL v3 Long Context and more than doubles its performance on τ-bench).

##### 4.2.1. Metrics Analysis for Multi-Turn Function Calling (ARTIST vs. Base-Prompt+Tools on τ-bench)

- **Reward Score (BFCL v3 Training):** During training, ARTIST's reward score significantly improves, demonstrating robust generalization and effective learning of tool-use strategies.
- **Reasoning Length per Tool Call:** ARTIST generates 33% longer reasoning (253 vs. 190.5 tokens), indicating deeper, more context-aware planning before each tool invocation.
- **Total Correct Tool Calls:** ARTIST makes 30% more correct tool calls (676 vs. 517), directly translating to higher task completion accuracy.
- **Total Steps to Completion:** Despite more frequent tool use, ARTIST completes tasks 15% faster (1284 vs. 1520 steps), due to its efficiency in making the right calls at the right time.

#### 4.3. Emergent Agentic Behaviors

Across both domains, ARTIST exhibits compelling emergent capabilities, arising naturally from the unified agentic RL framework without explicit supervision:
- **Self-Refinement:** The model incrementally adjusts its strategy or approach based on intermediate results or evolving context (e.g., increasing candidate values in math, reordering actions in function calling).
- **Self-Correction:** When encountering errors (e.g., tool execution failures, incorrect intermediate results, missing parameters), the model diagnoses the issue, executes corrective actions, and retries the intended operation.
- **Self-Reflection:** At each step, the model evaluates its reasoning, summarizes the current state, validates results, and explains its logic, ensuring coherent and interpretable workflows.

These findings collectively establish agentic RL with tool integration as a powerful new frontier, demonstrating that ARTIST not only achieves superior performance but also fosters more interpretable and robust problem-solving in LLMs.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The ARTIST framework represents a significant advancement in the capabilities of large language models, pushing them beyond static, text-based reasoning towards genuinely autonomous, adaptive, and environment-interacting agents. Its significance and potential impact are profound:

- **Paradigm Shift in LLM Capabilities:** ARTIST marks a shift from LLMs as passive knowledge repositories or reactive text generators to active problem-solvers. By tightly coupling agentic reasoning, dynamic tool integration, and reinforcement learning, it enables models to "think," "act," and "learn" in an integrated loop. This moves beyond brittle, prompt-engineered tool use to a system that intelligently learns *when* and *how* to leverage external resources.
- **Enhanced Problem-Solving in Complex Domains:** The demonstrated superior performance on challenging mathematical reasoning and multi-turn function calling benchmarks highlights ARTIST's ability to tackle problems previously intractable for LLMs. This has direct implications for scientific discovery, engineering design, financial analysis, and other domains requiring precise computation and logical deduction.
- **Robustness and Generalizability:** By learning from outcome-based rewards and dynamically adapting to environment feedback (including tool errors), ARTIST develops more robust and generalizable strategies. This resilience to unexpected situations and its ability to learn from failures are critical for real-world deployment, where perfect data and predictable environments are rare.
- **Increased Interpretability and Trust:** The explicit `think` tags and structured rollouts make ARTIST's reasoning process more interpretable. Users can follow the model's logic, understand *why* it invoked certain tools, and see how it self-corrected. This transparency is crucial for building trust in AI systems, especially in high-stakes applications.
- **Foundation for Next-Generation AI Agents:** ARTIST lays a strong foundation for building highly capable and autonomous AI agents. Its ability to manage complex multi-step workflows, maintain state, recover from errors, and interact with diverse external environments positions it as a crucial step towards general-purpose AI. Such agents could revolutionize various industries, from automated customer support and personal assistants to complex enterprise automation and scientific research.
- **Reduced Supervision Requirements:** The reliance on outcome-based RL, rather than labor-intensive step-level supervision for tool calls, offers a scalable and data-efficient training paradigm. This reduces the burden of manual annotation and allows for continuous improvement in dynamic environments.

In conclusion, ARTIST's success in unifying these critical components—agentic reasoning, tool integration, and reinforcement learning—not only surpasses prior performance benchmarks but also charts a promising course for the future of LLMs, paving the way for more intelligent, adaptable, and trustworthy AI systems that can seamlessly interact with and shape our complex world.
