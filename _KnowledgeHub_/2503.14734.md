# GR00T N1: An Open Foundation Model for Generalist Humanoid Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> NVIDIA researchers developed GR00T N1, an open foundation Vision-Language-Action model for generalist humanoid robots. The model achieves robust performance on real GR-1 humanoid robots and simulation tasks, utilizing a 'data pyramid' strategy for efficient training and strong generalization across diverse robot embodiments and scenarios.

## Problem

- The lack of scalable, diverse, and high-quality real-world robot data to train generalist foundation models.
- The challenge of adapting large language and vision models to generate continuous, high-frequency actions for physical robots.
- The need for generalist robots capable of performing diverse tasks in unstructured human environments, moving beyond task-specific policies and handling open-ended objectives for humanoid robots.

## Method

- A ==dual-system VLA== model, **GR00T N1**, combines a reasoning VLM (==NVIDIA Eagle-2==) for high-level understanding with a high-frequency action-generating ==Diffusion Transformer== for low-level control.
- A =='data pyramid' strategy== is employed to integrate heterogeneous data sources, including web data, human videos, large-scale synthetic simulation trajectories, and AI-generated ==neural trajectories==.
- Pseudo-actions are inferred for action-less video data using ==Latent Actions (LAPA)== and ==Inverse Dynamics Models (IDM)== to provide scalable action labels for diverse visual data.

## Results

- GR00T N1 outperforms state-of-the-art imitation learning baselines, BC-Transformer and Diffusion Policy, across multiple simulation benchmarks (e.g., **45.0%** vs. **33.4%** and **26.4%** respectively on average).
- On real GR-1 humanoid robot tasks, GR00T N1-2B achieves an average success rate of **76.8%** with a full dataset, and demonstrates high data efficiency, achieving **42.6%** with only **10%** of the training data, nearly matching Diffusion Policy's **46.4%** with the full dataset.
- Co-training with neural trajectories consistently improves performance across both simulation (e.g., **+4.2%** to **+8.8%**) and real-world tasks (e.g., **+5.8%** average for GR-1 humanoid), demonstrating their effectiveness as data augmentation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A dual-system architecture effectively integrates a high-level vision-language reasoning module with a low-level action generation module for robust control.
> - Integrating heterogeneous data sources through a 'data pyramid' strategy can effectively mitigate the 'data island' problem in robot learning.
> - AI-generated 'neural trajectories' and pseudo-action labeling techniques (Latent Actions, Inverse Dynamics Models) are effective methods to scale robot training data beyond real-world collection.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of GR00T N1 is a direct response to the escalating interest and progress in **foundation models** (FMs) for artificial intelligence, particularly their application to robotics. The success of large language models (LLMs) and vision-language models (VLMs) in digital domains has inspired a "promising roadmap" for building generalist robot intelligence. However, applying FMs to robotics faces unique challenges that distinguish it from digital applications.

One of the primary challenges is the **"data island" problem**: unlike the web-scale datasets available for text and images, there is no "Internet of humanoid robot datasets." Real-world robot data is expensive, time-consuming, and difficult to acquire at scale, and existing datasets are often fragmented across different robot embodiments, sensors, and control modes. This work tackles this challenge head-on by proposing and implementing a **"data pyramid" strategy** that effectively integrates heterogeneous data sources.

GR00T N1 directly builds upon and extends recent efforts in **Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models** for robotic control. Previous works in this area include models like RT-1, RT-2, OpenVLA, and Pi-0, which fine-tune pre-trained FMs on robotics data to enable end-to-end optimization. GR00T N1 distinguishes itself by adopting a dual-system architecture (System 1 for fast action, System 2 for high-level reasoning) and by its unique approach to leveraging diverse data. While some prior VLA models use complex mixture-of-experts architectures, GR00T N1 employs a simpler cross-attention mechanism to integrate VLM outputs with the action generation module, offering more architectural flexibility.

Furthermore, this paper contributes significantly to the field of **data generation for robot learning**. Traditional robot data collection through teleoperation is labor-intensive. This work leverages advancements in **synthetic data generation**, including large-scale simulation trajectories (e.g., via DexMimicGen) and, notably, the use of state-of-the-art **video generation models** to create "neural trajectories." This novel approach of augmenting real data with AI-generated video data and then inferring pseudo-actions (via latent actions or inverse dynamics models) is a powerful way to scale training data beyond what is physically collectable. This aligns with a growing trend in robotics research to reduce reliance on purely real-world data collection, bridging the gap between abundant non-robot data and scarce robot data.

Finally, the emphasis on **humanoid robots** as a "compelling form factor" positions GR00T N1 at the forefront of developing generalist autonomy for robots designed to operate seamlessly in human environments, directly addressing a long-standing goal in robotics.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to advance the creation of **autonomous robots capable of performing everyday tasks in the human world**, particularly focusing on humanoid robots. The authors advocate for a "full-stack solution" that synergistically integrates hardware, models, and data, recognizing that progress in these three areas is collectively paving the way for general-purpose robot autonomy.

The key motivations and specific objectives for developing GR00T N1 are:

- **Leveraging Humanoid Hardware:** To build "human-level physical intelligence," the authors identify humanoid robots as a compelling form factor due to their human-like physique and versatility, making them ideal embodied physical agents for diverse tasks in human environments.
- **Developing a Generalist Robot Model:** The real world's inherent diversity and variability necessitate robots that can operate on open-ended objectives and perform a wide range of tasks. GR00T N1 aims to be such a generalist robot model, sufficiently expressive and capable of handling various tasks and novel situations.
- **Overcoming Data Scarcity:** A major hurdle is the cost and time involved in acquiring large-scale, diverse, and high-quality real-world humanoid data. A central motivation is to develop an effective "data strategy" that mitigates this "data island" problem.
- **Enabling Generalization and Rapid Adaptation:** Inspired by the success of foundation models in digital realms, the goal is to imbue robots with "core competencies" that enable strong generalization to unseen scenarios, robust handling of real-world variability, and rapid adaptation to new tasks with minimal additional data.
- **Open-Sourcing for Community Progress:** A stated objective is to release the GR00T-N1-2B model checkpoint, training data, and simulation benchmarks to accelerate the community's progress toward building and deploying generally capable humanoid robots.

In essence, GR00T N1 is designed to be an "intelligent mind" for "versatile body" humanoid robots, addressing the critical need for a foundation model trained on massive, diverse data to enable sophisticated reasoning and robust action generation in complex, open-ended environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

GR00T N1 is designed as an **open foundation Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model** for generalist humanoid robots, characterized by its dual-system architecture and innovative data strategy.

**Model Architecture (Dual-System Design):**
Inspired by human cognitive processing (System 1 for intuitive, fast reactions; System 2 for deliberate, slow reasoning), GR00T N1 employs a compositional architecture:
- **System 2 (Vision-Language Module - VLM):** This serves as the reasoning module. It is powered by **NVIDIA Eagle-2**, a pre-trained VLM (finetuned from SmolLM2 LLM and SigLIP-2 image encoder). The VLM processes the robot's visual perception (images at 224x224 resolution) and language instructions (in chat format) to interpret the environment and understand the task goal. It extracts vision-language features from its middle layers (e.g., 12th layer for GR00T-N1-2B) for efficiency. This module runs at a lower frequency (10Hz).
- **System 1 (Diffusion Transformer Module):** This acts as the action module. It's a variant of a **Diffusion Transformer (DiT)**, using flow-matching for action generation. This module receives input from embodiment-specific encoders (for proprioceptive state and noised action) and cross-attends to the VLM's output tokens. It generates closed-loop motor actions at a higher frequency (120Hz), in chunks of 16 actions. An embodiment-specific Action Decoder MLP converts DiT outputs into final motor actions.
Both System 1 and System 2 are Transformer-based neural networks, tightly coupled and jointly optimized end-to-end during training, enabling coordination between high-level reasoning and low-level actuation. Embodiment-specific MLPs are used to project varying state and action dimensions to a shared embedding space for the DiT.

**Training Data Generation (Data Pyramid):**
To address the "data island" problem, GR00T N1's VLA training corpora are structured as a "data pyramid" (Fig. 1), organizing heterogeneous sources by scale and embodiment specificity:
1. **Base (Large Quantity, Low Embodiment Specificity):** Web data (used in VLM pretraining) and human video datasets (Ego4D, Ego-Exo4D, Assembly-101, EPIC-KITCHENS, HOI4D, HoloAssist, RH20T-Human). These provide broad visual and behavioral priors.
2. **Middle Layer (Medium Quantity, Medium Embodiment Specificity):** Synthetic data, comprising:
 - **Simulation Trajectories:** Generated using systems like DexMimicGen within physics simulators (e.g., RoboCasa framework). This scales up limited human demonstrations into vast, diverse datasets by transforming and replaying segmented demonstrations.
 - **Neural Trajectories:** Generated by fine-tuning pre-trained image-to-video generation models (e.g., WAN2.1-I2V-14B) on real-robot teleoperation data. This augments real data by generating counterfactual scenarios and novel behaviors.
3. **Peak (Lower Quantity, High Embodiment Specificity):** Real-world robot trajectories collected on physical hardware, including NVIDIA's internal GR00T N1 Humanoid Pre-Training Dataset (Fourier GR-1 teleoperation), Open X-Embodiment datasets (RT-1, Bridge-v2, Language Table, DROID, MUTEX, RoboSet, Plex), and AgiBot-Alpha. These ensure grounding in embodied, real-robot execution.

**Techniques for Action-less Data:**
For human videos and neural trajectories, which lack ground-truth actions, the authors employ two key techniques to generate pseudo-actions:
- **Latent Actions (LAPA):** A VQ-VAE model is trained to extract latent features from consecutive image frames in videos. This learned latent space effectively serves as a "latent action" codebook, and the continuous pre-quantized embedding is used as a pseudo-action label, treating it as a distinct "LAPA" embodiment. This unifies diverse data into a shared latent action space.
- **Inverse Dynamics Model (IDM):** For neural-generated robot videos, an IDM (a Diffusion Transformer trained with flow-matching) is trained on real-robot data to predict actions given current and future image frames. These predicted actions are then used as pseudo-labels.

**Co-Training Strategy:**
The model is pre-trained end-to-end across all three data layers by sampling training batches from this heterogeneous data mixture. During post-training (fine-tuning), the pre-trained model is adapted to single-embodiment datasets, often augmented with neural trajectories. An auxiliary object detection loss is also incorporated during training to enhance spatial understanding.

**Training Infrastructure:**
The training is performed on a large-scale cluster managed via NVIDIA OSMO, utilizing up to 1024 NVIDIA H100 GPUs, highlighting the significant computational resources required for training such a foundation model.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of GR00T N1 across both simulated and real-world benchmarks demonstrates its strong performance, generalization capabilities, and data efficiency.

**Quantitative Results:**

- **Simulation Benchmarks:** GR00T N1 consistently outperforms state-of-the-art imitation learning baselines, BC-Transformer and Diffusion Policy, across three distinct simulation benchmarks: RoboCasa Kitchen (Franka Emika Panda arm), DexMimicGen Cross-Embodiment Suite (bimanual Panda arms, dexterous hands, GR-1 humanoid), and GR-1 Tabletop Tasks (GR-1 humanoid with Fourier hands).
 - For instance, with 100 demonstrations per task, GR00T N1 achieves an average success rate of 45.0%, significantly higher than Diffusion Policy (33.4%) and BC Transformer (26.4%). The improvement is particularly pronounced on the complex GR-1 Tabletop tasks, where GR00T N1 achieves 50.0% compared to Diffusion Policy's 32.7%.
 - Performance generally improves for all models with increasing dataset sizes (30, 100, 300 demonstrations), but GR00T N1 maintains its lead, indicating better generalization and sample efficiency.
- **Real-World Benchmarks (GR-1 Humanoid):** GR00T N1-2B shows remarkably strong performance on real GR-1 humanoid robot tasks, including object-to-container pick-and-place, articulated object manipulation, industrial object manipulation, and multi-agent coordination.
 - In a low-data regime (10% of human teleoperation data), GR00T N1-2B achieves an average success rate of 42.6%, which is nearly equivalent to Diffusion Policy trained on the *full* dataset (46.4%). This highlights GR00T N1's exceptional data efficiency.
 - With the full dataset, GR00T N1-2B achieves an average success rate of 76.8%, significantly outperforming Diffusion Policy's 46.4%.
- **Pre-training Generalization:** The pre-trained GR00T-N1-2B model exhibits strong generalization to novel real-world scenarios without specific fine-tuning. It achieved 76.6% success in a coordinated bimanual handover task (object initially to the left of the left hand) and 73.3% in placing novel objects into unseen containers.
- **Impact of Neural Trajectories:** Co-training with neural trajectories during post-training consistently leads to substantial performance gains (e.g., +4.2% to +8.8% in RoboCasa simulation, +5.8% on average for GR-1 Humanoid real-world tasks), demonstrating the effectiveness of synthetic data augmentation. The choice between latent actions (LAPA) and IDM-labeled actions suggests LAPA might be slightly better in very low-data regimes, while IDM gains an advantage with more data available for IDM training.

**Qualitative Results:**

- GR00T N1 exhibits smoother motions and higher grasping accuracy compared to baselines like Diffusion Policy, which often suffers from initial immobility and inaccurate grasps.
- The pre-trained model demonstrated impressive language following and bimanual coordination (e.g., handing over an apple from left to right hand to place it in a basket), even when the initial object placement was unusual and not explicitly seen during training. This highlights the model's ability to transfer broad behavioral priors. However, post-training on single-handed data can cause the model to lose such generalized capabilities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

GR00T N1 represents a significant stride towards the realization of generalist humanoid robots capable of operating effectively in complex, unstructured human environments. Its key contributions and potential impacts are multi-faceted:

- **Enabling Generalist Robotics:** By demonstrating robust performance across diverse tasks, robot embodiments, and language conditions, GR00T N1 moves beyond task-specific or robot-specific policies. This generalist approach is crucial for deploying versatile robots in real-world scenarios where tasks are varied and unpredictable.
- **Addressing Data Scarcity through Innovation:** The "data pyramid" strategy, combining real-world robot data, large-scale synthetic simulation data, and pseudo-labeled human and neural-generated videos, is a highly significant methodological contribution. The techniques for inferring "latent actions" and using "inverse dynamics models" to create action labels for otherwise action-less video data are particularly impactful, offering a scalable solution to the fundamental challenge of data acquisition in robot learning. This could dramatically accelerate robot skill acquisition.
- **Powerful Full-Stack Approach:** As a product of NVIDIA, GR00T N1 exemplifies the power of a "full-stack" approach to AI and robotics. The seamless integration of cutting-edge hardware (GPUs), sophisticated software platforms (Omniverse, OSMO), and advanced AI models (VLM, DiT) creates a synergistic ecosystem that enables rapid iteration and deployment. This model validates NVIDIA's strategy to be a comprehensive provider for the robotics industry.
- **Advancing VLA Models:** The dual-system architecture, while inspired by cognitive science, offers a practical and effective way to combine high-level reasoning with low-level, high-frequency action generation. The architectural choices, such as using cross-attention for VLM output integration and embodiment-specific encoders/decoders, contribute to the design principles for future VLA models.
- **Open Science and Community Acceleration:** The decision to publicly release the GR00T-N1-2B model checkpoint, training data, and simulation benchmarks is a monumental contribution to the robotics and AI research communities. This open-sourcing fosters reproducibility, encourages further research, and lowers the barrier to entry for developing and experimenting with generalist robot policies, potentially accelerating progress on humanoid robotics across academia and industry.
- **Foundation for Future Capabilities:** While currently focused on short-horizon tabletop manipulation, GR00T N1 lays the groundwork for tackling more complex challenges like long-horizon loco-manipulation and human-robot collaboration. Its ability to generalize and learn efficiently from limited real data makes it a promising step towards truly autonomous, adaptable robots that can seamlessly integrate into human society, from industrial settings to domestic environments. The framework provides a strong base for future research into more robust synthetic data generation techniques and novel model architectures.
