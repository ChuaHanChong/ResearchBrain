# MoTo: A Zero-shot Plug-in Interaction-aware Navigation for General Mobile Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Tsinghua University and Beijing University of Posts and Telecommunications developed MoTo, a zero-shot plug-in module that enables mobile robots to perform complex manipulation tasks by integrating fixed-base manipulation models with an interaction-aware navigation policy. MoTo achieves this by generating optimal docking points based on visual-language model-derived interaction keypoints, leading to improved success rates by up to 3.52% on the OVMM benchmark and enhanced performance in real-world scenarios.

## Problem

- Existing mobile manipulation methods struggle with generalization across diverse tasks and environments due to data scarcity and high training costs.
- Modular approaches face the "last-mile" challenge of identifying optimal "docking points" for the mobile base, which are crucial for successful fixed-base manipulation.
- Powerful fixed-base manipulation foundation models lack inherent mechanisms for controlling a mobile platform, limiting their application in dynamic, expansive environments.

## Method

- **MoTo**, a zero-shot plug-in module, augments off-the-shelf fixed-base manipulation models with mobile capabilities by implementing an ==interaction-aware navigation== policy.
- This policy decomposes tasks, generates specific ==interaction keypoints== (Target Keypoint, Arm Keypoint) on objects and the robot arm using Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and multi-view fusion.
- A keypoint-guided ==trajectory optimization== problem is solved using ==Dual Annealing==, minimizing the distance between keypoints while adhering to collision, smoothness, and arm operational margin constraints.

## Results

- MoTo increased overall success rates by **3.52%** when integrated with the Home-Robot baseline and by an additional **2.32%** with OpenVLA on the OVMM validation set.
- Ablation studies demonstrated that all optimization constraints (collision, smoothness, margin) contributed positively to performance, with multi-view keypoint fusion being critical for accurate 3D localization.
- Real-world deployment on a dual-arm mobile robot showed consistent performance improvements across various manipulation models and tasks, validating MoTo's zero-shot plug-in scalability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Effective mobile manipulation necessitates an interaction-aware navigation policy that generates docking points specifically tailored for subsequent arm interaction, rather than just geometric proximity.
> - Vision-Language Models can robustly identify semantic "interaction keypoints" on objects and the robot arm, providing crucial guidance for zero-shot mobile manipulation without task-specific training data.
> - Incorporating collision, smoothness, and arm operational margin costs within a trajectory optimization framework is vital for ensuring physically feasible, safe, and effective mobile manipulation in real-world settings.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

Mobile manipulation represents a significant challenge in robotics, as it involves coordinating a robotic arm and a mobile base to perform tasks in expansive and dynamic environments. The ability to deploy robots for tasks such as household services, manufacturing, and logistics necessitates robust and generalizable mobile manipulation capabilities.

Current approaches to mobile manipulation generally fall into two categories: end-to-end frameworks and modular frameworks. End-to-end methods, which attempt to map visual observations directly to mobile manipulation actions, often struggle with generalization across diverse tasks and environments due to the prohibitive cost and scarcity of large-scale mobile manipulation training datasets. Examples of such methods are M2Diffuser, which guides diffusion processes with energy terms for physical constraints.

In contrast, modular mobile manipulation frameworks typically separate navigation and manipulation tasks. These methods benefit from existing, specialized modules for navigation and fixed-base manipulation. However, a primary limitation of simply combining these modules is the "compounding errors" issue; a naive integration often leads to a significant discrepancy between navigation goals (e.g., reaching a general area) and manipulation requirements (e.g., precise positioning for interaction). Identifying appropriate "docking points" where the mobile base should stop to enable successful fixed-base manipulation remains a critical and unresolved challenge within this modular paradigm.

Simultaneously, recent advancements in manipulation foundation models, such as OpenVLA, RDT-1B, VoxPoser, and ReKep, have demonstrated substantial generalization capabilities for various fixed-base manipulation tasks. These models, trained on extensive datasets, can perform diverse manipulation actions once the robot arm is within reach of the target. However, they are inherently limited to fixed-base settings and do not inherently manage the movement of a mobile platform.

This work addresses the gap between these two lines of research. It aims to leverage the generalization strengths of fixed-base manipulation foundation models while overcoming the limitations of conventional mobile navigation by introducing an "interaction-aware navigation" policy. By focusing on generating optimal docking points that consider the specific requirements for subsequent manipulation, this research seeks to enable zero-shot mobile manipulation, reducing the reliance on costly, task-specific mobile manipulation training data.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable robots to perform long-horizon mobile manipulation tasks with enhanced generalization capabilities, particularly in unseen environments and for varied goals, using a zero-shot approach.

The motivation for this work stems from several key challenges and observations in the field of mobile robotics:

1. **Limited Generalization of Existing Mobile Manipulation Approaches:** Conventional mobile manipulation frameworks, especially end-to-end models, often lack the ability to generalize across different tasks and environments. This limitation is primarily attributed to the prohibitive cost and difficulty of collecting large-scale, diverse training data for mobile manipulation. Consequently, robots struggle to operate effectively in unstructured, real-world settings that require adaptability.
2. **Untapped Potential of Fixed-Base Manipulation Foundation Models:** Recent advances have led to the development of powerful manipulation foundation models that exhibit impressive generalization capabilities for a wide array of fixed-base manipulation tasks. These models can understand and execute complex manipulation instructions. However, their utility is constrained to situations where the robot's base is stationary, as they do not provide mechanisms for mobile base control.
3. **The "Last-Mile" Challenge in Modular Mobile Manipulation:** While combining separate navigation and manipulation modules offers a modular approach, it often results in compounding errors. A significant hurdle is determining suitable "docking points" for the mobile base—positions from which the robot arm can successfully reach and interact with the target object. Traditional navigation policies are not "interaction-aware" and may guide the robot to a location that is geometrically close but functionally unfeasible for manipulation.
4. **Necessity of Zero-shot Capability:** To mitigate the high cost and impracticality of collecting expert demonstration data for every new mobile manipulation scenario, a zero-shot learning capability is highly desirable. This would allow robots to perform novel mobile manipulation tasks without prior task-specific training.

To address these motivations, the paper proposes MoTo, a plug-in module designed to empower any off-the-shelf fixed-base manipulation model with mobile manipulation ability. The core objective is to devise an "interaction-aware navigation policy" that generates optimal base docking points. This policy is driven by the insight that successful fixed-base manipulation requires the robot to be positioned such that its arm can comfortably reach and interact with specific "keypoints" on the target object while maintaining sufficient operational margin. By focusing on these interaction keypoints and formulating a trajectory optimization problem, MoTo aims to guide the robot to perform mobile manipulation in a zero-shot manner, bridging the gap between advanced fixed-base manipulation and the demands of dynamic mobile environments.

### 3. Methodology and approach

MoTo (Move and Touch) is presented as a zero-shot plug-in module designed to augment existing fixed-base manipulation models with mobile manipulation capabilities. The approach centers on an interaction-aware navigation policy that generates suitable robot docking points for subsequent manipulation.

The methodology can be broken down into several key stages:

1. **Scene Reconstruction and Task Planning:**
 - **Scene Scanning:** The robot first scans its environment using RGB-D observations to construct a 3D point cloud P and a 3D scene graph G. The scene graph represents objects (nodes with 3D coordinates, instance masks, and object-centric images) and their spatial relationships (edges, e.g., "inside," "on top of").
 - **LLM-based Task Decomposition:** A Large Language Model (LLM) is prompted with the overall task instruction (T) and the scene graph (G) to decompose the task into a sequence of subtasks {(T_k, o_k)}. Each subtask T_k is associated with a specific target object o_k.

2. **VLM-based Keypoint Generation:**
 - **Interaction Keypoints:** To enable zero-shot mobile manipulation, MoTo introduces the concept of "interaction keypoints." These are specific points on the target object (Target Keypoint, TK) and the robotic arm/end-effector (Arm Keypoint, AK) that are relevant for the given subtask. The goal of navigation is to align these keypoints.
 - **Two-stage Keypoint Generation for Images:**
 - **Keypoint Proposal:** To identify all potential actionable points, visual cues are exploited. DINOv2 is used to extract pixel-wise representations, followed by SAM for fine-grained part-level masks. Within each mask, clustering on DINOv2 features (normalized with depth) identifies candidate keypoint proposals.
 - **Keypoint Selection:** Given these proposals, a Vision-Language Model (VLM) is prompted with a modified image (showing indexed proposals) and the subtask description (T_k) to select the most suitable keypoint.
 - **Arm Keypoint (AK) Generation:** The AK is generated from the wrist camera observation (s_w) using a VLM and projected into 3D space. If the gripper is empty, the end-effector's 3D position is used directly. For dual-arm robots, the VLM acts as a binary classifier to select the appropriate arm based on T_k, Euclidean distances to the target, and gripper contents.
 - **Multi-view Voting for Target Keypoint (TK):** To ensure robust and accurate 3D localization of TK, especially for objects far from the robot, a voting module (V) is employed. The VLM is prompted to select the top 'V' most relevant keypoint proposals from multiple object-centric images {I_k}. These multi-view 2D keypoints are projected into 3D space using corresponding depth images and camera parameters. A voting mechanism (Equation 5) then aggregates these 3D projections to determine the final, most consistent 3D TK.

3. **Keypoint-Guided Trajectory Optimization:**
 - **Optimization Objective:** The core of MoTo's control mechanism is an optimization problem (Equation 4) that aims to minimize the Euclidean distance between the generated TK and AK over a time horizon, subject to various constraints. This guides the robot's base and arm to move towards a docking point where manipulation can occur.
 - **Decision Variables:** The optimization determines the optimal base pose (θ_base) and arm joint angles ({θ_arm}) over time.
 - **Cost Constraints (C_t):** To ensure safe, smooth, and feasible robot operation, three additional cost functions are incorporated:
 - **Collision Cost (F_c):** This cost penalizes potential collisions between the robot (sampled query points on its surface Ω) and the reconstructed scene point cloud (P). It is designed to maximize the distance to obstacles within a safety margin (ϵ_0).
 - **Smoothness Cost (F_s):** This cost promotes continuous and smooth changes in the robot's base pose and arm joint angles between consecutive time steps, preventing sudden jerks or movements that could damage the robot or compromise safety.
 - **Margin Cost (F_m):** This constraint ensures that the robotic arm maintains a sufficient operational margin when TK and AK make contact. It encourages the arm to operate within a desired range (bounded by r_min and r_max) by minimizing the difference between the arm's horizontal reach (radius r) and the midpoint of its operational range, preventing the arm from being overly extended or excessively curved.
 - **Optimization Algorithm:** The optimization problem is solved using the Dual Annealing algorithm (Algorithm 1). This iterative process searches for optimal base and arm trajectories by generating candidate proposals, evaluating their costs (combining keypoint distance and constraints), and updating solutions based on an annealing schedule until the cost improvement falls below a threshold or maximum iterations are reached. Actions (base movement, arm pose, gripper status) are then derived from the optimized trajectories using an IK solver like Pinocchio.

This comprehensive approach leverages the strengths of VLMs for semantic understanding and keypoint generation, coupled with a robust trajectory optimization framework, to achieve zero-shot mobile manipulation without requiring mobile manipulation expert data.

### 4. Main findings and results

The effectiveness of MoTo was evaluated through extensive experiments in both simulated environments (OVMM benchmark) and real-world scenarios.

#### 4.1. Comparison with State-of-the-art Methods on OVMM Benchmark

- **Overall Performance:** On the OVMM validation set, MoTo demonstrated an improvement in overall success rates. When integrated with the Home-Robot (RL) baseline, MoTo achieved a 3.52% higher overall success rate. Furthermore, when combined with a stronger manipulation foundation model like OpenVLA, MoTo's success rate increased by an additional 2.32% compared to the OpenVLA with L3MVN baseline.
- **Stage-wise Analysis:** The OVMM benchmark decomposes tasks into four stages: FindObj (finding target), Pick (grasping), FindRec (finding receptacle), and Overall SR (placing). MoTo's impact was observed particularly in the FindRec stage, where its interaction-aware navigation ensured that the robot found a suitable position for placing.
- **Comparison with Conventional Navigation:** MoTo was compared against a conventional navigation framework, L3MVN. While L3MVN showed a 6.65% performance improvement in the FindRec stage, its overall success rate gain was marginal (0.48%) when used for mobile manipulation. This indicates that L3MVN, by solely focusing on reaching a nearby target without considering manipulation feasibility, did not effectively translate to overall task success. MoTo, conversely, provides a mechanism to ensure navigation facilitates successful manipulation.

#### 4.2. Ablation Study

An ablation study on the OVMM benchmark investigated the contribution of each optimization cost term and keypoint generation variant:

- **Optimization Cost Terms:** The results indicated that each constraint cost (Collision, Smoothness, Margin) contributed positively to performance. The margin cost was highlighted as particularly important for pick-and-place tasks, as it directly impacts whether the arm can reach the target position effectively.
- **Keypoint Generation Variants:**
 - **Multi-view Fusion:** The "w/o Fusion" setting, which did not apply voting to generated multi-view keypoints, resulted in a 2.42% lower success rate compared to the "Single View" approach. This finding emphasizes the importance of the multi-view consistency voting module for aggregating 3D keypoints robustly and accurately. Inconsistent or cluttered keypoints can lead to ineffective guidance and subsequent robot failures.
 - **Visual Foundation Models:** The choice of visual foundation model for keypoint extraction (e.g., DINOv2 vs. CLIP or ViT) showed a minimal impact on MoTo's performance, suggesting the robustness of the keypoint extraction pipeline across different visual feature extractors.

#### 4.3. Real-World Experiments

To assess scalability and robustness in real-world scenarios, MoTo was deployed on a dual-arm mobile robot (HEXMOVE base with two PiPER arms) with various fixed-base manipulation models: AnyGrasp (heuristic), iDP3 (diffusion policy), and RDT-1B (foundation model).

- **Performance Improvement:** Across all tested real-world tasks (e.g., "Bring me food," "Serve me water," "Prepare a meal"), MoTo consistently achieved performance improvements when paired with these diverse manipulation models, compared to using the manipulation models with conventional navigation. This demonstrated MoTo's efficiency in mobile manipulation and its plug-in scalability.
- **Manipulation Model Viewpoint Generalization:** Interestingly, iDP3 and AnyGrasp sometimes outperformed the RDT-1B foundation model in these mobile manipulation tasks. This was attributed to their superior viewpoint generalization capabilities when working with 3D point cloud observations. The ability to handle varying viewpoints resulting from diverse docking points proved crucial for mobile manipulation success, highlighting a potential area for improvement in some foundation models.

In summary, the main findings indicate that MoTo effectively enhances the capabilities of fixed-base manipulation models for mobile tasks by providing an interaction-aware navigation strategy. It achieves this in a zero-shot manner, demonstrating significant improvements in success rates in both simulated and real-world environments, without requiring additional mobile manipulation training data.

### 5. Significance and potential impact

The research presented in this paper offers several areas of significance and potential impact within the field of robotics:

1. **Bridging the Gap between Fixed-Base and Mobile Manipulation:** MoTo addresses a core challenge in robotics by effectively integrating the capabilities of advanced fixed-base manipulation foundation models with mobile platforms. This allows robots to leverage the generalization strength of models trained on extensive manipulation datasets, extending their utility beyond static workstations to dynamic, expansive environments.
2. **Enabling Zero-Shot Mobile Manipulation:** A significant contribution is the achievement of zero-shot mobile manipulation. By generating task-relevant interaction keypoints using Vision-Language Models and optimizing trajectories, MoTo eliminates the need for expensive, large-scale mobile manipulation expert data. This drastically reduces the data collection and training overhead, making general mobile manipulation more accessible and deployable for novel tasks and unseen environments.
3. **Enhancing Generalization and Adaptability:** The plug-in nature of MoTo means it can be combined with *any* off-the-shelf fixed-base manipulation model. This modularity ensures that as manipulation foundation models continue to evolve, MoTo can readily integrate with them, providing a scalable and future-proof solution for mobile robotics. This approach enhances the overall adaptability of robotic systems to diverse tasks in unstructured settings, such as household services, manufacturing, and logistics.
4. **Robust Interaction-Aware Navigation:** The proposed interaction-aware navigation policy directly tackles the "last-mile challenge" in modular mobile manipulation. By focusing on generating optimal docking points that ensure the robot arm can reach and manipulate target objects effectively, MoTo overcomes the limitations of traditional navigation methods that often lead to infeasible manipulation scenarios. This mechanism promotes more reliable and efficient task execution.
5. **Improved Safety and Feasibility through Trajectory Optimization:** The incorporation of collision avoidance, smoothness, and arm margin constraints in the motion planning objectives contributes to the generation of physically feasible and safe robot trajectories. This is critical for real-world deployments where avoiding damage to the robot or its environment is paramount, and ensures that the robot operates within its physical capabilities.
6. **Insights into Manipulation Policy Viewpoint Generalization:** The real-world experiments revealed that viewpoint generalization of manipulation policies, particularly from 3D point cloud observations, can be crucial for success in mobile manipulation scenarios with diverse docking points. This finding suggests a promising direction for future research in designing more robust manipulation policies that are less sensitive to variations in observation viewpoints.

Despite some acknowledged limitations, such as the reliance on initial scene scanning for reconstruction and challenges with highly dynamic environments or complex whole-body control tasks like door opening, MoTo lays a foundation for future advancements. Potential future work includes integrating online scene reconstruction and perception, and developing more fine-grained whole-body control policies, which could further broaden the scope and impact of this research on embodied intelligence and robotic assistance in the open world.
