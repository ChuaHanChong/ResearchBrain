# SPG: Sandwiched Policy Gradient for Masked Diffusion Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Sandwiched Policy Gradient (SPG) introduces an algorithm for fine-tuning masked diffusion language models (dLLMs) using reinforcement learning, addressing the intractable log-likelihood problem by employing both Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO) for positive rewards and a novel Evidence Upper Bound (EUBO) for negative rewards. The method yielded performance improvements up to 27.0% on logical reasoning tasks and demonstrated enhanced stability and faster convergence compared to existing RL baselines.

## Problem

- Applying reinforcement learning (RL) to discrete diffusion language models (dLLMs) is challenging due to the computational intractability of their true log-likelihood.
- Previous RL approaches for dLLMs relied on surrogates like the Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO), which is a one-sided approximation, introducing bias into policy gradients when dealing with negative rewards.
- This bias limits the effectiveness of dLLMs in learning from negative feedback and integrating with advanced RL algorithms that require both increasing good outputs and decreasing bad outputs.

## Method

- The Sandwiched Policy Gradient (**SPG**) algorithm employs an ==advantage-weighted== log-likelihood objective that uses the Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO) for sequences with positive advantage and a newly derived Evidence Upper Bound (EUBO) for sequences with negative advantage.
- A tractable Evidence Upper Bound (EUBO) is derived based on the Rényi variational bound, allowing for principled minimization of the true log-likelihood for undesirable outputs.
- A block-wise masking strategy is implemented for Monte Carlo estimation, and a mixture of EUBO and ELBO is used for negative advantage traces to enhance training stability and efficiency.

## Results

- SPG consistently outperformed existing RL methods for dLLMs across reasoning and coding benchmarks, with accuracy improvements of **27.0%** on Sudoku, **18.4%** on Countdown, **3.6%** on GSM8K, and **2.6%** on MATH500.
- The algorithm also demonstrated improved performance on coding tasks, achieving a **1.9%** increase on HumanEval and a **4.7%** increase on MBPP.
- SPG led to faster convergence and higher reward levels during RL training, showing enhanced stability and efficiency compared to baselines like D1, WD1, and UniGRPO.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The one-sided nature of ELBO as a lower bound creates significant bias in policy gradients when trying to penalize undesirable outputs, as minimizing ELBO doesn't guarantee reducing the true log-likelihood.
> - By leveraging both an Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO) and a tractable Evidence Upper Bound (EUBO), SPG provides a more symmetric and robust approach to policy gradient estimation for dLLMs, allowing effective learning from both positive and negative feedback.
> - Introducing a mixture objective for negative advantage traces (a blend of EUBO and ELBO) can reduce gradient variance and lead to more stable and efficient training than using either bound alone.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Diffusion models, initially successful in continuous domains like image generation, have recently been adapted for discrete data generation, specifically text. Masked Diffusion Language Models (MDLMs) represent a significant advancement in this area by defining the diffusion process directly in the discrete token space through random masking. These models, including LLaDA and DREAM, offer a key advantage over autoregressive (AR) language models: the ability to decode multiple tokens in parallel, which can lead to reduced inference latency and improved scalability.

A crucial aspect of modern large language models (LLMs) is their alignment with human preferences or task-specific rewards through reinforcement learning (RL). While RL has been widely applied to AR-LLMs, its application to discrete diffusion language models (dLLMs) remains less explored due and faces specific challenges. A primary obstacle is the computational intractability of the dLLM's true log-likelihood, which is a fundamental component for accurate policy gradient estimation in standard RL algorithms.

Previous approaches to address this intractability in dLLMs often resort to surrogates such as the Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO) or one-step estimations. However, the ELBO is a one-sided approximation, meaning that while maximizing it can increase the true log-likelihood, minimizing it for undesirable outputs does not guarantee a reduction in the true log-likelihood. This can introduce significant bias into the policy gradients, particularly when dealing with negative rewards or relative advantage functions common in advanced RL algorithms like Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) or Direct Preference Optimization (DPO). Such bias can lead to suboptimal performance and hinder the model's ability to learn effectively from negative feedback. Some works have explored trajectory-level updates or structured noising, but these often incur high computational costs or introduce their own set of approximations.

This research aims to provide a more principled and robust method for applying RL to dLLMs by addressing the fundamental challenge of log-likelihood intractability, thereby enabling more effective preference alignment and reasoning capabilities for these parallel-decoding models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop a novel reinforcement learning algorithm, named Sandwiched Policy Gradient (SPG), that enables more effective and less biased fine-tuning of masked diffusion language models (dLLMs) for alignment with human preferences or task-specific rewards.

The primary motivation stems from the inherent challenge of applying standard policy gradient methods to dLLMs. In traditional RL for language models, policy gradients require the computation of the log-likelihood of generated sequences, log π<sub>θ</sub>(x|c). For dLLMs, however, this true log-likelihood is computationally intractable. Prior work has attempted to circumvent this by using surrogates like the Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO) or one-step estimations. While these approximations simplify the problem, they introduce a critical flaw:

1. **Bias in Policy Gradients:** The ELBO is only a lower bound on the true log-likelihood (ELBO ≤ log π<sub>θ</sub>). This means that maximizing the ELBO for positively rewarded sequences is a valid approach to increase the true log-likelihood. However, when dealing with negatively rewarded sequences (i.e., when penalizing undesirable outputs), minimizing the ELBO does not guarantee a reduction in the true log-likelihood. This asymmetry leads to misaligned policy gradients.
2. **Incompatibility with Advanced RL:** Many modern RL algorithms, especially those leveraging preference optimization or relative rewards (e.g., advantage functions that can be negative), rely on the ability to both increase the likelihood of good samples and decrease the likelihood of bad samples. The one-sided nature of ELBO-based approximations prevents effective learning from negative feedback, thereby biasing the final learned policy and limiting the effectiveness of these advanced RL techniques.
3. **Suboptimal Performance:** The accumulated bias from inaccurate log-likelihood estimation can lead to suboptimal performance in downstream tasks, especially complex reasoning or coding benchmarks where precise alignment and robust learning from both positive and negative examples are crucial.

Therefore, the key motivation is to overcome this limitation by introducing a method that can consistently and robustly estimate policy gradients for dLLMs, allowing for effective optimization regardless of the reward sign. This involves developing a mechanism to handle both positive and negative advantage traces in a theoretically sound manner, thereby reducing bias and improving the stability and performance of RL fine-tuning for dLLMs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology, Sandwiched Policy Gradient (SPG), introduces a novel reinforcement learning algorithm specifically designed to address the intractable log-likelihood problem in masked diffusion language models (dLLMs). The approach is built upon an advantage-weighted log-likelihood objective, similar to Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), but critically modifies how the intractable log-likelihood terms are estimated based on the reward sign.

#### 3.1. Core SPG Objective

For a given prompt `c`, SPG generates a group of `g` responses `{x_j}` from the policy π<sub>θ</sub>. It then computes an advantage score `A_j(c, x_j)` for each response, defined as `R(c, x_j) - (1/g) Σ R(c, x_ȷ)`. This advantage score indicates whether a response is better or worse than the average response in the group.

The core idea of SPG is to "sandwich" the intractable true log-likelihood `log π_θ(x_j | c)` using both an Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO) and an Evidence Upper Bound (EUBO). The optimization objective `J_SPG(θ)` is formulated as:
`J_SPG(θ) = E[(1/g) Σ (1_{A_j ≥ 0} ⋅ A_j L_ELBO(x_j | c; θ) + 1_{A_j < 0} ⋅ A_j L_EUBO(x_j | c; θ))]`

Here, `1_{A_j ≥ 0}` and `1_{A_j < 0}` are indicator functions. This objective ensures that:
- For sequences with positive advantage (`A_j ≥ 0`), the model maximizes the ELBO, `L_ELBO`, which is a valid lower bound for the true log-likelihood, thus increasing `log π_θ(x_j | c)`.
- For sequences with negative advantage (`A_j < 0`), the model minimizes the EUBO, `L_EUBO`, which is a valid upper bound for the true log-likelihood. Minimizing an upper bound helps to decrease `log π_θ(x_j | c)`.

Since `L_ELBO ≤ log π_θ ≤ L_EUBO`, maximizing `J_SPG(θ)` serves as a principled and less biased proxy for optimizing the true advantage-weighted log-likelihood objective `J_group(θ)`.

#### 3.2. Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO)

The ELBO, `L_ELBO(x; θ)`, is the standard objective used for pre-training masked diffusion models. It simplifies to training the model to predict the original tokens at masked positions given a corrupted sequence `z_t`. The formulation is `L_ELBO(x; θ) = E_{t,z_t}[w(t) ⋅ 1(z_{t,i}=m) ⋅ log π_θ(x_i | z_t)]`, where `w(t)` is a time-dependent weight and `1(z_{t,i}=m)` indicates a masked token. This is used directly for positive advantage traces.

#### 3.3. Tractable Evidence Upper Bound (EUBO)

A key contribution is the derivation of a tractable Evidence Upper Bound, `L_EUBO(x; θ)`. Building upon the Rényi variational bound, the paper presents Theorem 1 and Corollary 1, which derive `L_EUBO` for masked diffusion models. The working form for its gradient calculation is:
`˜L_EUBO(x_1:n; θ) = (1/β) Σ_{i=1}^n log E_{t,z_t}[w(t) ⋅ 1(z_{t,i}=m) ⋅ π_θ^β(x_i | z_t)]`
where `β ≥ 1` is a hyperparameter controlling the tightness of the bound. A crucial structural difference from `L_ELBO` is that the logarithm in `˜L_EUBO` appears *outside* the expectation. While Monte Carlo estimation of this form can introduce bias (due to Jensen's inequality), it yields a tighter bound compared to a looser, unbiased alternative, which was empirically found to perform worse.

#### 3.4. Practical Considerations

To enhance stability and efficiency during training, SPG incorporates two practical considerations:

1. **Block-Wise Masking Strategy for Monte Carlo Estimation:** During Monte Carlo estimation of `L_ELBO` and `˜L_EUBO`, perturbed samples `{z_t}` are generated. Instead of simple random masking of clean sequences, SPG employs a block-wise masking strategy. This aligns the data distribution seen during optimization with the semi-autoregressive unmasking strategies used by state-of-the-art dLLMs like LLaDA during inference. The sequence is divided into blocks; a random block is selected for masking, while preceding blocks are kept clean and following blocks are fully masked. Tokens within the chosen block are randomly masked. A small probability `p_mask = 0.15` is also applied to perturb prompt and clean blocks for robustness.
2. **Mixture of Upper and Lower Bounds for Negative Advantage Traces (`˜L_Mix`):** To mitigate the computational cost and potential instability of relying solely on `˜L_EUBO` for negative advantage traces, SPG introduces a mixture objective:
 `˜L_Mix(x | c; θ):= ω ⋅ ˜L_EUBO(x | c; θ) + (1 − ω) ⋅ L_ELBO(x | c; θ)`
 where `ω ∈` is a blend coefficient. Proposition 1 demonstrates that the gradient of this mixture objective can have strictly smaller coordinate-wise variance than using either bound alone (for `Var((ρ_β - w)s_k) > 0`), leading to more stable training. The mixture also offers a confidence-aware weighting, where tokens with low recovery chance (small `π_θ^β`) are weighted less, preventing vanishing gradients.

#### 3.5. Experimental Setup and Implementation Details

The approach is evaluated by fine-tuning LLaDA-8B-Instruct on mathematical/logical reasoning and coding benchmarks. Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) is used for efficient training. RL rollouts use a semi-autoregressive confidence-based decoding strategy with specific generation lengths and diffusion steps. A group size `g=6` and Monte Carlo samples `m=2` are used. Hyperparameters `β` and `ω` are tuned for optimal performance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several key findings and results demonstrating the effectiveness of the Sandwiched Policy Gradient (SPG) algorithm.

#### 4.1. Superior Performance on Reasoning Benchmarks

SPG consistently outperforms state-of-the-art RL methods for dLLMs across four mathematical and logical reasoning benchmarks: GSM8K, MATH500, Countdown, and Sudoku.
- **GSM8K (mathematical reasoning):** SPG w/ Mixture achieved an accuracy improvement of 3.6% over the previous state-of-the-art (UniGRPO) at a generation length of 256.
- **MATH500 (mathematical reasoning):** SPG w/ Mixture improved accuracy by 2.6% over UniGRPO at a generation length of 256.
- **Countdown (logical reasoning):** SPG w/ Mixture demonstrated a substantial improvement of 18.4% over WD1 (the previous best) at a generation length of 256.
- **Sudoku (logical reasoning):** SPG w/ Mixture achieved a significant accuracy increase of 27.0% over UniGRPO at a generation length of 256.

These results are robust across various generation lengths (128, 256, 512), with SPG w/ Mixture consistently yielding the best performance, followed closely by SPG w/ EUBO.

#### 4.2. Extension to Coding Tasks

SPG's effectiveness extends to coding domains, showing improved accuracy on HumanEval and MBPP benchmarks.
- **HumanEval:** SPG w/ EUBO improved accuracy by 1.9% at 256 tokens, and SPG w/ Mixture by 1.8% at 128 tokens over previous best.
- **MBPP:** SPG w/ Mixture showed an accuracy gain of 4.7% over WD1 at 256 tokens.

#### 4.3. Improved Reward Dynamics and Stability

During RL training, SPG consistently leads to a faster convergence and higher reward levels compared to baselines such as D1, WD1, and UniGRPO. This indicates improved training efficiency and stability, contributing to its superior final performance.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies and Component Contributions

- **Log-Likelihood Estimation for Negative Advantage Traces:** Ablations confirm the importance of explicitly penalizing negative advantage traces. Removing the negative advantage loss (SPG wo/ neg) resulted in a substantial performance drop. Both SPG w/ EUBO and SPG w/ Mixture significantly outperformed using only ELBO for negative traces (SPG w/ ELBO), highlighting the benefits of evidence upper bound regularization.
- **Mixture of Upper and Lower Bounds:** The SPG w/ Mixture approach consistently outperformed SPG w/ EUBO alone, achieving a balance between fast convergence and high final rewards. This supports the theoretical analysis that the mixture leads to lower variance and more stable training.
- **Masking Strategy:** The proposed block-wise masking strategy for Monte Carlo estimation consistently improved performance over random masking, demonstrating the importance of aligning data distributions between policy rollout and optimization.
- **Hyperparameter Sensitivity (β and ω):** The optimal `β` (controlling EUBO tightness) generally falls close to 1.0, indicating that tighter bounds are preferable. The mixture coefficient `ω` showed an inverted U-shaped performance curve, with `ω ∈ (0,1)` outperforming extreme values (pure ELBO or pure EUBO), further validating the mixture approach.
- **Inference Strategy Robustness:** Despite being trained under a specific confidence-based semi-autoregressive decoding strategy, SPG models generalized well and maintained their performance advantage over baselines across a variety of alternative inference strategies (e.g., different block sizes, random unmasking, full sequence decoding).
- **Other Ablations:** Studies on Pass@K performance indicate SPG's ability to generate diverse, high-quality outputs. Full fine-tuning yielded comparable or better results than LoRA, validating the robustness. Prompt masking had minimal impact, suggesting the core log-likelihood estimation method is robust. Increasing Monte Carlo samples from 2 to 4 generally improved performance further.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Sandwiched Policy Gradient (SPG) algorithm represents a significant advancement in the application of reinforcement learning to discrete diffusion language models (dLLMs). Its primary significance lies in providing a principled and robust solution to the long-standing challenge of intractable log-likelihood estimation in dLLMs, which has previously limited the effectiveness of RL-based fine-tuning.

**Key significance points include:**

- **Principled RL for dLLMs:** By employing both an Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO) for positive reward sequences and a derived Evidence Upper Bound (EUBO) for negative reward sequences, SPG addresses the bias inherent in one-sided approximations. This allows dLLMs to learn effectively from both positive and negative feedback, which is crucial for complex alignment tasks and the development of sophisticated reasoning behaviors.
- **Enhanced Performance:** The consistent and substantial performance improvements across diverse mathematical, logical reasoning, and coding benchmarks demonstrate SPG's practical utility. These gains, sometimes exceeding 20% in accuracy on challenging tasks, suggest that dLLMs fine-tuned with SPG can achieve higher levels of problem-solving capability than previously possible with existing RL methods.
- **Improved Training Stability and Efficiency:** The introduction of a mixture objective for negative advantage traces, combined with a block-wise masking strategy, contributes to more stable optimization and faster convergence. This addresses practical training challenges often encountered in complex RL setups.
- **Robustness and Generalizability:** SPG-trained models exhibit strong robustness across various inference strategies, indicating that the learned policy is not overly specialized to the training decoding method. This generalizability makes SPG a versatile tool for enhancing dLLMs in diverse application scenarios.

**Potential impacts include:**

- **Advancing dLLM Capabilities:** By unlocking more effective RL fine-tuning, SPG could accelerate the development of dLLMs with superior reasoning, coding, and instruction-following abilities, making them more competitive with, or even outperform, autoregressive models in specific niches.
- **Leveraging Parallel Decoding:** The enhanced capabilities provided by SPG enable dLLMs to fully realize their inherent advantage of parallel decoding. This could lead to significantly faster inference for complex tasks requiring long sequence generation, impacting applications like code generation, scientific writing, and automated problem-solving.
- **Broader Adoption of dLLMs:** The improved performance and stability offered by SPG may encourage wider adoption of dLLMs in research and industry, fostering further innovation in model architectures, training techniques, and downstream applications.
- **Foundation for Future RL Research:** SPG provides a new theoretical and practical foundation for future research in reinforcement learning for diffusion models, particularly in discrete state spaces. It opens avenues for exploring more advanced RL stabilization techniques, alternative reward formulations (e.g., RLOO), and combinations with decoding order prediction.

In summary, SPG represents a methodological step forward for fine-tuning dLLMs, potentially enabling them to reach new levels of performance and efficiency in complex language generation and reasoning tasks.
