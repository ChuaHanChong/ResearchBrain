# SPG: Sandwiched Policy Gradient for Masked Diffusion Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Sandwiched Policy Gradient (SPG) introduces an algorithm for fine-tuning masked diffusion language models (dLLMs) using reinforcement learning, addressing the intractable log-likelihood problem by employing both Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO) for positive rewards and a novel Evidence Upper Bound (EUBO) for negative rewards. The method yielded performance improvements up to 27.0% on logical reasoning tasks and demonstrated enhanced stability and faster convergence compared to existing RL baselines.

## Problem

- Applying reinforcement learning (RL) to discrete diffusion language models (dLLMs) is challenging due to the computational intractability of their true log-likelihood.
- Previous RL approaches for dLLMs relied on surrogates like the Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO), which is a one-sided approximation, introducing bias into policy gradients when dealing with negative rewards.
- This bias limits the effectiveness of dLLMs in learning from negative feedback and integrating with advanced RL algorithms that require both increasing good outputs and decreasing bad outputs.

## Method

- The Sandwiched Policy Gradient (**SPG**) algorithm employs an ==advantage-weighted== log-likelihood objective that uses the Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO) for sequences with positive advantage and a newly derived Evidence Upper Bound (EUBO) for sequences with negative advantage.
- A tractable Evidence Upper Bound (EUBO) is derived based on the Rényi variational bound, allowing for principled minimization of the true log-likelihood for undesirable outputs.
- A block-wise masking strategy is implemented for Monte Carlo estimation, and a mixture of EUBO and ELBO is used for negative advantage traces to enhance training stability and efficiency.

## Results

- SPG consistently outperformed existing RL methods for dLLMs across reasoning and coding benchmarks, with accuracy improvements of **27.0%** on Sudoku, **18.4%** on Countdown, **3.6%** on GSM8K, and **2.6%** on MATH500.
- The algorithm also demonstrated improved performance on coding tasks, achieving a **1.9%** increase on HumanEval and a **4.7%** increase on MBPP.
- SPG led to faster convergence and higher reward levels during RL training, showing enhanced stability and efficiency compared to baselines like D1, WD1, and UniGRPO.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The one-sided nature of ELBO as a lower bound creates significant bias in policy gradients when trying to penalize undesirable outputs, as minimizing ELBO doesn't guarantee reducing the true log-likelihood.
> - By leveraging both an Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO) and a tractable Evidence Upper Bound (EUBO), SPG provides a more symmetric and robust approach to policy gradient estimation for dLLMs, allowing effective learning from both positive and negative feedback.
> - Introducing a mixture objective for negative advantage traces (a blend of EUBO and ELBO) can reduce gradient variance and lead to more stable and efficient training than using either bound alone.
