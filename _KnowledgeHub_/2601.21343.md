# Self-Improving Pretraining: using post-trained models to pretrain better models

> [!summary] Summary
> FAIR at Meta introduced "Self-Improving Pretraining," a method leveraging strong, post-trained LLMs to guide the pretraining of new models. This approach integrates quality, safety, and factuality into core model behaviors from the initial development stage, resulting in an 87.9% win rate in generation coherence, a 36.2% relative improvement in factuality, and an 18.5% relative improvement in safety over standard pretraining baselines.

## Problem

- Current LLM alignment methods primarily involve post-training fine-tuning, which struggles to fully correct undesirable patterns already ingrained during the initial pretraining phase.
- Undesirable behaviors like toxicity, bias, and hallucination are deeply embedded during pretraining, leading to issues such as poor generalization and susceptibility to 'jailbreak' attacks.
- Standard next-token prediction pretraining on vast, often unfiltered, datasets means models learn from suboptimal or inconsistent examples, hindering the development of inherently aligned behaviors.

## Method

- The methodology redefines pretraining as a ==prefix-conditioned suffix generation task==, aiming for the generation of a superior sequence rather than just matching the original text.
- It utilizes existing strong, post-trained LLMs in two roles: a =='Suffix Rewriter'== to produce higher-quality, safer, or augmented suffixes, and an =='LLM-as-Judge'== to evaluate candidate generations and assign rewards.
- A policy model is trained using ==Reinforcement Learning (online DPO or RF-NLL)==, guided by rewards from the judge, learning dynamically from original, rewritten, and its own high-quality generated suffixes.

## Results

- In continual pretraining, the method achieved up to an **87.9%** win rate in generation coherence, a **36.2%** relative improvement in factuality, and an **18.5%** relative improvement in safety compared to standard baselines.
- When pretraining from scratch, the approach attained a **32.4%** generation quality win rate on standard prefixes and improved safety evaluations from **85.2** to **97.5**.
- Performance improvements across quality, factuality, and safety benchmarks correlated positively with an increased number of rollouts during training and the use of a more capable LLM-as-judge.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Embedding quality, safety, and factuality proactively during pretraining leads to more robust and inherently aligned foundation models, moving beyond reactive post-training corrections.
> - Strong, post-trained LLMs can function as effective 'teachers' within a self-improving pretraining loop, providing refined supervisory signals and effectively synthesizing 'superior' training data.
> - Online DPO proves effective for this paradigm, allowing learning from diverse candidate sequences (original, rewritten, and policy-generated), and consistently outperforming methods reliant on single rollouts or static rewritten data.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The conventional paradigm for training large language models (LLMs) involves two distinct stages: an initial pretraining phase on vast corpora of raw text, followed by post-training (fine-tuning and alignment) on curated datasets. This two-stage approach has been effective in imbuing models with foundational language knowledge and capabilities. However, the paper identifies a fundamental limitation: crucial behaviors such as safety, factuality, overall generation quality, and complex reasoning abilities are typically introduced and refined only during the later post-training stage. The patterns learned during pretraining, which largely dictate a model's ultimate capabilities, often lack explicit guidance towards these desirable properties, leading to models that still exhibit various weaknesses, including factuality issues, safety flaws, and reasoning limitations.

Existing research efforts have attempted to address these issues primarily at the post-training stage through supervised fine-tuning (SFT), reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF), and various alignment techniques. For safety, methods include collecting curated datasets and multi-stage fine-tuning, though issues persist, particularly with out-of-distribution inputs and jailbreak attacks. Some works have explored incorporating safety into pretraining, for example, using control tokens or altering training schemes. For factuality, approaches at post-training often involve SFT, offline RL, and rewards derived from retrieval tools. Reasoning capabilities, while emerging during standard pretraining (e.g., chain-of-thought), are significantly amplified during post-training through reinforcement learning on verifiable rewards (RLVR). More recent work has begun to explore moving these post-training techniques upstream, augmenting pretraining or introducing intermediate ("mid-training") stages with "thinking tokens" or reinforcement learning objectives for sequence generation.

This paper positions its work within this landscape by hypothesizing that the delayed incorporation of desirable behaviors is a core cause of current LLM failings. It proposes addressing this by leveraging stronger, already post-trained models to inform and guide the earlier stages of training, thereby integrating these properties "from the ground up" rather than attempting to correct deeply embedded patterns later. This represents a departure from the traditional next-token prediction pretraining paradigm, particularly by introducing sequence generation objectives and reinforcement learning earlier in the pipeline.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to fundamentally enhance the core capabilities of large language models by integrating desirable behaviors—specifically safety, factuality, overall generation quality, and reasoning ability—into earlier stages of the training pipeline, rather than solely relying on post-training to instill these properties.

The primary motivation stems from the recognition of a "fundamental limitation" in the classical LLM training paradigm. While pretraining equips models with foundational language patterns, it provides no explicit guidance for developing crucial attributes like safety, factual accuracy, or advanced reasoning. These attributes are typically "layered on" much later during post-training. The authors hypothesize that this temporal separation is a root cause for persistent weaknesses in current LLMs, which still exhibit flaws in factuality, safety, and reasoning. The patterns established during pretraining strongly shape a model's ultimate capabilities, and attempting to correct undesirable tendencies at a late stage may not fully succeed in overwriting deeply embedded behaviors.

To address this, the paper introduces a novel **self-improving pretraining framework**, which operates on two main objectives:

1. **Self-Improving Pretraining for Safety, Factuality, and Quality (Section 1)**:
 - **Objective**: To build models that are inherently of higher quality, safer, and more factual from the initial pretraining stages.
 - **Motivation**: Traditional pretraining data varies widely in quality, safety, and factuality. Simply filtering out low-quality or unsafe data might prevent models from learning how to steer towards desirable outputs when presented with such inputs at inference time. The goal is to provide explicit, reinforcement-based guidance during pretraining to directly foster these behaviors, rather than relying on expensive, curated fine-tuning datasets and subsequent alignment to mitigate issues learned earlier.

2. **Thinking Mid-training: Reinforcement Learning of Interleaved Reasoning (Section 2)**:
 - **Objective**: To bridge the gap in reasoning capabilities between pretraining and post-training by introducing an intermediate training phase that explicitly teaches models to reason.
 - **Motivation**: Pretraining data largely lacks explicit reasoning traces, forcing models to acquire complex reasoning skills almost entirely during post-training. This limits the efficiency of post-training and may prevent models from learning deeper, underlying thought processes. The authors hypothesize that exposing models to and optimizing for reasoning earlier will yield models that are not only better at reasoning initially but are also better prepared to benefit from reasoning-intensive post-training. This part of the work seeks to leverage the rich, implicit opportunities for intermediate thinking present even in raw pretraining data, by making such reasoning explicit.

In both objectives, a core motivation is to utilize the knowledge and capabilities of existing, strong, post-trained models to provide a "superior training signal" to models being trained at earlier stages. This leverages the expertise already absorbed by advanced models to accelerate and refine the learning process of new models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces a framework for self-improving pretraining and mid-training that integrates desirable behaviors earlier in the LLM lifecycle.

#### 3.1. Self-Improving Pretraining for Safety, Factuality, and Quality (Section 1)

This method re-envisions pretraining from a next-token prediction task to a sequence generation task, specifically prefix-conditioned suffix generation. The core idea is to use an existing strong, post-trained model in two capacities: as a **Suffix Rewriter** and a **Suffix Judge**.

- **Sequence Pretraining Task**: Instead of predicting the next token, the policy model is trained to generate a high-quality sequence (suffix of length N) conditioned on a preceding context (prefix). This moves closer to the real-world deployment goal of generating coherent sequences.
- **Suffix Rewriter**: A strong post-trained model is used to rewrite suffixes from the pretraining data. This rewrite (`ˆx_j`) is intended to be superior to the original suffix (`x_j`) by:
 - Improving overall quality if the original is low-quality.
 - Steering towards a safe suffix when given an unsafe prefix, without altering the unsafe prefix itself, ensuring the model learns to handle such inputs.
 - Augmenting data to improve diversity, knowledge, or quality, leveraging findings from offline data rewriting.
 The rewriter can be a directly prompted or fine-tuned post-trained model. For safety experiments, a Llama3.1-8B-Instruct model was fine-tuned to either copy safe suffixes or rewrite unsafe ones to be safe, using GRPO with a reward function that encouraged exact matches for safe suffixes and a combination of quality and safety judgments for unsafe rewrites.
- **Suffix Judge**: The same strong post-trained model acts as a judge to evaluate candidate completions for quality, safety, and factuality, providing rewards for policy training. Candidates include: (i) multiple rollouts from the current policy, (ii) the original suffix, and (iii) the rewritten suffix. The judge assigns rewards based on how well candidates meet the desired criteria. Prompts for quality, safety, and factuality judgments are detailed, often involving Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning for robustness. For safety and quality, Llama3.1-8B-Instruct was fine-tuned using GRPO on synthetic data with known rewards. For factuality and general quality, GPT-OSS-120B was directly prompted.
- **Policy Model Training**: The policy model is trained using online reinforcement learning algorithms, specifically online DPO (Direct Preference Optimization) or reward-filtered negative log-likelihood (RF-NLL). Online DPO is chosen for its off-policy nature, allowing learning from diverse sequences not necessarily generated by the current policy (e.g., original or rewritten suffixes). In DPO, the highest-scoring candidate is chosen, and the lowest-scoring is rejected. For RF-NLL, only the highest-scoring candidate is used for NLL updates.
 - During early training, when policy rollouts are expected to be low-quality, supervision primarily relies on the original and rewritten suffixes. As the policy improves, the judge increasingly rewards high-quality rollouts.
 - Experiments included training Llama2 1.4B models from scratch and via continual pretraining on SlimPajama and RedPajama datasets, with ablations on loss functions, candidate pools (e.g., number of rollouts), and judge models.

#### 3.2. Thinking Mid-training: Reinforcement Learning of Interleaved Reasoning (Section 2)

This method introduces an intermediate training phase to explicitly teach reasoning before standard post-training.

- **Mid-training Data Thinking Augmentation**: An annotator model (gpt-oss-120b) is used to augment pretraining corpora (DCLM, FineMath) by inserting interleaved "thoughts" (`τ_j`) at semantically appropriate positions within original text segments (`x_j`). These thoughts are generated with specific action tags like `<task>`, `<think>`, `<world_model>`, `<recall_knowledge>`, `<simulate>`, `<reusable_lessons>`, `<verification>`, and `<tool_use>`, designed to mimic an intelligent learner's reasoning process. The full augmented sequence is `[x_1, τ_1, x_2, τ_2..., x_K, τ_K]`.
- **Thinking SFT Mid-training**: A base model (`M_0`) undergoes supervised fine-tuning on a portion of the augmented corpus (`˜D_SFT`) using a standard next-token prediction objective. The loss is computed over the entire augmented sequence, training the model to produce both original content and the inserted thoughts. This "cold-start" phase aims to teach the mechanics of interleaved reasoning.
- **Thinking RL Mid-training**: The model (`M_1`), initialized from SFT mid-training, is further refined with reinforcement learning on the remaining augmented corpus (`˜D_RL`). Given a prefix, the model generates thinking tokens (`ˆτ_i`) followed by a predicted suffix (`ˆs_i`). An LLM judge (`M_judge`, gpt-oss-120b) evaluates the quality of `ˆs_i` against the ground truth suffix, providing a binary reward. The objective is to maximize this reward, optimizing for the utility of generated thoughts in predicting subsequent text. DrGRPO is used for optimization.
- **RL Post-Training**: After mid-training, standard RL post-training (e.g., with verifiable rewards, RLVR) is applied to mathematical reasoning tasks (DAPO-Math-14k). A rule-based reward model (`M_RLVR`) scores generated answers against ground truth. DrGRPO is also used here.
- **Experimental Setup**: Llama3-8B-Base models were used as policy models, with ablations on Qwen3-8B. Datasets included DCLM and FineMath for mid-training, and DAPO-Math-14k for post-training.

#### 3.3. Evaluations (Common to Both Sections)

Models were evaluated across several categories:
- **Generation Quality**: Win rates against a baseline using GPT-OSS-120B as a judge for coherence, safety, and general quality.
- **Standard Evaluations**: Aggregated scores on benchmarks like BoolQ, PIQA, HellaSwag, ARC, OpenBookQA, SIQA, and MMLU, measuring general reasoning.
- **Safety**: Weighted average across RealToxicityPrompts, RedPajama test split, ToxiGen, and XStest datasets, using GPT-OSS-120B as a judge.
- **Factuality**: Weighted average across RedPajama test split, FActScore, HaluEval (QA, dialogue, summarization), and TruthfulQA (MC1, MC2), using GPT-OSS-120B as a judge.
- **Mathematical Reasoning (for Section 2)**: Pass@k accuracy on GSM8K, MATH-500, Olympiad, AMC23, and GPQA-Diamond, verified using Math-Verify.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded a set of findings demonstrating improvements across various desirable LLM capabilities by incorporating guidance earlier in the training pipeline.

#### 4.1. Self-Improving Pretraining for Safety, Factuality, and Quality (Section 1)

- **Significant Gains in Continued Pretraining**:
 - **Quality Optimization**: Achieved an 86.3% win rate in generation quality on standard prefixes and an 87.9% win rate in coherence, representing substantial improvements over the Llama Base (50.0% win rate) and Llama Pretrain Baseline (49.0% win rate). Standard evaluation scores also improved.
 - **Factuality Optimization**: Increased average factuality evaluations from a baseline of 42.3 to 57.6, alongside significant gains in generation quality (84.0% win rate). Improvements were observed across individual factuality benchmarks.
 - **Safety Optimization**: Demonstrated significant gains in generation quality for unsafe prefixes (77.7% win rate) and an average increase in safety evaluations from 76.9 to 91.1. Wins were observed in most individual safety benchmarks.
 - Cross-objective results indicated that optimizing for one specific characteristic (e.g., safety) did not inherently optimize for others (e.g., factuality), suggesting the need to factor desired properties into the reward signals.
- **Improvements in Pretraining from Scratch**:
 - Training from scratch on rewritten suffixes using RF-NLL improved safety evaluations (from 85.2 to 96.7) but not overall quality.
 - Self-Improving Pretraining using RF-NLL (rollout vs. rewrite) yielded a generation quality win rate of 32.4% (compared to 1.3% for baseline next-token prediction) and an increase in safety evaluations from 85.2 to 97.5.
- **Ablation Studies**:
 - Online DPO with a post-trained suffix judge yielded larger gains compared to SFT on rewrites or single rollouts.
 - Increasing the number of rollouts from the policy model (up to 16) generally correlated with improved performance across quality, factuality, and safety benchmarks.
 - Early in training, the model relied more on original and rewritten suffixes, but as training progressed, the judge increasingly chose high-quality rollouts.
 - Prompted GPT-OSS-120B generally outperformed a fine-tuned Llama3 judge for quality training, though the fine-tuned model performed comparably.
 - Using a single pivot candidate for pairwise comparisons in DPO resulted in a deterioration of performance, suggesting that full pairwise comparisons were more effective.

#### 4.2. Thinking Mid-training: Reinforcement Learning of Interleaved Reasoning (Section 2)

- **Enhanced Mid-training Reasoning Capabilities**:
 - SFT on thinking-augmented data for Llama3-8B-Base models drastically improved average reasoning performance from 0.0475 (base model) to 0.2221 (SFT with thinking augmentation).
 - Further applying RL mid-training after SFT led to additional significant improvements, achieving an average reasoning score of 0.3348 to 0.3390.
 - During RL mid-training, a steady increase in rewards was observed, correlating with an increase in the average length of generated "thinking" tokens.
- **Superior Post-training Effectiveness**:
 - The full pipeline (SFT mid-training on thinking-augmented data + RL mid-training + RL post-training) achieved an average accuracy of 0.3837 across challenging mathematical and general reasoning benchmarks for Llama-3-8B. This represents a 3.2x improvement compared to direct RL post-training on the base model without mid-training (0.1197).
 - Models that underwent SFT mid-training alone demonstrated substantially higher post-training performance than those trained on raw data, indicating effective transfer of reasoning patterns.
 - Increasing the number of RL mid-training steps generally led to higher rewards during subsequent RL post-training.
- **Data Efficiency**: RL mid-training demonstrated efficiency in improving accuracy with fewer total training tokens compared to solely scaling SFT.
- **Ablation with Qwen3-8B-Base**: Even for a strong base model like Qwen3-8B-Base, where SFT (raw or thinking) initially reduced performance, RL mid-training still increased average accuracy (from 0.3572 to 0.3660), demonstrating its value.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research addresses a fundamental limitation in the current paradigm of large language model training by demonstrating that incorporating desirable behaviors and explicit reasoning capabilities earlier in the training pipeline can lead to more capable and reliable models.

The **Self-Improving Pretraining** framework (Section 1) has the potential to produce models that are inherently safer, more factual, and generate higher-quality content directly from pretraining. By leveraging the expertise of stronger, post-trained models as both data rewriters and judges, the approach introduces a superior training signal that guides the policy model from its initial stages. This could reduce the reliance on extensive and costly post-training alignment efforts to correct undesirable behaviors, building foundational robustness from the ground up. The ability to learn to "steer away" from unsafe prefixes to safe suffixes, rather than merely filtering data, suggests a more nuanced and robust safety mechanism.

The **Thinking Mid-training** phase (Section 2) introduces a novel approach to cultivate reasoning abilities by weaving explicit "thoughts" into the pretraining data and optimizing their utility through reinforcement learning. This effectively bridges the gap between pretraining's focus on surface-level patterns and post-training's demand for complex reasoning. Models trained with this approach are not only better at reasoning immediately after mid-training but are also significantly better prepared to benefit from subsequent reasoning-intensive post-training. This could accelerate the development of LLMs with advanced cognitive abilities, moving towards more intelligent and adaptable systems.

Collectively, this work suggests a shift in LLM training strategy:
- **Increased Model Capabilities**: By integrating objectives like safety, factuality, quality, and reasoning earlier, the framework aims to produce models with stronger intrinsic capabilities, potentially reducing the need for extensive "fixes" during post-training.
- **Data Efficiency and Robustness**: The approach may offer a pathway for continued LLM improvement as pretraining encounters "data walls," by effectively leveraging compute to optimize incentive-based objectives with existing data.
- **Iterative Improvement**: The "self-improving" nature of using stronger models to train newer ones suggests an iterative cycle of model development where each generation can provide better supervision for the next.
- **Generalizability**: The framework is presented as generalizable, allowing for different LLM-as-judge prompts to optimize for various skills. The discussion highlights the potential to combine multiple objectives (safety, factuality, quality) and extend to other complex skills like Chain-of-Thought reasoning.

While acknowledging the computational cost of reinforcement learning compared to next-token prediction, the authors suggest this approach aligns with a long-term view that favors incentive-based objectives as compute power increases. The work contributes to the ongoing evolution of LLM training, offering concrete methods to instill critical human-aligned capabilities much earlier in the learning process.
