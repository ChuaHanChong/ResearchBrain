# Diagnose, Correct, and Learn from Manipulation Failures via Visual Symbols

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Beihang University, Southern University of Science and Technology, and Shanghai Jiao Tong University developed ViFailback, a framework for diagnosing, correcting, and learning from real-world robotic manipulation failures. It introduces explicit visual symbols for efficient data annotation and provides multimodal corrective guidance, resulting in up to a 23.8% increase in task success rates for a leading VLA model in real-world experiments.

## Problem

- Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models lack robust capabilities to diagnose, learn from, or recover from manipulation failures, especially under out-of-distribution conditions.
- Existing large-scale failure datasets are predominantly simulated, creating a 'sim-to-real gap' that limits their utility for real-world robotic applications.
- Annotating real-world robotic failure data, including complex failure categories and detailed corrective guidance, is time-consuming and labor-intensive with current methods.

## Method

- A framework named **ViFailback** was introduced, leveraging ==seven explicit visual symbols== (e.g., arrows, crosshairs, ON/OFF labels) to efficiently annotate real-world robotic manipulation failures and provide ==multimodal corrective guidance==.
- A dataset, **ViFailback**, comprising **5,202** real-world trajectories and **58,128** VQA pairs, and a benchmark, **ViFailback**-Bench (Lite and Hard versions), were constructed for fine-grained failure analysis.
- **ViFailback**-8B, a VLM finetuned on the **ViFailback** dataset, was integrated as an ==external supervisor== with a VLA model (π0.5) to deliver textual and ==visual symbol-based corrective actions== in real-world robot execution.

## Results

- **ViFailback-8B** achieved **93.70%** average accuracy on the closed-ended ViFailback-Bench Lite, a **+39.14%** performance gain over the best baseline model (Gemini-2.5-Pro).
- On the open-ended ViFailback-Bench Hard, **ViFailback-8B** reached **72.64%** average accuracy, outperforming its zero-shot counterpart by **+39.6%** and all baseline models.
- Integrating **ViFailback-8B** with a π0.5 VLA model in real-world robotic experiments led to an average increase in task success rates of **+20.6%** (Visual Symbols-Following) to **+23.8%** (Point-based Motion Control) across unseen manipulation tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Existing state-of-the-art VLMs exhibit significant limitations in robotic manipulation failure diagnosis and correction, particularly in open-ended reasoning and multi-step guidance generation.
> - Utilizing explicit visual symbols significantly streamlines the annotation of complex real-world failure data, making it more practical to collect and scale such datasets.
> - Multimodal corrective guidance, combining textual explanations with precise visual symbols, offers a more actionable and interpretable form of feedback for VLA models, enabling effective policy recovery.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has seen substantial advancements with the emergence of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which leverage large-scale datasets and imitation learning to enable robots to acquire diverse manipulation skills. These models have demonstrated capabilities in task planning, spatial reasoning, and trajectory prediction. However, a significant challenge persists: VLA models often struggle when encountering out-of-distribution (OOD) conditions or unexpected events in real-world environments, leading to manipulation failures. Current VLA models are typically unable to inherently diagnose, learn from, or recover from such failures.

Existing approaches to address robotic failures have explored several avenues. Some works focus on generating large-scale failure datasets, predominantly by injecting perturbations in simulation, to fine-tune Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for improved diagnosis and correction. This strategy is limited by the "sim-to-real gap," which can hinder its effectiveness in real-world scenarios. While some methods incorporate human-in-the-loop feedback for refining corrective instructions, these often face scalability issues. Other benchmarks for VLM reasoning in robotics assess "what to do" and "how to do it" but do not extensively cover "what went wrong" and "why."

Furthermore, while visual prompts have been explored to guide robot policies, such as through trajectory-conditioned models or semantic cues like keypoints and hand-drawn symbols, these primarily focus on initial guidance rather than real-time failure correction. The feedback provided by many current diagnostic or corrective VLMs remains largely textual, which can be insufficient for directly guiding robotic recovery due to the inherent fragility of instruction-following in VLA models.

This work addresses these gaps by introducing a framework designed to leverage real-world failure data, directly confronting the sim-to-real challenge. It distinguishes itself by employing explicit visual symbols for efficient annotation and for generating direct, actionable visual correction guidance, aiming to bridge the gap between failure diagnosis and effective real-time policy recovery in real-world robotic manipulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations guiding this research are:

1. **Address Limitations in Failure Diagnosis and Learning for VLA Models:** Despite progress, VLA models lack robust capabilities for diagnosing failures, understanding their root causes, and learning from these experiences. This limitation restricts their reliability and autonomy in open-world robotic manipulation tasks, where encountering unexpected conditions and failures is inevitable.
2. **Overcome the Sim-to-Real Gap in Failure Data:** Existing large-scale failure datasets are predominantly generated synthetically in simulation, leading to a "sim-to-real gap" that limits their generalization and utility for real-world robotic applications. There is a need for datasets derived from real-world robotic manipulation failures to foster more robust and generalizable learning.
3. **Enhance Efficiency of Real-World Failure Data Annotation:** Annotating real-world robotic failure data, especially for complex and abstract categories like task planning errors or detailed corrective guidance, is time-consuming and labor-intensive when relying solely on manual textual descriptions. A more efficient and scalable annotation methodology is required to make real-world failure data practical for VLM training.
4. **Develop Multimodal Corrective Guidance (Textual and Visual):** Current VLM feedback for failure correction is often restricted to textual instructions. This modality can be insufficient for direct and precise robotic action adjustments, as VLA models may struggle with fragile instruction-following. The motivation is to provide more direct and interpretable guidance through explicit visual symbols, complementing textual explanations, to facilitate more effective policy recovery.
5. **Establish a Comprehensive Benchmark for Failure Analysis:** A standardized benchmark is needed to rigorously evaluate the failure diagnosis and correction capabilities of VLMs in robotic manipulation. This benchmark should cover fine-grained aspects of failure analysis and support both closed-ended and open-ended evaluations to assess different levels of reasoning.
6. **Demonstrate Real-World Recovery from Failures:** Ultimately, the motivation is to demonstrate that a system trained on real-world failure data and capable of generating multimodal guidance can effectively assist VLA models in recovering from failures during actual robotic task execution, thereby improving overall task success rates and enabling true learning from errors.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces ViFailback, a framework designed for systematic annotation, diagnosis, and correction of real-world robotic manipulation failures. The methodology is structured around leveraging explicit visual symbols to enhance annotation efficiency and provide multimodal corrective guidance.

#### 3.1. Visual Symbols of ViFailback

To streamline the annotation of complex failure categories and facilitate precise corrective guidance, the framework utilizes seven distinct visual symbols, which can be drawn directly onto video frames. These symbols are categorized as follows:

- **Motion Symbols:**
 - **Colored Straight Arrow:** Represents 3D spatial movement. Colors (Red, Green, Blue) correspond to X (forward-backward), Y (left-right), and Z (up-down) axes, respectively. The arrow's orientation indicates direction, and multiple colors signify multi-axis movement.
 - **Semi-circular Arrow:** Indicates end-effector rotation (clockwise or counter-clockwise).
- **Spatial Relation Symbols:**
 - **Dual Crosshairs:** Linked by a dashed line, indicates a required alignment between two target objects.
 - **Crosshair:** Highlights an ideal object or area in the frame.
- **State Symbols:**
 - **ON/OFF Labels:** Indicates the desired state of the gripper (open or closed).
 - **Prohibition Icon:** Placed on the end-effector, signifies that the end-effector should halt.
 - **Rewind Icon:** Placed on an end-effector or object, indicates a requirement to return to a previous state.

During annotation, key elements of each symbol (category, start/end points, center, direction, color) are recorded to enable VLMs to learn visual symbol generation.

#### 3.2. Fine-Grained Task Definition

Failure analysis is decomposed into two components:

- **Failure Diagnosis:**
 - **Failure detection:** Binary classification (task completed/failed).
 - **Failure keyframe localization:** Identifying the keyframe where failure is impending.
 - **Failure subtask localization:** Identifying the specific subtask where failure initiated.
 - **Failure type identification:** Categorization into four types: Task planning (e.g., wrong object, subtask sequence error), Gripper 6d-pose (incorrect position/orientation), Gripper state (improper open/close), and Human intervention (external disruption).
 - **Failure reason:** Providing a detailed textual explanation of the root cause.
- **Corrective Action Guidance:**
 - **Low-level textual guidance:** Specific instructions for end-effector movement and rotation.
 - **High-level textual guidance:** Strategic advice (e.g., subtask plan reformulation).
 - **Visual guidance:** Overlaying visual symbols onto keyframes with integrated semantic information.

#### 3.3. Dataset and Benchmark Construction

- **Data Collection:** 5,202 real-world robotic manipulation trajectories and ego-centric videos were collected from 100 diverse tasks using an ALOHA dual-arm robot platform. This included 657 successful and 4,545 failed trajectories, gathered primarily through human teleoperation and supplemented by rollouts from a finetuned π0.5 VLA model.
- **Data Annotation Pipeline:** A multi-stage pipeline ensures efficiency and quality:
 1. **Basic Task Semantic Information Filling:** Annotators use a UI with sliders and buttons to complete failure diagnosis (detection, keyframe localization, etc.), leveraging subtask decompositions generated by Qwen2.5-Max.
 2. **Textual Guidance Choosing and Visual Symbols Drawing:** Annotators select predefined action correction options and draw visual symbols on keyframes to indicate corrective actions.
 3. **Open-Ended Description Generation and Refining:** Qwen3-VL-235B is prompted with all annotated information and visual symbols to generate high-level descriptions for failure reasoning and textual guidance, which are then manually verified and refined.
- **ViFailback Dataset:** The culmination of this process is a dataset containing 58,128 high-quality Visual Question Answering (VQA) pairs for failure diagnosis and correction.
- **ViFailback-Bench Benchmark:** This benchmark is derived from 500 trajectories across 22 distinct tasks and comprises two settings:
 - **ViFailback-Bench Lite:** Utilizes closed-ended VQA tasks to assess core failure diagnosis abilities and low-level corrective action guidance.
 - **ViFailback-Bench Hard:** Employs open-ended VQA tasks to probe failure reasoning and more complex, Chain-of-Thought (CoT) based guidance, requiring models to detect, localize, and then output guidance.

#### 3.4. Model Development and Real-World Application

- **ViFailback-8B VLM:** Qwen3-VL-8B was finetuned on the ViFailback dataset using LoRA, resulting in ViFailback-8B.
- **Real-World Robotic Experiments:** ViFailback-8B was integrated with a leading VLA model, π0.5, to serve as an external supervisor. The system detects failures, generates CoT-based diagnoses, and provides low-level textual and visual guidance (as code for drawing visual symbols). Two methods were devised for π0.5 to follow visual symbols:
 - **Visual Symbols-Following (VSF) Method:** A dataset of low-level motion trajectories annotated with visual symbols was used to finetune π0.5 to directly follow these symbols, involving masking irrelevant regions in observations.
 - **Point-based Motion Control (PMC) Method:** A low-level motion controller was used to drive the end-effector to target points indicated by visual symbols, augmented by GraspNet for grasp pose estimation when required.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several key findings and results demonstrating the effectiveness of the ViFailback framework, dataset, and the developed ViFailback-8B model.

#### 4.1. Overall Model Performance on ViFailback-Bench

Evaluation of 16 state-of-the-art models (proprietary and open-source, general and embodied VLMs) on ViFailback-Bench revealed that existing models exhibit limitations in robotic manipulation failure diagnosis and correction, particularly in open-ended settings. The best baseline performance was 54.64% accuracy in the closed-ended (Lite) setting (Gemini-2.5-Pro) and 40.00% in the open-ended (Hard) setting (GPT-4o).

#### 4.2. Closed-Ended Performance Analysis (ViFailback-Bench Lite)

On ViFailback-Bench Lite, ViFailback-8B achieved a 93.70% average accuracy across tasks such as failure detection, keyframe localization, subtask localization, failure type identification, and low-level avoidance/correction. This represented a 39.14% performance gain over the best-performing baseline, Gemini-2.5-Pro (54.64%). While baselines showed reasonable accuracy for failure detection, their performance significantly declined in critical tasks like failure localization and low-level guidance.

#### 4.3. Open-Ended Performance Analysis (ViFailback-Bench Hard)

For ViFailback-Bench Hard, ViFailback-8B achieved an average accuracy of 72.64%. This demonstrated a 39.6% performance gain over its zero-shot counterpart (Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct, 33.04%) and significantly outperformed all baseline models. GPT-4o achieved the best baseline performance at 40.00%. Notably, open-source models like Qwen3-VL-32B-Instruct even surpassed proprietary models in the "Failure Reason" task (60.53%). The results also indicated that all models experienced substantial performance drops when multi-step reasoning was required (e.g., CoT-based low-level guidance), suggesting a general struggle for current VLMs in generating sequential guidance for robotic failures.

#### 4.4. Performance Scales with Data Size

Experiments on scaling the training data for ViFailback-8B, from 1,200 to 4,702 trajectories, showed consistent performance improvements across most VQA types. Significant gains were observed even with limited data in Lite tasks, while Hard tasks, which involve VQA formats less common in base models' pre-training, also improved. The model's ability to generate visual symbol codes scaled consistently with training data volume, reaching 38.73% accuracy. The observed trend indicated that performance had not yet saturated, suggesting potential for further improvements with additional data. Each additional person-hour of annotation yielded an average 0.64% accuracy improvement.

#### 4.5. Application in Real-World Manipulation

Real-world experiments were conducted on three unseen manipulation tasks (PlaceOne, PlaceTwo, Pull&Place) using an ALOHA dual-arm robot. ViFailback-8B was integrated with the π0.5 VLA model as an external supervisor, providing corrective textual and visual guidance upon failure detection.

- **Baseline Comparison:** The baseline π0.5 models (trained with expert demonstrations, with or without visual symbols-following data) achieved average success rates of 50.8% and 52.4%, respectively, showing no significant difference in their original task performance.
- **ViFailback Integration:** When ViFailback-8B was integrated:
 - The π0.5 model with the VSF method (visual symbols-following data) achieved a 73.0% average success rate, an improvement of 20.6% over its baseline.
 - The π0.5 model with the PMC method (point-based motion control) achieved a 74.6% average success rate, an improvement of 23.8% over its baseline.
- These results demonstrate that ViFailback-8B can effectively assist VLA models in recovering from failures in real-world manipulation tasks, leading to measurable improvements in task success rates. The PMC method slightly outperformed VSF, potentially due to the limited amount of visual symbols-following data for end-to-end policy learning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in this paper carries several significant implications and potential impacts on the field of robotic manipulation and VLM development:

1. **Advancement in Real-World Robotic Robustness:** By providing a framework, dataset, and model for diagnosing and correcting manipulation failures in real-world settings, this research addresses a critical limitation of current VLA models. Enabling robots to identify and recover from errors autonomously is a step towards more robust, reliable, and deployable robotic systems in unstructured and dynamic environments.
2. **Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap for Failure Data:** The creation of the ViFailback dataset, a large-scale collection of real-world manipulation failure trajectories and VQA pairs, directly confronts the sim-to-real gap. This dataset offers a valuable resource for training and evaluating VLMs on authentic failure modes, which are often difficult to accurately synthesize in simulation.
3. **Enhanced Efficiency in Data Annotation:** The introduction of explicit visual symbols for annotation significantly streamlines the process of labeling complex robotic failure data. This semi-automated approach makes it more feasible to scale up real-world failure data collection, which is essential for developing generalizable failure recovery capabilities.
4. **Multimodal and Actionable Corrective Guidance:** The framework's ability to generate both textual and direct visual symbol guidance provides a more interpretable and actionable form of feedback for VLA models. Visual symbols offer a precise, low-level supervisory signal that can guide policy adjustments in real-time, potentially overcoming the ambiguity and fragility of purely language-based instructions.
5. **Comprehensive VLM Evaluation Benchmark:** The ViFailback-Bench, with its Lite and Hard versions, establishes a standardized and fine-grained benchmark for evaluating VLM capabilities in robotic failure diagnosis and correction. This will facilitate systematic progress and comparison across different VLM architectures and training methodologies.
6. **Enabling Learning from Failures:** The successful integration of ViFailback-8B with a VLA model to achieve real-world failure recovery demonstrates a concrete mechanism for policies to "learn" from their mistakes. This moves beyond merely detecting failures to actively correcting them and, in the long term, could lead to policies that are inherently more adaptable and resilient.
7. **Future Research Directions:** The work highlights the potential of utilizing the action distribution of failure trajectories as a valuable source of information, pointing towards future research avenues that could further integrate learning signals from various aspects of failure data.

In summary, this research contributes to making robotic manipulation more resilient and autonomous by providing practical tools and methodologies for understanding, correcting, and ultimately learning from real-world failures.
