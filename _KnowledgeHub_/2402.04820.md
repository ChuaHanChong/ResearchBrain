# Kinematic Motion Retargeting for Contact-Rich Anthropomorphic Manipulations

> [!summary] Summary
> A kinematic retargeting framework that maps contact-rich human hand-object manipulations onto morphologically diverse target hands by treating time-varying contact areas as the transfer medium. Contact transfer is posed as non-isometric shape matching over an atlas of logarithmic-map charts seeded by artist-drawn axial curves, then markers plus transferred contacts drive a multi-stage IK optimization and spline fit. Thirty demonstrations across five hands and six objects validate it.

## Problem

- Existing hand mocap data is plentiful, but joint mapping and keypoint IK retargeting produce contact loss and motion misalignment artifacts that are hard to repair afterwards.
- Single point contacts suffice at full body scale but insufficiently model hand-object interaction, while target hands vary widely in finger count, proportions, and degrees of freedom.
- Non-isometric shape matching methods target globally smooth media like textures, are highly sensitive to landmark placement, and fail at exact point-to-point transfer across diverse hand geometries.

## Method

- ==Atlas-based local shape matching==: contacts are parameterized by disjoint ==logarithmic-map charts==, each anchored to an artist-annotated axial curve landmark, and reconstructed on the target hand by ==exponential-map geodesic tracing==.
- Two scaling hyperparameters, ==expmap scale== and ==axial geodesic length==, deform transferred contact distributions to absorb differences in finger girth, palm shape, and finger length across hands.
- Multi-stage motion synthesis: per-frame independent ==IK== minimizing marker, contact-distance, contact-normal, table-SDF, and pose-prior penalties via ==MMA==, then acceleration-threshold refinement and ==cubic B-spline fitting== for C2 continuity.

## Results

- **Thirty** retargets, **five** hands by **six** objects from GRAB, ran with one uniform parameter set; hand-object, self, and table intersection volumes stayed low without explicit penetration resolution.
- Against fingertip keypoint tracking and DexPilot functional pose equivalence on the **22-DOF** Allegro Hand, both baselines, even given ground-truth root poses, failed to reach contact and misaligned motion.
- Ablations: zeroing the contact weight leaves the hand failing to conform to the doorknob, and skipping root pre-conditioning causes root drift; splines used under **12%** of frames as control points.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Contact areas act as a cheap proxy for physical plausibility: matching them implicitly suppresses interpenetration and yields natural grasps without running a dynamics simulator.
> - Solving each frame independently, rather than conditioning on the previous pose, avoids error accumulation and local minima over long sequences; smoothness is restored later by splines.
> - Exact contact matching breaks down when target morphology diverges far from human, so very large or low-DOF hands may need a different problem formulation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper addresses a persistent challenge in computer graphics, robotics, and human-computer interaction: the effective transfer (retargeting) of captured human motion data to diverse target embodiments. While advancements in motion capture (mocap) technology have enabled the collection of highly detailed hand motion data, its utility is often limited by the inability to accurately apply it to target hands that differ significantly from the demonstrator's hand. These differences can include variations in geometry, number of degrees of freedom (DOFs), or finger count.

Current motion retargeting methods for full-body animation often rely on joint-based mappings or learning-based approaches, sometimes augmented with physics simulation to handle environmental and self-contact. However, the complexity of hand-object interactions, characterized by numerous and frequently changing contact points, presents a more intricate problem than typical full-body contacts. Existing hand-specific retargeting techniques include direct joint mapping, keypoint-based inverse kinematics (IK), inverse dynamics, and functional pose equivalence. While these approaches can adapt to more divergent hands, they often generate artifacts such as motion misalignment or lack of contact, which are challenging to rectify in post-processing. Physics-based simulation is used to improve plausibility in robotic contexts, but poor kinematic retargeting upstream can lead to learning failures downstream. The paper argues that a reliable upstream kinematic solution is necessary.

A critical component for robust retargeting, especially for highly varied target geometries, is shape matching. Traditional isometric shape matching, which assumes preserved geodesic distances, is unsuitable for the wide morphological variations seen in different hand designs (e.g., human, robot, alien). Non-isometric shape matching relaxes this assumption but increases problem difficulty. Existing non-isometric methods often rely on user-provided landmarks or curves, primarily for global media transfer (e.g., textures). However, these methods can be sensitive to landmark placement and may not provide the precision required for point-to-point contact transfer. Fully automated non-isometric methods struggle with the immense variety and deformable nature of hand models.

The paper identifies contact-driven grasp synthesis as a related field. While contact points are fundamental to grasp quality metrics, many approaches simplify interactions to single points or focus on static grasps. Recent research has shown increasing interest in exploiting dense contact *areas* for generating and optimizing poses. However, applying these contact patch-driven strategies to full manipulation trajectories has been hindered by the difficulty of transferring dense, complex, and constantly changing contact areas meaningfully between different hands.

This research aims to bridge these gaps by presenting a framework that specifically leverages dense contact areas and a novel non-isometric shape matching process to enable robust kinematic retargeting of contact-rich hand manipulations. It positions itself as providing a standardized, reliable, and simple solution for accurately transferring existing human hand motion data to a broad range of target hands, addressing bottlenecks in both media production and robot learning, and serving as a robust first pass for subsequent dynamic simulations.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a simple yet effective framework for kinematically retargeting complex human hand-object manipulations, characterized by rich contact, to a diverse range of target hands. These target hands can vary significantly in geometry, kinematic structure, and number of fingers.

The motivation behind this work stems from several challenges and bottlenecks in current practices:
- **Limited Utility of Motion Capture Data:** Despite the increasing ease of obtaining high-fidelity hand motion capture data, including complex grasps, its direct applicability to different target characters or robots is limited. This is because the target embodiment rarely matches the demonstrator's, necessitating retargeting.
- **Prevalence of Retargeting Artifacts:** Existing retargeting processes frequently produce undesirable artifacts such as motion misalignment or a complete loss of contact. These errors are often difficult and time-consuming to correct in post-production, particularly when dealing with non-human-like target hands with atypical finger proportions, DOFs, or finger counts.
- **Bottleneck in Creative Industries:** In film and game production pipelines, animators dedicate substantial time to manually adapting common manipulations to various character rigs, which is an inefficient and resource-intensive process. A standardized and reliable approach could significantly reduce this workload.
- **Data Sparsity in Robot Learning:** For robot learning, the difficulty in adapting existing human manipulation data to different robot hands exacerbates the data sparsity problem. This, in turn, constrains the feasibility and effectiveness of supervised and semi-supervised machine learning algorithms for robotic dexterous manipulation. A method for repurposing easily collectible human data for diverse robot hands is critically needed.
- **Lack of Standardized Solutions:** The research identifies a clear absence of a standardized and reliable method for adapting existing contact-rich hand motion data, particularly data originally collected for a different hand, across a broad spectrum of target manipulators.

To address these motivations, the paper sets forth the following specific technical contributions:
- **Development of a Local Shape Matching Algorithm:** A novel algorithm is introduced for robustly transferring time-series contact data. This algorithm formulates the mapping of contact distributions as a non-isometric shape matching problem, exploiting contact areas through dense correspondences between discretized point sets. The approach uses an atlas of coordinate charts (logmaps) to enable predictable and robust transfer of contact data over full manipulations, with an intuitive annotation scheme requiring relatively few artist inputs.
- **Creation of a Multi-Stage Optimization Pipeline:** A straightforward, yet effective, multi-stage optimization pipeline is designed to compute the retargeted motion. This pipeline progressively estimates, refines, and fits the final target hand trajectory using inverse kinematics (IK), integrating surface contact and marker data.

The overarching design philosophy for this framework emphasizes standardization, simplicity, and reliability. By focusing on kinematics rather than dynamics, the authors aim to achieve a robust solution that can either serve as a complete, high-quality retargeting method or provide a reliable first pass for further processing, such as physics simulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework for kinematic motion retargeting is structured as a five-step pipeline designed to transfer contact-rich hand-object manipulations from a source hand to a target hand.

#### 3.1. Inputs

The method requires specific inputs for both the source motion and the target hand:
- **Source Motion Data:**
 - Accurate 3D meshes of the original object and the source hand.
 - A set of dense, per-frame contact annotations on either the object or the source hand mesh. These contacts are stored as barycentric coordinates for triangulation-agnostic robustness.
 - A complete sequence of frames defining the manipulation motion.
 - The GRAB dataset is cited as a suitable source for this data.
- **Target Hand Data:**
 - A skeleton-driven target hand mesh.
 - A one-time artist annotation consisting of:
 - Corresponding virtual markers on both the source and target hands. These can be single points or area-based, supporting heterogeneous mappings.
 - Corresponding axial curves on both the source and target hands, used for contact transfer via local charts.
 - Target hands are assumed to be manifold, or can be approximated with a manifold wrapper (e.g., for the Allegro Hand).

#### 3.2. Dense Contact Pairing

If only one dense set of contact annotations (e.g., on the object or source hand) is available, the method generates corresponding contact points on the opposite mesh (hand or object) through a raycasting process. Rays are traced from existing contact locations along element normals to intersect the opposite mesh. Points failing to intersect or exceeding an `epsilon`-metric distance are discarded.

#### 3.3. Hand Shape Matching (Novel Contribution)

This is a core component, leveraging artist annotations to transfer dynamic contact distributions.
- **Virtual Marker Alignment:** Virtual markers, provided as a one-time artist annotation, define arbitrary collections of fixed corresponding mesh points. These markers assist with pose computation, particularly when contact data is sparse. They can be single points (traditional keypoint IK) or area-based, allowing for heterogeneous mappings (e.g., one source point to a target area) to model uncertainty or divergent link lengths.
- **Contact Alignment:** This constitutes the novel shape matching process for dynamic contact distributions.
 - **Formulation:** Bulk contact transfer is formulated as an intrinsic non-isometric shape matching problem governed by an atlas of multiple coordinate charts. The source hand manifold (Ω_S) is covered by `M` disjoint charts (U_i), each defined by a logarithmic map (logmap) function `ψ_i` that transforms contacts into local coordinates (radius `r_c`, angle `θ_c`) relative to an origin `q`. The target manifold (Ω_T) has a corresponding atlas defined by inverse exponential maps (`ψ_i^-1`), reconstructing contacts from corresponding origins.
 - **Intrinsic Properties:** The use of Laplacians and geodesics means the method relies on intrinsic (connectivity-based) rather than extrinsic (embedding-based) properties, making it robust to geometric variations and pose-induced deformations.
 - **Chart Determination:** Each chart `U_i` on Ω_S is determined by a landmark (designated as the logmap origin `q`). The closest landmark to each contact point dictates which chart it belongs to, effectively creating a geodesic Voronoi segmentation.
 - **Axial Curves:** Instead of individual landmarks, artist-annotated axial curves (a finite set of points, connecting geodesics, and turning angles) are used. These simplify annotation, enable generation of multiple landmarks at once, and allow consistent orientation of logmap zero angles.
 - **Flexibility and Customization:**
 - **`λ_s` (expmap scaling):** An exponential map scaling metric `λ_s(θ)` is introduced to permit "deforming" contact distributions from Ω_S into arbitrary shapes on Ω_T, addressing variations in finger/palm shape or girth. This breaks isometry but allows for semantically meaningful transfer.
 - **`λ_a` (axial curve length scaling):** A parameter `λ_a` is used to extend or contract the length of geodesics connecting axial curve points on Ω_T, addressing finger length variations.
 - These parameters, combined with the atlas model, enable fine-grained adaptation of contact distributions across diverse geometries.
 - **Handling Finger Count Differences:** Charts corresponding to unused fingers on the target hand can be set to empty, or charts can extend to cover regions of additional fingers, maintaining a full cover of Ω_T.

#### 3.4. Motion Retargeting (Optimization Pipeline)

The retargeted motion is synthesized in three stages using a common objective formulation (Eq. 3) for computing per-frame joint DOF vectors `θ_f*`:
`θ_f* = arg min_θ (λ_m Γ_M + λ_c Γ_C + λ_t Γ_T + λ_j Γ_J)` subject to joint limits `θ_L ≤ θ ≤ θ_U`.
- **Penalty Terms:**
 - `Γ_M` (Marker penalty): L2 distance between corresponding virtual markers on the source and target hands.
 - `Γ_C` (Contact penalty): Sum of L2 distances between corresponding contact points and a term penalizing deviation from surface normal inversion at contacts (`λ_cd Γ_CD,c + λ_cn Γ_CN,c`).
 - `Γ_T` (Table penalty): Penalizes hand-table intersection using a signed distance function (SDF) of the table evaluated at target hand sampling points (vertices or manifold wrapper).
 - `Γ_J` (Prior penalty): Regularizes joint DOFs against a default rest pose or previous keyed value.
- **Solver:** The Method of Moving Asymptotes (MMA), a local gradient-based solver from the NLOPT library, is used.
- **Stages of Motion Reconstruction:**
 1. **Initial Trajectory Estimation:** An optimal initial trajectory (`Θ*`) is estimated per frame. This is done independently for each frame to mitigate error buildup over long sequences, which is a departure from methods that condition on previous poses. The process has two passes: first solving only for the root joint position, then for the full pose using the root estimate as a seed.
 2. **Trajectory Refinement:** The initial trajectory is refined to improve temporal consistency by iteratively identifying and removing frames where DOF accelerations exceed a threshold (`E_acc`). Violating frames are replaced by linear interpolations between valid neighbors, and the pose is re-solved. Pre-processing with low-pass and peak removal filters helps reduce initial violations and computation time.
 3. **Spline Fitting:** Cubic B-splines are fitted to the refined DOF trajectories, ensuring C2 continuity. A fixed number of control points are used, and their values and temporal locations are solved simultaneously via least squares pseudo-inverse approximation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The validation of the framework involved thirty demonstrations across five distinct hand shapes (source MANO hand, alternate human hand, witch hand, alien hand, custom prosthetic hand, and Allegro Hand) and six different object manipulation motions (doorknob, apple, flashlight, hammer, phone, stapler).
- **Standardized Application:** A uniform set of optimization hyperparameters, transfer coefficients, and control points was used across all hands and motions, without requiring fine-tuning for individual cases. This demonstrates the framework's standardization and simplicity.
- **Low Intersection Volumes:** Despite not explicitly performing hand-object or self-intersection resolution during optimization, the method produced low intersection volumes (hand-object, self, and table intersection), as quantified by percentage of hand volume. This suggests that the dense contact areas implicitly encode natural grasp states and provide a viable approximation for physical motion and intersection minimization in the absence of a full physics simulation.
- **High-Quality Trajectories and Generalization:** The retargeted trajectories were found to be of generally high quality. The method demonstrated robustness and predictability, successfully mapping motions of varying complexity without requiring prior knowledge of contact distributions or customization of landmarks for individual distributions.
- **Novel Extensions:**
 - **Design Choice Visualization:** The framework enables visualizing the impact of design parameters on manipulation tasks. Examples include:
 - Testing different finger lengths to determine reachability (e.g., thumb reaching a flashlight switch, Figure 11).
 - Exploring alternate finger mappings for hands with different finger counts, such as creating a "Spock"-like configuration on a three-fingered alien hand by mapping multiple human finger contacts to a single alien finger (Figure 12).
 - Evaluating the impact of adding DOFs (e.g., 1-DOF vs. 2-DOF knuckle joints on a prosthetic hand) on grasp naturalness and dexterity (Figure 17).
 - **Object Substitution:** The method can retarget generic manipulations to different objects. This is achieved by generating new contact sets on the target object (e.g., via raytracing from the original object, diffusion models, or atlas-based transfer) and then applying the standard retargeting pipeline. Examples include adapting an "apple handoff" to a potato or a geometrically complex gargoyle figurine (Figures 13 and 14).
- **Baseline Comparisons:** The method was compared against two contact-free retargeting approaches commonly used in teleoperation: fingertip keypoint tracking and functional pose equivalence (DexPilot).
 - Results demonstrated that these contact-free methods visibly struggled to maintain hand-object contact and exhibited non-trivial motion misalignment artifacts, even when augmented with full source trajectory information. This highlights the critical importance of contact information for robust and accurate retargeting of interaction-rich manipulations (Figure 15).
- **Hand-Specific Observations:**
 - The three-fingered alien hand was surprisingly easy to retarget due to its thick size limiting pose uncertainty, despite slightly higher self-intersection (due to mapping multiple human fingers to fewer, thicker alien fingers).
 - The prosthesis exhibited noisier wrist position estimates due to its thin profile and higher hand-object intersection for certain tasks (hammer, stapler) due to small size and limited DOFs. The lack of a torsional MCP DOF on the prosthesis, for instance, caused the index finger to penetrate the hammer handle rather than wrapping around it (Figure 16).
 - The witch and Allegro hands were the most challenging due to unusually long finger lengths, identified as a significant factor in retargeting complexity.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework that offers a reliable, standardized, and user-friendly solution to the complex problem of kinematic motion retargeting for contact-rich anthropomorphic manipulations. Its significance and potential impact span several domains:

- **Addressing Bottlenecks in Media Production:** For film, animation, and game development, the method can significantly reduce the manual effort and animator time currently spent customizing common manipulations to different character rigs. By providing a predictable and robust transfer mechanism, it allows for more efficient content creation and character animation, enabling artists to specify correspondences with relatively few inputs.
- **Advancing Robot Learning and Design:**
 - **Data Augmentation for Robot Learning:** The framework offers a solution to the data sparsity problem in robot learning by enabling the adaptation of easily collectible human manipulation data to diverse robot hands. This provides a reliable source of "expert trajectories" for learning from demonstration (LfD), reinforcement learning, and physics-based simulations, where poor upstream kinematic retargeting can otherwise lead to significant learning failures.
 - **Robot Hand Design and Prototyping:** The "Design Choice Visualization" extension provides a powerful tool for rapidly prototyping and evaluating new robot hand designs. Engineers and designers can visualize the impact of different finger lengths, DOF counts (e.g., number of motors, types of joints), and kinematic structures on manipulation tasks *over full trajectories*. This allows for task-centric dexterity evaluation and informed decisions on under-actuation or selecting the optimal hand for a specific task set, potentially reducing development costs and time.
- **Generalization of Shape Matching and Data Transfer:** The core shape matching approach, based on an atlas of coordinate charts, is demonstrated to be generalizable beyond hand contacts. It enables the precise definition and transfer of arbitrary local data between different shapes, suggesting applications in modeling complex interactions between deformable surfaces, adaptable manipulation planning, or surface animation retargeting. This expands the utility of intrinsic geometry-based methods for practical applications.
- **Improved Plausibility and Reliability:** By explicitly incorporating dense contact information, the method achieves significantly more plausible and artifact-free results compared to contact-free retargeting approaches. This means the generated motions are more likely to look natural and physically correct, even without a full dynamics simulation, serving as a high-quality kinematic foundation.
- **Foundation for Future Work:** The paper concludes by outlining natural next steps, including integrating contact dynamics (requiring better real-world force/tactile data) and exploring the utility of contact information in reinforcement and imitation learning to improve policy learning, reduce convergence times, and encourage natural behaviors. Ultimately, it aims towards enabling real-world human-to-robot transfer of complex manipulations through online perception of contact data.

In summary, this research provides a practical, robust, and generalizable framework for a challenging problem, offering immediate benefits to creative industries and opening new avenues for research and development in robotics and computational design.
