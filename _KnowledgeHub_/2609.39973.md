# EWAM: Emergent Depth-Wise Specialization in a Unified Embodied Model -- From Semantic Understanding through Visual Foresight to Action

> [!summary] Summary
> EWAM is a unified embodied model joining a Qwen3-VL vision-language expert, a Wan2.2 video expert, and an action expert through asymmetric attention where only action tokens read all streams. Without layer supervision, action attention shifts with depth from semantics to predicted futures to action self-attention, a handoff shown causal by masking, reaching 72.1% RoboTwin clean-to-random and 98.8% LIBERO.

## Problem

- VLA policies inherit rich semantics from vision-language pretraining but get little explicit supervision of physical dynamics, while world-action models predict futures without guaranteeing semantic reasoning or fine control.
- Hybrid systems like π0.7 and Cortex 2.0 bolt a separately run world model or planner onto a policy through one-way interfaces, adding capability without structured cooperation inside the action computation.
- Attention maps show even multi-expert models collapse to single-source bias: π0.5 action queries read VLM tokens, Fast-WAM reads video tokens, and Motus stays dominated by its frozen understanding expert.

## Method

- Action-centric ==asymmetric joint attention==: action queries attend to vision-language, video, and action keys at all 30 layer-aligned blocks, while VL and video streams attend only within themselves, keeping expert roles distinct.
- ==Joint video-action flow matching== with independently sampled noise levels denoises future-video latents and action chunks concurrently in one forward pass, so evolving predicted-future features feed action computation directly.
- Subtask-aware post-training adds an ==autoregressive subtask-text decoder== and a ==phase-index regression head== on action features; human egocentric pretraining uses a ==camera-relative 16-D wrist-displacement action space== across 2,084 hours.

## Results

- VL takes **75.4%** of action attention in layers 0-9, action self-attention **59.5%** in layers 20-29; masking predicted-future keys in layers 10-19 collapses success from **92.80% to 3.55%**; donor future injection drops success **100% to 16%**.
- RoboTwin 2.0 clean-to-random **72.1%** beats GigaBrain-0.7 by **4.2** and π0.5 by **26.1 points**; in-domain average **92.9%** and LIBERO **98.8%**, with highest success on all seven real-robot tasks.
- Human pretraining lifts held-out Aloha-Agilex-2 transfer from **40.1% to 66.9%**; egocentric co-training raises real-robot robustness **10% to 60%**; subtask supervision lifts ranking-and-stacking **1.5% to 16.0%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Giving one stream exclusive cross-modal read access, rather than full symmetric attention, makes routing measurable and lets a semantic-to-foresight-to-action ordering self-organize without layer-wise supervision.
> - Attention maps alone are weak evidence: inference-time masking and counterfactual feature injection reveal which pathways are causally load-bearing, here showing predicted-future features steer arm selection and targeting.
> - A camera-relative end-effector action frame lets cheap human egocentric video supervise robot policies directly, but pouring failures trace to cup-alignment precision, so robustness gains do not remove fine-control bottlenecks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Overview

EWAM is a robot-control model built around a coordination problem. To follow an instruction, a policy must understand the current scene, anticipate how it may change, and produce precise movement. Recognizing objects or predicting plausible video is not enough. The authors argue that adding semantic and predictive modules does not guarantee that a policy will use them cooperatively, because action computation can remain concentrated on one expert. EWAM lets the action stream read both experts throughout the network. Without explicit layer-wise supervision, it learns a gradual handoff from understanding to foresight to action.

### 2. One action stream, three sources

The design makes action tokens the shared reader. At each layer they can attend to vision-language, video, and action tokens, while vision-language and video queries remain in their own streams. The authors call this asymmetric attention. It preserves the distinct predictive and semantic roles of the two perceptual experts and makes action the integration site.

Video and action are trained together. Predicted-future features can therefore inform action generation within the same forward process, rather than only arriving as a separately generated visual prompt.

### 3. A handoff the model learns

Attention allocation is the share of action-query attention assigned to each source. It measures where action queries retrieve information. It does not, by itself, prove what the representations encode or give a complete causal account.

In the 30-layer model, vision-language tokens dominate shallow layers (0–9), and action self-attention dominates deep layers (20–29). The middle is mixed rather than a discrete switch: vision-language 36.6%, video 24.3%, action 39.1%. Within the visual sources, predicted future frames overtake the current image around layer 13.

The authors measured this across 50 RoboTwin 2.0 tasks. The raw handoff appears in all 50 tasks. After adjusting for different numbers of tokens per source, it remains in 49 of 50. The profiles are also stable across denoising steps (mean pairwise correlation 0.998).

(a) Layer-resolved attention to condition-frame, future-frame, VL-image, and VL-text sources on ADJUST BOTTLE. (b) Attention mass (%) by transformer layer for VL-image and future-frame sources, macro-averaged over one sequence from each of 50 tasks; the curves cross around layer 13.

The mask alone does not account for the entire pattern. A fully bidirectional-mask variant also develops a coarse shallow-to-deep organization. However, it lacks the clear shallow reliance on the observed image and its later release. The authors' narrower claim is that the asymmetric mask preserves the perceptual experts' distinct roles and makes the visual handoff interpretable.

### 4. Does the future matter?

Two inference-time interventions test whether the policy relies on these features.

**Masking.** In one fixed robot-pretrained checkpoint, the authors masked intermediate future-frame keys. This cuts RoboTwin randomized success from 92.80% to 3.55%. Masking shallow vision-language keys cuts it to 76.53%.

**Counterfactual injection.** For each task, the authors cache future-frame features from a successful same-task donor rollout. They inject these in place of the target's own features at layers 10–19 during the first three policy calls. The target's condition-frame, vision-language, state, and action representations are left intact. Across five tasks, success falls from 25/25 to 4/25. This indicates sensitivity to the *content* of those features, not just the presence of a future-feature pathway.

Success without and with donor future-feature injection for ADJUST BOTTLE, PLACE EMPTY CUP, STACK BLOCKS TWO, CLICK BELL, and GRAB ROLLER: 25/25 overall without injection versus 4/25 with injection. On ADJUST BOTTLE, left-facing targets recover (2/2) and right-facing targets fail (0/3).

### 5. From forecast to control

In this tested model, the future stream carries intermediate features that the action policy uses. Its role extends beyond an auxiliary video target. EWAM's contribution is a learned, gradual attention handoff rather than a hard-routed pipeline. The main boundary is that attention measurements plus selected interventions characterize information routing; they are not a complete causal decomposition of the computation.
