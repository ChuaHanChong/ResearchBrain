# Goedel-Prover: A Frontier Model for Open-Source Automated Theorem Proving

> [!summary] Summary
> Goedel-Prover, from Princeton Language and Intelligence, presents an automated theorem prover for Lean 4, achieving 57.6% Pass@32 on miniF2F and solving 7 problems on PutnamBench, by generating 1.64 million formal statements and over 800K proofs through an expert iteration process. The work also fully open-sources all codes, models, and datasets, including nearly 30,000 newly solved Lean Workbook problems.

## Problem

- The scarcity of high-quality, large-scale formalized mathematical data and proofs hinders the training of Large Language Models (LLMs) for automated theorem proving.
- Traditional automated theorem proving (ATP) methods struggle with scalability to complex theorems and the generation of human-readable proofs.
- Manually creating machine-verifiable proofs in formal proof assistants like Lean 4 is labor-intensive and limits the scope of formal mathematics.

## Method

- Developed a two-stage data generation pipeline: large-scale ==statement formalization== and ==expert iteration== for proof generation.
- Generated 1.64 million ==formal statements== by training two distinct ==formalizers== (based on Qwen2.5-Coder-32B) on different informal-to-formal datasets to enhance diversity and filter for quality.
- Utilized an ==8-iteration expert iteration loop==, starting with DeepSeek-Prover-V1.5-RL, to cumulatively generate over 800K verified proofs through ==supervised fine-tuning== of successive provers.

## Results

- Goedel-Prover-SFT achieved **57.6% Pass@32** on the miniF2F benchmark, an absolute **7.6%** improvement over the previous open-source leader, DeepSeek-Prover-V1.5-RL.
- The model successfully solved **7 out of 644** problems on PutnamBench, securing the **#1 position** on the leaderboard for this challenging competition mathematics benchmark.
- Goedel-Prover cumulatively solved **29.7K** problems in the Lean Workbook (including Lean Workbook Plus), nearly doubling the previously available open-sourced proofs and providing a substantial new resource.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Increasing the scale of formal statements and proof data consistently improves model performance on theorem proving benchmarks.
> - Training the prover on diverse formalization styles, derived from different sources, enhances the model's generalization and reasoning abilities.
> - Reinforcement learning methods like DPO and GRPO, while showing initial gains, can lead to 'reward hacking' (e.g., excessively verbose proofs) and reduced output diversity, diminishing gains at higher inference-time compute compared to supervised fine-tuning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the forefront of **Automated Theorem Proving (ATP)**, specifically leveraging the transformative power of **Large Language Models (LLMs)** within formal proof assistants like Lean 4. Historically, ATP has been dominated by symbolic AI approaches, utilizing first-order logic, decision procedures (e.g., SMT solvers like Z3), and sophisticated search algorithms (e.g., E, Vampire). While effective for certain classes of problems, these traditional methods often struggle with scalability to complex theorems and the generation of human-readable proofs.

The landscape has significantly shifted with the advent of powerful LLMs. Pioneering work by Polu & Sutskever (2020) demonstrated the potential of generative language models for theorem proving in formal systems. Subsequent research has explored various facets, including lemma retrieval, Monte Carlo tree search for proof discovery, and natural language reasoning. A particularly influential technique, **expert iteration** (Polu et al., 2023), has emerged as a powerful paradigm for self-improvement in provers, iteratively enhancing models by incorporating newly discovered proofs into their training data. Goedel-Prover explicitly adopts and builds upon this expert iteration framework.

A critical bottleneck in training LLMs for formal theorem proving is the **scarcity of high-quality, large-scale formalized mathematical data**. While extensive informal math problem datasets exist (e.g., Numina, MATH, GSM8K), their formal counterparts are scarce and labor-intensive to produce. This paper directly addresses this challenge through **autoformalization**, the process of automatically translating informal mathematical statements into formal ones, a strategy also pursued by models like DeepSeek-Prover (Xin et al., 2024a) and InternLM2.5-StepProver (Wu et al., 2024).

Goedel-Prover distinguishes itself within this landscape by:
- **Scale of Autoformalization:** Generating an unprecedented 1.64 million formal statements, significantly expanding the available training data.
- **Diverse Formalization:** Employing two distinct formalizers trained on different sources to enhance the diversity of formalized statements, a novel contribution.
- **Whole-Proof Generation:** Focusing on generating complete proofs directly, rather than step-wise, which aims for lower latency and potentially more efficient resource use during inference. This contrasts with some step-wise provers like InternLM2.5-StepProver.
- **Open-Source Commitment:** A strong emphasis on fully open-sourcing all components (code, models, datasets, new proofs), which is crucial for reproducibility and accelerating future research in the field. This directly benefits the broader community by making frontier models and data accessible, contrasting with some proprietary datasets or models.
- **Benchmarking Performance:** Directly challenging and surpassing previous state-of-the-art models like DeepSeek-Prover-V1.5 (both SFT and RL versions), TheoremLamma, and InternLM-Math-Plus on established benchmarks like miniF2F and PutnamBench.

In essence, Goedel-Prover advances the frontier of AI in formal mathematics by tackling the data scarcity problem at scale, refining self-improvement methodologies, and contributing significantly to the open-source community, thereby setting a new benchmark for automated formal proof generation using LLMs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of the Goedel-Prover project is to develop and open-source a state-of-the-art language model capable of **automated formal proof generation for mathematical problems**, specifically in the Lean 4 proof assistant.

The primary motivation stems from a critical challenge in the field: the **scarcity of formalized mathematical statements and proofs**. While LLMs have demonstrated remarkable informal reasoning capabilities, the lack of machine-verifiable proofs undermines reliability and hinders further progress. Formal reasoning, enabled by proof assistants like Lean, offers machine verifiability but is severely limited by the manual effort required to create formal statements and proofs.

To address this core challenge, the authors set forth several key objectives:

1. **Large-Scale Statement Formalization:** To create a massive dataset of formal mathematical statements by training LLMs to convert natural language math problems (from the Numina dataset and other sources) into Lean 4. This process aims to generate `Goedel-Pset-v1`, comprising 1.64 million formal statements, thereby mitigating the data scarcity issue at the statement level.
2. **Extensive Proof Generation through Expert Iteration:** To develop a large dataset of formal proofs by employing an iterative self-improvement process. This involves training a series of provers, where each successive prover leverages proofs discovered by its predecessors. The ultimate goal is to obtain `Goedel-Pset-v1-solved`, containing proofs for over 800K statements.
3. **Achieve State-of-the-Art Performance:** To demonstrate that a model trained using this approach (Goedel-Prover-SFT) can achieve new best performance on widely recognized formal theorem proving benchmarks, particularly miniF2F and PutnamBench, surpassing existing proprietary and open-source models.
4. **Open-Source Contribution:** To fully open-source all codes, models, and datasets (including the newly found Lean Workbook proofs) to facilitate and accelerate future research in the automated theorem proving community, fostering transparency and reproducibility.
5. **Methodological Insights:** To provide extensive discussion and analysis of their training methodology and design choices, including the effects of data scale, formalization diversity, dataset correlations, and the implications of reinforcement learning versus supervised fine-tuning. This aims to offer valuable lessons and guidance for future model development.

In essence, the Goedel-Prover project aims to create a robust, high-performing, and openly accessible AI system for formal mathematics, tackling the fundamental data bottleneck and providing critical insights into effective training strategies for such systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of Goedel-Prover is structured around a two-stage data generation pipeline: **Statement Formalization** followed by **Expert Iteration for Proof Generation**.

#### 3.1. Statement Formalization

The first crucial step addresses the scarcity of formal statements by converting informal (natural language) math problems into Lean 4.
- **Source Data:** The primary informal dataset is Numina (860K question-answer pairs), supplemented by a private 68K collection from Art of Problem Solving (AOPS) and 140K statements from Lean Workbook.
- **Formalizer Training:** To enhance diversity, two distinct formalizers (both based on Qwen2.5-Coder-32B) are trained:
 - **Formalizer A:** Trained on Informal-Formal (I-F) statement pairs from Lean Workbook.
 - **Formalizer B:** Trained on 170K I-F pairs where informal statements from Numina were formalized by Claude-sonnet-3.5 and verified for Lean compilation.
- **Formalization Process:** These two formalizers process the informal datasets, generating formal Lean statements. LLMs are then used to ensure content preservation. This process yields **1.64 million formal statements**, forming the `Goedel-Pset-v1` dataset.
- **Quality Assessment:** Two tests ensure the quality of formalized statements:
 - **Compiling Correctness (CC) Test:** Verifies if the Lean statement compiles successfully with a placeholder proof.
 - **Faithfulness and Completeness (FC) Test:** Uses Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct to assess if the formal statement accurately captures the original informal problem. Statements with an FC score below 0.5 are filtered out.

#### 3.2. Expert Iteration for Proof Generation

With a vast collection of formalized statements, the team employs an iterative self-improvement technique called expert iteration (also known as "bootstrapping") to generate proofs.
- **Base Prover:** The process starts by using DeepSeek-Prover-V1.5-RL (the previous SOTA) to generate an initial set of proofs.
- **Iterative Loop:**
 1. **Proof Generation:** The current best prover (initially DeepSeek-Prover-V1.5-RL, then the `iter-k` prover) attempts to generate 16 proof candidates for each formal statement.
 2. **Verification:** The Lean compiler verifies the correctness of each generated proof.
 3. **Data Collection:** If at least one correct proof is found for a statement, one correct proof is randomly selected and added to the cumulative training dataset.
 4. **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** A new prover (`iter-(k+1)`) is trained by supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on `DeepSeek-Prover-V1.5-Base` using the augmented dataset of formal statements and their newly collected proofs.
- **Iterations:** A total of 8 iterations are conducted, with `Mathlib4` data also integrated from the sixth iteration onwards to potentially enhance general mathematical capabilities. This process cumulatively generates proofs for over **800K statements**, forming the `Goedel-Pset-v1-solved` dataset.
- **Training Specifics:** Training involves a packing trick, small batch sizes (8), and specific learning rates (1e-4, 5e-5). Computational resources include H100 GPUs for training and inference, and numerous CPUs for Lean proof verification.
- **Whole-Proof Generation:** Crucially, the model generates entire proofs without interactive feedback from the Lean compiler during the generation process, which simplifies the inference pipeline compared to step-wise provers.

#### 3.3. Exploration of RL and DPO Training

Beyond SFT, the authors explore the application of Reinforcement Learning (RL) techniques:
- **Direct Preference Optimization (DPO):** Used to fine-tune `Goedel-Prover-SFT` by constructing pairwise data from problems with a low-to-medium pass ratio, selecting a correct proof as preferred over an incorrect one. Length regularization is also explored to mitigate excessively verbose proofs.
- **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO):** An online RL algorithm applied using OpenRLHF, where correct proofs receive a positive reward and incorrect ones a penalty. Different prompt difficulties and timeout reward strategies are explored.

The methodology emphasizes generating high-quality, large-scale formal data as the foundation, followed by an effective self-improvement loop through expert iteration, primarily relying on supervised fine-tuning for its final high-performing model, while also investigating the complexities and challenges of applying RL in this domain.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research demonstrates significant advancements in automated theorem proving, primarily through the Goedel-Prover model trained with Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT).

- **State-of-the-Art Performance on MiniF2F:**
 - Goedel-Prover-SFT achieves a **57.6% Pass@32** on the miniF2F benchmark, significantly surpassing the previous open-source leader, DeepSeek-Prover-V1.5-RL, which achieved 50.0%. This represents a **7.6% absolute improvement**.
 - Goedel-Prover-SFT also consistently outperforms DeepSeek-Prover-V1.5-RL across varying inference budgets (Pass@32, 64, up to 25600), showcasing its robustness and efficiency. At Pass@3200, Goedel-Prover-SFT reaches 62.7% compared to DeepSeek-Prover-V1.5-RL's 54.9%.

- **Top Performance on PutnamBench:**
 - Goedel-Prover-SFT successfully solves **7 out of 644 problems** on PutnamBench (Pass@512), securing the **#1 position** on the leaderboard. This achievement places it on par with or above other advanced models like ABEL (tree search method) and InternLM2.5-StepProver on this challenging competition mathematics benchmark.

- **Significant Expansion of Formal Proofs in Lean Workbook:**
 - The model cumulatively solved **29.7K problems** in the Lean Workbook (including Lean Workbook Plus). This nearly **doubles the existing 15.7K proofs** previously found and open-sourced by InternLM2.5-StepProver and InternLM-Math-Plus, providing a substantial new resource for the research community.

- **Impact of Data Scale and Diversity:**
 - **Scaling Up:** The study explicitly shows that increasing the size of the formal statement set (and consequently, the training data) leads to a consistent improvement in model performance across benchmarks, underscoring the value of large-scale data in training effective provers.
 - **Formalization Diversity:** Training the prover on a mixture of formalization styles (from Formalizer A and Formalizer B) leads to better performance than training on a single style. This suggests that exposure to varied formalization patterns improves the model's generalization and reasoning abilities.

- **Dataset Correlations and Distribution Shift:**
 - An interesting finding is the **negative correlation** observed between the model's performance on ProofNet and its performance on miniF2F, Lean Workbook, and NuminaTest.
 - Further analysis reveals that incorporating Mathlib4 into the training data (which aligns with ProofNet's style) improves performance on ProofNet but **detracts from performance on miniF2F and NuminaTest**. This suggests a significant distribution shift: Mathlib4 and ProofNet focus on abstract mathematical concepts and manipulations, whereas miniF2F and related datasets emphasize Olympiad-style problems requiring complex reasoning with elementary facts.

- **Limitations of Alternative Data Synthesis:**
 - An exploratory "divide-and-conquer" pipeline using OpenAI's `o1-preview` for proof sketches and DeepSeek-Prover for subgoals was found to be largely **ineffective** in practice. It solved very few additional problems compared to the direct application of DeepSeek-Prover-V1.5-RL.

- **Challenges with DPO and RL Training:**
 - While DPO and GRPO (Goedel-Prover-DPO, Goedel-Prover-RL) did improve Pass@32 performance on miniF2F (to over 60%), they exhibited a tendency to **overfit to "shortcuts"** (e.g., excessive use of the `try` tactic) and increased average proof length, indicative of "reward hacking."
 - Crucially, these RL-trained models showed **diminished gains with increased inference-time compute** compared to the SFT model. This suggests that RL training, in this context, might reduce output diversity, leading to less efficient exploration of proof space at higher sampling budgets.

In summary, Goedel-Prover-SFT sets a new standard for automated theorem proving through a robust data generation and iterative SFT pipeline. The analysis also provides valuable empirical insights into the benefits of data scale and diversity, the nuances of dataset distribution shifts, and the inherent challenges of applying reinforcement learning in this complex domain.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Goedel-Prover project represents a significant leap forward in the field of automated theorem proving and the application of large language models to formal mathematics, with far-reaching implications:

- **New State-of-the-Art in ATP:** By achieving record-breaking performance on established benchmarks like miniF2F and PutnamBench, Goedel-Prover pushes the capabilities of AI in formal reasoning to new heights. This demonstrates that LLMs, when combined with large-scale formalized data and expert iteration, can tackle increasingly complex mathematical problems.

- **Mitigation of Data Scarcity:** The paper's core contribution is its effective methodology for generating a massive, high-quality dataset of formalized mathematical statements (`Goedel-Pset-v1`) and solved proofs (`Goedel-Pset-v1-solved`) from informal sources. This is a critical breakthrough, as data scarcity has long been a bottleneck in this domain. This approach provides a scalable pathway to expand the training resources for future ATP models.

- **Pivotal Open-Source Contribution:** The commitment to fully open-source all code, models, and datasets (including nearly 30,000 newly solved Lean Workbook problems) is arguably one of the most impactful aspects of this work. This empowers researchers globally to build upon Goedel-Prover's foundations, accelerate new discoveries, and ensure reproducibility. It democratizes access to frontier models and data, fostering innovation in both academic and industrial settings.

- **Deep Insights into LLM Training for Formal Reasoning:** The comprehensive analysis of training methodologies – particularly the benefits of data scale and formalization diversity, and the nuanced challenges of RL (e.g., "reward hacking" and reduced output diversity) – provides invaluable empirical guidance for future research. These insights can inform the development of more robust and generalizable AI systems for complex symbolic tasks.

- **Enhanced Reliability and Verifiability of AI Reasoning:** The ability of Goedel-Prover to generate machine-verifiable formal proofs significantly enhances the reliability of AI-driven mathematical reasoning. This is crucial for applications where correctness is paramount, such as formal verification of software and hardware, and potentially for scientific discovery where rigorous proofs are essential.

- **Foundation for Future AI-Assisted Mathematics:** The work lays a strong foundation for developing advanced AI tools that can truly assist mathematicians, educators, and engineers. This could include automated proof assistants that proactively suggest proof steps, intelligent tutoring systems for formal mathematics, or even AI collaborators that help explore complex mathematical theories.

- **Broader Implications for AI:** Beyond mathematics, the success of Goedel-Prover underscores the general potential of LLMs to master complex reasoning in domains requiring symbolic manipulation and logical deduction. The strategies employed could inspire similar data generation and self-improvement techniques in other challenging AI domains currently limited by data availability or the complexity of formal systems.

In conclusion, Goedel-Prover not only sets new performance benchmarks but also provides a powerful open-source ecosystem and critical empirical insights that are poised to accelerate research and development in automated theorem proving, making significant strides towards truly intelligent and verifiable AI systems for mathematics and beyond.
