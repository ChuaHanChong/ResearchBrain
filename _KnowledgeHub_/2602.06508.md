# World-VLA-Loop: Closed-Loop Learning of Video World Model and VLA Policy

> [!summary] Summary
> The paper "World-VLA-Loop: Closed-Loop Learning of Video World Model and VLA Policy" from the Show Lab at the National University of Singapore introduces a framework for efficient reinforcement learning (RL) of robotic policies by co-evolving a video world model and a VLA policy. This method enables substantial improvements in VLA policy success rates, achieving up to 50% in real-world tasks and over 97% in some simulated benchmarks, by minimizing the need for costly real-world interactions.

## Problem

- Real-world reinforcement learning for robotic policies is prohibitively expensive, time-consuming, and unsafe, hindering scalable VLA model refinement.
- Existing video world models suffer from imprecise action-following and unreliable reward signals, often hallucinating successful outcomes and making them unsuitable for robust RL.
- Imitation learning, while common, faces data scarcity and compounding errors, limiting the generalization of VLA policies.

## Method

- **World-VLA-Loop** co-evolves a state-aware ==video world model== and a VLA policy in a ==closed-loop learning== paradigm.
- A ==SANS (Success and Near-Success)== dataset is curated, containing both successful and slightly failed trajectories, to train the world model on fine-grained action dynamics and failure modes.
- The video world model integrates a jointly optimized ==reward prediction head==, enhancing its ability to provide accurate visual outcomes and reliable reward signals for RL.

## Results

- The developed world model demonstrates high visual fidelity (SSIM **0.91**, PSNR **28.09**) and accurate outcome prediction, with over **80%** alignment accuracy for both visual and internal reward signals.
- RL post-training using the world model improved VLA policy success rates by up to **+24.0%** on LIBERO benchmarks and from **13.3%** to **50.0%** in real-world tasks after iterative refinement.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the integrated reward prediction head and the SANS dataset are critical for precise action-outcome discrimination and overall generation accuracy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reliable neural simulators for robotics can be built by training video world models on diverse success and near-success data with an integrated, jointly optimized reward head.
> - A closed-loop, co-evolutionary process between the world model and the VLA policy facilitates continuous self-improvement, where each component enhances the other.
> - Efficient and scalable robot policy learning is achievable by leveraging learned world models as interactive simulators, significantly reducing the dependency on real-world interactions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research presented in "World-VLA-Loop" addresses challenges within the field of robotic manipulation, specifically concerning the training and refinement of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies. VLA models have emerged as a paradigm for controlling robots by leveraging large language model priors to translate natural language instructions into low-level robotic actions. Initially, these models relied heavily on behavior cloning from large-scale human demonstrations. However, recent advancements have explored integrating reinforcement learning (RL) to further refine VLA policies through interaction, aiming to overcome the limitations of static demonstrations, such as data scarcity and compounding errors.

A significant bottleneck for applying RL to VLA policies, particularly in real-world scenarios, is the prohibitive cost. Real-world RL necessitates extensive physical rollouts, carries safety risks during exploration, and incurs substantial operational overhead for environment resets and human supervision. To mitigate these challenges, researchers have explored the use of learned world models as virtual environments for policy training.

The existing landscape of world models for robotics can be broadly categorized into three paradigms:
1. **Handcrafted Digital Twins:** These models, such as RoboTwin, involve manually creating assets and physics engine models. While providing high fidelity in controlled settings, they often lack the photorealism and generalizability required for seamless transfer to diverse real-world environments.
2. **3D-based Reconstruction:** Approaches like Gaussian World Models (GWM) and DRAWER utilize geometric 3D representations of environments. These models offer a robust understanding of spatial relationships but frequently struggle with generalization across varied scenes and with supporting rich stochastic exploration.
3. **Action-conditioned Video World Models:** Recent progress in video generation, particularly with large pretrained video models (e.g., VLA-RFT, WorldEnv), has made these models an attractive option. They offer high photorealism and stronger scene generalization compared to 3D-based methods by predicting future video frames conditioned on robot actions.

This work specifically focuses on advancing the third paradigm—action-conditioned video world models—which, despite their advantages, face two critical limitations identified by the authors. Firstly, they often exhibit imprecise action following, leading to predicted trajectories that diverge from actual execution outcomes, particularly in "near-success" failure cases where minor action errors are mis-simulated. Secondly, these models do not natively provide reward signals necessary for RL, and generating reliable rewards from their visual predictions or through separate reward modules remains a challenge.

"World-VLA-Loop" positions itself as a development that addresses these identified limitations by introducing foundational designs and a closed-loop co-evolutionary paradigm. It aims to create a video world model that is both precise in its action-outcome alignment and capable of natively generating reliable reward signals, which then iteratively refines both the VLA policy and the world model itself. This approach aims to reduce the reliance on costly physical interactions, making RL post-training for VLA policies more practical for real-world deployment. The paper also acknowledges concurrent works such as WoVR and VLAW that investigate similar issues of imagination-outcome misalignment, noting that "World-VLA-Loop" differs by integrating intrinsic reward prediction directly from world model latents rather than relying on external reward modules.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation (detailed)

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and efficient framework for applying reinforcement learning (RL) to Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies, with a specific focus on real-world robotic manipulation. The motivation stems from the inherent difficulties and high costs associated with training VLA policies directly in physical environments.

The authors identify three main problems that necessitate their proposed "World-VLA-Loop" framework:

1. **Mitigating the High Cost and Risks of Real-World RL:**
 - **Motivation:** Standard RL post-training for VLA policies, while promising for refining behaviors beyond imitation learning, is largely confined to simulated environments. This is because real-world RL requires extensive physical robot rollouts, incurs significant operational overhead for manual resets and supervision, and presents safety risks during exploratory phases. These factors make real-world RL prohibitively expensive and slow.
 - **Objective:** To enable VLA policy refinement through RL in a virtual environment that accurately reflects real-world dynamics, thereby reducing the reliance on costly and risky physical interaction. The goal is to make RL post-training for VLA policies more scalable and practical for real-world deployment.

2. **Addressing Imprecise Action Following in Video World Models:**
 - **Motivation:** While action-conditioned video world models offer photorealism and generalization advantages as virtual environments, empirical analysis by the authors reveals a critical flaw: they often fail to accurately simulate the precise causal consequences of actions. This imprecision is particularly evident in "near-success" failure cases, where minor action errors lead to a failed outcome, but the world model might "hallucinate" a successful outcome. This weak grounding in fine-grained physical dynamics renders such simulators unreliable for effective RL, as policy improvements rely on accurate feedback regarding action outcomes.
 - **Objective:** To significantly enhance the action-outcome alignment and precision of video world models. This involves enabling the model to accurately predict the subtle differences between successful executions and various failure modes, thereby making the virtual environment a faithful representation of real-world physical interactions.

3. **Integrating Native and Reliable Reward Signals for RL:**
 - **Motivation:** A fundamental requirement for RL is a reward signal that guides policy learning. Existing video world models do not inherently provide such signals. Approaches that attempt to construct rewards from visual predictions (e.g., using visual similarity heuristics or external Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to interpret predicted frames) are often unreliable. This unreliability stems from the underlying inaccuracies in visual predictions, especially for critical near-success/failure distinctions, and the potential for external reward modules to misinterpret generated visual data.
 - **Objective:** To develop a robust mechanism for generating intrinsic reward signals directly within the world model. This aims to create a reward function that is inherently aligned with the world model's visual predictions and grounded in its understanding of success and failure, without relying on separate, potentially misaligned, external modules.

4. **Maintaining Alignment between Policy and Simulator in a Dynamic Learning Environment:**
 - **Motivation:** As a VLA policy undergoes RL training, its behavior evolves, leading to new action distributions and different failure modes. A static world model, trained on an initial dataset, may become misaligned with the updated policy's evolving characteristics. This misalignment can lead to predicted trajectories and rewards drifting from reality, rendering the simulator less effective for subsequent policy updates.
 - **Objective:** To establish a "closed-loop" or co-evolving paradigm where the world model and the VLA policy iteratively refine each other. This involves continuously updating the world model with new data generated by the improving policy, ensuring the simulator remains aligned with the policy's current behaviors and failure modes, thereby sustaining effective RL.

By addressing these motivations and objectives, "World-VLA-Loop" seeks to create a scalable, efficient, and accurate framework for training general robotic manipulation policies, reducing the barriers to real-world RL deployment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach (detailed)

The "World-VLA-Loop" framework is structured around a four-phase iterative cycle designed for the joint optimization of a video world model and a VLA policy (Figure 3).

1. **Success and Near-Success Dataset (SANS) Curation:**
 - **Purpose:** To provide a comprehensive dataset that enables the world model to learn fine-grained action-outcome dynamics, particularly distinguishing between successful and subtle near-success failure trajectories. Existing datasets primarily contain successful executions, limiting the world model's ability to simulate diverse failure modes.
 - **Content:** SANS comprises trajectories that either achieve the target goal or sub-goal, or nearly do so but fail due to minor inaccuracies. Each trajectory includes synchronized video frames, low-level robot actions (6-DoF end-effector poses and gripper state), and step-wise binary reward signals (success/failure).
 - **Collection Strategy:**
 - **ManiSkill:** Success trajectories are collected using simple control policies with ground-truth object poses. Failure trajectories are generated by perturbing these poses or captured from natural policy rollouts.
 - **LIBERO and Real-World:** Natural failure modes are captured from OpenVLA-OFT policy rollouts and through manual teleoperation.
 - **Scale:** A large ManiSkill SANS dataset (35,000 video-action pairs across 23 tasks) is used for world model pretraining. Smaller, task-specific SANS datasets (approximately 50 success and 50 failure trajectories per task) are collected for fine-tuning in LIBERO and real-world scenarios.

2. **State-aware Video World Simulator Training:**
 - **Base Architecture:** The world model is built upon Cosmos-Predict 2, which is a Diffusion Transformer (DiT) architecture pretrained on embodiment-related video data. It is adapted to incorporate robot action conditions.
 - **Action Conditioning:** An MLP action embedder maps robot end-effector poses and gripper states into latent tensors. These action embeddings are then integrated into the DiT modules by being added to the diffusion timestamp embeddings, grounding visual predictions in the control sequence.
 - **Integrated Reward Prediction Head:**
 - **Design:** A lightweight MLP projection layer is added on top of the DiT model's diffusion latents (z_t, obtained after denoising). This head ($\phi$) directly predicts a scalar reward value ($\hat{r}_t = \phi(z_t)$) for each timestep.
 - **Joint Optimization:** The reward prediction head is trained concurrently with the video generation component. The loss function includes both the original flow matching loss ($L_{flow}$) for video generation and a mean squared error loss for reward prediction: $L = L_{flow} + \lambda \sum_{t=1}^{T} \|\hat{r}_t - r_t\|^2$. The weighting factor $\lambda$ is modulated based on the sampled noise level to ensure robustness.
 - **Advantages:** This design provides rewards intrinsically aligned with the generated visual states, often more accurate than external reward models. Joint training encourages the generator to learn sharper distinctions between success and failure in the latent space, thus improving overall video prediction accuracy.
 - **Training Strategy:** The world model is initialized from a Cosmos-Predict checkpoint, first pretrained on the large ManiSkill SANS dataset to learn general robot-action relationships and physics. Subsequently, it is fine-tuned on smaller, task-specific SANS datasets to adapt to new environments and interactions.

3. **World Simulator for VLA Policy RL Post-Training:**
 - **Policy:** OpenVLA-OFT is adopted as the base VLA policy.
 - **RL Pipeline:** A GRPO-based (Group Relative Policy Optimization) post-training pipeline, adapted from SimpleVLA-RL, is implemented.
 - **Virtual Environment:** The trained state-aware video world model replaces traditional physics-based simulators.
 - **Rollout Generation:** During RL, only the initial frame is taken from the real environment or a static dataset. All subsequent observations are autoregressively generated by the world model, conditioned on the VLA policy's predicted actions.
 - **Reward Signal for RL:** The world model's integrated reward head provides step-wise rewards, which are thresholded into binary success signals (e.g., reward > 0.9 for success) to compute advantages for GRPO.

4. **Iterative Refinement (Closed-Loop Co-evolution):**
 - **Mechanism:** This phase closes the loop between the world model and the VLA policy. After each RL update, the newly refined VLA policy is deployed (either in the physical environment or a high-fidelity simulator) to collect new rollout trajectories.
 - **SANS Augmentation:** These new successful and near-success rollouts are added to the existing SANS dataset.
 - **World Model Fine-tuning:** The augmented SANS dataset is then used to further fine-tune the world model.
 - **Benefits:** This iterative cycle ensures that the world model continuously improves its ability to simulate precise execution outcomes, especially new failure modes encountered by the evolving policy. This, in turn, enhances the effectiveness of subsequent RL phases for the VLA policy, creating a self-improving system.

### 4. Main Findings and Results (detailed)

The research evaluates the World-VLA-Loop framework across simulated and real-world robotic manipulation tasks, reporting on the world model's generative performance, its utility as an RL simulator, and the efficacy of the iterative refinement process.

#### 4.1. World Model Evaluation

The generative quality of the world model was assessed based on video quality and action-following accuracy.

- **Video Quality (Table 1):** The world model generated high-fidelity video frames.
 - Average Structural Similarity Index (SSIM) of 0.91.
 - Average Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR) of 28.09.
 - Average Learned Perceptual Image Patch Similarity (LPIPS) of 0.045.
 - Average Mean Squared Error (MSE) of 0.0022.
 These metrics indicate low structural and perceptual distortion, and strong visual fidelity, with consistent performance across LIBERO simulations and real-world scenarios.

- **Generation Accuracy (Table 2):** The model effectively distinguished between successful and failed outcomes.
 - **Visual Alignment:** The average accuracy of classifying task outcomes (success/failure) by evaluating the final generated video frames was consistently above 80%, with examples such as 92% for LIBERO-Object Task 1 and 90% for Real-World Place Cup.
 - **Reward Alignment:** The average accuracy of classifying outcomes based on the world model's predicted reward (thresholded at 0.9) was also consistently above 80%, with 88% for LIBERO-Object Task 1 and 94% for Real-World Place Cup.
 - High correlation between visual and reward alignment indicated the reliability of the internal reward prediction head, suggesting its learned success criteria align with human judgment.

#### 4.2. World Simulator for VLA Post-training

The world model served as a learned simulator for RL post-training of the OpenVLA-OFT policy.

- **RL Post-Training Success Rate Improvements (Table 3, Figure 4):** Significant improvements in success rates were observed across LIBERO benchmarks and real-world tasks.
 - On LIBERO-Object tasks, success rates increased by +24.0% (Task 1, from 73.9% to 97.9%) and +18.0% (Task 2, from 73.9% to 91.9%).
 - On LIBERO-Goal tasks, improvements were +8.1% (Task 1, from 91.9% to 100%) and +10.1% (Task 2, from 86.1% to 96.2%).
 - On LIBERO-Spatial tasks, gains were +10.0% (Task 1, from 83.9% to 93.9%) and +6.1% (Task 2, from 87.9% to 94.0%).
 - In real-world scenarios, the "Place Cup" task saw a +23.4% increase (from 13.3% to 36.7%), and "Push Cube" increased by +13.3% (from 26.7% to 40.0%).
 - Comparison to Oracle RL (training in a physics simulator with ground truth): The performance of World-VLA-Loop RL post-training was comparable to, and in some cases, exceeded the Oracle baseline (e.g., 100% vs 98.8% for LIBERO-Goal Task 1).

- **Iterative Refinement through Policy Rollouts (Figure 1b, Section 4.3):** The closed-loop iterative process further enhanced real-world task accuracy.
 - On "Place Cup," the success rate improved from a SFT baseline of 13.3% to 36.7% after the first RL iteration, and further to 50.0% after the second iteration.
 - On "Push Cube," the success rate increased from a SFT baseline of 26.7% to 40.0% after the first iteration, and to 53.3% after the second iteration. This demonstrates the cumulative benefit of co-evolving the world model and VLA policy.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study (Table 4)

Ablation experiments validated the contributions of key components.

- **Reward Prediction Head:**
 - Removing the reward prediction head resulted in an approximate 30% drop in visual alignment, indicating that joint training with reward supervision significantly improves the model's ability to discriminate action outcomes.
 - When compared to using an external VLM (Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct) for reward prediction, the integrated reward head achieved higher reward alignment (e.g., 88% vs 84% for LIBERO-Object Task 1) and was more robust, as the VLM sometimes suffered from hallucination. This supported the reliability and efficiency of the integrated reward mechanism.

- **Near-Success Data:**
 - Excluding near-success trajectories from world model fine-tuning led to a significant decrease in visual alignment (e.g., 92% to 60% for LIBERO-Object Task 1). This confirms the importance of near-success data for teaching the world model to internalize diverse failure modes and accurately reflect action outcomes.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Results (Figure 5)

Qualitative examples illustrated that the iterative refinement allowed the world model to progressively cover a broader action space and represent the stochasticity of VLA exploration. Initial SFT policies sometimes generated imprecise pushes, and the first RL iteration could lead to reward hacking. After augmenting the SANS dataset with new rollouts, the refined world model developed a finer awareness of edge cases, leading to VLA policies that achieved more precise and robust grasps in subsequent RL iterations.

#### 4.5. Computation Cost (Appendix A.4)

The approach demonstrated computational efficiency compared to real-world interactions. A batched world model generation of 24 video frames took approximately 6 seconds on an NVIDIA H100 GPU. A full RL training session converged within 20-40 optimization steps, totaling 25-35 hours. World model fine-tuning on a specific task with 100 trajectories converged within 4 hours on 8 H100 GPUs, highlighting its affordability and scalability for new task adaptation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "World-VLA-Loop" framework presents a contribution to the field of robotic manipulation, particularly in making reinforcement learning (RL) more accessible and practical for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies in real-world settings.

1. **Enabling Scalable Real-World RL:** The work offers a method to overcome a primary bottleneck in robotic RL: the high cost, time consumption, and safety risks associated with extensive real-world robot interactions. By providing a reliable learned simulator, the framework allows for efficient policy refinement without incurring these physical interaction costs, which could significantly accelerate the development and deployment of advanced robotic capabilities.

2. **Enhancing World Model Fidelity:** The introduction of the SANS dataset, specifically curated with near-success trajectories, and the development of a state-aware world model with an integrated reward prediction head, collectively enhance the fidelity and action-outcome alignment of video world models. This improved accuracy in simulating subtle failure modes and providing intrinsic reward signals makes these models genuinely useful as virtual environments for policy learning, which has been a challenge for prior video world models.

3. **Establishing a Self-Improving System:** The closed-loop co-evolutionary paradigm is a significant aspect of this research. By iteratively refining the world model with new data generated by the improving VLA policy, the system ensures that the simulator remains aligned with the policy's evolving behaviors and failure distributions. This dynamic adaptation is crucial for maintaining the effectiveness of the virtual environment throughout the learning process and enables continuous improvement of both the policy and the simulator.

4. **Broader Applicability and Transferability:** Leveraging pretrained video models and demonstrating effective transfer to new tasks with limited fine-tuning data suggests the framework's potential for generalization across diverse robotic manipulation scenarios. This implies that the approach could be applied to a wider range of tasks and environments, increasing its impact beyond specific benchmarks.

5. **Informing Future Research Directions:** The paper identifies current limitations, such as performance degradation in long-horizon tasks (exceeding 300 frames) due to quality drift in autoregressive video models, and the potential for improving reward head accuracy with step-wise intermediate sub-goals. These insights provide clear avenues for future research to further advance the capabilities of learned world models and VLA policy training.

In summary, "World-VLA-Loop" advances the state of the art in VLA model training by providing a robust, efficient, and self-improving framework that bridges the gap between simulated and real-world robotic learning. This has the potential to make complex robotic skills more attainable and broadly applicable by mitigating the practical barriers of real-world RL.
