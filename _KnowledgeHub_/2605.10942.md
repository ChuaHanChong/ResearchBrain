# HarmoWAM: Harmonizing Generalizable and Precise Manipulation via Adaptive World Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> HarmoWAM introduces a unified World Action Model that resolves the trade-off between generalizable robot movement and precise manipulation by adaptively coordinating a predictive expert and a reactive expert. The framework achieved an 89% average success rate in-domain and maintained an 81% success rate across out-of-domain scenarios with only a 7.9% performance drop.

## Problem

- Existing robotic World Action Models (WAMs) face a fundamental trade-off, struggling to achieve both broad movement generalization and precise manipulation simultaneously.
- "Imagine-then-Execute" WAMs generalize well for transit but lack precision in fine-grained object interaction.
- "Joint Modeling" WAMs offer high precision near target objects but exhibit limited exploration and poor generalization to out-of-domain scenarios.

## Method

- **HarmoWAM** integrates a ==generative world model== with two complementary action experts: a ==predictive expert== for precise actions and a ==reactive expert== for generalizable exploration.
- A ==Process-Adaptive Gating Mechanism== dynamically switches between these experts based on the current task stage (transit or interaction), guided by visual observations.
- The world model provides ==spatio-temporal physical priors==, conditioning the predictive expert with current-step latent features and the reactive expert with future predicted frames and latents.

## Results

- **HarmoWAM** achieved an **89%** average success rate across six in-domain real-world robotic tasks, surpassing existing VLA and WAM baselines.
- The framework demonstrated robust zero-shot generalization to out-of-domain scenarios, experiencing only a **7.9%** average performance drop relative to in-domain success rates.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both the predictive and reactive action experts, along with the Process-Adaptive Gating Mechanism, are essential for HarmoWAM's combined exploration and precision capabilities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A systematic empirical study revealed a fundamental trade-off in existing WAMs between generalizable transit and precise manipulation, motivating the need for a unified approach.
> - Dynamically coordinating complementary action experts, a predictive one for precision and a reactive one for generalization, allows a single system to address both aspects.
> - Incorporating latent features from a generative world model into both action experts significantly enhances their ability to model physical dynamics and maintain temporal coherence.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robust robotic manipulation in complex environments requires policies that can both comprehend task instructions and adapt to the physical dynamics of the world. Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represent a prominent direction, adapting pre-trained vision-language models (VLMs) to embodied control. These models have advanced in scaling robot demonstration data, transferring VLM knowledge, and developing continuous generative policies for precise control. However, VLA models often remain constrained by their training data distribution, requiring extensive and costly data for generalization to novel scenarios.

World Action Models (WAMs) have emerged as an alternative paradigm, leveraging future visual prediction to provide spatio-temporal physical priors for action generation. WAMs are generally categorized into two main approaches:
1. **Imagine-then-Execute**: Models in this category first predict future visual trajectories and then infer actions through an inverse dynamics model (IDM). Examples include DreamGen and WoW. These methods explicitly use video prediction to capture environment dynamics and provide foresight.
2. **Joint Modeling**: This paradigm directly models the joint distribution of actions and video representations. Latent features from world models guide action prediction, either as latent supervision or action conditioning. Examples include VPP, Video2Act, Cosmos Policy, and Motus. These methods integrate predictive visual modeling into policy learning.

Existing WAMs, while promising, have been observed to exhibit a fundamental trade-off. Imagine-then-Execute approaches, due to their reliance on world models for explicit future prediction, tend to be generalizable for broad movements (transit) but may lack the precision required for fine-grained interactions. Conversely, Joint Modeling approaches often achieve high precision in manipulation when near target objects but can be constrained by their training data distribution, leading to difficulties in exploration and generalization to out-of-domain (OOD) scenarios.

This work, HarmoWAM, aims to address this identified trade-off by developing a unified WAM framework that integrates the strengths of both paradigms. It seeks to achieve both generalizable transit and precise manipulation within a single end-to-end system, thereby pushing the capabilities of robotic manipulation in unstructured and unseen environments beyond the limitations of current VLA and WAM approaches.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for HarmoWAM stems from an observed fundamental trade-off in existing World Action Models (WAMs) between generalizable object transit and precise manipulation. The authors conducted a systematic empirical study to characterize the manipulation boundaries of two representative WAM paradigms: "Imagine-then-Execute" and "Joint Modeling."

**Empirical Study Setup:**
The study was performed on two real-world manipulation tasks: "Put Flowers in Vase" (a dual-arm task requiring long-horizon coordination) and "Stack Coke Cans" (requiring precise, fine-grained stacking). Performance was evaluated under In-Domain (ID) settings and three Out-of-Domain (OOD) scenarios: variations in background, object position, and object semantics.
- **Imagine-then-Execute baseline:** Instantiated using Wan2.2-TI2V-5B for video generation and AnyPos for action inference.
- **Joint Modeling baseline:** Reimplemented by integrating latent features from Wan2.2-TI2V-5B into an Action Diffusion Transformer (DiT).

**Observed Trade-offs:**
1. **Imagine-then-Execute (e.g., Wan+AnyPos):** This paradigm demonstrated strong generalization in the transit phase, achieving high success rates (near-perfect in the study) in approaching objects, even under OOD conditions. This is attributed to the world model's ability to leverage generalizable physical dynamics. However, its performance in the interaction phase significantly decreased (e.g., as low as 60% in ID and 55% in OOD), indicating a lack of precision for fine-grained object manipulation.
2. **Joint Modeling (e.g., VPP-like):** This paradigm achieved stable control precision in the interaction phase (above 80% in ID and 95% in OOD when initialized near the target). However, its transit success degraded substantially in OOD scenarios (dropping to 32%), indicating a limited exploration space constrained by the supervised fine-tuning (SFT) data distribution. It struggled to reliably approach target objects in unseen environments.

**Key Objectives of HarmoWAM:**
Motivated by these findings, the core objective of HarmoWAM is to develop an end-to-end WAM that can **unify generalizable transit and precise manipulation**. Specifically, the authors aim to:
1. **Reconcile Generalization and Precision:** Design a framework that effectively combines the exploration capabilities of generalizable world models with the fine-grained control needed for precise interactions.
2. **Leverage World Model for Adaptive Control:** Utilize a world model to provide spatio-temporal physical priors that condition and adaptively coordinate two complementary action experts.
3. **Develop Complementary Action Experts:** Create a predictive expert for precise, temporally coherent action generation and a reactive expert for generalizable, real-time inference from future visual evolution.
4. **Implement Adaptive Coordination:** Introduce a mechanism that dynamically switches between these experts based on the current task stage (transit or interaction), guided by visual observations.
5. **Achieve Zero-Shot Generalization:** Demonstrate robust performance and strong zero-shot generalization across diverse real-world tasks and various OOD scenarios, including unseen backgrounds, object positions, and object semantics.
6. **Maintain Robustness in Long-Horizon Tasks:** Ensure that the unified framework can sustain performance over extended task sequences.

By addressing these objectives, HarmoWAM aims to unlock the full potential of world models for robotic control, enabling robots to perform complex manipulation tasks reliably in dynamic and unstructured real-world environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

HarmoWAM is designed as an end-to-end World Action Model (WAM) that integrates a generative world model with two complementary action experts, coordinated by a Process-Adaptive Gating Mechanism.

**Overall Architecture:**
The framework utilizes a world model to generate future visual observations and latent representations, which serve as spatio-temporal conditions for the action experts.
- **World Model:** The Wan2.2-TI2V-5B model is employed as the world model. It is pre-trained on large-scale robotic datasets (approximately 1.9M trajectories) to acquire physical modeling and generalization capabilities. Operating at a resolution of 256x320, it predicts a 13-frame future video for a given input observation. This world model provides both explicit future video predictions ($V_{t:t+H}$) and implicit latent representations ($F^V_{t:t+H}$).
- **Action Experts:** Two Transformer-based action experts are designed to leverage the world model's outputs:
 - **Predictive Action Expert:** This expert is a 1B-parameter Diffusion Transformer (DiT) with 28 Transformer blocks. It incorporates a SigLIP image encoder and a T5 text encoder. It leverages the current-step latent feature ($F^V_t$) from the world model as an implicit condition via cross-attention layers, along with current image features ($F^{img}_t$) and textual features ($F^{text}$). This expert is designed for fine-grained, temporally coherent action generation.
 - **Reactive Action Expert:** This expert is designed for real-time and interpretable action generation. It directly uses future predicted frames ($V_s$) and their corresponding latent representations ($F^V_s$) from the world model. It integrates a DINOv2-base model to extract patch-level geometric features ($F^{patch}_s$) from $V_s$. These patch features are concatenated with pooled latent features ($F^V_s$), forming a fused representation ($F^{fuse}_s$). An Orientation Decoder ($D_{ori}$) then extracts a global representation from $F^{fuse}_s$ to predict the action ($\hat{a}_s$). This expert is intended for generalizable transit.

**Coordination in HarmoWAM:**
Unlike approaches that loosely couple world models and policies, HarmoWAM tightly integrates these components within a differentiable framework.
- **World Model to Predictive Expert:** The current-step latent feature ($F^V_t$) from the world model is incorporated into the predictive expert's Action DiT via cross-attention. This conditioning provides rich temporal dynamics, enabling the expert to internalize physical dynamics for structured action prediction.
- **World Model to Reactive Expert:** For each future timestep, the reactive expert takes both the predicted frame ($V_s$) and its latent representation ($F^V_s$) as inputs. This allows inverse dynamics to be informed by both low-level visual observations and high-level spatio-temporal knowledge, enhancing its generalization.

**Process-Adaptive Gating Mechanism:**
This mechanism dynamically determines when to switch between the predictive and reactive experts.
- **Mechanism:** It is implemented as a lightweight MLP-based classification network. It reuses the visual tokens ($F^{img}_t$) from the current observation as input.
- **Function:** It outputs a confidence score ($s_t \in$) indicating whether the current task state favors precise interaction ($s_t > 0.5$, predictive expert) or generalizable transit ($s_t \le 0.5$, reactive expert).
- **Training:** A process-aware dataset is automatically constructed from robot demonstrations using a keyframe extraction pipeline. Interaction phases are identified by changes in gripper state or end-effector height thresholds and labeled for the predictive expert ($y=1$). Transit phases are labeled for the reactive expert ($y=0$). The gating network is optimized using a Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE) loss.

**Downstream Training Recipe:**
HarmoWAM is optimized through a two-stage training paradigm:
1. **Stage 1: World Model Finetuning:** A pre-trained Wan2.2-TI2V-5B world model is fine-tuned on real-world demonstrations using a conditional Flow Matching objective to capture task-specific visual dynamics. The loss function $L_{stage1}$ minimizes the difference between the predicted velocity and the target velocity.
2. **Stage 2: Action Experts Finetuning:** The world model is frozen. The predictive action expert, reactive action expert, and Process-Adaptive Gating network are optimized.
 - **Predictive Expert:** Trained with a standard diffusion denoising loss ($L_{pred}$) to predict Gaussian noise.
 - **Reactive Expert:** Supervised with a Smooth L1 loss ($L_{react}$) to align predicted actions with expert demonstrations.
 - **Gating Network:** Optimized with the BCE loss ($L_{gate}$).
 The overall training objective ($L_{stage2}$) is a weighted sum of these three losses, with $\lambda_{react} = 0.1$ and $\lambda_{gate} = 0.05$.

This methodology aims to leverage the inherent strengths of video prediction for robust exploration and the precision of conditional action generation for intricate manipulation, adaptively switching between them as dictated by the task's demands.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of HarmoWAM was conducted across six real-world robotic tasks (four single-arm, two dual-arm) under both in-domain (ID) and three distinct out-of-domain (OOD) scenarios (unseen backgrounds, object positions, and object semantics). HarmoWAM was compared against five baselines: π$_{0.5}$ (VLA), QwenVLA-OFT (VLA), Wan+AnyPos (Imagine-then-Execute WAM), VPP (Joint Modeling WAM), and Cosmos-Policy (Joint Modeling WAM).

#### 4.1. In-Domain (ID) Results

- **Overall Performance:** HarmoWAM achieved an average task-level success rate of 89% across all six ID tasks. This outperformed π$_{0.5}$ (74%), Cosmos-Policy (78%), and Wan+AnyPos (67%).
- **Precision Tasks:** HarmoWAM demonstrated an advantage in tasks requiring high precision and long horizons. For instance, in "Stack Coke Cans," it achieved 90% success, and in "Put Items to Bag and Zip" (a long-horizon dual-arm task), it achieved 85% success, outperforming the next-best baseline by 13 percentage points.
- **Bimanual Coordination:** On "Put Flowers in Vase," HarmoWAM achieved 85% success, indicating effective bimanual coordination.
- **Stage-wise Analysis (Appendix F.1):** HarmoWAM maintained high success rates across critical sub-stages, outperforming baselines in tasks like stacking alignment, continuous pouring, and bimanual handover followed by insertion, where baselines often failed. This suggests consistent control across the entire task execution.

#### 4.2. Generalization (Out-of-Domain - OOD) Results

HarmoWAM demonstrated strong zero-shot generalization across all OOD scenarios. The percentage drop in performance relative to ID scores for HarmoWAM was 7.9%, significantly lower than other baselines (e.g., π$_{0.5}$ at 33.8%, Cosmos-Policy at 43.6%, Wan+AnyPos at 20.9%).
- **Unseen Background:** HarmoWAM maintained an 81% success rate in cluttered OOD scenarios with unseen distractors and lighting changes, outperforming Wan+AnyPos (53%) and π$_{0.5}$ (60%). This suggests effective visual grounding and task-relevant visual evolution prediction by the world model.
- **Unseen Position:** This represented the most challenging generalization scenario, where target objects were placed outside the training data distribution. HarmoWAM achieved an 80% average success rate, while π$_{0.5}$ dropped to 32% and Cosmos-Policy to 26%. This indicates that HarmoWAM's world-model-guided expert collaboration, where the reactive expert generalizes exploration and the predictive expert handles precise interaction, effectively mitigates the transit-interaction trade-off in novel spatial configurations.
- **Unseen Objects:** When manipulating novel objects with different visual appearances and geometries, HarmoWAM achieved an 85% zero-shot success rate. This was higher than other methods, demonstrating effective leveraging of semantic knowledge from the world model for reliable object approach and interaction with new objects.
- **Stage-wise Analysis (Appendix F.2):** OOD stage-wise results reinforced that HarmoWAM's robustness extends to specific subgoals under distribution shifts, outperforming baselines in contact-sensitive stages (e.g., stacking, insertion) even with visual or spatial variations.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **HarmoWAM Structure (Figure 5a):**
 - Removing the reactive expert: Performance in position OOD dropped significantly to 14%, indicating the predictive expert alone struggles with exploration.
 - Removing the predictive expert: Success rates dropped to 56% in position OOD and 60% in object-instance OOD, suggesting the reactive expert, relying on visual predictions, is insufficient for fine-grained action generation under variations.
 - These results support the complementary roles of the two experts for effective exploration and precision.
- **Efficacy of Process-Adaptive Gating (Figure 5b):** Alternative routing strategies (Averaging and Keyframe-Based Averaging) showed greater performance degradation under OOD settings (e.g., 46% and 31% drop respectively in position OOD) compared to Process-Adaptive Gating. This indicates that stage-aware coordination, enabled by the gating mechanism, is crucial for effective use of world model knowledge.
- **World Model Latent Features (Figure 5c):** Removing the world model's latent features from the reactive expert reduced ID performance to 65% (from 82%) and OOD average to 54%. Similarly, for the predictive expert, ID performance dropped from 95% to 62% without these features. This demonstrates that world model latent features are essential for enhancing temporally coherent understanding and physical world modeling in both experts.
- **Denoising Steps (Appendix F.3):** Five denoising steps for the world model offered a balance between video prediction quality and inference efficiency (85% success at 4 Hz), with diminishing returns for more steps.

Inference speed for HarmoWAM was reported at 48 Hz with an action chunk size of 12. Visualizations of attention maps (Figure 3) indicated that the predictive expert focuses on manipulated objects, while the reactive expert attends to the robot gripper and task-relevant environment.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

HarmoWAM addresses a notable challenge in robotic manipulation: the trade-off between the ability to generalize broad movements (transit) and to execute precise, fine-grained interactions. By systematically identifying this limitation in existing World Action Models (WAMs), the research provides a framework that integrates complementary strengths, contributing to more robust and adaptable robotic policies.

The significance of HarmoWAM lies in several key aspects:
1. **Unified Control Paradigm:** It proposes an end-to-end WAM that effectively unifies predictive and reactive control. This integration allows the system to leverage the strengths of both explicit future visual prediction for generalizable exploration and implicit latent dynamics for precise manipulation within a single closed-loop policy.
2. **Adaptive Coordination of Experts:** The introduction of a Process-Adaptive Gating Mechanism is a central contribution. This mechanism enables stage-aware control, dynamically routing decision-making to the most suitable action expert based on real-time visual feedback of the task stage (transit or interaction). This adaptive approach allows the reactive expert to expand the exploration space under world model guidance, while the predictive expert ensures precise interactions.
3. **Enhanced Generalization Capabilities:** The experimental results demonstrate strong zero-shot generalization across diverse out-of-domain (OOD) scenarios (unseen backgrounds, object positions, and object semantics). This reduction in reliance on domain-specific training data has implications for reducing data collection costs and accelerating deployment in varied real-world environments.
4. **Robustness in Complex Tasks:** HarmoWAM shows robust performance not only in typical pick-and-place tasks but also in long-horizon and complex collaborative tasks requiring bimanual coordination and high precision (e.g., "Stack Coke Cans," "Put Flowers in Vase," "Put Items to Bag and Zip"). Its ability to maintain high success rates across critical sub-stages, even under OOD conditions, highlights its action coherence and reduced error propagation.
5. **Effective Use of World Model Knowledge:** The ablation studies confirm that both the explicit future predictions and implicit latent features provided by the world model are crucial for the performance of the action experts. This reinforces the utility of advanced generative world models as a foundation for intelligent robot control.

The potential impact of HarmoWAM extends to various applications where robots need to operate in unstructured and dynamic environments. This includes:
- **Smart Manufacturing and Logistics:** Robots could adapt more readily to changes in product lines, object variations, and workspace layouts without extensive retraining.
- **Assistive Robotics and Domestic Service:** Robots could perform tasks in human environments with varying objects, furniture arrangements, and backgrounds, making them more versatile and helpful.
- **Exploration and Disaster Response:** Increased generalization could enable robots to operate in novel, unknown, or hazardous environments.

While the current work operates with a fixed generation horizon and relies on pixel-level future generation, future work could explore adaptive prediction horizons and more efficient latent-level representations. Overall, HarmoWAM contributes to advancing robotic manipulation from controlled laboratory settings towards more flexible, reliable, and intelligent operation in complex real-world scenarios.
