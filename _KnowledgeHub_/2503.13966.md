# FlexVLN: Flexible Adaptation for Diverse Vision-and-Language Navigation Tasks

> [!summary] Summary
> A hierarchical navigation system combines large language models with supervised learning to enable vision-language navigation across diverse tasks and instruction types, achieving superior generalization performance on REVERIE, SOON, and CVDN-target benchmarks while reducing computational costs to 14% compared to existing LLM-based approaches.

## Problem

- Vision-language navigation systems struggle to generalize across different types of instructions and tasks
- Existing LLM-based methods use language models inefficiently and have difficulties with obstacle navigation
- Prior VLN generalization research has focused primarily on unseen environments, while generalizing across different types and granularities of instructions has been largely overlooked.

## Method

- Hierarchical architecture with ==LLM Planner== for high-level reasoning and Instruction Follower for low-level execution
- Integration of verification mechanism and multi-model ensemble to improve guidance generation and action selection
- Constrained action space and environmental perception using visual foundation models

## Results

- Outperforms previous methods on REVERIE dataset across all metrics
- First LLM-based method for SOON dataset with superior performance compared to GBE
- Reduces LLM call frequency by **52%** compared to NavGPT while maintaining performance and demonstrating strong generalization across different instruction types without additional training.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decomposing navigation into high-level planning and low-level execution enables better generalization
> - Selective use of LLMs for planning rather than direct action prediction improves efficiency
> - Verification mechanisms help ensure feasible guidance generation

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work addresses a significant challenge in Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN): **generalization across diverse tasks and datasets.** Existing VLN methods often require dataset-specific training or fine-tuning, limiting their applicability to new environments or tasks. The paper identifies that most past works focused on the generalization research on unseen environments, while generalizing different types of instructions has been overlooked.

The research landscape can be characterized as follows:

- **Traditional VLN approaches:** Supervised learning methods that achieve high performance on specific datasets but struggle to generalize.
- **LLM-based VLN methods:** Emerging approaches that leverage the reasoning and generalization abilities of Large Language Models (LLMs). NavGPT, DiscussGPT, and MapGPT are the pioneering LLM-based VLN methods, but these models have performance issues because their usage of LLMs is suboptimal. This paper aims to tackle these shortcomings.
- **The novelty lies in hierarchical navigation:** FlexVLN, using the generalization ability of LLM, with the fundamental navigation ability of the Instruction Follower to navigate and has a superior performance in handling out-of-domain instructions.
- **Focus on High-Level Instructions:** The paper emphasizes generalizability to high-level instructions, aligning closely with real-world applications, setting it apart from other works that primarily address object navigation or step-by-step instruction following.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind this research are:

- **Improve Generalization:** To develop a VLN agent capable of generalizing across diverse datasets and instruction types without requiring additional training or fine-tuning for each new task.
- **Leverage LLMs effectively:** To harness the reasoning and generalization capabilities of LLMs for high-level planning in VLN.
- **Address limitations of existing LLM-based methods:** To overcome the suboptimal utilization of LLMs in current VLN approaches, such as inefficient action prediction and difficulties in navigating around obstacles.
- **Real-world applicability:** To create a system that can handle coarse-grained, real-world instructions, making it more suitable for practical applications like domestic robots.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The paper proposes **FlexVLN**, a hierarchical navigation system that integrates:

- **LLM Planner:** Responsible for high-level planning and generating fine-grained guidance based on natural language instruction and visual input. It is designed to reason with instruction, navigation history and current observations to generate fine-grained guidance that aligns with the Instruction Follower's capabilities.
- **Instruction Follower:** A supervised-learning-based module responsible for executing the low-level navigation actions based on the guidance provided by the LLM Planner. This incorporates models that are trained on fine-grained instructions, so the out-of-domain instructions are considered those that deviate from the style.
- **Object Locator:** Responsible for identifying the target object at the end of the navigation process. This uses GPT-4o-mini to extract the target object from the instruction, and then employs BLIP-2 to compute the similarity between objects at the final node and the target object tokens.
- **Verification Mechanism:** A Multimodal Large Language Model is proposed to validate the feasibility of the guidance generated by the LLM Planner through a MLLM. In case of infeasibility, feedback on the reasons will be provided to the LLM Planner for re-planning and re-generating a new guidance.
- **Multi-Model Integration Mechanism:** To enhance the execution accuracy of the Instruction Follower, they propose a multi-model integration mechanism, where three models are ensembled. At each time step, if there are inconsistencies in action decisions among these models, a LLM will be consulted for assistance in determining optimal actions based on the guidance.

**Key aspects of the methodology:**

- **Hierarchical Approach:** The system decomposes the VLN task into high-level planning (LLM Planner) and low-level execution (Instruction Follower).
- **Action Space Restriction:** Constrains the action phrases in guidance to conform to the action space.
- **Environmental Perception:** Utilizes off-the-shelf visual foundation models to obtain scene descriptions, infer location, and identify objects.
- **Iterative Refinement:** The process involves iterative planning, guidance execution, and feedback to refine the navigation path.
- **Datasets:** The team uses the R2R dataset to train the Instruction Follower. To evaluate the generalization performance, they use REVERIE, SOON and CVDN-target as the other VLN benchmarks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate that FlexVLN achieves significant improvements in generalization performance compared to previous methods on the REVERIE, SOON, and CVDN-target datasets:

- **REVERIE:** FlexVLN outperforms all previous LLM-based methods across all evaluation metrics, surpassing the latest method MapGPT by a notable margin in SR and SPL.
- **SOON:** FlexVLN represents the first LLM-based method for SOON and demonstrates superior performance compared to GBE.
- **CVDN-target:** FlexVLN achieves superior performance compared to HAMT and comparable performance with DUET, while showing notable enhancements across all Instruction Follower baselines.
- **Ablation Studies:** Ablation studies validate the effectiveness of the verification mechanism, the constraint on action phrases, environmental perception, and navigation history.
- **Cost Analysis:** FlexVLN only invokes LLM for 48% times of NavGPT, resulting in a reduction of cost to merely 14% of NavGPT and halving the inference time.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in this paper has the following significance and potential impact:

- **Advancement of VLN Generalization:** FlexVLN introduces a novel approach to address the challenge of generalization across diverse VLN tasks, a long-standing problem in the field.
- **Efficient Use of LLMs:** The hierarchical architecture allows for efficient utilization of LLMs by reducing the frequency of LLM calls and optimizing their use for high-level planning rather than low-level action selection.
- **Practical Applications:** The ability to handle coarse-grained instructions makes FlexVLN suitable for real-world applications, such as domestic assistant robots, navigation systems for the visually impaired, and more.
- **Inspiration for Future Research:** The proposed architecture and techniques can inspire future research directions in VLN, particularly in exploring the integration of LLMs and supervised learning methods for improved generalization and efficiency.
- **First LLM-based VLN on SOON:** FlexVLN is the first LLM-based VLN to perform in the SOON dataset and is also a pioneer in testing generalization performance on this benchmark.

In conclusion, the research paper "FlexVLN: Flexible Adaptation for Diverse Vision-and-Language Navigation Tasks" presents a significant contribution to the field of VLN by addressing the critical issue of generalization and proposing an innovative hierarchical navigation system that leverages the strengths of both LLMs and supervised learning methods. The experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of FlexVLN in achieving superior generalization performance across diverse datasets, making it a promising approach for real-world applications.
