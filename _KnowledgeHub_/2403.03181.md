# Behavior Generation with Latent Actions

> [!summary] Summary
> VQ-BeT, developed by researchers primarily from NYU and Seoul National University, presents a framework for generative modeling of complex behaviors using transformers and residual vector quantization. The model achieves state-of-the-art performance in generating multimodal continuous actions across various embodied AI tasks while offering up to 25x faster inference speeds on real robots compared to diffusion models.

## Problem

- Generating actions for physical systems involves continuous, multimodal action spaces and uncurated, noisy datasets.
- Traditional methods like k-means clustering for action discretization suffer from scalability issues and lack gradient information, while diffusion-based models capture multimodality well but incur high computational costs from multiple forward passes, making them slow for real-time control.
- Temporal correlations in action sequences lead to compounding errors, which can cause catastrophic failures in physical systems.

## Method

- **VQ-BeT** uses a two-stage approach featuring ==Residual Vector Quantization (RVQ)== to learn discrete, hierarchical representations of continuous action sequences (action chunks).
- A ==GPT-like transformer== is trained to predict these discrete action tokens from observations, using a weighted ==focal loss== for primary and secondary codes.
- An additional ==offset head== predicts a continuous residual to fine-tune the decoded actions, maintaining high fidelity to the original continuous action space and mitigating compounding errors.

## Results

- VQ-BeT achieved state-of-the-art performance in **5 out of 7** unconditional and **6 out of 7** conditional simulated behavior generation tasks, demonstrating superior capture of behavior multimodality.
- The model exhibited a **5x speedup** in simulation and a **25x speedup** on real-world robots over diffusion policies due to its single-pass inference design.
- VQ-BeT successfully adapted to autonomous driving, matching or exceeding SOTA methods in trajectory following, and showed a **73%** relative improvement in real-world robotic manipulation tasks compared to baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Hierarchical residual vector quantization effectively captures continuous, multimodal action distributions, outperforming single-layer VQ approaches.
> - Combining a transformer with learned discrete action tokens and a continuous offset head enables efficient, single-pass inference suitable for real-time robotic control.
> - The explicit modeling of both discrete code predictions and a continuous offset is crucial for balancing expressive multimodality with high action fidelity in continuous control tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a fundamental challenge in decision-making and embodied AI: the generative modeling of complex behaviors from observational datasets. While impressive progress has been made in generative models for language (e.g., GPT), images (e.g., Stable Diffusion), audio, and video, generating actions for physical systems like robots or autonomous vehicles presents unique difficulties. The paper explicitly outlines these challenges:

1. **Continuous and Multimodal Action Spaces:** Actions in embodied environments are often continuous-valued vectors, but their distributions can be highly multimodal (e.g., multiple ways to achieve a goal).
2. **Uncurated and Noisy Data:** Real-world behavior datasets are often messy, noisy, and uncurated, making robust learning difficult.
3. **Temporal Correlations and Compounding Errors:** Action sequences exhibit strong temporal dependencies. Even small prediction errors can accumulate rapidly, leading to catastrophic failures in physical systems, unlike in language or image generation where errors might be more gracefully handled.

Previous approaches have attempted to tackle these issues. **Behavior Transformers (BeT)**, for instance, introduced a method of discretizing continuous actions using k-means clustering to capture different modes, then predicting these discrete tokens with a transformer. However, k-means suffers from scalability issues with high-dimensional action spaces or long sequences and lacks gradient information, limiting its ability to model long-range dependencies effectively. More recently, **Diffusion Models (e.g., Diffusion Policies, BESO)** have shown promise in generating diverse behaviors by modeling complex action distributions. Yet, these models typically involve multiple forward passes for each action prediction, leading to high computational costs and slow inference, which is a significant drawback for real-time control in robotics.

VQ-BeT positions itself as a novel solution that aims to combine the strengths of these prior works while mitigating their weaknesses. It builds on the long-horizon modeling capabilities of transformers (like BeT) but replaces the problematic k-means tokenization with a more sophisticated, learned **hierarchical Vector Quantization (VQ)** module. This approach is inspired by the success of VQ-VAEs and latent diffusion models in other generative domains (e.g., image and audio synthesis), where generating discrete latent representations first, before decoding, has proven highly effective. By doing so, VQ-BeT seeks to provide a versatile, performant, and computationally efficient model capable of handling multimodal continuous action prediction, conditional generation, and partial observations across various embodied AI tasks, thereby advancing the state-of-the-art in behavior generation for real-world applications.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the development of Vector-Quantized Behavior Transformer (VQ-BeT) are multifold:

- **Address Limitations of Existing Behavior Transformers (BeT):** The authors critically identify the shortcomings of previous transformer-based behavior generation models like BeT, particularly their reliance on k-means clustering for action tokenization. K-means is acknowledged as inefficient for high-dimensional action spaces, poor at capturing complex inter-dependencies over long sequences, and lacking gradient information, which restricts the overall model's learning capacity. A core motivation is to overcome these scaling and expressivity issues.
- **Improve Multimodal Action Prediction:** A key desideratum for any robust behavior generation model is its ability to capture and reproduce the diverse modes of human or expert behavior present in a dataset. VQ-BeT aims to significantly enhance this capability compared to prior methods, ensuring that the generated actions reflect the full spectrum of observed strategies rather than collapsing to an average or a single mode.
- **Enhance Versatility and Generalizability:** The researchers seek to create a single, versatile model capable of operating effectively across a broad range of decision-making tasks. This includes both unconditional (open-ended exploration of learned behaviors) and conditional (goal-directed behavior) generation, and demonstrating its applicability across diverse environments such as simulated manipulation, robot locomotion, autonomous driving, and real-world robotic tasks.
- **Achieve Computational Efficiency for Real-time Applications:** Diffusion-based policies, while powerful, are computationally expensive due to their iterative denoising process, making them slow for real-time inference, especially in robotics. A major motivation for VQ-BeT is to provide a single-pass inference model that offers significant speedups without compromising performance, thereby making it more suitable for online control of robots.
- **Maintain High Action Fidelity:** In embodied AI, even minor discrepancies in generated actions can lead to catastrophic failures. The model is motivated to precisely replicate learned behaviors, ensuring that the discretization process and subsequent decoding maintain sufficient fidelity to the continuous action space.
- **Leverage Success of VQ in Other Generative Domains:** Drawing inspiration from the profound success of vector quantization (VQ-VAEs) in generating continuous outputs like images, audio, and video, the authors hypothesize that a similar approach can effectively represent and model continuous, multimodal action vectors, which constitute a significant part of the difficulty in behavior modeling.

In essence, the motivation is to develop a robust, efficient, and versatile behavior generation model that pushes the boundaries of what is achievable in learning complex, multimodal, and long-horizon actions for real-world intelligent systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VQ-BeT (Vector-Quantized Behavior Transformer) introduces a novel two-stage approach to overcome the challenges of generating continuous, multimodal actions from observational data:

**Stage 1: Action Discretization Phase via Residual Vector Quantization (RVQ)**

The core idea here is to learn a discrete latent embedding space for continuous actions (or "action chunks," which are sequences of actions) before feeding them to a transformer. This is achieved using **Residual Vector Quantization (RVQ)**, inspired by its success in other generative domains.

1. **Encoder-Decoder Architecture:** A pair of neural networks, an encoder ($\phi$) and a decoder ($\psi$), are trained to discretize and reconstruct actions. The encoder maps a continuous action `a_t:t+n` to a latent embedding vector `x = $\phi$(a_t:t+n)`.
2. **Hierarchical Quantization:** Instead of a single codebook (like vanilla VQ or k-means), RVQ uses multiple layers ($N_q$) of codebooks. The input latent `x` is first mapped to the nearest vector in the *first* codebook (`z_1_q`). The *residual* (`x - z_1_q`) is then quantized by the *second* layer (`z_2_q`), and this process continues for all $N_q$ layers. The final quantized latent `z_q(x)` is the sum of vectors from all codebooks: `$\sum_{i=1}^{N_q}$ z_i_q`. This hierarchical structure allows the primary codes to capture coarse-grained modes, while secondary codes refine these modes, enabling fine-grained action representation and improved expressiveness. The paper typically uses $N_q = 2$ residual layers.
3. **Loss Function:** The RVQ-VAE is trained with a loss function `L_RVQ` that includes:
 - **Reconstruction Loss (`L_Recon`):** Measures the L1 distance between the original continuous action `a_t:t+n` and its reconstructed version `$\psi$(z_q($\phi$(a_t:t+n)))`.
 - **VQ Objective:** A term that pushes the codebook embedding vectors (`e`) towards the encoded actions (`x`), and another term that encourages the encoder output to commit to the chosen codebook vectors. Stop gradients (SG) are used to manage gradient flow, and codebook vectors are updated using moving averages for stability.

After this stage, the RVQ encoder/decoder pair is frozen. It effectively acts as a learned tokenizer, converting continuous actions into a sequence of discrete codebook indices (representing `z_i_q` for each layer) and back.

**Stage 2: VQ-BeT Learning Phase**

In this stage, a transformer architecture, akin to a GPT model, is trained to predict the discrete action tokens generated by the RVQ module.

1. **Transformer Architecture:** A GPT-like transformer is used to model the probability distribution of the action tokens. It takes sequences of observations (`o_t-h:t`) as input.
2. **Hierarchical Code Prediction:** The transformer's output head, `$\zeta$_code`, predicts the discrete codebook indices. It predicts both the primary and secondary codes (from the RVQ layers).
3. **Focal Loss for Code Prediction:** To handle potential class imbalance and better focus on hard-to-classify codes, Focal Loss (`L_focal`) is used. A crucial design choice is the weighted update for primary and secondary codes (`$\beta$`), where the primary code prediction is often given more importance, reflecting its role in coarse clustering. The loss `L_code` is `L_focal` for the primary code plus `$\beta$ * L_focal` for the secondary code.
4. **Offset Head for Fidelity:** To maintain high fidelity to the original continuous actions, an additional "offset head" (`$\zeta$_offset`) is incorporated. This head predicts a continuous residual that adjusts the center of the discretized action (obtained by decoding the predicted codebook sum) based on the observation. This fine-tuning is critical for avoiding compounding errors in continuous control.
5. **Total VQ-BeT Loss:** The overall training objective `L_VQ-BeT` combines the code prediction loss and the offset prediction loss: `L_VQ-BeT = L_code + L_offset`.
6. **Conditional and Unconditional Formulation:** VQ-BeT is designed to be versatile. For conditional tasks, an optional goal conditioning vector (e.g., future observations `o_N-g:N`) is simply concatenated or embedded alongside the observation sequence input to the transformer.

**Inference:** During inference, the VQ-BeT model performs a *single forward pass* to predict the action tokens and the continuous offset. These are then decoded by the frozen RVQ decoder to yield the final continuous action. This single-pass nature is a key differentiator from multi-pass diffusion models, leading to significant speedups.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted across a diverse set of environments demonstrate VQ-BeT's superior performance and efficiency:

1. **State-of-the-Art Performance in Behavior Generation:**
 - **Unconditional Generation:** VQ-BeT achieved state-of-the-art (SOTA) performance, outperforming baselines like BC (Behavior Cloning), BeT, and Diffusion Policies (both convolutional and transformer variants) in 5 out of 7 simulated environments (PushT, Image PushT, Kitchen, Multimodal Ant, UR3 BlockPush). While slightly outperformed in BlockPush and Image Kitchen by Diffusion Policies, VQ-BeT showed empirically stable performance across all tasks.
 - **Conditional Generation:** VQ-BeT also achieved SOTA performance on goal-conditional tasks, surpassing GCBC, C-BeT, and BESO (including its classifier-free guided variant) in 6 out of 7 environments, only matching BeT in BlockPush.

2. **Improved Capture of Multimodality:**
 - Quantitative metrics, such as higher behavior entropy (especially in Kitchen, Ant, BlockPush, UR3 BlockPush), indicate that VQ-BeT successfully captures a greater diversity of behavior modes present in the dataset, avoiding mode collapse.
 - Qualitative visualizations (e.g., PushT environment) explicitly show VQ-BeT generating smooth trajectories that cover multiple distinct modes of behavior, unlike baselines that might collapse to a single mode or produce erratic movements.

3. **Significant Inference Time Efficiency:**
 - VQ-BeT operates as a single-pass model, enabling drastically faster inference compared to iterative diffusion models. It achieves a 5x speedup in simulation and an even more impressive 25x speedup on real-world robots over Diffusion Policies. This efficiency is critical for real-time control applications.

4. **Successful Adaptation to Autonomous Driving:**
 - The model directly applies to and performs well on the nuScenes self-driving benchmark. VQ-BeT matched or exceeded SOTA methods in trajectory following (achieving the lowest average L2 distance to ground truth trajectories) and demonstrated comparable or better collision avoidance, even with partial environmental information (lacking lane and shoulder data available to some baselines).

5. **Robustness and Scalability to Real-World Robotics:**
 - VQ-BeT was evaluated on a real robotic platform (Hello Robot: Stretch) across single-phase, two-phase, and long-horizon manipulation tasks. It significantly outperformed baselines, including DiffusionPolicy-T, achieving a 73% relative improvement on two-phase tasks and demonstrating at least thrice the success rate for long-horizon tasks.
 - The real-world experiments highlighted VQ-BeT's robustness to controller noise, which caused receding-horizon control (used by some diffusion models) to fail completely on the low-cost manipulator. VQ-BeT's closed-loop, single-pass operation proved more practical.

6. **Impactful Design Choices (Ablation Studies):**
 - The ablation studies confirmed the critical role of several VQ-BeT components:
 - **Residual VQ:** Using multiple residual layers was significantly better than vanilla VQ, underscoring the importance of hierarchical representation.
 - **Weighted Loss for Codes ($\beta$):** Proper weighting between primary and secondary code prediction losses was crucial.
 - **Offset Head:** The continuous offset prediction was vital for maintaining full action fidelity and preventing performance degradation.
 - **Autoregressive Code Prediction and Action Chunking:** These aspects had mixed or negative impacts in some simulated environments, suggesting that for VQ-BeT's architecture, they might not always be necessary or beneficial, especially given its inherent speed.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of VQ-BeT represents a significant advancement in the field of behavior generation for embodied AI, with far-reaching implications for robotics, autonomous systems, and machine learning research:

- **Bridging the Gap in Generative Modeling:** VQ-BeT effectively addresses the unique challenges of generative modeling for continuous, multimodal actions in physical systems. By leveraging the strengths of transformers for long-horizon dependencies and vector quantization for rich action representation, it provides a robust alternative to prior methods that struggled with scalability (k-means) or computational efficiency (diffusion models). This could accelerate the adoption of advanced generative techniques in areas where they were previously impractical.
- **Enabling Real-time, High-Fidelity Robotic Control:** The most immediate impact is on real-world robotics. VQ-BeT's ability to achieve high performance with drastically faster inference speeds (up to 25x faster than diffusion policies on real robots) makes it highly suitable for real-time, closed-loop control. This is particularly crucial for affordable, less precise robotic hardware where controller noise can be a major challenge for open-loop or receding-horizon control strategies.
- **Advancing Imitation Learning from Diverse Data:** By effectively capturing multiple behavior modes from uncurated datasets, VQ-BeT enhances the capabilities of imitation learning. This means robots can learn more diverse and adaptable skills from human demonstrations, even when those demonstrations contain varying strategies. This is a critical step towards robots that can learn complex, open-ended tasks in human environments.
- **Versatile and Generalizable Behavior Policies:** The demonstrated success across a wide range of tasks—simulated manipulation, locomotion, autonomous driving, and real-world robot tasks—highlights VQ-BeT's versatility and generalizability. A single architectural paradigm can be applied to different domains, reducing the need for task-specific models and simplifying policy development.
- **Foundation for Future Research and Scaling:**
 - **Large-scale Behavior Datasets:** VQ-BeT is well-positioned to scale with ever-growing datasets of robot and human behavior, enabling the learning of even more complex and general skills.
 - **Shared Latent Action Spaces:** The learned discrete action space could serve as a "universal language" for actions, facilitating policy translation between different robot embodiments or even from human demonstrations to various robots. This is a key step towards more generalized and transferable robotic intelligence.
 - **Faster Reinforcement Learning:** The discrete action space learned by VQ-BeT could potentially accelerate real-world Reinforcement Learning (RL) applications by providing a more structured and manageable action representation for exploration and optimization.

In conclusion, VQ-BeT provides a powerful, efficient, and versatile framework for behavior generation, addressing a critical bottleneck in embodied AI. Its practical advantages in speed and robustness, combined with strong performance across diverse tasks, position it as a foundational contribution with significant potential to accelerate the development of more capable and autonomous robotic systems.
