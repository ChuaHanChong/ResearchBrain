# Safety-Gymnasium: A Unified Safe Reinforcement Learning Benchmark

> [!summary] Summary
> Peking University researchers developed Safety-Gymnasium, a unified benchmark suite for Safe Reinforcement Learning, accompanied by the SafePO algorithm library. This framework addresses the lack of dedicated, up-to-date environments and provides validated baselines, enabling comprehensive evaluation across diverse safety-critical tasks.

## Problem

- A significant lack of dedicated, up-to-date simulation environments specifically designed for Safe Reinforcement Learning (SafeRL).
- Existing SafeRL benchmarks, such as OpenAI's Safety Gym, suffered from outdated dependencies, limited task diversity, rudimentary visual support, and complex installation.
- Difficulty in rigorously evaluating and comparing SafeRL algorithms across diverse, complex, and safety-critical real-world scenarios, including multi-agent and vision-only settings.

## Method

- Introduced **Safety-Gymnasium**, a ==unified benchmark suite== built on Gymnasium and direct MuJoCo support, overcoming previous environmental limitations.
- Developed **SafePO** (==Safe Policy Optimization==), a meticulously implemented library of 16 state-of-the-art SafeRL algorithms for reliable baseline comparison.
- Integrated diverse environments, including ==multi-agent==, ==vision-only== tasks, and high-dimensional Isaac Gym-based environments, offering extensive ==safety constraints== and enhanced realism.

## Results

- SafePO's algorithms demonstrated high correctness, achieving leading or comparable performance in balancing reward and cost constraints, such as PPO-Lag achieving a **98% cost reduction** in velocity tasks.
- In low-stochasticity tasks, all SafeRL algorithms consistently maintained episodic costs within specified limits, while high-stochasticity tasks showed pronounced oscillations and performance divergence.
- CPPO-PID significantly reduced instances of "Strongly Unsafe" states during training and improved overall safety performance compared to PPO-Lag, maintaining comparable episodic rewards.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A fundamental trade-off exists between reward maximization and safety constraint satisfaction, with SafeRL algorithms effectively reducing costs at a performance trade-off.
> - Algorithm performance and stability are significantly influenced by task stochasticity, with highly stochastic environments leading to greater oscillations and varied algorithm efficacy.
> - PID-Lagrangian methods prove effective in stabilizing constraint adherence, reducing oscillatory behavior and the frequency of "Strongly Unsafe" states compared to standard Lagrangian approaches.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The deployment of Artificial Intelligence (AI) systems, despite their vast potential, is frequently hampered by significant safety concerns. This is particularly true for Reinforcement Learning (RL), where agents learn through trial and error, a process that can lead to unsafe behaviors in real-world, safety-critical environments such as autonomous driving, healthcare, and human-robot interaction. Safe Reinforcement Learning (SafeRL) emerges as a crucial subfield, aiming to develop policies that not only optimize performance but also rigorously adhere to predefined safety constraints.

The advancement of RL has historically relied heavily on standardized simulation environments like OpenAI Gym, Atari, and DeepMind's dm-control. These platforms enable researchers to rapidly develop, test, and compare algorithms. However, within the burgeoning field of SafeRL, the paper highlights a "notable dearth of dedicated simulation environments." While initial efforts like DeepMind's AI-Safety-Gridworlds and OpenAI's Safety Gym made strides in this direction, they presented limitations. Safety Gym, for instance, relied on the now-unsupported `mujoco-py` library, had a limited range of agents and tasks, offered simplistic visual support, and was often cumbersome to install. Subsequent environments like `safe-control-gym` and `MetaDrive` have also contributed but have not fully addressed the need for a unified, comprehensive benchmark.

Safety-Gymnasium directly addresses this gap by positioning itself as a unified and significantly expanded benchmark suite for SafeRL. It is presented as an evolution of OpenAI's Safety Gym, inheriting its core ideas while introducing substantial improvements and broader scope. By refactoring the physics engine to directly support MuJoCo, extending the diversity of agents and tasks (including multi-agent and vision-only scenarios), enhancing visual realism, and improving ease of installation, Safety-Gymnasium aims to become the de facto standard for evaluating SafeRL algorithms. The integration of high-performance simulators like Isaac Gym further extends its utility to complex, high-dimensional control problems requiring parallel processing.

In essence, this work provides a much-needed, robust infrastructure for SafeRL research. It aims to accelerate scientific progress by offering a standardized, comprehensive, and accessible platform for algorithm development, evaluation, and comparison, thereby enabling SafeRL to transition from theoretical exploration to reliable real-world deployment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of the "Safety-Gymnasium" project is to **facilitate the rigorous evaluation and comparison of safety performance in Safe Reinforcement Learning algorithms**, ultimately fostering the development of AI systems that are safer, more reliable, and responsible for real-world applications.

This overarching goal is driven by several specific motivations:

- **Addressing the Benchmark Gap in SafeRL**: The authors explicitly identify a "notable dearth of dedicated simulation environments" for SafeRL. While general RL has benefited immensely from standardized benchmarks, SafeRL lacked a comprehensive and up-to-date platform that could keep pace with the field's rapid advancements.
- **Overcoming Limitations of Existing Benchmarks**: Previous SafeRL benchmarks, particularly OpenAI's Safety Gym, had several critical shortcomings. These included:
 - **Outdated Physics Engine Dependency**: Safety Gym's reliance on `mujoco-py`, which ceased updates after 2021, posed significant maintenance and compatibility challenges.
 - **Limited Diversity**: The restricted number of agents and tasks in existing suites limited the scope of SafeRL research that could be effectively benchmarked.
 - **Simplistic Visuals**: The rudimentary visual components hindered the development and evaluation of vision-based SafeRL algorithms crucial for many real-world applications.
 - **Installation and Customization Difficulties**: Cumbersome installation processes and limited customization options created high barriers to entry for researchers.
- **Enabling Exploration of Complex Scenarios**: The growing complexity of real-world applications demands SafeRL solutions for multi-agent systems and scenarios relying solely on visual input. Safety-Gymnasium aims to provide environments that support these intricate challenges.
- **Providing a Unified and Accessible Platform**: The goal is to create a single, easy-to-install, and highly customizable environment suite that serves as a central hub for the SafeRL community, allowing researchers to focus on algorithm design rather than environment setup.
- **Establishing a Robust Algorithm Library (SafePO)**: Beyond just environments, the project aims to offer a meticulously implemented and validated library of state-of-the-art SafeRL algorithms. This library acts as a crucial validation tool, allowing researchers to compare their new algorithms against reliable baselines and to thoroughly analyze existing methods.
- **Generating Insights into Algorithm Behavior**: By combining the diverse environments of Safety-Gymnasium with the comprehensive algorithms in SafePO, the project intends to conduct and disseminate detailed analyses of algorithm strengths, weaknesses, and areas for improvement, thereby guiding future research directions.

In summary, the authors are motivated to build a foundation that lowers entry barriers, expands research capabilities, and standardizes evaluation, all to accelerate the development of SafeRL technologies ready for societal impact.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core methodology of this work revolves around the development of two interconnected components: **Safety-Gymnasium**, a comprehensive environment suite, and **Safe Policy Optimization (SafePO)**, an algorithm library. Both are designed for ease of use, extensibility, and rigorous evaluation.

#### 3.1. Safety-Gymnasium Environment Suite

Safety-Gymnasium is a collection of safety-oriented tasks built upon established RL frameworks, enhancing and expanding upon previous benchmarks.

- **Foundational Technologies**:
 - **Gymnasium**: Provides a standard API for RL environments, ensuring compatibility and ease of integration.
 - **MuJoCo**: The physics engine for most Gymnasium-based environments, chosen for its accuracy and speed. Crucially, Safety-Gymnasium directly supports MuJoCo, eliminating the reliance on `mujoco-py` and enabling access to the latest features.
 - **Isaac Gym**: Utilized for high-dimensional, parallel learning environments, leveraging GPU acceleration for significantly faster sample collection and training in complex scenarios.

- **Gymnasium-based Learning Environments**: These environments are characterized by their diversity in agents, tasks, and constraints.
 - **Supported Robots**:
 - **Single-Agent**: Inherits and refines "Point," "Car," and "Doggo" from Safety Gym, addressing issues like excessive oscillations. It also introduces new robots like "Racecar" and "Ant" to enrich scenarios.
 - **Multi-Agent**: Leverages configurations from multi-agent MuJoCo, allowing multiple agents to control distinct body segments (e.g., multi-agent Ant variants).
 - **Supported Tasks**: A range of tasks covering different behavioral objectives:
 - **Locomotion/Navigation**: "Velocity," "Run," "Circle," "Goal."
 - **Manipulation**: "Push," "Button."
 - These tasks vary in complexity and involve agents navigating to targets, maintaining speeds, or interacting with objects.
 - **Supported Constraints**: A comprehensive set of safety constraints designed to mimic real-world hazards:
 - **Velocity-Constraint**: Restricting agent speed.
 - **Static Obstacles**: "Pillars" (cylindrical obstacles) and "Vases" (fragile objects).
 - **Hazardous Areas**: "Hazards" (areas that incur costs upon entry) and "Sigwalls" (boundary walls for circular tasks).
 - **Dynamic Obstacles**: "Gremlins" (moving objects that interact with agents).
 - **Vision-only Tasks**: A significant enhancement offering more realistic visual inputs (RGB and RGB-D) for tasks like "Race" and "FormulaOne," and "Building"-themed tasks, enabling the evaluation of vision-based SafeRL algorithms.

- **Isaac-Gym-based Learning Environments (Safety-DexterousHands)**:
 - **Agents**: Involves two highly complex Shadow Hands, leveraging the parallel processing power of Isaac Gym.
 - **Tasks**: Tasks like "Hand Over" and "Hand Over Catch" involve bimanual dexterous manipulation, where one hand grasps and throws an object to the other.
 - **Constraints**: "Safety Joint" and "Safety Finger" constraints, which restrict the range of motion of specific robotic finger joints, modeling physical limits or safe operating envelopes.

#### 3.2. Safe Policy Optimization (SafePO) Algorithm Library

SafePO is an integral part of the benchmark, providing a library of 16 state-of-the-art SafeRL algorithms for validation and comparison.

- **Algorithm Coverage**: It includes a diverse range of algorithms, encompassing:
 - **Single-agent and Multi-agent approaches**.
 - **First-order and Second-order optimization methods**.
 - **Lagrangian-based and Projection-based constraint handling techniques**.
 - Examples include PPO-Lag, CPO, FOCOPS, CUP, CPPO-PID, MACPO, and MAPPO-Lag.
- **Design Principles**:
 - **Correctness and Reliability**: Algorithms are implemented strictly according to their original papers, cross-verified with existing open-source codebases, and benchmarked against other implementations (e.g., Safety Starter Agents) to ensure high fidelity.
 - **Extensibility**: The library boasts a modular architecture (Figure 7), allowing new algorithms to be easily integrated by inheriting from base classes and implementing only their unique features.
 - **Logging and Visualization**: Comprehensive support for TensorBoard and WandB, enabling visualization of over 40 training parameters and metrics (e.g., KL-divergence, steps per second, Lagrangian multipliers, PID controller components). This facilitates detailed analysis and debugging.
 - **Documentation**: Extensive online documentation covers installation, basic usage, advanced customization, and guidelines for ethical and responsible use.

By combining these robust environmental and algorithmic components, the methodology aims to provide a unified, performant, and user-friendly ecosystem for advancing SafeRL research.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents a comprehensive empirical analysis using Safety-Gymnasium and SafePO, comparing 16 state-of-the-art SafeRL algorithms across 54 distinct environments. The key findings and results are summarized as follows:

- **Performance Validation of SafePO**: The SafePO algorithm implementations demonstrate high correctness and reliability. Comparative benchmarking (Table 1) against existing implementations like OpenAI's Safety Starter Agents and RL-Safety-Algorithms shows that SafePO's algorithms either outperform or achieve comparable performance, particularly in their ability to respect cost limits while maximizing rewards. This validation underscores SafePO as a trustworthy baseline for research.

- **Reward-Cost Trade-off**: As expected, a fundamental trade-off exists between maximizing reward and ensuring safety (minimizing cost). Unconstrained algorithms (e.g., PPO) achieve high rewards but often incur substantial safety costs. In contrast, SafeRL algorithms like PPO-Lag significantly reduce costs (e.g., a 98% cost reduction in velocity tasks) with a moderate decrease in reward (e.g., a 45% decrease). This illustrates the effectiveness of SafeRL in constraint adherence, even if it comes at a performance cost.

- **Impact of Task Stochasticity on Algorithm Performance**:
 - **Low Stochasticity Tasks (e.g., Velocity tasks)**: In environments with less randomness, all SafeRL algorithms consistently achieved average episodic costs within the specified `cost_limit`. The optimal policies were tightly clustered, and the divergence in episodic rewards between algorithms was negligible, indicating a relatively straightforward safety optimization problem.
 - **High Stochasticity Tasks (e.g., Navigation tasks)**: Tasks characterized by higher randomness exhibited "pronounced oscillations" in algorithm performance. Optimal policies were more spread out, leading to observable and often significant differences in algorithm efficacy across various navigation challenges. This suggests that handling safety in stochastic environments is a more challenging problem for current SafeRL methods.

- **Comparison of Lagrangian vs. Projection-based Methods**:
 - **Lagrangian-based methods (CPO, PPO-Lag)**: These methods, while effective, demonstrated more oscillatory behavior in episodic costs around the safety constraints during training (Figure 8b). PPO-Lag, in particular, showed a higher proportion of instances classified as "Strongly Unsafe" and "Strongly Safe," indicating wider fluctuations. The paper notes that CPO maintained a more centered distribution. However, an overly cautious policy can sometimes compromise reward performance.
 - **Projection-based methods (PCPO)**: These methods tended to be more conservative, exhibiting lower average costs and rewards compared to CPO in both navigation and velocity tasks. This suggests a trade-off where projection-based methods might offer greater safety guarantees (lower cost violations) but potentially at the expense of optimal reward achievement.

- **Effectiveness of PID-Lagrangian Methods**: Incorporating a Proportional-Integral-Derivative (PID) controller within the Lagrangian framework (CPPO-PID) proved effective in mitigating the inherent oscillations observed in standard Lagrangian methods. CPPO-PID showed episodic rewards during training that were comparable to PPO-Lag but significantly reduced the frequency of instances entering the "Strongly Unsafe" region, leading to a higher proportion of "Safe" states and overall improved safety performance.

- **Multi-Agent Algorithm Performance**: In multi-agent velocity tasks, HAPPO achieved higher rewards than MAPPO but with a simultaneous increase in average costs, illustrating a similar reward-cost trade-off in the multi-agent setting. MACPO and MAPPO-Lag also demonstrated distinct performance profiles, highlighting the complexity and varied behaviors of SafeRL in cooperative multi-agent games.

These findings provide valuable insights into the empirical characteristics of different SafeRL algorithms under varying environmental conditions, contributing to a better understanding of their applicability and limitations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Safety-Gymnasium" project carries significant implications for the future of Safe Reinforcement Learning and the broader field of AI. Its contributions span standardization, research acceleration, and real-world applicability:

- **Standardization and Accessibility for SafeRL**: Safety-Gymnasium provides a much-needed unified, comprehensive, and up-to-date benchmark suite. By addressing the limitations of previous efforts (e.g., `mujoco-py` dependency, limited diversity, complex installation), it significantly lowers the entry barrier for researchers. This standardization fosters fair comparison of algorithms, prevents fragmentation of research efforts, and ensures reproducible science in a rapidly evolving field.
- **Acceleration of SafeRL Research and Development**: The combined offering of a diverse set of environments (single-agent, multi-agent, vector-based, vision-only, Gymnasium-based, Isaac-Gym-based) and a robust, validated algorithm library (SafePO) dramatically accelerates the research cycle. Researchers can quickly test new algorithmic ideas against a wide range of tasks and compare them with strong baselines, facilitating faster innovation and iteration.
- **Bridging the Gap to Real-World Applications**: The inclusion of multi-agent and vision-only tasks, alongside the leverage of high-performance simulators like Isaac Gym, directly addresses critical challenges in deploying SafeRL to complex real-world systems such as autonomous vehicles, robotics, and human-AI collaboration. More realistic and high-dimensional simulations are crucial for developing algorithms that can generalize to physical platforms.
- **Deepening Algorithmic Understanding**: The detailed empirical analysis provided in the paper offers valuable insights into the strengths, weaknesses, and behavioral characteristics (e.g., oscillations, conservatism) of different SafeRL algorithms. Understanding these nuances helps guide the development of more robust, efficient, and reliable safety-aware learning agents. For instance, the findings on PID-Lagrangian methods highlight promising avenues for improving the stability of constraint satisfaction.
- **Foundation for Future Work**: The authors explicitly highlight limitations and future directions, such as integrating human-centric safety constraints, addressing the sim-to-real gap, and transferring learned policies to physical robots. Safety-Gymnasium provides an excellent foundation for these advanced research endeavors, serving as a testbed for novel approaches to these challenges.
- **Fostering Community and Open Science**: The open-source nature of Safety-Gymnasium and SafePO, coupled with comprehensive documentation and a dedicated project website, promotes transparency, collaboration, and knowledge sharing within the scientific community. This encourages broader adoption and collective progress in SafeRL.

In conclusion, "Safety-Gymnasium" is a landmark contribution that provides essential infrastructure for the SafeRL community. By offering a meticulously crafted, diverse, and accessible benchmark, it is poised to significantly advance the field, enabling the development of more trustworthy and deployable AI systems that can safely and responsibly operate in complex real-world environments.
