# Integrating Learning-Based Manipulation and Physics-Based Locomotion for Whole-Body Badminton Robot Control

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Shandong University and collaborators developed Hamlet, a hybrid control system for an agile badminton robot that integrates a physics-based strategy for locomotion with a learning-based policy for arm manipulation. This system achieved a 94.5% success rate against a serving machine and a 90.7% return success rate against human players, demonstrating robust whole-body control in a dynamic sports environment.

## Problem

- Controlling agile robots in complex, dynamic sports environments like badminton demands high-speed reactions, precise synchronization, and handling flexible object dynamics within tight temporal constraints.
- Existing purely model-based methods struggle with accurate environmental modeling and extensive tuning, while learning-based approaches face challenges with policy convergence, long training times, and safety concerns.
- A lack of integrated hybrid control systems for agile badminton robots that synergistically combine the strengths of both model-based stability and learning-based adaptability for whole-body control.

## Method

- Developed Hamlet, a hybrid control system that decouples the robot's chassis and arm control, using a model-based strategy for chassis locomotion and a learning-based policy for agile arm manipulation.
- Introduced a physics-informed "IL+RL" (Imitation Learning + ==Reinforcement Learning==) training pipeline for the arm policy, which leverages a model-based teacher with privileged information to guide warm-up and supervise policy exploration.
- Designed the training scheme to enable zero-shot sim2real transfer and generalization, allowing the arm policy to function effectively with different chassis types without requiring retraining.

## Results

- The learning-based arm policy achieved a **97.0%** hit rate and **94.5%** success rate against a serving machine in real-world experiments, outperforming a model-based arm strategy which yielded **91.0%** hit rate and **77.5%** success rate.
- Hamlet demonstrated robust real-world performance against human players, achieving an average return success rate of **90.7%** over 300 rounds, including rallies extending up to 40 consecutive hits.
- Ablation studies confirmed the "IL+RL" training recipe's components (IL warm-up, pre-trained critic, model-based supervision) were crucial for accelerated learning, optimized policy performance, and reduced training volatility, while also showing zero-shot generalization of the arm policy to multiple chassis types.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling whole-body control into distinct components (locomotion and manipulation) and applying tailored control strategies (model-based for chassis, learning-based for arm) effectively leverages their respective strengths in dynamic tasks.
> - A physics-informed "IL+RL" training framework, guided by a model-based teacher with privileged information and pre-trained critic, can effectively accelerate learning, improve performance, and maintain stability in complex RL tasks with sparse rewards.
> - Achieving zero-shot sim2real transfer and generalization to varying hardware configurations is possible by incorporating noise into simulated chassis control and training the critic during the imitation learning phase.
