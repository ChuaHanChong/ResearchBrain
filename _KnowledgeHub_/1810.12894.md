# Exploration by Random Network Distillation

> [!summary] Summary
> Random Network Distillation (RND), developed by researchers from OpenAI and the University of Edinburgh, introduces an exploration method for reinforcement learning that addresses sparse reward challenges and the "noisy-TV problem." The method achieved unprecedented performance on hard exploration Atari games like Montezuma's Revenge, demonstrating a general approach to improve exploration in complex environments.

## Problem

- Reinforcement learning agents struggle in environments with sparse rewards, where positive feedback is rare and requires long action sequences, making learning through random exploration inefficient.
- Existing count-based exploration methods do not scale effectively to high-dimensional observation spaces like pixel inputs.
- Prediction error-based exploration methods suffer from the "noisy-TV problem," where agents become fixated on unpredictable but uninformative stochastic elements, leading to inefficient exploration.

## Method

- ==Random Network Distillation== (**RND**) uses two neural networks: a fixed, randomly initialized ==target network== (f) and a ==predictor network== (f̂) that learns to predict the target network's output.
- The ==exploration bonus== for an observation is defined as the ==prediction error== (mean squared error) between the predictor and target network outputs, rewarding states where the predictor has high error.
- The approach combines ==intrinsic and extrinsic rewards== by maintaining separate value functions (==dual value heads==) and utilizes ==non-episodic intrinsic returns== and flexible discount factors to manage different reward types.

## Results

- **RND** achieved state-of-the-art performance on Montezuma's Revenge without demonstrations, regularly finding **22 out of 24** rooms on the first level and occasionally completing the entire level (score of **17,500**).
- Agents trained with non-episodic intrinsic rewards explored significantly more rooms than those using episodic intrinsic rewards, confirming its advantage.
- The method demonstrated strong scaling properties, with increased numbers of parallel environments leading to improved performance, and consistently outperformed PPO and dynamics-based baselines across multiple hard exploration Atari games.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Random Network Distillation (RND) resolves the "noisy-TV problem" by using a fixed, randomly initialized target network, ensuring high prediction error indicates genuine novelty (epistemic uncertainty) rather than environmental stochasticity (aleatoric uncertainty).
> - Maintaining separate value functions for extrinsic and intrinsic rewards (dual value heads) and using non-episodic intrinsic rewards allows for more stable and effective integration of exploration bonuses, encouraging continuous discovery across episode boundaries.
> - The method's simplicity and use of only forward passes through neural networks results in low computational overhead, making it suitable for large-scale distributed training.
