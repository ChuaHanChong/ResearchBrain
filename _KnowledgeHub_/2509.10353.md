# Data-fused MPC with Guarantees: Application to Flying Humanoid Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Istituto Italiano di Tecnologia developed a Data-Fused Model Predictive Control (DFMPC) framework that integrates known physics-based models with data-driven representations of unknown dynamics, explicitly addressing measurement noise and tracking changing setpoints. This framework demonstrated improved CoM and orientation tracking accuracy on a flying humanoid robot while providing theoretical guarantees of recursive feasibility and practical stability.

## Problem

- Traditional Model Predictive Control (MPC) relies on complete, accurate parametric models, which are often unavailable or too complex for systems with unknown dynamics or non-linearities.
- Purely data-driven MPC methods are susceptible to measurement noise in offline data, degrading performance and theoretical guarantees, and can suffer from high computational burdens for real-time applications.
- Existing control methods struggle to simultaneously provide formal guarantees for robustness, constrained operation, and effective tracking of dynamic or unreachable reference signals in hybrid model-data systems.

## Method

- A Data-Fused ==Model Predictive Control== (DF**MPC**) framework was designed to integrate known Linear Time-Invariant (LTI) physics-based models with data-driven representations of unknown dynamics, leveraging Willems' Fundamental Lemma.
- The framework formulates a convex quadratic program (OCP) that includes slack variables and L2 regularization to explicitly account for and mitigate the effects of measurement noise in the data-driven component.
- An 'artificial equilibrium' formulation is incorporated to enable effective tracking of changing or potentially unreachable setpoints, allowing the controller to adapt to dynamic task requirements.

## Results

- The DFMPC (online) achieved superior tracking performance on the iRonCub flying humanoid robot, with a Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) of **0.27** m for Center of Mass and **0.59** degrees for orientation, outperforming purely model-based and robust semi-data-driven MPCs.
- The framework demonstrated real-time computational feasibility, with an average runtime of **6.38** ms and a maximum of **9.69** ms, allowing for a 100 Hz control loop frequency.
- Online updates of the Hankel matrices for the data-driven component significantly improved tracking accuracy (**0.27** m CoM RMSE vs. **0.36** m for fixed matrices), highlighting the practical benefits of adaptation for non-linear systems despite theoretical LTI assumptions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decomposing the system into known and data-driven unknown dynamics allows for effective control of complex systems, balancing model accuracy with adaptability to unmodeled behaviors.
> - Explicitly addressing measurement noise through slack variables and regularization in the OCP enhances robustness, allowing the control system to maintain feasibility and performance with corrupted data.
> - Theoretical guarantees of recursive feasibility and practical exponential stability are provided for the DFMPC framework, ensuring reliable and stable operation under input-output constraints and bounded noise.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Model Predictive Control (MPC) is a well-established control strategy known for its ability to handle multivariable systems and explicitly enforce constraints. Traditionally, MPC relies on accurate, complete parametric models of the system. However, for complex systems, obtaining such models can be challenging or impractical due to unknown dynamics, non-linearities, or environmental uncertainties.

This limitation has spurred the development of data-driven MPC methodologies. A foundational concept in this area is Willems’ Fundamental Lemma, which, along with behavioral system theory, allows for the direct representation of unknown linear time-invariant (LTI) dynamics from measured input-output trajectories without an explicit system identification step. This paradigm led to algorithms such as DeePC, which constructs non-parametric predictors from sufficiently rich data. While purely data-driven approaches offer advantages by circumventing model identification, they also present challenges. These include sensitivity to measurement noise in offline data, which can degrade performance and invalidate theoretical guarantees, and potentially high computational burdens as optimization problem size can scale with data length, hindering real-time deployment.

To mitigate these issues, hybrid modeling schemes have emerged. These approaches combine physics-based models for well-understood subsystems with data-driven representations for uncertain or unknown dynamics. Prior work in this domain includes incorporating model knowledge into DeePC-like formulations to reduce complexity or developing semi-data-driven MPC schemes that use limited parametric knowledge alongside models of residual errors.

This paper positions itself within this hybrid control landscape. It addresses the simultaneous treatment of noisy measurements, constrained operation, and reference tracking, while aiming to provide theoretical guarantees for combined model-based and data-driven systems. The work extends previous data-driven MPC frameworks by explicitly integrating known physics-based models with data-driven components for unknown dynamics, incorporating mechanisms for noise handling, and applying the concept of artificial equilibria for tracking changing setpoints, all while seeking to establish formal guarantees.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop and validate a Data-Fused Model Predictive Control (DFMPC) framework that integrates known physics-based models with data-driven representations of unknown dynamics, specifically addressing practical challenges such as measurement noise and tracking changing setpoints.

The motivation for this work stems from several limitations observed in existing control methodologies when applied to complex robotic systems:

- **Incomplete or Imperfect Physics-Based Models:** For systems like the iRonCub flying humanoid robot, certain dynamics (e.g., rigid body momentum) are well-understood and can be accurately modeled analytically. However, other critical components, such as jet engine thrust dynamics, are inherently non-linear, subject to environmental variations, and difficult to model precisely using first principles alone. Relying solely on simplified model-based MPC for such components can lead to reduced tracking accuracy and robustness.
- **Limitations of Purely Data-Driven Control:** While purely data-driven approaches can capture complex dynamics without explicit modeling, they are often sensitive to measurement noise in the training data. This noise can propagate through the system's prediction, compromising performance and invalidating theoretical guarantees. Furthermore, the computational cost associated with large datasets in purely data-driven MPC can be prohibitive for real-time applications, especially in high-dimensional systems like humanoid robots.
- **Need for Robustness and Guarantees:** For safety-critical applications in robotics, it is essential for control systems to operate under constraints (e.g., actuator limits, state boundaries) and to maintain stability despite uncertainties. Existing hybrid data-driven MPC schemes often face challenges in simultaneously addressing noisy measurements, constrained operation, and reference tracking while providing formal guarantees of recursive feasibility and practical stability.
- **Tracking Changing Setpoints:** Robotic tasks frequently involve tracking dynamic, piecewise-constant, or even unreachable reference signals from high-level planners. Developing a control framework that can effectively track such references while ensuring system stability and constraint satisfaction is a significant challenge. The concept of an "artificial equilibrium" is a known approach for addressing this in linear MPC but needs adaptation for data-driven and hybrid contexts with noise.

To address these motivations, the paper sets out to achieve the following specific objectives:
- To design a Data-Fused Model Predictive Control (DFMPC) framework that effectively combines known LTI physics-based models (Σ1) with data-driven representations of unknown dynamics (Σ2).
- To explicitly incorporate mechanisms, such as slack variables and regularization, to account for and mitigate the effects of measurement noise in the data-driven component.
- To enable the tracking of changing, potentially unreachable, setpoints by leveraging an artificial equilibrium formulation.
- To establish theoretical guarantees of recursive feasibility and practical stability for the proposed DFMPC framework under input-output constraints and for a specific class of reference signals.
- To validate the framework's effectiveness and real-time computational feasibility through simulations on a challenging physical system, the iRonCub flying humanoid robot, integrating its analytical momentum model with a data-driven model of its jet engine dynamics.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed Data-Fused Model Predictive Control (DFMPC) framework integrates model-based and data-driven dynamics within a receding horizon optimal control problem (OCP).

**System Decomposition:**
The methodology considers a composite system (Σc) composed of two interconnected subsystems with unidirectional coupling:
- **Subsystem Σ1 (Known Dynamics):** This component is described by known Linear Time-Invariant (LTI) state-space dynamics (A1, B1, C1, E1). In the iRonCub application, this represents the robot's momentum dynamics.
- **Subsystem Σ2 (Unknown Dynamics):** This component's behavior is unknown but represented by a single input-output trajectory {ud2, yd2} obtained offline. For iRonCub, this models the jet turbine thrust dynamics.

**Data-Driven Modeling with Willems' Fundamental Lemma:**
For the unknown subsystem Σ2, the framework leverages Willems' Fundamental Lemma. This lemma states that for an LTI system, any sufficiently long and persistently exciting input-output trajectory can be used to represent all possible future input-output trajectories of the system. This allows for predictive control without requiring an explicit identification of the system's internal state-space model. The relationship between past data and future trajectories is established through Hankel matrices.

**Artificial Equilibrium for Reference Tracking:**
To enable tracking of changing or potentially unreachable setpoints, the concept of an "artificial equilibrium" (us, ys) is employed. This equilibrium is optimized at each time step to be as close as possible to the desired reference (uref, yref) while still being a valid (data-consistent) equilibrium for the system. This allows the MPC to track transient references without requiring the system to truly reach a steady state, providing flexibility for dynamic tasks.

**DFMPC Optimization Problem Formulation:**
At each sampling time k, a convex quadratic program (OCP) is solved over a prediction horizon L. The OCP structure includes:
- **Cost Function (3a):** This function penalizes several terms:
 - Deviation of predicted outputs y(i) and inputs u(i) from the artificial equilibrium (us, ys) over the prediction horizon.
 - Magnitude of slack variables σ, which compensate for measurement noise in Σ2.
 - Magnitude of the Hankel vector g, which regularizes the data-driven prediction and mitigates overfitting to noise.
 - Deviation of the artificial equilibrium (us, ys) from the high-level reference (uref, yref).
- **Constraints (3b-3e):**
 - **Known Dynamics:** Σ1 dynamics are explicitly included using their state-space equations.
 - **Data-Driven Dynamics:** Σ2 dynamics are represented by a Hankel matrix equation relating the offline data trajectory to the predicted future input-output sequence (u2, y2). Crucially, slack variables σ are introduced here to account for measurement noise in y2.
 - **Initialization:** The known subsystem Σ1 is initialized with its current state x1(0), and the data-driven part Σ2 is initialized with past input-output values (u2, y2) from the current operating point to ensure consistency with historical data.
 - **Terminal Conditions:** At the end of the prediction horizon, a terminal equilibrium condition is imposed on Σ1 (x1(L) = xs1) and on Σ2 (requiring the predicted outputs to be constant at the artificial equilibrium for n2 steps).
 - **Hard Constraints:** Polytopic input and output constraints (u ∈ U, y ∈ Y) are enforced to reflect actuator limits and safe operating regions.

**Handling Measurement Noise:**
The framework explicitly addresses measurement noise (δ) in Σ2, both in the offline data (yd2 = yn2 + δ) and in online measurements. This is achieved through:
- **Slack Variables (σ):** Introduced directly into the Hankel matrix equation for Σ2 predictions. These variables absorb the discrepancies arising from noise, allowing the optimization problem to remain feasible even with corrupted measurements.
- **Regularization of 'g':** The l2-norm of the Hankel vector g is included in the cost function (∥g(k)∥2Λ). This regularization enhances numerical conditioning and helps prevent overfitting to noisy measurements in the Hankel matrix, improving prediction robustness.

**Theoretical Guarantees:**
The paper provides theoretical guarantees of recursive feasibility and practical stability under specific assumptions:
- **Recursive Feasibility:** Proposition 2 and Proposition 3 address recursive feasibility. Proposition 1 quantifies the output prediction error due to noise and the mismatch between predicted and actual trajectories. This error bound, combined with the concept of a "safe reference" (i.e., a reference well within output constraints), is used with Lemma 1 (signed-distance to boundary). Proposition 2 establishes that if the reference is sufficiently "safe," the predicted outputs will also remain within a safe margin of the constraints, and a feasible candidate for the next control step can be constructed. Proposition 3 further demonstrates that the optimal solution will not choose an unsafe trajectory, effectively guaranteeing recursive feasibility without relying on explicit constraint tightening mechanisms that can increase computational complexity.
- **Practical Stability:** Proposition 4 establishes practical exponential stability. It relies on a Lyapunov candidate function constructed from the optimal cost function of the OCP and an Input-to-Output Stability (IOSS) property of the composite system. The analysis shows that the Lyapunov function decreases by a certain factor at each step, ensuring the system state converges to a neighborhood around the artificial equilibrium, with the size of this neighborhood dependent on the noise level (ϵ).

**Application to iRonCub Robot:**
The iRonCub flying humanoid robot serves as the case study:
- **Σ1:** Represents the robot's momentum dynamics, as described in prior work.
- **Σ2:** Models the jet turbines' thrust dynamics. Due to the inherent non-linearity of the turbines, a key practical adaptation is the **online update strategy** for the Hankel matrices of Σ2. While the theoretical guarantees are derived under LTI assumptions and a fixed Hankel matrix, the online update strategy adapts the data-driven model to the varying turbine behavior (locally approximating it as LTI at high sampling rates). The linearization error is treated as a disturbance handled by slack variables.
- **Offline Data Collection:** Input-output data for the turbines is gathered by exciting them with frequency-sweeping step inputs to ensure persistent excitation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents both theoretical results concerning recursive feasibility and practical stability, as well as simulation-based validation on a flying humanoid robot.

**Theoretical Findings:**

- **Output Prediction Error Bound (Proposition 1):** The paper quantifies the prediction error between the actual system output and the optimal predicted output for the composite system. This bound explicitly depends on constants related to the known and unknown subsystems, the noise upper bound (ϵ), the magnitude of the Hankel vector (g), and the slack variables (σ). This provides a basis for understanding how noise and modeling inaccuracies propagate through the prediction.
- **Recursive Feasibility Guarantees (Propositions 2 and 3):**
 - **Feasible Candidate Construction:** Proposition 2 demonstrates that if the initial system state is in a compact feasible set and the reference signal (yref) is sufficiently "safe" (i.e., well inside the output constraints by a margin related to the prediction error bound), then a feasible candidate input-output trajectory exists for the next control step. This candidate ensures the actual outputs over the immediate future steps remain within the hard constraints.
 - **Optimal Choice of Safe Trajectory:** Proposition 3 establishes that under a specific "cost-dominance inequality," the optimizer will preferentially select a trajectory whose predicted outputs lie within the feasible region, even when considering potentially unsafe alternatives. This condition ensures that the DFMPC remains feasible over time (n2-step recursive feasibility) for all initial states within the specified feasible set. These guarantees are achieved without relying on explicit constraint tightening, which can increase the complexity of the OCP.
- **Practical Exponential Stability (Proposition 4):** The paper establishes practical exponential stability for the closed-loop system. By defining a Lyapunov candidate function based on the optimal cost and the IOSS property of the composite system, it is shown that the system state converges exponentially to a neighborhood around the optimal artificial equilibrium. The size of this neighborhood is proportional to the square of the noise bound (ϵ2), indicating that stability is maintained in the presence of bounded measurement noise.

**Simulation Results (iRonCub Robot):**

The DFMPC framework was validated on the iRonCub flying humanoid robot through comparative simulations against a purely model-based MPC (from) and a robust semi-data-driven MPC (rSD-MPC from). Two variants of DFMPC were tested: one with online Hankel matrix updates (DFMPC online) and one with fixed Hankel matrices (DFMPC fixed).

- **Tracking Performance (Figure 3, Table I):**
 - **CoM Tracking:** DFMPC (online) achieved the lowest Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) for Center of Mass (CoM) tracking (0.27 m). This was an improvement over the model-based MPC (0.47 m) and slightly better than rSD-MPC (0.28 m).
 - **Orientation Tracking:** All data-driven variants (DFMPC online, DFMPC fixed, rSD-MPC) exhibited lower orientation errors (roll, pitch, yaw) compared to the purely model-based MPC. DFMPC (online) showed an RMSE of 0.59 degrees, while model-based MPC had 2.46 degrees.
- **Ablation Study (Online vs. Fixed Hankel):**
 - The comparison between DFMPC (online) and DFMPC (fixed) revealed that online updates significantly improved tracking performance. DFMPC (fixed) had a CoM RMSE of 0.36 m, which was worse than DFMPC (online), despite similar rotation accuracy. This finding indicates the practical benefit of dynamically updating the data-driven model to cope with the inherent non-linearity and variability of the jet turbines in a real-world setting, even if it deviates from the strict LTI assumptions of the theoretical guarantees.
- **Computational Feasibility (Table I):**
 - DFMPC (online) demonstrated real-time computational feasibility, with an average runtime of 6.38 ms and a maximum runtime of 9.69 ms. This allows for operation at a 100 Hz control loop frequency.
 - rSD-MPC showed comparable runtimes (7.31 ms average, 13.19 ms max).
 - The purely model-based MPC was the fastest (2.2 ms average, 4.3 ms max), as expected due to the absence of data-driven components in the optimization. However, its speed came at the cost of reduced tracking accuracy.

In summary, the results indicate that the DFMPC framework offers improved tracking accuracy and robustness for complex, hybrid systems like iRonCub, effectively combining the strengths of physics-based models with data-driven adaptation, while maintaining computational feasibility for real-time applications.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of control systems by addressing the challenges of controlling complex, partially unknown, and noisy dynamic systems. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Advancement in Hybrid Control Systems:** The proposed DFMPC framework provides a structured and theoretically-supported method for integrating known physics-based models with data-driven representations of uncertain dynamics. This is significant for systems where complete analytical models are either impossible to derive or are too complex for real-time implementation, while purely data-driven methods might lack robustness or computational efficiency. It bridges the gap between model-based and data-driven control, leveraging the strengths of both.
- **Robustness to Unknown Dynamics and Measurement Noise:** The explicit incorporation of slack variables and regularization in the optimization problem allows the DFMPC to effectively handle measurement noise and unmodeled dynamics in the data-driven component. This enhances the robustness of the controller, which is crucial for real-world applications where perfect models and noise-free measurements are unrealistic. The online Hankel update strategy further boosts practical robustness against system non-linearities.
- **Guaranteed Performance:** The theoretical guarantees of recursive feasibility and practical stability under input-output constraints are a key contribution. These guarantees provide a formal basis for the controller's reliability, which is essential for deploying autonomous systems in safety-critical environments. The ability to maintain stability and feasibility in the presence of noise and changing references, without resorting to overly conservative or computationally expensive constraint tightening, is a notable advantage.
- **Applicability to Complex Robotic Systems:** The successful validation on the iRonCub flying humanoid robot demonstrates the framework's practical utility for high-dimensional, complex robotic platforms. Flying humanoids, with their coupled rigid-body dynamics and highly non-linear jet engine behavior, represent a challenging control problem. The DFMPC's ability to enhance tracking accuracy for both CoM and orientation while remaining real-time feasible suggests its potential for improving the autonomy and performance of advanced robots in diverse tasks.
- **Real-time Computational Feasibility:** Despite integrating data-driven components, the DFMPC maintains computational efficiency suitable for real-time control at frequencies like 100 Hz. This addresses a common limitation of data-driven approaches, which can become computationally intensive with large datasets, and paves the way for practical implementation in fast-reacting robotic systems.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The paper identifies a clear direction for future work: formally extending the theoretical guarantees to adaptive data-driven controllers for inherently nonlinear systems. This points to a significant open research problem in control theory, where current guarantees often rely on LTI assumptions or restrictive conditions. The practical success of the online Hankel update strategy in simulations motivates further theoretical investigation into such adaptive schemes.

In conclusion, this work offers a robust and theoretically-grounded hybrid control solution with demonstrated practical effectiveness on a challenging robotic system. It pushes the boundaries of how model knowledge and data are synergistically used in predictive control, with implications for a wide range of autonomous systems operating in uncertain environments.
