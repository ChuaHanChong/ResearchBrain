# Data-fused MPC with Guarantees: Application to Flying Humanoid Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Istituto Italiano di Tecnologia developed a Data-Fused Model Predictive Control (DFMPC) framework that integrates known physics-based models with data-driven representations of unknown dynamics, explicitly addressing measurement noise and tracking changing setpoints. This framework demonstrated improved CoM and orientation tracking accuracy on a flying humanoid robot while providing theoretical guarantees of recursive feasibility and practical stability.

## Problem

- Traditional Model Predictive Control (MPC) relies on complete, accurate parametric models, which are often unavailable or too complex for systems with unknown dynamics or non-linearities.
- Purely data-driven MPC methods are susceptible to measurement noise in offline data, degrading performance and theoretical guarantees, and can suffer from high computational burdens for real-time applications.
- Existing control methods struggle to simultaneously provide formal guarantees for robustness, constrained operation, and effective tracking of dynamic or unreachable reference signals in hybrid model-data systems.

## Method

- A Data-Fused ==Model Predictive Control== (DF**MPC**) framework was designed to integrate known Linear Time-Invariant (LTI) physics-based models with data-driven representations of unknown dynamics, leveraging Willems' Fundamental Lemma.
- The framework formulates a convex quadratic program (OCP) that includes slack variables and L2 regularization to explicitly account for and mitigate the effects of measurement noise in the data-driven component.
- An 'artificial equilibrium' formulation is incorporated to enable effective tracking of changing or potentially unreachable setpoints, allowing the controller to adapt to dynamic task requirements.

## Results

- The DFMPC (online) achieved superior tracking performance on the iRonCub flying humanoid robot, with a Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) of **0.27** m for Center of Mass and **0.59** degrees for orientation, outperforming purely model-based and robust semi-data-driven MPCs.
- The framework demonstrated real-time computational feasibility, with an average runtime of **6.38** ms and a maximum of **9.69** ms, allowing for a 100 Hz control loop frequency.
- Online updates of the Hankel matrices for the data-driven component significantly improved tracking accuracy (**0.27** m CoM RMSE vs. **0.36** m for fixed matrices), highlighting the practical benefits of adaptation for non-linear systems despite theoretical LTI assumptions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decomposing the system into known and data-driven unknown dynamics allows for effective control of complex systems, balancing model accuracy with adaptability to unmodeled behaviors.
> - Explicitly addressing measurement noise through slack variables and regularization in the OCP enhances robustness, allowing the control system to maintain feasibility and performance with corrupted data.
> - Theoretical guarantees of recursive feasibility and practical exponential stability are provided for the DFMPC framework, ensuring reliable and stable operation under input-output constraints and bounded noise.
