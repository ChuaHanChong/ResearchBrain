# GROW: Aligning GRPO with State-Action Modeling for Open-World VLM Agents

> [!summary] Summary
> The GROW framework adapts Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) for Vision-Language Model (VLM) agents in open-world settings by decomposing long trajectories into state-action samples for efficient credit assignment. This approach achieved state-of-the-art performance in Minecraft across embodied, GUI, and combat tasks, improving average success rates by up to 29.3 percentage points on GUI tasks while enhancing generalization to unseen scenarios.

## Problem

- Existing open-world VLM agents primarily rely on Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), which is limited by the high cost and difficulty of collecting extensive, high-quality expert demonstrations.
- Direct application of Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) to open-world VLM tasks is challenging due to excessively long interaction trajectories that exceed VLM context limits and introduce irrelevant noise, degrading policy gradient estimation.
- The standard GRPO objective assumes grouped samples are conditioned on identical prompts, which is not suitable for multi-turn open-world tasks where local states vary.

## Method

- The **GROW** framework formulates open-world tasks as an MDP and adapts ==GRPO== by changing the optimization unit from full trajectories to individual state-action samples.
- It decomposes long rollout trajectories into (state, action) pairs, assigning discounted episodic rewards backward to each sample to reflect its temporal relevance to task completion.
- Policy optimization then computes relative advantages among these decomposed state-action samples within their respective rollout groups, using a PPO-like clipped objective.

## Results

- GROW achieved state-of-the-art performance in Minecraft, improving Average Success Rate (ASR) by +**9.2%** for embodied tasks (to **59.6%**), +**29.3%** for GUI tasks (to **68.4%**), and +**9.8%** for combat tasks (to **49.0%**).
- The framework demonstrated strong generalization, significantly improving ASR on RL-unseen tasks (e.g., from **21.3%** to **68.2%** for GUI tasks), indicating the acquisition of transferable interaction skills.
- Ablation studies showed GROW outperformed PPO in terms of success rates and convergence speed, and specific behavior-level analysis confirmed enhanced skills like stable target fixation and active target reacquisition.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decomposing long trajectories into state-action samples effectively mitigates VLM context length issues and reduces noise, making advanced RL algorithms like GRPO applicable to open-world, multi-turn tasks.
> - A surrogate analysis demonstrates that the modified GRPO objective for state-action samples maintains a meaningful relative policy optimization signal even when samples are conditioned on different local states.
> - Propagating sparse episodic rewards backward with a discount factor to individual state-action samples effectively assigns learning signals, emphasizing decisions closer to task completion.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Vision-Language Model (VLM) agents for open-world tasks is a significant area of research, as these agents require multi-turn visual perception and action execution to achieve complex objectives. Existing approaches predominantly rely on Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) using expert demonstrations. While SFT has shown initial progress, it faces limitations such as the high cost and difficulty of collecting large quantities of high-quality expert data, as well as potential performance ceilings compared to agents trained with reinforcement learning (RL). This context highlights a gap in the application of advanced RL methods for training open-world VLM agents.

Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) has emerged as an effective RL algorithm for improving foundation model policies, particularly in multi-turn interaction settings. GRPO's mechanism of computing relative advantages across multiple sampled outputs within the same group, without requiring an explicit value model, makes it potentially suitable for large-scale VLM optimization where value estimation can be unstable or costly. However, direct application of standard GRPO to open-world tasks presents a challenge. Standard GRPO computes advantages across full trajectories that are typically conditioned on identical prompts. In open-world environments, agent-environment interactions can extend over many steps, leading to excessively long trajectories. These long trajectories can exceed the maximum context length of VLMs and introduce substantial irrelevant noise, thereby degrading policy gradient estimation.

This work addresses the aforementioned limitations by adapting GRPO to open-world VLM agents. It aims to overcome the reliance on SFT and to mitigate the practical challenges of applying standard GRPO in environments characterized by long interaction sequences. By proposing a novel framework, GROW, the paper attempts to enable efficient and scalable RL for VLM agents in complex, dynamic open-world settings.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an effective reinforcement learning framework, termed GROW, for training open-world Vision-Language Model (VLM) agents. This framework is specifically designed to address the challenges encountered when applying advanced RL algorithms, such as Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), to tasks requiring extensive multi-turn interactions in complex environments.

The motivation for this work stems from several observed limitations in the current state of VLM agent development:
- **Reliance on Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)**: Many existing VLM agents for open-world tasks are trained primarily through SFT on expert demonstrations. This approach is limited by the inherent difficulties and high costs associated with collecting large volumes of high-quality, task-specific expert data. Furthermore, prior research has indicated that SFT alone may not achieve the same level of performance as RL-trained agents, especially in terms of generalization and adaptation to novel scenarios.
- **Ineffectiveness of Direct GRPO Application**: GRPO has demonstrated efficacy in refining foundation model policies by utilizing group-wise relative optimization, which circumvents the need for an additional value model. This characteristic is appealing for VLM optimization, where value estimation can be unstable. However, directly transferring standard GRPO to open-world tasks is problematic. Standard GRPO computes advantages based on full trajectories originating from the same prompt context. In open-world tasks, interactions are often prolonged, leading to trajectories that become excessively long. Such long contexts can exceed the input token limits of VLMs and introduce considerable noise, thereby diminishing the effectiveness of policy gradient estimation and agent decision-making, which often relies on short-horizon states.

To address these motivations, the key objectives of GROW are:
- To propose an RL framework that effectively adapts GRPO for open-world VLM agents by modifying the optimization unit from full trajectories to state-action samples.
- To mitigate the issues of excessively long context and noise accumulation that arise from using full trajectories in multi-turn open-world tasks.
- To provide a theoretical justification, through surrogate analysis, demonstrating that the reformulated objective maintains a meaningful relative policy optimization signal even when grouped samples are conditioned on different local states.
- To empirically validate the effectiveness of the proposed framework by achieving state-of-the-art performance in complex open-world environments, specifically Minecraft, across a diverse set of tasks (embodied, GUI, combat).
- To demonstrate that the framework can foster generalization to unseen tasks and facilitate the acquisition of robust, reusable interaction skills.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The GROW framework formulates open-world tasks as a Markov Decision Process (MDP) defined by ⟨C, S, A, R, γ⟩, where C represents the task space, S the state space (visual observations plus task instructions), A the primitive keyboard and mouse actions, R a sparse binary reward (1 for task completion, 0 otherwise), and γ the discount factor. A trajectory τ is a sequence of state-action pairs.

The core of the GROW methodology involves adapting the GRPO algorithm to address the challenges of multi-turn interactions in open-world environments. This adaptation comprises three main components: trajectory decomposition, policy optimization with state-action samples, and a surrogate analysis for theoretical justification.

#### 3.1. GROW Framework Overview

Figure 2 illustrates the GROW framework. Unlike standard GRPO which processes full-trajectory samples, GROW first collects trajectories from parallel environments. These trajectories are then subjected to decomposition. The relative advantages are subsequently computed among the resulting state-action samples within their respective rollout groups. This design allows for managing context lengths while preserving the state-action modeling paradigm common in open-world tasks.

#### 3.2. Decomposition of Rollout Trajectories

During the rollout phase, `G` parallel environments generate trajectories {τ_i}. In standard GRPO, each τ_i would be a single training sample. However, for open-world tasks with many interaction turns, full trajectories can lead to excessive context accumulation and irrelevant information.
To counter this, GROW decomposes each trajectory `τ = {(s_1, a_1)..., (s_H, a_H)}` into individual state-action samples `(s_t, a_t)`. Each transition `(s_t, a_t)` functions as an independent optimization unit. To assign learning signals to these decomposed samples, the sparse episodic reward `R(τ_i)` for a successful trajectory is propagated backward with a discount factor γ: `r_i,t = γ^(H_i-t) R(τ_i)`. This temporal discounting ensures that samples closer to task completion receive stronger signals, reflecting their higher causal relevance. The initial rollout group of trajectories is thus transformed into a group of state-action samples `G_s = {(s_i,t, a_i,t, r_i,t) | i ∈ [1, G], t ∈ [1, H_i]}`.

#### 3.3. Policy Optimization with State-Action Samples

With the decomposed state-action samples, GROW computes relative advantages `A_i,t` across these samples within each rollout group. The discounted rewards `r_i,t` are normalized using their mean `μ` and standard deviation `σ` across the group: `A_i,t = (r_i,t - μ) / σ`.
This leads to the training objective `J`, which is formulated as:
`J = E_{c~C, a~π_old(·|s)} [1/G Σ_{i=1}^G 1/H_i Σ_{t=1}^{H_i} min(ρ_i,t(θ)A_i,t, clip(ρ_i,t(θ), 1-ϵ, 1+ϵ)A_i,t)]`
where `ρ_i,t(θ) = π_θ(a_i,t|s_i,t) / π_old(a_i,t|s_i,t)` is the probability ratio between the new and old policies, and `ϵ` is a clipping parameter, similar to PPO.

#### 3.4. Surrogate Analysis

A theoretical concern arises because, after trajectory decomposition, the grouped samples are conditioned on different local states rather than an identical prompt, which deviates from standard GRPO's assumption. The paper provides a surrogate analysis under the simplifying assumption that trajectory length `H_i` is approximately constant `H` for a given task.
The analysis defines `C_γ = (1 - γ^H) / (H(1 - γ))` as the average temporal discount coefficient within a trajectory. The mean reward `μ` can be approximated as `C_γ S`, where `S` is the average trajectory-level return.
The centered reward `ˆA_i,t = r_i,t - μ` is then analyzed, omitting the standard deviation `σ` for clarity. The objective `J` is decomposed into two terms: `J = C_γ J_traj + J_step`.
- `J_traj`: This term `E[1/G Σ_{i=1}^G 1/H_i Σ_{t=1}^{H_i} ρ_i,t (R(τ_i) - S)]` preserves the trajectory-level relative preference. It reinforces successful trajectories at a higher level, based on their overall reward `R(τ_i)` compared to the average `S`.
- `J_step`: This term `E[1/G Σ_{i=1}^G 1/H_i Σ_{t=1}^{H_i} ρ_i,t (γ^(H_i-t) - C_γ) R(τ_i)]` captures temporal discounting effects specifically on successful trajectories. It refines credit assignment within successful trajectories by emphasizing decisions that are more directly related to the final success through the `(γ^(H_i-t) - C_γ)` factor.
The analysis concludes that even with varying local states, the objective remains effective, facilitating both trajectory-level comparisons and step-level signal refinement.

#### 3.5. Experimental Setup

Experiments were conducted in Minecraft (Java Edition, v1.16.5) using the Qwen2-VL-7B-Instruct VLM as the base model. The agent perceives raw 640x360 RGB frames and executes primitive human-like keyboard and mouse actions. The MCU benchmark, comprising over 800 tasks across embodied, GUI, and combat categories, was used for evaluation. Performance was assessed using Average Success Rate (ASR) and average Steps per task.
The training involved a cold-start phase with 3M state-action samples for Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), followed by RL training on 8 representative tasks. The SFT dataset was constructed from JARVIS-VLA and VPT, involving detailed labeling, cleaning, chunking, and balancing. RL training utilized the Verl framework for distributed optimization over 240 iterations for approximately 5 days, with a discount factor of `γ = 0.995`.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents empirical results demonstrating the effectiveness of the GROW framework across various benchmarks and analyses.

#### 4.1. State-of-the-Art Performance in Minecraft (Table 1)

GROW achieved the highest performance across all three Minecraft task categories compared to existing VLM agents.
- **Embodied Tasks**: The Average Success Rate (ASR) improved from the previous best of 50.4% to 59.6% (+9.2 percentage points). The average steps required for task completion decreased from 263 to 128.
- **GUI Tasks**: GROW exhibited the largest performance gain, increasing ASR from 39.1% to 68.4% (+29.3 percentage points). The average steps reduced from 291 to 248.
- **Combat Tasks**: ASR improved from 39.2% to 49.0% (+9.8 percentage points). The average steps decreased from 248 to 172.
These results indicate that GROW not only enhances the agent's ability to successfully complete diverse Minecraft tasks but also significantly improves execution efficiency, leading to more direct and effective task completion.

#### 4.2. Generalization to Unseen Tasks (Table 2)

The framework demonstrated substantial improvements on both tasks used for RL training (in-domain) and tasks not included in the RL training set (out-of-domain).
- **RL-trained tasks**: ASR increased from 35.0% to 90.0% for embodied tasks, from 10.0% to 92.5% for GUI tasks, and from 45.0% to 85.0% for combat tasks.
- **RL-unseen tasks**: Consistent improvements were observed: ASR rose from 41.9% to 59.2% for embodied tasks, from 21.3% to 68.2% for GUI tasks, and from 19.8% to 47.5% for combat tasks.
This indicates that GROW fosters the acquisition of transferable interaction skills rather than merely memorizing specific training trajectories, demonstrating strong generalization capabilities.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study: Comparison with PPO (Table 3, Figure 3)

A comparison with Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), a widely used RL algorithm, showed that GROW provided more effective policy optimization.
- GROW achieved higher success rates and provided stronger generalization to RL-untrained tasks than PPO under the same training budget. For instance, on RL-trained tasks, GROW (with γ=0.995) achieved 59.6% ASR for embodied tasks, 68.4% for GUI, and 49.0% for combat, compared to PPO's 58.2%, 43.9%, and 45.4% respectively.
- Learning curves (Figure 3) further illustrated that GROW achieved higher success rates faster and converged earlier on most training tasks, suggesting more stable and efficient policy improvement dynamics.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study: Discount Factor (γ) (Table 3)

The choice of the discount factor γ was also investigated.
- A γ of 0.995 yielded the best overall performance, particularly for GUI tasks (68.4% ASR).
- Lower values of γ (0.9 and 0.95) resulted in decreased performance, especially for tasks requiring many interaction steps, indicating that an excessively small γ can make the model too short-sighted by weakening long-range credit signals.

#### 4.5. Behavior-Level Analysis (Figure 4)

Analysis on diagnostic tasks in Minecraft revealed specific skill improvements:
- **Stable Target Fixation (Mine Obsidian with Iron Pickaxe)**: GROW achieved 63.3% success, outperforming baselines which failed. This suggests enhanced precise control over consecutive interaction steps.
- **Active Target Reacquisition (Kill Witch Out of Sight)**: GROW achieved 50.0% success, while baselines failed. This indicates strengthened search, visual tracking, and sustained engagement behaviors, attributed to higher propagated rewards for active target-searching.
- **Distractor-Robust GUI Operation (Craft Cake with Distractions)**: GROW reached 50.0% success (JARVIS-VLA 40.0%, Initialized Policy 2.0%). This demonstrates improved precise recipe selection amidst visual clutter, linked to the discounted trajectory reward encouraging efficient execution paths.

#### 4.6. Extension to Simulated Language Table (Appendix E)

To demonstrate wider applicability, GROW was re-trained on the simulated Language Table environment.
- GROW achieved the highest success rate across all five task families (average 79.41%), outperforming PPO (average 65.02%) and the initialized policy (average 7.7%).
- Notable gains were observed on spatial reasoning tasks, indicating stronger grounding of visual states and action decisions in a continuous-control embodied manipulation setting.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The GROW framework represents a contribution to the field of reinforcement learning for Vision-Language Model (VLM) agents in open-world environments. Its significance and potential impact can be considered in several areas:

**Advancement in RL for VLM Agents:** GROW directly addresses a critical limitation in applying advanced RL algorithms like GRPO to open-world VLM tasks: the excessive context length and noise introduced by long interaction trajectories. By decomposing trajectories into state-action samples, GROW provides a mechanism to make GRPO practically applicable and efficient in such complex settings. This innovation broadens the applicability of GRPO to multi-turn, long-horizon decision-making problems.

**Improved Agent Capabilities and Performance:** The consistent achievement of state-of-the-art performance across diverse and challenging Minecraft tasks (embodied navigation, GUI manipulation, and dynamic combat) demonstrates that GROW significantly enhances VLM agents' abilities. The framework not only improves task completion rates but also substantially reduces the number of interaction steps, indicating increased execution efficiency. This suggests that GROW enables agents to learn more direct and effective strategies.

**Enhanced Generalization and Skill Transfer:** A notable finding is GROW's strong generalization to unseen tasks. This indicates that the framework fosters the acquisition of robust, reusable interaction skills rather than merely memorizing specific training trajectories. The ability to transfer learned behaviors to novel scenarios is a step towards developing more generalist and adaptable AI agents. The behavior-level analysis further illustrates that GROW cultivates sophisticated skills such as stable target fixation, active target reacquisition, and distractor-robust operation.

**Theoretical Justification for Modified GRPO:** The surrogate analysis provides a theoretical basis for GROW's objective function. By demonstrating that the objective preserves meaningful relative policy optimization signals even when grouped samples are conditioned on different local states, the research contributes to the theoretical understanding of GRPO's adaptability beyond its original formulation.

**Broader Applicability:** The successful application of GROW in both the Minecraft environment and the simulated Language Table suggests that its principles are not confined to a single domain. This indicates potential for extending the framework to a wider array of open-world simulators and real-world embodied AI applications that involve multi-turn interactions and complex visual reasoning.

**Future Directions in Agent Development:** The identified limitations, such as the absence of a memory module for long-horizon tasks, highlight avenues for future research. GROW provides a foundation upon which subsequent efforts can build, potentially integrating memory, planning, and more diverse training environments to further expand the agent's skill repertoire and general applicability.

In summary, GROW represents a methodological advancement for training open-world VLM agents, enabling the use of advanced RL techniques to achieve higher performance, greater efficiency, and better generalization in complex, dynamic environments.
