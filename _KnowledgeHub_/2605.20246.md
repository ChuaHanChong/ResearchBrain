# GROW: Aligning GRPO with State-Action Modeling for Open-World VLM Agents

> [!summary] Summary
> The GROW framework adapts Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) for Vision-Language Model (VLM) agents in open-world settings by decomposing long trajectories into state-action samples for efficient credit assignment. This approach achieved state-of-the-art performance in Minecraft across embodied, GUI, and combat tasks, improving average success rates by up to 29.3 percentage points on GUI tasks while enhancing generalization to unseen scenarios.

## Problem

- Existing open-world VLM agents primarily rely on Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), which is limited by the high cost and difficulty of collecting extensive, high-quality expert demonstrations.
- Direct application of Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) to open-world VLM tasks is challenging due to excessively long interaction trajectories that exceed VLM context limits and introduce irrelevant noise, degrading policy gradient estimation.
- The standard GRPO objective assumes grouped samples are conditioned on identical prompts, which is not suitable for multi-turn open-world tasks where local states vary.

## Method

- The **GROW** framework formulates open-world tasks as an MDP and adapts ==GRPO== by changing the optimization unit from full trajectories to individual state-action samples.
- It decomposes long rollout trajectories into (state, action) pairs, assigning discounted episodic rewards backward to each sample to reflect its temporal relevance to task completion.
- Policy optimization then computes relative advantages among these decomposed state-action samples within their respective rollout groups, using a PPO-like clipped objective.

## Results

- GROW achieved state-of-the-art performance in Minecraft, improving Average Success Rate (ASR) by +**9.2%** for embodied tasks (to **59.6%**), +**29.3%** for GUI tasks (to **68.4%**), and +**9.8%** for combat tasks (to **49.0%**).
- The framework demonstrated strong generalization, significantly improving ASR on RL-unseen tasks (e.g., from **21.3%** to **68.2%** for GUI tasks), indicating the acquisition of transferable interaction skills.
- Ablation studies showed GROW outperformed PPO in terms of success rates and convergence speed, and specific behavior-level analysis confirmed enhanced skills like stable target fixation and active target reacquisition.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decomposing long trajectories into state-action samples effectively mitigates VLM context length issues and reduces noise, making advanced RL algorithms like GRPO applicable to open-world, multi-turn tasks.
> - A surrogate analysis demonstrates that the modified GRPO objective for state-action samples maintains a meaningful relative policy optimization signal even when samples are conditioned on different local states.
> - Propagating sparse episodic rewards backward with a discount factor to individual state-action samples effectively assigns learning signals, emphasizing decisions closer to task completion.
