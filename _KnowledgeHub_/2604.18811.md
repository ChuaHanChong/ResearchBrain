# Rethinking Dataset Distillation: Hard Truths about Soft Labels

> [!summary] Summary
> This research systematically analyzes the impact of dataset quality, size, and computational resources across hard-label, fixed soft-label, and extensive soft-label supervision regimes in dataset distillation. It clarifies that extensive soft-label supervision often masks the true benefit of synthesized data quality, and introduces CAD-Prune and CA2D, new methods achieving state-of-the-art performance in the hard-label setting on ImageNet-1K.

## Problem

- The true contribution of data quality in large-scale Dataset Distillation (DD) methods, especially those using extensive soft labels (SL+KD regime), is unclear, as simple random baselines sometimes achieve comparable performance.
- Current DD evaluation practices often obscure the genuine impact of synthesized data quality, particularly by relying on soft-label supervision.
- There is a lack of efficient, 'zero-shot' evaluation metrics to assess the alignment of distillation objectives with downstream model generalization for larger models and datasets.

## Method

- Conducted a systematic ==scalability analysis== of dataset quality, size, and computational budget across Hard-Label (HL), Fixed Soft-Label (SL), and Soft Label + Knowledge Distillation (SL+KD) regimes.
- Systematically evaluated large-scale and small-scale DD methods exclusively in the HL setting against strong coreset baselines to isolate the effect of data quality.
- Introduced the Distillation Correlation Score (DCS), an efficient 'zero-shot' metric to evaluate distillation objectives without requiring full dataset synthesis and training.
- Proposed CAD-Prune (Compute-Aware Difficulty Pruning) and CA2D (Compute-Aligned Dataset Distillation) for improved data selection and synthesis tailored to the HL setting.

## Results

- In the SL+KD regime, model performance on ImageNet-**1K** saturated based on compute budget, showing insensitivity to data quality or size (IPC 10-700) and nullifying the Pareto frontier.
- Only RDED among large-scale DD methods reliably outperformed random baselines in the HL setting on ImageNet-**1K**, but still lagged behind strong coreset methods like EL2N-Best.
- The Distillation Correlation Score (DCS) revealed that trajectory-matching objectives (TM/DATM) fail to correlate with downstream generalization for larger models (ResNet-18), indicating a lack of scalability.
- The proposed CA2D method achieved state-of-the-art performance in the HL setting on ImageNet-**1K** across various IPCs (10, 50, 100), surpassing RDED and other existing DD baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Extensive soft-label supervision (SL+KD regime) primarily leverages the teacher model's knowledge, saturating performance quickly regardless of distilled dataset quality or size beyond a minimal threshold.
> - Fixed soft labels tend to homogenize sample difficulty, limiting the additional benefit of high-quality data beyond basic dataset size in the SL regime.
> - Many large-scale DD methods fail to genuinely outperform random baselines or strong coreset methods in the Hard-Label (HL) setting, with trajectory-matching objectives showing no correlation with generalization for larger models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of data-efficient learning aims to reduce the computational and storage costs associated with training deep learning models on large datasets. Two prominent sub-fields addressing this challenge are Dataset Distillation (DD) and Coreset Selection. Dataset Distillation focuses on synthesizing a small, representative dataset that can train a model to performance comparable to one trained on the original large dataset. Coreset Selection, conversely, identifies a small, informative subset of real data points from the original dataset.

Recent advancements in large-scale DD methods, particularly those built on frameworks like SRe2L, have shown promising results on large datasets such as ImageNet-1K. These methods often leverage soft labels, derived from a teacher model, to train student models, a practice termed the SL+KD (Soft Label + Knowledge Distillation) regime by the authors. This approach has been widely perceived as successful in enabling DD to scale effectively.

However, prior work by Qin et al. presented a counter-narrative, suggesting that the success of these large-scale DD methods in the SL+KD regime might primarily stem from the extensive use of soft labels rather than the intrinsic quality of the synthesized images. Their findings indicated that simple random image baselines could achieve comparable performance to state-of-the-art DD methods when provided with sufficient soft labels. This observation contrasts with the established understanding in coreset literature, where high-quality coresets consistently demonstrate superior performance over random subsets, particularly in the hard-label (HL) setting where only ground-truth labels are used.

This paper addresses this discrepancy by systematically re-evaluating the role of data quality, dataset size, and computational resources across different label supervision regimes prevalent in data-efficient learning. It aims to clarify the conditions under which dataset quality genuinely contributes to performance improvements, thereby providing a foundation for advancing dataset distillation beyond potentially misleading evaluation practices.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to conduct a systematic and comprehensive analysis of how dataset quality, size, and computational budget interact to influence model performance across different label supervision regimes in data-efficient learning. This investigation is driven by several specific motivations:

1. **Understand the discrepancy in SL+KD performance:** The work is motivated by the observed contradiction where large-scale DD methods show strong performance in the SL+KD regime, yet simple random baselines achieve similar results when extensive soft labels are used, as highlighted by Qin et al.. This stands in contrast to coreset literature where data quality significantly impacts performance in the hard-label (HL) setting. A detailed scalability analysis is undertaken to determine the true contribution of data quality in the presence of abundant soft labels. The authors specifically ask: "Is there any scope of improvement to be made in dataset distillation by continuing to operate under the SL+KD regime?"

2. **Examine the role of data quality in the fixed Soft-Label (SL) setting:** Beyond the SL+KD regime, many DD methods employ a fixed soft-label (SL) setting where one soft label per image is used. The paper aims to extend its scalability analysis to this regime, investigating how data quality, size, and compute interact when soft labels are present but not as extensively used as in SL+KD. This includes exploring how sample importance and difficulty scores behave in this context.

3. **Systematically evaluate DD methods in the Hard-Label (HL) setting:** Given the potential for soft labels to obscure the true impact of data quality, the research is motivated to conduct a thorough evaluation of both large-scale and small-scale DD methods exclusively in the HL setting. This is crucial because the HL setting is where data quality is understood to be a primary driver of performance, and many recent large-scale DD methods have moved away from reporting HL results. The goal is to identify which DD methods, if any, genuinely outperform random baselines and competitive coreset methods when relying solely on hard labels.

4. **Develop an efficient metric for evaluating distillation objectives (DCS):** Recognizing the computational expense of synthesizing distilled datasets and training downstream models for evaluation, the authors are motivated to create a more efficient, "zero-shot" evaluation framework. This framework, the Distillation Correlation Score (DCS), aims to quickly assess how well a distillation objective aligns with downstream model generalization without requiring the full synthesis and training process. This is particularly motivated by the need to understand the scalability of small-scale DD methods to larger models and datasets.

5. **Propose improved methods for data-efficient learning in the HL setting (CAD-Prune and CA2D):** The analysis of existing DD methods in the HL setting reveals that even the best-performing method (RDED) can sometimes lag behind strong coreset methods. This suggests limitations in current strategies for selecting data for distilled sets. Motivated by the importance of sample difficulty in the HL setting, the authors aim to introduce a compute-aware pruning metric (CAD-Prune) that efficiently identifies samples of optimal difficulty. This metric is then leveraged to develop an improved, compute-aligned dataset distillation method (CA2D) to advance the state-of-the-art in data-efficient learning under the hard-label regime.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a multi-pronged methodological approach involving systematic scalability analyses across different label regimes, evaluation of existing methods, development of a new evaluation metric, and proposal of novel pruning and distillation techniques.

#### 3.1. Scalability Analysis across Label Regimes

The core of the initial investigation involves a detailed scalability analysis to understand the interplay of data quality, dataset size (measured by Images Per Class, IPC), and compute budget.

- **Label Regimes Studied:**
 - **SL+KD (Soft Label + Knowledge Distillation):** Multiple soft labels (one per augmented image) obtained from a teacher model are used for student model training, akin to knowledge distillation.
 - **SL (Fixed Soft Label):** A single soft label per image, obtained from a teacher, is fixed during student model training.
 - **HL (Hard Label):** Only ground-truth labels are used for student model training.
- **Datasets and Models:** ImageNet-1K with ResNet-18 architecture for large-scale analysis; TinyImageNet with ConvNet-D4 for small-scale.
- **Data Subsets:** High-quality coresets, specifically EL2N, are used against random subsets. EL2N identifies samples of varying difficulty, allowing for the construction of "easy" and "difficult" subsets across IPC values (10 to 700).
- **Compute Budgets:** Models are trained on these subsets under four distinct compute budgets, ranging from 2 to 50 epochs of full-dataset training.
- **Metrics:** Performance is evaluated using downstream model test accuracy. Pareto frontier analysis is employed to visualize the trade-offs between dataset size, sample difficulty, and performance.
- **Sample Importance in SL:** For the SL setting, the authors extend the EL2N importance score formulation to account for KL-divergence loss with soft labels, defining EL2N-SL. This modified score is used to analyze sample importance distributions during training.

#### 3.2. Systematic Evaluation of Dataset Distillation Methods in HL Setting

Recognizing the limitations of soft-label evaluations, a focused assessment in the HL setting is conducted.

- **Large-Scale DD Methods:** Five methods are evaluated: SRe2L, DWA, RDED, D4M, and Minimax Diffusion on ImageNet-1K with ResNet-18.
- **Small-Scale DD Methods:** Four methods are evaluated: TM, DATM, DM, and DC on TinyImageNet with ConvNet-D4.
- **Baselines:** State-of-the-art coreset methods (e.g., Random Real, Cal, GraphCut, Forgetting, Dyn-Unc, EL2N, K-centers) are included for comparison, matched to respective image resolutions and evaluated for "hardest" and "optimally hard" subsets.
- **Fair Comparison:** For small-scale DD methods in the SL setting, learned soft labels are replaced with teacher-assigned soft labels to isolate the effect of label learning from data quality.

#### 3.3. Distillation Correlation Score (DCS)

To address the computational cost of evaluating distillation objectives, a new metric is proposed.

- **Definition:** DCS measures the Spearman-rank correlation between the distillation loss objective L_distill(S_j, D) for a given subset S_j and the generalization error ℓ_D_test(θ*_S_j) of a model trained on S_j.
- **Efficiency:** DCS bypasses the need for costly dataset synthesis and repeated downstream model training by reusing precomputed generalization errors for various subsets.
- **Application:** Applied to analyze the scalability of trajectory-matching based objectives (TM, DATM) to larger architectures like ResNet-18 on TinyImageNet.

#### 3.4. Proposed Methods: CAD-Prune and CA2D

Based on the insights from the HL analysis, two new methods are introduced.

- **CAD-Prune (Compute-Aware Difficulty Pruning):**
 - **Motivation:** Addresses the limitation of fixed sample importance scores and the computational expense of existing optimal difficulty search methods.
 - **Mechanism:** Estimates per-sample uncertainty (standard deviation of EL2N-like scores) by training a model on the full dataset for a duration equivalent to the downstream task's compute budget. Uncertainty scores are averaged over a small window near the end of this compute-matched training period to capture samples still being learned.
 - **Formula:** U_k(x) = sqrt(Σ(S(y|x, θ_{k+j}) - ¯S)² / (J-1)) for uncertainty, and CAD(x) = (Σ_{k=K-J-W}^{K-J-1} U_k(x)) / W for the final score, where W is the averaging window size.
- **CA2D (Compute-Aligned Dataset Distillation):**
 - **Mechanism:** Utilizes CAD-Prune to identify samples of appropriate difficulty. From this pruned coreset, confident patches are cropped, selected, and stitched in an RDED-style to construct a distilled set tailored to the given compute level.
- **Evaluation:** Both CAD-Prune and CA2D are evaluated on ImageNet-1K in the HL setting, compared against strong baselines like EL2N-Best coresets and RDED.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several findings across the investigated label regimes and method evaluations:

#### 4.1. Analysis of SL+KD (Soft Label + Knowledge Distillation) Setting

- **Performance Saturation:** In the SL+KD regime, model performance is primarily determined by the compute budget and is largely insensitive to both the quality and size of the dataset (beyond a very small IPC threshold, e.g., IPC 10). Performance quickly saturates to levels approaching that of training on the full dataset, regardless of whether a random subset or a high-quality coreset is used.
- **No Pareto-Frontier:** Unlike the HL setting, Pareto analysis revealed no discernible Pareto-frontier in the SL+KD regime, indicating that the trade-off between dataset size, sample difficulty, and performance is effectively nullified by extensive soft-label supervision.
- **Soft Label Dominance:** Increasing the amount of soft-label supervision (from HL to SL to SL+KD) consistently drives all methods (both DD and coresets) toward the teacher's full-dataset performance, regardless of their intrinsic data quality.

#### 4.2. Analysis of Fixed SL (Soft Label) Setting

- **Limited Quality Impact:** While scaling dataset size and compute remains important for performance in the fixed SL setting, improvements in data quality offer only limited additional benefit beyond minimal IPC values (e.g., IPC 10). Coresets of varying quality (e.g., EL2N-easy vs. random) showed converging performance.
- **Homogenization of Sample Difficulty:** An analysis using the proposed EL2N-SL score revealed that in the SL regime, per-sample score distributions are tightly clustered within the easy-to-mid difficulty range. This suggests that fixed soft labels tend to homogenize the perceived difficulty and influence of samples during training, diminishing the impact of underlying sample quality.
- **Small-Scale DD Performance:** For small-scale DD methods (e.g., TM, DATM), the performance advantage over coreset baselines observed in the HL setting diminishes or disappears when evaluated in the SL regime.

#### 4.3. Analysis of HL (Hard Label) Setting

- **Large-Scale DD:** Among five large-scale DD methods evaluated on ImageNet-1K in the HL setting, only RDED reliably outperformed random baselines. However, RDED could still lag behind strong coreset methods like EL2N-Best consistently across various IPC settings. The paper attributes this limitation of RDED to its potential over-reliance on selecting "easy" sample patches.
- **Small-Scale DD:** TM and DATM demonstrated strong performance in the small-scale HL setting (e.g., TinyImageNet with ConvNet-D4), outperforming both random and coreset baselines.

#### 4.4. Distillation Correlation Score (DCS) Evaluation

- **Scalability Limitations of TM-based Objectives:** Application of DCS revealed that trajectory-matching based loss objectives (from TM and DATM) fail to scale effectively to larger models like ResNet-18. For these larger architectures, the TM surrogate loss showed no correlation with downstream generalization performance; its values remained constant across various subsets despite significant variations in the actual model performance. This indicates that these objectives are unable to capture meaningful differences between subsets for effective synthesis at larger scales.
- **DM Objective Correlation:** The distribution-matching (DM) objective showed a modest positive correlation (ρ = 0.41) with downstream generalization, which was better than TM but not strong.
- **SRe2L and DWA Objective Correlation:** Large-scale DD objectives like SRe2L and DWA, which match BatchNorm statistics, showed no correlation or negative correlation with downstream generalization, indicating a misalignment between these objectives and actual performance.

#### 4.5. Proposed CAD-Prune and CA2D

- **CAD-Prune Effectiveness:** The proposed Compute-Aware Difficulty Pruning (CAD-Prune) metric, designed to identify samples of optimal difficulty for a given compute budget, outperformed the computationally intensive "sliding window" approach used by EL2N-Best for coreset selection on ImageNet-1K. CAD-Prune achieved comparable or better performance with significantly higher computational efficiency.
- **CA2D State-of-the-Art:** CA2D (Compute-Aligned Dataset Distillation), built upon CAD-Prune principles, effectively outperformed both RDED and other existing DD baselines on ImageNet-1K in the HL setting across various IPC configurations (e.g., IPC 10, 50, 100), establishing a new performance benchmark in this regime.
- **Compression-Extraction Trade-off:** Analysis of convergence showed that while coresets quickly saturate, distilled sets (like RDED and CA2D) continue to improve with longer training, especially at smaller IPCs. This suggests a "compression-extraction trade-off," where more compute is required to extract information from highly compressed synthetic datasets compared to real data subsets.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research provides a re-evaluation of current practices in dataset distillation and offers new directions for data-efficient learning.

- **Rethinking Evaluation Paradigms:** The findings challenge the widespread practice of evaluating dataset distillation methods in soft-label regimes, particularly SL+KD. By demonstrating that extensive soft-label supervision can mask the true impact of data quality and lead to performance saturation regardless of subset origin, the work advocates for greater emphasis on the hard-label (HL) setting. This shift in evaluation methodology could lead to more meaningful progress in DD research by compelling methods to demonstrate genuine data compression and information retention capabilities.

- **Advancing Data-Efficient Learning:** The study clarifies the conditions under which data quality plays a critical role. It provides a deeper understanding of how data quality, size, and compute interact across different supervision schemes, which is crucial for the principled design of future data-efficient learning algorithms.

- **Efficient Research Tools:** The introduction of the Distillation Correlation Score (DCS) provides a computationally efficient, "zero-shot" metric for evaluating the alignment of distillation objectives with downstream model generalization. This tool can accelerate research by enabling rapid assessment of new distillation objectives without the need for time-consuming dataset synthesis and student model training, particularly for larger models and datasets where traditional evaluation is prohibitive.

- **Improved Dataset Distillation and Coreset Selection:** The proposed CAD-Prune metric and the CA2D dataset distillation method represent advancements in creating high-quality, compute-aligned data subsets. By efficiently identifying samples of optimal difficulty, these methods offer superior performance in the challenging hard-label setting for large-scale datasets like ImageNet-1K, outperforming existing state-of-the-art DD methods and competitive coreset baselines. This can lead to more effective deployment of data-efficient techniques in practical applications where compute resources are constrained or hard labels are the only available supervision.

- **Implications for Model Security and Ethics:** The observation that even a small dataset can achieve near-optimal performance with sufficient teacher supervision has implications for model security and privacy, particularly in "model-stealing" scenarios. This suggests that robust defenses against such attacks might need to consider the impact of soft-label supervision.

- **Future Research Directions:** The work lays the groundwork for future research, including extending the analysis to multimodal setups and tasks beyond image classification, as well as addressing the challenges of evaluating non-optimizable DD objectives using the DCS framework.
