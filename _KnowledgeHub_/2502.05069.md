# Exploring the Generalizability of Geomagnetic Navigation: A Deep Reinforcement Learning approach with Policy Distillation

> [!summary] Summary
> A deep reinforcement learning framework, TD3-STEPD, enables geomagnetic navigation policies to generalize effectively across diverse, previously unseen environments. This approach, from researchers at Xi'an University of Technology and collaborators, combines multi-teacher policy distillation with a tailored reward shaping mechanism, achieving over 94% success rates in unknown regions and significantly improving exploration efficiency.

## Problem

- Existing geomagnetic navigation strategies, including matching and early reinforcement learning methods, typically overfit to training environments, leading to severe performance degradation in new or unmapped areas.
- The lack of generalizability necessitates frequent and impractical model re-training or adaptation for autonomous vehicles operating in varied or unexplored GPS-denied environments.
- Prior DRL methods often rely on state representations focused on current observations, limiting transferability, or are sensitive to stochastic search characteristics, leading to erratic or suboptimal paths.

## Method

- A framework called TD3 with Separate Training and Ensemble Policy Distillation (TD3-STEPD) is proposed, utilizing deep ==reinforcement learning== for geomagnetic navigation.
- Multiple 'teacher' DRL agents are independently trained in distinct geographical sub-regions using the TD3 algorithm, learning localized navigation strategies.
- Policy distillation is employed to synthesize the knowledge from these multi-region-trained teacher agents into a single, more generalized 'student' network, enhancing cross-domain transferability.
- A mixed reward shaping mechanism, combining extrinsic potential-based rewards and intrinsic rewards based on a 'predicted heading angle,' is introduced to improve exploration efficiency during teacher agent training.

## Results

- The proposed TD3-STEPD achieved a success rate over **94%** in unknown geomagnetic regions, demonstrating superior generalization compared to individually trained DRL models (which dropped below **70%** success rates) and metaheuristic baselines.
- The mixed reward shaping mechanism significantly accelerated DRL agent exploration and convergence, with first destination arrivals occurring as early as episodes 13-174 for TD3-ST, compared to 2298-357 for sparse reward methods.
- TD3-STEPD generated smoother navigation trajectories, exhibited lower Mean Absolute Error and Root Mean Square Error of Heading Deviation, and reduced Total Navigation Time in unknown areas compared to individual DRL agents.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating an intrinsic reward based on an ideal 'predicted heading angle' with extrinsic rewards substantially enhances DRL agent exploration efficiency and accelerates convergence in large-scale navigation tasks.
> - Multi-teacher policy distillation effectively merges diverse localized navigation strategies into a single, robust 'student' policy, enabling an agent-centric behavior representation that generalizes across unseen geomagnetic environments.
> - The inherent variability of geomagnetic fields across different regions underscores the need for generalizable learning approaches, as region-specific DRL models show significant performance degradation when transferred.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Autonomous vehicle navigation in unknown environments is a field of ongoing research. Geomagnetic navigation, which utilizes the Earth's magnetic field signals, presents an alternative to Global Positioning System (GPS) or inertial navigation, particularly in environments where these systems are unavailable or unreliable (e.g., remote areas, underwater). Despite its potential advantages, a significant challenge in geomagnetic navigation is the generalizability of learned navigation strategies.

Existing geomagnetic navigation approaches can be broadly categorized into:
- **Geomagnetic matching approaches:** These methods compare real-time magnetic field measurements with a pre-established geomagnetic map to determine location. While accurate, they inherently lack generalizability, as navigation fails outside the boundaries of the stored map. Examples include techniques based on mean absolute difference (MAD), mean squared deviation (MSD), and contour matching.
- **Bionic geomagnetic navigation methods:** Inspired by animal navigation, these approaches use trial-and-error to adjust navigation based on feedback, often employing heuristic or evolutionary algorithms (e.g., multi-objective search, evolutionary strategies, differential evolution). These methods avoid pre-stored maps, offering improved generalizability compared to matching methods. However, they typically rely on stochastic searches, which can lead to erratic trajectories, suboptimal paths, and sensitivity to configuration settings.
- **Reinforcement learning (RL) based geomagnetic navigation:** More recent efforts leverage RL to learn navigation models that can adapt to unmodeled uncertainties. Approaches have included deep Q-networks (DQN) and goal-oriented RL. Previous RL methods have focused on learning correlations between geomagnetic parameters and trajectories or optimizing actions within specific environments. A limitation identified in these studies is that the learned strategies often overfit to the training environment, leading to performance degradation in unexplored regions or when the destination changes. The state representations in some prior DRL methods also limit generalizability by focusing solely on current geomagnetic observations without considering destination information or requiring external localization.

This work addresses the critical gap of **generalizability** in learned geomagnetic navigation strategies. While previous RL methods have aimed to learn reusable models, the extent to which this knowledge generalizes to novel, unmapped navigation areas has remained largely unexplored. The paper aims to develop a mechanism to enhance and analyze the generalizability of trained RL models for geomagnetic navigation, moving beyond environment-specific learning to an agent-centric behavior representation. This involves mitigating the degradation of learned strategies when deployed in geomagnetic environments different from their training domains.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the recognized limitation that learned geomagnetic navigation strategies often exhibit poor generalizability. A strategy effective in one geographical region may degrade significantly or fail in another due to varying geomagnetic characteristics and a lack of prior knowledge about new areas. This necessitates frequent re-training or adaptation, which is impractical for autonomous vehicles undertaking long-range missions in unexplored or GPS-denied environments. The authors highlight that a generalized navigation strategy is essential to avoid "case-wise and frequent model training" and to ensure reliable operation beyond the initial learning domain.

The key objectives of this work are:

1. **To explore the generalizability of trained geomagnetic navigation models across diverse navigation areas:** This involves identifying the mechanisms through which learned strategies either succeed or fail when transferred to new geomagnetic environments.
2. **To develop a deep reinforcement learning (DRL) approach that achieves generalizability in geomagnetic navigation across different areas:** The goal is to create a DRL-based framework capable of learning navigation knowledge that is transferable and robust in previously unseen regions.
3. **To employ multi-teacher policy distillation to amalgamate navigation strategies from multiple DRL agents trained in distributed domains:** This aims to merge the knowledge learned by individual "teacher" models, each specialized in a particular region, into a single, more generalized "student" policy network. This process is intended to facilitate model compression and improve robust model representativeness.
4. **To design a reward shaping mechanism for DRL agents that integrates both potential-based and intrinsic-motivated rewards:** This objective aims to enhance the exploration efficiency of the DRL agent during the training of teacher models. The reward shaping is intended to guide the agent more effectively, improve the representation quality of the teacher models, and ultimately contribute to their adaptability across navigation areas.

By pursuing these objectives, the research seeks to enable autonomous vehicles to navigate effectively using geomagnetic cues in large, complex, and previously unmapped environments without requiring extensive prior mapping or frequent model retraining.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology, termed TD3 with Separate Training and Ensemble Policy Distillation (TD3-STEPD), combines deep reinforcement learning (DRL) with policy distillation and a tailored reward shaping mechanism to achieve generalizable geomagnetic navigation.

**A. Fundamentals of Geomagnetic Navigation from a DRL Perspective**

1. **Geomagnetic Field Description:** The Earth's magnetic field (GF) is characterized by parameters such as total intensity ($B_F$), horizontal component ($B_H$), vertical component ($B_z$), declination ($D$), and inclination ($I$). These parameters vary geographically and serve as the navigational cues. The total intensity of the GF varies from approximately 25,000 nT near the equator to 65,000 nT at the poles.
2. **Vehicle Movement Model:** The autonomous vehicle's movement is modeled in a 2D Cartesian coordinate system. At each time step $j$, its position $(x_j, y_j)$ is updated based on its previous position, a movement distance $L_{j+1}$, and an angular displacement (yaw angle) $\psi_{j+1}$. The navigation action comprises the yaw angle $\psi_j$ and movement velocity $v_j$.
3. **Objective Function:** The agent's goal is to minimize the difference between its current geomagnetic parameters and those of the destination. An objective function $F(B, j)$ is defined as the sum of squared differences of normalized geomagnetic parameters (Declination $D$, Inclination $I$, Horizontal Component $B_H$) between the current position and the destination. The navigation task is considered successful when $F(B, j)$ falls below a predefined threshold $\zeta$.
4. **DRL Formulation:** The navigation task is formulated as a goal-conditioned Markov Decision Process (GA-MDP). The state space ($S$) is augmented to include current geomagnetic parameters and the destination's geomagnetic parameters, represented as an input vector $I_j = [D_j, I_j, B_{jH}, D_d, I_d, B_{dH}]$. The action space ($A$) consists of continuous yaw angles and movement distances. The DRL agent learns a policy $\pi_{\theta}$ to maximize the expected cumulative return, aiming for a transferable strategy.

**B. Overall Framework of TD3-STEPD**

The framework consists of two main components:
1. **Multi-Localized Navigation Networks (Teacher Agents):** Multiple DRL agents, referred to as "teacher networks," are independently trained in distinct geographical sub-regions (domains). Each teacher network learns a localized navigation strategy specific to its training environment. The Twin Delayed Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (TD3) algorithm is chosen for training these agents due to its robustness and ability to address overestimation issues inherent in Q-learning. TD3 uses an actor-critic structure with two critic networks to estimate Q-values and selects the minimum estimate. The policy network parameters are updated via deterministic policy gradient ascent.
2. **Global Navigation Network (Student Agent):** A single "student network" is trained to synthesize the knowledge acquired by the multiple teacher networks. This involves policy distillation.

**C. Policy Distillation**

Policy distillation is employed to compress and combine the diverse navigation strategies learned by the teacher networks into a single, more generalized student network.
1. **Experience Collection:** Each teacher agent, after training in its respective region, collects experiences (state-action pairs) which are stored in separate datasets $D_k$. The actions $a_i$ are generated by the teacher's deterministic actor policy $\pi_{T_k}^{\theta}(s_i)$.
2. **Student Training:** The student agent learns from the aggregated datasets from all $k$ teachers. Instead of using Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence, the paper uses Mean Squared Error (MSE) to minimize the difference between the deterministic actor policies of the teachers and the student: $L_{MSE}(\pi_{T_k}^{\theta}, \pi_S^{\theta}) = \frac{1}{|D_k|} \sum_{i=1}^{|D_k|} (\pi_{T_k}^{\theta}(s_i) - \pi_S^{\theta}(s_i))^2$.
3. **Generalization:** By training on diverse experiences from multiple regions, the student network aims to learn an "agent-centric" navigation model that is less susceptible to overfitting to specific environmental features and more adaptable to unseen geomagnetic environments.

**D. Reward Shaping**

To address the issue of sparse rewards and improve exploration efficiency in large-scale navigation tasks, a mixed reward function is designed, combining extrinsic and intrinsic rewards.
1. **Extrinsic Reward ($r_e$):**
 - A positive reward ($r_{goal}$) is given when the vehicle successfully reaches the destination.
 - Otherwise, a weighted change in the objective function $\alpha(F(B, t) - F(B, t-1))$ is used. This provides a dense reward signal, penalizing movement away from the destination and rewarding movement towards it based on the convergence of geomagnetic parameters.
2. **Intrinsic Reward ($r_i$):**
 - Inspired by magnetoreception in animals and parallel approach methods in guidance systems, this reward provides additional directional guidance.
 - At each time step, a "predicted heading angle" ($\lambda'$) is calculated. This angle represents the ideal direction that would cause geomagnetic parameters to converge towards the destination's values at a consistent rate, based on the gradients of these parameters.
 - The intrinsic reward $r_i = \beta(\pi/4 - |\lambda_j - \lambda'_j|)$ is designed to decrease the difference between the agent's actual heading angle ($\lambda_j$) and the predicted optimal heading angle ($\lambda'_j$). It penalizes large discrepancies and rewards alignment with this predicted direction, fostering more efficient exploration.
3. **Total Reward:** The total reward for the agent is the sum of the extrinsic and intrinsic rewards: $r_t = r_e + r_i$.

**E. Simulation Environment and Training:**
- A custom geomagnetic navigation environment is built using OpenAI Gym.
- Geomagnetic field data is sourced from the International Geomagnetic Reference Field Model (IGRF).
- A large region is selected, with four 5-degree longitude/latitude sub-regions (A, B, C, D) designated for training teacher agents. The remaining area serves as an "unknown" region for testing generalizability.
- Hyperparameters for the TD3 agent are optimized using Optuna, with a replay buffer size of 50,000, a discount factor $\gamma=0.995$, and a policy network composed of MLPs (400 and 300 units).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research conducted numerical simulations to evaluate the proposed TD3-STEPD approach against ablation methods and existing metaheuristic baselines.

**A. Impact of Reward Shaping on Exploration Efficiency (Fig. 4)**
- **Convergence:** All DRL-based methods (TD3-SR, TD3-ER, TD3-ST) demonstrated the ability to learn and stabilize navigation tasks within their respective training regions, with average rewards reaching 200 (indicating successful navigation) within 6000 training episodes.
- **Regional Variability:** The convergence speed varied across different training regions (A, B, C, D), indicating that the geomagnetic field characteristics of an environment influence learning effectiveness.
- **Superiority of TD3-ST:** The proposed TD3-ST (with mixed extrinsic and intrinsic rewards) consistently achieved higher average rewards and faster convergence compared to TD3-SR (sparse rewards) and TD3-ER (extrinsic rewards only) across all four regions.
- **Accelerated Exploration:** TD3-ST reached the destination for the first time significantly earlier in training (e.g., episodes 13-174) compared to TD3-SR (episodes 2298-357) and TD3-ER (episodes 412-698). This suggests that the combined extrinsic and intrinsic rewards substantially enhance exploration efficiency and accelerate algorithm convergence by providing more informative feedback.

**B. Performance in Training Regions (Table I)**
- All DRL-based ablation algorithms (TD3-SR, TD3-ER, TD3-ST) and the proposed TD3-STEPD achieved high success rates (SR > 98%) in their specific training regions. This confirms the feasibility of using DRL for geomagnetic navigation tasks when trained within a known environment.
- In the training regions, TD3-ST and TD3-STEPD generally exhibited comparable or slightly better performance in terms of Success Rate (SR) and Success Weighted by Path Length (SPL) than TD3-SR and TD3-ER.
- TD3-STEPD, despite its goal of generalization, did not consistently outperform TD3-ST in *individual* training regions. This was attributed to the inherent complexity of policy distillation, where merging strategies from multiple regions might lead to some loss of environment-specific detail as the model transitions from environment-centric to agent-centric behavior representations.

**C. Performance in Unknown Regions (Table II & Fig. 5)**
- **Generalization Degradation:** TD3-SR, TD3-ER, and individual TD3-ST agents (trained in specific regions) experienced significant performance degradation when tested in unknown regions. Their success rates (SR) were generally below 70%, with TD3-SR performing poorly (SR as low as 2‰). This validates the initial hypothesis regarding the lack of generalizability in region-specific DRL models.
- **Superior Generalization of TD3-STEPD:** The proposed TD3-STEPD maintained a high success rate of over 94% in unknown regions, demonstrating its ability to generalize effectively to novel geomagnetic environments. This indicates that multi-teacher policy distillation successfully synthesizes diverse strategies, creating a more robust and flexible model.
- **Trajectory Straightness and Efficiency (Fig. 5):**
 - TD3-STEPD exhibited lower Mean Absolute Error (MAE) and Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) of Heading Deviation compared to individually trained TD3-ST agents. This suggests that the distilled policy leads to straighter and smoother navigation trajectories in unknown areas.
 - TD3-STEPD significantly reduced the Total Navigation Time (TNT) in unknown regions, indicating improved navigation efficiency compared to individual TD3-ST agents.
 - The Navigation Error (NE) of TD3-STEPD was observed to be within the range of the individual TD3-ST agents, suggesting that it maintains comparable accuracy in reaching the destination.

**D. Visualization of Navigation Trajectories (Fig. 6)**
- **Trajectory Smoothness:** Visualizations confirmed that TD3-STEPD generated smoother navigation trajectories compared to the four baseline metaheuristic algorithms (AFSA, DE, GA, PSO). This is attributed to the DRL agent learning a more stable strategy from extensive experience, reducing the randomness inherent in metaheuristic searches.
- **Precision Near Destination:** TD3-STEPD demonstrated the ability to gradually decrease navigation distance steps as it approached the destination, contributing to higher navigation accuracy, a characteristic not consistently observed in baseline methods.
- **Navigation Efficiency Variability:** While generally stable, TD3-STEPD's navigation path length was not always the shortest compared to baselines. In some scenarios (e.g., Fig. 6h), metaheuristic methods found shorter paths, while in others (e.g., Fig. 6c), TD3-STEPD was more efficient. This suggests that efficiency can be influenced by specific geomagnetic field characteristics.
- **Stability and Robustness:** TD3-STEPD demonstrated more stable performance, avoiding issues of premature convergence or failure to balance multiple geomagnetic parameter convergences, which were sometimes observed in baseline metaheuristic methods. This indicates its robustness in diverse magnetic field environments.

In summary, the results indicate that the proposed reward shaping significantly improves DRL agent exploration efficiency, and the multi-teacher policy distillation mechanism effectively endows the learned model with superior generalizability, robustness, and efficiency in cross-domain geomagnetic navigation, outperforming both ablation models and conventional metaheuristic approaches.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of autonomous navigation by addressing the critical challenge of generalizability in geomagnetic navigation strategies. The work introduces a novel approach that combines deep reinforcement learning with multi-agent policy distillation and a tailored reward shaping mechanism.

The significance of these findings lies in several areas:

- **Enhanced Generalizability:** The primary impact is the demonstration of a DRL model (TD3-STEPD) that can effectively generalize its navigation strategy to previously unexplored geomagnetic regions. This capability reduces the necessity for repeated model retraining for each new navigation environment, which is a practical bottleneck for deploying autonomous vehicles in varied or unmapped terrains, particularly in GPS-denied environments.
- **Improved Exploration Efficiency:** The proposed mixed reward shaping mechanism, incorporating both potential-based and intrinsic-motivated rewards, is shown to accelerate the learning process of DRL agents. This is crucial for real-world applications where extensive, inefficient exploration can be costly or impractical.
- **Robust Navigation Performance:** The TD3-STEPD approach delivers smoother trajectories, lower heading deviation errors, and competitive navigation accuracy compared to existing evolutionary-based methods. This suggests a more reliable and controlled navigation experience for autonomous platforms.
- **Foundation for Future Autonomous Systems:** The development of a generalizable geomagnetic navigation system has implications for various autonomous applications, including long-range underwater vehicles, planetary exploration rovers, and unmanned aerial vehicles operating in areas with intermittent or unavailable satellite signals. It provides a pathway for more self-sufficient and adaptable navigation solutions.
- **Advancement in DRL Techniques:** The application of multi-teacher policy distillation in this context provides a case study for how knowledge transfer from multiple specialized DRL agents can lead to a more generalized and robust "student" policy, a methodology applicable to other domain generalization problems in reinforcement learning.

Looking forward, the authors suggest investigating the impact of geomagnetic anomalies (such as daily fluctuations, abnormalities, and storms) on navigation performance. Understanding the robustness of these generalizable strategies under dynamic and uncertain geomagnetic conditions would be a natural extension, further enhancing the practical applicability of this work in challenging real-world scenarios.
