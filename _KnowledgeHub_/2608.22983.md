# CSymPlan: Certified Symbolic Planning and Control for High-DOF Manipulators

> [!summary] Summary
> CSymPlan is a certified symbolic planning-and-control framework for high-DOF manipulators. It reduces arm dynamics to a sampled perturbed double-integrator in operational space via feedback linearization, synthesizes reach-avoid feedback policies over a finite abstraction, and refines symbolic accelerations into joint torques. An offline variant precomputes a policy table; an online variant recomputes winning sets at runtime with pFaces. Both reach zero safety violations on Franka FR3 hardware and in simulation.

## Problem

- Decoupled plan-then-track stacks return geometrically valid paths that become unsafe once tracking error, actuator saturation, unmodeled dynamics, and sensing noise appear at execution time.
- Kinodynamic planning, MPC, MPPI, and learned controllers rely on local optimization, stochastic sampling, or training performance, so none delivers a set-wise closed-loop reach-avoid certificate.
- Symbolic control gives correct-by-construction certificates but suffers state-space explosion: uniform joint-space abstraction of a 7-DOF arm is intractable, so torque-controlled hardware demonstrations were missing.

## Method

- Operational-space ==feedback linearization== reduces the manipulator to a sampled perturbed ==double-integrator== in Cartesian position and velocity, folding torque-realization, modeling, and measurement error into bounded disturbance and uncertainty sets.
- **Offline synthesis** via ==SCOTS== grids the task space, over-approximates transitions, solves a ==reach-avoid fixed point==, and exports a lookup table run by a 1 kHz ==quantization-lookup-torque== loop with ==null-space posture regulation==.
- **Online mode** fixes grids and transition relation, requests finite-horizon ==winning sets== from a parallelized ==pFaces== server, buffers certified acceleration segments, ranks admissible actions by a one-step cost, and rejects stale commands.

## Results

- Offline PyBullet benchmark: **100%** success and **0%** violations at **2.3 s** to goal and **0.05 ms** per control step, versus **76%** success for RRT+tracker and **95%** for MPPI.
- Online Isaac Sim across **150** trials: **94%** success, **0%** violations, **100%** safe resolution, with pFaces synthesis latency of **155 ms** mean and **430 ms** at the **95th percentile**.
- Franka FR3 with depth-fused obstacle maps: **27/30** successes, zero violations, **30/30** safe resolution; online baselines violated in **1.3-9.3%** of trials, RRT-Connect worst at **74%** success.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Abstracting in operational space instead of joint space is what makes symbolic synthesis tractable on high-DOF arms, trading joint-level guarantees for end-effector-level ones.
> - Reporting safe resolution separately from task success is the honest metric for certified controllers: refusing to act when no winning action exists is a non-violating outcome.
> - Guarantees hold only under calibrated disturbance bounds and a valid inflated obstacle model; whole-arm collision avoidance stays uncertified and per-scene offline synthesis costs minutes.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper addresses a recognized challenge in robot motion generation for high-degree-of-freedom (high-DOF) manipulators: the discrepancy between high-level planning and low-level execution. Conventional robotic architectures often decouple motion planning, which computes a collision-free path, from feedback control, which tracks this path. While computationally convenient, this modularity can result in reference trajectories that are difficult to execute accurately on physical robots due to factors such as actuator limits, tracking errors, unmodeled dynamics, and sensing uncertainties. This mismatch is particularly pronounced in cluttered environments where safety margins are small.

Prior approaches to reduce this gap include kinodynamic planning, model predictive control (MPC), model predictive path integral (MPPI) control, and reinforcement learning (RL) based controllers. These methods incorporate dynamics into decision-making, offering improved performance compared to purely geometric planning. However, many rely on local optimization, stochastic sampling, or learned approximations and generally do not provide direct, closed-loop reach-avoid certificates for the executed trajectories, meaning they do not formally guarantee that the robot will remain safe and reach a goal within defined parameters.

Symbolic control offers a method to synthesize controllers with formal guarantees. It involves constructing a finite abstraction (a symbolic model) that over-approximates continuous system dynamics, then synthesizing a controller for this abstract system. Under a sound abstraction, the guarantees from the symbolic controller can be transferred to the continuous system. The primary limitation of symbolic control, however, is scalability, as the state space grows exponentially with the system's dimensionality, making direct abstraction of high-DOF manipulators computationally intractable.

This paper positions CSymPlan as a unified symbolic planning-and-control framework that aims to bridge this gap by offering formal reach-avoid certificates for high-DOF manipulators while addressing the scalability challenge of symbolic control. It distinguishes itself from Task and Motion Planning (TAMP), which uses symbols for semantic predicates and high-level actions, by using symbols to represent regions of continuous state and input spaces, focusing on certifying closed-loop dynamical behavior. By synthesizing over a lower-dimensional operational-space model rather than the full joint-space state, CSymPlan attempts to make symbolic control practical for these complex systems, thereby advancing the field towards more reliable and certifiable robot autonomy.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for developing CSymPlan stems from the challenges in achieving reliable and safe motion generation for robot manipulators, particularly those with high degrees of freedom, operating in complex or safety-critical environments. The conventional decoupled architecture, where a planner generates a path and a controller tracks it, frequently leads to a "planning-execution gap." Paths that appear collision-free in a planner's idealized model can become unsafe during physical execution due to real-world factors like actuator saturation, tracking errors, unmodeled dynamics, and sensor noise. This risk is amplified in cluttered workspaces where obstacle clearances are minimal. Existing integrated planning and control methods, while incorporating dynamics, often rely on approximations or local optimizations and generally do not offer formal, closed-loop reach-avoid certificates that guarantee safety and task completion under specified uncertainties.

Against this background, the key objectives of the CSymPlan framework are detailed through its two complementary implementations:

1. **Develop a tractable task-space abstraction for high-DOF arms:** The goal is to reduce the complexity of high-dimensional manipulators (e.g., 7-DOF) to a lower-dimensional representation suitable for symbolic control. This is achieved through operational-space feedback linearization, modeling the manipulator as a sampled perturbed double integrator in end-effector position and velocity, while explicitly accounting for bounded disturbances and measurement uncertainty. This abstraction aims to make symbolic synthesis computationally feasible.
2. **Formulate and synthesize offline certified symbolic policies for manipulation:** An objective is to cast manipulation reach-avoid tasks into symbolic feedback synthesis problems over the derived perturbed task-space dynamics. The framework aims to yield policies that are correct-by-construction, providing formal guarantees of reachability and safety under defined disturbance and measurement bounds for known, static workspaces.
3. **Implement real-time execution of precomputed policies on hardware:** This involves establishing a robust pipeline (quantization, policy lookup, and torque realization) that can execute the offline-synthesized symbolic policy at the robot's servo rate. A specific objective is to demonstrate its hardware operation on a torque-controlled Franka FR3 manipulator for reach-avoid tasks, validating its practical deployability.
4. **Extend the framework to online symbolic planning and control:** For dynamic environments where goals or obstacles change at runtime, a key objective is to adapt the symbolic control paradigm. This involves fixing the symbolic grids and transition relations once, then recomputing finite-horizon reach-avoid winning sets and policies on demand using parallelized synthesis (pFaces). The system aims to execute buffered certified acceleration commands while incorporating stale-command rejection to maintain safety in evolving environments.
5. **Establish a comprehensive evaluation protocol:** The research aims to rigorously evaluate both offline and online implementations through randomized simulated benchmarks (PyBullet, Isaac Sim) and real Franka FR3 experiments. This includes measuring success rate, safety violations, safe resolution, time-to-goal, path length, smoothness, effort, and online synthesis time against a range of planning, receding-horizon, safety-filter, and learned baselines. The motivation here is to provide empirical evidence of the framework's reliability and performance while characterizing its computational trade-offs.

In summary, the central motivation is to overcome the limitations of traditional and contemporary motion generation methods by providing a principled, formally certifiable approach to robot planning and control that can be deployed on high-DOF manipulators in both static and dynamic environments, thereby enhancing robot safety and autonomy.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

CSymPlan’s methodology integrates symbolic control with robotic manipulation through a two-pronged approach: an offline precomputation scheme and an online real-time synthesis loop. Both implementations share a common mathematical model that abstracts a high-DOF manipulator.

#### 3.1. Manipulator Reduction and Modeling

The framework begins by reducing the complex dynamics of an $n_q$-DOF manipulator to a tractable model in operational space. Through task-space feedback linearization, the manipulator's translational end-effector dynamics are approximated as a sampled perturbed double-integrator system. The chosen task-space command $F = \eta(q, \dot{q}) + \Lambda(q)u$, where $u$ is a virtual desired end-effector acceleration, transforms the manipulator's operational-space dynamics into $\ddot{x} = u + \Lambda(q)^{-1}F_{ext}$. All residual terms, including $\Lambda(q)^{-1}F_{ext}$, modeling errors, discretization errors, measurement errors, and torque-realization errors, are aggregated and modeled as a bounded additive disturbance. This results in a perturbed double-integrator model:
$\dot{\xi} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & I_d \\ 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix} \xi + \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ I_d \end{bmatrix} u \oplus \mathcal{J}[-w, w]$, where $\xi = [x^\top, v^\top]^\top$ is the task-space state (position and velocity), $u$ is the virtual acceleration input, and $w$ bounds the component-wise disturbance. Measurement uncertainty is also accounted for by $P(x) = x \oplus \mathcal{J}[-z, z]$, with $z$ bounding measurement error. The disturbance and measurement bounds ($w, z$) are calibrated conservatively from empirical data.

#### 3.2. Safe and Goal Set Definitions

Safety is defined with respect to an admissible Cartesian workspace $X_{ws}$ and a set of obstacle regions $O$. An inflated obstacle set $O_{d_{safe}}$ is constructed by adding a safety distance $d_{safe}$ around all obstacles. The position-level safe set is $X_x^{safe} = X_{ws} \setminus O_{d_{safe}}$. This is lifted to the full task-space state $X_{safe} = \{\xi \mid x \in X_x^{safe}, v \in V\}$, where $V$ is a compact velocity set. The $d_{safe}$ margin is chosen to cover the end-effector and tool geometry, in addition to tracking and measurement uncertainties.
The goal set $X_x^{goal}$ is defined as a region around a target position $x_T$ with a position tolerance $d_{tol}$. The full target set $X_{goal}$ also requires the robot to be at rest, defined as velocities within a tolerance $v_{tol}$. Sequential waypoint tasks are handled by synthesizing separate reach-avoid policies for each waypoint.

#### 3.3. Symbolic Controller Synthesis

The core of CSymPlan involves constructing a finite abstraction $S_{abs} = (X_{abs}, U_{abs}, F_{abs})$ that over-approximates the continuous sampled perturbed system.
- **Discretization:** The continuous state space $X = X_{ws} \times V$ and input space $U$ are discretized into uniform finite grids, $X_{abs}$ and $U_{abs}$ respectively.
- **Transition Relation:** For each abstract state-input pair, the set-valued transition relation $F_{abs}$ is computed by over-approximating all reachable sampled successors, considering the predefined disturbance and measurement bounds.
- **Abstract Sets:** The abstract safe and goal sets ($\hat{X}_{safe}$, $\hat{X}_{goal}$) are constructed conservatively, ensuring that every concrete state represented by an abstract cell lies entirely within the continuous safe or goal set.
- **Policy Computation:** Using tools like SCOTS or pFaces, fixed-point algorithms are applied to synthesize a feedback policy that maps each abstract state to one or more admissible control inputs, ensuring all successors remain within the certified winning set and eventually reach the goal, satisfying the reach-avoid specification.

#### 3.4. Offline Implementation

The offline approach precomputes a fixed symbolic controller for a known workspace, obstacle set, and goal sequence.
- **Synthesis Pipeline:** A one-time process discretizes the task space, builds the finite symbolic model, specifies the reach-avoid task, synthesizes the controller using fixed-point computation, and exports the resulting policy as a lookup table or Binary Decision Diagram (BDD).
- **Real-time Execution Pipeline:** At runtime (1 kHz servo rate on Franka FR3), the robot's joint feedback $(q, \dot{q})$ is used to compute the task-space state $\xi_k$. This state is quantized to an abstract state $\hat{\xi}_k$, which is then used to query the precomputed policy for a virtual acceleration $u_k$. This $u_k$ is realized as a task-space wrench $F_k = \eta(q_k, \dot{q}_k) + \Lambda(q_k)u_k$, and finally converted into joint torques $\tau_k = J(q_k)^\top F_k + \tau_{0,k}$. An additional null-space PD term $\tau_{0,k}$ is included for posture regulation and joint-limit avoidance.
- **Guarantees:** The offline system provides a formal reach-avoid guarantee (Proposition 1) provided that the abstraction and refinement assumptions (e.g., sound over-approximation, conservative set definitions, disturbance bounds encompassing physical errors, accurate environment models, $d_{safe}$ covering tool geometry) are met.

#### 3.5. Online Implementation

The online approach extends the framework to dynamic environments by moving symbolic synthesis into the control loop.
- **Dynamic Sets:** It reuses the pre-constructed symbolic grids and transition relations but updates the abstract safe and target subsets ($\hat{X}_{safe,r}$, $\hat{X}_{goal,r}$) at each replanning instant $r$ based on current perception (e.g., DNVblox voxel maps) and task changes.
- **Online Synthesis with pFaces:** A background planning thread communicates with an online server (pFaces), requesting a finite-horizon reach-avoid controller. pFaces performs parallelized fixed-point computations over the abstract transition relation for the current, dynamically updated safe and goal sets over a specified horizon $H$.
- **Action Selection:** If multiple admissible actions are returned, a heuristic cost function (Equation 26) is used to select an action that balances target proximity, velocity, control effort, and progress toward the target. This selection is performed only among actions already certified by the symbolic fixed point.
- **Buffered Execution:** The robot process and the online synthesis server operate asynchronously. The planner submits new requests when the remaining certified command buffer falls below a threshold. The robot's 1 kHz servo loop executes a sequence of buffered symbolic accelerations $u_{r,i}$. A desired task-space reference $(x_d(t), v_d(t))$ is generated, and a real-time acceleration command $u_{rt,k}$ is computed using a tracking controller (Equation 31) before torque realization.
- **Safety Mechanisms:** Before executing a buffered command, a state-matching condition (Equation 34) checks if the current measured state is sufficiently close to the state for which the command was certified. If this condition fails or if pFaces returns no certified action, the robot safely holds the last certified reference, re-requests a policy, or stops, preventing the execution of uncertified commands.
- **Guarantees:** The online system provides per-request reach-avoid guarantees that are local to the current sets, horizon, and state. These guarantees are preserved across replanning calls, provided that the state-matching condition is met and the obstacle model remains valid.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of CSymPlan encompassed both offline and online settings, leveraging simulated environments (PyBullet, Isaac Sim) and hardware experiments on a Franka FR3 manipulator.

#### 4.1. Offline Evaluation

- **Hardware Deployment (Franka FR3):** The offline implementation successfully demonstrated real-time execution of the quantization–lookup–torque pipeline on the Franka FR3. Across five static scenes of increasing obstacle complexity, CSymPlan completed all trials (N=5 per scene) with zero observed safety violations. Metrics such as end-effector tracking error, commanded joint-torque norm, and measured joint-velocity norm indicated stable and controlled execution.
- **Simulated Benchmarking (PyBullet):** In randomized reach-avoid tasks against baselines (RRT + tracker, LQR-RRT* + tracker, MPPI, Neural MPC), CSymPlan achieved a 100% success rate with 0% safety violations. In contrast, all baselines exhibited non-zero violation rates (ranging from 3.5% for Neural MPC to 10% for RRT + tracker), indicating that while some baselines could achieve competitive time-to-goal and path lengths, they lacked the same level of safety assurance.
- **Computational Cost:** The offline synthesis phase for CSymPlan incurred a one-time cost of approximately 290 seconds (75s for abstraction, 215s for synthesis), significantly higher than RRT's average planning time of 6.2 seconds. However, its runtime execution loop involved constant-time policy lookups, resulting in a mean CPU time of 0.05 ms per step, which was orders of magnitude faster than the baselines (e.g., Neural MPC at 3.6 ms, MPPI at 18.1 ms). This demonstrated the trade-off of higher offline computation for predictable, fast, and certified online execution in static environments.

#### 4.2. Online Evaluation

- **Simulated Experiments (Isaac Sim):** Across three task families (shelf reach-avoid, 3D maze, moving obstacle), aggregating 150 trials, CSymPlan achieved an overall 94% success rate, 0% safety violations, and a 100% safe-resolution rate. The 6% of non-successful trials were classified as safe stops or timeouts, occurring primarily in narrow maze passages or dynamic obstacle scenarios where no certified action could be found or buffered commands became stale. Mean pFaces synthesis latency was 155 ms (95th percentile 430 ms).
- **Hardware Experiments (Franka FR3 with DNVblox Perception):** In real-world dynamic tasks (high-speed reach, dynamic-obstacle reach-avoid, dynamic maze), CSymPlan succeeded in 27 out of 30 trials. Crucially, it recorded 0 observed safety violations and maintained a 100% safe-resolution rate. The three non-successful trials terminated as certified safe stops due to the pFaces server returning no winning action or stale-command rejection, not collisions. Mean pFaces latency was 153 ms (95th percentile 410 ms), and mean DNVblox obstacle map age at the time of pFaces requests was 78 ms. These results empirically supported the claim that the robot never executes an uncertified command.
- **Online Baseline Comparison (Simulation):** Comparison against NMPC, MPPI, RRT-Connect + tracker, CBF-QP, and Neural MPC in dynamic simulation tasks showed that while some baselines achieved comparable or sometimes better efficiency (e.g., faster solve times for Neural MPC, NMPC, CBF-QP), they all incurred non-zero safety violation rates (ranging from 1.3% for CBF-QP to 9.3% for RRT-Connect + tracker). CSymPlan consistently maintained 0% violations and 100% safe resolution, indicating its robustness under the explicit disturbance and measurement bounds. CBF-QP, while fast, could get trapped in maze-like environments due to its local safety enforcement.

#### 4.3. Key Outcomes

The main findings establish that CSymPlan successfully delivers its core promise: providing certified symbolic planning and control for high-DOF manipulators. Its ability to consistently achieve 0% safety violations and 100% safe resolution in both simulated and hardware environments, even when other methods fail to meet safety constraints, is a significant result. This demonstrates that by judiciously abstracting manipulator dynamics and integrating formal synthesis tools, it is possible to guarantee safe behavior under uncertainty, albeit with a computational cost trade-off during synthesis.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The CSymPlan framework represents a notable advancement in the field of robot motion generation, particularly for high-DOF manipulators operating in safety-critical and dynamic environments. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Formal Safety Guarantees for High-DOF Manipulators:** The most significant contribution is its ability to provide formal, closed-loop reach-avoid certificates for high-DOF manipulators. This directly addresses the long-standing planning-execution gap in robotics, where geometric plans often lack dynamic feasibility and safety guarantees under real-world uncertainties. By guaranteeing that every admissible successor, for every state within a certified abstract cell and under prescribed disturbances, remains safe and eventually reaches the goal, CSymPlan offers a level of assurance not typically found in conventional or learning-based planning and control methods.
- **Demonstrated Hardware Realizability:** CSymPlan is presented as the first unified symbolic planning-and-control framework demonstrated on torque-controlled hardware (Franka FR3). This moves symbolic control beyond theoretical or low-dimensional applications, proving its practical executability on complex robotic systems in real-time.
- **Tractable Abstraction for Scalability:** The methodology of reducing a high-DOF manipulator to a sampled perturbed double-integrator model in operational space makes symbolic synthesis tractable. This innovative abstraction technique addresses the critical challenge of state-space explosion, which has historically limited the application of symbolic control to higher-dimensional systems.
- **Dual Offline and Online Capabilities:** The framework's dual implementations offer flexibility. The offline mode is suitable for static, known workspaces, providing predictable, constant-time execution after initial synthesis. The online mode extends certified control to dynamic environments with changing tasks and perception-driven obstacle maps, enabling real-time reactivity while maintaining safety guarantees through tools like pFaces. This adaptability enhances the deployability of certified control in complex, unstructured environments.
- **Enhanced Reliability in Safety-Critical Applications:** The consistent achievement of 0% safety violations and 100% safe resolution in diverse simulated and hardware tasks indicates a substantial increase in system reliability. This has direct implications for applications where robot safety is paramount, such as human-robot interaction, collaborative robotics, precision assembly in cluttered industrial settings, and medical robotics. The framework's design ensures that if a certified action is not available, the robot will perform a safe stop or replan, rather than executing a potentially unsafe command.
- **Bridge Between Formal Methods and Robotics:** CSymPlan serves as a bridge between formal verification and control theory and practical robotic execution. By providing a principled way to integrate formal guarantees into the decision-making loop of a robot, it encourages further research and development into formally verifiable robotic systems.

Future work outlined in the paper, such as scalable abstractions, hybridization with other planning methods, richer temporal-logic task specifications, extending certification to whole-arm dynamics, and perception-aware certification, suggests avenues for expanding the framework's capabilities and further increasing its impact. These developments could lead to more robust, intelligent, and certifiably safe robotic systems capable of operating autonomously in highly complex and uncertain real-world environments.
