# Rethinking Policy Diversity in Ensemble Policy Gradient in Large-Scale Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Coupled Policy Optimization (CPO) introduces a method to regulate inter-policy diversity in ensemble reinforcement learning for massively parallel environments, demonstrating improved sample efficiency and stable learning. CPO consistently achieves higher performance and faster convergence across complex robotic manipulation tasks by controlling follower policies' divergence from the leader via KL constraints and an adversarial reward.

## Problem

- Single policies in massively parallel RL environments yield diminishing returns due to limited exploration diversity, generating similar trajectories despite vast data collection.
- Existing ensemble methods like SAPG enhance diversity but lack mechanisms to control the distance between policies, potentially leading to excessive divergence.
- Uncontrolled policy divergence reduces the Effective Sample Size (ESS) for off-policy data and increases clipping bias in PPO, undermining training stability and sample efficiency.

## Method

- Coupled Policy Optimization (CPO) extends SAPG by introducing a ==KL divergence== constraint on follower policy updates relative to the leader, ensuring follower policies explore within a bounded neighborhood.
- An intrinsic adversarial reward is incorporated, encouraging follower policies to explore distinct state-action regions to prevent overconcentration around the leader.
- The method combines these techniques to promote structured diversity, making collected samples more informative and stabilizing the leader's learning process.

## Results

- CPO consistently outperformed PPO, DexPBT, and SAPG, achieving stronger final performance and often reaching SAPG's performance levels with approximately half the environment steps in complex manipulation tasks.
- Ablation studies confirmed that KL constraints increased the Effective Sample Size (ESS) and reduced mean IS ratio deviations, validating the theoretical propositions.
- CPO maintained stable inter-agent distances during training, preventing the significant policy misalignments observed in SAPG and enabling more consistent and effective exploration.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Excessive inter-policy divergence inversely relates to the Effective Sample Size (ESS) and directly correlates with increased PPO clipping bias, degrading learning stability and sample efficiency.
> - KL divergence effectively upper-bounds the expected absolute deviation of Importance Sampling (IS) ratios from 1, providing a theoretical basis for regulating inter-policy distance.
> - Maintaining stable inter-agent distances and structured policy formation, where followers are distributed around the leader, generates more informative samples and leads to robust learning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Deep Reinforcement Learning (RL) has advanced with the advent of massively parallel physics simulators, such as Isaac Gym, which enable data collection from tens of thousands of environments simultaneously. This parallelism offers the potential to enhance learning efficiency for complex, high-dimensional tasks like dexterous manipulation. However, recent observations indicate that merely increasing the quantity of data via a single policy, as in methods like Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), does not consistently lead to improved learning efficiency. This is attributed to the limited exploration diversity afforded by a single policy, resulting in the generation of highly similar trajectories across parallel environments.

To address this limitation, agent ensemble approaches have emerged as a strategy to foster diverse sample collection. Existing methods in this area include:
- **DexPBT (Petrenko et al., 2023)**: This framework trains multiple policies in parallel, each with varying hyperparameters. While it promotes exploration, it discards data from less successful policies, which can reduce overall sample efficiency.
- **Split and Aggregate Policy Gradients (SAPG) (Singla et al., 2024)**: SAPG employs a leader-follower architecture where a leader policy aggregates off-policy samples from multiple follower policies using importance sampling (IS). This approach utilizes all collected data, thereby enhancing exploration diversity and improving training stability compared to methods that discard samples. It has been recognized as a notable advancement for RL in massively parallel environments.

Despite the benefits of agent ensembles like SAPG in promoting diversity, a question remains regarding the optimal level of inter-policy diversity. The current work identifies a gap in understanding whether unrestricted diversity invariably leads to superior performance. It proposes that excessive divergence among policies might compromise training stability and sample efficiency, an aspect not explicitly addressed by prior methods. Policy regularization techniques, which often employ KL divergence to constrain policy shifts, are commonly used in various RL contexts (e.g., in offline RL or between old and new policies in online RL) to maintain stability. This paper extends the concept of regularization to manage diversity *between* simultaneously learning policies in an ensemble, aiming to structure exploration rather than merely broadening it.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to analyze the influence of inter-policy diversity on learning efficiency in ensemble policy gradient methods operating within massively parallel environments. Based on this analysis, the authors aim to propose a novel method that regulates this diversity to achieve more stable and sample-efficient learning.

The motivation for this work stems from several observations and hypotheses:
1. **Limited Exploration Capacity of Single Policies**: While GPU-based simulators allow for vast parallel data collection, a single policy's exploration strategy can be insufficient to cover the high-dimensional state-action spaces of complex tasks. This results in diminishing returns for methods like PPO when scaled to tens of thousands of environments.
2. **Uncontrolled Diversity in Ensemble Methods**: Ensemble methods, such as SAPG, enhance exploration by employing multiple policies. However, SAPG's reliance on entropy regularization, while promoting diversity, lacks a mechanism to control the *distance* between the leader and follower policies. The authors hypothesize that this lack of explicit control can lead to follower policies diverging excessively from the leader.
3. **Negative Consequences of Excessive Diversity**: The paper motivates that such excessive inter-policy divergence can have detrimental effects:
 - **Reduced Effective Sample Size (ESS)**: When follower policies drift significantly from the leader, the importance sampling ratios for off-policy data aggregated by the leader can deviate considerably from unity. This increases the variance of the importance weights, reducing the ESS and thus making follower samples less informative or useful for the leader's learning.
 - **Increased Clipping Bias in PPO**: PPO algorithms stabilize updates by clipping importance sampling ratios. Excessive policy divergence leads to more frequent clipping, which introduces a larger bias into the gradient estimate. This can destabilize the leader's learning process.
 - **Compromised Training Stability**: The combination of reduced ESS and increased clipping bias can collectively undermine the overall training stability and the guarantees of monotonic improvement for the leader policy.

Therefore, the core motivation is to develop a method that promotes diverse exploration while simultaneously ensuring that this diversity is structured and regulated. This regulation is intended to keep follower policies within a meaningful proximity to the leader, thereby leveraging the benefits of diverse exploration without incurring the costs of instability and inefficiency caused by uncontrolled divergence.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research approach consists of a theoretical analysis of policy diversity effects, followed by the proposition and empirical evaluation of a new algorithm, Coupled Policy Optimization (CPO).

**Theoretical Analysis of Policy Diversity:**
The paper theoretically investigates how inter-policy diversity impacts ensemble policy gradient methods, specifically concerning data coverage and training stability.
- **Proposition 1: ESS Reduction:** The analysis demonstrates an inverse relationship between the expected absolute deviation of the Importance Sampling (IS) ratio from 1 and the Effective Sample Size (ESS). As leader and follower policies diverge, the IS ratio for off-policy samples collected by followers and used by the leader deviates more significantly from 1, leading to higher variance in IS ratios and a reduced ESS. This suggests that samples from highly misaligned follower policies contribute minimally to the leader's learning.
- **Proposition 2: PPO Clipping Bias:** The paper establishes an upper bound for the L2 norm of the gradient estimation bias introduced by PPO's clipping operator. This bound is shown to be related to the expectation involving the IS ratio deviation. Increased policy divergence leads to more pronounced clipping, which in turn results in a larger bias in the gradient estimate and can destabilize the leader's learning.
- **Proposition 3: KL Divergence as a Bound:** Crucially, the analysis proves that the expected absolute deviation of the IS ratio from 1 is upper-bounded by the KL divergence between the follower and leader policies (via Pinsker's inequality). This proposition provides a theoretical basis for using KL divergence constraints to regulate inter-policy distance and thereby mitigate the issues of reduced ESS and increased clipping bias.

**Proposed Method: Coupled Policy Optimization (CPO):**
CPO extends the SAPG framework by introducing mechanisms to regulate inter-agent distance during training.
- **Follower's Policy Update under KL Constraint (Section 5.1):**
 - Each follower policy update is formulated as a constrained optimization problem: maximizing the follower's advantage function subject to a KL divergence constraint relative to the leader policy: $D_{KL}(\pi_{F_i}(\cdot|s) || \pi_L(\cdot|s)) \le \epsilon_{KL}$.
 - Drawing inspiration from AWAC (Nair et al., 2020), this constrained problem is approximated by a parametric objective (Equation 10). This objective includes a term where the logarithm of the follower policy is weighted by the exponential of its advantage function, and an existing SAPG follower loss term. A temperature parameter, $\lambda_f$, controls the strength of the KL constraint. This mechanism aims to keep follower policies exploring within a bounded neighborhood of the leader, ensuring collected samples remain informative.
- **Adversarial Reward for Followers Distribution (Section 5.2):**
 - To counteract potential overconcentration of followers around the leader due to the KL constraint, an intrinsic adversarial reward is introduced. This reward encourages sufficient separation and distinct exploration among the follower policies.
 - Inspired by DIAYN (Eysenbach et al., 2018), a discriminator network, $D_\xi(y|s_t, a_t)$, is trained to predict the identity (index) of the policy that generated a given state-action pair $(s_t, a_t)$.
 - The classification loss of this discriminator is then used to generate an intrinsic reward, $r_{adv_t} = \lambda_{adv} \log D_\xi(y|s_t, a_t)$, where $\lambda_{adv}$ is a scaling factor. This reward is only provided to the follower agents, encouraging them to explore distinct state-action regions that allow the discriminator to differentiate them. The leader agent's updates are based solely on environmental rewards.

**Experimental Setup:**
- **Tasks:** Evaluation was conducted on a suite of ten robotic tasks, including six challenging dexterous manipulation tasks (e.g., ShadowHand, AllegroKuka Regrasping), two gripper-based manipulation tasks (FrankaCubePush, FrankaCubeStack), and two locomotion tasks (Humanoid, Anymal). All tasks provided dense rewards.
- **Simulation Environment:** Isaac Gym was used for massively parallel simulations, running $N = 24,576$ environments concurrently.
- **Baselines:** The performance of CPO was compared against PPO, DexPBT, and SAPG. All baselines are built upon the PPO algorithm.
- **Ensemble Configuration:** For DexPBT, SAPG, and CPO, the total environments were divided into $M=6$ blocks, corresponding to one leader and five follower policies.
- **Training Details:** Shared policy and value networks, conditioned on identification vectors, were used across agents. Hyperparameters were consistent with SAPG where applicable. Experiments were run with five random seeds for statistical robustness.
- **Computational Complexity (Appendix A.3):** CPO introduces additional computational overhead compared to SAPG due to the KL constraint and adversarial reward. While SAPG involves roughly 7 auto-differentiable forward-backward passes, CPO introduces approximately 5 additional components for follower updates from leader samples, and 6 more for discriminator training. However, given that data collection dominates wall-clock time in massively parallel settings, the overall increase in training time for CPO (approximately 24% for KL constraint, 52% with adversarial reward) is considered acceptable, especially considering its efficiency gains in terms of environment steps.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate the effectiveness of Coupled Policy Optimization (CPO) across various robotic tasks, with detailed analyses supporting its design principles.

**Training Performance:**
- CPO consistently exhibited higher sample efficiency and achieved stronger final performance compared to PPO, DexPBT, and SAPG, particularly in complex manipulation tasks (Figure 2, Table 1).
- For instance, while DexPBT failed to learn on tasks like AllegroKuka Regrasping and Franka tasks, and SAPG struggled with Two-Arms Reorientation, CPO demonstrated more robust learning capabilities.
- In several tasks, CPO reached the final performance levels of SAPG with approximately half the number of environment steps, indicating an acquisition of more efficient exploration behaviors.
- In locomotion tasks, where problems are comparatively simpler, performance differences between algorithms were less pronounced. However, CPO still converged slightly faster than SAPG, suggesting benefits from its stabilization mechanisms within the leader-follower framework.

**Ablation Study on KL Constraint:**
- An ablation study varying the KL coefficient ($\lambda_f$) in CPO (without adversarial reward) showed that CPO was robust to a range of values, consistently outperforming SAPG (Figure 3). Stronger constraints (smaller $\lambda_f$) generally led to improved performance.
- Empirical validation of theoretical propositions was observed (Table 2). Stronger KL constraints resulted in smaller mean IS ratio deviations from 1 and higher Effective Sample Sizes (ESS) at $5 \times 10^9$ environment steps. This directly supports Proposition 1 and Proposition 3, confirming that regulating policy divergence via KL constraints improves sample efficiency and makes off-policy samples more useful for the leader.

**KL Divergence Analysis:**
- Visualizations of inter-policy KL divergence during training (Figure 4) provided insights into agent interactions.
- In tasks where CPO surpassed SAPG (e.g., ShadowHand, AllegroKuka Reorientation), SAPG exhibited significant misalignment, with several follower policies diverging substantially from the leader. Such misaligned policies were hypothesized to generate unhelpful or destabilizing samples for the leader's learning.
- In contrast, CPO maintained stable inter-agent distances, with follower policies consistently distributed around the leader. This structured policy formation was observed to generate more informative samples for the leader.
- Notably, in CPO, the leader remained the closest agent to every follower, suggesting that the KL regularization, in conjunction with other regularization terms, effectively distributed followers without excessive concentration. This indicated that CPO's asymmetric leader-follower learning process successfully maintained diversity while controlling policy distances.

**Ablation Study on Adversarial Reward and KL Constraint (Appendix A.4):**
- **CPO (w/o KLC):** Removing the KL constraint resulted in a degradation of training performance and an increase in follower misalignment (Figure 5, Figure 7). This empirically confirms the importance of the KL constraint for maintaining sample efficiency and training stability.
- **CPO (w/o AdR):** Removing the adversarial reward resulted in only marginal differences in training performance compared to the full CPO, though it sometimes reduced variance across random seeds (Figure 5). The discriminator loss for CPO (w/o AdR) converged close to random classification (Figure 6), suggesting that the adversarial reward's direct impact on promoting distinct policy exploration within the KL-bounded region might be subtle or task-dependent, or that the KL constraint itself already provided sufficient separation.

**Entropy Regularization Ablation on SAPG (Appendix A.7):**
- An ablation on entropy regularization in SAPG demonstrated that increasing entropy regularization often led to higher inter-policy KL divergence and severe follower misalignment (Figure 9), even if its effect on overall performance varied (Figure 8). This observation supports the paper's central argument that uncontrolled diversity, even when intended to promote exploration, can lead to policy misalignment and instability in ensemble learning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the understanding and advancement of reinforcement learning in massively parallel environments by providing theoretical and empirical insights into the role of policy diversity in ensemble methods.

**Significance:**
- **Refined Understanding of Policy Diversity**: The work challenges the notion that greater policy diversity inherently leads to better performance. It demonstrates that *controlled and structured diversity* is critical for stable and sample-efficient learning, particularly in large-scale settings where single policies struggle with limited exploration.
- **Theoretical Foundations**: The paper provides theoretical grounding by linking excessive inter-policy divergence to measurable degradations in learning, specifically through reduced Effective Sample Size (ESS) and increased bias in PPO's gradient estimates. The use of KL divergence as a quantifiable measure and control mechanism for this phenomenon offers a theoretical framework for future developments.
- **Algorithmic Advancement**: Coupled Policy Optimization (CPO) offers a demonstrated improvement over existing state-of-the-art ensemble methods like SAPG, PBT, and PPO. Its consistent superior performance and sample efficiency, especially on challenging dexterous manipulation tasks, suggest its potential for practical applications in robotics and other complex control domains.
- **Structured Exploration**: CPO's ability to naturally induce a structured policy formation, where follower policies are distributed around the leader without excessive misalignment or overconcentration, presents a model for effective exploration strategies in multi-agent learning systems.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Accelerated Robotic Learning**: The improved sample efficiency of CPO could significantly reduce the time and computational resources required to train complex robotic skills, making advanced robotic manipulation more accessible and practical.
- **Enhanced Stability in Large-Scale RL**: By addressing the stability issues associated with uncontrolled policy diversity, CPO can enable more robust and reliable deployment of RL systems in production environments.
- **New Directions for Ensemble RL**: The findings may inspire further research into dynamic control of policy diversity, adaptive ensemble sizes, and more sophisticated regularization techniques that balance exploration with exploitation and stability.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The paper acknowledges a limitation regarding CPO's reliance on a fixed number of policies and environments assigned per policy. The optimal exploration range and the number of agents can vary significantly based on the specific task and the current stage of training. Future research could focus on developing adaptive algorithms capable of automatically adjusting these parameters. Such advancements would further unlock the potential of massively parallel environments, particularly for tasks demanding extensive exploration and high-dimensional action spaces.
