# Unleashing the Intrinsic Visual Representation Capability of Multimodal Large Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework named LaVer strengthens the intrinsic visual modeling capability of Multimodal Large Language Models by integrating direct visual supervisory signals in the LLM's latent semantic space. This approach enhances visual reasoning, improves visual attention, and yields consistent performance improvements across various multimodal benchmarks, including a 19.22% gain on OCR-Bench.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) exhibit a "modality imbalance," often underutilizing visual information and prioritizing textual cues.
- This imbalance leads to degraded visual performance and an increased propensity for visual hallucinations not grounded in the visual input.
- Current MLLM training paradigms, reliant on "next-text-token-prediction," cause visual tokens to lose discriminative power in deeper layers, resulting in "progressive visual representation homogenization."

## Method

- Introduces ==Latent Visual Reconstruction (LaVer)==, a masked image modeling approach applied to vision tokens within the LLM's joint latent semantic space, leveraging an ==EMA teacher== for direct visual supervision.
- Incorporates a mixed attention mechanism and ==2D-Rotary Position Embeddings (2D-RoPE)== to enhance spatial awareness and better align visual processing with image structure.
- Employs ==Clipped Gram-Anchoring (CGA)==, an asymmetric regularizer, to prevent visual feature inconsistency and foster the learning of discriminative visual representations.

## Results

- LaVer consistently achieved performance improvements across diverse multimodal benchmarks, including a **19.22%** gain on OCR-Bench and **6.72%** on MMVP when using SigLIP 2.
- The framework demonstrated broad generalizability across various vision encoders and robust scalability across different model sizes (1.5B, 3B, 7B) and data volumes.
- Qualitative and quantitative analyses confirmed enhanced visual attention allocation and improved cross-modal synergy, while preserving the model's core language capabilities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The "modality imbalance issue" in MLLMs stems from insufficient direct visual supervision, leading to a loss of discriminative power in visual representations over deeper layers.
> - Masked Image Modeling (MIM) at the latent semantic level of the LLM, coupled with a student-teacher framework, offers effective direct visual supervision.
> - Direct reconstruction loss alone can lead to "visual feature inconsistency," highlighting the need for asymmetric regularization like Clipped Gram-Anchoring to encourage discriminative feature learning.
