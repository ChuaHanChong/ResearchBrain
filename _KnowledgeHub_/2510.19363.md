# LoongRL: Reinforcement Learning for Advanced Reasoning over Long Contexts

> [!summary] Summary
> LOONGRL introduces a reinforcement learning method to enable large language models to perform advanced reasoning over long contexts. It achieves this by generating high-difficulty synthetic data to induce an emergent "plan-retrieve-reason-recheck" thinking pattern, with LoongRL-14B achieving an average score of 74.2 on LongBench v1 and 100% retrieval accuracy at 128K tokens.

## Problem

- Large language models (LLMs) primarily excel at information retrieval from long contexts but struggle with deep, multi-step reasoning across extensive documents.
- Existing methods for long-context reasoning are limited by base model capacity or data quality, and there is a lack of high-difficulty, verifiable RL training data compelling LLMs to perform strategic reasoning beyond simple retrieval in long contexts.
- Directly training LLMs with RL on full-length long contexts (e.g., 128K tokens) incurs prohibitive computational and memory costs.

## Method

- Developed ==KeyChain==, a novel data synthesis approach that transforms existing short multi-hop QA into high-difficulty long-context reasoning tasks by embedding UUID chains and distracting documents.
- Implemented a data-driven reinforcement learning framework using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) and a rule-based "two-way substring exact match" reward for stable training.
- Utilized a multi-stage training recipe, including warm-up, KeyChain augmentation, and difficulty-focused training, performed at a shorter context length (16K tokens) to generalize to longer contexts.

## Results

- LoongRL-**14B** achieved an average score of 74.2 on LongBench v1, closely approaching the performance of much larger frontier models like o3-mini (74.5) and DeepSeek-R1 (74.9).
- Realized absolute accuracy improvements of +**23.5%** for **7B** models and +**21.1%** for **14B** models on long-context multi-hop QA tasks compared to their base Qwen2.5-Instruct counterparts.
- Demonstrated enhanced long-context retrieval, with LoongRL-**7B** achieving **100%** accuracy on the "Needle in a Haystack" benchmark across all depths and context lengths up to **128K**, while preserving general short-context reasoning abilities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - KeyChain data synthesis effectively induces an emergent "plan–retrieve–reason–recheck" thinking pattern in LLMs, which is crucial for advanced long-context reasoning.
> - The emergent reasoning pattern learned from 16K token contexts generalizes remarkably well to much longer contexts, demonstrating effectiveness up to 128K tokens and mitigating high computational costs.
> - A carefully designed rule-based reward function (two-way substring exact match) is highly effective for robust RL training in general QA tasks, avoiding the need for expensive LLM-as-a-judge methods.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Large Language Models has seen remarkable progress in various domains, particularly in understanding and generating human-like text. Within this landscape, two critical capabilities are emerging as focal points: reasoning and long-context understanding.

Historically, advancements in LLM reasoning have been heavily influenced by techniques like Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting and reinforcement learning (RL), which aim to elicit more structured and deliberate thought processes. These methods have significantly enhanced short-context reasoning in tasks such as mathematics and code generation, often by encouraging emergent behaviors like self-reflection or "Aha" moments. However, the existing literature largely focuses on scenarios where the relevant information is either readily available within a short input context or relies on the model's internal knowledge base.

Simultaneously, LLMs have expanded their context windows, now capable of processing thousands to hundreds of thousands of tokens. While this enables models to ingest vast amounts of information, current state-of-the-art models primarily excel at *retrieval* from these long contexts, such as finding a specific "needle in a haystack." The more complex challenge of *reasoning* effectively over extensive, potentially noisy, and multi-document long contexts remains largely unaddressed. Many real-world applications, from legal document analysis to debugging large codebases, demand not just information retrieval but the ability to synthesize, infer, and reason across disparate pieces of information spread throughout a lengthy input.

Previous efforts to improve long-context reasoning have typically fallen into two categories: prompting-based methods and synthetic-data supervised fine-tuning (SFT). Prompting approaches are inherently limited by the base model's underlying reasoning capacity. Synthetic-data SFT, while expanding context, often introduces noise or bias and struggles to cultivate sophisticated reasoning patterns beyond simple information extraction. Even recent RL-based models that show promise in long-context tasks, like QwenLong-L1, have focused on extending reasoning trajectories but left open the critical question of designing high-quality, high-difficulty RL training data tailored to induce advanced reasoning.

LoongRL directly addresses this critical gap by proposing a novel data-driven RL approach specifically designed to foster *advanced reasoning capabilities* in LLMs over long contexts, moving beyond mere retrieval. It hypothesizes that discovering and mastering specific thinking patterns for long-context reasoning is key, and it uses RL to enable these patterns to emerge. By introducing a new method for generating challenging long-context reasoning data, LoongRL aims to teach models to not only locate relevant information but also to strategically process and reason across it, thus bridging the divide between extended context windows and deep contextual understanding.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of the LoongRL research is to enable large language models to move beyond mere information retrieval and acquire advanced reasoning capabilities when operating over exceptionally long input contexts. The authors identify that while LLMs have made strides in supporting longer context windows and improving short-context reasoning through reinforcement learning (RL), advanced thinking patterns required for complex, long-context reasoning remain largely unexplored.

The motivation for this work stems from several key observations and challenges:

1. **Gap in Long-Context Reasoning:** Current LLMs, despite their expanded context windows, predominantly excel at retrieving information from long documents rather than performing deep, multi-step reasoning that integrates information across extensive textual inputs. This limits their utility in real-world applications such as analyzing legal documents or debugging large codebases, which demand sophisticated contextual understanding.
2. **Lack of Specific Thinking Patterns:** The specific thinking patterns necessary for effective long-context reasoning are not well-understood or explicitly defined. Inspired by the success of RL in eliciting emergent reasoning patterns (like "Aha" moments or self-reflection) in short-context tasks, the researchers hypothesize that RL could similarly discover and foster advanced patterns for long contexts.
3. **Scarcity of High-Difficulty RL Data:** Effective RL training requires challenging long-context problems that cannot be solved by simple retrieval or internal knowledge. These problems must compel the model to reason, retrieve, and integrate facts from vast contexts, and have verifiable answers to enable reliable reward signals. Such high-quality, difficult data is currently scarce.
4. **Prohibitive Computational Costs:** Training LLMs with RL on full-length long contexts (e.g., 128K tokens) incurs prohibitive compute and memory costs, making direct training infeasible at standard scales. A method for efficient training that generalizes to longer contexts is crucial.
5. **Preservation of Core Capabilities:** Any enhancement to long-context reasoning must not degrade existing critical abilities, such as general short-context reasoning or long-context retrieval, which are vital for practical LLM deployment.

To address these challenges, the LoongRL project aims to achieve the following specific objectives:
- Introduce **KeyChain**, a novel data synthesis approach that transforms existing short multi-hop QA into high-difficulty long-context reasoning tasks by embedding UUID chains that hide the true question among distracting documents.
- Develop a **data-driven reinforcement learning method** that utilizes KeyChain data to induce an emergent, human-like "plan–retrieve–reason–recheck" thinking pattern.
- Demonstrate that this emergent reasoning pattern can **generalize effectively** far beyond the training context length (e.g., from 16K to 128K tokens), mitigating the prohibitive costs of full-length RL rollouts.
- Implement a **stable and effective RL training recipe**, including a rule-based reward design (two-way substring exact match) to enable robust optimization and a balanced data-mixing strategy to preserve short-context and general reasoning abilities.
- Achieve **state-of-the-art performance** in long-context multi-hop QA, rivaling much larger frontier models, while also improving long-context retrieval and maintaining general short-context reasoning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

LoongRL is a data-driven reinforcement learning method designed to train LLMs for advanced reasoning over long contexts. Its methodology is primarily characterized by the innovative **KeyChain data construction**, a robust **RL training algorithm**, and a carefully designed **multi-stage training recipe**.

#### 3.1. KeyChain Data Construction

The core innovation of LoongRL lies in its KeyChain data synthesis approach, designed to create high-difficulty long-context problems that compel models to engage in advanced reasoning. The construction adheres to three principles: (i) questions and answers are from real-world datasets to ensure reliability; (ii) solving questions requires reasoning over the full long input context, not just retrieval or internal knowledge; and (iii) questions are sufficiently challenging to trigger advanced reasoning.

The KeyChain data construction process involves three main steps:

1. **Seed Dataset Curation and Filtering:** The process begins with curating a high-quality seed dataset from three existing multi-hop QA datasets: HotpotQA, MuSiQue, and 2WikiMultiHopQA. Initially, 277K QA instances are collected. To ensure an appropriate difficulty level for RL training, tasks that are either too easy (pass rate of 1) or excessively hard (pass rate of 0) are filtered out using Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct, resulting in 72K examples of moderate difficulty.
2. **Long Context Filling (Context Extension):** Each short original context (`oL_i`) associated with a filtered QA pair (`oq_i`, `oa_i`) is expanded into a long context (`L'_i`) of approximately 16,384 tokens. This is achieved by inserting additional real-world distracting documents, sampled from the initially filtered-out QA tasks, ensuring no overlap with the original context. This step simulates real-world scenarios where relevant information is buried within extensive irrelevant text.
3. **KeyChain Insertion:** This is the most crucial step. For each long-context QA task (`L'_i`, `oq_i`, `oa_i`), linear key-value chains are inserted into the context, transforming `L'_i` into `L_i`. These chains use 32-character UUIDs as keys, where a key's value contains the next key in the chain, forming a step-by-step tracing path. Two types of chains are created: one that ultimately resolves to the original question (`oq_i`), and multiple others that resolve to plausible but irrelevant distracting questions. A new question (`q_i`) is then constructed, which requires the model to: (a) start from an initial key; (b) trace the correct UUID chain within `L_i` to recover `oq_i`; and (c) finally perform long-context reasoning over `L_i` to generate the correct answer (`a_i = oa_i`). This design significantly increases task difficulty and necessitates a structured reasoning process.

This KeyChain construction remarkably induces an emergent "plan–retrieve–reason–recheck" reasoning pattern in models during RL training (as illustrated in Figure 1a). This structured approach involves explicit problem decomposition, targeted information retrieval, and active re-checking, leading to more logical and reliable solutions. Critically, this pattern generalizes well beyond the 16K training context length, proving effective for tasks up to 128K tokens, thereby addressing the high computational cost challenge.

#### 3.2. Long-Context Reinforcement Learning

The LoongRL framework employs **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)** for training. GRPO is an off-policy RL algorithm that samples multiple rollout trajectories from an old policy and optimizes the current policy by maximizing an objective that incorporates an advantage function. The advantage is computed relative to a group of rewards, normalizing individual rollout rewards by the mean and standard deviation of the group.

To stabilize training, standard practices are followed: a small KL penalty is used to prevent drastic policy deviations, and the entropy loss term (often used for exploration) is omitted to avoid uncontrolled entropy growth and instability, based on prior successful works.

A novel **Rule-based Reward Design** is implemented for general QA tasks, where answers can vary in form. Instead of relying on expensive LLM-as-a-judge methods or strict exact match, LoongRL uses a binary accuracy reward (0 or 1) based on a "two-way substring exact match." The model is prompted to output its final answer within `\boxed{}`, and the reward is assigned if the extracted answer (`y_ans`) contains the ground truth answer (`a`) as a substring, or vice-versa (`a ⊆ y_ans ∨ y_ans ⊆ a`). This design tolerates minor variations in valid answers while maintaining high precision and mitigating reward hacking.

#### 3.3. Training Recipe

LoongRL training is conducted on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct and Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct models, both supporting a 128K context window. The recipe balances enhancing long-context reasoning with preserving general short-context abilities.

- **Training Length and Data Mix:** To leverage the generalization property of KeyChain data, RL training is performed at a 16K context length. The training dataset (Table 1) is mixed with four types of data:
 1. **High-difficulty KeyChain data:** 7,500 examples (2,500 each from HotpotQA, MuSiQue, 2WikiMultiHopQA).
 2. **Medium-level multi-hop QA data:** 7,500 examples (2,500 each from the same three datasets). Important for smaller models initially struggling with KeyChain.
 3. **Long-context needle retrieval data:** 1,024 synthetic examples based on RULER, maintaining retrieval ability.
 4. **Math data:** 5,000 short-context problems (2,500 hard from DAPO, 2,500 easy from MATH) to preserve general short-context reasoning.
- **Multi-Stage Training:** A three-stage curriculum is adopted:
 1. **Warm-up (for 7B model only):** One epoch of training on the dataset *excluding* KeyChain data. This allows smaller models to improve baseline retrieval and general reasoning before tackling the harder KeyChain tasks. The 14B model skips this due to its stronger base capabilities.
 2. **Stage I (KeyChain Augmentation):** KeyChain data is introduced, explicitly encouraging planning, precise retrieval, and integrated reasoning.
 3. **Stage II (Difficulty-Focused Training):** After Stage I, rollouts are generated, and examples correctly solved in all rollouts are discarded. RL continues on the remaining challenging subset (30–40% of data), focusing updates on difficult cases to improve efficiency and prevent overtraining on mastered problems.

This comprehensive methodology, from data synthesis to the multi-stage training curriculum and reward design, collectively enables LoongRL to develop models with advanced, generalizable reasoning capabilities over long contexts.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate that LoongRL significantly advances long-context reasoning in LLMs, while preserving other critical capabilities.

#### 4.1. Competitive Long-Context Reasoning at Smaller Scale

LoongRL models achieve frontier-level performance in long-context reasoning, rivaling much larger models.
- **Significant Absolute Gains:** LoongRL substantially boosts the performance of its base models. On long-context multi-hop QA tasks, LoongRL-7B-Instruct and LoongRL-14B-Instruct achieve absolute accuracy improvements of +23.5% and +21.1% respectively, compared to their base Qwen2.5-Instruct counterparts.
- **Rivaling Frontier Models:** The resulting LoongRL-14B model achieves an average score of 74.2 on LongBench v1, closely approaching the performance of much larger frontier models like o3-mini (74.5) and DeepSeek-R1 (74.9). LoongRL-7B also achieves an impressive 72.4, outperforming all R1-distilled models and even QwenLong-L1-32B, a model with a larger parameter count and longer training context.
- **Superior to Existing Methods:** The KeyChain-driven RL proves far more effective than other state-of-the-art approaches. R1-distilled Qwen models, trained on long-CoT reasoning data, show modest gains (+11.8% for 14B) or even performance degradation (-17.7% for 7B). QwenLong-L1-32B, a model trained with conventional long-context RL, only improves by +4.6% on average, whereas LoongRL-7B surpasses it by +2.3%.

#### 4.2. Generalization Beyond Training Length

A key finding is that the emergent "plan–retrieve–reason–recheck" thinking pattern induced by KeyChain data generalizes remarkably well to contexts much longer than the training length.
- **16K Training to 128K Inference:** Despite being trained only on 16K token contexts, LoongRL models effectively handle reasoning tasks up to 128K tokens.
- **Impressive Gains on Longer Benchmarks:** On NarrativeQA (benchmarks with input lengths from 32K to 64K), LoongRL-7B and 14B achieve substantial absolute gains of +14.8% and +16.0%, respectively, surpassing models explicitly trained with longer contexts.
- **Robust Performance on RULER:** On the RULER benchmark (up to 128K tokens), while other baselines show sharp performance degradation with increasing context length, LoongRL models maintain consistently strong performance, demonstrating robust transfer of the learned reasoning pattern.

#### 4.3. Near-Lossless General Short Reasoning and Improved Long-Context Retrieval

LoongRL effectively preserves and sometimes even enhances the base models' core capabilities, avoiding common trade-offs.
- **Preservation of Short-Context Abilities:** On standard general and short-context reasoning benchmarks (MMLU, MATH, IFEval), LoongRL models maintain strong performance. LoongRL even yields gains on MMLU (+2.8% for 7B, +1.1% for 14B), contrasting with performance drops observed in R1-distilled models and QwenLong-L1-32B. Minimal declines are observed on IFEval, and math reasoning abilities are stably preserved.
- **Enhanced Long-Context Retrieval:** LoongRL substantially improves retrieval capabilities. On the "Needle in a Haystack" benchmark, the base Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct fails, but LoongRL-7B achieves perfect 100% retrieval accuracy across all depths and context lengths up to 128K. Other baselines, including R1-Distill-7B and QwenLong-L1-32B, show limitations or failures at extended lengths. The emergent reasoning patterns also lead to more structured and human-readable retrieval trajectories (Appendix A.5).

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies Confirm KeyChain and Reward Effectiveness

- **KeyChain's Critical Role:** Ablation studies confirm that KeyChain data is crucial for the substantial gains. Training without KeyChain data yields moderate improvements (66.2 average score for 7B), while incorporating it drives a significant leap to 72.4, demonstrating its unique ability to induce advanced reasoning patterns. Models trained without KeyChain exhibit mixed, less systematic reasoning-retrieval patterns prone to errors (Figure 1b).
- **Effective Answer Verifier:** The two-way substring exact match reward design proves highly effective. It achieves an average score of 72.4, outperforming F1 score (65.1), LLM-as-a-judge (65.2), and strict exact match (69.2). This highlights its practical reliability for robust RL training by tolerating valid answer variations while maintaining precision.
- **Multi-Stage Training Benefits:** The multi-stage RL curriculum shows consistent growth in long-context reasoning accuracy and steadily increasing response lengths throughout the training stages, indicating sustained learning and pattern development.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The LoongRL research presents a significant advancement in the field of large language models, particularly concerning their ability to perform sophisticated reasoning over long and complex contexts. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Bridging the Long-Context Reasoning Gap:** LoongRL successfully tackles the long-standing challenge of enabling LLMs to move beyond mere information retrieval in extensive documents towards true, advanced reasoning. This is a critical step for deploying LLMs in real-world applications that require deep contextual understanding and inference across vast amounts of text, such as legal analysis, scientific literature review, or complex code auditing.

2. **Novel Data-Driven Inducement of Reasoning Patterns:** The introduction of **KeyChain data synthesis** is a groundbreaking methodological contribution. By transforming standard multi-hop QA into high-difficulty tasks through the insertion of UUID chains and distracting documents, LoongRL provides a scalable and effective way to generate training data that explicitly demands structured reasoning. This approach compels the model to develop an emergent "plan–retrieve–reason–recheck" thinking pattern, which is more robust and human-like than previously observed behaviors.

3. **Efficiency and Scalability through Generalization:** One of the most impactful findings is the remarkable generalization capability of the learned reasoning patterns. By training models on 16K token contexts, LoongRL enables them to effectively solve tasks up to 128K tokens without the prohibitive computational cost of full-length RL rollouts. This dramatically reduces the resource requirements for developing highly capable long-context LLMs, making advanced reasoning more accessible and scalable.

4. **Competitive Performance with Smaller Models:** LoongRL achieves frontier-level long-context reasoning performance with significantly smaller models (7B and 14B parameters). The LoongRL-14B model rivals much larger proprietary models like o3-mini and DeepSeek-R1, demonstrating that strategic data design and targeted RL can yield highly competitive results without necessarily relying on immense model scale. This has profound implications for democratizing access to powerful LLM capabilities and fostering more efficient AI development.

5. **Holistic Capability Improvement without Trade-offs:** Unlike some previous methods that enhance one capability at the expense of another, LoongRL demonstrates near-lossless preservation of short-context reasoning abilities and even *improves* long-context retrieval. This indicates a holistic and robust enhancement of the model's overall intelligence, making LoongRL-trained models more versatile and reliable for diverse tasks.

6. **Advancement in RL for LLMs:** The work contributes to the broader field of reinforcement learning for LLMs by showcasing the effectiveness of carefully designed, rule-based reward functions (two-way substring exact match) for general QA, mitigating reward hacking concerns often associated with LLM-as-a-judge approaches. The multi-stage training curriculum also provides a practical blueprint for stably optimizing LLMs on increasingly difficult tasks.

In summary, LoongRL represents a significant leap forward in empowering LLMs with sophisticated long-context reasoning. Its innovative data generation strategy, combined with efficient RL training, offers a powerful pathway to developing more intelligent, efficient, and versatile language models capable of tackling complex information processing demands across a wide array of real-world applications.
