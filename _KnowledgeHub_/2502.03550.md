# TD-M(PC)$^2$: Improving Temporal Difference MPC Through Policy Constraint

> [!summary] Summary
> Addressing persistent value overestimation in Temporal Difference Model Predictive Control, the TD-M(PC)2^22 framework introduces a policy constraint to align the learned policy with the data distribution. This modification significantly reduces value approximation errors and improves performance in high-dimensional continuous control tasks, yielding over 100% average gains on HumanoidBench locomotion benchmarks.

## Problem

- Existing Temporal Difference Model Predictive Control (TD-MPC) algorithms suffer from persistent value overestimation, particularly in high-dimensional continuous control tasks.
- A "structural policy mismatch" occurs where the data-generating MPC planner's policy diverges from the learned nominal policy, leading to out-of-distribution (OOD) queries during value function approximation.
- This mismatch causes approximation errors to accumulate through policy iteration and amplify during the planning process, hindering performance and stability.

## Method

- **TD-M(PC)²** introduces a ==policy regularization== term during policy iteration to constrain the learned policy, encouraging it to align with the data distribution generated by the MPC planner.
- Employs a minimalist modification to the ==TD-MPC2== framework, leveraging a ==Lagrangian approach== for policy improvement similar to offline reinforcement learning methods, requiring fewer than ten lines of code with no additional computational overhead.
- Implements an ==adaptive curriculum== for the regularization coefficient, allowing for initial exploration before gradually enforcing ==conservatism== to mitigate out-of-distribution actions.

## Results

- The proposed method substantially reduced value approximation error, aligning the learned value function more closely with true expected returns across all tested tasks, mitigating previous overestimations of up to **2159%**.
- Achieved an average performance improvement of over **100%** on **14** challenging 61-DoF HumanoidBench locomotion tasks compared to TD-MPC2 by the end of training.
- Demonstrated improved performance on several high-dimensional DeepMind Control Suite tasks, especially those with larger action spaces, while maintaining comparable performance in others, and confirmed that conservatism in policy updates is a dominant factor in these gains.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Persistent value overestimation in TD-MPC is primarily caused by a structural policy mismatch between the online planner's behavior policy and the learned nominal policy.
> - Theoretical analysis reveals how approximation errors and out-of-distribution (OOD) queries accumulate and amplify through policy iteration, leading to increased policy divergence and performance degradation.
> - Applying a policy constraint, which encourages the learned policy to operate within the data distribution, effectively mitigates these issues and enhances the stability and performance of TD-MPC.
