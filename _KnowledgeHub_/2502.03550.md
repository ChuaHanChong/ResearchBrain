# TD-M(PC)$^2$: Improving Temporal Difference MPC Through Policy Constraint

> [!summary] Summary
> Addressing persistent value overestimation in Temporal Difference Model Predictive Control, the TD-M(PC)2^22 framework introduces a policy constraint to align the learned policy with the data distribution. This modification significantly reduces value approximation errors and improves performance in high-dimensional continuous control tasks, yielding over 100% average gains on HumanoidBench locomotion benchmarks.

## Problem

- Existing Temporal Difference Model Predictive Control (TD-MPC) algorithms suffer from persistent value overestimation, particularly in high-dimensional continuous control tasks.
- A "structural policy mismatch" occurs where the data-generating MPC planner's policy diverges from the learned nominal policy, leading to out-of-distribution (OOD) queries during value function approximation.
- This mismatch causes approximation errors to accumulate through policy iteration and amplify during the planning process, hindering performance and stability.

## Method

- **TD-M(PC)²** introduces a ==policy regularization== term during policy iteration to constrain the learned policy, encouraging it to align with the data distribution generated by the MPC planner.
- Employs a minimalist modification to the ==TD-MPC2== framework, leveraging a ==Lagrangian approach== for policy improvement similar to offline reinforcement learning methods, requiring fewer than ten lines of code with no additional computational overhead.
- Implements an ==adaptive curriculum== for the regularization coefficient, allowing for initial exploration before gradually enforcing ==conservatism== to mitigate out-of-distribution actions.

## Results

- The proposed method substantially reduced value approximation error, aligning the learned value function more closely with true expected returns across all tested tasks, mitigating previous overestimations of up to **2159%**.
- Achieved an average performance improvement of over **100%** on **14** challenging 61-DoF HumanoidBench locomotion tasks compared to TD-MPC2 by the end of training.
- Demonstrated improved performance on several high-dimensional DeepMind Control Suite tasks, especially those with larger action spaces, while maintaining comparable performance in others, and confirmed that conservatism in policy updates is a dominant factor in these gains.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Persistent value overestimation in TD-MPC is primarily caused by a structural policy mismatch between the online planner's behavior policy and the learned nominal policy.
> - Theoretical analysis reveals how approximation errors and out-of-distribution (OOD) queries accumulate and amplify through policy iteration, leading to increased policy divergence and performance degradation.
> - Applying a policy constraint, which encourages the learned policy to operate within the data distribution, effectively mitigates these issues and enhances the stability and performance of TD-MPC.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated within the field of model-based reinforcement learning (MBRL), specifically focusing on approaches that integrate model-based planning with learned value or policy priors, often referred to as Temporal Difference Model Predictive Control (TD-MPC). MBRL algorithms aim to improve data efficiency and performance in continuous control tasks by learning a world model that predicts future states and rewards. This contrasts with model-free reinforcement learning, which directly learns policies or value functions without an explicit model.

Historically, MBRL has evolved from Dyna-style architectures, which use a learned model to augment real-world experience for model-free policy learning (e.g., Dyna-Q, MBPO, Dreamer), to planning-based approaches like PlaNet and PETS, which use the model for online decision-making through trajectory optimization. Recent advancements, particularly TD-MPC, combine the strengths of these paradigms by using sampling-based Model Predictive Control (MPC) for short-horizon planning and model-free RL to acquire policy or value priors. This hybrid approach seeks to balance scalability, adaptability, and data efficiency.

The present work addresses a specific challenge within the TD-MPC framework: persistent value overestimation. This issue is identified as a form of policy mismatch, where the data-generating policy (derived from the MPC planner) diverges from the learned nominal policy or value prior. This phenomenon shares characteristics with the distributional shift problem observed in offline reinforcement learning, where policies are learned from fixed, off-policy datasets. However, in TD-MPC, the behavior policy is dynamically generated by an online planner, presenting a distinct set of challenges related to error accumulation and self-correction during continuous interaction. By proposing a policy constraint mechanism, this paper aims to stabilize value learning and enhance the performance of planning-based MBRL in high-dimensional continuous control tasks, positioning itself as an advancement in the robust design of such hybrid algorithms.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to improve the performance and stability of Temporal Difference Model Predictive Control (TD-MPC) algorithms, particularly in high-dimensional continuous control tasks. The authors identify a significant limitation in existing TD-MPC implementations that rely on standard Soft Actor-Critic (SAC)-style policy iteration for value learning: the occurrence of persistent value overestimation.

The motivation behind addressing this issue stems from several observations and theoretical considerations:

- **Empirical Performance Gaps:** The authors observed that state-of-the-art TD-MPC algorithms, such as TD-MPC2, exhibit unsatisfactory performance in certain high-dimensional locomotion tasks despite their general effectiveness in continuous control benchmarks. This underperformance is linked to the observed value overestimation.
- **Structural Policy Mismatch:** The core of the problem is attributed to a "policy mismatch." In TD-MPC, the data used for training the value and policy functions is collected by an MPC planner, which acts as the behavior policy (denoted as $\pi_H$). This planner is bootstrapped by the learned value function and nominal policy ($\pi$). However, the learned nominal policy ($\pi$) is updated independently using this planner-generated data. Consequently, the distribution of actions generated by $\pi_H$ does not consistently align with the distribution of actions that $\pi$ (or the optimal policy $Q_{\pi}$) would take, especially in regions where $\pi$ is poorly trained or exhibits errors.
- **Undetected and Uncorrected Errors:** This mismatch means that errors in the value function, particularly overestimations, may not be adequately represented or rectified during training because the planner, guided by potentially overestimated values, avoids exploring regions where these errors are most pronounced. This creates a feedback loop where overestimation persists or worsens.
- **Error Accumulation and Amplification:** Through theoretical analysis, the authors argue that this structural mismatch causes approximation errors to accumulate through policy iteration. These accumulating errors are subsequently amplified by the planning process itself, leading to significant divergence between the H-step lookahead policy ($\pi_H$) and the nominal policy ($\pi$). This exacerbates the distributional shift and prevents the value function from converging to an accurate estimate.
- **Analogy to Offline RL Challenges:** The problem is noted to resemble the distributional shift issues encountered in offline reinforcement learning, where the learned policy must operate within the distribution of a fixed behavior policy. However, the dynamic nature of the planner in online TD-MPC distinguishes this challenge and necessitates specific solutions.
- **Need for Conservative Exploitation:** These findings highlight the necessity of adopting conservative exploitation strategies when combining planners with policy improvement mechanisms. The goal is to ensure that the learned policy and value function remain robust to out-of-distribution (OOD) queries and that the learning process can effectively exploit the high-quality data generated by the planner without incurring significant generalization errors.

In summary, the key motivation is to address the fundamental structural limitation of policy mismatch in planning-based MBRL, which leads to persistent value overestimation, hinders performance, and creates instability. The objective is to mitigate this mismatch through a minimalist intervention, thereby improving the accuracy of value learning and ultimately the performance of the overall TD-MPC framework.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this paper involves identifying a core problem in TD-MPC, providing theoretical justification, and proposing a minimalist modification to an existing state-of-the-art framework.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries of Planning with Value and Policy Prior (TD-MPC2)

The work builds upon the TD-MPC2 framework, which integrates a learned world model with model predictive control. The agent learns an encoder $z = h(s, e)$, a latent dynamics model $z' = d(z, a, e)$, a reward model $\hat{r} = R(z, a, e)$, a nominal policy $\hat{a} = \pi(z, e)$, and an action-value function $\hat{q} = \hat{Q}(z, a, e)$. These components are jointly trained to maximize a cumulative discounted reward.

During planning, TD-MPC2 leverages a local trajectory optimization method, such as Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI), to find an optimal action sequence over a horizon $H$. This involves sampling action sequences, evaluating them using the learned dynamics and reward models, and crucially, bootstrapping the terminal state's value using the learned value function $\hat{V}(z_{t+H}) = E_{a_{t+H} \sim \pi(z_{t+H}, e)}[\hat{Q}(z_{t+H}, a_{t+H}, e)]$. Only the first action of the optimized sequence is executed (receding horizon control). The overall loss for training the model components ($h, d, R, Q$) is a combination of terms for dynamics prediction, reward prediction, and Q-function approximation using a bootstrapped target.

#### 3.2. Identification of Value Overestimation and Policy Mismatch

The authors empirically observed persistent value overestimation in TD-MPC2, particularly in high-dimensional tasks. This overestimation is quantified by comparing the learned value function $\hat{V}$ with a Monte Carlo estimate of the true value $V_{\pi}$.
The root cause is identified as a "structural policy mismatch": the behavior policy $\pi_H$ (generated by the MPC planner for data collection) and the nominal policy $\pi$ (being learned for value bootstrapping) diverge. This divergence leads to "out-of-distribution (OOD) queries" during policy evaluation, where the value function is queried for state-action pairs not well represented in the training data, leading to extrapolation errors.

#### 3.3. Theoretical Analysis of Error Accumulation

The paper presents theoretical analyses to support their claims:
- **Theorem 3.1 (H-step Policy Suboptimality):** This theorem, adapted from prior work, bounds the suboptimality of the H-step lookahead policy ($\pi_H$) in terms of model approximation error ($\epsilon_m$), planner suboptimality ($\epsilon_p$), and value approximation error ($\epsilon_k = \|\hat{V}_k - V_{\pi_k}\|_{\infty}$). It demonstrates that errors in the model and value functions influence planning performance. While planning reduces reliance on value accuracy by a factor of $\gamma^H$ compared to a greedy policy, it does not guarantee a reduction in approximation error in practice.
- **Theorem 4.1 (TD-MPC Error Accumulation):** This theorem quantifies how the performance gap ($\delta_k = \|V_{\pi_{H,k}} - V_{\pi_k}\|_{\infty}$) between the H-step lookahead policy and the nominal policy accumulates across policy iterations. It shows that the performance gap is bounded by terms related to model error, planner sub-optimality, the previous performance gap, and the value approximation error. This suggests that the mismatch tends to increase, rather than resolve, over training.
- **Theorem 4.2 (Policy Divergence):** This theorem establishes a lower bound for policy divergence (measured by Total Variation Distance) based on the performance gap between two policies. This links the accumulation of approximation errors (leading to a larger performance gap) directly to an increase in policy mismatch, which in turn means the regions corresponding to the nominal policy $\pi$ are underrepresented in the planner's data, perpetuating extrapolation errors.

#### 3.4. Proposed Solution: Policy Constraint

To mitigate policy mismatch and value overestimation, the authors propose a "policy regularization term" during policy iteration. Unlike prior work that constrains the planner, this method constrains the *learned policy* to align better with the data distribution. The goal is to extract a performant policy from the online buffer while reducing OOD queries.

The approach is framed as a constrained optimization problem for policy improvement:
$\pi_{k+1} := \text{argmax}_{\pi} E_{a \sim \pi(\cdot|s)}[Q_k(a, s)]$ subject to $D_{KL}(\pi_{k+1} \| \mu_k) \leq \epsilon$, where $\mu_k$ is the behavior policy from the buffer.

#### 3.5. Implementation of a Minimalist Modification (TD-M(PC)$^2$)

Instead of directly solving the constrained optimization, the authors adopt a Lagrangian approach, resulting in a modified policy training loss inspired by offline RL methods like TD3-BC:
$L_{\pi} = - E_{a \sim \pi}[Q(s, a) - \alpha \log \pi(a|s) + \beta \log \mu(a|s)]$
This loss combines a maximum entropy objective (with coefficient $\alpha$) for exploration with a distribution regularization term (with coefficient $\beta$) that encourages the policy to select actions within the domain covered by the behavior policy $\mu$. The term $E_{\mu' \sim \{\mu\}}[\log \mu']$ is maximized as a lower bound for $\log(\mu)$, simplifying calculations.

**Key implementation aspects:**
- **Minimalist Change:** The modification requires minimal changes (fewer than ten lines of code) to the existing TD-MPC2 framework.
- **No Additional Computation:** The method does not introduce additional computational overhead.
- **Adaptive Regularization:** An adaptive curriculum for the regularization coefficient $\beta$ is introduced. $\beta$ is set to 0 (no regularization) initially until the Q-function's moving percentile ($S_q$) exceeds a small threshold. This allows initial exploration without constraints before conservatism is enforced.

#### 3.6. Experimental Setup

Experiments are conducted on 21 high-dimensional continuous control tasks from HumanoidBench (14 tasks, 61-DoF action space) and DeepMind Control Suite (7 tasks, up to 36-DoF). Baselines include TD-MPC2, DreamerV3, and Soft Actor-Critic (SAC). Performance is evaluated based on episode return, and value approximation error is explicitly measured. An ablation study is performed to understand the impact of regularization strength, including a behavior cloning variant ($\beta = \infty$).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted aimed to address three primary questions regarding the proposed TD-M(PC)$^2$ method.

#### 4.1. Improved Value Learning

- **Reduced Value Approximation Error:** Figures 1 and 5 illustrate the impact of TD-M(PC)$^2$ on value approximation error. The original TD-MPC2 (Figure 1) consistently exhibits significant value overestimation, particularly in high-dimensional tasks (e.g., h1hand-run-v0 with 2159% error, h1hand-slide-v0 with 746% error). In contrast, TD-M(PC)$^2$ (Figure 5) demonstrates a substantial reduction in this structural bias. The learned value function aligns more closely with the true expected returns across all tested tasks, suggesting that the policy constraint mechanism effectively mitigates extrapolation errors.
- **Convergence Trend:** The reduction in overestimation is observed to lead to more reliable value learning, which the authors correlate with more effective decision-making and increased confidence in the H-step lookahead policy.

#### 4.2. Benchmark Performance

- **HumanoidBench Locomotion Suite:** On the 14 challenging 61-DoF locomotion tasks from HumanoidBench (Figure 3), TD-M(PC)$^2$ demonstrated consistent and substantial performance improvements over TD-MPC2 and other baselines (DreamerV3, SAC). The paper reports an average performance improvement of over 100% for TD-M(PC)$^2$ compared to TD-MPC2 by the end of training. Significant gains were observed in tasks requiring regular and consistent motion, such as Run, Slide, and Pole, where TD-MPC2 had previously shown low data efficiency. The authors qualitatively note that TD-M(PC)$^2$ tends to perform with more cautious actions, while the baseline exhibited exaggerated motions.
- **DeepMind Control Suite:** Across seven high-dimensional DMControl tasks (Figure 4), TD-M(PC)$^2$ generally outperformed TD-MPC2, albeit with a smaller margin compared to HumanoidBench. Notable improvements were observed in three dog tasks. In Humanoid tasks with a slightly smaller action space (21-DoF), the performance remained comparable to the baseline. This suggests the method's benefits are particularly pronounced in environments with extremely high-dimensional action spaces where policy mismatch is more acute.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

- **Impact of Regularization Strength ($\beta$):** An ablation study (Figure 6) was conducted on the regularization coefficient $\beta$, comparing a mildly regularized version ($\beta=0.05$) with the standard setting ($\beta=1.00$) and an overly conservative behavior cloning (BC) variant ($\beta = \infty$). The results on 'dog-trot' and 'h1hand-run-v0' tasks indicated that all three variants achieved similar performance levels.
- **Dominant Role of Conservatism:** The finding that even the behavior cloning variant, which lacks value-based policy learning, performed nearly on par with the other variants, suggests that the reduction of out-of-distribution actions (i.e., conservatism) is a primary factor in the observed performance improvements in high-dimensional tasks. The method's performance was not highly sensitive to the specific strength of the regularization within the tested range.

In summary, the main findings indicate that TD-M(PC)$^2$ successfully reduces value overestimation, which translates into significant performance gains, especially in complex, high-dimensional control tasks like those in HumanoidBench. The ablation study further suggests that maintaining conservatism in policy updates by reducing OOD actions is a key component of this improved performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents several significant contributions to the field of model-based reinforcement learning and has potential implications for the development of robust and efficient control policies.

- **Addressing a Fundamental Limitation:** The paper identifies and systematically analyzes persistent value overestimation in TD-MPC algorithms, attributing it to a structural policy mismatch between the MPC planner and the learned nominal policy. This identification and theoretical elucidation of error accumulation and policy divergence represent a significant advancement in understanding the limitations of current hybrid MBRL approaches. By pinpointing policy mismatch as a root cause, the work moves beyond simply acknowledging approximation errors to understanding their specific manifestation and impact in planning-based contexts.

- **Theoretical Grounding:** The theoretical analyses (Theorems 3.1, 4.1, 4.2) provide a formal framework for understanding how model errors, planner suboptimality, and value approximation errors interact and accumulate, leading to performance degradation. This theoretical insight strengthens the foundation for designing more robust MBRL algorithms. It also helps to explain why existing TD-MPC approaches, despite their general effectiveness, struggle with certain complex tasks.

- **Practical and Minimalist Solution:** The proposed TD-M(PC)$^2$ offers a simple yet effective solution that requires minimal modifications to existing TD-MPC frameworks. By introducing a policy regularization term, the method directly addresses the policy mismatch by encouraging the learned policy to operate within the distribution of data generated by the planner. The ease of implementation (fewer than ten lines of code) and lack of additional computational cost make it readily applicable to current state-of-the-art systems.

- **Enhanced Performance in High-Dimensional Control:** The empirical results demonstrate substantial performance improvements, particularly in challenging 61-DoF humanoid locomotion tasks. This indicates that TD-M(PC)$^2$ is effective in scenarios where policy mismatch and value overestimation are most problematic, advancing the capabilities of MBRL for complex robotics applications. The improved stability and performance in these domains could accelerate research and deployment of highly dexterous robotic systems.

- **Insights into Conservative Exploitation:** The ablation study's finding that conservatism plays a dominant role, even when policy learning is simplified (e.g., through behavior cloning), provides crucial insights into the dynamics of combining planning with policy iteration. It suggests that prioritizing in-distribution actions and reducing extrapolation errors is paramount for stability and performance in such hybrid systems, particularly as action spaces become more complex. This could guide future research towards developing more conservative yet efficient exploration and exploitation strategies in MBRL.

- **Broader Implications for Off-Policy Learning:** While specifically addressing TD-MPC, the work contributes to the broader understanding of off-policy learning challenges, especially when the behavior policy is dynamic and non-stationary. The method's success highlights the importance of distributional constraints in ensuring stable learning from off-policy data generated by sophisticated online mechanisms.

In conclusion, this paper significantly contributes to the robustness and applicability of TD-MPC by identifying a critical problem, providing a theoretical explanation, and offering a practical, low-cost solution that demonstrably improves performance in complex control environments. Its impact lies in fostering more stable and effective model-based reinforcement learning agents capable of tackling high-dimensional, real-world control tasks.
