# Generalized Population-Based Training for Hyperparameter Optimization in Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> The GPBT-PL framework introduces a flexible hyperparameter optimization method for reinforcement learning, utilizing a dual-agent learning mechanism with pseudo-gradients to guide underperforming agents. This approach consistently outperforms traditional PBT and PB2, showing significant improvements (e.g., 31% in Breakout) and enhanced robustness across various tasks and conditions, particularly with constrained resources.

## Problem

- Population-Based Training (PBT) for hyperparameter optimization (HPO) in reinforcement learning (RL) often leads to premature convergence by discarding promising "late bloomer" agents that initially underperform.
- PBT's direct replacement strategy reduces population diversity, hindering broader exploration and increasing the risk of converging to local optima.
- Existing PBT extensions, such as Population-Based Bandits (PB2), often incur higher computational costs due to the integration of complex Bayesian optimization techniques.

## Method

- Introduces **Generalized Population-Based Training (GPBT)**, an ==asynchronous parallel HPO framework== that replaces PBT's direct substitution with a ==dual-agent learning mechanism==, allowing flexible integration of various optimization strategies.
- Develops ==Pairwise Learning (PL)== as a specific update method within **GPBT**, where underperforming agents adopt a "fast learner's" weights and adjust their own hyperparameters using ==pseudo-gradients== derived from performance differentials and a ==momentum term==.
- The integrated **GPBT-PL** approach combines **GPBT**'s modularity with **PL**'s ==pseudo-gradient==-driven updates to adapt hyperparameters, aiming to maintain population diversity and guide exploration in ==black-box HPO== landscapes.

## Results

- GPBT-PL outperformed traditional PBT in **75%** of evaluated on-policy RL scenarios and showed significant improvements in off-policy tasks (e.g., **31%** in Breakout, **14%** in SpaceInvaders).
- The framework achieved high performance with constrained computational resources, matching hand-tuned IMPALA with **32** workers using only **5** workers in SpaceInvaders, demonstrating efficiency.
- GPBT-PL consistently exhibited resilience to shorter perturbation intervals and broader hyperparameter ranges, outperforming PBT by **82%** in Breakout with expanded learning rate ranges.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Replacing direct substitution with a learning-based update mechanism (PL) within a generalized framework (GPBT) significantly improves the exploration-exploitation balance in HPO for RL.
> - Pseudo-gradients derived from performance differentials between paired agents, combined with a momentum term, can effectively guide hyperparameter adjustments in black-box optimization landscapes without requiring explicit gradients.
> - The GPBT-PL framework demonstrates superior adaptability and robustness, capable of correcting early performance misjudgments and maintaining higher performance bounds even under varied perturbation intervals and broad hyperparameter ranges.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Deep neural networks (DNNs) have become fundamental function approximators in reinforcement learning (RL), facilitating advancements in diverse applications from board games to robotics. A critical aspect enabling these achievements is hyperparameter tuning, which governs both RL algorithms and DNN architectures. Unlike traditional supervised learning, RL presents a unique challenge due to its non-stationary nature; agents continuously interact with a dynamically changing environment, meaning that a static set of hyperparameters may not remain optimal throughout training.

To address this dynamic requirement, hyperparameter optimization (HPO) has emerged as an essential component of automated machine learning (AutoML). HPO aims to reduce manual tuning efforts, enhance efficiency, and ensure reproducibility. HPO methods are broadly categorized into sequential optimization (e.g., Bayesian optimization) and parallel search strategies (e.g., random search, grid search).

Among these, Population-Based Training (PBT) has gained prominence. PBT optimizes DNN weights and hyperparameters concurrently by leveraging a population of agents. Its asynchronous nature allows agents to periodically refine their hyperparameters by learning from better-performing counterparts, thereby adapting hyperparameter schedules over time. This Lamarckian evolution approach has demonstrated empirical success across various computational domains, including complex RL tasks.

However, PBT exhibits limitations. Its primary focus on high-performing agents can lead to premature convergence, hindering broader exploration and potentially overlooking "late bloomers"—agents that might achieve superior performance given different conditions or extended training. This bias towards exploitation over exploration can result in suboptimal solutions by prematurely discarding promising regions of the search space. Maintaining population diversity is crucial in RL for fostering varied behaviors and resilient solutions, a aspect where PBT's structure, often yielding a single solution per generation, is constrained.

Prior attempts to mitigate PBT's issues include Population-Based Bandits (PB2), which incorporates Bayesian optimization but incurs higher computational costs, and other PBT variants like FIRE PBT and BG-PBT, which may still struggle with greedy behavior or time-intensive hyperparameter generation. The computational overhead of Bayesian optimization itself can sometimes outweigh the benefits of hyperparameter evaluations, limiting the efficiency of such approaches.

This work addresses these identified shortcomings of PBT by proposing a refined framework that aims to enhance exploration capabilities and adaptability while maintaining computational efficiency. It builds upon the asynchronous parallel nature of PBT but introduces mechanisms to foster greater diversity and provide more nuanced guidance for hyperparameter adaptation, particularly in the non-stationary, complex environments characteristic of reinforcement learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the identified limitations of the Population-Based Training (PBT) framework for hyperparameter optimization (HPO) in reinforcement learning (RL). While PBT has proven effective in its asynchronous parallel paradigm, its inherent design introduces specific challenges that this work aims to mitigate.

The core issues with PBT that motivate this research are:
1. **Premature Discarding of Promising Regions:** PBT's direct replacement strategy, where underperforming agents adopt attributes from superior ones, can lead to the premature termination of agents exploring potentially beneficial but currently suboptimal regions of the hyperparameter search space. These regions might contain superior solutions that only manifest in the longer term or under slightly different conditions, which PBT's "winner-take-all" approach tends to overlook.
2. **Risk of Local Optima Convergence:** The predominant focus on exploiting top-performing agents within PBT can lead to premature convergence to local optima. This strategy, while potentially yielding satisfactory short-term results, deviates from the objective of population-based HPO, which is to achieve a balance between exploration (discovering new solutions) and exploitation (refining known good solutions). An overemphasis on exploitation restricts the population's ability to discover globally optimal solutions.
3. **Insufficient Population Diversity:** Maintaining a diverse population is critical in RL, particularly for discovering resilient and holistic solutions, echoing principles found in novelty search techniques. PBT's structure, which often leads to a single solution per generation, can inadvertently reduce population diversity over time, hindering the emergence of varied behaviors and robust performance.

Existing extensions to PBT, such as Population-Based Bandits (PB2), have attempted to address these issues by incorporating Bayesian optimization to guide underperforming agents. However, such methods often introduce their own limitations, specifically elevated computational expenses due to the intricate nature of Bayesian optimization, potentially negating efficiency gains. Other advanced PBT variants have also encountered challenges related to greedy behavior or time-intensive hyperparameter generation.

In response to these challenges, the key objectives of this research are:
- **To introduce Generalized Population-Based Training (GPBT):** This objective is to develop a flexible HPO framework that builds on PBT's asynchronous parallelism but replaces its direct substitution method with a dual-agent learning mechanism. GPBT aims to be inherently adaptable, accommodating various optimization strategies to remain pertinent across diverse hyperparameter tuning contexts.
- **To conceptualize and develop Pairwise Learning (PL):** This objective is to create an optimization method tailored for HPO that can operate within the GPBT framework. PL is designed to leverage pseudo-gradients derived from performance differentials between paired agents. This approach aims to guide underperforming agents' update trajectories in complex, black-box HPO landscapes, where explicit gradients are unavailable. The goal is to ensure informed adjustments and comprehensive exploration.
- **To empirically assess the integrated GPBT-PL approach:** This objective is to rigorously evaluate the efficacy of the combined GPBT-PL framework in the context of HPO in RL. The aim is to demonstrate its robust performance against traditional PBT and its Bayesian-optimized variant across a suite of OpenAI Gym environments, particularly emphasizing its superior performance and adaptability, even under stringent computational resource constraints.

Ultimately, the research seeks to provide a more adaptable, efficient, and robust HPO solution for RL that effectively balances exploration and exploitation, maintains population diversity, and overcomes the limitations of existing population-based training methods.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology introduces a refined framework for hyperparameter optimization (HPO) in reinforcement learning (RL), termed Generalized Population-Based Training (GPBT), complemented by a specific hyperparameter update strategy called Pairwise Learning (PL).

**A. Problem Statement**
The HPO problem in RL is formulated as a bi-level optimization task. The outer loop seeks to identify an optimal hyperparameter vector `x` that maximizes the cumulative reward `f(x, θ*)`, where `θ*` represents the optimal neural network weights for a given `x`. The inner loop concurrently optimizes these network weights `θ` to maximize the expected discounted cumulative reward `J(θ; x)` for a given `x`. In population-based HPO, this involves `n` agents, each with evolving hyperparameters `(x_1..., x_n)` and weights `(θ_1..., θ_n)` over training steps.

**B. Generalized Population-Based Training (GPBT)**
GPBT builds upon the asynchronous parallel nature of PBT but introduces enhanced granularity and flexibility in hyperparameter adaptation. The framework operates as follows:
1. **Initialization:** A population of agents is initialized with random weights and hyperparameters.
2. **Parallel Training:** All agents are trained and evaluated in parallel.
3. **Asynchronous Pairing and Update:** At designated hyperparameter update intervals (perturbation intervals), agents that are "ready" (having completed a certain amount of training) undergo asynchronous random pairing.
4. **Weight Adoption:** If one agent in a pair is identified as an "underperformer" compared to its paired counterpart, it adopts the neural network weights of the "superior" agent.
5. **Hyperparameter Adjustment:** The underperforming agent then adjusts its hyperparameters using a user-defined, specialized learning technique. This is where GPBT offers flexibility, as it can accommodate various optimization strategies, including random perturbation (as in PBT) or the proposed Pairwise Learning (PL).
6. **Termination:** The process continues until stopping criteria are met, after which top-performing agents are identified.

From an evolutionary computation (EC) perspective, GPBT aligns with steady-state EC, where new agents are introduced iteratively. This paradigm is noted for its ability to address non-stationary environments, similar to the dynamic nature of RL HPO. The replacement strategy in GPBT is confined to the paired agents, typically resulting in the replacement of the ready and comparatively older underperforming agent. This approach aims to maintain asynchrony while fostering population diversity, preventing the system from engaging in continuous global evaluations that would undermine asynchronous benefits.

**C. Pairwise Learning (PL)**
PL is introduced as a specific optimization method designed for hyperparameter refinement within the GPBT framework, aiming to preserve promising candidates and guide underperforming agents effectively. In addition to hyperparameters `x` and weights `θ`, each agent maintains a `d`-dimensional velocity vector `v` for hyperparameter adjustments, initialized to zero.

The PL mechanism operates when agents are paired:
1. **Pairing and Comparison:** Two agents are randomly paired, and their performance is compared.
2. **Fast Learner:** The agent with superior performance is designated the "fast learner" and is directly retained in the population.
3. **Slow Learner Update:** The lesser-performing agent, the "slow learner," adopts the neural network weights of the fast learner. It then updates its own hyperparameters `x` and velocity `v` using a learning mechanism inspired by Stochastic Gradient Descent with Momentum (SGDM). The update equations are:
 - `x_s^(g+1) = x_s^g + v_s^(g+1)`
 - `v_s^(g+1) = r_1 * v_s^g + r_2 * (G_f^g(x_f; u_f) - G_s^g(x_s; u_s))`
 Here, `g` denotes the generation, `r_1` and `r_2` are uniformly distributed random vectors within `^d`, and `G_f` and `G_s` represent the distributions of the fast and slow learners, respectively. The term `(G_f^g - G_s^g)` acts as a pseudo-gradient, approximating the direction for hyperparameter adjustment based on the performance difference. `r_1 * v_s^g` functions as a momentum term, incorporating historical update directions to stabilize and accelerate convergence, similar to SGDM.

PL distinguishes between learners based on their performance distributions, treating top-tier agents as samples from "fast learner" distributions and lower-tier ones as "slow learners." This stratified approach aims to enhance the learning efficiency of slow learners by continually sampling and correcting gradients between distributions, moving towards optimal configurations. PL is presented as a pseudo-gradient-driven method that uses dual samples to estimate gradients, making it a variant of SGDM suitable for PBT's asynchronous nature by averaging out noise with the momentum term.

**D. GPBT-PL**
The integrated GPBT-PL approach combines the flexible, asynchronous framework of GPBT with the adaptive, pseudo-gradient-driven learning mechanisms of PL for hyperparameter updates. This modular design allows agents to undergo updates independently, maintaining dynamism. The framework's modularity also enables the accommodation of other learning or optimization methods for hyperparameter updates, making it extensible.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluated the GPBT-PL framework against Random Search (RS), traditional Population-Based Training (PBT), and Population-Based Bandits (PB2) across various reinforcement learning tasks and robustness tests.

**A. Experimental Settings**
Experiments targeted the HPO of RL algorithms using OpenAI Gym environments.
- **On-Policy RL:** Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) was used for six continuous control tasks (BipedalWalker, Ant, HalfCheetah, InvertedDoublePendulum, Swimmer, Walker2D). Four hyperparameters were optimized: batch size, GAE λ, PPO clip ϵ, and learning rate η.
- **Off-Policy RL:** Importance Weighted Actor-Learner Architecture (IMPALA) was used for two discrete control tasks (Breakout, SpaceInvaders). Three hyperparameters were optimized: epsilon, learning rate η, and entropy coefficient.
All experiments were run with seven distinct seeds, with performance measured by the average of the final 10 episodic rewards. Population sizes of 4 and 8 were primarily used, with 5 workers per agent. The perturbation interval was typically 5 × 10^4 timesteps.

**B. On-Policy Reinforcement Learning Results**
- **Overall Performance:** GPBT-PL demonstrated superior performance over PBT in 75% of the evaluated scenarios for on-policy RL. In the remaining cases, its performance was comparable to the top-performing algorithms.
- **Smaller Populations (n=4):** GPBT-PL showed notable improvements over PBT, specifically 24% for Swimmer and 32% for Walker2D. This suggests higher efficacy with smaller populations. In contrast, PBT sometimes performed worse than RS with smaller populations (e.g., BipedalWalker, Walker2D), indicating a dependency on extensive computational resources and a potential drawback of its aggressive premature-discarding strategy.
- **PB2 Performance:** PB2's performance was inconsistent, excelling in simpler tasks like BipedalWalker (4) and InvertedDoublePendulum, but underperforming compared to RS for Walker2D (4) and Swimmer (8). Its reliance on computationally intensive Bayesian optimization and challenges with exploration-exploitation were noted.
- **Computational Efficiency:** PBT and GPBT-PL exhibited comparable time efficiencies, neither showing increased time consumption with larger populations, unlike PB2 which showed increased time for some tasks with larger populations.
- **Evolutionary Trajectory:** GPBT-PL's performance exhibited a gradual increase initially, followed by a rapid ascent in later stages, attributed to its ability to preserve "late bloomers" and maintain superior global search capabilities.

**C. Off-Policy Reinforcement Learning Results**
- **Superiority:** GPBT-PL consistently outperformed PBT and PB2, with significant improvements of 31% in Breakout and 14% in SpaceInvaders.
- **Resource Efficiency:** GPBT-PL, with agents trained using only 5 workers, was able to match the performance of hand-tuned IMPALA with 32 workers in SpaceInvaders and A3C with 16 workers in Breakout, demonstrating its ability to achieve high performance with constrained computational resources.

**D. Robustness to Perturbation Interval**
The perturbation interval was reduced from 5 × 10^4 to 1 × 10^4 timesteps to test robustness.
- **Impact of Shorter Interval:** A shorter interval led to more erratic training curves and wider reward variances across all methods, particularly for PBT and PB2 in complex tasks.
- **GPBT-PL Resilience:** GPBT-PL consistently outperformed PBT and PB2, demonstrating resilience and often achieving higher performance upper bounds. This was attributed to its adaptive nature, which can correct early performance misjudgments. PBT's premature discarding and PB2's reliance on potentially suboptimal Bayesian models were cited as reasons for their relative instability.

**E. Scalability to Larger Populations**
Experiments on Ant and HalfCheetah with a population size of 16 were conducted.
- **Ant Task:** For Ant, increasing population size from 8 to 16 did not significantly amplify PBT's upper-performance limits. However, GPBT-PL demonstrated a sustained ability to surpass local optima in later stages, accessing higher reward regions.
- **HalfCheetah Task:** Both GPBT-PL and PBT showed a marked uplift in performance upper bounds, even if the average best mean rewards remained relatively stable.
- **General Trend:** Both GPBT-PL and PBT generally benefited from increased efficiency with more expansive populations, though scalability varied across tasks.

**F. Scalability to Many Hyperparameters**
The number of optimized hyperparameters was increased from 4 to 7 for Ant and HalfCheetah.
- **Performance Degradation:** Both GPBT-PL and PBT exhibited inferior performance with an increased number of hyperparameters compared to scenarios with fewer hyperparameters.
- **GPBT-PL Outperformance:** GPBT-PL consistently outperformed PBT across different population sizes even with more hyperparameters.
- **Challenges:** The study highlighted that increasing the number of hyperparameters escalates problem complexity and computational demands, emphasizing the need for careful hyperparameter selection and preliminary experiments.

**G. Robustness to Hyperparameter Ranges**
The learning rate range for IMPALA in Breakout and SpaceInvaders was expanded to [10^-5, 10^-2] to test robustness to broader search spaces.
- **Performance Decline:** Broader ranges led to a general decline in performance for all methods compared to a narrower range, attributed to potential unstable policy learning with poorly initialized learning rates.
- **GPBT-PL Superiority:** GPBT-PL consistently outperformed PBT, demonstrating an 82% improvement in Breakout. This suggests GPBT-PL's learning-based update mechanism is more robust to broad or suboptimally defined hyperparameter ranges compared to PBT's random sampling or incremental adjustments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research introduces Generalized Population-Based Training (GPBT) and Pairwise Learning (PL), forming the GPBT-PL framework, which presents several significant contributions and potential impacts in the field of automated machine learning and reinforcement learning.

**Enhanced Adaptability and Flexibility:** GPBT provides a more versatile and adaptable hyperparameter optimization (HPO) framework compared to traditional PBT. By allowing users to define and integrate various hyperparameter learning methods during the perturbation phase, it offers a flexible tool that can be tailored to specific challenges and optimization contexts, extending beyond PBT's fixed update strategies. This adaptability is particularly valuable in the diverse and dynamic landscape of RL problems.

**Improved Exploration and Convergence:** The Pairwise Learning (PL) method directly addresses the critical limitations of PBT, namely its tendency for excessive greed and reliance on random heuristics, which can lead to premature convergence and neglect of promising but underperforming agents. PL's pseudo-gradient-driven approach, inspired by SGDM, provides nuanced guidance to underperforming agents by leveraging insights from superior counterparts. This strategy fosters a more comprehensive exploration of the hyperparameter space, mitigating the risk of converging to local optima and promoting faster, more directed convergence towards optimal configurations. The incorporation of momentum helps to stabilize updates and average out noise in gradient estimates.

**Superior Performance and Resource Efficiency:** Rigorous empirical evaluations across a range of RL benchmarks demonstrate that GPBT-PL consistently outperforms not only the conventional PBT but also its Bayesian-optimized variant, PB2. This superiority is maintained even in scenarios with constrained computational resources (e.g., fewer workers for IMPALA), highlighting GPBT-PL's efficiency. Its ability to achieve high-level performance comparable to hand-tuned or more resource-intensive approaches suggests its practical utility for researchers and practitioners. The research indicates that GPBT-PL's strengths are more pronounced in complex tasks, where exploration and robust adaptation are critical.

**Robustness and Scalability:** The framework exhibits robustness against variations in perturbation intervals and broad hyperparameter ranges, which are common challenges for PBT-like algorithms. GPBT-PL's adaptive nature, which allows it to correct early misjudgments and learn potential update directions, contributes to this resilience. While increasing the number of hyperparameters remains a significant challenge, GPBT-PL generally benefits from larger population sizes in terms of efficiency, demonstrating beneficial scalability.

**Future Directions and Impact:** The work identifies key areas for future research, such as devising strategies for discerning accurate update directions in high-dimensional hyperparameter spaces and addressing combinatorial optimization challenges in hyperparameter selection. Successfully tackling these areas could further enhance the applicability and efficiency of EC-based HPO methods.

In summary, GPBT-PL has the potential to significantly impact the development and deployment of RL agents by offering a more robust, efficient, and adaptable method for hyperparameter optimization. By automating and improving this critical tuning process, it can accelerate RL research, facilitate the development of higher-performing and more resilient agents, and broaden the accessibility of advanced RL techniques by reducing the manual effort and expert knowledge required for tuning.
