# Dissecting Embodied Abilities in Multimodal Language Models through Skill-level Evaluation and Diagnosis

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Northeastern University and collaborators developed BEAR, the first systematic diagnostic benchmark and framework for embodied multimodal language models, revealing that perceptual limitations and unstable spatio-temporal modeling are key bottlenecks. The associated BEAR-AGENT, an MLLM augmented with visual and spatial tools, improved GPT-5's performance on BEAR by 9.12% and achieved a 23.4% improvement in real-robot grasping tasks.

## Problem

- Existing embodied benchmarks primarily offer task-level evaluation, obscuring specific capability bottlenecks by conflating various underlying abilities.
- Prior benchmarks often specialize in single domains, limiting comprehensive diagnosis across a full spectrum of embodied skills.
- There is a critical need for diagnostic tools to attribute MLLM failures at a fine-grained, skill-level resolution, rather than just reporting overall task success or failure.

## Method

- Introduced BEAR, a diagnostic benchmark comprising 4,469 image–video–text entries, categorized into 14 atomic skills across 6 categories for granular MLLM evaluation.
- Developed a hierarchical skill-level diagnosis framework to identify capability bottlenecks through horizontal, vertical, and cross-skill failure attribution.
- Proposed BEAR-AGENT, a multimodal conversable agent leveraging GPT-4V and augmented with external visual grounding and spatio-temporal ==world model==ing tools.

## Results

- Most MLLMs scored 20-**40%** on BEAR, with GPT-5 reaching **52%**, significantly below human performance (**89.4%**), and proprietary models outperforming open-source by **13.4%** on average.
- The diagnostic framework attributed **88%** of long-horizon task failures to perceptual and spatial reasoning bottlenecks, with **54.8%** of aggregated errors categorized as perceptual limitations.
- BEAR-AGENT enhanced GPT-5's performance on BEAR by **9.12%** and yielded a **23.4%** improvement in real-robot grasping tasks, validating the diagnostic insights.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current MLLMs demonstrate limited embodied capabilities, with top models achieving 52% performance on BEAR, substantially below human levels (89.4%).
> - Perceptual limitations account for a majority of MLLM errors (54.8%), often underpinning failures even in reasoning-oriented tasks due to insufficient visual grounding.
> - MLLMs consistently struggle with unstable spatio-temporal modeling, leading to issues in relative spatial relations, directional understanding, and maintaining coherent historical context.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The integration of multimodal large language models (MLLMs) into embodied agents necessitates an understanding of their capabilities and limitations in interacting with the physical world. Existing embodied benchmarks predominantly focus on task-level evaluation, where an agent’s performance is measured by its success or failure in completing an overall task. This approach, while providing a high-level performance metric, often conflates numerous underlying abilities, making it challenging to identify specific capability bottlenecks. For instance, a failure in a complex manipulation task could stem from poor object localization, incorrect spatial reasoning, or flawed action planning, but task-level evaluation alone does not distinguish these specific causes.

Previous benchmarks tend to specialize in single domains such as pointing, spatial reasoning, or multi-agent collaboration, limiting their scope for comprehensive diagnosis. While some works have attempted to decompose agent decision-making, they have not consistently focused on capability-oriented atomic skills or provided actionable insights for model improvement. This gap indicates a need for diagnostic tools that move beyond binary task success to evaluate and attribute failures at a finer-grained, skill-level resolution.

This research addresses this deficiency by introducing a diagnostic framework designed to pinpoint which capabilities fail and why. By dissecting embodied tasks into atomic skills, the work aims to provide a more granular and actionable understanding of MLLM performance, thereby guiding more targeted improvements in embodied intelligence. This approach contributes to the development of more robust and generalizable embodied agents by offering a systematic method for identifying and addressing their fundamental limitations.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to advance the understanding of capability bottlenecks in embodied multimodal large language models (MLLMs) to facilitate their systematic improvement. The authors identify a critical limitation in existing embodied benchmarks: their focus on task-level evaluation, which provides limited actionable insights into the specific reasons for performance failures.

The motivation for this work stems from three core requirements for effective diagnosis of embodied MLLMs:
1. **Skill-level evaluation and analysis:** The current reliance on overall task success is insufficient. A more granular assessment is needed to evaluate individual, atomic capabilities that compose complex embodied tasks. This enables a deeper understanding beyond a simple pass/fail outcome.
2. **Principled attribution of failures:** It is crucial to determine whether failures originate from underlying perceptual capabilities (e.g., misidentifying an object) or higher-level reasoning processes (e.g., flawed planning logic). Without this attribution, efforts to improve models remain largely unguided.
3. **Actionable guidance for improvement:** The diagnostic process should yield concrete insights that can inform the development of improved MLLM architectures, training strategies, or tool-use paradigms. This shifts research from symptomatic treatment to addressing fundamental causes.

To achieve these objectives, the authors propose BEAR, a benchmark specifically designed for capability diagnosis. BEAR aims to break down complex embodied tasks into 14 atomic skills across 6 categories, ranging from low-level perception to high-level planning. This structured approach allows for isolated evaluation of each skill, providing a framework for both horizontal (across task execution steps) and vertical (within fine-grained capabilities) diagnosis. Ultimately, the motivation is to move beyond superficial observations of model failures to uncover the root causes, thereby enabling more targeted and effective advancements in embodied MLLMs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research centers on the development of a novel diagnostic benchmark, BEAR, and a hierarchical skill-level diagnosis framework, followed by the proposition and evaluation of an actionable improvement, BEAR-AGENT.

#### 3.1. The BEAR Benchmark

BEAR is presented as a diagnostic benchmark comprising 4,469 interleaved image–video–text entries. It is structured around 6 categories and 14 atomic skills, which are derived from an analysis of embodied task execution processes in both agents and humans. The categories span from low-level perception to high-level planning.
- **Categories and Skills:**
 - **Five Core Categories:** Task Planning (Task Process Reasoning, Next Action Prediction), Spatial Reasoning (Object Localization, Path Planning, Relative Direction), Bounding Box (General Object, Spatial Relationship, Semantic Part), Pointing (General Object, Spatial Relationship, Semantic Part), and Trajectory Reasoning (Object, Gripper, Human Hand).
 - **Long-horizon Category:** This category integrates the core atomic skills into multi-step embodied tasks. It features 35 episodes from simulation environments (AI2-THOR), each decomposed into a sequence of skill-oriented VQA steps (e.g., path planning, object localization, pointing, trajectory reasoning). This design evaluates the compositionality and diagnosticity of the skill taxonomy.
- **Data Curation Process:**
 - **Diversity:** Data is curated from 13 distinct sources, including real-world images (OpenImages, AGD20K, PartImageNet), videos (Open-X-Embodiment, TASTE-Rob, EPIC-Kitchens, Ego4D), and simulation episodes (ScanNet, ARKitScenes, ManipulaTHOR, RoboTHOR).
 - **Category-Tailored Strategies:** Specific data sources and generation methods are used for different categories (e.g., OpenImages for Pointing, Open-X-Embodiment for Trajectory Reasoning, AI2-THOR for Long-horizon).
 - **Multi-stage Pipeline:** Automated semantic filtering (using GPT-o3 for initial classification and question generation) is combined with rigorous human verification (at least three rounds by 10 trained annotators) to ensure accuracy and quality. Ethical filtering is applied to exclude sensitive content.
 - **Control Mechanisms:** Efforts were made to ensure diverse question distribution, careful distractor design (including semantically similar options and "none of the above"), balanced correct answer key distribution to mitigate position bias, and calibrated difficulty levels (e.g., filtering mask sizes for Pointing). Only validation and test sets were used to prevent data contamination.

#### 3.2. Hierarchical Skill-level Diagnosis Framework

This framework is designed to uncover capability bottlenecks through three levels of analysis:
- **Horizontal Diagnosis:** Examines failures in the Long-horizon category to identify which skills consistently act as major bottlenecks during complex task execution.
- **Vertical Diagnosis:** Evaluates each atomic skill in isolation to trace failures to fine-grained underlying capabilities.
- **Cross-skill Failure Attribution:** Aggregates error patterns across different skills to reveal fundamental capabilities that repeatedly cause errors in diverse contexts, moving beyond task-level symptoms to expose shared MLLM limitations.

#### 3.3. Experiment Setup for MLLM Evaluation

- **Models:** 20 representative MLLMs were evaluated, including both proprietary (e.g., GPT-5, GPT-4o, Gemini 2.5 Pro, Claude-4-Sonnet) and open-source models (e.g., InternVL3-14B, DeepSeek-VL-7B, LLaVA-NeXT-Llama3-8B).
- **Input Processing:** Models received inputs in either a "Merged" setting (multiple frames combined into a single input) or a "Sequential" setting (frames processed individually).
- **Baselines:** Human performance was established using 5 volunteers on BEAR-mini (a subset of 40 samples per skill).
- **Metrics:** Success rate (%) was used for Pointing, Spatial Reasoning, Task Planning, and Long-horizon categories. Average Intersection over Union (IoU) was used for the Bounding Box category.
- **Additional Analysis:** Investigations into Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting and test-time compute scaling were conducted.

#### 3.4. BEAR-AGENT: An Actionable Improvement

Motivated by the insights from the diagnosis, the authors proposed BEAR-AGENT, a multimodal conversable agent.
- **Architecture:** Built on the AutoGen framework, leveraging GPT-4V as a backbone model, it interacts with MLLMs through dialogue.
- **Tool Augmentation:** BEAR-AGENT provides external tools wrapped as Python functions to enhance perceptual grounding and explicit world modeling.
 - **Visual Tools:** Foundation models like GroundingDINO (for object grounding), Set-of-Mask, and DepthAnything. Specialized functions for trajectory visualization and motion understanding.
 - **Spatio-temporal World Modeling Tools:** Functions to update a semantic scene graph (nodes for objects, edges for relations) and a notebook module for recording temporal events to aid long-horizon planning.
- **Evaluation:**
 - **Offline:** Tested on BEAR with GPT-5 and InternVL3-14B, comparing against direct prompting, one-shot, few-shot, and Chain-of-Thought baselines.
 - **Online:**
 - **Simulation:** Deployed in Maniskill environment for three manipulation tasks (General, Spatial, Part manipulation, each with four language instructions). Augmented MOKA (GPT-4V backbone) with BEAR-AGENT for keypoint selection.
 - **Real-robot:** Implemented on a UR5 robot with an agent-view camera for 10 object-picking tasks. Compared against InternVL3-8B.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several findings regarding the embodied capabilities of MLLMs and the effectiveness of the proposed diagnostic framework and agent.

#### 4.1. MLLM Performance on BEAR

- **Limited Capabilities:** Most MLLMs evaluated achieved overall performance ranging from 20% to 40%. GPT-5, identified as the strongest model, reached 52%, which is substantially below human performance (89.4%). This performance gap persisted across all 14 atomic skills.
- **Prompting and Scaling Limitations:**
 - Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting provided inconsistent gains, with most improvements being negligible (under 10%) and in some cases leading to performance degradation.
 - Test-time compute scaling also demonstrated limited effects.
- **Model Tier Performance:** Proprietary models exhibited an average performance of 39.2%, outperforming open-source models by 13.4%. GPT-5 specifically exceeded the best open-source model, InternVL3-14B, by 18.3%.

#### 4.2. Hierarchical Skill-level Diagnosis

The hierarchical diagnosis framework provided insights into the nature of MLLM failures:
- **Horizontal Diagnosis (Long-horizon Tasks):** Analysis of GPT-4o's performance in long-horizon tasks indicated that perceptual skills (Pointing and Bounding Box) and spatial reasoning skills were significant bottlenecks, accounting for 88% of failures. This suggests that enhancing these foundational perceptual abilities could yield substantial improvements in complex embodied tasks.
- **Cross-skill Capability Attribution (Aggregated Bottlenecks):** Aggregating failure patterns across all skills revealed two consistent trends:
 - **Perceptual Limitations as Bottlenecks:** A majority of errors (54.8%) were categorized as perceptual, compared to 22.4% reasoning errors. Even in reasoning-oriented tasks like planning and spatial reasoning, models often made incorrect decisions due to inaccurately perceived or insufficiently grounded visual information. For example, in Pointing and Bounding Box, errors were largely due to localization failures. In Trajectory Reasoning, errors stemmed from perceptual confusion (e.g., misinterpreting arrow colors). In Spatial Reasoning, misidentifying a reference object led to incorrect 3D layout estimations. In Task Planning, action understanding errors were a dominant failure mode, where models observed motion but failed to interpret the action correctly, even when attending to relevant visual cues. This indicated that many high-level reasoning failures originated from upstream perceptual errors.
 - **Unstable Spatio-temporal Modeling:** This limitation was observed consistently across skills requiring spatial grounding and planning. Models struggled to maintain coherent object-centric or scene-centric coordinate frames. In perception-oriented tasks like Pointing, models failed to determine relative spatial relations. In Spatial Reasoning, an unstable reference frame resulted in incorrect left-right judgments and distorted directional understanding (30.3% of spatial reasoning errors). In Task Planning, this instability contributed to models omitting prior steps or historical context (32.0% of planning errors).

#### 4.3. BEAR-AGENT Effectiveness

The proposed BEAR-AGENT, designed to address the identified bottlenecks, demonstrated performance enhancements:
- **Offline Evaluation on BEAR:**
 - BEAR-AGENT improved GPT-5's performance on BEAR by 9.12%, representing a relative improvement of 17.5%.
 - It also enhanced the performance of InternVL3-14B.
 - Performance gains were observed across all categories, with the most notable improvements in Pointing, Bounding Box, and Trajectory Reasoning, supporting the hypothesis that integrated visual tools provide meaningful cues.
- **Online Evaluation (Simulation and Real-robot):**
 - **Simulation:** When integrated with the MOKA framework, BEAR-AGENT resulted in an average 20.17% improvement in task performance across basic manipulation tasks (General, Spatial, Part).
 - **Real-robot:** A 23.4% improvement was observed over InternVL3-8B in real-robot grasping experiments.
 - These online results suggest that BEAR-AGENT effectively enhances MLLMs' decision-making processes in keypoint selection for manipulation tasks, thereby validating the diagnostic value of BEAR.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of embodied AI by addressing a methodological gap in the evaluation of multimodal large language models (MLLMs).

**Novel Diagnostic Benchmark:** The introduction of BEAR as the first systematic embodied diagnostic benchmark provides a structured approach to evaluate MLLMs beyond task-level success. Its decomposition of embodied tasks into 14 atomic skills across 6 categories offers a more granular understanding of model capabilities, covering the spectrum from low-level perception to high-level planning. This framework allows researchers to pinpoint specific areas of weakness.

**Actionable Insights into Bottlenecks:** The hierarchical skill-level diagnosis framework, encompassing horizontal, vertical, and cross-skill analyses, revealed fundamental limitations in current MLLMs that were not consistently exposed by prior benchmarks. Key findings include:
- **Perceptual Limitations:** The identification that perceptual errors constitute a significant bottleneck (54.8% of errors) and often underpin failures in higher-level reasoning tasks. This suggests that improvements in visual grounding and fine-grained perception can have a cascading positive effect on overall embodied intelligence.
- **Unstable Spatio-temporal Modeling:** The diagnosis of unstable spatio-temporal modeling as a recurring issue highlights a critical area for future research, particularly in tasks requiring consistent object tracking, relative positioning, and sequential action understanding over time.

**Development of an Actionable Solution:** The insights derived from the BEAR benchmark directly informed the design of BEAR-AGENT, a multimodal conversable agent augmented with visual and spatial tools. This agent demonstrates that providing explicit 2D perceptual grounding and 3D explicit world modeling capabilities can significantly enhance MLLM performance. The improvements observed across different models (e.g., 17.5% relative gain on GPT-5) and environments (simulation and real-robot) validate the diagnostic value of BEAR and the utility of tool-augmented approaches.

**Guidance for Future Research:** By systematically dissecting capabilities and identifying root causes of failure, this work provides a roadmap for developing more robust embodied agents. It indicates that future research efforts might benefit from focusing on improving MLLMs' perceptual grounding mechanisms and spatio-temporal reasoning, potentially through integration with specialized visual foundation models and explicit world modeling components. The benchmark and the diagnostic framework can serve as a valuable tool for guiding and evaluating such advancements, fostering the development of more general and capable embodied AI systems.
