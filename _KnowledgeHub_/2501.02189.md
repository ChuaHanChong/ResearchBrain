# A Survey of State of the Art Large Vision Language Models: Alignment, Benchmark, Evaluations and Challenges

> [!summary] Summary
> This survey provides a comprehensive overview of state-of-the-art Large Vision Language Models (VLMs), detailing their architectural evolution, alignment methods, benchmarks, and prevalent challenges through early 2025. It identifies a clear trend towards integrating pre-trained Large Language Models as backbones and critiques the limitations of current evaluation methodologies across a rapidly expanding benchmark ecosystem.

## Problem

- Despite the rapid growth and impact of multimodal Vision-Language Models (VLMs), a comprehensive and systematic survey specifically focusing on this field was missing.
- Researchers needed a consolidated resource to navigate the vast array of VLM architectures, alignment methods, benchmarks, and inherent challenges.
- There was a need to understand the evolution of VLM design paradigms, from training models from scratch to leveraging pre-trained LLMs.

## Method

- The authors conducted a ==systematic literature review== and synthesis of Large Vision Language Models (VLMs) up to early 2025.
- They categorized and organized VLMs based on ==architectural evolution and training paradigms==, detailing their core components and ==alignment techniques==.
- The paper comprehensively summarized **95** existing vision-language benchmarks, analyzing their data collection methods and current ==evaluation metric limitations==, and elaborated on persistent challenges in the field.

## Results

- A significant trend shows VLMs increasingly adopting pre-trained LLMs as backbones, improving upon earlier 'trained from scratch' models through sophisticated fusion techniques like projection layers and 'treating all modalities as tokens'.
- **Ninety-five** VLM benchmarks across **13** categories were identified, revealing a focus on data diversity and quantity over substantial improvements in evaluation quality, with methods often limited to answer matching or multiple choice.
- Key challenges identified include widespread hallucination, critical safety and fairness issues, complex multimodal alignment (especially for commonsense and physics), high training inefficiency, and increasing data scarcity.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - VLM development is shifting towards leveraging pre-trained Large Language Models (LLMs) as backbones rather than training from scratch, allowing VLMs to benefit from LLM reasoning capabilities.
> - Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) and its variants are being adapted for multimodal alignment, though VLM alignment introduces unique complexities such as context misinterpretation and sensitive information inference.
> - While VLM benchmarks have rapidly diversified, often using mixed data collection methods, current evaluation metrics frequently rely on simple formats (e.g., exact answer matching, multiple choice) that may not fully capture complex visual reasoning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This survey paper is highly relevant and timely given the explosive growth and transformative impact of multimodal Vision-Language Models (VLMs) in the broader artificial intelligence landscape. Pre-trained Large Language Models (LLMs) have achieved remarkable success in NLP tasks, but they are inherently limited by their single-modality nature and the eventual finite supply of high-quality textual data. VLMs emerge as a natural progression, enabling machines to process and reason about information from both visual (images, videos) and textual modalities, leading to a more comprehensive understanding of the real world.

The paper explicitly states that while numerous surveys exist for single-modality models, a comprehensive review focusing specifically on the burgeoning field of multimodal VLMs has been "still missing." This survey fills that critical gap. It serves as a vital resource for researchers, practitioners, and students by systematically organizing the vast amount of information, models, benchmarks, and challenges that have emerged in this dynamic field up to early 2025. It acts as a compass, guiding stakeholders through the architectural transitions, new alignment methods, evolving evaluation paradigms, and persistent issues like hallucination and bias, which are unique or exacerbated in multimodal contexts. By providing a structured overview, it helps to consolidate knowledge, identify key trends, and pinpoint promising future research directions, thereby accelerating progress in integrated visual and linguistic intelligence.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this survey is to provide a comprehensive, systematic overview of Large Vision Language Models (VLMs) given their rapid advancements and growing popularity. The authors identify a critical need for such a consolidated resource, noting the absence of a dedicated multi-modality survey in contrast to existing reviews on single-modality models.

The paper outlines four specific key objectives:

1. **Systematic Overview of Major VLMs:** To compile and present information on the significant VLMs developed through 2025, offering a snapshot of the state-of-the-art.
2. **Analysis of Architectural Transitions and Alignment Methods:** To trace the evolution of VLM architectures, particularly the shift from training models from scratch to leveraging pre-trained Large Language Models (LLMs) as backbones, and to detail the newest methods for aligning visual and textual information.
3. **Summary and Categorization of Benchmarks and Evaluation Metrics:** To comprehensively summarize and categorize the popular benchmarks and evaluation metrics used for VLMs, highlighting their design, data collection methods, and limitations.
4. **Identification and Discussion of Challenges:** To pinpoint and elaborate on the key challenges currently faced by VLMs, including prevalent issues such as hallucination, alignment complexities, and safety concerns.

By addressing these objectives, the authors aim to offer a structured understanding of VLMs, their foundational components, training paradigms, methods of assessment, and the obstacles that need to be overcome for their robust and ethical deployment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

As a survey paper, the methodology employed by the authors is primarily a systematic literature review and synthesis. Their approach involves:

- **Comprehensive Literature Collection:** The authors collected a wide array of research papers, from foundational models like CLIP and BLIP to more recent large-scale VLMs such as GPT-4V and Gemini, encompassing developments up to early 2025. This ensures a timely and relevant overview of the rapidly evolving field.
- **Categorization and Organization of Models:** They systematically categorized VLMs based on their architectural evolution, particularly distinguishing between models trained from scratch and those utilizing pre-trained LLMs as backbones. This classification aids in understanding the prevailing design philosophies.
- **Detailed Analysis of Building Blocks and Training Methods:** The survey meticulously breaks down the common architectural components of VLMs (Vision Encoder, Text Encoder, Text Decoder, Cross-Attention Mechanisms) and discusses the dominant training paradigms, including "training from scratch" (e.g., Contrastive Learning) and "using LLMs as backbone" (e.g., Projector-based alignment, Joint Training, Freeze Training Stages). They also touch upon newer architectural innovations like "treating all modalities as tokens."
- **In-depth Examination of Alignment Techniques:** The paper dedicates a significant portion to discussing VLM alignment, drawing parallels with LLM alignment (RLHF, DPO, PPO, GRPO) and highlighting the unique complexities introduced by the multimodal nature of VLMs.
- **Extensive Benchmarking Review:** A key part of their methodology involves summarizing and categorizing 95 existing vision-language benchmarks across 13 distinct categories (e.g., visual text understanding, visual math reasoning, hallucination, robotics). This includes an analysis of how benchmark data is collected (fully human-annotated, synthetic, simulator interaction) and the commonly used evaluation metrics (answer matching, multiple choice, image/text similarity). They also critically evaluate the strengths and weaknesses of current benchmarking practices.
- **Identification and Elaboration of Challenges:** The authors systematically identify and elaborate on the major challenges in VLM research, dedicating separate subsections to hallucination, safety, fairness, multimodal alignment (including commonsense and physics understanding), training efficiency, and data scarcity. For each challenge, they reference relevant research and benchmarks that attempt to address these issues.
- **Forward-Looking Perspective:** While surveying the current state, the paper also implicitly (and sometimes explicitly) points towards future research directions by highlighting unresolved issues and limitations, such as the "sim2real gap" in robotics benchmarks or the limitations of current evaluation metrics.

By adopting this structured, analytical, and comprehensive approach, the survey aims to provide a robust foundation for understanding and navigating the VLM domain.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The survey presents several key findings and observations across the dimensions of VLM architecture, alignment, benchmarks, and challenges:

**Architectural Evolution and Training Methods:**
- **Shift to LLM Backbones:** A significant trend identified is the transition from training VLMs "from scratch" (e.g., CLIP, BLIP) to increasingly leveraging powerful, pre-trained Large Language Models (LLMs) as the primary text generation backbone (e.g., LLaVA, GPT-4V, Gemini, Qwen2-VL). This allows VLMs to benefit from the extensive world knowledge and reasoning capabilities embedded within LLMs.
- **Standard Components with New Fusion:** While fundamental components like vision encoders, text encoders (or LLMs acting as such), and text decoders remain, the methods of fusing visual and textual features have evolved. Projection layers (e.g., MLP) are commonly used to align visual features with LLM embedding spaces.
- **Advanced Fusion Techniques:** Newer architectures are exploring approaches like "treating all modalities as tokens" (e.g., Emu3), where visual inputs are encoded into discrete tokens similar to text, and "Transfusion," which processes different modalities simultaneously within a single transformer, hinting at more unified multimodal representations.

**VLM Alignment:**
- **RLHF Adaptation:** Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF), successful in LLMs, is being adapted for VLMs (e.g., MM-Eureka, MM-RLHF). This includes using various RL algorithms like DPO, PPO, and GRPO, alongside human-annotated preference datasets.
- **Multimodal Complexity:** VLM alignment is more complex due to the multimodal nature, which can lead to sensitive information inference or misinterpretation of visual context, requiring specialized reward models (e.g., Critique-Based Reward Model). Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) is also gaining attention as an alternative to reward modeling.

**Benchmarks and Evaluation:**
- **Rapid Growth and Diversification:** The number of VLM benchmarks has rapidly increased since 2022, expanding beyond simple Visual Question Answering (VQA) to cover a wide range of capabilities, categorized into 13 distinct areas (e.g., visual text, math reasoning, chart understanding, hallucination detection, robotics, commonsense, safety, fairness).
- **Data Collection Methods:** Benchmarks are created using: 1) Fully human-annotated datasets (e.g., MMMU, VCR); 2) Partially human-annotated datasets scaled up with synthetic data generation and human validation (e.g., ChartQA, EgoSchema); and 3) Interaction data collected from simulators (primarily for embodied AI/robotics, e.g., Habitat, Gibson).
- **Evaluation Metric Limitations:** While diverse, most popular evaluations remain largely confined to simple formats:
 - **Answer Matching:** Widely used for short-form answers, but exact match often fails to account for semantic equivalence, and LLM-based evaluation, while correlating with human judgment, struggles with consistency.
 - **Multiple Choice/Yes/No:** Easiest to evaluate but prone to "blind guessing" by LLM components without true visual reasoning.
 - **Image/Text Similarity Scores:** Used for generative tasks (T2I-CompBench) but don't fully capture complex visual understanding.
- **Critique:** The authors highlight that while data diversity and quantity are prioritized, improvements in evaluation quality often lag, hindering effective VLM assessment and potentially undermining the primary goal of evaluating true visual reasoning.

**Challenges:**
- **Hallucination:** A pervasive issue where VLMs reference non-existent objects or misinterpret visual context, often relying on parametric knowledge. Numerous benchmarks (e.g., CHAIR, POPE, HallusionBench, GAIVE) have been developed to quantify and mitigate this.
- **Safety:** Critical for ethical deployment, encompassing jailbreaking and the generation of harmful content. Benchmarks like SafeBench and JailbreakV test VLM robustness against malicious queries.
- **Fairness:** VLMs exhibit disparate performance across marginalized groups, similar to unimodal LLMs. Research is ongoing to assess and mitigate biases related to religion, nationality, disability, gender, skin tone, and culture using specific datasets (e.g., MMBias, FMBench, CulturalVQA).
- **Multimodal Alignment (beyond RLHF):** Challenges arise from contextual deviation between modalities, leading to inconsistencies, especially in commonsense and physics understanding for AI-generated content (AIGC). Benchmarks like VBench and PhysBench aim to evaluate adherence to real-world physics and common sense.
- **Training Efficiency:** The high computational cost and difficulty of training and fine-tuning large VLMs remain a significant hurdle, with methods like LoRA and rule-based RL being explored for efficiency.
- **Data Scarcity:** The massive scale of current VLMs demands vast amounts of high-quality, diverse training data, which is becoming increasingly scarce. Solutions involve self-supervised learning, synthetic data generation, and data collection from physical simulators or world models.

In essence, the survey reveals a field characterized by rapid innovation in model architectures and training paradigms, a growing but imperfect ecosystem of benchmarks, and persistent, complex challenges that necessitate continued research efforts.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This survey paper holds significant value and potential impact for the advancement of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and the broader AI research community in several ways:

1. **Consolidation of Knowledge:** In a field experiencing exponential growth, this paper serves as a critical knowledge hub. It systematically aggregates information on state-of-the-art models, architectural trends, training methodologies, and evaluation practices that are otherwise scattered across numerous individual research papers. This consolidation saves researchers considerable time and effort, providing a coherent "road map" for understanding the VLM landscape.
2. **Identification of Trends and Gaps:** By meticulously analyzing the evolution of VLM architectures, the survey highlights the clear shift towards leveraging pre-trained LLMs as backbones. This trend informs future model design, suggesting that integration and adaptation, rather than training from scratch, might be the dominant paradigm. Furthermore, by pointing out the limitations of current benchmarks (e.g., over-reliance on simple evaluation formats, lack of comprehensive real-world scenarios), it identifies crucial gaps that future research needs to address.
3. **Guidance for Researchers:** The detailed categorization of benchmarks, along with a critique of their data collection and evaluation metrics, offers invaluable guidance for researchers designing new models or evaluating existing ones. It encourages the development of more robust, semantically aware, and less-exploitably-simple evaluation methodologies. For instance, the discussion on "blind guessing" in multiple-choice questions prompts a rethinking of benchmark design for true VLM reasoning.
4. **Highlighting Critical Challenges:** The comprehensive discussion of challenges—hallucination, safety, fairness, complex alignment, training efficiency, and data scarcity—is crucial. By emphasizing these persistent problems, the paper directs research efforts towards addressing the practical and ethical limitations of VLMs. This is particularly impactful for the safe and responsible deployment of AI, especially in sensitive applications like robotics or content generation where issues like hallucination and bias can have severe consequences.
5. **Facilitating Cross-Pollination of Ideas:** By drawing parallels between VLM alignment and LLM alignment (e.g., RLHF), the survey encourages the transfer of successful techniques from one modality to another while also highlighting unique multimodal complexities. Similarly, the discussion of data scarcity and solutions like self-supervised learning, synthetic data, and simulator interaction offers insights applicable across different subfields of AI.
6. **Educational Resource:** For students and new researchers entering the VLM field, this survey provides an excellent foundational text. It offers a structured understanding of key concepts, historical progression, and contemporary challenges, enabling a quicker ramp-up to contributing to the field.

In conclusion, this survey is more than just a summary; it's a critical analysis that structures the current understanding of VLMs, clarifies the trajectory of their development, and—most importantly—points to the essential work that remains to be done to achieve truly intelligent, reliable, and ethical multimodal AI systems.
