# FOCUS: Internal MLLM Representations for Efficient Fine-Grained Visual Question Answering

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Technical University of Berlin, Technical University of Munich, CARIAD SE, and Volkswagen AG developed FOCUS, a training-free visual cropping method that enhances multimodal large language model (MLLM) performance on fine-grained Visual Question Answering (VQA). This approach achieved up to 42% higher accuracy on LLaVA-1.5 and 17% higher on LLaVA-OneVision for fine-grained tasks, while requiring 3 to 6.5 times less computational effort than existing methods.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) struggle with fine-grained visual details in high-resolution images due to downscaling, leading to information loss.
- Existing visual cropping techniques are often computationally inefficient, require extensive task-specific fine-tuning, or are incompatible with modern efficient attention mechanisms.
- Global-local-view MLLMs, while using local crops, still face challenges in identifying the few truly relevant visual tokens amidst a large volume of visual input.

## Method

- ==FOCUS== extracts target objects from VQA prompts using in-context learning, then constructs ==object relevance maps== by comparing cached MLLM internal ==value features (V-V pseudo-attention)== from multiple transformer layers.
- The method proposes and expands potential ==image regions of interest (ROIs)== around high-relevance anchor points, applying non-maximum suppression for redundancy reduction.
- It ==re-ranks proposed ROIs== based on MLLM's confidence scores for object existence within each region, then performs VQA on the top-ranked or combined relevant regions.

## Results

- FOCUS improved LLaVA-1.5 accuracy by an average of **42%** and LLaVA-OneVision by **17%** on fine-grained VQA benchmarks (V*Bench, HRBench-4K, HRBench-8K).
- The method achieved comparable or superior accuracy to existing state-of-the-art baselines like ZoomEye, while requiring **3 to 6.5 times** fewer forward passes (compute) and significantly faster inference.
- When integrated with Qwen-2.5-VL, FOCUS set new state-of-the-art accuracy on HRBench-4K (**79.25%**) and HRBench-8K (**76.25%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - MLLMs' internal KV-cache value features can be effectively repurposed to construct object relevance maps, enabling precise localization without explicit attention weights or additional training.
> - A confidence-based re-ranking mechanism for proposed ROIs significantly improves the robustness and accuracy of fine-grained detail identification by filtering out noisy relevance scores.
> - Leveraging combined relevance maps and existence confidence allows for an efficient and training-free visual cropping approach that dramatically reduces computational overhead while boosting MLLM fine-grained perception.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research addresses a critical limitation of current Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) in the domain of Visual Question Answering (VQA), specifically their struggle with **fine-grained visual details** in high-resolution images.

**The Evolving Landscape of MLLMs and VQA:**
Initially, MLLMs like Instruct-BLIP and LLaVA-1.5 showcased remarkable cross-modal perception and reasoning capabilities. However, these "global-view" MLLMs typically downscale high-resolution input images to much lower resolutions (ee.g., 224x224 or 336x336 pixels), leading to a significant loss of information about small visual details. Consequently, their performance on VQA tasks requiring attention to fine particulars is poor.

To mitigate this, newer "global-local-view" MLLM architectures (e.g., LLaVA-OneVision, Gemma-3) emerged, designed to process both a downsampled global view and multiple local crops extracted from the original high-resolution image. While an improvement, these models still face challenges in identifying the *few relevant* visual tokens pertinent to a fine-grained question amidst the vast number of local crop tokens, often leading to limited effectiveness.

**The Rise of Visual Cropping Approaches:**
An orthogonal and increasingly popular research direction is **visual cropping**. Instead of feeding the entire (potentially large and redundant) image or numerous local crops to the MLLM, these methods aim to identify and pass *only the most relevant image regions* for a given VQA prompt. This strategy promises to reduce information overload for the MLLM and improve its focus on critical details.

However, existing visual cropping techniques, such as SEAL, DC², ZoomEye, and ViCrop, suffer from significant limitations that hinder their practical deployment:
1. **Task-Specific Fine-tuning:** Methods like SEAL require extensive fine-tuning of MLLMs for fine-grained VQA, adding considerable training cost and reducing generalizability.
2. **Inefficient Exhaustive Search:** Techniques like DC² and ZoomEye rely on hierarchical tree searches or multiple forward passes across numerous regions, leading to high computational overhead and slow inference times.
3. **Incompatibility with Efficient Attention:** Some methods, like ViCrop, depend on explicit Query-Key (Q-K) attention weights for generating importance maps. Modern MLLMs often use efficient attention implementations (e.g., FlashAttention) that do not explicitly materialize these weights, rendering such cropping methods incompatible or less efficient.

**FOCUS's Position:**
FOCUS directly addresses these three major limitations, positioning itself as a more advanced and practical solution within the visual cropping paradigm. By being **training-free**, employing an **informed search strategy** (avoiding exhaustive methods), and being **compatible with efficient attention implementations** (by leveraging readily available KV-cache value features), FOCUS represents a significant step forward. It aims to make high-resolution, fine-grained VQA more accessible, efficient, and deployable for a wider range of MLLMs and real-world applications.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an **efficient and training-free visual cropping method** for Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) to significantly improve their performance on **fine-grained Visual Question Answering (VQA)** tasks. These tasks specifically require MLLMs to perceive and reason about small visual details in high-resolution images.

The motivation for FOCUS stems from several critical challenges currently faced by MLLMs:
1. **Inherent Limitations of MLLMs for Fine-Grained Perception:** Standard MLLMs typically downscale high-resolution images, leading to a loss of crucial fine-grained information. While global-local-view architectures attempt to address this by incorporating local crops, they still struggle to selectively identify the *few* relevant details needed to answer specific questions, often being overwhelmed by the sheer volume of visual tokens.
2. **Inefficiencies and Practical Hurdles of Existing Visual Cropping Methods:** Although visual cropping is a promising direction, existing techniques are hampered by practical drawbacks. They often necessitate task-specific fine-tuning (e.g., SEAL), incur high computational costs due to inefficient, exhaustive search algorithms (e.g., DC², ZoomEye), or are incompatible with modern hardware-optimized attention mechanisms (e.g., ViCrop, which relies on explicit Q-K attention weights). These limitations reduce their appeal for real-world deployment and scalability.
3. **Desire for a "Plug-and-Play" Solution:** The authors are motivated to create a method that can enhance the fine-grained VQA capabilities of *any* MLLM without requiring additional training or architectural modifications. By leveraging the MLLM's internal representations during inference, FOCUS aims to be a versatile, immediately deployable tool.

In essence, FOCUS seeks to bridge the gap between MLLMs' powerful reasoning abilities and their often-limited fine-grained visual perception, doing so in a computationally efficient and universally compatible manner. The goal is to enable MLLMs to "focus" on precisely the most relevant parts of an image, thus improving accuracy and reducing inference cost for detail-oriented tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, **FOCUS (Fine-grained visual Object Cropping Using cached token Similarity)**, is a training-free visual cropping technique designed to leverage MLLM-internal representations for efficient and accurate fine-grained VQA. It operates in four distinct, sequential steps:

1. **Identify Target Object(s) in the VQA Prompt:**
 - FOCUS begins by extracting the specific object(s) mentioned in the VQA question (e.g., "paraglider" in "What is the color of the paraglider?").
 - This is achieved using **in-context learning (ICL)**, where the MLLM is provided with a few examples to guide the extraction.
 - For each identified target object, a generic prompt template, "Is there a {target object} in the image?", is formulated. This prompt, along with the image, is then fed to the MLLM to generate relevant internal representations.

2. **Construct Object Relevance Map Using Key-Value (KV) Cache:**
 - The core innovation here is the creation of an object relevance map that highlights regions pertinent to the target object(s).
 - **Challenge:** Many modern MLLMs use efficient attention implementations (e.g., FlashAttention) that do not explicitly generate Query-Key (Q-K) attention weights, which are often used for importance mapping.
 - **Solution: Value-Value (V-V) Pseudo-Attention:** FOCUS bypasses this by utilizing **value features** from the MLLM's **KV-cache**. The KV-cache stores intermediate representations (keys and values) to accelerate autoregressive inference.
 - For each target text token (e.g., "par", "ag", "l", "ider"), its cached value features are compared against the cached visual token value features using **cosine similarity**. This produces a pseudo-attention map (`A_l_i`) for each target token and transformer layer.
 - **Aggregation:** To obtain a more robust and refined relevance map, pseudo-attention maps from multiple transformer layers (e.g., layers 14 to 32 for LLaVA-1.5) are aggregated. This involves an attention rollout-like mechanism, incorporating residual connections (`(A_l_i + I)/2`).
 - Finally, the aggregated pseudo-attention maps for *all* target text tokens are combined via **element-wise multiplication** (`A = A_0 ⊙ A_1 ⊙ ... ⊙ A_s`). This ensures that only regions consistently highlighted by *all* tokens of the target object remain prominent, effectively filtering out noise (e.g., combining "red" and "car" to highlight only red cars).
 - For **global-local-view MLLMs**, the object relevance map is constructed using visual tokens from local crops, which are empirically found to capture finer details better. A Gaussian filter and downsampling are applied to further refine the map.

3. **Propose and Rank Relevant Image Regions (ROIs):**
 - **Proposal:** From the processed object relevance map, the top-k highest scoring locations are identified as "anchor points." These points are spatially distributed, with a minimum distance constraint (`s_dist`) to ensure diversity.
 - Initial symmetric ROIs of a minimum size (`s_min`) are generated around these anchor points.
 - **Expansion:** Each initial ROI is then iteratively expanded up to a maximum size (`s_max`), but only as long as the average relevance score within the ROI remains above a predefined threshold.
 - **Redundancy Reduction:** **Non-maximum suppression (NMS)** is applied to the expanded ROIs to eliminate highly overlapping or redundant regions, promoting broader spatial coverage.
 - **Confidence-Based Ranking:** To mitigate noise in the object relevance map, which might lead to proposing irrelevant ROIs, a crucial re-ranking step is introduced. For each proposed ROI, the MLLM is queried again with an "existence prompt" ("Is there a {target object} in the image?") specifically on that cropped region. An "existence confidence" score (`c_Yes`) is computed from the softmax logits of the MLLM's "Yes" or "No" response. The top `n_steps` ROIs are then re-ranked based on these confidence scores.

4. **Perform Fine-Grained VQA Using the Top-Ranked Region:**
 - **Question Type Handling:** FOCUS distinguishes between two types of VQA questions:
 - **Type-1 (Single-Object Instances):** For questions involving one or multiple specific target objects (e.g., "color of the paraglider" or "position of ball relative to bench"), the ROI with the highest existence confidence for each target object is selected. If multiple target objects are involved, their respective ROIs are combined into a single input for the MLLM. A fallback strategy is employed if objects are too far apart: individual ROIs are resized and pasted onto an empty canvas to preserve spatial relationships without including excessive irrelevant background.
 - **Type-2 (Multiple Instances of an Object Type):** For counting or identifying multiple instances of a general object (e.g., "How many bikes are in the image?"), all ROIs exceeding a certain confidence threshold are selected and then merged onto a canvas.
 - **Global-Local-View MLLMs:** For these models, the approach simplifies. Instead of canvas composition, the model's native text-image-interleaved capabilities are leveraged by providing a global view (with all target ROIs visually highlighted) alongside the individual high-confidence local crops.

This meticulous, multi-stage approach, particularly the innovative use of KV-cache value features for pseudo-attention and the confidence-based ROI re-ranking, allows FOCUS to efficiently and accurately identify fine-grained details for VQA, overcoming the limitations of previous methods.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

FOCUS demonstrates strong performance and efficiency gains across a variety of fine-grained VQA benchmarks and MLLM architectures. The key findings are summarized below:

**Performance on Fine-Grained VQA Datasets:**
- **Significant Accuracy Boost:** FOCUS consistently improves MLLM accuracy on fine-grained VQA tasks.
 - With **LLaVA-1.5 (global-view MLLM)**, FOCUS achieves an average of **42% higher accuracy** over the vanilla MLLM across V\*Bench, HRBench-4K, and HRBench-8K.
 - With **LLaVA-OneVision (global-local-view MLLM)**, it shows a **17% improvement** on average across the same datasets and a **6% improvement** on the multi-domain MME-RealWorld-Lite dataset.
 - For example, on V\*Bench, FOCUS boosts LLaVA-OneVision's accuracy from 74.46% to an impressive 92.15%.
- **State-of-the-Art (SOTA) with Qwen-2.5-VL:** When integrated with the powerful Qwen-2.5-VL model, FOCUS achieves SOTA accuracy on HRBench-4K (79.25%) and HRBench-8K (76.25%), further validating its compatibility and effectiveness across different MLLM architectures.

**Efficiency and Accuracy Trade-offs (vs. Baselines):**
- **Superior Efficiency:** FOCUS significantly outperforms existing visual cropping methods in terms of computational efficiency, measured by the number of Forward Passes (FPs) required for visual search.
 - Compared to **ZoomEye**, the best-performing baseline, FOCUS achieves comparable or superior accuracy while requiring **3.5 – 4.5× less compute** with LLaVA-1.5, and **3 – 6.5× less compute** with LLaVA-OneVision. For instance, on HRBench-4K, FOCUS surpasses ZoomEye while using 4.39× fewer FPs.
 - This efficiency translates directly to practical benefits: FOCUS typically reaches comparable accuracy with significantly shorter execution times and fewer FLOPs than ZoomEye (e.g., up to ~5x faster and 4x fewer FLOPs for similar accuracy levels).
- **Outperformance over other Baselines:** FOCUS consistently outperforms SEAL, DC², and ViCrop in both accuracy and efficiency across most evaluated scenarios. SEAL is noted for its high computational overhead, and ViCrop for high memory usage, despite being relatively efficient in FPs.

**Robustness and Generalizability:**
- **Large Object VQA:** FOCUS demonstrates strong robustness on VQA datasets with larger objects (A-OKVQA, GQA), showing only minor performance degradation compared to vanilla MLLMs, indicating its general applicability beyond strictly fine-grained tasks.
- **Compatibility:** The method is shown to be compatible with both global-view (LLaVA-1.5) and global-local-view (LLaVA-OneVision, Qwen-2.5-VL) MLLM architectures.

**Ablation Studies and Design Validation:**
- **Object Relevance Map Criticality:** Replacing the object relevance map with a randomly generated map leads to a substantial accuracy drop, confirming the map's crucial role in localization.
- **ROI Ranking Efficacy:** Discarding the confidence-based ROI ranking (i.e., directly selecting the highest relevance score ROI) also reduces accuracy, validating the robustness and precision added by the re-ranking mechanism.
- **Value Features over Key Features:** The study found that value features from the KV-cache perform better than key features (even when adjusted for positional encodings), suggesting that value features might encode more suitable semantic information for object localization in this context.
- **Layer Depth:** Representations from later transformer layers (e.g., 14th to 32nd for LLaVA-1.5) yield the best performance, aligning with the understanding that deeper layers encode more semantically rich representations.
- **Hyperparameter Robustness:** FOCUS exhibits low sensitivity to its hyperparameters (e.g., number of anchor points, ROI expansion thresholds), with accuracy degradation of at most ~4.7 percentage points for LLaVA-1.5 and ~2.6 percentage points for LLaVA-OneVision when parameters are varied significantly.

**Limitations:**
- **Resolution of Relevance Map:** For extremely high-resolution images (e.g., 8K) and MLLMs with low-resolution internal representations (e.g., LLaVA-1.5), the object relevance map might not be fine enough to detect very small objects, leading to reduced effectiveness (as observed on HRBench-8K).
- **Spatial Reasoning:** As a training-free method, FOCUS inherits the base MLLM's limitations in understanding complex spatial relationships (e.g., "on the left/right of the image"), suggesting areas for future enhancement.

In summary, FOCUS successfully demonstrates a novel, training-free, and highly efficient approach to fine-grained VQA, achieving significant accuracy improvements over vanilla MLLMs and outperforming or matching SOTA baselines in accuracy while dramatically reducing computational overhead.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in "FOCUS: Internal MLLM Representations for Efficient Fine-Grained Visual Question Answering" holds significant implications for the field of multimodal AI, particularly for real-world applications requiring precise visual understanding.

**1. Advancing Fine-Grained Visual Question Answering (VQA):**
FOCUS addresses a long-standing challenge in VQA: the inability of MLLMs to robustly perceive and reason about small visual details in high-resolution images. By offering a solution that significantly boosts accuracy (e.g., 42% over vanilla LLaVA-1.5) on fine-grained benchmarks, FOCUS pushes the boundaries of what MLLMs can achieve in detail-oriented tasks. This is crucial for domains where minute observations dictate critical outcomes, such as medical diagnostics, quality control, or surveillance.

**2. Enhancing Practicality and Deployability of MLLMs:**
Perhaps the most impactful aspect of FOCUS is its blend of **efficiency** and **training-freeness**:
- **Training-Free:** By leveraging existing MLLM internal representations (KV-cache) rather than requiring task-specific fine-tuning, FOCUS offers a "plug-and-play" solution. This drastically reduces the development time, computational resources, and expertise needed to deploy MLLMs for fine-grained tasks, making advanced capabilities more accessible to a broader range of users and organizations.
- **Computational Efficiency:** The method's ability to achieve SOTA-comparable accuracy with **3 to 6.5 times less compute** (Forward Passes) than existing baselines like ZoomEye is a game-changer. This efficiency directly translates to faster inference times, lower operational costs, and the feasibility of deploying high-resolution VQA systems on resource-constrained edge devices or in high-throughput applications. Its compatibility with efficient attention implementations like FlashAttention further future-proofs its utility.

**3. Unlocking Hidden MLLM Capabilities and Interpretability:**
The core idea of FOCUS—harnessing MLLM-internal representations (specifically KV-cache value features) to localize question-relevant regions—provides valuable insights into how these models process and associate visual and textual information. This "inference-time" method suggests that MLLMs already possess latent spatial understanding that can be effectively externalized and utilized without explicit training. This line of inquiry could open new avenues for model interpretability, allowing researchers to better understand and guide MLLMs' attention mechanisms.

**4. Inspiring Future Research Directions:**
The limitations identified, such as the relevance map's resolution bottleneck for extreme 8K images and the inheritance of base MLLM's spatial reasoning weaknesses, provide clear pathways for future work. This could include developing multi-scale or sliding-window approaches for relevance map construction, or integrating more explicit spatial reasoning modules into training-free frameworks to enhance geometric understanding. The success of leveraging KV-cache features could also inspire similar techniques for other MLLM-based tasks, beyond VQA, that benefit from targeted information extraction.

**5. Broad Real-World Impact:**
The applications of efficient fine-grained VQA are vast:
- **Automotive Industry:** Essential for autonomous vehicles to precisely identify small or distant road signs, traffic lights, pedestrians, or vehicle components in complex environments, directly aligning with the industrial affiliations of the authors.
- **Medical Imaging:** Accurately localizing subtle abnormalities or specific anatomical features in high-resolution scans.
- **Manufacturing and Quality Control:** Detecting tiny defects or verifying specific product features on assembly lines.
- **Remote Sensing and Environmental Monitoring:** Identifying small objects, changes, or phenomena in satellite or aerial imagery.
- **Robotics:** Enabling robots to interact with small components or perform intricate manipulation tasks by focusing on precise visual cues.

In conclusion, FOCUS represents a significant methodological advancement that not only elevates the performance of MLLMs on challenging fine-grained VQA tasks but also makes these advanced capabilities vastly more practical, efficient, and deployable. Its contribution lies in leveraging the intrinsic capabilities of existing MLLMs in an intelligent and resource-effective manner, charting a promising course for the future of multimodal AI.
