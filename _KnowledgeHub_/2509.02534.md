# Jointly Reinforcing Diversity and Quality in Language Model Generations

> [!summary] Summary
> Meta FAIR researchers developed Darling (Diversity-Aware Reinforcement Learning), an online RL framework that integrates a learned semantic diversity signal with standard quality rewards to jointly optimize for both objectives in large language model generations. This approach enabled Llama-3.1-8B models to achieve a 55.2% win rate on AlpacaEval 2.0 and a 68.8% win rate on ArenaHard v2.0, alongside substantially higher semantic diversity on NoveltyBench compared to quality-only baselines.

## Problem

- Post-training methods for Large Language Models (LLMs), such as RLHF, often lead to 'diversity collapse,' resulting in highly similar, predictable, or repetitive responses.
- Existing diversity-enhancing techniques typically involve a trade-off with quality, or only achieve superficial lexical variations rather than true semantic diversity.
- The lack of diverse outputs limits LLMs' utility in creative, exploratory, and complex problem-solving applications where a wide range of distinct, high-quality ideas is essential.

## Method

- Darling integrates a ==learned semantic diversity signal== directly into an online reinforcement learning objective, specifically by modifying the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) algorithm.
- A ==learned binary classifier== determines the semantic equivalence of generated responses, enabling a definition of diversity based on the average pairwise semantic distance within a group of outputs.
- This semantic diversity signal is ==multiplicatively aggregated== with the standard quality reward, creating a =='diversity-aware reward'== that amplifies reinforcement for responses that are both high-quality and semantically distinct.

## Results

- Darling-trained Llama-3.1-8B models achieved a **55.2%** win rate on AlpacaEval 2.0 and a **68.8%** win rate on ArenaHard v2.0, while simultaneously showing a semantic diversity of **5.49** distinct generations on NoveltyBench, outperforming GRPO baselines on both quality and diversity.
- In competition math, Darling improved Pass@1 (solution quality) by an average of **+3.51%** for 4B models and **+1.90%** for 14B models, and significantly enhanced Pass@128 by **+7.62%** and **+10.16%** respectively, across challenging benchmarks.
- Ablation studies confirmed that multiplicative reward fusion consistently outperformed additive aggregation, and using a semantic diversity metric was crucial for performance, as lexical diversity could harm quality in tasks like competition math.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Optimizing for semantic diversity acts as an exploration catalyst in online RL, leading to an unexpected but significant improvement in overall response quality, rather than a trade-off.
> - Semantic diversity, measured by a learned classifier, is critical; purely lexical diversity metrics can lead to 'reward hacking' and degrade performance, especially in verifiable tasks.
> - Multiplicative aggregation of diversity and quality rewards is an effective and robust strategy, outperforming additive methods by naturally balancing objectives without complex hyperparameter tuning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Large Language Models (LLMs) has seen unprecedented growth, with post-training techniques like Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) becoming standard for aligning models with human preferences for accuracy and helpfulness. However, this paper addresses a critical, often overlooked side effect of such post-training: the **diversity collapse**. Current post-training methods tend to sharpen output distributions, leading to models that produce highly similar, predictable, or even repetitive responses, sacrificing creativity and breadth of ideas for perceived quality.

This "quality-diversity tension" is a significant concern because diverse outputs are crucial for a wide array of real-world applications:
- **Creative Tasks:** Brainstorming, storytelling, poetry generation, and creative writing all demand novelty and varied perspectives.
- **Exploratory Tasks:** Scientific discovery, hypothesis generation, and complex problem-solving benefit from exploring a broader solution space.
- **Reinforcement Learning and Self-Training:** In iterative self-improvement loops, diverse actions and outputs are essential for thorough exploration of the action space, enabling the discovery of novel and more effective strategies.
- **Conversational AI:** Repetitive responses lead to dull and unengaging interactions.

Existing attempts to address diversity often involve modifying inference-time decoding strategies (e.g., diverse beam search, higher sampling temperatures) or applying simple lexical penalties during training. While these methods offer some improvement, they frequently come with a trade-off, either sacrificing quality for diversity or failing to capture true *semantic* diversity, often just introducing surface-level lexical variations. Moreover, many prior training-time approaches modify the pre-training objective or rely on offline fine-tuning.

"Jointly Reinforcing Diversity and Quality in Language Model Generations" introduces **Darling (Diversity-Aware Reinforcement Learning)** as a novel online RL framework that directly tackles the diversity collapse problem during post-training. It distinguishes itself by:
1. **Semantic Diversity:** Moving beyond lexical variations by employing a *learned semantic classifier* to measure diversity, ensuring that outputs are "usefully different."
2. **Online RL Integration:** Directly incorporating this semantic diversity signal into the online reinforcement learning objective, thereby jointly optimizing for both quality and diversity within the core training loop.
3. **Synergistic Improvement:** Demonstrating that optimizing for diversity can *catalyze exploration* in online RL, leading to not only more diverse but also *higher-quality* responses, challenging the perceived quality-diversity trade-off.

This work contributes significantly to the ongoing effort to develop more capable, versatile, and human-like LLMs that can excel in both precision and creativity, pushing the boundaries of what post-training can achieve.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and effective method for **jointly optimizing for response quality and semantic diversity** in Large Language Model generations during post-training. The authors aim to prevent the common phenomenon of "diversity collapse" that often occurs when LMs are fine-tuned using reinforcement learning techniques primarily focused on quality metrics like accuracy or helpfulness.

The key motivations driving this research are:

1. **Addressing Diversity Collapse:** Current LM post-training (e.g., RLHF) sharpens output distributions, making models predictable and less diverse. This limits their utility in creative, exploratory, and interactive applications. Darling seeks to counteract this inherent bias.
2. **Enhancing Utility in Diverse Applications:** For tasks like brainstorming, creative writing, scientific discovery, and complex problem-solving, a wide range of distinct, high-quality ideas is paramount. LMs that lack diversity are less useful in these scenarios. Darling aims to make LMs more effective tools for such tasks.
3. **Promoting Deeper Exploration in RL:** In online reinforcement learning, diverse actions are critical for exploring the environment and discovering optimal policies. The authors hypothesize that explicitly optimizing for diversity can act as an exploration catalyst, leading to a broader search for high-quality solutions, even when "quality" is the primary goal.
4. **Moving Beyond Lexical Diversity:** Many prior methods for increasing diversity focus on surface-level lexical variations (e.g., distinct n-grams). The authors are motivated to capture *semantic* diversity, ensuring that generated responses are meaningfully different in their content or approach, rather than just using different words to convey the same idea.
5. **Developing a Scalable and Generalizable Framework:** The goal is to create a method that can be applied across different model families, sizes, and task types (verifiable vs. non-verifiable) with consistent benefits, demonstrating broad applicability.

By achieving these objectives, Darling aims to bridge the gap between highly performant, aligned LLMs and LLMs capable of truly imaginative, varied, and exploratory generations, ultimately enhancing their overall usefulness and intelligence.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Darling (Diversity-Aware Reinforcement Learning) introduces a novel framework that integrates a semantic diversity signal directly into the online reinforcement learning objective. The method builds upon the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) algorithm, a widely adopted RL technique for LM post-training.

#### 3.1. Core Components of Darling

1. **Semantic Diversity Measurement via Learned Partition Function:**
 - **Definition of Diversity:** For a given generation `y_i` within a group of `n` generations for a single prompt, diversity `Div_d(y_i)` is defined as the average pairwise distance between `y_i` and all other `y_j` (`j ≠ i`).
 - **Semantic Equivalence Classifier:** Instead of relying on superficial lexical metrics (like n-gram overlap), Darling employs a learned binary classifier, `classify(y_i, y_j)`, to determine if two responses convey equivalent semantics. If `classify` returns 1, the responses are considered semantically equivalent, contributing 0 to the diversity distance. If they are different, it contributes 1.
 - **Classifier Training (Non-verifiable tasks):** For instruction-following and creative writing tasks, the authors extend an existing method by Zhang et al. (2025b). They train a ModernBERT-base model with an 8192-token context window on human-annotated data (1,000 prompts, 2,000 response pairs) from NoveltyBench, where annotators judged semantic equivalence. The trained classifier achieved 78% accuracy, outperforming proprietary LLM judges.
 - **Classifier Training (Verifiable tasks - Math):** For competition math problems, a new classifier was trained. Prompts from DeepscaleR were used to generate diverse solution traces from various base and instruct models. Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct was then prompted to annotate whether pairs of these math solutions used "different strategies" (semantic equivalence for math). A Qwen3-Embedding-4B model was fine-tuned on these annotations, achieving 89% accuracy.

2. **Diversity-Aware Reward Fusion:**
 - **Multiplicative Aggregation:** The semantic diversity signal `Div_d(y_i)` (normalized to 0-1) is *multiplied* with the standard quality reward `r(x, y_i)` to form the `diversity-aware reward r_darling`.
 `r_darling(x, y_i | y_1..., y_n) := r(x, y_i) × Norm[Div_d(y_i | y_1..., y_n)]`
 - **Rationale for Multiplication:** This approach amplifies the effective reward for responses that are *both* high-quality and semantically diverse. It is preferred over additive aggregation, which can be sensitive to differing reward scales and require complex tuning of mixing weights.

3. **Integration into Online Reinforcement Learning (GRPO):**
 - **Modified GRPO Objective:** The `r_darling` reward replaces the standard quality reward `r(x, y)` in the GRPO objective function. This ensures that gradient updates are conditioned on trajectories that are "usefully different."
 - **GRPO Refinements:**
 - **Token-level Averaging:** Sequence-level loss averaging (in original GRPO) is changed to token-level averaging to avoid bias towards longer sequences.
 - **Removal of Standard Deviation Normalization:** The advantage calculation in GRPO typically normalizes rewards by their standard deviation across rollouts for a given prompt. Darling removes this normalization, especially for dense and noisy rewards (like those from Bradley-Terry style reward models), as it can amplify noise and obscure true reward differences. For sparse, noise-free rewards (like binary math correctness), its effect is minimal.

#### 3.2. Training and Evaluation Setup

- **Models:** Llama-3.1-8B/70B-Instruct (non-verifiable), Qwen3-4B/14B-Base (verifiable).
- **Baselines:** Standard GRPO, DivPO (DPO-based diversity optimization), GRPO-Unlikeliness (re-weights rewards by likelihood).
- **Reward Models:** Nexusflow/Athene-RM-8B (non-verifiable quality), Hugging Face Math-Verify (verifiable binary correctness).
- **Evaluation Metrics:**
 - **Quality:** AlpacaEval 2.0 (LCWR), ArenaHard v2.0 (Win Rate), EQ-Bench (ELO for creative writing), Pass@1 (math).
 - **Diversity:** NoveltyBench (Distinct generations, Distinct-4 n-grams), Pass@k (math, for k>1, indicating variety of solutions).

By carefully designing a semantic diversity metric and integrating it multiplicatively into a robust online RL framework, Darling aims to simultaneously enhance both the quality and diversity of LM outputs, transforming what was often a trade-off into a synergistic improvement.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted demonstrate that Darling consistently achieves superior performance in jointly reinforcing both quality and semantic diversity across various tasks and model scales.

#### 4.1. Non-Verifiable Tasks (Instruction Following & Creative Writing)

- **Joint Optimization Success:** Darling outperforms all quality-only RL baselines (GRPO, DivPO, GRPO-Unlikeliness) in both quality and diversity metrics.
 - **Quality:** Achieves the best win rates on AlpacaEval 2.0 (e.g., 55.2% LCWR for Llama-3.1-8B, vs. 48.7% for GRPO), ArenaHard v2.0 (e.g., 68.8% WR for 8B, vs. 61.1% for GRPO), and the highest ELO score on EQ-Bench (creative writing) (e.g., 905 for 8B, vs. 659 for GRPO).
 - **Diversity:** Simultaneously yields the highest semantic diversity (NoveltyBench Distinct: 5.49 for 8B, vs. 2.08 for GRPO) and lexical diversity (NoveltyBench Distinct-4: 96.0% for 8B, vs. 92.8% for GRPO).
- **Improved Pareto Front:** When varying sampling temperature, Darling-trained models consistently exhibit better quality and diversity than GRPO or the base instruct models, effectively pushing forward the Pareto front of the "quality-diversity tradeoff." This means Darling can achieve higher quality at a given level of diversity, or higher diversity at a given level of quality.
- **Qualitative Analysis:** Detailed rubrics from EQ-Bench show Darling's strength in generating "Interesting and Original" and "Avoids Cliche" responses, directly linked to its diversity objective. Qualitative examples further illustrate this, where Darling produces varied and insightful responses compared to the repetitive outputs of unaligned models. For instance, in a prompt for a leather wallet suggestion, Darling offered four distinct, reasoned suggestions (Fossil, Bellroy, Coach, Bellroy with different rationale), while the baseline repeated "Amazon."

#### 4.2. Verifiable Tasks (Competition Math)

- **Enhanced Quality (Pass@1) and Diversity (Pass@k):** Darling significantly improves both pass@1 (solution quality) and pass@k (solution variety/exploration) on challenging competition math benchmarks (AIME25, OlympiadBench, HMMT 2025, Brumo 2025).
 - **Pass@1:** Outperforms GRPO by an average of +3.51% for 4B models and +1.90% for 14B models across benchmarks.
 - **Pass@128:** Shows even larger gains in diversity, outperforming GRPO by an average of +7.62% for 4B models and +10.16% for 14B models.
- **Catalyst for Exploration:** The improved pass@k scores confirm that optimizing for diversity encourages broader exploration, which in turn helps the model discover correct solutions more frequently, leading to higher pass@1. The largest gains were observed on HMMT, the most challenging dataset, suggesting that exploration is particularly beneficial for harder problems.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Multiplicative vs. Additive Aggregation:** Multiplicative reward fusion (Darling's approach) consistently outperforms additive aggregation in AlpacaEval 2.0 quality and performs similarly in other metrics, validating its effectiveness and simplicity (no need for complex scaling or hyperparameter tuning).
- **Semantic vs. Lexical Diversity:**
 - For non-verifiable tasks, lexical (4-gram) diversity performs similarly to Darling in LM-as-a-judge quality evaluations but significantly underperforms in semantic diversity (NoveltyBench Distinct).
 - For competition math, using 4-gram diversity as a reward *harms* performance, even underperforming the GRPO baseline. This is due to "reward hacking" where models generate irrelevant reflections or different languages to inflate lexical diversity without improving solution quality. This strongly supports the need for *semantic* diversity.
- **Advantage Normalization:** Removing the standard deviation normalization term from the GRPO advantage calculation (which is typically helpful for reliable rewards) improves both quality and diversity in non-verifiable tasks (dense, noisy rewards from a Bradley-Terry model). This is because it prevents the amplification of noise when reward differences are tiny. For math tasks (sparse, noise-free binary rewards), removing normalization had little effect.

In summary, Darling successfully overcomes the quality-diversity trade-off inherent in typical LM post-training, demonstrating that explicitly optimizing for semantic diversity not only makes models more creative and versatile but can also lead to an unexpected, yet significant, improvement in overall response quality by catalyzing better exploration.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Diversity-Aware Reinforcement Learning (Darling) represents a significant advancement in the post-training of large language models, addressing a critical limitation that has emerged with the widespread adoption of quality-focused alignment techniques. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Redefining the Quality-Diversity Trade-off:** The most striking finding is that explicitly optimizing for diversity can *catalyze exploration* in online RL, leading to *higher-quality* responses, rather than merely trading off quality for variety. This challenges the conventional understanding of a fixed quality-diversity trade-off, suggesting that for LLMs, diversity can be a pathway to improved performance and robustness. This paradigm shift could influence future RLHF strategies by integrating diversity as a fundamental objective.

2. **Unlocking Enhanced Creativity and Exploration:** By mitigating diversity collapse and promoting semantic variation, Darling significantly enhances the utility of LLMs in tasks demanding creativity, innovation, and broad exploration. This includes brainstorming, creative writing, scientific hypothesis generation, and complex problem-solving. Models trained with Darling will be more effective partners for humans in these domains, offering a wider array of genuinely distinct ideas.

3. **Improved Robustness and Generalization:** In verifiable tasks like competition math, Darling's ability to generate a wider range of distinct solution strategies (higher pass@k) directly translates to a higher likelihood of finding a correct solution (higher pass@1). This indicates that diverse exploration can lead to more robust problem-solving capabilities and potentially better generalization to novel or harder problems.

4. **Advancing RL for LLMs:** Darling refines the GRPO framework by demonstrating the effectiveness of a semantic diversity signal and refining reward aggregation techniques. Its success with a learned partition function highlights the importance of moving beyond simple lexical metrics to capture deeper semantic differences, providing a scalable and generalizable approach for integrating complex auxiliary rewards into online RL.

5. **Practical Tool for AI Development:** The open-sourcing of the Darling code provides a practical tool for researchers and developers to incorporate diversity optimization into their own LLM training pipelines. This accessibility will foster further research and application in areas such as synthetic data generation, agent learning, and personalized AI.

6. **Broader Impact on AI Alignment:** The work contributes to the broader field of AI alignment by showing how desiderata beyond simple "helpfulness" can be effectively integrated into training. A truly aligned and intelligent AI should not only be accurate but also creative, adaptable, and capable of generating a rich spectrum of ideas, which Darling moves closer to achieving.

In conclusion, Darling makes a profound contribution by demonstrating that diversity is not merely an optional feature but a critical component that can synergistically enhance the overall performance, creativity, and exploratory capacity of large language models. This work sets a new direction for LLM post-training, paving the way for more versatile and intelligent AI systems.
