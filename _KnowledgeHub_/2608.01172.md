# Complete Motion Planning using Workspace-Fibered Decomposition for nR-Planar Manipulator

> [!summary] Summary
> Aayush Rath and Antony Thomas (Robotics Research Center, IIIT Hyderabad) developed a workspace-fibered decomposition framework for complete motion planning of nR planar redundant manipulators, which either finds collision-free paths or certifies their infeasibility. This approach incrementally builds a reduced planning space by projecting obstacle-constrained reachable workspaces and lifting them through redundant orientation fibers, ensuring kinematic continuity while avoiding explicit high-dimensional configuration space construction.

## Problem

- Complete motion planning for high-dimensional redundant manipulators faces exponential computational complexity with traditional C-space decomposition methods, limiting scalability.
- Sampling-based motion planners, while efficient for path discovery, cannot definitively prove the infeasibility of a path when none exists, lacking a completeness guarantee.
- Redundant manipulators present challenges in maintaining kinematic continuity and managing complex obstacle avoidance within their high-dimensional configuration spaces.

## Method

- The framework employs a **workspace-fibered decomposition**, incrementally constructing a reduced planning space `R_mR × S`m`` by propagating reachable workspaces of `m`-link subchains and lifting them with `(m+1)`-th link orientation fibers.
- It uses the ==Jacobian determinant== of the 2R subchain as a continuous local label to preserve and enforce ==branch consistency== (e.g., elbow-up/elbow-down) throughout the decomposition, preventing discontinuous inverse-kinematic branch switches.
- Collision checking is localized to only the newly added link during each ==incremental fiber lift==, inheriting collision-free validity from previous stages and reducing the overall computational burden.

## Results

- The proposed planner successfully computed collision-free and branch-consistent trajectories for both **3R** and **5R** planar manipulators in cluttered environments on the respective reduced planning manifolds.
- Visualizations demonstrated that the Jacobian determinant field effectively partitioned reachable workspaces into distinct, continuous kinematic branches, and the continuity constraint prevented undesirable discontinuous branch switches in generated paths.
- Experiments on the 5R manipulator confirmed that obstacle-induced exclusions and reachable regions were consistently propagated and expanded through each successive lifting stage, maintaining collision-free reachability information across the hierarchy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decomposing the planning problem into a sequence of lower-dimensional workspace reachability problems combined with orientation 'fibers' effectively manages the complexity of redundant manipulators.
> - The 2R Jacobian determinant serves as an effective topological invariant for preserving kinematic branch structure, enabling branch-consistent path planning even through collision-free singular configurations.
> - Inherited collision-free validity from prior stages significantly reduces collision checking costs by only requiring checks for newly introduced links during recursive construction.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of motion planning, which seeks to find collision-free paths for robots, has been an active area of research for decades. Within this domain, **complete motion planning**—the ability to either find a path if one exists or certify its infeasibility otherwise—remains a significant challenge, particularly for high-dimensional robotic systems operating in cluttered environments.

Current approaches to motion planning generally fall into two categories with distinct limitations:
- **Sampling-Based Motion Planners (SBMPs):** These methods, such as PRM and RRT variants, are effective at quickly finding paths in high-dimensional configuration spaces (C-spaces) when a path exists. However, they are probabilistically complete, meaning they cannot definitively prove that no path exists and will continue searching until a timeout or termination condition is met, without offering an infeasibility certificate.
- **Complete Planning Methods:** These approaches, often based on C-space decomposition techniques like vertical cell decomposition or Cylindrical Algebraic Decomposition (CAD), guarantee completeness by partitioning the free C-space into connected cells. They can certify infeasibility. However, their computational complexity scales exponentially with the dimensionality of the C-space, rendering them impractical for systems with more than a few degrees of freedom (typically 4-DOF or less). Recent advancements in complete planning have improved scalability but are generally limited to systems of up to 5-DOF.

To address the scalability issues of complete planning, researchers have explored hierarchical abstractions and lower-dimensional relaxations of the C-space. **Quotient-space planning methods** and **multilevel sparse roadmap methods** construct nested hierarchies of reduced C-spaces, allowing planning to proceed from simpler, lower-dimensional representations to guide exploration in the full C-space. These methods still operate primarily by decomposing or abstracting the configuration space itself.

For **redundant manipulators**, the Inverse Kinematics Problem (IKP) typically yields an infinite number of solutions for a given end-effector pose, forming r-dimensional submanifolds of the C-space known as Self-Motion Manifolds (SMMs). Research in this area has evolved from local, velocity-based methods to global, topological approaches that characterize the entire solution space. Methods include using (r+1)-dimensional feasibility maps, space discretization based on self-motion topology (c-bundles), sampling SMMs to identify collision-free workspaces, and unified frameworks combining topological analysis with numerical optimization. These methods often focus on navigating or characterizing the self-motion behavior within the C-space.

The work presented in this paper contributes to the landscape of complete motion planning for redundant manipulators by proposing a **workspace-centered hierarchical representation**. This contrasts with many existing hierarchical C-space decomposition methods by focusing on obstacle-constrained *reachable workspace manifolds* induced by lower-dimensional non-redundant subchains. Instead of direct C-space decomposition, the method incrementally propagates reachability structure through redundant orientation "fibers." This approach aims to preserve branch-consistent reachability information while avoiding the explicit construction of the full, high-dimensional C-space obstacle geometry, thereby addressing the scalability and infeasibility certification challenges for redundant systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a complete motion planning framework for nR planar redundant manipulators operating in cluttered environments. This framework seeks to construct collision-free paths when they exist and provide certificates of infeasibility otherwise, overcoming the known limitations of existing methods.

The specific motivations and objectives driving this work are detailed as follows:

1. **Addressing the Scalability of Complete Planning:** Traditional complete motion planning methods suffer from exponential complexity with increasing C-space dimensionality, limiting their practical application to low-DOF robots. For redundant manipulators, where the number of joints (and thus C-space dimension) can be high, explicit C-space decomposition becomes computationally prohibitive. This work aims to develop a method that scales more favorably by avoiding explicit full C-space construction.
2. **Overcoming Infeasibility Certification Limitations of SBMPs:** Sampling-based planners are efficient for path discovery but cannot definitively prove that no collision-free path exists. The proposed framework aims to provide guaranteed completeness, including the ability to certify infeasibility.
3. **Exploiting Redundancy Structure for Planning Efficiency:** Redundant manipulators possess additional degrees of freedom that can be exploited for obstacle avoidance and task versatility. This work is motivated to leverage the inherent structure of redundant kinematics to simplify the planning problem, specifically by decomposing the problem into lower-dimensional, manageable components.
4. **Preserving Topological Structure and Branch Consistency:** The method seeks to maintain the topological structure of the obstacle-constrained reachable workspace at each stage of decomposition. This includes respecting the distinct inverse-kinematic branches (e.g., elbow-up/elbow-down for a 2R subchain) and ensuring that planned paths exhibit kinematic continuity without discontinuous branch switches.
5. **Enabling Early Infeasibility Detection:** A significant motivation is to detect task infeasibility as early as possible in the planning process, ideally before considering all redundant degrees of freedom or constructing the full planning space. This can save substantial computational resources by terminating unfeasible queries prematurely.
6. **Reducing Collision Checking Cost:** Collision checking is often the most computationally intensive part of motion planning. The framework aims to reduce this cost by inheriting collision-free validity from previous stages of construction, requiring checks only for newly introduced links.

Formally, the problem statement is: Given a start configuration `q_s` in the n-dimensional C-space `T^n` and a target end-effector position `x_g` in the 2D workspace `R^2`, the objective is to find a continuous, collision-free path `γ` from `q_s` to a configuration `q_f` such that the end-effector of `q_f` is `x_g`. If no such path exists, the algorithm must provide a certificate of infeasibility.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology is a workspace-fibered decomposition framework that incrementally constructs the reachable planning domain for nR planar redundant manipulators. It operates by building up the planning space one redundant degree of freedom at a time, rather than directly planning in the full n-dimensional configuration space.

#### 3.1. Overview of Workspace Fibration

The core idea is an incremental construction sequence:
`R_2R` `lift`→ `R_2R × S1` `project`→ `R_3R` `lift`→ `R_3R × S1` `project`→ `R_4R` → ...
At each stage `m`, the planning domain is a product space `R_mR × S1`, where `R_mR` represents the obstacle-constrained reachable workspace of the `m`-link subchain, and `S1` is the orientation fiber for the `(m+1)`-th redundant link. The complete `nR` planning problem is ultimately solved on the final reduced space, `R_(n-1)R × S1`.

#### 3.2. Base Stage: Non-Redundant 2R Subchain

The process begins by establishing a base planning domain for the minimal non-redundant subchain, which for a planar arm with a position-only task is the 2R subchain.
1. **Configuration Space Construction:** The toroidal C-space `T^2 = S1 × S1` for the 2R subchain is uniformly discretized.
2. **Collision Occupancy and Connected Component:** Collision occupancy is computed by performing exact geometric intersection tests between the manipulator links and workspace obstacles. A Union-Find structure with toroidal adjacency is used to extract the connected free-space component `C^(s)_free` containing the initial configuration `q_s`.
3. **Reachable Workspace Projection:** The obstacle-constrained reachable workspace `R_2R` is then obtained by projecting `C^(s)_free` through the forward kinematics map `h: T^2 → R^2`. This `R_2R` inherently reflects obstacle-induced disconnections from `C^(s)_free`.
4. **Early Infeasibility Detection:** If the target goal position `x_g` is not contained within `R_2R`, the query is immediately declared infeasible, preventing further computation.

#### 3.3. Jacobian-Induced Branch Structure

For the 2R subchain, the inverse kinematics typically admits two solutions (elbow-up and elbow-down). To preserve this branch information:
1. **Jacobian Determinant as Branch Label:** Each workspace sample in `R_2R` stores its associated Jacobian determinant, `det(J) = l1 * l2 * sin(θ2)`. The sign of `det(J)` serves as a continuous local label, partitioning `C^(s)_free` into two open subsets (`C+` for `det(J) > 0` and `C-` for `det(J) < 0`) separated by the singular set `Σ` where `det(J) = 0`.
2. **Self-Motion Leaves:** For a generic workspace point `x ∈ R_2R`, the self-motion leaf `S_x` (set of `q` such that `h(q) = x`) has at most two connected components, corresponding to the elbow-up and elbow-down configurations.

#### 3.4. Incremental Fiber Lifting

After the base stage, the framework iteratively adds redundant links:
1. **Reduced Planning Space:** Given `R_mR` (the reachable workspace of the `m`-link subchain), the next reduced planning space `M^(m)_red = R_mR × S1` is constructed by discretizing the orientation `θ_(m+1)` of the `(m+1)`-th link into `Nθ` samples over `S1`.
2. **Local Collision Checking:** For each state `(x, θ_(m+1))` in `M^(m)_red`:
 - The `m`-link inverse kinematic solution consistent with the stored `det(J)` branch label is recovered. Collision checking for this `m`-link subchain is omitted because its collision-free validity is inherited from `C^(s)_free` or previous stages.
 - Only the newly added `(m+1)`-th link is checked for collision against obstacles.
3. **Projected Reachable Workspace:** The free subset of `M^(m)_red` is then projected through the forward kinematics of the `(m+1)`-link subchain to obtain the updated reachable workspace `R_(m+1)R`. This `R_(m+1)R` replaces `R_mR` for the subsequent fiber lift.
4. **Stage-wise Infeasibility Detection:** If `x_g` is not contained in `R_mR` at any intermediate stage, the query is declared infeasible.

#### 3.5. Branch-Consistent Connectivity

To ensure path continuity and branch consistency:
1. **Jacobian Continuity Constraint:** Neighbor connectivity within `M^(m)_red` is enforced only if the absolute difference in Jacobian determinants `|det(J_a) - det(J_b)|` between adjacent nodes `a` and `b` is below a prescribed threshold `ε`. This `det(J)` always refers to the 2R non-redundant subchain.
2. **Effect:** This constraint prevents discontinuous inverse-kinematic branch switches. It allows smooth passage through singular configurations (where `det(J)` is near zero) if they are collision-free.

#### 3.6. Planning on the Final Reduced Manifold

1. **Final Manifold:** For an `nR` arm, the construction ends at stage `m = n-1`, yielding the final reduced planning space `M_red = R_(n-1)R × S1`.
2. **Path Search:** Path planning is performed on the reduced occupancy lattice of `M_red` using A* search. Adjacency considers spatial proximity in `R_(n-1)R`, periodic continuity in `S1`, and the `det(J)` branch consistency constraint.
3. **Path Validity:** Any path found in `M_red` under the determinant continuity constraint is proven to lift to a valid, continuous, and collision-free path in the original `T^n` configuration space. This is because collision-free validity is inherited or locally checked, and the continuity constraint prevents discontinuous IK branch switches while allowing traversal of collision-free singularities.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of the workspace-fibered decomposition framework was evaluated through experiments on nR planar manipulators in cluttered environments.

#### 4.1. Experimental Setup

Two classes of redundant planar manipulators were used:
- **3R Manipulator:** A minimal redundant planar manipulator (l1=1.0, l2=1.0, l3=0.6) operating in a workspace with circular obstacles. Planning was performed on the reduced space `R_2R × S1`.
- **5R Manipulator:** A higher-dimensional system (link lengths l1=1.0, l2=1.0, l3=0.8, l4=0.6, l5=0.4) with polygonal obstacles. The reduced space was constructed incrementally through successive fiber lifts: `R_2R → R_2R × S1 → R_3R → R_3R × S1 → R_4R → R_4R × S1`.

#### 4.2. 2R Reachability Construction

For both experiments, the initial step involved discretizing the `T^2` C-space of the 2R subchain, computing collision occupancy, and identifying the connected free-space component `C^(s)_free`. This component was then projected to obtain `R_2R`, with the Jacobian determinant `det(J)` stored for each sample.

#### 4.3. 3R Workspace Structure

Visualizations of the reachable workspace `R_2R` for the 3R experiment, colored by the Jacobian determinant field (Figure 2b), demonstrated:
- The `det(J)` field effectively partitioned `R_2R` into distinct continuous regions corresponding to the elbow-up and elbow-down inverse-kinematic branches.
- These regions intersected along the singular set (`Σ` where `det(J) = 0`).
- Obstacles in the workspace induced disconnections in the singular transition region, leading to a topological separation between reachable workspace branches without requiring explicit configuration-space reasoning. This implies that valid paths for the 3R subchain could not traverse certain regions directly due to branch separation.

#### 4.4. Reduced-Space Planning

The proposed planner successfully computed collision-free trajectories:
- For the 3R manipulator, paths were found on `R_2R × S1`.
- For the 5R manipulator, paths were found on the recursively constructed manifold `R_4R × S1`.
- The resulting lifted trajectories were continuous in the original configuration space and avoided discontinuous branch transitions, validating the effectiveness of the `det(J)` continuity constraint. The figures provided (Figure 3a, 3b) visually demonstrate successful path planning for both systems.

#### 4.5. Incremental Reachability Propagation

The experiments on the 5R manipulator illustrated the recursive propagation of reachable workspace information (Figure 4):
- The reachable regions (`R_2R, R_3R, R_4R, R_5R`) geometrically expanded as additional redundant links were introduced.
- Obstacle-induced exclusions were consistently propagated through each successive lifting stage, demonstrating that the method maintains collision-free reachability information across the hierarchy.
- This behavior suggests that the decomposition effectively performs a recursive workspace extrusion, where obstacle-constrained reachability is continuously transported through subsequent redundant fibers.

#### 4.6. Computational Structure

The analysis of the computational structure revealed:
- The primary computational cost occurs during the construction of the reduced manifold.
- A key efficiency gain is that during fiber lifting, collision evaluation is only required for the *newly added* redundant link. The collision-free validity of the preceding subchains is directly inherited from earlier stages, eliminating redundant full-chain collision checks.
- After the preprocessing phase (manifold construction), the trajectory search itself operates solely on the reduced manifold graph, decoupling it from explicit configuration-space obstacle geometry reasoning.

These results indicate that the framework effectively preserves collision-free connectivity structure across lifting stages while reducing the complexity of collision checking compared to direct configuration space methods.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work presents a significant contribution to the field of complete motion planning for redundant manipulators, offering a novel approach to address long-standing challenges:

1. **Scalability for Complete Planning of Redundant Manipulators:** The most prominent impact is its potential to extend complete motion planning capabilities to higher-dimensional redundant robots, which are typically intractable for traditional cell decomposition methods. By operating on reduced workspace-fibered manifolds, the exponential scaling of explicit C-space reasoning is circumvented.
2. **Guaranteed Completeness and Infeasibility Certification:** Unlike sampling-based methods, this framework provides theoretical guarantees of completeness, meaning it can both find a path if one exists and certify its infeasibility otherwise. This is crucial for applications requiring high reliability and formal verification.
3. **Computational Efficiency through Incremental Design:** The incremental construction and localized collision checking significantly reduce the computational burden. Inheriting collision-free validity from previous stages avoids repeated full-chain collision checks, and early infeasibility detection further optimizes the planning process by terminating unfeasible queries promptly.
4. **Novel Workspace-Centered Decomposition:** The workspace-fibered decomposition offers a distinct paradigm compared to C-space-centric hierarchical methods. By focusing on obstacle-constrained reachable workspaces and propagating reachability through orientation fibers, it provides an alternative geometric template for handling redundancy and environmental constraints.
5. **Robust Branch Consistency:** The use of the 2R Jacobian determinant as a continuous branch label, coupled with a local continuity constraint, ensures that planned paths are kinematically continuous and respect the underlying inverse-kinematic branch structure. This is critical for practical robot operation, preventing undesirable discontinuous "flips" while correctly allowing passage through collision-free singularities.
6. **Decoupling Preprocessing from Search:** The approach effectively decouples the intensive task of constructing the collision-free planning domain from the subsequent path search. Once the reduced manifold is built, planning becomes a graph search problem on a lower-dimensional representation, making trajectory search more efficient.

The framework provides a geometric template for combining task-induced foliations with fiber-type factorizations of configuration space, which could complement existing approaches to redundancy resolution. Future work, including extensions to spatial manipulators, investigation of stronger theoretical guarantees regarding transverse distributions and holonomy, and quantitative comparisons with state-of-the-art planners, could further solidify its position and broaden its applicability.
