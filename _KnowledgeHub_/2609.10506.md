# DUET-DINO: Simultaneous Cross-View World Modeling for Latent Planning in Robot Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> DUET-DINO is a latent world model that jointly predicts future DINOv3 features for a static side camera and a wrist camera, coupling the two streams through cross-attention so each view-specific predictor exploits complementary global-scene and gripper-centric cues. Planning runs CEM over full 7-DoF end-effector actions against a normalized dual-view goal cost, reaching 92% reach, 72.5% angled-reach, and 60% lift success.

## Problem

- Existing action-conditioned latent world models rely on a single external camera, so their rollouts capture only coarse translational motion over short horizons.
- Rotational and gripper-state effects are degraded in latent predictions, restricting prior deployments to translation-only planning for simple pick-and-place tasks.
- A single global view gives a poorly informed goal cost, and naively combining separately trained view predictors yields negligible gains.

## Method

- ==Frozen DINOv3 encoder== produces patch latents per view; learnable ==cross-attention blocks== let each view's latents query the other view as keys and values before prediction.
- ==View-specific predictor heads== consume cross-view-conditioned latents plus the shared 7D end-effector action and state, trained from scratch with a combined ==teacher-forcing== and 2-step ==autoregressive L1 loss==.
- Planning uses ==CEM== over 7-DoF action sequences with a ==dual-view goal cost== summing per-view L1 latent distances, normalized by a zero-action rollout.

## Results

- On ten reach tasks DUET-DINO hits **92%** success and **5.4 cm** final position error, versus **79%** wrist-only, **78%** independent dual-view, and **55%** for the official V-JEPA 2-AC checkpoint.
- Angled-reach reaches **72.5%** success with **1.9 cm** and **22.7 degree** errors; under five backgrounds with distractors it holds **63%** versus **45%** independent and **25%** wrist-only, and **26.7%** on real hardware.
- Removing cross-attention drops reach to **81%** and angled-reach to **42.5%**; under camera perturbation side-only predictors fail at **0%** while dual-view variants hold **66.7%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Cross-view latent conditioning, not mere view concatenation, is what unlocks fine-grained rotation planning; independently trained dual-view predictors add almost nothing.
> - Encoder choice dominates: V-JEPA 2 wrist predictions underestimate action-induced latent change, while DINOv3 tracks it, so representation sensitivity gates downstream planning quality.
> - Extra training data improved spatial reaching but not orientation planning, indicating rotational bottlenecks are architectural rather than data-limited.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated within the field of robot learning, specifically focusing on the development and application of world models for robot manipulation. World models, particularly latent world models, represent a direction for addressing generalization in robotics by learning representations of the world and its dynamics. These models predict future states or visual representations based on actions, enabling capabilities such as planning, policy training, or safety verification. Latent world models are recognized for their efficiency in closed-loop planning and control due to their focus on task-relevant features rather than photorealistic pixel generation.

However, a documented limitation of existing latent world models, such as V-JEPA 2-AC and DINO-WM, is their reliance on single-view observations, typically from an external camera. This often leads to predictions that primarily capture coarse translational motions over short horizons, making them less reliable for fine-grained spatial and rotational actions. Consequently, tasks requiring full 7-Degrees of Freedom (7-DoF) end-effector control, which includes precise translational, rotational, and gripper-state changes, have remained challenging for these single-view static models.

Previous works on generative video world models, like Cosmos and Wan, aim for high visual fidelity but incur significant computational costs and may produce physically implausible scenes. Latent world models, including I-JEPA, V-JEPA, and DINOv2/v3, have focused on learning transferable representations without pixel reconstruction, enabling faster planning. While some approaches like V-JEPA 2-AC and DINO-WM have demonstrated zero-shot planning by optimizing action sequences against a latent goal cost using methods like the Cross-Entropy Method (CEM), their effectiveness for full 7-DoF control, especially involving complex rotations and gripper actions, has been limited by the information captured from a single viewpoint.

DUET-DINO addresses this gap by proposing a dual-view approach that integrates information from both a global side-view camera and a gripper-centric wrist-mounted camera. By employing cross-view conditioning in the latent space, the model aims to leverage the complementary strengths of each viewpoint—global context from the side view and fine-grained interaction details from the wrist view—to improve predictions for full 7-DoF actions. This extends the capabilities of latent world models to more complex and precise manipulation tasks, moving beyond predominantly translation-focused control. The work also explores the impact of different pretrained visual encoders (V-JEPA 2 and DINOv3) on the performance of action-conditioned predictions and downstream planning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable reliable latent planning over the full 7-DoF end-effector action space for robot manipulation. This includes accurate prediction of future visual representations induced by fine-grained translational, rotational, and gripper actions, which are critical for precise manipulation tasks.

The motivation for this work stems from several limitations observed in existing latent world models:
- **Unreliable Fine-Grained Action Predictions:** Current action-conditioned latent world models struggle to produce reliable predictions for fine-grained spatial and rotational changes caused by robot actions. This makes them less suitable for tasks requiring high precision in 7-DoF end-effector control, such as angled grasping or delicate object placement. Prior deployments often focused on translation-only planning, indicating a gap in handling full action spaces.
- **Limitations of Single-View Observations:** Traditional latent world models predominantly rely on a single static external camera. This single viewpoint often fails to capture all necessary information for complex tasks. A global view might provide insufficient detail for gripper-centric interactions, while a purely gripper-centric view might lack global scene context, both of which are crucial for effective planning.
- **Need for Complementary Information:** Precise robot manipulation requires understanding both the broader scene context (e.g., object locations relative to the workspace) and detailed, close-up information about the gripper's interaction with objects (e.g., specific contact points or object orientation relative to the gripper). Neither a single side-view nor a single wrist-view camera can provide both comprehensively.
- **Exploiting Pretrained Visual Features:** The research is also motivated by an interest in understanding how different pretrained visual latent representations, specifically V-JEPA 2 and DINOv3, impact the ability of a world model to capture action-conditioned scene changes, particularly for fine-grained motions. There was an existing hypothesis that V-JEPA 2 might underestimate visual dynamics from fine-grained actions, while DINOv3 might offer better sensitivity.

To address these motivations, the DUET-DINO model was developed with the following specific objectives:
- To jointly model scene changes from static side-view and dynamic wrist-camera observations.
- To enable cross-view conditioning in the latent space, allowing each view predictor to leverage complementary information from the other.
- To facilitate latent planning over the full 7-DoF action space by minimizing a dual-view goal cost.
- To evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed approach on challenging manipulation tasks, including spatially diverse reach, orientation-intensive angled-reach, and multi-goal grasp-and-lift tasks.
- To analyze the impact of different pretrained encoders (V-JEPA 2 and DINOv3) on the quality of action-conditioned predictions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, DUET-DINO (Simultaneous Cross-View World Modeling for Latent Planning in Robot Manipulation), integrates dual camera views and cross-attention mechanisms within a latent world model framework.

**A. DUET-DINO Architecture:**
The architecture consists of three main components:
1. **Pretrained Visual Encoder:** Side-view ($o_{side_t}$) and wrist-view ($o_{wrist_t}$) observations are independently encoded into patch-wise latent representations ($z_{v_t}$) using a frozen, pretrained DINOv3 encoder. The study also evaluates V-JEPA 2 as an alternative encoder. The DINOv3 encoder utilized a ViT-H+/16 backbone with 840M parameters, while the V-JEPA 2 encoder used a ViT-G/16 backbone with 1B parameters.
2. **Cross-Attention Blocks:** Learnable cross-attention blocks ($C_{\phi_{side}}$, $C_{\phi_{wrist}}$) are applied to the latent representations. These blocks allow the latents of each view to query and be conditioned on the latents of the complementary view. For instance, side-view latents query wrist-view latents, and vice-versa, resulting in cross-view-conditioned latents ($\tilde{z}_{side_t}$, $\tilde{z}_{wrist_t}$).
3. **View-Specific Predictor Heads:** These are separate, view-specific predictor heads ($P_{\psi_{side}}$, $P_{\psi_{wrist}}$) that adopt the architecture introduced in previous work. They predict future latent representations ($\hat{z}_{v_{t+1}}$) for their respective views. The predictions are conditioned on the cross-view-conditioned latents ($\tilde{z}_{v_t}$), the current 7-DoF end-effector action ($a_t$), and the current end-effector state ($s_t$).

**B. Training Procedure:**
- **Data:** DUET-DINO is trained from scratch on a combination of two large-scale robot manipulation datasets: DROID (62,877 trajectories) and RoboArena (5,856 trajectories). This diverse dataset aims to expose the model to a wide range of behaviors, scenes, and failure modes.
- **Input:** The model processes 8-frame video clips with synchronized side- and wrist-view observations, captured at 4 FPS. Input images are resized to 224x224 for DINOv3 and 256x256 for V-JEPA 2 encoders.
- **Action Space:** The predictor is conditioned on a full 7-DoF end-effector action ($a_t = [\Delta x, \Delta y, \Delta z, \Delta r, \Delta p, \Delta y, \Delta g]$), comprising Cartesian translation, changes in end-effector roll, pitch, and yaw, and change in gripper state.
- **Loss Function:** A dual-view prediction loss ($L_{pred}(\phi, \psi)$) is used, combining a teacher-forcing (one-step prediction) loss ($L_{v_{TF}}$) and an autoregressive rollout loss ($L_{v_{AR}}$) computed over a horizon of K=2 steps. This loss is computed for both camera views and summed to jointly train the cross-attention blocks and predictor heads.
- **Optimization:** The model is optimized using AdamW for 120,000 steps with a batch size of 256, a learning rate of 4.25x10^-4, and a weight decay of 0.04. A learning rate warmup and annealing schedule is applied. The total computational cost for training DUET-DINO is reported as 5 × 10^20 FLOPs.

**C. Visual Planning:**
Once trained, DUET-DINO is used for zero-shot goal-conditioned planning of full 7-DoF end-effector motions.
- **Goal Definition:** A goal observation ($o_g = (o_{side_g}, o_{wrist_g})$) is provided, and its latent representations ($z_{side_g}, z_{wrist_g}$) are extracted using the frozen visual encoder.
- **Optimal Control Problem:** The planning problem is formulated as an optimal control problem where a sequence of actions $a_{0:H-1}$ is sought to minimize a cost function $J(z_g, \hat{z}_H)$, representing the distance between the predicted final latent state ($\hat{z}_H$) and the goal latent state ($z_g$).
- **Solver:** The Cross-Entropy Method (CEM) is employed. In each CEM iteration:
 - N action sequences are sampled from a Gaussian proposal distribution.
 - These sequences are rolled out within DUET-DINO to generate imagined latent trajectories.
 - **Dual-View Planning Cost:** The quality of each action sequence is assessed by computing an L1 distance for both side-view ($\ell_{side_i}$) and wrist-view ($\ell_{wrist_i}$) predicted latents to their respective goal latents. These are summed and normalized by the dual-view cost of a zero-action rollout to form the overall planning cost ($J_i(z_g, \hat{z}_{t+H})$).
 - The Gaussian proposal distribution is updated using a weighted top-k CEM approach, where candidates are weighted by a softmax over their negative costs.
- **Planning Parameters:** For simulation, planning uses 800 action samples, 50 top-k candidates, and 2 CEM iterations. For hardware experiments, these parameters are reduced due to computational constraints (1 CEM iteration, 500 actions, 15 planning steps).

**D. Experimental Setup and Tasks:**
Evaluations are conducted in the RoboLab simulator and on a real DROID robot setup. Three main categories of tasks are used to assess full 7-DoF control:
1. **Reach Task:** Requires spatially diverse end-effector translation to target objects. Success is defined as the end-effector position being within 5 cm of the goal position within 100 steps.
2. **Angled Reach Task:** Focuses on orientation-intensive movements. Success requires both translational (≤ 5 cm) and rotational (≤ 12 degrees) errors to be within tolerance within 100 steps. This task is evaluated under fixed backgrounds, diverse backgrounds with distractors, and on hardware.
3. **Angled Grasp and Angled Lift-to-Home:** A sequential multi-goal task comprising angled reaching, grasping, and lifting the object to a home pose.
The study compares DUET-DINO against single-view (side-only or wrist-only) predictors, independent dual-view predictors (separately trained views combined only in planning), and a baseline V-JEPA 2-AC* checkpoint.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate that DUET-DINO consistently outperforms single-view and independent dual-view baselines across various manipulation tasks, particularly those requiring full 7-DoF end-effector control.

**A. Overall Performance:**
- **Reach Task:** DUET-DINO (with DINOv3 encoder) achieved a 92% success rate (SR) and a final position error (FPE) of 5.4 ± 7.4 cm.
- **Angled-Reach Task:** DUET-DINO (DINOv3) obtained a 72.5% SR, 1.9 ± 1.2 cm FPE, and 22.7 ± 32.2° final angular error (FAE) in simulation. This robustness extended to visual distribution shifts, where it achieved 63% SR.
- **Angled Grasp and Lift-to-Home:** DUET-DINO (DINOv3) achieved a 60.0% SR for completing the full sequential task.

**B. Detailed Task Results:**
- **Reach Task (Table I):**
 - Trained V-JEPA 2 baselines performed poorly (20-41% SR), with V-JEPA 2-AC* achieving a higher 55% SR, suggesting potential degradation from longer training on diverse data for this encoder.
 - DINOv3 wrist-view predictors (79% SR) showed substantially stronger spatial planning than V-JEPA 2 wrist-view (37% SR).
 - Independent dual-view models provided minimal improvement over their corresponding wrist-view baselines.
 - DUET-DINO significantly improved performance by successfully executing reaches to objects near table corners, which were initially outside the wrist camera's field of view.
- **Angled Reach Task (Table II):**
 - V-JEPA 2-AC* (2.5% SR) and V-JEPA 2-based predictors generally struggled with orientation-intensive tasks, indicating difficulties with fine-grained control.
 - DINOv3 wrist-view (62.5% SR) demonstrated strong performance, suggesting DINOv3 latent features are effective in capturing orientation changes.
 - Independent dual-view DINOv3 improved FPE but increased FAE, resulting in a lower SR than the DINOv3 wrist-view alone.
 - DUET-DINO reduced both FPE and FAE, leading to the highest success rate.
- **Robustness to Visual Distribution Shifts (Table III):** DUET-DINO maintained robustness, achieving 63% SR under varied backgrounds and distractors, while the DINOv3 single-view wrist predictor showed a noticeable performance drop (25% SR).
- **Hardware Evaluation (Table IV):** DUET-DINO achieved the highest success rate (26.7%) on challenging angled-reach poses on a real DROID robot. The lower success rates compared to simulation were attributed to a reduced planning budget and real-world complexities. The wrist-view predictor frequently proposed unsafe actions on hardware.
- **Angled Grasp and Lift-to-Home (Table V):** DUET-DINO successfully completed the full task for 60% of runs. In contrast, the DINOv3 wrist-view predictor often succeeded in initial stages but failed during the lift-to-home phase due to a loss of task context.

**C. Ablation Studies:**
- **Cross-view Cross Attention:** Removing the cross-attention blocks from DUET-DINO resulted in reduced performance, with SR dropping to 81% for reach tasks and 42.5% for angled-reach tasks. This confirms the importance of cross-view attention for fine-grained dual-view planning.
- **Camera Perturbations:** Under camera-pose shifts, DUET-DINO (DINOv3) achieved 66.7% SR, demonstrating robustness gained from integrating the wrist view, whereas single-view side predictors failed completely (0% SR).
- **Training Data:** Training DUET-DINO solely on DROID data yielded 78% SR for reach tasks, indicating that additional training data from RoboArena improved spatial planning performance. However, DROID-only training showed no improvement in orientation planning.

**D. Embeddings Analysis (Fig. 5, Fig. 6):**
- **DINOv3 vs. V-JEPA 2 Latent Changes:** Analysis of latent changes revealed that DINOv3 encoders and action-conditioned predictors better captured fine-grained action-conditioned dynamics. DINOv3 predictions generally followed the ground-truth temporal patterns, albeit with some overestimation.
- **V-JEPA 2 Underestimation:** The V-JEPA 2 wrist-view predictor was observed to underestimate latent changes induced by actions, suggesting weaker modeling of fine-grained dynamics compared to DINOv3.
- **Patch-wise Correspondences:** DINOv3 embeddings showed more coherent patch-wise correspondences over semantically related object regions, with its predictor closely preserving encoded patterns. V-JEPA 2 embeddings exhibited noisier correspondences and less semantic coherence in predicted low-distance regions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work presents a notable advancement in the field of robot manipulation by addressing a critical limitation of existing latent world models: their struggle with reliable prediction and planning for full 7-DoF end-effector actions. The introduction of DUET-DINO, a simultaneous cross-view latent world model with cross-view conditioning, offers a method to integrate complementary visual information from multiple cameras.

The significance of this research includes:
- **Enhanced Manipulation Capabilities:** By enabling robust planning over the full 7-DoF action space, DUET-DINO expands the range and complexity of manipulation tasks that can be addressed by world models. This is particularly relevant for tasks requiring precise spatial and rotational control, such as angled grasping, intricate assembly, or dexterous object interaction.
- **Effective Information Integration:** The cross-view conditioning mechanism demonstrates a method for effectively fusing global scene context (from a side camera) with fine-grained, gripper-centric details (from a wrist camera). This architecture provides a blueprint for leveraging multi-modal sensory inputs in latent planning systems.
- **Insights into Latent Representations:** The comparative analysis of DINOv3 and V-JEPA 2 encoders highlights the importance of the underlying latent representations for capturing action-conditioned dynamics. The finding that DINOv3 embeddings more effectively capture fine-grained motion provides valuable guidance for the selection and development of future self-supervised visual encoders in robotics.
- **Improved Robustness:** The demonstrated robustness of DUET-DINO under visual distribution shifts (diverse backgrounds, distractors) and camera perturbations indicates its potential for generalization in real-world, unconstrained environments. This property is crucial for the deployment of autonomous robotic systems.
- **Hardware Validation:** The successful (albeit challenging) hardware evaluations on a real DROID robot confirm the practical applicability of the proposed methodology, moving beyond purely simulated environments.

The potential impact of DUET-DINO lies in advancing the capabilities of autonomous robots to perform more complex and precise manipulation tasks. This could have implications across various domains, including:
- **Advanced Manufacturing:** Enabling robots to perform delicate assembly tasks or handle diverse components with higher precision.
- **Service Robotics:** Improving the ability of robots to interact with objects in unstructured home or office environments.
- **Hazardous Environments:** Allowing remote-controlled or autonomous robots to perform complex tasks in dangerous or inaccessible locations with greater dexterity.
- **General-Purpose Robotics:** Laying groundwork for more generalized robot policies by demonstrating a world model capable of learning and planning fine-grained interactions.

The stated intention to open-source the code and model checkpoints will contribute to further research and development in the robotics community, enabling broader adoption and iteration on the proposed methods. Future work involving integration with generalist robot policies, such as vision-language-action (VLA) models, aims to reduce planning search space and facilitate faster closed-loop control, which would further enhance the practical utility of this approach for real-time robotic operation.
