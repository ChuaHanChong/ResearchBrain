# ADEPT: Accelerating Dexterity via Pre-Training and Post-Training using Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> ADEPT is a large-scale reinforcement learning framework that pretrains a dexterous policy on a generic object-reposing task, then post-trains it for contact-rich downstream tasks via behavior-cloning distillation, critic warm-up, and conservative PPO updates, mediated by a joint-space geometric fabric; distilled perceptive students achieve zero-shot sim-to-real long-horizon pick-reorient-insert on 23-DoF and 29-DoF visuo-tactile arm-hand robots, 2-14x faster than the parallel-jaw FMB baseline.

## Problem

- RL specialists trained from scratch per task rarely transfer; each new dexterous task rediscovers reaching, grasping, lifting, and reorientation from sparse task-specific rewards.
- Prior fabric-guided dexterous policies like DextrAH-G and DextrAH-RGB restrict hand control to a low-dimensional PCA grasp subspace, limiting the finger coordination needed for contact-rich manipulation.
- No prior work had demonstrated zero-shot sim-to-real reinforcement learning for long-horizon pick-reorient-insert on high-DoF arm-hand systems directly from raw visual or visuo-tactile perception, without demonstrations or pose estimators.

## Method

- **ADEPT** pretrains a dexterous teacher actor-critic via ==PPO== with an ==ADR curriculum== and ==Population-Based Training== on a generic object-reposing task spanning reach, grasp, lift, reorient, and transport.
- A structured post-training recipe, ==BC actor distillation== into the new observation space, frozen-actor critic warm-up, then conservative PPO with reduced actor learning rate, stabilizes transfer and prevents catastrophic forgetting.
- A full joint-configuration-space ==geometric fabric== exposes the policy's full kinematic dexterity, and a two-stage ==DAgger distillation== combining TacMap penetration-depth maps with SaTA-style FiLM fingertip anchoring yields zero-shot deployable vision/visuo-tactile students.

## Results

- Zero-shot real-world student success rates: **5/10** (star) and **3/10** (square/round) vision-only on Kuka-Allegro, versus **8/10** with tactile on Flexiv-Sharpa (vs **3/10** vision-only), and **6/10** on dish placement.
- Ablations show standard PPO finetuning at learning rate 1e-3 collapses to **0%** success at ADR 20 across all seeds, while the full ADEPT recipe reaches ADR 50 in **19.9±1.0 hours** on 5/5 seeds.
- Multi-fingered ADEPT policies solve insertion in **5-10s** per trial, a **2x-14x** speedup over the 20-70s FMB parallel-jaw pipeline, while pretrained teachers generalize zero-shot to OOD FMB pegs and 152 VisDex objects at **76-77%** success.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Pretraining need not solve the downstream task outright; placing the policy in a useful region of parameter space lets downstream RL discover skills the prior never exhibited.
> - Naive fine-tuning collapse stems from critic miscalibration under a changed reward and observation space, not insufficient pretraining; recalibrating the critic before resuming actor updates prevents it.
> - Separating perception pretraining from policy distillation, rather than training both jointly from scratch, avoids the objective conflict between visual grounding and contact-rich behavioral refinement.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Dexterous manipulation using high degree-of-freedom (DoF) robotic systems, such as arms equipped with multi-fingered hands, presents significant challenges in robot learning. Existing approaches often struggle with the high-dimensional state and action spaces, the complexities of contact-rich interactions, and the difficulty of discovering useful behaviors from sparse, task-specific rewards. A common issue is that policies trained from scratch for one task rarely generalize to others, necessitating the repeated re-learning of fundamental skills like reaching, grasping, lifting, and reorientation for each new task.

Prior work in sim-to-real dexterous manipulation has explored large-scale reinforcement learning (RL) with domain randomization, in-hand reorientation, and methods for long-horizon tasks using local policies and motion planners. Some approaches have focused on task-agnostic object-centric policies or leveraged emergent dexterity through diverse reset states. However, many of these approaches either rely on explicit object and goal pose estimates as inputs, require demonstrations to seed learning, or restrict hand control to lower-dimensional subspaces.

In parallel, research in RL pretraining and transfer has investigated methods to acquire general behaviors and adapt them to specific downstream tasks. This includes using behavior priors and regularization techniques to mitigate distribution shift and catastrophic forgetting during online adaptation. While existing pretraining methods have shown promise, their application to high-DoF dexterous policies trained from scratch in simulation, particularly for complex contact-rich tasks, remains an active area of development.

This paper positions itself within this landscape by proposing a paradigm shift: instead of training RL specialists from scratch, it aims to first acquire a general-purpose dexterous foundation and then adapt it for specific downstream tasks. The work distinguishes itself by:
- Developing a large-scale RL framework (ADEPT) for learning sim-to-real transferable dexterity across high DoF arm-hand robots.
- Enabling policies to solve long-horizon tasks directly from raw visuo-tactile perception, without relying on pose estimators or human demonstrations.
- Introducing a novel joint-space Geometric Fabric that mediates between the RL policy and the robot, allowing the exploitation of the full kinematic dexterity of high-DoF arm-hand systems while ensuring hardware safety, in contrast to prior fabric-guided policies that used restricted hand control subspaces.
- Addressing the challenge of stable transfer from pretrained policies to contact-rich downstream tasks, which often leads to the degradation of pretrained behaviors during fine-tuning.
- Integrating recent advances in tactile sensing for sim-to-real, such as TacMap for geometry-consistent penetration-depth maps and SaTA for spatially anchored tactile features, to provide spatially disambiguated per-finger contact information.

The authors state that their work represents the first demonstration of zero-shot sim-to-real RL for long-horizon pick-reorient-insert tasks on high-DoF arm-hand systems directly from raw visual or visuo-tactile perception, without demonstrations or a pose estimator.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the inherent difficulties in teaching high degree-of-freedom (DoF) arm-hand robot systems to perform dexterous manipulation. These systems combine expansive state and action spaces with a high degree of contact-rich interactions, rendering the discovery of useful behaviors through standard reinforcement learning (RL) particularly challenging, especially when relying on sparse, task-specific rewards. A significant limitation of current approaches is the lack of transferability; policies trained for one task often do not generalize to others. This necessitates a repetitive learning process where foundational skills like reaching, grasping, lifting, and reorientation must be rediscovered for each new task, leading to inefficiencies in development and computational cost.

Against this backdrop, the key objectives of the ADEPT framework are multifaceted:

1. **Establish a Foundational Dexterity Prior:** To move away from training task-specific RL specialists from scratch, the primary objective is to develop a method for acquiring a general-purpose dexterous foundation. This foundation is intended to serve as an initialization for policies, placing them in a parameter space region where task-specific behaviors can build upon, rather than overwrite, existing skills. This is achieved through pretraining on a generic object reposing task designed to instill broad manipulation capabilities.

2. **Enable Efficient Adaptation to Downstream Tasks:** Following pretraining, a critical objective is to adapt this foundational dexterity to specific, contact-rich downstream tasks. The framework aims to accelerate the learning of new behaviors that would otherwise be difficult to discover from scratch on multi-fingered robots, thereby avoiding redundant skill acquisition.

3. **Ensure Stable Transfer and Prevent Catastrophic Forgetting:** A significant challenge identified is the tendency for naive RL fine-tuning to degrade pretrained capabilities rapidly during transfer to new tasks. This degradation is attributed to mismatched observation spaces, misaligned value estimates, and excessive policy drift. Therefore, an explicit objective is to develop a stable post-training recipe that mitigates these issues, allowing the policy to retain core pretrained skills while specializing for the new task.

4. **Exploit Full Kinematic Dexterity Safely:** High-DoF arm-hand systems offer a vast kinematic space, but safely exploiting this dexterity without risking hardware damage (e.g., collisions, joint limits) is crucial. An objective is to introduce a control mechanism that mediates between the RL policy and the robot to enforce hardware safety while providing the policy access to the full joint configuration space.

5. **Achieve Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer from Raw Perception:** A central goal is to enable real-world deployment of policies that can perform long-horizon, contact-rich tasks directly from raw perceptual inputs (visual and tactile) without requiring human demonstrations or explicit pose trackers. This involves distilling state-based teachers into end-to-end perceptive students.

6. **Demonstrate Robustness Across Multiple Robot Embodiments:** The framework aims to be applicable and robust across different high-DoF arm-hand robot platforms, demonstrating its generality. This includes showcasing performance on systems with varying degrees of freedom and sensor modalities (vision-only vs. visuo-tactile).

7. **Improve Task Execution Efficiency:** Ultimately, the project seeks to achieve faster and more efficient task execution compared to conventional multi-stage manipulation pipelines that often rely on external fixtures and regrasp decompositions.

These objectives collectively aim to make the learning and deployment of complex dexterous manipulation skills for robotic systems more efficient, robust, and transferable.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The ADEPT framework is structured into three main stages: (1) pre-training, (2) post-training, and (3) teacher-student distillation for real-world deployment. The underlying problem is cast as a discrete-time Markov Decision Process (MDP), where a stochastic policy is learned via Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) with an asymmetric actor-critic to maximize expected discounted return. Two MDPs, M_pre and M_post, are defined for pretraining and post-training, respectively, sharing an action space but differing in observation space, dynamics, and reward functions.

#### 3.1. Pre-Training Foundational Dexterity

A dexterous teacher actor-critic (π_pre, V_pre) is pretrained in simulation on a generic object reposing task. This task requires the policy to execute a sequence including reaching, grasping, lifting, in-hand reorientation, transporting, and reposing an object to a sampled target pose.
- **Objects:** The task utilizes 16 primitive shapes (cylinders, cuboids, spheres, cones) with randomized scales. Object observations are represented as point clouds.
- **Curriculum Learning:** Automatic Domain Randomization (ADR) is employed as an online curriculum, progressively advancing task goals and increasing environmental complexity (e.g., gravity annealing from 0 to -9.81 m/s², fabric damping gain, goal sharpness) as the agent’s success rate improves.
- **Hyperparameter Optimization:** Population-Based Training (PBT) is used to search for optimal PPO hyperparameters (e.g., learning rate, PPO clip, entropy coefficient).

#### 3.2. Post-training Dexterous RL Specialists on Downstream Tasks

Transferring and adapting the pretrained policy to a new, contact-rich downstream task (M_post) is non-trivial. Direct fine-tuning with standard RL often leads to rapid degradation of pretrained behaviors. The paper identifies key challenges: mismatched reward functions, additional task-specific observations (e.g., receptacle pose, object-receptacle contact forces) in M_post, poor value/advantage estimates, and excessive policy drift during PPO updates. ADEPT addresses these with a structured RL adaptation procedure:
- **BC Actor Distillation:** The pretrained actor π_pre is distilled into a new downstream actor π_post using supervised imitation. This step runs for 40,000 iterations to transfer knowledge into the new actor, which operates on the downstream observation space. This process is initiated at an ADR level (ADR 20) where the pretrained policy can reliably perform the reposing segment of the task.
- **Critic Warm-up:** Following BC distillation, π_post is frozen, and a fresh downstream critic V_post is trained. This critic is updated on rollouts collected by the frozen actor under the downstream task’s reward function for approximately 20 PPO iterations (~1 million environment steps per GPU). This calibrates the value estimates for the new task before any policy updates occur.
- **Conservative PPO:** After critic warm-up, both π_post and V_post are unfrozen and jointly updated using PPO. This stage employs conservative updates: the actor learning rate is substantially reduced (from 1e-3 to 1e-5 with linear decay), and the PPO clip ratio (ϵ) is tightened (from 0.2 to 0.05). The goal pose for this stage is set directly to the final insertion goal (ADR 50), while other ADR randomizations continue to anneal from ADR 20.

#### 3.3. Teacher-Student Distillation

A state-based teacher (the post-trained π_post) is distilled into an end-to-end perceptive student policy (π_student) capable of zero-shot sim-to-real transfer.
- **Student Observations:** The student consumes proprioception, fabric state, and two RGB camera images. For the Flexiv-Sharpa embodiment, five per-finger tactile maps are additionally used.
- **Distillation Process:** DAgger is employed, where the teacher provides per-step action targets (mean and variance) based on rollouts collected by the student. The student's vision encoder uses an initially frozen pretrained ResNet backbone with a DextrAH-RGB unfreeze schedule.
- **Auxiliary Loss:** To improve perception of critical object properties like orientation, an 8-keypoint object pose prediction head is added on top of the shared stereo image features. This head is supervised against ground-truth peg pose, with a combined loss L = L_BC + L_aux.
- **Two-stage Student Curriculum:**
 1. **Student pretraining via reposing teacher:** The pretrained reorientation teacher is used to distill perception-heavy skills (peg detection, 8-keypoint pose prediction, tracking, reorientation) into the stereo RGB student.
 2. **Vision distillation from downstream teacher:** The student, initialized from the Stage-1 checkpoint, is then trained against the post-trained downstream teacher on the insertion task, refining contact-rich policy based on the already competent visual encoder.
- **Tactile Perception:** For the Flexiv-Sharpa, vision-based tactile sensors are simulated using TacMap-derived penetration-depth maps. These maps, along with binary contact maps, are processed by a per-finger CNN. Features are spatially anchored using SaTA-style FiLM conditioning on fingertip positions.
- **Domain Randomization:** Aggressive domain randomization is applied across physics, object perturbations, visual conditions (lighting, backgrounds, camera intrinsics/pose), and sensor noise during both distillation stages to enhance sim-to-real transferability.

#### 3.4. Joint Configuration Space Geometric Fabrics

A Geometric Fabric is integrated between the RL policy and the robot.
- **Functionality:** It enforces hardware constraints (joint limits, self- and environmental collision avoidance), provides a smooth second-order action prior, shapes RL exploration, and ensures a consistent low-level controller in both simulation and the real robot.
- **Control Mechanism:** The fabric operates in the full joint configuration space (Cspace). The policy outputs per-joint relative deltas (a_t ∈ [-1, 1]^n_q), which are interpreted as Cspace targets. The fabric's differential equation (M_f ¨q_f + f_f + f_π(a) = 0) is integrated, and the resulting positions and velocities are issued to the joint controller.
- **Benefits:** This approach allows the policy to access the full kinematic dexterity of the arm-hand system while maintaining safety and stability guarantees. Fabric parameters (e.g., damping gain, speed-control energy target) are also annealed via ADR.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents findings from both simulation and real-world experiments, evaluating the ADEPT framework's effectiveness in learning and transferring dexterous manipulation skills.

#### 4.1. Simulation Experiments

- **Pretrained Teacher Generalization:** The pretrained reposing teacher demonstrated generalization capabilities to unseen objects. On the reposing task, the Kuka-Allegro teacher achieved success rates (SR) on out-of-distribution (OOD) FMB pegs (0.76 ± 0.003) and VisDex objects (0.77 ± 0.011) that matched or slightly exceeded its in-distribution performance on primitive shapes (0.73 ± 0.003). The Flexiv-Sharpa teacher also showed robust generalization, with OOD SR (0.58 ± 0.011 for FMB pegs, 0.61 ± 0.015 for VisDex) remaining close to its in-distribution SR (0.64 ± 0.007).
- **Zero-Shot Downstream Task Performance:** When evaluated on FMB peg insertion without post-training, the pretrained reposing teacher zero-shot the reposing segment reliably, maintaining over 50% success up to ADR level 35 (above the receptacle hole). However, its success rate dropped to 0% at the final insertion goal (ADR 50), indicating a failure to handle the contact-rich phase, which was outside its pretraining distribution. This finding supported the necessity of post-training for task specialization.
- **Training Efficiency and Stability:**
 - **ADEPT vs. From Scratch:** ADEPT post-training enabled a downstream task teacher to be trained in 3 billion environment steps, following 8 billion steps of pretraining (11 billion total). In contrast, training from scratch was highly seed-sensitive, with most seeds plateauing well below full task completion (ADR 6) even after 9 billion steps, highlighting the significant acceleration and reliability provided by ADEPT. The 8 billion pretraining cost is amortized across multiple downstream tasks, making the marginal cost per new task 3 billion steps.
 - **Failure of Naive Fine-tuning:** Direct PPO fine-tuning of the pretrained teacher led to a rapid collapse of success rate to 0% at ADR 20 within a small number of updates. This was attributed to the pretrained critic's miscalibration to the new reward, leading to unreliable advantage estimates and excessive policy drift.
- **Post-training Component Ablations:** Controlled ablations demonstrated the contribution of each component of the structured post-training recipe:
 - The **reduced actor learning rate (1e-5)** was identified as essential; all variants with a higher learning rate (1e-3) collapsed with 0% success.
 - **Critic warm-up** contributed a +17.6% improvement in success rate.
 - **BC distillation** nearly halved the adaptation time (19.9 hours vs. 35.2 hours) and improved success and stability.
 - The **tightened PPO clip (0.05)** had minimal effect, with a looser clip (0.20) performing comparably or slightly better.
- **Emergent Grasps and Novel Behaviors:**
 - Reposing pretraining led to the emergence of natural, multi-contact "finger-wrap" grasps. Post-training refined these grasps to be task-aligned, eliminating undesirable grasping modes (e.g., bottom-up grasps for insertion) observed in the zero-shot pretrained policy.
 - Policies trained from scratch did not develop such natural finger-wrap behaviors, instead converging to functional but often unnatural grasps using fewer fingers.
 - For the dish-rack placement task, which involved an object (plate) geometrically dissimilar to pretraining primitives, reaching transferred zero-shot but grasping did not. However, post-training successfully learned both novel grasping behaviors and a flip-and-regrasp strategy not present in the pretrained policy. This indicated that pretraining provides a useful initial distribution even for tasks requiring qualitatively different manipulation skills.

#### 4.2. Real World Experiments

- **Hardware and Tasks:** Experiments were conducted on a 23 DoF Kuka iiwa7 + Allegro (vision-only) and a 29 DoF Flexiv Rizon + Sharpa (visuo-tactile). Tasks included FMB star peg insertion, FMB square/round peg insertion (most challenging), and dish-rack placement.
- **Overall Performance (Table 4):**
 - The Kuka-Allegro vision student achieved episodic success rates of 5/10 for FMB star insertion, 3/10 for FMB square/round insertion, and 6/10 for dish-rack placement.
 - The Flexiv-Sharpa visuo-tactile student achieved an 8/10 success rate for FMB square/round insertion.
- **Impact of Peg Geometry:** The symmetric star peg's geometry allowed more stable grasps and multiple valid insertion orientations, leading to higher success. The asymmetric square/round peg, with its round leg leading to unstable point contacts, resulted in greater per-stage drops (especially during lifting and reorientation) and a lower overall success rate for vision-only policies.
- **Vision vs. Visuo-Tactile Sensing:** Tactile sensing was observed to be decisive for robustness on the Flexiv-Sharpa, improving the FMB square/round insertion success from 3/10 (vision-only) to 8/10. Vision-only policies frequently failed due to grasp confidence issues, leading to repeated grasping attempts. Tactile feedback enabled the policy to confirm successful grasps and maintain reliability through subsequent manipulation stages.
- **Distillation Curriculum Effectiveness:** The two-stage student distillation curriculum was critical for sim-to-real transfer, outperforming a single-stage baseline (0/10 success) by approximately 10% in instantaneous success rates throughout training. Stage-1 perception pretraining was identified as essential.
- **Execution Speed:** ADEPT policies executed the entire manipulation sequence (pick-reorient-insert) as a single continuous behavior in 5–10 seconds per trial. This represented a 2×–14× speedup compared to the FMB parallel-jaw pipeline, which requires 20–70 seconds per trial, relies on external fixtures, and decomposes the task into multiple regrasp stages.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The ADEPT framework presents a methodological contribution to the field of robotic dexterous manipulation by offering a structured approach to learning and transferring complex skills across high-DoF arm-hand systems. Its significance can be understood through several aspects:

- **Paradigm Shift in RL for Dexterity:** The work advocates for a shift from training task-specific RL agents to leveraging a pretrained, general-purpose dexterous foundation. This approach addresses the inefficiency of repeatedly learning low-level manipulation primitives for every new task, thereby streamlining the development process for complex robotic behaviors.
- **Enhanced Sim-to-Real Transferability:** ADEPT demonstrates zero-shot sim-to-real transfer for long-horizon, contact-rich manipulation tasks directly from raw visuo-tactile perception. This capability is particularly significant given the challenges of bridging the reality gap, and it moves beyond reliance on demonstrations or explicit pose estimators that often simplify the perception problem.
- **Stable and Efficient Post-Training:** The structured post-training recipe, incorporating behavior cloning distillation, critic warm-up, and conservative on-policy updates, effectively mitigates catastrophic forgetting and policy degradation during transfer. This ensures that valuable pretrained skills are preserved while the policy specializes for downstream tasks, making the adaptation process more robust and efficient.
- **Safe Exploitation of High-DoF Systems:** The integration of a full joint configuration space Geometric Fabric allows the RL policy to leverage the entire kinematic dexterity of high-DoF arm-hand systems safely. This is a technical advancement that enables more agile and versatile manipulation while providing inherent collision and joint-limit avoidance, which is critical for real-world deployment.
- **Improved Manipulation Capabilities:** The framework facilitates the emergence of natural, multi-contact grasps and enables the learning of novel, complex behaviors like flip-and-regrasp strategies, even for objects not explicitly covered during pre-training. This suggests a pathway towards more generalizable and robust manipulation skills.
- **Validation of Multisensory Integration:** The decisive impact of tactile sensing on the Flexiv-Sharpa platform highlights the importance of multisensory integration for robust contact-rich manipulation, particularly in scenarios where vision alone may struggle with grasp confidence or occlusions.
- **Increased Task Execution Speed:** By performing complex sequences like pick-reorient-insert as a single, continuous behavior at human-comparable speeds, ADEPT offers a significant efficiency improvement (2×–14× faster) over multi-stage, fixture-dependent pipelines.

The potential impact of ADEPT extends to several areas:

- **Accelerated Robotic Skill Development:** By providing a reusable foundation of dexterous skills, the framework could substantially reduce the time and computational resources required to develop new manipulation capabilities for robots, making dexterous robots more accessible and adaptable.
- **Deployment in Unstructured Environments:** The ability to operate from raw perception and adapt to diverse tasks makes ADEPT-trained robots potentially suitable for deployment in less structured and more dynamic environments where precise object models or pre-programmed sequences are not feasible.
- **Foundation for Future Dexterity Research:** The work establishes a strong baseline for future research in generalizable dexterity, prompting further investigation into broadening pre-training distributions to include more complex interactions such as in-hand manipulation, tool use, clutter, and bimanual tasks.
- **Industrial and Service Robotics:** The enhanced speed, robustness, and adaptability of dexterous manipulation could find applications in manufacturing, logistics, healthcare, and service robotics, where fine-grained control and versatile object handling are crucial.

In summary, ADEPT offers a principled and effective approach to developing dexterous manipulation skills for high-DoF robots, addressing key challenges in RL, sim-to-real transfer, and robust real-world performance.
