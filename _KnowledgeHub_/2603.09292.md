# See, Plan, Rewind: Progress-Aware Vision-Language-Action Models for Robust Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> The See, Plan, Rewind (SPR) framework improves robust robotic manipulation by providing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models with spatially-grounded progress awareness and an integrated error recovery mechanism. It demonstrated reduced performance degradation (18.8%) on out-of-distribution tasks and enabled successful execution of complex real-robot tasks where baselines failed.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models often lack a concrete, quantitative understanding of task progress, leading to error accumulation and inefficient execution.
- Prior attempts at progress monitoring rely on abstract linguistic or binary signals, which lack explicit spatial grounding for precise robot action and reliable error detection.
- Current failure recovery mechanisms are typically data-intensive, requiring costly collection of failure-specific data or dependence on auxiliary models.

## Method

- The See, Plan, Rewind (SPR) framework provides progress-aware reasoning through a continuous cycle: See (perceive state), Plan (generate 2D trajectory to subtask), and Rewind (error recovery).
- Tasks are decomposed into fine-grained, spatially-grounded 2D subgoals with semantic descriptions, extracted automatically from existing demonstrations without extra human annotation.
- An intrinsic 'Rewind' mechanism is implemented for error recovery, using progress-based anomaly detection to trigger a brief, learned retraction to disengage from erroneous states.

## Results

- Achieved an average **91.8%** success rate on the LIBERO benchmark, representing a **5.0%** improvement over the MolmoAct baseline, with an **8.2%** gain on long-horizon tasks.
- Demonstrated state-of-the-art out-of-distribution (OOD) robustness on LIBERO-Plus, exhibiting the smallest average performance degradation (**18.8%**) compared to baselines like OpenVLA-OFT (**27.0%**).
- Successfully executed real-robot tasks such as 'Tidy up the Table' (**30%** success rate for 3 objects, baselines failed) and 'Push-T' (**40%** success, baselines failed), showcasing generalization beyond pick-and-place.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Formalizing task progress as explicit, spatially-grounded 2D subgoals provides robot-interpretable milestones, crucial for fine-grained task execution and effective error detection.
> - Integrating a data-efficient error recovery mechanism (Rewind), trained on synthesized reverse trajectories, allows for autonomous failure management without requiring additional failure data or auxiliary models.
> - A unified framework combining progress awareness and intrinsic recovery significantly enhances robustness and generalization capabilities in challenging long-horizon and out-of-distribution manipulation scenarios.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation is progressing towards increasingly autonomous and generalized systems, often leveraging Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. These models aim to enable robots to understand high-level language instructions, perceive their environment visually, and execute corresponding actions. Existing VLA approaches have demonstrated capabilities in basic task execution and flexible behaviors. However, a persistent challenge remains in ensuring robust performance, particularly in dynamic and unpredictable real-world environments.

This work addresses the issue of "progress awareness," defined as the robot's ability to quantitatively measure its task execution against a sequence of concrete, actionable milestones. Prior research has explored progress monitoring through various mechanisms, such as visual chain-of-thought (e.g., ECOT) or structured mid-level spatial plans (e.g., MolmoAct). However, these methods often rely on abstract linguistic signals, binary flags, or coarse spatial waypoints, which may lack the explicit spatial grounding necessary for precise robot action and reliable failure detection.

Concurrently, research in failure recovery for robotic systems has sought to enhance robustness. Approaches include generating synthetic failure-reasoning data (e.g., AHA, RoboFAC, FailSafe) or using Large Language Models (LLMs) for error analysis and correction (e.g., REFLECT, COME-robot). A common limitation of these methods is their reliance on extensive, costly data collection for failure scenarios or dependency on pre-defined LLM prompts, which can limit adaptability to unseen situations. The paper notes that these recovery mechanisms often depend on auxiliary models or substantial data, motivating the need for a more intrinsically embedded and data-efficient recovery system.

The "See, Plan, Rewind" (SPR) framework positions itself within this landscape by integrating spatially-grounded progress awareness with an intrinsically embedded error recovery mechanism. It aims to bridge the gap between abstract progress monitoring and actionable robotic recovery, providing a unified approach that does not require additional training data or auxiliary models for failure recovery. This distinguishes it from previous work by offering robot-interpretable, spatially-grounded progress planning combined with a data-efficient recovery mechanism.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the robustness of robotic manipulation by endowing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models with explicit, spatially-grounded progress awareness and an integrated, data-efficient error recovery mechanism. The authors formalize this capability as "progress awareness," which involves the ability to measure task execution against a sequence of concrete and actionable milestones.

The motivation for this work stems from several limitations observed in current VLA models and their approaches to progress monitoring and failure recovery:
- **Lack of Spatially Grounded Progress Signals:** Many existing VLA models for robotic manipulation operate with abstract linguistic plans or coarse intermediate waypoints. These signals often lack explicit spatial grounding, making it difficult for a robot to precisely understand its current task status, anticipate verifiable intermediate states, or accurately detect when progress stalls. The absence of fine-grained, spatially-anchored milestones can lead to ambiguity in progress evaluation and hinder precise action generation.
- **Data-Intensive Failure Recovery:** Current failure recovery methods often rely on extensive additional data collection specific to failure scenarios or the deployment of auxiliary models. This makes them costly to implement and difficult to generalize to novel or unseen situations. There is a need for recovery mechanisms that can leverage existing successful demonstrations and operate without substantial extra training data or separate recovery-specific models.
- **Ineffectiveness in Long-Horizon and Out-of-Distribution Tasks:** Without explicit progress awareness and robust recovery, VLA models often struggle with complex, multi-step tasks (long-horizon) and exhibit significant performance degradation when faced with variations in environment, objects, or instructions that deviate from their training distribution (out-of-distribution robustness). Errors can accumulate over time, leading to cascading failures that cannot be autonomously rectified.
- **Desire for a Unified and Interpretable Framework:** The authors aim to create a unified framework where progress planning and error recovery are intrinsically linked and interpretable by the robot. This means decomposing tasks into measurable subgoals that directly inform action generation and enable self-correction, rather than relying on external reasoning or human intervention.

To address these motivations, the SPR framework proposes to dynamically ground language instructions into a sequence of spatial subgoals, operating through a continuous "See, Plan, Rewind" cycle to enable robust error correction without requiring additional training data or auxiliary models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The See, Plan, Rewind (SPR) framework integrates fine-grained spatial subtask planning with an error recovery mechanism, operating in a continuous closed loop. The methodology comprises three main components: Fine-Grained Spatial Subtask Planning, See-Plan Data Curation, and Error Recovery via Progress-Aware Rewind.

#### 3.1. Fine-Grained Spatial Subtask Planning

Inspired by human cognitive processes, this mechanism enables VLA models to identify remaining subtasks and plan trajectories to concrete spatial waypoints.
- **Spatial Subtask See and Plan:** Upon receiving an observation and task instruction, the model enters the "See" phase. It outputs depth perception tokens (following MolmoAct), predicts the remaining subtask count, and generates semantic descriptions with 2D coordinates for each subtask. This establishes comprehensive progress awareness. In the subsequent "Plan" phase, the model generates a 2D trajectory of up to 5 waypoints from the current gripper position to the next subtask goal. This approach aims to provide more robust execution guidance compared to direct planning toward a final goal, especially for long-horizon tasks.
- **Action Reasoning with Subtask Awareness:** The framework extends MolmoAct's depth-conditioned reasoning by incorporating explicit subtask planning into its autoregressive generation process. Given an RGB observation ($I$) and language instruction ($T$), the model sequentially generates five token streams:
 1. Depth perception tokens ($d$).
 2. Remaining subtask count ($n$).
 3. Subtask specifications ($s = (s_1, \dots, s_n)$), where each $s_i = (\text{sem}_i, \text{coord}_i)$ consists of a semantic description and 2D completion coordinates.
 4. Trajectory tokens ($\tau$) from the current gripper position to the next subtask waypoint.
 5. Action tokens ($a = (a_1, \dots, a_D)$) with $D$ degrees of freedom.
 Each component conditions on the current observation, instruction, and all previously generated tokens in the sequence.

#### 3.2. See-Plan Data Curation

An automated pipeline is designed to extract all necessary supervision signals (subtask boundaries, spatial coordinates, and rewind trajectories) from existing demonstration data, removing the need for additional human annotation or auxiliary models during training and inference.
- **Subtasks Segmentation and Description:**
 - For pick-and-place tasks, subtask boundaries are identified directly from gripper state transitions (open/close), which reliably mark completion points.
 - For other manipulation types (e.g., pushing, closing cabinets), Gemini-3 is employed to annotate subtask segments with boundary frames and semantic descriptions.
 - For LIBERO tasks, which are predominantly pick-and-place, gripper-based segmentation is used, and DeepSeek-R1 is prompted with the task instruction and detected subtask count to generate semantic descriptions for each segment. The remaining number of subtasks for each frame serves as ground truth for training.
- **Gripper Trajectory Extraction:** To obtain spatial coordinates for subtask waypoints and 2D trajectories, a combination of DINOv3 and SAM is used for robust gripper detection without task-specific training.
 - DINOv3's patch-level features identify the image region with the highest similarity to a reference gripper image, providing coarse localization.
 - SAM refines this localization using the DINOv3 position as a point prompt for precise gripper segmentation.
 - Final 2D coordinates are computed by leveraging SAM's bounding box center for the x-coordinate (horizontal precision) and DINOv3's detection for the y-coordinate (vertical endpoint accuracy). All 2D coordinates are discretized to.
 - Trajectories are smoothed by detecting and interpolating outlier points and applying a median filter, while preserving subtask boundaries. Subtask waypoints are extracted as gripper positions at detected boundary frames. 1-5 gripper positions are uniformly sampled from the current frame to the next subtask completion frame to provide intermediate waypoints for trajectory planning.

#### 3.3. Error Recovery via Progress-Aware Rewind

This mechanism allows the model to disengage from erroneous states by executing a brief learned retraction.
- **Rewind Data Construction:** Reverse trajectories are constructed by inverting successful forward demonstrations from the first subtask waypoint back to the robot's initial position. This involves:
 1. Temporal reversal of frame sequences.
 2. Negation of action token values (inverting end-effector delta movements).
 3. Setting the task instruction to "return to initial position."
 All other supervision signals (subtask boundaries, waypoint coordinates, and 2D trajectories) are inherited automatically from the data curation pipeline. The model is jointly trained on these forward and constructed rewind data.
- **Progress-Based Anomaly Detection:** A first-in-first-out (FIFO) state recorder continuously tracks the model's predicted subtask counts over the most recent 4 timesteps and planned 2D trajectories over the most recent 8 timesteps. Anomalies are detected using two criteria:
 1. **Subtask Count Anomaly:** If the subtask count increases across both the current and immediately prior window in the queue, it indicates sustained execution failure and regression to earlier stages.
 2. **Progress Stagnation:** If planned 2D trajectories remain identical across all 8 recorded timesteps, it signals that the robot is trapped in an out-of-distribution state (e.g., collision, misalignment), repeatedly generating identical plans without progress.
 Both criteria require persistence over multiple timesteps to filter transient noise.
- **Rewind Execution Strategy:** If an anomaly is detected, the original task instruction is temporarily replaced with the "return to initial position" command for a fixed duration of N timesteps (empirically set to N=3). After N steps, the system reverts to the original instruction to resume the task from the new state. This strategy allows the robot to backtrack to a stable, in-distribution state.

The SPR framework leverages a 7B architecture, initialized from a MolmoAct checkpoint, and employs LoRA fine-tuning. Inference cost is maintained at similar levels to MolmoAct, with negligible overhead from additional planning outputs or anomaly detection logic.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates SPR across simulation benchmarks (LIBERO and LIBERO-Plus) and real-robot tasks, focusing on overall performance, out-of-distribution (OOD) robustness, and ablation studies.

#### 4.1. Effectiveness Evaluation on LIBERO

- **Overall Performance:** On the LIBERO benchmark, SPR demonstrated improved performance. Separately-trained SPR models achieved an average success rate of 90.6%, representing a 3.8% improvement over the MolmoAct baseline. A jointly-trained SPR model on all subsets achieved an even higher average of 91.8%, a 5.0% improvement over MolmoAct.
- **Complex Tasks:** SPR showed notable gains on the challenging Long benchmark, with increases of 5.6% (separately trained) and 8.2% (jointly trained), indicating its effectiveness in handling multi-step manipulation sequences.
- **Generalizability:** The performance improvement observed with joint training, rather than degradation, suggests that SPR learns generalizable progress-aware reasoning across different task distributions.

#### 4.2. Robustness Evaluation on LIBERO-Plus

- **Out-of-Distribution Robustness:** LIBERO-Plus, which introduces various distribution shifts, revealed that all state-of-the-art models experienced substantial performance degradation. SPR exhibited the smallest overall performance drop, achieving an average success rate of 71.8% with a degradation of 18.8%. This compares favorably to OpenVLA-OFT (27.0% degradation) and UniVLA (37.5% degradation).
- **Specific Perturbations:** SPR achieved competitive performance degradation on Language (-12.1%), Light (-5.6%), and Robot (-42.9%) perturbations. This indicates the effectiveness of semantic-based progress awareness in handling linguistic ambiguity and the utility of the Rewind mechanism in adapting to novel robot initial configurations.

#### 4.3. Real-Robot Results

SPR was validated on three real-robot tasks:
- **Pick up the Object:** SPR achieved a 70% success rate, outperforming MolmoAct (50%). This suggests that explicit spatial grounding of target objects as subtask waypoints enhances comprehension and precision even for basic tasks.
- **Tidy up the Table (3-object setting):** SPR achieved a 30% success rate, whereas MolmoAct failed entirely (0%). This demonstrates SPR's ability to plan and execute long-horizon multi-object tasks through structured subtask decomposition.
- **Push-T:** SPR achieved a 40% success rate, while MolmoAct scored 0%. This indicates SPR's generalization beyond pick-and-place, successfully decomposing a continuous-contact manipulation task into sequential phases (approach, adjust, push, align, fine-tune) even without gripper state transitions to define subtask boundaries.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study

- **Effect of Spatial Subgoals and Semantics:**
 - The model with only See and Plan capabilities (w/o Rewind) achieved a 4.0% performance improvement over the MolmoAct baseline on LIBERO, confirming the effectiveness of progress-aware planning.
 - Removing semantic descriptions (w/o Rewind & Semantics) while retaining 2D coordinates led to a 3.4% performance drop compared to the w/o Rewind model on LIBERO-Long, and significant degradation in OOD robustness. This suggests that both spatial coordinates and semantic descriptions are crucial and complementary for effective progress-aware reasoning.
- **Effect of Rewind Mechanism:** The full SPR model, including the Rewind mechanism, achieved higher or comparable success rates across all LIBERO subsets compared to the w/o Rewind model, with an overall performance gain of 1%. On LIBERO-Plus (Language and Robot variants), the Rewind mechanism maintained superior performance, confirming its contribution to robustness in complex, OOD scenarios.
- **Impact of Inference Episode Length:** Both SPR and the w/o Rewind model demonstrated a superior ability to leverage extended episode lengths, achieving higher success rates after the MolmoAct baseline plateaued (Figure 4). The full SPR model exhibited faster task completion efficiency compared to the w/o Rewind model, particularly on LIBERO-Long and LIBERO-Goal, indicating that the Rewind mechanism effectively reduces time for re-executing failed subtasks.
- **Effect of Rewind Step Count N:** Testing different rewind step counts (N=2, 3, 4) revealed that N=3 consistently achieved optimal performance across LIBERO subsets. Values too small failed to provide sufficient operational space, while values too large caused the arm to drift excessively or assume unrecoverable poses.

#### 4.5. Failure Analysis

Despite its robustness, SPR exhibited certain failure modes:
- **Precision Limitation from Discrete Action Tokens:** The discretization of continuous control commands into discrete token sequences limited precision, particularly for tasks requiring fine-grained manipulation (e.g., precise object placement).
- **Rewind Ineffectiveness When Physically Stuck:** When the robot encountered physical constraints or became stuck, the rewind mechanism failed because physical obstruction prevented meaningful state transitions, even though logical anomalies were detected.
- **Persistent Failure Despite Successful Rewind:** In some cases, the model successfully detected errors and executed rewind but still failed to complete the task due to persistent spatial misalignment or a disconnect between planned waypoints and executed actions (i.e., the generated actions did not faithfully follow the plans).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The See, Plan, Rewind (SPR) framework introduces a paradigm for robust robotic manipulation by explicitly integrating spatially-grounded progress awareness with an autonomous, data-efficient error recovery mechanism. The significance and potential impact of this work can be summarized as follows:

- **Enhanced Robustness and Generalization:** By decomposing tasks into verifiable 2D spatial subgoals and continuously monitoring progress against these milestones, SPR enables robots to maintain a grounded understanding of their task status. This fine-grained awareness, combined with the Rewind mechanism, significantly improves robustness against unexpected perturbations, environmental changes, and suboptimal initial conditions. The state-of-the-art performance on the challenging LIBERO-Plus benchmark, with minimal performance degradation across diverse distribution shifts, indicates SPR's superior generalization capabilities compared to existing VLA models. This has implications for deploying robots in varied and unstructured environments where unforeseen events are common.

- **Data-Efficient Error Recovery:** Unlike many prior failure recovery methods that require extensive additional failure-specific data collection or auxiliary models, SPR’s recovery mechanism is intrinsically embedded within the VLA framework. It leverages successful demonstration data to construct rewind trajectories and employs simple logical comparisons within a state recorder for anomaly detection. This data-efficient approach reduces the overhead and complexity associated with implementing robust error correction, making it more scalable and practical for real-world applications.

- **Interpretable and Actionable Progress Monitoring:** The framework's ability to generate semantic descriptions and 2D spatial coordinates for each subtask provides interpretable progress signals. This allows for fine-grained, robot-executable tracking of task execution. Such concrete, verifiable waypoints replace abstract plans, enabling a clearer understanding of the robot's intent and status, which could facilitate debugging and human-robot collaboration.

- **Scalability to Long-Horizon and Diverse Tasks:** The structured decomposition of tasks into sequential spatial subgoals allows SPR to scale effectively to complex, multi-step manipulation tasks. Its performance on real-robot scenarios, including multi-object tidying and continuous-contact pushing, demonstrates its applicability beyond simple pick-and-place operations. This indicates a potential for tackling more intricate real-world problems that require extended sequences of actions and varied interaction types.

- **Foundation for Future Research:** The identified failure modes, particularly limitations due to discrete action tokens, ineffectiveness when physically stuck, and discrepancies between planned and executed actions, highlight important avenues for future research. Addressing these challenges could lead to advancements in continuous control, physical state awareness, and enhanced consistency between perception, planning, and action generation in VLA models.

In conclusion, SPR represents a step toward more autonomous and reliable robotic systems by integrating proactive progress awareness with reactive self-correction. Its contributions in robustness, data efficiency, and task interpretability could influence the development of future VLA models for complex and adaptive robotic manipulation.
