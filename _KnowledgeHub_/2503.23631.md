# Intrinsically-Motivated Humans and Agents in Open-World Exploration

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at UC Berkeley's BAIR group compared human (adults, children) and AI agent exploration in the open-ended Crafter environment, finding human progress correlated with Entropy and Empowerment, but not Information Gain. Adults explored more efficiently than agents, and children's goal-oriented private speech showed a positive link to exploration success.

## Problem

- Artificial intelligence agents often lag behind human performance in open-ended exploration, despite incorporating intrinsic rewards inspired by information theory.
- It is unclear which specific information-theoretic intrinsic objectives (Entropy, Information Gain, Empowerment) effectively drive human curiosity and exploration.
- Previous computational studies examining human curiosity were confined to simplified environments, limiting generalizability to complex, real-world scenarios.

## Method

- The study directly compared exploration behaviors of adults, children, and various reinforcement learning agents (intrinsic, extrinsic, random) within the same complex, Minecraft-like 'Crafter' environment.
- It quantified human exploration progress using achievement and map coverage scores, and correlated these with three ==information-theoretic intrinsic objectives== (==Entropy==, ==Information Gain==, ==Empowerment==).
- Audio recordings of human participants were transcribed and analyzed using an LLM to classify ==private speech== utterances, specifically looking for expressions of goals or questions.

## Results

- Adults achieved higher exploration scores and higher levels of Entropy and Empowerment compared to children and intrinsically-motivated AI agents.
- The extrinsic Reward agent generally outperformed the intrinsic agents on most achievement-based metrics and achieved Mean Achievement Scores comparable to adults.
- Children's fraction of goal utterances showed a strong positive correlation (**ρ = 0.8**, **p = 0.005**) with their Mean Achievement Score, while adults' verbalizations did not correlate with performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Human exploration progress consistently correlated with Entropy and Empowerment, suggesting these are more indicative intrinsic objectives for effective exploration than Information Gain.
> - The temporal dynamics of Entropy (rapid initial increase, then plateau) and Empowerment (steady increase) suggest potential strategies for dynamically weighting intrinsic rewards in AI.
> - Children's goal-oriented private speech strongly correlated with their exploration success, indicating a functional role for explicit goal-setting in open-ended tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The study addresses a long-standing challenge at the intersection of cognitive science and artificial intelligence: understanding and implementing intrinsic motivation for effective exploration in open-ended environments. Human beings, including children, demonstrate remarkable efficiency in exploring novel environments without explicit external rewards. In contrast, while artificial intelligence (AI) agents have achieved significant successes in task-specific domains (e.g., game playing), their open-ended exploration capabilities often lag behind human performance.

In reinforcement learning (RL), intrinsic rewards, often inspired by information theory and human curiosity, are a primary approach to guide domain-agnostic exploration. Common intrinsic objectives include Entropy (rewarding state diversity), Information Gain (rewarding learning about environment dynamics), and Empowerment (rewarding increased control over the environment). However, the precise intrinsic objectives underpinning human curiosity remain a subject of ongoing investigation in cognitive science, and it is unclear which of these, if any, effectively drive human exploration. Previous computational studies in cognitive science have largely been confined to simplified environments with limited states or actions, limiting their generalizability to complex, real-world scenarios.

This research aims to bridge the gap between human and agent exploration by directly comparing their behaviors in a complex, open-ended, Minecraft-like environment called Crafter. By analyzing how human exploration progress correlates with established information-theoretic intrinsic objectives, the authors seek to inform the design of more effective intrinsic reward mechanisms for AI agents. Furthermore, the study integrates insights from developmental psychology, particularly regarding the role of private speech in children's problem-solving, to explore its potential contribution to exploration, drawing parallels with recent AI work on language-guided agents. The work thus situates itself as an empirical, interdisciplinary investigation into the foundations of intelligent exploration, seeking to leverage human behavioral data to advance AI capabilities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to directly compare the exploratory behaviors of adults, children, and AI agents within the same complex, open-ended virtual environment, Crafter, to gain insights into the mechanisms driving effective exploration.

This overarching objective is motivated by several specific aims:

- **Identify Correlates of Human Exploration:** The study seeks to determine which of three commonly proposed information-theoretic intrinsic objectives—Entropy, Information Gain, and Empowerment—are consistently positively correlated with human exploration progress. Understanding these correlations is crucial for designing intrinsic reward functions for AI agents that more accurately reflect human-like exploratory drives.
- **Evaluate Intrinsic Motivation in AI Agents:** A key motivation is to assess the effectiveness of state-of-the-art intrinsically-motivated RL agents (NovelD, APT) in comparison to humans and an extrinsically-rewarded agent in a rich exploration setting. The observed gap between human and agent exploration capabilities, despite sophisticated intrinsic objectives, suggests room for improvement in intrinsic reward design.
- **Analyze Temporal Dynamics of Intrinsic Objectives:** The research aims to investigate how Entropy, Information Gain, and Empowerment evolve over the course of exploration in both humans and agents. This temporal analysis could reveal which objectives are more relevant at different stages of exploration (e.g., early novelty-seeking versus advanced mastery).
- **Investigate the Role of Language in Exploration:** Motivated by theories in developmental psychology (Vygotsky's work on private speech) and recent advancements in language-guided AI agents, a specific objective is to analyze the private speech utterances of human participants. The goal is to determine if verbalizations, particularly goal-oriented language, are correlated with exploration success in children and adults.

The overarching motivation is to address the persistent challenge of endowing AI agents with human-like exploration abilities. By studying humans, especially children who are considered foundational learners, in a rich environment, the authors hypothesize that they can uncover fundamental cognitive processes that underpin successful exploration. This understanding is intended to directly inform the development of more effective and general-purpose intrinsic motivation mechanisms for artificial intelligence.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The study employed a comparative approach, collecting and analyzing behavioral data from human participants (adults and children) and multiple types of AI agents in a standardized open-ended environment.

**Environment:**
The chosen environment was **Crafter** [Hafner, 2021], a Minecraft-like 2D grid-world that offers a broad range of activities including resource collection, crafting, and survival. The world is procedurally generated at the start of each episode. Player progress is tracked via "achievements" which mark semantically meaningful state changes. Actions available include movement and interaction with the environment (e.g., "do" action for eating, cutting, mining, fighting, and specific actions for placing items).
- **Modifications:** The game was modified to optimize for short play sessions and controller accessibility: lengthened daylight phase, explicit "Game Over" screen, and pruning of five advanced actions to fit a conventional game controller.

**Participants:**
- **Children:** 26 children, aged 8-12 years (mean age 9.4; 11 female, 15 male), recruited from the Bay Area Discovery Museum.
- **Adults:** 24 adults, aged 18-25 years (mean age 24.8; 10 female, 14 male), recruited from the University of California, Berkeley campus.
- **Selection:** Participants who could not complete at least one full episode or received significant external interference were excluded. Prior video game and Minecraft experience was recorded.
- **Procedure:** Participants were given a brief tutorial on controller buttons but no explicit game objectives or scores were displayed, encouraging intrinsically-motivated play. Each participant played for up to 20 minutes, with an option to quit early.

**Data Collection:**
For every time-step of gameplay, all actions taken and the complete world state were recorded. Audio recordings were also collected from consenting participants (17 children, 23 adults) and subsequently transcribed manually with timestamps.

**AI Agent Training:**
Four types of RL agents were trained for comparison:
- **Random Agent:** A baseline agent that took no action with probability 0.475 (to match human action rate) and sampled available actions uniformly otherwise.
- **Intrinsic Agents:**
 - **NovelD:** [Zhang et al., 2021] incentivizes information gain by providing intrinsic rewards at the boundary between familiar and novel regions, using RND (Random Network Distillation) for state novelty measurement.
 - **APT (Active Pre-training):** [Liu and Abbeel, 2021] rewards maximizing state entropy in an abstract representation space using a particle-based entropy estimator.
- **Extrinsic Agent:**
 - **Reward Agent:** Trained with a hand-crafted reward function that incentivized staying alive and unlocking in-game achievements (reward of 1 for first-time achievement, ±0.1 for health changes).
- **Training Details:** All RL agents were trained with Rainbow DQN [Hessel et al., 2018] for one million timesteps, using ϵ-greedy exploration (ϵ decayed from 1 to 0.01 over 250,000 timesteps). Each agent type was trained with 13 random initializations to reduce variance. For analysis, 25 episodes were subsampled from each agent's training trajectory.
- **Agent Policy Input:** A simplified semantic representation of the game state (labels of cells in view, status, inventory) was used as input to the RL agents.

**Exploration Performance Metrics:**
Five scores were developed to quantify different aspects of exploration:
1. **Mean Achievement Score (Mean AS):** Average fraction of unique achievements reached per episode.
2. **Map Coverage Score:** Average fraction of the world map visited per episode.
3. **Overall Achievement Score (Overall AS):** Fraction of unique achievements unlocked over all played episodes.
4. **Overall Breadth Score:** Fraction of steps taken in a breadth-first traversal of the achievement tree, counting achievements up to the first incomplete level.
5. **Overall Depth Score:** Normalized depth of the deepest achievement reached in the achievement tree.

**Information-Theoretic Intrinsic Objectives Calculation:**
To measure Entropy, Information Gain, and Empowerment, a reduced state representation was used, combining the semantic label of the faced cell, inventory contents, and status changes. This aimed to mimic human prior knowledge and filter out irrelevant environmental variations.
- **Entropy (H(S)):** Calculated as the negative sum of `p(s) log p(s)` over all states, where `p(s)` is the state visitation probability based on accumulated counts across all episodes.
- **Information Gain (IG):** Approximated using log-counts of novel state-action transitions. `IG_e(s,a)` for an episode `e` was the difference in `log(1 + N^e_s,a)` from `e-1`, summed over all `(s,a)` pairs and normalized by the number of transitions in `e`.
- **Empowerment (E(s)):** Defined as the channel capacity `max_p(a) I(S'; A|s)` (mutual information between actions and next states from a given state `s`), approximated using the Blahut-Arimoto algorithm. Transition probabilities `p(s'|a,s)` were estimated from observed counts, treating unexplored transitions as uniform (low empowerment). Movement transitions were filtered out to focus on environment-influencing actions.

**Private Speech Analysis:**
Audio transcripts from consenting participants were used.
- **Utterance Classification:** ChatGPT (gpt-3.5-turbo) was employed to classify private speech utterances as expressing a "goal" and/or a "question," with the LLM generating reasoning for each classification to improve accuracy.
- **Filtering:** Analysis was limited to participants with at least 5 utterances to reduce noise (10 children, 12 adults).

**Statistical Analysis:**
Permutation tests were used for discrete scores (Overall AS, Breadth, Depth), and Wilcoxon Rank Sum tests for continuous scores (Mean AS, Map Coverage) to compare group distributions. Pearson/Spearman correlations were computed to examine relationships between exploration scores, intrinsic objectives, and private speech metrics. A significance threshold of p < 0.05 was applied.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study yielded several findings regarding human and agent exploration capabilities, their relationship to information-theoretic objectives, and the role of private speech.

**Exploration Performance:**
- **Human-Agent Comparison:** Adults demonstrated higher average scores than children on most exploration metrics (Mean AS, Overall AS, Map Coverage, Depth), except for Breadth where performance was comparable. Adults consistently scored higher than intrinsically-motivated RL agents across all metrics.
- **Agent Performance:** The extrinsic Reward agent generally outperformed the intrinsic agents on most scores, and achieved comparable Mean Achievement Scores to adults. This suggests the substantial impact of well-designed reward functions and highlights areas for improvement in intrinsic reward design for agents. The NovelD and APT intrinsic agents did not surpass the Reward agent's performance in achievement-based metrics.
- **Human Exploration Strategies:** In children, Breadth and Depth scores showed a significant positive correlation (ρ = 0.51, p = 0.01), particularly among lower-scoring children, indicating that early difficulties affected both aspects of exploration. For adults, no significant correlation was observed, suggesting more diverse exploration styles among proficient players.

**Information-Theoretic Intrinsic Objectives:**
- **Human Attainment:** Adults achieved higher levels of Entropy and Empowerment compared to all agents and children. Children's Information Gain was comparable to adults, and their Entropy was higher or comparable to agents.
- **Agent Attainment:** NovelD and Reward agents exhibited comparable Entropy and Empowerment levels, both surpassing children in Empowerment. NovelD, an information gain-inspired agent, achieved higher Information Gain than other agents. APT, designed to maximize entropy, surprisingly performed worse than other RL agents on all information-theoretic measures, including Entropy.
- **Temporal Dynamics:**
 - **Information Gain:** Decreased for human participants over time, suggesting the exhaustion of easily accessible novel observations.
 - **Entropy:** Increased rapidly in early stages of learning for all groups (humans and agents), then plateaued.
 - **Empowerment:** Increased at a steady rate throughout learning for all groups.
- **Relationship to Exploration Scores:**
 - Significant positive correlations were found between human achievement-based exploration scores (Mean AS, Overall AS) and both **Entropy** and **Empowerment**.
 - No significant correlation was observed between human exploration scores and Information Gain. This may be because high Information Gain could be achieved through ineffective actions.
 - Overall Entropy and Empowerment for human participants displayed a non-linear positive correlation, where Empowerment increased more steeply for players who attained higher Entropy levels. This suggests a potential sequential or combined utility, with Entropy guiding early exploration and Empowerment providing a signal for advanced exploration.

**Private Speech Analysis:**
- **Speaking Rate:** Children spoke to themselves almost twice as much as adults (average 16 words/minute vs. 8 words/minute).
- **Correlation with Exploration:**
 - Children's overall word rate showed positive correlations with Mean Achievement Scores, Entropy, and Empowerment, with the correlation for Empowerment being statistically significant (p < 0.05).
 - Adults' word rates were negatively correlated with their achievement-based scores and intrinsic objectives, with significance for Entropy (p < 0.05).
- **Utterance Type:** Children were more than twice as likely to express goals (21%) compared to adults (10%). The fraction of question utterances was similar for both groups (12-13%).
- **Goal Verbalizations and Performance:** A strong positive correlation (ρ = 0.8, p = 0.005) was observed between the fraction of goal utterances and children's Mean Achievement Score. No significant correlation was found for the fraction of questions or for any adult verbalization metrics.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to both artificial intelligence and cognitive science by providing empirical data on intrinsically-motivated exploration in a complex, shared environment.

**For Artificial Intelligence:**
- **Informing Intrinsic Reward Design:** The consistent positive correlation between human exploration progress and Entropy and Empowerment suggests that these two objectives are more indicative of effective human exploration than Information Gain. This finding can directly inform the design of intrinsic reward functions for RL agents, potentially leading to more human-like and efficient open-ended exploration.
- **Addressing the Human-Agent Gap:** The observation that current intrinsically-motivated agents lag behind humans, and even behind extrinsically-rewarded agents, underscores the ongoing challenge in AI exploration. The results highlight that merely adopting information-theoretic objectives is insufficient; methods for better approximating and integrating these objectives in complex, sparse-reward environments are needed.
- **Dynamic Objective Selection:** The distinct temporal dynamics of Entropy (rapid initial increase, then plateau) and Empowerment (steady increase) offer a potential strategy for dynamically weighting intrinsic rewards. Entropy could be prioritized for early-stage exploration (novelty seeking), while Empowerment could guide later, more advanced exploration focused on mastering control and influence over the environment.
- **Language-Guided Exploration:** The preliminary evidence linking children's goal verbalizations to exploration success aligns with recent trends in AI using large language models for planning and goal generation. This suggests a promising avenue for integrating linguistic reasoning and explicit goal-setting into AI agents to enhance their exploratory capabilities.

**For Cognitive Science and Developmental Psychology:**
- **Computational Models of Curiosity:** The study provides support for computational theories of human curiosity that emphasize entropy maximization and empowerment. By using a richer environment than previous studies, it strengthens the ecological validity of these models.
- **Role of Private Speech:** The findings regarding children's private speech, particularly goal verbalizations, corroborate Vygotsky's theories on the role of inner speech in problem-solving and self-regulation. The strong positive correlation between goal utterances and exploration success in children suggests a functional role for overt verbalization in directing attention and maintaining focus on objectives, offering a behavioral link to cognitive processes.
- **Developmental Differences in Exploration:** The comparison between adults and children highlights developmental trajectories in exploration, with adults generally more efficient. The differing correlations between breadth and depth in children versus adults suggest changes in strategic approaches to exploration with experience and cognitive development.
- **Interdisciplinary Research Paradigm:** By directly comparing human and AI behavior in the same environment, the work establishes a robust interdisciplinary paradigm. This approach can facilitate a feedback loop where insights from human cognition inspire AI development, and computational models offer testable hypotheses for psychological research.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The authors acknowledge limitations such as the small sample size for human participants, particularly for the verbalization analysis which is considered exploratory. The reliance on a specific state representation for calculating intrinsic objectives also indicates that results might vary with different representations of human "prior knowledge." However, this work lays a foundation for future research, advocating for larger datasets, pre-registered studies, and investigations in more naturalistic and diverse settings.
