# Evolutionary System Prompt Learning for Reinforcement Learning in LLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework called Evolutionary System Prompt Learning (E-SPL) jointly optimizes Large Language Model weights using reinforcement learning and system prompts via an evolutionary algorithm, achieving enhanced performance, sample efficiency, and generalization on mathematical reasoning and agentic search tasks.

## Problem

- Existing LLM self-improvement mechanisms (self-reflection for context updates, RL for weight updates) are applied in isolation, so the potential of jointly combining declarative (prompt) and procedural (weight) knowledge remains largely unexplored.
- There is a representational gap between evolutionary methods, which explore discrete high-level structures like programs, and reinforcement learning, which optimizes continuous policy parameters.
- Current RL methods for LLMs often assume a fixed system prompt during training, neglecting the potential for dynamic evolution of declarative knowledge.

## Method

- ==E-SPL== integrates a reinforcement learning (RL) component for updating LLM policy weights and an ==evolutionary algorithm (EA)== for optimizing system prompts.
- The RL component conditions the LLM's policy on multiple evolving system prompts in parallel, using rewards from sampled trajectories to compute policy gradients for weight updates.
- The EA maintains a ==population of system prompts==, evaluating fitness via a ==TrueSkill== rating system on RL rollout performance; LLM-driven mutation self-reflects on trajectories to propose `git diff`-like edits, while crossover uses differential performance to suggest edits based on problem-wise strengths.

## Results

- E-SPL consistently outperformed RL-only, evolution-only, and self-reflection baselines on mathematical reasoning (DeepSeek-v3.1: **60.6%** on AIME 25 vs. **56.3%** RL-only, **45.1%** vs. **38.8%** on BeyondAIME) and agentic search (gpt-oss-120b).
- Analysis of learning dynamics showed E-SPL achieved better sample efficiency and higher asymptotic performance, adapting effectively to leverage either declarative or procedural knowledge for improvement.
- Learned system prompts externalized interpretable strategies, such as explicit generative self-verification instructions and codified domain expertise, enhancing the LLM's ability to self-correct and apply sophisticated reasoning principles.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Jointly optimizing LLM weights and system prompts through a tightly coupled RL and EA framework leads to synergistic self-improvement, surpassing methods that optimize either component in isolation.
> - The E-SPL framework facilitates a natural division of knowledge: explicit, interpretable declarative knowledge is encoded and evolved in system prompts, while implicit, intuitive procedural knowledge is refined in the model's weights.
> - Evolutionary search, guided by LLM self-reflection for mutation and crossover, discovers and externalizes domain-specific strategies and self-verification heuristics into human-readable system prompts; this explicit nature improves interpretability and oversight, as errors or overgeneralizations in declarative statements are more easily identified and corrected by humans than those embedded within model weights.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of agentic systems capable of autonomous self-improvement from experience represents a significant objective in the field of Artificial Intelligence. Current approaches for Large Language Models (LLMs) to self-improve predominantly involve two distinct mechanisms: self-reflection for updating contextual information, and reinforcement learning (RL) for modifying model weights. These mechanisms are often applied in isolation.

This work addresses the challenge of unifying these two paradigms—evolutionary methods and reinforcement learning—which have historically operated on different representational levels. Evolutionary algorithms typically explore discrete, high-level structures such as programs or architectures, while RL focuses on fine-grained optimization of policy parameters through gradient-based learning. In the context of LLMs, both model weights and model contexts (system prompts) are recognized as critical shapers of behavior. The paper identifies a gap where the potential synergies between optimizing contexts and weights are largely unexplored.

The research situates itself within several ongoing areas of inquiry:
- **Prompt Evolution and Contextual Memory:** Recent advancements demonstrate the efficacy of prompt engineering and iterative context refinement techniques (e.g., EvoPrompt, PromptBreeder, Reflexion, Self-Refine, ACE) in improving LLM performance by encoding declarative knowledge without altering model weights. Agentic frameworks like ReAct and AutoGen also highlight the role of system prompts in steering behaviors. This work builds upon the idea of prompts as optimizable objects but seeks to integrate it with parametric learning.
- **Scaling RL for LLMs:** RL has emerged as a primary method for enhancing LLM reasoning and agentic capabilities (e.g., o1, R1, CISPO, GRPO). However, these methods typically assume a fixed system prompt during training. While some work has explored discovering new prompts within RL loops (Ye et al., 2025), these are often treated as synthetic training data rather than evolving system prompts that encode strategic principles. This paper aims to integrate dynamic system prompt evolution directly into the RL process.
- **Combining Evolutionary Search with RL:** Hybrid approaches have long existed in traditional AI (e.g., ERL, PBT, CEM-RL), leveraging population dynamics to complement gradient-based optimization. More recent LLM-guided systems (FunSearch, AlphaEvolve) apply evolutionary search to code or algorithms. The Darwin Gödel Machine (Zhang et al., 2025a) explored self-referential self-modification in prompting but without weight updates. This work distinguishes itself by performing joint optimization of system prompts and model weights within a single training loop.
- **Genetic Programming (GP) with LLMs:** LLMs have been used to design mutation and crossover operators for evolving code or prompts (Hemberg et al., 2024; Agrawal et al., 2025; Novikov et al., 2025). Some prior work combines LLM-based genetic operators with DPO for combinatorial optimization. This research extends this concept to the more general domain of LLM post-training, where the LLM generates general-purpose system prompts to guide its own subsequent iterations.

By proposing Evolutionary System Prompt Learning (E-SPL), this work aims to bridge the representational gap between high-level behavioral specifications (prompts) and low-level parametric policies (weights), thereby unlocking potential synergies for LLM self-improvement that are not achieved by optimizing either component in isolation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to advance the state of AI agents capable of autonomous self-improvement by proposing a method that jointly improves both the contextual knowledge and the underlying weights of Large Language Models.

The motivation for this approach stems from the observation that existing self-improvement mechanisms for LLMs, specifically self-reflection for context updates and reinforcement learning for weight updates, are typically applied in isolation. This isolated optimization fails to exploit the potential synergistic relationship between declarative knowledge (high-level behavioral specifications encoded in prompts) and procedural knowledge (tacit skills and execution refined through weight adjustments). The authors hypothesize that a more effective and scalable path to self-improving agents involves a tight coupling of these two paradigms.

The specific objectives of the Evolutionary System Prompt Learning (E-SPL) framework are detailed as follows:
- **Joint Optimization:** To develop a framework that simultaneously optimizes LLM model weights through reinforcement learning and LLM system prompts through an evolutionary algorithm. This is intended to overcome the limitations of separate optimization by enabling a continuous interaction where improvements in one aspect can amplify gains in the other.
- **Structured Knowledge Exploration:** To allow for structured exploration over declarative knowledge. By maintaining and evolving a population of system prompts, E-SPL aims to discover and refine high-level principles, strategies, and behavioral specifications that guide the LLM's operation.
- **Efficient Procedural Knowledge Encoding:** To facilitate the encoding of procedural knowledge into the LLM's weights. By conditioning the RL process on increasingly refined and informative system prompts, the optimization pressure on the weights can be reduced, allowing RL to focus more effectively on acquiring and honing implicit, intuitive "know-how." This separation is hypothesized to lead to improved sample efficiency and learning stability.
- **Seamless Integration with RL Pipelines:** To design the evolutionary algorithm such that it is directly compatible with existing RL post-training pipelines, minimizing additional computational overhead. This is achieved by reusing the trajectories and performance metrics already generated during RL rollouts for fitness evaluation within the evolutionary process.
- **Enhanced Performance and Generalization:** Through experimental evaluation, to demonstrate that this joint optimization approach (E-SPL) leads to superior performance in terms of learning speed, asymptotic performance, and generalization capabilities across various reasoning and agentic tasks, compared to methods that optimize prompts or weights individually.
- **Interpretability and Oversight:** To promote the emergence of interpretable declarative knowledge in system prompts, which, unlike opaque weight updates, can be more easily monitored, analyzed, and corrected by humans, thereby contributing to scalable oversight.

In summary, the core motivation is to move beyond isolated optimization of LLM components towards a unified framework that leverages the complementary strengths of evolutionary search and reinforcement learning for more comprehensive and effective autonomous self-improvement.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Evolutionary System Prompt Learning (E-SPL) is a framework designed to jointly optimize the weights of a Large Language Model (LLM) using reinforcement learning (RL) and its system prompts (contexts) using an evolutionary algorithm (EA). The approach ensures that these two optimization processes are tightly coupled and synergistic.

#### 3.1. Reinforcement Learning for Weight Update

The RL component of E-SPL is responsible for updating the LLM's policy weights ($\pi_\theta$).
- **Parallel Prompt Conditioning:** In each RL iteration, the model's policy is conditioned on multiple system prompts, $\{s_i\}_{i=1}^M$, in parallel. This strategy prevents the model weights from overfitting to a single prompt and introduces competitive pressure among the prompts.
- **Trajectory Sampling and Reward Computation:** For each problem $x$ in a batch and for each system prompt $s_i$, the LLM samples $N$ trajectories (solutions) $y_{i,j} \sim \pi_\theta(\cdot | s_i, x)$. An outcome reward $r_{i,j} = R(x, y_{i,j})$ is computed for each trajectory, and an average value estimate $V_i = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{j=1}^N r_{i,j}$ is calculated for each prompt.
- **Policy Gradient Update:** The policy gradient objective for a given problem $x$ is defined as $J(\theta, x) = \frac{1}{NM} \sum_{i=1}^M \sum_{j=1}^N (r_{i,j} - V_i) \nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(y_{i,j} | s_i, x)$. The LLM weights $\theta$ are updated using this gradient, potentially with a KL regularization term to keep the updated policy close to a reference policy $\pi_{ref}$: $\theta \leftarrow \theta + \alpha \nabla_\theta (J(\theta, x) - \beta KL(\pi_\theta, \pi_{ref}))$. The paper notes that with low-rank adaptation (LoRA), KL regularization can sometimes be implicit.
- **Knowledge Division:** This conditioning on learned system prompts is intended to facilitate a natural division: system prompts encode declarative knowledge (explicit instructions, strategies), while weight updates focus on acquiring procedural knowledge (tacit, intuitive execution skills). This design aims to reduce the global optimization pressure on the model weights, allowing for more targeted and efficient learning of implicit capabilities.

#### 3.2. Evolutionary Algorithm for Context Update

The EA component continually optimizes the LLM's system prompts to enhance its capabilities and explicitly encode learned knowledge.
- **Population Management:** An evolutionary population $S$ of system prompts is maintained, initially containing a single root prompt, and evolving over time.
- **Fitness Evaluation using TrueSkill:** Instead of a separate validation set, the EA reuses the trajectories and rewards generated during the RL rollouts for fitness evaluation. Since absolute returns can vary across iterations due to changing data and weights, a Bayesian skill rating system called TrueSkill (Herbrich et al., 2006) is employed. TrueSkill models each prompt's rating as a Gaussian distribution $N(\mu, \sigma^2)$ and updates these ratings based on the relative performance (rank ordering of $V_i$) of sampled prompts within each RL iteration, thereby aggregating noisy, train-time comparisons into a persistent competence estimate.
- **Selection:** System prompts for each RL iteration are selected from the population $S$ based on an optimistic estimate of their skill, $score_i = \mu_i + \lambda\sigma_i$, where $\lambda > 0$ controls optimism (encouraging exploration). Sampling probability $p_i \propto \exp(score_i/T)$ is used, often within a sliding window of the $K$ latest prompts.
- **Mutation Operator (Figure 5):**
 - **Selection:** Mutation is applied only to the highest-performing prompt ($s_k = \operatorname{argmax}_i V_i$) from the current batch.
 - **LLM Self-Reflection:** A fixed reference policy $\pi_{ref}$ is prompted with the trajectories generated under $s_k$ to perform self-reflection ($\Psi_{reflect}$). This involves summarizing successful and failed trajectories, diagnosing mistakes, and identifying potential strategies.
 - **Edit Proposal:** Based on the self-reflection lessons $\ell$, $\pi_{ref}$ proposes concrete edits to $s_k$ in a `git diff`-like format: $diff \sim \pi_{ref}(\cdot | s_k, \ell)$.
 - **Prompt Generation:** The mutated prompt $s_{mutate}$ is created by applying the `diff` to $s_k$.
 - **Population Addition:** $s_{mutate}$ is added to $S$ with its parent's mean rating $\mu_k$ but with increased uncertainty $\sigma_k + \Delta\sigma$, which favors its selection in future rounds.
 - The mutation process involves four stages: Trajectory Summarization, Self-Critique, Local Edit Proposal (max $K_{ops}$ edits per problem), and Local-to-Global Aggregation to consolidate edits across the batch.
- **Crossover Operator (Figure 6):**
 - **Trigger:** Activated with a probability $p_{crossover}$.
 - **Performance Matrix:** It computes a score matrix $\Phi \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times B}$ from the average rewards $\Phi_{i,b}$ for each prompt $s_i$ on each problem $x_b$ in a batch. This matrix highlights problem-wise strengths.
 - **LLM Self-Reflection:** A crossover self-reflection prompt ($\Psi_{cr}$) is constructed using this differential performance information ($\phi$, a list of prompt-specific wins). $\pi_{ref}$ uses this context to analyze why certain prompts excelled on specific problems.
 - **Edit Proposal:** $\pi_{ref}$ proposes `git diff` edits to the overall best prompt $s_k$: $diff_{cr} \sim \pi_{ref}(\cdot | s_k, \Psi_{cr})$.
 - **Prompt Generation:** The child prompt $s_{crossover}$ is created by applying $diff_{cr}$ to $s_k$.
 - **Population Addition:** $s_{crossover}$ is added to $S$ with a precision-weighted average of its parents' ratings and increased uncertainty.
- **Hyperparameters:** Key hyperparameters include LoRA rank (32), learning rate ($\alpha=4e-5$), batch size (10 problems), number of rollouts per prompt ($N=5$), sliding window size ($K=10$), number of prompts per batch ($M=3$), uncertainty parameters ($\Delta\sigma=1, \lambda=2.0$), crossover probability ($p_{crossover}=0.2$), and TrueSkill defaults.

This methodology combines the gradient-based optimization of LLM weights with an LLM-driven evolutionary search for declarative knowledge, aiming for a self-improving agent that leverages both explicit instructions and refined internal parameters.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted evaluate E-SPL on mathematical reasoning and agentic search tasks to assess its performance against baselines and to understand the emergent properties of the learned system prompts.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance

E-SPL consistently demonstrated improved performance compared to methods that optimize either prompts or weights in isolation.
- **Mathematical Reasoning (DeepSeek-v3.1):**
 - **DAPO100 → AIME 25:** E-SPL achieved a 60.6% success rate, outperforming RL-only (GRPO) at 56.3%, Evolution-only at 54.6%, and Self-reflection at 48.6%.
 - **HMMT 23/24 → 25:** E-SPL reached 52.7% success, compared to RL-only (GRPO) at 50.0%, Evolution-only at 48.6%, and Self-reflection at 47.0%.
 - **AIME → BeyondAIME (Easy-to-Hard Generalization):** This setting yielded the most significant relative gains. E-SPL improved the success rate to 45.1%, considerably higher than RL-only (GRPO) at 38.8%, Evolution-only at 40.0%, and Self-reflection at 39.1%. This suggests E-SPL enhances generalization capabilities.
- **Agentic Search (NQ + HotpotQA, gpt-oss-120b):** E-SPL achieved a 48.6% success rate, outperforming RL-only at 44.2% and Evolution-only at 34.0%.

Across all tasks, E-SPL consistently surpassed both RL-only and evolution-only baselines, suggesting a synergistic relationship between context and weight optimization. Evolution-only methods generally outperformed self-reflection, and sometimes even RL-only, underscoring the value of population-based prompt search.

#### 4.2. Learning Dynamics

Analysis of learning curves (Figure 12) indicated that E-SPL exhibits better sample efficiency (faster learning) and achieves higher asymptotic performance compared to both RL-only and evolution-only approaches. Evolution-only methods often learned quickly in early stages but achieved lower asymptotic performance due to the inability to update model weights and thus refine procedural knowledge. E-SPL's combined approach allows it to leverage the strengths of both, adapting to whichever knowledge axis (declarative or procedural) is most critical for improvement at a given stage.

#### 4.3. Emergent Patterns in Learned System Prompts

The system prompts discovered by E-SPL exhibited several recurring patterns:
- **Generative Self-Verification:** Prompts frequently included instructions for explicit self-verification. For example, in agentic search, a learned prompt defined detailed criteria for marking search results as "VERIFIED" or "NOT VERIFIED" and specified workflows for re-querying if verification failed (Figure 7). In mathematical reasoning, prompts advised "perform sanity checks: test small instances, ensure bounds, and verify extreme cases" (Figure 10). The LLM demonstrated its ability to explicitly cite and follow these self-verification strategies, leading to self-correction.
- **Codified Domain Expertise and Heuristics:** The learned prompts codified highly domain-specific strategies, such as "When optimizing sums, set parameters to cancel dominant terms" or "In modular arithmetic and sums, decompose terms via remainders or moduli. Use periodicity for remainder sums and CRT for coprime factors" (Figure 9, Figure 16). These examples illustrate that E-SPL can effectively externalize declarative knowledge, allowing RL to focus on refining core reasoning skills without needing to internalize vast domain-specific facts into weights.
- **Failure Mode: Missing Caveats:** While generally effective, some discovered strategies in the system prompts were not universally true or contained overgeneralizations (Table 1). For instance, a principle regarding polynomial divisibility misunderstood a lower bound as the exact minimum, and a graph theory heuristic vaguely described an algorithm for finding a minimum T-join. The authors argue that despite these inaccuracies, such mistakes in explicit, propositional statements are more interpretable and correctable by humans than errors embedded within model weights, facilitating improved transparency and oversight.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Effect of Crossover:** The crossover operator was found to accelerate early learning by propagating high-fitness substructures across evolutionary branches (Figure 14). However, its impact on final performance was mixed, sometimes leading to slightly better results (HMMT) and sometimes slightly worse (AIME → BeyondAIME) compared to mutation-only. Both mutation-only and mutation+crossover variants, when combined with RL, consistently outperformed RL-only, validating the core evolutionary search.
- **Sampling Policy for Evolution:** Using a fixed reference policy ($\pi_{ref}$) for the evolutionary updates consistently improved E-SPL's final performance compared to using the continually changing online RL policy ($\pi_\theta$) (Figure 15). This suggests that a stable "editor" is beneficial for generating high-quality system prompt edits.

These findings indicate that E-SPL's joint optimization approach effectively leverages the complementary strengths of RL and evolutionary context search, leading to enhanced learning and problem-solving capabilities in LLMs, particularly in generalization tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in this paper, through the introduction of Evolutionary System Prompt Learning (E-SPL), signifies a conceptual and practical advancement in the field of Large Language Model (LLM) self-improvement. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Unified Self-Improvement Paradigm:** E-SPL demonstrates that reinforcement learning (RL) and evolutionary prompt search are not competing methodologies but deeply synergistic ones. By tightly coupling these two approaches, the paper offers a unified framework that surpasses the capabilities of either method when applied in isolation. This paradigm shift could lead to more robust and comprehensive self-improving AI agents.
- **Enhanced Performance and Generalization:** The consistent improvements in sample efficiency, asymptotic performance, and, notably, generalization across diverse reasoning (mathematical) and agentic (search) tasks highlight the practical utility of E-SPL. The pronounced gains in easy-to-hard generalization settings suggest that the framework enables LLMs to acquire more transferable and robust knowledge, which is a critical step towards more capable and flexible AI.
- **Natural Division of Knowledge:** E-SPL encourages a natural and beneficial division between declarative knowledge, explicitly articulated and stored in human-readable system prompts, and procedural knowledge, implicitly refined within the model's weights. This separation means that complex strategies, rules, and domain-specific heuristics can be learned and maintained in a transparent, editable format, while the model's internal parameters focus on honing the subtle, intuitive skills required for execution.
- **Improved Interpretability and Scalable Oversight:** A significant challenge in advanced AI is understanding and controlling complex model behaviors. By externalizing a substantial portion of learned knowledge into interpretable system prompts, E-SPL makes a part of the LLM's "thinking" process transparent. The discovery of partially incorrect or overgeneralized heuristics in the learned prompts, as identified in the ablation studies, further underscores this point: such errors in explicit statements are far more amenable to human monitoring, verification, formalization, and correction than opaque changes in billions of model parameters. This transparency is crucial for developing safer and more reliable AI systems.
- **Reduced Optimization Pressure for RL:** By providing increasingly informative declarative knowledge in prompts, E-SPL reduces the burden on RL to learn everything from scratch through weight updates. This targeted application of RL allows it to concentrate on refining "tacit knowledge" or "intuition," which can lead to better sample efficiency and potentially prevent catastrophic forgetting of high-level strategies during fine-tuning.
- **Foundation for Future AI Development:** The work represents a meaningful step towards "self-referential recursive self-improvement" or "Seed AI," where an AI system can modify its own learning algorithms and fundamental instructions. While E-SPL's current self-reflection and self-rewrite processes are still governed by a fixed reference policy, the concept of allowing a model to self-modify its system-level instructions opens avenues for future research into fully autonomous, open-ended learning systems.
- **Practical Implications for LLM Engineering:** For practitioners, E-SPL offers a method to systematically evolve and manage complex prompt strategies alongside model fine-tuning. This could streamline the development of specialized LLMs by automating parts of prompt engineering and making the acquired knowledge more modular and auditable.

In conclusion, E-SPL offers a principled and empirically validated approach to enhancing LLM self-improvement by harmonizing evolutionary context search with reinforcement learning. Its contributions extend beyond mere performance gains, impacting areas of interpretability, knowledge representation, and the foundational pursuit of autonomous intelligence.
