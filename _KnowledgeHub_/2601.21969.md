# Token-Guard: Towards Token-Level Hallucination Control via Self-Checking Decoding

> [!summary] Summary
> Token-Guard introduces a multi-stage self-checking decoding framework for large language models that explicitly controls and mitigates hallucinations at a token level. This method improves task completion across six HALU datasets, achieving an average EM/F1 of 0.29/51.03 for Llama-3.1-8B and 0.35/53.98 for Qwen3-8B, while maintaining competitive computational efficiency.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) frequently generate factually incorrect or inconsistent content (hallucinations), posing a critical challenge in knowledge-intensive applications.
- Existing decoding-based hallucination mitigation methods lack explicit token-level verification, allowing errors to propagate and compound throughout the generation process.
- Current decoding approaches often fail to explicitly quantify hallucination risk for candidate tokens and offer limited dynamic correction capabilities.

## Method

- A three-stage ==self-checking decoding== framework is proposed, including Prompt Self-Checking, Token-Level Hallucination Self-Checking, Candidate Segment Representation and Local Refinement, and Global Iteration and Correction.
- Token-level hallucination scoring combines semantic consistency (cosine similarity of hidden states) and standard token probability to prune low-confidence or potentially hallucinated tokens early.
- Segments of tokens undergo further evaluation for local consistency and global alignment, allowing for iterative local refinement of erroneous parts or discarding of unreliable segments, followed by global consistency checks on entire reasoning chains.

## Results

- Token-Guard consistently outperformed conventional decoding baselines, achieving up to **16.3%** relative improvement in Exact Match (EM) and F1 scores on HALU datasets using both Llama-3.1-**8B**-Instruct and Qwen3-**8B**.
- Ablation studies confirmed that token-level scoring contributed most significantly to performance gains, while all proposed components (prompt initialization, segment-level scoring, global iteration) played vital roles.
- The framework demonstrated superior factual precision, relevance, and logical consistency, maintaining competitive computational efficiency and memory usage across various LLM scales, including Llama-3.2-**3B** and Llama-3.2-**13B**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit token-level self-checking, which quantifies hallucination risk, is crucial for guiding early-stage token selection and preventing error propagation in LLM generation.
> - A multi-stage, iterative refinement process, from individual tokens to segments and entire reasoning chains, enables dynamic error correction and enhances factual consistency.
> - Integrating semantic consistency with token probability in hallucination scoring provides a robust mechanism for more reliable token and segment selection during decoding.
