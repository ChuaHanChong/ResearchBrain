# Token-Guard: Towards Token-Level Hallucination Control via Self-Checking Decoding

> [!summary] Summary
> Token-Guard introduces a multi-stage self-checking decoding framework for large language models that explicitly controls and mitigates hallucinations at a token level. This method improves task completion across six HALU datasets, achieving an average EM/F1 of 0.29/51.03 for Llama-3.1-8B and 0.35/53.98 for Qwen3-8B, while maintaining competitive computational efficiency.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) frequently generate factually incorrect or inconsistent content (hallucinations), posing a critical challenge in knowledge-intensive applications.
- Existing decoding-based hallucination mitigation methods lack explicit token-level verification, allowing errors to propagate and compound throughout the generation process.
- Current decoding approaches often fail to explicitly quantify hallucination risk for candidate tokens and offer limited dynamic correction capabilities.

## Method

- A three-stage ==self-checking decoding== framework is proposed, including Prompt Self-Checking, Token-Level Hallucination Self-Checking, Candidate Segment Representation and Local Refinement, and Global Iteration and Correction.
- Token-level hallucination scoring combines semantic consistency (cosine similarity of hidden states) and standard token probability to prune low-confidence or potentially hallucinated tokens early.
- Segments of tokens undergo further evaluation for local consistency and global alignment, allowing for iterative local refinement of erroneous parts or discarding of unreliable segments, followed by global consistency checks on entire reasoning chains.

## Results

- Token-Guard consistently outperformed conventional decoding baselines, achieving up to **16.3%** relative improvement in Exact Match (EM) and F1 scores on HALU datasets using both Llama-3.1-**8B**-Instruct and Qwen3-**8B**.
- Ablation studies confirmed that token-level scoring contributed most significantly to performance gains, while all proposed components (prompt initialization, segment-level scoring, global iteration) played vital roles.
- The framework demonstrated superior factual precision, relevance, and logical consistency, maintaining competitive computational efficiency and memory usage across various LLM scales, including Llama-3.2-**3B** and Llama-3.2-**13B**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit token-level self-checking, which quantifies hallucination risk, is crucial for guiding early-stage token selection and preventing error propagation in LLM generation.
> - A multi-stage, iterative refinement process, from individual tokens to segments and entire reasoning chains, enables dynamic error correction and enhances factual consistency.
> - Integrating semantic consistency with token probability in hallucination scoring provides a robust mechanism for more reliable token and segment selection during decoding.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Large Language Models have achieved remarkable success across numerous Natural Language Processing tasks. However, a persistent and critical challenge, particularly in knowledge-intensive or high-stakes applications, is their propensity to "hallucinate"—generating content that is factually incorrect, inconsistent with the input, or entirely fabricated. Mitigating these hallucinations is a central focus of current LLM research.

Existing strategies to combat hallucination fall into several categories:
- **Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG):** Methods like RAG (Lewis et al., 2020) aim to ground LLM generations in external, verifiable knowledge sources. While effective, RAG systems can be resource-intensive, requiring robust retrieval pipelines and potentially domain-specific adaptations (Luo et al., 2025b; Li et al., 2024).
- **Alignment Methods (e.g., RLHF):** Techniques such as Reinforcement Learning with Human Feedback (RLHF) (Stiennon et al., 2020) fine-tune models to align with human preferences and factual correctness. However, RLHF demands large-scale human annotation and computational resources for fine-tuning (Bai et al., 2022).
- **Decoding-Based Methods:** A lighter approach involves modifying the decoding process itself to improve reliability without external retrieval or extensive fine-tuning. These include Auto-Regressive Chain-of-Thought (CoT), Tree-of-Thought (ToT), Guided Decoding, and Predictive Decoding (Wei et al., 2022; Yao et al., 2023; Xie et al., 2023; Ma et al., 2024). These methods often generate outputs progressively, explore multiple reasoning paths, and incorporate some form of intermediate self-evaluation.

Despite their advantages in efficiency, current decoding methods suffer from key limitations:
- **Limited Token-Level Verification:** Most methods lack explicit mechanisms to check for hallucinated tokens at a fine-grained level, allowing errors to propagate and compound through the generation process (Zhang et al., 2025).
- **Unquantified Hallucination Risk:** Traditional probability scores do not explicitly quantify the risk of hallucination in the encoded space, leading to less guided token selection and potentially unstable reasoning (Zeng et al., 2024).
- **Insufficient Dynamic Correction:** Many methods offer only single-pass generation or limited iterative correction, which hampers output consistency and can lead to unnecessary computational costs (Ugare et al., 2024).

Token-Guard enters this landscape as a direct response to these limitations in decoding-based approaches. It positions itself as a scalable and modular solution by introducing explicit, token-level hallucination control within the decoding process. By addressing the propagation of errors, quantifying risk, and enabling dynamic correction, Token-Guard aims to significantly enhance the factual consistency and reliability of LLM outputs, building upon and extending concepts from multi-level control (Hiriyanna & Zhao, 2025) and self-reflective decoding (Ji et al., 2023b).

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel decoding-based method, named Token-Guard, that effectively controls and mitigates hallucinations in Large Language Model-generated content at a granular, token-level. The motivation stems from the recognition that while existing hallucination mitigation strategies like RAG and RLHF are powerful, they often incur significant resource costs through external retrieval or extensive fine-tuning. Lighter decoding-based methods, while more efficient, currently lack explicit and robust control mechanisms, allowing subtle errors to infiltrate and propagate throughout the generated sequence.

Specifically, Token-Guard is motivated by and aims to address three major challenges inherent in current decoding methods:
1. **Limited Token-Level Hallucination Checking:** Existing methods typically do not implement explicit token-level validation. This means that a hallucinated token generated early in a sequence can easily propagate its error through subsequent steps, leading to compounded inaccuracies in the final output. Token-Guard seeks to introduce an internal verification mechanism at each reasoning step to detect and suppress these individual erroneous tokens before they can cause cascading failures.
2. **Lack of Explicit Hallucination Risk Quantification:** Current probabilistic scoring in the encoded space often fails to explicitly quantify the hallucination risk associated with candidate tokens or fragments. This deficiency leads to less informed token selection, resulting in unstable or factually inconsistent reasoning paths. The motivation here is to provide an explicit, interpretable hallucination risk score that can directly guide more reliable fragment selection.
3. **Limited Dynamic Correction Capability:** Many decoding methods support only a single pass or offer very limited iterative refinement. This constrains their ability to dynamically correct errors, leading to inconsistent outputs and potentially wasted computational effort on unproductive reasoning paths. Token-Guard is designed to incorporate iterative pruning and regeneration strategies, allowing for dynamic error correction and efficient resource allocation.

By tackling these specific challenges, Token-Guard aims to provide a scalable, modular, and more reliable solution for producing accurate LLM outputs in knowledge-intensive applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Token-Guard employs a sophisticated three-stage self-checking decoding framework to achieve token-level hallucination control: Prompt Self-Checking, Token-Level Hallucination Self-Checking, Candidate Segment Representation and Local Refinement, and Global Iteration and Correction.

#### 3.1. Prompt Self-Checking

Before the main decoding pipeline, Token-Guard uses a unified prompting strategy. This involves a general template that enforces reliance on input context and a standardized output format. Crucially, it incorporates domain-specific constraints (e.g., "Yes/No/Maybe" for PubMedQA, financial reporting formats for FinanceBench) to guide the LLM's initial generation toward desired answer types and factual adherence. This initial stage ensures that the model's output intent is aligned with the task requirements, laying a foundation for controlled generation.

#### 3.2. Token-Level Hallucination Self-Checking

This is the first core stage of granular control. At each generation step $t$, Token-Guard constructs a latent environment $S_t$ to store semantic representations and contextual states of previously accepted tokens.
- **Latent Token Environment Initialization:** For $t=1$, the mean hidden state of the input context tokens ($h_x$) serves as an initial anchor, ensuring coherence from the very first token.
- **Candidate Tokens and Hidden States:** The LLM generates a set of candidate tokens $A_t$, each with its corresponding penultimate-layer hidden state ($h^{(i)}_t$).
- **Hybrid Token-Level Hallucination Scoring:** For each candidate token, a composite score ($F^{\text{token}}_{\text{halu}}$) is calculated. This score is a weighted combination (with $\lambda=0.6$) of two components:
 1. **Semantic Consistency:** Cosine similarity between the candidate token's hidden state ($h^{(i)}_t$) and the mean hidden state of previously accepted tokens ($\bar{h}_{<t}$). High similarity indicates consistency with the ongoing context.
 2. **Token Probability:** The standard conditional probability ($P(a^{(i)}_t | a_{<t}, x)$) assigned by the LLM. This balances factual coherence with the model's preferred generation likelihood.
- **Candidate Token Selection and Environment Update:** Only tokens exceeding a predefined threshold ($\tau_{\text{token}}=0.4$) are selected and appended to the current segment. This mechanism effectively prunes low-confidence, potentially hallucinated tokens early. Memory usage is optimized by maintaining only a running average of hidden states, independent of total generation length.

#### 3.3. Candidate Segment Representation and Local Refinement

Once a sequence of self-checked tokens forms a candidate segment $C_k$, this stage further evaluates and refines it.
- **Segment Representation:** A weighted average of its constituent token hidden states ($H_k$) is computed, where weights are derived from token-level hallucination scores via a softmax function, prioritizing more reliable tokens.
- **Segment-Level Evaluation:** Each segment is assessed based on three criteria:
 1. **Weighted Token-Level Hallucination Score:** An aggregate of the individual token scores within the segment.
 2. **Local Consistency:** Measures the semantic smoothness between adjacent tokens in the segment; abrupt changes may signal hallucinations.
 3. **Global Alignment:** Cosine similarity between the segment representation ($H_k$) and the overall input context representation ($H_x$), ensuring relevance.
- **Segment-Level Hallucination Score:** These three components are combined into a weighted sum ($F^{\text{seg}}_{\text{halu}}$) with coefficients $\alpha=0.5, \beta=0.3, \gamma=0.2$.
- **Acceptance, Refinement, or Discard:** Segments scoring above a high threshold ($\tau^{\text{high}}_{\text{seg}}=0.75$) are accepted. Those between a low ($\tau^{\text{low}}_{\text{seg}}=0.55$) and high threshold undergo local refinement. Segments below the low threshold are discarded.
- **Local Refinement:** For segments requiring refinement, the lowest-scoring token is identified. A local window around this token is regenerated by the LLM, conditioned on the surrounding context and the segment's representation ($H_k$). This process is iterative (up to $N_{\max}=3$ steps) and aims to correct local errors without regenerating the entire segment. Memory is managed efficiently by buffering only segment-level hidden states during processing.

#### 3.4. Global Iteration and Correction

The final stage focuses on ensuring global consistency and factual accuracy across entire reasoning chains composed of reliable segments.
- **Reasoning Chain Assembly and Clustering:** Valid segments are grouped into reasoning chains ($R$). To explore diverse but coherent paths, segments are clustered (e.g., via TF-IDF and KMeans) and candidate chains are generated from cluster centroids.
- **Chain Evaluation:** Two key metrics are computed for each chain:
 1. **Factual Consistency ($F_{\text{fact}}(R)$):** A weighted average of segment-level hallucination scores, emphasizing segments with higher token confidence and evidence alignment.
 2. **Logical Coherence ($F_{\text{logic}}(R)$):** Measures the semantic continuity between adjacent segments using cosine similarity of their representations, adjusted by a contextual similarity factor derived from input embeddings.
- **Global Score and Iterative Refinement:** These two metrics are combined using a soft minimum to produce a global score ($F_{\text{global}}(R)$). If this score falls below a threshold ($\tau_{\text{global}}=0.7$), the system triggers a global iteration. This involves dynamically adjusting segment thresholds and potentially re-applying the segment-level refinement logic. This process continues up to $M_{\max}=2$ iterations. If consistency is not achieved, the system outputs "cannot answer." Memory usage remains low by operating on compact segment vectors.

The combined multi-stage approach, detailed in Algorithm 1, ensures that hallucinations are addressed at multiple levels of granularity—from individual tokens to entire reasoning paths—leading to robust and reliable LLM outputs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

Token-Guard's performance was rigorously evaluated across six standard HALU datasets (FinanceBench, DROP_history, DROP_nfl, CovidQA, PubMedQA, HaluEval, RAGTruth), using Exact Match (EM), F1, and BLEU scores, and two backbone LLMs (Meta-Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct and Qwen3-8B).

#### 4.1. Overall Performance (RQ1)

Token-Guard consistently outperformed all conventional decoding-based baselines (BaseModel, Guided Decoding, Predictive Decoding, Chain-of-Thoughts, Tree-of-Thought). For Meta-Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, it achieved an average EM/F1 of 0.29/51.03, and for Qwen3-8B, 0.35/53.98. This represents a substantial relative improvement of up to 16.3% over the strongest baseline, demonstrating its effectiveness in hallucination mitigation and generation accuracy. The degree of improvement varied by task; larger gains were observed in multi-step reasoning tasks like DROP_nfl, while knowledge-intensive tasks saw smaller gains, highlighting that Token-Guard primarily controls decoding-induced hallucinations but cannot entirely compensate for missing domain knowledge in the backbone LLM.

#### 4.2. Ablation Study (RQ2)

An ablation study confirmed the critical contribution of each of Token-Guard's core components: prompt initialization (P), token-level scoring (T), segment-level scoring (S), and global iteration (G). Removing any component led to performance degradation. Token-level scoring had the most significant impact on EM and F1 scores, underscoring its crucial role in guiding early-stage token generation and preventing error propagation. Prompt initialization and segment-level scoring contributed to overall stability, while global iteration notably improved BLEU scores, indicating enhanced linguistic fluency and coherence.

#### 4.3. Factual Precision (RQ3)

Token-Guard exhibited superior factual precision. It consistently outperformed other token-level decoding methods (Fixed-Size Filtering, Beam Search, Only Probability) in token-level F1 scores across multiple datasets. The analysis also showed a strong positive correlation between token-level accuracy and the correctness of the final answer, validating the effectiveness of its token-level self-correction mechanism in improving overall task performance.

#### 4.4. Relevance (RQ4)

In terms of relevance, Token-Guard significantly surpassed Self-Adaptive Sampling (SAS) and LSTM-based decoders in segment-level F1 scores, demonstrating its superior ability to identify and preserve relevant segments while suppressing irrelevant fragments during decoding. Furthermore, it consistently achieved the highest BLEU scores across all six datasets (e.g., 63.19 on DROP_history and 75.13 on HaluEval), confirming its robustness in maintaining output relevance and fluency.

#### 4.5. Logical Consistency and Dynamic Resource Management (RQ5)

Qualitative evaluations showed Token-Guard's clear gains in Factual Precision, Logical Coherence, and Relevance compared to baselines. Its multi-stage filtering process effectively preserved factual correctness and stabilized reasoning, even in ambiguous contexts. While slightly less concise, this reflected a prioritization of accuracy and completeness. A case study on DROP_nfl illustrated Token-Guard's ability to correctly answer a numerical reasoning query through dynamic local regeneration, where other baselines either reported "insufficient information" or provided incorrect values.

#### 4.6. Time and Memory Consumption (RQ6)

Token-Guard demonstrated competitive computational efficiency. Its dynamic iteration strategy allowed for efficient computation on tasks like PubMedQA and HaluEval, while allocating more resources for challenging, complex datasets like CovidQA and RAGTruth. Despite sometimes requiring higher output token budgets, Token-Guard maintained competitive or superior throughput, balancing iterative refinement with efficient decoding. In terms of memory, Token-Guard's multi-stage approach managed states efficiently: token-level buffering (1.2–9.4%), brief segment-level peaks (21.6–19.6%), and stable global-level memory (10.3–19.0%). Its peak and average memory usage remained competitive with baselines.

#### 4.7. Portability Across Backbone Models (RQ7)

Token-Guard showed strong portability, achieving consistent F1 and BLEU gains on both smaller (Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct) and larger (Llama-3.2-13B-Instruct) backbone models. This indicates that the method scales favorably with increased backbone capacity without requiring additional tuning. Notably, Predictive Decoding, a baseline, triggered out-of-memory errors on the 13B model, highlighting Token-Guard's comparative efficiency. While it performed slightly lower on RAGTruth with the 3B model due to the dataset's long-context structure (challenging for smaller models), the 13B model largely mitigated this, confirming its general effectiveness across diverse model scales.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Token-Guard represents a significant advancement in the field of Large Language Model hallucination control, particularly for decoding-based methods. Its primary significance lies in introducing a novel, multi-stage self-checking framework that enables explicit, token-level control over factual consistency and logical coherence. This granular control addresses critical shortcomings of existing decoding strategies, which often allow subtle errors to propagate undetected.

The potential impact of Token-Guard is substantial:
- **Enhanced LLM Reliability:** By substantially reducing hallucinations and improving generation accuracy, Token-Guard makes LLMs more trustworthy for high-stakes applications in domains like finance, healthcare, and legal analysis, where factual precision is paramount.
- **Improved Efficiency and Scalability:** Unlike resource-intensive RAG or RLHF methods, Token-Guard offers a lighter, decoding-based solution that is competitive in terms of computational overhead and memory usage. This allows for more efficient deployment and integration with existing LLMs without requiring extensive external knowledge bases or retraining. Its proven portability across different model scales further enhances its practical applicability.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The explicit quantification of hallucination risk and the dynamic, iterative correction mechanisms provide a robust foundation for future research in fine-grained control over LLM generation. This could inspire new directions in self-correction, adversarial training, and uncertainty estimation within LLMs.
- **Democratization of Hallucination Control:** By offering a modular and scalable solution, Token-Guard could democratize access to advanced hallucination control techniques, enabling a broader range of developers and researchers to build more reliable LLM-powered applications.

Despite its strengths, the paper also acknowledges limitations, such as potential computational overhead for very long passages, its inability to supplement missing knowledge (relying on the backbone LLM's inherent knowledge), and reduced effectiveness with highly fragmented or noisy inputs. Future work will focus on optimizing for these challenges, potentially integrating with external knowledge sources more dynamically, improving robustness to diverse input qualities, and extending the framework to multi-modal inputs. The public availability of the code is a positive step, facilitating reproducibility and further exploration of this promising methodology.
