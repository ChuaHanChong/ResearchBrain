# FFHFlow: Diverse and Uncertainty-Aware Dexterous Grasp Generation via Flow Variational Inference

> [!summary] Summary
> FFHFlow introduces a flow-based variational inference framework for dexterous multi-finger grasp generation from partial point clouds. The method synthesizes diverse, high-quality grasps while explicitly quantifying perceptual uncertainty, achieving a 65.0% success rate in confined spaces compared to cVAE's 10.0% and offering significantly faster inference (130ms) than diffusion models (1610ms).

## Problem

- Existing generative models for dexterous grasping often suffer from mode collapse, simplistic prior distributions, and limited diversity, leading to poor performance in cluttered environments.
- Diffusion models, while capable of high-quality generation, typically incur substantial runtime costs due to iterative denoising, making them less practical for real-time robotic applications.
- Many approaches for grasping from partial observations do not explicitly quantify perceptual uncertainty, resulting in less robust grasps and reliance on computationally intensive shape completion modules.

## Method

- **FFHFlow-lvm**, a ==Deep Latent Variable Model (DLVM)==, utilizes ==Normalizing Flows (NFs)== for both the conditional grasp distribution (==Grasp Flow==) and an input-dependent prior distribution (==Prior Flow==) to model complex, multi-modal grasp distributions.
- The framework explicitly quantifies perceptual uncertainty, distinguishing between 'view uncertainty' (due to partial observation) and 'object uncertainty' (due to novel object shapes) by leveraging the exact likelihoods of the NFs.
- An ==uncertainty-aware grasp evaluation== strategy is introduced, combining a discriminative grasp evaluator with view uncertainty to penalize grasps directed towards uncertain or unobserved object regions, thereby enhancing robustness.

## Results

- FFHFlow-lvm achieved superior grasp success rates in simulation (**94.6%** on similar objects, **52.7%** on novel objects) and demonstrated strong generalization in real-world unconfined grasping (**77.5%** average success rate).
- The method showed substantial performance improvements in complex real-world scenarios, with a **65.0%** success rate in confined spaces (compared to cVAE's **10.0%**) and **76.0%** in cluttered scenes.
- An efficient sampling runtime of **130ms** was achieved, making it approximately **10 times** faster than diffusion-based models (**1610ms**) while delivering higher grasp success rates and improved diversity.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Leveraging Normalizing Flows within a DLVM structure effectively mitigates mode collapse and models the complex, multi-modal distribution of dexterous grasps, leading to improved diversity and quality.
> - The exact likelihoods from flow-based generative models provide an efficient and direct mechanism for quantifying both view and object uncertainty, enabling crucial introspective capabilities for robotic systems.
> - Integrating quantified perceptual uncertainty into the grasp evaluation process significantly enhances grasp reliability and success rates, particularly in challenging real-world scenarios with partial observations and environmental constraints.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

The field of robotic manipulation, particularly dexterous grasping with multi-fingered hands, presents challenges due to the high dimensionality of hand configuration spaces, variability in object shapes, and perceptual uncertainty stemming from partial observations. Current learning-based approaches for grasp synthesis, utilizing generative models such as Conditional Variational Autoencoders (cVAEs), Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), and diffusion models, have demonstrated progress. However, these methods often encounter limitations such as mode collapse, restrictive priors on grasp distributions, or computational inefficiencies.

Specifically, cVAEs struggle to model complex, multi-modal grasp distributions due to mode collapse and the use of simplistic prior distributions (e.g., isotropic Gaussian). This can lead to a lack of diversity in generated grasps, which is a drawback in cluttered or confined operational environments where varied grasp poses are necessary to avoid collisions. Diffusion models, while potentially mitigating mode collapse, typically incur significant runtime costs due to their iterative denoising processes, hindering real-time application. Furthermore, a limited number of previous studies have explicitly addressed the quantification of perceptual uncertainty arising from partial object observations, often relying on computationally intensive shape completion modules, which further exacerbates inference latency.

This work addresses these limitations by introducing a flow-based variational inference framework. It positions itself as an advancement over existing generative models by aiming to provide both diverse and high-quality grasp synthesis while explicitly incorporating uncertainty awareness without relying on slow shape completion. By leveraging normalizing flows within a Deep Latent Variable Model (DLVM) framework, the proposed method seeks to model intricate grasp distributions more effectively and to quantify shape uncertainty, offering a more robust and efficient solution for dexterous grasping from partial observations.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a generative model capable of synthesizing diverse and uncertainty-aware multi-finger grasps for dexterous robotic hands, given only partial point cloud observations of objects. This overarching goal is driven by several specific motivations and aims:

1. **Overcome limitations of existing generative models:** Prior generative methods, particularly cVAEs, are frequently hampered by mode collapse and the use of overly simple, input-independent prior distributions. These issues restrict their ability to capture the inherently complex and multi-modal distribution of dexterous grasps, leading to a lack of diversity. The authors aim to mitigate these problems by introducing a more expressive generative framework.
2. **Enhance grasp diversity for complex environments:** The limited diversity of grasps generated by previous methods reduces the manipulability of robots, especially in common cluttered or confined spaces. A key motivation is to enable the generation of a wider range of feasible grasps that can effectively navigate and interact with objects in such challenging environments, thereby reducing collisions and improving operational success.
3. **Achieve runtime efficiency:** While recent diffusion models show promise in mitigating mode collapse, their iterative denoising processes can lead to substantial inference times, making them less suitable for real-time robotic applications. The authors aim to develop a method that provides diverse, high-quality grasps with efficient runtime sampling.
4. **Incorporate explicit perceptual uncertainty quantification:** A critical challenge in grasping from partial observations is the inherent uncertainty about the complete object shape. Few prior studies have provided introspective capabilities to quantify this perceptual uncertainty. The research is motivated by the need to explicitly quantify and leverage this uncertainty, differentiating between "view uncertainty" (due to incomplete observations) and "object uncertainty" (due to novel or out-of-distribution (OOD) object shapes). This quantification is intended to inform more robust grasp synthesis and evaluation.
5. **Develop an uncertainty-aware grasp evaluation strategy:** Building upon the ability to quantify perceptual uncertainty, the authors aim to integrate this information into the grasp evaluation process. The goal is to formulate a strategy that prioritizes grasps robust to shape ambiguity, potentially by penalizing grasps directed towards uncertain or unobserved object regions, thereby enhancing reliability and success rates.
6. **Generalize to novel objects and real-world scenarios:** The method is motivated by the need for robust performance not only on known objects but also on novel or OOD objects, which often present distinct geometries. Furthermore, demonstrating practical value in complex real-world setups (cluttered and confined spaces) is a crucial objective.

By addressing these points, the research aims to contribute to more advanced, human-like robotic manipulation capabilities.

### 3. Methodology and approach

The FFHFlow framework is designed to generate diverse and uncertainty-aware dexterous grasps from partial point cloud observations. The core of the methodology lies in leveraging Normalizing Flows (NFs) within a Deep Latent Variable Model (DLVM) structure, specifically termed FFHFlow-lvm, to address the limitations of prior generative methods.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The objective is to learn a probabilistic model $p_\theta(g|x)$ that approximates the ground truth conditional distribution $p_*(g|x)$, where $g$ represents a grasp configuration (15-DOF hand joint configuration and 6D palm pose) and $x$ is a partial object point cloud. The model is trained on a dataset of object point clouds and corresponding feasible grasps. A crucial aspect is to exploit the model's log-likelihoods to represent perceptual uncertainty.

#### 3.2. Flow-based Grasp Sampler: FFHFlow-cnf (Initial Exploration)

Initially, the authors explored a direct application of Conditional Normalizing Flows (cNF) to model $p_\theta(g|x)$, conditioning the flow transformation and base distribution on features extracted from the object point cloud. Point clouds are encoded using a Basis Point Set (BPS) for fixed-length feature representation. While this approach showed improvements over cVAE in diversity and accuracy, it exhibited limited generalization. The authors attributed this to the insufficient expressivity of latent features when directly modeling complex grasp-point cloud relationships without a dedicated latent variable.

#### 3.3. Flow-based Variational Grasp Sampler: FFHFlow-lvm

To enhance expressivity, FFHFlow-lvm introduces a latent variable $z$ into the FFHFlow-cnf structure, forming a DLVM. This approach aims to overcome the over-regularization from simplistic priors and latent feature collapse often observed in cVAE-based methods.

- **Learning Grasp Distribution via DLVMs:** The conditional likelihood $p_\theta(g|x)$ is factorized as $p_\theta(g|x) = \int p_\theta(g|x,z)p_\theta(z|x)dz$. Since this integral is intractable, a tractable variational lower bound is derived for optimization using Stochastic Gradient Variational Bayes (SGVB):
 $\log p_\theta(g|x) \ge E_{q_\phi(z|x,g)}[\log p_\theta(g|x,z)] - \beta KL(q_\phi(z|x,g)||p_\theta(z|x))$.
 Here, $\beta$ is a hyperparameter balancing the latent information and prior regularization.

- **Grasp Flow $p_\theta(g|x,z)$:** To address mode collapse and the restrictive nature of Gaussian likelihoods in cVAEs, FFHFlow-lvm uses a cNF for the likelihood function $p_\theta(g|x,z)$. This allows the model to learn a more general, non-Gaussian likelihood function without underlying distributional assumptions, and the diffeomorphic nature of NFs helps alleviate the unboundedness issue. The base distribution $p_{u|z}$ is conditional on $z$.

- **Prior Flow $p_\theta(z|x)$:** To overcome the limitations of simplistic, input-independent priors (e.g., isotropic Gaussian) that restrict latent representation quality, a second cNF is employed for an input-dependent prior distribution, $p_\theta(z|x)$. This point cloud-dependent prior is learnable and flexible, crucial for generating diverse grasps at inference time.

- **Variational Network $q_\phi(z|x,g)$:** This network approximates the intractable true posterior $p_\theta(z|x,g)$ for amortized variational inference. It is implemented as a deep neural network predicting the parameters (mean and diagonal covariance) of a factorized Gaussian distribution over the latent space. This network is used only during training.

- **Grasp Generation and Shape-Aware Introspection:** During inference, grasps $g^*$ are generated by ancestral sampling: $z^* \sim p_\theta(z|x^*)$ followed by $g^* \sim p_\theta(g|z^*)$. The exact likelihoods from Grasp Flow ($p_\theta(g|z^*)$) and Prior Flow ($p_\theta(z|x^*)$) are leveraged for shape-aware introspection.
 - **View Uncertainty:** Grasp Flow likelihoods quantify inverse view uncertainty. Higher values indicate grasps towards visible object parts, while lower values suggest grasps towards unseen or incomplete regions.
 - **Object Uncertainty:** Prior Flow likelihoods represent inverse object uncertainty. Lower likelihoods are assigned to novel (OOD) object shapes, indicating higher uncertainty, which can be used to detect objects with unfamiliar geometry.

#### 3.4. Uncertainty-Aware Grasp Evaluation

To enhance grasp reliability, a discriminative grasp evaluator $f_\psi(g,x)$ is trained to score grasp quality. This evaluator is combined with the view uncertainty quantified by Grasp Flow likelihoods to formulate an uncertainty-aware ranking strategy:
$\epsilon f_\psi(g^*, x^*) + (1-\epsilon) \log p_\theta(g^*|z^*)$
where $\epsilon \in$ balances the influence of grasp quality and view uncertainty, penalizing grasps approaching uncertain or partially observed sides of the object to avoid collisions.

#### 3.5. Experimental Setup

Experiments were conducted in both Gazebo simulation and on a real robot (Panda arm with DLR-HIT II hand).
- **Data:** Simulated data was generated using a heuristic grasp planner. The training set consisted of 77 objects from the KIT dataset. Testing used 12 "similar" (in-distribution) KIT objects and 9 "novel" (OOD) YCB objects. Real-world evaluation used 12 unknown YCB objects.
- **Input Processing:** Partial point clouds are segmented and then pre-processed with BPS encoding before feature extraction by fully-connected residual blocks.
- **Network Architecture:** Grasp Flow and Prior Flow are implemented as 8-layered conditional Glow models. The variational network and grasp evaluator use networks with multiple fully-connected residual blocks.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** Success rate (lifting object 20cm without slippage), runtime, and Coverage (for diversity). Collision detection used Flexible Collision Library (FCL), and grasp stability was assessed via Gazebo simulation.

### 4. Main findings and results

The experiments were designed to evaluate FFHFlow-lvm's performance in terms of grasp diversity, quality, efficiency, uncertainty awareness, and generalization across simulation and real-world scenarios.

#### 4.1. Grasp Diversity, Quality, and Efficiency (Q1, Q2)

- **Simulation Performance (Table 1a):** FFHFlow-lvm demonstrated superior performance in simulation. It achieved a 94.6% success rate on "similar" objects and 52.7% on "novel" objects, outperforming state-of-the-art baselines including cVAE (84.6%, 52.4%), GAN (86.0%, 49.4%), and diffusion models (88.2%, 51.7%).
- **Runtime Efficiency:** FFHFlow-lvm achieved a sampling runtime of 130ms. This is significantly faster than the diffusion-based approach (1610ms), while still being competitive with cVAE (30ms) and GAN (30ms). The trade-off between speed and performance was highlighted, with FFHFlow-lvm being about 10x faster than diffusion models while offering higher success rates. An ablation study in the supplementary material (Table 1) also shows a "light" version (FFHFlow-lvm-light) achieving 29.9% coverage and 60ms runtime, demonstrating flexibility in balancing these aspects.
- **Grasp Diversity (Supplementary Table 1 & Figure 14):** FFHFlow-lvm achieved a Coverage score of 30.3% (compared to cVAE's 22.5% and FFHFlow-cnf's 30.0%), indicating improved diversity in generated grasps. Visualizations of grasp palm pose distributions (Supplementary Figure 14) further illustrate FFHFlow-lvm's ability to model multi-modal distributions with higher fidelity compared to cVAE, which often suffers from mode collapse.

#### 4.2. Shape-Aware Introspection and Uncertainty-Aware Grasp Evaluation (Q3)

- **Uncertainty Quantification (Figure 4b, Figure 5b):**
 - **Object Uncertainty:** Prior Flow likelihoods effectively distinguished between in-distribution (ID) and novel/out-of-distribution (OOD) objects. OOD objects consistently yielded lower Prior Flow likelihoods, indicating higher object uncertainty. This capability suggests that the model can identify objects with unfamiliar shapes.
 - **View Uncertainty:** Grasp Flow likelihoods correlated with view uncertainty. Grasps directed towards incomplete or partially observed faces of an object (visible in Figure 5a) received lower likelihoods.
 - **Impact on Collisions and Stability (Figure 5b):** Both Prior Flow and Grasp Flow likelihoods demonstrated a capacity to reduce collisions. Grasp Flow, in particular, showed a more optimal balance between reducing collisions and improving grasp stability compared to the discriminative grasp evaluator alone or Prior Flow.
- **Uncertainty-Aware Grasp Evaluation Strategy (Table 1b):** Integrating Grasp Flow likelihoods into the discriminative grasp evaluator (using an additive coefficient $\epsilon=0.01$) led to the highest success rates: 94.6% for similar objects and 52.7% for novel objects. This indicated that incorporating view uncertainty into the evaluation process facilitated the selection of more robust grasps. The ablation study (Supplementary Table 6) confirmed $\epsilon=0.01$ as the optimal balance point.

#### 4.3. Generalization to Complex Real-World Scenarios (Q4)

- **Unconfined Space (Table 2):** In real-world unconfined grasping, FFHFlow-lvm achieved a 77.5% average success rate, outperforming cVAE (62.5%). This indicated its ability to generalize effectively from simulation to the real robot.
- **Confined Space (Table 2):** FFHFlow-lvm demonstrated substantial performance gains in confined environments (e.g., a two-tier shelf), achieving a 65.0% success rate compared to cVAE's 10.0%. This was attributed to FFHFlow-lvm's ability to generate diverse grasps, which helped mitigate collisions with the environment, unlike cVAE's tendency for biased top grasps.
- **Cluttered Scenes (Table 2):** In cluttered scenes with unknown objects and obstacles, FFHFlow-lvm achieved a higher success rate (76.0%) and clearance rate (75.0%) compared to cVAE (68.2% SR, 50.0% CR). The diverse grasp generation by FFHFlow-lvm enabled better navigation and grasping of occluded objects (Supplementary Figure 7).
- **Influence of Point Cloud Noise (Supplementary Table 10):** The model showed a performance drop with increasing Gaussian noise in point clouds. A linear performance drop was observed between 0mm and 5mm noise, with a dramatic decrease at 10mm. Real-world point cloud noise was estimated to be between 0-1mm, suggesting a 3-4% performance drop due to noise, excluding other sim-to-real gaps.

### 5. Significance and potential impact

The FFHFlow framework represents a notable advancement in dexterous grasp generation for robotic manipulation, particularly for tasks involving partial observations and complex environments.

The **primary significance** lies in its ability to generate diverse and high-quality multi-fingered grasps while explicitly quantifying perceptual uncertainty. By introducing a novel flow-based Deep Latent Variable Model (DLVM), FFHFlow-lvm addresses critical limitations of prior generative approaches:
- **Overcoming Mode Collapse and Rigid Priors:** Unlike cVAEs, which struggle with mode collapse and overly simplistic priors, FFHFlow leverages the expressivity of normalizing flows to model the complex, multi-modal distribution of dexterous grasps more accurately. This leads to a wider variety of generated grasps, which is crucial for adaptability.
- **Improved Efficiency over Diffusion Models:** While diffusion models can generate high-quality samples, their iterative nature often results in slow inference times. FFHFlow achieves comparable or superior performance with significantly faster sampling, making it more suitable for real-time robotic applications.
- **Explicit Uncertainty Quantification:** The method introduces a novel way to quantify shape-aware uncertainty using the exact likelihoods of normalizing flows. This distinction between "view uncertainty" (from partial observation) and "object uncertainty" (from novel shapes) provides valuable introspection into the model's confidence. This is a crucial step towards more robust and intelligent robotic systems that can reason about their own limitations.

The **potential impact** of this work is substantial for several aspects of robotics:

- **Enhanced Robustness and Reliability:** The uncertainty-aware grasp evaluation strategy, which integrates view uncertainty with a discriminative evaluator, allows the robot to prioritize grasps that are less ambiguous or less likely to result in collisions with unseen object parts. This directly contributes to higher grasp success rates and overall task reliability, particularly when operating with noisy or incomplete sensor data.
- **Deployment in Complex Environments:** The demonstrated performance in cluttered and confined real-world scenarios highlights the practical value of FFHFlow. The ability to generate diverse grasps that can navigate tight spaces and grasp partially occluded objects is critical for deploying robots in unstructured human environments (e.g., homes, warehouses, manufacturing lines).
- **Generalization to Novel Objects:** The explicit quantification of object uncertainty enables the robot to detect and potentially adapt to novel objects. While the paper notes limitations in efficient adaptation, the ability to identify "unknown" shapes is a foundational step towards more generalized manipulation capabilities. Robots could use this information to request human intervention, switch to a more conservative grasping strategy, or trigger active exploration.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The framework provides a strong basis for further research in several directions, including addressing the sim-to-real gap more effectively, improving adaptation capabilities for drastically different objects, and exploring the trade-off between runtime and performance in varied applications. The use of normalizing flows as expressive and tractable generative models is likely to inspire further applications in robot learning beyond grasping.

In summary, FFHFlow contributes a robust, efficient, and introspective solution for dexterous grasp generation, pushing the boundaries of what is achievable with learning-based methods in real-world robotic manipulation tasks.
