# JARVIS-VLA: Post-Training Large-Scale Vision Language Models to Play Visual Games with Keyboards and Mouse

> [!summary] Summary
> JARVIS-VLA, a Vision Language Action model, is introduced for playing visual games in Minecraft using keyboards and mouse, leveraging ActVLP, a three-stage post-training paradigm that improves world knowledge and visual grounding before action tuning. This enabled JARVIS-VLA-Qwen2-VL to achieve state-of-the-art performance on over 1,000 diverse atomic tasks in Minecraft, demonstrating a 40% improvement over prior baselines.

## Problem

- Existing VLA models rely heavily on imitation learning, limiting their generalization and robust multi-task decision-making in open-world environments.
- Prior VLA approaches primarily focus on action post-training, neglecting to enhance the foundation model's core world knowledge, visual recognition, and spatial grounding capabilities.
- Acquiring comprehensive world knowledge solely from action-labeled trajectory data is difficult and inefficient, especially given the scarcity of large-scale, high-quality datasets.

## Method

- ==Introduce ActVLP== (Act from Visual Language Post-Training), a novel three-stage paradigm that systematically enhances a VLM's world knowledge, visual recognition, and spatial grounding using non-trajectory data.
- Develop **JARVIS-VLA**, an end-to-end VLA model designed for Minecraft, capable of directly generating actions from textual instructions and visual inputs after ActVLP.
- Construct a comprehensive multimodal dataset incorporating large-scale non-trajectory (textual, visual-language alignment, spatial grounding) and trajectory data for the multi-stage training process.

## Results

- JARVIS-VLA-Qwen2-VL achieved state-of-the-art success rates across the MCU benchmark in Minecraft, outperforming prior baselines by over **40%** on 1,000 diverse atomic tasks.
- ActVLP's multi-stage approach yielded approximately **57%** and **23%** improvements on tasks like 'Smelt iron ingot' and 'Crafting the ladder' respectively, compared to a one-stage co-finetuning baseline.
- The ActVLP paradigm demonstrated robustness across different VLM backbones (Llava-Next, Qwen2-VL), with both models showing more than a **30%** increase in downstream task success rates after post-training.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - ActVLP's multi-stage post-training, which separates vision-language enhancement from action learning, is crucial for building a stronger foundational understanding, leading to substantial performance gains.
> - Non-trajectory vision-language data, particularly spatial grounding tasks, significantly enhances core VLM capabilities and improves downstream decision-making and generalization.
> - Scaling up off-trajectory vision-language datasets shows a linear correlation with downstream task success, indicating a new dimension for optimizing VLA performance beyond just trajectory data.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper positions itself within the rapidly evolving landscape of large language models (LLMs) and vision-language models (VLMs) applied to decision-making and embodied AI. The success of large models like GPT and LLAMA in natural language processing has inspired efforts to extend similar capabilities to interactive, open-world environments.

Historically, approaches like OpenAI’s Video Pre-Training (VPT) for Minecraft have demonstrated the potential of pretraining on massive datasets of human gameplay videos, followed by imitation learning (IL) and reinforcement learning (RL), to achieve impressive feats, such as crafting a diamond pickaxe. However, the authors identify critical limitations in these prior methods:
1. **Reliance on Imitation Learning:** Traditional IL-based pretraining often leads to models that excel at next-action prediction but struggle with robust, multi-task decision-making and generalization to unseen environments or tasks due to the inherent complexity and variability of observations and behaviors.
2. **Neglect of Foundation Model Enhancement:** Existing VLA approaches primarily focus on *action post-training*, assuming that pretrained VLMs possess sufficient generalization capabilities. They often overlook the opportunity to enhance the foundation model's underlying world knowledge, visual recognition, and spatial grounding *before* action-specific tuning.
3. **Challenges in World Knowledge Acquisition:** Learning comprehensive world knowledge solely from action-labeled trajectory data is inherently difficult and often inefficient. Furthermore, the scarcity of large-scale, high-quality action-labeled datasets limits the scalability of such approaches for expansive models.

JARVIS-VLA directly addresses these gaps by introducing ActVLP (Act from Visual Language Post-Training), a novel training paradigm that proactively enhances the VLM's core understanding of the environment. This paradigm shifts the focus from merely teaching a VLM to *act* (via imitation learning on trajectories) to first teaching it to *understand* the world it operates in (via self-supervised learning on environment-specific visual and linguistic tasks). By strengthening the VLM's internal representations of world knowledge, visual entities, and spatial relationships *prior* to action-specific tuning, ActVLP aims to overcome the generalization limitations of previous VLA models.

This work also differentiates itself from hierarchical VLM-based agents in Minecraft (e.g., STEVE-EYE, MineDreamer, Voyager, JARVIS-1, OmniJARVIS) which typically use VLMs for high-level planning or reasoning while relying on separate, specialized policies for low-level action grounding. JARVIS-VLA, in contrast, aims to develop an *end-to-end VLA model* that directly generates actions from textual instructions and visual inputs, thus integrating the enhanced VLM capabilities into the action generation pipeline itself. It represents a significant step towards creating more autonomous and capable agents in complex open-world environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The motivation for this research stems from the recognized shortcomings of existing Vision Language Action (VLA) models, particularly their struggle to achieve flexible, generalizable, and robust decision-making in open-world environments like Minecraft. The authors observe that while large-scale pretraining has been successful in NLP and vision, its application to decision-making has been limited by the heavy reliance on imitation learning and the neglect of foundational model enhancement.

The key objectives of this paper are therefore:

1. **Introduce ActVLP (Act from Visual Language Post-Training):** To propose and validate a novel, multi-stage training paradigm that systematically enhances a VLM's capabilities in world knowledge, visual recognition, and spatial grounding *before* it undergoes action-specific tuning. This is intended to address the limitations of direct action post-training in previous VLA models.
2. **Develop JARVIS-VLA:** To create the first VLA model specifically designed for Minecraft that can robustly follow human instructions across a broad spectrum of over 1,000 diverse atomic tasks, including crafting, smelting, cooking, mining, and killing, demonstrating state-of-the-art performance.
3. **Improve Foundational Capabilities:** To explicitly enhance the VLM's understanding of the Minecraft world through targeted post-training on non-trajectory visual and linguistic tasks, thereby improving its ability to integrate language understanding with visual perception for decision-making.
4. **Investigate Scaling Laws:** To explore whether VLAs exhibit scaling laws, particularly how the scaling of non-trajectory vision-language tasks during post-training impacts downstream task performance, a factor previously overlooked.
5. **Contribute to Open Science:** To open-source the code, models, and datasets associated with JARVIS-VLA and ActVLP to foster further research and accelerate progress in the field of embodied AI and VLA models.

In essence, the overarching motivation is to move beyond mere action prediction and enable VLA models to truly *understand* their environment, leading to more intelligent, adaptable, and generalizable embodied agents.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of JARVIS-VLA's methodology lies in its novel **ActVLP (Act from Visual Language Post-Training)** paradigm, a three-stage structured post-training process designed to progressively refine a Vision Language Model (VLM) for decision-making tasks.

#### 3.1. Model Architecture (JARVIS-VLA)

JARVIS-VLA adopts an architecture similar to existing VLMs like Llava, but with key modifications for embodied control in partially observable environments:
- **Components:** It comprises a Visual Encoder (Vision Transformer, ViT), an Image Projection Module (a lightweight MLP), and Language Model Transformers (autoregressive LLM like Llava-Next or Qwen2-VL).
- **Temporal Context:** To handle partially observable environments and multi-step reasoning, it incorporates a history of observation images within the prompt, making it a non-Markovian architecture.
- **Action Decoder:** A specialized module decodes both discrete (e.g., keyboard presses) and continuous (e.g., mouse movements) actions. Discrete actions are consolidated into unified categories for efficiency, while continuous actions are discretized into bins and mapped to discrete tokens.
- **Action Tokenization:** Instead of retraining the VLM's tokenizer, JARVIS-VLA repurposes 51 of the least-used tokens from the language tokenizer's vocabulary to represent actions (22 for mouse, 29 for keyboard). This maintains model generalizability and broad compatibility.

#### 3.2. Training Pipeline (ActVLP)

The ActVLP paradigm consists of three distinct stages:

- **Stage I: World Knowledge Post-Training (Language Models):**
 - **Objective:** Enhance the VLM's textual understanding of world knowledge specific to the target environment (Minecraft).
 - **Process:** The language transformer of the VLM is refined using large-scale *textual datasets*. The vision-related components (ViT, vision adapter) are *frozen* during this stage.
 - **Learning:** Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) with a next-token prediction objective ($L_{SFT}$).

- **Stage II: Visual Knowledge and Spatial Grounding Post-Training (Vision Encoder and Language Models):**
 - **Objective:** Improve vision-language alignment, visual recognition, and spatial grounding capabilities, enabling the model to integrate world knowledge with visual perception.
 - **Process:** The *entire VLM is unfrozen* and fine-tuned using *multimodal datasets* including captioning, Visual Question Answering (VQA), and spatial grounding tasks. These datasets contain images and relevant linguistic annotations.
 - **Learning:** Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) with a next-token prediction objective ($L_{SFT}$).

- **Stage III: Action Post-Training for Interaction (Action Learning):**
 - **Objective:** Enable the VLM to transition from a passive observer to an active agent, generating appropriate actions based on instructions and observations.
 - **Process:** The VLM is fine-tuned on *trajectory data* (textual instructions, visual observations, and expert action sequences). The language tokenizer is modified to include action tokens, and the *language transformer undergoes full parameter fine-tuning*, while vision-related modules *remain frozen*. Action chunking is employed for efficiency and temporal coherence.
 - **Learning:** Imitation Learning (IL) objective, maximizing the likelihood of expert actions ($L_{IL}$).

#### 3.3. Datasets

A comprehensive multimodal dataset was constructed to support the ActVLP pipeline:
- **Non-Trajectory Datasets (for Stages I & II):**
 - **World Knowledge Comprehension:** ~277K text-only question-answering entries generated using GPT-3.5-turbo, sourced from Wikipedia and DigMinecraft.
 - **Visual-Language Alignment:** 35K keyframes from gameplay videos, contractor screenshots, and internet resources. Captions and VQA pairs generated by advanced VLMs (GPT-4o, Claude 3.5 Sonnet, Molmo) and validated by Llama-3.1-70B.
 - **Spatial Grounding:** Over 404K data points for object localization. For 3D environments, objects are traced using Backward Trajectory Relabeling and SAM. For 2D GUI, item positions are determined from inventory messages and augmented via cropping.
- **Imitation Learning Trajectory Dataset (for Stage III):**
 - Over 7.4 million frames of Minecraft gameplay: includes human play (OpenAI contractor dataset/VPT), rollouts from existing agents (VPT, JARVIS-1), and 6.4 million synthesized expert data entries for structured GUI tasks (crafting, smelting).
 - This data is partitioned, with ~100 random trajectories per MCU benchmark task used for targeted fine-tuning, and ~10 billion tokens for action post-training.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

- **Environment:** Minecraft 1.16.5, with action and visual observation spaces aligned with human players. High image resolution (644x364) is used.
- **Benchmarks:** MCU Benchmark (Mine Blocks, Kill Entities, Craft Items, Smelt Items) for instruction-following; custom vision-language tasks (QA, spatial grounding) for VLM understanding.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** Success rate for instruction-following; LLM-as-judge (GPT-4o) and rule-based scoring for vision-language tasks.
- **Training Infrastructure:** 32 NVIDIA A800-80G GPUs, AdamW optimizer, cosine learning rate schedule, bfloat16 precision, DeepSpeed ZeRO-1.
- **Inference Infrastructure:** Single NVIDIA RTX 3090 (or vLLM on 4 A800 GPUs) for efficient deployment.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several significant findings, demonstrating the efficacy of the ActVLP paradigm and the performance of JARVIS-VLA:

1. **State-of-the-Art Performance in Minecraft (Q1):** JARVIS-VLA-Qwen2-VL consistently achieved the highest success rates across all four categories of the MCU benchmark (Mine Blocks, Kill Entities, Craft Items, Smelt Items), significantly outperforming previous state-of-the-art open-world agents such as VPT-BC/RL, STEVE-1, GROOT, and MineDreamer. The abstract claims a 40% improvement over the best agent baseline on diverse atomic tasks. Notably, tasks involving 2D GUI manipulation like "Craft Items" and "Smelt Items" saw dramatic improvements, with success rates more than doubling compared to baselines, attributed to the ViT in VLM and high-resolution processing enhanced by ActVLP. Even raw Qwen2-VL, when fine-tuned, showed strong performance, underscoring the benefit of capable VLM backbones.

2. **Impact of Vision Language Post-Training (ActVLP Paradigm) (Q2):** An ablation study directly comparing ActVLP’s multi-stage post-training with a one-stage co-finetuning approach (combining non-trajectory and trajectory data) revealed substantial performance gains for ActVLP. For instance, JARVIS-VLA-Qwen2-VL showed approximately 57% and 23% improvements on "Smelt iron ingot" and "Crafting the ladder" tasks, respectively, over the one-stage baseline. This confirms that separating visual-language post-training from action learning is crucial, as it builds a stronger foundational understanding before specialized action tuning.

3. **Contribution of Non-Trajectory Datasets (Q3, Ablation):** Further ablation studies on the specific types of non-trajectory datasets demonstrated that post-training with these tasks significantly enhances core VLM capabilities. Models enhanced with **spatial grounding** datasets showed the *most substantial improvement* in downstream decision-making tasks, followed by knowledge-based tasks. This highlights the critical role of environment-specific visual and linguistic knowledge in improving action prediction and generalization, suggesting that VLA models benefit from the same knowledge types that empower modular LLM-based agent pipelines.

4. **Evidence of Scaling Laws (Q3, Scaling Experiments):** The research investigated scaling behaviors in VLAs:
 - **Downstream Imitation Learning Trajectories:** Increasing the number of downstream imitation learning trajectories improved VLA task success rates, but only when the evaluation loss dropped below a certain threshold (e.g., 0.30). This suggests a data sufficiency requirement for effective fine-tuning.
 - **Non-Trajectory Vision-Language Tasks:** A linear correlation was observed between the evaluation loss during post-training on non-trajectory vision-language tasks and the downstream task success rate. Lower post-training loss (implying more extensive/effective non-trajectory data utilization) directly translated to higher success rates in downstream tasks. This finding indicates that scaling up off-trajectory vision-language datasets can significantly enhance VLA performance, a scaling dimension previously overlooked.

5. **Robustness Across VLM Backbones (Q4):** ActVLP demonstrated robustness across different VLM backbones, including Llava-Next and Qwen2-VL. Both models showed more than a 30% increase in downstream task success rates after undergoing ActVLP post-training, indicating the paradigm's broad applicability and independence from specific base VLM architectures.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The JARVIS-VLA project, driven by the ActVLP paradigm, marks a significant advancement in the field of Vision Language Action (VLA) models and embodied AI, with several key implications:

1. **A New Paradigm for VLA Training:** The introduction of ActVLP fundamentally shifts the approach to VLA model training. By systematically enhancing the VLM's understanding of world knowledge, visual recognition, and spatial grounding *prior* to action-specific imitation learning, it provides a more robust and principled foundation. This pre-action-tuning enhancement addresses a critical limitation of previous methods that primarily focused on direct imitation learning, offering a blueprint for developing more intelligent and capable embodied agents.

2. **State-of-the-Art Performance in Complex Open-Worlds:** JARVIS-VLA's achievement of state-of-the-art performance in Minecraft across over 1,000 diverse atomic tasks is a strong validation of the ActVLP paradigm. Minecraft, as a highly dynamic, partially observable, and open-ended environment, serves as an excellent benchmark for real-world complexity. The model's ability to handle complex tasks, especially those requiring GUI interaction like crafting and smelting, demonstrates a significant leap in functional capability compared to prior agents.

3. **Enhanced Generalization and Flexibility:** By embedding a deeper understanding of the environment within the VLM backbone, ActVLP promotes more flexible and generalizable decision-making. This is crucial for agents operating in open-world settings where novel situations and unseen task variations are common. The improvements in visual recognition and spatial grounding enable the model to better interpret its observations and connect them with abstract instructions, leading to more robust behavior.

4. **New Insights into Scaling Laws for VLAs:** The discovery and investigation of scaling laws concerning non-trajectory vision-language datasets offer valuable guidance for future research and development. It highlights that the quality and quantity of *off-trajectory knowledge* can linearly enhance downstream task performance, providing a new dimension for optimizing VLA training efforts beyond merely scaling trajectory data or model size. This suggests that investment in curated, environment-specific visual-language datasets is as crucial as expert demonstrations.

5. **Bridging Research Disciplines:** This work effectively bridges the gap between traditional VLA models and modular LLM-based agent pipelines. By demonstrating that the same types of non-trajectory vision-language tasks (knowledge, grounding) that benefit modular agents can significantly enhance end-to-end VLA models, it suggests a convergence of effective strategies across different embodied AI architectures.

6. **Acceleration of Research through Open Science:** The commitment to open-sourcing the code, models, and datasets is a substantial contribution to the research community. This will undoubtedly lower the barrier to entry for researchers, facilitate replication, foster new innovations, and accelerate progress in the field of generalist robotic policies and embodied intelligence.

Despite these significant advancements, the authors acknowledge limitations, such as inference throughput constraints and the gap in performance compared to top human players. However, the foundational shift introduced by ActVLP, and the empirical success of JARVIS-VLA, lay critical groundwork for developing truly intelligent, adaptable, and autonomous agents in complex digital and potentially real-world environments.
