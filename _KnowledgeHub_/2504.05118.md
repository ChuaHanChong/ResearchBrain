# VAPO: Efficient and Reliable Reinforcement Learning for Advanced Reasoning Tasks

> [!summary] Summary
> VAPO introduces a value-model-based reinforcement learning framework that reliably enhances large language model performance on advanced Chain-of-Thought reasoning tasks. The framework achieves a new state-of-the-art score of 60.4 on the AIME 2024 dataset using Qwen2.5-32B, outperforming previous value-model-free methods by over 10 points while demonstrating exceptional training efficiency.

## Problem

- Value models for long Chain-of-Thought (CoT) sequences suffer from positive bias due to objective mismatch and exponential decay of reward signals for earlier tokens.
- Heterogeneous sequence lengths in reasoning tasks lead to suboptimal fixed lambda parameters in GAE, causing high variance in short sequences and high bias in long sequences.
- Verifier-based reward models provide sparse, end-of-sequence feedback, creating difficult exploration-exploitation dilemmas and reducing the efficiency of positive sample utilization.

## Method

- Mitigates value model bias over long sequences through ==Value-Pretraining== and ==Decoupled-GAE==, using a lambda of 1.0 for value updates for unbiased gradient descent.
- Manages heterogeneous sequence lengths by introducing ==Length-Adaptive GAE== that dynamically adjusts the policy's lambda based on sequence length, and uses Token-Level Policy Gradient Loss for uniform token weighting.
- Addresses reward signal sparsity via ==Clip-Higher== (modifying PPO clipping range), Positive Example LM Loss (imitating successful trajectories), and Group-Sampling (generating richer contrastive signals).

## Results

- Achieved a state-of-the-art score of **60.4** on the AIME 2024 dataset with Qwen2.5-32B, outperforming DeepSeek-R1-Zero-Qwen-32B (**47** points) by over **13** points and DAPO (**50** points) by over **10** points.
- Demonstrated exceptional training efficiency, reaching state-of-the-art performance within **5,000** steps, matching DAPO's performance using only **60%** of its training steps.
- Exhibited high training reliability with no observed training crashes and consistently similar peak scores (**60-61**) across multiple independent runs, a notable improvement over vanilla PPO which collapsed.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Value-model-based reinforcement learning can outperform value-model-free methods in complex, long Chain-of-Thought reasoning tasks by systematically addressing biases, variance, and sparse reward challenges.
> - A robust RL system for large language models benefits from the synergistic integration of multiple technical modifications, with components like proper value initialization and adaptive credit assignment being crucial for stability and performance.
> - Significant training efficiency and reliability improvements are achievable in large-scale RL for LLMs, leading to reduced computational costs and more predictable experimental outcomes.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the forefront of **Reinforcement Learning (RL) applied to Large Language Models (LLMs)**, specifically targeting advanced reasoning tasks that demand step-by-step problem-solving through long Chain-of-Thought (CoT). The success of contemporary LLMs like OpenAI O1, DeepSeek R1, Gemini, and Grok 3 in complex tasks such as mathematical reasoning has heavily relied on RL techniques to refine their performance on verifiable problems.

The landscape of RL for LLMs is broadly divided into two main paradigms:
1. **Value-model-free methods:** These approaches, exemplified by GRPO (used in DeepSeek R1) and DAPO, directly optimize the policy based on trajectory-level rewards without explicitly learning a value function. They have gained traction due to their ability to simplify computation and provide stable advantage estimates in challenging scenarios, particularly for long sequences where training a reliable value model is difficult. DAPO, for instance, has achieved state-of-the-art performance in industry-level LLM RL.
2. **Value-model-based methods:** These methods, like Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), maintain a separate value model to estimate the expected future rewards, which is then used to compute more precise, lower-variance advantage estimates for policy updates. While theoretically offering a "higher performance ceiling" due to finer-grained credit assignment and better sample efficiency, they have historically struggled with instability and bias in complex, long-CoT tasks (as identified in works like VC-PPO and ORZ).

VAPO directly intervenes in this landscape by challenging the prevailing notion that value-model-free methods are inherently superior for long-CoT reasoning. The authors argue that the perceived limitations of value-model-based approaches stem from unaddressed technical challenges rather than fundamental shortcomings. By systematically tackling these issues, VAPO aims to unleash the full potential of value-model-based RL, demonstrating that it can not only match but significantly *outperform* value-model-free counterparts on challenging reasoning benchmarks.

The paper builds heavily on prior work, explicitly citing inspirations from VC-PPO (for value pre-training and decoupled GAE), DAPO (for Clip-Higher and Token-level Loss), SIL (self-imitation learning), and GRPO (Group-Sampling). This demonstrates an incremental yet highly significant contribution, synthesizing and refining multiple advanced techniques to create a cohesive and robust RL framework. By achieving a new state-of-the-art on AIME 2024, VAPO shifts the focus back to optimizing value-model-based approaches, potentially influencing future research directions in LLM alignment and reasoning capabilities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to develop an **efficient and reliable reinforcement learning framework (VAPO)** that significantly enhances the performance of large language models on advanced, long Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning tasks.

The motivation for pursuing a value-model-based approach, despite its historical challenges in this domain, is rooted in the belief that such methods possess a **higher theoretical performance ceiling**. The authors argue that value models enable:
- **More precise credit assignment:** By accurately tracing the impact of each action on subsequent returns, allowing for finer-grained optimization, which is critical for complex reasoning where subtle errors can lead to catastrophic failures.
- **Lower-variance value estimates:** Unlike Monte Carlo methods, value models can provide more stable estimates, enhancing training stability.
- **Enhanced generalization:** A well-trained value model can generalize better to unseen samples, leading to more efficient utilization of online exploration.

However, realizing this potential requires overcoming specific, formidable challenges inherent in training value models for long-CoT tasks. The paper pinpoints three critical issues that motivate the design of VAPO:

1. **Value Model Bias over Long Sequences:**
 - **Problem:** Initializing a value model from a reward model often introduces a positive bias due to objective mismatch (reward models typically score only the `<EOS>` token). This bias accumulates over long trajectories.
 - **Problem:** Standard GAE with a common `λ` (e.g., 0.95) leads to exponential decay of reward signals for earlier tokens in long sequences, making value updates almost entirely reliant on potentially biased bootstrapped estimates.
 - **Motivation:** To design techniques that mitigate this initial bias and ensure that reward signals effectively propagate through long sequences.

2. **Heterogeneous Sequence Lengths during Training:**
 - **Problem:** Reasoning tasks often generate responses of highly variable lengths (from very short to extremely long). A fixed `λ` parameter in GAE struggles to adapt: short sequences suffer from high variance, while long sequences suffer from high bias due to accumulated bootstrapping errors.
 - **Motivation:** To develop adaptive mechanisms that optimize the bias-variance trade-off for sequences of varying lengths, ensuring robustness across the entire range of generated responses.

3. **Sparsity of Reward Signal in Verifier-based Tasks:**
 - **Problem:** Verifier-based reward models, common in reasoning tasks (e.g., for mathematical problems), provide sparse, often binary (0 or 1) feedback, typically only at the end of a long CoT. This sparsity is exacerbated by longer outputs, reducing the frequency of positive rewards.
 - **Problem:** This creates a difficult exploration-exploitation dilemma: the model needs to maintain high uncertainty to explore and find correct answers but also efficiently utilize the rare positive samples.
 - **Motivation:** To devise strategies that improve the efficiency of exploration (preventing entropy collapse) and maximize the utility of the scarce but valuable positive samples, leading to more efficient learning.

By systematically addressing these three core challenges, VAPO aims to overcome the practical limitations that have historically hindered value-model-based RL, thereby unleashing its full potential for building highly capable and reliable reasoning LLMs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VAPO (Value-model-based Augmented Proximal Policy Optimization) is a novel reinforcement learning framework built upon the core principles of Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), augmented with seven key modifications designed to specifically address the challenges in long Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning tasks. The base model used for experiments is **Qwen2.5-32B**, ensuring comparability by avoiding additional Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) data.

The methodology integrates and refines techniques from prior works, systematically targeting the three identified challenges:

#### 3.1. Mitigating Value Model Bias over Long Sequences

1. **Value-Pretraining:** To counter the initial bias stemming from the objective mismatch between a reward model and a value model (which is often used for initialization), VAPO performs a pre-training phase for the value network. During this phase, responses are sampled from a fixed policy (e.g., SFT policy), and the value model is updated using Monte-Carlo returns. This process aims to ensure the value model is well-initialized with low bias and high explained variance before policy training begins.
2. **Decoupled-GAE:** VAPO decouples the Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE) computation for the value and policy networks. For value updates, a `λ` of 1.0 is used (`λ_critic = 1.0`). This ensures an unbiased gradient descent for the value function, critically addressing the reward-decay issues over long CoT sequences. For policy updates, a separate, adaptively set `λ_policy` is used to accelerate convergence.

#### 3.2. Managing Heterogeneous Sequence Lengths During Training

3. **Length-Adaptive GAE:** This is a novel contribution designed to address the inconsistency of optimal `λ_policy` values across sequences of varying lengths. Instead of a fixed `λ_policy`, VAPO dynamically adjusts it based on the output sequence length (`l`). The formula `λ_policy = 1 - 1/(αl)` ensures that the sum of GAE coefficients remains proportional to the sequence length, providing a more uniform distribution of TD-errors and balancing the bias-variance trade-off for both short and long responses. The hyperparameter `α` controls this overall trade-off.
4. **Token-Level Policy Gradient Loss:** Traditional PPO often averages losses at the sequence level before batch-level averaging. This implicitly assigns less weight to tokens from longer sequences. VAPO modifies the PPO objective to compute the policy gradient loss at the token level, assigning uniform weights to all tokens within a training batch. This ensures that critical issues arising from processing long sequences (especially during exploration) are adequately addressed, enhancing training stability.

#### 3.3. Dealing with Sparsity of Reward Signal in Verifier-based Tasks

5. **Clip-Higher:** Inspired by DAPO, VAPO modifies the PPO clipping range by setting `ε_high` (upper bound) higher than `ε_low` (lower bound). By increasing `ε_high` (e.g., to 0.28 while `ε_low` is 0.2), VAPO encourages more exploration of low-probability tokens without causing entropy collapse or prematurely suppressing beneficial rare tokens, which is crucial in sparse reward environments.
6. **Positive Example LM Loss:** To maximize the utility of rare correct answers, VAPO incorporates an additional Negative Log-Likelihood (NLL) loss. This loss is applied specifically to the set of correctly sampled trajectories (`T`), effectively making the policy imitate these successful examples. This imitation learning component helps the model learn efficiently from the extremely sparse positive feedback, complementing the standard policy gradient. A weighting coefficient `μ` balances this loss with the PPO loss.
7. **Group-Sampling:** Instead of sampling a high number of distinct prompts once per batch, VAPO reduces the number of unique prompts and increases the number of repetitions for each prompt (e.g., 512 prompts, each sampled 16 times). This approach generates richer contrastive signals within the same prompt, which empirically enhances the policy model's learning capability, especially when rewards are sparse and specific prompt-response pairs are valuable.

**Training Details:**
- **Optimizer:** AdamW.
- **Learning Rates:** Actor (policy) at `1×10^-6`, Critic (value) at `2×10^-6` (faster updates for critic).
- **Scheduler:** Warmup-constant.
- **Batch Size:** 8192 prompts (overall), mini-batch size 512.
- **Evaluation Metric:** Average pass rate on AIME 2024 dataset over 32 times, with sampling parameters `topp=0.7` and `temperature=1.0`.

The systematic combination and validation of these techniques allow VAPO to create a robust and high-performing RL system for challenging reasoning tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results demonstrate that VAPO achieves state-of-the-art performance, remarkable efficiency, and superior reliability in training large language models for advanced reasoning tasks, particularly on the AIME 2024 benchmark.

- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** VAPO, built on the Qwen2.5-32B pre-trained model, achieves a score of **60.4** on the AIME 2024 dataset. This establishes a new state-of-the-art for RL-tuned reasoning models using this base model.
- **Significant Outperformance:** In direct comparison under identical experimental settings, VAPO substantially outperforms previous state-of-the-art value-model-free methods:
 - It surpasses **DeepSeek-R1-Zero-Qwen-32B** (which scored 47 points) by more than 13 points.
 - It outperforms **DAPO** (which scored 50 points) by more than 10 points. This is a crucial finding, as DAPO was previously considered a leading method for large-scale LLM RL.
- **Exceptional Training Efficiency:** VAPO reaches its state-of-the-art performance within a "mere **5,000 steps**." This is highly efficient; the paper notes that VAPO matches DAPO's performance using only 60% of DAPO's training steps, and then continues to improve further. This rapid convergence significantly reduces computational costs and training time.
- **High Reliability and Stability:** A critical finding is VAPO's training stability. Across multiple independent runs, **no training crashes** were observed, and results were consistently similar (peak scores of 60-61). This contrasts sharply with the "Vanilla PPO" baseline, which suffered from "value model learning collapse" and achieved a dismal 5 points, indicating its unsuitability for long-CoT tasks without VAPO's modifications.

#### 4.1. Ablation Study Results (Table 1 Summary)

The ablation studies systematically validate the necessity and individual contribution of each of VAPO's seven proposed modifications. Removing any single component leads to a noticeable drop in performance, confirming their collective and synergistic impact:
1. **VAPO w/o Value-Pretraining:** Performance drops drastically to **11 points**. This highlights that proper value model initialization is crucial to prevent training collapse, a key limitation of vanilla PPO.
2. **VAPO w/o Decoupled-GAE:** Performance drops significantly to **33 points**. This indicates the importance of unbiased value updates and proper reward signal propagation for long responses.
3. **VAPO w/o Length-adaptive GAE:** Performance drops to **45 points**. This demonstrates the 15-point improvement gained by adapting GAE to heterogeneous sequence lengths.
4. **VAPO w/o Clip-Higher:** Performance drops to **46 points**. This component is vital for encouraging exploration and exploitation, preventing premature convergence.
5. **VAPO w/o Token-level Loss:** Performance drops to **53 points**. This modification contributed a 7-point gain by implicitly increasing the weight of long responses.
6. **VAPO w/o Positive Example LM Loss:** Performance drops to **54 points**. This shows a 6-point contribution from effectively leveraging rare positive samples through imitation learning.
7. **VAPO w/o Group-Sampling:** Performance drops to **55 points**. This demonstrates a 5-point improvement from using richer contrastive signals via repeated sampling of fewer prompts.

#### 4.2. Training Dynamics (Figure 2 Analysis)

Visualizations of training curves further support VAPO's advantages:
- **Smoother Training Curve:** VAPO's reward score curve is smoother than DAPO's, indicating more stable algorithmic optimization.
- **Superior Length Scaling:** VAPO exhibits better scaling of mean response length during training, a recognized indicator of improved model performance and generalization.
- **Faster Score Growth:** VAPO's reward score grows faster than DAPO's, attributing this to the value model providing more granular signals for accelerated optimization.
- **Controlled Entropy:** VAPO's generation entropy drops lower in later stages compared to DAPO. While this could potentially hinder exploration, the final results confirm it doesn't negatively impact performance, instead contributing to improved stability and reproducibility.

In summary, VAPO not only sets a new benchmark in performance for reasoning tasks but also addresses critical practical concerns of training efficiency and reliability, making it a highly compelling advancement in RL for LLMs.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The VAPO framework represents a significant advancement in the field of Reinforcement Learning for Large Language Models (LLMs), with profound implications for both research and practical applications.

1. **Re-establishing Value-Model-Based RL's Dominance:** The most critical significance of VAPO is its ability to demonstrate that value-model-based RL can **outperform** value-model-free methods (like GRPO and DAPO) in complex, long Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning tasks. This directly challenges the prevailing narrative that value-model-free approaches are inherently superior for these challenging scenarios due to the difficulties in training stable value models. VAPO effectively showcases that by systematically addressing the biases, variance issues, and sparse reward challenges, the theoretical advantages of value models—finer-grained credit assignment and lower variance estimates—can be fully realized. This could catalyze a renewed research interest in optimizing value functions for LLMs.
2. **Enhanced Performance on Reasoning Tasks:** Achieving a new state-of-the-art score of 60.4 on the AIME 2024 dataset (a difficult mathematical reasoning benchmark) with the Qwen2.5-32B model signifies a substantial improvement in LLM reasoning capabilities. This means models trained with VAPO can solve more complex problems, requiring deeper logical steps and extended reasoning chains, which is a crucial step towards more intelligent AI systems.
3. **Improved Training Efficiency and Reliability:** The demonstration that VAPO achieves state-of-the-art performance in "mere 5,000 steps" and exhibits "no training crashes" across multiple runs is immensely impactful for practical LLM development.
 - **Efficiency:** Fewer training steps translate directly to reduced computational costs (GPU hours) and faster iteration cycles for researchers and developers. This is a game-changer in a field where training runs can cost millions of dollars and take weeks or months.
 - **Reliability:** The stability of VAPO, preventing training collapses, minimizes wasted resources and time, making large-scale RL experiments more predictable and manageable. This is a significant advantage for industrial-scale research and deployment.
4. **A Blueprint for Robust RL Algorithm Design:** VAPO's success stems from the systematic integration and validation of seven distinct, yet synergistic, technical modifications. The detailed ablation studies provide a clear empirical understanding of how each component contributes to the overall performance and stability. This comprehensive approach offers a valuable blueprint for future RL algorithm design in LLMs, highlighting the importance of addressing subtle but critical issues like value initialization, adaptive credit assignment, and efficient utilization of sparse feedback.
5. **Broader Applicability:** While primarily demonstrated on mathematical reasoning, the challenges addressed by VAPO (long sequence handling, sparse rewards, value function stability) are common across various complex generative AI tasks. Therefore, the principles and techniques introduced in VAPO are highly generalizable to other domains requiring advanced CoT, such as complex code generation, scientific discovery, multi-turn dialogue, and intricate problem-solving.
6. **Reinforcing ByteDance's AI Leadership:** This research underscores ByteDance's strong capabilities in foundational AI research and its commitment to pushing the boundaries of LLM performance. Developing such a robust and efficient RL framework positions ByteDance competitively in the global AI race, potentially leading to more advanced and reliable reasoning abilities in their diverse product offerings.

In conclusion, VAPO is a landmark contribution that not only elevates the performance ceiling for LLMs in reasoning tasks but also fundamentally shifts the conversation around the viability and advantages of value-model-based reinforcement learning, paving the way for more stable, efficient, and powerful AI systems.
