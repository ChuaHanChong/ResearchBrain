# GroundSet: A Cadastral-Grounded Dataset for Spatial Understanding with Vector Data

> [!summary] Summary
> GroundSet, a new cadastral-grounded dataset, was introduced to address deficiencies in fine-grained spatial understanding for Earth Observation with Multimodal Large Language Models. This dataset features 3.8 million annotated objects across 510,000 high-resolution images with 135 granular semantic categories, enabling a fine-tuned LLaVA-1.6 7B baseline to achieve a CIDEr score of 104.48 for captioning and an F1@0.5 of 49.47 for object detection, significantly outperforming existing models.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) exhibit limitations in fine-grained spatial understanding, crucial for high-precision Earth Observation (EO) applications like urban planning.
- Existing EO datasets are often repurposed, limited in semantic scope (few generic categories), lack geometric precision, and do not scale, hindering MLLM adaptation.
- High-quality spatial annotations are scarce and costly to generate, creating a bottleneck for training MLLMs to achieve precise sub-meter accuracy.

## Method

- Created **GroundSet**, a large-scale dataset leveraging verified ==cadastral vector data== from France, offering high-fidelity geometry and **135** granular semantic categories.
- Developed a comprehensive ==instruction fine-tuning== benchmark comprising seven spatial understanding tasks (e.g., Captioning, Object Detection, Segmentation, VQA) derived from the vector data.
- Provided a robust baseline by fine-tuning a standard **LLaVA-1.6 7B** ==Vision-Language Model== on **GroundSet**, demonstrating its ability to master complex grounding tasks without architectural modifications.

## Results

- The GroundSet-trained baseline significantly outperformed all other state-of-the-art models across seven spatial understanding tasks, achieving a CIDEr score of **104.48** for captioning and an F1@0.5 of **49.47** for object detection, compared to **24.03** and **18.08**, respectively, for the next best zero-shot models.
- Ablation studies confirmed that auxiliary geometric tasks improved spatial grounding capabilities and that a frozen vision encoder was optimal for the training recipe.
- The GroundSet baseline demonstrated strong zero-shot generalization on the VRSBench dataset, surpassing leading RS-specialists in core grounding tasks (e.g., **15.47** F1@0.5 for object detection vs. **12.81** for SkySenseGPT), despite encountering out-of-distribution categories.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - High-fidelity and semantically rich cadastral data are crucial for MLLMs to acquire robust, fine-grained spatial understanding, addressing a "knowledge gap" not present in web-scale or existing RS-specific pre-training.
> - Standard Vision-Language Models can achieve state-of-the-art performance in complex spatial grounding tasks when trained on high-quality, dense vector data, negating the need for specialized architectural modifications.
> - Spatial grounding capabilities learned from cadastral data exhibit strong transferability, allowing the GroundSet-trained model to generalize and outperform specialized RS models on out-of-distribution benchmarks.
