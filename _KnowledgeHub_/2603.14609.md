# GroundSet: A Cadastral-Grounded Dataset for Spatial Understanding with Vector Data

> [!summary] Summary
> GroundSet, a new cadastral-grounded dataset, was introduced to address deficiencies in fine-grained spatial understanding for Earth Observation with Multimodal Large Language Models. This dataset features 3.8 million annotated objects across 510,000 high-resolution images with 135 granular semantic categories, enabling a fine-tuned LLaVA-1.6 7B baseline to achieve a CIDEr score of 104.48 for captioning and an F1@0.5 of 49.47 for object detection, significantly outperforming existing models.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) exhibit limitations in fine-grained spatial understanding, crucial for high-precision Earth Observation (EO) applications like urban planning.
- Existing EO datasets are often repurposed, limited in semantic scope (few generic categories), lack geometric precision, and do not scale, hindering MLLM adaptation.
- High-quality spatial annotations are scarce and costly to generate, creating a bottleneck for training MLLMs to achieve precise sub-meter accuracy.

## Method

- Created **GroundSet**, a large-scale dataset leveraging verified ==cadastral vector data== from France, offering high-fidelity geometry and **135** granular semantic categories.
- Developed a comprehensive ==instruction fine-tuning== benchmark comprising seven spatial understanding tasks (e.g., Captioning, Object Detection, Segmentation, VQA) derived from the vector data.
- Provided a robust baseline by fine-tuning a standard **LLaVA-1.6 7B** ==Vision-Language Model== on **GroundSet**, demonstrating its ability to master complex grounding tasks without architectural modifications.

## Results

- The GroundSet-trained baseline significantly outperformed all other state-of-the-art models across seven spatial understanding tasks, achieving a CIDEr score of **104.48** for captioning and an F1@0.5 of **49.47** for object detection, compared to **24.03** and **18.08**, respectively, for the next best zero-shot models.
- Ablation studies confirmed that auxiliary geometric tasks improved spatial grounding capabilities and that a frozen vision encoder was optimal for the training recipe.
- The GroundSet baseline demonstrated strong zero-shot generalization on the VRSBench dataset, surpassing leading RS-specialists in core grounding tasks (e.g., **15.47** F1@0.5 for object detection vs. **12.81** for SkySenseGPT), despite encountering out-of-distribution categories.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - High-fidelity and semantically rich cadastral data are crucial for MLLMs to acquire robust, fine-grained spatial understanding, addressing a "knowledge gap" not present in web-scale or existing RS-specific pre-training.
> - Standard Vision-Language Models can achieve state-of-the-art performance in complex spatial grounding tasks when trained on high-quality, dense vector data, negating the need for specialized architectural modifications.
> - Spatial grounding capabilities learned from cadastral data exhibit strong transferability, allowing the GroundSet-trained model to generalize and outperform specialized RS models on out-of-distribution benchmarks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) has significantly advanced artificial intelligence, demonstrating capabilities in diverse tasks. However, within specialized domains such as Earth Observation (EO) and Remote Sensing (RS), MLLMs exhibit deficiencies in fine-grained spatial understanding. This limitation is particularly critical in applications requiring high precision, such as urban planning, environmental monitoring, and disaster management, where sub-meter accuracy is essential.

Existing efforts to adapt MLLMs for EO have often relied on repurposing legacy datasets (e.g., DOTA, DIOR) that were originally designed for closed-set object detection. These datasets typically offer a limited number of generic semantic categories and lack the scalability and granular detail required for modern machine learning and spatial reasoning tasks. While some large-scale initiatives utilize OpenStreetMap (OSM) for broad coverage, they sometimes compromise on resolution and geometric precision. The common approach of synthetically generating question-answer pairs from these older datasets also inherits their inherent limitations in quality and semantic scope, preventing the acquisition of novel domain-specific knowledge.

This work addresses the bottleneck of scarce high-quality spatial annotations in Remote Sensing. It proposes a novel approach to data curation by leveraging verifiable cadastral vector data, which offers a higher fidelity and semantic depth compared to previously used methods. By doing so, it aims to establish a more robust foundation for training MLLMs to achieve precise spatial understanding in EO, thereby contributing a critical resource that overcomes the limitations of existing datasets and facilitates the operational deployment of MLLMs in high-stakes geospatial applications.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to bridge the identified gap in fine-grained spatial understanding within Remote Sensing MLLMs. This deficiency stems from their reliance on limited or repurposed legacy datasets, which lack the precision, semantic richness, and scalability necessary for critical Earth Observation applications.

The motivation for this work is multifaceted:
- **Operational Necessity:** Precise spatial understanding is fundamental for translating raw aerial imagery into actionable insights across various critical applications, including urban planning, environmental monitoring, and disaster management. Current MLLM inaccuracies actively hinder their operational use in these high-stakes domains where sub-meter precision is required.
- **Data Scarcity and Quality:** While unlabelled satellite imagery is abundant, the creation of dense and accurate annotations poses a significant challenge. Human labeling is cost-prohibitive and lacks the scalability needed to support contemporary machine learning research. Existing legacy datasets are constrained by their quality, limited semantic scope (often restricted to a few dozen generic classes), and inherent nature, failing to adequately address the demand for novel domain knowledge.
- **Advancing MLLM Capabilities:** The adaptation of MLLMs to Earth Observation is compelling due to their demonstrated zero-shot capabilities and broad world knowledge. However, their practical application is stalled by the aforementioned data limitations. This work aims to equip standard MLLM architectures with fine-grained spatial grounding abilities without requiring complex architectural modifications.

To address these motivations, the authors define three main contributions:
1. **The Data:** To introduce a novel large-scale Earth Observation dataset, named GroundSet. This dataset is characterized by its verified cadastral origin, providing high-fidelity geometric data. It offers an unprecedented semantic richness with 135 granular semantic categories and high density, comprising 3.8 million annotated objects across 510,000 high-resolution images.
2. **The Benchmark:** To establish a comprehensive instruction fine-tuning benchmark derived from this vector data. This benchmark comprises seven spatial understanding tasks designed to demonstrate the applicability of GroundSet to modern Vision-Language Model (VLM) training and evaluate models on diverse spatial reasoning capabilities.
3. **The Baseline:** To provide a robust, fine-tuned model based on a standard VLM architecture. This baseline aims to demonstrate that standard architectures can master complex grounding tasks effectively when trained on the proposed high-density, verified vector data, thereby serving as a reference for future research and quantifying the unique domain knowledge contained within the cadastral annotations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on the creation and validation of GroundSet, a large-scale dataset leveraging cadastral vector data, and the subsequent benchmarking of MLLMs on spatial reasoning tasks.

**Data Source and Coverage:**
The dataset utilizes cadastral records from France’s open-data infrastructure, specifically high-resolution (20 cm) aerial orthophotos (BD ORTHO®) and legally verified vector data (BD TOPO®) provided by the national mapping agency (IGN). This choice prioritizes annotation fidelity and 1-meter precision for entities (multi)polygons, lines, and points. The dataset spans 85,864 km² across 20 administrative departments, ensuring diverse geographical and urban environments.

**Data Processing Pipeline:**
Over 4 million image patches (672 × 672 pixels, approximately 134 × 134 meters) were extracted. A rigorous pipeline was applied:
1. **Preprocessing:**
 - **Filtering:** Entities not visually discernible in orthophotos (e.g., tunnels, subterranean structures) were removed to ensure alignment between visual features and vector data.
 - **Semantic Translation:** The 735 unique semantic terms in the database were automatically translated into English and manually verified, forming 135 ground-truth categories.
 - **Resampling:** A strategy was implemented to group images by their unique set of entities and cap each combination at 64 samples. This reduced the raw dataset size by 85% and balanced the distribution of scene compositions, mitigating semantic redundancy from sparse or repetitive scenes.
 - **Geometric Merging:** Contiguous polygons of the same class were merged to ensure the vector ground truth reflected visual footprints rather than legal partitions.
 - **Padding:** Small or partial objects cropped at image borders, lacking sufficient context, were excluded.
 - **Normalization:** Geographical coordinates were normalized into relative pixel coordinates in the range.
 - The final preprocessed dataset comprises 510,483 patches containing 3,829,755 objects across 135 unique categories, with a median density of 8 objects per image.

2. **Annotations:**
 - Beyond the original polygons, lines, and points, annotations were augmented with Horizontal Bounding Boxes (HBB) and Oriented Bounding Boxes (OBB).
 - Segmentation masks are represented as coordinate sequences.
 - Expansive or irregular geometries (e.g., airports, rivers) were assigned to a "segmentation-only" category to avoid ambiguous bounding boxes.
 - The use of native vector data ensures precise, human-verified geometric primitives.

3. **Descriptive Text Generation:**
 - Scene descriptions were automatically generated using state-of-the-art VLMs.
 - This process was strictly constrained: VLMs elaborated on pre-defined, rule-based raw captions (generated using geometric rules and templates) rather than perceiving new information. This aimed to prevent hallucination while incorporating novel semantic categories.

4. **Visual-Semantic Discrepancy Characterization:**
 - An automated profiling pipeline using Gemini-2.5 Flash identified potential misalignments (e.g., annotations without visual evidence, incomplete records, occluded instances).
 - Instances deemed "invisible" were flagged rather than discarded, preserving cadastral record integrity and allowing users to select between "clean" (visually confirmed) and "hard" (occluded/inferred) training splits.

5. **Postprocessing:**
 - Semantic simplification aggregated subtypes lacking distinctive visual features (e.g., primary vs. high school) into super-categories (e.g., School).
 - A comprehensive, hierarchical taxonomy was provided to guide training and evaluation, addressing the non-mutually exclusive nature of the label space.

**Dataset Releases:**
Two distinct datasets were released:
- A large-scale pre-training dataset (3.8M objects, 510k images).
- A curated VQA dataset for instruction fine-tuning (880k objects, 60k images, 1.8M questions).

**Seven Spatial Reasoning Tasks:**
The benchmark defined seven tasks: Captioning, Localized Classification, Object Detection, Multi-Class Detection, Referring Expression Comprehension (REC), Segmentation, and Visual Question Answering (VQA).

**Instruction Generation:**
- **Test Set:** Comprises 14,000 images and 72,597 fixed instructions, generated with diverse templates (10 linguistic variations per question) and including negative samples (21% probability) for detection/segmentation.
- **Training Set:** Contains 45,988 images and 1,845,076 instructions. It includes standard task instructions augmented with auxiliary objectives: exhaustive segmentation queries and text-only geometric reasoning tasks (e.g., polygon simplification, bounding box prediction for synthetic polygons) to acclimatize the model to a discretized coordinate system [0–1000].

**Evaluation Protocol:**
- State-of-the-art models were benchmarked in a zero-shot setting.
- Input prompts were tailored to each architecture's expected template, and output predictions were geometrically recovered (e.g., converting OBB to HBB) and rescaled for fair comparison.
- **Metrics:** Standard Natural Language Generation metrics (BLEU@4, METEOR, CIDEr) for Captioning; Semantic Similarity (Acc@k) and exact string matching for Classification; mIoU, F1, Precision, Recall for Grounding tasks (Detection, Multi-Class Detection, REC, Segmentation), with taxonomy-aware evaluation; and Precision, Recall, F1, Accuracy for VQA.

**Baseline Fine-tuning:**
- LLaVA-1.6 7B architecture was fine-tuned for one epoch using LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) without auxiliary spatial modules or architectural modifications.
- LoRA adapters were applied to all linear layers of the language model, the multi-modal projector was fully fine-tuned, and the vision tower remained frozen.
- Training was conducted on 8 × A100 GPUs using FlashAttention-2, BFloat16 precision, TF32, and DeepSpeed ZeRO-2.

**Ablation Studies:**
Ablation studies were performed on a 100k-instruction subset to validate the training strategy. These studies investigated the impact of auxiliary geometric tasks (text-only polygon reasoning and exhaustive segmentation queries), unfreezing the vision encoder, and filtering occluded instances on classification, detection, and segmentation performance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides a comprehensive evaluation of various state-of-the-art models against the GroundSet benchmark, alongside an analysis of the proposed fine-tuned baseline.

**Zero-Shot Evaluation on GroundSet (Table 2):**
- **Captioning:** The GroundSet fine-tuned baseline significantly outperformed all other models, achieving a CIDEr score of 104.48, compared to the next best, SkySenseGPT, at 24.03. Other models clustered around lower values.
- **Classification:** The GroundSet baseline demonstrated a substantial lead with an acc@0.8 of 94.18. Commercial models like Gemini-2.5 achieved 49.84, while general-purpose models (LLaVA-1.5/1.6) ranged from 28-29. Remote Sensing (RS)-specialized models (GeoChat, SkySenseGPT) performed considerably lower (7.59-8.15 acc@0.8), suggesting potential catastrophic forgetting or lack of specific semantic knowledge for the granular categories introduced by GroundSet. This indicates a "knowledge gap" in existing web-scale and RS-specific pre-training.
- **Visual Question Answering (VQA):** GroundSet baseline achieved the highest F1 score (97.09). RS-specialized models (e.g., SkySenseGPT: 89.69 F1) showed a slight advantage over generic baselines in VQA, suggesting some benefit from domain adaptation, though still significantly trailing the GroundSet baseline.
- **Grounding Tasks (Object Detection, Multi-Class Detection, REC, Segmentation):** The GroundSet baseline consistently achieved superior performance across all grounding tasks. For Object Detection, it scored an F1@0.5 of 49.47, compared to the best zero-shot model, Ferret, at 18.08. Similarly, for Segmentation, GroundSet reached 44.65 F1@0.5, while Ferret, the next best, achieved 19.77. Specialized architectures like Ferret and PaliGemma generally outperformed both RS-specialized models and commercial APIs in zero-shot grounding tasks, indicating the impact of architectural priors for general localization. However, RS-specialized models sometimes underperformed their foundational LLaVA baselines.

**The Data Factor:** The significant performance disparity observed is primarily attributed to the semantic richness and geometric precision of the cadastral annotations within GroundSet. By fixing the architecture (LLaVA-1.6), the results demonstrate that standard Vision-Language Models can master fine-grained spatial understanding when trained on high-fidelity data, without requiring complex architectural modifications. This highlights the value of the dataset in providing unique domain knowledge currently absent in web-scale pre-training.

**Ablation Studies (Table 3):**
- Augmenting the training mixture with auxiliary geometric tasks (text-only polygon reasoning and exhaustive segmentation queries) improved spatial grounding capabilities, boosting detection and segmentation F1 scores.
- These augmentations resulted in a negligible decrease in classification accuracy.
- Unfreezing the vision encoder led to inconsistent results and notably degraded segmentation performance.
- Filtering out occluded "invisible" instances improved segmentation scores but slightly reduced performance in other tasks.
- Based on these findings, the final training recipe maintained a frozen vision encoder and incorporated both geometric augmentation strategies.

**Cross-Dataset Generalization (Zero-Shot on VRSBench, Table 4):**
- The GroundSet-trained baseline was evaluated zero-shot on the VRSBench dataset, which features categories (e.g., vehicles, ships, airplanes) absent from GroundSet's cadastral training data.
- Despite this domain shift, the GroundSet model outperformed leading RS-specialists in core spatial grounding tasks, specifically Object Detection (15.47 F1@0.5 vs. SkySenseGPT 12.81) and Referring Expression Comprehension (21.49 F1@0.5 vs. GeoChat 12.07).
- This demonstrates that the spatial grounding capabilities acquired from the cadastral vector data are transferable and robust to domain shifts, even when facing out-of-distribution visual content and semantic vocabulary. Conversely, existing RS-specialists, even with in-domain pre-training advantages, collapsed when evaluated zero-shot on the GroundSet benchmark.

**Qualitative Results (Figures 8, 9, 10):** Qualitative comparisons on object detection and segmentation tasks illustrated that the GroundSet-trained model achieved superior overlap with ground truth and more accurately handled irregular boundaries compared to other baselines. The model effectively identified and segmented a range of new and specialized semantic categories.

**Fine-grained Analysis (Table 8):** Detailed analysis showed that GroundSet maintains robust performance across diverse semantic groups (e.g., Building, Heritage, Civil infrastructure, Sport, Transport, Land use zones), while existing baselines exhibited significant performance drops on rarer and specialized classes, often relying on common objects present in legacy datasets.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work makes a notable contribution to the field of Remote Sensing and Multimodal AI by addressing the fundamental challenge of acquiring high-fidelity spatial annotations.

**Elevating Data Quality and Scale:** The introduction of GroundSet provides a large-scale dataset grounded in verifiable cadastral records. This approach offers unprecedented semantic granularity (135 classes) and geometric precision, establishing a rigorous foundation for spatial AI research that surpasses the limitations of previously available synthetic, crowd-sourced, or repurposed legacy datasets. Its legal verification ensures a level of ground truth reliability not typically found in other resources.

**Advancing Spatial Understanding in MLLMs:** The research demonstrates that fine-tuning a standard, unmodified Vision-Language Model on GroundSet is sufficient to acquire robust, fine-grained spatial grounding capabilities. This finding is significant as it suggests that the bottleneck for advanced spatial understanding lies primarily in data quality and richness rather than requiring complex architectural modifications or specialized model designs. The baseline model's performance notably surpasses that of both specialized Remote Sensing architectures and large commercial models in specific spatial tasks.

**Exposing a "Knowledge Gap":** The substantial performance disparity between the GroundSet-trained baseline and other models highlights a critical "knowledge gap" in current web-scale pre-training and existing Remote Sensing datasets. GroundSet introduces specific and granular domain knowledge essential for precise geospatial analysis, which has been largely absent in models trained on more generic data.

**Enabling Cross-Domain Transferability:** The demonstration of robust cross-dataset generalization, where the GroundSet-trained model outperforms RS-specialists on an out-of-distribution benchmark (VRSBench) in core grounding tasks, indicates that the learned spatial reasoning capabilities are transferable and robust to semantic domain shifts. This suggests that the geometric understanding fostered by cadastral data is a foundational skill that can be leveraged across diverse Remote Sensing applications.

**Catalyst for Future Research:** By releasing both the instruction dataset and the raw vector data, the authors aim to foster broader methodological innovation. This resource uniquely positions the research community to explore novel modeling paradigms beyond conventional autoregressive MLLMs, potentially leveraging techniques such as Graph Neural Networks (GNNs), Joint Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPA-inspired architectures), or diffusion LLMs for spatial understanding. This could lead to new avenues for improving the efficiency and accuracy of spatial AI systems.

**Practical Applications:** Ultimately, this work provides a foundational resource crucial for translating raw aerial imagery into actionable insights. Its impact can extend to critical applications such as more precise urban planning, enhanced environmental monitoring, and more effective disaster management, where fine-grained spatial understanding is paramount for informed decision-making.
