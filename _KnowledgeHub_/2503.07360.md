# AffordDexGrasp: Open-set Language-guided Dexterous Grasp with Generalizable-Instructive Affordance

> [!summary] Summary
> AffordDexGrasp enables language-guided dexterous grasping of unseen objects by introducing a Generalizable-Instructive Affordance (GIA) as an intermediate representation. The framework, combining MLLM for intention understanding, cascaded flow matching, and a non-parametric optimization, demonstrates improved intention consistency and grasp quality in open-set scenarios across simulations and real-world tests.

## Problem

- Developing robotic dexterous grasping capabilities for novel, unseen object categories in real-world environments poses a challenge for generalization.
- Bridging the semantic gap between high-level natural language commands and low-level, high-dimensional dexterous robot actions remains difficult, particularly for open-set scenarios.
- Existing affordance representations for grasping face a trade-off: fine-grained contact maps offer detailed guidance but lack generalization, while coarse object segmentations generalize better but are insufficiently instructive for complex dexterous hands.

## Method

- Introduced AffordDexGrasp, a framework that employs a Generalizable-Instructive Affordance (GIA) as an intermediate representation to bridge language semantics and robot actions.
- Utilized a Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM) for an Intention Pre-Understanding stage, extracting key cues like object category, user intention, contact part, and grasp direction from diverse language commands.
- Developed a cascaded architecture with Affordance Flow Matching (AFM) to generate GIA maps and Grasp Flow Matching (GFM) to generate dexterous grasp poses, followed by a non-parametric affordance-guided grasp optimization for refinement.

## Results

- AffordDexGrasp consistently surpassed state-of-the-art methods in open-set scenarios, achieving higher R-Precision (e.g., 0.480 on Open Set A) for intention consistency and improved grasp quality (e.g., 45.1% success rate on Open Set A, lower penetration).
- Ablation studies confirmed the essential roles of GIA, the MLLM-assisted intention pre-understanding, and the non-parametric grasp optimization in enhancing both generalization and grasp quality.
- The framework demonstrated improved zero-shot and one-shot generalization capabilities to novel object categories and successfully transferred from simulation to real-world robotic platforms (Leap Hand and Kinova Gen3 arm) with higher success rates on unseen objects.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A Generalizable-Instructive Affordance (GIA) effectively balances generalization to unseen objects with sufficiently instructive guidance for multi-finger dexterous hands, addressing the inherent trade-off in affordance representations.
> - Leveraging MLLMs for pre-understanding and standardizing diverse natural language commands into structured cues enhances the robustness and generalization of language-guided grasping systems.
> - Cascaded Flow Matching models provide an efficient and effective generative approach for both predicting GIA maps and generating complex dexterous grasp poses, outperforming diffusion models in terms of performance and inference speed.
> - A non-parametric, test-time optimization method is effective for refining grasp quality (e.g., reducing object penetration) without compromising the semantic intention conveyed by the language command, especially crucial for out-of-domain samples.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics and computer vision is actively pursuing the development of generalizable robot dexterous grasping capabilities for applications in human-robot interaction and advanced manipulation. Prior research has largely focused on achieving stable grasping or on language-guided dexterous grasp generation for objects belonging to known categories. These data-driven approaches have demonstrated performance on objects within their training distributions.

However, a significant challenge arises in real-world scenarios where robots encounter diverse object categories not present during their training. This is known as the open-set problem. While some research has explored open-set tasks for parallel grippers—often leveraging pre-trained visual grounding models, implicit semantic feature fields, or multimodal large models—the complexity of dexterous hands, characterized by a higher number of degrees of freedom, presents greater challenges for open-set generalization. Consequently, there has been limited research addressing open-set generalization specifically for dexterous robotic hands in a language-guided context.

Within the domain of grasp affordance, two primary types exist: fine-grained contact information and coarse-grained object segmentation. Fine-grained affordances, such as contact maps, can effectively guide grasp generation for specific objects but exhibit limitations in generalizing to unseen categories. Conversely, coarse information, like object part segmentation, can be generalized more readily but often lacks the specificity required to guide complex dexterous hand actions. This trade-off between generalizability and instructiveness represents a gap in existing affordance representations for open-set dexterous grasping.

This work addresses the problem of open-set language-guided dexterous grasping, which is identified as a gap between high-level natural language semantics and low-level robot actions, particularly regarding generalization to unseen categories.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable robots to perform dexterous grasps on objects from both seen and unseen categories, guided by human language commands, a task defined as "Open-set Language-guided Dexterous Grasp."

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges in current robotic grasping systems:
- **Real-world Variability:** Real-world environments contain a vast array of object categories, many of which may not be present in a robot's training data. Achieving robot autonomy necessitates the ability to interact with novel objects.
- **Data Collection Cost:** Collecting extensive training data for dexterous hands is resource-intensive and expensive. This makes it impractical to train models for every conceivable object category, highlighting the need for generalization.
- **Intention Understanding and Execution:** Previous data-driven methods for language-guided dexterous grasping, while effective for known categories, often struggle to interpret human intentions and execute appropriate grasps when confronted with unseen object types.
- **Semantic-Action Gap:** A core challenge identified is the significant disparity between the high-level semantic information conveyed by natural language and the low-level, high-dimensional actions required by dexterous robotic hands. This gap hinders the generalization of intent understanding and grasping abilities from training domains to novel categories.

To address these motivations, the study aims to propose a framework that bridges this semantic-action gap by introducing a novel intermediate representation. This representation is intended to be both **generalizable** to unseen categories and sufficiently **instructive** to guide the complex movements of a dexterous hand, thereby facilitating robust open-set language-guided dexterous grasping.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, Affordance Dexterous Grasp (AffordDexGrasp), is designed to address open-set language-guided dexterous grasping by bridging the gap between language semantics and robot actions through a novel intermediate representation. The framework consists of three main stages: Intention Pre-Understanding, Affordance Flow Matching (AFM), and Grasp Flow Matching (GFM) with an integrated optimization module.

#### 3.1. Generalizable-Instructive Affordance (GIA)

The core of AffordDexGrasp is the Generalizable-Instructive Affordance (GIA), an intermediate representation designed to be both generalizable to unseen categories and instructive for dexterous grasp generation.
- **Rationale:** The authors observe a trade-off: fine-grained contact maps provide detailed guidance for grasping but are difficult to generalize to novel objects. Conversely, coarse representations like object part segmentation are more generalizable but often lack the precision to effectively guide high-DOF dexterous hands. GIA aims to balance these characteristics.
- **Definition:** GIA is defined as a representation that aligns general graspable affordances of objects with category-agnostic semantic attributes. Unlike contact maps that describe specific hand contact areas, GIA represents all potential graspable regions on an object that share the same semantic intention (e.g., "handle," "body," and "grasp from right"). This abstraction is intended to reduce the complexity of learning intricate hand contact patterns, facilitating generalization.
- **Ground Truth Generation:** To create the ground truth for GIA, grasps on an object with identical intentions, contact parts, and grasp directions are grouped into semantic categories. For each grasp in a group, a contact map is computed by determining the minimum distance from each scene point to any hand surface point. These individual contact maps are then unified by taking the minimum distance value across all grasps within the semantic group, effectively creating a union of potential contact areas. Finally, Gaussian filtering is applied to this union map to ensure smoothness.

#### 3.2. AffordDexGrasp Framework Stages

##### 3.2.1. Intention Pre-Understanding

To address the variability in human language commands, the framework incorporates a pre-understanding stage assisted by a Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM), specifically GPT-4o. The MLLM processes user language commands and a rendered RGB image of the scene. It is prompted to extract key cues: the object category, the user's intention (e.g., "use it," "hold"), the specific contacting part of the object (e.g., "handle"), and the desired grasp direction (discretized into six predefined world coordinates and then translated from image coordinates). The MLLM is configured to output a concise, information-dense sentence (e.g., "use the mug from the left by contacting the handle"), which serves as a consistent input for subsequent modules. Chain-of-Thought (CoT) and visual prompts are utilized to enhance the MLLM's reasoning capabilities.

##### 3.2.2. Affordance Flow Matching (AFM)

AFM is a generative model based on conditional flow matching, responsible for generating GIA maps conditioned on the MLLM's output.
- **Process:** It learns a velocity vector field that transforms a noised affordance map (sampled from a Gaussian distribution) into the target ground truth affordance map over time.
- **Inputs:** The input scene object point cloud is encoded using Pointnet++. The language instruction is encoded by a pre-trained CLIP model, and the direction vector and time embedding are processed by Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs). Noisy affordance maps are concatenated with object features to provide spatial and semantic context.
- **Architecture:** A Perceiver IO Transformer integrates these features, using language and direction features as latent arrays and noisy affordance features as input/output arrays.
- **Loss Function:** The model is trained using an L2 loss between the predicted velocity vector field and the target flow (the difference between the target and noisy affordance).

##### 3.2.3. Grasp Flow Matching (GFM)

GFM is another flow matching model designed to generate dexterous grasp poses (global rotation, translation, and joint angles) under the guidance of the MLLM output and the AFM-generated affordance.
- **Process:** The model learns to predict a velocity vector field to generate the final grasp pose from a noised initial grasp. During training, it estimates the target pose in a single step.
- **Inputs:** Condition features include object affordance features (derived by processing the AFM-generated affordance map and point clouds with PointNet++), as well as the language and direction features from the MLLM.
- **Loss Functions:** Training incorporates an L2 loss for grasp pose regression, a Chamfer distance loss to minimize discrepancies between generated and ground-truth hand shapes, and a fingertip keypoint loss to align fingertip positions. Notably, an object penetration loss is *excluded* during GFM training. This decision is made to prevent hindering intention alignment and generation diversity, with penetration handled in a subsequent optimization stage.

##### 3.2.4. Affordance-guided Grasp Optimization

A non-parametric, test-time adaptation (TTA) stage is introduced to refine the generated grasps. This optimization aims to enhance grasp quality (e.g., reducing object penetration) while preserving the semantic intention.
- **Objectives:** The optimization minimizes a weighted sum of several loss terms:
 1. **Affordance contact loss:** Encourages candidate hand contact points to align with the affordance area.
 2. **Affordance fingertip loss:** Maintains the positions of fingertips that are already within the affordance area.
 3. **Object penetration loss:** Penalizes the penetration of the hand into the object.
 4. **Self-penetration loss:** Penalizes penetration among different parts of the hand itself.
 5. **Joint limitation loss:** Ensures joint angles remain within physical limits.
This non-parametric approach is intended to mitigate performance degradation often observed in learning-based TTA methods when encountering out-of-domain open-set samples.

#### 3.3. Open-set Dexterous Grasp Dataset

To evaluate the framework, an open-set dataset was constructed based on existing language-guided dexterous grasp datasets.
- **Scale:** The dataset comprises 33 categories, 1536 objects, 1909 scenes, and 43,504 dexterous grasps for both Shadow Hand and Leap Hand.
- **Open-set Splits:** Two independent open-set splits, A and B, were created. For each split, a distinct set of categories was designated as "unseen" during training. 80% of objects from "seen" categories were used for training, while the remaining 20% from "seen" categories and all objects from "unseen" categories were reserved for testing.
- **Scene Construction:** Scenes were generated using Blenderproc to simulate a tabletop environment. Objects were placed on shelves to avoid hand-table collisions, and a physics engine was used to ensure plausible object placements and valid grasping poses.
- **Data Rendering:** Five RGB-D cameras captured partial depth maps, which were merged into a global point cloud. Realistic RGB images for MLLM processing were generated by applying textures to objects using Paint3D.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents comprehensive experiments in both simulated and real-world environments, evaluating the AffordDexGrasp framework against existing methods across metrics for intention consistency, grasp quality, and grasp diversity.

#### 4.1. Evaluation Metrics

- **Intention Consistency:** Measured by Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) to assess distribution similarity between generated and ground truth grasps; R-Precision to evaluate semantic alignment between language instructions and generated grasps; and Chamfer Distance (CD) to quantify the discrepancy between predicted and target hand point clouds.
- **Grasp Quality:** Assessed using success rate (Suc.) in Isaac Gym and the Q1 metric for grasp stability. Maximal penetration depth (Pen.) measures hand-object interpenetration.
- **Grasp Diversity:** Quantified by the standard deviations of translation (δt), rotation (δr), and joint angles (δq) for grasps generated under identical conditions.

#### 4.2. Comparison with State-of-the-Art Methods

Table 1 demonstrates that the AffordDexGrasp framework exhibits superior performance compared to all baseline methods (ContactGen, Contact2Grasp, GraspCVAE, SceneDiffuser, DexGYS) in open-set scenarios.
- **Intention Consistency:** AffordDexGrasp achieved lower FID and CD values, and notably higher R-Precision (e.g., Top-1 R-Precision of 0.480 on Open Set A and 0.532 on Open Set B, compared to baselines in the range of 0.164-0.397). This indicates better alignment with language instructions and closer resemblance to ground truth grasp distributions.
- **Grasp Quality:** The framework attained higher success rates (45.1% on Open Set A, 38.9% on Open Set B) and Q1 scores (0.0531, 0.0352), coupled with lower maximal penetration depths.
- **Grasp Diversity:** The framework also maintained a reasonable level of diversity (δt, δr, δq values).
The observed performance degradation of previous language-guided and contact-based methods in open-set scenarios supports the authors' hypothesis regarding their limited generalization capabilities. Furthermore, in close-set experiments (Table 3), AffordDexGrasp also demonstrated superior performance across intention consistency and grasp quality metrics.

#### 4.3. Necessity of Generalizable-Instructive Affordance

Ablation studies (Table 2) validate the role of the Generalizable-Instructive Affordance (GIA).
- **Without Affordance:** The model without any affordance input performed poorly, indicating that a direct mapping from language to grasp actions struggles to generalize.
- **Object Part vs. Contact Map:** Using object part segmentation (even ground truth) resulted in lower grasp quality and intention consistency compared to GIA, suggesting it is too coarse to effectively guide dexterous grasps. Conversely, contact maps (especially predicted ones) showed a significant drop in performance in generalization (higher CD, lower Top1), confirming their fine-grained nature hinders open-set generalization. Table 8 further showed that GIA achieves better Point IOU consistency on test sets compared to contact maps, indicating stronger generalization in affordance prediction.
- **GIA Performance:** Only GIA, whether predicted or ground truth, demonstrated a balance between generalization and instructiveness, leading to improved intention consistency and grasp quality.

#### 4.4. Effectiveness of Each Component

Further ablation studies (Table 2) highlight the contribution of individual components:
- **Intention Pre-Understanding (MLLM):** Incorporating MLLM for key cue extraction and direction reasoning (labeled "w MLLM") consistently improved intention consistency (lower FID/CD, higher Top1) and grasp quality (higher Q1, lower Pen.) compared to directly using user commands or omitting direction information. This suggests MLLM's role in enhancing generalization across diverse user commands.
- **Affordance-guided Grasp Optimization:** The proposed non-parametric optimization method significantly reduced maximal penetration depth (e.g., 0.293 cm on Open Set A) and maintained high Q1 scores and intention consistency. It outperformed alternative post-processing methods like learning-based ContactNet and RefineNet, as well as direct training with penetration loss, which was observed to negatively impact intention alignment and diversity. Visualization (Figure 11) confirms the optimization's role in preventing object penetration while preserving original intention.
- **Flow Matching vs. Diffusion Models:** Experiments (Table 6) indicated that Flow Matching-based models performed better than Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPM and DDIM) in terms of FID, CD, Top1, Q1, and Pen. Furthermore, Flow Matching offered faster inference speeds (Table 7).

#### 4.5. Generalization Capabilities

- **Zero-shot and One-shot Generalization (Table 4):** The framework demonstrated improved zero-shot generalization over a baseline without affordance. In a one-shot setting (adding one object from each unseen category to training), AffordDexGrasp showed a more substantial performance improvement, particularly for novel categories.
- **Real-world Experiments (Table 5, Figure 15):** Real-world experiments using a Leap Hand and Kinova Gen3 arm verified the framework's simulation-to-reality transferability. AffordDexGrasp achieved a higher success rate compared to the DexGYS baseline for various unseen objects, indicating its practical applicability and robust generalization.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of robotics and computer vision by addressing the open-set language-guided dexterous grasp problem, which has remained largely unexplored for dexterous hands due to its inherent complexities. The work introduces a novel framework designed to overcome the challenge of generalizing grasp generation to unseen object categories guided by natural language commands.

The primary technical contribution is the conceptualization and implementation of the **Generalizable-Instructive Affordance (GIA)**. This intermediate representation effectively bridges the semantic gap between high-level human language and low-level robot actions. By balancing the need for generalization with the requirement for specific guidance for multi-finger hands, GIA allows the framework to generalize by aligning object local structures with category-agnostic semantic attributes, thereby facilitating grasp generation for novel objects.

The AffordDexGrasp framework integrates several components:
- **MLLM-assisted intention pre-understanding** enhances the system's ability to interpret diverse user commands.
- **Cascaded flow matching models (AFM and GFM)** provide an efficient and effective mechanism for generating affordance maps and subsequent dexterous grasp poses. The comparison with diffusion models indicates computational advantages and performance benefits of the flow matching approach.
- **Non-parametric affordance-guided grasp optimization** improves grasp quality and robustness without compromising semantic intent, a crucial aspect for practical deployment.

The experimental results, spanning both simulation and real-world scenarios, demonstrate that AffordDexGrasp consistently outperforms existing state-of-the-art methods in terms of intention consistency and grasp quality in open-set environments. Its capability for zero-shot and one-shot generalization indicates a significant step toward developing truly adaptable robotic systems. The creation of a dedicated open-set dataset also provides a valuable resource for future research in this domain.

The potential impact of this work is significant for advancing human-robot interaction and robot manipulation. By enabling robots to robustly grasp and interact with novel objects based on natural language instructions, the framework could lead to more versatile and intelligent robotic assistants capable of operating in unstructured, dynamic environments, reducing the need for extensive, category-specific data collection and programming.
