# A Survey on Efficient Training of Transformers

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers systematically categorize techniques for efficient Transformer training, offering the first comprehensive overview of methods that reduce computational cost, memory footprint, and optimize for hardware co-design. The work compiles strategies such as sparse training, various parallelism approaches, and low-precision computation to mitigate the substantial resource demands of large-scale models.

## Problem

- Large Transformer models incur immense computational costs, leading to high energy consumption and making cutting-edge AI inaccessible to many.
- Training these models demands an exponentially growing memory footprint, posing significant hardware and scaling challenges.
- A lack of systematic overview existed for the diverse efficient training techniques developed to mitigate these Transformer efficiency issues.

## Method

- A systematic review and organization of existing literature on efficient Transformer training methods is presented.
- Techniques are categorized into three main themes: ==computation efficiency==, ==memory efficiency==, and ==hardware/algorithm co-design==.
- Methods are analyzed and compared based on their mechanisms for reducing computational load and memory footprint during training.

## Results

- The survey consolidates and explains diverse methods for computation efficiency, including advanced optimizers (e.g., Lion, SAM), effective initialization (e.g., Fixup, ReZero), **sparse training**, large batch training, incremental learning, and data selection.
- It details memory efficiency techniques such as various parallelism strategies (Data, Model, Tensor, Pipeline), **quantized training** (e.g., AMP, FP8), rematerialization, offloading (e.g., ZeRO-Offload), and parameter-efficient tuning (e.g., LoRA).
- Hardware/algorithm co-design approaches are highlighted, covering sparse matrix multiplication, hardware-aware low-precision computations (e.g., 4-bit queries), and optimized attention mechanisms (e.g., **FlashAttention**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Efficient Transformer training necessitates a holistic strategy, integrating algorithmic optimizations for computation and memory with hardware/algorithm co-design principles.
> - Diverse techniques, ranging from advanced optimizers and sparse training to various parallelism strategies and parameter-efficient tuning, contribute to making large models more manageable.
> - Further advancements require a focus on joint training and deployment optimization, on-device training, and the establishment of a standardized benchmarking framework.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of deep learning, propelled by architectures like Transformers, has achieved unprecedented success across various domains, from natural language processing (NLP) to computer vision. However, this success comes at a steep price: an escalating demand for computational resources and memory. The introduction of the paper starkly highlights two "pressing challenges": the intensive computation required for training large Transformer models and the exponentially growing training memory footprint.

The authors cite alarming statistics, such as the energy cost of training a single Transformer model with neural architecture search (equivalent to the lifetime emissions of five cars) and the immense financial and temporal resources required for models like GPT-3 (estimated at 355 GPU-years and over $4.6M for a single training run). These figures underscore a critical bottleneck preventing the broader adoption and sustainability of cutting-edge AI. The paper frames this challenge within the context of "Green AI," advocating for more computationally and energy-efficient deep learning to democratize AI beyond large, rich enterprises.

Against this backdrop, the survey positions itself as a crucial contribution by systematically organizing and analyzing the diverse approaches developed to mitigate these efficiency issues. Before this work, no comprehensive overview specifically focused on the *efficient training* of Transformers existed, despite the rapid proliferation of individual techniques. The survey therefore acts as a vital consolidation point, offering researchers and practitioners a structured understanding of the current state-of-the-art. It bridges the gap between the burgeoning complexity of Transformer models and the practical limitations of available hardware and energy resources. By doing so, it contributes to the overarching goal of making advanced AI more accessible, sustainable, and broadly applicable, which is a major thrust in contemporary AI research and development.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind this survey are explicitly stated in the abstract and introduction:

1. **Systematic Overview:** To provide the "first systematic overview of the efficient training of Transformers." This objective emphasizes the need for a structured and comprehensive compilation of existing methods, given the fragmented nature of research in this rapidly evolving area.
2. **Cover Recent Progress:** To cover "the recent progress in acceleration arithmetic and hardware," with a specific focus on the former. This highlights the intention to synthesize the latest advancements, ensuring the survey remains relevant and informative for current research.
3. **Analyze and Compare Methods:** To "analyze and compare methods that save computation and memory costs for intermediate tensors during training, together with techniques on hardware/algorithm co-design." This goes beyond mere listing, aiming for a critical examination of the techniques, understanding their mechanisms, and identifying their comparative advantages.
4. **Discuss Challenges and Future Research:** To finally "discuss challenges and promising areas for future research." This forward-looking objective aims to guide subsequent research efforts by identifying gaps, potential synergies, and critical directions for innovation in efficient Transformer training.
5. **Address Pressing Challenges (Motivation):** The core motivation stems from the "huge requirement on computing resources" by recent Transformer advances. This translates into two pressing challenges:
 - **Intensive Computation:** Large models require immense computational power, leading to high energy consumption (Green AI concerns) and exorbitant costs, thus limiting access to cutting-edge AI to only well-resourced entities.
 - **Exponentially Growing Memory:** The increasing size of Transformer models (up to hundreds of billions or even trillions of parameters) necessitates memory-efficient training techniques to fit models on available hardware and reduce data communication overheads.

In essence, the authors are motivated by the desire to make Transformer training faster, at lower cost, and to higher accuracy by optimizing the use of computation and memory resources, ultimately striving for a more democratized and sustainable AI future.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

This paper is a survey, and as such, its methodology is centered around the systematic review, categorization, and analysis of existing literature rather than presenting novel experimental work. The authors' approach can be broken down as follows:

1. **Literature Review and Selection:** The authors extensively reviewed a broad range of literature pertaining to the efficient training of Transformers. While the exact selection criteria are not explicitly detailed (e.g., specific publication venues or date ranges), the references indicate a focus on prominent conferences (e.g., NeurIPS, ICLR, ICML, CVPR) and arXiv preprints, encompassing works from 2015 up to early 2023. This ensures coverage of foundational methods as well as the most recent breakthroughs.
2. **Categorization Framework:** The core of the methodology lies in establishing a clear and logical categorization framework for the diverse efficient training techniques. The authors divide these methods into three main overarching themes, with further sub-categories:
 - **Computation Efficiency:** Focuses on reducing the computational load during training. This category includes:
 - Optimization (e.g., optimizers like AdamW, Lion; initialization techniques like Fixup, ReZero)
 - Sparse Training (e.g., Lottery Ticket Hypothesis, dynamic sparsity)
 - Overparameterization
 - Large Batch Training (e.g., LARS, LAMB)
 - Incremental Learning (e.g., progressive layer stacking, stochastic depth)
 - Data Selection (e.g., Token Masking, Importance Sampling)
 - **Memory Efficiency:** Addresses the challenge of memory consumption for large models. This category includes:
 - Parallelism (Data Parallelism, Model Parallelism, Tensor Parallelism, Pipeline Parallelism)
 - Quantized Training (e.g., AMP, FP8, Activation Compressed Training)
 - Rematerialization and Offloading (e.g., checkpointing, ZeRO-Offload)
 - Parameter-efficient Tuning (e.g., addition-based, reparameterization-based like LoRA)
 - Open-source frameworks (e.g., DeepSpeed, Colossal-AI, Megatron-LM)
 - **Hardware/Algorithm Co-design:** Explores techniques that optimize training by considering the interplay between software algorithms and hardware capabilities. This category includes:
 - Sparse Matrix Multiplication (e.g., SpGEMM, custom accelerators)
 - Hardware-aware Low-precision (e.g., 4-bit queries, DOTA)
 - Efficient Attention (e.g., A3, ELSA, FlashAttention)
3. **Analysis and Comparison:** Within each category, the authors briefly describe the core idea of each technique, cite seminal or representative works, and highlight their mechanisms for improving efficiency. For instance, they explain how sparse training works by identifying "winning tickets" or how different parallelism strategies distribute computation and memory.
4. **Scope Definition:** The survey explicitly defines its scope, focusing on "arithmetic innovations that improve the training efficiency of Transformers" and "hardware/algorithm co-design advances." It also states what is *not* covered extensively, such as "hardware accelerator design" as a broad class, indicating a deliberate focus on algorithmic and systems-level techniques directly impacting training.
5. **Future Directions:** The methodology concludes by synthesizing the current state to identify open challenges and promising avenues for future research, providing a roadmap for continued innovation in the field.

This structured approach allows the survey to provide a clear, comprehensive, and organized understanding of a complex and multifaceted problem space, making it a valuable resource for navigating the landscape of efficient Transformer training.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The survey systematically categorizes and summarizes a wide array of techniques for efficient Transformer training, revealing distinct strategies to tackle computation, memory, and hardware-specific bottlenecks. The main findings are structured according to the three primary categories:

**A. Computation Efficiency:**
The authors identify several algorithmic innovations designed to reduce the computational cost and accelerate convergence:

- **Optimization:** This includes advanced optimizers like AdamW and the more recent Lion, which demonstrates faster convergence and memory efficiency. Sharpness-aware minimization (SAM) is also discussed for improving generalization, with techniques proposed to mitigate its computational overhead.
- **Initialization:** Methods like Fixup, ReZero, SkipInit, and T-Fixup are presented as crucial for stabilizing training, enabling higher learning rates, and accelerating convergence by ensuring proper gradient norms or initializing layers close to identity functions.
- **Sparse Training:** This involves training sparse subnetworks from scratch, inspired by the Lottery Ticket Hypothesis (LTH). Techniques include finding sparse networks at initialization, identifying "early-bird" tickets, and dynamic pruning/growing schedules throughout training.
- **Overparameterization:** The survey notes that overparameterization, while seemingly inefficient, can accelerate convergence and improve generalization. This insight is leveraged to train large models and then compress them.
- **Large Batch Training:** Methods like LARS and LAMB enable the use of larger batch sizes to reduce iterations, improving resource utilization through layer-wise adaptive learning rates. Weight averaging and compute-optimal scaling laws are also discussed.
- **Incremental Learning:** Approaches that break down training into easier sub-problems, such as progressively stacking layers (BERT pre-training), stochastic depth, or increasing input resolution, are shown to accelerate training.
- **Data Selection:** Strategies to make training data more efficient include Token Masking (for MLM/MIM to reduce sequence length and computation) and Importance Sampling (prioritizing informative examples based on gradient norms or self-supervised metrics).

**B. Memory Efficiency:**
To address the enormous memory footprint of large Transformers, the survey highlights:

- **Parallelism:** This is a fundamental technique, encompassing:
 - **Data Parallelism (DP):** Distributes data across devices, suitable for small to moderate models.
 - **Model Parallelism (MP):** Allocates model subgraphs across devices, good for memory but often low in communication/computation efficiency.
 - **Tensor Parallelism (TP):** Partitions tensor operations within a layer (e.g., Megatron-LM).
 - **Pipeline Parallelism (PP):** Splits layers across devices, processing micro-batches in a pipeline (e.g., GPipe, torchgpipe).
 - **Combined Parallelism:** Advanced frameworks like Megatron-LM* and DeepSpeed combine DP, TP, and PP to scale to thousands of GPUs.
- **Quantized Training:** Training models with reduced precision (e.g., FP16, FP8, INT8) significantly reduces memory and often accelerates computation. Automatic Mixed Precision (AMP) is a widely adopted approach. Activation Compressed Training (ACT) specifically targets reducing activation memory.
- **Rematerialization and Offloading:**
 - **Rematerialization (Checkpointing):** Stores a fraction of activations and recomputes others during the backward pass, trading computation for memory.
 - **Offloading:** Utilizes external memory (e.g., CPU RAM) as an extension of GPU memory, carefully optimizing data transfer (e.g., ZeRO-Offload).
- **Parameter-efficient Tuning (PET):** For pre-trained models, PET fine-tunes only a small subset of parameters (freezing the rest), drastically cutting storage and memory. This includes addition-based methods (e.g., adding adapter modules) and reparameterization-based methods (e.g., LoRA, BitFit) that yield no inference overhead.
- **Open-source frameworks:** The paper acknowledges the critical role of platforms like Microsoft DeepSpeed, HPC-AI Tech Colossal-AI, and Nvidia Megatron-LM in implementing and democratizing these large-scale training techniques.

**C. Hardware/Algorithm Co-design:**
Recognizing the synergy between software and hardware, the survey details:

- **Sparse Matrix Multiplication (SpGEMM):** Leverages the inherent sparsity in attention matrices to reduce computation. The challenge of irregular sparsity often requires specialized hardware accelerators (FPGAs, ASICs) or custom architectures (e.g., OuterSPACE, ViTCoD).
- **Hardware-aware Low-precision:** Implements low-precision computations (e.g., 4-bit, fixed-point integers) directly in hardware for significant power and speed advantages, often combined with pruning or low-rank approximation (e.g., Sanger, DOTA).
- **Efficient Attention:** Techniques that optimize the attention mechanism at a fundamental level for hardware. Examples include A3 and ELSA, which reduce computation by selecting relevant keys, and FlashAttention, which minimizes I/O communication between GPU memory layers.

In summary, the survey finds that efficient Transformer training is a multi-faceted problem requiring a holistic approach combining sophisticated optimization algorithms, intelligent data handling, innovative memory management strategies, and hardware-conscious algorithm design.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This survey holds significant importance for the deep learning community due to its comprehensive and systematic compilation of efficient training techniques for Transformers. Its potential impact is multifaceted:

1. **Consolidation of Knowledge:** By providing the "first systematic overview," the survey serves as a critical reference point for researchers and practitioners. In a field characterized by rapid advancements and scattered literature, this consolidation saves immense time and effort, allowing individuals to quickly grasp the landscape of efficient training methods.
2. **Democratization of AI:** The paper explicitly highlights the high costs and resource demands of training large Transformers as a barrier to "democratizing AI." By detailing techniques that make training faster, cheaper, and less memory-intensive, the survey directly contributes to making state-of-the-art AI more accessible to a wider community, including smaller research groups, startups, and individuals with limited resources. This has a direct impact on fostering innovation beyond well-funded organizations.
3. **Guidance for Researchers:** The structured categorization and detailed discussion of various techniques offer a clear roadmap for new researchers entering the field or experienced ones seeking to apply efficiency methods. It helps in understanding the trade-offs (e.g., computation vs. memory) and selecting appropriate techniques for specific scenarios.
4. **Inspiration for Future Work:** The concluding section on "Future Research" is particularly impactful. The authors identify four promising directions:
 - **Joint training and deployment efficiency optimization:** Moving towards models that are efficient both in training and adaptable for diverse deployment platforms and tasks (e.g., elastic supernetworks).
 - **On-device training:** Developing methods for resource-constrained edge devices to address privacy and latency issues.
 - **Combine efficient approximation techniques:** Synergistically integrating methods like pruning, low-rank factorization, lightweight architectures, and dynamic networks for compounded benefits.
 - **Standard benchmark:** The crucial need for a standardized evaluation framework to objectively compare and accelerate the adoption of efficient training methods.
 These recommendations provide a clear agenda for the next generation of research, helping to focus efforts on high-impact areas.
5. **Promoting Sustainable AI (Green AI):** By emphasizing the carbon footprint and energy consumption of large models, the survey implicitly promotes the development and adoption of "Green AI" practices. Its insights can lead to more environmentally conscious AI development.
6. **Catalyst for Framework Development:** The acknowledgment of open-source frameworks (DeepSpeed, Colossal-AI, Megatron-LM) as enablers reinforces the practical utility of the discussed techniques and encourages further development of robust, user-friendly tools that integrate these advancements.

In essence, "A Survey on Efficient Training of Transformers" is more than just a literature review; it is a strategic document that maps the current landscape, articulates the critical challenges, and illuminates pathways for future innovation, ultimately aiming to make powerful AI models more sustainable, accessible, and pervasive.
