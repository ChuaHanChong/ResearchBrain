# PAIWorld: A 3D-Consistent World Foundation Model for Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> The PAIWorld framework introduces a world foundation model capable of generating multi-view videos with robust 3D consistency, designed for robotic manipulation tasks. It achieves state-of-the-art performance on robotic benchmarks, demonstrating improved action-conditioned video generation and reducing multi-view 3D reconstruction error by 2.64 MEt3R points on the AgiBot-World benchmark.

## Problem

- Existing multi-view world models for robotics lack robust 3D consistency across viewpoints, leading to object drift and misalignment crucial for manipulation tasks.
- Current approaches often concatenate multi-view tokens without explicit inter-view communication, hindering coordinated predictions and conflict resolution.
- These models typically lack a 3D geometric prior, resulting in visually plausible but physically inconsistent outputs across views.

## Method

- **PAIWorld** integrates Geometry-Aware Cross-View Attention blocks, employing Geo-RoPE to encode 3D camera geometry and facilitate explicit inter-view information exchange.
- It uses Latent 3D-REPA, a token-relation distillation objective, to align the model's intermediate representations with features from frozen 3D foundation models like Depth Anything 3.
- This framework jointly leverages architectural components for communication and a training objective for geometric supervision to enforce 3D consistency.

## Results

- PAIWorld secured 1st place on the WorldArena benchmark with an EWMScore of **72.31%** and 2nd place on AgiBot-Challenge2026, demonstrating strong action-conditioned generation.
- It achieved the best MEt3R score of **14.20** on the AgiBot-World benchmark, showing a **2.64** point improvement in 3D reconstruction error for text-conditioned generation.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the combined application of Geometry-Aware Cross-View Attention and Latent 3D-REPA yielded a super-additive gain in 3D consistency metrics.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Robust multi-view 3D consistency in world models requires both an explicit inter-view communication mechanism and a dedicated 3D geometric supervisory signal.
> - Embedding 3D geometric information directly into attention mechanisms (Geo-RoPE) using both ray and pose components enables accurate pixel-level and view-level biases.
> - Distilling relational structures from a pre-trained, 3D-aware feature extractor significantly improves the world model's understanding of 3D geometry.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of artificial intelligence has seen the emergence of world foundation models (WFMs) as a class of powerful simulators capable of modeling physical environments. These models learn to predict future visual observations conditioned on inputs such as actions or text, thereby serving as simulators for applications including model-based planning, policy evolution, and data synthesis in robotic learning. Recent advancements, particularly with diffusion-transformer (DiT) architectures trained on large-scale video data, have demonstrated the capacity to generate temporally coherent and physically plausible visual rollouts, positioning WFMs as a potential backbone for embodied intelligence systems.

However, the authors identify a critical gap in the existing WFM landscape concerning multi-view 3D consistency. While many WFMs, such as Sora, Cosmos, CogVideoX, and Vista, generate high-quality temporal predictions, they are primarily designed for single-view operation. Robotic manipulation systems inherently rely on multiple cameras—including egocentric, eye-to-hand, and wrist-mounted views—to capture complementary geometric and semantic information for policy learning. When a WFM is employed as a simulator for such systems, it must generate future observations across all these viewpoints while maintaining strict 3D consistency. This implies that objects should appear at geometrically compatible locations with coherent depth and texture across all views and time steps. Existing multi-view world models, like Genie and iVideoGPT, typically approach this by concatenating view tokens without explicit geometric reasoning. This "flat concatenation" strategy treats multi-view tokens similarly to temporal tokens, implicitly expecting the model to discover cross-view correspondences from data. This approach is noted to become unreliable as the number of viewpoints and scene complexity increase, leading to issues such as cross-view object drift, depth contradictions, and texture misalignment. These inconsistencies can propagate errors into downstream planning and control in robotics.

The current work distinguishes itself from other lines of research. Unlike object-centric multi-view generation methods (e.g., Zero-1-to-3, SyncDreamer, MVDream, SV3D), PAIWorld focuses on complex, cluttered scenes involving manipulators and dynamic backgrounds, which are typical in robotic manipulation. Furthermore, while previous 3D generation methods often synthesize static objects or short orbital trajectories with dense, smoothly varying viewpoints, PAIWorld addresses dynamic, temporally evolving dynamics driven by text or actions, with few fixed, wide-baseline cameras that offer limited view overlap—a challenging scenario for implicit correspondence learning. The paper also differentiates from camera control in video generation methods by prioritizing multi-view consistency rather than single-view camera trajectory control.

PAIWorld leverages recent advances in 3D representations and geometric reconstruction, such as Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF), 3D Gaussian Splatting, and foundational models like the Depth Anything series, which provide robust 3D-aware features. It also builds upon the REPA framework, extending its principle of aligning intermediate representations of diffusion transformers from 2D image generation to multi-view video world models, specifically for injecting geometric consistency. By addressing the explicit need for inter-view communication and a 3D geometric prior, PAIWorld aims to bridge the identified gap, providing a more robust and physically plausible world model suitable for complex robotic manipulation tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a world foundation model that can generate multi-view videos with robust 3D consistency, specifically tailored for robotic manipulation applications. The motivation for this work stems from two fundamental deficiencies identified in existing multi-view world modeling approaches:

1. **Absence of an Explicit Inter-View Communication Mechanism**: Current multi-view world models often concatenate tokens from different viewpoints without providing a dedicated pathway for these viewpoints to exchange information during the generation process. This "flat concatenation" strategy forces each view's tokens to attend to the entire sequence indiscriminately, making it challenging for the model to distinguish between same-view and cross-view tokens. Consequently, each viewpoint effectively generates its observations in isolation, lacking the means to coordinate predictions or resolve conflicts in cross-view object representation. This leads to inconsistencies such as object drift and misalignment across different camera perspectives.

2. **Absence of a 3D Geometric Prior**: Even if a mechanism for inter-view communication were present, existing models typically lack a supervisory signal or inductive bias that specifies what constitutes geometrically correct 3D structure across views. Without this guidance, any cross-view information exchange tends to gravitate towards superficial shortcuts, such as matching color palettes or copying textures, rather than learning and enforcing genuine 3D correspondences. This results in visual outputs that may appear plausible in 2D but violate physical plausibility when considered in a 3D context across multiple views.

The authors argue that resolving both these deficiencies simultaneously is necessary and sufficient to achieve genuine multi-view 3D consistency. They propose that an information pathway across views must coexist with a geometric signal that steers this pathway. A communication pathway without geometric guidance is insufficient because it cannot guarantee that the exchanged content respects 3D geometry and may lead to learning trivial shortcuts. Conversely, a geometric prior without an inter-view pathway is also insufficient, as it might improve each view's 3D awareness in isolation, but the resulting constraints would have no effective route to propagate and enforce consistency across viewpoints. Therefore, the core motivation is to design a framework where information can flow between views, and this flow is guided by an explicit understanding of 3D geometry.

In summary, the key objectives are to:
- Articulate the fundamental limitations of existing multi-view world models in terms of inter-view communication and 3D geometric priors.
- Propose PAIWorld, a novel framework that integrates architectural components for explicit inter-view communication and a training objective for 3D geometric supervision.
- Demonstrate that PAIWorld achieves state-of-the-art multi-view 3D consistency on robotic manipulation benchmarks, thereby enhancing the physical plausibility of imagined trajectories for model-based planning and other downstream robotic tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PAIWorld is designed as a framework built upon a Diffusion Transformer (DiT) based flow matching backbone, augmented with two technical pillars realized by three modular components.

**Base World Model Architecture:**
The framework utilizes a DiT architecture trained with a flow matching objective, operating in the latent space of a pretrained video Variational Autoencoder (VAE). Similar to Wan2.1, a spatial-temporal VAE compresses video both spatially and temporally into a compact latent representation, reducing token count for multi-view video modeling. The model learns a velocity field to transport samples from a noise distribution towards the data distribution. Conditioning signals (text embeddings or spatial action maps) are injected into each DiT block via Adaptive Layer Normalization (AdaLN). For multi-view generation, tokens from all views are concatenated, but unlike naive approaches, PAIWorld introduces explicit mechanisms for geometric reasoning.

**Pillar 1: Inter-View Communication Pathway (Architectural)**
This pillar aims to create an explicit information exchange pathway across views, biased by camera geometry. It is realized through two components:

1. **Geometry-Aware Cross-View Attention blocks**: These blocks are strategically interleaved within selected DiT layers. For a given temporal frame, feature maps from all views are processed. Each view's queries and keys are first projected and then rotated by Geo-RoPE using its specific camera geometry. The query from one view then attends to the concatenated keys and values from *all* views. This design ensures that tokens observing the same 3D point across different views receive higher attention weights due to their geometrically compatible rotations. The attention mechanism includes a gate, initialized to zero via AdaLN-Zero, to incrementally introduce cross-view interaction while preserving the pretrained single-view model's initial state.

2. **Geometric Rotary Position Embedding (Geo-RoPE)**: This component encodes 3D geometric information into the attention mechanism using rotary position encoding. It employs a dual-component design where query and key vectors are split into a ray subspace and a pose subspace.
 - **Ray Component**: For each spatial location (pixel) within a view, a world-space ray direction is computed by unprojecting the pixel through the camera intrinsics and rotating by the inverse of the camera rotation. These 3D ray directions are cyclically expanded and used as position coordinates for RoPE rotation on the ray subspace. This enables fine-grained pixel-level geometric correspondences.
 - **Pose Component**: For each view, a 12-dimensional pose feature vector (Euler angles, translation, camera position, optical axis) is extracted. This pose vector, which is shared across all spatial positions within a view, is applied via RoPE to the pose subspace. This encodes view-level identity.
 The split application of RoPE prevents interference between the spatially-varying ray signal and the spatially-uniform pose signal, ensuring both pixel-level and view-level geometric biases are captured.

A **Spatial-Concat Self-Attention** mechanism also periodically flattens view and spatial dimensions into a single token axis. This allows each token to attend to all spatial positions across all views within the same temporal context, complementing the dedicated cross-view blocks.

**Pillar 2: Geometric Learning Signal (Training Objective)**
This pillar provides the explicit supervisory signal for 3D-consistent content, realized by one component:

1. **Latent 3D-REPA (3D Geometric Prior)**: This component introduces a token-relation distillation objective that aligns the DiT’s intermediate representations with features from frozen 3D foundation models.
 - **3D-Aware Feature Extraction**: Depth Anything 3 is used as the 3D-aware feature extractor. This model is chosen because it is trained under direct 3D geometric supervision (predicting depth, point maps, camera poses) and thus internalizes explicit 3D structure, making its features a reliable source of geometric knowledge. It also recovers camera intrinsics and extrinsics necessary for Geo-RoPE.
 - **Token Relation Distillation**: Instead of direct token-by-token regression, the method distills the pairwise relational structure between tokens. The DiT's intermediate feature map is projected to the feature dimension of Depth Anything 3. Anchor sampling is employed to reduce computational cost, where a random subset of K tokens are drawn as anchors, and cosine similarity is measured between every token and these anchors.
 - **Loss Granularity**: The distillation loss (`L_REPA`) is composed of two terms:
 - `L_spatial`: Aligns intra-frame relational structure (within a single time step, across all views and spatial positions).
 - `L_temporal`: Aligns temporal relational structure (across all frames, views, and spatial positions within the entire video clip).
 This distillation ensures that the DiT's features develop relations mirroring those of a geometry-aware encoder.

**Joint Mechanism and Training Objective:**
The core premise is that neither pillar alone is sufficient. The pathway enables information flow, while the objective ensures that this information is geometrically meaningful and 3D-consistent. Their combination establishes a reinforcing loop where the pathway, guided by Geo-RoPE, routes information along geometrically corresponding tokens within a shared 3D coordinate frame, and Latent 3D-REPA supervises this exchanged content to be faithful to true 3D structure.

The total training objective combines the flow matching loss (`L_diff`) with the Latent 3D-REPA distillation loss (`L_REPA`) as: `L_total = L_diff + λ · L_REPA`, where `λ` is set to 0.5. The Depth Anything 3 encoder remains frozen during training.

**Implementation Details:**
PAIWorld is built upon Cosmos-Predict2.5, a 14B parameter flow-matching DiT, using Cosmos-Reason1 as the text embedder. It was trained on a large-scale multi-view robotic manipulation dataset comprising approximately 2.5 million video clips from five sources (AgiBot-World, RoboMIND, Galaxea, RoboTwin, RoboCOIN) for 30,000 iterations using the AdamW optimizer on NVIDIA H200 GPUs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research demonstrates that PAIWorld achieves advanced multi-view 3D consistency on robotic manipulation benchmarks, outperforming existing state-of-the-art methods in various evaluation settings.

**Action-Conditioned Video Generation:**

- **WorldArena Benchmark**: PAIWorld ranked 1st overall, achieving an EWMScore of 72.31%. This score reflects a balanced performance across seven fine-grained metrics. Notably, PAIWorld secured the second-best Motion Quality score (80.45%), indicating that it generates temporally coherent and physically plausible dynamics crucial for action-conditioned rollouts. While other methods might excel in specific metrics, PAIWorld demonstrated consistent strength across all dimensions, including Visual Quality, Content Consistency, Physics Adherence, 3D Accuracy, and Controllability, contributing to its top aggregate score. Qualitative results illustrate the model's ability to produce physically plausible dynamics and consistent scene layouts over long prediction horizons, accurately tracking commanded robot actions.

- **AgiBot-Challenge2026 Benchmark**: PAIWorld achieved an EWMScore of 0.8245, placing second overall. Crucially, it attained the best Scene Consistency score (0.9041%), which suggests that its explicit cross-view geometric reasoning directly contributes to superior multi-view coherence when conditioned on actions. A high nDTW score of 0.9531 further validated the alignment between generated trajectories and ground-truth action sequences. Qualitative observations confirm that rollouts faithfully reflect commanded robot actions while maintaining visual sharpness and temporal coherence.

**Text-Conditioned Multi-View Generation:**

- **AgiBot-World Benchmark**: PAIWorld demonstrated superior performance, achieving the best score on 6 out of 7 metrics when compared against Genie-Envisioner, Cosmos-Predict2.5, and Wan2.1.
 - **Perceptual Quality**: PAIWorld recorded an SSIM of 0.7683 and an LPIPS of 0.1844, indicating higher perceptual fidelity and sharper, more structurally faithful frames compared to baselines.
 - **Distributional Fidelity**: It achieved an FID of 45.04 and a FVD of 175.78, reflecting that its generations more closely match the real data distribution in terms of both individual frames and temporal coherence.
 - **3D Geometric Consistency**: Most significantly, PAIWorld obtained the best MEt3R score of 14.20, directly quantifying a reduction in multi-view 3D reconstruction error. This metric, along with a Geometric consistency score of 0.4056 (lower is better), confirms that the integration of Geo-RoPE, Geometry-Aware Cross-View Attention, and Latent 3D-REPA successfully injects geometric consistency into the generation process. Qualitative comparisons highlight PAIWorld's capacity to produce multi-view frames with coherent object placement, depth structure, and texture alignment, in contrast to the object drift and texture misalignment observed in baseline models.

**Ablation Study:**

An ablation study on the AgiBot-World benchmark was conducted to evaluate the contributions of the architectural pathway (Geometry-Aware Cross-View Attention with Geo-RoPE, "CVA") and the geometric objective (Latent 3D-REPA, "REPA"). The results support the authors' central claim:
- Adding CVA alone resulted in a modest MEt3R improvement of 0.93.
- Adding REPA alone led to a modest MEt3R improvement of 0.72.
- The combination of both CVA and REPA yielded a significant MEt3R improvement of 2.64, which is substantially greater than the sum of individual gains (0.93 + 0.72 = 1.65). This super-additive effect empirically validates the reinforcing loop described by the authors, where the architectural pathway enables information flow and the geometric objective ensures its 3D consistency.
- Perceptual quality metrics (SSIM, LPIPS, FID) also demonstrated the most significant improvements when both components were present, indicating that geometric gains did not compromise visual fidelity.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The PAIWorld framework represents an advancement in the field of world foundation models for embodied artificial intelligence, particularly for robotic manipulation. Its primary significance lies in explicitly addressing the long-standing challenge of multi-view 3D consistency, which has been a limiting factor in leveraging WFMs for real-world robotic applications.

**Contribution to World Modeling and Embodied AI:**
By identifying and providing a joint solution to the deficiencies of lacking inter-view communication and 3D geometric priors, PAIWorld offers a more robust and physically plausible approach to simulating dynamic environments. The demonstrated state-of-the-art performance in 3D consistency metrics across both action- and text-conditioned generation settings suggests that PAIWorld generates imagined realities that are more faithful to physical laws and spatial relationships. This is a foundational step towards building AI agents that can truly understand and interact with the 3D physical world.

**Impact on Robotic Manipulation:**
The enhanced 3D consistency directly translates into several potential impacts for robotic systems:
- **Improved Model-Based Planning**: Robotic planning algorithms can rely on the physically plausible and geometrically coherent imagined trajectories generated by PAIWorld. This could lead to more effective and safer planning outcomes, as the simulated environment more accurately reflects real-world constraints.
- **Robust World Action Models (WAMs)**: The framework can enable stronger action-visual causal alignment in WAMs, which learn to predict the effects of actions in a simulated world. This means policies learned from these improved world models could be more generalizable and perform better in real-world scenarios.
- **Effective Multi-View Policy Post-training**: Policies trained or refined using multi-view data from PAIWorld are likely to be more effective, as they would operate within a consistent 3D context, reducing ambiguities and potential errors arising from inconsistent views.

**Broader Future Directions and Applications:**
The work opens several avenues for future research and applications:
- **Physics-Aware World Modeling**: Expanding beyond geometric consistency to incorporate complex physical interaction modeling, such as contact dynamics, deformable objects, and fluid simulation, could further enhance the realism and utility of WFMs.
- **Long-Horizon Planning**: The framework could be extended to maintain 3D consistency over extended temporal rollouts, which is critical for complex, multi-step robotic tasks and long-horizon planning scenarios. This might involve hierarchical or recurrent architectures.
- **World-Model-Driven Data Closed Loop**: A significant potential impact lies in creating a closed-loop system for embodied intelligence. PAIWorld could generate diverse imagined experiences from which a WAM learns to improve its policies. These improved policies could then gather higher-quality real-world data, which in turn refines the world model, leading to continuous self-improvement and autonomous evolution of embodied agents.
- **Industrial Manufacturing Foundation Models**: The principles developed in PAIWorld could be applied to industrial settings, enabling the development of foundation models for manufacturing. These could support applications such as dynamic scheduling of production lines and real-time control of manufacturing processes, where accurate physical simulation and multi-view monitoring are crucial for intelligent automation.

In conclusion, PAIWorld's contribution to achieving 3D-consistent multi-view generation is a significant technical achievement that enhances the foundational capabilities of world models, positioning them for more reliable and impactful deployment in complex physical AI and robotic applications.
