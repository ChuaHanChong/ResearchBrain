# REJEPA: A Novel Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture for Efficient Remote Sensing Image Retrieval

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the Indian Institute of Technology Bombay developed REJEPA, a joint-embedding predictive architecture for efficient remote sensing image retrieval. The framework learns high-level semantic features, achieving improved F1-scores on various datasets (e.g., 76.38% on BEN-14K S1) and reducing computational complexity by 40-60% compared to pixel-reconstruction methods.

## Problem

- Efficiently retrieving semantically relevant images from large and continuously growing remote sensing (RS) archives remains challenging.
- Existing contrastive self-supervised learning methods struggle with negative pair construction in RS, where distinct images can share high semantic similarity.
- Generative self-supervised methods, focused on pixel-level reconstruction, often fail to capture high-level semantics crucial for RS-CBIR and incur high computational complexity with large decoders.

## Method

- **REJEPA**, a Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) framework, learns representations by predicting target image features from context features.
- It utilizes a Siamese encoder-predictor design with a disjoint random masking strategy to enforce learning of non-trivial semantic dependencies.
- Variance-Invariance-Covariance Regularization (VICReg) is integrated to prevent encoder collapse by promoting diverse, decorrelated, and invariant feature embeddings.

## Results

- REJEPA achieved improved F1-scores for retrieval, notably **76.38%** on BEN-**14K** (S1 data), **73.53%** on FMoW-RGB, and **75.87%** on FMoW-Sentinel, often surpassing state-of-the-art baselines.
- The framework demonstrated computational efficiency, reducing model size by 10-**40%** and computational complexity by 40-**60%** compared to pixel-reconstruction MAE-based models.
- Visualization analysis revealed REJEPA's attention focused on salient geographic structures, and ablation studies confirmed the critical role of VICReg and a disjoint masking strategy for robust learning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Shifting the learning objective from pixel-level reconstruction to high-level semantic feature prediction in feature space enables more efficient and semantically rich representation learning.
> - Employing a disjoint random masking strategy for context and target regions is critical for forcing the model to learn meaningful, non-trivial semantic relationships.
> - Variance-Invariance-Covariance Regularization (VICReg) effectively prevents representation collapse, a common issue in non-contrastive self-supervised learning, by ensuring feature diversity and stability.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid expansion of remote sensing (RS) image archives necessitates the development of efficient and robust techniques for Content-Based Image Retrieval (CBIR). This work addresses the challenge of retrieving semantically relevant images from these large and continuously growing datasets. Deep learning (DL) methods, particularly self-supervised learning (SSL), have become central to CBIR by learning meaningful image representations without extensive manual annotation.

Within the SSL paradigm, two primary approaches have been explored in remote sensing: contrastive learning and generative methods. Contrastive learning methods, such as those employing Siamese networks, aim to learn discriminative features by aligning embeddings of semantically similar images while pushing apart those of dissimilar images. While effective, these methods often rely on negative pairs, which can be problematic in RS where distinct images may still share semantic similarities, thereby limiting their effectiveness.

Generative SSL methods, particularly masked image modeling (MIM) and masked autoencoders (MAEs), reconstruct missing pixels to learn representations. These models, often leveraging Vision Transformers (ViTs), have been adapted for RS tasks by incorporating domain-specific properties like scale invariance and temporal information. However, their emphasis on pixel-level reconstruction can limit their ability to capture high-level semantics, a crucial aspect in RS where diverse resolutions, dense objects, and environmental noise are prevalent. Furthermore, pixel-level reconstruction often entails high computational complexity due to the need for large decoders.

Feature predictive architectures, also known as Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs), represent an emerging SSL paradigm. These approaches shift the learning objective from pixel-level reconstruction to high-level semantic feature prediction. JEPAs utilize a Siamese encoder-predictor design to infer missing information directly in feature space, demonstrating efficiency gains and versatility across various modalities like audio, image, and text. Existing JEPA applications have shown competitive performance in downstream tasks and improved efficiency during pretraining by bypassing complex data augmentations or decoder networks. SAR-JEPA, for instance, has specifically applied JEPA concepts to Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR) data.

This research introduces REJEPA, a novel JEPA-based framework specifically designed for RS-CBIR. It positions itself as an alternative that addresses the limitations of both contrastive and generative SSL methods in the context of remote sensing imagery. By predicting in feature space and incorporating robust regularization, REJEPA aims to provide a computationally efficient and semantically rich solution that generalizes across diverse sensor modalities, overcoming challenges inherent to RS data.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an efficient and robust self-supervised learning framework for unimodal Remote Sensing Content-Based Image Retrieval (RS-CBIR). This overarching goal is motivated by the rapid proliferation of remote sensing image archives, which necessitates advanced techniques to facilitate the efficient retrieval of semantically relevant information.

The specific objectives and motivations underpinning the development of REJEPA include:

- **Learning High-Level Semantic Features:** Traditional generative SSL methods, particularly those based on masked autoencoders (MAEs), primarily focus on reconstructing pixel-level details. However, in remote sensing, capturing high-level semantic features is crucial for accurate retrieval amidst varying resolutions, high object density, and complex backgrounds. REJEPA aims to directly learn these abstract representations by predicting features in a high-dimensional space, thereby eliminating unnecessary pixel-level reconstruction and focusing on semantic coherence.
- **Reducing Computational Complexity:** Pixel-reconstruction-based models typically employ large decoders, contributing significantly to computational overhead during training. A key motivation for REJEPA is to reduce this complexity. By operating entirely within the feature space, REJEPA seeks to achieve substantial reductions in computational burden, making it more scalable for the large-scale RS datasets prevalent today.
- **Overcoming Limitations of Existing SSL Paradigms:**
 - **Contrastive Learning:** While effective, contrastive methods rely on constructing negative pairs. In remote sensing, this can be challenging as images from different geographical locations might still share high semantic similarity (e.g., two distinct cities, two different forest patches), potentially leading to suboptimal representations. REJEPA aims to avoid this reliance on negative pairs.
 - **Generative Methods (MIM/MAEs):** As noted, the pixel-centric focus of these methods often falls short in capturing the abstract, high-level semantics required for robust RS-CBIR, especially in complex multi-resolution and multi-modal scenarios. REJEPA's feature-space prediction is designed to address this.
- **Addressing Challenges Specific to Remote Sensing Data:** Remote sensing imagery presents unique challenges, including diverse sensor modalities (RGB, multispectral, SAR), varying spatial and spectral resolutions, dense and complex object distributions, and environmental noise. The framework is motivated by the need for a solution that can effectively generalize across these conditions, leading to sensor-agnostic representations.
- **Preventing Encoder Collapse:** A common challenge in self-supervised learning, particularly in joint-embedding architectures that do not use negative pairs, is representation collapse, where the encoder produces degenerate or non-informative features. REJEPA is motivated to integrate a robust regularization mechanism to mitigate this risk, ensuring the learned features are diverse, stable, and discriminative.

In essence, REJEPA is motivated by the need for a more efficient, semantically aware, and generalizable self-supervised learning framework that can handle the specific complexities of remote sensing data for effective content-based image retrieval.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

REJEPA (Retrieval with Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture) is a self-supervised framework designed for unimodal RS-CBIR. Its methodology is built upon the principles of Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs), shifting from pixel-level reconstruction to feature-space prediction to learn high-level semantic representations.

**Problem Definition:**
Given a remote sensing image archive $X = \{x_i\}_{i=1}^N$, the objective is to retrieve images semantically similar to a query image $x_q \in X$. REJEPA learns representations by predicting the feature representation of a target image $x_t$ from a context image $x_c$, conditioned on a transformation variable $z$. The core predictive task is formulated as $\hat{z}_t = P_{\phi}(z_c, z_t)$, where $z_c = E_{\theta}(x_c)$ and $z_t = E_{\theta'}(x_t)$. For retrieval, a query image $x_q$ is encoded as $z_q = E_{\theta}(x_q)$, and a k-nearest neighbors (k-NN) search is performed in the learned feature space.

**REJEPA Architecture:**
The architecture comprises three main components:

1. **Context and Target Encoders ($E_{\theta}$, $E_{\theta'}$):**
 - **Disjoint Random Masking Strategy:** A crucial aspect is the independent sampling of context mask ($B_c$) and target mask ($B_i$). This ensures no overlap between context and target regions, preventing information leakage and enforcing a more challenging predictive learning objective.
 - **Context Encoder ($E_{\theta}$):** This encoder processes the visible, unmasked regions of the input image, referred to as context tokens. Its output, $z_c = E_{\theta}(x_c)$, captures spatially distributed contextual features.
 - **Target Encoder ($E_{\theta'}$):** This encoder processes the independently masked target patches ($x_t$). Its output, $z_t = E_{\theta'}(x_t)$, generates the reference feature representations that the predictor network aims to estimate.

2. **Predictor Network with Learned Transformations ($P_{\phi}$):**
 - The predictor module estimates the feature representations of the masked target regions using only the context features.
 - It takes as input the context representation $z_c$ from $E_{\theta}$ and a set of mask tokens $\{m_j\}_{j \in B_i}$ corresponding to the masked target patch locations.
 - The predictor network learns to infer $\hat{z}_t^{(i)} = P_{\phi}(z_c, \{m_j\}_{j \in B_i})$, which are the predicted embeddings for the $i$-th target token.
 - **Mask Token Parameterization:** The mask tokens are learnable vectors, enriched with positional embeddings, which allows the predictor to implicitly account for spatial information and contribute to sensor-invariant feature learning. The predictor network is applied multiple times to estimate all masked target tokens.

3. **Feature Regularization via VICReg:**
 - To prevent encoder collapse, a common issue in non-contrastive self-supervised learning where embeddings can become degenerate, Variance-Invariance-Covariance Regularization (VICReg) is integrated.
 - **Variance Regularization:** Ensures feature diversity by maintaining a minimum variance across each feature dimension, preventing collapse to a single point.
 - **Covariance Regularization:** Reduces redundancy by decorrelating different feature dimensions, ensuring each dimension captures distinct semantic information.
 - **Invariance Regularization:** Promotes consistency between embeddings generated from different augmented views of the same input image, enhancing robustness.
 - VICReg is applied to both context and target representations, enforcing diverse, independent, and stable embeddings.

**Training Objective:**
The training objective combines a prediction loss ($L_{pred}$) and a regularization loss ($L_{VICReg}$):

- **Prediction Loss ($L_{pred}$):** An L2 loss that minimizes the discrepancy between the predicted target representation $\hat{z}_t^{(i)}$ and the actual target representation $z_t^{(i)}$ over all masked target tokens.
- **Regularization Loss ($L_{VICReg}$):** A weighted sum of the variance, covariance, and invariance terms from VICReg, controlled by hyperparameters $\lambda_v, \lambda_c, \lambda_i$.
- **Total Loss:** The final loss function is $L = L_{pred} + L_{VICReg}$, where $\alpha$ balances the two components.

**Implementation and Evaluation Details:**
- **Backbone:** Vision Transformer Base/16 (ViT-B/16) is used for the context encoder, target encoder, and predictor. The predictor is a lightweight ViT with 384-dimensional embeddings and 12 layers.
- **Optimizer:** AdamW, with a scheduled learning rate and weight decay.
- **Target Encoder Update:** Weights of the target encoder are updated via an exponential moving average (EMA) of the context encoder weights.
- **Masking:** A random disjoint masking strategy is employed with an optimal masking ratio of 0.25.
- **VICReg Coefficients:** Set to for variance, covariance, and invariance terms, respectively.
- **Evaluation:** F1-score is the primary metric, calculated based on the top-10 retrieved images using k-NN search in the learned feature space.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of REJEPA involved benchmarking its performance against several state-of-the-art self-supervised learning (SSL) models for RS-CBIR on three diverse remote sensing datasets: BEN-14K (Sentinel-1 SAR and Sentinel-2 multispectral data), FMoW-RGB, and FMoW-Sentinel.

**Retrieval Performance (F1-score):**
REJEPA demonstrates improved retrieval performance across all tested datasets compared to prominent SSL techniques of comparable backbone (ViT-B/16), while also offering computational advantages.

- **FMoW-RGB Dataset:** REJEPA achieved an F1-score of 73.53%, showing a significant improvement over SS-CMIR (66.71%, 6.82 percentage points improvement) and SatMAE (61.85%, 11.68 percentage points improvement).
- **FMoW-Sentinel Dataset:** REJEPA achieved an F1-score of 75.87%, outperforming Mask-VLM (65.23%, 10.64 percentage points improvement) and SS-CMIR (63.46%, 12.41 percentage points improvement).

**Computational Efficiency:**
REJEPA reduces the model size by 10–40% compared to parameter-heavy MAE-based models such as SatMAE, SatMAE++, Scale-MAE, and MAE. The abstract also states a 40–60% reduction in computational complexity compared to pixel-reconstruction baselines like MAE.

**Visualization Analysis:**
- **Qualitative Retrieval:** Visualizations (Figure 4a, 5) demonstrate that REJEPA retrieves images with greater semantic coherence, focusing on structural and semantic relationships rather than just texture-based similarities. For instance, in complex scenes and for crop field queries, REJEPA identified homogeneous vegetation areas and similar crop structures more consistently than SatMAE and SatMAE++.
- **Attention Heatmaps:** Attention heatmaps from REJEPA's context encoder (Figure 4b, 6, 7) show a selective focus on salient geographic structures (e.g., roads, buildings, vegetation patches), while suppressing background clutter. In contrast, pixel-reconstruction methods exhibited more dispersed and less discriminative attention patterns. This suggests that feature-space prediction enables the model to attend to key structural regions, enhancing retrieval accuracy.

**Ablation Studies (on BEN-14K):**
- **Predictor Depth:** Increasing the predictor depth from 6 to 12 layers resulted in substantial performance gains (e.g., 26.5% for S1→S1 and 27.8% for S2→S2), indicating that deeper predictors are more effective at capturing high-level semantic relationships.
- **Masking Strategy:** The disjoint random masking strategy (F1-score 76.38% S1→S1, 75.42% S2→S2) outperformed multi-block masking (71.45% S1→S1, 69.84% S2→S2) by approximately 6.9-7.9 percentage points. This supports the effectiveness of enforcing non-trivial contextual dependencies.
- **Masking Ratio:** An optimal masking ratio of 0.25 yielded the best performance (76.38% S1→S1, 75.42% S2→S2), showing improvements over higher ratios like 0.85 (68.47% S1→S1, 66.46% S2→S2).
- **VICReg Regularization:** The incorporation of VICReg was critical, enhancing retrieval performance by 18.4% (S1→S1) and 20.3% (S2→S2). Without VICReg, the model suffered from representation collapse, leading to significantly reduced retrieval effectiveness. This validates VICReg's role in enforcing feature diversity, decorrelation, and invariance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of REJEPA marks a progression in self-supervised learning for remote sensing image retrieval, offering several areas of significance and potential impact:

- **Advancement in RS-CBIR Efficiency and Scalability:** The primary significance of REJEPA lies in its provision of a computationally efficient and scalable solution for content-based image retrieval in remote sensing. By operating within feature space and eliminating pixel-level reconstruction, the framework substantially reduces computational complexity and model size. This makes it particularly suitable for processing the ever-growing, large-scale remote sensing archives, where efficiency is paramount.
- **Enhanced Semantic Representation Learning:** REJEPA's feature-space prediction mechanism is designed to capture high-level semantic features more effectively than methods focusing on pixel reconstruction. This capability is crucial for remote sensing imagery, which is characterized by diverse resolutions, complex scenes, and varying object densities. The resulting semantically rich representations are more robust to noise and distortions, leading to improved retrieval accuracy for meaningful content.
- **Sensor-Agnostic Capability:** The research demonstrates REJEPA's ability to generalize across different sensor modalities, including RGB, multispectral, and Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR) data. This sensor-agnostic property is a significant advantage, as it reduces the need for developing and training modality-specific models, fostering a more unified approach to remote sensing data analysis. This contributes to the development of more general-purpose foundation models for Earth observation.
- **Robustness against Representation Collapse:** The integration of Variance-Invariance-Covariance Regularization (VICReg) directly addresses the challenge of encoder collapse, a common issue in self-supervised learning without negative pairs. By promoting feature diversity, decorrelation, and invariance, REJEPA ensures that the learned representations are stable, distinct, and highly discriminative, which is fundamental for effective retrieval.
- **Broad Applicability in Real-World RS Tasks:** The capabilities of REJEPA have direct implications for a wide range of real-world remote sensing applications. Examples include:
 - **Disaster Response:** Rapid retrieval of relevant pre- and post-disaster imagery for damage assessment and recovery planning.
 - **Land Use and Land Cover Mapping:** Efficient identification of similar land parcels for monitoring changes over time.
 - **Precision Agriculture:** Retrieval of images showing similar crop health or soil conditions to support monitoring and yield prediction.
 - **Environmental Surveillance and Climate Change Monitoring:** Tracking and identifying specific environmental patterns or changes.
 - **Defense Intelligence:** Enhanced capabilities for identifying objects and activities across vast geographical areas.
- **Establishment of a New Benchmark:** REJEPA establishes itself as a benchmark for efficient, scalable, and precise RS-CBIR by addressing existing limitations of contrastive and generative SSL methods. This can serve as a foundational work for future research into feature-predictive SSL architectures tailored for the unique characteristics of remote sensing data.
