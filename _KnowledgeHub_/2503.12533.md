# Being-0: A Humanoid Robotic Agent with Vision-Language Models and Modular Skills

> [!summary] Summary
> Being-0, a full-sized humanoid robot agent, successfully performs complex, multi-step tasks in real-world environments. Its framework integrates a high-level Foundation Model with robust modular skills via a novel VLM-based "Connector" module, which enhances embodied scene understanding, real-time control, and efficient task execution.

## Problem

- Integrating powerful, slow cloud-based Foundation Models (FMs) with fast, low-level humanoid robot skills leads to poor robustness and efficiency for complex, long-horizon tasks.
- Humanoid robots face unique challenges like inherent bipedal instability and FMs' limited 3D spatial understanding, which hinder accurate navigation and manipulation planning.
- Existing systems struggle to seamlessly chain diverse skills for long-horizon tasks, often resulting in suboptimal pose transitions between navigation and manipulation that lead to failures.

## Method

- A hierarchical agent framework is proposed, featuring a cloud-based ==Foundation Model== for high-level planning, an onboard Vision-Language Model (VLM) "Connector," and a modular skill library.
- The VLM-based Connector module acts as an intelligent intermediary, translating FM plans into grounded skill commands, handling real-time visual navigation, and adjusting robot pose for seamless skill transitions.
- The system leverages reinforcement learning for stable locomotion and imitation learning with teleoperation for acquiring dexterous manipulation skills, complemented by active vision for enhanced perception.

## Results

- Being-0 achieved an **84.4%** average completion rate on challenging long-horizon tasks, with the Connector improving performance (e.g., Fetch-bottle from **0%** to **90%** success rate) compared to baselines.
- The Connector module increased average navigation speed by **4.2x** (**9.6** cm/s vs. **2.3** cm/s) while maintaining a perfect success rate, demonstrating improved efficiency and robustness for navigation.
- Ablation studies confirmed that pose adjustment improved grasping success rates (e.g., Grasp-coffee from 1/5 to 4/5), and active vision enabled consistent 5/5 success across all navigation and manipulation tasks, unlike fixed camera setups.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An efficient, onboard VLM can effectively bridge the "mind-body" gap between abstract FM plans and real-time physical actions, overcoming latency and embodied grounding issues critical for humanoids.
> - Dynamic pose adjustment during navigation and active camera control are crucial for achieving seamless transitions between different task phases and ensuring high success rates in precise manipulation.
> - A distributed computational architecture, leveraging cloud for high-level reasoning and onboard processing for embodied decision-making, significantly enhances the robustness and efficiency of humanoid agents.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of autonomous robotic agents capable of operating in complex, unstructured real-world environments is a grand challenge in embodied AI. Within this ambitious goal, humanoid robots represent a particularly compelling platform due to their human-like morphology, which makes them ideally suited for interacting with human-designed environments and tools. This paper directly contributes to this burgeoning field by addressing the critical gap in enabling humanoid robots to perform complex, long-horizon tasks autonomously.

Historically, research in humanoid robotics has largely focused on improving individual low-level skills such as stable bipedal locomotion, dexterous manipulation, and whole-body control. Significant progress has been made in these areas, with reinforcement learning (RL) and sim-to-real transfer proving effective for robust locomotion, and teleoperation combined with imitation learning (e.g., Diffusion Policies, ACT) advancing complex manipulation. However, integrating these disparate skills into a coherent, intelligent agent capable of understanding high-level natural language instructions and executing multi-step tasks has remained a largely unsolved problem for humanoids.

The recent advent of large Foundation Models (FMs), such as GPT-4o, has revolutionized AI by demonstrating powerful capabilities in general-purpose vision-language understanding, reasoning, and planning. In robotics, FMs have been successfully leveraged for high-level cognitive functions in robot arms, wheeled robots, and quadruped robots for tasks like skill planning, success detection, and semantic reasoning. However, the authors identify a critical bottleneck: directly applying these FMs to humanoid robots often leads to issues with robustness, efficiency, and accurate embodied scene understanding. Humanoids introduce unique challenges such as inherent instability in bipedal locomotion, demanding frequent pose adjustments, and the need for seamless transitions between navigation and manipulation tasks, which current FMs struggle to manage efficiently due to their high inference latency and limited 3D spatial awareness.

"Being-0" positions itself precisely at this intersection, aiming to overcome these humanoid-specific challenges. It adopts a hierarchical agent framework, a common paradigm in complex robotic systems, but introduces a novel "Connector" module as its key innovation. This Connector, a lightweight Vision-Language Model (VLM), acts as an intelligent intermediary between the slow, high-level FM (operating in the cloud) and the fast, low-level modular skill library (running onboard). This architectural choice directly addresses the issues of latency and embodied grounding that plague direct FM-to-skill interfaces for humanoids.

Furthermore, the work aligns with current trends in robotics that emphasize:
1. **Modular Skill Development:** Building a library of robust, specialized skills that can be flexibly combined.
2. **Imitation Learning and Teleoperation:** Utilizing human demonstrations for efficient and high-quality skill acquisition, especially for dexterous manipulation.
3. **Active Perception:** Integrating active cameras to enhance the robot's visual understanding and task performance.
4. **Distributed Computing:** Optimizing computational deployment by distributing tasks between powerful cloud-based FMs and efficient onboard processors.

By addressing the nuanced challenges of humanoid robots, "Being-0" pushes the boundaries of autonomous embodied agents, demonstrating how a thoughtfully designed hierarchical system, with an emphasis on embodied grounding and real-time responsiveness, can unlock unprecedented capabilities for humanoids in real-world settings.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to advance the field of humanoid robotics by building autonomous agents that can achieve human-level performance in real-world embodied tasks. This ambitious goal necessitates overcoming several fundamental challenges that arise when trying to integrate advanced AI models with complex robotic hardware.

The primary motivations driving the development of Being-0 are rooted in the identified limitations of existing approaches:
1. **Bridging the Gap between High-Level Cognition and Low-Level Control:** Recent advances have provided powerful Foundation Models (FMs) for high-level reasoning and planning, alongside sophisticated low-level skills for humanoid locomotion and manipulation. However, directly combining these components often results in poor robustness and efficiency, leading to a significant performance degradation in long-horizon tasks. This is mainly due to compounding errors propagating through a long sequence of actions and the varied, often high, latency of large FMs.
2. **Addressing Humanoid-Specific Challenges:** Unlike simpler robotic platforms (e.g., wheeled or quadruped robots) that have seen success with FM-based agents, humanoid robots pose unique difficulties. Their inherent bipedal instability requires continuous and precise locomotion adjustments. Furthermore, existing FMs often lack accurate 3D scene understanding, making it challenging for them to correctly estimate navigation targets or ensure optimal initial poses for subsequent manipulation tasks. These limitations can lead to incorrect skill plans and frequent task failures.
3. **Enhancing Real-time Responsiveness and Efficiency:** Large FMs typically operate on cloud services, incurring significant inference latency. This slow feedback loop makes humanoid agents less reactive and robust in dynamic environments or during alternating phases of navigation and manipulation that demand high control frequencies. A key motivation was to enable efficient, real-time performance by carefully distributing computational loads.
4. **Improving Seamless Skill Stitching:** Executing complex, long-horizon tasks requires reliably chaining diverse skills (e.g., navigating to a table, then grasping an object). A particular challenge is ensuring that the termination state of one skill (e.g., navigation) provides a suitable initialization for the next (e.g., manipulation). Suboptimal poses after navigation frequently lead to manipulation failures.
5. **Leveraging Dexterous Hands and Active Vision:** The authors aim to fully exploit the capabilities of advanced humanoid hardware, specifically multi-fingered dexterous hands for human-like manipulation and an active camera (actuated neck) for dynamic visual perception, which are often underutilized or inadequately integrated in existing agent frameworks.

To address these motivations, the key objectives for Being-0 are:
- To propose a hierarchical agent framework that optimally deploys different components (FM on cloud, VLM/skills onboard) to achieve efficient execution of long-horizon embodied tasks.
- To introduce a novel VLM-based "Connector" module that acts as an intelligent bridge, translating FM's language-based plans into actionable skill commands and dynamically coordinating locomotion and manipulation.
- To equip the Connector with enhanced embodied decision-making capabilities, including grounded skill planning, efficient visual navigation, and pose adjustment for improved skill transitions.
- To enable the humanoid robot to effectively control multi-fingered dexterous hands and active cameras, thereby improving its dexterity and task success rates in real-world environments.

By pursuing these objectives, Being-0 seeks to demonstrate a robust and efficient approach to controlling humanoid robots for complex tasks, pushing the frontier of autonomous humanoid capabilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Being-0 proposes a hierarchical agent framework structured into three core components: a high-level Foundation Model (FM), a novel VLM-based Embodied Connector, and a Modular Skill Library. This architecture is designed to address the challenges of efficiency, robustness, and embodied understanding in humanoid robots.

#### 3.1. Hierarchical Framework Overview

As illustrated in Figure 1, the workflow begins with a natural language instruction given to the FM. The FM generates high-level plans. The Connector then translates these plans into executable low-level skill commands, drawing from the Modular Skill Library, which controls the robot's physical actions. The robot's observations (binocular RGB images and proprioception) are fed back to both the Connector and the FM for closed-loop control.

#### 3.2. Modular Skill Library

The foundation of Being-0's physical capabilities lies in its robust and diverse skill library, which separates lower-body locomotion from upper-body manipulation for greater stability and ease of acquisition.

- **Stable Locomotion with Joystick Commands:** A goal-conditioned proprioceptive policy, $\pi_L$, is trained using reinforcement learning (RL) in simulation (Isaac Gym) for lower-body control. This policy maps proprioceptive observations and joystick velocity commands to target joint positions for the lower body, enabling stable bipedal walking in various directions while maintaining balance. Domain randomization and external forces are used during training to enhance sim-to-real transfer. The skill library includes a set of discrete locomotion actions (e.g., `go straight`, `turn left`, `sidestep left`) and active vision controls (e.g., `tilt head`, `turn head`). These skills operate at a high control frequency (50 Hz).
- **Acquiring Manipulation Skills:** Dexterous manipulation skills are acquired using a combination of teleoperation and imitation learning. Human demonstrations are collected using an Apple VisionPro, which projects binocular robot images to the human operator and retargets human head, wrist, and finger motions to robot actions at 10 Hz. These teleoperated trajectories are then used to train manipulation policies, $\pi_{M_i}$, for each skill $M_i$ (e.g., "grasp bottle") using ACT (Action Chunking with Transformers), a behavior cloning method. ACT predicts sequences of future actions based on current visual and proprioceptive observations, ensuring scalability (new skills in <1 hour with 50-150 trajectories).

#### 3.3. Foundation Model (FM)

Being-0 leverages GPT-4o as its high-level planner, deployed on the cloud. The FM performs three critical functions:
- **Reasoning:** Understands instructions and observed images to determine the current task stage.
- **Detection:** Evaluates the success of previously executed skills and identifies failures.
- **Planning:** Based on reasoning and detection, it selects the next high-level skill from the library.
Prompt engineering (detailed in Appendix B.3) is used to guide the FM's decision-making process. The FM provides language-based plans, but its latency and limited embodied understanding pose challenges when directly interfacing with low-level control.

#### 3.4. Embodied Connector (Novel Contribution)

The core innovation of Being-0 is the Connector module, a lightweight Vision-Language Model (VLM) that acts as an intermediary between the FM and the skill library. This VLM is deployed on an onboard NVIDIA Jetson AGX device, enabling real-time operation (approx. 1-second inference time).

- **Training the VLM:** The VLM is based on the VideoLLaMA2 architecture, fine-tuned on a custom dataset of first-person navigation images. These images are richly annotated with language descriptions, skills, object labels, and bounding boxes. The training involves multi-task learning, including image description, skill prediction, and object detection, distilling embodied knowledge from visual-language navigation data. Additionally, it incorporates filtered data from general visual grounding datasets to enhance spatial and object understanding.
- **Grounded Skill Planning:** The VLM translates the FM's language-based plans into concrete, executable skill commands. It leverages its real-time visual understanding to ground these plans in the physical environment. For example, if the FM plans "grasp a cup" but the robot is far, the VLM might output "move towards (table)." Conversely, if the FM plans "find a table" but the robot is already there, the VLM signals success to the FM. This corrective capability reduces errors and improves task success.
- **Visual Navigation with Locomotion Skills:** The Connector uses the VLM's object detection and synthetic depth (from binocular images) to estimate the relative position of target objects. It then selects appropriate locomotion skills (e.g., `move towards`, `search for`) to guide the robot. If a target is not visible, it triggers an exploration routine combining locomotion with active camera movements. This significantly improves navigation efficiency and robustness compared to FM-only approaches.
- **Coordinating Navigation and Manipulation (Pose Adjustment):** A critical feature for seamless skill stitching. The VLM predicts not only object locations but also the optimal robot alignment for manipulation. If the robot's pose after navigation is suboptimal, the VLM triggers composite skills (e.g., head rotation and slight forward movement) to adjust the robot's position, ensuring an ideal initial state for subsequent manipulation tasks. This allows the robot to approach targets along an arc-shaped path for precise positioning.

#### 3.5. Hardware Setup

Experiments are conducted on a Unitree H1-2 humanoid robot with 41 DoFs, including 13-DoF lower body, 7-DoF arms, 6-DoF Inspire dexterous hands, and a 2-DoF neck with a ZED-mini binocular RGB camera. The Jetson AGX handles onboard computation for the Connector and skill library.

#### 3.6. Experimental Environment and Tasks

The robot operates in a large (20x20m) office environment, challenging for navigation and long-horizon tasks. A diverse set of manipulation skills (grasping, placing, operating machines) are collected. Evaluation focuses on complex long-horizon tasks like "Fetch-bottle," "Deliver-basket," "Prepare-coffee," "Make-coffee," and "Deliver-coffee," which require combinations of navigation, dexterous manipulation, and multi-step reasoning.

This comprehensive methodology, integrating high-level AI, an intelligent VLM intermediary, and modular, robust low-level skills, represents a significant step towards practical humanoid autonomy.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on the Being-0 humanoid robot demonstrate significant advancements in performing complex, long-horizon embodied tasks, particularly highlighting the crucial role of the novel VLM-based Connector module and active vision.

#### 4.1. Solving Long-Horizon Embodied Tasks

Being-0 achieved an impressive average completion rate of 84.4% on challenging long-horizon tasks, showcasing its ability to integrate high-level planning with robust low-level execution. A direct comparison with a baseline system (w/o Connector) revealed substantial performance improvements attributable to the Connector:
- **Fetch-bottle:** Performance surged from 0.00 to 0.90 with the Connector.
- **Deliver-basket:** Improved from 0.00 to 0.80.
- **Prepare-coffee:** Increased from 0.00 to 0.75.
Even for tasks like "Make-coffee," where the baseline already achieved 0.90 (likely due to starting conditions close to the coffee machine), the Connector ensured reliable performance. These results unequivocally establish the Connector's critical role in bridging the gap between the Foundation Model and the skill library, enabling the robot to effectively complete multi-step, complex tasks.

#### 4.2. Ablation Study: Adjustment in Navigation

The proposed pose adjustment method within the Connector proved vital for seamless transitions between navigation and manipulation:
- **Grasping Tasks (Grasp-bottle, Grasp-coffee):** Being-0 with adjustment showed significant success rate gains (e.g., Grasp-bottle: 2/5 to 4/5, Grasp-coffee: 1/5 to 4/5). This highlights that properly aligning the robot after navigation is crucial for precise grasping, preventing failures caused by suboptimal initial poses.
- **Placing Tasks:** While placing objects on a large surface like a table (Place-basket, Place-coffee (t)) was less sensitive to adjustment, placing on a small, precise target like a coffee machine (Place-coffee (m)) benefited significantly (0/5 to 3/5). This emphasizes the adjustment method's importance for tasks requiring high precision in object placement.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study: Active Vision

The active camera, integrated with the robot's neck, was shown to be indispensable for versatile task execution:
- **Fixed Camera Limitations:** No single fixed camera pitch angle could achieve high success rates for both navigation and manipulation tasks. Small pitch angles were suitable for navigation (e.g., Fixed Cam (0.3): 5/5 navigation), but failed for tabletop manipulation (0/5). Conversely, larger pitch angles improved manipulation (e.g., Fixed Cam (0.9): 4/5 grasp coffee, 5/5 place coffee) but caused navigation failures (0/5).
- **Being-0 (Active Cam.):** With its active camera, Being-0 consistently achieved perfect success rates (5/5) across all navigation and manipulation tasks. This demonstrates that dynamically adapting the field of view is critical for meeting the diverse visual requirements of different task phases.

#### 4.4. Efficiency

The Connector module significantly boosted the system's efficiency:
- Being-0 with the Connector achieved a 4.2x increase in average moving speed (9.6 cm/s) compared to the baseline without the Connector (2.3 cm/s) for navigation tasks, while also maintaining a perfect success rate. The baseline often failed to reach distant targets due to GPT-4o's frequent errors in planning locomotion directions, underscoring the VLM's role in providing grounded, real-time navigation commands.

#### 4.5. Robustness and Scalability

- **Navigation Robustness:** Being-0 exhibited strong robustness in navigation across various environments. It achieved perfect success (1.00) in simple in-room navigation, maintained high performance (0.80) with obstacles, and successfully handled complex cross-room navigation (0.83), which required multi-step reasoning by the FM.
- **Manipulation Scalability:** The learned manipulation policies demonstrated robustness to unseen objects (slight performance decline) and visual perturbations. The framework's scalability was further highlighted by its ability to acquire skills for dexterous hands with tactile sensors (e.g., Play-chess) and tasks involving precise manipulation of small objects, indicating its potential for supporting increasingly complex manipulation.

In summary, the results clearly validate the effectiveness of the Being-0 framework, particularly the Connector module, the pose adjustment mechanism, and the active vision system. These components collectively enable a full-sized humanoid robot to perform complex, long-horizon tasks robustly, efficiently, and with high success rates in real-world environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Being-0 marks a significant step forward in the pursuit of truly autonomous humanoid robots and has far-reaching implications for embodied AI, robotics, and human-robot interaction.

#### 5.1. Advancement in Humanoid Autonomy

Being-0 directly addresses the long-standing challenge of enabling humanoid robots to perform complex, long-horizon tasks in human-centric environments. By successfully integrating high-level cognitive abilities (from FMs) with robust low-level physical skills, it brings humanoid robots closer to achieving human-level performance. This work moves beyond demonstrating individual skills to showcasing a cohesive agent framework capable of autonomous multi-stage operation.

#### 5.2. Novel Hierarchical Control Paradigm

The VLM-based Connector module is a particularly significant contribution. It offers a principled and effective solution for bridging the notorious "mind-body" gap in robotics—the challenge of translating abstract, language-based plans from powerful but slow cognitive models into precise, real-time physical actions. By offloading embodied scene understanding, grounded skill planning, and real-time coordination to an efficient onboard VLM, Being-0 overcomes the latency and limited spatial reasoning of large FMs, which are critical bottlenecks for humanoids. This hybrid cloud-onboard computational architecture sets a new standard for designing intelligent robotic agents.

#### 5.3. Enhanced Robustness and Efficiency

The experimental results vividly demonstrate that the Connector dramatically improves task success rates (e.g., from 0.00 to 0.90 for Fetch-bottle) and navigation efficiency (4.2x speed increase). This boost in robustness and real-time performance is crucial for deploying humanoid robots in dynamic, unpredictable real-world settings. The ability to dynamically adjust poses for manipulation and leverage active vision for optimal perception further contributes to this enhanced reliability.

#### 5.4. Practical Deployment and Scalability

The design choice to deploy the Connector and modular skills on low-cost onboard computation (NVIDIA Jetson AGX) while keeping the FM in the cloud ensures the system's practical deployability. The scalable approach to acquiring manipulation skills through teleoperation and imitation learning allows for a continuous expansion of the robot's repertoire without extensive manual programming, making it adaptable to new tasks and environments.

#### 5.5. Future Research Directions

The work also opens several avenues for future research, as acknowledged by the authors. Integrating more complex locomotion skills (e.g., crouching, climbing stairs) would broaden the humanoid's functional envelope. Furthermore, developing more lightweight, robot-specific Foundation Models that can operate onboard could further reduce latency and enhance the overall autonomy and efficiency, potentially moving away from reliance on cloud-based FMs.

#### 5.6. Societal and Economic Impact

Successful autonomous humanoids like Being-0 have the potential to revolutionize various sectors. They could assist in challenging or hazardous environments (e.g., disaster response), perform intricate tasks in manufacturing and logistics, provide companionship and assistance in healthcare, or even undertake domestic chores. The dexterity afforded by multi-fingered hands and active vision enables humanoids to interact with tools and objects designed for humans, paving the way for seamless integration into human society.

#### 5.7. Ethical and Safety Considerations (Impact Statement)

The authors' inclusion of an impact statement regarding safety is commendable and crucial. The deployment of full-sized humanoid robots with advanced AI, especially those using FMs, introduces significant risks, including unpredictable actions, damage to surroundings, or harm to humans. The emphasis on controlled experimental environments, robust error handling, fail-safes, and ethical guidelines is paramount. As humanoid capabilities advance, ongoing vigilance and proactive measures in safety and ethical design will be essential for responsible development and eventual real-world deployment. This paper contributes not only to the technical advancement but also implicitly to the ongoing discourse about the safe and ethical future of robotics.
