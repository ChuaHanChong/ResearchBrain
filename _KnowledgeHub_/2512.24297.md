# Figure It Out: Improve the Frontier of Reasoning with Executable Visual States

> [!summary] Summary
> FIGR, developed by WeChat AI, Tencent Inc., enhances large language models' reasoning by integrating executable visual construction into multi-turn inference via reinforcement learning. It improves problem-solving involving implicit spatial and geometric relationships, achieving an an average accuracy of 74.11% on mathematical benchmarks, marking a 6.77% gain over its base policy and over 11% on complex geometric reasoning tasks.

## Problem

- Text-only reasoning methods (e.g., Chain-of-Thought) struggle with complex problems requiring implicit spatial or geometric understanding, leading to errors due to the high cognitive burden on internal representations.
- Unconstrained visual generation models (Large Vision-Language Models) lack precise control over their visual outputs, often generating noisy or spatially imprecise images that are unreliable for tasks demanding fine-grained geometric accuracy.
- Tool-augmented visual reasoning models operate on given images with fixed sets of predefined transformations, preventing them from autonomously constructing novel, task-specific diagrams or abstract visual representations from scratch.

## Method

- Introduces ==executable visual construction== within the reasoning loop, where the model dynamically generates code to construct and refine diagrams, with a sandboxed interpreter providing precise visual and textual feedback.
- Employs a reinforcement learning procedure with ==Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)== to train the model, optimizing long-horizon, multi-turn reasoning trajectories using a combined reward for final answer correctness, output format, and visual construction appropriateness.
- Utilizes an ==Adaptive Reward Mechanism (ARM)== to guide purposeful visual thinking by conditionally rewarding diagrammatic reasoning based on a predicted suitability for the given problem, encouraging effective use of visual construction.

## Results

- FIGR achieved an average accuracy of **74.11%** across seven mathematical reasoning benchmarks (e.g., AIME, BeyondAIME, MATH 500), representing a **6.77%** improvement over its base policy and a **4.88%** gain over a text-only reinforcement learning baseline.
- Demonstrated substantial performance gains on benchmarks requiring complex geometric and structural understanding, with increases of **13.12%** on AIME 2025 and **11.00%** on BeyondAIME.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the Adaptive Reward Mechanism is critical for guiding purposeful visual construction, and that explicit, executable visual feedback is superior to passively injected visual information or static image generation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Human reasoning for complex problems involving spatial or structural relationships benefits from externalizing information visually, a capability that AI models need to emulate more effectively.
> - Active, executable construction and iterative refinement of visual states within the reasoning loop are crucial for enforcing geometric consistency and providing actionable feedback, unlike static or passively injected images.
> - End-to-end reinforcement learning, particularly with an adaptive reward mechanism, can effectively teach AI models when and how to judiciously invoke multimodal (visual construction) capabilities without relying on extensive supervised visual reasoning annotations.
