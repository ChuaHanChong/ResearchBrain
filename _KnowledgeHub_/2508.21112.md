# EO-1: An Open Unified Embodied Foundation Model for General Robot Control

> [!summary] Summary
> The EO-1 model unifies multimodal reasoning and robot control for general-purpose operation in open-world scenarios. It introduces an interleaved vision-text-action pre-training paradigm, achieving high performance and strong generalization across diverse robot embodiments and complex tasks.

## Problem

- Prior Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models struggled to achieve open-world generalization and flexible, interleaved reasoning-acting in robots.
- Existing VLA approaches predominantly generated robot actions at the end of the output sequence, overlooking the temporal dynamics and causal dependencies inherent in real-world embodied interactions.
- Architectures often separated reasoning and acting into distinct stages or used components that hindered seamless integration and cross-modal knowledge transfer.

## Method

- Developed **EO-1**, a unified ==decoder-only transformer== model initialized from **Qwen 2.5 VL**, to integrate multimodal embodied reasoning and continuous robot control.
- Pioneered an ==interleaved vision-text-action pre-training== paradigm using **EO-Data1.5M**, a massive dataset combining web, robot control, and novel embodied reasoning data.
- Implemented a hybrid decoding mechanism with a language-modeling head for discrete text and a ==flow-matching head== for continuous actions, all within the shared transformer backbone.

## Results

- EO-1 achieved superior embodied reasoning performance, including 58.5 BLEU-4 on RoboVQA (outperforming GPT-4o's 47.2) and an overall score of 44.8 on the EO-Bench benchmark.
- Demonstrated state-of-the-art robot control with a **98.2%** average success rate on the LIBERO benchmark and an **86.0%** overall completion score across 28 real-world tasks on multiple robot embodiments.
- Exhibited robust open-world generalization, particularly in language generalization (**79.0%**), and enhanced long-horizon dexterity, achieving an **81.0%** average completion score on complex multi-step tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Interleaved vision-text-action pre-training enables superior, mutual-informed integration of reasoning and acting, more closely resembling human intelligence.
> - A unified decoder-only transformer architecture with hybrid decoding effectively facilitates cross-modal knowledge transfer and alignment without introducing bottlenecks.
> - The quality and task-alignment of interleaved multimodal data are more critical than sheer quantity for achieving robust physical grounding and open-world generalization in robots.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of EO-1 addresses a critical challenge in the field of artificial intelligence: creating general-purpose embodied intelligent systems that can seamlessly perform multimodal reasoning and physical interaction in the open world, mimicking human-level flexibility.

The current research landscape for generalist robots is dominated by Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which extend pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs) by co-training them on large-scale robot and visual-text data. While these models have shown impressive progress in dexterous manipulation and long-horizon control within constrained environments, the paper identifies significant limitations in their ability to achieve open-world generalization and flexible, interleaved reasoning and interaction.

Prior efforts can be broadly categorized:
- **Early VLA models (e.g., Kim et al., Black et al., Pertsch et al.):** These primarily extended VLMs with domain-specific robotic data. However, being trained exclusively on robotic datasets, they were restricted to narrow task domains, specific environments, and often suffered from diminished general semantic knowledge inherited from their VLM backbones, leading to limited instruction-following capabilities.
- **Co-trained VLA models with web and robotic data (e.g., Black et al., Driess et al., Lin et al.):** More recent studies have explored co-training VLAs with both web data and robotic data, demonstrating promising generality when interacting with new objects and unseen backgrounds. However, a fundamental bottleneck remains: these approaches predominantly generate robot actions at the *end* of the VLA output sequence. This sequential action generation overlooks the rich temporal dynamics and causal dependencies inherent in real-world embodied interactions, where human intelligence exhibits a continuous, interleaved synergy between reasoning and action—reasoning guides action, and action outcomes inform subsequent reasoning.

EO-1 positions itself precisely by tackling this "fundamental research question: How can we design an effective training paradigm for generalist robot policies that support flexible and mutual-informed reasoning-acting integration?" It draws inspiration from recent successes in advanced multimodal understanding and generation systems (e.g., Deng et al., Ma et al., Xie et al.), which have demonstrated the superiority of interleaved multimodal pretraining in digital domains. By proposing a *unified embodied foundation model* with *interleaved vision-text-action pre-training*, EO-1 aims to bridge the gap between sequential action generation and human-like integrated reasoning-acting, offering a novel paradigm for achieving greater flexibility and open-world generalization in robotics. The open-source nature of EO-Robotics is also a key aspect, contributing to the trend of democratizing access to large-scale models and datasets in the robotics community, following initiatives like Octo and OpenVLA.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching goal of the EO-Robotics project is to develop generalist robot policies capable of operating effectively in open-world scenarios. This translates into enabling autonomous robots to robustly handle diverse tasks, accurately follow instructions, adapt to novel situations, and even manage unforeseen events in real-world environments.

The specific key objectives and their underlying motivations are:

1. **Develop a Unified Embodied Foundation Model (EO-1):** The primary objective is to create a single model that can achieve superior performance in both multimodal embodied reasoning (understanding) and robot control (acting). This is motivated by the observed limitation of prior VLA models that either separate reasoning and acting into distinct stages or use architectures that hinder seamless integration.
2. **Pioneer Interleaved Vision-Text-Action Pre-training:** A core objective is to design and implement a training paradigm that supports flexible and mutual-informed reasoning-acting integration. This is motivated by the contrast with existing VLA models, which typically treat action generation as a final output, thereby overlooking the natural, temporal, and causal interplay between vision, language, and action that characterizes human-like intelligence.
3. **Construct a Unified Model Architecture:** The aim is to create a single decoder-only transformer architecture that processes various multimodal inputs (image, text, video, and action) indiscriminately. This objective is motivated by the desire to avoid introducing extra bottleneck components or action-specific parameters, which can complicate cross-modal alignment and knowledge transfer in previous models.
4. **Curate a Massive, High-Quality Multimodal Embodied Reasoning Dataset (EO-Data1.5M):** A critical objective is to build a large-scale dataset specifically designed for interleaved vision-text-action comprehension. This is motivated by the understanding that effective training of a truly generalist embodied model requires not only an effective architecture but also carefully structured data that captures rich world knowledge and nuanced cross-modal interactions.
5. **Integrate Auto-Regressive Decoding and Flow Matching Denoising:** The goal is to combine these two mechanisms within the unified model's training. This is motivated by the need to efficiently generate both discrete textual outputs for reasoning and continuous actions for precise physical control, leveraging the strengths of both paradigms.
6. **Validate Open-World Generalization and Dexterous Control:** A key objective is to empirically demonstrate that the interleaved vision-text-action learning approach leads to robust open-world understanding and generalization. This is achieved through extensive experiments on a variety of long-horizon, dexterous manipulation tasks across multiple robot embodiments, directly addressing the central challenge of deploying autonomous AI agents in the physical world.
7. **Open-Source the Entire EO-Robotics Toolchain:** The project aims to release the EO-1 model weights, training code, and all components of the EO-Data1.5M dataset. This objective is driven by a strong commitment to fostering community research, accelerating progress in general-purpose autonomous robots, and facilitating further advancements in embodied foundation models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The EO-Robotics initiative comprises three main components: the EO-1 model, the EO-Data1.5M dataset, and the EO-Bench benchmark. The methodology intricately links the design of the model with the construction of its training data and a specialized evaluation framework.

#### 3.1. The EO-1 Model Architecture and Training Paradigm

EO-1 is conceptualized as a generalist robot policy $\pi_\theta$ that unifies multimodal embodied reasoning and robot control. Its core design principles revolve around a single, integrated architecture and a synergistic training approach:

- **Unified Decoder-Only Transformer:** The model uses a single decoder-only transformer backbone, initialized from **Qwen 2.5 VL**, thereby inheriting extensive visual-language knowledge. This shared backbone is crucial for seamless cross-modal knowledge transfer and alignment.
- **Multimodal Input Processing:** Inputs (text, images, videos, and actions) are converted into an interleaved token sequence $x_{1:N}$. This includes:
 - Discretized text tokens ($x_w$).
 - Continuous image patch tokens ($x_I$) from a visual encoder.
 - Linearly projected robot state tokens ($x_q$) from a randomly initialized projector.
 - Partially denoised robot action tokens ($x_a$), derived from `noisy action` $a_t^\tau = \tau a_t + (1-\tau)z_\tau$ (where $z_\tau$ is random noise) embedded via a linear projector that also incorporates the flow matching timestep $\tau$.
- **Hybrid Decoding Mechanisms:** Unlike prior models that might use separate modules, EO-1 employs two heads on the shared transformer backbone:
 - A **language-modeling head** for discrete text token decoding (e.g., for reasoning outputs).
 - A **flow-matching head** for continuous action denoising, utilizing rectified flow to generate action signals $\hat{a}_t$ by integrating a predicted vector field. This approach avoids introducing extra action-specific parameters, promoting tighter integration.
- **Unified Prompting and Causal Attention:** The model is trained on three data types: multimodal understanding, robot action generation, and mixed-modality generation. It employs an omnidirectional attention mask with causal attention across the entire interleaved sequence, allowing it to capture sequential dependencies between reasoning and acting. Special tokens (e.g., `[BOI]`, `[EOI]`, `[BOS]`, `[EOS]`, `[BOR]`, `[EOR]`, `[BOA]`, `[EOA]`) delineate different modalities within the sequence.
- **Interleaved Rectifying Sampling:** To address the challenge of action denoising disrupting causal relationships in interleaved sequences (where subsequent tokens might attend to noisy actions), a rectifying sampling strategy is introduced. For sequences containing multiple action generation segments, noisy action tokens in middle segments are replaced with clean action tokens. This ensures that each action generation segment contributes to both flow-matching denoising and indirect gradient backpropagation for subsequent interleaved generation.
- **Combined Training Objective:** The model is trained end-to-end by optimizing a sum of two objectives:
 - **Next Token Prediction Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{ar}$):** Standard cross-entropy loss for language generation tasks.
 - **Denoising Vector Field Prediction Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{fm}$):** A mean squared error loss for the flow-matching head, predicting the vector field for action generation.

#### 3.2. EO-Data1.5M Dataset Construction

The development of EO-Data1.5M is a cornerstone of the methodology, emphasizing scalability and interleaved multimodal content:

- **Data Sources:** It combines diverse data:
 - **Web Multimodal Data (5.7M samples, 7.1B tokens):** Curated from sources like LLaVA-1.5, LLaVA-Video-178K, PixMo-Points, RefCOCO, and RoboVQA, covering visual instruction following, video understanding, and referring expressions.
 - **Robot Control Data (1.2M episodes, 127.3B tokens):** Aggregated from large-scale real robot datasets such as AgiBotWorld, Open X-Embodiment, RoboMIND, SO100-Community, and IPEC-Franka, providing diverse demonstrations across multiple robot types and skills.
 - **Interleaved Embodied Data (EO-Data1.5M, 1.5M samples, 1.0B tokens):** This is the novel component, derived by annotating existing robot control data.
- **Interleaved Data Construction Pipeline:**
 1. **Robot Video Filter and Curation:** To ensure diversity, videos are filtered and sampled using visual feature clustering and human inspection, especially for multiview reasoning.
 2. **Video Splitting and Captioning:** Videos are segmented into short clips corresponding to individual subtasks, and both human annotators and VLMs generate detailed action captions for each clip.
 3. **Generating QA Pairs (Embodied Reasoning Data):** VLMs and humans are used to create two types of QA pairs based on subtask clips and captions:
 - **Embodied Temporal Reasoning Data (70k Physical Commonsense, 0.7M Task Reasoning):** Focuses on task planning (sequencing subtasks), physical common sense (effect of actions, feasibility), episode captioning, affordance QA, process verification, subtask QA, and failure detection.
 - **Embodied Spatial Reasoning Data (1.5M QA pairs):** Focuses on spatial understanding and reasoning, including relation reasoning (object relationships), trajectory prediction (future motion), object referring (grounding with bounding boxes), object pointing (identifying specific objects), and multiview pointing (locating objects across views).
 4. **Cleaning and Rewriting:** Rule-based cleaning and LLM-based rewriting are applied to validate answers, improve text diversity, and inject fuzzy semantics.
- **Flexible Interleaved Formats:** Three formats integrate embodied reasoning QA with robot control data along temporal order:
 - **Interleaved Temporal Reasoning Format:** Combines image tokens, next subtask planning QA, subtask instruction, robot action, and task-completion verification QA.
 - **Interleaved Spatial Reasoning Format:** Links image tokens, robot trajectory prediction QA (with coordinates), a trajectory instruction, robot action, and task-completion verification QA.
 - **Interleaved Free Chatting Format:** Randomly connects other temporal/spatial reasoning QA with robot control data via the original task instruction.

#### 3.3. Embodied Onevision Reasoning Benchmark (EO-Bench)

- **Design:** A self-constructed benchmark with 648 manually labeled QA pairs on diverse robot control data. It emphasizes a disentangled evaluation, where each question targets a clearly defined reasoning aspect (spatial understanding, physical common sense, task reasoning, state estimation) rather than conflating multiple.
- **Categories:** Spatial Understanding (trajectory, visual grounding, relation, multiview), Physical Commonsense (direct influence, counterfactual), Task Reasoning (planning, captioning, verification), and State Estimation (object states, robot-object interactions, action consequences).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive evaluation of EO-1 across various benchmarks—encompassing embodied reasoning, simulated robot control, and real-world manipulation—consistently demonstrates its superior capabilities and validates the effectiveness of its unified architecture and interleaved pre-training paradigm.

#### 4.1. Superior Performance in Embodied Reasoning

EO-1 (3B parameters) significantly outperforms both leading open-source and proprietary VLMs, as well as co-trained VLA models, on a suite of embodied reasoning benchmarks:
- **RoboVQA:** Achieves a BLEU-4 score of 58.5, surpassing strong models like GPT-4o (47.2). This highlights its robust visuospatial inference in egocentric settings.
- **ERQA:** Achieves an accuracy of 45.5, outperforming InternVL2.5 8B (45.2), indicating strong spatially grounded reasoning from real-world visual scenes.
- **EO-Bench:** On this self-constructed, disentangled reasoning benchmark, EO-1 achieves an overall score of 44.8, significantly higher than mainstream VLMs (average 32). It scores 36.4 on Spatial reasoning (multiview, trajectory, grounding) and 38.9 on Temporal reasoning (multimodal, temporal prediction), validating its enhanced scene understanding and temporal prediction.

#### 4.2. State-of-the-Art Robot Control

EO-1 demonstrates leading performance on established robot manipulation benchmarks:
- **LIBERO Benchmark:** Attains an impressive average success rate of 98.2%, surpassing state-of-the-art models such as OpenVLA-OFT (97.1%), $\pi_0$ (94.2%), and GR00T-N1 (93.9%). This indicates robust generalization across spatial layouts, object categories, goal semantics, and long-horizon planning.
- **SimplerEnv Benchmark:** Outperforms $\pi_0$ by substantial margins across WidowX (72.7% vs. 69.2%), Google-VM (76.5% vs. 71.4%), and Google-VA (63.0% vs. 54.7%). These results confirm its robustness under visual distribution shifts (lighting, textures, viewpoints) and precise visuomotor grounding.

#### 4.3. Real-world Generalization on Multiple Embodiments

Evaluations on 28 real-world tasks using Franka Panda, WidowX 250 S, AgiBot G-1, and Lerobot SO100 robots showcase EO-1's adaptability:
- EO-1 achieves an overall completion score of 86.0%, significantly outperforming $\pi_0$-Fast (43.0%), GR00T-N1.5 (71.0%), and $\pi_0$ (68.0%) across diverse manipulations.
- Notable performance in long-horizon tasks (AgiBot G-1: 81.0%) and complex embodied reasoning control tasks (83.0%), demonstrating precision and adaptability across various environments.

#### 4.4. Specialization to Long-horizon Dexterity

Through fine-tuning on high-quality demonstration data for complex, multi-step long-horizon tasks (e.g., Make Breakfast Sandwich, Roast Beef Steak):
- EO-1 consistently achieves higher completion scores (e.g., Roast Beef Steak 56.0% vs. GR00T-N1.5 47.0%; Make Breakfast Sandwich 85.0% vs. $\pi_0$ 73.0%).
- The average completion score for these tasks is 81.0%, significantly outperforming GR00T-N1.5 (68.0%) and $\pi_0$ (67.0%), highlighting its capacity for stable, precise execution in sequential actions.

#### 4.5. Emerging Open-World Embodied Generalization

EO-1 exhibits strong robustness to distribution shifts in real-world scenarios across three generalization axes (visual, action, language variations):
- It achieves the highest overall completion score (73.0%), surpassing GR00T-N1.5 (60.0%) and $\pi_0$ (51.0%).
- The largest relative gain is observed in **Language Generalization (79.0%)**, indicating strong linguistic robustness to typos, rephrasing, and fuzzy instructions—a persistent weakness in many VLM-based policies. This confirms that interleaved vision-text-action training enhances resilience to perceptual, control, and instructional shifts.

#### 4.6. Enhanced Generalization with Unified Reasoning

On a reasoning-control benchmark (Visual Rearrangement, Tic-Tac-Toe, Make Breakfast Sandwich, Roast Beef Steak), where planning and control are integrated within the single decoder:
- EO-1 achieves the highest average completion score, with significant gains, particularly in Tic-Tac-Toe (76.0% vs. $\pi_0$ 24.0%) and Make Breakfast Sandwich (84.0% vs. $\pi_0$ 70.0%).
- This success is attributed to the alignment of symbolic planning and continuous control within a cohesive backbone, eliminating interface gaps and enabling smooth, context-aware action execution.

#### 4.7. Discussion: Architecture and Data Contributions to Generalization

Ablation studies reveal key insights:
- **Unified Hybrid Decoding vs. Autoregression:** The hybrid model (EO-1 (base)) consistently outperforms fully autoregressive EO-1 (fast), especially in Action Generalization, due to superior precision from flow-matching. Pure autoregression struggles with low-frequency action modes and generalization under distribution shifts.
- **Scaling Interleaved Data:** Scaling interleaved demonstrations (EO-1 (interleaved)) consistently leads to faster and more robust generalization than training solely on robot control data (EO-1 (base)), particularly enhancing Language Generalization significantly.
- **Data Alignment:** Crucially, incorporating *generic* instruction-following data (EO-1 (llava)) can lead to a *performance drop* if not specifically aligned with embodied control tasks, highlighting that data quality and task alignment are more important than mere quantity for effective physical grounding. Multi-task multimodal datasets (like Bridge Data for WidowX) are more effective for action generalization than limited single-task data.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The EO-Robotics project, with its EO-1 model, EO-Data1.5M dataset, and EO-Bench benchmark, represents a substantial stride forward in the quest for general-purpose embodied AI. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Breaking Through Interleaved Reasoning-Acting:** EO-1's most significant contribution is its effective demonstration of human-like, interleaved vision-text-action reasoning and control. By unifying symbolic planning and continuous motor execution within a single, shared transformer backbone, it addresses a fundamental limitation of previous VLA models that suffered from "plan-act mismatches" or treated action generation as a separate, terminal step. This architectural innovation significantly enhances the coherence and context-awareness of robot behavior.

2. **State-of-the-Art Performance:** The model consistently achieves superior performance across a wide array of embodied reasoning and robot control benchmarks, spanning simulated and real-world environments, short- and long-horizon tasks, and diverse robot embodiments. This strong empirical validation underscores its readiness for more complex, real-world deployment challenges.

3. **Robust Open-World Generalization:** EO-1 exhibits remarkable resilience to distribution shifts in perception, control, and instructions, as evidenced by its strong performance across visual, action, and language generalization benchmarks. Its particular strength in linguistic robustness—a common weakness for VLM-based policies—is a crucial step towards truly generalist robots that can reliably interpret and execute varied human commands in unstructured settings.

4. **High-Quality, Aligned Data for Embodied AI:** The introduction of EO-Data1.5M, a massive and meticulously curated dataset emphasizing interleaved vision-text-action comprehension, is a critical contribution. Its scalable construction pipeline for annotating robot episodes with rich temporal and spatial QA pairs provides a valuable resource for training future embodied foundation models, addressing the scarcity of high-quality, task-aligned multimodal data. The findings on data alignment also offer important guidance, emphasizing that not all large-scale multimodal data are beneficial, and task-specific alignment is key.

5. **Comprehensive and Disentangled Evaluation:** The EO-Bench benchmark offers a novel and structured evaluation suite for embodied reasoning, enabling researchers to disentangle and diagnose model strengths and weaknesses across various cognitive dimensions (spatial understanding, physical common sense, task reasoning, state estimation). This facilitates more targeted research and development.

6. **Accelerating Open-Science and Community Research:** The full open-sourcing of the EO-1 model weights, training code, and the entire EO-Data1.5M dataset is a profound act of collaboration. This democratizes access to powerful tools and large-scale data, significantly lowering the barrier for entry into advanced embodied AI research and fostering accelerated innovation within the academic and industrial communities. It positions EO-Robotics as a cornerstone for future general-purpose autonomous robot development.

7. **Paving the Way for Future Robot Capabilities:** While EO-1 makes a solid step, the identified future work outlines its potential impact on expanding robot capabilities further into complex scenarios like navigation, obstacle avoidance, human intent recognition, and seamless human-robot interaction/cooperation. The exploration of asynchronous inference for simultaneous reasoning and action promises to move robots closer to real-time, human-like responsiveness. Incorporating more diverse human and humanoid robot data could also extend its applicability to a broader range of real-life robotic tasks.

In conclusion, EO-1 pushes the boundaries of embodied AI by demonstrating a unified approach to reasoning and acting that is more aligned with human intelligence. Its impressive empirical results and commitment to open science set a new benchmark and provide a robust foundation for the next generation of general-purpose autonomous robots.
