# Exploratory Diffusion Model for Unsupervised Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Tsinghua University developed the Exploratory Diffusion Model (ExDM), integrating diffusion models for the first time into unsupervised reinforcement learning to capture complex data distributions for intrinsic reward generation and policy initialization. ExDM achieved 51% higher state coverage in complex maze environments during pre-training and improved the interquartile mean (IQM) by 13% on URLB benchmarks during downstream task adaptation.

## Problem

- Existing Unsupervised Reinforcement Learning (URL) policies lack the expressiveness to accurately model the diverse and heterogeneous data distributions generated during exploration, limiting effective intrinsic reward design and policy initialization.
- Integrating highly expressive generative models like diffusion models into online URL for unsupervised pre-training faces challenges of training instability and high computational costs due to their multi-step sampling processes.
- Efficiently fine-tuning pre-trained diffusion policies for downstream tasks is complex due to their multi-step action generation and the difficulty in directly calculating closed-form probabilities required by some RL objectives.

## Method

- Utilizes ==two diffusion models== during unsupervised pre-training to accurately model heterogeneous state and state-action distributions from the replay buffer.
- Generates a score-based intrinsic reward from the state diffusion model to guide a computationally efficient Gaussian behavior policy for diverse exploration.
- Develops an alternating optimization method for efficiently fine-tuning the pre-trained diffusion policy, combining Implicit Q-learning (IQL) for Q-function updates and Contrastive Energy Prediction (CEP) for policy distillation.

## Results

- Demonstrated superior unsupervised exploration in Maze2d environments, achieving up to **51%** higher state coverage in complex mazes and comparable performance with **37%** fewer timesteps.
- When fine-tuning the Gaussian behavior policy, ExDM surpassed existing URL baselines on the URLB benchmark, showing a **13%** higher Interquartile Mean (IQM) than the next best method.
- The proposed alternating optimization effectively fine-tuned the diffusion policy, outperforming other diffusion policy fine-tuning methods on URLB, although its overall performance was lower than ExDM's Gaussian policy fine-tuning due to online interaction limitations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Policies aimed at maximizing state entropy for diverse exploration are inherently complex and non-deterministic, requiring highly expressive models to capture their distributions.
> - Diffusion models can effectively learn complex, heterogeneous data distributions observed during unsupervised exploration, providing a robust basis for deriving intrinsic rewards that guide agents to novel states.
> - It is possible to efficiently fine-tune multi-step sampling diffusion policies for downstream tasks via an alternating optimization method that guarantees policy improvement and convergence.
