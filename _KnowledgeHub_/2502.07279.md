# Exploratory Diffusion Model for Unsupervised Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Tsinghua University developed the Exploratory Diffusion Model (ExDM), integrating diffusion models for the first time into unsupervised reinforcement learning to capture complex data distributions for intrinsic reward generation and policy initialization. ExDM achieved 51% higher state coverage in complex maze environments during pre-training and improved the interquartile mean (IQM) by 13% on URLB benchmarks during downstream task adaptation.

## Problem

- Existing Unsupervised Reinforcement Learning (URL) policies lack the expressiveness to accurately model the diverse and heterogeneous data distributions generated during exploration, limiting effective intrinsic reward design and policy initialization.
- Integrating highly expressive generative models like diffusion models into online URL for unsupervised pre-training faces challenges of training instability and high computational costs due to their multi-step sampling processes.
- Efficiently fine-tuning pre-trained diffusion policies for downstream tasks is complex due to their multi-step action generation and the difficulty in directly calculating closed-form probabilities required by some RL objectives.

## Method

- Utilizes ==two diffusion models== during unsupervised pre-training to accurately model heterogeneous state and state-action distributions from the replay buffer.
- Generates a score-based intrinsic reward from the state diffusion model to guide a computationally efficient Gaussian behavior policy for diverse exploration.
- Develops an alternating optimization method for efficiently fine-tuning the pre-trained diffusion policy, combining Implicit Q-learning (IQL) for Q-function updates and Contrastive Energy Prediction (CEP) for policy distillation.

## Results

- Demonstrated superior unsupervised exploration in Maze2d environments, achieving up to **51%** higher state coverage in complex mazes and comparable performance with **37%** fewer timesteps.
- When fine-tuning the Gaussian behavior policy, ExDM surpassed existing URL baselines on the URLB benchmark, showing a **13%** higher Interquartile Mean (IQM) than the next best method.
- The proposed alternating optimization effectively fine-tuned the diffusion policy, outperforming other diffusion policy fine-tuning methods on URLB, although its overall performance was lower than ExDM's Gaussian policy fine-tuning due to online interaction limitations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Policies aimed at maximizing state entropy for diverse exploration are inherently complex and non-deterministic, requiring highly expressive models to capture their distributions.
> - Diffusion models can effectively learn complex, heterogeneous data distributions observed during unsupervised exploration, providing a robust basis for deriving intrinsic rewards that guide agents to novel states.
> - It is possible to efficiently fine-tune multi-step sampling diffusion policies for downstream tasks via an alternating optimization method that guarantees policy improvement and convergence.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of agents capable of efficient adaptation and generalization across a diverse range of tasks remains a core challenge in reinforcement learning (RL). Traditional RL methods often require task-specific rewards, which can be scarce or difficult to design. Unsupervised Reinforcement Learning (URL) addresses this by pre-training agents in reward-free environments to acquire generalizable skills or representations that facilitate rapid adaptation to new tasks with minimal additional interaction.

Existing URL approaches primarily rely on intrinsic rewards to guide exploration during pre-training. These intrinsic rewards typically aim to maximize state entropy or discover diverse skills. However, a significant limitation of many current URL methods is their reliance on relatively simple pre-trained policies, such as Gaussian policies or discrete skill-based policies. While these are easy to train and sample from, they often lack the expressive power to adequately model the complex, heterogeneous data distributions generated during diverse exploration. This limitation can hinder both the effectiveness of unsupervised exploration and the efficiency of subsequent fine-tuning on downstream tasks. Incorporating more expressive models, such as deep generative models, could overcome this, but often introduces challenges related to training instability and high computational costs, particularly with multi-step sampling processes.

Diffusion models have recently demonstrated considerable success in accurately representing complex data distributions and generating high-fidelity samples across various domains, including image synthesis and, more recently, in reinforcement learning for tasks like offline RL and planning. However, their application to the specific challenge of unsupervised exploration within URL, particularly in leveraging their strong modeling capabilities for intrinsic reward design and policy initialization, has been less explored.

This work introduces the Exploratory Diffusion Model (ExDM) to bridge this gap. It positions itself by proposing the first integration of diffusion models into the URL paradigm to specifically address the challenges of capturing heterogeneous data distributions for improved exploration and providing powerful policy initialization for efficient fine-tuning. By doing so, ExDM aims to enhance the generalization capabilities of RL agents by leveraging the strengths of diffusion models while mitigating their inherent computational complexities in an online learning context.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop the Exploratory Diffusion Model (ExDM) to enhance unsupervised reinforcement learning by leveraging the expressive capabilities of diffusion models. This involves improving the efficiency and diversity of exploration during the pre-training phase and providing effective policy initializations for rapid adaptation to downstream tasks.

The motivation for ExDM stems from several key challenges observed in current URL methodologies:

1. **Limited Policy Expressiveness for Heterogeneous Data:** Unsupervised pre-training in reward-free environments aims to maximize exploration, often by maximizing state entropy. This requires policies capable of generating diverse behaviors and accurately modeling the distribution of collected data. However, the data collected during exploration is inherently diverse and heterogeneous, as the agent continuously visits new states. Existing URL methods often employ simple policies (e.g., Gaussian policies, discrete skill-based policies) for ease of training and sampling. These simple policies frequently fail to capture the full diversity of the explored data in the replay buffer. This limitation constrains both the effectiveness of unsupervised exploration (as intrinsic rewards based on inaccurate models might misguide the agent) and the quality of the policy initialization for downstream tasks. The paper theoretically supports this by demonstrating that policies maximizing state entropy are inherently complex and not deterministic with high probability (Theorem 3.1), thus demanding powerful modeling capabilities.

2. **Computational Challenges with Expressive Models:** While deep generative models, such as diffusion models, offer superior expressive power to address the limitations of simple policies, their direct integration into online reinforcement learning (particularly during pre-training) can introduce significant practical challenges. These include potential training instability and high computational costs, primarily due to the multi-step sampling procedures typically required by diffusion models for action generation. Overcoming these efficiency and stability issues is crucial for making such expressive models practical for URL.

3. **Inefficient Fine-tuning of Diffusion Policies:** Beyond pre-training, the goal of URL is efficient adaptation to downstream tasks, often under conditions of limited online interaction. While pre-trained Gaussian policies can be fine-tuned using standard RL algorithms, adapting a pre-trained diffusion policy presents additional complexities. The multi-step sampling process inherent to diffusion models makes online fine-tuning challenging due to instability and the difficulty of directly calculating closed-form probabilities required by some RL objectives. Therefore, a robust and efficient method for adapting diffusion policies to specific task rewards within limited interaction steps is necessary.

ExDM is motivated to address these challenges by proposing a novel framework that integrates diffusion models to effectively capture complex data distributions for intrinsic reward generation, while simultaneously addressing the computational and fine-tuning complexities associated with these powerful models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ExDM operates in two primary stages: unsupervised pre-training and efficient online fine-tuning for downstream tasks.

#### 3.1. Unsupervised Pre-training

The pre-training phase in ExDM focuses on efficient exploration in reward-free environments by leveraging the expressive power of diffusion models.

1. **Modeling Heterogeneous Data Distributions:** A core component of ExDM is the use of two diffusion models, $\epsilon_\theta'$ and $\epsilon_\theta$. These models are trained to accurately estimate the distribution of collected states and state-action pairs, respectively, within the replay buffer $\mathcal{D}$. This addresses the challenge of heterogeneous data that simpler policies struggle to model. The objective for training these diffusion models is to minimize the expected squared error between the predicted noise and the actual noise injected during a forward diffusion process (Equation 6).

2. **Score-Based Intrinsic Reward:** To guide exploration, ExDM introduces a score-based intrinsic reward, $R_\text{score}(s)$. This reward is derived from the evidence lower bound (ELBO) of the negative log-probability of the state distribution, $-\log p_{\theta'}(s)$, as estimated by the state diffusion model $\epsilon_{\theta'}$. Specifically, $R_\text{score}(s) = E_{\epsilon,t}[\lVert \epsilon_{\theta'}(s|t) - \epsilon \rVert^2]$. Intuitively, this reward measures how well the diffusion model fits the observed state data. By maximizing this intrinsic reward, the agent is encouraged to explore regions that are poorly represented or infrequently visited in the replay buffer, thus promoting the discovery of novel states and maximizing state entropy.

3. **Efficient Action Sampling during Pre-training:** Directly sampling actions from a diffusion policy involves a multi-step reverse diffusion process, which is computationally expensive and potentially unstable in an online pre-training loop. To circumvent this, ExDM employs an additional *Gaussian behavior policy* ($\pi_g$). This policy is responsible for efficiently interacting with the reward-free environment and collecting data for the replay buffer. The Gaussian behavior policy ($\pi_g$) is trained using a standard RL algorithm (e.g., DDPG) with the score-based intrinsic reward $R_\text{score}(s)$ as its objective. This approach ensures efficient data collection while benefiting from the sophisticated distribution modeling of the diffusion model for intrinsic motivation.
 Algorithm 1 outlines this process: diffusion models and the score model are updated using sampled data from the replay buffer, intrinsic rewards are calculated, $\pi_g$ is trained, and then used to collect more data.

#### 3.2. Efficient Online Fine-tuning to Downstream Tasks

The second stage focuses on adapting the pre-trained policies to specific downstream tasks with limited online interaction.

1. **Fine-tuning the Gaussian Behavior Policy:** The pre-trained Gaussian behavior policy ($\pi_g$) can be fine-tuned using standard online RL algorithms, directly optimizing for the extrinsic task-specific reward $R(s, a)$. This provides a baseline comparison with other URL methods.

2. **Fine-tuning the Diffusion Policy:** ExDM also facilitates the fine-tuning of the pre-trained diffusion policy ($\pi_d$), which is expected to capture more diverse behaviors. This presents a challenge due to the multi-step sampling and lack of closed-form probability calculations for diffusion policies.
 - **Fine-tuning Objective:** The fine-tuning objective (Equation 9) is formulated to maximize the expected cumulative return while penalizing deviations from the pre-trained diffusion policy ($\pi_d$) using a KL-divergence regularization. This objective can be rewritten in terms of a state-action Q-function (Equation 10).
 - **Alternating Optimization:** ExDM proposes an alternating optimization method, formally established by Theorem 3.2, which guarantees policy improvement and convergence to the optimal policy for the fine-tuning objective. In each iteration, the Q-function is updated, and then the diffusion policy is distilled based on this updated Q-function.
 - **Q-function Optimization:** Implicit Q-learning (IQL) is utilized for updating the Q-function. IQL employs expectile regression (Equation 31) to effectively penalize out-of-distribution actions, which is beneficial in online settings with limited data.
 - **Diffusion Policy Distillation:** To update the diffusion policy $\pi_d$, ExDM employs Contrastive Energy Prediction (CEP) to estimate the "energy guidance" (a term related to the gradient of the Q-function) at each diffusion timestep (Equation 33, 35). This guidance is then used to distill a new, fine-tuned diffusion policy $\epsilon_\psi$. The distillation objective (Equation 14) minimizes the difference between the new policy's score function and a combination of the pre-trained policy's score function and the estimated energy guidance. This allows the new diffusion policy $\epsilon_\psi$ to be sampled to generate actions for the fine-tuned policy $\pi_n$.
 Algorithm 2 details the diffusion policy fine-tuning, iteratively updating the Q-function and energy guidance, then optimizing the diffusion policy through distillation, and finally interacting with the environment using the updated diffusion policy.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

ExDM's performance was evaluated across various benchmarks focusing on both unsupervised exploration efficiency and rapid adaptation to downstream tasks.

#### 4.1. Unsupervised Pre-training for Exploration (Maze2d)

- **Qualitative Visualization (Figure 2, Appendix C.4):** In challenging Maze2d environments such as Square-bottleneck and Square-large, ExDM exhibited superior exploration capabilities. While baseline methods often struggled, failing to cover entire maze regions or getting stuck at wall corners, ExDM successfully explored nearly the entirety of these complex mazes. In simpler mazes like Square-a, ExDM converged faster to near-complete coverage compared to other baselines.
- **Quantitative Metrics (Table 1, Figure 3):** ExDM achieved substantially higher state coverage ratios during pre-training than existing state-of-the-art baselines. In structurally complicated mazes, ExDM demonstrated an improvement in performance by 51% and achieved comparable performance with approximately 37% fewer timesteps relative to the best baselines. These results indicate that the score-based intrinsic reward, combined with the diffusion model's data estimation ability, effectively guides agents to visit more diverse states.

#### 4.2. Fine-tuning the Gaussian Policy to Downstream Tasks (URLB)

- **Aggregate Metrics (Figure 4, Table 4):** When fine-tuning the pre-trained Gaussian behavior policy ($\pi_g$) using DDPG (for a fair comparison with other URL baselines), ExDM significantly outperformed all existing exploration (e.g., RND) and skill-based (e.g., CIC, CeSD) methods across aggregate metrics including mean, median, interquartile mean (IQM), and optimality gap. For instance, ExDM's IQM was reported to be 13% higher than that of the second-best method, CeSD.
- **Detailed Task Performance (Table 3):** ExDM achieved the highest scores in a greater number of individual downstream tasks compared to baselines. Notably, it attained state-of-the-art performance in the Quadruped and Jaco domains. These findings suggest that the exploration guided by ExDM's diffusion-model-derived intrinsic rewards leads to more generalizable pre-trained representations.

#### 4.3. Fine-tuning the Diffusion Policy to Downstream Tasks (URLB)

- **Aggregate Metrics (Figure 5):** ExDM, when fine-tuning its pre-trained diffusion policy ($\pi_d$) using its proposed alternating optimization method, substantially outperformed existing diffusion policy fine-tuning baselines (DQL, IDQL, QSM, DIPO).
- **Comparison to Gaussian Policy Fine-tuning:** While superior to other diffusion policy fine-tuning methods, the performance of ExDM's diffusion policy fine-tuning was observed to be lower than its Gaussian policy fine-tuning counterpart. The paper attributes this to the limited interaction timesteps available during the online fine-tuning stage for diffusion policies.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies (Figure 6, Appendix C.6)

- **Pre-training Steps:** Increasing pre-training timesteps from 100k to 2M significantly improved ExDM's fine-tuned performance, surpassing all baselines from 500k steps onwards. This indicates that more extensive unsupervised exploration with ExDM increasingly benefits downstream task adaptation.
- **Q-function Optimization:** The study demonstrated that using Implicit Q-learning (IQL) for Q-function optimization during diffusion policy fine-tuning consistently yielded better performance than an alternative "In-support Softmax Q-Learning" approach, thereby validating IQL's efficiency in this context.
- **Diffusion Policy Sampling Steps:** Performance of the fine-tuned diffusion policy improved as the number of sampling steps for action generation increased, with stabilization observed once the sampling steps exceeded approximately 5. This suggests a balance between computational cost and policy fidelity.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Exploratory Diffusion Model (ExDM) contributes to the field of reinforcement learning by addressing key challenges in unsupervised reinforcement learning (URL).

- **Novel Integration of Diffusion Models in URL:** To the best of the authors' knowledge, this work represents the first successful integration of diffusion models into the URL framework for unsupervised exploration. This is significant because diffusion models possess strong expressive capabilities to capture heterogeneous data distributions, a feature often lacking in prior URL methods that rely on simpler policy representations. By demonstrating that diffusion models can be effectively leveraged for intrinsic reward design, ExDM expands the scope of generative models in RL.

- **Enhanced Exploration and Generalization:** The core contribution of ExDM is its ability to boost unsupervised exploration efficiency. By using diffusion models to accurately estimate the distribution of collected data and deriving a score-based intrinsic reward, ExDM encourages agents to explore less-visited or poorly-fitted states. The empirical results in Maze2d environments confirm this, showing superior state coverage compared to existing state-of-the-art URL methods. This improved exploration translates to more diverse and generalizable pre-trained policies, leading to faster and more efficient adaptation to a wide array of downstream tasks, as demonstrated in the URLB continuous control benchmark.

- **Theoretical and Algorithmic Advancements for Diffusion Policies:** ExDM provides theoretical analyses for fine-tuning diffusion policies in the URL context, particularly under limited online interactions. The proposed alternating optimization method, with theoretical guarantees of policy improvement and convergence, offers a practical approach to adapt pre-trained diffusion policies to new tasks. This addresses a significant challenge associated with the computational cost and instability of directly fine-tuning multi-step sampling diffusion policies. The use of Implicit Q-learning for Q-function optimization and Contrastive Energy Prediction for policy distillation further refines the practical implementation.

- **Potential for Future Research and Applications:** The work opens new avenues for research into high-fidelity generative models in URL. It suggests that more complex and diverse behaviors can be acquired during pre-training, which could be particularly beneficial for large-scale pre-trained agents or real-world control applications where diverse skill sets are crucial. Future directions include exploring the application of diffusion policies to discrete action spaces, further optimizing the computational efficiency of diffusion models within online RL, and investigating their use in more complex, real-world robotic control scenarios. The insights gained from ExDM could inspire the development of more robust and adaptable agents capable of handling increasingly complex and varied environments.
