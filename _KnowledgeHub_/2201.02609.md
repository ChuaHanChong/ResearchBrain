# Generalized Category Discovery

> [!summary] Summary
> The Visual Geometry Group (VGG) at Oxford introduces Generalized Category Discovery (GCD), a new problem setting where unlabelled data may contain instances from both known and novel categories. Their method, which combines self-supervised Vision Transformers fine-tuned with a joint contrastive loss and semi-supervised clustering, achieves substantial performance gains of up to 27% in accuracy over adapted baselines on challenging fine-grained recognition tasks.

## Problem

- Existing image recognition paradigms (e.g., SSL, OSR, NCD) impose restrictive assumptions on unlabelled data, assuming it's either all from known classes, entirely novel, or only requiring detection of unknowns.
- Real-world scenarios often present unlabelled data that is a mix of both known and novel categories, requiring a system that can concurrently categorize known instances and discover/categorize novel ones.
- Many clustering-based methods for novel category discovery assume the number of unknown classes is known a priori, which is often not the case in practical applications.

## Method

- Leverages a ==ViT-B-16 backbone pre-trained with DINO== and fine-tunes it using a ==combined unsupervised and supervised contrastive loss==, enabling effective learning from labeled and unlabelled data without overfitting.
- Employs a ==semi-supervised k-means clustering== algorithm for label assignment, which guides the clustering process by fixing assignments for known labeled instances while allowing unlabelled instances to be assigned to any category.
- Proposes a novel method to estimate the total number of categories by optimizing the clustering accuracy on the labeled subset using Brent's algorithm, avoiding the need for prior knowledge of the total cluster count.

## Results

- Substantially outperforms adapted SOTA baselines (+**9.3%** generic object recognition, +**8.9%** fine-grained), maintaining high accuracy on known ('Old') classes while improving novel ('New') ones (**97.9%** ACC 'Old' / **88.2%** 'New' on CIFAR10).
- The proposed method for estimating the total number of categories achieves good accuracy, with a maximum error of **10%** on generic datasets and an average discrepancy of **18.9%** on more complex fine-grained datasets.
- Ablation studies confirm the critical role of the DINO pre-trained ViT backbone (nearly **20%** ACC drop without it) and the combined supervised/unsupervised contrastive fine-tuning (boosting ACC by 16-**19%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Direct clustering in the feature space of powerful, self-supervised ViT representations is more robust to overfitting on labeled data than traditional parametric classifiers in open-world settings, and a combined unsupervised and supervised contrastive loss during fine-tuning creates highly separable, semantically rich feature spaces for distinguishing known and novel categories.
> - Semi-supervised k-means clustering, where known class instances are anchored to their true labels, provides essential guidance that significantly improves recognition accuracy for both known and novel categories.
> - Clustering accuracy on the labeled subset can serve as a reliable heuristic to estimate the total number of categories in the entire dataset, addressing a common challenge in unsupervised and semi-supervised learning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of image recognition has traditionally been dominated by supervised learning, where models are trained on extensively labeled datasets, and test images are assumed to belong to the same known classes. However, real-world scenarios are often more "open-world," where complete labeling is impractical or impossible, and new, unforeseen categories may appear. This paper addresses a significant gap in existing recognition paradigms by introducing **Generalized Category Discovery (GCD)**, a highly realistic and generalized setting.

The authors meticulously position GCD relative to several existing, related research areas:

- **Image Classification:** This is the most restrictive setting, assuming all training and test images belong to predefined, known classes. GCD relaxes this entirely.
- **Semi-Supervised Learning (SSL):** SSL aims to leverage unlabelled data to improve a model's performance when only a small portion of data is labeled. However, a key limitation of SSL is its assumption that all unlabelled images still belong to the *same* set of classes as the labeled ones. GCD explicitly challenges this by allowing unlabelled images to come from *both* known and novel classes.
- **Open-Set Recognition (OSR):** OSR focuses on classifying known classes while merely *detecting* instances of unknown classes. It does not require further categorization or clustering of these unknown instances. GCD goes beyond OSR by requiring the categorization of *all* unlabelled images, whether from known or novel categories.
- **Novel Category Discovery (NCD):** NCD is perhaps the closest existing setting to GCD. In NCD, the goal is to discover new classes within an unlabelled dataset, assuming that *all* unlabelled images come *exclusively* from novel categories, entirely disjoint from the labeled set. This is a strong and often unrealistic assumption. GCD specifically relaxes this by allowing the unlabelled set to contain a mix of instances from both labeled and novel classes, providing a more comprehensive and real-world-applicable problem definition.

By formalizing GCD, the paper presents a unified framework that encompasses and extends aspects of SSL, OSR, and NCD. It acknowledges the practical reality that in many applications (e.g., supermarket product recognition, medical image analysis, autonomous driving), one cannot assume whether new images are from known categories, entirely new ones, or a mix of both. This work highlights the limitations of prior, more constrained settings and proposes a more flexible and robust solution for truly open-world image recognition. The authors specifically note concurrent work on "Open World Semi-Supervised Learning" by Cao et al. which addresses a similar setting, further emphasizing the emerging importance of this research direction.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this paper is to formalize and provide a robust solution for **Generalized Category Discovery (GCD)**. The authors are motivated by the inherent limitations of existing image recognition paradigms when applied to real-world, dynamic environments.

The key motivations and objectives can be summarized as follows:

- **Address Realistic Use Cases:** The paper argues that real-world image recognition systems rarely encounter scenarios where all categories are known beforehand, or where new data is strictly from entirely novel or entirely known classes. They liken this to an infant learning about the world, encountering both known objects (e.g., a "dog" they've been taught) and new objects (e.g., a "cat" or "bicycle" they haven't seen but can group). This motivates the need for a system that can concurrently categorize known instances and discover/categorize novel ones within a single unlabelled dataset.
- **Overcome Restrictive Assumptions:** Existing methods in SSL, OSR, and NCD impose restrictive assumptions about the nature of unlabelled data (e.g., all from known classes in SSL, all from unknown classes in NCD, or only detecting unknowns in OSR). GCD aims to remove these constraints, allowing unlabelled images to originate from both labeled and novel categories. Furthermore, it challenges the common assumption in clustering-based methods that the number of unknown classes is known a priori.
- **Establish Strong Baselines:** A crucial objective is to establish robust baselines for the newly defined GCD setting. This involves adapting state-of-the-art NCD algorithms (RankStats and UNO) to the more general GCD problem, providing a clear benchmark for future research.
- **Develop a Robust and Effective Method:** The core objective is to propose a novel approach for GCD that can substantially outperform these baselines. The authors aim to design a method that avoids the overfitting tendencies of parametric classifiers to labeled classes, particularly in this generalized setting where novel classes are present.
- **Solve the Class Number Estimation Problem:** A practical challenge in unsupervised and semi-supervised clustering is the need to specify the number of clusters (categories). The paper aims to propose an algorithm to estimate the total number of categories in the unlabelled data, a problem that is largely understudied but critical for real-world deployment.
- **Rigorous Evaluation:** The paper sets out to thoroughly evaluate the proposed method and baselines on diverse datasets, including generic object recognition benchmarks (CIFAR, ImageNet-100) and more challenging fine-grained datasets from the Semantic Shift Benchmark (CUB, Stanford Cars, Herbarium19), which are specifically designed to isolate the problem of semantic novelty.

In essence, the paper seeks to push the boundaries of image recognition towards more human-like, adaptive intelligence, capable of learning from partial supervision and autonomously expanding its categorical understanding of the world.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology for Generalized Category Discovery (GCD) hinges on leveraging powerful self-supervised pre-trained models, particularly Vision Transformers, combined with a novel semi-supervised clustering approach and a method for estimating the number of categories.

The GCD setting is formally defined: given a dataset $D$ comprising a labeled subset $D_L = \{(x_i, y_i)\}_{i=1}^N \in X \times Y_L$ and an unlabelled subset $D_U = \{(x_i, y_i)\}_{i=1}^M \in X \times Y_U$, where $Y_L \subset Y_U$. The task is to categorize all images in $D_U$, knowing that $Y_U$ contains both known classes ($Y_L$) and novel classes ($Y_U \setminus Y_L$).

The overall approach, as illustrated in Figure 1, can be broken down into three main components:

1. **Feature Extraction with Vision Transformer (ViT):**
 - **Backbone:** The core of the method relies on a **ViT-B-16** model pre-trained with **DINO** (Self-supervised Vision Transformers) on ImageNet. DINO is known for producing robust representations that exhibit strong "nearest neighbor" classification properties. This choice is motivated by the desire to perform direct clustering in the feature space without relying on parametric classification heads, which are prone to overfitting.
 - **Fine-tuning:** The pre-trained ViT model is further fine-tuned on the target dataset ($D_L \cup D_U$) using a combined contrastive loss:
 - **Unsupervised Contrastive Loss ($\mathcal{L}_u$):** Applied to *all* images in a mini-batch, without using labels. This ensures that the features are not overfit to the subset of labeled classes and learn generalizable representations.
 - **Supervised Contrastive Loss ($\mathcal{L}_s$):** Applied specifically to the *labeled* instances within the mini-batch. This component uses ground-truth labels to pull embeddings of same-class instances closer and push different-class instances apart.
 - **Total Loss ($\mathcal{L}_t$):** A weighted sum of the unsupervised and supervised contrastive losses: $\mathcal{L}_t = (1 - \lambda) \sum_{i \in B} \mathcal{L}_{u_i} + \lambda \sum_{i \in B_L} \mathcal{L}_{s_i}$. The use of a contrastive framework for labels means labeled and unlabelled data are treated similarly, minimizing overfitting to specific classes.

2. **Label Assignment with Semi-supervised K-Means Clustering:**
 - After obtaining the fine-tuned feature representations, the model assigns class labels to each unlabelled data point. Instead of using a parametric classifier, which can overfit to labeled data, the authors propose a non-parametric approach based on a modified k-means algorithm.
 - **Initialization:** The initial centroids for the known classes ($|Y_L|$ of them) are directly derived from the average of the ground-truth labeled instances. Additional centroids for the novel classes ($|Y_U \setminus Y_L|$ of them) are initialized using k-means++ on the unlabelled data, constrained by the existing labeled centroids.
 - **Clustering:** During the iterative k-means process (centroid update and cluster assignment), a crucial constraint is applied: instances from $D_L$ are *always* forced to remain assigned to their correct, ground-truth-defined clusters. Unlabelled instances from $D_U$ can be assigned to any cluster (both "old" and "new" class centroids) based on distance. This semi-supervised approach guides the clustering process with reliable label information.

3. **Estimating the Number of Classes in Unlabelled Data:**
 - Addressing the common assumption that the total number of classes ($k = |Y_L \cup Y_U|$) is known, the paper proposes a novel method for estimating $k$.
 - **Heuristic:** The core idea is that the clustering accuracy *on the labeled subset* ($D_L$) will be maximized when the total number of clusters $k$ used for clustering the *entire dataset* ($D_L \cup D_U$) is indeed equal to the true total number of classes.
 - **Optimization:** They empirically show that clustering accuracy on $D_L$ forms an approximately bell-shaped function of $k$. Therefore, they treat clustering accuracy as a "black-box" scoring function and optimize it using **Brent's algorithm** to find the optimal $k$. This is more efficient than exhaustive search used in some prior works.

**Baselines:** To provide strong comparative results, the authors adapt two state-of-the-art Novel Category Discovery (NCD) methods for the GCD setting:
- **RankStats+:** Originally designed for disjoint labeled/unlabeled classes, it's adapted by training a single classification head for all potential classes ($|Y_U|$), using cross-entropy for labeled instances and binary cross-entropy with pseudo-labels for unlabelled ones.
- **UNO+:** Similarly, UNO's mechanism, which involves separate heads for labeled and unlabelled data and a SwAV-like training, is adapted by concatenating these heads, allowing unlabelled pseudo-labels to belong to any class in the dataset.

The overall methodology emphasizes non-parametric clustering of robust, self-supervised representations over traditional parametric classification, guided by limited supervision and a novel mechanism for discovering the correct number of categories.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents compelling quantitative and qualitative results demonstrating the effectiveness of their proposed Generalized Category Discovery (GCD) method.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Comparison with Baselines

The authors compare their method against modified state-of-the-art NCD baselines (RankStats+ and UNO+) and a simple k-means baseline on raw DINO features, across various generic object recognition (CIFAR10, CIFAR100, ImageNet-100) and fine-grained (CUB, Stanford Cars, Herbarium19, FGVC-Aircraft) datasets. The evaluation metric is clustering accuracy (ACC) on the unlabelled set ($D_U$), broken down into 'All' (total), 'Old' (known classes within $D_U$), and 'New' (novel classes within $D_U$).

- **Overall Performance (ACC on 'All' instances):**
 - Their method (`Ours`) substantially outperforms all baselines across all datasets.
 - On generic object recognition datasets, `Ours` achieves an average absolute improvement of 9.3% and a proportional improvement of 11.5% over RankStats+ and UNO+.
 - On the more challenging fine-grained datasets, the improvements are even more pronounced: an average absolute gain of 8.9% and a remarkable proportional increase of 27.0%. For instance, on CUB, `Ours` achieves 51.3% ACC vs. UNO+'s 35.1%; on Herbarium19, it's 35.4% vs. UNO+'s 28.3%.
- **Performance on 'Old' vs. 'New' Classes:**
 - Baselines (RankStats+, UNO+), which use parametric classifiers, often show higher ACC on 'Old' (labeled) classes. However, this comes at a significant expense to performance on 'New' (novel) classes, indicating overfitting to the labeled data.
 - `Ours` consistently achieves a better balance, maintaining high performance on 'Old' classes while drastically improving ACC on 'New' classes. For example, on CIFAR10, `Ours` gets 97.9% on 'Old' and 88.2% on 'New', whereas UNO+ gets 98.3% on 'Old' but only 53.8% on 'New'. This highlights the superior generalization capability of their clustering-based approach.

#### 4.2. Estimating the Number of Classes

- The proposed method for estimating $k$ (the total number of categories) achieves good accuracy.
- On generic datasets, the maximum error is only 10% (e.g., ImageNet-100: ground truth 100, ours 109).
- On fine-grained datasets, the average discrepancy is higher (18.9%), reflecting the inherent difficulty of these datasets with many visually similar categories. However, this is still a significant contribution in a domain where $k$ is typically assumed to be known.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

The ablation study thoroughly investigates the contribution of each component of their method, performed on CIFAR100 and Herbarium19.

- **ViT Backbone:** Replacing the ViT-B-16 DINO backbone with a ResNet-50 DINO backbone leads to a nearly 20% drop in overall accuracy, emphasizing the critical role of the Vision Transformer architecture and its DINO pre-training for generating strong, clusterable features.
- **Contrastive Fine-tuning:** Using only raw DINO features (k-means baseline) is much weaker. While individual contrastive components (unsupervised or supervised alone) offer marginal gains, the full benefit is realized when combining *both* unsupervised and supervised contrastive losses on the target dataset. This combination boosts aggregated clustering accuracy by 19% on CIFAR100 and 16% on Herbarium19, highlighting the synergy.
- **Semi-supervised K-Means:** This component provides further performance gains (2% on CIFAR100, 7% on Herbarium19 overall). Notably, it significantly improves ACC on 'New' classes (e.g., 10% on CIFAR100) and particularly on 'Old' classes in challenging fine-grained datasets (19% on Herbarium19), indicating its effectiveness in guiding the clustering process using known labels.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Results (Attention Maps)

- Visualizations of the ViT attention mechanism (Fig. 3 and 7) reveal that after fine-tuning with their approach, the attention heads become more specialized and focus on semantically meaningful object parts (e.g., "Windshield," "Headlight," "Wheelhouse" for cars; "Beak," "Head," "Belly" for birds).
- This specialization transfers effectively between 'Old' and 'New' categories. This suggests that the model learns generalizable part-based representations, which helps it to better discover and categorize novel classes by leveraging knowledge from seen categories.

In summary, the main findings strongly support the paper's claims: GCD is a challenging and realistic setting, and their method, by combining a powerful pre-trained ViT backbone with joint contrastive fine-tuning and a semi-supervised clustering approach, provides a highly effective solution that significantly outperforms competitive baselines and addresses the crucial problem of estimating the number of categories.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The paper "Generalized Category Discovery" makes several significant contributions to the field of computer vision, particularly in addressing the challenges of open-world recognition. Its potential impact is substantial, both theoretically and practically.

#### 5.1. Significance of the GCD Setting

- **Bridging Gaps:** GCD effectively unifies and extends prior problem settings like Semi-Supervised Learning (SSL), Open-Set Recognition (OSR), and Novel Category Discovery (NCD). By explicitly allowing unlabelled data to come from *both* known and unknown classes, it removes restrictive assumptions that limit the applicability of previous methods in real-world scenarios.
- **Real-World Relevance:** The paper convincingly argues that GCD reflects how intelligent systems (like a human infant) or practical applications (e.g., classifying supermarket products, medical pathologies, or vehicles in autonomous driving) would encounter and categorize information. In such settings, it's often impossible to pre-classify whether an incoming image belongs to a known or novel category.
- **New Benchmark:** By formalizing GCD and establishing strong baselines, the paper effectively defines a new benchmark for future research in robust, adaptive image recognition.

#### 5.2. Significance of the Proposed Method

- **Robustness against Overfitting:** A key insight is that parametric classifiers (used in baselines) tend to overfit to the labeled classes in an open-world setting, sacrificing performance on novel categories. The proposed method's reliance on **direct clustering in the feature space of contrastively trained Vision Transformers** proves surprisingly effective. This non-parametric approach inherently avoids the pitfalls of classification heads trained on noisy pseudo-labels or limited known categories.
- **Leveraging Modern Architectures and Learning Paradigms:** The work effectively capitalizes on recent advancements in self-supervised learning (DINO) and Vision Transformers (ViT). It demonstrates that these powerful pre-trained models, when further fine-tuned with a combined supervised and unsupervised contrastive loss, yield highly separable and semantically rich feature spaces suitable for direct clustering.
- **Addressing the "Unknown $k$" Problem:** The proposed method for estimating the number of categories ($k$) in unlabelled data is a practical and important contribution. Most clustering and NCD methods assume prior knowledge of $k$, which is unrealistic in many real-world applications. By showing that clustering accuracy on the *labeled subset* can serve as a reliable proxy for optimal $k$ and using an efficient optimization algorithm, the paper makes the GCD problem much more tractable for deployment.
- **Transferability of Semantic Knowledge:** The qualitative analysis of attention maps reveals that the fine-tuned ViT models learn to attend to semantically meaningful object parts. This ability to form part-based representations, which are transferable between 'Old' and 'New' classes, is crucial for generalizing knowledge and discovering novel categories.

#### 5.3. Potential Impact

The potential impact of this work is significant for several practical applications:

- **Autonomous Systems:** Improved recognition of both known and unforeseen objects (e.g., new types of vehicles, road debris) in dynamic environments.
- **Medical Imaging:** Automated identification of known diseases and potentially the discovery of new pathologies in medical scans, assisting diagnosis.
- **Biodiversity Monitoring:** Efficient categorization of known species and automated discovery of new or rare species in environmental surveys (as exemplified by the Herbarium19 dataset).
- **Industrial Automation:** Recognizing known products on an assembly line while also identifying novel or defective items without explicit re-training for every new variant.
- **Content Moderation/Recommendation:** Identifying and categorizing evolving content (e.g., new memes, emerging trends) in online platforms.

#### 5.4. Limitations and Broader Impact Considerations

The authors acknowledge a few limitations:
- **No Domain Shift:** The current method assumes no significant domain shift between labeled and unlabelled subsets, meaning it might struggle if the data distributions are vastly different (e.g., photos vs. cartoons).
- **Streaming/Continual Learning:** The method requires re-training from scratch as new data becomes available, rather than incrementally learning in a streaming setting.
- **Ethical Considerations:** As with any unsupervised labeling method, caution is advised in sensitive contexts involving personal data, requiring manual checking of results to prevent biases or misinterpretations. The use of large-scale datasets like ImageNet (even if self-supervised) carries inherent societal implications regarding data collection and representation.

Despite these limitations, "Generalized Category Discovery" represents a substantial step forward in developing more adaptive and robust machine vision systems for truly open-world scenarios, moving closer to how biological systems learn and categorize information in unconstrained environments.
