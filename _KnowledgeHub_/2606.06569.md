# PhyRoGen: Synthetic Generation of Physical Robot Manipulation Puzzles Using Procedural Content Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> PhyRoGen is a procedural content generation framework that synthesizes physical robot manipulation puzzles with interlocking object dependencies, where one articulated object must be moved before another. A seeded maker algorithm builds kinematic chains, validating each added joint so it is both blocking when fixed and solvable when free. Six generators produced 24 puzzles, all solvable in 1 to 300 seconds and executable by a simulated mobile manipulator.

## Problem

- Manually curating manipulation datasets is tedious, limits scenario diversity, and makes systematic variation of benchmark difficulty difficult to produce at scale.
- Existing procedural generators for embodied AI and motion planning create scenes and clutter but never interlocking dependencies where one articulated object blocks another.
- Procedural game environments for reinforcement learning target mostly two-dimensional spaces with simplified action spaces, so they do not transfer to robot task and motion planning.

## Method

- **PhyRoGen** maker algorithm iteratively appends an object, joint, and transformation drawn by seed to a kinematic chain, guaranteeing deterministic, interdependent, solvable puzzles.
- Two-stage validation per iteration: an ==RRT-Connect== check rejects the candidate if solvable with the new joint fixed, and rejects it if unsolvable when the joint is free.
- Six concrete generators (Simple Slider, Continuous Space, Grid World, Lockbox Random, Move N Times, Rooms) built in Blender with Phobos exporting ==URDF==, plus a ==Jacobian-based grasp-and-regrasp== manipulation module.

## Results

- Generation is fast: **4** seconds for Simple Sliders up to **83** seconds for Continuous Space, producing **24** unique puzzles across six generators and four seeds each.
- Across **100** repeats per planner, BIT* solved **24** of **24** puzzles at **100%** success and LA-RRT **23** of **24**, while LBT-RRT and RRT* reached **100%** on only **7** and **6**.
- LA-RRT achieved the lowest action cost on all **24** scenarios; full simulated manipulation with a KUKA LBR iiwa took between **4** and **51** seconds per puzzle.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Generate-and-test with a planner in the loop enforces semantic properties: rejecting non-blocking joints guarantees every element is load-bearing, not decorative.
> - Domain-specialized planners pay off on structured tasks: a planner designed for minimal action counts dominated generic optimal sampling planners on both success and cost.
> - Procedural generation of dependency structure, not just geometry, is what makes synthetic data useful for task and motion planning rather than pure motion planning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper addresses challenges in generating large, diverse datasets for robot manipulation tasks, particularly those involving physical puzzles with interlocking object dependencies. This area is crucial for the development and evaluation of robot manipulation algorithms, including motion planners and learning-based systems.

Existing methods for dataset creation often involve manual curation, which is labor-intensive, limits scenario diversity, and makes systematic variations difficult to produce. To overcome these limitations, procedural content generation (PCG) has been explored in robotics to algorithmically create synthetic datasets. Prior PCG frameworks, such as ProcTHOR for interactive physical environments and MotionBenchMaker for motion planning datasets, have advanced dataset generation. However, these systems generally do not focus on problems characterized by "interlocking object dependencies," where the manipulation of one object is conditional on the prior manipulation of another. Such scenarios are termed "physical manipulation puzzles" and are particularly relevant for Task and Motion Planning (TAMP) algorithms, which require reasoning over both discrete logical constraints and continuous state spaces.

The current research positions itself by explicitly filling this gap. It introduces a PCG framework designed to generate complex manipulation puzzles with these interlocking dependencies, thereby distinguishing itself from previous work that primarily focused on simpler, non-interdependent manipulation scenarios like tabletop rearrangement or top-down planning. By generating environments that necessitate physical exploration and reasoning about object interactions, PhyRoGen aims to provide data suitable for both advanced TAMP algorithms and learning frameworks that require complex, physically constrained environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a framework for the automated generation of synthetic datasets of physical robot manipulation puzzles. This framework, named Physical Robot Manipulation Puzzle Generation Framework (PhyRoGen), is specifically designed to create puzzles that feature "interlocking object dependencies," a characteristic often absent in datasets generated by previous PCG methods for robotics.

The motivation behind this work stems from several challenges in current robot manipulation research:
- **Dataset Requirements:** Developing and evaluating robot manipulation algorithms, including motion planners and learning-based systems, necessitates access to large, diverse, and systematically variable datasets.
- **Limitations of Manual Curation:** Manually creating such datasets is a time-consuming and tedious process. It also inherently restricts the diversity of scenarios that can be generated and makes it difficult to introduce systematic variations required for robust algorithm development and benchmarking.
- **Gap in Existing PCG for Robotics:** While PCG has been successfully applied to generate synthetic environments for robot manipulation, most existing frameworks do not generate problems that involve complex interlocking dependencies between objects. These dependencies are critical for tasks requiring a sequence of manipulations where one object must be moved to enable access to or movement of another. Such problems are characteristic of physical puzzles, lockboxes, or assembly/disassembly tasks.
- **Support for Advanced Planning and Learning:** Physical manipulation puzzles, by their nature, require robots to reason about object interactions and physical constraints, which is a key aspect of Task and Motion Planning (TAMP). The availability of procedurally generated puzzles with these characteristics can serve as a crucial ingredient for benchmarking TAMP algorithms and for training robust foundation models capable of complex manipulation.

Therefore, the research aims to provide a general-purpose puzzle generator that can create physically manipulatable puzzles, benchmark their solvability using existing planning algorithms, and demonstrate their manipulation by a simulated robot.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The PhyRoGen framework consists of three main components: kinematic chain generators, the general PhyRoGen maker algorithm, and a set of concrete kinematic chain generators. The methodology also includes puzzle benchmarking and puzzle manipulation demonstrations.

#### 3.1. PhyRoGen Framework

The core of PhyRoGen is its ability to procedurally generate kinematic chains that represent physical manipulation puzzles.

- **Kinematic Chain Generators:** These are functions that take an integer seed, a set of objects, a set of joints, a set of transformations, and a specified number of objects as input. They output a kinematic chain composed of links and joints with their relative transformations. Key attributes of these generators include:
 - **Determinism:** For identical inputs, the same kinematic chain is always produced.
 - **Interdependence:** Puzzles are designed such that they require all joints to be actuated for a solution, ensuring no superfluous components.
 - **Solvability and Manipulability:** Generated chains are intended to result in puzzles that are solvable by planning algorithms and manipulable by a mobile robot.

- **PhyRoGen Maker Algorithm (Algorithm 1):** This is an iterative algorithm designed to construct a kinematic chain representing a physical puzzle.
 1. **Initialization:** An empty kinematic chain is initialized.
 2. **Iterative Object Addition:** For a predefined number of objects (N), the algorithm enters a loop. In each iteration, it selects a random object, joint, and transformation based on the input seed.
 3. **Chain Extension:** These selected components are used to extend the current kinematic chain (K) into a temporary chain (Kt).
 4. **Validation Steps:** Two critical validation steps are performed to ensure the generated puzzle has interlocking dependencies and is solvable:
 - **Necessity Check:** The algorithm first fixes the newly added joint (disables its movement) and checks if the puzzle is *still solvable*. If it is, the component is deemed superfluous (not contributing to the puzzle's difficulty or dependency) and the temporary chain is rejected. This ensures that each added object creates a necessary interlocking dependency in its default state. This check utilizes the RRT-Connect planner.
 - **Solvability Check:** The algorithm then unfixes the joint and checks if the puzzle *is solvable* when the new object is movable. If no solution exists, the puzzle is deemed unsolvable and the temporary chain is rejected.
 5. **Chain Update:** If both validation checks pass, the temporary chain (Kt) becomes the new current kinematic chain (K), and the process continues to the next iteration or terminates.
 6. **Goal Definition:** For each generated chain, the problem is defined as moving the first link to a specific goal position, which varies based on the joint type (e.g., opposite end for prismatic, 90-degree rotation for revolute, random placement for SE(2)).

- **Six Concrete Kinematic Chain Generators:** Based on the general PhyRoGen maker, six specific generators were defined by configuring different input tuples of objects, joints, transformations, and the number of objects (Table I). These include "Simple Sliders," "Continuous Space," "Grid World," "Lockbox Random," "Move N Times," and "Rooms." These generators are implemented using Blender for 3D modeling and the Phobos plugin for URDF export.

#### 3.2. Puzzle Benchmarking and Manipulation

- **Puzzle Benchmarking:** To assess the solvability of the generated puzzles, four sampling-based motion planning algorithms—LA-RRT, RRT*, LBT-RRT, and BIT*—were benchmarked. The objective was to find solutions with a minimal number of actions (individual joint moves). The implementation utilized Robowflex and the Open Motion Planning Library (OMPL) with its internal benchmarking tools. Experiments involved repeating runs 100 times per planner with specific time limits for each puzzle type. Metrics collected included time to find the first feasible solution and cost over time.
- **Puzzle Manipulation:** To demonstrate physical manipulability, a KUKA LBR iiwa robot simulated in PyBullet with an OMPL interface was used.
 1. **Path Extraction:** The lowest-cost solution (object sequences and paths) from the LA-RRT planner was extracted.
 2. **Inverse Kinematics (IK):** For each step in an object path, an IK solution for the robot's end-effector at designated grasp points on the objects was computed using PyBullet's internal IK solver.
 3. **Motion Generation:** The end-effector Jacobian was used to determine joint directions that move the object along its path.
 4. **Singularity Handling:** If a robot joint singularity was detected during execution, the robot would release its grasp and re-grasp the object at a new position to continue the motion, using the Batch Informed Trees (BIT*) planner in OMPL for path planning to new grasp points and IK solutions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents findings across puzzle generation, benchmarking, and manipulation.

#### 4.1. Puzzle Generation

The PhyRoGen framework successfully generated unique physical manipulation puzzles. Using the six defined kinematic chain generators, 24 distinct puzzles were created by running each generator for its first four seeds (Figure 3). The generation times for these puzzles varied from 4 seconds for "Simple Sliders" to 83 seconds for "Continuous Space," with "Grid World" at 5s, "Lockbox Random" at 13s, "Move N Times" at 5s, and "Rooms" at 17s. This indicates the framework's capability to rapidly produce diverse puzzle configurations.

#### 4.2. Puzzle Benchmarking

Benchmarking with four sampling-based motion planning algorithms (LA-RRT, RRT*, LBT-RRT, BIT*) on all 24 generated puzzles yielded the following results (Figure 4):
- **Success Rates:**
 - LA-RRT solved 23 out of 24 scenarios with a 100% success rate within the allocated time limits (ranging from 20s to 600s), with one outlier ("Lockbox Random 1") achieving a 95% success rate.
 - BIT* successfully solved all 24 scenarios with a 100% success rate.
 - LBT-RRT and RRT* showed lower success rates, solving 7 out of 24 and 6 out of 24 scenarios with 100% success, respectively, indicating their limited effectiveness for these types of puzzles compared to LA-RRT and BIT*.
- **Optimality:** LA-RRT consistently achieved the lowest solution cost (minimal number of actions) across all 24 scenarios. This aligns with its design for solving manipulation puzzles with minimal actions.

These results collectively demonstrate that the procedurally generated puzzles are solvable using established sampling-based planning algorithms, with LA-RRT and BIT* exhibiting strong performance in terms of success rate and solution quality.

#### 4.3. Puzzle Manipulation

The study demonstrated that all 24 generated puzzles are manipulatable by a simulated KUKA LBR iiwa mobile manipulator (Figure 5). The manipulation module successfully translated the planned solutions into robot movements, including handling of re-grasping sequences and singularities. Execution and planning times for the scenarios ranged from 4 seconds ("Simple Sliders 1") to 51 seconds ("Move N Times 4"). The demonstrations illustrate the robot moving dependent red objects out of the way before manipulating the target green object to its goal configuration. This validates the practical applicability of the generated puzzles for simulated robotic tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The PhyRoGen framework addresses a notable gap in the field of robot manipulation by offering a method for the procedural generation of synthetic datasets specifically designed for "physical manipulation puzzles" with interlocking object dependencies. This capability has several significant implications:

- **Enhanced Dataset Generation:** The framework provides a method to reliably and rapidly generate large, diverse synthetic datasets of unique manipulation puzzles (in seconds) that feature complex physical constraints. This overcomes the limitations of manual dataset curation, which is often tedious, time-consuming, and restrictive in terms of diversity and systematic variation.
- **Support for Task and Motion Planning (TAMP):** By focusing on puzzles with interlocking dependencies, PhyRoGen creates environments that necessitate advanced reasoning about object interactions and physical constraints. This makes the generated datasets particularly suitable for the development, benchmarking, and training of learning-based TAMP algorithms, which integrate both discrete logical planning and continuous motion planning.
- **Benchmarking and Foundation Models:** The ability to generate and systematically vary complex manipulation puzzles provides a valuable resource for benchmarking different motion planning and TAMP algorithms. Furthermore, these datasets are crucial for training robust robot foundation models that can generalize to unforeseen manipulation challenges in complex, interdependent environments.
- **Demonstrated Solvability and Manipulability:** The thorough benchmarking process confirms that the generated puzzles are solvable by state-of-the-art sampling-based planners, and the manipulation demonstrations using a simulated robot confirm their physical manipulability. This validation is important for ensuring the utility of the generated content for robotics research.

Despite these advancements, the authors identify several limitations and areas for future work:
- **Narrow Passages:** The current method may struggle with generating and solving puzzles involving narrow passages, an issue common in motion planning, suggesting a need for integration with specialized planners.
- **Dependency Structures:** The framework currently generates puzzles with sequential (chain-like) dependencies. Future work aims to incorporate tree-like dependency structures, where multiple objects might independently block a common target, requiring more complex reasoning.
- **Online Learning Integration:** While the framework generates data for offline learning, a future goal is to integrate it into an online learning system where puzzle difficulty adapts based on robot progress.
- **Baseline Comparisons and Real-World Validation:** The current evaluation focuses on solvability and manipulability. Future research could include systematic comparisons with other generation frameworks, ablation studies, and, critically, real-world validation to assess the transferability of generated puzzles and solutions to physical robots.

Overall, PhyRoGen represents a contribution to robot manipulation research by enabling the creation of complex, physically constrained puzzle environments, thereby supporting the development and evaluation of more capable robotic systems.
