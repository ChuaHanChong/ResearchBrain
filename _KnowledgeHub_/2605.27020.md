# Black-box Membership Inference Attacks on the Pre-training Data of Image-generation Models

> [!summary] Summary
> A black-box membership inference attack framework, SD-MIA, detects pre-training data in diffusion-based image generation models by analyzing their cross-modal behavior under structured textual perturbations. The method achieves higher detection rates on pre-training samples across open-source and proprietary models, including DALL·E and Gemini, compared to existing baselines.

## Problem

- Existing black-box membership inference attacks (MIAs) relying on image-space perturbations are ineffective for pre-training data due to VAE compression and stochastic denoising.
- Gray-box and white-box MIA methods, while potentially more effective for pre-training data, require access to internal model features that are unavailable in commercial closed-source APIs.
- Many MIA evaluation protocols suffer from artificial overfitting induced by fine-tuning setups or distributional biases from non-member selection, leading to unrealistic performance assessments.

## Method

- Introduces **SD-MIA**, a black-box framework that leverages ==cross-modal perturbation== by transforming textual instructions rather than modifying images directly.
- Utilizes a large language model (GPT-5) to generate diverse but semantically coherent textual perturbations across token, style, and semantic views.
- Estimates membership by computing maximum cross-modal relevance scores between original images and reconstructions from perturbed prompts, mitigating stochasticity with relevance pooling.

## Results

- SD-MIA consistently outperformed all black-box and gray-box baselines across various Stable Diffusion models, achieving an AUC of **66.28%** on Stable-diffusion-v1-2 (balanced) and **66.12%** on v1-4 (imbalanced).
- The framework successfully generalized to leading closed-source models (DALL·E, GPT-4o, Gemini) and showed robust performance under sample imbalance and image distortions.
- Achieved over **95%** AUC for set-level membership inference with a set size of L=30, and ablation studies validated the combined efficacy of its multi-view perturbation strategy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Image-space perturbations are largely ineffective for identifying pre-training data due to the inherent compression and stochasticity of diffusion pipelines.
> - Diffusion models create a "representation-region collapse" for pre-training samples, where semantically similar text prompts consistently generate the original image, unlike non-members.
> - This cross-modal asymmetry, where members show stable generations under textual perturbations while non-members exhibit high variability, offers a robust black-box membership signal.
