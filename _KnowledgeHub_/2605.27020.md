# Black-box Membership Inference Attacks on the Pre-training Data of Image-generation Models

> [!summary] Summary
> A black-box membership inference attack framework, SD-MIA, detects pre-training data in diffusion-based image generation models by analyzing their cross-modal behavior under structured textual perturbations. The method achieves higher detection rates on pre-training samples across open-source and proprietary models, including DALL·E and Gemini, compared to existing baselines.

## Problem

- Existing black-box membership inference attacks (MIAs) relying on image-space perturbations are ineffective for pre-training data due to VAE compression and stochastic denoising.
- Gray-box and white-box MIA methods, while potentially more effective for pre-training data, require access to internal model features that are unavailable in commercial closed-source APIs.
- Many MIA evaluation protocols suffer from artificial overfitting induced by fine-tuning setups or distributional biases from non-member selection, leading to unrealistic performance assessments.

## Method

- Introduces **SD-MIA**, a black-box framework that leverages ==cross-modal perturbation== by transforming textual instructions rather than modifying images directly.
- Utilizes a large language model (GPT-5) to generate diverse but semantically coherent textual perturbations across token, style, and semantic views.
- Estimates membership by computing maximum cross-modal relevance scores between original images and reconstructions from perturbed prompts, mitigating stochasticity with relevance pooling.

## Results

- SD-MIA consistently outperformed all black-box and gray-box baselines across various Stable Diffusion models, achieving an AUC of **66.28%** on Stable-diffusion-v1-2 (balanced) and **66.12%** on v1-4 (imbalanced).
- The framework successfully generalized to leading closed-source models (DALL·E, GPT-4o, Gemini) and showed robust performance under sample imbalance and image distortions.
- Achieved over **95%** AUC for set-level membership inference with a set size of L=30, and ablation studies validated the combined efficacy of its multi-view perturbation strategy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Image-space perturbations are largely ineffective for identifying pre-training data due to the inherent compression and stochasticity of diffusion pipelines.
> - Diffusion models create a "representation-region collapse" for pre-training samples, where semantically similar text prompts consistently generate the original image, unlike non-members.
> - This cross-modal asymmetry, where members show stable generations under textual perturbations while non-members exhibit high variability, offers a robust black-box membership signal.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancements in diffusion-based image generation models have led to concerns regarding the potential unauthorized use of copyrighted and private data during their training. Membership Inference Attacks (MIAs) have emerged as a method to determine whether specific data samples were included in a model's training dataset.

Prior research on MIAs for image generation models primarily falls into two categories. The dominant paradigm involves perturbing a suspect image with noise and then assessing the model's ability to denoise or reconstruct the image as an indicator of membership. These methods have often been evaluated in synthetic settings where open-source diffusion models are fine-tuned on partitioned datasets. While such evaluations frequently report high detection accuracy, the paper argues that these results are often not representative of real-world scenarios. Specifically, these methods tend to be effective for data introduced during fine-tuning, which the model typically memorizes more profoundly. However, their efficacy diminishes significantly when applied to pre-training data, which constitutes the vast majority of training samples and is less exposed to individual memorization effects.

A second category of existing MIA approaches attempts to improve performance on pre-training data by leveraging internal model features, such as intermediate noise predictions or reconstruction losses along the denoising trajectory. These are often referred to as gray-box or white-box methods. While these techniques can better characterize behavioral discrepancies between members and non-members, they require access to the model's internal computational states, which are typically inaccessible in mainstream, closed-source image generation platforms (e.g., DALL·E, Gemini). This limitation renders gray-box methods impractical for auditing commercial systems.

This work addresses the identified gap by focusing on developing a principled *black-box MIA* method specifically designed to reliably operate on *pre-training data*. It departs from image-space perturbations, which are shown to be ineffective due to VAE compression and stochastic denoising, and instead explores a cross-modal perturbation mechanism. Furthermore, the authors highlight limitations in existing MIA evaluation protocols, noting that many studies validate attacks on fine-tuning setups which may induce artificial overfitting, and that non-member selection procedures sometimes introduce distributional biases, compromising fairness. This research aims to provide an unbiased evaluation using benchmarks that align member and non-member distributions within the same data domain.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a black-box membership inference attack framework capable of reliably detecting pre-training data in diffusion-based image generation models. This objective is driven by several motivations:

1. **Addressing the Failure of Image-Space Perturbation for Pre-training Data:** The paper identifies a significant limitation of existing MIA methods that rely on perturbing an image and analyzing the model's denoising response. In modern diffusion pipelines, the Variational Autoencoder (VAE) encoder aggressively compresses fine-grained image variations, effectively smoothing away moderate perturbations. Subsequently, the stochastic denoising trajectory quickly extinguishes any remaining perturbation signal. This results in member and non-member samples exhibiting almost indistinguishable reconstruction behavior under visual space perturbation, rendering such methods largely ineffective for pre-training data where memorization is subtler. The authors' empirical evaluation supports this, showing negligible distributional differences between member and non-member samples under visual perturbations.

2. **Overcoming Inaccessibility of Internal Model Features:** While some recent gray-box methods have attempted to improve pre-training MIA performance by leveraging internal features like latent generative priors, these features are generally not exposed by commercial, closed-source image generation systems. This practical constraint highlights the necessity for a truly black-box approach that relies solely on input-output interactions with the model.

3. **Exploiting Cross-Modal Asymmetry:** The core motivation for the proposed approach stems from an insight into the differential processing of text and image modalities in diffusion models. Textual instructions are mapped into embeddings that remain unnoised throughout the generation process, acting as persistent conditioning signals. The authors hypothesize that diffusion models internalize a locally overfitted textual–visual mapping for pre-training samples, leading to a "representation-region collapse." In this scenario, a neighborhood of semantically proximate text variants associated with a member image funnels into a single visual mode.
 - For **member images**, small textual perturbations applied to their descriptions are expected to remain within this collapsed region, leading to stable generations that closely resemble the original image.
 - For **non-member images**, no such collapsed structure exists. Textual perturbations are more likely to shift the conditioning signal to disparate regions of the representation space, producing outputs with noticeably higher variability and divergence from the original image. This "structural asymmetry" in how the model responds to textual perturbations is posited as a more distinctive membership cue than image-space variations.

4. **Ensuring Unbiased Evaluation:** The authors are motivated to conduct a fair and realistic evaluation of MIA performance on pre-training data. This involves using benchmarks where member and non-member samples have aligned distributions (e.g., from the same data domain), avoiding the artificial overfitting induced by fine-tuning setups or distributional biases from unrelated non-member datasets.

In summary, the motivation is to develop a practical, theoretically grounded black-box MIA that can reliably detect pre-training data by exploiting the model's cross-modal behavior under textual perturbations, rather than relying on image-space signals or inaccessible internal features.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed black-box membership inference framework, named **SD-MIA**, is designed to detect pre-training data in diffusion models by analyzing how the model denoises a target image in response to perturbed textual instructions. The methodology consists of two main components: indirect textual representation perturbation and maximum cross-modal relevance estimation.

**Adversarial Assumptions:**
SD-MIA operates under a standard black-box threat model. The adversary can only interact with the target model (M_θ) by issuing query-based text-to-image generation requests, providing a textual prompt {c_i} and receiving a corresponding generated image {x̂_i}. The adversary has no access to model parameters, training data, optimization procedures, or any intermediate latent representations. This setup mirrors the practical constraints of commercial text-to-image APIs.

**Motivation of SD-MIA (revisited for methodology):**
The empirical failure of image-space perturbation methods for pre-training data is reiterated. The core issue is that VAE encoders contract fine-grained image variations, and stochastic denoising extinguishes residual perturbation signals, making reconstruction behavior similar for members and non-members (Equation 3). In contrast, text embeddings remain unnoised, and models overfit local textual-visual mappings for pre-training samples, leading to a "representation-region collapse." Small textual perturbations for members stay within this region, yielding stable generations, while for non-members, perturbations lead to divergent outputs, offering informative signals (Equation 5). This theoretical basis motivates the cross-modal perturbation approach.

**Indirect Textual Representation Perturbation:**
Since direct manipulation of internal text embeddings is impossible in a black-box setting, SD-MIA employs an indirect, structured mechanism to induce embedding shifts using natural-language transformations. A large language model (GPT-5) is used to generate linguistically diverse but semantically regulated re-writings of the original textual prompt. This strategy aims to probe the "representation-region collapse" associated with member samples by ensuring perturbations remain coherent and induce measurable embedding displacements.

Three complementary perturbation views are designed:
1. **Token-view perturbation:** This involves lexical and syntactic rewritings that preserve the core semantic content and descriptive intent. These minimally invasive changes induce fine-grained embedding displacements, suitable for testing whether the representation stays within a subtle collapsed region.
2. **Style-view perturbation:** This alters stylistic attributes (e.g., register, detail density) while keeping semantic content intact. These moderate perturbations test the stability of the collapsed region against stylistic drift.
3. **Semantic-view perturbation:** This introduces controlled modifications to semantic attributes (e.g., changing objects in the image) while retaining the original stylistic profile. These yield stronger representation shifts, designed to explore the boundary where a perturbed representation might exit a collapsed neighborhood, especially for non-members.

To prevent perturbations from drifting too far from the original description, a similarity constraint `sim(f_e(c), f_e(ĉ)) ≥ τ` is imposed, where `τ` controls the maximum allowed semantic deviation. This process yields a set of perturbed textual descriptions for each view.

**Maximum Cross-Modal Relevance Estimation:**
The unobservable generation probability `p(x|c; θ*)` is approximated using a maximum cross-modal relevance estimator, which amplifies observable probability curvature differences.

1. **Relevance as a Surrogate Signal:** The relevance between the target image `x` and the reconstructed image `x̂` (generated from a perturbed description `ĉ`) serves as a stable surrogate for `p(x|ĉ; θ*)`. Perturbations within the collapsed region are expected to yield reconstructions closely aligned with `x`, while those outside lead to semantic and structural deviations. A cross-modal relevance score `s(x, ĉ)` is computed using CLIP (ViT-L/14) visual `h_v(·)` and textual `h_t(·)` encoders. If the original description `c` is unavailable, a surrogate is generated using an image captioning model (Blip2-opt-6.7b). The score is calculated by concatenating features and computing a dot product: `s(x, ĉ) = [h_v(x) ⊕ h_t(d_x)] · [h_v(x̂) ⊕ h_t(d_x̂)]`, where `d_x` and `d_x̂` are textual descriptions of `x` and `x̂` from an image caption model.

2. **Mitigating Stochasticity with Maximum Relevance Pooling:** Diffusion models are stochastic, introducing variability into relevance estimation. For non-members, faithful reconstruction is unlikely even with repeated queries. For members, repeated queries can reproduce the target image with non-negligible probability. This difference in reproducibility is leveraged by a maximum-relevance mechanism. For each suspected pair (`c`, `x`) and each perturbation view, the diffusion model is queried multiple times (e.g., 10 times) to obtain multiple reconstructions. The top K% of relevance scores are then pooled to derive a maximum relevance score for that view (e.g., `s_t` for token-view). These view-specific maxima (`s_t`, `s_s`, `s_c`) are integrated into a unified maximum cross-modal relevance estimator `s_f(x, ĉ)`. A corresponding estimator `s_f(x, c)` approximates the unperturbed likelihood. The final membership score `s_f = s_f(x, ĉ) - s_f(x, c)` empirically approximates `δ_c p`, reflecting text perturbation-induced shifts in generation probability.

**Experimental Setup:**
The methodology is evaluated on two benchmarks:
- **LAION-mi:** Contains pre-training samples from Stable Diffusion v1.2–v1.5 and non-membership samples with aligned distributions, ensuring fairness.
- **FlickrMIA-25 (newly constructed):** Extends the benchmark to Stable Diffusion v3.5 and closed-source models (DALL·E, OpenAI GPT-4o, Gemini). Member samples are from LAION-2B; non-members are from Flickr images released after Jan 1, 2025, with embedding-level distribution matching to reduce bias.

Baselines include various white-box, gray-box, and black-box MIA methods. Experiments are repeated five times with different random seeds, and performance is reported using AUC and TPR@5% FPR. Both instance-level and set-level membership inference are assessed.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted across various diffusion models and benchmarks yielded several key findings:

1. **Superior Performance of SD-MIA:** SD-MIA consistently achieved the highest membership inference performance across all evaluated Stable Diffusion models (v1.2, v1.4, v1.5, v3.5) in both balanced and imbalanced (1:10 member-to-non-member ratio) settings. For instance, on Stable-diffusion-v1-2 (balanced), SD-MIA achieved an AUC of 66.28 ± 1.47 and a TPR@5% FPR of 16.73 ± 2.98, significantly outperforming the next best baseline, Reconstruction (AUC 59.66 ± 1.57), and gray-box methods like DRC (AUC 54.66 ± 1.40). This advantage was maintained across all Stable Diffusion versions, demonstrating the efficacy of probing representation-region collapse with structured cross-modal perturbations.

2. **Robustness to Sample Imbalance:** SD-MIA exhibited strong robustness under varying positive-to-negative sample ratios. In highly imbalanced scenarios (1:10), SD-MIA consistently surpassed all baselines. For example, on Stable-diffusion-v1-4 (imbalanced), SD-MIA achieved 66.12 ± 2.78 AUC and 17.02 ± 5.55 TPR@5% FPR, indicating that its maximum cross-modal relevance pooling mechanism effectively preserves membership-dependent signal strength even when positive samples are scarce.

3. **Cross-Model Consistency and Generalization:** The performance advantage of SD-MIA was consistent across different Stable Diffusion model versions, suggesting that the underlying principle of representation-region collapse is a general property of diffusion pre-training. Furthermore, SD-MIA successfully generalized to leading closed-source image generation models, including DALL·E, GPT-4o-Image, and Gemini, despite severe black-box constraints. This indicates that the method captures a modality-level behavioral property of modern large-scale generative models rather than relying on architectural specifics of open-source diffusion models.

4. **Enhanced Set-Level Membership Inference:** When evaluating set-level membership inference, where the goal is to determine if an entire dataset contributed to training, SD-MIA demonstrated a consistent performance gain as the set size (L) increased. For L=30, SD-MIA achieved an AUC exceeding 95%, significantly outperforming existing baselines. This indicates that accumulating membership signals from multiple perturbed cross-modal interactions amplifies the separability between member and non-member sets, making SD-MIA suitable for dataset-level auditing.

5. **Robustness to Image Distortions:** SD-MIA exhibited stronger resilience than baseline methods when target images were subjected to common geometric and photometric transformations (Gaussian blur, Gaussian noise, brightness adjustment, shear transformation). For example, under Gaussian blur, SD-MIA achieved an AUC of 61.5%, whereas the Reconstruction baseline dropped to near-random performance. This suggests the stability of its cross-modal perturbation mechanism and its robustness to input disturbances.

6. **Ablation Study Insights:**
 - **Contribution of Perturbation Views:** The ablation study showed that all three perturbation views (token-view, style-view, semantic-view) contributed positively to attack performance. Token-view perturbations, inducing the smallest embedding shifts, demonstrated sensitivity to subtle memorization patterns. The integration of all three views yielded superior overall performance, confirming the importance of multi-view perturbation for stable and generalizable inference.
 - **Impact of Paired Textual Description:** While having the original paired training description provided slightly stronger membership signals, SD-MIA remained highly effective even when using a captioning model (BLIP) to generate a surrogate description. This demonstrates the framework's applicability in realistic scenarios where original descriptions may not be available.

7. **Efficiency and Performance on IAR Models:** SD-MIA demonstrated a favorable efficiency-utility trade-off, achieving substantial improvements over baselines within comparable running times. It also effectively transferred to and outperformed black-box baselines on image autoregressive models (IARs) like Infinity, showcasing its broad applicability beyond diffusion-specific architectures.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces a novel and practically significant black-box membership inference attack framework, SD-MIA, with several implications for the field of generative AI and data privacy:

1. **Addressing a Critical Gap in Black-Box Auditing:** SD-MIA provides a robust solution for detecting pre-training data in image generation models, particularly in black-box, closed-source commercial environments where traditional gray-box methods are infeasible. This addresses a critical gap in auditing capabilities for these widely used systems.

2. **Novel Cross-Modal Vulnerability Insight:** The work highlights a previously underexplored vulnerability in text-to-image diffusion models, stemming from the "representation-region collapse" phenomenon under textual perturbations. This insight shifts the focus from image-space artifacts to cross-modal interactions, opening new avenues for understanding memorization in multimodal generative models.

3. **Enhanced Privacy and Copyright Protection:** By offering a more effective tool for identifying unauthorized data usage, SD-MIA can contribute to stronger enforcement of data privacy regulations and intellectual property rights. This is particularly relevant given the increasing legal and ethical concerns surrounding the use of human-created data in training large generative models.

4. **Improved Evaluation Benchmarks:** The construction of the FlickrMIA-25 benchmark and the emphasis on unbiased evaluation protocols using distribution-aligned member and non-member samples provide a more realistic and rigorous setting for future research in membership inference, moving beyond artificial fine-tuning scenarios.

5. **Robustness and Generalization:** The demonstrated robustness of SD-MIA to image distortions and its generalization across various Stable Diffusion models, leading closed-source APIs, and even image autoregressive models, underscores its broad applicability and practical utility in diverse real-world settings.

6. **Catalyst for Defensive Measures:** The existence of a potent black-box MIA like SD-MIA may incentivize the development of more effective defensive mechanisms against membership inference in generative models, prompting further research into privacy-preserving training techniques and architectures.

In conclusion, SD-MIA represents a significant advancement in the field of membership inference attacks, offering a powerful, practical, and theoretically grounded approach to auditing the pre-training data of black-box image generation models, thereby contributing to greater transparency and accountability in generative AI.
