# STI-Bench: Are MLLMs Ready for Precise Spatial-Temporal World Understanding?

> [!summary] Summary
> STI-Bench introduces a novel benchmark to evaluate Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) for precise spatial-temporal understanding in real-world scenarios, revealing that current models achieve only modest accuracy, with the best performing model, Gemini-2.5-Pro, scoring 41.4%. The benchmark highlights MLLMs' significant limitations in quantitative spatial and temporal reasoning, crucial for embodied AI and autonomous driving applications.

## Problem

- MLLMs are increasingly used for embodied AI and autonomous driving, but their precise and quantitative spatial-temporal understanding remains largely unevaluated.
- Existing video benchmarks for MLLMs primarily focus on high-level semantic understanding or 2D visual reasoning, neglecting 3D spatial measurements and dynamic properties.
- There is uncertainty regarding MLLMs' readiness for real-world physical interactions due to a lack of rigorous, task-specific evaluation in precise spatial-temporal intelligence.

## Method

- Developed ==STI-Bench==, a novel benchmark comprising over **300** real-world videos and more than **2,000** carefully curated Question-Answering pairs, spanning desktop, indoor, and outdoor scenarios.
- Designed ==eight distinct tasks== covering both ==static== (dimensional measurement, spatial relation, 3D video grounding) and ==dynamic== (displacement, speed, ego-centric orientation, trajectory description, pose estimation) understanding.
- Utilized a robust construction pipeline involving ground truth computation from detailed ==3D annotations==, automatic QA generation with MLLMs, and rigorous human quality control with fine-grained distractors to ensure precision and challenge.

## Results

- The best-performing MLLM, Gemini-2.5-Pro, achieved an average accuracy of only **41.4%** on STI-Bench, closely followed by the open-source Qwen2.5-VL-72B at **40.7%**, indicating substantial room for improvement.
- Models performed weakest on tasks requiring precise numerical estimation of physical quantities (e.g., Dimensional Measurement **38.7%**, Displacement & Path Length **33.9%**, Speed & Acceleration **33.1%** for Gemini-2.5-Pro).
- MLLMs showed relatively stronger capabilities in tasks involving relative understanding or object state estimation, such as Pose Estimation (Claude-3.7-Sonnet **62.7%**) and Spatial Relation (Gemini-2.5-Pro **53.8%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current state-of-the-art MLLMs exhibit significant limitations in precise spatial-temporal understanding, especially for quantitative tasks, despite their advancements in general visual comprehension.
> - MLLMs struggle with accurate spatial quantification, often failing to precisely estimate static properties like object dimensions and distances due to difficulties in inferring metric scale and depth from 2D pixel data.
> - Models demonstrate flawed temporal dynamics understanding, struggling to integrate information across sparse video frames and to accurately interpret motion, including differentiating ego-motion from object motion.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Artificial Intelligence has seen a rapid ascent of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), which combine the powerful language understanding and generation capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs) with visual perception. Initially, MLLMs demonstrated impressive performance in tasks like Visual Question Answering (VQA), primarily focusing on 2D visual semantic understanding. This involved interpreting images and videos to answer questions that often required high-level semantic reasoning, object recognition, and event understanding.

However, a significant frontier for AI lies in **Embodied AI** and **Autonomous Driving**, where intelligent agents must operate and interact precisely within the physical world. These applications demand far more than just semantic understanding; they necessitate a deep, quantitative comprehension of 3D space and time. This includes abilities like accurately estimating object dimensions, relative positions, ego-motion, object trajectories, speeds, and accelerations.

This paper positions itself directly at the intersection of these trends, identifying a critical gap: while MLLMs are increasingly being proposed as end-to-end solutions for embodied tasks (e.g., RT-2, GR-2 for robotic control; Emma, DriveGPT4 for autonomous driving), their actual "Spatial-Temporal Intelligence" – their ability to perform *precise and quantitative spatial-temporal understanding* in real-world scenarios – remains largely unexamined. Existing video benchmarks for MLLMs, such as LongVideoBench, LVBench, Video-MME, and MMBench-Video, predominantly focus on high-level semantic understanding, event localization, or temporal reasoning, often as an extension of 2D image understanding. Some recent efforts, like VSI-Bench and EmbSpatial-Bench, have started to touch upon spatial intelligence but are limited in their scope, scenes, or the precision of the tasks.

STI-Bench emerges as a direct response to this deficiency. It critically evaluates whether MLLMs, primarily trained on vast amounts of internet-scale data, possess the granular 3D spatial and temporal reasoning skills required for real-world physical interactions. By focusing on *real-world* videos (sourced from Waymo, ScanNet, Omni6DPose) across diverse environments (outdoor, indoor, desktop) and demanding *numerical, precise answers* to questions about spatial measurements and dynamic properties, STI-Bench distinguishes itself from prior benchmarks. It aims to bridge the gap between MLLMs' celebrated semantic understanding and the precise physical understanding indispensable for truly intelligent embodied agents. The work implicitly critiques the prevailing assumption that general visual-language understanding automatically translates to precise physical world comprehension, setting a new standard for evaluating MLLM capabilities in this crucial domain.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to **assess the readiness of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) for precise spatial-temporal world understanding**, particularly in the context of real-world applications like Embodied AI and Autonomous Driving.

The motivation behind this objective stems from several critical observations and needs:

- **Emergence of MLLMs as End-to-End Solutions:** There is a strong and growing trend to utilize MLLMs as comprehensive, end-to-end solutions for complex AI tasks that require interaction with the physical world. This includes robotic manipulation, navigation, and autonomous vehicle operation.
- **Gap in Current Evaluation Paradigms:** While MLLMs have demonstrated impressive capabilities in general visual semantic understanding and 2D visual question answering, existing benchmarks largely neglect their ability to perform *precise and quantitative* spatial-temporal reasoning. Most evaluations focus on high-level semantic labels, object detection, or general event understanding, without delving into the exact physical properties, distances, movements, and poses of objects in 3D space and over time.
- **Uncertain Prospects for Real-World Deployment:** The lack of rigorous evaluation in precise spatial-temporal understanding leads to uncertainty regarding the true capabilities and limitations of MLLMs for real-world embodied tasks, where even small inaccuracies can have significant consequences (e.g., in robotic grasping or autonomous navigation).
- **Need for 3D and Temporal Reasoning from Common Inputs:** Many state-of-the-art MLLMs (e.g., GPT-4o, Gemini) accept images or videos as primary inputs rather than 3D point clouds. It is crucial to evaluate whether these models can infer the necessary 3D spatial and temporal information from these more common and information-rich 2D visual sequences.
- **Guiding Future MLLM Development:** By systematically identifying the specific strengths and weaknesses of current MLLMs in this domain, the researchers aim to provide clear diagnostic insights. These insights are intended to guide the research community toward developing more robust, reliable, and "physically intelligent" MLLMs that can meet the stringent requirements of embodied agents and autonomous systems.

In essence, the paper seeks to answer the fundamental question: "Are MLLMs ready for precise spatial-temporal world understanding?" by providing a dedicated, challenging benchmark that probes these specific, yet underexamined, capabilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors introduce **STI-Bench**, a novel benchmark meticulously designed to evaluate MLLMs' precise spatial-temporal understanding. The methodology encompasses benchmark design, data collection, ground truth computation, automated QA generation, human quality control, and rigorous experimental evaluation.

#### 3.1. Benchmark Design (STI-Bench Overview)

- **Input Modality:** MLLMs are evaluated using single video or multiple images as input, aligning with the common input formats of leading models like GPT-4o and Gemini, and reflecting real-world data availability.
- **Scale:** STI-Bench comprises over 300 real-world videos and more than 2,000 carefully curated Question-Answering (QA) pairs.
- **Scenario Diversity:** To ensure comprehensive evaluation across varied real-world contexts, the benchmark covers three major scenarios:
 - **Desktop:** Close-range manipulation and understanding.
 - **Indoor:** Human-scale interaction within structured environments.
 - **Outdoor:** Large-scale navigation and understanding, relevant to autonomous driving.
- **Eight Distinct Tasks:** These tasks are divided into "Static Understanding" (spatial measurements) and "Dynamic Understanding" (motion analysis):
 - **Static Understanding:**
 1. **Dimensional Measurement:** Estimating geometric sizes and distances (e.g., object height).
 2. **Spatial Relation:** Identifying relative positions between objects or camera and objects (e.g., "left or right").
 3. **3D Video Grounding:** Locating an object's 3D bounding box from semantic descriptions.
 - **Dynamic Understanding:**
 4. **Displacement & Path Length:** Measuring travel distance between time points (e.g., how far a car traveled).
 5. **Speed & Acceleration:** Computing motion parameters by integrating displacement and time.
 6. **Ego-Centric Orientation:** Examining camera's azimuth orientation changes.
 7. **Trajectory Description:** Describing motion paths throughout the video in language.
 8. **Pose Estimation:** Estimating camera pose at specified time points using RGB data, potentially with an initial pose.
- **Answer Format:** All tasks are presented in a multiple-choice format with five options, including a correct ground truth and challenging distractors.

#### 3.2. Benchmark Construction Pipeline (Figure 4, Appendix A & B)

1. **Data Collection:**
 - Videos are sourced from three publicly available datasets known for rich 3D annotations:
 - **Waymo:** For outdoor autonomous driving scenarios.
 - **ScanNet:** For indoor 3D scene reconstruction.
 - **Omni6DPose:** For desktop-scale 6D object pose estimation.
 - These datasets provide crucial auxiliary data, including frame-by-frame camera intrinsic and extrinsic parameters, 3D point clouds, and object 3D bounding box annotations. This multi-modal data is unified into a consistent format for ground truth computation (Appendix A.1).
2. **Ground Truth (GT) Computation:**
 - Leveraging the detailed auxiliary data, precise numerical ground truth for each task is computed. For example, distances are derived from 3D coordinates, speeds from displacement over time, and orientations from camera extrinsics. Appendix A.2 provides the specific formulas used for each task.
 - For tasks like Trajectory Description, algorithms like Ramer-Douglas-Peucker (RDP) are used to simplify trajectories into piecewise linguistic descriptions.
3. **Automatic QA Pair Generation:**
 - MLLMs (e.g., GPT) are utilized to generate detailed semantic descriptions for objects (e.g., "A beige minivan with a roof rack").
 - These descriptions, combined with the computed ground truth and task-specific requirements, are fed into MLLMs to generate diverse questions and initial sets of answer options.
4. **Human Quality Control (Appendix A.3):**
 - Recognizing the limitations of purely automated generation (e.g., inaccurate descriptions, unreasonable questions, insufficient visual information), a dedicated online platform was developed for multi-round manual filtering and sampling-based review by human annotators.
 - Reviewers ensured clarity, accuracy, logical coherence, sufficiency of visual information, and effectiveness of distractors. Approximately 20% of the initially generated QA pairs were retained after this rigorous process.
 - Answer options were randomly shuffled to prevent bias.
5. **Fine-Grained Adjustment of Distractors (Section 3.2, Appendix B):**
 - To make the benchmark challenging and robust, distractors are carefully constructed. This involves:
 - Defining scene-specific error ranges: millimeter-level for desktop, centimeter-to-decimeter for indoor, and decimeter-to-meter for outdoor.
 - Logarithmically sampling an error value within the defined range.
 - Adjusting initial distractor options by shifting them towards the correct answer using a weighted average. This ensures that the closest distractor is precisely within the sampled error value, while others maintain reasonable dispersion, preventing trivial choices.

#### 3.3. Experimental Settings

- **Evaluated Models:** A comprehensive set of state-of-the-art MLLMs were evaluated, including four proprietary models (GPT-4o, Gemini-2.0-Flash, Gemini-2.5-Pro, Claude-3.7-Sonnet) and six open-source models (Qwen2.5-VL-72B, InternVL2.5-78B, MiniCPM-V-2.6, VideoChat-Flash, VideoChat-R1, VideoLLaMA3-7B).
- **Sampling Protocol:** To standardize input, 30 frames were uniformly sampled from each video, with the sampling FPS explicitly indicated in the prompt (20 frames for Claude-3.7 due to API constraints).
- **Evaluation Metric:** Accuracy, defined as the direct comparison of the model's chosen answer with the ground truth, was used. A random guess baseline yields 20% accuracy for the five-option multiple-choice format.

This rigorous methodological framework ensures that STI-Bench provides a high-quality, diverse, and challenging evaluation platform for MLLMs' spatial-temporal understanding.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive evaluation of state-of-the-art MLLMs on STI-Bench reveals significant limitations in their precise spatial-temporal understanding capabilities, underscoring that current models are not yet ready for the demanding requirements of embodied AI and autonomous driving.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance

- **Struggling Models:** Even the best-performing models achieve only modest accuracy rates, significantly above the random baseline (20%) but far from satisfactory for real-world reliability.
- **Top Performers:** **Gemini-2.5-Pro** leads with an average accuracy of **41.4%**. It is closely followed by **Qwen2.5-VL-72B** (an open-source model) at **40.7%**, demonstrating competitive performance. Other models range down to MiniCPM-V-2.6 at 26.9%.
- **Proprietary vs. Open-Source:** Proprietary models generally show stronger performance, with Gemini-2.5-Pro and Claude-3.7-Sonnet (40.5%) being top contenders, but open-source models like Qwen2.5-VL-72B are closing the gap.

#### 4.2. Performance Across Different Scenes (Table 3)

- **Varied Strengths:** Model performance varies across outdoor, indoor, and desktop scenarios, suggesting different specializations or biases in their training data.
 - **Outdoor:** Qwen2.5-VL-72B performs exceptionally well (50.6%), followed by Gemini-2.5-Pro (48.7%).
 - **Indoor:** Claude-3.7-Sonnet (38.2%) and Gemini-2.5-Pro (37.1%) are the leaders.
 - **Desktop:** Gemini-2.5-Pro achieves the highest accuracy (35.8%), followed by Qwen2.5-VL-72B (33.5%).
- This scene-dependent performance highlights that robust generalization across vastly different scales and environments remains a challenge.

#### 4.3. Task-Specific Performance (Table 2, Figure 5)

- **Quantitative Tasks are the Weakest Link:** Models consistently struggle most with tasks requiring precise numerical estimation of physical quantities.
 - **Dimensional Measurement:** Gemini-2.5-Pro scores 38.7% (best).
 - **Displacement & Path Length:** Gemini-2.5-Pro scores 33.9% (best).
 - **Speed & Acceleration:** Gemini-2.5-Pro scores 33.1% (best), but overall scores remain low across models.
- **Better on Relative and State Tasks:** MLLMs show relatively stronger capabilities in tasks involving relative understanding or object state estimation.
 - **Pose Estimation:** Claude-3.7-Sonnet achieves an impressive 62.7%.
 - **Spatial Relation:** Gemini-2.5-Pro scores 53.8%.
 - **Ego-Centric Orientation:** Gemini-2.5-Pro scores 52.5%.
- This indicates that MLLMs are better at qualitative or high-level spatial understanding than precise metric estimation.

#### 4.4. Experimental Analysis and Error Patterns (Section 4.3, Figure 7)

An in-depth error analysis using Gemini-2.5-Pro, which demonstrated strong multi-modal reasoning, revealed three fundamental limitations:

1. **Inaccurate Spatial Quantification (56.44% of sampled errors):**
 - **Issue:** Models struggle with accurately estimating static spatial properties (object dimensions, distances, absolute 3D coordinates).
 - **Causes:** Lack of clear visual size references, difficulty distinguishing numerically close options (especially with fine-grained distractors), and inherent challenges in inferring metric scale and depth from 2D pixel observations with monocular cameras.
 - **Impacted Tasks:** Dimensional Measurement, Spatial Relation, 3D Video Grounding.

2. **Flawed Temporal Dynamics Understanding (23.08% of sampled errors):**
 - **Issue:** Models fail to accurately perceive, track, and interpret changes over time, particularly motion and its dynamics.
 - **Causes:** Difficulty integrating information across sparse video frames, lack of internal physical/kinematic models, inability to accurately separate ego-motion (camera movement) from object motion, and information loss due to sparse temporal sampling.
 - **Impacted Tasks:** Displacement & Path Length, Speed & Acceleration, Ego-Centric Orientation, Trajectory Description, Pose Estimation.

3. **Weak Cross-Modal Grounding and Integration (16.68% of sampled errors):**
 - **Issue:** Models struggle to correctly connect textual instructions or queries with relevant visual-temporal content, or to integrate provided non-visual data (e.g., initial poses, specific timestamps) with visual information.
 - **Causes:** Deficiencies in parsing complex structured or natural language instructions, and difficulty in fusing information from disparate modalities into a coherent reasoning process.
 - **Impacted Tasks:** All tasks relying on specific instructions, temporal constraints, or initial conditions.

**Conclusion of Findings:** The results unequivocally show that current MLLMs, despite their advancements in general visual understanding, possess significant limitations in precise spatial-temporal intelligence. Their inaccuracies in quantitative estimation and complex spatiotemporal reasoning mean they are not yet sufficiently reliable for safety-critical applications like embodied AI and autonomous driving.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of STI-Bench carries substantial significance for the advancement of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) and their application in real-world intelligent systems.

#### 5.1. Novel Diagnostic Tool for MLLMs

- STI-Bench fills a critical void in MLLM evaluation by focusing specifically on *precise and quantitative spatial-temporal understanding*—a capability largely overlooked by existing benchmarks that emphasize semantic or qualitative reasoning. It moves beyond "what is in the image?" to "how far is it, how fast is it moving, and where will it be?".
- The benchmark’s diverse real-world scenarios (desktop, indoor, outdoor) and meticulously crafted tasks provide a comprehensive and challenging testbed, allowing researchers to diagnose fine-grained weaknesses in MLLMs.

#### 5.2. Redefining Benchmarking Standards

- By deriving ground truth from detailed 3D annotations and camera parameters, and by implementing a robust fine-grained distractor generation mechanism, STI-Bench sets a new standard for precision in MLLM evaluation. This level of rigor ensures that models are truly tested on their ability to infer metric scale and motion dynamics, rather than merely making educated semantic guesses.

#### 5.3. Guiding Future Research and Development

- The detailed error analysis (inaccurate spatial quantification, flawed temporal dynamics understanding, and weak cross-modal grounding) provides concrete, actionable insights for the research community. These findings pinpoint specific areas where MLLM architectures, training data, and reasoning mechanisms need significant improvement.
- It encourages the development of MLLMs with stronger internal 3D world models, enhanced capabilities for integrating information across frames (beyond sparse sampling), better understanding of physical laws, and improved mechanisms for grounding complex textual instructions in dynamic visual content.

#### 5.4. Accelerating Embodied AI and Autonomous Driving

- The limitations highlighted by STI-Bench are directly relevant to the core challenges of Embodied AI and Autonomous Driving. For robots to safely navigate and manipulate objects, or for autonomous vehicles to drive reliably, they need millimetre-to-meter level precision in understanding their environment.
- By exposing the current shortcomings, STI-Bench motivates the development of MLLMs that can meet these stringent demands, ultimately paving the way for more robust, safe, and effective intelligent agents that can operate seamlessly in the physical world. It serves as a crucial touchstone for measuring progress towards this goal.

#### 5.5. Community Resource

- The publicly available benchmark (via its website) provides a valuable resource for researchers globally, fostering collaborative efforts to advance MLLM capabilities in spatial-temporal reasoning.

In conclusion, STI-Bench is more than just a new dataset; it is a critical diagnostic tool that challenges the community to push the boundaries of MLLM intelligence beyond semantic understanding, towards a precise, quantitative comprehension of the physical world—a prerequisite for the next generation of truly intelligent embodied systems.
