# STI-Bench: Are MLLMs Ready for Precise Spatial-Temporal World Understanding?

> [!summary] Summary
> STI-Bench introduces a novel benchmark to evaluate Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) for precise spatial-temporal understanding in real-world scenarios, revealing that current models achieve only modest accuracy, with the best performing model, Gemini-2.5-Pro, scoring 41.4%. The benchmark highlights MLLMs' significant limitations in quantitative spatial and temporal reasoning, crucial for embodied AI and autonomous driving applications.

## Problem

- MLLMs are increasingly used for embodied AI and autonomous driving, but their precise and quantitative spatial-temporal understanding remains largely unevaluated.
- Existing video benchmarks for MLLMs primarily focus on high-level semantic understanding or 2D visual reasoning, neglecting 3D spatial measurements and dynamic properties.
- There is uncertainty regarding MLLMs' readiness for real-world physical interactions due to a lack of rigorous, task-specific evaluation in precise spatial-temporal intelligence.

## Method

- Developed ==STI-Bench==, a novel benchmark comprising over **300** real-world videos and more than **2,000** carefully curated Question-Answering pairs, spanning desktop, indoor, and outdoor scenarios.
- Designed ==eight distinct tasks== covering both ==static== (dimensional measurement, spatial relation, 3D video grounding) and ==dynamic== (displacement, speed, ego-centric orientation, trajectory description, pose estimation) understanding.
- Utilized a robust construction pipeline involving ground truth computation from detailed ==3D annotations==, automatic QA generation with MLLMs, and rigorous human quality control with fine-grained distractors to ensure precision and challenge.

## Results

- The best-performing MLLM, Gemini-2.5-Pro, achieved an average accuracy of only **41.4%** on STI-Bench, closely followed by the open-source Qwen2.5-VL-72B at **40.7%**, indicating substantial room for improvement.
- Models performed weakest on tasks requiring precise numerical estimation of physical quantities (e.g., Dimensional Measurement **38.7%**, Displacement & Path Length **33.9%**, Speed & Acceleration **33.1%** for Gemini-2.5-Pro).
- MLLMs showed relatively stronger capabilities in tasks involving relative understanding or object state estimation, such as Pose Estimation (Claude-3.7-Sonnet **62.7%**) and Spatial Relation (Gemini-2.5-Pro **53.8%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current state-of-the-art MLLMs exhibit significant limitations in precise spatial-temporal understanding, especially for quantitative tasks, despite their advancements in general visual comprehension.
> - MLLMs struggle with accurate spatial quantification, often failing to precisely estimate static properties like object dimensions and distances due to difficulties in inferring metric scale and depth from 2D pixel data.
> - Models demonstrate flawed temporal dynamics understanding, struggling to integrate information across sparse video frames and to accurately interpret motion, including differentiating ego-motion from object motion.
