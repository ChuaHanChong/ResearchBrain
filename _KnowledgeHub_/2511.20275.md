# HAFO: A Force-Adaptive Control Framework for Humanoid Robots in Intense Interaction Environments

> [!summary] Summary
> A control framework named HAFO enables humanoid robots to perform robust and precise whole-body motion in environments with intense physical interactions, such as those involving heavy loads and rope suspension. It integrates a dual-agent reinforcement learning architecture with explicit dynamic modeling of external forces, demonstrating superior performance in both simulation and real-world experiments on Unitree G1 and H1-2 robots.

## Problem

- Existing reinforcement learning (RL) controllers for humanoid robots often exhibit instability and lack precision when subjected to intense external forces, such as collisions, human intervention, heavy loads, or rope interference.
- Current whole-body control paradigms either use open-loop control for the upper body, limiting real-time adaptation, or struggle with inefficient sample utilization and overfitting due to weak task correlation between upper-body manipulation and lower-body locomotion.
- Force-adaptive control for humanoid robots is underdeveloped, typically relying on precise pre-planned force trajectories or quasi-static assumptions, which limits generalization to dynamic and unknown environments.

## Method

- **HAFO** introduces a ==dual-agent reinforcement learning== framework that decouples control into robust lower-body locomotion and precise upper-body manipulation policies, trained collaboratively with a ==constrained residual action space==.
- The framework explicitly models external tension disturbances using a ==virtual spring-damper system==, enabling the RL policy to autonomously generate fine-grained, force-adaptive responses without predefined switching logic.
- Robust training strategies are employed, including ==adversarial upper-body perturbations==, high-frequency random pushing disturbances, comprehensive ==domain randomization==, and a ==curriculum learning== schedule to progressively increase external forces.

## Results

- **HAFO** achieved superior tracking performance in simulation, consistently outperforming baselines and ablation studies in velocity and upper-body angle following, especially under increasing external forces (**10N-50N** hand-induced disturbances).
- Real-world experiments on a Unitree G1 robot validated **HAFO**'s robustness and precision, demonstrating stable loco-manipulation under **1kg** hand loads and successfully initiating and operating from a rope-suspended state for high-altitude work.
- The framework demonstrated scalability to larger humanoid platforms like the Unitree H1-2 in simulation with minimal parameter adjustments, achieving stable control for tasks such as walking with **100N** hand loads and recovering from thrust disturbances.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling humanoid control into dual, collaboratively trained policies for lower-body locomotion and upper-body manipulation can enhance training efficiency and robustness under external forces while maintaining whole-body coordination.
> - Integrating explicit dynamic modeling of external forces, such as with a virtual spring-damper system, within an RL framework allows for autonomous learning of adaptive force-rejection behaviors, eliminating the need for hard-coded mode-switching.
> - A constrained residual action space for upper-body control improves training stability and sample efficiency by guiding policy exploration relative to reference trajectories, mitigating issues like overfitting and instability during adversarial interactions.
