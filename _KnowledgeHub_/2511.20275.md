# HAFO: A Force-Adaptive Control Framework for Humanoid Robots in Intense Interaction Environments

> [!summary] Summary
> A control framework named HAFO enables humanoid robots to perform robust and precise whole-body motion in environments with intense physical interactions, such as those involving heavy loads and rope suspension. It integrates a dual-agent reinforcement learning architecture with explicit dynamic modeling of external forces, demonstrating superior performance in both simulation and real-world experiments on Unitree G1 and H1-2 robots.

## Problem

- Existing reinforcement learning (RL) controllers for humanoid robots often exhibit instability and lack precision when subjected to intense external forces, such as collisions, human intervention, heavy loads, or rope interference.
- Current whole-body control paradigms either use open-loop control for the upper body, limiting real-time adaptation, or struggle with inefficient sample utilization and overfitting due to weak task correlation between upper-body manipulation and lower-body locomotion.
- Force-adaptive control for humanoid robots is underdeveloped, typically relying on precise pre-planned force trajectories or quasi-static assumptions, which limits generalization to dynamic and unknown environments.

## Method

- **HAFO** introduces a ==dual-agent reinforcement learning== framework that decouples control into robust lower-body locomotion and precise upper-body manipulation policies, trained collaboratively with a ==constrained residual action space==.
- The framework explicitly models external tension disturbances using a ==virtual spring-damper system==, enabling the RL policy to autonomously generate fine-grained, force-adaptive responses without predefined switching logic.
- Robust training strategies are employed, including ==adversarial upper-body perturbations==, high-frequency random pushing disturbances, comprehensive ==domain randomization==, and a ==curriculum learning== schedule to progressively increase external forces.

## Results

- **HAFO** achieved superior tracking performance in simulation, consistently outperforming baselines and ablation studies in velocity and upper-body angle following, especially under increasing external forces (**10N-50N** hand-induced disturbances).
- Real-world experiments on a Unitree G1 robot validated **HAFO**'s robustness and precision, demonstrating stable loco-manipulation under **1kg** hand loads and successfully initiating and operating from a rope-suspended state for high-altitude work.
- The framework demonstrated scalability to larger humanoid platforms like the Unitree H1-2 in simulation with minimal parameter adjustments, achieving stable control for tasks such as walking with **100N** hand loads and recovering from thrust disturbances.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling humanoid control into dual, collaboratively trained policies for lower-body locomotion and upper-body manipulation can enhance training efficiency and robustness under external forces while maintaining whole-body coordination.
> - Integrating explicit dynamic modeling of external forces, such as with a virtual spring-damper system, within an RL framework allows for autonomous learning of adaptive force-rejection behaviors, eliminating the need for hard-coded mode-switching.
> - A constrained residual action space for upper-body control improves training stability and sample efficiency by guiding policy exploration relative to reference trajectories, mitigating issues like overfitting and instability during adversarial interactions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of humanoid robot control has seen progress with learning-based solutions, particularly reinforcement learning (RL) controllers. These methods leverage large-scale parallel simulations and domain randomization to enable robots to handle environmental uncertainties and achieve zero-shot transfer to real robots for tasks such as locomotion and light-weight object manipulation.

However, a notable challenge within this landscape is the development of robust and precise motion control strategies for humanoid robots operating under intense external force interactions. Existing RL controllers frequently oversimplify or overlook interactive forces, leading to instability when subjected to significant collisions, human intervention, or environmental contact. Current approaches often rely on high-level commands, which may not adequately address dynamic and unpredictable disturbances, potentially compromising robot stability and safety. For instance, in applications requiring heavy-load manipulation or high-altitude operations, the impact of large payloads or supporting ropes introduces substantial control complexities that traditional RL methods struggle to manage without explicit modeling of contact dynamics.

Previous RL-based whole-body control paradigms typically fall into two categories: "lower-RL-upper-IK" (inverse kinematics for the upper body) and "integrated-whole-body-RL." The lower-RL-upper-IK paradigm often utilizes an open-loop control mode for the upper body, limiting its ability to perceive and adapt to real-time external interference. Conversely, the integrated-whole-body-RL paradigm controls all body joints with a single controller, but the potentially weak task goal correlation between upper-body manipulation and lower-body locomotion can lead to inefficient sample utilization and overfitting.

Research into force-adaptive control for robots is comparatively less developed, especially for humanoids. While some methods exist for quadruped robots or manipulation tasks, they often depend on explicit mode switching, assume quasi-static conditions, or require precise modeling of pre-planned force trajectories, thereby limiting their generalization to dynamic and unknown environments. For humanoid robots, inherent characteristics such as a high center of gravity and a small support base further complicate force-adaptive control.

This paper positions HAFO as a contribution that addresses these specific limitations by proposing a dual-agent reinforcement learning framework designed to explicitly incorporate dynamic modeling of external forces. It aims to bridge the gap between advanced locomotion and precise manipulation under significant external disturbances, offering a unified policy that adapts to intense physical interactions without relying on predefined switching logic or simplified dynamic assumptions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to achieve robust and precise whole-body motion control for humanoid robots in intense force interaction environments. This encompasses scenarios involving heavy loads, thrust disturbances, and rope suspension, where existing reinforcement learning controllers typically exhibit limitations.

The motivation for this work stems from several practical challenges observed in current humanoid robot applications:
- **Instability under Strong External Forces:** Existing RL controllers often become unstable when exposed to strong collisions, human intervention, or significant environmental contact. This instability is attributed to their primary reliance on high-level movement commands (e.g., velocity, root height) and a lack of explicit modeling for contact dynamics.
- **Safety Hazards in Practical Applications:** The inability to maintain balance under external disturbances can lead to safety hazards. Examples include rope interference during high-altitude operations, which can undermine stability, and large payloads that significantly impair balance, demanding high load-bearing capacity.
- **Limitations of Current Whole-Body Control Paradigms:**
 - The "lower-RL-upper-IK" paradigm struggles with real-time adaptation to external interference due to its open-loop upper-body control.
 - The "integrated-whole-body-RL" paradigm, while controlling all joints, can suffer from inefficient sample utilization and overfitting because of the weak task goal correlation between upper-body manipulation and lower-body locomotion.
- **Scarcity and Limitations of Existing Force-Adaptive Control:** Prior work on force-adaptive control for humanoid robots is limited in scope. Model-based methods rely on precise force trajectory modeling, hindering generalization, while estimation-based approaches are often restricted to quasi-static conditions and cannot address dynamic loco-manipulation tasks effectively.

To address these motivations, HAFO sets out to achieve the following specific objectives:
1. **Develop a Dual-Agent Reinforcement Learning Framework:** This framework aims to facilitate the collaborative evolution of decoupled upper and lower body control strategies. The lower-body strategy focuses on robust locomotion, while the upper-body strategy emphasizes precise manipulation under external forces. A constrained residual action space is introduced to enhance training stability and sample efficiency.
2. **Incorporate Explicit Dynamic Modeling of External Forces:** A spring-damper mechanical system is developed to model external tension disturbances. This allows for fine-grained force control through virtual spring manipulation, enabling the RL policy to autonomously generate disturbance-rejection responses without predefined switching logic. A curriculum learning schedule progressively increases external forces and randomizes their application to enhance generalization.
3. **Demonstrate Force-Adaptive Whole-Body Control Across Diverse Scenarios:** The objective is to validate that a single dual-agent policy can achieve force-adaptive control for humanoid robots under various challenging conditions, including heavy load, thrust disturbance, and rope suspension. This includes demonstrating stable operation and safe startup under rope-suspension conditions as preliminary work for high-altitude applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The HAFO framework is designed to address the challenges of whole-body control for humanoid robots in intense interaction environments. Its methodology is centered on a dual-agent reinforcement learning architecture, explicit dynamic modeling of external forces, and robust training strategies.

#### 3.1. Dual-Agent Learning Framework

HAFO decomposes the robot's total degrees of freedom (DOFs) into upper and lower bodies, with `n_dof = n_upper_dof + n_lower_dof`. Two distinct policies are employed:
- **Lower-body policy (π_lower):** Controls `n_lower_dof` and focuses on robust locomotion. Its command inputs include root linear and angular velocities.
- **Upper-body policy (π_upper):** Controls `n_upper_dof` and emphasizes precise manipulation. Its command inputs are reference joint trajectories.

Both agents share the same state space `s_t = [s_prop_t, c_l, g_u]`, which includes body perception information, lower-body command information, and upper-body command information, allowing for coordinated control. The policies are trained using the Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) algorithm, maximizing cumulative rewards for each policy in a dual-objective optimization problem. An asymmetric Actor-Critic architecture is utilized, where the Critic network has access to privileged information, such as base linear velocity and external force values, to provide more accurate state-action value approximations.

#### 3.2. Robust Lower-Body Locomotion Under External Forces

To enhance the lower-body policy's (π_lower) robustness, various external disturbances are introduced during training:
- **Adversarial upper-body perturbations:** The dynamic motions of the upper body, especially under loads, serve as an adversarial disturbance, compelling the lower body to learn more generalized compensation mechanisms.
- **High-frequency random pushing disturbances:** Direct external forces are applied to the robot.
- **Domain randomization:** Key simulation parameters such as ground friction, Kp and Kd values, base mass, link mass, base center of mass (CoM), and control delay are randomized within specified ranges to improve the policy's generalization to real-world uncertainties.

#### 3.3. Upper-Body Motion Generation Under External Forces

The upper-body policy (π_upper) is trained to generate adaptive motions rather than relying on open-loop replay of demonstrations.
- **Demonstration Data:** Upper-body motion sequences are derived from the AMASS human-motion dataset, retargeted to the humanoid robot, and filtered to suppress jitter.
- **Coupled Training:** The upper-body policy is trained concurrently with the lower-body policy, and its state space includes information about the entire body to ensure whole-body coordination and self-collision avoidance.
- **Constrained Residual Action Space:** The policy outputs a corrective offset `a_u_t` relative to a sampled reference trajectory joint position `θ_ref_t`, instead of directly learning absolute joint targets. This offset is constrained (`|a_u_t| ≤ Δ`) to prevent training collapse due to adversarial interactions between the dual agents, ensuring network action outputs remain within a controllable range.
- **Progressive Imitation Curriculum:** To manage the learning complexity, especially for the lower-body policy, a curriculum learning strategy is implemented. The upper-body reference trajectory is gradually learned from simple to complex via a course gain coefficient `α_i` that modulates the motion amplitude (`q_u_curr(t) = q_u_0(t) + α_i(q_u_tar(t) - q_u_0(t))`).
- **External Force Disturbances:** To enhance load capacity, disturbances in the form of random 3D spatial forces are applied to the robot's end-effectors during training. Domain randomization is also applied to these disturbances.

#### 3.4. Virtual Spring-Damper System

A key component of HAFO is the explicit modeling of external tension forces using a virtual spring-damper system. This system is integrated into the training loop to improve robustness to unknown external disturbances.
- **Dynamic Model:** The system is governed by `mẍ = K_p(x_des - x) + K_d(ẋ_des - ẋ) + f_ext`, where `K_p` and `K_d` are stiffness and damping coefficients, `x_des` and `ẋ_des` are desired position and velocity, and `f_ext` is the external force.
- **Force Control:** By progressively adjusting `x_pelvis_des`, the tensile force can be tuned to achieve stable position control. The damping term (`K_d`) is critical for stability and precision, as demonstrated by comparisons showing pronounced oscillations with stiffness-only control.
- **Curriculum Learning and Randomization:** During training, the spring-damper forces are gradually increased, and the force application position, cycle, and dropout timing are randomized to enhance the policy's generalization capability. The RL policy autonomously evolves force-adaptive control modes for ground locomotion and aerial suspension, generating mode-switching behaviors based on environmental feedback without explicit state machines.

#### 3.5. Reward Design

The reward function combines standard regularization rewards (e.g., orientation, action rate, feet orientation) with task-specific rewards (e.g., linear/angular velocity tracking, upper-body DOFs position tracking, lower-body stand still). Modifications are made to accommodate full-body control and force adaptation, with specific rewards conditioned on the presence of spring forces (`I(||F_spring||)`).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents simulation and real-world experiments to evaluate the HAFO framework across various intense interaction environments.

#### 4.1. Simulation Experiments (Unitree G1 Humanoid Robot)

Simulations were conducted in MuJoCo, leveraging zero-shot transfer from Isaac Gym to mitigate overfitting and verify policy robustness.
- **Force-Adaptive Control Under Hand-Induced Disturbances:**
 - **Setup:** Forces ranging from 10N to 50N were applied to the robot's end-effectors while it executed random velocity commands and diverse upper-limb motions.
 - **Metrics:** Upper-body motion tracking error (E_upper_error) and velocity tracking error (E_vel_error).
 - **Comparison:** HAFO was compared against `upper-OL-lower-RL` (open-loop upper-body, RL lower-body), `upper-FIX-lower-RL` (fixed upper-body, RL lower-body), and two ablation variants (`HAFO w.o. DA` for dual-agent, `HAFO w.o. Force` for force-adaptive module).
 - **Results:** HAFO consistently achieved superior tracking performance in both velocity and upper-body angle following across all force magnitudes. Its performance advantage became more pronounced with increasing external forces. The `upper-FIX-lower-RL` policy failed to maintain stability under dynamic upper-body motions. Ablation studies demonstrated performance degradation without the dual-agent architecture or the force-adaptive module, affirming their contributions.
- **Force-Adaptive Control Under Rope Suspension:**
 - **Setup:** Evaluation across lifting, operating (teleoperation), and landing scenarios in a suspended state.
 - **Metrics:** Action smoothness (∆a) and upper-body motion tracking error (E_upper_tracking).
 - **Results:** HAFO maintained excellent performance in suspended scenarios, exhibiting lower upper-body motion tracking error (0.20 rad) and higher action smoothness (0.38 m/s) compared to existing strategies. This indicated substantial compliance and stability during dynamic lifting and landing transitions. Ablation studies confirmed that without the dual-agent mechanism, performance degraded significantly, even with force curriculum learning.
- **Force-Adaptive Control Under Thrust Disturbance:**
 - **Setup:** Sustained constant forces and one-second transient forces were applied from eight horizontal directions, with magnitudes progressively increased until stability loss.
 - **Results:** HAFO demonstrated greater tolerance to external disturbances in most directions compared to `HAFO w.o. force` and baseline strategies, suggesting that adversarial upper-body training and the force curriculum enhanced the robot’s force adaptation under thrust perturbations.

#### 4.2. Real-World Experiments (Unitree G1 Humanoid Robot)

The HAFO strategy was deployed on a physical Unitree G1 humanoid robot through zero-shot transfer.
- **Loco-Manipulation Under Hand Loads:**
 - **Setup:** Experiments with 1 kg loads carried in a single hand and in both hands.
 - **Metrics:** Velocity tracking error and upper-body motion tracking error.
 - **Results:** HAFO maintained the lowest velocity tracking and upper-limb joint errors in both single-hand and dual-hand load scenarios, outperforming baseline and ablation methods. This validated its robustness and precision under real-world load-bearing conditions.
- **Stable Control Under Suspension:**
 - **Setup:** Validation of suspended startup and high-altitude work scenarios (lifting, operating, landing).
 - **Results:** HAFO successfully initiated directly from a rope-suspended state and rapidly reached a steady state, simplifying policy deployment. Unlike baseline strategies that misinterpret rope-induced dynamics as instability, HAFO leveraged environmental feedback for adaptive compensation, robustly maintaining locomotion stability throughout lifting, operating, and landing phases. The proposed method was the only one to complete the full experiment sequence, demonstrating its potential for high-rise dangerous scenes.

#### 4.3. Scalability (Unitree H1-2 Humanoid Robot)

The HAFO strategy was extended to the larger Unitree H1-2 humanoid robot (1.78 m height, 70 kg weight) in simulation.
- **Results:** With minimal parameter adjustments, stable control was achieved on the H1-2 in simulation environments, including upper-body swinging, balancing under punching, walking with 100 N hand loads, suspended operation, and recovering from thrust disturbances. This demonstrated the framework’s generalization and scalability to full-sized humanoid platforms.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The HAFO framework represents an advancement in the control of humanoid robots, specifically addressing the challenge of robust and precise motion in environments characterized by intense physical interaction. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Enhanced Robot Robustness and Adaptability:** HAFO enables humanoid robots to maintain stable and precise motion capabilities under a diverse range of challenging conditions, including heavy loads, significant thrust disturbances, and complex rope suspension states. This is achieved through a single, unified dual-agent policy, reducing the need for scenario-specific controllers or explicit mode switching.
- **Dual-Agent Control for Whole-Body Coordination:** The proposed dual-agent reinforcement learning framework, with its decoupled yet collaboratively trained upper and lower body policies, offers a method for efficient whole-body control. The introduction of a constrained residual action space for the upper body enhances training stability and sample efficiency, mitigating issues like overfitting and instability commonly seen in integrated or open-loop control approaches.
- **Explicit Force Interaction Modeling:** The incorporation of a virtual spring-damper system to explicitly model external tension disturbances allows the reinforcement learning policy to autonomously generate fine-grained force-adaptive responses. This dynamic modeling, coupled with curriculum learning and randomization, improves the policy's generalization to unknown and variable external forces, moving beyond reliance on precise pre-planned force trajectories.
- **Enabling New Applications and Operational Scenarios:** The demonstrated ability to operate stably under rope suspension, including safe startup and high-altitude work capabilities, represents exploratory work that could significantly expand the practical applications of humanoid robots in industries such as construction, maintenance, or disaster response in elevated or precarious environments. Similarly, enhanced load-bearing and thrust-rejection capabilities open avenues for more practical heavy-load manipulation tasks and robust human-robot collaboration in dynamic settings.
- **Improved Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The use of large-scale parallel simulations, domain randomization, and cross-platform validation (Isaac Gym to MuJoCo, then real Unitree G1/H1-2) contributes to reducing the simulation-reality gap, making the developed policies more readily deployable on physical hardware.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** HAFO's approach of combining explicit dynamic modeling with dual-agent RL for force-adaptive whole-body control provides a foundation for future research in areas such as more complex loco-manipulation tasks, human-robot physical interaction, and the development of even more generalized and robust humanoid behaviors in unpredictable real-world environments. The demonstrated scalability to larger humanoid platforms (Unitree H1-2) further highlights its potential for broad applicability.
