# WorldToken: Time-First Sequence Modeling for Robotic Imitation Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> WorldToken is a time-first imitation policy: within each policy timestep a shared encoder fuses multiview images, proprioception, and language conditioning into one world token; a causal Transformer contextualizes that token sequence, and a DiT diffusion head decodes action chunks. Trained from scratch, an 85M-parameter policy reaches roughly 59.5% mean closed-loop success across 23 RoboCasa household-manipulation tasks, matching billion-scale pretrained systems.

## Problem

- Sequence-modeling robot policies disagree on what a token represents: one frame may expand into many visual tokens, or actions may be interleaved, blurring the temporal axis.
- Mixing modal, spatial, and generation roles in top-level positions couples perceptual encoding with temporal modeling, so context length is not interpretable as physical decisions.
- Large input windows establish only that history is available; prior work rarely separates task-intrinsic necessity from whether a trained policy actually utilizes that history.

## Method

- Within-timestep encoder projects per-view patch tokens, proprioception, and task conditioning, then four learned readout tokens aggregate them into a single **world token** via ==RMSNorm== and linear projection.
- Causal temporal backbone is a from-scratch ==Qwen2==-style decoder with ==1D RoPE== over policy time; sliding windows reindex retained tokens contiguously so ablations change only visible history.
- ==DiT== denoiser conditioned on the current history representation and diffusion step through ==adaLN-Zero== generates H-step action chunks, executing a prefix under receding-horizon replanning; only actions are supervised.

## Results

- A **5x5x2** sweep over data, capacity, and seed spanning **172,500** episodes gives **59.45%** success at **2,900** demos per task, versus **31.28%** for BC-Transformer at **300** demos.
- Holdout RMSE follows power laws with exponents **0.151-0.211** and R-squared **0.990-0.999**; capacity gains concentrate between **44.3M** and **218.8M**, with **648.9M** and **1.49B** adding nothing consistent.
- History truncation lowers success for all **50** policies; on Blocks Ranking, shrinking context from **608** to **32** tokens drops success **95%** to **28%**, one rollout sustaining **31** ordered swaps.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Making one decision step equal one token gives a physically interpretable context axis and lets perception, temporal modeling, and action decoding be varied independently.
> - Offline action-fitting error tracks closed-loop success across large scaling changes but diverges between nearby policies, so it cannot substitute for rollout evaluation.
> - Much measured history dependence is adaptation to the training context, not task necessity: retrained short-context policies recover most of the truncation loss.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robot learning is progressing from short-horizon reactive control towards large-scale vision-language-action sequence modeling, often leveraging Transformer architectures. Within this progression, a fundamental question concerns the structural organization of temporal information: what constitutes the basic unit (token) in a policy's sequence, and how should heterogeneous inputs (e.g., multiple camera views, proprioception, language) be integrated?

Existing approaches to sequence organization in robotic policies vary. Some, like Decision Transformer and Gato, conceptualize decision-making as sequence modeling, but differ in their tokenization. Robot Transformers (e.g., RT-1, ICRT, GR-1) employ diverse strategies, from compressing visual frames into multiple modality-specific tokens (RT-1) to interleaving state and action features (ICRT) or sharing a unified GPT-style sequence across modalities (GR-1). These designs all incorporate temporal information but organize it through different sequence units, defining top-level positions through combinations of temporal, modal, spatial, and generation structures.

A parallel line of research focuses on action generation, often decoupling it from the main temporal backbone. Diffusion Policy, ACT, Octo, and π0 utilize methods like conditional diffusion, VAE decoders, or flow-matching experts to generate action sequences. These approaches modularize expressive action generation, but the question of how temporal context is organized and accessed prior to generation remains.

Furthermore, compact timestep representations are a recognized area, with examples such as the BC-Transformer baseline in RoboCasa, which processes observation timesteps with non-causal self-attention, and recent proposals like HAMLET and Chronos, which compress timesteps into specific token types for aggregation or state propagation.

WorldToken positions itself within this landscape by proposing and evaluating a "time-first" sequence organization. This approach establishes the policy timestep itself as the primary unit of the top-level temporal sequence, requiring within-timestep multimodal fusion to produce a single "world token." This design aims to provide a physically interpretable interface for studying embodied sequence modeling by standardizing the semantic unit of temporal context, thereby separating multimodal encoding from temporal contextualization and action generation. The work contributes an empirical characterization of a complete policy instantiation under this principle, examining its scaling behavior and temporal context utilization.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to investigate whether a robot can effectively organize its physical world interactions as a language model processes text, using physical time as the fundamental organizing axis. This translates into the specific goal of designing and evaluating "WorldToken," a policy instantiation based on a "time-first" principle.

The motivation for this specific approach stems from several observations and challenges in current robot learning paradigms:
1. **Lack of a Unified Temporal Unit:** Current robot policies often lack a common, physically intuitive unit for organizing temporal sequences. Inputs within a single policy timestep can be heterogeneous (e.g., multiple camera views, proprioception, language commands), leading to diverse strategies for handling history (e.g., recurrent modules, explicit memory, retrieval mechanisms, or modality-specific tokens). This heterogeneity complicates the design and analysis of sequence models for robotics.
2. **Interpretable Context Axis:** The authors propose that treating each policy timestep as a singular "observation–decision–replanning event" and condensing all heterogeneous inputs from that timestep into a single "world token" creates a directly interpretable context axis. This allows the length of the temporal sequence to directly correspond to the number of policy decisions, independent of the number of perceptual or modality-specific tokens.
3. **Modular Architecture Design:** By resolving multimodal heterogeneity *within* each policy timestep to produce a single world token *before* temporal modeling, WorldToken aims to create a more modular architecture. This factorization separates three key components:
 - Within-timestep multimodal encoding.
 - Temporal context modeling.
 - Action generation.
 This separation is intended to facilitate controlled interventions and attribution of behavioral differences to specific architectural stages, enhancing the understanding of how design choices impact performance.
4. **Empirical Feasibility and Scaling:** A key motivation is to empirically establish the feasibility of this time-first organization. This involves assessing whether a concrete WorldToken instantiation can learn effective multitask control and how its performance scales systematically with varying data quantities and model capacities.
5. **Understanding Temporal Context Utilization:** The research seeks to characterize how policies trained with this time-first organization utilize recent history for decision-making and how extended temporal context influences sustained ordered behavior over long horizons. This involves investigating both fixed-policy history truncation and adaptation to different training context lengths.

In summary, the work is motivated by the desire to bring a clear, time-centric organizational principle to embodied sequence modeling, provide a robust empirical characterization of a policy built on this principle, and use this framework to study the effects of data, capacity, and temporal context in robotic imitation learning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The WorldToken policy architecture instantiates the time-first principle through a three-component sequential design: a multimodal encoder, a causal temporal Transformer, and a diffusion action head.

#### 3.1. WorldToken Policy Architecture

The policy operates at discrete "policy timesteps," which represent observation–decision–replanning events. At each timestep `t`, the policy receives an observation `o_t` (multiview images `I_1:K_t`, proprioception `p_t`, and a task condition `c`). It then predicts an `H`-step action chunk `A_t`. The policy decomposes as:
`z_t = E_θ(o_t)` (within-timestep encoding)
`h_t = T_ϕ(z_≤t)` (causal temporal contextualization)
`A_t ~ D_ψ(· | h_t)` (generative action decoding)

#### 3.2. Within-Timestep Multimodal Encoding (Eθ)

This module compresses heterogeneous inputs of a single timestep `o_t` into a fixed-width world token `z_t`.
- **Visual Stem:** For each camera, a CNN stem maps input images (`128x128 RGB`) to visual patch tokens. Features are projected to the model width and augmented with embeddings identifying spatial position, camera view, and modality.
- **Proprioception and Task Conditioning:** Proprioception (`16-D`) and a frozen CLIP text encoder output (`768-D`) for task condition are projected into `u_prop_t` and `u_task_t`.
- **Multimodal Fusion:** All visual patch tokens, proprioception, and task conditioning tokens form the within-timestep sequence `X_t`. Learned readout tokens `Q` (R=4) are appended to `X_t`. A Transformer-like block (`F_enc`) processes this sequence, where readout tokens aggregate information from observation tokens.
- **World Token Production:** The final outputs from the readout positions (`r_1_t, ..., r_R_t`) are concatenated, RMSNormalized, and linearly projected to produce the single `z_t` token, which is then passed to the temporal backbone.

#### 3.3. Causal Temporal Backbone (Tϕ)

This module processes the sequence of world tokens `Z_1:T = (z_1, z_2, ..., z_T)` to produce a history-conditioned representation `h_t`.
- **Architecture:** It is instantiated as a Transformer based on the Qwen2 decoder architecture, initialized from scratch.
- **Causal Self-Attention:** Ensures that `h_t` at time `t` depends only on past and current world tokens `z_≤t`. During training, sampled windows or episode prefixes are processed in parallel with this causal mask.
- **Positional Encoding:** One-dimensional RoPE (Rotary Position Embedding) is applied along the world-token sequence to encode policy time.
- **Context Window (C):** The policy observes `z_max(1,t-C+1):t`. When `t` exceeds `C`, the window slides, and earlier tokens are evicted. RoPE's relative position dependence allows reindexing retained tokens without explicit absolute position embeddings. The default `C` for the main sweep is 10 policy timesteps.

#### 3.4. Generative Action Decoding (Dψ)

This module generates an `H`-step action chunk `A_t` from the history representation `h_t`.
- **Action Space:** Actions are 12-dimensional commands, normalized to `[-1, 1]` during training and unnormalized for execution. The predicted chunk length `H` is 10.
- **Diffusion Model:** A DiT (Diffusion Transformer) denoiser is used to model the conditional action distribution. During training, it predicts noise `ϵ` injected into a ground-truth chunk `A_t` based on the diffusion step `k` and `h_t`. The loss is `L_act = E_{t,k,ϵ}[ϵ - ϵ_ψ(A_t^(k), k, h_t)]^2`.
- **Inference:** Action chunks are generated from Gaussian noise using stochastic DDPM sampling.
- **Receding-Horizon Control:** Each generated chunk `A_t` contains `H` actions, but only the first `H_exec` actions (`H_exec=4`) are executed before the policy observes the environment again and replans, appending a new world token. Executed actions are not explicitly inserted into the temporal sequence; their effects are observed through subsequent observations.

#### 3.5. Evaluation Protocol

- **Tasks:** Evaluation is conducted on 23 RoboCasa household-manipulation tasks (a subset of the official 24 tasks), and for the extended context study, on RMBench Blocks Ranking.
- **Metrics:** Two main metrics are used:
 - **Closed-loop success rate (SR):** Measures task completion under the policy's own rollouts.
 - **Holdout stochastic RMSE:** An offline measure of how closely the policy reproduces expert actions on held-out expert observation histories.
- **Scaling Study:** A 5x5x2 sweep is performed across five training-set sizes (D ∈ {50, 100, 300, 1000, 2900} demonstrations per task), five model sizes (44.3M to 1.49B parameters), and two training seeds per condition.
- **Context Interventions:**
 - **Same-checkpoint history truncation:** Fixed policies (trained with C=10) are evaluated with reduced visible history lengths (C_test ∈ {1, 2, 5}).
 - **Matched training and evaluation context:** Policies are trained with C_train = C_test ∈ {1, 2, 5, 10} to assess adaptation.
- **Training Details:** All trainable modules are initialized from scratch, except for the frozen CLIP text encoder. Policies are trained for approximately 100 passes through the training set, with varying optimizer steps. All evaluation runs use the scheduled final checkpoint.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents findings across three empirical questions: effectiveness and scaling, recent history utilization, and extended context for sustained behavior.

#### 4.1. Effective Multitask Control and Systematic Scaling

- **Multitask Control Performance:** On 23 RoboCasa tasks, an 85.3M-parameter WorldToken policy, trained from scratch with 2,900 generated demonstrations per task, achieved a task-averaged closed-loop success rate of 59.45% (mean over training seeds), with the best individual run reaching 60.1%. With 300 demonstrations per task, the same policy reached 46.83% SR, outperforming a BC-Transformer reproduction (31.28%) under matched conditions. The performance at larger data scales is noted to be within the range reported by some billion-scale pretrained systems, though direct comparison is complicated by differing protocols and pretraining.
- **Data Scaling:** Across the complete 5x5x2 scaling grid, increasing target-domain data consistently improved performance. Holdout RMSE decreased in all 40 paired comparisons (47.0–56.8% reduction from D=50 to D=2900), and closed-loop SR also improved in all 40 comparisons (32.7–39.3 percentage point increase). RMSE exhibited a regular scaling behavior, well-described by a power law (`RMSE ~ D^-α`), with exponents ranging from 0.151 to 0.211. Closed-loop SR gains became smaller at the largest dataset sizes, indicating that while action prediction error continued to decrease, closed-loop task success approached a plateau.
- **Model Capacity Scaling:** Increasing model size from 44.3M to 218.8M parameters consistently reduced holdout RMSE. Beyond 218.8M parameters, further increases in capacity (up to 1.49B) did not yield consistent additional RMSE reductions. Closed-loop SR showed a weaker, less stable trend with capacity, with higher-capacity policies not exhibiting a clear ordering.
- **Interaction between Data and Capacity:** The benefit of increased model capacity on RMSE became more pronounced with larger dataset sizes. For example, the RMSE reduction from 44.3M to 218.8M parameters increased from approximately 5% at small datasets to 23% at large datasets. The exponent of the RMSE-data power law also increased with model size, suggesting a coordinated scaling behavior.

#### 4.2. Utilization of Recent History

- **Fixed Policy Truncation:** For all 50 policies trained with a maximum context length `C_train = 10`, truncating visible history at inference time (to `C_test = 1` or `2` policy timesteps) consistently lowered closed-loop success rates. The smallest decline was 3.5 percentage points for `C_test = 1` and 1.4 percentage points for `C_test = 2`. Most of the observed benefit of history was present at five visible policy timesteps, where 47 of 50 policies were within two percentage points of their `C_test = 10` performance.
- **Adaptation to Training Context:** Policies specifically trained with shorter context lengths (`C_train = C_test ∈ {1, 2, 5}`) recovered most of the success rate lost by inference-time truncation. For instance, policies trained and evaluated with `C = 1` achieved 46.75% SR, compared to 27.91% SR for `C = 10`-trained policies evaluated with `C_test = 1`. This indicates that WorldToken policies systematically use temporal input and adapt their history utilization to the context length available during training.
- **RMSE vs. SR with Context:** Longer training contexts consistently reduced holdout RMSE, demonstrating improved action prediction fidelity on expert trajectories. However, closed-loop SR was not monotonic with context length, with policies trained and evaluated at `C = 5` achieving the highest SR in the D=300, 218.8M-parameter anchor study. This suggests that improved imitation of expert actions does not always directly translate to better closed-loop task success.

#### 4.3. Extended Context for Sustained Ordered Behavior

- **RMBench Blocks Ranking:** This task requires sustained, order-dependent behavior. Reducing visible history from 608 world tokens (146 seconds) to 32 world tokens (8 seconds) for a fixed checkpoint led to a substantial drop in evaluator success, from 95% to 28%. This degradation was most pronounced in episodes requiring multiple sequential swaps. Failures primarily involved execution instability (inaccurate placements or stalls) rather than errors in determining the next swap in the sequence. Extended context was found to improve the reliability of sustained execution.
- **Periodic Behavior Beyond Horizon:** Exploratory stress tests where policies continued executing beyond the first success and the demonstrated training horizon showed that the learned periodic behavior could be sustained. One trajectory completed 31 correctly ordered swaps, including 26 after the context window began to slide (at 145.92 seconds), with the final correct swap at 856.44 seconds. This suggests that the policy learned a repeatable regularity rather than merely reproducing finite demonstrated prefixes.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work provides an empirical realization of time-first sequence modeling, contributing a functional policy family for studying key aspects of robot learning. The primary significance of WorldToken lies in the architectural interface it defines, rather than an established performance advantage over all alternatives.

#### 5.1. Time-First Organization as an Interface

- **Modularity and Interpretability:** By defining the policy timestep as the fundamental semantic unit of the temporal sequence and enforcing within-timestep multimodal fusion, WorldToken creates a clear interface. This separation of within-timestep perceptual processing, cross-timestep temporal computation, and action generation allows for more controlled experimentation. Architectural changes in one stage can be made without altering the meaning of a historical position, facilitating cleaner attribution of behavioral differences. The context axis is directly interpretable in terms of policy timesteps.
- **Motivation for Aggressive Compression:** The design encourages aggressive compression of past observations into compact world tokens. This is justified by the embodied agent's ability to renew its perception through interaction, potentially recovering task-relevant visual details when needed, rather than retaining high-bandwidth representations of all past observations.

#### 5.2. Characterization of Scaling Behavior

- The systematic scaling studies on RoboCasa provide a characterization of how WorldToken performs with increasing data and model capacity. The consistent gains from additional target-domain data and the observed diminishing returns from model capacity (beyond a moderate size) contribute to understanding the empirical properties of this policy family. This scaling behavior is an important criterion for evaluating sequence architectures in robot learning.

#### 5.3. Evidence for Temporal Context Utilization

- The controlled interventions regarding temporal context (fixed-policy truncation and matched training/evaluation context lengths) provide evidence for how WorldToken policies learn to utilize recent history and adapt to the available context. The RMBench Blocks Ranking study further demonstrates the role of extended context in enabling sustained, ordered behavior beyond the demonstrated horizon and visible context window.

#### 5.4. Limitations and Future Directions

- The study acknowledges several limitations. The results pertain to the *complete WorldToken instantiation* under the tested recipes, not time-first organization in isolation. It does not establish superiority over alternative sequence organizations, nor does it isolate the causal effect of individual components. The work is limited to simulation and action-only imitation learning, without addressing real-robot uncertainty, explicit action history, predictive objectives, or system-level efficiency. These limitations highlight that the current work establishes empirical feasibility and provides an interpretable interface for future comparative studies. Investigating whether this organization yields measurable advantages over other embodied sequence structuring methods remains an open question.
