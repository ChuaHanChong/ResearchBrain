# WorldDiT: A Unified Diffusion Architecture for World and Action Modeling

> [!summary] Summary
> Bagel Labs developed WorldDiT, a sub-billion-parameter diffusion transformer that unifies continuous action generation with an auxiliary future visual prediction objective. The model achieved a 94.9% mean success rate across four LIBERO manipulation suites, demonstrating competitive performance without relying on a multi-billion-parameter pre-trained vision-language model action backbone.

## Problem

- Robot policies often integrate action generation with multi-billion-parameter pre-trained vision-language models (VLMs), making it difficult to disentangle the contributions of architectural design versus the VLM's inherent capabilities to control performance.
- The reliance on large VLM backbones (billions of parameters) for robot control incurs substantial computational costs for training and deployment.
- There is a need to explore parameter-efficient solutions that can achieve strong robot control performance without the extensive resource demands of large VLM-backed policies.

## Method

- Introduces **WorldDiT**, a unified ==diffusion transformer== architecture that couples continuous action generation with an auxiliary future visual prediction objective (normalized RGB patches).
- Employs a single, shared ==Diffusion Transformer (DiT)== backbone conditioned on observations, robot states, and language instructions, trained using a ==flow matching== objective.
- Utilizes a sub-billion-parameter configuration (399M total, 135M trainable) and a ==receding-horizon control== scheme for inference, omitting the visual prediction component during deployment.

## Results

- WorldDiT achieved a mean success rate of **94.9%** across the four LIBERO manipulation benchmark suites (LIBERO Spatial, Object, Goal, and Long).
- The model operates with **399.084 million** total parameters, including frozen modules, with only **135.107 million** parameters being trainable.
- Its performance places it on the Pareto frontier for mean LIBERO success versus total parameters, indicating an efficient trade-off between model size and performance compared to existing methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - High-level robot manipulation capabilities can be achieved with a sub-billion-parameter architecture that does not require a large pre-trained vision-language model in its action backbone.
> - A unified diffusion transformer for world and action modeling, incorporating auxiliary future visual prediction, provides an effective and compact basis for learning robust robot policies.
> - WorldDiT establishes a parameter-efficient baseline on the Pareto frontier of success rate versus total parameters, informing future scaling studies in robot learning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robot learning, particularly robot manipulation, has seen a recent trend towards integrating large pre-trained vision-language models (VLMs) as the core action backbone for robot policies. This approach aims to leverage the broad perception and language understanding capabilities inherent in these large models to achieve more robust control. Examples of such VLA (Vision-Language-Action) models include ACoT VLA, MMaDA VLA, VLANeXt, and OpenVLA. These models often encompass several billion parameters, with the action generation component heavily reliant on the pre-trained VLM.

While these VLM-backed policies have demonstrated strong performance, their complexity introduces challenges in dissecting the individual contributions of architectural design, the scale of the pre-trained VLM, and their combined effects. The sheer size of these models can make it difficult to ascertain whether strong control performance stems primarily from the action design itself or is largely inherited from the pre-existing capabilities of the massive VLM backbone. Furthermore, such large models typically demand significant computational resources for training and deployment.

WorldDiT positions itself within this landscape by exploring an alternative paradigm. Instead of building upon a large pre-trained VLM for action generation, it proposes a unified diffusion transformer architecture that couples continuous action generation with an auxiliary future visual prediction objective. The central premise is to ascertain if strong control performance can be achieved without the reliance on a multi-billion-parameter VLM action backbone, thereby potentially offering a more parameter-efficient solution. The work specifically contributes a sub-billion-parameter baseline for future scaling studies, evaluating its performance against existing methods on the LIBERO manipulation benchmark. The objective is to demonstrate that a unified model for world and action modeling can achieve competitive results, thus potentially simplifying the architecture and reducing parameter count while maintaining high task success rates.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to investigate whether a unified diffusion transformer architecture can effectively combine continuous action generation with an auxiliary future visual prediction objective, and subsequently, maintain strong control performance without requiring a large pre-trained vision-language model as its action backbone.

This objective is driven by several motivations:

- **Disentangling Scale and Architecture Contributions:** Current robot policies often integrate action generation with large pre-trained VLMs, making it challenging to isolate whether strong control performance is attributable to the action design, the inherent capabilities of the pre-trained backbone, or a combination of both. The authors aim to explore an architecture where these factors might be more distinct.
- **Exploring Parameter Efficiency:** The reliance on multi-billion-parameter VLMs for robot control implies substantial computational costs for both training and inference. By developing an architecture that foregoes such a large backbone, the research seeks to investigate if competitive performance can be achieved with a significantly reduced parameter count, potentially leading to more deployable and resource-efficient robotic systems.
- **Unified World and Action Modeling:** The paper is motivated by the hypothesis that jointly modeling the robot's actions and its perception of the future world (through visual prediction) within a single, unified architecture can provide a compact and effective basis for learning robust robot policies. This unification aims to allow the model to learn a more comprehensive understanding of its environment and the consequences of its actions.
- **Establishing a Baseline for Scaling Studies:** By presenting a model that achieves strong performance at a sub-billion-parameter count and positions itself on the Pareto frontier of success rate versus total parameters, the authors aim to provide a concrete baseline. This baseline is intended to inform future research into scaling laws for robot policies, particularly concerning architectures that do not heavily rely on existing large VLMs.
- **Investigating Auxiliary Supervision:** The inclusion of an auxiliary future visual prediction objective (predicting normalized RGB patches from future camera frames) serves to explore whether additional supervisory signals, beyond direct action imitation, can enhance the learning of control policies within a unified framework. The motivation is to provide richer feedback to the model during training, potentially improving its internal representation of the environment and task dynamics.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

WorldDiT is introduced as a unified diffusion transformer architecture designed to learn action generation concurrently with an auxiliary future normalized RGB patch objective. The methodology encompasses several key components and stages:

#### 3.1. Architecture Overview

The core of WorldDiT is a single, shared Diffusion Transformer (DiT) backbone. This backbone is conditioned on a recent temporal context of observations, robot states, and a language instruction.
- **Encoders:**
 - **Visual Encoders:** Frozen MAE (Masked Autoencoder) image encoder for primary and wrist camera RGB images.
 - **Language Encoder:** Frozen CLIP text encoder for the language instruction.
 - **Robot State Encoder:** Trainable encoders for the arm and gripper components, whose embeddings are concatenated and projected into a single state token per temporal slot.
 - **Multimodal Integration:** A shared Perceiver Resampler processes the visual tokens, and projections are used to integrate language and robot state tokens. These form "clean context tokens" (34 visual, 1 language, 1 state token per temporal slot).
- **WorldDiT Backbone:** A DiT backbone with a depth of 4, a hidden size of 1024, 16 attention heads, and 4 learned register tokens. It employs per-token AdaLN-Zero. The backbone processes a combination of clean context tokens and corrupted target tokens. It utilizes a strict block-causal, action-safe attention mechanism, ensuring that action queries do not access noised RGB targets.

#### 3.2. Training Methodology

WorldDiT is trained using a flow matching objective (Lipman et al., 2023), which involves regressing the velocity of a straight path from Gaussian noise to a clean target.
- **Training Window and Targets:** A ten-step window is used during training, comprising:
 - **Observed Context:** Three past/current observation steps (C=3), consisting of multi-view RGB images and robot states.
 - **Action Target:** A seven-step continuous action chunk (H=7).
 - **Future Visual Target:** Normalized RGB patches selected from the primary-camera and wrist-camera frames at a temporal offset of H (i.e., the frame at `i+H`).
- **RGB Patch Target Construction:** Each CLIP-preprocessed 224x224 RGB frame is divided into 16x16 patches. Each 768-dimensional patch vector is normalized (mean and variance). 64 evenly spaced patches are retained from each camera, resulting in a 128x768 dimensional `Y_rgb` target.
- **Tokenization for Backbone:** Corrupted action tokens and normalized RGB patch tokens (with Gaussian noise at a sampled timestep τ) are passed to the backbone, along with their timestep embeddings and learned register tokens. Trainable linear input and output projections handle the RGB patch vectors.
- **Loss Function:** The total loss is a weighted sum of the action velocity loss (`L_action_flow`) and the RGB patch velocity loss (`L_rgb_flow`).
 `L_total = w_action * L_action_flow + w_rgb * L_rgb_flow`
 The loss is applied only to the final temporal slot's targets.
- **Training Protocol:**
 - **Data:** The LIBERO manipulation benchmark is used, including `libero_spatial`, `libero_object`, `libero_goal`, and `libero_10` (referred to as LIBERO Long) for downstream evaluation. `libero_90` (a large multi-task split) is used for pretraining.
 - **Pretraining:** 30 epochs on `libero_90` with a per-GPU batch size of 40 and gradient accumulation of 2, learning rate of 1e-4 with cosine schedule and one warmup epoch.
 - **Fine-tuning:** Independent fine-tuning on each downstream suite from the pretrained `libero_90` checkpoint. Effective batch size of 512, per-GPU batch size of 32, two gradient accumulation steps on eight GPUs. Loss weights `w_action = 0.1` and `w_rgb = 0.001`.
 - **Hardware:** All training runs conducted on one node with eight RTX Pro 6000 GPUs.

#### 3.3. Inference Methodology

During deployment, the future RGB patch prediction component is omitted. The process is action-only and follows a receding-horizon control scheme.
- **Input:** The unified backbone receives a C-step history (observations and states) ending at the current control step, along with the language instruction.
- **Action Generation:**
 - Action tokens are initialized from Gaussian noise.
 - The learned action velocity field (predicted by the DiT backbone) is numerically integrated from τ=0 to τ=1 using 20 Euler steps.
 - The final integrated state defines the predicted H-step action chunk (seven actions).
- **Execution and Replanning:** A prefix of the generated action chunk (three actions) is executed in the simulator. The history is then updated with new observations and states, and the process of action generation is repeated (replanning).
- **Evaluation:**
 - Evaluation is performed over 500 episodes per suite.
 - Techniques like action ensembling and temporal ensembling (for overlapping absolute-time action predictions) are applied.
 - Simulator rollouts use headless EGL rendering.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents results indicating the performance of WorldDiT across four LIBERO simulation suites, comparing it against 23 other methods.

- **Overall Success Rates:** WorldDiT achieved a mean success rate of 94.9% across the four LIBERO suites.
 - LIBERO Spatial: 98.0%
 - LIBERO Object: 97.0%
 - LIBERO Goal: 92.8%
 - LIBERO Long: 91.8%
 These results indicate that LIBERO Long remained the most challenging suite, consistent with its requirement for extended multi-stage behaviors.

- **Parameter Efficiency:** The WorldDiT configuration evaluated utilizes a total of 399.084 million parameters, which includes all frozen modules required for inference. Of these, 135.107 million parameters are trainable.

- **Position on Pareto Frontier:** When plotting mean LIBERO success against total instantiated model parameters (on a logarithmic scale), WorldDiT's performance and parameter count place it on the reported Pareto frontier. This indicates an efficient trade-off between model size and performance. Specifically:
 - Among methods that report mean success across all four LIBERO suites, WorldDiT achieves 94.9% success with 399M parameters.
 - All methods that report a higher mean success rate than WorldDiT (e.g., ACoT VLA, MMaDA VLA, VLANeXt) utilize more total parameters, generally exceeding 1 billion parameters.
 - Conversely, all other methods reported at or below WorldDiT's parameter count demonstrate lower mean success rates across the four suites.

- **Performance Without Large VLM Backbone:** The results demonstrate that WorldDiT, which does not employ a large pre-trained vision-language model as its action backbone, can achieve success rates comparable to, or exceeding, several methods that do rely on such large backbones, while maintaining a significantly lower parameter count. This addresses the core objective of the research.

- **Evaluation Protocol Notes:** The reported 94.9% mean success rate includes 300 episodes per suite that were used during staged checkpoint selection, meaning it is not a fully held-out test estimate. The authors also note that due to incomplete public availability of model weights and training code for compared methods, scores were taken from cited reports, and some parameter counts were reconstructed and labeled as approximate.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development and evaluation of WorldDiT carry several implications for the field of robot learning:

- **Demonstration of ArchitecturaL Efficiency:** WorldDiT's performance on the LIBERO benchmark, particularly its position on the parameter-success Pareto frontier, suggests that high-level robot manipulation capabilities are achievable without requiring a multi-billion-parameter pre-trained vision-language model in the action backbone. This challenges the prevailing assumption that scaling up pre-trained VLMs is the primary path to robust robot control.
- **Validation of Unified World-Action Modeling:** The work supports the concept that a single, unified diffusion transformer architecture can effectively couple continuous action generation with an auxiliary future visual prediction objective. This unification provides supervision that may lead to more comprehensive internal representations of the environment and task dynamics, potentially enabling a more compact and coherent approach to learning.
- **Foundation for Future Scaling Studies:** By providing a strong sub-billion-parameter baseline, WorldDiT offers a new reference point for future research into scaling laws in robot learning. This allows for investigations into how model capacity, data diversity, and deployment settings influence performance within architectures that explicitly integrate world modeling and action generation.
- **Potential for Resource-Constrained Deployment:** A significant implication is the potential for deploying sophisticated robot policies on platforms with limited computational resources. If strong performance can be maintained with fewer parameters, it opens avenues for wider adoption of advanced robot learning techniques in real-world scenarios, including those with heterogeneous hardware and compute-constrained robot systems.
- **Direction for Auxiliary Objectives:** The use of a normalized future RGB objective highlights the potential of auxiliary tasks to provide valuable training signals. Future research can further characterize how such visual prediction objectives specifically shape and improve control policies, and whether similar performance characteristics persist at even larger model scales.
- **Exploration of Model Decomposition:** The shared flow-matching formulation for both action and visual targets suggests possibilities for decomposing WorldDiT into independently trained experts. This could align with research directions seen in models like Paris and Paris 2.0, potentially leading to more modular, flexible, and resource-adaptable robotic systems.
