# PLAICraft: Large-Scale Time-Aligned Vision-Speech-Action Dataset for Embodied AI

> [!summary] Summary
> PLAICraft introduces a large-scale, time-aligned vision-speech-action dataset and data collection platform for embodied AI, recorded from over 10,000 players in a persistent, multiplayer Minecraft environment. The platform captures five modalities with millisecond precision, enabling research into real-time, socially situated, and speech-interactive agents.

## Problem

- A lack of large-scale datasets that are simultaneously real-time, multi-modal, and socially interactive hinders the training of human-level embodied AI agents.
- Many current AI agents interact indirectly (e.g., via text), preventing engagement in temporally grounded perceptual-motor learning loops essential for true embodied intelligence.
- Existing simulated environments for real-time sensorimotor learning often do not offer the rich social interaction or linguistic complexity found in natural settings, limiting the scope of trainable behaviors.

## Method

- Developed PLAICraft, a data collection platform based on a modded multiplayer Minecraft server, configured for survival mode with proximity-based 3D voice communication and custom social features.
- Implemented an AWS-based infrastructure that spawns dedicated virtual machines for each user, ensuring isolated and uniform data recording while capturing five time-aligned modalities (video, two audio streams, mouse, keyboard actions) with millisecond precision.
- Recruited over 10,000 global Minecraft players, incentivizing social engagement through persistent multiplayer environments and an incentive system that allocates play time based on the quality of player interactions, determined by LLM analysis of speech transcription.

## Results

- The PLAICraft dataset comprises over 10,000 hours of gameplay from more than 10,000 global participants, capturing five time-aligned modalities (video, game audio, microphone audio, mouse, keyboard actions) with millisecond precision.
- The data originates from an unbounded multiplayer social Minecraft environment featuring proximity-based voice chat, reflecting diverse player activities and social interactions, with a continually evolving global world state over a year.
- An accompanying evaluation suite, aligned with the Cattell-Horn-Carroll theory of cognitive abilities, probes agents' reasoning, memory, and communicative competence through curated prompts requiring specific actions or concise verbal answers, quantifiable by metrics like Hamming distance or exact-match accuracy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The integration of real-time speech audio and a multiplayer social environment can enable the training of embodied agents capable of real-time social cue interpretation, collaborative play, and complex social reasoning.
> - The dataset's task-free, continual world, and persistent agent states offer a suitable foundation for investigating continual learning paradigms, allowing agents to develop long-term memory and adapt to non-stationary environments.
> - The application-agnostic design of the data collection platform suggests extensibility beyond Minecraft to other desktop applications, potentially leading to general computer-use agents capable of operating full computer interfaces with high temporal precision.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Embodied Artificial Intelligence (EAI) seeks to develop agents capable of perceiving, acting, and learning within dynamic environments through real-time sensory-motor interaction. This research area emphasizes agents that must effectively respond to environmental changes and possess a clear purpose in their actions, aligning with principles articulated by Brooks. While contemporary agentic AI systems, such as Devin and Claude Code, demonstrate autonomous task execution, many remain disembodied, interacting primarily through high-level text interfaces and asynchronous API calls. This indirect interaction limits their capacity for temporally grounded, perceptual-motor learning loops.

Existing simulated environments, including Habitat, RoboTHOR, and ThreeDWorld, facilitate real-time sensorimotor learning but often do not incorporate socially interactive or linguistically rich scenarios. Prior Minecraft-based datasets, such as MineRL, OpenAI’s VPT, and CraftAssist, have advanced research in perception-action coupling and language learning. MineDojo further contributed large-scale web-mined video-language data for open-ended instruction following. However, these datasets frequently operate in offline contexts, lack interactive capabilities, exhibit heterogeneity in data collection, or do not offer real-time social grounding, which involves linking symbols to perceptual input, physical action, and shared environmental context.

PLAICraft addresses these limitations by providing a dataset and platform specifically designed for the study of real-time, socially situated, and speech-interactive embodied agents. It aims to bridge the gap by offering a large-scale, real-time, multi-modal, and socially interactive dataset that captures the sensory-motor complexity inherent in natural environments, positioning itself to support the development of agents that can operate fluently and purposefully in dynamic, social settings.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce PLAICraft, a novel data collection platform and an associated large-scale dataset. This platform and dataset are designed to facilitate the study of real-time, socially situated, and speech-interactive embodied agents within a complex, open-ended environment.

The motivation for developing PLAICraft stems from several limitations identified in the existing research landscape for embodied AI:

- **Absence of Comprehensive Datasets:** A significant barrier to progress in training human-level embodied agents is the lack of large-scale datasets that are simultaneously real-time, multi-modal, and socially interactive. Current datasets often fall short in one or more of these aspects, limiting the development of agents that can operate effectively in environments demanding dynamic interaction and social intelligence.
- **Need for Temporally Grounded Interaction:** Many modern AI agents interact with their environments indirectly, typically through text or asynchronous API calls. This disembodied interaction prevents them from engaging in the tightly coupled, temporally grounded perceptual-motor loops that are characteristic of true embodied intelligence. The researchers are motivated to provide data where perception, speech, and action are intrinsically linked in time.
- **Limitations of Existing Simulated Environments:** While some simulated environments support real-time sensorimotor learning, they frequently do not offer the rich social interaction or linguistic complexity found in natural settings. This restricts the scope of behaviors and cognitive abilities that can be trained and evaluated.
- **Gaps in Prior Minecraft Datasets:** Previous Minecraft datasets have contributed to embodied AI, but they often lack real-time, social grounding. They might be restricted to single-player modes, limited task sets, or consist of heterogeneous, non-systematic online videos, which do not fully capture dynamic, multiplayer social interactions.
- **Embracing Environmental Complexity:** The work is motivated by a philosophy of not simplifying environmental complexity but rather embracing it. The researchers aim to create a dataset that reflects the open-ended, non-episodic nature of real-world interactions, where agents must act with purpose amidst uncertainty, social influence, and ambiguity, without explicit, global reward signals or predefined tasks. This approach supports research in large-scale imitation learning as a foundational goal.

By addressing these motivations, PLAICraft aims to create a foundation for training and evaluating agents that can navigate and interact effectively within complex, social, and dynamic virtual worlds.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The PLAICraft project employs a multi-faceted methodology encompassing platform development, data collection, and evaluation suite design.

#### 3.1. Environment Selection and Configuration

Minecraft was chosen as the environment due to its open-ended dynamics, procedurally generated 3D worlds, and capacity for diverse activities. The platform utilizes a modded multiplayer Minecraft server, configured to run in survival mode with normal difficulty, making all three standard worlds (Overworld, Nether, End) accessible. Key modifications include:
- **Autojoin Plugin:** A custom plugin designed to manage player states (inventory, position, level) in a backend database, decoupling them from specific game licenses. This enables the platform to support a large number of non-concurrent players using a limited pool of licenses.
- **Simple Voice Chat Plugin:** An integration that enables immersive, proximity-based 3D voice communication, allowing players to communicate naturally with dynamically adjusting audio volume based on in-game distance.
- **Visual Enhancements:** The Complementary Shader Pack (Unbound) was included to improve visual detail and environmental complexity.
- **Social Interaction Features:** Teleportation capabilities, a global chat interface, and custom minigame worlds (Lobby, BedWars, Murder Mystery) were added to encourage player interaction and collaborative gameplay.
- **Client-Side Restrictions:** In-game settings were restricted, and instance termination protocols were implemented if players minimized the Minecraft window or disconnected, ensuring recorded data pertained solely to gameplay.

#### 3.2. Data Collection Platform Setup

The data collection platform is primarily built on Amazon Web Services (AWS):
- **Backend:** AWS Lambda functions manage interactions with AWS resources, storing user information and gameplay status in Amazon DynamoDB. These functions are exposed via Amazon API Gateway.
- **Frontend:** A web client hosted in Amazon S3 and delivered via Amazon CloudFront allows users to log in and access the game.
- **Virtual Machines:** Upon login, a dedicated g5.2xlarge Amazon EC2 instance (Ubuntu, NVIDIA A10G Tensor Core GPUs) is spawned for each user. This ensures isolated and uniform data recording.
- **Remote Display:** NICE DCV, a high-performance remote display protocol, streams the instance's display to the user at up to 60 FPS and 2K resolution.
- **Privacy and Security:** Recording occurs within the secure VM environment, and auxiliary scripts monitor player activity, terminating instances and uploading data to S3 upon disconnection or loss of window focus.

#### 3.3. Modality Capture and Precision

The platform records five time-aligned modalities with millisecond precision:
- **Video:** Captured by Open Broadcaster Software (OBS) at 30 FPS, 1280x720 resolution, H.264 codec. OBS is configured to capture only the Minecraft window.
- **Audio (Game Output and Microphone Input):** Simultaneously recorded by OBS at 48 kHz stereo, AAC codec. Separate streams are captured for game output (`audio_hear`) and player microphone input (`audio_speak`).
- **Mouse and Keyboard Actions:** Captured by a custom Forge mod integrated into the Minecraft client. This method provides precise, event-driven timestamps at a maximum polling rate of 100 Hz. System-level logging (pyxhook) serves as a secondary validation measure.

#### 3.4. Data Recruitment and Social Interaction Incentives

The project recruited over 10,000 voluntary Minecraft enthusiasts globally through media outreach and promotions. Participants span diverse age groups, genders, and experience levels (Figure 3). To encourage social engagement, the platform integrates persistent multiplayer environments and voice chat. An incentive system allocates additional solo play time based on the quality and quantity of player interactions. This is determined by live transcription of speech audio using WhisperX, followed by analysis with a large language model (LLM) to detect meaningful conversations (Figure 4).

#### 3.5. Data Pre-processing and Encoding

A rigorous pre-processing pipeline converts raw data into a structured format:
- **Video:** MP4 conversion, trimmed to gameplay segments.
- **Audio:** WAV format. `audio_speak` undergoes Silero Voice Activity Detection (VAD) and Pyannote speaker diarization. Both audio tracks are transcribed using WhisperX.
- **Mouse & Keyboard:** Mouse click events are segregated from movement trajectories. Data are binned into fixed 100 ms temporal intervals.
- **Encoding:** Raw data are pre-encoded into lower-dimensional neural representations to reduce computational load for model training. Video data are encoded using SDXL-VAE-FP16-Fix (10 FPS latents). Audio signals are encoded using EnCodec 24 kHz (75 Hz latents). Keyboard and mouse click data are compressed using a temporal convolutional autoencoder. Mouse movement data are retained without additional encoding.

#### 3.6. Continual Global World State

The server maintains a single, persistent world state that has continuously evolved over a year without resets. Player attributes, inventories, and experience levels persist across sessions. Environmental changes, such as constructed structures or mined blocks, are permanent (Figure 6).

#### 3.7. Evaluation Suite Design

An evaluation suite is provided, inspired by frameworks like Animal-AI and BIB, and structured around the Cattell-Horn-Carroll (CHC) theory of cognitive abilities. It contains curated prompts designed to assess perception, understanding, reasoning, and action within PLAICraft. Prompts map to 10 broad CHC cognitive abilities and elicit either keyboard/mouse actions or concise verbal answers. Evaluation metrics include Hamming distance for keyboard actions and exact-match accuracy for transcribed verbal responses (Table 2).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The PLAICraft project has yielded a large-scale, multimodal dataset and an accompanying evaluation framework, demonstrating several key characteristics:

#### 4.1. Dataset Scale and Modality

- The dataset comprises over 10,000 hours of gameplay.
- It was collected from more than 10,000 global participants.
- It includes five time-aligned modalities: screen video, game output audio, player microphone input audio, mouse actions, and keyboard actions.
- All modalities are logged with millisecond time precision, enabling the study of synchronous, embodied behavior.
- Encoded data representations are provided for all modalities, with video encoded at 10 FPS, audio at 75 Hz, mouse movement at 100 Hz, and key presses at 50 Hz.

#### 4.2. Environmental and Social Complexity

- The data originates from an unbounded multiplayer social environment in Minecraft, featuring proximity-based voice chat (Figure 1).
- Participants span diverse age groups, genders, and experience levels, as reported through self-declared demographic statistics (Figure 3).
- The dataset captures a wide range of player activities, including basic actions (moving, breaking blocks), moderately complex behaviors (mining, crafting, combating), and advanced skills (defeating bosses, constructing redstone mechanisms).
- It also records numerous social interactions, ranging from simple greetings and self-introductions to collaborative activities like team formation and collective base construction, as well as occasional negative interactions (conflicts, theft) (Figure 2, 4).
- The environment maintains a continual global world state, with player attributes and environmental changes persisting across sessions over an extended period (Figure 6).

#### 4.3. Evaluation Suite Capabilities

- An evaluation suite has been developed to probe agents' reasoning, memory, and communicative competence.
- The suite consists of curated prompts designed to assess a broad spectrum of cognitive abilities, aligned with the Cattell-Horn-Carroll (CHC) theory.
- Representative prompts demonstrate the ability to test object recognition, spatial awareness, world-model understanding, arithmetic, spelling, and audio-to-action binding (Table 2).
- The responses required are either specific keyboard/mouse actions or concise verbal answers, allowing for quantitative evaluation using metrics such as Hamming distance or exact-match accuracy.

#### 4.4. Initial Utility Demonstration

- A subset of the PLAICraft dataset (50 hours from an anonymous player) has been leveraged by Yoo et al. to train a video-diffusion model in a continual-learning setting. This demonstrates an early application of the dataset in developing models that can learn from continuous data streams.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

PLAICraft introduces a large-scale, multimodal dataset and a standardized data collection platform designed to facilitate new directions in embodied AI research.

**Advancement of Social Embodied Agents:** The inclusion of real-time speech audio and the multiplayer social environment is intended to enable the training of agents capable of incorporating real-time social cues. This feature supports research into collaborative play and complex social reasoning, which prior single-player datasets generally did not capture. Agents could potentially develop capabilities for reactive speech and social influence.

**Support for Continual and Third-Person Learning:** The dataset's task-free, continual world, and persistent agent states are suitable for investigating continual learning paradigms. Agents could develop and maintain long-term memory over extended gameplay periods and adapt to non-stationary environments. Furthermore, the extensive collection of diverse player trajectories in a shared world could facilitate systematic studies of third-person learning, where agents learn from observing the behavior of others rather than solely from their own on-policy experiences.

**Research in World Models and Simulation:** The vast collection of precisely aligned high-resolution videos and ambient audio, recorded from multiple player perspectives within a persistent world, provides a resource for world model training, 3D environment understanding, and physics simulation research. This could contribute to the development of AI systems with a more robust understanding of their operating environments.

**Generalization to Computer-Use Agents:** The data collection platform's application-agnostic design means that Minecraft can be replaced with other desktop applications, such as web browsers or productivity tools, while maintaining the same data schema and time-alignment guarantees. This extensibility could pave the way for research into general computer-use agents capable of operating a full computer interface (screen, audio, keyboard, mouse) with high temporal precision. This has potential applications in automating information workflows and even acting as de facto control policies for remote teleoperators of robots.

**Quantitative Investigation of Scaling Laws:** The controlled data and compute budgets afforded by the dataset can enable quantitative investigations into scaling laws for embodied agents. This research could relate training resources to the development of control, communication, and social coordination capabilities in AI systems.

**Ethical Considerations:** The researchers acknowledge and discuss the societal impacts of combining embodied AI with immersive game environments, including the potential for blurring the lines between artificial and human players, risks of deception or manipulation, and concerns regarding data privacy and labor displacement in virtual economies. Measures, such as obtaining consent, routine review by a Behavioral Research Ethics Board, and systematic anonymization of identifiable data, have been implemented to address these concerns.
