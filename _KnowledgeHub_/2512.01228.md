# On the Tension Between Optimality and Adversarial Robustness in Policy Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers analyzed the practical trade-off between optimality and adversarial robustness in deep reinforcement learning, proposing the Bilevel Adversarially Robust Policy Optimization (BARPO) framework to achieve both high natural returns and superior robustness, particularly across MuJoCo continuous control tasks.

## Problem

- Deep reinforcement learning agents are highly vulnerable to adversarial attacks, posing a significant challenge for their reliable deployment in safety-critical environments.
- Despite theoretical work suggesting a potential alignment between optimal and robust policies, practical policy optimization methods typically exhibit a trade-off, where robust policies sacrifice natural performance.
- The underlying mechanisms causing this empirical tension between optimality and robustness in current policy optimization algorithms were not fully understood.

## Method

- The authors developed a general ==Bilevel Optimization== Framework (**BARPO**) that unifies standard and adversarially robust policy optimization by modulating adversary strength rather than always using the strongest adversary.
- Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence between original and perturbed policies was identified and utilized as a theoretically justified surrogate for the strongest adversary in the inner optimization loop.
- The BARPO framework was instantiated as BAR-PPO, integrating Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) dynamics with SPO-guidance and employing Stochastic Gradient Langevin Dynamics (SGLD) to find adversarial perturbations.

## Results

- BARPO consistently and significantly outperformed vanilla ARPO across MuJoCo environments, achieving 75–**156%** higher natural returns and 4–**209%** higher worst-case robust returns.
- The BAR-PPO method achieved high natural returns comparable to or exceeding vanilla PPO while demonstrating superior worst-case robust returns, ranging from **4%** to **154%** improvement over state-of-the-art robust baselines.
- The framework's efficacy was further validated in the complex Humanoid task, maintaining strong natural and robust performance, with ablation studies confirming the importance of both the bilevel structure and KL surrogate.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The practical trade-off arises because strong adversaries in adversarially robust policy optimization (ARPO) reshape the optimization landscape, introducing 'deceptive sticky FOSPs' (local minima) that trap optimization algorithms in suboptimal robust solutions.
> - Modulating adversary strength through a KL-divergence surrogate effectively smooths the optimization landscape, enabling agents to navigate towards First-Order Stationary Policies (FOSPs) that are both high-performing and robust.
> - A unified bilevel optimization approach, guided by SPO dynamics, allows for the simultaneous achievement of high natural returns and strong adversarial robustness, reconciling the theoretical alignment with practical implementation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Deep reinforcement learning (DRL) has demonstrated considerable success across various complex tasks, including robotics and autonomous driving. However, a significant challenge to the reliable deployment of DRL agents in safety-critical environments is their vulnerability to adversarial attacks. These attacks involve subtle, imperceptible perturbations to state observations that can lead to severe performance degradation or system failure.

The broader research landscape in adversarial DRL has developed along two main lines: understanding and mitigating these vulnerabilities. Early theoretical work, such as the state-adversarial Markov decision process (SA-MDP) framework, explored the nature of adversarial robustness, sometimes suggesting a conflict between optimality and robustness. More recent theoretical advancements, notably the intrinsic state-adversarial Markov decision process (ISA-MDP) formulation by Li et al. (2024; 2025), have posited a potential alignment: an optimal robust policy (ORP) can exist and coincide with the Bellman optimality policy (BOP) in many practical tasks. This theoretical consistency suggests that robust and optimal performance might, in principle, be achievable simultaneously.

Despite these theoretical insights, a persistent gap exists between theory and practice. Conventional policy optimization methods, even those designed for robustness (like adversarially robust policy optimization, ARPO), often struggle to achieve this dual objective. Standard policy optimization (SPO) typically yields high-performing but vulnerable policies, while ARPO frequently trades off optimal performance for increased robustness. This paper directly addresses this theory-practice gap by analyzing the underlying causes of this empirical tension and proposing a novel framework to reconcile it. It builds upon previous robust training methods like SA-PPO, RADIAL-PPO, and WocaR-PPO by offering a new perspective on the optimization dynamics.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to investigate whether the theoretical alignment between optimality and adversarial robustness in DRL, as suggested by recent ISA-MDP theory, can be practically realized through policy optimization methods. The authors aim to understand the barriers to achieving this alignment and to develop a computational framework that bridges the observed gap between theory and practice.

The motivation for this work is multi-faceted:

- **Addressing DRL Vulnerability for Practical Deployment**: The vulnerability of DRL agents to adversarial attacks is a critical impediment to their deployment in real-world, safety-critical applications. Agents must be capable of maintaining performance while withstanding malicious perturbations.
- **Reconciling Theoretical Consistency with Empirical Discrepancy**: While theoretical advancements in ISA-MDP suggest that an Optimal Robust Policy (ORP) aligns with the Bellman Optimality Policy (BOP), practical policy optimization methods exhibit a persistent trade-off. Standard Policy Optimization (SPO) yields high-performing but vulnerable policies, whereas Adversarially Robust Policy Optimization (ARPO) prioritizes robustness often at the expense of natural performance. This discrepancy highlights a fundamental unresolved challenge in current DRL training paradigms.
- **Elucidating the Nature of the Optimality-Robustness Tension**: A core motivation is to understand the precise mechanisms underlying this practical trade-off. The paper aims to systematically compare the optimization behaviors and learning dynamics of SPO and ARPO to identify *why* they diverge in their convergence properties, particularly concerning policy vulnerability and natural return. This involves examining how the optimization landscape is shaped under different training objectives.
- **Developing an Effective Solution**: Based on a deeper understanding of the trade-off's root causes, the motivation extends to proposing a novel optimization framework. This framework should be capable of guiding policy learning towards solutions that are simultaneously high-performing in clean environments and robust against adversarial attacks, thereby offering a practical approach to achieve the theoretically consistent ORP.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a multi-pronged methodology encompassing theoretical analysis, empirical investigation of optimization dynamics, and the development and evaluation of a novel algorithmic framework.

### 4. Analysis of Standard and Adversarially Robust Policy Optimization

- **Formal Definitions**: The paper formally defines Standard Policy Optimization (SPO) as maximizing the standard value function (max\_π V\_π(s)) and Adversarially Robust Policy Optimization (ARPO) as maximizing the worst-case adversarial value function (max\_π min\_ν V\_π◦ν(s)).
- **Convergence Analysis**: The authors conduct a rigorous theoretical analysis to demonstrate that both SPO and ARPO, when using policy gradient methods, generally converge to First-Order Stationary Policies (FOSPs) rather than global optima. This establishes a theoretical limit on their achievable performance and sets the stage for investigating the practical trade-off. The analysis includes deriving a policy gradient expression for the adversary and proving ARPO's convergence rate to an FOSP.
- **Empirical Comparison of FOSPs**: The paper empirically examines the characteristics of FOSPs obtained by SPO and ARPO. This involves evaluating natural returns and robustness against six types of adversarial attacks (Random, Critic, MAD, RS, SA-RL, PA-AD) in a toy ISA-MDP model and across four continuous control tasks in the MuJoCo environment (Hopper, Walker2d, Halfcheetah, Ant), utilizing neural network policies.
- **Optimization Landscape Analysis**: The study delves into the optimization landscape and value geometry induced by SPO and ARPO. It identifies a "reshaping effect" where the strongest adversaries in ARPO significantly alter the landscape. This includes demonstrating, via a toy ISA-MDP, how adversaries can introduce "deceptive sticky FOSPs" (local minima) and "vulnerable connectivity" in the robust policy space, hindering effective policy improvement.

### 5. Development of the Bilevel Optimization Framework (BARPO)

- **Unified Bilevel Formulation**: To address the identified challenges, the authors propose a general bilevel optimization framework that unifies SPO and ARPO. This framework relaxes the inner optimization, allowing for modulation of adversary strength rather than strictly focusing on the strongest adversary. The outer loop aims to maximize policy performance, while the inner loop defines the adversary's behavior. The framework preserves the theoretically optimal robust policy of ISA-MDP.
- **KL-Divergence as Surrogate Adversary**: A key component of BARPO is the identification of the Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence between the original and perturbed policies, D\_KL(π(·|s)∥π◦ν(·|s)), as an effective and theoretically justified surrogate for the strongest adversary. Theorem 4.1 demonstrates that, for gradient-based adversaries, minimizing the adversarial value is lower-bounded by a term related to this KL divergence.
- **BARPO Instantiation**: The specific instantiation of the framework, named Bilevel ARPO (BARPO), is defined as `max_θ V_π_θ◦ν_⋄(θ)(μ_0)`, where `ν_⋄(s;θ)` is the adversary that maximizes the KL divergence.
- **Integration with PPO (BAR-PPO)**: For practical implementation and enhanced convergence, the BARPO framework is built upon the Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) algorithm, a widely used policy-gradient method. It incorporates SPO dynamics via a regularization weight (κ) to smooth the optimization landscape. Stochastic Gradient Langevin Dynamics (SGLD) is employed to solve the inner maximization problem for finding adversarial perturbations.

### 6. Experimental Validation

- **Benchmarks**: Experiments are conducted on challenging continuous control MuJoCo benchmarks: Hopper, Walker2d, Halfcheetah, and Ant. An extended experiment on the Humanoid task, known for its high-dimensional observation and action spaces, is also performed.
- **Comparison with Baselines**: BAR-PPO is compared against several state-of-the-art robust training baselines, including SA-PPO, RADIAL-PPO, WocaR-PPO, and vanilla PPO (SPO).
- **Evaluation Metrics**: Performance is assessed based on natural returns (performance in clean environments), robust returns (performance under various adversarial attacks), and a worst-case robustness metric (relative performance degradation).
- **Ablation Studies**: Ablation studies are performed to analyze the individual contributions of the SPO-guidance component and the bilevel structure with the KL surrogate. The sensitivity of BARPO to hyperparameters like regularization coefficients, inner steps, and step sizes is also investigated. All experiments are repeated multiple times (17 runs) to account for variability and report median performance.

### 7. Main Findings and Results

The research yields several key findings concerning the optimality-robustness tension in policy optimization and the effectiveness of the proposed BARPO framework.

### 8. Identification of Optimality-Robustness Tension

- **SPO's Vulnerability**: Standard Policy Optimization (SPO) is shown to converge to First-Order Stationary Policies (FOSPs) that achieve high natural returns but are demonstrably vulnerable to adversarial attacks. This highlights a practical failure to achieve robust policies, despite theoretical models (ISA-MDP) suggesting an alignment between optimality and robustness.
- **ARPO's Trade-off**: Adversarially Robust Policy Optimization (ARPO) consistently produces policies with significantly higher robustness. However, this robustness comes at a cost, with ARPO-trained policies exhibiting substantially lower natural returns compared to those from SPO, often achieving less than half the performance. This substantiates a practical trade-off where robustness is gained by sacrificing optimality.

### 9. Underlying Mechanism: Adversarial Reshaping of the Optimization Landscape

- **Landscape Distortion**: The study attributes the observed trade-off to the "reshaping effect" of the strongest adversaries in ARPO. While these adversaries guide the agent towards robust FOSPs, they also introduce deep valleys and ruggedness into the optimization landscape.
- **Deceptive Sticky FOSPs**: These landscape distortions create numerous "deceptive sticky FOSPs," which are local minima that trap optimization algorithms. Agents trained with ARPO often become stuck in these robust but suboptimal solutions, preventing them from reaching better FOSPs or the globally optimal robust policy. This fragmentation of the robust policy space (termed "vulnerable connectivity" in a toy example) complicates gradient-based navigation.

### 10. BARPO's Performance and Landscape Refinement

- **Improved Navigability and Performance**: The Bilevel ARPO (BARPO) framework effectively reshapes the optimization landscape. By modulating adversary strength through a KL-divergence surrogate, BARPO elevates low-value but robust regions, creating smoother optimization paths. This facilitates navigation towards better FOSPs that offer both higher natural returns and stronger robustness.
- **Empirical Superiority over ARPO**: BARPO, even without explicit SPO guidance, consistently and significantly outperforms vanilla ARPO across all tested MuJoCo environments. It demonstrates substantial gains in both natural and robust returns (e.g., 75–156% increase in natural return and 4–209% in worst-case robust return over ARPO).
- **Overall Leading Performance (BAR-PPO)**: When integrated with SPO dynamics (BAR-PPO), the method exhibits strong overall performance, achieving high natural returns comparable to or exceeding vanilla PPO, alongside significantly superior worst-case robust returns compared to all state-of-the-art baselines (SA-PPO, RADIAL-PPO, WocaR-PPO). Improvements in worst-case robust returns ranged from 4% to 154% over the second-best methods.
- **Humanoid Task Validation**: BARPO's effectiveness is further confirmed in the complex Humanoid task, maintaining strong natural and robust performance.

### 11. Ablation Study Insights

- **Effect of SPO-Guidance**: Incorporating SPO-based guidance into BARPO generally enhances natural performance, sometimes with a trade-off in worst-case robustness. This suggests that while SPO guidance can improve optimality, careful balancing is needed to preserve robustness.
- **Bilevel Structure vs. KL Surrogate**: The combination of the bilevel structure and the KL surrogate is crucial. BARPO's improvements are attributed to this combined effect, as neither component in isolation fully achieves the same performance. The bilevel framework effectively leverages the KL surrogate to refine the landscape, offering a better trade-off than a direct minimax formulation with KL.
- **Hyperparameter Robustness**: BARPO's performance is not highly sensitive to variations in inner optimization steps or step sizes, indicating practical stability.

### 12. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of deep reinforcement learning by providing a detailed analysis of the practical challenges in achieving robust and optimal policies, and by proposing an effective solution.

- **Theoretical-Practical Bridge**: The work significantly narrows the gap between theoretical understanding (e.g., ISA-MDP's alignment of optimality and robustness) and practical implementation in DRL. By elucidating the specific mechanisms behind the optimality-robustness trade-off in policy gradient methods, it offers a more nuanced understanding of DRL's vulnerabilities and limitations.
- **Enhanced DRL Reliability and Safety**: The development of BARPO, which consistently achieves high natural performance and superior adversarial robustness, presents a practical pathway for deploying DRL agents in safety-critical applications such as autonomous driving, robotics, and medical systems. Increased reliability under adversarial conditions fosters greater trust in AI systems.
- **Novel Optimization Insights**: The analysis of optimization landscape reshaping by adversaries provides novel insights into the dynamics of non-convex and minimax optimization problems. The concept of "deceptive sticky FOSPs" and "vulnerable connectivity" can inform future research in optimization theory beyond DRL, particularly in understanding challenges in robust learning.
- **Foundation for Future Robust AI**: BARPO's framework, which intelligently modulates adversary strength, can serve as a foundation for developing more sophisticated robust AI systems. Future work could extend this approach to other forms of robustness (e.g., against environmental noise or model uncertainty) or integrate it with other DRL paradigms.
- **Catalyst for Advanced Bilevel Optimization in RL**: While BARPO currently employs a first-order approximation, its success highlights the potential of advanced bilevel optimization techniques in reinforcement learning. This could stimulate further research into more sophisticated algorithms for solving complex nested optimization problems efficiently and with stronger theoretical guarantees.
