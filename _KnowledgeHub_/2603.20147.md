# AGILE: A Comprehensive Workflow for Humanoid Loco-Manipulation Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> AGILE, a comprehensive workflow from NVIDIA, streamlines humanoid reinforcement learning development by integrating environment verification, scalable training, unified evaluation, and descriptor-driven deployment. The framework successfully demonstrated stable sim-to-real transfer for five distinct humanoid skills, including loco-manipulation, on Unitree G1 and Booster T1 robots, with training times between 6 to 25 hours per task on a single L40 GPU.

## Problem

- The 'workflow gap' in humanoid RL development refers to fragmented pipelines, leading to late discovery of basic environment issues and inefficient diagnosis due to reliance on stochastic policy rollouts.
- The 'transfer gap' highlights the fragility and labor-intensity of deploying learned policies to hardware or for external validation, often requiring manual adjustments due to a lack of standardized input/output contracts.
- Existing research often addresses specific aspects of robotic RL, but a holistic, end-to-end development lifecycle from systematic debugging to robust deployment is lacking.

## Method

![[2603.20147-method.png]]
*The AGILE workflow integrates four stages: interactive preparation for model verification, scalable training with algorithmic enhancements, unified quantitative evaluation across simulators, and descriptor-driven deployment to physical hardware.*

- **AGILE** provides a four-stage, configuration-driven workflow (==Prepare==, ==Train==, ==Evaluate==, ==Deploy==) built upon Isaac Lab and RSL-RL, wrapping the RL training loop with tools for verification, reproducibility, evaluation, and deployment.
- The 'Prepare' stage utilizes interactive GUIs (Joint Position, Object Manipulation, Reward Visualizer) for real-time verification of robot models and ==MDP== configurations before training.
- The 'Train' stage integrates scalable infrastructure with an algorithmic toolbox, including ==L2C2 regularization==, online reward normalization, ==value-bootstrapped terminations==, and a ==virtual harness==, to enhance training stability and sim-to-real transfer.
- The 'Evaluate' stage offers a unified pipeline for scenario-based and stochastic tests, cross-simulator compatibility (Isaac Lab and MuJoCo), and motion-quality diagnostics (acceleration, jerk, joint-limit violations) with HTML reports.
- The 'Deploy' stage enables descriptor-driven policy export to ==TorchScript== with self-contained I/O descriptors, facilitating unified and automatic inference in secondary simulators or on real hardware via Python or C++.

## Results

- Achieved successful qualitative sim-to-real transfer for **5 humanoid skills** (velocity tracking, height-controlled locomotion, stand-up, motion imitation, loco-manipulation) on Unitree G1 and Booster T1 platforms, with training times of **6–25 hours** per task on a single L40 GPU.
- Ablation studies showed reward normalizer improved training stability, L2C2 reduced RMS joint acceleration and jerk, value-bootstrapped terminations lowered training variance, and the virtual harness prevented early episode collapse.
- The modular architecture enabled freezing a trained locomotion policy and fine-tuning a GR00T N1.5 VLA model for loco-manipulation, achieving a **90%** success rate in pick-and-place tasks in closed-loop simulation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A structured, configuration-driven workflow can significantly enhance efficiency, reproducibility, and robustness in humanoid RL development by enabling early problem detection and standardizing interfaces across the development lifecycle.
> - Integrating specific algorithmic enhancements and regularization techniques (e.g., L2C2 for smoother control, value-bootstrapped terminations for robust learning, virtual harness for early stabilization) is crucial for achieving successful and safe sim-to-real transfer.
> - Unified evaluation, combining deterministic scenario testing with hardware-critical motion diagnostics and cross-simulator compatibility, provides essential quantitative pre-validation, reducing reliance on costly and potentially unsafe physical trials.
> - Standardized, descriptor-driven policy export and inference pipelines are vital for bridging the 'transfer gap,' ensuring consistent policy execution across different environments and facilitating robust deployment to physical hardware.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Recent advancements in reinforcement learning (RL) have facilitated the development of increasingly complex humanoid behaviors in simulated environments. However, transferring these learned policies effectively and reliably from simulation to physical robots (sim-to-real transfer) continues to present significant challenges. The paper identifies two primary bottlenecks in this process: the "workflow gap" and the "transfer gap."

The **workflow gap** refers to the fragmented and unsystematic nature of current humanoid RL development pipelines. Researchers often encounter basic environment issues, such as incorrect joint configurations or reward function errors, late in the development cycle, frequently after significant computational resources have been expended on training. Policy evaluation is also often reliant on stochastic rollouts, which can obscure critical hardware-specific issues like joint limit violations or high-frequency actuation. This ad hoc approach impedes reproducibility and efficient diagnosis.

The **transfer gap** pertains to the fragility and labor-intensity of deploying learned policies for external validation or hardware execution. Without standardized input/output (I/O) contracts, manual adjustments are frequently required for aspects such as joint order, observation history buffers, and action scaling. Such manual translation introduces potential errors and precludes the use of unified evaluation methods across different simulators or directly on hardware without prior rigorous validation.

Existing research has focused on various aspects of robotic RL. GPU-accelerated simulation platforms like Isaac Gym and Isaac Lab provide high-performance simulation primitives and modular architectures, primarily enhancing simulation speed and environment modeling. Other frameworks, such as Holosoma, HumanoidVerse, ProtoMotions, and RoboVerse, address challenges like large-scale training infrastructure, cross-simulator compatibility, motion tracking, and scalable robot learning across tasks. While these platforms contribute to training scalability and interoperability, they generally do not comprehensively address the entire development lifecycle, including systematic debugging, unified evaluation, and robust deployment.

Algorithmic techniques have also been developed to improve sim-to-real transfer, such as CAPS for smoother control policies and L2C2 for local Lipschitz continuity to enhance stability. Domain randomization is a widely used method to improve policy robustness to real-world dynamics discrepancies. Furthermore, efforts in evaluation and benchmarking, exemplified by OpenAI Gym, DeepMind Control Suite, HumanoidBench, and RoboGauge, aim to establish standardized task evaluation and metrics for quantifying sim-to-real transferability.

AGILE positions itself within this landscape by focusing on the **holistic development lifecycle** for humanoid RL. It integrates existing simulation primitives, algorithmic enhancements, and evaluation methodologies into a structured, end-to-end workflow. Rather than introducing a new RL algorithm or a new physics engine, AGILE provides an infrastructure layer that standardizes the policy-development process, thereby addressing the workflow and transfer gaps by formalizing interfaces across verification, training, evaluation, and deployment. This approach aims to shift humanoid RL development from empirical trial-and-error to a more repeatable, quantitatively validated engineering discipline.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to establish a systematic and comprehensive workflow for the development of humanoid reinforcement learning policies, aiming to mitigate common challenges associated with sim-to-real transfer and improve the overall reliability and reproducibility of the development process.

The motivation for AGILE stems from the identified "workflow gap" and "transfer gap" in contemporary humanoid RL research:

1. **Addressing the Workflow Gap**: Current humanoid RL development often lacks structured infrastructure, leading to inefficient and error-prone processes. Basic environment misconfigurations (e.g., incorrect joint axes, reward terms) are frequently discovered only after significant computational investment in training runs. Furthermore, policy evaluation often relies on stochastic rollouts, which can hinder the diagnosis of critical hardware-related behaviors, such as joint limit violations or high-frequency actuation, making it difficult to pinpoint the root cause of performance issues or safety concerns. The absence of a coherent loop connecting environment verification, scalable training, systematic evaluation, and deployment makes the entire lifecycle poorly structured and difficult to reproduce. AGILE seeks to formalize and integrate these stages to enable earlier detection of issues and more efficient iteration.

2. **Bridging the Transfer Gap**: Deploying learned policies to physical hardware or even for robust sim-to-sim validation is often a fragile process. The lack of standardized I/O contracts necessitates manual adjustments for joint order, observation history buffers, and action scaling. This ad hoc translation introduces potential sources of error ("silent bugs") and prevents the application of a unified evaluation pipeline across different simulation environments (e.g., MuJoCo for validation) before committing to real-world deployment. AGILE aims to standardize the policy export and inference process, ensuring consistency and enabling rigorous pre-validation.

To achieve these objectives, AGILE proposes an end-to-end workflow that:
- **Standardizes the policy-development lifecycle**: By providing a structured pipeline, AGILE aims to transform humanoid RL development from a collection of isolated scripts into a cohesive engineering process.
- **Mitigates common sim-to-real failure modes**: Through systematic verification, robust training enhancements, unified evaluation with hardware-critical diagnostics, and descriptor-driven deployment, the workflow is designed to reduce the occurrence of issues that prevent successful real-world deployment.
- **Improves reliability and reproducibility**: Formalizing interfaces, enabling deterministic regression testing, and providing comprehensive logging mechanisms are central to making the development process more reliable and results more reproducible.
- **Facilitates quantitative validation**: The framework introduces deployment-oriented motion diagnostics and descriptor-consistent policy export to enable quantitative pre-validation before physical trials, moving away from purely empirical trial-and-error.

In essence, AGILE's key objective is to provide an open-source, integrated solution that streamlines the entire humanoid RL development process, making it more robust, efficient, and amenable to real-world application.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

AGILE (A Generic Isaac-Lab based Engine) is implemented as a comprehensive workflow layer built upon existing GPU-accelerated simulation platforms and RL algorithms. Specifically, it leverages Isaac Lab for parallel GPU simulation and MDP primitives, and RSL-RL for reinforcement learning algorithms. The core methodology involves a four-stage, configuration-driven pipeline designed to wrap the RL training loop with tools for verification, reproducibility, evaluation, and deployment. Each task is defined by a self-contained configuration file specifying scene parameters, observations, actions, rewards, terminations, and curriculum, allowing for rapid prototyping and parameter sweeps.

The four stages of the AGILE workflow are:

1. **Prepare (Training Preparation)**: This stage focuses on interactively verifying the robot model and MDP configuration to identify potential issues before costly training. AGILE provides three composable GUI plugins:
 - **Joint Position GUI**: Allows per-joint slider control with real-time torque readouts, including a symmetry mode for identifying mirrored sign errors.
 - **Object Manipulation GUI**: Enables 6-degree-of-freedom object positioning with live contact-sensor visualization to verify reward activation for manipulation tasks.
 - **Reward Visualizer**: Displays per-term reward contributions and weights as the user manipulates the scene, confirming expected reward behavior without initiating training.

2. **Train**: This stage emphasizes scalable, reproducible training with integrated algorithmic enhancements.
 - **Training Infrastructure**: A unified entry point manages training, evaluation, and hyperparameter sweeps. Reproducibility is ensured by recording a lightweight git snapshot (commit hash, branch, uncommitted diffs) and YAML configuration dumps for every run, supported by Docker-based orchestration and Weights & Biases (W&B) logging. Structured hyperparameter sweeps are facilitated by "scaled-dict" parameters, allowing joint-parameter scaling for structured groups (e.g., PD gains).
 - **Algorithmic Toolbox**: AGILE integrates several stabilization and transfer techniques as independently toggleable modules:
 - **L2C2 Regularization**: Enforces local Lipschitz continuity on policy and value function outputs to promote smoother, hardware-safe actions by penalizing output changes for interpolated observations.
 - **Online Reward Normalization**: Normalizes rewards using a running standard deviation and a return-scale correction factor, making training invariant to reward magnitude changes during curriculum.
 - **Value-Bootstrapped Terminations**: Modifies terminal rewards by adding a scaled value function term and an outcome-specific offset ($\sigma$), making terminations value-neutral or consistently worse/better than continuing, thus removing the need for fragile, manually tuned termination penalties.
 - **Virtual Harness**: Applies external PD forces to the robot's root body during early training to stabilize it and prevent immediate collapse, gradually decaying the assistance.
 - **Velocity Profile**: Provides pluggable velocity profiles (EMA, Trapezoidal, Linear) for interpolating between current and sampled target joint positions, preventing abrupt changes that can destabilize policies, especially for upper-body joints during locomotion training.
 - **Symmetry Augmentation**: Mirrors observations and actions to encourage symmetric gaits and effectively double training data, driven by configuration rather than index-based mapping.
 - **Additional Modules**: Includes adaptive command sampling, upper-body velocity profiles, state caching for efficient resets (pre-collecting diverse initial states), and teacher-student distillation with symmetry mirror loss.
 - **Sim-to-Real Transfer Focus**: Integrates domain randomization (dynamics, mass, contact, actuator delays) and action regularization penalties (norm, rate, acceleration) and L2C2 to enhance policy smoothness and robustness for real-world deployment.

3. **Evaluate**: This stage provides a unified framework for assessing policies, combining both stochastic and deterministic testing.
 - **Unified Evaluation Pipeline**: Supports scenario-based tests (scripted command sequences, e.g., velocity sweeps) for reproducible, low-variance benchmarks, and stochastic rollouts (randomized commands) for assessing robustness.
 - **Cross-Simulator Compatibility**: This pipeline operates seamlessly in both Isaac Lab (GPU) and MuJoCo (CPU), allowing consistent sim-to-sim validation with the same scenarios and metrics.
 - **Motion-Quality Diagnostics**: Beyond aggregate task performance, the pipeline analyzes deployment-critical, per-joint metrics such as RMS acceleration, jerk, and joint-limit violations.
 - **HTML Reports**: All evaluation results are exported as interactive HTML reports for rapid diagnosis of hardware safety-critical behaviors.

4. **Deploy**: This stage focuses on robust and standardized policy export and deployment.
 - **Descriptor-Driven Export**: Trained policies are auto-exported to TorchScript alongside self-contained YAML I/O descriptors. These descriptors capture the full I/O contract (joint names, observation ordering, history buffers, action scaling).
 - **Unified Inference Pipeline**: The descriptors enable automatic reconstruction of observation assembly, action mapping, and history buffers for inference in secondary simulators (e.g., MuJoCo for sim-to-sim validation) or on real hardware.
 - **C++ Translation**: The I/O contract facilitates translating the inference stack from Python to C++ for real-time execution on hardware, with core inference logic remaining identical and only the state provider changing for hardware drivers.

AGILE's methodology reframes RL development as a structured engineering lifecycle, emphasizing configuration-driven architecture and quantitative validation at each stage.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The AGILE workflow was validated across five representative humanoid skills on two hardware platforms: the Unitree G1 and Booster T1. The tasks encompassed locomotion, recovery, motion imitation, and loco-manipulation, demonstrating consistent sim-to-real transfer. Training times for these tasks ranged from 6 to 25 hours on a single L40 GPU.

**Case Studies & Results:**

1. **Velocity Tracking (Unitree G1 and Booster T1)**:
 - A generic MDP template and training pipeline were successfully applied to both robots.
 - AGILE's symmetry augmentation was used, doubling effective training data and enforcing symmetric gaits.
 - The virtual harness module was employed for early stabilization.
 - Policies trained on procedurally generated rough terrain successfully tracked commanded linear and angular velocities.

2. **Height-Controlled Locomotion (Unitree G1)**:
 - This task extended velocity tracking to include a commanded pelvis height.
 - A "separated body control" design was implemented: the RL policy controlled only leg joints, while upper-body joints were randomized or controlled by independent IK/VLA modules, facilitating modularity.
 - The unified evaluation pipeline (Isaac Lab and MuJoCo) was demonstrated. Deterministic scenarios showed consistent tracking metrics with lower variance compared to stochastic rollouts, which exhibited higher variance but assessed robustness over longer durations.
 - The evaluation pipeline successfully compared a privileged teacher policy against distilled LSTM and history-MLP student architectures in MuJoCo, demonstrating its generalizability across policy structures (e.g., Table 3, Figure 4 showing command tracking).

3. **Stand-Up (Booster T1 & Unitree G1)**:
 - Policies were developed for fall recovery, coordinating all joints to transition from random fallen configurations to a stable standing pose.
 - Online reward normalization was critical for handling scale differences between postural rewards and sparse standing bonuses.
 - State caching, utilizing a pre-collected dataset of diverse fallen poses, prevented wasting compute on repeated drop simulations.
 - Both policies successfully transferred to real hardware.

4. **Motion Imitation (Unitree G1)**:
 - A BeyondMimic-style motion imitation task was implemented, tracking an 8-second dancing sequence with adaptive segment sampling.
 - The policy, using only hardware-available observations, deployed directly to the real Unitree G1 without distillation.
 - Successful sim-to-real transfer required additional domain randomization and L2C2 regularization to suppress high-frequency actuator oscillations.

5. **Loco-Manipulation & VLA Fine-Tuning (Unitree G1)**:
 - AGILE's modular architecture enabled freezing a trained locomotion policy as a lower-body controller while independently developing an upper-body module for pick-and-place.
 - An RL expert, trained with physics randomization, generated 100 successful trajectories, providing paired RGB observations, proprioception, and actions.
 - A GR00T N1.5 VLA model was fine-tuned on this dataset.
 - In closed-loop simulation, the VLA model achieved a 90% success rate in picking and placing objects across 100 test cases, validating effective transfer from privileged RL to perception-driven control.

**Ablation Studies:**

- **Reward Normalizer**: Demonstrated improved training stability and performance, especially under large reward scale changes (e.g., 100x scale, the normalizer recovered near-original performance).
- **L2C2**: Quantitatively reduced RMS joint acceleration, RMS joint jerk, joint limit violations, and high-frequency energy ratio, particularly under increasing observation noise. This contributed to smoother, safer actions on hardware.
- **Value-Bootstrapped Terminations**: Led to more robust training with significantly lower seed variance and a higher time-out ratio (fewer bad terminations) compared to manually tuned termination penalties, without requiring per-task tuning.
- **Virtual Harness**: Enabled faster recovery from initial negative-reward phases and convergence to higher final rewards with lower variance, preventing early episode collapse.
- **Symmetry Augmentation**: Showed modest but consistent reward improvement and enforced behavioral symmetry, primarily benefiting gait consistency.

**Sim-to-Real Transfer:** All five tasks achieved qualitative sim-to-real transfer to hardware, defined by stable execution, absence of controller instability, and task completion. The main failure modes (sim-to-real gaps and overly aggressive policies) were addressed by domain randomization and policy regularization, which were identified as effective levers. Quantitative metrics were reported via the MuJoCo pipeline for sim-to-sim validation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The AGILE workflow represents a significant contribution to the field of humanoid reinforcement learning by providing a standardized, end-to-end framework that addresses critical gaps in the current development landscape.

**Key Significance:**

- **Standardized Development Lifecycle**: AGILE reframes humanoid RL from a collection of fragmented scripts to a structured engineering lifecycle. This standardization enhances predictability, efficiency, and robustness throughout the policy development process.
- **Improved Sim-to-Real Transfer Reliability**: By systematically integrating environment verification, reproducible training, unified evaluation with motion-quality diagnostics, and descriptor-driven deployment, AGILE directly confronts common sim-to-real failure modes. This results in policies that are more likely to perform successfully and safely on physical hardware.
- **Quantitative Pre-Validation**: The unified evaluation framework, capable of operating in both Isaac Lab and MuJoCo with scenario-based tests and detailed motion diagnostics, enables rigorous, quantitative pre-validation. This allows researchers to identify and resolve deployment-critical issues, such as joint limit violations or high-frequency actuation, before committing to costly and potentially hazardous hardware trials.
- **Reproducibility and Debuggability**: Features like detailed logging, git snapshots, configuration dumps, and interactive debug GUIs significantly enhance the reproducibility of experiments and facilitate the early detection and diagnosis of problems. This reduces development time and minimizes wasted computational resources.
- **Modular and Extensible Algorithmic Toolbox**: The integration of various stabilization and transfer techniques (e.g., L2C2, reward normalization, virtual harness) as toggleable modules provides researchers with a validated toolbox, allowing them to selectively apply enhancements based on specific task requirements.
- **Enabling Complex Behaviors**: The demonstration of decoupled whole-body control and VLA fine-tuning highlights AGILE's capacity to facilitate the development of sophisticated loco-manipulation skills and integrate with perception-driven control systems. This modularity can accelerate the development of more intelligent and versatile humanoid robots.
- **Open-Source Contribution**: The release of AGILE as an open-source workflow, including pre-trained checkpoints, can foster community adoption, encourage collaborative development, and lower the barrier to entry for researchers working on humanoid robotics.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Accelerated Research and Development**: By streamlining the development process and improving reliability, AGILE can enable faster iteration cycles and accelerate progress in humanoid robotics research, particularly in areas requiring robust sim-to-real transfer.
- **Enhanced Robot Capabilities**: The ability to reliably transfer learned policies for complex tasks like loco-manipulation, recovery, and motion imitation can lead to more capable and autonomous humanoid robots in real-world applications.
- **Safer Robot Deployment**: The emphasis on motion-quality diagnostics and pre-validation contributes to safer policy deployment, reducing the risk of hardware damage or unexpected behaviors.
- **Broader Accessibility**: A standardized and well-documented workflow can make advanced humanoid RL techniques more accessible to a wider range of researchers and engineers.

**Limitations and Future Work:**

The paper acknowledges certain limitations. AGILE has been validated on two humanoid platforms (Unitree G1 and Booster T1), and broader hardware validation remains future work. The framework's dependency on Isaac Lab means it is tied to that specific API. Additionally, the tasks studied were primarily proprioceptive; future work aims to expand coverage to perception-driven manipulation and more dynamic locomotion behaviors like running or stair climbing. These limitations indicate directions for continued development to further enhance the generality and applicability of the AGILE workflow.
