# AGILE: A Comprehensive Workflow for Humanoid Loco-Manipulation Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> AGILE, a comprehensive workflow from NVIDIA, streamlines humanoid reinforcement learning development by integrating environment verification, scalable training, unified evaluation, and descriptor-driven deployment. The framework successfully demonstrated stable sim-to-real transfer for five distinct humanoid skills, including loco-manipulation, on Unitree G1 and Booster T1 robots, with training times between 6 to 25 hours per task on a single L40 GPU.

## Problem

- The 'workflow gap' in humanoid RL development refers to fragmented pipelines, leading to late discovery of basic environment issues and inefficient diagnosis due to reliance on stochastic policy rollouts.
- The 'transfer gap' highlights the fragility and labor-intensity of deploying learned policies to hardware or for external validation, often requiring manual adjustments due to a lack of standardized input/output contracts.
- Existing research often addresses specific aspects of robotic RL, but a holistic, end-to-end development lifecycle from systematic debugging to robust deployment is lacking.

## Method

- **AGILE** provides a four-stage, configuration-driven workflow (==Prepare==, ==Train==, ==Evaluate==, ==Deploy==) built upon Isaac Lab and RSL-RL, wrapping the RL training loop with tools for verification, reproducibility, evaluation, and deployment.
- The 'Prepare' stage utilizes interactive GUIs (Joint Position, Object Manipulation, Reward Visualizer) for real-time verification of robot models and ==MDP== configurations before training.
- The 'Train' stage integrates scalable infrastructure with an algorithmic toolbox, including ==L2C2 regularization==, online reward normalization, ==value-bootstrapped terminations==, and a ==virtual harness==, to enhance training stability and sim-to-real transfer.
- The 'Evaluate' stage offers a unified pipeline for scenario-based and stochastic tests, cross-simulator compatibility (Isaac Lab and MuJoCo), and motion-quality diagnostics (acceleration, jerk, joint-limit violations) with HTML reports.
- The 'Deploy' stage enables descriptor-driven policy export to ==TorchScript== with self-contained I/O descriptors, facilitating unified and automatic inference in secondary simulators or on real hardware via Python or C++.

## Results

- Achieved successful qualitative sim-to-real transfer for **5 humanoid skills** (velocity tracking, height-controlled locomotion, stand-up, motion imitation, loco-manipulation) on Unitree G1 and Booster T1 platforms, with training times of **6–25 hours** per task on a single L40 GPU.
- Ablation studies showed reward normalizer improved training stability, L2C2 reduced RMS joint acceleration and jerk, value-bootstrapped terminations lowered training variance, and the virtual harness prevented early episode collapse.
- The modular architecture enabled freezing a trained locomotion policy and fine-tuning a GR00T N1.5 VLA model for loco-manipulation, achieving a **90%** success rate in pick-and-place tasks in closed-loop simulation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A structured, configuration-driven workflow can significantly enhance efficiency, reproducibility, and robustness in humanoid RL development by enabling early problem detection and standardizing interfaces across the development lifecycle.
> - Integrating specific algorithmic enhancements and regularization techniques (e.g., L2C2 for smoother control, value-bootstrapped terminations for robust learning, virtual harness for early stabilization) is crucial for achieving successful and safe sim-to-real transfer.
> - Unified evaluation, combining deterministic scenario testing with hardware-critical motion diagnostics and cross-simulator compatibility, provides essential quantitative pre-validation, reducing reliance on costly and potentially unsafe physical trials.
> - Standardized, descriptor-driven policy export and inference pipelines are vital for bridging the 'transfer gap,' ensuring consistent policy execution across different environments and facilitating robust deployment to physical hardware.
