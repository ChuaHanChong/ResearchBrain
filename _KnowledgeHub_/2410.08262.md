# ROMAN: Open-Set Object Map Alignment for Robust View-Invariant Global Localization

> [!summary] Summary
> A global localization method, ROMAN, robustly aligns open-set object maps by integrating semantic, geometric, and gravity direction priors within a graph-theoretic framework. This approach enables stable localization despite extreme viewpoint changes, such as opposite-direction or ground-aerial traversals, and improves multi-robot SLAM by reducing trajectory errors by 35% in challenging environments.

## Problem

- Traditional global localization methods often fail with drastic changes in viewpoint, lighting, or appearance, especially in complex outdoor or unstructured environments.
- Existing segment-based or object-based methods are limited by relying on controlled settings, specific object variations, or high-fidelity LiDAR, and struggle with noisy data or ambiguous scenes.
- Inefficient map representations (dense point clouds or detailed features) pose challenges for scalable multi-robot applications due to high communication and storage demands.

## Method

- Developed **ROMAN**'s open-set object mapping pipeline that utilizes ==FastSAM== for segmentation and ==CLIP== for semantic descriptors from RGB-D data to generate sparse, view-invariant 3D object maps.
- Created a robust object data association module that extends ==graph-theoretic data association== methods by fusing open-set semantics, segment-level geometric attributes (shape, volume), and an IMU-derived ==gravity direction prior==.
- Integrated the gravity prior into the pairwise consistency score to effectively decouple horizontal and vertical consistency checks, reducing the solution space for alignment from 6-DOF to 4-DOF.

## Results

- ROMAN achieved a **75%** higher success rate in opposite-direction localization scenarios compared to other segment-based methods and demonstrated **31%** better mean success than image-based methods like MASt3R.
- When integrated into multi-robot SLAM, ROMAN reduced trajectory estimation error by **35%** in challenging loop closure scenarios compared to existing methods, showing robust performance gains.
- Demonstrated robust cross-view localization between ground and aerial maps with a **0.60** recall, indicating its ability to handle drastic viewpoint changes effectively.
- The system operates efficiently with submap packet sizes under **250 KB** and an alignment runtime of **28.9 ms**, supporting real-time and bandwidth-constrained multi-robot applications.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Fusing open-set semantic and geometric features with an IMU-derived gravity prior significantly enhances the robustness and accuracy of graph-theoretic object data association, particularly under extreme viewpoint changes.
> - Utilizing vision-language foundation models (FastSAM, CLIP) enables the creation of lightweight, open-set, and view-invariant 3D object maps from RGB-D input, removing the need for domain-specific object detectors.
> - A geometric mean fusion strategy effectively combines individual segment similarities and pairwise consistency scores, improving alignment success rates by correctly merging scores of potentially different scales.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Global localization is a fundamental capability for autonomous robots, enabling long-term, drift-free navigation, particularly in environments where Global Positioning System (GPS) signals are unavailable or unreliable. Traditional global localization methods often rely on visual features or point cloud registration, which can struggle significantly when faced with drastic changes in viewpoint, lighting conditions, or appearance. This vulnerability is especially pronounced in complex outdoor environments, unstructured settings, or when operating with different sensor modalities or platforms (e.g., ground vs. aerial).

Recent research has explored object- or segment-level representations as a more robust alternative to traditional feature-based approaches. These representations offer greater stability against sensor noise and environmental variations, and are often more lightweight for transmission in multi-robot systems. Prior segment-based methods, however, have primarily been demonstrated in controlled indoor environments, with limited object variations, or have relied on high-fidelity Lidar sensing. Early approaches to object-level localization often employed Random Sample Consensus (RANSAC)-based geometric verification, which can become computationally intractable in scenarios with a high proportion of outliers.

More recently, graph-theoretic approaches, particularly those using consistency graphs, have emerged as a robust solution for the data association problem at the heart of object-level localization. These methods formulate the problem as finding densely connected subsets of nodes in a graph, where nodes represent putative object correspondences and edges encode their geometric consistencies.

This work, ROMAN, aims to advance the state of object-level global localization by addressing key limitations of existing methods. It specifically targets challenging domains characterized by unseen environments, noisy segmentations (from RGB-D only sensing), and extreme viewpoint changes, including situations where maps are generated by robots traversing in opposite directions. ROMAN extends existing graph-theoretic data association techniques by integrating richer information, specifically open-set semantics, segment-level geometric attributes, and an inertial sensor-derived gravity direction prior, to enhance robustness and accuracy in these difficult scenarios. This positions ROMAN as an effort to bridge the gap between robust theoretical data association frameworks and practical application in diverse and unconstrained real-world environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a global localization method that is robust and accurate in challenging, diverse, and previously unseen environments, even when faced with significant viewpoint differences. The authors identify several key limitations of existing global localization approaches that motivate the development of ROMAN:

1. **Failure under Viewpoint Changes:** Current global localization methods, particularly those based on visual features, often fail to relocalize when the robot's current view differs significantly from the reference map. This includes scenarios where mapping and localization occur from opposite directions or drastically different perspectives (e.g., ground-level versus aerial views).
2. **Sensitivity to Environmental Variability:** Many existing segment-based or object-based methods have primarily been validated in structured indoor settings or environments with limited object diversity. Their performance degrades in unstructured outdoor, urban, or forested environments where objects are less distinct, segmentation is noisier, and visual appearances are highly variable.
3. **Reliance on High-Fidelity Sensors or Prior Knowledge:** Some robust localization solutions rely on accurate Lidar sensing or require pre-trained, domain-specific object detectors. The motivation for ROMAN is to achieve robust localization using only RGB-D camera input and open-set object representations, without making strong assumptions about the environment or requiring extensive prior training data for specific object classes.
4. **Limitations of Existing Data Association Methods:** While graph-theoretic data association has shown promise, its foundational implementations often rely solely on geometric consistency (e.g., distances between object centroids). This can be insufficient in ambiguous scenes with repetitive structures, few distinct objects, or when object centroids are noisy due to partial observations. RANSAC-based methods, while general, face intractability under high outlier rates.
5. **Inefficient Map Representations:** For large-scale or multi-robot applications, efficient communication and storage of map information are crucial. Representing environments with dense point clouds or detailed feature sets can be resource-intensive. The authors seek a compact, view-invariant object-level map representation.

To address these motivations, ROMAN aims to:
- Develop a novel graph-theoretic data association formulation that integrates open-set semantic information, segment-level geometric attributes (shape, volume), and a gravity direction prior. This unified approach is intended to implicitly guide the solver to correct associations even when geometric information alone is ambiguous.
- Create a pipeline for generating open-set 3D segment maps from a single RGB-D camera. This mapping process leverages foundation models (FastSAM for segmentation, CLIP for semantic descriptors) to produce sparse, view-invariant, and information-rich object representations.
- Demonstrate that this approach enables global localization in highly challenging scenarios, such as when robots traverse paths in opposite directions or when localizing across ground-aerial viewpoints.
- Evaluate the performance against state-of-the-art visual feature-based and segment-based methods, particularly in terms of pose estimation accuracy and its impact as a loop closure module in multi-robot SLAM.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ROMAN operates through two main interconnected modules: an **Open-Set Object Mapping** pipeline and a **Robust Object Data Association** module.

#### 3.1. Open-Set Object Mapping

The mapping pipeline generates abstract, object-level representations of the environment from RGB-D images and robot pose estimates (e.g., from a Visual-Inertial Odometry system like Kimera-VIO).

1. **Per-Image Object Observations:**
 - **Segmentation:** FastSAM, an open-set image segmentation model, is applied to color images to extract initial object proposals.
 - **Filtering:** To ensure mapping of distinct and stationary objects, several preprocessing steps are applied:
 - YOLO-V7 is used to detect and remove segments corresponding to people.
 - Segments are projected into 3D using depth information, and large planar segments (e.g., ground, non-distinct walls) are removed as they are not well-represented as discrete objects.
 - **Semantic Descriptors:** For each remaining 3D segment, CLIP, a vision-language foundation model, computes a semantic feature descriptor, providing open-set semantic information.

2. **Data Association and Tracking (Aggregation):**
 - **Frame-to-Frame Association:** Incoming 3D object observations are associated with existing 3D segment tracks. This is achieved by computing a grid-aligned voxel-based Intersection over Union (IOU) to quantify 3D voxel overlap between observations and tracks.
 - **Assignment:** A global nearest neighbor approach assigns observations to existing tracks. Unassociated observations initiate new tracks.
 - **Semantic Merging:** Semantic descriptors of associated segments are merged using a weighted average.
 - **Object Merging:** A mechanism addresses inconsistent segmentations by FastSAM (where one physical object might be segmented differently across views). 3D segments are merged if they have high grid-aligned voxel IOU or a high 2D IOU when projected onto the image plane.
 - **Abstract Representation:** While mapping, objects are represented by dense voxels. For global localization, this is abstracted to a low-data representation comprising a 3D centroid, a four-dimensional shape attribute vector, and a mean semantic embedding. This ensures efficient communication and storage.

3. **Submap Creation:**
 - Submaps are periodically created as the robot moves. A new submap is triggered when the robot's odometry estimate exceeds a threshold distance (`cd`) from the previous submap's center.
 - The submap is centered at the current robot's pose, with pitch and roll components removed using an IMU's gravity estimate, ensuring a gravity-aligned frame.
 - Objects within a radius `r` of the submap center are added until the robot moves beyond `r`.
 - A maximum submap size `N` limits computation by removing objects farthest from the center if `N` is exceeded.
 - The created submap is then used for global data association. Localization is reported if the number of associated objects exceeds a threshold `τ`.

#### 3.2. Robust Object Data Association

This module aligns two object submaps by finding correspondences between their constituent objects.

1. **Problem Formulation:** Global localization is formulated as estimating a 6-Degrees-of-Freedom (DOF) transformation (`ˆTij`) between the two local frames `Fi` and `Fj` of the submaps `Mi` and `Mj`. The object associations are used to compute the transformation `ˆTMiMj` via Arun's method. The final relative pose is then `ˆTij = TiMi ˆTMiMj (TjMj)^-1`.

2. **Consistency Graph (Building upon CLIPPER):**
 - **Nodes:** Each node `ap = (pi, pj)` represents a putative association between a segment `pi` from `Mi` and `pj` from `Mj`.
 - **Edges:** An edge is formed between two putative associations `ap` and `aq` if they are geometrically consistent. Consistency is defined if the difference in distances between their centroids within their respective maps, `d(ap, aq) = | ||c(pi) - c(qi)|| - ||c(pj) - c(qj)|| |`, is below a threshold `ϵ`.
 - **Edge Weight:** The weight `sa(ap, aq)` quantifies this consistency using a Gaussian kernel: `exp(-1/2 * d(ap,aq)^2 / σ^2)`.

3. **Improving Affinity Metrics:**
 ROMAN enhances the original affinity matrix `M` in the consistency graph, which in its basic form only considers centroid distances, by incorporating richer information.
 - **Fusion Strategy:** Instead of simple multiplication or diagonal insertion, ROMAN fuses individual segment similarity scores (`so(·)`) with pairwise consistency scores (`sa(·,·)`) using the geometric mean: `M_p,q = GM(sa(ap, aq), so(ap), so(aq))`. This approach is selected based on its properties for correctly merging scores of potentially different scales [53–55].
 - **Segment-to-Segment Similarity (`so(ap)`):** This score is a geometric mean of semantic and shape similarities: `so(ap) = GM(sshape(ap), ssemantic(ap))`.
 - **Semantic Similarity (`ssemantic(ap)`):** Calculated as the cosine similarity of the CLIP descriptors of `pi` and `pj`. This score is then linearly rescaled from a predefined range `[ϕmin, ϕmax]` to `[0, 1]` to increase its discriminative power.
 - **Shape Similarity (`sshape(ap)`):** Computed as the geometric mean of element-wise ratios of four-dimensional shape attributes (`f(p)`) derived from PCA of the 3D points of each segment. These attributes include bounding box volume, linearity, planarity, and scattering.
 - **Gravity Prior (`sgravity(·, ·)`) for Pairwise Score `sa(·, ·)`:** To guide the solver towards gravity-aligned transformations, the pairwise consistency score `sa(·,·)` is redesigned to `sgravity(·, ·)`. This new score decouples horizontal (x-y plane) and vertical (z-axis) consistency checks. It penalizes pairs of associations where the vertical distance difference between objects within the same submap is inconsistent, while still considering horizontal distance consistency. This leverages the readily available gravity vector information from IMUs to reduce the solution space from 6-DOF to 4-DOF (x, y, z, yaw).

4. **Inlier Association Solver:** The affinity matrix `M` (incorporating all improved metrics) is then used by CLIPPER's solver to find the densest subset of consistent associations, formulated as an optimization problem.

#### 3.3. Integration into SLAM

ROMAN is integrated as a loop closure detection module into a pose-graph SLAM system. Kimera-VIO provides front-end odometry. New submaps are registered against existing ones, and detected loop closures (with sufficient associations) are fed, along with sparsified VIO odometry, into the robust pose graph optimization backend of Kimera-Multi to estimate multi-robot trajectories.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The authors conducted extensive experiments across diverse real-world datasets, including urban, off-road/forested, and ground-aerial scenarios. The evaluations focused on place recognition, submap alignment (relative pose estimation), and the impact of ROMAN as a loop closure module in full SLAM systems.

1. **MIT Campus Global Localization (Table II):**
 - **Place Recognition:** ROMAN (0.552 AUC) demonstrated superior performance over all other segment-based baselines. Larger variants, ROMAN-L (0.663 AUC) and ROMAN-XL (0.654 AUC), showed further improvements.
 - **Pose Estimation Success Rate (≤ 5 deg, 1 m error):**
 - ROMAN achieved a mean success rate of 0.287, outperforming all other segment-based methods (e.g., CLIPPER/Prune: 0.215, TEASER++/Prune: 0.216).
 - In particularly challenging opposite-direction scenarios (120-180 deg relative heading), ROMAN's success rate (0.189) was 75% higher than the next-best segment-based method (CLIPPER/Prune: 0.108).
 - Compared to image-based methods, ROMAN-XL (0.536 mean success) outperformed MASt3R (GT Scale) (0.408 mean success) in most categories, particularly showing a 45% better success rate in opposite directions and 31% better mean success.
 - **Runtime:** ROMAN operated efficiently (28.9 ms), being faster than several image-based methods and comparable to other efficient segment-based approaches like CLIPPER/Prune. Even the larger ROMAN-XL variant (213 ms) was significantly faster than MASt3R (2950 ms).
 - **Data Efficiency:** Submap packet size was less than 250 KB for N=40 objects, with an entire map for a 1 km trajectory requiring less than 25 MB.

2. **Loop Closures in Visual SLAM (Table III):**
 - **Single-Robot Tunnel Runs (Easy):** ROMAN loop closures resulted in trajectory errors (4.08m ATE mean) comparable to Kimera-Multi (4.10m) and significantly better than ORB-SLAM3 (13.60m).
 - **Full Multi-Robot Datasets (Medium):** ROMAN showed moderate gains over Kimera-Multi (6.03m ATE mean vs. 6.53m) in large-scale multi-robot scenarios.
 - **Challenging Multi-Robot Combinations (Difficult):** In scenarios with difficult visual loop closures (e.g., perpendicular path crossings, high visual aliasing), ROMAN demonstrated significant improvements, achieving a 35% reduction in trajectory estimation error compared to Kimera-Multi (5.79m ATE mean vs. 8.86m). This indicates that ROMAN's advantages become more pronounced as the difficulty of loop closure scenarios increases.

3. **Loop Closures in Off-Road Environment (Fig. 4):**
 - Qualitative evaluation in a visually ambiguous off-road environment demonstrated ROMAN's ability to successfully detect loop closures in easy, medium, and difficult cases, including opposite-direction traversals where Kimera-Multi failed to find any loop closures.

4. **Ground-Aerial Cross-View Localization (Table IV):**
 - ROMAN achieved a recall of 0.60 in cross-view localization experiments between ground and aerial maps, maintaining an advantage over other baselines and demonstrating robustness to drastic viewpoint changes.

5. **Ablation Studies (Table V):**
 - **Fusion Methods:** The geometric mean method for fusing similarity scores was shown to be superior for alignment success rate compared to product, arithmetic mean, or diagonal-only fusion.
 - **Affinity Component Contributions:** The gravity prior was the most significant contributor to alignment success. Semantic similarity provided the largest improvement for place recognition, while shape similarity also offered positive contributions to alignment.
 - **Robustness to Segmentation Errors and Dynamic Objects:** ROMAN exhibited robustness to over-segmentation (only 12% performance drop) and maintained better-than-baseline performance even with severe under-segmentation or when dynamic object filtering was disabled.
 - **Hyperparameter Sensitivity:** The study showed that submap size parameters (N, r) could be adjusted to trade off alignment success and runtime, and the method maintained robustness across variations in segment noise parameters (σ, ϵ).
 - **Scalability (Computational):** The mapping pipeline ran at 9.6 Hz with CLIP embeddings and 17.9 Hz without. Removing CLIP embeddings resulted in only a 12% drop in alignment success but reduced map size by 100 times, offering a trade-off for compute-constrained platforms.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ROMAN addresses a core challenge in robotics: achieving robust and accurate global localization in highly variable and challenging real-world environments. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key contributions:

1. **Enhanced Robustness to Viewpoint and Environmental Changes:** By explicitly incorporating a gravity prior and rich metric-semantic object attributes (shape, open-set semantics) into a graph-theoretic data association framework, ROMAN demonstrates superior performance in scenarios with extreme viewpoint differences, such as opposite-direction traversals and ground-aerial cross-view localization. This capability is critical for long-term robot autonomy and persistent operations in dynamic or expansive environments, reducing reliance on consistent visual conditions.
2. **Advancement in Object-Level Localization:** The work pushes the boundaries of object-level localization by effectively utilizing open-set segmentation and vision-language models (FastSAM, CLIP) to create view-invariant and information-rich object maps. This eliminates the need for domain-specific object detectors and allows for generalization to novel environments. The method for merging and abstracting object observations into compact representations contributes to practical map management.
3. **Improved Multi-Robot SLAM Performance:** ROMAN's integration as a loop closure module significantly improves trajectory estimation accuracy in multi-robot Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) systems, particularly in challenging scenarios where traditional visual feature-based methods often fail. This directly translates to more reliable and drift-free collaborative mapping and navigation for robot teams. The demonstrated 35% improvement in trajectory error for difficult sequences underscores its practical value.
4. **Practicality and Efficiency:** The use of lightweight, abstracted object maps ensures efficient communication and storage, which is vital for scalable multi-robot systems. Furthermore, ROMAN achieves competitive runtime performance compared to state-of-the-art methods, suggesting its suitability for real-time applications even on mobile platforms. The identified trade-offs between accuracy, runtime, and map size (e.g., by adjusting CLIP usage) provide flexibility for different deployment constraints.
5. **Foundation for Future Work:** The paper highlights limitations, such as the current exclusion of non-object segments (e.g., roads, walls) and the potential for increased scalability with faster place recognition stages. These directly point to promising avenues for future research, suggesting that ROMAN could serve as a foundational component for more comprehensive scene understanding and localization systems that integrate both object and non-object structural information.

Overall, ROMAN represents a substantial step towards truly robust and adaptable global localization for autonomous systems, particularly those operating in dynamic, unstructured, and visually ambiguous environments with varied sensor platforms.
