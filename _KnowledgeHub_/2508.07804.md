# Pose-RFT: Enhancing MLLMs for 3D Pose Generation via Hybrid Action Reinforcement Fine-Tuning

> [!summary] Summary
> Pose-RFT introduces a reinforcement fine-tuning framework for Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), optimizing 3D human pose generation from images and text using a novel hybrid action reinforcement learning algorithm, HyGRPO. The framework achieved improved Mean Per Joint Position Error (MPJPE) on 3DPW and Human3.6M datasets and higher Recall@K scores on PoseScript-H2, outperforming previous MLLM-based methods.

## Problem

- Existing pose-specific MLLMs, relying on supervised training, struggle to model the inherent ambiguity in 3D pose generation from single 2D images or subjective natural language descriptions.
- Standard supervised losses (e.g., MSE) fail to adequately capture task-specific spatial and semantic alignment, often generating semantically distinct poses with similar reconstruction errors.
- Traditional reinforcement fine-tuning (RFT) algorithms are designed for discrete token spaces, making them unsuitable for directly optimizing continuous 3D pose parameters.

## Method

- **Pose-RFT** frames 3D pose generation as a ==hybrid action== reinforcement learning problem, concurrently optimizing discrete textual responses and continuous 3D pose parameters.
- A novel hybrid reinforcement learning algorithm, HyGRPO, is introduced to perform group-wise reward normalization and jointly optimize both discrete and continuous actions through a PPO-style objective.
- Task-specific reward functions are employed, including joint location reward for image-to-pose accuracy, semantic alignment reward for text-to-pose consistency, and additional rewards for format adherence and text quality.

## Results

- Pose-RFT reduced MPJPE to **85.9** and PA-MPJPE to **51.6** on the 3DPW dataset for image-to-pose generation, outperforming UniPose (**94.7** MPJPE, **59.1** PA-MPJPE).
- The framework achieved superior text-to-pose retrieval performance on the PoseScript-H2 dataset, with RT2P_{T2P}T2P of **42.2**@5, **53.0**@**10**, **65.5**@**20** and RP2T_{P2T}P2T of **45.3**@5, **57.2**@**10**, **70.4**@**20**, surpassing previous MLLM methods.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the pose-aware encoder enhances image-to-pose performance and that HyGRPO consistently improves both image-to-pose (joint location reward) and text-to-pose (semantic alignment reward) quality during training.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Jointly optimizing discrete (text) and continuous (3D pose) outputs using a hybrid reinforcement learning approach effectively enhances MLLMs' ability to generate robust and semantically aligned 3D human poses.
> - Verifiable, task-specific reward functions are critical for guiding policy optimization, particularly the semantic alignment reward which significantly improves text-to-pose generation beyond reconstruction-focused metrics.
> - Incorporating a pose-aware encoder, specifically a pretrained Vision Transformer, provides crucial fine-grained pose-relevant features, leading to notable improvements in image-to-pose spatial accuracy.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of 3D human pose generation from multimodal inputs, such as images and text, has seen increasing attention, particularly with the emergence of pose-specific Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). These models aim to leverage the reasoning capabilities of general-purpose language models and extend them with specialized pose decoders to integrate language, vision, and 3D pose understanding. MLLMs like ChatPose and UniPose have demonstrated capabilities in both image-to-pose and text-to-pose generation.

However, existing pose-specific MLLMs are typically trained using supervised objectives, such as SMPL parameter regression or token-level prediction. While these methods enforce consistency with annotated data, the paper identifies limitations in their ability to capture the inherent ambiguity present in 3D pose generation and achieve optimal task-specific spatial and semantic alignment. For instance, in image-to-pose tasks, depth and perspective limitations in 2D images can correspond to multiple plausible 3D poses. In text-to-pose generation, the subjective nature of natural language can describe a broad distribution of valid poses. Furthermore, standard supervised losses, such as Mean Squared Error (MSE), may not adequately differentiate between semantically distinct poses that yield similar reconstruction errors.

Reinforcement learning (RL) and reinforcement fine-tuning (RFT) have been employed in various domains, particularly for aligning large language models with desired behaviors and human preferences beyond explicit labels. While RFT algorithms have shown promise in language-level alignment by operating in discrete token spaces, their application to tasks requiring fine-grained, continuous outputs, such as 3D human pose generation, has remained underexplored. This is due to the fundamental difference between discrete token prediction and continuous regression.

This work addresses this gap by introducing a reinforcement fine-tuning framework that explicitly handles hybrid action spaces comprising both discrete language tokens and continuous 3D pose parameters. By proposing a novel hybrid RL algorithm and task-specific reward functions, the paper positions itself at the intersection of multimodal large language models and reinforcement learning, aiming to advance the state of the art in robust and semantically aligned 3D human pose generation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the limitations observed in current pose-specific MLLMs for 3D human pose generation. Despite their ability to reason over language, vision, and 3D pose, these models predominantly rely on supervised training objectives. The paper highlights two key shortcomings of this approach:

1. **Inability to Model Inherent Ambiguity**:
 - **Image-to-Pose**: A single 2D image often corresponds to multiple plausible 3D poses due to depth ambiguities and perspective effects. Supervised learning struggles to capture this multi-modal distribution of possible poses effectively, often converging to a single "best" fit rather than representing the range of valid interpretations.
 - **Text-to-Pose**: Natural language descriptions are inherently vague and subjective. A given textual prompt can describe a wide array of slightly different, yet semantically correct, 3D poses. Supervised methods often struggle to generate diverse and contextually appropriate poses under such conditions.

2. **Insufficient Task-Specific Alignment**:
 - Standard supervised losses, such as mean squared error (MSE) for SMPL parameters, primarily focus on reconstruction accuracy. However, a low MSE loss does not necessarily guarantee semantic correctness or naturalness from a human perspective. As illustrated in the paper (Fig. 1), two distinct 3D poses might yield comparable MSE losses but exhibit clear semantic differences. This suggests that existing loss functions are inadequate for guiding the model towards outputs that are both spatially accurate and semantically consistent with the input modality.

These challenges motivate the adoption of reinforcement learning (RL). RL offers a framework to optimize models using reward signals that can directly evaluate task-specific alignment and subjective quality, moving beyond the constraints of fixed supervised labels. However, a significant challenge arises because most reinforcement fine-tuning (RFT) algorithms are designed for discrete action spaces (e.g., generating text tokens) and are ill-suited for the continuous output space of 3D human poses.

Against this background, the **key objectives** of this work are:

1. **To propose Pose-RFT**: A reinforcement fine-tuning framework specifically tailored to enhance 3D human pose generation in MLLMs by addressing the limitations of supervised training and the challenges of continuous action spaces in RL.
2. **To formulate 3D pose generation as a hybrid action reinforcement learning problem**: This involves simultaneously optimizing discrete language prediction (text tokens) and continuous pose generation (3D pose parameters).
3. **To develop a novel hybrid reinforcement learning algorithm, HyGRPO**: This algorithm is designed to perform group-wise reward normalization over sampled responses, enabling the joint optimization of both discrete and continuous actions within a unified framework.
4. **To incorporate verifiable, task-specific reward functions**: These functions are intended to guide the policy optimization towards explicit spatial alignment in image-to-pose generation and semantic consistency in text-to-pose generation, alongside ensuring format correctness and general text quality.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed **Pose-RFT** framework addresses the challenge of 3D human pose generation in MLLMs by formulating it as a hybrid action reinforcement learning problem. This involves concurrently generating discrete textual responses and continuous 3D pose parameters.

#### 3.1. Reformulating 3D Pose Generation for Reinforcement Learning

- **Hybrid Action Space Policy**: The core idea is to define a policy $\pi_\theta(a, p|q)$ that operates over a hybrid action space, where $q$ is a multimodal input (image or text), $a$ represents discrete textual responses, and $p$ denotes continuous 3D pose parameters.
- The policy is factorized into a discrete sub-policy $\pi_\theta(a|q)$ for textual responses and a continuous sub-policy $\pi_\theta(p|q, a)$ for 3D poses, conditioned on both the input query and the generated language.
- **Continuous Policy Parameterization**: The continuous policy $\pi_\theta(p|q, a)$ is modeled as a multivariate Gaussian distribution, $N(p; \mu_\theta(q, a), \Sigma_\theta(q, a))$. The mean ($\mu$) and covariance ($\Sigma$) are predicted by a continuous pose head, which is conditioned on the multimodal input $q$ and the discrete response $a$. This probabilistic approach enables modeling the inherent uncertainty in 3D pose generation and supports stochastic sampling during training as well as gradient-based optimization.
- **Shared Embedding Space**: Both discrete and continuous policies are built upon a shared, language-aligned multimodal embedding space, leveraging the cross-modal alignment established during MLLM pretraining.
- **Pose-Aware Encoder**: To improve pose understanding, the MLLM is augmented with a pose-aware encoder module. This module incorporates a pose-specific Vision Transformer, pretrained on human pose estimation tasks, to extract high-resolution, pose-sensitive features. These features are then fused with the language-aligned multimodal embeddings. The fusion strategy involves projecting features from both the CLIP visual encoder and the pose-aware encoder individually using separate linear layers, then combining them at the token level, to maintain the distinct representational strengths of each encoder.
- During optimization, each policy head receives its corresponding reward signal, while the shared backbone integrates gradients from both, enabling end-to-end training.

#### 3.2. HyGRPO: Hybrid Action Space Group Relative Policy Optimization

- **Algorithm**: Pose-RFT introduces HyGRPO, an online hybrid reinforcement learning algorithm based on GRPO. It optimizes policies directly over the hybrid action space.
- **Optimization Objective**: For each training sample, the MLLM generates a group of $G$ hybrid responses (text + pose). Rewards are computed for each candidate, and their scores are normalized within the group. The policy is then updated to favor responses with higher rewards.
- The objective function (Eq. 6) decomposes the advantage signal into discrete ($\hat{F}$) and continuous ($\hat{\Delta}$) components. It applies PPO-style clipped importance sampling to both components for training stability and includes a KL regularization term to prevent large policy shifts.
- **Advantage Calculation**: Group relative advantage estimation is used, where advantages for both discrete and continuous actions are computed by comparing a candidate's reward to the mean and standard deviation of rewards within the sampled group. This approach helps stabilize training.

#### 3.3. Task-Specific Reward Functions

To guide policy optimization effectively, HyGRPO incorporates four verifiable, task-specific reward functions:

1. **Joint Location Reward (for Image-to-Pose Generation)**: For image-to-pose tasks, the reward ($R_{joint}$) is defined as the inverse of the mean joint position error ($1/||J_{pred} - J_{gt}||_2$). This metric encourages spatial accuracy, assigning higher rewards to poses that align more closely with the ground-truth 3D joint locations.
2. **Semantic Alignment Reward (for Text-to-Pose Generation)**: For text-to-pose tasks, the reward ($R_{semantic}$) quantifies the semantic consistency between the textual description and the generated pose. It uses a pretrained text-pose retrieval model (comprising a text encoder $\phi_t$ and a pose encoder $\phi_p$) to compute the cosine similarity between the embedded text query and the embedded generated pose: $cos(\phi_t(q), \phi_p(p))$.
3. **Format Reward**: To ensure responses adhere to a specified structure (e.g., "The SMPL pose of this person is <POSE>."), a binary format reward ($R_{format}$) is applied. It is 1 if the output matches the expected format via regular expression matching, and 0 otherwise.
4. **Text Embedding Similarity Reward**: To maintain general question-answering capabilities during fine-tuning, a text reward ($R_{text}$) is incorporated. It measures the cosine similarity between the dense embeddings of the model-generated text answer and the ground-truth text response, using a BGE-M3 encoder.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

- **Datasets**: Training uses diverse data sources: PoseScript (Text-Pose), Human3.6M, MPI-INF-3DHP, COCO, MPII (Image-Pose), BEDLAM-Script (Image-Text), and LLaVA-Instruct-150k (VQA). Evaluation utilizes 3DPW and Human3.6M for image-to-pose, and PoseScript-H2 for text-to-pose.
- **Metrics**:
 - Image-to-Pose: Mean Per Joint Position Error (MPJPE) and Procrustes-aligned MPJPE (PA-MPJPE).
 - Text-to-Pose: Recall@K (K=5, 10, 20) for text-to-pose (R$_{T2P}$) and pose-to-text (R$_{P2T}$) retrieval.
- **Implementation**: LLaVA-1.5V-7B serves as the vision-language backbone. A pretrained Vision Transformer is used for the pose-aware encoder. CLIP and the pose-aware encoder are kept frozen. The projector, task head, and the large language model (via LoRA) are fine-tuned.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate the effectiveness of the Pose-RFT framework in enhancing MLLMs for 3D human pose generation.

#### 4.1. Comparison on Human Pose Generation Tasks

- **Image-to-Pose Generation (Table 1)**: Pose-RFT achieves lower MPJPE and PA-MPJPE scores on both the 3DPW and Human3.6M datasets compared to other MLLM-based approaches (ChatPose, UniPose). For example, on 3DPW, Pose-RFT records 85.9 MPJPE and 51.6 PA-MPJPE, outperforming UniPose (94.7 MPJPE, 59.1 PA-MPJPE). While traditional task-specific models sometimes achieve lower errors, Pose-RFT significantly narrows this gap for MLLM-based systems. The improvement is attributed to the integration of the pose-aware encoder, which contributes more comprehensive pose-relevant information.
- **Text-to-Pose Generation (Table 2)**: Pose-RFT demonstrates superior performance across most Recall@K retrieval metrics on the PoseScript-H2 dataset under both "Full Retrieval" and "Random Sampling" protocols. For instance, in Full Retrieval, Pose-RFT achieves R$_{T2P}$ of 42.2@5, 53.0@10, 65.5@20, and R$_{P2T}$ of 45.3@5, 57.2@10, 70.4@20, outperforming UniPose and ChatPose. This improvement is primarily attributed to the reinforcement fine-tuning process, guided by the semantic alignment reward, which refines the model's ability to capture fine-grained text-pose correspondences.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies and Discussions

- **Pose-Aware Encoder (Fig. 4)**: The ablation study confirms the benefit of the pose-aware encoder for image-to-pose generation. Models incorporating this encoder achieve a higher joint location reward score on the 3DPW dataset compared to a baseline relying solely on the CLIP encoder. This indicates that the pose-specific Vision Transformer, combined with token-level feature fusion, more effectively captures fine-grained pose information. However, the pose-aware encoder showed minimal benefit for the text-to-pose semantic reward, suggesting that additional visual tokens may be less aligned with textual input for cross-modal understanding in this specific context.
- **GRPO vs. HyGRPO (Fig. 3)**: A comparison between GRPO (designed for discrete actions) and the proposed HyGRPO (for hybrid actions) shows that HyGRPO yields consistent improvements in both joint location reward (image-to-pose) and semantic alignment reward (text-to-pose) during training. GRPO, which optimizes only discrete token prediction, showed negligible improvement in 3D pose generation quality. This result validates HyGRPO's efficacy in jointly optimizing discrete and continuous outputs under task-specific reward guidance.
- **Reinforcement Fine-tuning (Fig. 4)**: Reinforcement fine-tuning using HyGRPO consistently improves both text-to-pose and image-to-pose generation performance. Notably, the text-to-pose task exhibited more substantial gains, which the authors attribute to the strong joint modeling capability of the pretrained text-pose retrieval model used for reward computation. This demonstrates that RL effectively enhances the alignment between multimodal inputs and 3D pose representations.
- **Distributional Modeling in Pose Generation (Table 3)**: An ablation study on modeling 3D pose generation as a probabilistic distribution (multivariate Gaussian policy) versus deterministic regression revealed that while the probabilistic model performed slightly worse without RFT, it achieved significantly better performance when combined with reinforcement signals. This suggests that distributional modeling facilitates more effective reward-driven learning, allowing the model to better integrate semantic and spatial feedback.

#### 4.3. Qualitative Results (Fig. 5)

Qualitative examples of 3D poses generated from text prompts illustrate a progressive improvement in semantic consistency and structural plausibility as reinforcement fine-tuning advances. Poses sampled at later training steps demonstrate better alignment with the textual descriptions, visually confirming the quantitative findings.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "Pose-RFT" contributes to the fields of multimodal large language models and 3D human pose generation through several advancements:

1. **Novel Framework for Hybrid Action Reinforcement Fine-Tuning**: Pose-RFT introduces the first reinforcement fine-tuning framework specifically designed for 3D human pose generation within MLLMs. This addresses a critical gap in existing MLLM-based methods that primarily rely on supervised learning and discrete action spaces.
2. **Introduction of HyGRPO Algorithm**: The development of HyGRPO provides a methodological contribution by enabling the joint optimization of discrete language outputs and continuous 3D pose parameters. This algorithm’s ability to handle hybrid action spaces, coupled with group-wise reward normalization, offers a stable and effective approach for training models in complex multimodal, multi-output scenarios.
3. **Enhanced Performance and Robustness**: The experimental results consistently demonstrate that Pose-RFT significantly improves performance over prior pose-specific MLLMs across both image-to-pose and text-to-pose generation tasks. This suggests a more robust and semantically aligned generation capability, particularly for ambiguous inputs where supervised objectives fall short.
4. **Effective Task-Specific Reward Design**: The proposed set of verifiable, task-specific reward functions (joint location, semantic alignment, format, and text embedding similarity) provides a blueprint for guiding policy optimization in complex, multi-modal tasks. These rewards effectively translate high-level spatial and semantic objectives into trainable signals.
5. **Integration of Pose-Aware Features**: The incorporation of a pose-aware encoder, a pretrained Vision Transformer, effectively enhances the model’s ability to extract fine-grained pose-relevant information from images, leading to improved spatial accuracy in image-to-pose tasks.
6. **Broader Applicability of Hybrid RL**: The principles established by HyGRPO could be extended to other domains that require simultaneous generation or control over discrete symbolic actions and continuous physical parameters, such as robotics, character animation, or complex human-computer interaction.

**Potential Impact**:
The ability to generate accurate and semantically consistent 3D human poses from diverse multimodal inputs has implications for several applications:

- **Virtual Reality and Augmented Reality**: Creation of more realistic and interactive avatars and characters that respond naturally to textual commands or visual cues.
- **Robotics and Human-Robot Interaction**: Enabling robots to better understand and replicate human actions or poses from instructions or observations, leading to more intuitive control.
- **Content Creation and Animation**: Automating the process of generating complex 3D character animations directly from scripts, descriptions, or reference images, reducing manual effort.
- **Healthcare and Sports Analysis**: Assisting in the analysis of human movement and posture from various inputs, potentially aiding in diagnostics, rehabilitation, or performance enhancement.

**Limitations**: The paper acknowledges that the effectiveness of the method is constrained by the quality of the designed reward functions, and that designing semantically meaningful rewards for nuanced human preferences remains challenging. Additionally, the reliance on sampling multiple candidate responses for group-wise reward normalization introduces computational overhead, which may limit scalability.
