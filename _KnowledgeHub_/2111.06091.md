# A Survey of Visual Transformers

> [!summary] Summary
> This survey paper provides a comprehensive review of over one hundred Visual Transformers, organizing them into a systematic taxonomy based on tasks and data types. It synthesizes key design principles, evaluates comparative performance across various configurations, and identifies promising future research directions in computer vision.

## Problem

- The rapid and extensive proliferation of hundreds of Transformer-based models in computer vision led to a fragmented and unorganized research landscape, making it challenging to track progress and understand underlying principles.
- A lack of systematic organization and in-depth comparative analysis made it difficult for researchers to grasp the state-of-the-art, identify effective design patterns, and understand the relationship between Visual Transformers and traditional CNNs.
- There was an urgent need to evaluate existing Visual Transformers under various configurations and synthesize their architectural components to guide future research and applications.

## Method

- The paper provides a comprehensive literature review of over ==one hundred Visual Transformers==, systematically organizing them based on fundamental computer vision tasks (classification, detection, segmentation) and data stream types (images, point clouds, multi-sensory data).
- A novel ==taxonomy== is proposed to categorize methods by their motivations, structures, and application scenarios, facilitating an intuitive understanding of the field's diverse approaches.
- The authors conduct detailed ==comparative analyses== of existing Visual Transformers, evaluating their performance under various training settings and configurations to synthesize promising architectural components and discuss their internal connections.

## Results

- Visual Transformers demonstrate impressive performance improvements over modern CNNs on multiple benchmarks, especially with large-scale pre-training data.
- Hybrid models combining CNNs and Transformers (e.g., hierarchical structures, local attention) generally achieve superior performance by introducing appropriate inductive biases and addressing computational costs.
- Query-based Transformer models, exemplified by DETR for detection and MaskFormer for segmentation, unify diverse vision sub-tasks into end-to-end set prediction problems, often outperforming previous methods and simplifying pipelines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Effective Visual Transformers often integrate inductive biases from CNNs, such as locality and hierarchical processing, demonstrating a strong synergy between attention and convolution rather than a complete replacement.
> - Transformers show significant promise in unifying diverse computer vision tasks (e.g., classification, detection, segmentation) into a common framework, particularly through query-based approaches for set prediction.
> - Large-scale self-supervised pre-training, especially with multi-modal data like image-text pairs, enables robust and transferable visual representations, reducing reliance on extensive human annotations.
