# A Survey of Visual Transformers

> [!summary] Summary
> This survey paper provides a comprehensive review of over one hundred Visual Transformers, organizing them into a systematic taxonomy based on tasks and data types. It synthesizes key design principles, evaluates comparative performance across various configurations, and identifies promising future research directions in computer vision.

## Problem

- The rapid and extensive proliferation of hundreds of Transformer-based models in computer vision led to a fragmented and unorganized research landscape, making it challenging to track progress and understand underlying principles.
- A lack of systematic organization and in-depth comparative analysis made it difficult for researchers to grasp the state-of-the-art, identify effective design patterns, and understand the relationship between Visual Transformers and traditional CNNs.
- There was an urgent need to evaluate existing Visual Transformers under various configurations and synthesize their architectural components to guide future research and applications.

## Method

- The paper provides a comprehensive literature review of over ==one hundred Visual Transformers==, systematically organizing them based on fundamental computer vision tasks (classification, detection, segmentation) and data stream types (images, point clouds, multi-sensory data).
- A novel ==taxonomy== is proposed to categorize methods by their motivations, structures, and application scenarios, facilitating an intuitive understanding of the field's diverse approaches.
- The authors conduct detailed ==comparative analyses== of existing Visual Transformers, evaluating their performance under various training settings and configurations to synthesize promising architectural components and discuss their internal connections.

## Results

- Visual Transformers demonstrate impressive performance improvements over modern CNNs on multiple benchmarks, especially with large-scale pre-training data.
- Hybrid models combining CNNs and Transformers (e.g., hierarchical structures, local attention) generally achieve superior performance by introducing appropriate inductive biases and addressing computational costs.
- Query-based Transformer models, exemplified by DETR for detection and MaskFormer for segmentation, unify diverse vision sub-tasks into end-to-end set prediction problems, often outperforming previous methods and simplifying pipelines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Effective Visual Transformers often integrate inductive biases from CNNs, such as locality and hierarchical processing, demonstrating a strong synergy between attention and convolution rather than a complete replacement.
> - Transformers show significant promise in unifying diverse computer vision tasks (e.g., classification, detection, segmentation) into a common framework, particularly through query-based approaches for set prediction.
> - Large-scale self-supervised pre-training, especially with multi-modal data like image-text pairs, enables robust and transferable visual representations, reducing reliance on extensive human annotations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This survey paper enters the research landscape at a crucial juncture, reflecting and attempting to make sense of a seismic shift in computer vision. For nearly a decade, Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) have been the dominant paradigm in computer vision, achieving state-of-the-art results across fundamental tasks like image classification, object detection, and semantic segmentation. However, the emergence of the Transformer architecture, initially popularized in Natural Language Processing (NLP) with models like BERT and GPT, has recently begun to challenge CNNs' supremacy in vision.

The seminal "Vision Transformer (ViT)" paper in late 2020 demonstrated that a pure Transformer, when pre-trained on sufficiently large datasets, could achieve competitive or even superior performance to CNNs on image classification tasks. This breakthrough triggered an explosion of research, with "hundreds of Transformer-based models" being proposed for various vision applications within a short period. This rapid proliferation, while exciting, has led to a fragmented landscape where it's challenging to track progress, understand underlying motivations, and identify effective design principles.

This survey directly addresses this fragmentation. It aims to provide a much-needed comprehensive and systematic review, categorization, and evaluation of this nascent field. By categorizing visual Transformers based on fundamental CV tasks (classification, detection, segmentation) and different data stream types (images, point clouds, multi-sensory data), it offers a structured framework for understanding the diverse approaches. Furthermore, by comparing these models under different configurations and discussing their internal connections, it goes beyond a simple compilation to provide insights into *why* certain designs work and how visual Transformers relate to traditional CNNs and their NLP counterparts. It explicitly positions itself as more comprehensive than earlier, less detailed reviews of nascent visual Transformers, providing an in-depth analysis that fills a critical gap for researchers and practitioners navigating this fast-evolving domain.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this survey stems from the rapid and extensive proliferation of Transformer-based models in computer vision following the initial success of Vision Transformers (ViT). The authors observe that "hundreds of Transformer-based models have been proposed for various vision applications within the last year," leading to an urgent need for systematic organization and analysis.

The key objectives of this survey are explicitly outlined in the introduction:

- **Comprehensiveness & Readability:** To provide a comprehensive review of over one hundred visual Transformers, organizing them logically according to their applications across three fundamental CV tasks (classification, detection, segmentation) and different data stream types (images, point clouds, multi-stream data). The goal is to offer detailed descriptions of representative methods while briefly introducing others, building "internal connections" among them.
- **Intuitive Comparison:** To evaluate and compare existing visual Transformers under various training settings and configurations. This involves presenting "multiple lateral comparisons over different datasets and restrictions" to synthesize promising architectural components for each task, such as hierarchical structures for backbones, sparse attention for detectors, and general-purpose mask prediction for segmentation.
- **In-depth Analysis:** To provide insightful discussions on several critical aspects. These include:
 - Explaining *why* Transformers are effective in computer vision and how they bridge the gap between sequential NLP tasks and visual ones (e.g., handling high-level semantic embeddings).
 - Elucidating the correspondence and relationship between visual Transformers and other neural networks, particularly CNNs.
 - Highlighting the "double edges" or trade-offs inherent in visual Transformers (e.g., global property vs. inefficiency for low-level processing, slack bias vs. data dependence).
 - Analyzing the correlation of learnable embeddings (class token, object query, mask embedding) across different tasks and data types.
- **Suggesting Future Research Directions:** To identify and outline promising avenues for future investment, building upon the comprehensive review and analysis.

In essence, the authors aim to be a guiding light in a rapidly expanding and complex research area, offering clarity, comparative analysis, and forward-looking perspectives to empower researchers for future explorations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this survey is primarily a comprehensive literature review and systematic organization, coupled with comparative analysis. The authors meticulously reviewed **"over one hundred different visual Transformers,"** demonstrating a significant effort to capture the breadth of research in this nascent field.

Their approach can be broken down into several key components:

- **Taxonomy Development:** A central methodological contribution is the proposal of a novel taxonomy to organize the reviewed methods. This taxonomy is multi-dimensional, categorizing approaches based on:
 - **Fundamental CV Tasks:** Image Classification (Section III), Object Detection (Section IV), and Segmentation (Section V). These represent the core applications where Transformers have been applied.
 - **Data Stream Types:** Moving beyond standard 2D images to include 3D Visual Recognition (Section VI, primarily point clouds) and Multi-Sensory Data Stream (Section VII, including multi-view, multi-modality, and visual-linguistic data).
 - **Motivations, Structures, and Application Scenarios:** Within each task/data type, models are further grouped based on their underlying design principles (e.g., Original Visual Transformer, Transformer Enhanced CNN, CNN Enhanced Transformer, Local Attention Enhanced Transformer, Hierarchical Transformer, Deep Transformer, Self-Supervised Transformer for classification).
- **Representative Method Selection and Description:** For each category in the taxonomy, the authors select "more representative methods with detailed descriptions and analyses" while briefly introducing "other related works." This allows for in-depth understanding of key paradigms without overwhelming the reader with exhaustive details on every single variant.
- **Internal Connections and Comparative Analysis:** Beyond mere categorization, the survey emphasizes building "internal connections from certain senses such as progressive, contrastive, and multi-view analysis." Crucially, they conduct "intuitive comparison" by evaluating and comparing existing visual Transformers "under different configurations." This includes summarizing performance on standard benchmarks (e.g., ImageNet-1k for classification, COCO for detection and segmentation), often presented in tables (like Tables I-V referenced in the text) and figures (like Fig. 8 for classification performance), though the raw tables are not provided in the abstract. This evaluation helps identify effective components and trends.
- **In-depth Discussion and Future Directions:** The final phase of their methodology involves synthesizing their observations into "well-thought insights" regarding fundamental questions (e.g., bridging language and vision, relationship with CNNs, inherent trade-offs of Transformers, learnable embeddings). This analytical depth leads to the suggestion of "promising research directions for future investment," guiding the community forward.

In essence, the methodology transitions from extensive data collection (paper review) to structured organization (taxonomy), followed by critical analysis (comparisons, insights) and forward-looking recommendations. The commitment to continuous updates via a GitHub repository further underscores a dynamic and evolving methodological stance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The survey presents a wealth of findings, synthesizing the progress and trends of Visual Transformers across various computer vision tasks and data modalities.

**General Observations on Visual Transformers:**

- **Effectiveness:** Visual Transformers have demonstrated impressive performance improvements over modern CNNs on multiple benchmarks, particularly when large-scale pre-training data is available.
- **Evolution from Pure to Hybrid:** The initial "pure" Transformer models (like ViT) have evolved significantly. The most effective current approaches often incorporate elements or inductive biases traditionally associated with CNNs, such as locality, hierarchical processing, and convolutional operations, suggesting a strong synergy between the two architectures.
- **Computational Cost & Convergence:** Pure Transformers often suffer from high computational costs (quadratic complexity with input size) and slow convergence, especially compared to CNNs. Many advancements aim to address these issues through sparse attention, spatial priors, and hierarchical designs.
- **Importance of Embeddings:** Learnable embeddings (class tokens, object queries, mask embeddings) play a crucial role in adapting Transformers to different vision tasks and unifying various prediction problems.

**Task-Specific Findings:**

- **Image Classification:**
 - **Hybrid Models are Key:** Combining CNNs and Transformers (e.g., CNN-enhanced Transformers like DeiT, ConViT, CeiT, LocalViT; Transformer-enhanced CNNs like BoTNet) often leads to superior performance by introducing appropriate inductive biases.
 - **Locality is Indispensable:** Mechanisms like shifted windows (Swin Transformer) or outlook attention (VOLO) that reintroduce local feature extraction significantly improve performance, especially for dense prediction tasks.
 - **Hierarchical Structures:** Adopting hierarchical architectures (e.g., PVT, CvT, T2T-ViT) from CNNs helps manage computational costs and allows for multi-scale feature learning, crucial for dense prediction.
 - **Deep Transformers:** Strategies to address "attention collapse" and "patch over-smoothing" in very deep Transformers (e.g., CaiT, DeepViT, Refiner) show potential for further scalability.
 - **Self-Supervised Learning:** Generative (iGPT, BEiT, MAE) and discriminative (MoCo v3, DINO, MoBY) self-supervised pre-training schemes, inspired by NLP, are highly effective in vision, enabling strong feature learning, sometimes even outperforming supervised counterparts.

- **Object Detection:**
 - **DETR as a Paradigm Shift:** DETR revolutionized object detection by formulating it as a set prediction problem, eliminating hand-designed anchors and NMS.
 - **Convergence and Efficiency Improvements:** Efforts focus on accelerating DETR's slow convergence and high computational cost:
 - **Sparse Attention:** Deformable DETR's multi-scale deformable attention drastically reduces computation and improves convergence.
 - **Spatial Prior:** Explicitly incorporating spatial information (e.g., Conditional DETR, Anchor DETR, DAB-DETR, SAP-DETR) helps object queries converge faster and more accurately.
 - **Bipartite Matching Optimization:** Denoising strategies (DN-DETR, DINO) address instability in bipartite matching, leading to better end-to-end detection.
 - **Backbone Impact:** Hierarchical and local-global Transformer backbones (Swin, PVT, Focal Transformer) are crucial for dense prediction tasks, offering multi-scale features and efficient local dependency modeling.

- **Segmentation:**
 - **Patch-Based Transformers:** Extensions of ViT (SETR, TransUNet, Segformer) demonstrate Transformer's capability for pixel-level prediction, often with simpler decoders due to global modeling.
 - **Query-Based Transformers (Key for Unification):** This is a significant finding. Models using "object queries" or "mask embeddings" (e.g., Panoptic DETR, MaskFormer, SOLQ, QueryInst) can unify different segmentation sub-tasks (semantic, instance, panoptic) into a single "mask prediction problem" framework, often outperforming previous methods. MaskFormer, in particular, shows promise for a box-free, unified segmentation approach.
 - **Performance Gap:** A notable finding is the remaining performance gap between `AP_box` and `AP_seg` in some instance segmentation models, suggesting areas for improvement.

- **3D Visual Recognition (Point Clouds):**
 - **Natural Fit:** Transformers are naturally suited for irregular and sparse point cloud data due to their ability to handle permutation invariance and learn semantic representations without rigid grid biases.
 - **Representation Learning:** Point Transformers and PCT demonstrate effectiveness in learning from raw point clouds, often diminishing the need for explicit positional encoding due to inherent coordinate information.
 - **Cognition Mapping:** End-to-end 3D object detection models (3DETR, Group-Free 3D DETR) leveraging Transformers show strong results.
 - **Specific Processing:** Transformers are applied to tasks like point cloud completion and reconstruction, demonstrating adaptability to generative tasks beyond basic recognition.

- **Multi-Sensory Data Stream Fusion:**
 - **Transformer's Advantage:** Transformers are highly appropriate for multi-modal fusion because of their non-specific embedding and dynamic attention mechanisms, allowing flexible interaction between different data types.
 - **Homologous Streams:** Interactive fusion (e.g., MVT, TransFuser, mmFormer) by concatenating features and applying Transformer encoders, or transfer fusion via cross-attention.
 - **Heterologous Streams (Vision-Language):**
 - **VLP Evolution:** Initial VLP methods relied on pre-trained object detectors and text encoders (ViLBERT, LXMERT). Later models moved towards end-to-end learning without rigid object detectors (ViLT) and unified tasks (UniT).
 - **Contrastive Learning:** CLIP and ALIGN demonstrate the power of large-scale contrastive pre-training on noisy image-text pairs, enabling remarkable zero-shot transfer capabilities across diverse CV tasks.
 - **Visual Grounding:** Transformers are effective for tasks like phrase grounding and referring expression comprehension (MDETR, TransVG, Referring Transformer), linking language descriptions to visual regions or objects.

In summary, the survey highlights that visual Transformers are not just a trend but a fundamental shift, especially when combined with inductive biases from CNNs and leveraging large-scale self-supervised pre-training. They show significant promise in unifying various vision tasks and excel in multi-modal understanding.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The significance and potential impact of this survey paper are substantial, both for the academic community and the practical application of computer vision technologies.

**1. Clarifying a Rapidly Evolving Field:** The most immediate impact is the provision of a much-needed structured and comprehensive overview of the Visual Transformer landscape. In a field experiencing exponential growth, keeping track of new architectures, methodologies, and performance benchmarks is challenging. This survey acts as a critical reference point, organizing over a hundred papers into a coherent taxonomy based on tasks, data types, and architectural motivations. This clarity helps researchers quickly grasp the state-of-the-art, identify relevant prior work, and avoid redundant efforts.

**2. Synthesizing Key Design Principles:** The detailed analysis and intuitive comparisons across different Transformer variants reveal crucial design principles for their effective application in vision. Insights like the indispensable role of locality, the benefits of convolutional inductive biases (e.g., convolutional stems, local attention), the advantages of hierarchical structures for efficiency, and the challenges/solutions for deep Transformers are invaluable. These findings provide empirical guidance for practitioners and architects designing next-generation vision models.

**3. Highlighting the CNN-Transformer Synergy:** The survey underscores that the "Visual Transformer" paradigm is not about replacing CNNs entirely but often about finding the optimal "marriage" between convolution and attention. This nuanced understanding dispels the "either/or" mentality and emphasizes the strengths of a hybrid approach, where CNNs excel at low-level feature extraction and locality, while Transformers provide global context and dynamic interactions. This insight will likely drive future research towards more powerful and efficient hybrid architectures.

**4. Unifying Vision Tasks:** A significant potential impact highlighted by the survey is the Transformer's ability to unify diverse computer vision tasks. The discussion on query-based Transformers for segmentation (e.g., MaskFormer) demonstrates how a single, end-to-end framework can handle semantic, instance, and panoptic segmentation, often without complex post-processing like NMS. This unification simplifies model design, training pipelines, and potentially enables more general-purpose vision systems. The extension to 3D vision and multi-sensory data further supports this unifying potential.

**5. Advancing Multi-modal Understanding:** The survey meticulously details how Transformers facilitate multi-sensory data fusion, particularly in vision-language tasks. The success of large-scale pre-trained models like CLIP and ALIGN, which leverage contrastive learning on vast image-text datasets, suggests a powerful paradigm for learning transferable visual representations from natural language supervision. This approach could democratize access to powerful vision models, reducing reliance on extensive human annotation for specific tasks and paving the way for more general AI.

**6. Guiding Future Research Directions:** By discussing "essential but unexploited aspects" and suggesting "promising research directions" (e.g., set prediction for classification, self-supervised encoder-decoder Transformers for unified learning), the survey directly influences the future trajectory of the field. These suggestions serve as a roadmap for researchers seeking to push the boundaries of Visual Transformers, addressing their current limitations and unlocking new capabilities.

In essence, this survey serves as a pivotal document, not just summarizing the past but actively shaping the understanding and future development of Visual Transformers. Its comprehensive nature, critical analysis, and forward-looking perspectives make it an indispensable resource for anyone working in or studying advanced computer vision.
