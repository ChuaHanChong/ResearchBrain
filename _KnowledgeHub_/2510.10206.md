# It Takes Two: Learning Interactive Whole-Body Control Between Humanoid Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Shanghai Jiao Tong University developed Harmanoid, a framework enabling two humanoid robots to perform physically grounded, interactive whole-body motions. It achieves a 0.92 success rate in complex interactive tasks, significantly outperforming single-robot imitation methods.

## Problem

- Existing humanoid motion imitation methods primarily focus on single-robot scenarios, neglecting multi-agent interactions.
- Applying single-humanoid control to multiple robots leads to an 'isolation issue,' causing collisions, interpenetrations, and uncoordinated movements.
- There was a lack of robust frameworks to transfer and control complex, physically grounded human-to-human interactions onto two humanoid robots.

## Method

- ==Introduce Harmanoid==, a two-stage framework for dual-humanoid motion imitation, combining contact-aware motion retargeting with an interaction-driven reinforcement learning controller.
- Implement contact-aware motion retargeting to adapt human interaction data to robot morphology, preserving inter-body coordination and minimizing penetrations.
- Develop an interaction-driven motion controller using augmented observations, shared interaction-aware rewards, and specific contact rewards to enable physically consistent multi-robot behaviors.

## Results

- Harmanoid achieved a **0.92** success rate in dual-humanoid interactive tasks, significantly outperforming single-humanoid baselines that failed to execute these motions (**0.00**-**0.20** success rates).
- The contact-aware motion retargeting component produced physically more feasible reference motions, evidenced by a **0.74** tracking success rate and lower errors (E_g-MPJPE: **157.6** mm) compared to other retargeting methods.
- Ablation studies confirmed that all components of the interaction-driven controller, including interactive rewards, contact rewards, and curriculum learning, collectively contributed to the highest success rate (**0.92**) and contact fidelity (**0.18** Contact F1).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Treating humanoid robots independently for interactive tasks leads to catastrophic failures, highlighting the necessity of explicitly modeling inter-agent dynamics.
> - A systematic approach that first generates contact-aware, interaction-preserving reference motions via retargeting and then refines them with an interaction-driven controller is more effective.
> - Integrating interaction-aware rewards and real-time contact feedback within a reinforcement learning framework enables robots to learn physically plausible and coordinated interactive behaviors.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of humanoid robotics has seen rapid advancements, particularly in equipping robots with the ability to perform complex, human-like motions. A significant portion of this progress stems from **humanoid motion imitation**, where robots learn behaviors by replicating motions captured from humans. This domain has benefited immensely from two key areas:
1. **Data:** The availability of large-scale motion capture (MOCAP) datasets, such as AMASS and Inter-X, provides rich, diverse human motion examples that serve as invaluable training data.
2. **Algorithms:** Improvements in motion imitation pipelines, often leveraging reinforcement learning (RL) in simulation environments (e.g., DeepMimic, PHC, Mink, GMR), allow these captured motions to be retargeted to robot embodiments, refined for physical plausibility, and then deployed.

Despite these strides, the vast majority of existing humanoid motion imitation research has focused on **single-humanoid scenarios**. These methods excel at enabling an individual robot to replicate a single human's movements, achieving expressive and physically realistic full-body motions for tasks ranging from locomotion to manipulation.

However, the authors identify a critical gap: the true promise of humanoid robotics extends beyond individual autonomy to **multi-robot interaction**. Applications like elder care, collaborative manufacturing, and socially assistive robotics inherently demand that two or more humanoids engage in physically grounded and socially meaningful interactions. Current single-humanoid methods are inherently **ill-suited** for these multi-agent contexts, suffering from what the authors term the "isolation issue." When applied to multiple robots, these independent approaches overlook inter-agent dynamics, leading to severe problems such as:
- **Misaligned contacts and interpenetrations:** Robots might collide or pass through each other due to a lack of awareness of the other agent's physical presence.
- **Loss of coordinated behaviors:** Closely coupled motions (e.g., hand-holding, synchronized dancing, shared object manipulation) cannot be faithfully reproduced if each robot acts independently.
- **Physically implausible executions:** Even if individual robots move plausibly, the joint interaction might be unrealistic or unstable due to unaddressed interdependencies and constraints.

This paper directly addresses this critical limitation by introducing **Harmanoid**, a pioneering framework designed for *dual-humanoid motion imitation*. It attempts to bridge the gap between highly expressive single-agent control and the complexities of multi-agent, physically interactive behaviors. By explicitly modeling and leveraging inter-agent contact information and interaction-aware dynamics, Harmanoid positions itself at the forefront of enabling more sophisticated and socially intelligent humanoid robotic systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to enable **two humanoid robots to engage in physically grounded, socially meaningful whole-body interactions**, mirroring the richness and coordination observed in human-to-human interactions. This moves beyond the current paradigm of single-robot autonomy towards truly collaborative and interactive robotic systems.

The specific key objectives and motivations driving this work are:

- **To address the "isolation issue" in multi-humanoid motion imitation:** Existing methods, designed for single robots, treat each agent independently. This leads to a fundamental breakdown when robots need to physically interact, resulting in interpenetrations, uncoordinated motions, and a failure to replicate the nuanced dynamics of human interaction. The authors are motivated to overcome this by explicitly accounting for inter-agent dependencies.
- **To transfer interacting human motions to two humanoid robots with high fidelity:** The goal is not just to make two robots move, but to ensure that their combined motion accurately preserves both the individual kinematic movements and, crucially, the interactive coherence observed in original human-to-human MOCAP data.
- **To ensure both kinematic fidelity and physical realism in interactive scenarios:** This involves two critical aspects:
 - **Kinematic fidelity:** The individual movements of each robot should closely match their human counterparts.
 - **Physical realism:** The interaction between robots must be physically plausible, avoiding collisions, maintaining balance, and achieving realistic contact forces and durations. This is particularly challenging given the morphological differences between humans and robots and the rigid body dynamics involved.
- **To develop a robust framework that integrates motion retargeting and control for dual-humanoid tasks:** The motivation is to create a complete pipeline that can take raw human interaction data, adapt it for robot embodiments while preserving interaction features, and then train robust controllers capable of executing these complex, coordinated behaviors.

Ultimately, the motivation stems from the belief that the true value and potential applications of humanoid robots, from assistive roles to advanced manufacturing, will only be fully realized when they can fluidly and safely interact with each other and with humans in dynamic, physically coupled environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose **Harmanoid**, a novel dual-humanoid motion imitation framework comprising two integrated stages: (i) contact-aware motion retargeting and (ii) an interaction-driven motion controller. This two-stage approach systematically addresses the challenges of both transferring human interaction data to robots and controlling their physical execution.

#### 3.1. Contact-Aware Motion Retargeting

This module is designed to convert synchronized human motion sequences (represented by SMPL meshes) into physically plausible robot-reference motions while explicitly preserving inter-body coordination and minimizing undesirable penetrations.

- **Problem Identification:** Traditional single-agent retargeting methods (e.g., PHC, GMR) treat each human independently. When applied to interacting humans, the resulting robot motions often suffer from root pose deviations, interpenetrations, or unnatural distances due to morphological mismatches and the lack of explicit interaction modeling.
- **Three-Step Process:**
 1. **Contact Mesh Detection:**
 - Human motions are represented as SMPL-format meshes (vertices and faces).
 - A mesh collision detection algorithm (Trimesh) is applied per-frame to identify all intersecting face pairs between the two human meshes.
 - This process yields a set of collision face pairs (C_t) for each frame, explicitly encoding where and how the two humans are in physical contact.
 2. **Regularized Shape Optimization:**
 - To adapt the SMPL human body shape to the humanoid robot's structure, a shape optimization procedure is performed.
 - Crucially, an L2 regularization term (𝜆∥𝛽′∥²) is added to the standard keypoint matching loss. This term encourages the optimized human shape parameters (𝛽′) to remain close to the robot's default structure.
 - This regularization prevents "overfitting" and unrealistic deformations of the human mesh, which could distort contact information and lead to misalignments between corresponding vertices on the robot surface. It ensures that the mesh-level contact points remain consistent with the robot's morphology.
 3. **Contact-Aware Root Pose Optimization:**
 - Even after shape optimization, direct retargeting can lead to misalignments. To correct this, a learnable root pose offset (translation Δ𝑝_root and rotation Δ𝜃_root) is introduced for one agent (without loss of generality).
 - This offset is optimized by minimizing the sum of squared distances between the centroids of the identified colliding face pairs (mapped to the robot-shaped SMPL mesh) over the entire motion sequence.
 - This adaptive adjustment of the relative root pose directly aligns contact points, significantly reducing interpenetration and ensuring realistic interaction distances.
- **Output:** This stage generates detailed kinematic reference trajectories (joint angles, positions, velocities) and per-frame contact masks (one-hot vectors indicating which robot links are in contact) for both robots, serving as input for the motion controller.

#### 3.2. Interaction-Driven Motion Controller

This module refines the retargeted motions using reinforcement learning (RL) to achieve physically feasible, balanced, and interaction-consistent behaviors in simulation.

- **Problem Identification:** Directly applying a standard RL policy, even with the improved retargeted motions, would still treat each robot independently, leading to suboptimal or uncoordinated interactions. The controller needs explicit guidance on how to interact.
- **Components of the Controller:**
 1. **Interactive Observations:** The state observed by each robot's policy is augmented beyond its proprioceptive state and individual reference motion targets. It now includes:
 - A summary of the *partner's current state* (e.g., keypoint positions, velocities), enabling motion synchronization.
 - Per-link *contact masks* for both agents, derived from the retargeted sequence (b𝑐^(𝑖)_𝑡, b𝑐^(−𝑖)_𝑡), indicating intended interaction points.
 - The agent's *measured contact state* (𝑐^(𝑖)_𝑡), providing real-time feedback on physical interactions.
 2. **Interaction-Aware Global Reward (𝑟_int):**
 - This shared reward encourages the maintenance of spatial relationships between the *upper-body keypoints* of the two robots that match the reference human interaction.
 - It uses a reference-weighted importance (𝑤_𝑡(𝑢,𝑣)) based on the proximity of keypoints in the reference motion, and a symmetric relative discrepancy (𝐸_𝑡(𝑢,𝑣)) to penalize deviations in simulated pairwise offsets from the reference.
 - This reward intrinsically couples the two policies, driving them to maintain mutual proximity and alignment, crucial for actions like hand-holding or hugging.
 3. **Contact Reward (𝑟_con and 𝑝_con):**
 - This reward shapes physically realistic contact behaviors on non-feet body links, leveraging the retargeted contact masks (b𝑐^(𝑖)_𝑡) and measured contact forces (𝐹^(𝑖)_𝑡,𝑏).
 - **Expected Contact Reward (𝑟_exp):** Rewards forces within a specified range [𝑓_min, 𝑓_max] when the reference indicates contact (b𝑐^(𝑖)_𝑡 = 1), using a sigmoidal decay outside this range.
 - **Unexpected Contact Penalty (𝑝_unexp):** Penalizes forces above a threshold 𝜏 when the reference indicates no contact (b𝑐^(𝑖)_𝑡 = 0), using a sigmoidal increase for excess forces.
 - These rewards ensure that robots make contact where and when expected, with appropriate forces, and avoid spurious contacts.
 4. **Curriculum Learning:**
 - To mitigate early conflicts between learning to track individual motions and learning to interact, an online curriculum is employed.
 - Reward scales for tracking-related terms (𝑟_goal) and interaction/contact-related terms (𝑟_int, 𝑟_contact) are adaptively adjusted.
 - Initially, the curriculum emphasizes tracking proficiency (measured by joint-velocity tracking reward). Once a certain proficiency (𝑠_𝑡 ≥ 1) is achieved, tracking terms are gradually down-weighted, while interaction and contact terms are up-weighted.
 - This progressive learning approach stabilizes training and allows the policy to first master individual movements before focusing on the more complex, coupled interactive dynamics.

The entire system is trained using the Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) algorithm in the IsaacSim simulator, leveraging its high-performance GPU-based physics simulation capabilities.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments comprehensively evaluate Harmanoid's effectiveness in dual-humanoid motion imitation, comparing it against single-humanoid baselines and performing ablation studies on its components.

#### 4.1. Dual Motion Imitation Evaluation

- **Baselines:** Exbody (direct joint rotation copying) and HOVER (uses PHC for retargeting), both representing state-of-the-art single-humanoid motion imitators. For these baselines, two robots were independently retargeted and then a controller was trained on combined data.
- **Metrics:** Success Rate, Global Mean Per-Joint Position Error (E_g-MPJPE), Root-Relative Mean Per-Joint Position Error (E_MPJPE), Acceleration Error (E_Acc), and Root Velocity Error (E_Vel).
- **Results:**
 - Single motion imitation baselines (Exbody, HOVER) demonstrated *catastrophic failure* in interactive scenarios, with success rates of 0.00 and 0.20 respectively. This was primarily attributed to collisions between robots during rollout, confirming the "isolation issue" and the generation of physically infeasible reference motions.
 - **Harmanoid (Ours)** achieved a significantly higher success rate of **0.92** and substantially lower errors across all metrics (e.g., E_g-MPJPE: 156.8 mm vs. 193.6/187.4 mm for baselines). This demonstrates its superior ability to handle coordinated, interactive motions while maintaining stability and accuracy.

#### 4.2. Motion Retargeting Evaluation

This evaluation focused on the quality of the reference motions generated by different retargeting approaches, by deploying a single-motion controller (trained on AMASS using PHC) to track these references. The success rate of this tracking serves as an indicator of physical executability and reasonableness.

- **Methods Compared:**
 - **Single PHC:** Standard single-humanoid retargeting using PHC.
 - **Single PHC + IC:** Single PHC augmented with an interaction constraint in the objective function.
 - **Harmanoid-Retarget (Ours):** The proposed contact-aware motion retargeting.
- **Results:**
 - Single PHC showed a success rate of 0.62, indicating limitations due to unrealistic reference motions and interpenetration.
 - Single PHC + IC surprisingly performed *worse* in tracking performance (E_g-MPJPE of 215.8 mm, compared to 170.8 mm for Single PHC), achieving only a 0.68 success rate. This highlights that simply adding an interaction constraint to a single-agent retargeting pipeline creates a trade-off, compromising individual robot pose expressiveness and deviating from the motion distribution expected by the controller.
 - **Harmanoid-Retarget** achieved the highest success rate of **0.74** and the lowest tracking errors (E_g-MPJPE: 157.6 mm). Qualitative comparisons visually demonstrated its ability to better preserve interactivity and significantly reduce interpenetrations. It consistently outperformed baselines across various interactive motion categories.

#### 4.3. Motion Control Evaluation (Ablation Study)

This study assessed the individual contributions of Harmanoid's interaction-driven controller components using Harmanoid-Retargeted motions.

- **Metrics:** Success Rate and Contact F1 Score (measuring how well the robot matches ground-truth contact patterns).
- **Results:**
 - **Baseline (no interactive/contact reward, no curriculum):** 0.84 Success Rate, 0.10 Contact F1.
 - **Adding Interactive Reward:** Improved to 0.88 Success Rate, 0.16 Contact F1, showing better coordination.
 - **Adding Contact Reward (with Interactive Reward):** Further improved to 0.90 Success Rate, 0.15 Contact F1, indicating more refined physical interactions.
 - **Full Harmanoid (all components including Curriculum Learning):** Achieved the best performance with **0.92 Success Rate** and **0.18 Contact F1**. This demonstrates the complementary benefits of interaction modeling, contact awareness, and the progressive training strategy, leading to the most stable, realistic, and synchronized interactive behaviors. Qualitative results visually confirmed better preservation of physical contact features and more coordinated actions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Harmanoid marks a significant advancement in the field of humanoid robotics, particularly in addressing the long-standing challenge of multi-agent interaction.

**Key Significance:**

- **Pioneering Framework for Dual-Humanoid Interaction:** Harmanoid is presented as the first framework specifically designed for dual-humanoid motion imitation, moving beyond the prevalent single-agent focus. This addresses a critical unmet need for enabling complex, coordinated behaviors between multiple robots.
- **Explicit Modeling of Inter-Agent Dynamics:** Unlike prior work that suffers from the "isolation issue," Harmanoid explicitly incorporates and leverages inter-agent contact information and interaction-aware dynamics throughout its pipeline. This is crucial for bridging the gap between individual robot capabilities and the intricate demands of coupled physical interaction.
- **Robust Two-Stage Methodology:** The framework's modular two-stage design—contact-aware motion retargeting followed by an interaction-driven motion controller—provides a comprehensive and effective solution. The retargeting module uniquely tackles morphological mismatches and interpenetration artifacts at the data preparation stage, while the RL controller robustly learns to execute these interactions with physical plausibility and coordination.
- **Demonstrated Superior Performance:** The experimental results clearly show Harmanoid significantly outperforming existing single-humanoid baselines, which largely fail in interactive scenarios. Its improved success rates, reduced errors, and enhanced contact fidelity underscore its practical utility.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Enabling Advanced Humanoid Applications:** Harmanoid's capabilities could unlock a new generation of applications for humanoid robots. This includes:
 - **Elder care and rehabilitation:** Robots could assist with transferring, supporting, or guiding individuals in a physically safe and coordinated manner.
 - **Collaborative manufacturing and assembly:** Humanoids could work together on complex tasks requiring synchronized manipulation of objects or shared workspaces.
 - **Social robotics and entertainment:** More realistic and expressive interactive behaviors would enhance the social acceptance and engagement of humanoids in public and domestic settings.
- **Foundation for Future Multi-Robot Research:** This work provides a strong foundation for future research in several directions:
 - **More complex interaction types:** Extending to scenarios involving dynamic load sharing (e.g., carrying a partner), varied contact modalities, or interactions with objects.
 - **Scalability to N-robot systems:** Adapting the principles of interaction-aware design to scenarios with more than two interacting humanoids.
 - **Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The authors themselves identify the need for deploying Harmanoid on real-world robots, which will necessitate advancements in perception, robust communication, and robust control for real-world uncertainties.
- **Integration with Other AI Fields:** The methodology could be integrated with advancements in human-robot interaction (HRI), natural language processing for command interpretation, and adaptive learning to enable robots to learn new interaction styles on the fly.

In conclusion, "It Takes Two" presents a foundational step towards realizing the full interactive potential of humanoid robots. By meticulously addressing the inherent limitations of single-agent approaches, Harmanoid paves the way for a future where humanoid robots can seamlessly and robustly collaborate and interact in complex, dynamic environments.
