# Exploring Embodied Multimodal Large Models: Development, Datasets, and Future Directions

> [!summary] Summary
> This paper presents a comprehensive review of Embodied Multimodal Large Models (EMLMs), analyzing their development, integration of large language and vision models, and the role of datasets and simulation environments. It highlights advancements in embodied perception, navigation, and interaction, while outlining persistent challenges and future research directions.

## Problem

- Existing literature reviews often focus narrowly on LLMs or LVMs, overlooking their integration with embodied agents.
- Previous reviews either covered embodied intelligence too broadly or too narrowly, lacking a 'full-stack' analysis.
- A systematic and timely overview of Embodied Multimodal Large Models (EMLMs) was needed to consolidate rapid advancements.

## Method

- The paper conducts the first systematic review of ==Embodied Multimodal Large Models (EMLMs)==.
- It analyzes approximately 300 research papers to provide a 'full-stack' understanding of EMLMs, covering foundational models, embodied components, simulation, and datasets.
- It identifies current main findings, key challenges, and promising future research directions for the field.

## Results

- The review systematically categorizes EMLM development based on embodied perception, navigation, and interaction, detailing various models and approaches.
- It provides a comprehensive overview of foundational models (LLMs, LVMs, LVLMs) and their role in EMLMs.
- It summarizes critical benchmark datasets and simulation environments essential for EMLM research and development.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - EMLMs are increasingly integrating LLMs and LVLMs to enhance capabilities in embodied perception, navigation, and interaction.
> - Simulation environments and diverse, high-quality multimodal datasets are crucial for training and evaluating EMLMs.
> - Key challenges include cross-modal alignment, computational efficiency, generalization, temporal information handling, and dataset diversity.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The concept of embodied intelligence, rooted in philosophical and cognitive science theories from the late 20th century, posits that cognition emerges from physical interaction with the environment. Initially explored in robotics through projects like Cog, this field has recently seen a dramatic acceleration with the advent of large-scale pre-trained models. Embodied Multimodal Large Models (EMLMs) represent the cutting edge of this integration, aiming to develop AI systems that can perceive, understand, and interact with the physical world through multiple sensory modalities (vision, language, audio, touch) in a physically grounded manner.

This paper positions itself within this rapidly evolving landscape by addressing several critical gaps in existing literature reviews. The authors argue that previous reviews often:
- Primarily focused on traditional Large Language Models (LLMs) or Large Vision Models (LVMs) rather than their integration with embodied agents.
- Covered embodied intelligence too broadly (including both software and hardware) or too narrowly (focusing only on specific components of the embodied intelligence stack).
- Were published before the most recent, rapid advancements in the field, thus lacking state-of-the-art developments.

By specifically focusing on the integration of large models with embodied capabilities and providing a "full-stack analysis" (covering foundational models, embodied perception, navigation, interaction, simulation, and datasets), this review distinguishes itself. It aims to offer a timely and holistic overview that captures the synergies between large models and embodied AI, thereby bridging the gap between abstract reasoning and real-world physical interaction. This work serves as a crucial reference point for researchers seeking to understand the current state and future trajectory of this interdisciplinary domain.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this comprehensive review is to provide an in-depth, systematic analysis of the emerging field of Embodied Multimodal Large Models (EMLMs). The authors explicitly identify several key objectives:

1. **Systematic Review of EMLM Progress**: To offer the first systematic review of EMLMs, addressing a significant void in the existing literature that often separates discussions of large models from embodied AI or focuses too broadly/narrowly on either.
2. **Comprehensive Full-Stack Analysis**: To conduct an extensive analysis of 300 research papers, providing a "full-stack" understanding of EMLMs. This includes foundational large models (LLMs, LVMs), embodied components (perception, navigation, interaction), simulation techniques, and datasets, enabling readers to grasp the entire development landscape of EMLMs.
3. **Detailed Analysis of Benchmark Datasets**: To summarize the critical benchmark datasets used in EMLMs, detailing their collection methods, key features (data format, functionality, platforms), advantages, and limitations. This highlights the crucial role of data in EMLM development.
4. **Identification of Main Findings and Future Directions**: To discuss the principal advancements, provide insights into EMLM applications in embodied agents, and outline promising future research directions, thereby inspiring further progress in this rapidly evolving field.

In essence, the authors are motivated by the need to consolidate the scattered knowledge in this nascent yet critical area, offer a structured understanding of its components, evaluate its current state, and chart a course for future innovation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The paper adopts a comprehensive review methodology, analyzing approximately 300 research papers to provide a "full-stack" perspective on EMLMs. The approach is structured into several logical sections, each delving into a specific aspect of EMLM development:

- **Foundational Model Development**: The review begins by detailing the evolution of essential large models that serve as building blocks for EMLMs. This includes a thorough discussion of Large Language Models (LLMs) like GPT variants and BERT, Large Vision Models (LVMs) such as ViT and SAM, and crucially, Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) like CLIP and PaLM-E, which are central to multimodal understanding. It also briefly touches upon other modalities like audio and touch, acknowledging their growing importance.
- **Embodied Multimodal Large Model Development**: This core section categorizes EMLM advancements based on key embodied capabilities:
 - **Embodied Perception**: Discusses how EMLMs enable deeper understanding of objects, 3D relationships, and complex task execution, categorizing approaches into GPT-based (e.g., Octopus, CoPa) and non-GPT large models (e.g., PaLM-E, RT series).
 - **Embodied Navigation**: Explores how EMLMs facilitate dynamic, environmentally perceptive navigation, distinguishing between methods that leverage general large models (e.g., LM-Nav, NavGPT) and those that craft specialized EMLMs (e.g., NaviLLM, GNM).
 - **Embodied Interaction**: Covers how EMLMs enable intelligent agents to understand natural language instructions and perform tasks by interacting with the environment, classifying strategies into short-horizon (e.g., R3M, RT-1) and long-horizon action policies (e.g., SayCan, Text2Motion).
- **Simulation Environments**: The paper analyzes the role of simulation in EMLM development, categorizing platforms into general simulators (e.g., Isaac Sim) and those based on real-world scenarios (e.g., SDF-Sim, iGibson 2.0, Habitat-Sim).
- **Datasets**: A dedicated section elaborates on the importance of data, discussing dataset collection methods (real-world vs. simulator-based) and providing an extensive overview of datasets used for embodied perception, interaction (e.g., Open X-Embodiment, ARIO), and navigation (e.g., HM3D, R2R, ScanQA).
- **Challenges and Future Directions**: The review concludes by identifying existing technological, data, and ethical challenges, and proposing key avenues for future research, such as cross-modal alignment, computational efficiency, generalization, temporal information handling, dataset diversity, and end-to-end models.

This structured approach allows the authors to systematically dissect the field, highlighting key models, techniques, and trends across different functional layers of embodied AI, providing a comprehensive and well-organized overview.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The review presents a comprehensive landscape of EMLMs, highlighting key developments across foundational models, embodied capabilities, simulation, and data.

#### 4.1. Development of Foundational Models

The paper traces the evolution of core large models:
- **Large Language Models (LLMs)**: These serve as the "language brain" of EMLMs, enabling natural language understanding and generation. Key models include BERT, GPT series (GPT-3, ChatGPT, GPT-4, DeepSeek-V3), and LLaMA, which have scaled in parameters and capabilities, facilitating language-based command interpretation and reasoning in embodied systems.
- **Large Vision Models (LVMs)**: Crucial for perceiving and understanding the visual world. Examples include ResNet, Vision Transformer (ViT), Swin Transformer, Segment Anything Model (SAM), and DINO/DINOv2. These models excel in tasks like image recognition, object detection, and self-supervised representation learning.
- **Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs)**: These integrate visual and linguistic information to enhance environmental understanding, reasoning, and task execution. Noteworthy models include CLIP, DALL·E, BLIP/BLIP-2, Flamingo, and PaLM-E, which enable cross-modal reasoning and adaptive decision-making. PaLM-E is specifically highlighted for bridging vision, language, and robotic actions.
- **Other Modal Models**: The review briefly acknowledges the growing role of audio (e.g., SoundSpaces for navigation, audio for manipulation contact) and touch (e.g., vision-tactile fusion for 6D pose estimation and grasping) in embodied tasks, though research in these areas is less extensive for EMLMs compared to vision and language.

#### 4.2. Development of Embodied Multimodal Large Models

The core findings are categorized by embodied functionality:

#### 4.3. Embodied Perception

EMLMs for perception go beyond simple object identification, focusing on understanding 3D relationships and complex task execution based on visual information.
- **GPT-Based Models**: Approaches like Octopus, CoPa, and AlphaBlock leverage GPT-4V for dynamic scene descriptions, grasping area identification, and reasoning, often with visual adapters to extract multi-stage features. Voxposer and RobotGPT directly use LLMs for generating executable code based on observations.
- **Non-GPT Large Models**: Models like PaLM-E integrate sensor inputs directly into the language model's latent space for grounded inferences. RT-1 and RT-2, and their successor RT-H, use vision-language models to output low-level robot actions. RoboFlamingo and Openvla adapt open-source VLM/VLA models for manipulation tasks, emphasizing efficiency and real-world demonstrations. RoboMamba integrates Mamba with a visual encoder for efficient reasoning.

#### 4.4. Embodied Navigation

EMLMs enable dynamic, environmentally perceptive navigation, moving beyond static map reliance to real-time sensor processing and semantic understanding.
- **General Large Models**: LVLMs like LM-Nav (GPT-3 based), L3MVN (RoBERTa/CLIP based), and VLMaps leverage LLMs for natural language instruction parsing and semantic understanding of scenes. NavGPT and NavGPT-2 use BLIP-2 and InstructBLIP for multimodal inputs and decision-making, demonstrating strong performance in visual language navigation.
- **Specialized EMLMs**: Models like NavCoT and NaviLLM combine LLMs (e.g., LLaMA, Vicuna) with visual encoders (e.g., ViT) and Transformer architectures, training on specialized VLN datasets to enhance navigational reasoning. GOAT emphasizes causal learning for generalizability, while VER uses volumetric representations for 3D scene understanding. The Berkeley AI Research team's GNM, ViNT, and NoMaD represent generalizable navigation models adaptable to various robot platforms without extensive prior training.

#### 4.5. Embodied Interaction

EMLMs facilitate natural language-based human-robot interaction for task completion, categorized by action policy horizons:
- **Language-based Short-horizon Action Policy**: Focuses on quick, immediate decisions. R3M and Vi-PRoM utilize pre-trained visual encoders (often from large human video datasets like Ego4D) to learn sparse representations for robot manipulation. RT-1/RT-2/RT-H, Q-transformer, Octo, Vima, and Openvla leverage Transformer models or VLMs to directly output robot actions, emphasizing multi-task learning, generalization, and cost-effectiveness.
- **Language-based Long-horizon Action Policy**: Involves complex planning and reasoning over longer time spans. SayCan connects LLMs with pre-trained skills for complex task decomposition. Zero-Shot Planners and Text2Motion leverage LLMs' world knowledge for task planning. Models like EmbodiedGPT, PaLM-e, TPVQA, TaPA, ViLaIn, and PG-InstructBLIP integrate visual information and LLMs for robust task planning, subtask sequencing, and error correction. ScreenAgent and Autotamp focus on tightly coupling task planning with motion planning. Mutex and Octopus use unified multimodal architectures or generated executable code to bridge high-level planning and real-world operations. Recent trends also emphasize 3D scene information (3d-vla, Grid, LEO, Sayplan) for enhanced understanding and planning.

#### 4.6. Simulation Environments

Simulators are critical for controlled training and testing of EMLMs:
- **General Simulators**: NVIDIA Omniverse™ Isaac Sim provides a powerful, physically realistic environment for robotics simulation, supporting advanced physics and multi-sensor rendering.
- **Simulators Based on Real-world Scenarios**: SDF-Sim uses learned signed distance functions for efficient large-scale scene simulation. TRUMANS generates human-scene interaction sequences. WonderWorld and GenZI focus on generating diverse 3D scenes and human-scene interactions from single images or text descriptions, respectively. iGibson 2.0 and Habitat-Sim provide interactive 3D environments for embodied AI research, focusing on environmental realism, task simulation, and high-performance agent training. Genesis is highlighted as a new general physics engine for automated data generation across modalities.

#### 4.7. Datasets

The quality and diversity of datasets are crucial for EMLMs:
- **Collection Methods**: Data is collected either from real-world environments using diverse sensors (e.g., RGB, depth, LiDAR, pressure, sound) or through simulators (e.g., Unity, Gazebo) for rapid, controlled data generation.
- **Perception and Interaction Datasets**: Key examples include the large-scale, multimodal Open X-Embodiment Dataset (data from 22 robot types), RH20T (110k+ robot operation sequences), ManiWAV (audio-visual human demonstrations), and ARIO (over 3 million samples across various robot platforms and modalities). Simulated datasets like ManiSkill and 3D-VLA complement real-world data, often integrating human-object interaction data. Specific datasets for touch modalities (e.g., TVL, Touch 1k) are also mentioned, though their application to robotics is still nascent.
- **Navigation Datasets**: These datasets aim to improve robots' navigation capabilities using visually-linguistic instructions. Prominent examples include HM3D (large-scale 3D scene reconstructions), Gibson (virtualized real spaces), Matterport3D (RGB-D dataset for indoor environments), and datasets built upon it like R2R, REVERIE, and SOON, which provide natural language navigation instructions and require agents to navigate to unseen locations. ScanQA focuses on 3D question answering, while LLaVA provides visual instruction tuning data. SoundSpaces and SoundSpaces-v2 incorporate audio for navigation tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The paper underscores the transformative potential of Embodied Multimodal Large Models (EMLMs) in bridging the gap between abstract AI reasoning and real-world physical interaction. By systematically reviewing the state-of-the-art, it provides a valuable roadmap for future research and development in artificial intelligence and robotics.

#### 5.1. Challenges Identified

Despite significant advancements, the review identifies several persistent challenges that impede the widespread deployment and full realization of EMLMs:
- **Cross-modal Alignment**: Achieving precise, real-time fusion and alignment across diverse modalities (vision, language, motion, audio, touch) remains a fundamental hurdle, impacting the accuracy and efficiency of EMLM responses.
- **Computational Resources and Efficiency**: EMLMs are computationally intensive. The demand for high-performance GPUs for training and inference, combined with large model parameters, presents challenges in terms of energy consumption, inference speed, and cost. While progress like Openvla (7B parameters) shows promise, integrating more modalities often escalates these resource demands.
- **Generalization Across Domains**: Current EMLMs struggle to generalize effectively across diverse contexts or tasks. Models often remain fixed to specific functions (e.g., perception, navigation, interaction), lacking robust transferability to unseen environments or novel tasks.
- **Handling Temporal and Sequential Information**: Managing dynamic, real-time data and processing continuous actions, environmental events, and their temporal dependencies for complex, long-horizon tasks (especially in navigation) remains a significant challenge.
- **Diversity and Quality of Datasets**: There is a persistent shortage of high-quality, large-scale, and diverse real-world datasets that capture complex multimodal interactions in dynamic environments. Existing datasets often prioritize specific tasks (e.g., household chores) and rely heavily on camera-based sensors, limiting the breadth of real-world perception and interaction data. The need to incorporate more diverse sensors (LiDAR, sound, force, GPS) and dynamic, non-static real-world data is critical.
- **Ethical and Bias Issues**: The potential for EMLMs to inherit biases from training data, leading to unfair or discriminatory outcomes, necessitates the development of methods to ensure fairness, transparency, and accountability in autonomous systems.

#### 5.2. Future Directions

The authors outline several promising avenues for future research to overcome these challenges and unlock the full potential of EMLMs:
- **Cross-modal Pre-training and Fine-tuning**: Developing more efficient strategies for pre-training and fine-tuning across modalities will enable models to perform well across a broader range of tasks (perception to decision-making) without extensive retraining.
- **Self-supervised Learning**: Further advancements in self-supervised learning techniques are crucial to reduce dependence on large, manually labeled datasets, allowing models to learn richer, more adaptable representations from unlabeled data.
- **Integration with Multimodal Reinforcement Learning**: Combining EMLMs with reinforcement learning (RL) will enable embodied agents to learn and adapt their behaviors continuously through perception, action, and feedback loops in dynamic, real-world environments.
- **End-to-End Large Models**: The future trend points towards developing monolithic EMLMs that can handle the entire pipeline from processing input instructions to executing complex tasks, simplifying development and enhancing overall efficiency. This will require seamless integration of all modalities and functionalities within a single, powerful model.
- **Dynamic Real-World Data Integration**: A continued focus on collecting and integrating more real-world, dynamic data, specifically leveraging a wider array of multimodal sensors beyond just cameras, will be essential for improving the practical applicability and robustness of EMLMs in unpredictable physical environments.
- **Safety, Reliability, and Interpretability**: As EMLMs are deployed in safety-critical applications like autonomous driving and robotics, research must prioritize ensuring their safety, reliability, and interpretability of decision-making processes, along with robust risk mitigation strategies.

#### 5.3. Conclusion

The paper "Exploring Embodied Multimodal Large Models: Development, Datasets, and Future Directions" offers a timely and invaluable contribution to the rapidly advancing field of artificial intelligence. By systematically reviewing the integration of large language and vision models with embodied capabilities, the authors effectively map out the current landscape of EMLMs, covering their foundational models, advancements in embodied perception, navigation, and interaction, the role of simulation environments, and the critical importance of diverse datasets.

The review highlights that EMLMs represent a significant leap towards creating more intelligent, adaptable, and autonomous systems capable of bridging the gap between abstract AI reasoning and real-world physical engagement. While acknowledging the remarkable progress, the authors candidly address the multifaceted challenges that still persist, including issues of precise cross-modal alignment, significant computational resource demands, limitations in generalization across diverse domains, the complexity of handling temporal information, and the inherent difficulties in acquiring high-quality, diverse, real-world multimodal datasets. Furthermore, the report emphasizes the crucial ethical considerations concerning bias and the need for robust safety and reliability measures as these technologies move towards broader application.

Looking forward, the paper provides a clear and inspiring vision for future research, advocating for advanced cross-modal pre-training, greater reliance on self-supervised learning, deeper integration with reinforcement learning, and the ambitious pursuit of end-to-end large models. This comprehensive analysis serves as a vital reference for researchers, identifying critical gaps and outlining promising directions that will undoubtedly shape the next generation of intelligent agents and robotic systems, driving significant advancements across various domains.
