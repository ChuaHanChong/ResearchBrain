# Plan-and-Solve Prompting: Improving Zero-Shot Chain-of-Thought Reasoning by Large Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Plan-and-Solve (PS) Prompting, developed by researchers at Singapore Management University and collaborators, enhances zero-shot Chain-of-Thought reasoning in Large Language Models by guiding them through a two-phase process of planning and execution. This method reduces calculation and missing-step errors, achieving accuracy comparable to few-shot methods on arithmetic reasoning tasks and outperforming Zero-shot CoT across multiple domains.

## Problem

- Zero-shot Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting, while reducing manual effort, still suffers from specific reasoning errors in Large Language Models (LLMs).
- Key error types identified in Zero-shot CoT include calculation errors (e.g., numerical mistakes), missing-step errors (e.g., omitted intermediate reasoning), and semantic misunderstanding errors.
- Existing zero-shot methods often produce incomplete or incorrect reasoning paths, limiting their reliability for complex multi-step problems.

## Method

- A novel ==zero-shot prompting== strategy, **Plan-and-Solve (PS) Prompting**, is introduced, which guides the LLM to first devise a ==plan== and then systematically execute it.
- An enhanced variant, **PS+ Prompting**, extends PS by explicitly instructing the LLM to ==extract relevant variables and numerals== and pay attention to calculations and commonsense for intermediate results.
- The approach uses a ==two-step prompting process==: generating structured reasoning with PS/PS+ triggers, followed by a second ==answer-extraction prompt== to extract the final numerical answer from the generated text.

## Results

- Plan-and-Solve (PS+) Prompting significantly outperforms Zero-shot CoT, achieving an average accuracy gain of **6.3%** across arithmetic reasoning datasets and demonstrating improvements in commonsense and symbolic reasoning.
- Zero-shot PS+ achieves accuracy competitive with or surpassing few-shot methods like Manual-CoT and Auto-CoT on several arithmetic benchmarks (e.g., **76.7%** average accuracy for PS+ vs. **77.6%** for Manual-CoT).
- PS+ prompting effectively reduces specific error types: calculation errors decreased from **7%** to **5%**, and missing-step errors decreased from **12%** to **7%** compared to Zero-shot CoT.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large Language Models possess emergent planning abilities that can be effectively elicited and guided by explicit, structured prompts.
> - Detailed prompting instructions, such as asking for variable extraction and intermediate calculation attention, significantly improve the quality and accuracy of LLM-generated reasoning steps.
> - There is a strong negative correlation between the presence of clear variable definitions and reasoning plans in LLM output and the occurrence of calculation and missing-step errors.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancements in Large Language Models (LLMs) like GPT-3 have revolutionized Natural Language Processing (NLP), demonstrating impressive capabilities across a wide array of tasks. However, directly fine-tuning these models for downstream tasks is often challenging or impossible due to their proprietary nature and computational demands. Consequently, **prompting engineering** has emerged as a crucial paradigm for eliciting desired behaviors from LLMs without altering their internal parameters.

Within this landscape, the focus has shifted towards enabling LLMs to perform complex, multi-step reasoning. Initially, **few-shot Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting** (Wei et al., 2022b) showed remarkable success. This method involves providing LLMs with a few manually crafted examples that demonstrate the step-by-step reasoning process required to solve a problem. While effective, the manual effort of creating these demonstrations is a significant practical limitation.

To address this, **Zero-shot CoT prompting** (Kojima et al., 2022) was introduced, which simplifies the process by merely appending a trigger phrase like "Let’s think step by step" to the problem statement. Surprisingly, this simple approach enabled LLMs to generate reasoning steps and achieved performance comparable to few-shot CoT on many tasks, significantly reducing manual effort.

However, Zero-shot CoT is not without its flaws. As identified by this paper, it still suffers from specific error types: calculation errors, missing-step errors, and semantic misunderstanding errors. Other contemporary works aim to improve reasoning, such as **Program-of-Thought (PoT) prompting** (Chen et al., 2022), which leverages LLMs to generate executable code for numerical tasks, and **Self-Consistency (SC)** (Wang et al., 2022b), an ensemble method that generates multiple reasoning paths and uses majority voting for the final answer. **Auto-CoT** (Zhang et al., 2022) attempts to automate the selection of demonstration examples for few-shot CoT.

This paper directly positions itself as an advancement over Zero-shot CoT. By proposing "Plan-and-Solve (PS) Prompting," the authors aim to enhance the quality of generated reasoning and reduce specific error types prevalent in Zero-shot CoT, *while retaining its zero-shot nature*. This work contributes to the growing body of research focused on making LLM reasoning more robust, reliable, and efficient by leveraging smarter, more structured prompting strategies rather than relying on extensive fine-tuning or costly manual demonstrations. It aligns with the broader goal of enabling LLMs to exhibit more human-like cognitive processes, such as planning and problem decomposition.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to significantly improve the accuracy of Large Language Models (LLMs) on multi-step reasoning tasks when operating in a zero-shot setting. This is motivated by the recognized limitations of existing zero-shot Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting.

Specifically, the authors identify three prevalent pitfalls in Zero-shot CoT, based on an error analysis of arithmetic reasoning problems:
1. **Calculation errors (7%):** Errors occurring in numerical computations within the reasoning steps.
2. **Missing-step errors (12%):** Instances where the LLM omits crucial intermediate reasoning steps, leading to an incomplete or incorrect solution path.
3. **Semantic misunderstanding errors (27%):** Broader errors related to misinterpreting the problem statement or logical incoherence in the reasoning.

The core motivation is to address the first two categories of errors—calculation errors and missing-step errors—without reintroducing the manual effort required by few-shot CoT. By mitigating these specific issues, the authors aim to make zero-shot reasoning more reliable and competitive with, or even surpass, few-shot approaches. The ultimate goal is to enable LLMs to generate a higher-quality, more complete, and accurate reasoning process for complex problems, thus "unlocking" their inherent reasoning capabilities more effectively with minimal human intervention.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The paper proposes a novel zero-shot prompting strategy called **Plan-and-Solve (PS) Prompting**, along with an enhanced variant, **PS+ Prompting**, to address the identified shortcomings of Zero-shot CoT. The core idea revolves around guiding the LLM to adopt a more structured, two-phase problem-solving approach: first devising a plan, and then executing it.

The methodology is detailed in two steps:

#### 3.1. Step 1: Prompting for Reasoning Generation

This step focuses on constructing specialized prompt templates designed to elicit a more structured and accurate reasoning process from the LLM. The authors aim for templates that:
- Encourage LLMs to determine and accomplish subtasks.
- Guide LLMs to pay closer attention to calculations and intermediate results.

**a. Plan-and-Solve (PS) Prompting:**
Instead of the generic "Let’s think step by step" used in Zero-shot CoT, PS prompting introduces a more prescriptive instruction. The input prompt is structured as "Q:[X]. A:[T]", where [X] is the problem statement and [T] is the trigger instruction. For PS, [T] is:
"**Let’s first understand the problem and devise a plan to solve the problem. Then, let’s carry out the plan and solve the problem step by step.**"
This prompt explicitly guides the LLM to first articulate a plan (decomposing the task into smaller subtasks) and then systematically execute that plan. This approach is designed to mitigate "missing-step errors" by encouraging a more comprehensive and organized thought process.

**b. PS+ Prompting (Enhanced Plan-and-Solve):**
To further address "calculation errors" and improve the overall quality of generated reasoning, PS+ prompting extends the PS trigger sentence with more detailed instructions. The key additions are:
- "**extract relevant variables and their corresponding numerals**": This aims to ensure the LLM identifies and utilizes all necessary numerical information from the problem statement, reducing oversight and potential for missing relevant steps.
- "**calculate intermediate results (pay attention to calculation and commonsense)**": This explicitly instructs the LLM to perform calculations accurately and consider common sense during intermediate steps.

The comprehensive prompt for PS+ is:
"**Let’s first understand the problem, extract relevant variables and their corresponding numerals, and make a plan. Then, let’s carry out the plan, calculate intermediate variables (pay attention to correct numerical calculation and commonsense), solve the problem step by step, and show the answer.**"
This nuanced instruction set aims to guide the LLM towards more precise numerical processing and a more robust generation of reasoning steps.

The LLM (specifically GPT-3's `text-davinci-003` engine) generates the reasoning text based on these prompts, using a greedy decoding strategy (temperature 0).

#### 3.2. Step 2: Prompting for Answer Extraction

Similar to Zero-shot CoT, a second prompt is used to extract the final numerical answer from the reasoning text generated in Step 1. This prompt appends an answer extraction instruction, such as "Therefore, the answer (arabic numerals) is," to the initial prompt and the LLM's generated reasoning. This ensures the output is in the desired format for evaluation.

#### 3.3. Experimental Setup

- **LLM Backbone:** GPT-3 (175B parameters) specifically using the `text-davinci-003` engine.
- **Datasets:** Ten benchmark datasets spanning three reasoning categories:
 - **Arithmetic Reasoning:** GSM8K, AQuA, MultiArith, AddSub, SingleEq, SVAMP.
 - **Commonsense Reasoning:** CommonsenseQA, StrategyQA.
 - **Symbolic Reasoning:** Last Letter Concatenation, Coin Flip.
- **Baselines:**
 - **Zero-shot:** Zero-shot CoT, Zero-shot PoT.
 - **Few-shot:** Manual-CoT (8 hand-crafted examples for most math datasets), Auto-CoT (automatically selected examples).
- **Evaluation Metric:** Accuracy.
- **Analysis:** The authors conduct several in-depth analyses, including:
 - **Self-Consistency:** Evaluating PS+ performance with and without Self-Consistency (SC) to understand its robustness.
 - **Effect of Prompts:** Comparing the performance of different variations of the trigger sentences.
 - **Error Analysis:** Quantitatively and qualitatively assessing how PS+ impacts calculation, missing-step, and semantic misunderstanding errors.
 - **Correlation Analysis:** Examining the correlation between the existence of specific reasoning components (variable definition, plan, solution) in the LLM's output and the occurrence of different error types.
 - **Presence of Plans:** Verifying if the LLM indeed generates discernible plans when prompted by PS.

This comprehensive methodological approach allows the authors to rigorously evaluate the effectiveness of their proposed prompting strategies and gain insights into *why* they perform better.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of Plan-and-Solve (PS) and PS+ prompting across ten diverse datasets demonstrates significant improvements over existing zero-shot methods and competitive performance against few-shot baselines.

#### 4.1. Main Performance Results

- **Arithmetic Reasoning (Table 2):**
 - **Zero-shot PS+ vs. Zero-shot CoT:** PS+ consistently and substantially outperforms Zero-shot CoT across all six arithmetic datasets. The accuracy improvement ranges from 2.9% (GSM8K) to over 7% (e.g., AddSub, SingleEq), with an average accuracy gain of 6.3% over Zero-shot CoT.
 - **Zero-shot PS vs. Zero-shot CoT:** Even the simpler PS prompting (without detailed instructions) outperforms Zero-shot CoT across all datasets, showing a 2.5% average accuracy increase.
 - **PS(+) vs. Zero-shot PoT:** PS+ prompting outperforms Zero-shot Program-of-Thought (PoT) on five out of six arithmetic datasets, and PS prompting outperforms PoT on three. This is significant as PoT uses code execution for numerical problems, which is often considered more robust for calculations.
 - **Zero-shot PS+ vs. Few-shot Manual-CoT/Auto-CoT:** Remarkably, Zero-shot PS+ (76.7% average accuracy) achieved performance comparable to Manual-CoT (77.6%) and even slightly higher than Auto-CoT (75.9%). This indicates that zero-shot PS+ can nearly match or surpass few-shot methods that require hand-crafted or automatically selected demonstrations.

- **Commonsense Reasoning (Table 3):**
 - Zero-shot PS+ consistently outperforms Zero-shot CoT on both CommonsenseQA (71.9% vs. 65.2%) and StrategyQA (65.4% vs. 63.8%), although it still underperforms Few-Shot Manual-CoT.

- **Symbolic Reasoning (Table 4):**
 - On Last Letter Concatenation, Zero-shot PS+ (75.2%) outperforms both Manual-CoT (70.6%) and Zero-shot CoT (65.2%).
 - On Coin Flip, PS+ (99.6%) is very close to Manual-CoT (100.0%) and significantly better than Zero-shot CoT (96.8%).

#### 4.2. Analysis and Insights

- **Results of Prompting with Self-Consistency (Figure 4):** When combined with Self-Consistency (SC), PS+ prompting shows substantial improvements (e.g., from 58.7% to 73.7% on GSM8K). Critically, PS+ with SC consistently outperforms Zero-shot CoT with SC, indicating that the benefits of PS+ are additive to other ensemble methods.
- **Effect of Prompts (Table 5):** The analysis of different trigger sentences confirms that providing more detailed instructions significantly improves performance. Prompts that explicitly ask for variable extraction, intermediate calculations, and a plan (like PS and PS+) perform much better than simpler variations or those lacking planning components. This validates the hypothesis that detailed guidance helps LLMs generate higher-quality reasoning.
- **Error Analysis (Table 6):** PS+ prompting successfully reduces specific error types. It exhibits the lowest rates for:
 - **Calculation errors:** 5% for PS+ vs. 7% for Zero-shot CoT.
 - **Missing-step errors:** 7% for PS+ vs. 12% for Zero-shot CoT.
 Semantic misunderstanding errors remain comparable across methods, indicating this is a harder problem not fully addressed by the current approach.
- **Correlation Analysis of Generated Reasoning and Error Types (Figure 5):** A strong negative correlation was observed between the existence of variable definitions and reasoning plans in the generated text and the occurrence of calculation errors and missing-step errors. This empirically supports the authors' design choices: explicitly prompting for variables and a plan leads to fewer errors in these categories.
- **Exploring the Presence of Plans in PS Predictions:** A qualitative review showed that 90% of PS predictions indeed incorporated a discernible plan, confirming that LLMs, particularly advanced ones like GPT-3, possess emergent planning abilities that can be effectively triggered by appropriate prompts.

In summary, the main findings clearly demonstrate that the "Plan-and-Solve" prompting strategies, especially PS+, are highly effective in guiding LLMs to produce more accurate and complete reasoning steps in a zero-shot setting. This improvement is attributed to the explicit instructions for problem decomposition, variable extraction, and careful intermediate calculation, leading to a significant reduction in common reasoning errors.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Plan-and-Solve Prompting" strategies proposed in this paper offer significant contributions to the field of Large Language Models (LLMs) and their application in complex reasoning tasks, with notable potential impacts.

#### 5.1. Significance

- **Advancement in Zero-Shot Reasoning:** The work makes a substantial step forward in zero-shot reasoning, demonstrating that with carefully designed prompts, LLMs can achieve performance competitive with, or even superior to, few-shot methods that rely on labor-intensive manual demonstrations. This effectively lowers the barrier to entry for deploying LLMs for complex tasks, as it obviates the need for costly data annotation or extensive prompt engineering for examples.
- **Targeted Error Reduction:** By specifically addressing "missing-step errors" and "calculation errors," the research provides a concrete and effective method to enhance the reliability and accuracy of LLM-generated reasoning. The detailed error analysis and correlation studies provide valuable insights into *why* these prompting strategies work, fostering a deeper understanding of LLM capabilities and limitations.
- **Eliciting Emergent Capabilities:** The observation that LLMs exhibit strong planning abilities when prompted appropriately highlights an emergent capability that can be harnessed for more structured and robust problem-solving. This moves LLM reasoning beyond simple token-by-token generation towards more cognitive, human-like processes of decomposition and execution.
- **Generalizable Prompting Paradigm:** The core idea of "plan-and-solve" is intuitively appealing and broadly applicable. While demonstrated on reasoning tasks, the principle of guiding an LLM through a structured problem-solving flow could be extended to a wide range of other complex tasks, potentially improving coherence and accuracy across various NLP applications.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

- **Reduced Development Costs:** By enabling high-performance reasoning in a zero-shot setting, this research can significantly reduce the development time and costs associated with fine-tuning LLMs or creating extensive few-shot demonstrations for specific applications. This makes LLM technology more accessible and practical for a broader range of users and enterprises.
- **Improved LLM Reliability in Critical Applications:** For domains requiring high accuracy and robust reasoning (e.g., scientific problem-solving, medical diagnosis support, financial analysis), the reduction in calculation and missing-step errors translates into more trustworthy LLM outputs.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** This work opens several avenues for future research. The remaining challenge of "semantic misunderstanding errors" is explicitly identified, providing a clear direction for subsequent investigations. Furthermore, the concept of refining the LLM's generated "plan" could lead to more interactive and self-correcting reasoning systems.
- **Catalyst for Advanced Prompt Engineering:** The success of PS+ prompting, despite its simplicity, underscores the power of specific, well-thought-out instructions. It could inspire further innovation in prompt engineering, pushing the boundaries of what LLMs can achieve with minimal external data.

#### 5.3. Limitations

The authors transparently acknowledge two main limitations:
1. **Prompt Sensitivity and Design Effort:** Despite being "zero-shot" in terms of examples, designing the optimal prompt for PS+ still requires careful effort and an understanding of LLM sensitivities to phrasing.
2. **Unaddressed Semantic Misunderstanding Errors:** While successful in mitigating calculation and missing-step errors, the proposed methods do not significantly improve performance on semantic misunderstanding errors. This suggests a deeper challenge in LLM comprehension that current prompting techniques alone may not fully resolve.

Despite these limitations, the paper delivers a valuable contribution by demonstrating that principled prompt design can unlock more sophisticated reasoning abilities in LLMs, paving the way for more reliable and efficient AI systems.
