# Turning Adaptation into Assets: Cross-Domain Bridging for Online Vision-Language Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> The Inter-Domain BridgE with Historical Assets (IDEA) framework improves Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) by turning adaptation into continuously accumulated and composed "knowledge assets," addressing catastrophic forgetting and negative transfer in dynamic environments. It consistently outperformed existing test-time adaptation methods on REVERIE, R2R, and R2R-CE, showing up to +2.8% SPL gain and being significantly more inference-efficient.

## Problem

- Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) agents exhibit performance degradation in non-stationary environments with visual and spatial layout shifts.
- Current Test-Time Adaptation (TTA) methods for VLN suffer from catastrophic forgetting, where online updates overwrite valuable historical adaptation knowledge.
- These methods also experience negative transfer, applying adaptations learned in one context inappropriately to dissimilar ones.

## Method

- Reconceptualizes adaptation as the continuous accumulation of "soft visual prompts" as lightweight, context-aware assets in a dynamic library.
- Employs a multi-layer, Fisher-guided alignment mechanism to optimize prompt assets, ensuring they capture task-essential knowledge and are transferable.
- Constructs a "training-free adaptation bridge" for novel domains by optimally composing existing assets using convex-hull projection, with a closed-form solution for mixture weights.

## Results

- Consistently surpassed existing Test-Time Adaptation methods across REVERIE, R2R, and R2R-CE benchmarks, e.g., achieving **+2.5%** SR and **+2.8%** SPL for HAMT/DUET on REVERIE Test Unseen.
- Exhibited high inference efficiency, being approximately 20 times faster than FeedTTA and 15 times faster than ATENA, enabled by the training-free adaptation bridge.
- Showcased asset transferability with immediate performance gains from pre-loaded assets and improved performance with multi-source or cross-task asset aggregation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Online adaptation can be reframed as accumulating and composing transferable "knowledge assets" (soft visual prompts) rather than performing isolated, transient updates.
> - A sensitivity-aware weighting mechanism, guided by Fisher Information, helps distill task-essential knowledge into assets, improving their transferability and preventing overfitting.
> - A training-free adaptation bridge, constructed by optimally projecting target domain statistics onto the convex hull of historical assets, offers efficient and robust adaptation for novel environments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) is an embodied AI task requiring an agent to interpret natural language instructions and navigate a 3D environment based on visual observations to reach a goal. A significant challenge in deploying VLN agents in real-world settings is their performance degradation in non-stationary environments, which exhibit visual and spatial layout differences from training data, and rapid intra-episode shifts.

Existing methods to mitigate these "distribution shifts" often fall under Test-Time Adaptation (TTA), where models adapt to target distributions via online updates during inference. These TTA approaches generally categorize into:
- **Uncertainty self-training:** Methods that leverage entropy measures or consistency constraints to drive step-wise updates, aiming to improve action selection.
- **Feedback-driven adaptation:** Approaches that incorporate corrective signals from large foundation models (FMs) or human feedback to guide adaptation, often through reinforcement learning.

However, the paper identifies two limitations within existing TTA methods for VLN, particularly in the context of online, streaming data:
1. **Catastrophic forgetting:** Current methods often treat online updates as transient, isolated events. This can lead to new adaptations overwriting previously learned knowledge, preventing the agent from leveraging historical experience when revisiting similar environments.
2. **Negative transfer:** Without explicit modeling of relationships between different environmental contexts (domains), updates derived from one scene may be inappropriately applied to a dissimilar one. This can introduce mismatched priors, leading to performance degradation.

These issues result in inefficient adaptation and hinder the accumulation of transferable knowledge across diverse and dynamic environments. This work, named Inter-Domain BridgE with Historical Assets (IDEA), aims to address these identified bottlenecks by introducing a framework that reconceptualizes adaptation as the continuous accumulation and composition of knowledge assets. The goal is to move beyond transient, isolated updates to a system that preserves and reuses historical adaptations, thereby enhancing the agent's ability to generalize and perform robustly in novel settings with reduced computational overhead.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) agents to adapt effectively and robustly to diverse and dynamic environments encountered during real-world deployment. The paper aims to overcome the limitations of current Test-Time Adaptation (TTA) methods in VLN, specifically catastrophic forgetting and negative transfer.

The motivation stems from the inherent nature of real-world navigation:
- **Environmental Variability:** Real-world environments rarely conform to the controlled conditions of training data. Agents frequently encounter unseen visual appearances, novel spatial layouts, and rapid distribution shifts within a single navigation episode.
- **Performance Degradation:** These dynamic shifts cause significant performance drops for pre-trained VLN agents, compromising their reliability and practical utility.
- **Inefficiency of Current TTA:** Existing TTA methods, while attempting to adapt models online, typically treat each adaptation instance as an independent, ephemeral task. This "series of isolated domain-transfer tasks" approach leads to critical drawbacks:
 - **Catastrophic Forgetting:** Online updates modify a fixed set of model parameters. Without mechanisms to preserve prior adaptations, valuable knowledge acquired from earlier encountered environments is overwritten, preventing the agent from benefiting from past experiences upon re-encountering similar situations.
 - **Negative Transfer:** Lacking an understanding of domain relationships, an agent might apply adaptations learned in one context to another, dissimilar context. Such "blind application" of priors can be detrimental, leading to mismatched assumptions and further degrading navigation performance.
- **Computational Overhead of Feedback-Driven Methods:** While some TTA methods leverage external feedback (e.g., from foundation models or humans), their high inference costs can render them impractical for real-time deployment on resource-constrained devices.

To address these motivations, the key objectives of the IDEA framework are:
1. To reformulate online VLN adaptation from isolated updates into a continuous process of knowledge accumulation and composition.
2. To develop a mechanism for distilling and storing historical adaptation knowledge as lightweight, context-aware, and plug-and-play "assets."
3. To design a strategy for efficiently retrieving and composing these assets to form an "adaptation bridge," providing a training-free shortcut to new target domains.
4. To ensure that the adaptation process is sensitive to task-essential knowledge, preventing overfitting to spurious domain-specific noise.
5. To demonstrate the framework's ability to provide stable performance gains and accelerate adaptation across various VLN benchmarks and model architectures, with minimal computational overhead.

By achieving these objectives, the research seeks to enhance the practical value and reliable deployment of VLN agents in dynamic, real-world settings.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, Inter-Domain BridgE with Historical Assets (IDEA), is designed to transform online adaptation in VLN into an accumulation and composition of transferable knowledge assets. The methodology encompasses two core designs: the construction of a domain asset library with sensitivity-aware alignment and a cross-domain bridge construction mechanism via convex-hull projection.

#### 3.1. Turning Adaptation into Assets

IDEA reformulates adaptation as the continuous accumulation of portable assets, aiming to store and reuse knowledge rather than performing isolated updates.

- **Soft Visual Prompts:** Adaptation knowledge is encoded as `L` learnable soft prompt tokens `P`. These prompts, `P ∈ R^(L×C)`, are injected into the model by concatenating them with the visual tokens `V_t` extracted from the environment graph at navigation step `t`. The augmented tokens `~V_t = [P; V_t]` are then fed into the cross-modal fusion module. This approach allows the prompts to modulate visual-language alignment without altering the pre-trained policy's frozen backbone parameters, facilitating a "plug-and-play" mechanism.

- **Multi-Layer Alignment:** To ensure assets are reusable and effectively bridge distribution gaps, a hierarchical alignment mechanism is introduced. It regularizes the prompt-augmented representations to match precomputed source-domain statistics (`Γ^(ℓ)_S = {μ^(ℓ)_S, σ^(ℓ)_S}`) across multiple fusion layers (`ℓ = 1..., M`). For each layer `ℓ`, the prompt `P` is optimized to minimize a moment-matching loss, `d^(ℓ)(P) = ∥μ^(ℓ)_S - μ^(ℓ)_t(P)∥₂ + ∥σ^(ℓ)_S - σ^(ℓ)_t(P)∥₂`, between the source statistics and the online-estimated statistics `Γ^(ℓ)_t(P)` of the current prompt-augmented features. The overall optimization objective sums these layer-wise losses, weighted by `α_ℓ`: `P* = arg min_P Σ α_ℓ d^(ℓ)(P)`.

- **Fisher-Guided Weighting:** To enhance the transferability of assets and prevent overfitting to domain-specific noise, the layer-wise weights `α_ℓ` are determined adaptively. This is achieved by assessing the sensitivity of each layer's representation to the policy's decision output. Instead of using the computationally expensive Hessian matrix, the Fisher Information Matrix `Φ(z)` is employed as a tractable proxy. The trace of `Φ(Z_t^(ℓ))` for layer `ℓ` (computed on un-prompted features) is used to update `α_ℓ` via an exponential moving average, emphasizing layers that significantly impact policy decisions and encode task-essential semantics.

- **Triplet-Structured Asset:** Each adaptation knowledge unit is formalized as a triplet asset `A = {P*, Γ, u}`:
 - `P*`: The learned soft prompt that modulates the policy.
 - `Γ = {μ(Z_t), σ(Z_t)}`: Feature statistics from the final fusion layer, computed *without* prompt injection, serving as a prompt-independent descriptor of the environment.
 - `u = Ent(π_θ,P*)`: An uncertainty score, defined as the prediction entropy with the adapted prompt, indicating the reliability of the adapted behavior. These assets populate a dynamic asset library.

#### 3.2. Cross-Domain Bridge Construction

To exploit the accumulated assets, IDEA avoids brittle hard retrieval and instead constructs a soft "bridge" for adaptation.

- **Convex-Hull Projection Fitting:** When encountering a new domain, IDEA attempts to identify a training-free shortcut by composing existing assets. It formulates this as finding the optimal mixture weights `w ∈ R^K` (where `K` is the number of assets in the library) that project the target domain's statistics `Γ_t` onto the convex hull spanned by the historical assets. This yields a composite bridge prompt `P_b(w) = Σ w_j P_j` and induced bridge statistics `Γ_b(w) = Σ w_j Γ_j`.

- **Constrained Optimization:** The optimal mixture weights `w` are determined by minimizing the 2-Wasserstein distance between the target statistics `Γ_t` and the composite bridge statistics `Γ_b(w)`. An uncertainty-aware regularizer `λ Σ u_j w_j^2` is added to penalize large weights on unreliable assets, where `u_j` is the uncertainty score of asset `A_j`. The optimization problem is `min_w W(Γ_t, Γ_b(w)) + λ Σ u_j w_j^2`, subject to `1^T w = 1` and `w ≥ 0`.

- **Closed-Form Solution:** To avoid iterative solvers and ensure efficiency, a closed-form solution for the optimal weights `w*` is derived using Karush–Kuhn–Tucker (KKT) conditions. This transforms the problem into a linearly constrained quadratic program, solvable via matrix operations: `w* = H⁻¹(g - ν1)`, where `H = A^T A + λU` (regularized Hessian based on asset statistics and uncertainties) and `g = A^T b` (projection of target onto asset basis). This analytical solution provides a training-free adaptation bridge.

#### 3.3. Overall Procedure

At each navigation step, IDEA dynamically decides whether to use the pre-constructed bridge or optimize a new asset:
1. The optimal weights `w` are computed to construct the bridge prompt `P_b(w)`.
2. The statistics discrepancy (`d_p` with bridge vs. `d_0` without bridge) is measured.
3. If the bridge effectively reduces the discrepancy (i.e., `d_p < τ * d_0`, where `τ` is a threshold), the domain is considered "covered," and `P_b(w)` is applied for inference.
4. Otherwise (novel domain), a new prompt is optimized using the multi-layer alignment and Fisher-guided weighting. This newly optimized prompt forms a new asset `A*`.
5. `A*` is added to the asset library. If the library exceeds its capacity `K_max`, `A*` is merged with its nearest neighbor in the library to maintain a fixed budget.

#### 3.4. Theoretical Analysis

The paper includes a theoretical analysis (detailed in Appendix A) demonstrating that the uncertainty-aware adaptation bridge formulation stably reduces generalization risk on novel target domains. It shows that the mixture weights derived are optimal within the convex hull of historical assets and that the closed-form solution is Lipschitz-stable to perturbations in estimated statistics, implying robustness to noise.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of IDEA was conducted on three VLN benchmarks: REVERIE, R2R (discrete environments), and R2R-CE (continuous environments), using four base models (HAMT, DUET, BEVBert, ETPNav). The experimental results demonstrate IDEA's consistent performance and efficiency advantages.

- **Superior Performance on Diverse Benchmarks:**
 - **REVERIE (Table 1):** IDEA consistently outperformed existing TTA methods across Val Seen, Val Unseen, and Test Unseen splits. Notably, on the challenging Test Unseen split, IDEA was the only method to achieve positive gains across all metrics (e.g., +2.5% SR and +2.8% SPL for HAMT/DUET base models). For instance, with DUET, IDEA achieved an SR of 55.12% and SPL of 39.84% on Test Unseen, compared to the baseline DUET's 52.51% SR and 36.06% SPL.
 - **R2R (Table 2) & R2R-CE (Table 3):** IDEA consistently enhanced performance across both discrete (R2R) and continuous (R2R-CE) environments, improving two different base models (DUET/BEVBert for R2R, BEVBert/ETPNav for R2R-CE). On R2R, IDEA boosted SR by approximately +2% and SPL by +3% compared to baselines on various splits. Similar consistent gains were observed on R2R-CE.

- **Inference Efficiency:** IDEA demonstrated efficiency in terms of inference time. For example, on REVERIE (Table 1), IDEA's inference time was significantly lower than methods like FSTTA (e.g., 245.8ms vs. 613.4ms for HAMT) and orders of magnitude faster than feedback-driven approaches (e.g., ~300ms vs. ~7000ms for FeedTTA, Table 7 in Appendix B.2). This efficiency is attributed to the training-free adaptation bridge, which bypasses iterative optimization for a majority of incoming domains.

- **Asset Transferability:**
 - **Training-free Gain (Table 4):** Shared assets from the library consistently provided immediate performance improvements without any additional online adaptation steps.
 - **Synergy with Adaptation:** Combining pre-loaded assets with online adaptation yielded the highest performance, indicating a complementary relationship.
 - **Scaling Benefit:** Aggregating assets from multiple source environments (multi-source setting) further improved performance (e.g., +1.2% SR and +1.0% SPL), suggesting that a richer library leads to more robust bridge construction.
 - **Cross-Task Transferability (Table 6, Appendix B.1):** Assets demonstrated versatility even when transferred between tasks with significant semantic differences (REVERIE ↔ R2R), providing performance gains.

- **Comparison with Feedback-Driven Methods (Tables 7, 8, 9, Appendix B.2):** IDEA achieved competitive or superior performance compared to state-of-the-art feedback-driven methods, particularly on challenging unseen splits. This was accomplished without relying on external guidance (e.g., from large foundation models or human feedback), which incur significant computational overhead and latency. IDEA was approximately 20 times faster than FeedTTA and 15 times faster than ATENA.

- **Ablation Studies and Component Effectiveness (Table 5):**
 - **Fisher-guided weighting:** Showed notable improvements (e.g., +3.5% SR for DUET on Val Unseen) compared to traditional layer-wise decay weighting, validating its role in capturing transferable knowledge.
 - **Closed-form bridge:** Demonstrated clear superiority over hard nearest-neighbor asset retrieval, underscoring the importance of optimal projection for robust adaptation.
 - The complete IDEA framework consistently achieved the best navigation performance, demonstrating the complementary effects of its components.

- **Sensitivity Analysis (Figure 3, Figure 6 in Appendix C):** Performance improved with increasing prompt length (optimal `L=4`) and library capacity (`K_max=32`), eventually plateauing. Similarly, an optimal regularization strength `λ=0.4` and coverage threshold `τ=0.7` were identified, balancing adaptation quality and efficiency. The memory overhead for the asset library was found to be negligible (0.58 MB).

- **Qualitative Results (Figure 5):** Qualitative comparisons illustrated that IDEA significantly enhanced the agent's ability to identify discriminative landmarks and ground fine-grained textual cues, leading to successful navigation in complex scenarios where baseline models failed.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces IDEA, a novel framework that addresses critical limitations in Test-Time Adaptation (TTA) for Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) agents operating in dynamic, real-world environments. The significance and potential impact of this work are multifaceted:

- **Overcoming Core TTA Challenges:** IDEA directly tackles the problems of catastrophic forgetting and negative transfer, which hinder the long-term effectiveness of online adaptation in VLN. By transforming adaptation knowledge into reusable assets, the framework enables agents to accumulate and leverage past experiences, rather than discarding them after each adaptation instance.

- **Novel Paradigm for Adaptation:** The paper proposes a "knowledge-as-asset" paradigm, shifting the understanding of online adaptation from transient, isolated updates to a continuous process of asset accumulation and composition. This structured approach facilitates "plug-and-play reuse," making adaptation more systematic and efficient.

- **Enhanced Efficiency for Real-time Deployment:** A key contribution is the "training-free adaptation bridge," which provides a rapid shortcut to new domains by optimally composing historical assets. This mechanism significantly reduces computational overhead and inference latency compared to iterative online optimization methods or resource-intensive feedback-driven approaches (e.g., those relying on large foundation models). Such efficiency is crucial for deploying intelligent agents on resource-constrained edge devices like domestic service robots, enabling practical, real-time operation.

- **Improved Generalization and Robustness:** Through its Fisher-guided weighting scheme and convex-hull projection, IDEA ensures that the adaptation process captures transferable, task-essential knowledge while mitigating overfitting to domain-specific noise. This leads to superior and more stable generalization performance across diverse VLN benchmarks, including both discrete and continuous environments, and various base model architectures.

- **Potential for Scalable Knowledge Sharing:** The concept of an asset library, with demonstrated cross-domain and cross-task transferability, opens avenues for collaborative intelligence. Aggregating assets from multiple agents or diverse deployment scenarios could lead to continuously improving, more robust collective knowledge, potentially bypassing "cold-start" phases for new agents and fostering broader collaboration.

- **Contribution to Sustainable AI:** By frequently leveraging a training-free adaptation bridge and minimizing the need for iterative gradient updates during inference, IDEA promotes more energy-efficient AI practices. This aligns with the broader goals of developing environmentally responsible and sustainable autonomous systems.

- **Inspiration for Future Research:** This work lays a foundation for future research in lifelong experience accumulation and the development of standardized, generalized asset protocols. The identified limitations, such as focusing on single-agent benchmarks and asset coupling with specific feature spaces, provide clear directions for extending the framework to multi-agent scenarios and architecture-agnostic asset designs.

In summary, IDEA presents a significant step towards developing more intelligent, adaptable, and efficient VLN agents, with broad implications for real-world robotic navigation and the broader field of embodied AI.
