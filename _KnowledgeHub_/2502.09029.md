# MTDP: A Modulated Transformer based Diffusion Policy Model

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed MTDP, a Modulated Transformer based Diffusion Policy, that enhances robot manipulation by improving condition integration through a novel Modulated Attention module. This approach yielded a 12% higher success rate on the Toolhang task and an average 4% increase across six tasks, while also reducing inference time by 40% using DDIM.

## Problem

- Existing Transformer-based diffusion policies struggle with effectively integrating guiding conditions (e.g., visual observations, timesteps) into the action generation process.
- Traditional Behavior Cloning (BC) and simpler Diffusion Policy (DP) models exhibit limitations in generalization and capturing diverse behavior patterns for complex robot tasks.
- Diffusion models, particularly those based on DDPM, often require a large number of iterative steps for action generation, leading to slow inference times unsuitable for real-time robot control.

## Method

- The authors propose ==Modulated Transformer Diffusion Policy== (**MTDP**) and Modulated UNet Diffusion Policy (MUDP), both incorporating a Modulated Attention module to improve condition integration.
- The Modulated Attention module (specifically M-SelfAttention configuration) modulates self-attention layers and feedforward networks with guiding parameters while also using cross-attention for direct conditional input.
- The research explores integrating Denoising Diffusion Implicit Models (DDIM) into the policy to reduce the number of sampling steps and accelerate action sequence generation.

## Results

- MTDP achieved a **12%** higher success rate on the Toolhang task and an average **4%** increase across six manipulation tasks compared to baseline Transformer-based diffusion policies.
- MUDP, utilizing the Modulated Attention module, matched or exceeded the performance of the baseline DP-UNet across all evaluated tasks, affirming the module's generality.
- Integrating DDIM allowed MTDP-I and MUDP-I to reduce action generation timesteps by **40%** (from 100 to 60) while maintaining performance competitive with or surpassing existing baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Traditional Transformer cross-attention alone is insufficient for fully leveraging guiding conditions in diffusion policies for complex robot manipulation tasks.
> - Modulating internal attention mechanisms and feedforward networks with conditional information, alongside standard cross-attention, significantly enhances the decoder's ability to utilize guiding conditions.
> - The proposed Modulated Attention module is architecturally versatile, demonstrating improved performance not only in Transformer-based models but also when applied to UNet architectures.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper is situated within the field of robot manipulation, specifically addressing challenges in imitation learning and policy generation. Behavior Cloning (BC) has traditionally been a straightforward approach for robot control, mapping states directly to actions based on expert demonstrations. However, BC methods often exhibit limitations such as poor generalization and a tendency to learn overly simplistic behavior patterns from data.

To overcome these issues, Diffusion Policy (DP) has emerged as a method that combines diffusion models with BC. DP utilizes iterative denoising, guided by visual inputs, to generate diverse and executable action sequences. This approach enables robots to capture multiple behavior patterns from demonstration data, thereby enhancing generalization capabilities and achieving higher success rates in manipulation tasks. Diffusion models themselves have seen rapid advancements, with key architectures like Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPM) and Denoising Diffusion Implicit Models (DDIM) demonstrating robust and diverse generative capabilities, initially in image synthesis and now extending to other domains.

The Transformer architecture, renowned for its scalability and capacity, has significantly impacted Natural Language Processing (NLP) and Computer Vision (CV), leading to large models like Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs). More recently, Transformers have been adapted for robotics, including applications in diffusion policies. Previous work, such as Diffusion Transformer (DIT), has explored Transformer-based diffusion models. However, when applied to diffusion policies for robot control, Transformer-based models have encountered a specific limitation: difficulty in effectively integrating guiding conditions with the main input, which often results in suboptimal performance.

This research specifically addresses this integration challenge within Transformer-based diffusion policies. It also explores the generality of its proposed architectural improvements by applying them to the UNet architecture, another commonly used model in diffusion policies. Furthermore, the paper contributes to the efficiency aspect of diffusion models by investigating the use of DDIM as an alternative to DDPM, aiming to reduce generation time while maintaining performance. The work contributes to the growing body of research on generalist robot policies, such as OCTO and RDT, which leverage Transformer-based diffusion models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the performance of robot manipulation tasks by improving how Transformer architectures integrate guiding conditions within a diffusion policy framework. The authors aim to develop a novel model that effectively leverages the high capacity of Transformers while overcoming their existing limitations in condition integration for robotic tasks.

The motivation for this work stems from several observations:

- **Ineffective Guiding Condition Integration in Transformers:** Existing Transformer-based models within Diffusion Policy (DP) exhibit poor performance in robot manipulation tasks. This is attributed to the traditional Transformer decoder's reliance on cross-attention for condition fusion, which the authors identify as insufficient for fully utilizing the guiding conditional features (e.g., image observations, timestep information). This limitation prevents Transformer models from realizing their full potential in complex robotic tasks where precise conditional guidance is crucial.
- **Leveraging Transformer's Capacity:** Given the success of Transformers in other high-capacity generative applications, there is a strong motivation to adapt their architectural strengths more effectively for action generation in robotics. The goal is to design a Transformer-based model that can effectively capture multiple behavior patterns and generate diverse action sequences, thereby improving generalization and task success rates.
- **Enhancing Generative Output Quality:** By improving the integration of guiding conditions, the authors aim to increase the quality of the action trajectories generated by the diffusion model. Higher quality outputs are expected to translate directly into higher robot task success rates.
- **Investigating Architectural Universality:** To demonstrate that the proposed architectural improvements are not confined to a specific model type, the authors are motivated to apply their core modification, the Modulated Attention module, to another prevalent architecture in diffusion policies: UNet. This aims to verify the broad applicability and versatility of their design.
- **Improving Generation Efficiency:** Diffusion models, particularly those based on DDPM, often require a significant number of iterative steps for action generation, which can be computationally intensive and time-consuming. The authors are motivated to explore alternative diffusion models, specifically DDIM, to reduce the number of sampling steps and accelerate the generation process without substantially compromising performance. This addresses practical concerns regarding real-time robot control and deployment efficiency.

In summary, the research seeks to develop more robust, efficient, and versatile diffusion policy models for robot manipulation by focusing on architectural innovations for condition integration and exploring faster generative processes.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on proposing a novel Transformer-based architecture, Modulated Transformer Diffusion Policy (MTDP), and a UNet-based counterpart, Modulated UNet Diffusion Policy (MUDP), both incorporating a key innovation: the Modulated Attention module.

**A. Model Architectures**

- **MTDP (Modulated Transformer Diffusion Policy):** This model is a Transformer-based architecture designed for robot diffusion policy. It maintains the traditional encoder-decoder structure of a Transformer. The encoder, responsible for processing guiding conditions (image and timestep features), remains unchanged. The core modification occurs in the decoder, where the standard multi-head self-attention, multi-head cross-attention, and feedforward neural network structure are replaced by the proposed Modulated Attention module.
- **MUDP (Modulated UNet Diffusion Policy):** To assess the generality of Modulated Attention, it is also applied to the UNet architecture. In MUDP, both the upsampling and downsampling modules of the UNet are modified. Specifically, one of the two conditional convolution blocks within each of these modules is replaced by a Modulated Attention block, while the rest of the UNet structure is preserved.

**B. Modulated Attention Module**

The Modulated Attention module is the central contribution, designed to better integrate guiding conditions. The input to this module includes features from the decoder's input and outputs from the encoder (which encapsulate guiding conditions like image features). The authors explored four configurations for this module through an ablation study:

1. **M-SelfAttention:** In this configuration, encoder outputs are passed through a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) to generate modulation parameters. These parameters then modulate both the self-attention layers and feedforward neural network layers. Additionally, the cross-attention layers directly receive the guiding conditions from the encoder. This structure allows both self-attention and cross-attention to be influenced by the guiding conditions.
2. **M-CrossAttention:** This structure modulates only the cross-attention layers and feedforward neural network layers using guiding parameters. The cross-attention layers also directly receive guidance from the encoder. The self-attention layers, however, remain unmodulated by the guiding conditions.
3. **DIT-SelfAttention:** This configuration is inspired by the Diffusion Transformer (DIT) architecture. It only includes modulated self-attention layers, and specifically lacks a cross-attention layer, differing from M-SelfAttention in its primary role within the Transformer decoder.
4. **DIT-CrossAttention:** Similar to DIT-SelfAttention, this structure's modulation module is shifted from self-attention to cross-attention. It only includes modulated cross-attention layers and lacks a self-attention layer.

Based on ablation study results, the **M-SelfAttention** configuration was selected as the optimal structure for Modulated Attention due to its superior performance, suggesting it most effectively utilizes guiding conditions.

**C. Overall Training and Generation Process**

The diffusion policy operates in two phases:

- **Training Phase:** The objective is to train a noise prediction network, $\epsilon_\theta(x_t, c_{obs}, t)$. Here, $x_t$ represents a noise vector, $c_{obs}$ denotes image observations (guiding conditions), and $t$ is the timestep. During training, noise $x_t$ is added to an expert action $a$ to form a noisy action $a_t$. The noise prediction network then predicts the noise $\hat{x}_t$ present in $a_t$, guided by $c_{obs}$ and a randomly selected timestep $t$. The network weights are updated by minimizing the Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss between the true noise $x_t$ and the predicted noise $\hat{x}_t$.
- **Generation Phase:** Once trained, the noise prediction network is used to iteratively denoise random Gaussian noise $x_T$ over a fixed number of timesteps $T$. Guided by the image conditions $c_{obs}$, this process progressively transforms the random noise into an executable action sequence for the robot.

**D. Diffusion Models (DDPM vs. DDIM)**

The standard diffusion model typically employed in diffusion policy is DDPM. The authors also investigate the use of DDIM, which offers potential for faster generation by reducing the number of sampling timesteps while maintaining quality. The mathematical formulations for the generative processes for DDPM and DDIM are provided.

**E. Experimental Setup**

- **Tasks:** Six robot manipulation tasks were used for evaluation: PushT, Can, Lift, Square, Toolhang, and Transport. These tasks encompass both 2D and 3D manipulation scenarios.
- **Baselines:**
 - DP-Transformer: The original diffusion policy based on the Transformer architecture.
 - DP-DIT: A custom-built baseline based on the DIT architecture, where the entire Transformer (encoder and decoder) is replaced with DIT components.
 - DP-UNet: The original diffusion policy based on the UNet architecture.
- **Parameters:**
 - DDPM-based models (MTDP, MUDP) used $T=100$ timesteps for generation.
 - DDIM-based models (MTDP-I, MUDP-I) used $T=60$ timesteps for generation.
- **Evaluation:** Results were averaged over three random seeds, and performance was measured by success rate.

**F. Ablation Studies**

- **Modulated Attention Structure:** All four proposed configurations of Modulated Attention were evaluated on PushT, Square, and Toolhang tasks using the Transformer architecture to determine the most effective design.
- **DDIM Timestep:** The impact of varying sampling timesteps (T=20, 40, 60, 80, 100) for DDIM was studied on PushT and Square tasks to identify an optimal balance between generation speed and performance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments yielded several key findings regarding the performance of the proposed MTDP and MUDP models, the effectiveness of Modulated Attention, and the benefits of integrating DDIM.

**A. Performance of MTDP (Transformer-based Diffusion Policy)**

- **Comparison with Baselines:** MTDP demonstrated improved performance compared to both the baseline DP-Transformer and the custom-built DP-DIT across nearly all six robotic manipulation tasks.
- **Significant Improvement:** A notable improvement of 12% in success rate was observed in the Toolhang experiment, with an average increase of 4% across all tasks compared to the baselines. This indicates that the MTDP architecture effectively integrates with diffusion models and enhances task execution.

**B. Performance of MUDP (UNet-based Diffusion Policy)**

- **Generality of Modulated Attention:** MUDP either matched or exceeded the performance of the baseline DP-UNet across all six experimental tasks. This finding supports the claim that the Modulated Attention module is versatile and applicable beyond Transformer architectures, improving the performance of UNet-based diffusion policies as well.

**C. Integration of DDIM for Faster Generation (MTDP-I and MUDP-I)**

- **Reduced Timesteps:** The MTDP-I and MUDP-I models, which utilize DDIM as the diffusion model, successfully reduced the generation timestep from 100 (used by DDPM) to 60. This represents a 40% reduction in the number of sampling iterations required for action generation.
- **Maintained Performance:** While MTDP-I showed a slight decrease in success rate compared to MTDP, it still outperformed existing Transformer-based diffusion policies. MUDP-I achieved performance comparable to MUDP and surpassed the DP-UNet baseline. This indicates that DDIM can significantly accelerate the generation process without substantial performance degradation, offering a balance between speed and effectiveness.

**D. Ablation Study on Modulated Attention Structure**

- **Importance of Cross-Attention:** The DIT-SelfAttention structure, which lacks a cross-attention layer, performed poorly on PushT and Toolhang tasks. This result underscores the necessity of the cross-attention mechanism for effectively integrating external guiding conditions.
- **Effectiveness of M-SelfAttention:** Among the four configurations tested, M-SelfAttention consistently demonstrated the best performance. This configuration integrates guiding parameters by modulating self-attention layers and feedforward networks, while also allowing cross-attention layers to directly receive guiding conditions. This approach was found to enable the decoder to leverage encoder-provided guiding conditions more fully, leading to enhanced generative performance.
- **Limitations of Other Configurations:** DIT-CrossAttention exhibited inconsistent performance with high variance. M-CrossAttention showed weaker performance in the Toolhang task, suggesting that modulating cross-attention layers (which already receive direct guidance) might introduce redundancy or less efficient condition integration compared to M-SelfAttention.

**E. Ablation Study on DDIM Timestep**

- **Optimal Timestep for DDIM:** The study on DDIM sampling timesteps (T) revealed that the average success rate remained stable when T was reduced from 100 down to 60. However, reducing T below 60 resulted in a decline in success rate. Therefore, T=60 was selected as the optimal balance point, providing a significant speedup (40% fewer steps) while maintaining experimental effectiveness.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in this paper offers several significant contributions and has the potential for impact in the field of robot learning and artificial intelligence.

- **Enhanced Robot Manipulation Performance:** The introduction of MTDP and its core Modulated Attention module directly addresses a limitation in existing Transformer-based diffusion policies. By demonstrating improved task success rates, particularly a 12% increase in the Toolhang task, the work provides a more effective architecture for complex robot manipulation. This could lead to more reliable and capable robotic systems in various applications, from industrial automation to service robotics.

- **Architectural Innovation for Conditional Generation:** The Modulated Attention module represents a novel approach to integrating guiding conditions within generative models. By modulating internal attention mechanisms and feedforward networks, it allows for a more comprehensive utilization of conditional information than traditional cross-attention alone. This architectural insight is not limited to robotics and could be adopted in other generative AI domains, such as image synthesis, video generation, or other forms of conditional data generation where Transformer-based diffusion models are employed.

- **Demonstrated Generality of the Approach:** The successful application of Modulated Attention to both Transformer (MTDP) and UNet (MUDP) architectures highlights its versatility. This suggests that the proposed mechanism for condition integration is broadly applicable across different neural network backbones commonly used in diffusion models, potentially simplifying future architectural designs and promoting wider adoption.

- **Improved Efficiency of Diffusion Policies:** The successful integration of DDIM, leading to MTDP-I and MUDP-I, significantly reduces the inference time (by 40% fewer sampling timesteps) while maintaining comparable performance. This efficiency gain is crucial for deploying diffusion policies in real-time robotic control scenarios, where quick decision-making is often required. It also makes these models more practical for applications with limited computational resources.

- **Foundation for Future Advanced AI Models:** The authors state that MTDP is scalable and can be extended to construct new Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model architectures. This indicates the potential for MTDP to serve as a foundational component in the development of more generalist robot policies that can understand and execute complex instructions across diverse environments, bridging the gap between perception, language, and action.

- **Contribution to Open-Source Embodied AI:** The work is part of the BrainCog Embot platform, an open-source Embodied AI framework. This contribution fosters collaborative research and development in brain-inspired AI and spiking neural networks, making the advancements accessible to a broader scientific community.
