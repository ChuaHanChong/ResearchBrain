# Beyond Success: Refining Elegant Robot Manipulation from Mixed-Quality Data via Just-in-Time Intervention

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework refines robot manipulation elegance from mixed-quality demonstration data by introducing a Just-in-Time Intervention (JITI) mechanism that guides pre-trained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies. The method boosts Elegant Success Rate (ESR) by 17.4 to 21.2 percentage points in simulation and 23.7 percentage points in real-world tasks, improving execution quality without altering the base VLA model.

## Problem

- Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, despite task success, often exhibit inconsistent execution elegance due to the mixed-quality nature of human demonstration data.
- Existing imitation learning and reinforcement learning methods struggle to reliably satisfy fine-grained Implicit Task Constraints (ITCs) beyond binary task completion, leading to suboptimal or unsafe robot behaviors.
- Fine-tuning large pre-trained VLA models is computationally expensive and risks catastrophic forgetting, while continuous online guidance is inefficient and can be invasive.

## Method

- A novel LIBERO-Elegant benchmark is introduced, featuring 8 tasks with explicit 'success' and 'elegance' criteria (based on Implicit Task Constraints) to quantify execution quality.
- A decoupled three-stage framework is proposed: a base generative VLA policy is trained, followed by an ==Elegance Critic== (using Calibrated Q-Learning) that evaluates both task success and elegance.
- A ==Just-in-Time Intervention== (**JITI**) mechanism is implemented at inference time, selectively applying multi-sample guidance from the Elegance Critic only at 'decision-critical moments' identified by Q-value fluctuations, ensuring efficient refinement.

## Results

- JITI-guided Elegance Critic improved the average Elegant Success Rate (ESR) by 17.4 to 21.2 percentage points for SmolVLA and Isaac GR00T N1.5 base policies in simulation.
- JITI reduced critic interventions by over **60%** compared to continuous full-guidance, demonstrating efficiency while maintaining higher ESR across tasks.
- The Elegance Critic, trained with task-specific elegance rewards, generalized effectively to unseen objects and tasks, improving ESR from **53.0%** to **68.6%**, and yielded a 23.7 percentage point ESR gain in real-world robotic tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Formalizing and explicitly rewarding 'elegant execution' via Implicit Task Constraints (ITCs) allows for a more nuanced evaluation and improvement of robotic manipulation quality beyond mere task success.
> - A non-invasive, lightweight Elegance Critic, trained with dense, task-specific elegance rewards, can effectively guide and refine large, pre-trained VLA policies without modifying their core parameters.
> - Just-in-Time Intervention (JITI) offers an efficient balance between performance gains and computational cost by providing targeted guidance only at critical junctures, enabling selective high-quality action selection.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has experienced significant advancements with the emergence of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. These models, trained on extensive datasets, enable robots to interpret natural language instructions and generalize across diverse scenes, moving towards general-purpose robotic intelligence. However, a persistent challenge in deploying these models in real-world scenarios is the variable quality of their learned policies. This variability is often attributed to the inherently mixed-quality nature of human demonstration data used for training. Such datasets frequently contain a spectrum of behaviors, from expert-level executions to inefficient movements and suboptimal actions, which standard behavioral cloning approaches tend to replicate. Consequently, policies derived from such data may achieve task success but often lack consistency in execution elegance, exhibiting behaviors like premature object releases or imprecise placements.

Current research efforts to address mixed-quality data in imitation learning can be broadly categorized. Data-centric approaches aim to improve the data distribution itself through filtering, re-weighting, or retrieval mechanisms. While these methods enhance the static dataset, they typically do not learn value functions that reason about long-term consequences. Online Reinforcement Learning (RL) seeks to refine policies through active interaction with the environment, often following a "pre-train and fine-tune" paradigm. However, online RL is resource-intensive, can be unsafe in real-world robotic contexts, and fine-tuning large VLA models can be invasive and unstable. Offline Reinforcement Learning (Offline RL) offers an alternative by improving policies solely from static datasets, thereby mitigating the costs and safety concerns of online interaction. A primary challenge in Offline RL is the out-of-distribution (OOD) problem, where policies may overestimate values for actions outside the training data's support. Algorithms like Conservative Q-Learning (CQL), Implicit Q-Learning (IQL), and Calibrated Q-Learning (Cal-QL) have been developed to address this through conservative objectives and value calibration.

Within the Offline RL paradigm for VLA models, two main strategies have emerged: invasive policy optimization, which fine-tunes pre-trained VLA models but risks catastrophic forgetting, and decoupled inference-time guidance. The latter approach, which is non-invasive, preserves the base VLA model and employs a lightweight critic to guide policy outputs during inference, thereby maintaining the generality of pre-trained policies.

This work situates itself within the decoupled inference-time guidance paradigm. Its distinguishing contribution is the explicit focus on "elegant execution," defined as the satisfaction of fine-grained, implicit task constraints (ITCs) often overlooked by conventional success metrics. To this end, the authors introduce a novel benchmark and a method that leverages a lightweight critic to provide "just-in-time" and non-invasive guidance, aiming to improve the *quality* of manipulation beyond mere task completion.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observation that while Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have achieved notable progress in general-purpose robotic manipulation, their learned policies frequently exhibit variable execution quality. This variability is attributed to the mixed-quality nature of human demonstrations used for training. Such demonstrations, despite leading to task success, often fail to consistently satisfy Implicit Task Constraints (ITCs), which are the latent rules governing *how* actions should be performed rather than merely *what* outcome should be achieved. Examples of ITCs include proper timing of releases, precise object placement, accurate pose alignment, and avoidance of unintended contacts. When these constraints are satisfied alongside task completion, the execution is considered "elegant." The core problem is that existing VLA models, trained via methods like behavioral cloning, inherit the full spectrum of behaviors present in the training data, leading to inconsistent expression of high-quality movements at inference time.

The key objectives of this work are detailed as follows:

- **Formalize Elegant Execution:** The research aims to shift the focus from a binary notion of task success to a more nuanced concept of execution quality. This involves formalizing "Elegant Execution" as the reliable satisfaction of Implicit Task Constraints (ITCs), which delineate the qualitative aspects of manipulation beyond merely achieving the final goal.
- **Develop a Benchmark for Execution Quality:** To systematically study and evaluate manipulation quality, the authors developed the LIBERO-Elegant Benchmark. This benchmark extends existing LIBERO tasks by introducing explicit success and "elegance" criteria. These criteria are designed to make the variation in execution quality measurable, facilitating the development and assessment of methods aimed at improving elegance.
- **Propose a Non-Invasive Refinement Framework:** The work seeks to introduce a method for improving execution elegance without modifying or retraining the underlying base VLA policy. This objective is motivated by the challenges associated with invasively fine-tuning large pre-trained models, such as computational cost and the risk of catastrophic forgetting. The proposed framework decouples execution (handled by the base VLA policy) from evaluation (handled by a lightweight Elegance Critic).
- **Implement a Just-in-Time Intervention Mechanism:** Drawing inspiration from how humans refine motor skills by focusing on "decision-critical moments," the research aims to develop a "Just-in-Time Intervention" (JITI) mechanism. This mechanism is designed to monitor the Elegance Critic's confidence and intervene selectively at critical states where subtle adjustments can significantly influence overall execution quality. The goal is to provide non-invasive, value-guided improvements on-demand rather than through continuous control or comprehensive policy retraining.
- **Demonstrate Effectiveness and Generalization:** A final objective is to validate the proposed framework through comprehensive experiments in both simulation and real-world environments. This includes demonstrating significant improvements in execution quality, confirming the effectiveness of the individual components (JITI and elegance-oriented reward calibration), and assessing the robustness and generalization capabilities of the Elegance Critic to unseen tasks and objects.

In summary, the motivation is to enable robots to reliably perform tasks with human-like dexterity and safety, moving beyond mere functionality to encompass the qualitative aspects of "how" a task is performed.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology is a decoupled, three-stage framework designed to refine robotic manipulation elegance from mixed-quality demonstrations without modifying the base Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policy.

#### 3.1. LIBERO-Elegant Benchmark

To facilitate the study of execution quality, the authors introduce the LIBERO-Elegant Benchmark, an extension of the existing LIBERO suite. This benchmark is curated to make execution quality measurable beyond binary task success.

- **Task Design and Evaluation Criteria:** It comprises eight manipulation tasks selected for their sensitivity to execution quality (e.g., precise placement, controlled insertion, collision-free pushing). Each task features:
 - **Success Criteria:** Standard goal conditions from the original LIBERO tasks.
 - **Elegance Criteria:** Four dimensions assessing execution quality:
 - **Task Sequence Integrity:** Adherence to intended action order and timing.
 - **Target Pose Accuracy:** Precision of final object position.
 - **Pose Alignment:** Correctness of final object orientation.
 - **Collision-Free Execution:** Avoidance of unintended contacts.
 These criteria are operationalized by defining task-specific Implicit Task Constraints (ITCs), such as "no premature release" or "centering the bowl on the plate within a tight tolerance." These ITCs are integrated into the LIBERO simulator's Behavioral Domain Definition Language (BDDL) via extended predicates (e.g., `IsGrasping`, `IsPreciselyOn`).
- **Annotation and Reward Design:** To supervise elegance evaluation, short temporal segments within demonstrations, where ITCs are most relevant, are manually annotated. Binary rewards ($r_t \in \{0, 1\}$) are assigned based on whether the constraints are satisfied. This process creates an "Elegance-Enriched Dataset" ($D_{elegant}$).
- **Data Statistics:** The benchmark includes 8 tasks, 327 demonstration episodes, and approximately 52.7K frames of data. 148 episodes were identified as high-quality, with ITCs annotated over single 25-frame windows.

#### 3.2. Decoupled Three-Stage Framework

The core of the methodology involves three distinct stages:

- **Stage 1: Base Generative Policy Training ($\pi_\theta$)**
 - A base generative policy ($\pi_\theta$) is trained on the mixed-quality demonstrations to learn the overall distribution of human actions. This stage does not differentiate between good and poor demonstrations.
 - **Implementation:** The policy is implemented as a flow-matching generative model, using a Transformer-based network ($v_\theta$). This network learns a continuous-time vector field that transforms a noise sample into a clean action.
 - **Objective:** The model is optimized by minimizing the mean squared error between the predicted and target vector fields, enabling it to generate diverse sets of candidate actions for a given state.

- **Stage 2: Offline Elegance Critic Training ($Q_\phi$)**
 - An Elegance Critic ($Q_\phi(s_t, a_t)$) is trained to estimate the expected cumulative return, incorporating both task success and behavioral elegance as encoded by the graded rewards from $D_{elegant}$.
 - **Architecture:** The critic leverages the frozen VLM backbone of the pre-trained VLA foundation model from Stage 1 to extract intermediate representations. A VLM-based refinement head then processes these features, concatenated with the action ($a_t$) and annotated reward ($r_t$), to estimate the value function.
 - **Calibrated Q-Learning (Cal-QL):** To learn from mixed-quality demonstrations, Cal-QL is adapted. This technique balances the critic's sensitivity to fine-grained elegance rewards with distributional conservatism, preventing overestimation on out-of-distribution or low-quality actions. The objective combines a Bellman consistency term with a calibration regularizer to ensure accurate and conservative value estimates.

- **Stage 3: Just-in-Time Intervention (JITI)**
 - This stage integrates the trained base policy ($\pi_\theta$) and Elegance Critic ($Q_\phi$) at inference time. The core idea is to intervene only at "decision-critical moments" where subtle action refinements can significantly impact overall elegance.
 - **Identifying Critical Moments:** Critical moments are detected by monitoring fluctuations in the critic's predicted Q-values. When the base policy proposes an action in a familiar or elegant region, Q-values remain stable. Conversely, a sharp change (either a drop due to suboptimal action or a spike indicating a high-stakes segment) in the Q-value for the default action signals a critical moment. This fluctuation is quantified as $\Delta q_t = |q_t - \bar{q}_t|$, where $q_t$ is the current Q-value and $\bar{q}_t$ is its moving average.
 - **JITI Guidance Algorithm:**
 - At each timestep, the system computes $\Delta q_t$ for the default action sampled from $\pi_\theta$.
 - If $\Delta q_t \le \tau$ (a predefined threshold), the moment is non-critical, and the default action is executed directly (requiring a single critic evaluation).
 - If $\Delta q_t > \tau$, JITI identifies a critical moment. It then samples multiple candidate actions from $\pi_\theta$, evaluates each using $Q_\phi$, and executes the action with the highest predicted elegance.
 This event-driven guidance mechanism allows for efficient, single-sample execution during routine tasks and selective, multi-sample refinement during critical decisions, without retraining $\pi_\theta$.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted aimed to evaluate the effectiveness, component contribution, and generalization capabilities of the proposed framework in both simulation and real-world settings.

#### 4.1. Effectiveness (RQ1)

The research investigated whether the JITI-guided Elegance Critic improves task performance and behavioral elegance compared to baseline VLA policies. Experiments were conducted on the LIBERO-Elegant benchmark using two VLA architectures: SmolVLA (450M) and Isaac GR00T N1.5 (3B). The primary metric was Elegant Success Rate (ESR), where an episode is considered an elegant success only if it achieves the task goal and satisfies all predefined elegance constraints.

- The JITI-guided Elegance Critic consistently yielded substantial performance gains across all tasks and both base models.
- When integrated with SmolVLA, the average ESR increased from 49.8% to 67.2%, an improvement of 17.4 percentage points.
- Similarly, with Isaac GR00T N1.5, the average ESR improved from 46.0% to 67.2%, marking an increase of 21.2 percentage points.
- These results demonstrate that the decoupled critic functions as a model-agnostic module capable of enhancing diverse VLA architectures without requiring their modification.

#### 4.2. Ablation Study (RQ2)

Ablation studies were performed to assess the contribution of the Just-in-Time Intervention (JITI) mechanism and the elegance-oriented reward calibration.

- **Just-in-Time Intervention (JITI) Mechanism:** Comparing the base policy, a "Full-Guidance" variant (N-sample evaluation at every timestep), and the JITI model revealed that JITI consistently achieved higher ESR than both baselines across all eight tasks. Concurrently, JITI reduced the number of critic interventions by over 60% compared to the Full-Guidance strategy, indicating that targeted, event-driven refinement can maintain efficiency while improving execution quality.
- **Elegance-Oriented Reward Calibration:** The contribution of the task-specific elegance signal was evaluated by training the Elegance Critic under two reward schemes: sparse "Binary Reward" (end-of-episode success label) and the proposed "Task-Specific Reward" (dense, intermediate supervision encoding implicit task constraints). The critic trained with task-specific elegance rewards achieved substantially higher average ESR (67.2%) compared to the binary reward scheme (56.8%). This outcome suggests that dense, targeted rewards are crucial for enabling the critic to discern and prioritize subtle, high-quality execution patterns that sparse binary feedback cannot adequately capture.

#### 4.3. Generalization (RQ3)

To assess the transferability of the learned notion of motion quality, experiments were conducted on a set of pick-and-place tasks from the LIBERO-Object suite. The Elegance Critic was trained on a subset of "seen" objects (milk, ketchup, alphabet soup) and then directly applied to "unseen" but semantically similar objects (salad dressing, BBQ sauce, tomato sauce, orange juice) without retraining.

- The method consistently improved the Elegant Success Rate (ESR) on both seen and unseen tasks.
- On seen tasks, ESR increased from 54.6% to 72.0%.
- On unseen tasks, ESR rose from 53.0% to 68.6%.
- These results suggest that the Elegance Critic acquires a transferable behavioral prior rather than memorizing task-specific motion patterns, enabling zero-shot refinement of motion quality across novel manipulation contexts.

#### 4.4. Real-World Validation

The JITI framework was validated on a physical robotic platform (SO-100 arm with SmolVLA as the base policy) across a suite of six real-world manipulation tasks, including drawer closing, object placing, and multi-object stacking. Each task was evaluated over 50 rollouts to assess robustness to real-world latency, uncertainty, and stochasticity.

- The base policy (SmolVLA) achieved an average ESR of 34.3% across the six tasks.
- The JITI-guided system improved the average ESR to 58.0%, demonstrating a gain of 23.7 percentage points.
- Notable improvements were observed in precision-demanding tasks such as stacking and placing, affirming JITI's effectiveness in refining motion quality under real-world constraints.
- These findings indicate that the framework effectively translates from simulation to the real world, providing a lightweight, training-free mechanism for enhancing motion elegance through critic feedback and temporal Q-value fluctuation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework that addresses a notable challenge in general-purpose robotic manipulation: the inconsistent execution quality of policies derived from mixed-quality human demonstrations. By explicitly defining and targeting "elegant execution" through Implicit Task Constraints (ITCs), the work shifts the focus from a binary measure of task success to a more comprehensive evaluation of manipulation quality.

The introduction of the LIBERO-Elegant benchmark provides a standardized platform for the systematic study and development of methods aimed at improving execution quality. This benchmark, with its explicit success and elegance criteria, enables fine-grained assessment that was previously unavailable in existing datasets.

The proposed decoupled refinement framework offers a non-invasive approach to enhance existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies. By employing an Elegance Critic and a Just-in-Time Intervention (JITI) mechanism, the method improves execution quality without requiring modification or retraining of the base VLA policy. This non-invasive nature is significant as it preserves the generality and capabilities of pre-trained large models, while circumventing the computational costs and risks of catastrophic forgetting associated with invasive fine-tuning. The JITI mechanism, by selectively intervening only at decision-critical moments, represents an efficient strategy for balancing performance enhancement with computational overhead, avoiding continuous, high-cost multi-sample evaluations.

The empirical results, demonstrating substantial gains in Elegant Success Rate (ESR) across diverse tasks and VLA architectures in both simulation and real-world settings, support the effectiveness and robustness of the approach. Furthermore, the demonstrated generalization capabilities of the Elegance Critic to unseen tasks and objects indicate that the system learns a transferable notion of motion quality rather than merely memorizing task-specific patterns. This ability for zero-shot refinement of motion quality across novel contexts could contribute to greater adaptability and scalability in robotic deployments.

The potential impact of this work lies in its contribution to advancing robotic control towards more human-like dexterity, safety, and efficiency. By enabling robots to not only complete tasks but also perform them with precision, stability, and collision avoidance, the research moves closer to deploying autonomous agents that can operate seamlessly and safely in human environments. This framework promotes efficient and scalable quality-aware manipulation, fostering a new dimension in the development of robotic intelligence where the "how" of task execution is as valued as the "what."
