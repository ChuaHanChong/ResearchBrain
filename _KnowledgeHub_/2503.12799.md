# Grounded Chain-of-Thought for Multimodal Large Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Xiamen University introduce Grounded Chain-of-Thought (GCoT), a framework that integrates explicit visual-spatial grounding into MLLM reasoning steps to mitigate visual hallucination. They construct the MM-GCoT dataset and show that fine-tuning models with GCoT significantly boosts answer-grounding consistency, indicating more reliable visual reasoning.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) suffer from visual hallucination, specifically 'language bias' where they provide correct answers without genuinely relying on visual evidence.
- Existing Chain-of-Thought methods for MLLMs primarily focus on textual reasoning or external knowledge without explicitly grounding each reasoning step to specific visual regions.
- Current benchmarks often overlook this subtle form of hallucination, focusing on explicit visual failures rather than the trustworthiness of the reasoning process.

## Method

- Introduces ==Grounded Chain-of-Thought (GCoT)==, a novel task requiring MLLMs to generate step-by-step reasoning where each step includes both textual reasoning and associated ==visual bounding box coordinates==.
- Constructs ==MM-GCoT dataset== with **23,028** examples, designed with multi-step reasoning chains linked to precise spatial grounding for diverse task types.
- Proposes a comprehensive evaluation system including ==Answer-Grounding Consistency (Consist.)== to quantify reliable reasoning.

## Results

- State-of-the-art MLLMs exhibit a large gap between answer accuracy (e.g., **75.7%** A-Acc for LLaVA-OneVision-72B) and answer-grounding consistency (e.g., **11.1%** Consist. for same model).
- Training with MM-GCoT data dramatically improves consistency; LLaVA-7B GCoT achieved a **55.7%** boost in consistency (from **10.1%** to **65.8%**) and a **4.5%** increase in answer accuracy.
- GCoT-trained models demonstrated improved visual understanding and reliable reasoning, with qualitative analyses showing successful generalization of grounded reasoning to unseen tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Visual hallucination, particularly language bias (correct answers without visual grounding), is pervasive in current MLLMs, with many exhibiting low answer-grounding consistency (e.g., as low as 11.1%).
> - Simply scaling up MLLM parameters does not necessarily alleviate visual hallucination; some larger models show worse consistency compared to smaller variants, suggesting data overfitting.
> - Explicit GCoT training effectively cultivates reliable visual reasoning, significantly improving consistency and enabling generalization to other multimodal tasks like open-world QA and Referring Expression Comprehension.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancements in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have enabled unprecedented capabilities in vision-language tasks, allowing these models to approach near-human performance on various benchmarks. Models like LLaVA, Qwen-VL, and InternVL have demonstrated impressive zero-shot and few-shot learning abilities, driven by large-scale pre-training and instruction tuning. However, a critical and persistent challenge that plagues MLLMs, greatly impeding their trustworthy deployment in real-world applications, is **visual hallucination**.

Visual hallucination manifests in several forms. One common manifestation is a "visual shortcoming," where the MLLM simply fails to accurately perceive or understand the visual content, leading to incorrect answers. Existing evaluation systems and benchmarks, such as POPE and HallusionBench, often focus on identifying these explicit failures, typically through binary verification tasks (e.g., checking for the presence of an object).

This paper, however, addresses a more subtle yet equally problematic form of visual hallucination: **language bias**, or what the authors refer to as "correct but miss" scenarios. In these cases, an MLLM might provide a correct answer to a question but does so by relying on statistical correlations, dataset biases, or its language priors, rather than truly grounding its response in the visual evidence. The model might "attend to irrelevant regions" or simply generate plausible text that happens to be correct, without genuinely "seeing" the visual cues that justify the answer. This issue severely undermines the reliability and interpretability of MLLMs, as their reasoning process becomes opaque and potentially untrustworthy. The authors emphasize that while language bias has been noted in traditional vision-language research, it receives less attention in the context of modern MLLMs.

To address this, the paper draws inspiration from the successful **Chain-of-Thought (CoT)** paradigm in Large Language Models (LLMs), which encourages step-by-step reasoning. While recent "visual CoT" studies for MLLMs (e.g., LLaVA-CoT, ScienceQA, MME-CoT) have explored providing more detailed textual explanations or leveraging external knowledge for reasoning, they primarily focus on visual *knowledge* reasoning without explicitly verifying whether each reasoning step is *grounded* in the visual scene with corresponding spatial information. These existing visual CoT methods often lack the crucial component of linking textual reasoning steps directly to specific visual regions.

Furthermore, this work relates to **visual grounding** and **grounded question answering (GQA)** tasks. Visual grounding aims to locate objects mentioned in a caption or query, while GQA extends this by requiring models to ground the instance related to their final answer. However, these tasks typically only ground the *final* answer or specific phrases without exposing the intermediate reasoning steps or requiring grounded evidence for *each* step of a complex multi-hop question. The proposed Grounded Chain-of-Thought (GCoT) significantly extends these paradigms by requiring models to explicitly decompose a question into multiple reasoning steps, with each step involving the identification and grounding of relevant visual cues (spatial coordinates). This multi-step, visually grounded reasoning is a novel contribution that bridges the gap between high-level conceptual understanding and low-level visual perception, aiming to foster a more reliable and transparent reasoning process in MLLMs.

By integrating explicit spatial grounding into the CoT paradigm, this research positions itself at the forefront of efforts to enhance the trustworthiness, interpretability, and robustness of MLLMs, moving beyond mere accuracy metrics to evaluate the *process* of multimodal reasoning. The work implicitly suggests a path toward more sophisticated Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for embodied AI, where precise spatial understanding and sequential, grounded action planning are indispensable.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind this research stems from the critical problem of **visual hallucination** in existing Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), particularly the subtle yet pervasive issue where models produce correct answers without genuinely relying on pertinent visual evidence (language bias). The authors argue that this lack of visual-spatial grounding in the reasoning process severely undermines the trustworthiness and reliability of MLLM applications.

Against this backdrop, the paper sets forth several key objectives:

1. **Introduce Grounded Chain-of-Thought (GCoT) as a New Learning Task:** The primary objective is to propose and define GCoT as a novel paradigm for MLLMs. GCoT aims to alleviate visual hallucination by enhancing MLLMs' visual-spatial reasoning capabilities. This task requires models to perform step-by-step reasoning, where each step involves recognizing and explicitly grounding relevant visual cues with spatial coordinates, thereby providing an intuitive, verifiable basis for the final answer. This is formulated as transforming a direct question-answering function into a multi-step decision process that incorporates visual evidence (G_t) alongside textual reasoning (R_t) at each step.

2. **Construct a Comprehensive Dataset (MM-GCoT) for GCoT Research:** To facilitate the development and evaluation of GCoT capabilities, a major objective is to design and construct a dedicated, large-scale dataset named "Multimodal Grounded Chain-of-Thought (MM-GCoT)." This dataset is meticulously designed to contain examples with multi-step reasoning chains, each linked to precise spatial grounding information. It aims to serve both as a training resource for cultivating GCoT in MLLMs and as a benchmark for evaluating hallucination.

3. **Develop a Comprehensive Consistency Evaluation System:** Beyond traditional accuracy metrics, the paper aims to introduce a robust evaluation system to specifically measure the consistency between an MLLM's answers and its visual grounding. This system includes three key metrics: Answer Accuracy (A-Acc), Grounding Accuracy (G-Acc), and crucially, Answer-Grounding Consistency (Consist.). The consistency metric is designed to quantify the degree to which correct answers are supported by correct visual evidence, directly targeting the "language bias" form of hallucination.

4. **Conduct Extensive Experiments to Benchmark Existing MLLMs:** A significant objective is to thoroughly evaluate a wide range of advanced MLLMs (including various scales of LLaVA, Qwen2.5-VL, and InternVL2.5) on the newly proposed MM-GCoT benchmark. This aims to:
 - Quantify the extent of visual hallucination in current state-of-the-art MLLMs, particularly regarding answer-grounding consistency.
 - Investigate whether visual hallucination correlates with model parameter size or general multimodal performance, hypothesizing that larger or stronger MLLMs might not necessarily be less affected by this issue.
 - Compare the performance of MLLMs under different prompting strategies (answer-first vs. grounding-first) to understand how explicit grounding demands affect their reasoning.

5. **Demonstrate the Efficacy of GCoT Training:** Finally, the paper aims to empirically demonstrate that fine-tuning MLLMs using the proposed MM-GCoT dataset can effectively cultivate their GCoT capability. This includes showing that such training significantly reduces inconsistencies between answers and their grounded proofs, and that this cultivated GCoT ability can generalize to existing multimodal tasks like open-world QA and Referring Expression Comprehension (REC), thereby proving its potential in alleviating visual hallucination and fostering trustworthy visual reasoning.

In essence, the motivation is to move MLLMs from merely providing correct answers to providing *reliably correct* answers that are transparently supported by visual evidence, thereby enhancing their trustworthiness for real-world applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers around the novel concept of Grounded Chain-of-Thought (GCoT) and the development of a dedicated dataset, MM-GCoT, to train and evaluate MLLMs on this task.

#### 3.1. Grounded Chain-of-Thought (GCoT) Task Formulation

GCoT re-frames the traditional MLLM question-answering process from a direct mapping (Image, Text -> Answer) into a multi-step decision process. For a given input image (I) and text query (T), the model is required to generate a sequence of reasoning steps. Each step *t* involves both a textual reasoning state (R_t) and associated visual evidence in the form of spatial coordinates (G_t). The final answer (A) is then derived based on these grounded reasoning steps. This formulation, P(A|I,T) = Product(P(R_t, G_t | I,T,G_<t,R_<t)), explicitly encourages the model to break down complex queries into visually verifiable sub-tasks, ensuring that its reasoning is rooted in the visual scene. This structure also opens avenues for reinforcement learning on non-GCoT labeled data in future work, providing a flexible framework for cultivating grounded reasoning.

#### 3.2. MM-GCoT Dataset Construction

To facilitate GCoT research, the authors carefully designed and constructed MM-GCoT, a new large-scale Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) dataset.
- **Statistics:** MM-GCoT comprises 23,028 training/validation examples and 994 dedicated test samples for hallucination evaluation.
- **Task Types:** Examples are categorized into three types to cover diverse reasoning scenarios:
 - **Attribute:** Questions inquiring about specific attributes of a target object, given its type and location.
 - **Judgment:** Tasks requiring verification of a given description's correctness.
 - **Object:** Questions focused on identifying an object category at a specified location.
- **Construction Pipeline (Four-Stage):**
 1. **Region Alignment:** Leveraged region descriptions and object annotations from Visual Genome, aligning them using Intersection over Union (IoU) matching to ensure precise spatial correspondence between text and visual regions.
 2. **Spatial Relationship Graph Construction:** Built a graph where matched objects are nodes, and edges represent spatial and semantic relationships between them.
 3. **Multi-Step Reasoning Chain Generation:** Iteratively sampled relationship paths from the constructed graph. Structured templates were used to collect detailed information including bounding box coordinates, object attributes, and contextual relationships for each step.
 4. **Natural Language Question Generation & Quality Assurance:** An LLM (DeepSeek-V3 was mentioned in the references as a recent powerful LLM) was employed to translate the structured templates into fluent natural language questions and answers. A rigorous quality assurance process was then applied: LLM-based verification for answer accuracy and grounding consistency was performed on training data, while test samples underwent additional manual validation to ensure the highest quality and reliability for benchmarking.

#### 3.3. Evaluation Metrics

Recognizing the limitations of standard accuracy metrics for assessing hallucination, the authors introduced a comprehensive evaluation system for MLLMs on MM-GCoT:
- **Answer Accuracy (A-Acc):** Measures the correctness of the textual answer using text matching against ground truth.
- **Grounding Accuracy (G-Acc):** Assesses the precision of predicted bounding boxes using the Acc@0.5 metric, requiring an IoU > 0.5 with the ground-truth box.
- **Answer-Grounding Consistency (Consist.):** This is the key metric for hallucination. It quantifies the proportion of "correct and reliable" samples, defined as cases where both the answer and its corresponding bounding box are correct. It is calculated as:
 `Consist. = |S_ca,cb| / (|S_ca,cb| + |S_ca,wb| + |S_wa,cb|)`
 where `S_ca,cb` are samples with correct answer and correct box, `S_ca,wb` are samples with correct answer but wrong box (hallucination due to language bias), and `S_wa,cb` are samples with wrong answer but correct box. This metric directly captures the trustworthiness of the MLLM's reasoning by penalizing answers that are correct but visually ungrounded.

#### 3.4. Evaluation Prompt Settings for Existing MLLMs

To evaluate existing MLLMs, which are not inherently designed for GCoT, two prompting strategies were devised to elicit grounded answers:
- **Answer-First Prompting:** The MLLM is prompted to provide the textual answer first, followed by a request for the corresponding bounding box coordinates. This setting is considered easier and checks if the MLLM can ground an answer it has already formulated.
- **Grounding-First Prompting:** The MLLM is instructed to first provide a bounding box for the potential answer, and then generate the textual answer based on that grounded region. This is more challenging as it forces the model to rely on visual evidence *before* formulating the answer, making it closer to the objective of hallucination evaluation.

#### 3.5. GCoT Training (Fine-tuning Strategy)

To demonstrate the benefits of GCoT, the authors fine-tuned LLaVA models. They replaced the original instruction-tuning samples in LLaVA's training set with an equivalent number of examples from the MM-GCoT dataset. LLaVA-GCoT models were trained from scratch using this modified dataset, adhering to LLaVA's default training settings. This approach directly assesses the impact of explicit GCoT training on MLLMs' ability to reason reliably with visual grounding.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive experiments conducted on 12 advanced MLLMs, including LLaVA variants, Qwen2.5-VL, and InternVL2.5, using the MM-GCoT dataset, revealed several significant findings:

#### 4.1. Prominent Visual Hallucination in Existing MLLMs

- **Low Consistency:** Most state-of-the-art MLLMs exhibited severe visual hallucination, as evidenced by a substantial gap between their answer accuracy (A-Acc) and answer-grounding consistency (Consist.). For example, LLaVA-OneVision-72B achieved a high average A-Acc of 75.7% but a very low average Consist. of only 11.1% under the "answer-first" setting. Even InternVL2.5-78B, a top-performing model, managed only 35.4% average consistency. This indicates that while MLLMs can often provide correct answers, they frequently do so without genuinely basing them on the correct visual information, confirming the "correct but miss" hallucination problem.
- **No Direct Correlation with Model Scale:** Counter-intuitively, the experiments showed that visual hallucination is not directly alleviated by increasing model parameter size. The Qwen2.5-VL series demonstrated this clearly: while A-Acc slightly increased with scale (from 3B to 72B), the 7B model surprisingly outperformed the 72B model in answer-grounding consistency by a substantial 18.2%. This finding challenges the assumption that larger models are inherently more reliable and suggests significant data overfitting in super-large MLLMs on common benchmarks.

#### 4.2. Performance Across Different Subsets

- **Attribute Task:** MLLMs achieved superior grounding accuracy in the "Attribute" subset (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-7B at 82.6% G-Acc under grounding-first). This is attributed to questions in this category often containing explicit spatial relationship descriptions, which align well with pre-training objectives for grounding.
- **Judgement Task:** MLLMs showed the highest answer accuracy in the "Judgement" task (average 86.1% A-Acc under answer-first). These tasks, typically involving fact verification, are comparatively straightforward for MLLMs, leading to higher textual accuracy.
- **Object Task:** MLLMs demonstrated the strongest answer-grounding consistency in the "Object" task (e.g., InternVL2.5-8B at 39.4% Consist.). Once the target object is correctly identified, MLLMs can consistently link it to its visual region.

#### 4.3. Impact of Prompting Strategies

- **Answer-First vs. Grounding-First:** MLLMs generally achieved higher answer accuracy when prompted "answer-first." For instance, LLaVA-7B's A-Acc was 8.9% higher in the answer-first setting. This discrepancy was more pronounced in smaller models (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-3B had a 45.4% A-Acc gap).
- **Trade-off:** While the "grounding-first" setting often led to improved grounding performance, it frequently resulted in a deterioration of answer accuracy. Qwen2.5-VL-7B, for example, saw a 7.9% G-Acc increase but a 21.2% A-Acc drop in the grounding-first setting. This indicates that MLLMs may not consistently integrate visual and textual information, and their correct answers are not always predicated on accurate visual evidence.

#### 4.4. Efficacy of GCoT Training

- **Significant Consistency Improvement:** Training MLLMs with the proposed MM-GCoT dataset (LLaVA GCoT) led to significant improvements in consistency and overall performance. LLaVA-7B GCoT achieved a remarkable 55.7% boost in consistency (from 10.1% to 65.8%) and a 4.5% increase in answer accuracy compared to the original LLaVA-7B.
- **Enhanced Visual Understanding:** The consistency improvement primarily stemmed from enhanced visual understanding and reliable reasoning, rather than merely better grounding capabilities. LLaVA-13B GCoT, for example, maintained similar grounding performance but dramatically improved consistency by 61.2%.
- **Generalization:** Qualitative analyses demonstrated that LLaVA-1.5 13B GCoT successfully generalized its GCoT capability to open-world QA and Referring Expression Comprehension (REC) tasks not explicitly included in its training, providing detailed, grounded reasoning steps.
- **Parameter Efficiency:** LLaVA-1.5 13B GCoT, with significantly fewer parameters, was shown to effectively reduce hallucinations compared to much larger models like InternVL2.5-78B, accurately identifying target regions and performing step-by-step reasoning even for ambiguous questions.

These findings collectively highlight the pervasive nature of visual hallucination in current MLLMs and underscore the effectiveness of GCoT in fostering more trustworthy and visually grounded reasoning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research on "Grounded Chain-of-Thought for Multimodal Large Language Models" carries substantial significance and potential impact across several dimensions of AI research and application:

1. **Directly Addressing a Critical Trustworthiness Issue:** The most immediate impact is its contribution to mitigating visual hallucination, a severe impediment to the adoption of MLLMs in sensitive and high-stakes applications. By focusing on "language bias" – where models give correct answers but for the wrong visual reasons – the paper tackles a subtle yet profound reliability challenge that current benchmarks often overlook. Developing MLLMs that not only provide correct answers but also *can explain why* (with visual evidence) is crucial for building user trust and ensuring responsible AI deployment.

2. **Introducing a Novel Learning and Evaluation Paradigm (GCoT):** The conceptualization and formalization of Grounded Chain-of-Thought (GCoT) represent a significant leap beyond existing Chain-of-Thought (CoT) and visual grounding approaches. GCoT mandates explicit visual grounding at each step of the reasoning process, creating a verifiable and interpretable chain. This shifts the focus from mere output accuracy to the *trustworthiness of the reasoning process itself*. This new paradigm provides a powerful framework for developing more robust and transparent multimodal AI.

3. **Establishing a Unique Benchmark (MM-GCoT):** The construction of the MM-GCoT dataset is a critical contribution. It is explicitly designed for both training and, more importantly, *evaluating* hallucination in MLLMs by uniquely combining multi-step reasoning with explicit grounding. The proposed `answer-grounding consistency` metric offers a nuanced tool for assessing whether an MLLM's answers are genuinely based on visual evidence or merely on linguistic patterns. This dataset and evaluation system fill a significant gap in existing benchmarks, pushing the research community to build MLLMs that are not just performant, but also reliable.

4. **Revealing Key Insights into MLLM Limitations:** The extensive experimental results yield invaluable insights into the behavior of current state-of-the-art MLLMs. The finding that visual hallucination is pervasive and *not necessarily alleviated by increased model scale* (with larger models sometimes exhibiting *worse* consistency) challenges common assumptions in large model development. This suggests that simply scaling up parameters or pre-training data might lead to overfitting on common benchmarks and does not inherently cultivate true visual-spatial reasoning and grounding. This insight strongly advocates for dedicated training strategies like GCoT.

5. **Providing a Concrete Solution for Enhanced Reliability:** The successful demonstration that fine-tuning with MM-GCoT data significantly improves answer-grounding consistency and reduces hallucination (e.g., LLaVA-GCoT's substantial gains) offers a practical and actionable path forward. This shows that MLLMs *can* be taught to connect their linguistic reasoning to visual perception in a more reliable manner, even with a relatively modest amount of targeted data.

6. **Paving the Way for Advanced Embodied AI:** The explicit nature of grounded reasoning in GCoT has profound implications for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models and embodied AI. For robots or intelligent agents operating in physical environments, precise spatial understanding and step-by-step, visually-grounded action planning are non-negotiable. GCoT's framework of linking reasoning steps to visual coordinates directly feeds into the requirements for more capable and reliable embodied systems, enabling them to "think in space" and execute complex tasks with higher fidelity.

7. **Inspiring Future Research Directions:** This work opens up numerous avenues for future research, including:
 - Exploring reinforcement learning (RL) techniques for GCoT training on even larger, unlabelled datasets, leveraging the structured GCoT formulation.
 - Developing more integrated training approaches that seamlessly align visual and textual reasoning to overcome the observed disconnect between MLLMs' perception and inference.
 - Designing novel MLLM architectures specifically optimized for grounded chain-of-thought, moving beyond adapting existing models.

In conclusion, this paper makes a substantial contribution by identifying and providing a novel, verifiable approach to a critical problem in MLLMs. By emphasizing trustworthy reasoning and introducing a unique dataset and evaluation framework, it pushes the boundaries of multimodal AI towards more reliable, interpretable, and ultimately, deployable systems.
