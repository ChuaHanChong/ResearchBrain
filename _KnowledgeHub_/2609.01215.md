# REFACTOR-VLA: Unsupervised Library Learning of Typed Motor Programs

> [!summary] Summary
> REFACTOR-VLA discovers reusable robot skills via a wake/sleep loop: a sleep phase clusters trajectory fragments with a Behavioral-Equivalence Kernel derived from latent world-model rollouts, then compiles them into typed lambda abstractions gated by minimum-description-length and return-preservation tests, which a rectified-flow action decoder consumes. On LIBERO it beats the strongest skill-discovery baselines on all four suites by +0.184 mean clustering NMI.

## Problem

- Monolithic VLA models emit raw short action chunks without reusable abstractions, so long-horizon multi-step tasks fail and learned behavior stays uninterpretable.
- Skill-discovery systems sidestep the equivalence question: gating experts or contrastive keyframe clustering treats similar-effect trajectories with different action paths as distinct skills.
- LLM-based library builders synthesize pre/post-conditions or refactor policy code with judges that are never calibrated to the robot's own learned dynamics.

## Method

- **Behavioral-Equivalence Kernel** scores fragment pairs by value difference plus k-step latent-rollout ==Wasserstein distance== under a ==DreamerV3-style world model==, amortized by a ==Siamese encoder== distilled label-free.
- **Typed Program Emitter** decodes ==Hindley-Milner-typed lambda terms== over a Twist/Wrench/GripperPhase/Pose/Lang signature, unifier-checked at each beam step, driving a ==rectified-flow library-conditioned action decoder==.
- **Sleep-phase library refactor** anti-unifies parsed programs top-down and admits abstractions only under joint kernel soundness, return preservation, and ==MDL-gain== gates.

## Results

- Adding a supervised-contrastive InfoNCE term in world-model warmup lifts clustering NMI on all four suites (mean **+0.252** absolute), beating the strongest baseline by **+0.184** mean.
- Scaling the world model from **188M** to **430M** parameters lowers NMI on **4-of-4** suites (**-0.018** to **-0.146**) despite strictly lower warmup loss, falsifying the capacity hypothesis.
- The sleep phase admits **3** language abstractions (**1211** nats), rewriting **256/256** demonstrations with **2** of them while shifting decoder velocity error only **-0.0059**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Behavioral equivalence should be defined by dynamics-model outcomes at a shared call site, not by token identity or ungrounded semantic judgments.
> - Representation-objective shape dominates parameter count: a larger world model can encode a different, worse partition of fragment space while fitting its targets better.
> - Syntactic anti-unification compresses language but admits zero motor abstractions, so symbolic library learning does not transfer directly to continuous control.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of vision-language-action (VLA) models is advancing, with systems such as OpenVLA, π0, RT-2, and RDT-1B demonstrating capabilities in various robotic tasks. However, a common characteristic of many contemporary VLA models is their "monolithic" nature, where they generate raw motor commands or short action sequences without organizing behaviors into reusable abstractions. This architectural choice has been observed to lead to limitations in performance on long-horizon, multi-step tasks and can impede the interpretability of learned behaviors.

Existing approaches to skill discovery have attempted to address this limitation but often encounter challenges in rigorously defining "behavioral equivalence" for continuous action spaces. For instance, methods like AtomicVLA and AtomSkill cluster action sequences based on contrastive embeddings, which may not fully capture the underlying physical dynamics. Other approaches, such such as BLADE and LRLL, leverage large language models (LLMs) to infer skill equivalence or refactor policies. However, these LLMs are typically ungrounded and not inherently calibrated to the robot's specific dynamics, potentially leading to discrepancies between symbolic representations and actual physical outcomes. This lack of dynamics-aware equivalence can result in action sequences with similar surface forms but different consequences being grouped, or physically equivalent but syntactically different sequences being treated as distinct.

The challenge of establishing behavioral equivalence in continuous-action robotic policies stands in contrast to discrete, symbolic domains where syntactic anti-unification can be directly applied to define skill libraries. In physical robot demonstrations, identical action trajectories are infrequent, necessitating a more robust, dynamics-grounded approach to skill abstraction.

This research aims to integrate the strengths of VLA models with formal guarantees derived from wake-sleep library learning and dynamics-aware behavioral equivalence. It builds upon foundational work in bisimulation metrics for Markov Decision Processes (MDPs), which measure long-term behavioral similarity of states, and extends these concepts to continuous trajectory fragments using learned world models. The work also draws from typed program induction frameworks, such as DreamCoder, which use alternating wake and sleep phases for skill discovery in symbolic domains, adapting these principles to the complexities of continuous robotic control. By addressing the definition of behavioral equivalence through a dynamics-aware kernel and integrating this into a structured program induction framework, the work seeks to enable VLA models to learn reusable, interpretable skills that can enhance performance on complex, long-horizon manipulation tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a system, REFACTOR-VLA, capable of learning reusable and interpretable skills for robot manipulation, thereby overcoming limitations observed in monolithic vision-language-action (VLA) models. This overarching goal is driven by several motivations and broken down into specific technical objectives:

**Motivation and Problem Statement:**

1. **Limitations of Monolithic VLA Models:** Current VLA models generate low-level motor commands without hierarchical organization, which restricts their ability to perform complex, long-horizon (multi-step) tasks effectively. This flat action generation also hinders the interpretability of the behaviors learned by these models.
2. **Intractability of Flat Learning:** In environments with large state spaces and sparse rewards, direct reinforcement learning or imitation learning at low-level sampling frequencies becomes computationally intractable for multi-step tasks. A hierarchical approach, leveraging reusable skills, is posited as a more efficient strategy, analogous to human problem-solving.
3. **Absence of Dynamics-Aware Behavioral Equivalence:** A significant barrier in learning reusable skills for continuous-action robotics is the lack of a mathematically rigorous and dynamics-aware definition of "behavioral equivalence." Existing skill discovery methods either group action sequences based on surface-level similarities (e.g., contrastive embeddings of visual features) or rely on large language models (LLMs) that are not calibrated to the robot's specific physical dynamics. This can lead to incorrect skill grouping, where physically different actions are considered equivalent or vice versa.
4. **Challenge of Continuous Actions:** Unlike discrete, symbolic domains where syntactic identity can define skill equivalence, physical robot demonstrations for tasks like "pour water" rarely involve identical action trajectories. A robust equivalence metric must account for varied action paths that lead to similar physical outcomes.

**Specific Objectives and Contributions:**

To address these motivations, REFACTOR-VLA pursues the following specific objectives:

1. **Formulate a Behavioral-Equivalence Kernel (BEK):** Develop a fragment-level divergence metric, Dφ(τ, τ'), that quantifies the similarity of trajectory fragments based on their expected returns and k-step latent rollout distributions within a learned latent world model (Mφ). This BEK is intended to serve as a clustering kernel during skill discovery. The work includes a theorem on the cluster recovery rate of this kernel.
2. **Develop a Typed Program Emitter (TPE) and Library-Conditioned Action Decoder (LCAD):** Design a TPE, implemented as a causal-decoder Transformer, that generates type-checked lambda terms using a vocabulary inspired by the Hindley–Milner type system. This TPE is designed to coordinate an LCAD, which is a rectified-flow action decoder that produces 16-step action chunks, conditioned on the learned library entries.
3. **Implement a Wake/Sleep Loop with Gated Admission:** Integrate the BEK and the TPE/LCAD into a wake-sleep architectural framework. This loop includes a library refactor phase where candidate abstractions are admitted into a growing skill library only if they satisfy joint minimum description length (MDL) gain and return-preservation criteria. This ensures that admitted skills are both compact and do not degrade policy performance.
4. **Conduct End-to-End Empirical Validation:** Evaluate the entire REFACTOR-VLA system comprehensively on the full LIBERO benchmark suite, alongside ablations and cross-provider analyses, to demonstrate its empirical efficacy and validate the core hypotheses regarding the importance of objective function shape over model capacity.

These objectives collectively aim to create a system that can autonomously discover, represent, and utilize structured, dynamics-aware skills, leading to more capable and understandable robotic agents.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

REFACTOR-VLA operates on a "wake/sleep" architecture, which includes four primary modules: a backbone VLA policy (πθ), a latent world model (Mφ), a library of typed-λ programs (Ft), and a posterior over their use (ρt). The system's training schedule is divided into three phases, orchestrated by an outer driver that alternates between policy optimization and skill discovery.

**Training Schedule:**

1. **Phase A (World-model warmup):** The latent world model (Mφ) is trained.
2. **Phase B (Wake-phase policy optimization):** The policy, comprising the Typed Program Emitter (TPE) and Library-Conditioned Action Decoder (LCAD), is optimized using the skills from the current library.
3. **Phase C (Sleep-phase skill discovery):** Action fragments are clustered into reusable skills, which are then considered for admission into the library.

**Latent World Model (Mφ):**
Mφ is structured as a DreamerV3-style hierarchical world model, leveraging a frozen DINOv2-base visual encoder. It processes interleaved [image, state, action] tokens using a causal Transformer, which includes posterior (qφ) and prior (pφ) heads. Reconstruction is performed in the DINOv2-feature space, with an optional return head that is inactive for the LIBERO benchmark due to the absence of reward annotations. The default model configuration contains 188.16 million total parameters, with 101.58 million trainable parameters. Phase A pretrains this model across all four LIBERO suites.

**Behavioral-Equivalence Kernel (BEK) Dφ(τ, τ'):**
The BEK is central to defining behavioral equivalence. For an MDP, Dφ(τ, τ') measures the divergence between two trajectory fragments (τ, τ') when inserted at the same initial state. It consists of two components:
1. **Value-Difference Component:** The absolute difference in expected returns (Vτφ(s) - Vτ'φ(s)) under Mφ when inserting each fragment at state 's'.
2. **k-Step Latent Rollout Wasserstein Term:** The Wasserstein-2 distance (W2) between the k-step latent rollout distributions (Pkφ(· | s, τ) and Pkφ(· | s, τ')) after insertion.
This kernel is employed for fixed-k KMeans clustering, with 'k' matched to the number of tasks in each LIBERO suite.
To address the computational cost of directly computing Dφ for every pair, a 2.4-million-parameter Siamese Transformer (kχ) serves as an amortizer. This network generates L2-normalized embeddings, and pairwise BEK is approximated as 1 - ⟨kχ(τ), kχ(τ')⟩. It is trained via strict feature distillation from the frozen Mφ, minimizing the Mean Squared Error (MSE) between its normalized outputs and Mφ's fragment embeddings. The system employs two modes for BEK: `legacy_cosine` (default, higher NMI, faster) and `separable_VPk` (direct implementation of Equation 1 for ablation). A theoretical result (Theorem 1 and 2) supports the cluster recovery capabilities of fixed-k KMeans under specific assumptions on Mφ error and class separation.

**Typed Program Emitter (TPE) and Library-Conditioned Action Decoder (LCAD):**
The **TPE** is a causal-decoder Transformer that operates over a Hindley–Milner-typed vocabulary. This vocabulary (Σ = {Twist, Wrench, GripperPhase, Pose, Lang}) defines primitive actions and structural terms. At each beam step, a Robinson unifier performs type-checking to filter out ill-typed candidate tokens before scoring.
The **LCAD** is a 4-layer Transformer with approximately 10.9 million trainable parameters. It receives a typed program term and the current state as input, then generates a 16-step action chunk using rectified-flow matching with 10 Euler steps. This maintains action continuity while enabling the library to manage sub-skill routing.
**Library Conditioning** integrates the learned skills. A grammatical compiler converts raw language strings and action sequences into library-conditioned targets. It assigns unique IDs to new abstractions and greedily rewrites primitive sequences into sub-program calls, prioritizing those with higher MDL gain.

**Wake/Sleep Alternating Loop:**
For each outer iteration, the driver executes three steps:
1. **Wake Phase:** The TPE is trained using cross-entropy loss on program tokens derived from library-rewritten primitive bodies. The LCAD is trained using rectified-flow MSE on 16-step action chunks.
2. **Sleep Phase:** The BEK head (kχ) is trained by distilling against the Mφ-rollout target.
3. **Library Refactor Phase:** Top-down syntactic anti-unification is performed on the parsed program corpus to identify common sub-structures. Candidate abstractions are admitted to the library only if they satisfy three criteria:
 - **BEK Soundness:** The abstraction must be within a specified divergence (εt) from its cluster centroid, ensuring behavioral consistency.
 - **Return-Preservation:** The abstraction must preserve expected return within a small margin (ε=0.05) over K_v=32 verifier rollouts, preventing performance degradation.
 - **MDL Gain:** The abstraction must yield a minimum description length gain (> δMDL=4 nats), ensuring compression efficiency.
The top Kmax=8 admitted candidates are selected, and low-usage entries are pruned. A theoretical Lemma 1 supports the return-preservation guarantee.

**Experimental Setup:**
Experiments were conducted on the full LIBERO benchmark suite, using real robot data from four LeRobot v3 suites. The visual encoder (DINOv2-base) was frozen. Models were trained using Distributed Data Parallel (DDP) on eight H100 GPUs with bfloat16 precision for 4,000 optimization steps. A 430M-parameter Mφ model was also evaluated as part of a capacity-falsification probe.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of REFACTOR-VLA on the LIBERO benchmark suite yielded several findings, particularly concerning the influence of world model capacity versus training objectives on skill representation quality.

**1. World Model Capacity vs. Training Objective (Twin Lever):**
The research investigated the impact of world model size and its training objective on skill clustering performance, measured by Normalized Mutual Information (NMI) against task labels.
- **Capacity Falsification:** Increasing the world model Mφ size from 188 million to 430 million parameters, despite achieving a lower Phase A loss on `libero_object`, resulted in *decreased* NMI performance on 4 out of 4 LIBERO benchmark suites. For example, on `libero_object`, NMI dropped from 0.285 to 0.245, and on `libero_spatial` from 0.475 to 0.329. This outcome contradicts the hypothesis that larger world models invariably lead to better skill representations.
- **Training Objective Impact:** Conversely, modifying the training objective significantly improved skill clustering. Adding an auxiliary supervised contrastive InfoNCE loss during the world-model warmup (Phase A) substantially enhanced the quality of skill clustering in the sleep phase (Phase C). The mean NMI under n=3 multi-seeding across all suites improved:
 - `libero_object`: 0.462 ± 0.021
 - `libero_spatial`: 0.867 ± 0.025
 - `libero_goal`: 0.915 ± 0.013
 - `LIBERO-10`: 0.754 ± 0.010
 This change recovered up to 98.5% of the supervised upper bound and resulted in a mean absolute NMI improvement of +0.252 (+50.7% relative) over the "no-InfoNCE" baseline. This indicates that the choice of training objective is a more critical factor than simply increasing model size.

**2. Performance Against Baselines:**
REFACTOR-VLA, when employing the improved InfoNCE training objective, demonstrated enhanced performance compared to existing skill discovery methods.
- It outperformed the strongest published baselines (AtomSkill on `libero_object` and `libero_spatial`; AtomicVLA on `libero_goal` and `libero_10`) on all 4 LIBERO suites. The mean score improvement was ∆ = +0.184.
- For instance, on `libero_object`, REFACTOR-VLA achieved 0.462 NMI versus the best baseline's 0.348, and on `libero_spatial`, 0.867 versus 0.617.
- A cross-comparison with a frozen ∼7B OpenVLA extractor showed its NMI to be 0.4094 on `libero_10`, significantly lower than REFACTOR-VLA's 0.754.

**3. Cross-Provider Seed Convergence:**
A cross-provider analysis, involving n=12 pairs, yielded a 95% bootstrap confidence interval for the mean pairwise NMI of [0.683, 0.729], with a mean (¯x) of 0.705. This result suggests a degree of consistency in skill discovery across different data splits and model initializations.

**4. Library Learning and Abstraction Discovery:**
The sleep phase of REFACTOR-VLA successfully produced the first structured task-language library for LIBERO.
- It identified 3 abstractions, accounting for 1211 nats of MDL compression, primarily within the `Lang` slot of the vocabulary. The top abstraction (672 nats) corresponded to a "pick up X, place in basket" template.
- The wake-phase decoder was observed to utilize 2 out of the 3 admitted abstractions, successfully rewriting all 256 sampled demonstrations with minimal change to LCAD velocity error (∆ = −0.0059), indicating effective end-to-end integration of the learned library.
- In contrast, the motor-primitive subspace (`{Twist, Wrench, GripperPhase, Pose}`) yielded 0 abstractions across various MDL thresholds, even with DTW pre-alignment within BEK clusters. This indicates a limitation of the symbolic anti-unification method for continuous motor primitives, suggesting that the behavioral equivalence is effective, but the current abstraction mechanism is more suited for higher-level `Lang` concepts.

**5. Contribution of the Wasserstein Term:**
A specific ablation demonstrated that dropping the Wasserstein term (wE = 0) from the Dφ formulation led to a substantial reduction in NMI (e.g., -0.175 on `libero_object`), indicating its critical role in carrying dynamics-aware signal for skill clustering.

**6. Ablations and Mechanism Alternatives:**
Further ablations at 430M capacity ruled out several alternative explanations for improved performance: linear/non-linear projections (PCA-64, VAE-64), different reconstruction targets (state-vec recon), and smoothness regularizers (VIB + spectral norm) all failed to rescue the embedding, further confirming the specific importance of the InfoNCE objective.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in "REFACTOR-VLA" contributes to the field of robotic learning in several ways, with implications for the development of more capable and interpretable robotic systems.

**1. Challenging the "Capacity Hypothesis":** A primary finding is the demonstration that simply increasing the parameter count of a world model does not necessarily lead to improved skill representations; in fact, it can degrade performance. This result challenges a prevailing assumption in deep learning, suggesting that the design of the training objective is a more critical determinant of learning quality than model scale alone, particularly for structured behavior acquisition in robotics. This shift in focus could guide future research towards more efficient and targeted model development.

**2. Foundation for Dynamics-Aware Skill Discovery:** REFACTOR-VLA introduces a novel Behavioral-Equivalence Kernel (BEK) that rigorously defines behavioral equivalence between trajectory fragments using a learned latent world model. By incorporating both value differences and k-step latent rollout distributions, the BEK provides a dynamics-aware metric for clustering, which is crucial for handling the continuous and variable nature of robotic actions. This approach offers a more robust and theoretically grounded method for identifying reusable skills compared to prior methods that rely on syntactic similarity or uncalibrated language models.

**3. Enhanced Performance on Long-Horizon Tasks:** The system's ability to learn and leverage a library of typed motor programs results in improved performance on complex, multi-step manipulation tasks, as demonstrated by its superior results on the LIBERO benchmark suite. This indicates a step towards addressing a core limitation of monolithic VLA models, enabling robots to decompose and execute longer, more intricate sequences of actions.

**4. Improved Interpretability of Robot Behaviors:** By learning structured, typed-lambda programs, REFACTOR-VLA offers a higher level of interpretability for learned robotic skills. These symbolic representations provide clear abstractions of behaviors, moving beyond opaque neural network outputs. This interpretability can facilitate debugging, verification, and human understanding of how robots perform tasks, potentially fostering greater trust and enabling more effective human-robot collaboration.

**5. Advancing Hierarchical Robotic Control:** The wake/sleep framework, coupled with MDL-gated abstraction admission, provides a mechanism for building hierarchical control policies. This allows high-level policies to orchestrate abstract skills from the library, freeing them from the burden of generating low-level motor commands. Such hierarchical control is essential for scaling robotic intelligence to a wider range of complex, real-world tasks.

**6. Future Directions for Real-World Robotics:** The successful empirical validation on a comprehensive benchmark like LIBERO positions REFACTOR-VLA as a foundational step towards more generalist robots. Future work on real-robot transfer and application to larger, uncurated datasets (e.g., OXE-mixed) could extend the system's applicability. The exploration of self-supervised contrastive learning or VLM-emitted pseudo-labels for open-corpus settings suggests pathways for autonomous skill discovery beyond supervised task labels.

Overall, REFACTOR-VLA presents a framework that could contribute to the development of autonomous agents capable of learning, representing, and applying abstract, dynamics-aware skills, moving towards more intelligent and adaptable robotic systems.
