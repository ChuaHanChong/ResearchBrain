# DualVLA: Building a Generalizable Embodied Agent via Partial Decoupling of Reasoning and Action

> [!summary] Summary
> DualVLA introduces a post-training framework that partially decouples reasoning and action learning to address "action degeneration" in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. The framework achieved a 61.0% average success rate on the SimplerEnv benchmark, an 8.0% improvement over its baseline, and increased real-world task success to 60.0% while also introducing a new VLA Score for comprehensive evaluation.

## Problem

- Existing reasoning-capable Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models suffer from "action degeneration," where enhancing reasoning capabilities often leads to degraded manipulation performance.
- Embodied reasoning data frequently contains repetitive, low-entropy segments that can dilute crucial visuomotor learning, causing models to overfit linguistic patterns.
- Current VLA evaluation metrics, primarily task success rates, are too coarse to capture the nuances of reasoning quality, action smoothness, or the alignment between reasoning and action.

## Method

- A dual-layers data pruning strategy identifies and retains only "action-critical" reasoning content by considering both scene event changes and kinematic keyframes.
- A dual-teacher adaptive distillation approach provides differentiated supervision, using a specialist VLA (action teacher) for robot data and the reasoning VLA's initial state (reasoning teacher) for multimodal data.
- ==The VLA Score== evaluation framework leverages a powerful VLM (GPT-4o) as an "intelligent judge" to provide fine-grained, multi-dimensional assessment across reasoning, action, intention, and reason-action alignment.

## Results

- Achieved a **61.0%** average success rate on the SimplerEnv benchmark, representing an **8.0%** improvement over the InstructVLA-G baseline and outperforming other top models.
- Increased success rates from **45%** to **60.0%** in challenging real-world dual-arm robotic manipulation tasks, demonstrating robust generalizability.
- The VLA Score evaluation revealed that specialist VLAs bottleneck in action modeling, while reasoning VLAs struggle with effective action execution, a gap DualVLA effectively bridges with a **20%** inference speedup.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Partially decoupling the learning objectives for reasoning and action at both the data and loss levels is crucial for building generalizable embodied agents that balance cognitive and physical proficiency.
> - Selective data pruning, guided by both semantic events and kinematic changes, effectively reduces data redundancy, focusing training on meaningful reasoning and mitigating action degeneration.
> - An adaptive dual-teacher distillation strategy enables balanced skill acquisition by providing specialized supervision for action execution and general multimodal reasoning, resolving conflicts from mixed-modality training.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the forefront of embodied artificial intelligence, specifically within the rapidly evolving domain of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. VLAs aim to create AI systems that can perceive their environment, understand natural language instructions, reason about tasks, and execute physical actions in the real world.

The evolution of embodied agents can be broadly categorized:
1. **Vision-Language Models (VLMs):** These foundational models (e.g., GPT-4V, LLaVA) excel at multimodal understanding and reasoning across images and text, leveraging vast internet-scale data. However, they lack direct action capabilities in physical environments.
2. **Specialist VLAs:** These models fine-tune VLMs on large-scale robotic datasets (e.g., RT-1, OpenVLA, Octo-Base) to acquire reliable manipulation skills. While effective for specific robotic tasks, they often sacrifice the general multimodal reasoning abilities inherited from their VLM predecessors.
3. **Reasoning VLAs:** Recent efforts aim to reintroduce and enhance reasoning capabilities into specialist VLAs by augmenting robot trajectories with rich reasoning annotations (e.g., Chain-of-Thought) and mixing them with broader multimodal corpora during fine-tuning (e.g., ECoT, Emma-X, InstructVLA-G). This approach seeks to bridge the gap between high-level cognition and low-level control.

**The "Action Degeneration" Problem:** The paper identifies a critical challenge within the reasoning VLA paradigm: a phenomenon termed "action degeneration." It observes that while introducing reasoning data can improve a VLA's understanding, it often leads to a *degradation* in its core manipulation performance compared to the initial specialist VLA. This counter-intuitive outcome, where more data and richer supervision lead to a decline in a key capability, represents a significant hurdle for building truly generalizable embodied agents. This work directly addresses this problem, aiming to create VLAs that excel at *both* reasoning and action simultaneously.

**Evaluation Landscape:** Furthermore, the paper highlights a gap in existing VLA evaluation methodologies. Traditional evaluation primarily relies on task success rates, which are coarse and fail to capture nuances like reasoning quality, action smoothness, intentionality, or the alignment between reasoning and action. This limitation hinders a comprehensive understanding of VLA performance and failure modes. DualVLA introduces "VLA Score," extending the "LLM-as-a-Judge" paradigm from natural language processing to the multimodal, embodied domain, providing a more fine-grained and holistic assessment. This contribution is crucial for advancing the field, as robust evaluation metrics drive better model development.

In essence, DualVLA attempts to reconcile the trade-off between reasoning and action proficiency in embodied agents, a central tension in current VLA research, while also providing a better lens through which to evaluate progress.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **construct a generalizable Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model that can perform both robust multimodal reasoning and precise, reliable action execution in complex environments.** This goal directly confronts a significant challenge observed in the current state-of-the-art: the "action degeneration" problem.

The motivation for DualVLA stems from the following critical observations and challenges:

1. **The Action Degeneration Phenomenon:** Existing approaches to build reasoning-capable VLAs often involve fine-tuning a specialist VLA (trained for manipulation) with mixed data that includes reasoning annotations and general multimodal corpora. The authors consistently observe that this process, while enriching reasoning, paradoxically leads to a *degraded* manipulation performance compared to the original specialist VLA. This "action degeneration" indicates a fundamental imbalance or conflict in the learning process.
2. **Root Causes of Degeneration:** The authors hypothesize that action degeneration arises from two main factors:
 - **Redundant and Low-Entropy Embodied Reasoning:** Embodied reasoning annotations in robotic datasets can be repetitive and weakly coupled to the underlying visuomotor dynamics. For instance, a robot moving towards an object might have the same "Move Near" reasoning annotated across many frames, diluting action-relevant gradients and biasing the model towards linguistic correctness over motor control.
 - **Lack of Differentiated and Fine-Grained Supervision:** When reasoning and action are trained jointly with mixed data, the supervision signals may not be appropriately balanced or specialized. Action tokens might receive weak or noisy gradients, while abundant reasoning tokens dominate the optimization, leading the model to prioritize reasoning at the expense of precise motor control.
3. **Inadequate Evaluation Metrics:** Current VLA evaluation primarily relies on task success rates, which are sparse and coarse-grained. These metrics cannot effectively disentangle the model's reasoning abilities from its action execution, nor can they capture other critical aspects like intention, action smoothness, or the alignment between reasoning and action. This limits researchers' ability to diagnose failures and track progress in building truly intelligent agents.

Therefore, the key motivations for developing DUALVLA are to:
- Overcome the action degeneration problem by designing a post-training framework that explicitly enhances action performance while carefully preserving multimodal reasoning capabilities.
- Decouple the learning of reasoning and action to ensure balanced and effective supervision for both.
- Introduce a more comprehensive and fine-grained evaluation framework (VLA Score) to accurately assess the multifaceted capabilities of reasoning-capable VLAs.

By addressing these issues, DualVLA aims to advance the development of truly generalizable embodied agents that can intelligently interact with the world.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DUALVLA tackles the problem of action degeneration and the need for comprehensive evaluation through a carefully designed post-training framework and a novel assessment pipeline. The core insight is to partially decouple reasoning and action learning at both the data and loss levels.

#### 3.1. Dual-Layers Data Pruning

**Motivation:** The authors observe that embodied reasoning data often contains repetitive, low-entropy segments (e.g., "Move Near" repeated across many frames as a robot approaches an object). This redundancy can dominate the training signal, causing the model to overfit linguistic patterns rather than learning essential visuomotor relationships, thus deteriorating action execution. Manual annotation of critical reasoning segments is costly, and kinematic-only approaches ignore semantic context.

**Approach:** DUALVLA introduces a two-stage data pruning strategy that jointly considers scene event changes and action dynamics to identify and retain only "action-critical" reasoning content. This creates a sparse, information-dense dataset.

1. **Video Event Boundary Detection (Reasoning Scene Label):**
 - This layer identifies moments where significant scene-level events occur, which typically correspond to points where additional reasoning is required.
 - A small set of trajectories is manually annotated with "reasoning scene labels."
 - A specialized event boundary detection model (DDM-Net) is retrained using this high-quality annotated data to automatically generate reasoning scene labels for the rest of the dataset. Frames marked with a reasoning scene label of 1 are considered important from a semantic/cognitive perspective.

2. **Kinematic Key-Frame Selection (Reasoning Action Label):**
 - This layer focuses on changes in the robot's physical motion, which often signal critical action points.
 - Keyframes are identified by detecting abrupt changes in the end-effector pose (velocity changes) or gripper state transitions (open/close).
 - The reasoning action label is set to 1 for frames where the squared Euclidean norm of the end-effector acceleration exceeds the average acceleration across the trajectory OR when the gripper state changes. This is a parameter-free approach.

**Pruning Mechanism:** Only frames where *both* the reasoning scene label and the reasoning action label are 1 are considered "keyframes," and their reasoning content is retained. Reasoning content for all other frames is masked. This selective retention naturally forms a curriculum, allowing the model to first ground actions and then learn reasoning only when truly necessary, preventing early overfitting and mitigating action degeneration.

#### 3.2. Dual-Teacher Adaptive Distillation

**Motivation:** Even with data pruning, action degeneration can still arise from misaligned supervision during mixed-modality finetuning. Specialist VLAs possess strong action skills, while reasoning VLAs benefit from general multimodal understanding. A unified loss might not effectively balance these two distinct objectives.

**Approach:** DUALVLA employs a dual-teacher adaptive distillation strategy that provides differentiated, fine-grained supervision signals based on the data modality.

1. **Action Teacher for Robot Data:**
 - A specialist VLA (e.g., InstructVLA-E), known for its strong action execution capabilities, serves as the "action teacher."
 - For robot-centric data (which primarily requires action skills), an action distillation loss ($L_{action KD}$) is applied. This uses Kullback-Leibler (KL) Divergence to distill the soft-label output distribution of the action teacher's policy ($\pi_{\theta_a}$) into the student model's policy ($\pi_{\theta}$), encouraging the student to learn smooth, structured action patterns.

2. **Reasoning Teacher for Multimodal Data:**
 - The finetuning initialization of the reasoning VLA (e.g., InstructVLA-G), which already possesses strong general reasoning abilities, acts as the "reasoning teacher."
 - For multimodal reasoning data (which primarily requires general understanding), a reasoning distillation loss ($L_{reason KD}$) is applied. This uses KL Divergence to preserve the reasoning capabilities of the student model by aligning its reasoning output with that of the reasoning teacher.

**Adaptive Strategy:** The key is that the distillation is *adaptive*: robot data receives action-aligned supervision, while multimodal reasoning data receives reasoning-aligned supervision.

**Total Loss:** The overall training loss combines the standard VLA training loss (cross-entropy with hard labels, $L_{VLA}$) with the adaptive distillation loss ($L_{KD}$), where $L_{KD}$ switches between $L_{action KD}$ and $L_{reason KD}$ based on the data type. This isomorphic dual-teacher approach reduces gradient conflicts and restores balance, leading to a more generalizable agent.

#### 3.3. VLA Score Evaluation Framework

**Motivation:** Current VLA evaluation metrics (task success rate) are insufficient to capture the nuances of reasoning, action quality, and their alignment.

**Approach:** VLA Score is introduced as a comprehensive, fine-grained evaluation pipeline that leverages a powerful VLM (GPT-4o) as an "intelligent judge," extending the MLLM-as-a-Judge paradigm.

1. **Input:** The evaluation takes a policy trajectory (sequence of images), task description, optional reasoning content (if available from a reasoning VLA), and a simulation success indicator as input.
2. **Four Evaluation Dimensions (0-10 scale):**
 - **Reasoning Score (R):** Correctness, logical consistency, and usefulness of the reasoning process.
 - **Action Score (A):** Coherence, precision, and efficiency of the action sequence.
 - **Intention Score (I):** Whether actions constructively contribute to solving the task.
 - **Reason-Act Alignment Score (RA):** How well actions align with the reasoning content.
3. **Retrieval-Enhanced Judge:** To improve accuracy and generalization, a "knowledge base" of 100 diverse, human-refined trajectories and their preliminary scores is created. For each new input trajectory, a dual-retrieval mechanism (task retrieval for relevant text, scene retrieval for similar visual trajectories) fetches contextual examples from this knowledge base. These retrieved samples serve as few-shot context for the GPT-4o judge, enhancing its evaluation capabilities.
4. **Overall VLA Score:** The individual scores are combined with the binary simulation result (B: 1 for success, 0 for failure) using a weighted formula. For reasoning VLAs: $VLA Score = (R + A \cdot I / 2) \cdot RA \cdot B$. For specialist VLAs (without explicit reasoning): $VLA Score = A \cdot I \cdot RA \cdot B$. This formula ensures that unsuccessful tasks receive a score of zero, emphasizing task completion while penalizing poor sub-scores.

This comprehensive evaluation framework provides deeper insights into model behavior and failure modes, crucial for guiding future VLA development.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate that DUALVLA successfully mitigates action degeneration, enhances overall VLA performance, and offers a robust evaluation framework.

#### 4.1. Simulation Results (SimplerEnv)

- **Overall Performance:** DUALVLA achieved an average success rate of **61.0%** on the SimplerEnv benchmark, significantly outperforming baseline and competitor models.
- **Mitigating Action Degeneration:** DUALVLA improved upon its baseline (InstructVLA-G) by **8.0%** in average success rate, directly demonstrating its effectiveness in resolving action degeneration.
- **Outperformance:** DUALVLA surpassed top-performing specialist VLAs (e.g., InstructVLA-E by 5.0%) and other reasoning VLAs (e.g., ThinkACT by 3.9%), indicating a superior balance between action execution and multimodal understanding.
- **Teacher Surpassing:** An "emergent teacher surpassing phenomenon" was observed, where DUALVLA achieved higher scores than its action teacher, suggesting that the distillation process not only transfers knowledge but also enables the student model to synthesize and potentially exceed the teacher's capabilities.
- **Efficiency:** The dual-layer pruning strategy resulted in approximately a **20% inference speedup** over the baseline, contributing to practical deployment.

#### 4.2. Real-World Results (Galaxea R1-lite dual-arm tasks)

- **Significant Improvement:** DUALVLA demonstrated substantial gains in real-world robotic tasks ("Move Objects" and "Handover Objects" with ordered manipulation of three objects), increasing the average success rate from 45% (InstructVLA baseline) to **60.0%**.
- **Robustness:** These gains across complex dual-arm scenarios underscore DUALVLA's ability to generate more reliable and coordinated actions in physical environments.

#### 4.3. VLA Score Results

- **VLM as Evaluator Validation:** The paper asserts that powerful VLMs like GPT-4o are capable of evaluating VLA capabilities, drawing parallels to their proven video understanding and reasoning abilities.
- **DUALVLA's Superiority:** DUALVLA achieved the highest VLA Score among reasoning VLAs, confirming its balanced performance across all dimensions.
- **Insights into Bottlenecks:**
 - **Specialist VLAs:** Analysis revealed that for specialist VLAs, the intention score was slightly higher than the action score. This indicated that their primary bottleneck lies in achieving smoother, more robust, and efficient *action modeling*.
 - **Reasoning VLAs:** For reasoning VLAs, the reasoning score was significantly higher than both action and alignment scores. This highlighted that while these models could reason correctly, they often struggled with *effective action execution* (e.g., approaching or manipulating targets precisely).
- **DUALVLA's Mechanism:** DUALVLA's success is attributed to its ability to inherit the reasoning prowess of the reasoning teacher while simultaneously acquiring the refined, smooth actions from the action teacher, a combination that traditional cross-entropy training alone cannot achieve.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Overall Strategy Validation:** Macro-level ablations confirmed that both data pruning and dual-teacher adaptive distillation strategies are crucial. Fine-tuning without these strategies yielded only marginal gains.
- **Pruning Effectiveness:** Two-layer data pruning significantly outperformed single-layer pruning and random dropout, proving that the deliberate design (combining scene and action cues) is key to its effectiveness. Scene pruning contributed the majority of the gain, with action pruning providing a complementary boost.
- **Distillation Effectiveness:**
 - The dual-teacher adaptive design was essential for simultaneously strengthening action generation and preserving general multimodal understanding.
 - Using *only* the action teacher improved action performance but led to a substantial drop in multimodal capability, underscoring the necessity of the reasoning teacher to maintain generalization.
 - Distillation from the reasoning teacher effectively preserved multimodal reasoning capabilities.
- **Extrapolation Ability:** Applying the DUALVLA framework to OpenVLA and ECoT (yielding OpenVLA-Dual) showed that it surpassed the action teacher (OpenVLA) in action and outperformed the reasoning teacher (ECoT) in multimodal generalization, confirming the broad applicability of the method. Modest action gains with OpenVLA were attributed to its discrete-token action representation limitations.

In summary, the results consistently demonstrate DUALVLA's ability to achieve a superior balance between reasoning and action, outperforming existing methods in both simulated and real-world environments, and providing a powerful new tool for evaluating embodied agent capabilities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The DUALVLA paper presents a significant contribution to the field of embodied AI, addressing a fundamental challenge and offering innovative solutions with broad implications.

1. **Resolution of the "Action Degeneration" Problem:** The most significant impact of DUALVLA is its direct and effective solution to the "action degeneration" phenomenon. This problem has been a major bottleneck in developing truly generalizable embodied agents, as it forces a trade-off between cognitive abilities (reasoning) and physical proficiency (action). By demonstrating that it's possible to enhance both simultaneously, DUALVLA paves the way for robots that are not only intelligent in their understanding but also capable and reliable in their physical execution. This moves the field closer to agents that can seamlessly integrate high-level planning with low-level control.

2. **Novel Methodological Contributions:**
 - **Dual-Layer Data Pruning:** This innovative approach to curating embodied reasoning data, leveraging both semantic event changes and kinematic cues, offers a principled way to reduce redundancy and focus supervision on action-critical moments. This method can inspire future data preprocessing techniques for complex multimodal datasets, making training more efficient and effective.
 - **Dual-Teacher Adaptive Distillation:** The adaptive distillation strategy provides a powerful paradigm for training models with multiple, potentially conflicting objectives. By assigning domain-specific teachers (action teacher for robot data, reasoning teacher for multimodal data), the framework ensures balanced and fine-grained supervision, mitigating gradient conflicts and fostering balanced skill acquisition. This can be generalized to other multi-task or multi-capability learning scenarios in AI.

3. **Introduction of VLA Score:** The proposed VLA Score is a crucial advancement for the evaluation of embodied agents. By extending the "LLM-as-a-Judge" paradigm to VLAs and providing a fine-grained assessment across reasoning, action, intention, and reason-action alignment, it offers:
 - **Deeper Insights:** Researchers can move beyond simple success rates to understand *why* a robot succeeds or fails, identifying specific weaknesses in reasoning, execution, or alignment.
 - **Targeted Development:** This detailed feedback enables more focused and efficient development cycles, as engineers can pinpoint and address specific bottlenecks in their models.
 - **New Benchmark Standard:** VLA Score has the potential to become a new standard for evaluating reasoning-capable VLAs, promoting fair comparisons and accelerating progress in the field.

4. **Enhanced Generalizability and Real-World Applicability:** The demonstrated success of DUALVLA in both diverse simulation environments (SimplerEnv) and challenging real-world dual-arm robotic tasks showcases its robust generalizability. This indicates that the proposed methods are not merely theoretical improvements but have practical implications for deploying more capable and autonomous robots in various domains, from manufacturing and logistics to assistive robotics.

5. **Setting Future Research Directions:** The paper explicitly acknowledges limitations, such as the reliance on two teacher models and increased computational overhead during distillation. These point towards important future research avenues, including simplifying distillation pipelines, reducing teacher dependency, and extending the framework to even broader embodied settings. This clear articulation of next steps further positions the work as a foundational contribution.

In essence, DUALVLA represents a significant step forward in the quest for truly generalizable embodied intelligence. By providing both a novel training methodology to reconcile reasoning and action, and a sophisticated evaluation framework to assess them, it lays crucial groundwork for the development of more intelligent, versatile, and reliable robotic systems.
