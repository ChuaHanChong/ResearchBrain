# Sample-efficient Low-level Motion Planning for Robotic Manipulation Tasks via Zero-shot Transfer Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework named iCEM+TL, developed by Cardiff University researchers, enhances low-level motion planning for complex robotic manipulation tasks like stacking and shelf placement by integrating Transfer Learning and Reward Redesign into the iCEM algorithm. This approach achieves success rate improvements of up to 23% in simulation compared to baseline iCEM and was successfully validated on a physical Franka Emika FR3 robot.

## Problem

- Increasing complexity of robotic manipulation tasks leads to computationally intensive motion planning models and extensive training times for high-level Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) approaches.
- Existing low-level motion planners, such as iCEM, show performance limitations in complex, long-horizon manipulation scenarios (e.g., multi-object stacking, shelf placement).
- Current transfer learning research primarily focuses on DRL policies, leaving an opportunity to improve optimization-based low-level planners through knowledge reuse.

## Method

- Developed **iCEM+TL**, a model-based planning framework that integrates Transfer Learning (TL) and ==Reward Redesign== (RR) into the Sample-efficient ==Cross-Entropy Method== (iCEM).
- TL is implemented through zero-shot transfer of iCEM sampling distribution parameters (mean and standard deviation) and continuous transfer of elite trajectories from simpler "upstream" tasks.
- RR decomposes complex task objectives into elementary reward modules (e.g., for object placement, gripper proximity, and vertical lifting) to provide more granular guidance to the planner.

## Results

- The iCEM+TL framework achieved an absolute success rate gain of approximately **23.0%** on the FetchStack task compared to the vanilla iCEM baseline in simulation.
- iCEM+TL consistently outperformed other learning-based baselines like CEE-US and PointFlowMatch in the FetchSlide and newly introduced Shelf tasks, demonstrating superior generalization.
- Successful real-world validation on a Franka Emika FR3 robot for a stacking task confirmed the practical feasibility and deployment potential of the framework by executing simulation-identified optimal trajectories.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Transferring knowledge, specifically iCEM sampling distribution parameters and elite trajectories, from structurally aligned "upstream" tasks significantly boosts performance in complex "downstream" tasks, guided by an "elite reward advantage" criterion.
> - The synergistic combination of Transfer Learning and Reward Redesign provides substantial performance gains for complex, long-horizon manipulation tasks, outperforming either component applied in isolation.
> - Enhanced optimization-based low-level planners can effectively tackle challenging robotic manipulation tasks, demonstrating a computationally efficient alternative to methods that rely heavily on high-level learned policies.
