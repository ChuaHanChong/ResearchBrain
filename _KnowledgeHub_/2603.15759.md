# Simulation Distillation: Pretraining World Models in Simulation for Rapid Real-World Adaptation

> [!summary] Summary
> SimDist enables robotic systems to rapidly adapt to real-world tasks by pretraining a modular world model in simulation and selectively finetuning its latent dynamics in the physical environment. This approach demonstrates consistent performance improvements, achieving 1.5x to 2x higher task throughput within 15-30 minutes of real-world interaction across various manipulation and locomotion tasks.

## Problem

- Real-world robotic learning struggles with data scarcity, instability, and catastrophic forgetting for complex, long-horizon tasks.
- Training robust world models suitable for planning in the real world is data-prohibitive due to the need for vast, diverse, and off-policy trajectories.
- The sim-to-real gap complicates direct transfer of simulated models, as real-world dynamics often differ significantly.

## Method

- Pretrains a modular, planning-oriented latent ==world model== in simulation using diverse trajectories, including perturbed actions, and dense supervision.
- Transfers and freezes the state encoder, reward model, and value function to the real world, adapting only the latent dynamics model using limited real-world data.
- Utilizes Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) control, guided by the transferred reward and value functions, for efficient real-world task execution.

## Results

- Achieved **1.5x** to **2x** higher task throughput and superior success rates on four manipulation and quadruped locomotion tasks, outperforming model-free RL baselines.
- Demonstrated rapid and monotonic improvement within 15-30 minutes of real-world interaction, effectively closing the sim-to-real dynamics gap.
- Ablation studies confirmed the importance of freezing core world model components and the necessity of diverse, large-scale simulation pretraining data for robust transfer.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Global task structure, embodied in the state encoder, reward model, and value function, is largely invariant to the sim-to-real gap and directly transferable.
> - Latent dynamics are sensitive to the sim-to-real gap but can be efficiently adapted in the real world as a supervised system identification problem.
> - Pretraining with diverse simulated trajectories and dense supervision, without a pixel reconstruction loss, yields robust, transferable representations for planning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses the challenge of enabling robotic systems to adapt their behavior efficiently and reliably in novel real-world environments with limited interaction data. This is particularly difficult for long-horizon, contact-rich tasks, where small errors can compound, requiring robust planning capabilities.

Current approaches in robotic learning face several limitations:
- **End-to-end policy finetuning (model-free reinforcement learning):** Methods such as RLPD and IQL often struggle with data scarcity, can be unstable, and are prone to catastrophic forgetting of learned priors when finetuned in new domains. This is partly due to the tight entanglement of task representations, reward/value estimates, dynamics, and action selection, making adaptation updates challenging.
- **Model-based reinforcement learning (MBRL):** While MBRL, exemplified by approaches like Dreamer and TD-MPC, learns dynamics and reward models to reason about unseen trajectories, training such world models directly in the real world demands diverse, action-conditioned data at scales that are prohibitively expensive to collect. Planning algorithms, especially, require models that generalize over various outcomes, including mistakes and recoveries, to effectively discriminate between action sequences over long horizons.
- **Adaptation with physics-based models:** Traditional adaptive control and model-predictive control (MPC) methods frequently rely on simplified, low-dimensional state representations, which can be brittle in partially observed, contact-rich scenarios. Neural physics engines aim to adapt high-fidelity simulators but often require privileged information like object poses or contact labels, which are difficult to obtain in real-world settings.
- **Generative world models for robotics:** Recent efforts involve training large video models on extensive datasets, sometimes augmented with simulation data, to learn physical priors. However, these models often focus on pixel-level reconstruction or reproducing demonstrated behaviors, and their ability to generalize to novel, off-policy actions for effective planning can be constrained by the distribution of real-world training data.

This research positions itself by leveraging the modularity of world models and the scalability of simulation to overcome these limitations. It seeks to combine the benefits of rich simulation data for pretraining robust representations, rewards, and value functions with an efficient, targeted adaptation strategy for real-world dynamics. The approach aims to provide the broad data coverage needed for planning-oriented models that can reliably predict outcomes beyond expert behavior, without incurring the data cost of real-world collection or the instability of end-to-end policy updates.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce Simulation Distillation (SimDist), a framework designed to pretrain robust world models in simulation and enable their rapid, reliable adaptation to real-world robotic tasks with minimal real-world interaction.

The motivation for developing SimDist stems from several core challenges in current robot learning paradigms:

1. **Inefficiency and Brittleness of Real-World Reinforcement Learning:** Training robots directly in the real world is data-intensive, slow, and often unstable, especially for complex, contact-rich tasks with long horizons. End-to-end policy finetuning methods frequently suffer from catastrophic forgetting, where new real-world data overwrites critical priors learned during pretraining, leading to performance degradation.
2. **Limitations of Model-Free vs. Model-Based RL:**
 - **Model-free methods:** Tightly entangle task representations, reward/value estimates, dynamics, and action selection. This makes adaptation updates broad and difficult to manage, especially with sparse, long-horizon rewards.
 - **Model-based methods:** Offer a compelling alternative by modularizing decision-making into environment prediction (dynamics) and credit assignment (reward/value). This separation could allow for more targeted adaptation. However, learning a world model suitable for planning requires vast amounts of diverse, action-conditioned data, including off-policy trajectories and examples of mistakes and recoveries, which are impractical to collect purely in the real world.
3. **Untapped Potential of Simulation:** Physics simulators offer a scalable and cost-effective source of robot experience. Crucially, they provide privileged access to the underlying state, allowing for dense, informative supervision (e.g., ground-truth rewards, optimal value functions, and expert policies) that is often difficult or impossible to obtain at scale in the real world. This rich supervision can help train robust state representations and accurate reward/value models.
4. **Addressing the Sim-to-Real Gap:** While simulation provides abundant data, a significant gap often exists between simulated and real-world dynamics. The core hypothesis driving SimDist is that not all components of a world model are equally sensitive to this gap. Specifically, global task structure, captured by the state encoder, reward model, and value function, is hypothesized to be largely invariant and transferable, as it primarily defines a ranking over states for planning. In contrast, the latent dynamics model, which predicts future states given actions, is sensitive to the sim-to-real gap but could be efficiently adapted.

By addressing these points, SimDist aims to:
- **Bootstrapping in Simulation:** Systematically generate diverse rollouts in simulation, including optimal, sub-optimal, and perturbed action sequences, to expose the world model to a broad range of experiences relevant for planning.
- **Efficient Real-World Adaptation:** Transfer the learned encoder, reward model, and value function directly to the real world and only finetune the latent dynamics model using limited real-world interaction data. This reduces real-world adaptation to a supervised system identification problem, avoiding the complexities of long-horizon credit assignment.
- **Leverage Planning Signals:** Ensure that the transferred reward and value models provide immediate, dense, long-horizon planning signals, allowing a short-horizon planner to improve performance rapidly as dynamics predictions become more accurate.
- **Achieve Robustness and Reliability:** Develop a framework that enables steady, monotonic improvement with real-world experience, overcoming the degradation and instability often observed in prior adaptation methods.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SimDist operates in two main phases: pretraining a world model in simulation and adapting it in the real world. The framework leverages a modular world model architecture combined with sampling-based planning.

**A. World Model Architecture**
The core of SimDist is a planning-oriented latent world model that maps raw observations to a latent state and predicts future states, rewards, and values. The model comprises several components:
- **Latent Representation (Eθ):** Encodes the most recent raw observation (o_t) into a latent state (z_t).
- **History Representation (Cθ):** Processes a window of past observations and actions (o_{t-H:t-1}, a_{t-H:t-1}) to form a history embedding (h_t). To minimize inference cost, it primarily uses proprioceptive history and only the most recent exteroceptive observation.
- **Latent Dynamics (fθ):** A transformer-based model that predicts a sequence of T future latent states (ẑ_{t+1:t+T}) conditioned on the current latent state (z_t), history (h_t), and a sequence of candidate actions (a_{t:t+T-1}). This model uses chunked prediction, generating T future states in a single forward pass for parallelism.
- **Reward Prediction (Rθ) and Value Prediction (Vθ):** Transformer-based models that evaluate the predicted latent trajectory (ẑ_{t:t+T}) and action sequence to output predicted reward (r̂_{t:t+T-1}) and value (v̂_{t+1:t+T}) sequences. These models attend over the entire trajectory to capture sequence-level structure.
- **Base Policy (πθ):** Predicts action chunks (â_{t:t+H}) to warm-start sampling-based planning.

**B. Pretraining on Simulated Data**
1. **Expert Policy Training:** A state-based expert policy (πe) and an optimal state-based value function (Ve) are initially trained using reinforcement learning in a physics simulator (e.g., using existing sim-to-real pipelines). Intermediate policy checkpoints are saved.
2. **Generating Diverse Trajectories and Dense Supervision:** To ensure broad coverage and robust generalization for planning, a large-scale dataset (Dsim) is generated in simulation. This involves:
 - Alternating between the expert policy (πe) and sub-optimal policy checkpoints ({πk}).
 - Periodically injecting random, contiguous action perturbations into the policy's actions over short temporal windows.
 - This strategy yields diverse trajectories encompassing expert behaviors, failures, and recovery attempts, crucial for a world model that must predict counterfactual outcomes.
 - **Supervision:** Rewards (rt) are computed directly from privileged simulator state. Value targets (vt) are provided by querying the expert value function (Ve) at each state. An expert action flag (b^e_t) distinguishes expert actions from perturbed ones.
 - **Perceptual Randomization:** Extensive randomization of visual observations is applied to promote robust encoder transfer to the real world.
3. **World Model Pretraining Loss (Lsim):** The world model is pretrained on Dsim using a combined loss function that minimizes:
 - L2 loss between predicted latent states (ẑ_{t+i+1}) and ground-truth latent states (Eθ(o_{t+i+1})), with a stop-gradient operator on the target to stabilize training.
 - L2 loss for reward prediction (r̂_{t+i}) against simulator rewards (r_{t+i}).
 - L2 loss for value prediction (v̂_{t+i+1}) against expert value targets (v_{t+i+1}).
 - L2 loss for behavior cloning (π̂_{t+i}) against expert actions (a_{t+i}), only for uncorrupted expert actions.
 - **No Reconstruction Loss:** Unlike many world models, SimDist explicitly avoids pixel-level reconstruction. The authors argue this prevents the latent state from encoding task-irrelevant visual artifacts from randomization, benefiting sim-to-real transfer.

**C. Real-World Transfer and Efficient Dynamics Adaptation**
1. **Component Transfer:** At deployment, the state encoder (Eθ), history encoder (Cθ), reward model (Rθ), and value function (Vθ) learned in simulation are transferred directly to the real robot and **frozen**. Only the latent dynamics model (fθ) is subject to finetuning. The base policy (πθ) is also frozen.
2. **Dynamics Adaptation Loss (Lreal):** Real-world adaptation focuses solely on updating fθ. The loss function is an L2 loss between predicted latent states (ẑ_{t+i+1}) and ground-truth latent states derived from real-world observations (Eθ(o_{t+i+1})), with Eθ frozen. This approach reduces adaptation to a supervised system identification problem.
3. **Iterative Improvement:** SimDist employs an iterative loop for real-world improvement:
 - The robot collects M on-policy rollouts using MPPI with the current world model.
 - This real-world data is added to a real-world dataset (Dreal).
 - The latent dynamics model (fθ) is finetuned on Dreal to minimize Lreal.
 - This process iterates, enabling the robot to continuously improve. This off-policy learning strategy also allows for incorporating diverse data sources like demonstrations.
4. **Sampling-Based Planning:** Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) control is used to control the robot in the real world. At each control step, MPPI samples a batch of candidate action sequences. These are evaluated by the world model, which predicts future rewards and terminal values. MPPI then calculates the action to execute by importance-weighting trajectories based on their predicted returns. The base policy (πθ) warm-starts the sampling process by providing noise-corrupted action sequences.

**D. Robotic Systems and Tasks**
The framework was evaluated on four real-world tasks across two robotic systems:
- **Manipulation Tasks (UR5e robot):**
 - **Peg Insertion:** Inserting a square peg into a hole (16mm).
 - **Table Leg:** Picking, aligning, and threading a table leg into a hole.
 - Both tasks were tested under "Narrow" (2x2 cm) and "Wide" (35x35 cm) initial condition distributions, representing varying difficulty.
 - Observations: Joint states and three 224x224 RGB images. Actions: 6D relative end-effector pose targets and a binary gripper action.
- **Quadruped Locomotion Tasks (Unitree Go2 robot):**
 - **Slippery Slope:** Traversing two inclined PTFE-covered panels with low-friction feet.
 - **Foam:** Traversing two 5cm thick compliant memory foam pads.
 - Observations: Proprioception and a local terrain height map. Actions: 12 joint position targets.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate SimDist's effectiveness in rapid real-world adaptation compared to existing methods.

**A. Real-World Improvement Across Tasks**
- **Superior Performance:** SimDist consistently achieves higher success rates and greater forward progress across all four manipulation and quadruped locomotion tasks, significantly outperforming model-free RL baselines (RLPD, IQL, SGFT-SAC) and behavior cloning methods (Diffusion Policy, π0.5).
- **Rapid and Consistent Adaptation:** SimDist exhibits rapid and monotonic improvement with limited real-world data (15-30 minutes), successfully overcoming the dynamics gap. In contrast, model-free RL baselines often show limited progress or degrade due to catastrophic forgetting during online finetuning.
- **Sample Efficiency:** SimDist is more sample-efficient than SGFT-SAC, a model-free method that also transfers a simulated value function, by leveraging the world model for counterfactual predictions.
- **Robustness to Task Difficulty:** The performance gap between SimDist and baselines widens with increasing task difficulty (e.g., Peg Insertion and Table Leg with "Wide" initial conditions), indicating that broad simulation pretraining enables SimDist to retain structural priors and improve over a larger state-space.
- **Throughput Improvement:** SimDist reliably improves the rate of successful task completions, achieving 1.5x to 2x higher throughput over its zero-shot performance.
- **Integration of Demonstrations:** SimDist's performance can be further boosted when provided with real-world demonstrations (SimDist+BC), highlighting its ability to integrate heterogeneous data sources.

**B. Analysis of Real-World Predictions and Effects on Planning**
- **Value Function Transfer:** Visualizations show that the transferred encoder (Eθ) and value function (Vθ) reliably track task progress and effectively discriminate between successful and failed real-world trajectories. Predicted values increase during successful rollouts and drop sharply during failures.
- **Latent Dynamics Accuracy:** On the Quadruped Slippery Slope task, finetuning drastically reduces the latent dynamics prediction loss (from 0.076 to 0.019). The pretrained model incorrectly predicts stable contact and fails to anticipate slip, whereas the finetuned model accurately predicts future slippage, closely matching the real trajectory.
- **Impact on Planning Behavior:** Improved dynamics predictions directly alter the planner's behavior. Plans generated with the finetuned dynamics model account for real-world dynamics (e.g., slippage), leading to qualitatively different and more effective actions compared to plans derived from the pretrained model.
- **Robust Representations without Reconstruction:** Even without a pixel reconstruction loss, the encoder learns robust representations of the real-world scene. Auxiliary decoders, trained on real-world data, can reconstruct visual observations from these latent states, suggesting the encoder captures essential scene features relevant for the task.

**C. Ablation Studies**
- **Unfreezing World Model Components (Manipulation tasks):**
 - **Unfrozen Encoder:** Leads to complete performance loss. The frozen reward and value heads receive latent representations that are outside their training distribution, rendering them ineffective.
 - **Unfrozen Value Function:** Causes catastrophic forgetting and performance degradation. This reintroduces the problem of long-horizon credit assignment from limited real-world data, which SimDist aims to avoid.
- **Data Scale and Diversity (Simulation):**
 - Reducing the simulation pretraining dataset to 50% or 10% of its full size results in sharp performance drops for both manipulation and quadruped tasks, emphasizing the importance of large-scale data.
 - Pretraining solely on "expert-only" trajectories (without diverse failures and recoveries) also leads to a substantial performance decrease, confirming the necessity of broad data coverage for robust planning.
- **Reward and Value Transformers (Simulation):** Replacing the sequence-to-sequence transformer-based reward and value models with per-timestep MLP decoders consistently degrades performance across tasks. This indicates the benefit of capturing trajectory-level structure for accurate return estimation.
- **Reconstruction Loss (Simulation):** Adding an observation reconstruction loss to the training objective results in a steep performance drop for manipulation tasks, supporting the claim that it can force the latent state to encode task-irrelevant visual details, hindering transfer. For the quadruped, it shows a slight improvement.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The SimDist framework presents a significant contribution to the field of robotic learning, particularly in the domain of sim-to-real transfer and real-world reinforcement learning.

**Key areas of significance include:**

- **Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap:** SimDist offers a robust and efficient method for overcoming the dynamics gap between simulation and reality, a longstanding challenge in robotics. By strategically leveraging the strengths of simulation for pretraining and targeting real-world adaptation, it enables robots to operate reliably in physical environments.
- **Addressing Data Scarcity in Real-World RL:** The framework addresses the critical problem of data scarcity in real-world robot learning. By achieving rapid adaptation with only 15-30 minutes of real-world interaction, SimDist substantially reduces the time, cost, and risk associated with deploying and improving robotic systems. This is particularly impactful for complex, contact-rich, and long-horizon tasks where real-world data collection is expensive and prone to failures.
- **Robustness and Reliability:** SimDist demonstrates consistent and monotonic performance improvement, which contrasts with the instability and catastrophic forgetting often observed in prior real-world RL methods. This reliability is crucial for practical robotic deployment.
- **Validation of World Model Modularity:** The work reinforces the advantages of modular world model architectures. By effectively separating and freezing components (encoder, reward, value) that represent global task structure while adapting only the environment-specific latent dynamics, SimDist highlights a powerful paradigm for efficient and stable learning.
- **Enhanced Planning Capabilities:** The ability to pretrain reward and value functions that provide dense, long-horizon planning signals from raw observations allows the system to engage in effective counterfactual reasoning immediately upon deployment. This enables the planner to leverage improved dynamics predictions for better behavior, even with limited real-world experience.
- **Practical Applicability for Industry:** The rapid adaptation capabilities and robustness of SimDist hold significant potential for industrial applications, such as automated manufacturing, logistics, and hazardous environment operations, where robots need to quickly adapt to changing conditions or new task variations.

**Potential Future Impact:**

- **Foundation for More Complex Tasks:** The principles of SimDist could be extended to more complex tasks, including those requiring dexterous manipulation, human-robot interaction, or multi-robot coordination, by providing a scalable pathway for initial model training and subsequent real-world refinement.
- **Integration with Broader Data Sources:** While the current work focuses on simulation data, the framework's ability to incorporate heterogeneous data (e.g., demonstrations) suggests potential for integration with internet-scale video datasets or other rich sensing modalities, further enhancing its pretraining capabilities.
- **Advancements in Adaptive Control:** SimDist's approach to dynamics adaptation through supervised system identification could inspire new research in adaptive control for partially observable and high-dimensional robotic systems, moving beyond simplified analytical models.
- **Inspiration for Next-Generation World Models:** The ablation studies, particularly regarding the role of reconstruction loss and the benefits of trajectory-level return modeling, offer valuable insights for the design of future world models tailored for robotic control and adaptation.

The authors acknowledge limitations, such as potential performance caps when the transferred value function saturates, suggesting future work on selectively updating value functions. Additionally, the approach still relies on sufficient simulation coverage, indicating a need for continued advancements in simulator fidelity and data generation techniques. Despite these, SimDist represents a substantial step towards more autonomous, adaptable, and deployable robotic systems.
