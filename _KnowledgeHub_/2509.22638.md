# Language Models Can Learn from Verbal Feedback Without Scalar Rewards

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces the Feedback-Conditional Policy (FCP) framework, enabling Large Language Models to learn directly from verbal feedback without converting it into scalar rewards. The FCP, particularly when enhanced with an online bootstrapping stage, achieves performance comparable to or exceeding established scalar-based reinforcement learning methods while preserving the richness of feedback.

## Problem

- Reliance on scalar rewards in Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) causes information loss from rich, nuanced verbal feedback.
- Mapping complex, mixed, or ambiguous human and AI feedback to precise scalar values is inherently ambiguous and arbitrary.
- Maintaining consistent reward scales across diverse tasks (e.g., math, coding) is difficult, leading to imbalanced learning signals and potential biases.

## Method

- A ==Feedback-Conditional Policy (FCP)== is proposed, treating verbal feedback as a direct conditioning signal for LLMs, similar to captions in text-to-image generation.
- An ==offline training stage== initializes the FCP by maximizing the likelihood of observed response-feedback pairs, utilizing diverse feedback types.
- An ==online bootstrapping stage== iteratively refines the policy by generating responses conditioned on desired positive feedback and updating with fresh, real-world verbal feedback from an environment.

## Results

- FCP+Bootstrap achieved an average accuracy of **38.7%** on mathematical reasoning and **47.8%** on general reasoning benchmarks, matching or surpassing leading scalar-based baselines like GRPO and RFT.
- The model demonstrated strong controllability, significantly increasing accuracy when conditioned on 'fully_positive' feedback (**68.5%** on MATH500) and adapting to stylistic requests like 'has_code' (**74.3%** of responses containing code).
- FCP exhibited robustness against reward hacking, maintaining high accuracy with lower assigned scalar rewards compared to GRPO, suggesting it learns a more direct alignment to desired behavior.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large Language Models can effectively learn and be aligned directly from verbal feedback, challenging the long-standing necessity of scalar rewards in language-centric systems.
> - Direct verbal conditioning preserves the full nuance and context of feedback, avoiding information loss and ambiguity inherent in scalarization.
> - The framework enables fine-grained control over LLM behavior and stylistic attributes at inference time by leveraging diverse feedback without explicit categorization of positive/negative signals.
