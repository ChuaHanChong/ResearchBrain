# Contextual Integrity in LLMs via Reasoning and Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Purdue University and Microsoft developed a framework that enhances large language models' adherence to Contextual Integrity (CI) by integrating Chain-of-Thought reasoning and reinforcement learning. This approach reduces the inadvertent disclosure of private information, achieving improved integrity scores and allowing smaller models to outperform larger baselines in CI tasks, while also maintaining or enhancing helpfulness on established benchmarks.

## Problem

- Large language model (LLM) based agents frequently struggle with Contextual Integrity (CI), inadvertently revealing confidential data even without malicious prompting.
- Existing mitigation methods primarily rely on external safeguards or strict information access limits, which can be challenged by complex, entangled information in systems like retrieval-augmented generation.
- LLMs possess strong reasoning capabilities but do not consistently apply correct judgments regarding the nuanced, context-dependent appropriateness of information sharing.

## Method

- A ==Contextual Integrity== Chain of Thought (**CI-CoT**) prompting strategy was developed, guiding LLMs through a structured reasoning phase to assess information appropriateness before generating a response.
- A synthetic dataset of approximately 700 examples was automatically generated using GPT-4 to efficiently represent diverse CI scenarios and norms for training and validation.
- A post-training framework called Contextual Integrity Reinforcement Learning (CI-RL) was implemented, leveraging the GRPO algorithm with a rule-based reward function to fine-tune LLMs to internalize CI reasoning.

## Results

- The CI-RL framework consistently improved Integrity and Complete metrics across various LLM sizes and families on the synthetic dataset; for example, Qwen2.5-**7B**-Instruct showed an Integrity increase from **46.9%** to **75.0%**.
- Smaller models, after CI-RL training, outperformed larger baseline models in CI metrics, with Qwen2.5-**7B**-Instruct + CI-RL surpassing the baseline Qwen2.5-**14B**-Instruct in both Integrity and Completeness.
- On the PrivacyLens benchmark, CI-CoT reduced leakage rates, and CI-RL further optimized the balance between safety and helpfulness by maintaining or enhancing utility while achieving lower leakage rates compared to CI-CoT alone.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly instructing LLMs to reason about contextual norms (CI-CoT) significantly improves their ability to discern appropriate information sharing.
> - Reinforcement learning (CI-RL) can effectively instill deeper, intrinsic CI reasoning capabilities within LLMs, enabling smaller models to achieve better CI performance than larger baseline models.
> - The reward function in the RL framework can be adjusted to manage the trade-off between integrity (avoiding leaks) and utility (providing helpful information), allowing for fine-tuning based on specific safety and performance requirements.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work addresses a critical challenge in the development and deployment of autonomous agents powered by large language models (LLMs): ensuring their actions are safe, particularly concerning information disclosure. As LLM-based agents increasingly operate on behalf of users and interact with the external world, the concept of Contextual Integrity (CI) becomes central. CI, originally defined by Helen Nissenbaum, posits that information flow is considered private and appropriate if it aligns with the social norms of a specific context, encompassing sender, receiver, data subject, information type, and transmission principles.

Existing research has demonstrated that LLMs frequently struggle with CI, often inadvertently revealing confidential data even without malicious prompting. This vulnerability arises because models may not discern the appropriateness of sharing certain information within a given context, leading to privacy breaches and erosion of trust. While some approaches attempt to mitigate this by strictly limiting an agent's access to information or employing external safeguards (e.g., "data minimizer" LLMs, firewalls), these methods can be challenged by entangled information in complex systems like retrieval-augmented generation.

This paper differentiates itself by proposing a method that trains LLMs to *internalize* CI norms directly. The authors aim to move beyond external filters by enhancing the LLM's intrinsic reasoning capabilities regarding CI. This approach is presented as orthogonal to and potentially complementary with existing system-level defenses, as it focuses on modifying the model's behavior rather than solely controlling its environment or inputs. The core novelty lies in being the first to explicitly leverage Reinforcement Learning (RL) to instill CI reasoning capabilities in LLMs, demonstrating successful transfer from synthetic training scenarios to established human-annotated CI benchmarks. The work positions CI reasoning as a fundamental component of the alignment process for real-world LLM-based agents.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the ability of large language models (LLMs) to adhere to the principles of Contextual Integrity (CI) when operating as autonomous agents. This overarching goal is broken down into two main hypotheses and motivations:

1. **Leveraging LLM Reasoning for CI Assessment:** The first objective is to investigate whether the inherent reasoning capabilities of LLMs can be harnessed to improve their adherence to CI principles. The motivation is based on the observation that LLMs, while possessing strong reasoning abilities and knowledge about sensitive information, may not consistently apply correct judgments due to the nuanced nature of contextual integrity. The researchers hypothesize that by instructing models to engage in structured reasoning about contextual norms before disclosing information, their ability to discern appropriate sharing can be improved. This led to the development of the Contextual Integrity Chain of Thought (CI-CoT) prompting strategy.

2. **Enhancing Contextual Awareness through Reinforcement Learning:** The second, and central, objective is to develop a post-training framework that significantly enhances LLMs' contextual awareness and CI reasoning through reinforcement learning (RL). The motivation stems from the insight that CI is fundamentally a reasoning task. Analogous to how LLMs are trained for other complex reasoning domains (e.g., coding, mathematics) using RL to reward correct reasoning behavior, the authors propose training LLMs to reason explicitly about CI using Chain-of-Thought (CoT) within an RL framework. This approach, termed Contextual Integrity Reinforcement Learning (CI-RL), is motivated by the potential to instill a deeper, more consistent understanding of CI within the model's parameters, rather than solely relying on prompt-time instructions.

To achieve these objectives, the research also set out to:
- Construct a synthetic dataset that efficiently and diversely represents CI scenarios and norms, enabling the exploration and validation of the proposed RL approach.
- Evaluate the effectiveness of the developed methods not only on this synthetic dataset but also on established human-annotated benchmarks to demonstrate the generalizability and real-world relevance of the framework.

Ultimately, the motivation is to mitigate the risk of LLMs inadvertently revealing confidential data, thereby increasing the safety and trustworthiness of LLM-based agents operating in diverse, real-world tasks on behalf of users.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology involves a three-pronged approach: structured reasoning prompting, synthetic dataset curation, and reinforcement learning-based fine-tuning.

#### 3.1. Chain of Thought Reasoning for Contextual Integrity (CI-CoT)

The initial step leverages Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting. The model is presented with a structured prompt (Figure 2 in the paper) that explicitly guides it through a two-phase process:
1. **Reasoning Phase:** Enclosed within `<think>` and `</think>` tags, the model is instructed to:
 - Understand the user's task and the broader context.
 - Evaluate each provided personal attribute, determining if sharing it is "necessary, helpful, optional, or inappropriate."
 - Justify this decision based on the principles of Contextual Integrity (purpose, role, transmission principles).
2. **Response Phase:** Enclosed within `<answer>` and `</answer>` tags, the model generates its final response to the user's request, utilizing only those attributes justified as appropriate during the reasoning phase.

#### 3.2. Synthetic Dataset Curation

A synthetic dataset of approximately 700 examples was automatically generated using GPT-4. This dataset is designed to be efficient for training and validation across diverse contexts and CI norms. The curation process involves three stages:
1. **Initial Seeds:** These seeds define core variables for each scenario, including the communication scenario (chat messages, email), task domain (e.g., Hospitality, Healthcare), and a governing "transmission principle" (Confidentiality, Proportionality, or Consent).
 - *Confidentiality*: Information unrelated to the context should not be shared.
 - *Proportionality*: Shared information should be minimal and relevant to the task.
 - *Consent*: Information sharing depends on the data subject's explicit agreement.
2. **Vignettes:** GPT-4 expands these seeds into vignettes by specifying the user's task, actors (sender, recipient, data subject), and generating two disjoint sets of information categories: 'required' and 'restricted' for the given context and principle.
3. **Final Dataset Examples:** The vignettes are then transformed into natural-language examples. GPT-4 populates specific values for user queries, names, roles, and generates natural conversations. Information is presented as key-value pairs, with neutral keys to avoid obvious cues about their classification. Each example includes an annotation, indicating which key-value pairs belong to the 'required' and 'restricted' categories, facilitating rule-based reward calculation. The dataset is split into training (590), evaluation (66), and test (73) sets.

#### 3.3. Contextual Integrity Optimized RL Training (CI-RL)

The core of the methodology is the reinforcement learning framework that builds upon the CI-CoT prompting. The GRPO algorithm, which avoids the need for a critic network to reduce computational overhead, is employed. The LLM's policy ($\pi_\theta$) is optimized by maximizing an objective function (Equation 1 in the paper) that incorporates a rule-based reward function and a Kullback–Leibler (KL) divergence regularization term against a reference policy.

The reward function $R$ (Equation 2) is bipartite:
1. **Format Adherence**: A penalty of -1 is assigned if the model's response violates the required format (e.g., missing `<think>` or `<answer>` tags).
2. **Contextual Integrity Scoring**: If the format is correct, the reward is calculated as: $R = \frac{|A_{\text{present}}|}{|A|} - \frac{|D_{\text{present}}|}{|D|}$.
 - $A$: Set of all required keywords in the ground truth.
 - $D$: Set of all restricted keywords in the ground truth.
 - $A_{\text{present}}$: Required keywords found in the model's generated answer.
 - $D_{\text{present}}$: Restricted keywords found in the model's generated answer.
 This reward function incentivizes the model to include all necessary information while strictly avoiding restricted information.

**Models and Training Details**
The method was applied to various LLMs, including different sizes of Qwen2.5-Instruct (1.5B, 3B, 7B, 14B) and models from different families like Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct and Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3. Training was based on the VERL framework, with specific hyperparameters for learning rate, KL divergence weight, clip ratio, and response length.

**Metrics**
On the synthetic dataset, the following metrics were used:
1. **Integrity (I)**: Percentage of examples where all restricted information was *excluded*.
2. **Utility (U)**: Percentage of examples where all required information was *included*.
3. **Complete (C)**: Percentage of examples where all required information was included *and* all restricted information was excluded.

For external evaluation, the human-annotated **PrivacyLens** benchmark was used, employing GPT-4o as an LLM judge for:
1. **Helpfulness**: Model utility (0-3 scale).
2. **Leakage Rate (LR)**: Percentage of responses with sensitive information disclosure.
3. **Adjusted Leakage Rate (ALR)**: LR calculated only for helpful responses (score ≥ 2), balancing safety and helpfulness.
The **ConfAIde** benchmark was also used to further validate generality.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several findings across the synthetic dataset and established benchmarks.

**Results on Synthetic Dataset (Table 1):**
- **Consistent Improvement in Integrity and Completeness**: The CI-RL framework consistently improved both the Integrity (I) and Complete (C) metrics across all evaluated model sizes and families (Qwen2.5, Mistral, Llama-3.1). These improvements were achieved while maintaining or improving Utility (U), indicating that the method effectively reduced inappropriate information sharing without compromising task performance.
 - For example, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct showed an Integrity increase from 46.9% to 75.0% and a Complete increase from 29.7% to 48.4% with CI-RL.
- **Scaling and Performance**: Larger baseline models generally achieved higher absolute scores, supporting the hypothesis that increased model size contributes to better reasoning and adherence to integrity.
- **Smaller Models Outperform Larger Baselines with CI-RL**: A notable finding was that smaller models, after CI-RL training, could outperform larger baseline models in CI metrics. For instance, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct + CI-RL (Integrity: 75.0%, Complete: 48.4%) surpassed the baseline Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct (Integrity: 51.6%, Complete: 37.5%). This suggests that RL effectively bridges or even reverses performance gaps between model sizes for CI tasks.

**Ablation Studies (Tables 2 & 4):**
- **LLMs vs. LRMs**: Instruction-tuned LLMs (e.g., Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct) combined with CI-RL significantly outperformed Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) like DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-14B on Integrity, Utility, and Complete scores. This was attributed to the LRMs' optimization for scientific/code domains, potentially at the expense of broader, real-world understanding required for CI.
- **Integrity-Utility Trade-off**: By adjusting the weights ($\alpha$ for required, $\gamma$ for restricted) in the reward function, the study demonstrated control over the integrity-utility trade-off. Increasing $\alpha$ generally improved utility and helpfulness, while increasing $\gamma$ led to improved integrity and reduced leakage rates, showing that the model's prioritization can be fine-tuned.

**Evaluation on PrivacyLens Benchmark (Table 3):**
- **CI-CoT Effectiveness**: Explicit Chain-of-Thought reasoning (CI-CoT) consistently reduced the Leakage Rate (LR) and Adjusted Leakage Rate (ALR) across various frontier models (Claude 3.7 Sonnet, Gemini 2.5 Flash, Claude 3.7 Sonnet Thinking, Gemini 2.5 Pro) and open-weight models. However, this often came with a modest decrease in overall helpfulness, indicating a safety-helpfulness trade-off.
- **CI-RL Optimization**: Integrating Reinforcement Learning (CI-RL) further optimized this balance. CI-RL achieved lower leakage rates (LR and ALR) for some models (e.g., Llama-3.1-8B-IT) or maintained comparable leakage rates while simultaneously preserving or enhancing helpfulness metrics compared to CI-CoT alone. This demonstrated CI-RL's ability to navigate the safety-helpfulness trade-off more effectively.
- **Frontier vs. Open-weight Models**: Frontier models consistently exhibited lower leakage rates and higher helpfulness compared to the smaller open-weight models, even with CI-CoT and CI-RL applied.

**Supplementary Results on ConfAIde Benchmark (Tables 5, 6, 7):**
- **Generality of CI-RL**: CI-RL-trained Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct demonstrated improved correlation with human judgments across various tiers of the ConfAIde benchmark (Tier 1 and 2.a/b), which assess basic sensitivity understanding and information flow expectations.
- **Reduced Leakage in Complex Scenarios**: For Tier 3 (theory of mind in multi-party scenarios), CI-RL led to a substantial reduction in private information leakage. In Tier 4 (private and public information flow), CI-RL significantly improved task completion while adhering to CI constraints, reducing the rate of completions that either leak a secret or omit required information.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of AI safety and responsible AI development by addressing the critical challenge of Contextual Integrity (CI) in LLM-based agents. The significance and potential impact of this work are multifaceted:

1. **Enhanced Trustworthiness and Safety of LLM Agents**: By explicitly teaching LLMs to reason about and adhere to CI norms, the work directly contributes to the development of safer and more trustworthy AI agents. Reducing the inadvertent disclosure of private or inappropriate information can prevent privacy breaches, build user confidence, and facilitate the responsible deployment of LLM-powered systems in sensitive domains like healthcare, finance, and personal assistance.

2. **Novel Approach to Operationalizing Contextual Integrity**: The paper introduces a novel approach by integrating Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning with Reinforcement Learning (RL) to internalize CI principles within LLMs. This is a significant step beyond purely external safeguards or simple prompt engineering, offering a method to embed a more nuanced understanding of context-dependent information flow directly into the model's behavior. The demonstration of successful transfer from synthetic data to human-annotated benchmarks like PrivacyLens validates the practical applicability of this approach.

3. **Efficiency and Scalability**: The use of a relatively small synthetic dataset (~700 examples) for RL training, coupled with the ability of CI-RL to enable smaller models to outperform larger baselines, suggests a more data-efficient and computationally viable pathway for achieving CI. This could reduce the resources required for developing CI-aware models, making advanced privacy-preserving capabilities more accessible.

4. **Complementary to Existing Safeguards**: The proposed CI-RL framework is not presented as a replacement for existing privacy-preserving methods (e.g., inference-time gating, firewalls) but rather as an orthogonal and potentially complementary mechanism. By internalizing CI norms, LLMs can act as a more intelligent first line of defense, potentially enhancing the effectiveness of layered privacy architectures.

5. **Foundation for Adaptable Privacy Systems**: The modular nature of the CI-CoT prompting and the rule-based reward function in CI-RL offers avenues for future adaptability. This design supports the potential for dynamic policy injection, versioning, and customization to account for evolving privacy norms, user-specific preferences, or organizational policies. This flexibility is crucial for real-world applications where privacy requirements are not static.

6. **Insights into LLM Reasoning**: The ablation studies comparing LLMs and LRMs provide insights into how different model architectures respond to CI training, suggesting that models optimized for diverse real-world understanding may be more receptive to CI reasoning than those narrowly focused on specific technical domains. The exploration of the integrity-utility trade-off also offers practical guidance for fine-tuning models to meet specific risk appetite and performance requirements.

In summary, this research offers a foundational advancement in making LLMs more privacy-aware and contextually intelligent. By integrating reasoning and reinforcement learning, it paves the way for a new generation of AI agents that can operate more responsibly and effectively in complex, human-centric environments.
