# Neural feels with neural fields: Visuo-tactile perception for in-hand manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> NeuralFeels performs online visuo-tactile SLAM for in-hand manipulation: a multi-fingered hand with four DIGIT touch sensors and one RGB-D camera learns an object's neural signed-distance field on the fly while jointly tracking its pose via a pose-graph optimizer. On 70 in-hand rotation trials it reaches 81% reconstruction F-score, 4.7 mm pose drift (2.3 mm with CAD models), and up to 94% tracking gains under heavy occlusion.

## Problem

- In-hand perception is largely vision-only and restricted to tracking a priori known objects, so novel-object shape and pose remain unrecovered during dexterous manipulation.
- Practitioners sidestep perception with fiducials, motion capture, or sensing cages; visual occlusion by fingers is unavoidable in contact-rich rotation, sliding, and reorientation tasks.
- Prior visuo-tactile work reconstructs fixed objects or tracks known ones with a single finger, and lacks a standardized benchmark dataset for visuo-tactile perception.

## Method

- **NeuralFeels** ==posed neural field==: an ==instant-NGP hash-encoded neural SDF== whose weights and object pose are alternately optimized, flipping standard ==neural SLAM== by posing the field relative to known-pose sensors.
- Frontend pairs kinematics-prompted ==Segment Anything== visual segmentation (grasp-center prompts) with a 21.7M-parameter ==tactile transformer== predicting DIGIT contact depth, trained purely in the TACTO simulator with lighting and noise randomization.
- Backend pose optimizer builds a ==sliding-window nonlinear least-squares pose graph== over keyframes with ==SDF==, ==frame-to-frame ICP==, and pose-regularization terms; touch is sampled as surface-only depth, vision as free-space plus surface.

## Results

- Across **70** experiments (**40** sim, **30** real; **14** objects, **5** seeds each) touch raised reconstruction F-score **15.3%** in simulation and **14.6%** in the real world, averaging **81%** final F-score.
- Pose tracking improved **21.3%** in simulation and **26.6%** in the real world over the vision-only baseline; with known CAD models average error fell to **2.3 mm**.
- A **200-camera** occlusion sweep gave **21.2%** average tracking gain, peaking at **94.1%** for heavily occluded viewpoints; touch also curbed error as simulated depth noise scaled from **0 to 50**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Vision-based touch can be treated as a local perspective camera, letting one depth-based neural field ingest both modalities once scale and free-space sampling differ per source.
> - Touch refines estimates when vision is clean but disambiguates them when occlusion or depth noise is severe; touch-only tracking fails for lack of global context.
> - Modularity lets pre-trained foundation models handle the frontend while online least-squares SLAM handles the backend, avoiding scarce in-hand training data entirely.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses a crucial bottleneck in achieving advanced robot dexterity: robust and generalizable perception during contact-rich in-hand manipulation of novel objects. It intersects several prominent research areas:

- **Robot Manipulation and Dexterity:** Current robotic in-hand manipulation often relies on *a priori* known object models, fiducials, or end-to-end learning that lacks interpretability. This work pushes beyond these limitations by enabling robots to perceive and interact with novel objects, a key step towards human-level dexterity in unstructured environments like homes. It builds upon previous work in in-hand rotation policies, but integrates a more advanced perception system.
- **Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM):** The paper extends classical SLAM paradigms by performing "object-centric SLAM" where the goal is to simultaneously localize (track pose) and map (reconstruct shape) an object *in-hand*. Unlike traditional SLAM focusing on environmental mapping or ego-motion, this work focuses on a manipuland. It draws inspiration from recent advancements in neural SLAM, but uniquely applies it to a multi-modal, interactive robotics context.
- **Neural Fields and 3D Representation:** The adoption of neural fields, specifically Neural Signed Distance Fields (SDFs) using `instant-NGP`, is a direct application of recent breakthroughs in computer graphics and vision. Neural fields offer continuous, high-fidelity 3D representations superior to traditional discrete methods like point clouds or meshes for online learning and scene modeling. This work adapts these representations for *online*, *interactive*, and *multimodal* learning in robotics, moving beyond their typical offline or vision-only applications.
- **Multimodal Perception (Vision and Touch):** The research directly confronts the limitations of vision-only perception in scenarios with heavy occlusion, poor lighting, or challenging object properties (transparency, specularity). By integrating vision and touch, it leverages the complementary strengths of global visual context and local, high-fidelity tactile feedback. This aligns with human perception, where touch often disambiguates or refines visual information. Hardware advancements in vision-based tactile sensors (like DIGIT and GelSight) have enabled this rich data stream, and this paper demonstrates an effective way to integrate it.
- **Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The methodology for training the tactile transformer in simulation (TACTO) and transferring policies trained in physics simulators (IsaacGym) to the real world is a testament to the ongoing efforts in bridging the sim-to-real gap, which is crucial for data-intensive learning in robotics.
- **Benchmarking and Datasets:** The introduction of the "FeelSight" dataset addresses a recognized gap in standardized benchmarks for visuo-tactile manipulation, akin to the impact of datasets like KITTI for visual SLAM or BOP for 6D object pose estimation. This contribution is vital for reproducible research and accelerating progress in the field.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research stem from the acknowledged shortcomings of current robotic in-hand manipulation systems:

1. **Overcome Vision-Only Limitations in In-Hand Perception:** The status quo for in-hand perception primarily relies on vision, which is susceptible to critical failures during contact-rich tasks due to self-occlusion (fingers obscuring the object), poor illumination, transparency, or specularity. The paper is motivated to move beyond this narrow scope to enable robots to operate in more general, unstructured environments.
2. **Enable Spatial Awareness for Novel Objects:** Current systems often restrict to tracking *a priori* known objects or require instrumented environments with fiducials. A key objective is to enable robots to infer an object's pose and shape *online* for novel, unknown objects, mirroring human ability to interact with unfamiliar items.
3. **Develop a Robust Multimodal Perception System:** The core motivation is to effectively combine vision, touch, and proprioception to create a perception system that is more resilient and informative than individual modalities alone. This system, dubbed "NeuralFeels," aims to unify these sensing modalities into a coherent, evolving 3D object representation.
4. **Advance Robot Dexterity through Generalizable Perception:** Ultimately, the goal is to provide a "perception backbone" that can drive progress towards more general and dexterous robot manipulation. Knowledge of an object's pose and geometry is crucial for policy generalization, planning, and executing complex in-hand tasks.
5. **Facilitate Online Learning and Reconstruction:** The method seeks to learn object geometry (shape) and track its state (pose) simultaneously and continuously during interaction, without requiring prior knowledge or extensive offline training for each new object.

In essence, the paper aims to equip robots with a more human-like "deep perception" capability, allowing them to truly "sense fully" and understand their spatial relationship with objects through active, multimodal interaction.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

NeuralFeels employs an elegant, modular architecture that combines recent advances in neural fields, SLAM optimization, and multimodal sensing. It operates as an online, object-centric SLAM system.

#### 3.1. Overall Architecture (Frontend and Backend)

The system is divided into two main components, analogous to classical SLAM:
- **Frontend:** Responsible for processing raw sensor data (RGB-D vision, RGB touch, proprioception) and abstracting it into segmented depth measurements. These modules are pre-trained.
- **Backend:** Fuses the processed depth data into an optimization framework that simultaneously infers the object's neural shape model and tracks its pose. This process occurs online.

#### 3.2. Robot Hardware and Exploration Policy

- **Hardware:** An Allegro hand equipped with four DIGIT vision-based tactile sensors at the fingertips, mounted on a Franka Panda arm. An Intel D435 RGB-D camera provides visual input.
- **In-hand Manipulation Policy:** A proprioception-driven policy, pre-trained in the IsaacGym simulator using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) and then transferred to the real world, is used to explore the object through stable in-hand rotation. This policy is designed to maintain contact and expose different object surfaces.

#### 3.3. Frontend Processing

- **Segmented Visual Depth:**
 - Utilizes the Segment Anything Model (SAM) for robust object instance segmentation from the RGB-D camera stream.
 - Employs "embodied prompts" for SAM: The 3D center of grasp and projected fingertip positions (derived from robot kinematics) guide the segmentation, addressing challenges like finger-object occlusion and ambiguous foreground-background. Negative prompts are used to exclude robot fingers from the mask.
- **Tactile Transformer for Depth:**
 - A transformer-based neural network, inspired by dense prediction transformers (DPT), is trained to predict high-resolution contact depth maps from raw RGB images provided by the DIGIT tactile sensors.
 - Training is conducted entirely in the TACTO optical touch simulator using a large corpus of interactions with YCB objects. Extensive data augmentation (real-world backgrounds, pose variations, LED lighting randomization, pixel noise) is used to facilitate sim-to-real transfer and generalization across multiple sensors.
 - The output is a segmented depth image, masking out non-contact regions.

#### 3.4. Backend Optimization (Shape and Pose Optimizer)

The backend represents the object as a continuous Neural Signed Distance Field (SDF) using `instant-NGP` (a multiresolution hash encoding with a small MLP) for efficient online learning. Optimization alternates between refining the neural SDF weights and the object's pose.
- **Shape Optimizer:**
 - Maintains a bank of keyframes, incorporating new frames based on information gain and replaying older ones to prevent catastrophic forgetting.
 - Optimizes the neural network weights (θ) of the SDF using an Adam optimizer.
 - Samples 3D points from visuo-tactile depth measurements (both surface and free-space points for vision, only surface points for touch) and minimizes an SDF loss, which resembles a truncated SDF loss.
- **Pose Optimizer:**
 - Formulates pose estimation as a nonlinear least squares optimization problem using a sliding-window factor graph (implemented with Theseus).
 - Employs a Levenberg-Marquardt solver to refine the object's 6-DoF pose (x_t) with respect to the *frozen* neural field.
 - The loss function (L_pose) combines three factors:
 - **SDF loss (L_sdf):** Aligns sampled surface points from current depth measurements with the zero level-set of the neural SDF (frame-to-model constraint).
 - **Pose regularizer (L_reg):** Weakly constrains relative pose updates between consecutive keyframes for stability.
 - **ICP loss (L_icp):** Provides frame-to-frame constraints by aligning the current visuo-tactile point cloud with the previous one.

#### 3.5. Evaluation Dataset (FeelSight)

To benchmark their method, the authors introduce FeelSight, a dataset comprising 70 in-hand rotation trials (5 trials each for 6 real-world objects and 8 simulated objects). It includes ground-truth object meshes (obtained via 3D scanner) and ground-truth object poses (directly from simulator or pseudo-ground-truth from multi-camera tracking in real-world).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents compelling evidence for the efficacy of NeuralFeels in enhancing robotic in-hand perception, particularly through the fusion of vision and touch.

- **Neural SLAM for Novel Objects (Pose and Shape Estimation):**
 - **Reconstruction Accuracy:** Achieved an average F-score of 81% for object reconstructions across both simulation and real-world experiments on *novel* objects. This represents a significant gain of 15.3% (simulation) and 14.6% (real-world) compared to vision-only methods, demonstrating touch's role in shape completion and refinement, especially for occluded surfaces.
 - **Pose Tracking Accuracy:** Demonstrated stable pose tracking with an average drift of 4.7 mm on novel objects. Improvements of 21.3% (simulation) and 26.6% (real-world) were observed over vision-only baselines.
 - **Qualitative Observations:** The system successfully builds evolving neural SDFs, progressively reconstructing object surfaces over time, even with limited initial visual information.

- **Neural Tracking for Known Objects (Pose Estimation with CAD models):**
 - When provided with *a priori* CAD models, NeuralFeels achieved very robust pose tracking with an average error of 2.3 mm, reducing to 2 mm during the sequence.
 - Touch refined pose estimates by 22.29% in simulation and 3.9% in the real-world, indicating its role in providing precise local contacts even when overall visibility is good.

- **Robustness Under Adverse Conditions (Occlusion and Visual Noise):**
 - **Heavy Occlusion:** Under severe visual occlusion from the robot's hand, visuo-tactile fusion showed up to **94% improvements** in pose tracking compared to vision-only methods. This highlights touch's ability to provide an "unobstructed local perspective" when global vision is compromised.
 - **Noisy Visual Depth:** When visual depth maps were progressively corrupted with realistic noise, multimodal fusion significantly curbed large pose tracking errors, especially in high-noise regimes, demonstrating its robustness-enhancing capability.
 - **Complementary Roles:** The results consistently show that touch *refines* perception in low-occlusion/noise scenarios and *robustifies/disambiguates* it in high-occlusion/noise conditions.

- **FeelSight Dataset Release:** The release of a benchmark dataset comprising 70 visuo-tactile experiments (simulated and real-world, with ground-truth) is a significant contribution to the community, facilitating future research and standardized evaluation in this domain.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Neural feels with neural fields" paper presents a significant advancement in robotic manipulation, with profound implications for future robot dexterity and autonomy.

- **Advancing General Robot Dexterity:** By enabling robust online pose and shape estimation for novel objects during in-hand manipulation, NeuralFeels provides a crucial "perception backbone" for general robot dexterity. This moves robots closer to human-like capabilities in handling unfamiliar objects in unstructured environments, a prerequisite for widely deployed home or service robots.
- **First-of-its-Kind Multimodal SLAM:** This work marks the first demonstration of full Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) for multimodal (vision, touch, proprioception) and multi-finger manipulation, specifically for *novel* objects and *online* learning. This achievement unifies disparate fields like SLAM, neural rendering, tactile sensing, and robot learning.
- **Robustness in Challenging Environments:** The explicit demonstration of improved performance under visual occlusion and noise is highly significant. Many real-world manipulation tasks inherently involve occluded views and imperfect visual sensing. NeuralFeels shows that rich multimodal sensing can provide robustness where vision alone fails, making robots more reliable in practical scenarios.
- **Power of Continuous 3D Representations:** The effective application of neural SDFs for online, interactive object modeling showcases the growing utility of neural fields beyond traditional computer graphics. Their continuous, high-fidelity nature is well-suited for dynamic robotic perception tasks.
- **Modular and Interpretable Design:** Unlike some end-to-end learning approaches, NeuralFeels' modular design (frontend for data processing, backend for optimization) offers interpretability. This allows for the integration of pre-trained foundation models (like SAM) and classical optimization techniques, making the system adaptable and easier to debug and extend.
- **Foundation for Perception-Driven Planning:** The output of NeuralFeels—a persistent, evolving 3D object model and its pose—is directly usable by higher-level planning and control systems. This lays the groundwork for future research in perception-driven planning for complex dexterous tasks like reorientation, pick-and-place, and insertion, which can benefit from accurate, real-time spatial understanding.
- **Catalyst for Benchmarking:** The release of the FeelSight dataset is a crucial contribution to the research community. It provides a standardized platform for evaluating and comparing future visuo-tactile perception systems, accelerating progress in a field that previously lacked comprehensive benchmarks.

**Potential Limitations and Future Directions (as identified by the authors):**
- **Generic 3D Priors:** Enhancing initial reconstruction for novel objects by leveraging pre-trained category-level or multi-view reconstruction models could improve performance during the initial phase of interaction.
- **Sim-to-Real Adaptation:** Addressing domain gaps (e.g., tactile sensor sensitivity, policy stability, proprioception noise) is essential for more robust real-world deployment.
- **System Design:** Improvements in efficiency (for real-time operation), relaxing fixed-camera constraints (e.g., using egocentric vision), incorporating depth uncertainty, and leveraging color information for feature tracking are identified.
- **Perceiving Latent State:** Extending neural fields to embed and estimate latent object properties beyond geometry, such as texture, friction, or dynamics, could unlock even more sophisticated manipulation capabilities.

Overall, "Neural feels with neural fields" represents a substantial step towards more capable and autonomous robotic manipulation by demonstrating a robust, online multimodal perception system. Its contributions in integrating diverse technologies and providing a valuable dataset will undoubtedly inspire and facilitate future research in dexterous robotics.
