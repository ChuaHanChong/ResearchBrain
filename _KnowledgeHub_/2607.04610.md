# RoboVista: Evaluating Vision Language Models for Diverse Robot Applications

> [!summary] Summary
> The paper introduces RoboVista, an expert-annotated benchmark for evaluating Vision-Language Models (VLMs) through a modular Robot Question Answering (RQA) framework, covering 39 robot task types across six diverse domains. This evaluation revealed that even state-of-the-art VLMs achieve a maximum accuracy of 56.5% and primarily fail due to visual perception errors, with benchmark performance showing strong correlation to physical robot task success.

## Problem

- Existing VLM evaluations in robotics primarily rely on end-to-end, teleoperated datasets that often fail to capture the modular nature of real-world robotic applications.
- Complex robot behaviors, which are frequently decomposed into distinct task-level decisions (e.g., perception, high-level planning, motion execution, failure recovery), are difficult to evaluate using end-to-end trajectories.
- There is a lack of fine-grained, diagnostic evaluation methods for VLMs, especially in data-scarce domains, to systematically identify and address specific capability gaps.

## Method

- Developed ==Robot Question Answering (RQA)==, a modular evaluation framework that transforms critical robot decision points into a robot-centric ==Visual Question Answering (VQA)== formulation.
- Curated **RoboVista**, an expert-annotated benchmark of 474 VQA questions spanning 39 robot tasks across six diverse domains (industrial, agricultural, domestic, surgical, autonomous driving, open robot datasets).
- Conducted comprehensive evaluations of state-of-the-art open-source and closed-source VLMs on **RoboVista** using ==zero-shot==, ==Chain-of-Thought (CoT)==, and ==In-Context Learning (ICL)== prompting strategies.

## Results

- The best-performing VLM, Gemini 2.5 Pro, achieved an overall accuracy of **56.5%** on the RoboVista benchmark, indicating substantial areas for improvement in VLM capabilities for robotics.
- Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting unexpectedly led to up to a **12%** reduction in scene understanding accuracy, and In-Context Learning (ICL) consistently decreased accuracy (**2.8%** to **6.5%**) and increased calibration error (up to **9.7%**) across models.
- Failure analysis showed that visual perception errors, particularly misidentification (e.g., **30.2%** for Qwen2.5-VL-7B) and spatial errors, were the predominant sources of VLM failures.
- Physical robot experiments confirmed a strong correlation between higher RoboVista scores and lower task execution errors (Pearson **r = -0.70 to -0.93**) and greater shared autonomy task progress.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - State-of-the-art VLMs demonstrate significant performance gaps in complex, modular robotic tasks, with the highest accuracy observed at 56.5%.
> - The majority of VLM failures stem from visual perception errors, such as misidentification and spatial reasoning issues, rather than higher-level logical reasoning.
> - Prompting strategies have nuanced effects: CoT can degrade scene understanding while improving planning, and ICL consistently reduces accuracy while increasing calibration error.
> - Performance on the RoboVista benchmark strongly correlates with actual task execution success on physical robotic systems, establishing its relevance as a predictive diagnostic tool.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The deployment of robotic systems in real-world domains such as industrial automation, agriculture, and surgery necessitates a general-purpose interface capable of reasoning over diverse decisions, robot embodiments, and environmental conditions. Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have emerged as a potential foundational technology for these systems, demonstrating capabilities in language-driven reasoning, task planning, and manipulation. Recent advancements have positioned VLMs as backbones for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies (e.g., OpenVLA, π0), semantic planners in task-and-motion planning frameworks (e.g., Text2Motion, PaLM-E), and orchestrators of specialized robotic modules (e.g., Maestro).

However, current evaluation paradigms for VLMs in robotics predominantly rely on end-to-end, teleoperated datasets, such as those derived from imitation learning benchmarks like Open X-Embodiment, Robo2VLM, and RoboBrain. While these benchmarks are effective for improving spatial capabilities and learning direct action mappings, they often fall short in capturing the modular nature of many real-world robotic applications. Complex robot behaviors are frequently decomposed into distinct task-level decisions—spanning perception, high-level planning, motion execution, and failure recovery—which are challenging to evaluate using end-to-end trajectories. This limitation is particularly pronounced in domains where public data is scarce, and critical decisions are managed by analytical pipelines or require substantial domain expertise (e.g., surgical procedures, agricultural tasks).

This research addresses this gap by proposing Robot Question Answering (RQA), a modular evaluation framework, and RoboVista, an expert-annotated benchmark. RoboVista complements existing large-scale, trajectory-based benchmarks by emphasizing fine-grained, diagnostic quality and reasoning depth. It provides a structured approach to integrate modular decision points—crucial for real-world robustness and interpretability—into VLM evaluation, especially for underrepresented domains lacking extensive trajectory data. This work aims to systematically reveal the gaps in current VLM capabilities for supporting the full spectrum of robotic decision-making in practical settings.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to advance the development and evaluation of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for real-world robotic applications by focusing on modular decision-making processes. To achieve this, the authors delineate several key objectives and motivations:

1. **Develop a Modular Evaluation Framework:** The core objective is to propose Robot Question Answering (RQA), a novel and modular evaluation framework. RQA is designed to systematically convert critical decision points within diverse robot systems into a robot-centric Visual Question Answering (VQA) formulation. This framework aims to provide a structured interface for diagnosing VLM capabilities across different functional layers of a robotic pipeline (perception, high-level decision making, motion/action estimation, and failure recovery). The motivation stems from the observation that real-world robot behaviors are often decomposed into a series of modular decisions, which traditional end-to-end benchmarks struggle to capture.

2. **Curate a Comprehensive and Expert-Annotated Benchmark:** Building upon the RQA framework, a significant objective is to create RoboVista, a high-quality, expert-annotated benchmark. This benchmark is meticulously curated to cover 39 distinct robot task types across six diverse application domains: agricultural, industrial, domestic, surgical robotics, autonomous driving, and open robot datasets. The motivation here is to provide a realistic and challenging dataset that reflects the varied embodiments, complex visual conditions (deformable objects, cluttered scenes), and long-horizon contextual understanding required for practical robotic operation. Furthermore, by emphasizing expert annotation, the benchmark ensures the realism, consistency, and operational meaningfulness of the VQA instances, particularly in data-scarce domains.

3. **Evaluate State-of-the-Art VLMs:** A crucial objective is to perform a comprehensive evaluation of state-of-the-art open-source and closed-source VLMs on the RoboVista benchmark. This evaluation aims to identify the current capabilities and limitations of these models, including their performance under different prompting strategies such as Chain-of-Thought (CoT) and In-Context Learning (ICL). The motivation is to benchmark existing technologies against the proposed fine-grained, modular tasks to quantify performance gaps and diagnose specific areas for improvement, thus informing future VLM research and development for robotics.

4. **Correlate Benchmark Performance with Physical Robot Execution:** A final objective is to validate the practical relevance of the RoboVista benchmark by investigating the correlation between VLM performance on RoboVista and actual task execution success on physical robotic systems. This is motivated by the need to ensure that improvements on the benchmark translate into tangible gains in real-world robot capabilities. Physical experiments, such as bimanual gripper alignment and surgical knot tying, are conducted to establish this correlation, underscoring the significance of RoboVista as a predictive measure of real-world performance.

In essence, the motivation is to bridge the gap between advanced VLM capabilities and the specific, modular reasoning demands of diverse real-world robot applications, thereby enabling more general-purpose, robust, and interpretable robotic systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of this research revolves around the development of the Robot Question Answering (RQA) framework and its instantiation as the RoboVista benchmark, followed by a comprehensive evaluation of Vision-Language Models (VLMs).

**RQA Framework (Robot Question Answering):**
The RQA framework is designed to convert decision points from robot systems into a robot-centric VQA formulation. It operates on a modular abstraction of robot systems, decomposing robot behavior into four functional layers:
1. **Perception:** Estimates task-relevant state from robot-centric visual observations (e.g., object geometry, spatial relationships).
2. **High-level Decision Making:** Focuses on grounded task reasoning, including goal selection and action sequencing under task constraints.
3. **Motion and Action Awareness:** Evaluates the physical feasibility of candidate actions (e.g., reachability, collision avoidance).
4. **Failure Recovery:** Assesses the ability to detect execution failures and infer corrective actions.

Each functional block is formally described by a tuple M = (E, X, U, C), where:
- **E (Embodiment Setup):** Defines the robot’s physical configuration and sensing capabilities.
- **X (State Space):** Represents observed and latent variables of the robot and environment.
- **U (Task Output Space):** Defines the decision or control output, such as discrete actions or motion parameters.
- **C (Constraints):** Encodes feasibility conditions like collision avoidance or joint limits.

The Robot-VQA data structure extends standard VQA instances (visual input V, question q, ground-truth answer a*, candidate answer set A, textual rationale r) by requiring visual input to be robot-centric (V_rv for robot visible, V_ob for onboard camera). This formulation maps the module's latent state and embodiment to embodied visual observations, and its decision semantics to structured question-answering.

To address challenges in VQA construction (domain-specific pipelines, non-visual state, plausible incorrect answers, data curation difficulty), RQA adheres to design principles:
1. VQAs are decomposed and structured by domain experts.
2. Each question is answerable solely with provided robot-centric visual and language context.
3. All questions have a single, verifiable correct answer.
4. Curation prioritizes coverage across diverse tasks and limits redundancy.

Case studies, such as ambidextrous bin picking and surgical knot tying, exemplify how RQA translates continuous physical robot states into modular, discrete VQA pairs representing critical decision points.

**RoboVista Benchmark Construction:**
RoboVista is an expert-annotated benchmark created using the RQA framework. Its construction follows a three-stage data curation pipeline:
1. **Data Collection:** A wide range of robotics datasets and peer-reviewed publications (18 papers, 3 public datasets: DROID, Open X-Embodiment, Agibot) from six domains (industrial, agricultural, domestic, surgical, autonomous driving, open robot datasets) are surveyed. Robot-centric visual data (images or sequences) and task episodes are extracted.
2. **Question Construction:** Human domain experts (postdoctoral researchers and students with master's degrees in Robotics) manually formulate 474 multiple-choice VQA questions, each with five options and a detailed reasoning explanation. Each question corresponds to a concrete decision point within a modular robotics pipeline.
3. **Quality Control:** All VQA instances undergo a multi-pass review by additional robotics experts to verify visual grounding, answer correctness, and linguistic clarity, ensuring high quality and consistency across domains.

**Experimental Setup:**
The study evaluates state-of-the-art open-source (Qwen2.5-VL, Qwen3-VL, Robo2VLM-ER, RoboBrain 2.5) and closed-source (GPT-4o, GPT-5, Gemini 2.5 Pro) VLMs. Evaluations are conducted with deterministic decoding settings. Three prompting strategies are used:
- **Zero-Shot:** Standard question-answering.
- **Chain-of-Thought (CoT):** Models are instructed to "Think step by step" before providing an answer.
- **In-Context Learning (ICL):** Models are provided with a single example from the same domain and task.
Performance is measured by accuracy, and for ICL, calibration error is also assessed. Failure analysis is conducted using model-generated reasoning chains, categorized into visual perception errors (misidentification, spatial, counting) and higher-level reasoning failures (task/semantic, state/temporal).

**Physical Evaluation:**
To validate the real-world relevance of RoboVista, two physical robot tasks are performed:
1. **Bimanual Gripper Position Alignment:** A bimanual robot task where VLMs estimate the distance between grippers and generate symbolic motion plans. Visual input comes from wrist and top-down cameras. Metrics include distance estimation error and final gripper position error. Experiments are conducted across five distinct scene configurations (e.g., kitchen objects, industrial parts, plants).
2. **Surgical Knot-Tying (dVRK):** A shared-autonomy setting where VLMs provide sequential guidance for 16 critical decision points in a simulated surgical knot-tying procedure. Human operators follow VLM guidance unless intervention is required. Metrics include task progress (stages completed with/without intervention).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of VLMs on the RoboVista benchmark yielded several key findings regarding their capabilities and limitations in diverse robotic applications:

**A. Zero-Shot Performance:**
- **Substantial Gaps:** RoboVista presents significant challenges for state-of-the-art VLMs, with even the best-performing models exhibiting substantial gaps in achieving perfect accuracy. Gemini 2.5 Pro achieved the highest overall accuracy at 56.5% among all models tested.
- **Model Scale Correlation:** For open-source models, performance generally improved with increasing model scale, with Qwen 3-235B-A22B reaching 51.3% overall accuracy.
- **Domain-Specific Performance:** Performance varied considerably across application domains. Domestic robotics tasks generally yielded higher accuracies due to visually structured and human-scale scenes. Conversely, agricultural robotics remained one of the most challenging domains, attributed to fine-grained plant morphology, severe occlusions, and deformable structures poorly represented in VLM pretraining data. Robotics-specialized models (e.g., RoboBrain 2.5) showed localized gains in domains like Driving and Surgery but did not uniformly outperform general-purpose VLMs across all domains.

**B. Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Prompting:**
- **Scene Understanding Degradation:** CoT prompting generally *reduced* scene understanding accuracy across most models and domains, with drops of up to 12%. This suggests that explicit reasoning traces can sometimes lead models to "over-think" or interfere with fine-grained perceptual processing, particularly in tasks requiring direct spatial perception.
- **Planning Improvement:** In contrast, CoT typically *improved* planning performance for several models, especially in multi-step domains like surgical and agricultural robotics. These tasks often benefit from explicit intermediate reasoning for sequencing actions, reasoning over constraints, and anticipating outcomes. This indicates that CoT's effectiveness is task-dependent, aiding higher-level planning but hindering low-level perception.

**C. In-Context Learning (ICL):**
- **Accuracy Reduction:** Providing in-context examples, even from the same domain and task, consistently *reduced* accuracy across all evaluated models, with drops ranging from 2.8% to 6.5%. This counter-intuitive result suggests that longer contexts or the specific nature of spatial relationship examples might distract VLMs.
- **Increased Hallucination:** ICL consistently *increased calibration error* (up to 9.7%), indicating that models became more confident in their predictions despite being less accurate. This suggests ICL may amplify hallucinated reasoning, particularly when visual evidence is ambiguous.

**D. Failure Analysis:**
- **Perception Dominance:** A significant finding was that the majority of VLM failures originated from visual perception errors rather than logical reasoning failures.
- **Misidentification and Spatial Errors:** Misidentification (incorrect object identity, state, or spatial location) was the most frequent failure mode (e.2., 30.2% for Qwen2.5-VL-7B). Spatial reasoning errors (e.g., relative positioning, depth estimation, motion inference) formed the second largest category. Task and semantic misinterpretation also contributed to failures.
- **Scaling Limitations:** While scaling VLMs (e.g., Qwen3-235B-A22B compared to Qwen2.5-VL-7B) reduced overall error rates and misidentification errors, spatial and semantic errors *persisted* as dominant sources of failure. This suggests that fundamental challenges in geometry-aware perception and detailed spatial understanding are not fully resolved by increased model capacity alone.

**E. Physical Evaluation:**
- **Bimanual Gripper Position Alignment:** A strong negative correlation was observed between RoboVista performance and physical error metrics. Models with higher RoboVista scores consistently achieved lower distance estimation errors (Pearson r = -0.70; Spearman ρ = -0.75) and smaller final gripper position errors (Pearson r = -0.78; Spearman ρ = -0.93) in physical robot execution. This indicates a robust monotonic relationship between benchmark accuracy and real-world task performance.
- **Surgical Knot-Tying (dVRK):** Higher RoboVista-surgical scores correlated with greater shared autonomy task progress in the closed-loop knot-tying procedure. Models with stronger benchmark performance completed more stages before requiring human intervention. A common failure mode related to pointing for motion execution underscored how even minor errors can be amplified across sequential decisions in long-horizon tasks, further validating the benchmark's predictive capability for practical robot success.

In summary, the research demonstrates that current VLMs possess substantial performance gaps in complex, modular robotic tasks, particularly in fine-grained perception and spatial reasoning. The study highlights the nuanced effects of prompting strategies and validates RoboVista as a relevant benchmark for diagnosing VLM weaknesses and predicting real-world robot performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents several significant contributions and potential impacts on the fields of robotics and artificial intelligence:

1. **Diagnostic Framework for Embodied AI:** The introduction of the Robot Question Answering (RQA) framework and the RoboVista benchmark offers a novel diagnostic tool for evaluating Vision-Language Models (VLMs) in the context of robotic applications. By decomposing complex robot behaviors into modular decision points, RoboVista enables a fine-grained assessment of VLM capabilities in perception, high-level planning, motion awareness, and failure recovery. This moves beyond opaque, end-to-end evaluations, providing clearer insights into specific strengths and weaknesses of VLMs.

2. **Guiding Future VLM Development:** The comprehensive evaluation and detailed failure analysis reveal persistent performance gaps in state-of-the-art VLMs, particularly in areas like fine-grained spatial understanding, accurate object identification under occlusion, and robust reasoning in challenging real-world scenarios (e.g., agriculture). These findings provide concrete targets for future VLM research, encouraging the development of models specifically designed to address these fundamental challenges rather than relying solely on increased model scale. The nuanced effects of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) and In-Context Learning (ICL) also offer guidance for effective prompting strategies in robotics.

3. **Bridging Classical Robotics and Modern AI:** RQA’s modular abstraction directly mirrors the design principles of classical robot systems, which rely on well-defined functional blocks. By translating these modular decision points into a VQA format, the work facilitates a structured integration of VLMs into established robotic architectures. This approach has the potential to combine the flexibility and generality of foundation models with the robustness and interpretability of traditional robotics pipelines, accelerating the deployment of AI in safety-critical and complex domains.

4. **Addressing Data Scarcity in Critical Domains:** RoboVista's expert-annotated nature makes it a valuable resource for evaluating VLMs in domains like surgical robotics, agriculture, and industrial automation, where large-scale, end-to-end trajectory datasets are inherently difficult and costly to acquire due to safety, privacy, and operational constraints. By focusing on quality over sheer quantity, RoboVista enables meaningful benchmarking in areas crucial for societal impact.

5. **Validation of Benchmark Relevance:** The strong correlation demonstrated between VLM performance on RoboVista and actual task execution success on physical robotic systems (bimanual gripper alignment and surgical knot tying) validates the benchmark's relevance as a predictive measure of real-world capability. This empirical evidence reinforces RoboVista's utility as a tool for evaluating the progress of embodied AI and ensures that research efforts on the benchmark directly contribute to more capable and reliable robotic systems.

In conclusion, RoboVista provides a foundational tool for systematically evaluating and improving VLMs for real-world robotics. Its impact lies in its ability to diagnose specific model limitations, guide future architectural and training innovations, and ultimately foster the development of more robust, interpretable, and deployable intelligent robotic systems across diverse application domains. The framework is also designed for extensibility, suggesting future work can expand its coverage to more robot tasks and embodiments, and explore automated question generation.
