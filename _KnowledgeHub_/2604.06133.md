# Learning-Guided Force-Feedback Model Predictive Control with Obstacle Avoidance for Robotic Deburring

> [!summary] Summary
> A control framework integrates learning-guided force-feedback Model Predictive Control with obstacle avoidance for robotic deburring, allowing a torque-controlled manipulator to execute precise, contact-rich industrial tasks. The method leverages diffusion-based motion priors for robust guidance and achieves 100% success in multi-hole deburring, maintaining stable contact and avoiding collisions even in challenging configurations.

## Problem

- Existing control methods struggle with contact-rich industrial tasks like deburring, as traditional MPC lacks real-time force feedback while reactive force control lacks advanced planning capabilities.
- Complex, multi-stage manipulation in cluttered environments is challenging due to highly non-convex feasible spaces and the inability of local optimizers to retain task-specific motion strategies.
- Robotic deburring specifically demands precise tool insertion, stable contact with regulated normal and tangential forces, circular motion, and collision avoidance in geometrically complex, often constrained, workspaces.

## Method

- Developed a force-feedback ==Model Predictive Control== (MPC) formulation that augments the state with contact forces and explicitly regulates both normal and tangential contact forces using a 3D contact model.
- Generated an offline dataset of feasible deburring motions and trained a conditional Diffusion Transformer (DiT) to learn rich distributions of these motions, capable of generating diverse and collision-aware trajectories.
- Integrated the diffusion model as a motion prior into the MPC, where its generated trajectories serve as a reference to guide the MPC's local optimizer towards favorable basins of attraction and accelerate convergence.

## Results

- The 3D force-feedback MPC successfully maintained stable in-hole contact and avoided collisions, even when path deviations were necessary, achieving **100%** success in multi-hole deburring sequences.
- Diffusion priors effectively guided the robot through challenging, obstacle-constrained environments and narrow passages, facilitating efficient reach-and-deburring operations.
- Force tracking accuracy was robust overall (RMSE as low as **4.40** N for normal force), though it showed degradation in configurations near workspace limits due to reduced manipulability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Maintaining stable contact during collision avoidance requires explicit regulation of tangential forces, as demonstrated by the 3D contact model preventing slip-out during path deviations.
> - Diffusion-based motion priors provide effective 'memory of motion,' offering robust, task-specific, and collision-aware initializations that guide the MPC's local optimizer through complex, non-convex environments.
> - Combining learning-based global guidance with reactive, constraint-aware force-feedback MPC creates a robust framework capable of simultaneously addressing geometric feasibility and contact stability in real-time.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper addresses a confluence of challenges within robotics, specifically in the areas of Model Predictive Control (MPC), force control, and learning for motion planning.

Model Predictive Control has been widely applied for motion generation in torque-controlled robots, including applications in locomotion and collision avoidance. However, classical MPC formulations typically optimize motion alone, often neglecting real-time force feedback. This characteristic makes force tracking a purely feedforward process, which is susceptible to modeling errors and uncertainties, particularly in contact-rich tasks.

In parallel, force control has been a foundational area in robotics, with methods such as hybrid force/motion control, impedance control, and admittance control offering reactivity and robustness in contact. These traditional techniques, however, generally lack advanced planning capabilities, which limits their effectiveness in complex, multi-stage contact tasks.

Efforts to bridge the gap between MPC and force control have involved incorporating optimization into force control, often through MPC formulations that utilize simplified contact models like linear spring models or bounded admittance behavior. While these approaches improve motion-force coordination, they frequently rely on simplified or low-dimensional dynamics, which may not capture the full complexity of real-world interactions. Other attempts to extend MPC with force feedback have involved relaxing rigid contact models, using spring models, or modifying control structures. These "hybrid" approaches demonstrate the value of contact force prediction but often involve trade-offs, such as reliance on simplified dynamics, low planning rates, or increased state size and model approximations. Force feedback in many of these formulations is either ignored or estimated from joint torques. A more recent advancement by Kleff et al. proposed a unified formulation combining force control and nonlinear MPC without these common compromises.

Despite these developments, executing such controllers online and in real-time remains challenging, particularly for industrial tasks like deburring. Deburring requires maintaining stable contact and precise tool motions under uncertainty, demanding both accurate force magnitude and specific spatiotemporal force profiles. A significant limitation of existing MPC warm-start strategies is their inability to retain and reproduce task-specific motion strategies (e.g., characteristic insertion poses and trajectories for different holes) across multiple instances of the task. While MPC can leverage previous solutions for local convergence, it does not provide a true memory of diverse motion strategies.

To address this memory limitation, generative models, and more recently, diffusion models, have emerged. Earlier representations like dynamic movement primitives or Gaussian mixtures could encode demonstrations but struggled with capturing the high variability and multi-modality of real robot behaviors. Diffusion models overcome these limitations by learning rich distributions of feasible motions, enabling the generation of diverse and feasible trajectories conditioned on specific goals or sensory inputs. This makes them suitable as a "memory of motion" for complex planning problems.

This research integrates the advancements in force-feedback MPC, particularly the formulation described in, with the capabilities of diffusion models as motion priors. It aims to develop a robust framework that leverages the strengths of both, providing a novel solution for collision-aware, contact-rich industrial tasks such as robotic deburring, which has not been previously explored in this specific combined context.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop and validate a robotic control framework that enables a torque-controlled manipulator to perform complex, contact-rich industrial tasks, specifically deburring, while simultaneously ensuring collision avoidance and precise force regulation. This objective is motivated by several challenges inherent in such tasks:

**Challenges with Traditional Control and Planning Approaches:**
- **Neglect of Real-time Force Feedback in Classical MPC:** Standard MPC formulations primarily focus on motion optimization. The lack of explicit real-time force feedback makes force tracking purely feedforward and vulnerable to modeling errors and environmental uncertainties, which is problematic for tasks requiring stable contact.
- **Limited Planning Capabilities of Reactive Force Control:** While traditional force control methods offer robustness in contact, they typically lack advanced planning capabilities needed for complex, multi-stage tasks that involve geometric reasoning and path planning.
- **Difficulty with Online Execution in Contact-Rich Tasks:** Running sophisticated controllers online in real-time is challenging, especially for tasks like deburring where stable contact and precise tool motions must be maintained under uncertainty. The spatiotemporal profile of force application is critical, requiring the robot to both track desired forces and execute specific motion strategies.
- **Lack of Task-Specific Memory in MPC Warm-starts:** MPC often uses the previous solution as a warm-start to accelerate convergence. However, this only provides local acceleration and does not enable the controller to retain or adapt task-specific motion strategies (e.g., unique insertion poses or trajectories for different holes) across varying task instances.

**Specific Challenges of Robotic Deburring:**
- **Precision and Stability:** Deburring requires precise tool insertion into a hole, followed by stable application of a constant normal force while performing a circular motion. Maintaining contact stability and preventing slip-out necessitates careful regulation of both normal and tangential forces, ideally within a defined friction cone.
- **Geometric Complexity and Collision Avoidance:** Industrial workpieces often feature highly non-convex geometries, thin corridor-like features, and tight clearances. This creates narrow passages where even minor path deviations can lead to collisions or loss of reachability. Manually repositioning workpieces or robot bases for each hole is costly and increases setup time; therefore, planning from a single, fixed robot pose is desirable but significantly harder.
- **Real-time Constraints:** Satisfying these requirements under robot dynamics and rate limits at high control frequencies leaves limited time for computations such as collision checking, Jacobian/Hessian updates, or replanning, thereby stressing both planning and control systems.

**Overall Goal and Contributions:**
To address these motivations, the paper proposes a framework that integrates force-feedback MPC with diffusion-based motion priors. The core objectives and contributions are:

1. **Formalizing the Deburring Task:** To precisely define the deburring process as an Optimal Control Problem (OCP), incorporating requirements for precise tool insertion, constant normal force application, circular motion, and tangential force limitation to prevent slippage.
2. **Integrating Diffusion-based Motion Priors into Force-Feedback MPC:** To leverage diffusion models as a memory of motion strategies. These priors are intended to provide robust initialization and adaptation for the predictive controller, guiding it through complex, multimodal feasible spaces.
3. **Experimental Validation:** To demonstrate the effectiveness and robustness of the integrated framework on a torque-controlled manipulator performing industrial deburring tasks, evaluating its ability to achieve reliable tool insertion, accurate normal force tracking, circular deburring motions in challenging configurations, and collision avoidance under explicit constraints.

This work aims to establish a control pipeline that combines the reactive precision of force-feedback MPC with the adaptive planning capabilities offered by learned motion priors, presenting a comprehensive solution for contact-rich manipulation tasks in unstructured or complex environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework integrates force-feedback Model Predictive Control (MPC) with diffusion-based motion priors to enable robust and collision-aware robotic deburring. The methodology can be broken down into the formulation of the force-feedback MPC and the use of diffusion priors to guide it.

#### 3.1. A. Force-Feedback Model Predictive Control

The core of the control system is an Optimal Control Problem (OCP) formulation, which seeks to minimize a cost function over a finite prediction horizon, subject to system dynamics and constraints.

1. **Optimal Control Problem (OCP) Definition:**
 The OCP minimizes a sum of running and terminal costs over state and control trajectories, subject to discrete-time dynamics and inequality constraints.
 - **State (x):** Unlike classical MPC which often uses only joint position and velocity (q, $\dot{q}$), this approach augments the state to include contact forces ($\lambda$), making $x = (q, \dot{q}, \lambda) \in \mathbb{R}^{2n+m}$ where $n$ is the number of DoF and $m$ is the dimension of the contact force.
 - **Control Input (u):** Joint torques $\tau \in \mathbb{R}^n$.

2. **System Dynamics:**
 - **Joint Dynamics:** The standard forward dynamics of a fully actuated $n$-DoF manipulator in contact are used: $\ddot{q}(q, \dot{q}, \lambda, \tau) = M^{-1}(q) (\tau - h(q, \dot{q}) + J(q)^\top \lambda)$, where $M$ is the inertia matrix, $h$ accounts for gravity/Coriolis, and $J$ is the contact Jacobian.
 - **Contact Force Dynamics:** To allow force feedback, contact forces are modeled with a linear spring-damper law: $\lambda(q, \dot{q}) = -K \Delta p(q, p_c) - B \dot{p}(q, \dot{q})$, where $K$ and $B$ are stiffness and damping matrices, $\Delta p$ is end-effector deflection, and $\dot{p}$ is Cartesian velocity. Differentiating this law yields the force dynamics $\dot{\lambda}(x, u)$, which depends on $\dot{q}$ and $\ddot{p}$.
 - **Overall System Dynamics:** The joint and force dynamics are combined into a single system dynamics function $f_t(x_t, u_t)$, describing the evolution of $(q, \dot{q}, \lambda)$.

3. **Cost Function and Constraints:**
 - **Stage and Terminal Costs:** The cost function penalizes deviations from desired end-effector pose (position and orientation), desired contact force components, and state/input effort.
 - $e_p(t) = p(q_t) - p_{ref,t} \in \mathbb{R}^3$: Position error.
 - $e_R(t) = \text{vec}(\log(R_{ref,t}^\top R(q_t))) \in \mathbb{R}^3$: Orientation error using a rotation vector.
 - $e_{\lambda, \parallel}(t) = \lambda_{t, \parallel} - \lambda_{ref,t, \parallel} \in \mathbb{R}$: Normal force error.
 - $e_{\lambda, \perp}(t) = \lambda_{t, \perp} \in \mathbb{R}^2$: Tangential force penalization (used with 3D model).
 - The terms are weighted by block-diagonal matrices (e.g., $Q_e$) that allow independent tuning of different residuals. A regularization term penalizes deviation from gravity/Coriolis compensation torque.
 - **Contact Models:** Two contact models are considered for deburring:
 - **1D Model ($m=1$):** Predicts and regulates only the normal contact force along the tool axis, assuming unconstrained tangential movement.
 - **3D Model ($m=3$):** Additionally predicts and regulates tangential forces, which is crucial for preventing slip-out and maintaining contact stability (implicitly via the $e_{\lambda, \perp}$ term in the cost).
 - **Task-Phase Weighting:** Cost terms are adaptively weighted across different phases of the task (trajectory following, pre-contact, insertion, deburring) to activate or deactivate specific objectives (e.g., increasing normal force tracking during insertion, tracking circular paths during deburring).
 - **Collision Avoidance:** Hard constraints are imposed to ensure a signed-distance margin $\epsilon > 0$ between robot and environment bodies ($d_{ij}(q_t) - \epsilon \ge 0$). This constraint is linearized for the Sequential Quadratic Programming (SQP) solver.

#### 3.2. B. Diffusion Priors as Memory of Motion

To overcome the limitations of local optimizers in complex, non-convex environments and provide task-specific warm-starts, diffusion-based motion priors are integrated.

1. **Motivation for Diffusion Priors:**
 Local optimizers in MPC often struggle with highly non-convex feasible sets (e.g., cluttered workspaces with narrow passages), converging to poor local minima or taking many iterations to recover feasibility. A warm-start that already encodes task-specific strategies and collision-aware geometry can significantly improve performance.

2. **Trajectory Generation (Offline Dataset):**
 A synthetic dataset of feasible deburring motions is generated using the Humanoid Path Planner. This process aims to capture the multimodality of motions around different holes and approach poses.
 - Steps: Solve inverse kinematics from multiple seeds, generate collision-free paths using bi-directional RRT* in configuration space, and refine paths with an OCP for smoothness and feasibility.

3. **Diffusion Model Training:**
 A conditional Diffusion Transformer (DiT) is trained on this dataset. This model learns rich distributions of feasible motions, allowing for diverse and controlled sampling.
 - **Input:** A noise sequence, conditioned on the initial robot state $x_0$ and the target end-effector pose.
 - **Output:** A trajectory proposal.
 - **Efficiency:** The diffusion process is restricted to a small number of denoising steps ($D_s = 25$) to maintain fast inference speeds for real-time applications.

4. **Integration with MPC:**
 The trajectory proposed by the diffusion model does not replace the MPC's output but serves as a reference. The MPC's state regularization term (part of $||x_t - x_{ref,t}||_{Q_x}^2$ in the cost function) is set to track this diffusion-generated trajectory. This mechanism "pulls" the MPC solution towards the prior, guiding the local optimizer towards favorable basins of attraction and accelerating convergence, while MPC simultaneously enforces dynamics, force objectives, torque limits, and collision avoidance as hard constraints.

#### 3.3. C. Implementation Details

- **Hardware:** Franka Emika Panda robot, custom end-effector with ATI Mini45 force-torque sensor and Intel RealSense D435i camera. Spherical tool tip.
- **Software:** MPC implemented in Python using Crocoddyl with the CSQP solver. Diffusion model uses a dedicated GPU. Communication via ROS2.
- **Control Frequencies:** Controlled torques interpolated at 1 kHz. Measured forces low-pass filtered at 10 Hz.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The validation of the proposed framework was conducted through a series of experimental scenarios on a torque-controlled Franka Emika Panda robot. The experiments aimed to evaluate the controller's ability to handle force regulation, collision avoidance, and complex motion planning under varying conditions.

#### 4.1. A. Scenario 1: Accessible Single-Hole Deburring (Baseline)

This scenario served as a baseline to evaluate the force-feedback MPC with 1D and 3D contact models, particularly under collision avoidance constraints. The robot performed deburring on an accessible single hole.

- **No Obstacle Condition:** Both the 1D and 3D contact models successfully tracked the desired end-effector trajectories. The Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) for normal force tracking was 5.35 N for the 1D model and 4.40 N for the 3D model, indicating comparable performance in an obstacle-free setting.
- **With Obstacle Condition:** A planar obstacle was placed close to the robot, such that the nominal circular deburring motion would result in a collision.
 - The **3D contact model**, with active collision avoidance, successfully forced the solver to deviate the end-effector path during execution, trading off perfect trajectory tracking for constraint satisfaction, while maintaining stable in-hole contact (Fig. 5d).
 - The **1D contact model** failed to maintain stable in-hole contact (Fig. 5b). The tool slid out of the hole because the optimizer prioritized clearance over maintaining the correct orientation, and tangential forces were not explicitly regulated, leading to a violation of the no-slip condition.
- **Conclusion from Scenario 1:** The explicit regulation of tangential forces, as provided by the 3D contact model, was demonstrated to be crucial for preserving contact stability when collision avoidance constraints necessitate path deviations that might otherwise lead to slip-out.

#### 4.2. B. Scenario 2: Obstacle-Constrained Multi-Hole Reach-and-Deburring Sequence

This scenario evaluated the force-tracking performance in more demanding configurations, involving both collision-avoidance constraints and targets near the robot's workspace boundary. The workpiece contained five holes, some requiring near fully-extended arm configurations or being adjacent to a plane collision constraint.

- **Success Rate and Robustness:** The deburring task was completed across all five targets with a 100% success rate, without slip or collisions. This indicated that the controller could jointly satisfy contact and geometric constraints even in adverse poses.
- **Accuracy Degradation in Challenging Poses:** Normal-force tracking RMSE increased as the robot operated farther from its baseline pose or in configurations with reduced manipulability (Table I). For instance, holes 3, 4, and 5 exhibited higher RMSE (17.20 N, 17.41 N, 15.34 N respectively) compared to hole 2 (7.98 N).
- **Factors Affecting Accuracy:** This degradation was attributed to:
 - Poor Jacobian conditioning near workspace limits, reducing the robot's authority to apply forces along the hole axis.
 - Proximity to joint and torque bounds, limiting corrective actions.
 - Tighter orientation feasibility requirements when aligning the end-effector with the hole while respecting plane constraints.
 These factors collectively reduced the effective bandwidth available for force regulation.
- **Collision-Aware Path Deflections:** For holes adjacent to the plane constraint, the nominal circular deburring trajectory could not be executed completely. The solver produced collision-aware path deflections that preserved contact stability by prioritizing desired normal force maintenance over perfect trajectory tracking.

#### 4.3. C. Scenario 3: Long Sequence Integration

This scenario demonstrated the complete integration of the deburring task pipeline, performing an ad-hoc sequence of machining multiple holes on the workpiece, as shown in the accompanying video.

- **Diffusion-Guided Motion:** The diffusion model was queried for each hole to produce a reference guide. This guidance enabled the robot to reach difficult locations and navigate through narrow passages efficiently, which would be computationally expensive for an online planner alone.
- **MPC for Execution and Robustness:** The MPC executed the motion, regulating contact forces, compensating for model inaccuracies and object pose deviations, and enforcing local collision avoidance.
- **Human-Robot Interaction:** The torque-controlled compliance of the robot allowed it to tolerate physical disturbances from a human co-operator, highlighting the safety aspect of the approach.
- **Overall System Performance:** Holes were reached and machined within the accuracy range observed in Scenario 2, confirming the integrated pipeline's effectiveness and robustness in a more complex, sequential task.

#### 4.4. Summary of Findings

The experiments collectively demonstrated that integrating diffusion-based motion priors with force-feedback MPC provides a robust and effective framework for challenging contact-rich tasks like deburring. The 3D contact model proved essential for maintaining contact stability under collision avoidance. While the system exhibited high success rates, force-tracking accuracy could degrade in configurations near workspace limits due to manipulability constraints. The diffusion priors effectively guided the MPC, enabling it to solve complex planning problems in cluttered environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework that integrates diffusion-based motion priors with force-feedback Model Predictive Control (MPC) for collision-aware, contact-rich industrial tasks, exemplified by robotic deburring. This integration represents a notable contribution to the field, offering several significant implications and potential impacts:

1. **Novel Integration for Complex Tasks:** To the authors' knowledge, this is the first documented integration of diffusion-based motion priors with force-feedback MPC specifically for collision-aware, contact-rich industrial tasks such as deburring. This combination addresses critical limitations in existing robotics workflows by bridging the gap between high-level planning (motion strategies via diffusion) and low-level, reactive control (force-feedback MPC).

2. **Enhanced Robustness and Adaptability:** The framework significantly enhances the robustness and adaptability of robotic systems in complex, contact-rich environments.
 - **Robust Initialization:** Diffusion priors provide a "memory of motion," offering robust, task-specific, and collision-aware initializations to the MPC. This overcomes the challenge of local optimizers converging to poor minima in non-convex spaces and accelerates convergence, especially in cluttered environments with narrow passages.
 - **Stable Contact Management:** The force-feedback MPC, particularly with the 3D contact model, ensures stable contact by explicitly regulating both normal and tangential forces, preventing slip-out even when external constraints (like collision avoidance) force the robot to deviate from nominal trajectories.

3. **Practical Industrial Application:** The method offers a practical alternative to conventional robotic deburring setups, which often rely on expensive specialized tools, rigid manipulators, and dedicated insertion guides. By demonstrating comparable performance with a collaborative robot and standard tools, the approach can potentially reduce costs, increase flexibility, and simplify deployment in various manufacturing settings.

4. **Improved Safety and Human-Robot Collaboration:** Operating in torque control inherently enhances safety. The robot's compliance allows it to be interrupted by human co-operators and reduces the risk of damage even in the presence of undetected obstacles. This characteristic makes the proposed approach suitable for applications requiring close human-robot collaboration, a key aspect of Industry 4.0.

5. **Addressing Planning and Control Challenges Simultaneously:** The research directly tackles the dual challenge of geometric feasibility and contact stability under real-time computational constraints. It demonstrates that integrating a learning-based global guidance mechanism (diffusion priors) with a reactive, constraint-aware local controller (force-feedback MPC) is effective for tasks that stress both planning and control systems.

6. **Foundation for Future Research:** The paper identifies several avenues for future work, indicating its foundational nature:
 - **Generalization to Unknown Environments:** Extending the offline training process to unknown or dynamic environments by conditioning diffusion models on sensor-based representations of obstacles.
 - **Real-time Prior Refinement:** Exploring faster run-time refinement of motion priors (e.g., with sample-based optimizers like MPPI).
 - **Scalable Collision Handling:** Improving MPC's ability to handle a large number of collision pairs efficiently, potentially through advancements in numerical optimization.
 - **Manipulability-Aware Control:** Incorporating manipulability-aware cost shaping and constraint handling to maintain force fidelity and accuracy, especially in configurations near workspace limits.
 - **Mobile Manipulation:** Transferring the task to mobile manipulators, which would introduce complexities such as base movement sequencing and external perception for robot repositioning.

7. **Open-Source Contribution:** The commitment to release the code, built upon open-source modules, contributes to the reproducibility of the research and fosters further development and adoption within the robotics community.

In summary, this work advances the state of the art in robotic manipulation by combining robust learning-based motion generation with precise force and collision-aware control. Its potential impact lies in enabling more versatile, safer, and cost-effective robotic solutions for complex industrial tasks, thereby expanding the capabilities of collaborative robots in manufacturing and beyond.
