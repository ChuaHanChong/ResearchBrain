# Learning-Guided Force-Feedback Model Predictive Control with Obstacle Avoidance for Robotic Deburring

> [!summary] Summary
> A control framework integrates learning-guided force-feedback Model Predictive Control with obstacle avoidance for robotic deburring, allowing a torque-controlled manipulator to execute precise, contact-rich industrial tasks. The method leverages diffusion-based motion priors for robust guidance and achieves 100% success in multi-hole deburring, maintaining stable contact and avoiding collisions even in challenging configurations.

## Problem

- Existing control methods struggle with contact-rich industrial tasks like deburring, as traditional MPC lacks real-time force feedback while reactive force control lacks advanced planning capabilities.
- Complex, multi-stage manipulation in cluttered environments is challenging due to highly non-convex feasible spaces and the inability of local optimizers to retain task-specific motion strategies.
- Robotic deburring specifically demands precise tool insertion, stable contact with regulated normal and tangential forces, circular motion, and collision avoidance in geometrically complex, often constrained, workspaces.

## Method

- Developed a force-feedback ==Model Predictive Control== (MPC) formulation that augments the state with contact forces and explicitly regulates both normal and tangential contact forces using a 3D contact model.
- Generated an offline dataset of feasible deburring motions and trained a conditional Diffusion Transformer (DiT) to learn rich distributions of these motions, capable of generating diverse and collision-aware trajectories.
- Integrated the diffusion model as a motion prior into the MPC, where its generated trajectories serve as a reference to guide the MPC's local optimizer towards favorable basins of attraction and accelerate convergence.

## Results

- The 3D force-feedback MPC successfully maintained stable in-hole contact and avoided collisions, even when path deviations were necessary, achieving **100%** success in multi-hole deburring sequences.
- Diffusion priors effectively guided the robot through challenging, obstacle-constrained environments and narrow passages, facilitating efficient reach-and-deburring operations.
- Force tracking accuracy was robust overall (RMSE as low as **4.40** N for normal force), though it showed degradation in configurations near workspace limits due to reduced manipulability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Maintaining stable contact during collision avoidance requires explicit regulation of tangential forces, as demonstrated by the 3D contact model preventing slip-out during path deviations.
> - Diffusion-based motion priors provide effective 'memory of motion,' offering robust, task-specific, and collision-aware initializations that guide the MPC's local optimizer through complex, non-convex environments.
> - Combining learning-based global guidance with reactive, constraint-aware force-feedback MPC creates a robust framework capable of simultaneously addressing geometric feasibility and contact stability in real-time.
