# Action-Sketcher: From Reasoning to Action via Visual Sketches for Long-Horizon Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Peking University, BAAI, and the University of Sydney developed Action-Sketcher, a framework for long-horizon robotic manipulation that uses explicit visual sketches as an intermediate representation. This approach improves task success rates by grounding high-level language instructions in concrete spatial intent and facilitates human-in-the-loop adaptability for real-time corrections.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models struggle with long-horizon robotic manipulation due to a lack of interpretability and brittleness in task decomposition.
- Implicit latent representations or text-only reasoning in VLA models lead to spatial ambiguity and referential uncertainty in cluttered environments.
- Human-robot collaboration in robotic manipulation is hindered by a lack of transparency and weak support for real-time human intervention.

## Method

- Introduces "Visual Sketch," an explicit, sparse intermediate representation using geometric primitives (points, boxes, arrows) to render spatial intent from language instructions.
- Implements a See "," Think "," Sketch "," Act pipeline with an adaptive token-gated mechanism to dynamically switch between high-level reasoning and low-latency action execution.
- Utilizes a multi-stage curriculum training strategy, progressively teaching foundational spatiotemporal reasoning, accurate sketch generation, and robust sketch-to-action translation.

## Results

- Action-Sketcher achieved superior performance compared to state-of-the-art VLA baselines across LIBERO and custom long-horizon tasks, demonstrating a significant advantage in complex scenarios (e.g., **96.0%** success on LIBERO Long).
- In-depth error analysis revealed that **61%** of failures were due to inaccuracies in Visual Sketch generation, yet human-in-the-loop corrections on these sketches dramatically improved success rates (e.g., **+23.0%** on "Tidy Table").
- Ablation studies confirmed that the Visual Sketch itself and its individual primitives (points, boxes, arrows) are critical for precise spatial grounding and overall task performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit visual sketches provide low-entropy geometric guidance, effectively disambiguating spatial references and making the robot's plan verifiable.
> - A dynamic See "," Think "," Sketch "," Act loop, governed by an adaptive token-gated strategy, allows for robust sequential planning and execution in complex, evolving environments.
> - Enabling human-in-the-loop corrections on the visual sketch enhances debuggability and adaptability, fostering more reliable human-robot collaboration.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research directly addresses critical limitations in the field of robotic manipulation, particularly concerning **Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models** and their application to **long-horizon tasks**. Historically, robotic manipulation has progressed from simple, short-horizon primitive actions to more complex, multi-step tasks. Current state-of-the-art VLA models, while showing impressive capabilities, often fall into two categories with inherent drawbacks:

- **End-to-End VLAs:** These models directly map observations and language instructions to actions, embedding the plan intent in latent representations. While effective for short-horizon tasks, this approach suffers from a lack of interpretability, difficulty in task decomposition for long sequences, and brittleness when faced with ambiguous instructions or dynamic scene changes. Errors can propagate unnoticed, and human intervention is challenging.
- **Hierarchical VLAs:** These models introduce planner-controller splits but often lack persistent modeling of global intent and struggle with referential disambiguation in cluttered environments. Their reasoning can be instantaneous and localized, not robustly handling evolving goals or emerging errors.

The "Action-Sketcher" framework carves out a new niche by focusing on an **explicit visual intermediate representation** called **Visual Sketch**. This approach bridges the gap between high-level language understanding and low-level motor control in a human-interpretable and verifiable manner. It builds upon "think before act" paradigms seen in works like EO-1 and OneTwoVLA, but critically moves beyond text-only intermediate reasoning. By externalizing spatial intent through visual primitives (points, boxes, arrows), Action-Sketcher directly tackles the "spatial referential disambiguation" problem that implicit latent representations or text-only reasoning often fail to resolve effectively.

Moreover, the emphasis on **human-in-the-loop adaptability** and **explainable planning artifacts** positions this work at the forefront of safe and reliable robot deployment. The ability for humans to inspect and correct the robot's plan at the "sketch level" addresses a significant hurdle in trust and collaboration, distinguishing it from prior work in visual prompting or trajectory sketches that are often static or non-editable latents. This aligns with a growing trend in AI research towards more transparent, debuggable, and interactive intelligent systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The paper identifies two primary bottlenecks hindering reliable long-horizon robotic manipulation: **spatial ambiguity** in language-to-action grounding and **temporal brittleness** in human-in-the-loop coordination. Action-Sketcher is explicitly designed to overcome these challenges with the following key objectives:

1. **Externalize Spatial Intent:** The core motivation is to move beyond latent, text-only cues by introducing an "implausible visual intermediate" called **Visual Sketch**. This sketch, composed of primitives like points, boxes, and arrows, aims to explicitly render spatial intent, connect language to scene geometry, and provide a human-verifiable bridge between high-level reasoning and low-level control. This directly addresses the issue of referential uncertainty and underspecified instructions in cluttered environments.
2. **Enable Robust Long-Horizon Planning and Execution:** The framework seeks to improve task decomposition and temporal resilience. By operating in a cyclic **See → Think → Sketch → Act** workflow, Action-Sketcher aims to allow for sequential, step-by-step planning and execution, which is crucial for complex, multi-stage tasks where goals and environments can evolve.
3. **Enhance Interpretability and Debuggability:** A major goal is to make the robot's decision-making process more transparent. By exposing the Visual Sketch as a human-readable plan, the system allows for easier error detection, causal explanation of action choices, and efficient recovery from mistakes, fostering greater trust and collaboration.
4. **Facilitate Human-in-the-Loop Adaptability:** The explicit nature of Visual Sketch provides a natural interface for human interaction. The model is designed to support real-time reactive corrections and intent supervision through direct sketch modification, thereby overcoming limitations of traditional VLA policies that offer weak support for human intervention.
5. **Develop a Scalable Training and Evaluation Paradigm:** To support the novel framework, the authors aim to curate a diverse corpus with interleaved images, text, Visual Sketch supervision, and action sequences. Furthermore, they seek to develop a multi-stage curriculum training recipe that effectively unifies modalities, ensures precise linguistic grounding to sketches, and robustly translates sketches into actions.

In essence, the motivation is to create a more intelligent, adaptable, and understandable robotic agent capable of tackling complex, real-world manipulation tasks by making its internal "thought process" visible and interactive.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Action-Sketcher operates on a **See → Think → Sketch → Act** loop, orchestrated by an adaptive token-gated strategy. The framework is built upon a VLA model (specifically, π0 is used as the backbone) and consists of several key components:

#### 3.1. Visual Sketch: Explicit Spatial Intent

Visual Sketch is the central innovation, serving as a sparse tuple of geometric primitives rendered on the robot's ego-view camera frame. It explicitly represents spatial intent at each time step `t`:
`S_t = {B_t, P_t, A_t}`

- **Boxes (B_t):** Bounding boxes `(x1, y1, x2, y2)` delineate target regions or objects, disambiguating object references and abstracting appearance while preserving scale and placement.
- **Points (P_t):** Keypoints `(x, y)` specify precise interaction locations, such as part-level affordances (e.g., a teapot spout tip, a handle contact point) or motion waypoints.
- **Arrows (A_t):** Arrows `(p_start, p_end)` or `(p, axis, direction)` encode intended motions.
 - **Translation Arrows:** Guide end-effector trajectories between keypoints.
 - **Rotation Arrows:** Specify rotations about canonical axes (x, y, z) centered at a keypoint, indicating tilt or yaw adjustments.

This explicit representation acts as a verifiable "contract" between high-level reasoning and low-level control, providing low-entropy geometric guidance.

#### 3.2. See-Think-Sketch-Act Pipeline

The model dynamically switches between two modes using a token-gated mechanism (`<BOR>` for Begin-of-Reasoning, `<BOA>` for Begin-of-Action):

1. **Reasoning Mode:**
 - Triggered by `<BOR>` (e.g., after a subtask completion, error, or human intervention).
 - **Temporal Reasoning:** The model analyzes the current scene, task instruction, and history of completed subtasks to deduce the *next logical subtask*.
 - **Spatial Reasoning:** Based on the new subtask, it performs spatial reasoning to generate the text-based elements for the Visual Sketch (bounding boxes, keypoints, arrows).
 - Concludes with `<EOR>` (End-of-Reasoning), after which the text-based sketch is rendered into an image, and the input context is updated.

2. **Action Mode:**
 - Triggered by `<BOA>` (during routine subtask execution).
 - The model's action expert (a Flow Matching head) generates low-latency action chunks, conditioned on current observations, the rendered Visual Sketch image, and the robot's proprioceptive state.

This dual-mode design allows adaptive switching, balancing deliberation with real-time execution.

#### 3.3. Model Structure

Action-Sketcher uses π0 as its backbone, which comprises:
- **Vision Encoder:** A pre-trained Vision Transformer (SigLIP-SO400M) processes multi-view RGB images and the Visual Sketch image into visual tokens.
- **Auto-Regressive LLM Backbone:** A Transformer-based LLM (Gemma-3B) takes concatenated visual, textual (task instruction, subtask history), and sketch tokens. It auto-regressively performs high-level reasoning, generates text-based sketch parameters, and subtask descriptions in Reasoning Mode.
- **Flow Matching Action Expert:** A Flow Matching head acts as a conditional generative model for low-level control (Action Mode), predicting continuous, high-frequency action chunks (end-effector poses/joint angles and gripper states) conditioned on robot state and LLM embeddings.

#### 3.4. Multi-Stage Curriculum Training Strategy

The model is trained using a three-stage curriculum to build robust capabilities:

1. **Stage 1: Foundational Spatiotemporal Learning:**
 - **Objective:** Develop general spatiotemporal modeling and reasoning.
 - **Data:** Large-scale corpus (∼3.4M spatial samples from Visual Grounding, Spatial Pointing, Scene Understanding, VQA datasets; 870k temporal sequences from EgoPlan, ShareRobot, AgiBot-World).
 - **Method:** Pre-trains Vision Encoder and LLM. 20% of the temporal corpus is annotated with GPT-4o-generated textual rationales.

2. **Stage 2: Reasoning-to-Sketch Enhancement:**
 - **Objective:** Specialize in coherent reasoning and Visual Sketch generation.
 - **Data:** 21k samples from 2.6k real-world episodes (tidying, pouring, pick-and-place) and 1.7k annotated trajectories from LIBERO and RoboTwin 2.0.
 - **Method:** Fine-tunes LLM to generate next subtask and corresponding text-based Visual Sketch based on scene analysis, task instruction, and execution history. Data is generated using both automatic (SAM + GPT-4o) and human-in-the-loop pipelines.

3. **Stage 3: Sketch-to-Action and Mode Adaptation:**
 - **Objective:** Jointly train the action policy and mode-switching mechanism for robust real-world execution.
 - **Data:** ∼2.8M state-action pairs from 6.1k episodes combining simulation (LIBERO, modified RoboTwin 2.0) and real-world teleoperation data, aligning Visual Sketches with action sequences.
 - **Method:**
 - **Action Expert Training:** Trains the Flow Matching head to predict action chunks conditioned on Visual Sketches. To enhance robustness to inference-time inaccuracies, Visual Sketches are augmented (perturbed bounding boxes, points, and arrows).
 - **Mode Adaptation:** Addresses data imbalance (Action Mode steps outnumber Reasoning Mode steps) using a mode-balanced sampling strategy to prevent bias towards the `<BOA>` token.

This curriculum progressively refines the model's capabilities, from general understanding to precise sketch generation, and finally to robust action execution guided by these sketches.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments comprehensively evaluate Action-Sketcher's performance, robustness, and interpretability in both simulated and real-world environments.

#### 4.1. Task Performance Evaluation (RQ1)

- **Superior Performance:** Action-Sketcher consistently outperforms state-of-the-art VLA baselines (e.g., Diffusion Policy, Octo, OpenVLA, π0, π0.5, OpenVLA-OFT, TraceVLA, Molmo-ACT, PixelVLA) across all evaluated benchmarks: LIBERO (Spatial, Object, Goal, Long) and specialized long-horizon/spatially complex tasks in RoboTwin 2.0 and real-world setups.
- **Significant Long-Horizon Advantage:** The most pronounced advantage is observed in "Long" categories of LIBERO and custom long-horizon tasks (e.g., "Stack Blocks," "Tidy Table," "Pour Tea"). For instance, in LIBERO Long, Action-Sketcher achieved 96.0% success compared to the next best at 94.5%. On RoboTwin 2.0 "Stack Blocks," Action-Sketcher reached 34.5% success, significantly higher than π0.5's 7.0%.
- **Validation of Explicit Planning:** These results strongly validate the core hypothesis: for tasks requiring extended planning and intricate spatial goals, the explicit See-Think-Sketch-Act cycle offers a significant advantage over methods relying solely on latent plan intent.

#### 4.2. Error Analysis and Human-in-the-Loop (RQ2)

- **Primary Failure Mode:** An in-depth failure analysis revealed that 66% of failures stemmed from errors in the Reasoning Mode, with the vast majority (61% of all failures) specifically attributed to inaccuracies in **Visual Sketch generation** (inaccurate spatial grounding). Incorrect mode switching accounted for 12%, and Action Mode failures for 19%.
- **Effectiveness of Human-in-the-Loop:** Crucially, the explicit and human-interpretable nature of Visual Sketch allowed for effective human intervention. When a human supervisor could pause execution and make minor edits to the generated sketch before action execution, task success rates dramatically improved. For example, on "Tidy Table," success increased from 52.0% to 75.0% (+23.0%), and on "Pour Tea," from 27.6% to 44.0% (+16.4%).
- **Enhanced Collaboration:** This demonstrates Action-Sketcher's capability not only for autonomous performance but also as a powerful framework for human-robot collaboration, allowing for both sketch correction and intent supervision.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies (RQ3)

Ablation studies confirmed the critical contribution of each framework component, Visual Sketch primitive, and training stage:

- **Framework Components:**
 - Removing **Spatial Reasoning** caused a significant performance drop (e.g., "Stack Blocks" success from 34.5% to 13.8%).
 - Eliminating the **Visual Sketch** entirely resulted in an even more severe decline (e.g., "Stack Blocks" success from 34.5% to 9.8%). This confirms Visual Sketch is a fundamental bridge, not just an auxiliary visualization.
- **Visual Primitives:** All primitives were essential for optimal performance:
 - Removing **Keypoints** led to the largest drop (e.g., "Stack Blocks" from 34.5% to 26.6%), highlighting their criticality for precise coordinate grounding.
 - Omitting **Arrows** or **Bounding Boxes** also noticeably reduced reliability, confirming their importance for dynamic guidance and disambiguation.
- **Training Curriculum:**
 - Skipping **Stage 1 (Foundational Spatiotemporal Learning)** resulted in a moderate performance drop (e.g., "Stack Blocks" from 34.5% to 29.2%).
 - Omitting **Stage 2 (Reasoning-to-Sketch Enhancement)** led to a sharp decline (e.g., "Stack Blocks" from 34.5% to 18.1%), indicating incoherent sketch generation without this specialized fine-tuning.
 - Removing **Stage 3 (Sketch-to-Action and Mode Adaptation)** resulted in total failure (0.0% success), proving its indispensability for the policy to interpret sketches and translate them into actions robustly.

#### 4.4. Additional Findings (Supplementary)

- **Sub-task vs. Task-level Sketching:** Sub-task-level sketching (Action-Sketcher) significantly outperformed task-level sketching, especially on longer, more complex tasks, affirming the benefit of decomposing tasks and generating progressive, targeted sketches.
- **Automated vs. Human Annotation:** While automated sketch generation improved over baselines, human-annotated sketches yielded higher success rates, indicating the value of high-quality, precise annotations.
- **Inference Speed:** Despite the overhead of sketch generation (3.5-6.4s per sub-task), Action-Sketcher maintained competitive or superior efficiency (success rate normalized by total time) due to its high success rate, especially on complex tasks.
- **Embodied Benchmarks:** Action-Sketcher demonstrated strong performance on diverse embodied reasoning benchmarks (BLINK, CV-Bench, EmbSpatial-Bench, RoboSpatial, RefSpatial-Bench), indicating that its explicit spatial reasoning training generalizes beyond manipulation to other spatial understanding tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Action-Sketcher framework represents a significant advancement in robotic manipulation, offering a novel paradigm for building more capable, transparent, and collaborative robotic agents. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Bridging High-Level Reasoning and Low-Level Control:** By introducing Visual Sketch as an explicit, human-readable, and machine-executable intermediate representation, Action-Sketcher effectively bridges the semantic gap between abstract language instructions and concrete robot actions. This is a critical step towards more reliable and generalizable robotic policies.
- **Enhanced Interpretability and Debuggability:** The explicit nature of Visual Sketches provides an unprecedented level of transparency into the robot's spatial understanding and planned actions. This moves VLA models away from "black box" operation, enabling researchers and users to understand *why* a robot plans to act in a certain way, identify errors, and debug failures efficiently. This interpretability is vital for deploying robots in real-world, safety-critical applications.
- **Improved Human-Robot Collaboration:** The human-in-the-loop capabilities, demonstrated through sketch correction and intent supervision, are transformative. They allow non-expert users to intuitively guide or rectify robot behavior in real-time by modifying visual cues, fostering trust and enabling seamless collaboration. This capability is crucial for making robots more adaptable to human preferences and unforeseen circumstances.
- **Robustness to Ambiguity and Dynamic Environments:** By explicitly grounding language instructions in scene geometry, Action-Sketcher inherently addresses spatial disambiguation in cluttered or underspecified scenes. The adaptive See-Think-Sketch-Act loop also enhances temporal resilience, allowing the robot to react to dynamic changes and progress through long-horizon tasks more reliably than prior methods.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The paper not only presents a novel framework but also contributes a curated interleaved corpus and a multi-stage curriculum training recipe. These resources and methodologies provide a strong foundation for future research in:
 - **Scaling laws:** Investigating how larger models and different architectures could further enhance the precision and fidelity of visual sketches.
 - **Design of visual languages:** Systematically exploring how different visual attributes (e.g., color, shape, line style) in sketches impact robot performance and human understanding.
 - **Advanced human-in-the-loop paradigms:** Developing more intuitive and sophisticated interfaces for real-time interaction.
 - **Broader generalization:** Extending the framework to more complex scenarios like mobile manipulation and multi-robot collaboration.

In conclusion, Action-Sketcher pushes the boundaries of robotic manipulation by offering a principled and empirically validated approach to long-horizon tasks. Its focus on explicit, human-interpretable visual planning lays the groundwork for a new generation of more transparent, adaptable, and collaborative intelligent robots.
