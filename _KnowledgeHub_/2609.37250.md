# V-JEPA Policy: Building Effective World-Action Models on Predictive Visual Latents

> [!summary] Summary
> V-JEPA Policy builds a world-action model on a frozen V-JEPA 2.1 encoder's latent space. It trains two modules jointly from scratch: an instruction-conditioned future-latent predictor and a flow-matching action expert. They are linked through the predictor's future-informed key-value states in a Mixture-of-Transformers. With 0.9B parameters it reaches 97.3% on LIBERO and 79.3% on LIBERO-Plus, rising to 91.5% after action-free predictor pretraining on DROID videos.

## Problem

- Leading world-action models adapt large pretrained video generators or image-editing models, so they inherit the whole generative backbone along with the predictive knowledge they actually need.
- Carrying a full visual generator makes these models large and memory-hungry at deployment, even for methods like FastWAM that skip pixel decoding at inference time.
- Nobody had tested whether a predictive latent space learned at scale, without a generative model, is enough on its own to learn an effective, robust world-action model.

## Method

- **Instruction-conditioned future-query predictor**: a ==V-JEPA-2-style ViT== with ==3D RoPE== and ==T5/state cross-attention== fills learnable ==mask queries== at future tubelet positions, regressing frozen-encoder future latents with an ==L1 loss==.
- **Future-informed context interface**: the action expert reads the predictor's layer-wise context ==key-value states==, shaped by ==bidirectional attention== with future queries, through shared attention in a ==Mixture-of-Transformers==. The predictor cannot attend to action tokens.
- **Joint single-stage objective plus action-free predictor pretraining**: ==flow-matching== action loss plus future-latent loss with weight 1. The predictor can be pretrained on DROID video-instruction pairs without action labels, then transferred.

## Results

- The from-scratch 0.9B model scores **97.25%** on LIBERO, **79.25%** on LIBERO-Plus and **50.92%** on RoboCasa-GR1. It beats FastWAM's **51.5%** on LIBERO-Plus and exceeds GR00T N1.6 and pi0.5 on GR1.
- Under one shared recipe, V-JEPA 2.1 ViT-L beats DINOv2 by **2.35 points** in-distribution and **12.23 points** on LIBERO-Plus. The WAN2.2 VAE latent collapses to **46.43%** under distribution shift.
- DROID predictor pretraining lifts LIBERO-Plus from **79.25%** to **91.50%**, beating **81.64%** from 60k-update extended training, and lifts real-world bimanual success from **45%** to **80%** with **4.66 GiB** peak VRAM.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Predictive self-supervised latents, not generative backbones, may be the part of video pretraining that matters for control. Inheriting a full generator looks unnecessary for competitive world-action models.
> - Robustness to distribution shift follows representation objective more than encoder size. Scaling ViT-L to ViT-G helps in-distribution but adds little or even hurts out-of-distribution performance.
> - Action-free future prediction on in-the-wild videos transfers knowledge that longer task-specific training cannot recover, so cheap unlabeled video is a strong lever for policy generalization.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Overview

A robot told to manipulate objects should still succeed when the instruction wording, the camera viewpoint, or the scene's appearance changes from training. World-action models (WAMs) couple prediction of future visual states to generation of robot actions, and they often get that predictive ability from a large pretrained visual generator. V-JEPA Policy tests whether a robot can benefit from learning to predict what a scene will look like next without carrying along that full generator.

The policy predicts a future in features and then uses that future-informed context to act, instead of adapting an entire visual generator. LIBERO-Plus tests robustness to seven controlled shifts. On this benchmark, the policy trained from scratch reaches 79.3% overall success without fine-tuning on perturbed demonstrations. FastWAM reaches 51.5%, and ImageWAM scores higher at 83.1%.

The from-scratch V-JEPA Policy scores 97.3% on LIBERO and 79.3% on LIBERO-Plus. These are comparisons with published baselines, not a controlled architecture ablation.

### 2. Predict the future, then act

A "latent" here is a learned feature representation of an image or video, rather than pixels. A frozen V-JEPA 2.1 encoder turns observed camera frames into context features and turns future frames into training targets.

A predictor, trained from scratch and conditioned on the instruction and the robot's state, combines the observed features with learned future queries. The context and the future queries interact inside the predictor. The action expert does not simply consume the final predicted future features. Instead, it attends to the predictor's layer-by-layer context key–value states, which have been informed by the future queries. The action expert learns to generate continuous action chunks using flow matching.

The predictor and the action expert are trained jointly. Future-feature prediction and action generation each have their own supervision.

The policy predicts future features in one predictor pass. The action expert then generates an action chunk using ten Euler integration steps. The default model has 0.9B parameters, of which 0.6B are trainable.

Method overview. Observed frames pass through the frozen V-JEPA encoder to become context tokens, and future queries join those tokens in the trainable predictor. The predictor's context keys and values inform the trainable action expert, which receives noisy action tokens and produces action predictions. Snowflakes mark frozen components and flames mark trainable ones.

An ablation tests whether the future prediction itself matters. Every variant keeps action supervision. The encoder, data, seed, batch size, number of updates, and action-chunk setup are held fixed.

- **Future-prediction loss removed, query pathway kept:** LIBERO success falls to 91.65% from 97.25%, and LIBERO-Plus falls to 68.81% from 79.25%.
- **Future queries removed (context-only):** the model scores 92.55% on LIBERO and 65.86% on LIBERO-Plus.

This supports a contribution from explicit future-latent supervision beyond merely adding the query pathway.

Future-prediction controls, as LIBERO / LIBERO-Plus success rates (%). Full model: 97.25 / 79.25. Future queries without future loss: 91.65 / 68.81. Context-only: 92.55 / 65.86.

### 3. Is a predictive feature space a better foundation?

[[2603.16666|FastWAM]] skips future-video generation at test time but still uses a pretrained video-model backbone. [[2606.19531|ImageWAM]] uses a pretrained image-editing model's internal features. V-JEPA Policy instead asks whether a predictive feature space can suffice.

The authors compare frozen visual encoders while holding the downstream WAM architecture, demonstrations, and training budget fixed. LIBERO-Plus has 10,030 perturbed tasks across seven axes, and its overall success rate is pooled over all tasks.

Under this shared recipe, V-JEPA 2.1 ViT-L scores 97.25% on LIBERO and 79.25% on LIBERO-Plus. DINOv2 ViT-L scores 94.90% and 67.02%. The margin grows from 2.35 percentage points in-distribution to 12.23 under shifts. This result is limited to these tested representations; it does not show that predictive latents always outperform other visual foundations.

### 4. Keeping the predictive prior

The evidence suggests that a robot-control model can use future-oriented visual knowledge through learned features and future-informed context, without carrying a complete pretrained visual generator. The controlled encoder comparison and the no-future-loss ablation support this beyond the leaderboard comparison. The approach is competitive rather than best: ImageWAM scores higher on LIBERO-Plus. The claim holds for the tested representations, tasks, and training protocols.
