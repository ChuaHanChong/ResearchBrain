# EMOTION: Expressive Motion Sequence Generation for Humanoid Robots with In-Context Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> The EMOTION framework enables humanoid robots to generate expressive, continuous-valued motion sequences for non-verbal communication using large language models and in-context learning. Its EMOTION++ iteration, incorporating natural language human feedback, produced gestures perceived as more natural and understandable than initial generations, sometimes even exceeding human-demonstrated motions.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots often lack the diverse and subtle human-like non-verbal communication crucial for natural and engaging Human-Robot Interaction (HRI).
- Traditional methods for generating expressive robot behaviors (e.g., manual crafting, pre-defined sequences) are resource-intensive, lack scalability, and struggle to adapt to varied social contexts.
- Existing LLM applications in robotics typically focus on high-level task planning or discrete skill orchestration, rather than direct, continuous-valued motion generation for expressive gestures.

## Method

- The **EMOTION** framework utilizes a ==Vision-Language Model (VLM)== for social context analysis and an LLM (GPT-4o) as a motion sequence generator to output 22-value continuous motion sequences.
- It employs in-context learning, providing the LLM with a small set of human-demonstrated motion sequences (e.g., 'idle' and 'wave') to guide generation.
- EMOTION++ introduces an iterative human-in-the-loop refinement process, where natural language feedback from a user is used by the LLM to improve generated motion sequences.

## Results

- EMOTION-generated behaviors were perceived as statistically comparable to human-demonstrated 'oracle' behaviors in terms of naturalness and understandability across all gestures.
- EMOTION++ (with human feedback) generated behaviors were rated significantly more natural and understandable than initial EMOTION generations, and significantly more understandable overall than human oracle behaviors.
- Qualitative feedback identified key factors influencing perception, such as the importance of correct hand and arm positioning, smooth movement patterns, precise finger poses, and appropriate gesture speed.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large Language Models (LLMs) can directly generate continuous-valued, fine-grained expressive motion sequences for humanoid robots from just a few in-context human demonstrations.
> - Natural language human feedback effectively refines LLM-generated robot gestures, significantly improving their perceived naturalness and understandability.
> - Specific robotic motion variables such as hand position, movement patterns, finger pose, and gesture speed critically influence human perception of robot expressiveness.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research sits at the intersection of Human-Robot Interaction (HRI), artificial intelligence (AI), and specifically, the burgeoning field of expressive robotics leveraging large language models (LLMs). The broader research landscape of HRI has long recognized the critical role of non-verbal communication—including facial expressions, gestures, and body movements—in fostering natural and effective human-robot interactions. Previous studies have demonstrated that robots capable of displaying human-like emotions and reactions can significantly improve user satisfaction, engagement, and the perception of a robot's intelligence and friendliness.

Historically, the generation of expressive robot behaviors has relied on labor-intensive methods. These include manual crafting by robot designers and animators, heuristic approaches to map human motions from video or demonstrations, and pre-defined sequences. While these methods allow for precise control, they are severely limited in their adaptability, diversity, and scalability for real-world, dynamic interactions. The sheer "unlimited number of diverse and subtle gestures" makes such approaches impractical for general-purpose humanoid robots.

More recently, generative models like Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) and Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) have been explored to create or modify expressive behaviors from smaller datasets. These models offered improvements in efficiency and adaptability compared to manual methods. However, a significant shift has occurred with the advent of large language models (LLMs) and vision-language models (VLMs). These models have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in contextual understanding, natural language processing, and sequence generation, making them a powerful new tool for generating complex, multimodal robot behaviors with minimal input.

EMOTION explicitly positions itself within this LLM-driven paradigm. The paper acknowledges existing LLM applications in HRI, such as conversational interactions and generating high-level control code (e.g., Mahadevan et al.'s GenEM). However, EMOTION distinguishes itself by focusing on the direct generation of *continuous-valued motion sequences* for complex, human-like gestures on a *humanoid platform*. Unlike GenEM, which uses LLMs to arrange pre-defined, high-level robot skills (like "move head" or "change light") primarily for a mobile robot with limited expressiveness, EMOTION directly outputs detailed hand and finger trajectories for a humanoid robot, allowing for "richer and more nuanced behaviors, combining hand gestures and body language." This represents a leap from orchestrating abstract skills to synthesizing fine-grained, embodied actions.

Furthermore, EMOTION stands apart from other LLM applications in robotics that focus on manipulation or locomotion policies, or those aimed at few-shot adaptation from single-task expert demonstrations. EMOTION is designed to generate *novel expressive sequences* for social HRI and uniquely explores the potential of *natural language human feedback* to refine these sequences iteratively. This human-in-the-loop aspect, particularly with natural language, is a critical differentiator, broadening the practical applications of LLMs in robotics by making behavior customization more intuitive and accessible.

In essence, EMOTION advances the state-of-the-art by pushing the boundaries of what LLMs can directly generate in terms of complex, continuous robot motions for expressive social interaction, addressing the limitations of prior methods in adaptability and human-likeness.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching motivation behind the EMOTION framework is to **enhance the ability of humanoid robots to engage in human-like non-verbal communication**, thereby fostering more natural and pleasant human-robot interactions. The authors identify a critical gap: despite advancements in robotic behaviors, existing methods often fall short in mimicking the diversity and subtlety of human non-verbal cues.

The specific objectives and motivations can be broken down as follows:

- **Addressing the Expressiveness Gap:** Human communication heavily relies on non-verbal cues, which can convey emotions and intentions more profoundly than verbal means alone. Integrating these capabilities into robots is crucial for improving user satisfaction and engagement. However, current robotic behaviors lack the adaptability and richness found in human non-verbal communication.
- **Overcoming Limitations of Traditional Methods:** Existing approaches, such as manually crafting motions or using pre-recorded trajectories, are resource-intensive, demand high levels of expertise, and struggle to generate the "unlimited number of diverse and subtle gestures" required for varied social contexts. The authors aim to develop a method that requires "minimal human efforts."
- **Leveraging In-Context Learning of LLMs:** The core objective is to harness the powerful contextual understanding and sequence generation capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to dynamically generate socially appropriate and expressive gesture motion sequences. This is envisioned as a pathway to produce varied, rich, and tailor-made sequences for specific interaction scenarios, making robots' behaviors feel more natural and understandable.
- **Enabling Contextually Appropriate and Dynamic Responses:** The goal is for humanoid robots to interpret human social cues and respond with natural and engaging gestures that are appropriate for the social environment and interaction goals.
- **Investigating Human Feedback for Refinement:** A key objective is to explore the utility of natural language human feedback in iteratively improving the quality of generated gestures. This leads to the EMOTION++ version of the framework, aiming to demonstrate how human intuition can guide and refine AI-generated behaviors.
- **Evaluating Perceived Naturalness and Understandability:** Through rigorous user studies, the authors aim to quantitatively and qualitatively assess how well the LLM-generated behaviors (both with and without human feedback) are perceived by human users in terms of naturalness and understandability, comparing them against human-performed "oracle" gestures.
- **Deriving Design Implications:** Beyond demonstrating a functional system, the research aims to extract valuable insights and design implications for future research and development in expressive robotic gesture generation, identifying specific robotic variables that influence human perception.

In essence, the research is motivated by the desire to make robots more effective and relatable social partners by granting them sophisticated non-verbal communication abilities, achieved through the novel application and refinement of advanced AI models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The EMOTION framework is designed to generate continuous-valued motion sequences for humanoid robots, integrating large language models (LLMs) and vision-language models (VLMs) with human feedback.

**A. EMOTION Framework Overview:**
The system takes user inputs, which can be a robot image observation ($o_{in}$) and/or a language instruction ($l_{in}$), and outputs a continuous-valued motion sequence $\tau = \{s_1, \dots, s_T\}$. In this work, the sequence length $T$ is fixed at 10 timesteps. The framework also supports iterative human feedback to refine the generated motions.

**B. Motion Sequence Representation:**
Each state $s_t$ in a motion sequence $\tau$ is represented by 22 real values. These values parameterize the motion of both left and right hands using:
- **Cartesian Position (3D):** $(x, y, z)$ coordinates for each hand.
- **Orientation (3D):** Euler angles for each hand.
- **Finger State (5D):** A binary or continuous value (0-1) for the opening/closing of each of the five fingers.
This detailed, continuous representation allows for fine-grained control over hand and finger movements, crucial for nuanced gestures.

**C. Social Context Analysis (VLM Agent $f_1$):**
This is the initial step where the system interprets the social context.
- **Input:** Robot image observation ($o_{in}$) and/or user language instruction ($l_{in}$).
- **Process:** A VLM agent ($f_1$) is used with Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting. CoT prompting encourages the model to break down complex problems into intermediate reasoning steps, improving the quality of the output.
- **Output:** The VLM generates a social context analysis ($l_a$) and a suggested gesture $\langle g \rangle$. For example, observing a person solving a math problem might lead to the analysis "I see a person excitingly solving a complicated math problem... I would communicate approval" and the gesture "⟨thumbs-up⟩". The paper notes that this social context analysis capability has been explored in existing work, and for the user study, a fixed set of gestures was used to reduce randomness.

**D. Human Demonstrations (D):**
The framework incorporates a small set of human demonstrations as in-context learning examples for the LLM.
- **Content:** The authors found that just two human demonstrations—"idle" and "right-hand wave"—were sufficient. Each demonstration includes the gesture name ($\langle g_{demo} \rangle$), a one-line description ($l_{demo}$), and the corresponding motion sequence ($\tau_{demo}$).
- **Collection:** Human demonstrations were collected using Apple Vision Pro, and then transformed into the same 22-value motion sequence representation required by the LLMs. This standardization is key for the LLM's ability to learn from and generate in the specified format.

**E. LLM as a Motion Sequence Generator (LLM Agent $f_2$):**
This is the core generation component of the EMOTION framework.
- **Input:** The gesture $\langle g \rangle$ identified by $f_1$, along with instructions on coordinate definition, motion sequence representation, and CoT prompts.
- **Process:** An LLM agent ($f_2$) takes these inputs and, conditioned on the human demonstrations (D), generates the initial motion sequence $\tau^{(1)}$.
- **Model:** OpenAI's GPT-4o (specifically, `gpt-4o-2024-05-13`) APIs are used for text completion, meaning the LLM generates the sequence as structured text that is then parsed into numerical motion data.

**F. Iterative Improvements from Human Feedback (LLM Agent $f_3$ for EMOTION++):**
This component enables the EMOTION++ version, which incorporates human feedback.
- **Input:** The history of previously generated motion sequences $\tau^{(i)}$ and natural language human feedback $l_f^{(i)}$.
- **Process:** A third LLM agent ($f_3$) takes this historical context and feedback to generate an improved motion sequence $\tau^{(i+1)}$.
- **Feedback Nature:** Human feedback ($l_f$) can be either explicit modifications (e.g., "put your hands higher") or high-level suggestions (e.g., "add some random motion").
- **Iteration:** For the study, a maximum of 5 feedback iterations ($i_{max}=5$) were allowed.

**G. Robot Execution:**
Once a motion sequence is generated, it is executed on the physical humanoid robot using standard robotics techniques:
- **Inverse Kinematics (IK):** Converts the desired end-effector (hand) poses into robot joint angles.
- **Trajectory Interpolation:** Smoothly fills in the motion path between the discrete timesteps provided by the LLM.
- **Trajectory Tracking:** Ensures the robot's joints follow the interpolated trajectory accurately.

**H. User Study Design:**
To evaluate the framework, an online within-subject user study was conducted.
- **Research Questions:**
 - RQ1: How do EMOTION-generated behaviors compare to human oracle behaviors?
 - RQ2: How much does human feedback (EMOTION++) improve perception compared to EMOTION?
- **Hypotheses:**
 - H1: EMOTION behaviors will not significantly differ from human oracle behaviors.
 - H2: EMOTION++ behaviors will be perceived as more natural and understandable than EMOTION.
- **Experiment Materials:**
 - **Robot:** GR-1 humanoid robot from Fourier.
 - **Gestures:** 10 expressive gestures covering four categories: emblems ("thumbs-up", "okay", "v-sign"), illustrators ("air-quotes", "come-closer"), affect displays ("fist-pump", "jazz-hands", "spread-hands"), and regulators ("stop", "listening"). These were either generated by models or recorded from a human operator, then verified and selected.
 - **Video Recording:** Videos of the robot performing these gestures were recorded, minimizing differences in lighting and background.
- **Survey Procedure:**
 - Participants watched videos of gestures across three conditions: Human oracle, EMOTION, and EMOTION++, presented in a randomized order.
 - **Measures:** After each video, participants rated "Naturalness" and "Understandability" on 7-point Likert scales. Optional qualitative feedback was collected.
 - **Context:** A video of a human performing the gesture and a description was provided for context before evaluation.
 - **Demographics:** Collected information on age, gender, ethnicity, attitudes toward robots, and gesture use.
- **Participants:** 22 valid participants (from an initial 30 recruited internally at Apple), filtered for completion time.

This comprehensive methodology allows for both a technical validation of LLMs in motion generation and a user-centered evaluation of the perceived quality of the generated expressive behaviors.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study's findings are presented quantitatively, based on user ratings, and qualitatively, derived from participant comments.

**A. Quantitative Results:**

- **EMOTION vs. Human Oracle (H1 Test):**
 - **H1 Supported:** When combining all gestures, EMOTION-generated behaviors were **not statistically different** from human oracle behaviors in terms of perceived naturalness (p=0.267) and understandability (p=0.528). This is a significant finding, suggesting that LLMs can generate gestures that are on par with human-demonstrated ones without explicit human refinement.

- **EMOTION++ vs. EMOTION (H2 Test):**
 - **H2 Supported:** Combining all gestures, EMOTION++ (with human feedback) was rated **significantly more natural** (p=0.0014) and **more understandable** (p=0.019) than EMOTION (without human feedback). This clearly demonstrates the value of incorporating human feedback for refining LLM-generated motions.
 - **Individual Gesture Improvements:** Specific gestures like "fist pump," "jazz hand," and "okay" (for naturalness only) showed significant improvements with EMOTION++.

- **EMOTION++ vs. Human Oracle:**
 - **Overall Superiority in Understandability:** When combining all gestures, EMOTION++ behaviors received **significantly higher ratings for understandability** (p=0.003) than human oracle behaviors. Perceived naturalness for EMOTION++ was also higher but not statistically significant. This suggests that EMOTION++, guided by human intuition, can sometimes produce more clearly interpretable robot gestures than a single human demonstration.
 - **Gesture-Specific Performance:** EMOTION++ generated behaviors for "air quotes," "come closer," and "thumbs-up" were perceived as significantly more natural than human oracle ones, with "air quotes" also being significantly more understandable. Conversely, the human oracle "v-sign" was significantly better in both naturalness and understandability than EMOTION++. This highlights that performance varies across different gestures, and fine-tuning may still be needed for certain complex expressions.

- **Influence of Participant Background:**
 - **Empathy:** Participants' self-rated empathy levels were significantly positively correlated with perceived understandability and naturalness across all conditions (Oracle, EMOTION, EMOTION++). Higher empathy meant higher ratings. This aligns with psychological literature on narrative comprehension.
 - **Age:** Participants' age was negatively correlated with the perceived naturalness of the behaviors (significant for Oracle and EMOTION, borderline for EMOTION++). Older participants tended to rate naturalness lower.
 - No significant correlations were found for other demographic variables like gender or general attitudes toward robots.

**B. Qualitative Results (Thematic Analysis):**

Qualitative feedback provided deep insights into the factors influencing perception, categorized into three main themes:

1. **Robot Variables:**
 - **Position of Hands:** Crucial for interpretation and naturalness. Participants preferred higher hand positions for visibility, and hands closer to ears for "listening." Incorrect positions (e.g., "listening" resembling "surrender") led to confusion. Hand orientation (e.g., upward-facing for "spread-hands") and two-hand configuration (distance, symmetry, synchronization) also mattered.
 - **Movement Pattern:** Jerkiness, trajectory path, and scale significantly impacted naturalness. "Shortest and most direct path" (human-like) was generally preferred. However, subtle movements (e.g., hand shaking for "fist pump," emphasis at trajectory end for "thumbs-up") were appreciated for enhancing expressiveness in some gestures. Timing and coordination of finger and hand movements were also noted (e.g., fingers forming shape *after* hand reaches ideal position).
 - **Arm and Shoulder Articulation:** Important for "human-like" gestures. For "come closer," arm motions alongside fingers were generally liked as more "human-like," though some found them "too much." Arm extension angle was critical for clarity (e.g., "stop").
 - **Finger Pose:** Critically important, especially for emblematic gestures like "okay" (requiring finger contact to form "O") and "v-sign" (requiring distinct "v" shape). Hardware limitations in finger dexterity were noted, leading to unnatural or misinterpreted poses (e.g., "v-sign" resembling "smoking cigarettes").
 - **Speed:** A common critique was that robot gestures were generally much slower than human gestures, negatively affecting perceived naturalness, particularly for "air quotes."

2. **Human Perception:**
 - Participants provided direct comments on naturalness ("human-like," "awkward") and understandability (whether the gesture was clear or misinterpreted as another, e.g., "listening" as "hugging").
 - Engagement: Human oracle gestures were often perceived as more engaging due to their movement patterns.
 - Emotion: Despite potential misinterpretation of the specific gesture, participants could perceive the underlying emotion (e.g., "jazz-hands" conveying happiness or dancing).

3. **Contextual Factors:**
 - Some participants noted issues with the human demonstration provided in the instructions, suggesting it could influence their perception of robot gestures (e.g., "spread hands" resembling "I don't know" more than "apology").
 - Gesture interpretation could depend on individual experience and context. Unfamiliar gestures (e.g., "jazz-hands") were harder to understand without context.

**C. Additional Experiments:**

- **Computation Time:** Average generation time for initial sequences was 26.8 seconds, and 21.2 seconds for a single round of human feedback. This is too long for real-time interaction, though the authors suggest mitigation through model distillation and local inference.
- **Human Feedback Analysis:** On average, 1.9 feedback iterations were provided per gesture for EMOTION++. 21% of feedback was high-level (e.g., "add some nuanced motion"), while the rest were explicit modifications (e.g., "make both hands lower").
- **Failure Mode:** The primary failure mode was the LLM generating motion sequences that were infeasible for the robot to track due to collision or kinematic limits, as LLMs are not inherently aware of the robot's workspace. This required regeneration or manual feedback.

Overall, the results strongly support the potential of LLMs for generating expressive robot motions, particularly when combined with intuitive natural language human feedback, demonstrating that such a system can achieve or even surpass human oracle performance in perception.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The EMOTION framework represents a significant step forward in the field of expressive Human-Robot Interaction, with far-reaching implications for how robots communicate and integrate into human society.

**A. Scientific and Technical Significance:**

- **Pioneering LLM Application for Embodied Expressiveness:** EMOTION pioneers the use of LLMs for direct generation of *continuous-valued, fine-grained motion sequences* for complex expressive gestures on a humanoid robot. This moves beyond previous LLM applications that focused on high-level task planning or discrete skill orchestration, demonstrating a deeper, more embodied form of AI-driven behavior generation.
- **Validation of In-Context Learning for Robotics:** The success of EMOTION, particularly with only two human demonstrations, underscores the powerful in-context learning capabilities of modern LLMs in synthesizing novel, complex robot trajectories. This suggests that LLMs can effectively generalize from minimal examples to produce a diverse range of behaviors.
- **Demonstration of Natural Language Human-in-the-Loop Control:** The EMOTION++ framework vividly illustrates the effectiveness of using natural language feedback to iteratively refine and improve AI-generated robot behaviors. This intuitive human-in-the-loop approach lowers the barrier for non-experts to customize and fine-tune robot expressiveness, making robot programming more accessible and user-friendly.
- **Matching or Surpassing Human Oracle Performance:** The quantitative results showing EMOTION matching and EMOTION++ surpassing human oracle performance in perceived understandability and naturalness (for many gestures) is a robust validation of the proposed approach. It suggests that AI-driven synthesis can achieve a level of communicative clarity and human-likeness comparable to, or even exceeding, a single human's demonstration.
- **Empirical Insights into Expressive Gesture Design:** The detailed qualitative analysis provides invaluable empirical insights into which specific robotic variables (hand position, movement patterns, arm/shoulder articulation, finger poses, speed) critically influence human perception of robot expressiveness. These findings offer concrete "design implications" for future research, guiding the creation of more effective generative models and more natural robot designs.

**B. Potential Impact:**

- **More Natural and Engaging Human-Robot Interaction:** By enabling robots to communicate more effectively through non-verbal cues, EMOTION can make HRI significantly more natural, intuitive, and pleasant. This is crucial for applications in social robotics, customer service, education, healthcare, and even personal companionship, where robots need to build rapport and convey empathy.
- **Reduced Development Effort for Robotic Behaviors:** The ability to generate complex, expressive motions with minimal human effort (e.g., via a few demonstrations and natural language feedback) drastically reduces the traditional burden on animators and robot programmers. This can accelerate the deployment of socially capable robots in diverse real-world settings.
- **Democratization of Robot Programming:** The intuitive natural language interface for behavior refinement could allow a wider range of users, even those without robotics expertise, to customize robot gestures, fostering greater creativity and personalization in HRI.
- **Foundation for Adaptive Embodied AI:** EMOTION lays groundwork for future embodied AI systems that can dynamically adapt their non-verbal communication based on real-time social context and user feedback. This moves towards truly intelligent and socially aware robots capable of nuanced, context-dependent interactions.
- **Informing Hardware Design and Perception Studies:** The qualitative findings, particularly those identifying hardware limitations (e.g., finger dexterity) and the impact of physical parameters on perception, can inform the design of future humanoid robots, emphasizing features that enhance expressive capabilities. Additionally, the correlation between human empathy/age and perception highlights the importance of considering user demographics in evaluating and designing HRI experiences.

While the current computation time poses a challenge for real-time applications, and hardware constraints still limit some expressions, EMOTION demonstrates a powerful paradigm shift in generating expressive robot behaviors. Its contributions pave the way for a future where robots are not just functional tools, but truly effective and empathetic social agents.
