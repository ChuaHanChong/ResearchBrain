# HuB: Learning Extreme Humanoid Balance

> [!summary] Summary
> HuB, a unified learning-based framework, enables humanoid robots to achieve extreme quasi-static balance, a challenging task for existing methods. The system integrates refined reference motions, balance-aware policy learning, and sim-to-real robustness training, demonstrating the Unitree G1 robot's ability to stably perform tasks like "Swallow Balance" and "Bruce Lee’s Kick" with resilience to disturbances.

## Problem

- Existing learning-based humanoid control paradigms struggle with sustained quasi-static balance under extreme conditions, often prioritizing locomotion over precise, stable static poses.
- Reference motions derived from human data often contain inaccuracies (e.g., foot sliding) and morphological mismatches, which lead to instability when directly tracked by a robot.
- The sim-to-real gap, particularly due to sensor noise and unmodeled contact dynamics, severely compromises the stability and reliability of learned balance policies on real hardware.

## Method

- ==Reference Motion Refinement==: Employs SMPL-initialized retargeting, grounded foot correction, Center of Mass (COM) filtering, and transition stabilization to generate high-quality, physically feasible reference motions.
- Balance-Aware Policy Learning: Utilizes relaxed reference tracking with a specified tolerance and tailored balance shaping rewards (COM, foot contact mismatch, close feet penalties) to guide the policy toward stable, robot-specific behaviors.
- Sim-to-Real Robustness Training: Incorporates localized reference tracking, IMU-centric observation perturbation with correlated Ornstein-Uhlenbeck noise, and high-frequency push disturbances to enhance real-world policy robustness.

## Results

- HuB achieved a **100%** success rate on challenging quasi-static balance tasks (e.g., "Swallow Balance", "Bruce Lee’s Kick") in simulation, even under external pushes and IMU noise, while baseline methods consistently failed.
- The framework successfully transferred to the real Unitree G1 humanoid robot, enabling stable and fluid execution of extreme poses and demonstrating robust recovery from forceful external impacts.
- Ablation studies confirmed that each component of HuB (motion refinement, balance-aware rewards, and sim-to-real robustness training) individually and synergistically contributed to superior performance and stability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A multi-faceted approach simultaneously addressing reference motion quality, policy learning objectives, and sim-to-real transfer is essential for robust extreme humanoid balance.
> - Allowing the policy a degree of flexibility in tracking reference motions enables it to discover more stable, robot-specific balance configurations than strict imitation.
> - Designing explicit balance-shaping rewards and realistic sensor noise modeling during training significantly improves policy performance and robustness on real hardware.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of humanoid robots capable of emulating human-like versatility, agility, and robustness has been a long-standing grand challenge in robotics. A crucial aspect of this challenge is achieving sophisticated balance control, especially in extreme or dynamic scenarios. The "HuB" paper positions itself squarely within this landscape, specifically targeting the gap in achieving **sustained quasi-static balance under extreme conditions** for humanoids.

**Traditional approaches** to humanoid balance control often rely on model-based methods, such as Zero-Moment Point (ZMP) control, Capture Point (CP) control, and various optimization-based strategies. While these methods have achieved success in structured environments and for stable locomotion, they are highly dependent on accurate dynamics models and often struggle with uncertainty, external perturbations, or highly dynamic and complex poses.

In **recent years, learning-based approaches**, particularly reinforcement learning (RL), have revolutionized robot control, demonstrating impressive capabilities in dynamic locomotion, agile behaviors (running, jumping, dancing, parkour), and even loco-manipulation for humanoids. A common paradigm involves training RL policies to track human motion data, often obtained from motion capture systems and then retargeted to the robot's morphology. Examples include works like H2O and OmniH2O, which the authors use as baselines. These methods excel at skill acquisition and diverse motion generation.

However, the authors identify a critical limitation: while these learning-based methods are adept at dynamic tasks and motion tracking, they often **fall short when applied to precise, sustained balance-intensive tasks** that require intricate whole-body coordination, especially in **extreme quasi-static poses** (e.g., balancing on one foot with a horizontally extended torso). The paper explicitly states that prior works primarily focus on "locomotion or transient stabilization rather than sustained quasi-static balance under extreme conditions." This is where HuB makes its significant contribution.

Furthermore, the **sim-to-real transfer gap** remains a fundamental hurdle in robot learning. While various techniques like domain randomization, system identification, and real-to-sim feedback have been developed, their effectiveness in the highly sensitive domain of humanoid quasi-static balance, where even minor sensor noise or contact inconsistencies can lead to catastrophic failure, is largely "underexplored." HuB directly addresses this by proposing balance-specific robustness training.

In summary, HuB advances the field by bridging the gap between general learning-based motion tracking and the specialized requirements of extreme, sustained humanoid balance. It provides a comprehensive framework that tackles the specific challenges preventing humanoids from achieving human-like balance capabilities in highly demanding, static or near-static poses.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation behind the "HuB" project stems from the remarkable motor capabilities of the human body, particularly its ability to perform highly precise and stable balance tasks, such as standing on one foot or executing high kicks. Replicating these "exceptional motor capabilities" in humanoid robots is a long-standing aspiration in robotics research, yet it presents significant challenges.

The paper identifies three key obstacles that hinder the application of existing reinforcement learning-based humanoid control paradigms to these complex, balance-intensive tasks:

1. **Instability from Reference Motion Errors:** Current methods often rely on tracking human motions derived from motion capture data. However, video-based motion capture can introduce inaccuracies, and marker-based systems, while more precise, are impractical for diverse scenarios. Moreover, the process of retargeting these human poses to a robot's specific morphology, often through optimization, can further degrade motion quality, leading to physically implausible artifacts like foot sliding, which are detrimental to demanding balance tasks.
2. **Learning Difficulties Due to Morphological Mismatch:** Even with accurate reference data, directly tracking human motions can be problematic for a robot. Humanoids have different masses, inertia distributions, and joint limits compared to humans. Consequently, strictly mimicking a human pose may not result in a stable equilibrium for the robot, as its center of mass (COM) might not align with a stable configuration given its unique dynamics. This mismatch makes it difficult for a policy to inherently learn stable balance behaviors if it is too rigidly constrained to the human reference.
3. **Sim-to-Real Gap Caused by Sensor Noise and Unmodeled Dynamics:** Training robot control policies in simulation offers efficiency and safety, but deploying them on real hardware often encounters the "sim-to-real gap." In the context of balance, this gap is particularly problematic. Real-world sensors (e.g., IMUs, VIO) are noisy, leading to inaccurate policy inputs and potentially jittery, unstable actions. Furthermore, unmodeled real-world dynamics, especially concerning ground contact and friction, can cause subtle oscillations that amplify over time, leading to instability and falls, even when the policy performs well in simulation.

**The overarching objective of HuB is to propose a unified framework that systematically addresses these three interconnected challenges.** The framework aims to enable humanoid robots, specifically the Unitree G1, to perform extreme quasi-static balance tasks with high stability and robustness, even under external disturbances. This involves developing specific solutions for each identified problem, integrating them into a coherent learning pipeline, and demonstrating their efficacy in both simulation and the real world.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

HuB (Humanoid Balance) is a unified learning-based framework designed to enable humanoid robots to perform extreme quasi-static balance tasks by systematically tackling the identified challenges. The approach integrates three main components: Reference Motion Refinement, Balance-Aware Policy Learning, and Sim-to-Real Robustness Training.

#### 3.1. Background and Overall Pipeline

The framework adopts a tracking-based control paradigm, formulating the balance task as a goal-conditioned Reinforcement Learning (RL) problem (Markov Decision Process). The robot's policy outputs desired joint angles, which are executed by a low-level Proportional-Derivative (PD) controller. A reward function encourages accurate tracking and stable control.

The overall pipeline involves:
1. **Motion Capture:** Video clips are used to extract human poses (e.g., using WHAM in SMPL format).
2. **Retargeting:** These human poses are retargeted to humanoid-specific reference motions.
3. **Policy Training:** A teacher-student learning paradigm is employed. A teacher policy is trained using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) with privileged observations in simulation (IsaacGym). A student policy is then distilled from the teacher using DAgger, utilizing only onboard observations accessible during deployment.
4. **Deployment:** The final student policy is deployed on the real Unitree G1 humanoid robot.

#### 3.2. Reference Motion Refinement

To counter the "Challenges due to Reference Motion Errors," HuB introduces several motion refinement strategies:

- **SMPL-Initialized Retargeting:** Instead of initializing joint angles to a zero pose during optimization-based retargeting, HuB initializes them using corresponding Euler angles from the human SMPL pose. This provides a closer starting point to the optimal solution, accelerating convergence and improving retargeting accuracy and feasibility for challenging poses.
- **Grounded Foot Correction:** For single-legged phases, the global root position is adjusted (keeping local joint angles constant) to ensure the support foot remains static across consecutive frames. This eliminates unrealistic foot-sliding artifacts from noisy pose estimation and imperfect retargeting, crucial for stable balance.
- **Center-of-Mass Filtering:** Trajectories where the ground-projected Center of Mass (COM) deviates significantly (more than 0.2 m) from the center of the support foot are discarded. This ensures that only physically feasible reference trajectories, considering the robot's URDF-defined mass properties, are used for training.
- **Transition Stabilization:** The double-support phase at the beginning and end of the reference motion is extended by duplicating initial and final frames. This provides the policy with more training time in stable double-foot stance and allows the real robot more time to settle before transitioning into extreme balance motions.

#### 3.3. Balance-Aware Policy Learning

To address "Challenges in Training Balance Policies" stemming from morphological mismatch and the need for structured balance guidance, HuB employs:

- **Relaxed Reference Tracking:** Instead of strict tracking, the policy is given a relatively large tolerance (σ = 0.6 m) in the reward function. This flexibility allows the RL policy to explore deviations from the reference motion that lead to more stable behaviors specifically suited to the humanoid's own dynamics and COM, rather than rigidly adhering to potentially unstable human-derived poses.
- **Balance Shaping Rewards:** A set of tailored rewards guides the policy towards physically plausible and stable balance:
 - **Center of Mass (COM) reward:** Encourages the vertical projection of the COM to remain within the support polygon, explicitly teaching the robot to adjust its posture for balance.
 - **Foot Contact Mismatch Penalty:** Penalizes discrepancies between the robot's and the reference's foot contact states. This is critical for single-leg tasks, preventing unintended ground contact by the non-supporting foot, which would violate task constraints.
 - **Close Feet Penalty:** Prevents the robot's feet from coming too close to each other, reducing collision risks and encouraging more stable lower-body stances.

#### 3.4. Sim-to-Real Robustness Training

To overcome the "Challenges in Sim-to-Real Transfer" due to sensor noise and unmodeled dynamics, HuB implements:

- **Localized Reference Tracking Training:** Odometry information (VIO) is discarded during both student policy training and deployment. All tracking targets are expressed in the robot's local coordinate frame, aligned with its current root pose. This ensures consistency between training and deployment and mitigates the adverse effects of noisy VIO in real-world scenarios.
- **IMU-Centric Observation Perturbation:** Instead of uniform noise, HuB perturbs the observed root orientation (Euler angles) with temporally correlated Ornstein-Uhlenbeck (OU) noise during student training. All other observations (e.g., projected gravity, localized angular velocity) are then computed based on this perturbed orientation. This models realistic IMU noise characteristics, including temporal dependencies and structural coupling, leading to more robust policies.
- **High-Frequency Push Disturbance:** During teacher policy training, small, high-frequency external velocity offsets are applied to the robot's root (pushes every 1s at up to 0.5 m/s). This contrasts with prior work using low-frequency, high-magnitude pushes, which are often too severe for precise balance tasks. These high-frequency, smaller pushes better approximate real-world jitter and unmodeled dynamics, enhancing the policy's resilience to subtle instabilities critical for balance tasks.

The developed policies are evaluated on the **Unitree G1 humanoid robot** using the **IsaacGym** simulation environment, with performance compared against adapted H2O and OmniH2O baselines.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted for "HuB: Learning Extreme Humanoid Balance" provide compelling evidence for the framework's effectiveness in enabling humanoid robots to achieve extreme balance tasks with high stability and robustness.

#### 4.1. Simulation Results

- **Superior Performance on Extreme Balance Tasks:** HuB achieved a **100% success rate** on challenging quasi-static balance tasks like "Swallow Balance" and "Bruce Lee’s Kick" in simulation, even under introduced external pushes and IMU noise. In stark contrast, baseline methods (H2O and OmniH2O) consistently failed these tasks, primarily due to contact mismatches (e.g., the non-supporting foot touching the ground) or falling.
- **Enhanced Stability and Smoothness:** HuB demonstrated superior stability metrics, exhibiting significantly lower ground slippage, shorter airborne durations, and reduced action variability (lower action rates) compared to baselines. This indicates smoother, more controlled, and stable motion execution.
- **Lower Tracking Errors:** Despite relaxed tracking objectives, HuB achieved lower overall tracking errors in position, velocity, and acceleration, demonstrating that stability was not achieved at the expense of task fidelity.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies

Detailed ablation studies confirmed the critical contribution of each component to HuB's overall performance:

- **Relaxed Tracking:** Experiments with varying tracking tolerance (σ) showed that an optimal moderately large tolerance (σ = 0.6 m) was essential. Too small a tolerance led to increased slippage and action rates (instability), while too large a tolerance degraded tracking accuracy and increased contact mismatches, ultimately reducing success rates.
- **Balance Shaping Rewards:**
 - Removing the **Center of Mass (COM) reward** led to increased slippage and airborne time, highlighting its importance for maintaining balance.
 - Eliminating the **Foot Contact Mismatch Penalty** resulted in a significant drop in success rates and a rise in unintended ground contacts, proving its necessity for successful single-foot balancing.
 - Removing the **Close Feet Penalty** degraded performance by allowing feet to come too close, reducing overall standing stability.
- **Sim-to-Real Robustness Training:**
 - Training without **Localized Reference Tracking** (reverting to global tracking during training) introduced a mismatch between training and deployment, severely degrading performance, particularly for tasks dependent on precise preceding motions.
 - Omitting **IMU-Centric Observation Perturbation** led to a deterioration across all metrics, showing policies not exposed to realistic sensor noise during training are highly sensitive to real-world deployment errors. Further analysis (Appendix C.2, Table 9) confirmed that coupled Ornstein-Uhlenbeck (OU) noise, modeling temporal correlations and structural dependencies, performed best compared to independent or uniform noise variants.
 - Removing **High-Frequency Push Disturbances** during training increased contact mismatches, lowered success rates, and degraded stability, underscoring the importance of this type of perturbation for robustness. Replacing them with low-frequency, high-magnitude pushes (common in prior work) also degraded stability, indicating that task-specific perturbation strategies are crucial for balance tasks with narrow feasible regions.
- **Retargeting Results:** SMPL-initialized retargeting consistently achieved lower retargeting losses across all tasks compared to optimization without this initialization, confirming its effectiveness in generating higher quality reference motions.

#### 4.3. Real-World Results

The policies trained with HuB successfully transferred to the real Unitree G1 humanoid robot:

- **Successful Execution of Challenging Poses:** HuB enabled the robot to perform highly challenging tasks such as "Swallow Balance," "Bruce Lee’s Kick," "Ne Zha Pose," and "Single-Leg Stand" with stability and fluidity. The robot maintained steady balance with only minor, necessary foot adjustments.
- **Baselines' Real-World Failures:** In contrast, the baseline OmniH2O struggled or failed on these real-world tasks, exhibiting excessive shaking, falling, or abandoning the intended single-leg motions to avoid falling.
- **Robustness to External Perturbations:** HuB demonstrated strong disturbance tolerance. When struck by a forcefully kicked soccer ball, the humanoid rapidly reacted and recovered with minimal corrective motion, quickly returning to a stable state.
- **Long-Horizon Task Consistency:** HuB successfully completed 10 consecutive executions of the "Bruce Lee’s Kick" task in a single continuous rollout without any resets or external intervention. This highlights the framework's reliability and consistent balance control in real-world deployment.

In conclusion, the experimental results rigorously validate HuB's comprehensive approach, confirming that each component contributes significantly to achieving robust and stable extreme humanoid balance, outperforming existing tracking-based methods in both simulated and real-world scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "HuB: Learning Extreme Humanoid Balance" paper marks a substantial advancement in the field of humanoid robotics, particularly in extending the capabilities of learning-based control to highly demanding balance tasks. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Pioneering Extreme Quasi-Static Balance:** HuB addresses a critical gap in current humanoid control research. While much work has focused on dynamic locomotion and agile movements, achieving sustained, precise quasi-static balance in extreme human-like poses has remained a significant challenge. By enabling tasks like "Swallow Balance" and "Bruce Lee's Kick," HuB pushes the boundaries of what humanoids can stably achieve, moving closer to human-level dexterity and control.
- **Comprehensive and Unified Framework:** The strength of HuB lies in its holistic approach. Instead of addressing individual problems in isolation, it provides a unified framework that systematically tackles three intertwined challenges: reference motion quality, policy learning difficulties due to morphological differences, and the sim-to-real gap. This integrated strategy offers a more robust and effective solution compared to piecemeal approaches.
- **Enhanced Real-World Robustness and Reliability:** The demonstrated robustness against strong external perturbations (e.g., soccer ball strikes) and the ability to perform long-horizon, consecutive executions without failure are crucial for practical robot deployment. This reliability makes humanoids more viable for real-world applications where unexpected disturbances are common and consistent performance is essential.
- **Valuable Technical Contributions:** Beyond the overall framework, HuB introduces several specific technical innovations that are independently valuable:
 - **SMPL-Initialized Retargeting:** A practical improvement for generating higher-quality, physically plausible reference motions from human data.
 - **Balance Shaping Rewards:** A set of domain-specific rewards that effectively guide RL policies towards stable, physically grounded behaviors, which can inform future reward design for other complex robot tasks.
 - **Localized Reference Tracking:** A robust strategy for dealing with noisy odometry, ensuring consistent performance between simulation and reality.
 - **IMU-Centric Observation Perturbation:** A more realistic approach to modeling sensor noise in simulation, crucial for developing policies that are truly robust to real-world sensory input.
 - **High-Frequency Push Disturbances:** A refined domain randomization technique that better captures the subtle instabilities relevant to precise balance, offering a valuable lesson for sim-to-real transfer in sensitive control tasks.
- **Foundation for Future Humanoid Dexterity:** By mastering extreme balance, HuB lays a foundational capability for future advancements in humanoid robotics. Complex manipulation, interaction with dynamic environments, and versatile agile locomotion all depend on a robot's ability to maintain exquisite balance. This work contributes directly to the long-term vision of humanoids acting as capable assistants or partners in unstructured human environments.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
The authors acknowledge certain limitations, such as the task-specific design of some components and the limited generalization to substantially different motions without retraining. This points to future research directions focused on developing more versatile motor skills, hierarchical control, or meta-learning approaches that allow for faster adaptation to novel tasks and environments, further enhancing the autonomy and intelligence of humanoid robots.

Overall, HuB represents a significant step forward in humanoid control, showcasing how a carefully designed, multi-faceted learning framework can empower robots to achieve motor skills previously thought to be beyond their grasp, with direct implications for real-world applications and the ongoing pursuit of human-level robot capabilities.
