# COSMO: Combination of Selective Memorization for Low-cost Vision-and-Language Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> A new vision-and-language navigation (VLN) framework, COSMO, integrates customized selective State Space Models with Transformers to overcome the high computational costs and limitations with long instructions faced by existing VLN models. It improves navigation performance, exemplified by a 3.83% higher Success Rate on REVERIE validation unseen, while drastically cutting computational demands by reducing parameters by 85% and FLOPs by over 90% compared to a strong Transformer baseline.

## Problem

- Current state-of-the-art Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) models, largely Transformer-based, incur high computational costs (parameters and FLOPs) due to complex architectures, limiting deployment on resource-constrained devices.
- Existing VLN models often show degraded performance when handling lengthy and complex natural language instructions, struggling to selectively process relevant information.
- General State Space Models (SSMs) struggle with critical VLN requirements such as intricate spatial relationship modeling among visual observations, robust cross-modal interaction, and explicit input selection for dynamic action decisions.

## Method

- **COSMO** proposes a hybrid architecture that strategically integrates VLN-customized selective ==State Space Models (SSMs)== for efficient selective memorization and Transformer modules for precise action decision-making.
- It introduces the Round Selective Scan (RSS) for efficient, non-causal spatial relationship modeling within panoramic visual observations, addressing the limitations of traditional multi-directional SSMs.
- It develops the Cross-modal Selective State Space Module (CS3), a dual-stream SSM designed for robust cross-modal interaction and mutual selective filtering between visual and linguistic inputs, even with disparate sequence lengths.

## Results

- COSMO achieved competitive or superior navigation performance on REVERIE, R2R, and R2R-CE benchmarks, demonstrating a **3.83%** SR improvement over DUET on REVERIE validation unseen.
- The framework significantly reduced computational costs, utilizing only **15%** of DUET's parameters (28M vs 181M) and **9.3%** of its FLOPs (**0.46** GFLOPs vs **4.95** GFLOPs), while also improving inference and training speed.
- COSMO demonstrated enhanced robustness for long instructions, showing a **7.42%** higher SR than DUET for instructions exceeding 30 words on REVERIE, validating its selective memorization capabilities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A hybrid architecture combining the memory-filtering capabilities of selective SSMs with the contextual selection strengths of Transformers can effectively balance high performance and low computational cost in VLN.
> - SSMs can be successfully adapted for complex multimodal tasks like VLN by designing specialized modules (RSS for spatial, CS3 for cross-modal) that address their inherent limitations in non-linear sequences and multi-modal interactions.
> - Employing selective memorization through customized SSMs allows the agent to filter out irrelevant information, particularly benefiting navigation performance for lengthy and complex instructions where traditional Transformer-based models often struggle.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) is a core task in artificial intelligence, aiming to enable robots to understand natural language instructions and navigate real-world environments. Early VLN methods primarily utilized recurrent neural networks (RNNs) to compress navigation history. However, this approach often led to information loss due to fixed-size feature vectors.

The field progressed with the adoption of Transformer-based architectures, which can retain historical observations in variable-length sequences. Models like DUET integrated topological maps and dual-scale Transformer architectures to enhance performance. Subsequent advancements in VLN research have focused on incorporating additional components, such as external knowledge bases, depth information, or sophisticated map representations, to further boost navigation accuracy. While these additions have improved performance, they have simultaneously led to significantly larger models and increased computational costs, which can be a limitation for deployment in resource-constrained applications like home assistant robots. Furthermore, Transformer-based models have demonstrated a decline in performance when confronted with increasingly lengthy and complex instructions.

Concurrently, State Space Models (SSMs), particularly selective SSMs like Mamba, have gained attention across various disciplines for their linear computational complexity and robust capabilities in modeling long sequences. This makes them a potential alternative to Transformers for tasks requiring efficient long-range dependency capture. However, SSMs are inherently designed for single 1-D sequence modeling. This design poses challenges for VLN tasks, which require intricate spatial relationship modeling among visual observations and effective cross-modal attention between visual and linguistic inputs. Additionally, SSMs have been noted to perform suboptimally in tasks requiring explicit input selection, a crucial aspect of dynamic action decision-making in VLN. Prior attempts to directly apply Mamba to VLN tasks have resulted in performance degradation.

This work, COSMO, positions itself within this landscape by seeking to address the trade-off between performance and computational cost in VLN. It aims to leverage the efficiency of SSMs while overcoming their limitations in spatial and cross-modal reasoning, thereby providing a low-cost, high-performance solution for navigation, particularly for tasks involving lengthy instructions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) agent that simultaneously achieves high navigation performance and low computational costs. This objective is motivated by several identified shortcomings in existing VLN methodologies:

1. **High Computational Costs of Current SOTA Models:** Contemporary VLN approaches, predominantly based on Transformer architectures, have increasingly integrated complex components such as external knowledge bases or detailed map information to enhance navigation performance. While effective, these additions lead to larger model sizes and significantly increased computational overhead (parameters and FLOPs). This makes them less practical for real-world deployment on devices with limited computational resources, such as home assistant robots. The researchers aim to mitigate this computational burden without sacrificing performance.

2. **Suboptimal Performance on Lengthy Instructions:** Despite the adoption of Transformers, existing VLN models often exhibit a decline in navigation performance when dealing with longer and more complex natural language instructions. This suggests that current architectures may struggle with effectively processing and filtering relevant information from extended sequences, potentially leading to information overload or misinterpretation. Improving robustness to instruction length is a critical motivation.

3. **Limitations of State Space Models (SSMs) for VLN:** State Space Models (SSMs) like Mamba have emerged as efficient alternatives to Transformers for long-sequence modeling due to their linear computational complexity. This property makes them attractive for reducing VLN computational costs. However, SSMs are inherently designed for processing one-dimensional sequences and face specific challenges when applied directly to VLN:
 - **Spatial Relationship Learning:** They struggle to effectively learn intricate spatial relationships among visual observations, which are crucial for understanding panoramic views and environmental layouts.
 - **Cross-modal Interaction:** They are not inherently designed for robust cross-modal attention mechanisms, making it difficult to effectively fuse and align visual and linguistic information, especially when modalities have disparate sequence lengths.
 - **Input Selection for Action Decisions:** SSMs have shown limitations in tasks requiring input selection, which is fundamental to the dynamic action decision-making process in VLN where the agent must select a view or an action from multiple candidates.

To address these motivations, the specific objectives of COSMO are:
- To propose a novel hybrid architecture that combines the memory-filtering capabilities of selective SSMs with the contextual selection strengths of Transformers.
- To design two VLN-customized selective state space modules: the Round Selective Scan (RSS) for efficient spatial modeling within visual observations, and the Cross-modal Selective State Space Module (CS3) for enhanced cross-modal interaction and mutual selection between modalities.
- To validate that this hybrid approach can achieve competitive navigation performance across standard VLN benchmarks while significantly reducing computational costs compared to prominent Transformer-based baselines.
- To demonstrate COSMO's improved ability to handle long and complex instructions by selectively memorizing instruction-relevant visual observations and filtering out redundant information.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

COSMO proposes a hybrid architecture designed to combine the strengths of selective state space models (SSMs) for efficient selective memorization and Transformers for precise action decision-making. The baseline for comparison is DUET, a dual-scale graph Transformer.

#### 3.1. Baseline Method (DUET)

The DUET model utilizes a dual-stream architecture:
- **Text Encoder:** Embeds and encodes natural language instructions using a pre-trained language encoder.
- **Panorama Encoder:** Extracts global representations from panoramic views using a pre-trained vision transformer, then models spatial relationships among views with a multi-layer Transformer.
- **Cross-modal Encoders:** Operate at coarse and fine scales. The coarse-scale encoder constructs a topological map of visited and candidate nodes. Both scales use multiple cross-modal Transformer blocks to fuse visual and textual features.
- **Dynamic Action Fusion:** Action predictions from both scales are dynamically combined to yield the final action.

#### 3.2. COSMO Architecture

COSMO integrates VLN-customized selective state space modules within a revised DUET-like framework.

#### 3.2.1. Round Selective Scan (RSS)

RSS is designed to capture comprehensive inter-token spatial relationships within visual sequences more efficiently than multi-directional SSMs.
- **Problem Addressed:** Previous multi-directional SSMs (e.g., bi-directional selective scan) perform multiple scans (e.g., forward and backward) and still adhere to a causal mode, limiting contextual information for each token. VLN requires non-causal comprehension of spatial relationships among panoramic views.
- **Mechanism:** Instead of multiple scans, RSS takes an input sequence `x'`, flips it, and concatenates it with the original sequence to form `x = [x' | flip(x')]`. A single selective scan is then performed on this doubled sequence.
- **Benefit:** This allows tokens at the "middle" of the conceptual sequence (originally the end of `x'`) to gather global information from both directions. The class token, present at both ends of `x`, encapsulates compressed information of the entire sequence. After the scan, the output is divided and reversed in the second half, then added, leveraging hardware-aware parallel algorithms for efficiency. This facilitates comprehensive contextual information acquisition and efficient encoding.

#### 3.2.2. Cross-modal Selective State Space Module (CS3)

CS3 adapts selective SSMs to a dual-stream structure to handle cross-modal interactions, particularly when modalities have disparate sequence lengths.
- **Problem Addressed:** Standard SSMs are designed for single-stream sequences and struggle with cross-modal attention, unlike Transformers, or efficient fusion of modalities with imbalanced sequence lengths (e.g., long visual sequences, short text).
- **Mechanism:** When modality `x` (e.g., visual features, sequence length S) needs to be updated by modality `y` (e.g., instruction features, sequence length L), CS3 constructs the state space parameters (`B` and `∆`) based on `y` (after applying RSS-like flipping and concatenation to `y` for global context). The output projection parameter `C` is derived from the class token of `x`. The output from the state space (associated with `y`'s class token) then functions as a gate to selectively filter relevant information from `x`.
- **Benefit:** This dual-stream design enables fine-grained modal alignment and mutual selection, overcoming the limitations of single-stream SSMs for multimodal tasks and better suiting the requirements of VLN.

#### 3.2.3. Hybrid Architecture Integration

COSMO integrates these customized SSMs into its overall structure:
- **Node Encoder:** Unlike DUET, COSMO first establishes the topological map within the node encoder. It encodes observations of each node using a two-layer self-attention (SA), followed by average pooling to obtain a compressed node representation. The encoded observations of the current node serve as input for the local cross-modal encoder.
- **Global Cross-modal Encoder:** Processes the topological map (`G_t`) and a prepended stop action token. It employs:
 - A graph-aware self-attention (GASA) layer for intra-modality interaction.
 - **CS3** to facilitate semantic alignment between node features and instruction features, and to filter irrelevant visual information.
 - A cross-attention layer for token-level grounding.
 - Another GASA layer.
- **Local Cross-modal Encoder:** Focuses on the current node's views (`~v_t`) and a prepended class token for the stop action. It uses a cross-modal fusion module comprising:
 - **CS3** for alignment and selective memorization.
 - **RSS** for broadcasting contextual visual information within the current view features.
 - Cross-attention for token-level grounding.
 - Self-attention for intra-modality interaction.
- **Dynamic Action Fusion:** The predictions from global and local cross-modal encoders are dynamically fused for the final action selection, similar to DUET.

This hybrid approach ensures that SSMs are used for efficient selective memorization, filtering out redundant or irrelevant visual information, especially for lengthy instructions, while Transformers handle the precise action decision-making.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings regarding COSMO's navigation performance, computational efficiency, and the efficacy of its architectural components.

#### 4.1. Comparison with State-of-the-Art

COSMO was evaluated on three mainstream VLN benchmarks: REVERIE, R2R, and R2R-CE.

- **Navigation Performance:**
 - **REVERIE:** COSMO demonstrated competitive performance. On the validation unseen set, it achieved a 3.83% improvement in Success Rate (SR) over the DUET baseline. Compared to NaviLLM (a model leveraging a large language model and extensive data), COSMO showed superior performance with an 8.66% higher SR on the validation unseen split and notably better generalization on the test unseen split (+12.73% SR and +3.79% SPL).
 - **R2R:** COSMO achieved comparable performance to DUET, with a 2% improvement in SR on the test split. It also reduced Navigation Error (NE) by 0.16m on validation unseen and 0.22m on the test split compared to DUET. COSMO surpassed KERM (a model with external commonsense knowledge) by 1% SR on both splits.
 - **R2R-CE:** COSMO outperformed DUET by 5% in both SR and SPL on the test split and also surpassed GridMM on the test split.

- **Computational Costs:**
 - COSMO exhibited significantly lower computational costs compared to baseline and other SoTA methods.
 - **Parameters:** COSMO utilized 28 million parameters, representing approximately 15% of DUET's 181 million parameters.
 - **FLOPs:** COSMO incurred 0.46 GFLOPs, which is roughly 9.3% of DUET's 4.95 GFLOPs.
 - **Inference Time and Training Speed:** COSMO demonstrated faster inference (10.64s vs. 13.20s for DUET) and higher training speed (36 samples/s vs. 29 samples/s for DUET).

#### 4.2. Ablation Study

Ablation studies were conducted on the REVERIE validation unseen set to assess the contribution of COSMO's key designs.

- **Indispensability of Dual-stream Structure:** Directly applying vanilla Mamba layers (single-stream, concatenating textual and visual features) to VLN resulted in a substantial performance degradation, achieving a 32.25% SR, which is 14.73% lower than DUET and 18.56% lower than COSMO. Even using a bi-directional Mamba (Bi-Mamba) yielded only a 3.26% SR improvement over vanilla Mamba, confirming the necessity of a dual-stream architecture like CS3.
- **Superiority of RSS:** Replacing the Round Selective Scan (RSS) with a vanilla Mamba layer led to a 3.61% decrease in SR and a 3.89% decrease in SPL. Substituting it with Bi-Mamba also resulted in lower SPL and longer inference time compared to RSS, validating the efficiency and effectiveness of RSS for spatial modeling.
- **Superiority of CS3:** Replacing the Cross-modal Selective State Space Module (CS3) with a Bi-Mamba layer resulted in a 3.86% decrease in SR and a 4.53% decrease in SPL, highlighting CS3's effectiveness in learning cross-modal interactions.
- **Necessity of Hybrid Architecture:** Experiments confirmed that a hybrid architecture (SSM + Trans) outperformed pure Transformer (Trans + Trans) and pure SSM (SSM + SSM) configurations. The "SSM + Trans" approach (COSMO's design) achieved the highest SR, supporting the strategy of using SSMs for selective memorization followed by Transformers for action decision-making.

#### 4.3. Quantitative and Qualitative Analysis

- **Efficacy on Long Instructions:** Quantitative analysis on R2R and REVERIE validation unseen splits showed that COSMO provides a more substantial improvement over DUET on longer instructions. For instructions exceeding 30 words in REVERIE, COSMO achieved a 7.42% higher SR than DUET. COSMO also surpassed BEVBert by 6.01% SR on REVERIE's long instructions (>30 words). This suggests COSMO's improved ability to selectively filter relevant memories when dealing with complex, lengthy directives.
- **Qualitative Analysis:** Visualizations of predicted paths indicated that COSMO could correctly identify orientations and explore potential rooms, including backtracking, to locate target destinations, whereas DUET sometimes failed due to incorrect orientation or getting stuck in irrelevant rooms.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The COSMO framework represents a notable contribution to the field of Vision-and-Language Navigation, particularly by addressing the critical trade-off between model performance and computational cost.

**Key Contributions:**
- **Efficient Architecture for VLN:** The development of COSMO demonstrates an effective approach to achieve high navigation performance while drastically reducing computational expenses. This is crucial for deploying VLN agents in real-world applications with limited hardware resources, such as home assistant robots.
- **Novel VLN-Tailored SSMs:** The introduction of the Round Selective Scan (RSS) and the Cross-modal Selective State Space Module (CS3) significantly advances the application of State Space Models to complex multimodal tasks like VLN. These modules overcome the inherent limitations of general SSMs in handling spatial relationships within visual data and facilitating effective cross-modal interactions between visual and linguistic inputs of varying lengths.
- **Improved Handling of Complex Instructions:** COSMO's enhanced performance on lengthy and intricate instructions suggests its superior capability in selectively filtering and memorizing relevant information over extended sequences, which is a common challenge for existing Transformer-based models. This selective memorization capability contributes to more robust and accurate navigation.
- **Proof of Hybrid Architectural Efficacy:** The research provides empirical evidence for the effectiveness of a hybrid architecture that strategically combines SSMs for efficient data filtering (selective memorization) and Transformers for precise decision-making. This paradigm offers a blueprint for designing future efficient multimodal AI systems.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Broader Accessibility of Embodied AI:** By significantly reducing the computational footprint (15% parameters, 9.3% FLOPs of a strong baseline), COSMO makes advanced VLN capabilities more accessible for integration into a wider range of edge devices and resource-constrained platforms.
- **Advancement in Multimodal Foundation Models:** This work opens avenues for further research into adapting and specializing SSMs for other complex vision-and-language tasks, potentially influencing the design of future multimodal foundation models that prioritize efficiency without compromising performance.
- **Enhanced Human-Robot Interaction:** More capable and efficient VLN agents can lead to more natural and reliable human-robot interaction in environments like homes or workplaces, improving the utility and adoption of autonomous systems.
- **Inspiration for Efficient AI Design:** COSMO serves as an example of how architectural innovations, rather than solely scaling up existing models, can lead to substantial gains in efficiency and performance, encouraging further exploration of optimized model structures in AI research.
