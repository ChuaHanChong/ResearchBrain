# ActiveVLA: Injecting Active Perception into Vision-Language-Action Models for Precise 3D Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Fudan University researchers developed ActiveVLA, a Vision-Language-Action (VLA) framework that injects active perception into 3D robotic manipulation by enabling robots to actively select optimal camera viewpoints and dynamically zoom in on crucial regions. This approach achieves new state-of-the-art success rates of 91.8% on RLBench and 65.9% on COLOSSEUM, and demonstrates robust performance in real-world occlusion-heavy tasks.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models primarily rely on passive perception from fixed camera viewpoints, limiting adaptability to dynamic environments.
- Passive perception leads to challenges in handling occlusions, acquiring fine-grained details for precise manipulation, and maintaining robustness in cluttered or complex scenarios.
- Robots struggle with tasks requiring adaptive observation strategies, as they cannot actively adjust their sensory input to the task's demands.

## Method

- **ActiveVLA** introduces a coarse-to-fine ==active perception== design, starting with 3D Crucial Area Perception to identify task-relevant regions using multi-view rendering and a VLM backbone.
- It implements 3D Active Perception through ==Active Viewpoint Selection==, which generates and evaluates candidate camera positions based on visibility, distance, and diversity, and Active 3D Zoom-in, which re-renders crucial areas with a narrowed field of view.
- A 3D Action Prediction module utilizes refined VLM outputs from actively perceived views to predict translational targets, rotation, gripper state, and collision flags for precise robot control.

## Results

- Achieved new state-of-the-art on RLBench with an average success rate of **91.8%** and on COLOSSEUM with **65.9%**, outperforming previous methods in visual generalization and robustness.
- Demonstrated strong performance on GemBench, with an overall average success rate of **51.3%**, improving upon the previous state-of-the-art by 1.3 percentage points for long-horizon tasks.
- Validated on a real-world KINOVA GEN2 robot, ActiveVLA successfully performs complex, occlusion-heavy tasks (e.g., retrieving a towel, stacking blocks) with high accuracy and stability, significantly surpassing baselines like TriVLA and RVT-2.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating active perception, specifically dynamic viewpoint selection and 3D zoom-in, significantly enhances a robot's ability to overcome occlusions and achieve fine-grained manipulation.
> - A hierarchical coarse-to-fine perception strategy (identifying a crucial region, then actively refining observation of it) is effective for improving precision and robustness in VLA models.
> - Active perception, while introducing a moderate increase in inference time, yields substantial improvements in task success rates and generalization across diverse manipulation benchmarks and real-world scenarios.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has rapidly advanced with the advent of large pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs), giving rise to the Vision-Language-Action (VLA) paradigm. These models, often pre-trained on massive internet-scale image and text datasets, have demonstrated impressive generalization capabilities in understanding natural language instructions and translating them into robotic actions.

Initially, many VLA approaches primarily processed 2D visual inputs. However, a significant trend in the research landscape has been the incorporation of 3D modalities, such as point clouds or voxel representations, to provide richer spatial reasoning. Methods like Lift3D, PointVLA, and SpatialVLA have sought to inject explicit or implicit 3D structural cues into VLM backbones, leading to improved sample efficiency and spatial understanding for complex manipulation tasks. These 3D-aware policies often outperform their 2D counterparts in tasks requiring precise spatial interactions.

Despite these advancements, a critical limitation persists in most existing VLA and even 3D-aware VLA models: they largely rely on *passive perception*. Robots typically observe the scene from fixed viewpoints, such as static or wrist-mounted cameras, and process whatever sensory input is available. This passive approach severely restricts their ability to adapt to dynamic environments, resolve occlusions, or acquire fine-grained details necessary for precise manipulation, especially in long-horizon or cluttered scenarios. The paper cites Richard Gregory's observation, "Perception is not a passive process. It’s an active process of hypothesis testing," to underscore this fundamental gap.

ActiveVLA directly addresses this gap by introducing *active perception* into the VLA framework. It stands at the forefront of research aiming to imbue robots with the intelligence to dynamically and adaptively adjust their sensory input strategy. Rather than merely processing given observations, ActiveVLA empowers the robot to actively choose optimal viewpoints and zoom levels, much like humans adjust their gaze to gather more information. This positions ActiveVLA as a crucial step towards developing more robust, adaptive, and human-like embodied intelligence capable of navigating and interacting effectively in complex, partially observable real-world environments. It pushes the boundaries beyond static 3D understanding towards dynamic, inquiry-driven visual information acquisition.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind ActiveVLA stems from the limitations of existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, particularly their reliance on passive perception. Current VLA systems, even those incorporating 3D information, are typically constrained by fixed camera viewpoints—often wrist-mounted or static. This inherent lack of perceptual flexibility means robots cannot adaptively adjust their observation strategy (e.g., selecting a better angle or increasing resolution) to the specific demands of a task or the complexities of the environment. As a result, they frequently encounter challenges in:
- **Occlusion Handling:** Fixed viewpoints often miss critical details or suffer from severe occlusions, leading to incomplete or ambiguous scene understanding.
- **Fine-grained Manipulation:** Tasks requiring high precision, such as welding a small hole or manipulating tiny objects, demand a level of detail that a fixed-resolution, distant camera might not provide.
- **Long-horizon Tasks:** In multi-step or extended tasks, the importance of different regions of the scene can change over time, and a static view cannot adapt its focus.
- **Generalizability and Robustness:** Policies learned under fixed viewpoints can be brittle when faced with novel object arrangements, clutter, or environmental variations.

Inspired by the principle that perception is an active process of hypothesis testing, the **key objective** of this work is to develop a novel VLA framework, ActiveVLA, that explicitly integrates active perception capabilities into robotic manipulation. This framework aims to empower robots to:
1. **Actively select optimal camera viewpoints** during task execution to maximize visibility of target objects, minimize occlusions, and gather task-relevant information.
2. **Dynamically adjust camera resolution through 3D zoom-in** on critical regions of interest to acquire high-fidelity visual data for precise, fine-grained manipulation.

By achieving these objectives, ActiveVLA seeks to overcome the limitations of passive perception, leading to more precise, context-aware, and reliable action prediction, thereby enhancing the adaptability and performance of robots in complex and challenging manipulation scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ActiveVLA introduces a novel **coarse-to-fine active perception design** that integrates 3D spatial reasoning with vision-language understanding. The framework operates in two main stages: 3D Crucial Area Perception (coarse) and 3D Active Perception (fine), followed by 3D Action Prediction.

#### 3.1. 3D Crucial Area Perception (Coarse Stage)

This initial stage focuses on broadly identifying the task-relevant regions in the 3D scene.
- **Multi-View Rendering:** Given initial RGB-D images, a 3D point cloud of the scene is reconstructed. To interface with the 2D-based VLM backbone, this point cloud is then rendered into multiple 2D orthographic projection images. Specifically, three views (top, front, right) are generated, each containing RGB, depth, and 3D world coordinates (7 channels total) to maintain spatial correspondence. PyTorch3D is used for efficient rendering.
- **3D Crucial Area Extraction:** The rendered multi-view images, along with the language instruction, are fed into a pre-trained VLM backbone (PaliGemma). The VLM processes these inputs to generate a feature grid. A heatmap prediction module, employing a convex upsampling block, refines these features into high-resolution 2D heatmaps for each view, indicating regions of attention. These 2D heatmaps are then back-projected into 3D space to identify and localize the overall "critical 3D region" relevant to the task.

#### 3.2. 3D Active Perception (Fine Stage)

Building upon the coarse localization, this stage refines the perceptual input through active mechanisms.
- **Active Viewpoint Selection:**
 - **Hypothesis Testing:** ActiveVLA generates a set of candidate camera positions uniformly distributed around the identified 3D crucial region (e.g., the centroid of a partially observed object). A geodesic sampling strategy is used to ensure isotropic coverage on a spherical surface.
 - **Multi-objective Scoring:** Each candidate viewpoint is evaluated based on three criteria:
 - **Visibility:** Assesses whether the line of sight from the candidate camera to the crucial region is occluded by other scene geometry, using KDTree-based nearest-neighbor search on the point cloud.
 - **Distance:** Prefers moderate viewing distances that balance field-of-view and detail resolution.
 - **Diversity:** Encourages geometrically diverse viewpoints by maximizing angular separation among selected views.
 - These scores are normalized and combined using weighted coefficients. The top-K highest-scoring views are selected as the next observation poses, aiming for informative and unoccluded observations. The most informative view is chosen for the next step.
- **Active 3D Zoom-in:** To achieve fine-grained detail, ActiveVLA re-renders the scene from the optimal viewpoint selected, but with a narrowed field of view. This "virtual optical zoom" magnifies the local region around the crucial area while preserving high pixel resolution. Crucially, this is done using the existing 3D point cloud, ensuring scale-invariant view synthesis without geometric loss, unlike physical camera zoom which might compromise fidelity.

#### 3.3. 3D Action Prediction

Once the actively selected and zoom-in views are obtained and processed by the refined VLM, ActiveVLA predicts the robot's action.
- **Translational Prediction:** The 2D heatmaps generated from the actively refined views are back-projected onto a discretized 3D workspace grid. These form a multi-view score volume, and the translation target for the end-effector is determined by finding the grid point with the maximum score.
- **Rotation, Gripper State, and Collision Flag Prediction:** A hierarchical feature fusion module is employed:
 - **Global Context Encoding:** Max-pooling over the VLM's vision encoder outputs for each orthographic projection provides global scene semantics.
 - **Local Context Encoding:** An ROI-aware sampler extracts local tokens, capturing detailed appearance and geometry from the actively perceived regions.
 - These global and local features are concatenated and passed through a Multilayer Perceptron (MLP) head to predict Euler angles for rotation, a binary gripper state (open/close), and a binary collision flag (on/off).

**Implementation Details:** ActiveVLA uses a pre-trained PaliGemma backbone (SigLIP encoder, Gemma decoder), fine-tuned on a RoboPoint subset. Experiments are conducted in simulation (RLBench, COLOSSEUM, GemBench) using a Franka Panda robot and validated on a real-world KINOVA GEN2 robot with a RealSense D455 camera.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

ActiveVLA demonstrates significant advancements in precise 3D robotic manipulation across multiple benchmarks and real-world scenarios.

#### 4.1. Simulation Benchmarks

- **RLBench:** ActiveVLA achieves a new state-of-the-art with an average success rate of **91.8%** and an average rank of **1.22** across 18 diverse tasks. It particularly excels in precision-demanding tasks like "Insert Peg" and "Open Drawer," and maintains robustness under occlusions (e.g., "Place Cups" at 65.6%), showcasing strong generalization.
- **COLOSSEUM:** On this challenging benchmark designed to test robustness under various perturbations (object color, size, texture, lighting, camera pose), ActiveVLA sets a new state-of-the-art with an average success rate of **65.9%** and an average rank of **1.07**. It outperforms all previous methods, demonstrating superior visual generalization and invariant representation learning even in the presence of clutter, distractors, and significant viewpoint changes.
- **GemBench:** ActiveVLA achieves the best performance on core hierarchical levels L1–L3, with success rates of **92.4%, 66.3%, and 45.1%** respectively. Its overall average success rate is **51.3%**, improving upon the previous state-of-the-art by 1.3 percentage points. This indicates strong long-horizon reasoning and robust 3D perception for precise manipulation and skill recomposition.

#### 4.2. Real-Robot Analysis

Evaluations on a KINOVA GEN2 robot in complex, real-world scenarios with severe occlusions confirm ActiveVLA's robustness and strong generalization. Tasks included retrieving a towel from layered drawers, stacking an occluded red block onto a green block, grasping an occluded banana among other fruits, and retrieving an occluded purple cup from a hanging rack. ActiveVLA successfully infers object geometry, actively selects informative viewpoints, and executes fine-grained actions with high accuracy and stability, even when target objects are heavily obscured. It achieves an overall success rate of 91.8% (96.3% in supplementary material, likely a typo in main text), significantly outperforming baselines like TriVLA and RVT-2 on these challenging real-world tasks (e.g., 92% vs. 77% on "Retrieving Towel," 95% vs. 63% on "Red to Green Block").

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

Component analysis on the active perception modules demonstrates their individual contributions:
- The baseline (fixed view) achieves 87.6% success.
- Adding **Active View Selection (A-VS)** alone increases success to 89.4%, highlighting its role in improving scene coverage and reducing occlusion.
- Further incorporating **Active 3D Zoom-in (A-3Z)** alongside A-VS boosts performance to 91.8%, confirming that the hierarchical strategy of determining *where to look* (A-VS) and *how closely to observe* (A-3Z) is crucial for precise manipulation.
- Hyperparameter analysis indicates that **3 selected views** and a **zoom-in factor of 4** provide the optimal balance between performance gains and computational cost.

Inference times are also reported, showing that the active perception modules introduce a moderate increase in processing time (e.g., 0.26s for baseline to 0.53s for full ActiveVLA on RLBench), which is a reasonable trade-off for the substantial performance improvements.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ActiveVLA represents a significant leap forward in robotic manipulation, primarily due to its pioneering integration of **active perception** into the Vision-Language-Action (VLA) paradigm. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Paradigm Shift from Passive to Active Perception:** By enabling robots to dynamically select optimal viewpoints and zoom levels, ActiveVLA moves beyond the conventional passive observation model. This fundamental shift allows robots to actively "test hypotheses" about their environment, acquire more informative sensory data on demand, and overcome limitations imposed by static camera setups. This active approach is more akin to human visual intelligence and critical for truly autonomous systems.

- **Enhanced Precision and Robustness:** The coarse-to-fine active perception strategy, combining critical region localization with adaptive view selection and 3D zoom-in, directly addresses common failure modes in robotic tasks, particularly those involving occlusions, clutter, and fine-grained manipulation. The state-of-the-art results across RLBench, COLOSSEUM, and GemBench, coupled with robust real-world performance, demonstrate ActiveVLA's ability to achieve unprecedented precision and reliability in complex scenarios.

- **Improved Generalization:** The ability to dynamically adapt perceptual inputs makes the learned policies more robust to variations in object appearance, lighting, background, and camera poses—challenges highlighted by the COLOSSEUM benchmark. This enhanced generalization is crucial for deploying robots in unstructured and unpredictable real-world environments.

- **Foundation for Adaptive Embodied Intelligence:** ActiveVLA lays a critical foundation for developing more intelligent and adaptive embodied agents. By demonstrating that perception itself can be an actionable component of a robot's policy, it opens avenues for richer interaction with the environment, where the robot actively seeks information to reduce uncertainty and improve decision-making.

- **Practical Implications for Robotics:** The successful transfer of ActiveVLA from simulation to real-world robots, especially in occlusion-heavy tasks, underscores its practical utility. This could lead to advancements in various robotic applications, including complex assembly, delicate object handling, surgical robotics, and assistive robotics in home or industrial settings where precision and robustness under varying conditions are paramount.

- **Inspiration for Future Research:** This work will likely inspire further research into active sensing, perception-action loops, and adaptive observational strategies across various domains of embodied AI. Exploring more sophisticated active perception mechanisms, integrating predictive modeling for view planning, and applying similar principles to other sensory modalities (e.g., touch, sound) could be exciting future directions.

In conclusion, ActiveVLA marks a significant milestone by introducing a novel, effective, and validated framework for integrating active perception into VLA models. It pushes the boundaries of current robotic capabilities, enabling robots to perform complex manipulations with greater precision, robustness, and adaptability, thereby bringing us closer to truly intelligent and autonomous robotic systems.
