# When Replanning Becomes the Bottleneck: Budgeted Replanning for Embodied Agents

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers introduce BRACE, a framework for budgeted replanning in embodied agents, and E-RECAP, a context compression module. This system significantly reduces replanning tail latency and token consumption, lowering Service-Level Objective violation rates (e.g., from 85.5% to 4.7% in Meta Habitat) while maintaining task success across diverse tasks.

## Problem

- Existing evaluation metrics like average task success or mean latency fail to capture critical real-time performance issues, such as tail latency and Service-Level Objective (SLO) violations, during embodied agent replanning.
- The necessity for frequent replanning in dynamic environments leads to an accumulation of textual context for LLM/VLA planners, causing superlinear increases in computational cost and unpredictable latencies.
- The systemic computational costs associated with frequent LLM-based replanning, beyond just the quality of the generated plans, are often overlooked in current embodied AI research.

## Method

- The **BRACE** framework introduces a ==budgeted control loop== that manages replanning frequency and assigns explicit per-call token and latency budgets to embodied agents.
- **E-RECAP**, a ==context compression module== integrated into BRACE, prunes textual history using a learned token importance predictor to reduce input size for LLM/VLA planners within budget constraints.
- The methodology includes ==phase accounting== to instrument the replanning call path, providing auditable, granular insights into computational costs and enabling precise overhead attribution for different modules.

## Results

- BRACE and E-RECAP consistently reduced replanning latency SLO violation rates across various environments, such as from **85.5%** to **4.7%** in Meta Habitat navigation, while maintaining **100%** task success.
- The framework achieved a **62-92%** reduction in mean replanning-call token counts and yielded **2.1-2.6x** replanning-latency speedups, demonstrating significant computational efficiency gains.
- In challenging scenarios (e.g., RoboFactory Pass-Shoe), BRACE + E-RECAP enabled **80%** task success where baselines achieved **0%**, with validation on a real robot showing improved task success (e.g., from **24%** to **40%** in PICKFRUIT).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Evaluating embodied agents requires moving beyond aggregate success rates to include real-time metrics like tail latency and Service-Level Objective (SLO) violation rates for individual replanning calls.
> - Treating replanning as a 'budgeted systems primitive' enables explicit management of computational resources (tokens, latency) on a per-call basis, leading to more robust and stable agent behavior.
> - Intelligent context compression is highly effective, as the overhead of learned pruning is minimal compared to the latency reduction achieved by providing smaller inputs to the LLM planner.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Embodied agents typically operate in dynamic environments characterized by partial observability, actuation noise, and environmental changes. To address these challenges and maintain task efficacy over long horizons, these agents commonly employ an "observe → plan → act → replan" loop, relying on frequent replanning to recover from execution drift or unforeseen circumstances. Recent advancements in vision-language-action (VLA) models and large language model (LLM)-based planners have enhanced the quality and complexity of plans that embodied agents can generate.

However, the existing research landscape, while focusing on plan quality and task completion rates, has largely overlooked the systemic costs associated with frequent replanning. A single replanning call to an LLM-based planner is not a trivial operation; it involves processing a textual context that can accumulate substantial information, including task specifications, recent history, failure traces, and inter-agent communication summaries in multi-agent settings. As this context grows, the computational demands on transformer-based planners increase, leading to higher latency and variability in response times.

The current paradigm of evaluating embodied agents often emphasizes aggregate metrics such as average task success or mean latency. The paper identifies a critical gap: these aggregate metrics can obscure instances where replanning calls consistently miss real-time deadlines (Service-Level Objectives, SLOs), a phenomenon referred to as "tail latency." Such deadline misses, even if overall task success remains high, can destabilize closed-loop agent control, hinder coordination, and compromise real-time performance.

This research positions itself by addressing this systemic oversight. It frames replanning as a "budgeted systems primitive," advocating for the explicit consideration and management of computational resources—specifically token usage and latency—at the granularity of individual replanning calls. This contrasts with prior work on VLA efficiency, which often focuses on per-step inference, visual token pruning, or single-step queries. The work also builds upon classical metareasoning, which considers the cost-benefit of computation, but applies these principles to the practical context of LLM/VLA replanning pipelines in embodied systems. By introducing a framework for budgeted replanning and a method for context compression, the paper aims to shift the evaluation focus to include real-time stability, tail latency, and auditable resource consumption, thereby contributing to the development of more robust and deployable embodied AI systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations for this research stem from the recognition that while LLM/VLA-based planners improve embodied agent capabilities, their computational demands pose a significant bottleneck, particularly in real-time and dynamic environments. The core motivations are:

1. **Addressing Latency and Variability from Context Growth:** Embodied agents frequently replan to adapt to execution errors, partial observations, and coordination requirements. Each replanning query to an LLM involves sending a textual context that includes the task, history, failure logs, and inter-agent messages. This context grows over time and with the number of agents, causing the computational cost of replanning (specifically, transformer attention mechanisms) to increase superlinearly. This accumulation leads to highly variable and often lengthy replanning latencies, creating "heavy tails" in the latency distribution.
2. **Exposing Hidden Failures: Insufficiency of Aggregate Metrics:** Current evaluation methodologies for embodied agents predominantly report average task success rates or mean latency. The authors demonstrate that these aggregate metrics can mask critical system failures. Specifically, an agent might achieve 100% task success while consistently violating real-time latency deadlines (Service-Level Objectives, SLOs) on a large proportion of its replanning calls. Such frequent deadline misses indicate a lack of real-time reliability and can lead to instability in closed-loop control, which is not captured by success rates alone. The motivation is to introduce metrics that explicitly account for per-call tail latency and SLO violations.
3. **Establishing Replanning as a Budgeted Systems Primitive:** Given the aforementioned issues, the research is motivated to treat replanning not merely as an algorithmic planning problem but as a "budgeted systems primitive." This perspective necessitates managing the computational cost of replanning as a first-class concern, rather than an afterthought. The goal is to develop a control loop that explicitly considers resource allocation for each replanning event.
4. **Developing Efficient Context Management:** A direct consequence of context growth is increased replanning cost. Therefore, a key objective is to develop efficient methods for compressing or managing the replanning context to fit within defined budgets without significantly degrading plan quality. This aims to reduce the raw token count fed into the LLM, thereby shortening processing times.
5. **Enhancing Audibility and Comparability of Replanning Costs:** To facilitate clearer analysis and development, the motivation extends to creating an auditable framework. This involves instrumenting the replanning call path to log phase-level timings and token counts, enabling researchers to precisely attribute overheads to different modules (e.g., context compression, retrieval, planning) and perform budget-matched comparisons between different efficiency strategies. This allows for a more granular understanding of performance trade-offs.

In summary, the key objective is to develop a robust, real-time-aware replanning framework for embodied agents that explicitly manages computational budgets, mitigates tail latency, and provides comprehensive, auditable performance insights beyond traditional success metrics.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces **BRACE (Budgeted Replanning for Agentic Control in Embodied Systems)**, a framework that integrates a budgeted control loop for replanning with an efficiency module called **E-RECAP (Embodied REplanning with Cost-Aware Pruning)**. The methodology is structured to address the computational costs and real-time constraints of LLM-based replanning.

#### 3.1. BRACE Controller

BRACE operates as a closed-loop controller that manages replanning decisions and resource allocation at each step `t`.
- **Replanning Decision:** At every potential replanning point, the controller determines whether to initiate a replanning call. This decision is influenced by trigger signals (`τ_t`), such as periodic checks, failure detection (e.g., repeated action failures), or hazard signals in multi-agent settings (e.g., safety/coordination concerns).
- **Budget Selection:** If replanning is triggered, BRACE selects a `replanning mode (m_t)` and assigns two explicit per-call budget variables:
 - **Token Budget (`B_t`):** The maximum number of tokens allowed as input to the planner after any compression or budgeting.
 - **Latency Target SLO (`SLO_t`):** A real-time deadline (in milliseconds) for the entire end-to-end replanning call.
- **Controller State and Stability Policies:** BRACE maintains an internal controller state (`σ_t`) that tracks short-horizon execution and replanning history, including counters for steps since the last replanning call (`∆_t`) and steps since the last plan update (`κ_t`). This state enables stabilization policies to prevent excessive replanning:
 - **Cooldown Window (`δ`):** Prevents immediate, back-to-back replanning calls by enforcing a minimum interval between planner invocations.
 - **Commit Window (`ω`):** Ensures that a newly generated plan is executed for a minimum number of steps before the controller considers replacing it.
 - **Failure-Aware Overrides:** Allows the controller to temporarily bypass cooldown/commit gates or relax budgets (e.g., increase `B_t` or `SLO_t`) when persistent failures indicate a critical need for recovery, thus balancing stability with responsiveness.
- **Modularity:** BRACE is designed to be modular, allowing for the integration of various cost-reduction and reuse mechanisms on the replanning call path, such as token pruning (E-RECAP), retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), plan caching, and communication compression. The framework ensures that the overheads of these modules are accounted for.

#### 3.2. E-RECAP: Pruning for Replanning Context

E-RECAP is a specific context-compression module integrated into BRACE, designed to enforce the token budget `B_t` by pruning the replanning context `C_t`. Unlike traditional visual token pruning, E-RECAP focuses on the accumulated textual history and task-related context.
- **Context Growth Model:** The methodology explicitly acknowledges that context length (`N_t`) can grow significantly, especially in multi-agent scenarios (e.g., `N_t = N_0 + Σ_i Σ_a n_i,a`), leading to `O(N_t^2)` complexity in transformer attention.
- **Progressive Pruning Across Layers:** E-RECAP prunes tokens progressively at a predefined set of intermediate transformer layers `P`.
- **Token Importance Prediction:** A lightweight predictor, trained on gradient-based saliency scores (`ˆπ_i = |⟨∇_X_i ℓ(X), X_i⟩|`), estimates the importance score (`π_i`) of each token using its hidden state (`h^(l)_i`) at a given layer. The predictor uses a simple feed-forward network (`π_i = W_2 σ(W_1 h_i + b_1) + b_2`).
- **Combined Training Objective:** The predictor is trained with a composite loss function (`L = L_L + λ_1 L_M + λ_2 L_R`), which includes terms for preserving generation quality (`L_L`), matching saliency magnitudes (`L_M`), and preserving relative token ordering (`L_R`, a pairwise ranking term).
- **Token Selection Strategy:** At each pruning layer `l`, E-RECAP selects a subset of tokens to form the next layer's input (`N_{l+1} = ⌊r_l N_l⌋`) based on:
 - **Fixed Preservation:** Always retaining a small set of "head" tokens (e.g., task specification) and a "tail" window (e.g., most recent observations/actions).
 - **Utility-Based Selection:** Filling the remaining budget by selecting tokens with the highest predicted importance scores from the rest of the context.

#### 3.3. Phase Accounting and Audit Logging

To provide auditable and granular insights into replanning costs, BRACE instruments the entire replanning call path:
- **Timestamp and Token Logging:** For each controller step, and particularly when replanning occurs, BRACE records timestamps and token counts at the boundaries of various phases (e.g., context compression, retrieval, planner invocation, clarification, execution dispatch).
- **Metric Reporting:** This detailed logging enables the calculation of:
 - **End-to-End Replanning Latency (`ℓ_t`):** Wall-clock time for the entire call.
 - **Phase-Level Latencies:** Time spent in each sub-module (e.g., pruning time vs. planner time).
 - **Tail Latency Percentiles (P95/P99):** To capture worst-case performance.
 - **SLO Violation Rate (`v`):** The fraction of replanning calls exceeding the defined latency deadline.
This approach allows for distinguishing between overheads introduced by different modules and attributing tail events to their specific sources, ensuring transparent performance analysis.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several key findings across various embodied agent platforms, emphasizing the effectiveness of BRACE and E-RECAP in managing replanning costs and improving real-time stability.

1. **Tail Latency and SLO Violation Reduction:** A central finding is that "No BRACE" baselines (without budgeting or compression) frequently violate replanning latency SLOs, even when achieving 100% task success.
 - In **Meta Habitat navigation**, "No BRACE" incurred an 85.5% SLO violation rate (P95 latency of 2,677 ms against a 2,500 ms SLO). BRACE + E-RECAP reduced this to 4.7% (P95 latency of 2,500 ms) while maintaining 100% task success.
 - In **RoboFactory Pass-Shoe manipulation**, "No BRACE" had a 100% SLO violation rate (P95 latency of 1,604 ms against a 250 ms SLO). BRACE + E-RECAP reduced this to 50.0% (P95 latency of 1,213 ms).
 - In **Microsoft AirSim K=8 intersection**, "No BRACE" showed a 100% SLO violation rate (P95 latency of 8,520 ms against a 2,500 ms SLO). BRACE + E-RECAP reduced this to 4.7% (P95 latency of 1,640 ms).
 This consistently demonstrates that task success alone is an insufficient metric for real-time embodied agent performance.

2. **Significant Token Reduction and Replanning Acceleration with E-RECAP:** E-RECAP proved effective in compressing replanning contexts.
 - Across Meta Habitat, RoboFactory, and AirSim, BRACE with E-RECAP reduced mean replanning-call token counts by 62–92%.
 - Specifically, on **Habitat-Lab PointNav** with increasing agent counts (K=1, 4, 8), E-RECAP consistently reduced tokens per replanning call by 71–76% and yielded 2.1–2.6× replanning-latency speedups. This was achieved with minimal changes to task success or Success Weighted by Path Length (SPL).

3. **Effectiveness in Harder Settings:** In a challenging **RoboFactory Pass-Shoe** setting, where open-loop, frozen-plan, and the unbudgeted "No BRACE" all resulted in 0% task success, BRACE + E-RECAP achieved 80.0% task success with a 4.6% SLO violation rate and reduced coordination wait times. This indicates that BRACE is not only beneficial for efficiency in saturated regimes but also crucial for enabling recovery and task completion in difficult scenarios.

4. **Budget-Matched Comparisons:** When compared against alternative context reduction strategies (e.g., random truncation, recency truncation, structured summaries) under the same explicit token budget (B=128 on RoboFactory), E-RECAP demonstrated superior or comparable performance in terms of tail latency and SLO violations. For example, on Meta Habitat with B=20, E-RECAP achieved 3.6% SLO violation compared to 33.3% for random truncation and 36.4% for structured summary, while recency truncation achieved 0.0%. This highlights the value of learned, utility-aware pruning.

5. **Phase Accounting for Overhead Attribution:** The phase accounting mechanism revealed that E-RECAP's pruning overhead (approximately 35 ms) was small compared to the planner's latency (approximately 2.4 s) on the Habitat slice. This indicates that the primary system gain comes from reducing the input size to the LLM planner, rather than from high efficiency of the pruning module itself.

6. **Real-Robot Validation:** A focused evaluation on a single-arm real robot for **PICKFRUIT and PUSHT** manipulation tasks corroborated the simulation findings. BRACE + E-RECAP improved task success rates (e.g., PICKFRUIT from 24.0% to 40.0%) and significantly reduced replanning tail latency and SLO violation rates (e.g., PICKFRUIT SLO violation from 42.7% to 18.6%) compared to "No BRACE" baselines. This provides empirical evidence for the framework's applicability in physical systems.

7. **Diagnostic Insights into Stability:** Diagnostic visualizations and ablations (e.g., controller sweep in a proxy environment) illustrated how BRACE's stability policies (cooldown and commit windows) reduce planning churn and coordination delays, offering transparent insights into cost-stability trade-offs.

Overall, the main findings consistently demonstrate that explicit budgeting of replanning calls, combined with intelligent context compression, significantly improves the real-time reliability and efficiency of embodied agents, making tail latency and SLO violations auditable and manageable, even when task success is high.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research has several significant implications and potential impacts on the field of embodied AI and robotics, particularly concerning the deployment and evaluation of LLM/VLA-driven agents.

1. **Shift in Performance Evaluation Paradigms:** The most prominent impact is the advocacy for, and demonstration of, new evaluation metrics for embodied agents. By highlighting that high average task success can conceal pervasive real-time deadline misses, the paper strongly motivates reporting tail latency (e.g., P95, P99) and Service-Level Objective (SLO) violation rates at the granularity of individual replanning calls. This shift is crucial for understanding the true real-time reliability and robustness of LLM/VLA-based robotic systems, enhancing their reproducibility and safety reviewability beyond mere task completion.

2. **Improved Real-Time Control and Stability:** BRACE offers a practical framework for achieving more stable and timely closed-loop control in embodied agents. By explicitly budgeting token usage and latency, and incorporating stability policies (cooldown/commit windows), the framework helps mitigate issues like excessive planning churn, coordination delays, and unpredictable latency spikes that can destabilize operations in dynamic environments. This can lead to more predictable and robust agent behavior in real-world applications.

3. **Enhanced Computational Efficiency and Resource Management:** The E-RECAP module, by intelligently pruning replanning contexts, significantly reduces the number of tokens processed by LLM/VLA planners. This translates directly into lower computational demands, faster inference times, and potentially reduced energy consumption. Such efficiency gains are critical for deploying interactive embodied agents, especially in resource-constrained environments, multi-agent systems where context grows rapidly, and latency-sensitive tasks like dexterous manipulation or autonomous navigation.

4. **Auditable and Composable Architecture:** BRACE's phase-level accounting and modular design allow for transparent analysis of where computational costs are incurred. This auditable approach enables researchers to accurately assess the overhead of different efficiency modules (e.g., pruning vs. retrieval vs. caching) and conduct fair, budget-matched comparisons. This transparency fosters systematic improvement and informed design choices for future embodied AI systems.

5. **Foundation for Advanced Metareasoning:** By explicitly exposing token and latency budgets as decision variables, this work provides a practical systems interface for implementing more sophisticated metareasoning strategies. Future research can build upon BRACE to develop risk-aware budget allocation, failure-conditioned overrides, or adaptive resource management policies that dynamically adjust replanning efforts based on the real-time needs and perceived utility of additional computation.

6. **Broader Applicability:** The empirical validation across diverse simulation platforms (Meta Habitat, RoboFactory, AirSim) and a focused real-robot evaluation suggests the generalizability of the BRACE framework and E-RECAP module. This indicates potential for impact across various embodied tasks, from navigation and manipulation to multi-agent coordination.

**Ethical Considerations:** The paper's impact statement acknowledges potential dual-use concerns. While the efficiency gains primarily aim for positive impacts like reliable real-time control and lower deployment costs, faster and more frequent replanning capabilities could, in principle, be applied to systems with malicious intent (e.g., surveillance, weaponized systems). The authors recommend deployment practices that prioritize application-level access control, safety constraints with monitoring, and domain-appropriate human oversight, particularly in high-stakes or human-facing scenarios. This acknowledgment demonstrates a responsible consideration of the broader societal implications of advanced autonomous decision-making technologies.
