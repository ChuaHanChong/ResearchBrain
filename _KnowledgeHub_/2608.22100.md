# Contact-Rich Robotic Manipulation in Construction via Zero-Shot Learning: A Diffusion Policy-Guided Adaptive Control

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework for construction-scale contact-rich robotic assembly that trains a CNN-based diffusion policy on Isaac Sim-generated flange position and force/torque trajectories, then corrects its predicted actions online with an L1-inspired adaptive controller that estimates contact disturbances and damps actions under high force. Zero-shot sim-to-real deployment on a UR20 reaches 100% success on timber joinery and pipe fitting, and 90-100% across sequential truss subtasks, with lower, more stable contact forces.

## Problem

- Preplanned construction assembly and perception-based pose correction handle loose tolerances but give no online force-driven action correction during tight-clearance mating, so fabrication deviations cause jamming.
- Prior diffusion-policy timber joinery work needed hundreds of teleoperated demonstrations on a physical robot: slow, degrading of repeatedly assembled workpieces, and accelerating equipment wear.
- Classical impedance and hybrid force-position controllers need predefined task frames and tuned models, while simulation-trained policies stay sensitive to unmodeled friction, compliance, and contact-dynamics mismatch.

## Method

- Synthetic data pipeline in Isaac Sim: parallel environments, pre-insertion poses randomized in a 5 mm disc, band-limited 2 mm lateral noise, force-and-position success labeling, 1000 demonstrations per task at 10 Hz.
- CNN-based ==diffusion policy== maps a two-step history of flange position and six-axis wrench to a 16-step delta-action horizon, executing three steps per ==receding-horizon replan==.
- ==L1-inspired adaptive layer==: state predictor, tanh force-gated ==adaptation law== with disturbance projection and low-pass filtering, plus inverse-force action scaling and box-projection clipping of corrections.

## Results

- Timber mortise-tenon reached **100%** real success versus **87%** baseline and **95%** compliant policy; in simulation **90%** versus **73%** for both baselines over **200** rollouts.
- Peak forces dropped: real timber joint **152.5 N** versus **166.0 N** compliant (CLES **0.93**, Cohen's d **1.60**); simulation **101.5 N** versus **197.1 N** baseline, both highly significant.
- Full-scale gable truss gave **100%** post, **100%** right rafter, **90%** left rafter over **10** real rollouts each; pipe fitting hit **100%** real success for all three modes.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Gating adaptation on measured contact force corrects a simulation-trained policy only where contact makes it necessary, leaving free-space motion untouched.
> - Success rate alone hides contact quality: force distributions separated methods even where every mode scored 100%, so report peak-force statistics too.
> - Material stiffness decides how much control precision buys; compliant PVC absorbs misalignment, so adaptive gains concentrate on stiff, high-friction interfaces.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The construction industry faces challenges including low productivity growth, significant workforce shortages, and the inherent risks associated with physically demanding tasks. Construction robotics and automation offer potential solutions to these issues. However, realizing these benefits requires robotic systems capable of reliably manipulating full-scale building components through tasks such as grasping, transporting, orienting, placing, and joining. Within this context, assembly is critical for many building systems, often involving tight-fitting connections that are susceptible to fabrication inaccuracies and accumulated tolerances. Traditional approaches, relying on pre-planned trajectories or basic perception-based feedback for pose correction, frequently prove unreliable for contact-rich assembly, where precise alignment and robust control during physical interaction are necessary.

Prior research in construction robotics has explored various methods to address these challenges. Early work focused on accurate positioning and sequencing, often assuming negligible uncertainties. More advanced systems incorporated perception-based feedback and adaptive fabrication to compensate for deviations, as seen in multi-robot timber assembly. However, these methods primarily address pose correction rather than providing online action adjustments based on interaction forces during tight-clearance mating.

Learning-based control methods, particularly sensory-motor policy learning, have emerged as a promising avenue for capturing complex contact dynamics without explicit analytical modeling. Diffusion policies, a form of generative modeling for policy learning, have demonstrated performance across diverse manipulation tasks, including contact-rich assembly in construction using pose and force/torque (F/T) feedback. A notable limitation of these learning-based approaches, especially for construction-scale applications, is the high cost, time consumption, and labor intensiveness of collecting extensive expert demonstrations on physical robots. Repeated physical trials also contribute to material degradation and equipment wear. While simulation-based learning can mitigate data collection burdens, it often introduces a significant "sim-to-real gap" due due to discrepancies in component geometry, material properties, and contact dynamics between simulated and physical systems. Domain randomization and noise injection have been employed to bridge this gap, but accurately modeling complex contact interactions remains challenging.

Model-based control methods, such as hybrid force-position control and impedance control, provide structured ways to manage compliant interaction. However, their effectiveness often depends on accurate task representations, appropriate contact structures, and task-specific tuning, which can be difficult to generalize under varying uncertainties.

This paper positions itself at the intersection of these fields, aiming to combine the benefits of scalable simulation-based policy training with robust, online action correction. It specifically addresses the sim-to-real gap and the need for force-aware control in construction-scale contact-rich assembly. By integrating a diffusion policy trained solely on synthetic data with an L1-inspired adaptive controller, the work seeks to enable zero-shot transfer from simulation to the real world, providing online compensation for unmodeled contact dynamics and material imperfections that current methods struggle with. This approach aims to advance the automation of multi-stage assembly, reducing reliance on real-world data collection and improving performance and safety in complex construction manipulation tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable robust, force-aware contact-rich robotic assembly of construction-scale components without requiring physical demonstrations for policy training. This is motivated by several challenges inherent in the construction industry and current robotic manipulation techniques:

1. **Addressing Construction Industry Challenges:** The construction sector suffers from low productivity growth compared to other industries, faces substantial workforce shortages (e.g., projected need for hundreds of thousands of additional workers in the U.S. by 2027), and involves physically demanding tasks that expose workers to high risks of injury and musculoskeletal disorders. Robotic automation is proposed as a means to improve productivity, alleviate labor constraints, and enhance worker safety.

2. **Reliable Manipulation of Full-Scale Components:** Realizing the benefits of construction robotics depends on systems that can reliably manipulate large building components, including grasping, transporting, orienting, placing, and joining them. Assembly tasks are particularly critical, as they involve joining discrete components into larger systems (e.g., walls, floors, structural frames).

3. **Challenges of Tight-Tolerance and Contact-Rich Assembly:** Many assembly processes in construction involve tight-fitting connections where small deviations due to fabrication inaccuracies, calibration errors, or accumulated tolerances can lead to assembly failure, jamming, or damage to components. Executing pre-planned trajectories without real-time feedback is often unreliable in such scenarios. Prior work on perception-based feedback addresses pose errors but does not directly provide online correction based on interaction forces during complex contact.

4. **Limitations of Learning-Based Methods for Construction Scale:** While learning-based methods, including diffusion policies, show promise for contact-rich assembly by learning complex sensory-motor mappings, they typically require extensive datasets. For construction-scale tasks, collecting real-world expert demonstrations is costly, time-consuming, and labor-intensive. Moreover, unsuccessful physical trials can generate excessive contact forces that damage expensive workpieces and accelerate equipment wear.

5. **Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap:** Training policies exclusively in simulation offers a scalable alternative to real-world data collection. However, discrepancies between simulated and physical systems (e.g., in component geometry, material properties, friction, and contact dynamics) can create a significant "sim-to-real gap," leading to poor performance when transferring policies to the real world without fine-tuning.

To address these motivations, the specific objectives and contributions of this paper are outlined as follows:
- To introduce a scalable synthetic data generation pipeline that enables training diffusion policies in simulation, thereby reducing reliance on costly real-world demonstrations for construction-scale physical robots.
- To develop a novel control architecture that integrates learned diffusion policies with an L1-inspired adaptive controller. This controller is designed to correct policy-predicted actions online, enhancing robustness to material imperfections and unmodeled contact dynamics during tight-tolerance physical assembly.
- To experimentally evaluate and demonstrate that this combined framework enables zero-shot sim-to-real transfer with improved success rates and lower, more stable contact forces in both simulation and real-world environments.
- To validate the framework in a full-scale timber prefabrication experiment involving sequential, contact-rich insertion tasks for assembling a gable-shaped truss, demonstrating its scalability to multi-stage construction workflows.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research proposes an integrated framework for contact-rich robotic manipulation in construction that couples a diffusion policy with an L1-inspired adaptive controller. The methodology involves synthetic data generation, policy learning, and the design of the adaptive controller, followed by experimental evaluation in both simulation and real-world settings.

#### 3.1. Experimental Setup

- **Hardware:** A six-axis UR20 robotic arm, equipped with a built-in flange-mounted six-axis force/torque (F/T) sensor and a custom pneumatically actuated parallel gripper. The UR20 has a 1750 mm reach and a 20 kg payload capacity.
- **Simulation:** NVIDIA Isaac Sim 5.0, utilizing its GPU-accelerated PhysX backend for parallel simulations. It supports URDF models and six-axis F/T sensing, replicating the physical hardware and environment. The UR20 manipulator and custom gripper are modeled with their kinematic and dynamic parameters.

#### 3.2. Data Collection (Synthetic Data Generation)

The core idea is to train policies exclusively on simulation-generated data to avoid costly real-world data collection.
- **Task Formulation:** Contact-rich assembly tasks (e.g., timber joinery, pipe fitting) are defined where the robot moves a grasped male component into a rigidly mounted female component. The focus is on the contact-rich insertion phase.
- **Demonstration Trajectories:** Motion-planned trajectories are generated in Isaac Sim. Each demonstration consists of two phases: an approach phase (from a randomized start to a pre-insertion pose) and an insertion phase.
- **Randomization:** To ensure diversity and robustness, the initial *xy* coordinates of the gripped element are uniformly sampled within a disc of radius *r_samp* around the goal, and the vertical *z* coordinate is perturbed within a range *±z_max* around a nominal approach height *z_app*. Larger clearances are used in simulation for data collection than in real-world evaluations to increase trajectory diversity.
- **Noise Injection:** Nominal trajectories (100 Hz) are perturbed with low-frequency, band-limited noise (max 2 mm amplitude) applied to the lateral *xy* coordinates. This generates physically plausible, corrective-like motions, enhancing policy robustness.
- **Data Recording and Curation:** Timestamped robot position at the flange center and six-axis wrench (F/T) data at the flange are recorded at 100 Hz. Only "successful" demonstrations (full insertion within 2 mm Euclidean distance of the goal and maximum contact force below 1000 N) are retained for policy training.
- **Preprocessing:** Data is transformed to a local coordinate frame, downsampled to 10 Hz, and stored in HDF5 format.

#### 3.3. Policy Learning

- **Model:** A convolutional neural network (CNN)-based diffusion policy is trained as the noise prediction network.
- **Input:** The observation vector at time *k* is `o(k) = [p(k), F(k)]`, combining the 3D robot position and 6D force/torque measurements.
- **Output:** The policy `π` predicts a sequence of future relative robot actions `u_pol(k : k + T_p - 1)`, defined as delta positions `(∆x, ∆y, ∆z)`.
- **Execution:** During policy evaluation, only the first `T_a` steps of the predicted action sequence are executed, enabling receding horizon replanning.
- **Training Data:** Policies are trained exclusively on the curated simulation data.

#### 3.4. Adaptive Controller

An L1-inspired adaptive controller is developed to correct the policy-predicted actions online, aiming to improve robustness to unmodeled contact dynamics, material imperfections, and bridge the sim-to-real gap.
- **Dynamic Model:** The system dynamics are represented as a first-order model `ṗ(t) = u(t) + σ(t)`, where `p(t)` is robot position, `u(t)` is the policy-predicted action, and `σ(t)` represents unknown motion disturbances.
- **Components:**
 - **State Predictor:** Estimates the system state `p̂(t)` using a reference model, incorporating the control command and estimated disturbance, while regulating the prediction error `e(t) = p̂(t) - p(t)`. A safety bound prevents excessive drift.
 - **Adaptation Law:** Updates the disturbance estimate `σ̂(t)` based on the prediction error `e(t)`.
 - **Force-Based Activation:** A `tanh`-based function `α(t)` modulates the adaptation rate based on the magnitude of the measured contact force `||F(t)||`, activating adaptation predominantly during contact.
 - **Projection and Filtering:** The disturbance estimate is projected to remain within bounds (`σ_max`) and then low-pass filtered using an exponentially-weighted average (with `f_c = 10 Hz`) to attenuate high-frequency noise.
 - **Control Law:** Computes the corrected control action `u_corr(t)`.
 - **Force-Based Scaling:** A factor `β(t)` reduces the magnitude of the policy's predicted action under high contact forces, promoting compliance.
 - **Action Bounding:** The final commanded action `u_cmd(t)` is projected onto a bounded set (within `±δ_max`) to prevent large deviations.
- **Implementation:** All continuous-time expressions are implemented in discrete time using a forward Euler integration scheme. Controller parameters are manually tuned in simulation and remain fixed for real-world deployment.

#### 3.5. Experiments and Evaluation

- **Single Assembly Experiments:**
 - **Tasks:** Mortise and tenon timber joint assembly (1 mm clearance, stiffer material) and PVC pipe fitting (0.25 mm radial clearance, more compliant material).
 - **Execution Modes:** Three modes are compared: "Baseline" (diffusion policy only), "Ours" (diffusion + adaptive controller), and "Compliant policy" (diffusion + benchmark compliance controller from prior work).
 - **Metrics:**
 - **Average Success Rate:** Rollouts are deemed successful if the component reaches the insertion goal within 3 mm without exceeding a Cartesian force threshold (200 N) or deviating more than 100 mm from the goal.
 - **Peak Contact Force Analysis:** Distributions of maximum contact force magnitudes (`F_max`) are analyzed using the Kruskal-Wallis test for overall differences and pairwise one-sided Mann-Whitney U tests (with Bonferroni correction) to compare "Ours" against baselines. Common Language Effect Size (CLES) and Cohen's d are reported as effect size measures.
- **Full-Scale Assembly Experiment:**
 - **Task:** Multi-step assembly of a four-member, gable-shaped timber truss, involving sequential insertions of a central post and two rafters into a bottom chord. This includes mortise-and-tenon and lap joints.
 - **Method:** Three separate diffusion policies are trained for each insertion task and deployed sequentially with the proposed adaptive controller.
 - **Metrics:** Success rate for 10 real-world rollouts per insertion task, with an increased force threshold of 300 N due to larger components.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation encompassed single assembly tasks in both simulation and real-world environments, as well as a full-scale multi-stage assembly experiment.

#### 4.1. Single Assembly Experiments

##### 4.1.1. Average Success Rate (Table 5)

- **Timber Joint Assembly:**
 - In simulation, the proposed method ("Ours") achieved a 90% success rate, outperforming the "Baseline" (73%) and "Compliant policy" (73%).
 - In real-world experiments, "Ours" achieved 100% success, while the "Baseline" achieved 87% and the "Compliant policy" achieved 95%. The proposed controller reportedly eliminated failure cases such as jamming and force-threshold violations observed in the other two methods.
- **Pipe Fitting:**
 - In simulation, "Ours" achieved 73% success, slightly higher than "Baseline" (66%) and "Compliant policy" (69%).
 - In real-world experiments, all three execution modes achieved 100% success. This high success rate across methods was attributed to the inherent compliance of the PVC material, which accommodated minor misalignments.

##### 4.1.2. Force Analysis (Figure 12, Table 6)

Statistical analysis of peak contact force magnitudes (`F_max`) provided further insights into the performance of the methods.
- The Kruskal-Wallis test revealed statistically significant differences in peak force distributions among the three execution modes for all tasks in both simulation and real-world settings (p < 0.05 in all cases).
- **Timber Joint Assembly:**
 - **Real-world:** "Ours" produced lower peak forces than the "Compliant policy," with a statistically significant difference (p < 0.001, CLES = 0.93, Cohen's d = 1.60), indicating a strong effect size. The difference compared to the "Baseline" was not statistically significant after Bonferroni correction (p = 0.161), which was attributed to higher variance in baseline forces.
 - **Simulation:** "Ours" consistently exhibited statistically significantly lower peak forces compared to both "Baseline" (p < 0.001, CLES = 0.74, Cohen's d = 0.849) and "Compliant policy" (p < 0.001, CLES = 0.72, Cohen's d = 0.786).
- **Pipe Fitting:**
 - **Real-world:** "Ours" showed significantly lower forces compared to the "Compliant policy" (p = 0.004, CLES = 0.69, Cohen's d = 0.669). However, no statistically significant evidence was found for "Ours" producing lower forces than the "Baseline" (p = 1.000).
 - **Simulation:** "Ours" produced statistically significantly lower forces compared to the "Compliant policy" (p = 0.008), though with a small effect size (CLES = 0.58, Cohen's d = 0.054). No statistically significant difference was found between "Ours" and "Baseline" (p = 0.186).

**Summary of Single Assembly Findings:** The benefits of the proposed adaptive controller were most evident in tasks involving stiffer timber material, where it improved both success rates and contact quality by reducing peak forces and stabilizing interaction. For the pipe fitting task, the inherent compliance of PVC materials meant that all methods achieved high success rates, and differences in force control were less pronounced, though "Ours" still demonstrated more controlled contact dynamics compared to the compliant policy.

#### 4.2. Full-Scale Assembly Experiment

The full-scale assembly of a gable-shaped timber truss involved three sequential contact-rich insertion tasks: post insertion, left rafter insertion, and right rafter insertion.
- **Post Insertion:** Achieved 100% success across 10 real-world rollouts.
- **Right Rafter Insertion:** Achieved 100% success across 10 real-world rollouts.
- **Left Rafter Insertion:** Achieved 90% success across 10 real-world rollouts.

The overall truss assembly process was successfully completed using independently trained policies integrated with the adaptive controller. These results indicate the framework's scalability to multi-stage construction workflows involving larger components, higher contact forces, and sequential deployment of multiple learned policies.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework that integrates policy learning with an adaptive controller, demonstrating advancements in construction-scale contact-rich robotic assembly. The work addresses several critical challenges in the field, offering a pathway toward more robust and autonomous construction processes.

**Advances in Robotic Construction Automation:** The framework contributes to improving productivity, alleviating workforce shortages, and enhancing worker safety in the construction industry by enabling reliable automation of complex, physically demanding assembly tasks. By demonstrating successful manipulation of full-scale building components, the research extends the capabilities of construction robotics beyond tasks with loose tolerances.

**Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer for Force-Aware Assembly:** A significant contribution is the enablement of zero-shot transfer from simulation-only policy training to real-world force-aware execution. This eliminates the need for costly, time-consuming, and labor-intensive real-world data collection, which is particularly critical at construction scale where material damage and equipment wear from failed trials can be substantial. The synthetic data generation pipeline, coupled with the adaptive controller, effectively bridges the sim-to-real gap arising from discrepancies in geometry, material properties, and contact dynamics.

**Enhanced Robustness and Performance in Contact-Rich Manipulation:** The integration of a CNN-based diffusion policy with an L1-inspired adaptive controller demonstrably improves task success rates. For stiffer materials like timber, the adaptive controller not only increased success but also resulted in lower and more stable contact forces compared to baseline and compliant policy methods. This enhanced control during contact interaction contributes to safer operations, reduces the risk of damaging components, and potentially extends the lifespan of robotic equipment. The finding that task performance is influenced by both material properties and geometric tolerances, with stiffer materials benefiting most from adaptive compensation, provides important insights for future applications.

**Scalability to Multi-Stage Construction Workflows:** The successful completion of a full-scale timber truss assembly, involving sequential deployment of multiple independently trained policies, demonstrates the framework's scalability. This indicates its potential for integration into complex, multi-stage construction prefabrication workflows, facilitating coordinated assembly of structural systems with larger and heavier components and higher interaction forces. This extends the applicability of robotic fabrication beyond component machining to physical assembly.

**Foundations for Future Research and Practical Deployment:** The research lays a foundation for future work to address remaining limitations, such as incorporating vision-based perception for more unstructured environments, exploring richer multimodal representations, and developing automatic parameter tuning strategies for the adaptive controller. By providing a practical approach for deploying construction-scale robotic systems under conditions of high precision and uncertainty, this work motivates broader adoption of advanced robotics in construction, potentially leading to more integrated and repeatable prefabrication processes.
