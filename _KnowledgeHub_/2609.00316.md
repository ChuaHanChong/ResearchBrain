# Geometric analysis of generic 3R robots, and necessary and sufficient conditions for a class of orthogonal robots to have four IKS

> [!summary] Summary
> This paper analyzes the inverse kinematics of generic 3R positional robots by recasting the conformal-geometric-algebra inverse kinematic model as the intersection of a fixed circle with a torus swept by a rotating circle. Toric cross-sections plus tangency conditions and Groebner-basis elimination yield necessary and sufficient conditions, and explicit height ranges, for orthogonal robots with zero second offset to admit four inverse kinematic solutions.

## Problem

- Algebraic treatments of 3R inverse kinematics give concrete conditions but stay confined to special architecture classes or simplifying assumptions, so they do not cover generic robots.
- Existing four-solution criteria for orthogonal manipulators assume a vanishing third offset, leaving architectures with nonzero third offset outside the known necessary and sufficient conditions.
- Prior analyses characterize how many solutions exist but not the end-effector height range over which a robot switches between four and two solutions.

## Method

- Parameterizes the rotating circle of the ==conformal-geometric-algebra inverse kinematic model== as a ==torus==, so counting solutions becomes counting intersections of the fixed circle with a toric section at a given height plane.
- Imposes ==tangency== by equating implicit-curve slopes of the torus section and circle, then eliminates variables via a ==lexicographic Groebner basis== to obtain the admissible fixed-circle radii.
- Substitutes the tangential radii back and eliminates the square root through a ==change of variable==, reducing quaternary existence to sign conditions on four polynomial inequalities and critical height values.

## Results

- The tangency analysis produces a **degree-16** polynomial with **1185 terms** in the circle radius; restricting the second offset to zero factors the slope condition and collapses this to **four closed-form radii**.
- **Six orderings** of the link length and the torus major and minor radii are tabulated with signs of **three critical squared heights** and the inequality values, giving explicit height bounds for four solutions in each case.
- One ordering, minor radius below major radius below link length, never yields **four solutions**, extending a prior condition to robots with nonzero third offset; equality of link length and major radius is also always binary.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Choosing the right geometric manifold can convert an intractable elimination problem into a tractable one, here shrinking a thousand-term resultant to four closed-form radii.
> - Tangency plus small perturbation is a general counting device: find configurations where two roots merge, then perturb to certify additional real intersections nearby.
> - Solution-count conditions are more useful when paired with the workspace region over which they hold, since counts change with end-effector height, not just architecture.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The kinematic analysis of robotic manipulators, particularly the determination of inverse kinematic solutions (IKS), constitutes a fundamental problem in robotics. For 3R (three revolute joint) positional robots, this problem has been extensively studied since Pieper's foundational work in 1968. The broader research landscape for 3R robots encompasses several analytical approaches, each with its strengths and limitations.

**Algebraic Approaches:** These methods typically involve formulating the inverse kinematics as a system of polynomial equations. While they have yielded concrete results for specific robot classes or simplified architectures, their complexity often limits their applicability to generic manipulators. For instance, algebraic analyses have provided conditions for orthogonal 3R robots (where the third prismatic offset, d3, is zero) to possess four IKS, and for the inverse kinematic model (IKM) to decompose into quadratic polynomials. However, extending these purely algebraic methods to fully generic 3R robots often results in systems that are computationally intensive and challenging to interpret geometrically.

**Geometric Approaches:** In contrast, geometric methods offer an intuitive understanding of kinematic properties and have demonstrated the capability to extend analyses, such as conditions for cuspidality or the number of IKS, to generic robot configurations. These approaches often visualize robot kinematics through the intersection of geometric entities, providing insights into the physical behavior of the manipulator.

**Conformal Geometric Algebra (CGA):** More recently, methods leveraging Conformal Geometric Algebra have emerged as a powerful tool for robot kinematics. This approach allows for the representation of geometric entities (points, lines, circles, spheres) and their transformations in a unified algebraic framework. Previous work, such as, has interpreted the IKM as the intersection of rotating and fixed circles using CGA.

This paper positions itself at the intersection of these methodologies, aiming to combine the intuitive understanding offered by geometric approaches with the analytical rigor provided by algebraic methods, particularly within a framework informed by conformal geometric algebra. It seeks to generalize existing conditions for IKS to a broader class of robots, specifically extending results for orthogonal robots to configurations not fully covered by previous studies (e.g., d3 ≠ 0). By parameterizing the rotating circle as a torus, the authors aim to provide a more direct geometric intuition for the IKM, which facilitates a clearer understanding of how robot parameters influence the number of IKS.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to present a detailed kinematic analysis of generic 3R robots. This overarching goal is broken down into several specific objectives and is motivated by the desire to overcome existing limitations in the field:

1. **Develop a Generic Framework for IKS Analysis:** The central objective is to establish a generic framework for studying the conditions under which a 3R robot possesses four inverse kinematic solutions (IKS). This framework is intended to be applicable to generic 3R robots, extending beyond the specialized classes often addressed by purely algebraic methods. The motivation here is to bridge the gap between abstract algebraic results and intuitive geometric understanding for generic robot architectures.

2. **Utilize a Conformal Geometric Algebra (CGA)-Derived IKM:** The paper aims to leverage an inverse kinematic model inherited from a method based on conformal geometric algebra, specifically one that interprets the IKM as the intersection of a rotating and a fixed circle. The motivation for this choice is that CGA provides a unified and often simpler representation of geometric transformations, which can lead to a more direct and intuitive geometric interpretation of the robot's kinematics.

3. **Introduce Torus Parameterization for Enhanced Intuition:** A key objective is to parameterize the rotating circle of the IKM as a torus. By studying the intersection of this torus with a fixed circle, the authors aim to gain a more straightforward geometric intuition for the IKM. This approach is motivated by the belief that a clearer geometric visualization of the IKM (as a torus-circle intersection) will allow for a better understanding of how robot parameters influence the number of IKS and the distribution of these solutions within the workspace.

4. **Analyze IKS Distribution and Singularities in Workspace:** The research seeks to demonstrate how the torus parameterization, particularly being linked to the last two joint axes, can facilitate the plotting of joint space singularities onto this torus. This, in turn, allows for a better visualization and understanding of the distribution of IKS within the robot's workspace. This objective is motivated by the need for comprehensive understanding of a robot's operational capabilities and limitations.

5. **Derive Necessary and Sufficient Conditions for a Class of Orthogonal Robots:** As an application of the proposed framework, a specific objective is to present the necessary and sufficient conditions for a particular class of orthogonal robots to have four IKS. Specifically, the paper focuses on orthogonal robots with d2 = 0 and d3 ≠ 0. This is motivated by the desire to extend previously known results (which primarily addressed d3 = 0) to a broader and distinct class of orthogonal robots.

6. **Determine Limits for IKS Transition:** An additional, more specific motivation is to provide the limits on the z-coordinate of the end-effector (z_c) that dictate the transition from having four IKS to two IKS. This level of detail was reportedly not available in previous analyses, thus offering a more complete understanding of the robot's workspace characteristics regarding the number of IKS.

In summary, the motivation stems from the perceived advantages of a geometrically intuitive yet algebraically rigorous approach to analyze the inverse kinematics of 3R robots, particularly for generic and specific orthogonal configurations where existing analyses are either too complex or incomplete.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research integrates geometric interpretation with algebraic analysis, building upon an inverse kinematic model derived from Conformal Geometric Algebra. The core approach involves visualizing the inverse kinematics problem as the intersection of specific geometric primitives.

**1. Inverse Kinematic Model (IKM) Foundation:**
The work is based on an IKM previously established in, which, following Selig’s approach, reduces the inverse kinematics of a generic positional 3R robot to finding the intersection of a fixed circle (C_B) and a rotating circle (C_A) for a given end-effector position.

**2. Geometric Interpretation via Torus Parameterization:**
The central methodological innovation is to consider the torus (T_A) traced by the rotating circle C_A as it sweeps through its motion. The problem of finding IKS is then reformulated as finding the intersection points between this torus T_A and the fixed circle C_B.
- **Simplification:** To analyze these intersections, the authors focus on the cross-section of the torus that lies on the same plane as C_B. This plane is identified as z = z_c, assuming C_B is parallel to the xy-plane.
- **Conditions for Four IKS:** To determine the conditions for a 3R robot to have four IKS, the methodology involves identifying end-effector positions P=(x, y, z-d1) for which four real intersections occur between the toric section (T_A_z=zc) and C_B. This is achieved by first identifying radii of C_B that result in tangential intersections with the toric section. The rationale is that if tangential intersections exist, a slight modification of the radius can lead to two additional intersections close to each tangential point, resulting in a total of four intersections. This systematic approach aims to cover all configurations that yield four IKS, thereby establishing necessary and sufficient conditions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings related to the kinematic analysis of generic 3R robots, culminating in specific necessary and sufficient conditions for a class of orthogonal robots to exhibit four inverse kinematic solutions (IKS).

**1. Geometric Interpretation for Generic 3R Robots:**
- The paper establishes a geometric interpretation of the inverse kinematic model for a generic 3R robot. This model is reduced to finding the intersection between a fixed circle (C_B) and a torus (T_A) traced by a rotating circle (C_A).
- This approach is stated to provide a more intuitive understanding of the IKM and the influence of robot parameters on the number of IKS compared to purely algebraic methods.
- The torus, parameterized by the last two joint axes, allows for the visualization of singularities in the joint space and their distribution within the workspace (Figure 2b).

**2. Method for Determining Four IKS:**
- The methodology for identifying conditions for four IKS involves analyzing the intersection of the fixed circle C_B with the cross-section of the torus T_A at a specific plane z=z_c.
- The core idea is to find radii of C_B that are tangential to the toric section (T_A_z=zc). If such tangential intersections exist, minor variations in the radius can lead to two additional intersections, yielding a total of four solutions.

**3. Necessary and Sufficient Conditions for Orthogonal Robots (d2=0, d3≠0):**
The primary application of the developed framework is to a class of orthogonal 3R robots where the second prismatic offset (d2) is zero, and the third prismatic offset (d3) is non-zero (d2=0, d3≠0). For these robots, the following key results were obtained:
- **Algebraic Simplification:** The condition d2=0 simplifies the tangency equations, causing a factor corresponding to the x-axis (y=0) to emerge in the slope equation (Equation 3).
- **Identification of Tangential Radii:** Four radii for C_B (r_B) are derived (Equation 4) corresponding to the outermost and innermost circles tangential to the torus cross-section along the x-axis.
- **Conditions for Additional Intersections:** By analyzing the existence of real solutions for y (y≠0) for these tangential radii (Equations 5 and 6), specific inequalities (q1, q2, q3, q4) are derived, which must be satisfied for four IKS to exist.
- **Critical Values of z_c:** Critical values for z_c (z_1^2, z_2a^2, z_2b^2) are determined, which define the boundaries for the existence of four IKS.
- **Comprehensive Conditions Table:** Table 2 provides a comprehensive list of necessary and sufficient conditions for a 3R orthogonal robot (with d2=0) to be quaternary (i.e., have four IKS). These conditions are categorized based on the relationships between the robot's D-H parameters (a1, R, r):
 - **a1 < R < r**: 0 ≤ z_c < z_2a
 - **a1 < r < R**: 0 ≤ z_c < max(z_2a, R-r)
 - **R < a1 < r**: Ranges for z_c depend on the sign of (a1^2 - Rr).
 - **R < r < a1**: 0 ≤ z_c < r-R
 - **r < a1 < R**: Ranges for z_c depend on the sign of (a1^2 - Rr), involving minimums and maximums of critical z_c values and (R-r).
- **Identification of Binary Robot Cases:** It is specifically found that the case r < R < a1 cannot lead to a quaternary robot, as all relevant z^2 values are negative and q(w) values are positive. This indicates that robots in this configuration will have fewer than four IKS.
- **Degeneracy Condition (a1=R):** The condition a1 = R is identified as a case where the robot is never quaternary and can lead to degeneracy (infinite IKS) (Figure 6).

**4. Extension of Previous Work:**
- The derived conditions extend previous results for orthogonal robots, which were primarily focused on the condition d3 = 0. This work specifically addresses orthogonal robots with d3 ≠ 0.
- The analysis provides explicit limits on the end-effector's z-coordinate (z_c) that mark the transition between having four IKS and two IKS (Figure 5), a detail reportedly not available in prior studies.

In summary, the paper successfully develops a geometric-algebraic framework to characterize the IKS of 3R robots, providing specific, detailed conditions and z-limits for a particular class of orthogonal robots to possess four IKS, thereby extending existing knowledge in the field.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research offers several points of significance and potential impact within the field of robotics and kinematic analysis:

**1. Enhanced Understanding of 3R Robot Kinematics:** By reformulating the inverse kinematic problem as the intersection of a circle and a torus, the paper provides a more intuitive and geometrically transparent understanding of how robot parameters influence the number of inverse kinematic solutions. This geometric insight can simplify the comprehension of complex kinematic behaviors of 3R manipulators.

**2. Bridging Geometric and Algebraic Approaches:** The work successfully integrates the strengths of geometric intuition with algebraic rigor. This hybrid approach offers a robust framework that is both analytically sound and conceptually clear, potentially fostering the development of similar integrated methods for other robotic systems.

**3. Advanced Robot Design and Selection:** The derived necessary and sufficient conditions for a class of orthogonal 3R robots to have four IKS (Table 2) are directly applicable to robot design. Engineers can use these conditions to select kinematic parameters (a1, R, r) to ensure that a manipulator possesses a desired number of IKS within specific regions of its workspace. This is crucial for applications requiring multiple solutions for tasks like obstacle avoidance, singularity avoidance, or reaching a target pose with different joint configurations.

**4. Improved Motion Planning and Control:** The ability to delineate the regions of the workspace where four IKS exist, along with the specific limits on the z-coordinate for IKS transitions, can significantly improve motion planning algorithms. Controllers can leverage this knowledge to choose between multiple solutions to optimize trajectories, avoid singularities, or handle redundancies more effectively. The visualization of IKS distribution and singularities on the torus further aids in understanding operational envelopes.

**5. Expansion of Kinematic Knowledge Base:** The research extends existing results for orthogonal robots by analyzing a class where d2=0 and d3≠0, filling a gap left by previous studies that primarily focused on d3=0. Furthermore, providing explicit limits on the z-coordinate for the transition between two and four IKS adds a new layer of detail to the kinematic characterization of these robots.

**6. Foundation for Future Research:** The generic framework developed for analyzing 3R robots can serve as a foundation for future investigations into more complex or generic serial manipulators. It opens avenues for exploring conditions for different numbers of IKS, analyzing other types of singularities, or extending the methodology to parallel manipulators or robots with more degrees of freedom.

**7. Educational Tool:** The clear geometric interpretation using torus and circle intersections could potentially serve as an effective pedagogical tool for teaching advanced robot kinematics, making abstract algebraic concepts more accessible to students and researchers.

In summary, the paper contributes to both theoretical understanding and practical application in robotics by providing a powerful analytical framework and concrete conditions that can inform the design, selection, and control of 3R manipulators.
