# Light-R1: Curriculum SFT, DPO and RL for Long COT from Scratch and Beyond

> [!summary] Summary
> Qiyuan Tech and Renmin University researchers developed Light-R1, an open-source suite for training long Chain-of-Thought reasoning models using public data, achieving state-of-the-art mathematical performance. Their Light-R1-32B model, trained for approximately $1000, surpassed DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-32B on AIME24 (76.6 vs. 72.6) and AIME25 (64.6 vs. 54.9), while the 14B variant demonstrated a ~2% absolute improvement on AIME24 via Reinforcement Learning without typical response length reduction.

## Problem

- High computational costs of large language models (LLMs) like DeepSeek-R1 (671B parameters) make them impractical for widespread industrial and academic use.
- Reliance of advanced LLMs on proprietary data and methodologies creates barriers to transparency, reproducibility, and open-source development.
- Existing challenges in effectively applying reinforcement learning (RL) to smaller, pre-trained long-Chain-of-Thought (COT) models without negatively impacting response length.

## Method

- Introduced ==Light-R1==, an open-source framework leveraging only public data and models to train compact LLMs (under 32B parameters) capable of long Chain-of-Thought reasoning.
- Employed a ==multi-stage curriculum post-training strategy==, sequentially applying ==Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)==, ==Direct Preference Optimization (DPO)==, and Reinforcement Learning (RL).
- Curated a high-quality, difficulty-filtered mathematical dataset, including a crucial ==3k subset of highly challenging problems==, to optimize model training.

## Results

- The Light-R1-32B model, trained from scratch using Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct, achieved an AIME24 score of **76.6** and AIME25 of **64.6**, surpassing DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-32B on both benchmarks.
- Fine-tuning existing DeepSeek-R1-Distill models with only the 3k highly challenging examples led to consistent performance gains, with Light-R1-7B-DS reaching 59.1 on AIME24, outperforming its base model (55.5).
- Applying Reinforcement Learning (GRPO) to Light-R1-14B-DS resulted in a ~**2%** absolute improvement on AIME24 (74.0), successfully increasing both response length and reward score without the typical initial length reduction.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Multi-stage curriculum training, particularly the use of a small set of meticulously curated, highly challenging problems (3k examples), significantly enhances long-Chain-of-Thought reasoning across various model sizes.
> - Reinforcement Learning (RL), when applied with stabilizing techniques like modified length rewards and broad importance sampling weight clipping, can successfully improve performance on already strong 14B long-COT models without adverse effects on response length.
> - Reproducible, cost-effective methodologies using only public data can yield compact language models (under 32B parameters) that achieve state-of-the-art reasoning capabilities, challenging the necessity of massive, proprietary models.
