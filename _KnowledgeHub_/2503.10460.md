# Light-R1: Curriculum SFT, DPO and RL for Long COT from Scratch and Beyond

> [!summary] Summary
> Qiyuan Tech and Renmin University researchers developed Light-R1, an open-source suite for training long Chain-of-Thought reasoning models using public data, achieving state-of-the-art mathematical performance. Their Light-R1-32B model, trained for approximately $1000, surpassed DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-32B on AIME24 (76.6 vs. 72.6) and AIME25 (64.6 vs. 54.9), while the 14B variant demonstrated a ~2% absolute improvement on AIME24 via Reinforcement Learning without typical response length reduction.

## Problem

- High computational costs of large language models (LLMs) like DeepSeek-R1 (671B parameters) make them impractical for widespread industrial and academic use.
- Reliance of advanced LLMs on proprietary data and methodologies creates barriers to transparency, reproducibility, and open-source development.
- Existing challenges in effectively applying reinforcement learning (RL) to smaller, pre-trained long-Chain-of-Thought (COT) models without negatively impacting response length.

## Method

- Introduced ==Light-R1==, an open-source framework leveraging only public data and models to train compact LLMs (under 32B parameters) capable of long Chain-of-Thought reasoning.
- Employed a ==multi-stage curriculum post-training strategy==, sequentially applying ==Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)==, ==Direct Preference Optimization (DPO)==, and Reinforcement Learning (RL).
- Curated a high-quality, difficulty-filtered mathematical dataset, including a crucial ==3k subset of highly challenging problems==, to optimize model training.

## Results

- The Light-R1-32B model, trained from scratch using Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct, achieved an AIME24 score of **76.6** and AIME25 of **64.6**, surpassing DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-32B on both benchmarks.
- Fine-tuning existing DeepSeek-R1-Distill models with only the 3k highly challenging examples led to consistent performance gains, with Light-R1-7B-DS reaching 59.1 on AIME24, outperforming its base model (55.5).
- Applying Reinforcement Learning (GRPO) to Light-R1-14B-DS resulted in a ~**2%** absolute improvement on AIME24 (74.0), successfully increasing both response length and reward score without the typical initial length reduction.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Multi-stage curriculum training, particularly the use of a small set of meticulously curated, highly challenging problems (3k examples), significantly enhances long-Chain-of-Thought reasoning across various model sizes.
> - Reinforcement Learning (RL), when applied with stabilizing techniques like modified length rewards and broad importance sampling weight clipping, can successfully improve performance on already strong 14B long-COT models without adverse effects on response length.
> - Reproducible, cost-effective methodologies using only public data can yield compact language models (under 32B parameters) that achieve state-of-the-art reasoning capabilities, challenging the necessity of massive, proprietary models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research directly addresses several critical challenges and trends in the current landscape of large language models. The advent of highly capable but computationally prohibitive models like DeepSeek-R1 (with 671 billion parameters) has highlighted a significant barrier to widespread adoption and research. The "Light-R1" project positions itself as a crucial response to this, aiming to develop **compact yet capable models (under 32B parameters)** that can perform extended long COT reasoning, which is essential for complex tasks like mathematical problem-solving, algorithmic planning, and scientific analysis.

The paper contributes significantly to the growing movement of **open-source LLMs** by providing an alternative to proprietary models and datasets. By exclusively leveraging public data and models, Light-R1 offers a reproducible pathway to achieve DeepSeek-R1-level reasoning performance without reliance on closed-source information, thereby democratizing access to advanced AI capabilities.

Furthermore, this work is at the forefront of **efficient post-training strategies** for LLMs. It refines and combines supervised fine-tuning (SFT), direct preference optimization (DPO), and reinforcement learning (RL) into a multi-staged curriculum. The paper explicitly engages with ongoing research in curriculum learning for long-COT training, demonstrating superior performance compared to conventional single-stage SFT approaches. A particularly notable contribution is the **first publicly documented demonstration of significant improvement through RL on already strong, long-COT 14B models**, which addresses a known scalability challenge of applying RL to smaller models without undesirable side effects like response length reduction.

By meticulously reproducing existing benchmarks and then surpassing them with more efficient and accessible models, Light-R1 directly challenges the notion that only massive, proprietary models can achieve state-of-the-art reasoning. This positions it as a significant step towards making sophisticated reasoning systems practical and implementable for diverse real-world applications and resource-constrained environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of the Light-R1 project is to introduce an **open-source suite for training long reasoning models using reproducible and cost-effective methodology**, leveraging exclusively public data and models. This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Overcoming Resource Barriers:** The immense computational costs associated with training and deploying R1-level models (typically 70B+ parameters, or even DeepSeek-R1's 671B parameters) render them impractical for many industrial AI applications, edge devices, and academic research labs. The authors aim to develop compact models (e.g., 7B, 14B, 32B) that can still achieve high-level reasoning performance.
2. **Addressing Proprietary Data Limitations:** Given the proprietary nature of the data used in the DeepSeek-R1 series, the researchers were motivated to develop an alternative approach that relies solely on publicly available datasets, ensuring transparency and reproducibility.
3. **Enhancing Accessibility and Reproducibility:** A core motivation is to make sophisticated reasoning models more accessible and implementable in real-world applications by providing open-source models, training data, and code. This includes establishing robust and trustworthy evaluation protocols to ensure reliable comparison of model performances.
4. **Advancing Training Methodologies:** The project seeks to systematically address challenges in dataset curation, optimal utilization of diverse difficulty data through curriculum training, and effective implementation of reinforcement learning for enhancing long-COT reasoning in smaller models, particularly where previous attempts faced scalability issues.

In essence, Light-R1 strives to democratize access to advanced reasoning capabilities, demonstrating that state-of-the-art performance can be achieved efficiently and transparently, without requiring massive computational resources or proprietary datasets.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Light-R1 project employs a multi-faceted methodology encompassing robust evaluation, meticulous data preparation, and a multi-staged curriculum training strategy combining Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), and Reinforcement Learning (RL).

#### 3.1. Evaluation Protocol

To ensure stable and trustworthy evaluation, the authors first established robust protocols, rigorously reproducing results from DeepSeek-AI (2025) and Qwen (2025). They verified that generating 64 responses per query for `pass@1` metrics was essential to mitigate randomness and achieve deviation around 1 point, adapting an existing evaluation code (Luo et al., 2025b).

#### 3.2. Data Preparation

The data preparation process focused on creating an efficient, high-quality, and challenging dataset for post-training:
- **Data Collection:** Approximately 1000k math questions with ground truth answers were collected from diverse public sources, including OpenR1-Math-220k, OpenThoughts-114k, OpenMathInstruct-2, OmniMath, s1K-1.1, LIMO, and hendrycks-math, along with in-house data.
- **Data Diversity and Decontamination:** The collected data was filtered for diversity using an in-house tagging system to downsample overrepresented categories. Comprehensive decontamination was performed using exact matching (excluding digits for numerical changes) and N-gram (N=32) matching against benchmarks like AIME24&25, MATH-500, and GPQA to remove any compromised questions.
- **Difficulty-Based Data Generation:** A two-stage difficulty filtering methodology was implemented to generate comprehensive Chain-of-Thought (COT) responses for SFT:
 - **Stage 1:** Initial filtering used DeepScaleR-1.5B-Preview to identify questions with a pass rate below a threshold (α). For these, DeepSeek-R1 was queried to generate long-COT responses, resulting in an SFT dataset of over 70k examples.
 - **Stage 2:** Recognizing the need for more challenging problems, a second, more stringent filtering was applied using the *full* DeepSeek-R1 model on the 70k dataset. This stage retained only questions with a pass rate below α and where DeepSeek-R1's sampled responses were neither uniformly correct nor incorrect. This yielded a highly curated dataset of approximately 3k most challenging examples, which proved exceptionally valuable.

#### 3.3. Curriculum Post-Training (Light-R1-32B)

The training pipeline for models trained "from scratch" (e.g., Light-R1-32B, based on Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct) comprised three stages:
1. **SFT Stage 1:** Training on the 76k filtered mathematical problems, incrementally building reasoning capacity.
2. **SFT Stage 2:** Fine-tuning on the 3k most challenging problems, addressing areas where the model needed further knowledge assimilation.
3. **DPO Optimization:** Direct Preference Optimization using the NCA loss (Chen et al., 2024). A semi-on-policy approach was used, where chosen responses were verified correct answers from DeepSeek-R1, and rejected responses were sampled from the SFT-stage-2 model with verified incorrect answers. The `360-LLaMA-Factory` was utilized for DPO with sequence parallelism to handle long context lengths (up to 32k tokens).
Finally, **TIES-merging** (Yadav et al., 2023) was applied to combine models from SFT-stage2, DPO, and another DPO variant, yielding the final Light-R1-32B.

#### 3.4. Reinforcement Learning (RL) for 14B Models (Light-R1-14B-DS)

For enhancing existing DeepSeek-R1-Distill models, particularly the 14B variant (Light-R1-14B-DS'), a sophisticated two-pass RL process was developed:
- **Offline Data Selection:** To optimize RL training, Light-R1-7B-DS was used to sample results for RL prompts. Only prompts with a pass rate between 0.25 and 0.625 were selected, effectively filtering out questions that were either too easy, too hard, or unverifiable by the rule-based verifier.
- **Online Reinforcement Learning:** GRPO (Generalized Reinforcement Policy Optimization) (Shao etal., 2024) was applied to the filtered dataset, implemented using the `verl` framework (Sheng et al., 2024).
- **RL Stabilization Techniques:** To counteract common RL instability issues, a modified version of length reward (Yeo et al., 2025) was used, with clipped shortening reward to prevent "initial length collapse." Additionally, a broader two-sided importance sampling weight clipping mechanism was implemented (Mini-Max, 2025) to mitigate the impact of extreme values during policy optimization. A rule-based reward and the deduplicated Big-Math dataset were utilized.

The entire process, including SFT and DPO, was conducted with a focus on computational efficiency, with the 32B model trained from scratch costing approximately $1000 over 6 hours on 12×H800 GPUs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several significant findings, demonstrating the effectiveness and efficiency of the proposed curriculum training and RL strategies:

1. **Reproducibility:** The authors successfully reproduced the evaluation scores of DeepSeek-R1-Distill and QwQ models on AIME24 with high fidelity, validating their robust evaluation protocols.
2. **Light-R1-32B Performance (From Scratch):** The Light-R1-32B model, trained from Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct using the curriculum SFT, DPO, and model merging, achieved state-of-the-art mathematical reasoning performance. It **outperformed DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-32B** on AIME24 (76.6 vs. 72.6) and AIME25 (64.6 vs. 54.9). This was achieved with a low training cost of approximately $1000. Despite its math-focused training, Light-R1-32B demonstrated strong cross-domain generalization, indicating a robust underlying reasoning capacity.
3. **Impact of High-Quality 3k Dataset (SFT Stage 2 Data):** The meticulously curated 3k challenging examples from SFT Stage 2 proved exceptionally effective. Fine-tuning existing DeepSeek-R1-Distill models (which were already strong, having been pre-tuned on proprietary data) with *only* this 3k dataset led to **universal and significant performance improvements** across all model sizes (7B, 14B, 32B).
 - Light-R1-7B-DS achieved an AIME24 score of 59.1, surpassing DeepSeek-R1-Distill-7B (55.5).
 - Light-R1-32B-DS, trained with this data, scored 78.1 on AIME24 and 65.9 on AIME25, performing comparably to QwQ-32B and DeepSeek-R1, and demonstrating unexpectedly high GPQA performance, suggesting enhanced generalization in stronger base models.
4. **Reinforcement Learning Effectiveness on 14B Models:** The paper presents the **first publicly documented successful application of RL for significant performance improvement on already long-COT finetuned 14B models**. The Light-R1-14B-DS model, after SFT Stage 2 and GRPO, achieved an AIME24 score of 74.0 and AIME25 score of 60.2. This is a ~2% absolute improvement over its SFT-only counterpart (Light-R1-14B-DS'). Crucially, the RL training curves showed a **simultaneous increase in response length and reward score without the typical initial length reduction**, a breakthrough in scaling RL to smaller long-COT models. The RL also demonstrated positive out-of-domain generalization on GPQA.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Light-R1 research carries profound significance and potential impact across several dimensions:

1. **Democratization of Advanced Reasoning AI:** By providing a fully open-source suite including models, training data, and code, Light-R1 significantly lowers the barrier to entry for developing and deploying R1-level reasoning capabilities. This democratizes access to sophisticated AI, enabling researchers, developers, and small companies to build powerful reasoning systems without needing prohibitive resources or access to proprietary datasets.
2. **Cost-Effectiveness and Reproducibility:** The demonstration that state-of-the-art long-COT models can be trained from scratch for as little as ~$1000 challenges the prevailing notion that only multi-million dollar projects can yield competitive results. This establishes a new benchmark for cost-efficiency and reproducibility in LLM training.
3. **Insights into Curriculum Learning and Data Efficiency:** The research provides valuable insights into the power of multi-stage, difficulty-progressive curriculum training. The remarkable effectiveness and transferability of the highly curated 3k challenging examples highlight the critical role of data quality and strategic data selection over sheer quantity, especially for improving reasoning capabilities. This can guide future dataset design and training methodologies.
4. **Advancements in RL for Smaller Models:** The successful application of GRPO on a 14B model, achieving significant performance gains without the common pitfalls of length collapse, represents a crucial advancement in the field of Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF). This breakthrough expands the applicability and scalability of RL to a broader range of model sizes, making it a more viable tool for enhancing more compact LLMs.
5. **Accelerating Real-World Applications:** The development of compact yet powerful reasoning models directly enables their deployment in resource-constrained environments, such as edge devices or real-time industrial applications. This will accelerate the integration of sophisticated AI into practical scenarios requiring complex problem-solving.
6. **Foundation for Future Research:** Light-R1 lays a robust foundation for future work, encouraging further exploration into enhancing generalization capabilities for long reasoning models and optimizing the efficiency of RL training. Its open-source nature fosters collaboration and innovation within the AI community.

In conclusion, Light-R1 represents a substantial contribution to the field of large language models, pushing the boundaries of what is achievable with accessible resources and open-source methodologies, and paving the way for more democratized and efficient AI development.
