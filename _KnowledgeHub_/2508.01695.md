# DexReMoE:In-hand Reorientation of General Object via Mixtures of Experts

> [!summary] Summary
> DexReMoE presents a robotic manipulation framework utilizing a Mixture-of-Experts architecture for in-hand reorientation of objects with complex geometries. The method achieves an average of 19.62 consecutive successes on training objects and maintains robust performance on out-of-distribution objects, significantly enhancing worst-case success rates for challenging shapes.

## Problem

- Generalizing in-hand object reorientation to a wide range of objects with complex and diverse geometries remains a significant challenge.
- Existing generalization strategies, like fine-tuning, often lead to catastrophic forgetting and limit robustness when dealing with varied object shapes.
- Developing an efficient and expressive object feature representation is difficult; raw point clouds are computationally intensive and may not effectively distinguish similar objects under rotation.

## Method

- A DexReMoE (Dexterous Reorientation Mixture-of-Experts) framework is introduced, employing multiple specialized expert policies combined by a gating network.
- The system uses a compact privileged object representation, combining a PointNet++ derived embedding with a one-hot category vector, to describe object shape and physical properties.
- Reinforcement learning with Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) trains a base policy, and then fine-tunes specialized experts on specific shape categories, with a soft gating network for dynamic expert selection.

## Results

- DexReMoE achieved an S_min (minimum consecutive successes) of 6.05 on training objects, a considerable improvement over the best baseline (MMoE: 3.36), indicating better performance on the most difficult objects.
- On 50 out-of-distribution objects, DexReMoE maintained its performance advantage, securing an average of 19.12 consecutive successes and an S_min of 4.11 for the hardest novel objects, surpassing MMoE (18.18 average, 3.80 S_min).
- Ablation studies confirmed that a 4-expert configuration provided the most balanced performance, and the combined use of point-cloud and category embeddings for the gating network yielded superior worst-case results.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A Mixture-of-Experts architecture can effectively enhance robustness and generalization in dexterous manipulation tasks, especially for objects with intricate and diverse geometries.
> - Combining learned point-cloud features with object category information provides a potent and low-dimensional representation that effectively guides both policy learning and expert routing.
> - Dynamic, shape-dependent expert selection via a soft gating network allows for improved performance on challenging objects that typically cause failures in generalist policies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Dexterous manipulation represents a significant challenge in robotics, particularly concerning generalizable capabilities across a wide range of objects. In-hand object reorientation, the ability of a robot to precisely rotate an object to a target orientation while holding it, is a fundamental component of dexterous manipulation and is critical for tasks requiring flexible tool use and object rearrangement.

Prior research in dexterous manipulation, particularly with the advent of reinforcement learning (RL), has shown progress. Efforts such as OpenAI's work demonstrated end-to-end deep RL for multi-fingered manipulation, while DeXtreme achieved sim-to-real transfer for agile reorientation using vision. Other studies have explored rapid motor adaptation for simple objects or multi-axis reorientation of novel shapes using depth cameras. However, a recurring limitation in these works is their focus on single objects, simple geometries, or the substantial computational resources required, often struggling to generalize reliably to objects with complex and diverse shapes under generalized conditions.

Existing strategies for generalization, such as fine-tuning, typically tailor policies to individual objects and can lead to catastrophic forgetting, thereby limiting robustness across diverse geometries. Domain adaptation techniques primarily address the sim-to-real gap rather than significant variations in object shape. Furthermore, efficient extraction of meaningful object features, especially shape information, remains an open problem. Raw point clouds, while detailed, can be computationally intensive and may not distinguish symmetric objects under certain rotations.

This work, DexReMoE, addresses the challenge of generalizing in-hand reorientation to objects with complex geometries. It proposes a novel approach that aims to overcome the limitations of prior methods by employing a Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) framework coupled with an expressive object shape representation. This positions DexReMoE as an advancement in developing adaptable and precise robotic manipulation systems capable of handling a broad spectrum of real-world objects.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable robust and generalizable in-hand object reorientation for objects possessing diverse and complex geometries. The authors identify several specific problems that motivate the development of the DexReMoE framework:

1. **Generalization to Complex Shapes:** Previous studies in in-hand reorientation have often focused on single objects or simple geometric shapes like cubes or cylinders. While some progress has been made in manipulating multiple distinct objects, reliably reorienting objects with highly intricate, non-uniform, or elongated geometries remains a significant challenge. The objective is to develop a control policy capable of handling a wide range of such complex shapes.
2. **Robust Control for Dexterous Manipulation:** In-hand reorientation necessitates robust control policies to manage diverse object geometries, maintain stable grasps throughout the manipulation, and execute precise complex orientation trajectories. The goal is to achieve reliable and precise repositioning across these varying conditions.
3. **Limitations of Existing Generalization Strategies:** Current transfer learning strategies, such as fine-tuning, often require tailoring policies to individual objects. This can lead to a loss of previously acquired skills (catastrophic forgetting) and limits overall robustness when confronted with a broad array of geometries. Domain adaptation methods primarily focus on bridging the gap between simulation and reality rather than handling substantial variations in object shape. The research aims to develop a framework that mitigates these issues.
4. **Efficient and Expressive Object Feature Extraction:** Representing object shape information accurately and efficiently is crucial for generalizable manipulation. Using full point clouds can capture detailed geometry but results in high computational demands and slower learning. The motivation is to create a compact yet expressive object representation that encodes critical properties, including local surface geometry, mass distribution, and pose, without excessive computational cost. This involves integrating point-cloud-based features with object category information.

By addressing these challenges, the authors aim to equip robotic systems with advanced in-hand reorientation capabilities, moving closer to the versatility observed in human dexterous manipulation for tasks requiring flexible tool use and object rearrangement.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology introduces DexReMoE (Dexterous Reorientation Mixture-of-Experts), a framework designed to enable in-hand reorientation of general objects with complex geometries. The approach leverages a multi-stage training process within a reinforcement learning paradigm and employs a novel object representation.

**A. DexReMoE System Architecture**

The DexReMoE system is structured around a Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) framework, where a set of specialized expert policies are dynamically combined by a gating network. The overall learning process is divided into two distinct stages: base policy training and MoE policy training.

**B. Base Policy Training**

1. **Privileged Information:** At each time step `t`, privileged information `e_t` is constructed as a concatenation of the object's physical state `e_phys_t` and its shape descriptor `e_shape_t`.
 - `e_phys_t` (23 dimensions): Includes mass, center of mass, friction coefficient, uniform scale, position, orientation (quaternion), linear velocity, and angular velocity.
 - `e_shape_t` (38 dimensions): Derived from the object's point cloud and category. The object's point cloud is processed by PointNet++ to extract a 100-dimensional feature `p_t`. A learned point-cloud encoder `μ_pc` then maps `p_t` to a 32-dimensional embedding `f_t`. This `f_t` is appended with a 6-dimensional one-hot category vector `c` (representing 6 categories) to form `e_shape_t = [f_t, c]`.
 - The complete privileged vector `e_t = [e_phys_t, e_shape_t]` is then passed through an object encoder `μ_e` to produce a 66-dimensional extrinsics embedding `z_t`. This `z_t` serves as a privileged input to the policy.

2. **Observations and Outputs:** The policy input state consists of the robot's proprioceptive observation `o_t` and the privileged object encoding `z_t`.
 - `o_t`: Encodes a short temporal window of the robot's state, specifically the last three joint positions (`q_t-2`, `q_t-1`, `q_t`) and the three previously applied actions (`a_t-3`, `a_t-2`, `a_t-1`).
 - The base policy `π_base` receives `[o_t, z_t]` and outputs an action `a_t`. To enhance control smoothness, an exponential moving average is applied to the action outputs: `a_t = α a_t + (1 − α) a_t-1`.

3. **Reward Function:** The reward function (Equation 1) is composed of three terms:
 - `r1`: A positive reward `c_success` for successfully reaching the target pose within a fixed time horizon, encouraging consecutive repositioning.
 - `r2`: Penalties for the relative distance (`|δ_p|`) and orientation difference (`|δ_θ|`) between the object and the target pose, scaled by `c_dist` and `c_rot` respectively.
 - `r3`: Penalties for the magnitude of joint angular velocities (`|ω_i,t|`) above a threshold `ω_clip` and the magnitude of the action vector (`||a_t||^2`), scaled by `c_ω` and `c_a`.
 - The total reward `R = r1 + r2 + r3`. Notably, no penalty for dropping the object is included, as this was observed to suppress exploratory actions.

4. **Policy Optimization:** Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) is employed to train the base policy `π_base`, the point-cloud encoder `μ_pc`, and the object encoder `μ_e` simultaneously. Weights are shared between the policy and a critic network. Training is conducted across 32768 parallel environments in the IsaacGym simulator, with each environment initialized with a random object pose and the dexterous hand in a stable grasp configuration.

**C. MoE Training**

After the base policy training converges, the system enters the MoE training phase:

1. **Expert Knowledge:** The pre-trained `μ_pc` and `μ_e` encoders are frozen. A set of `n` expert policies, typically four (`{π_e_i}i=1^4`), are initialized with the parameters of the base policy. Each expert is then fine-tuned on data restricted to a specific shape category, allowing it to specialize. The paper details four specialists: a generalist, an airplane expert, a train expert, and a complex animal expert.

2. **Task-Specific Feature Extraction and Soft Router Formulation:**
 - The object's shape descriptor `e_shape_t` serves as the sole task-specific feature for the gating network, providing salient and discriminative aspects of each object that are invariant during reorientation.
 - A lightweight gating network `π_gate` (a two-layer MLP with ELU activation) maps `e_shape_t` to a vector of unnormalized scores (`ℓ_i`). These scores are then normalized via softmax to produce non-negative routing weights `p_i` for each expert.
 - The final action `y` is computed as a weighted sum of the outputs of the specialized experts: `y = Σ p_i * y_i`, where `y_i = f_i(x)` are the outputs of expert `i`. This dense soft gating is reported to converge more reliably than sparse Top-K routing.

**D. Experiment Setup**

- **Simulation Environment:** IsaacGym simulator with 32768 parallel environments. Both simulation and control frequencies are 60 Hz.
- **Task:** Fully mid-air object reorientation with a downward-facing palm, which is described as a more challenging configuration.
- **Robot:** Custom GX11 three-fingered dexterous hand with eleven degrees of freedom. Target orientations are randomly sampled from SO(3).
- **Object Dataset:** 150 object models from Google Scanned Objects, encompassing intricate nonconvex geometries (vehicles, footwear, animal forms). 100 objects are used for training, and 50 are reserved for out-of-distribution evaluation. Objects are scaled to 0.8 to fit the hand's workspace and undergo approximate convex decomposition (V-HACD) for collision detection.
- **Reorientation Success Criterion:** Success is declared only when four conditions are met continuously throughout the final control cycle: rotational distance to goal ≤ `τ_θ` (0.1), finger joint velocity ≤ `τ_q` (10.0), object linear velocity ≤ `τ_v` (0.04), and object angular velocity ≤ `τ_ω` (0.5). This sustained-hold criterion aims to ensure precise alignment and stable maintenance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of DexReMoE involved extensive simulation experiments, comparing its performance against nine baseline RL algorithms, both within the training distribution and on out-of-distribution objects. Performance was quantified using five metrics: minimum (S_min), maximum (S_max), average of the five worst-performing (¯S_5−), average of the five best-performing (¯S_5+), and overall average (¯S) consecutive success counts.

**A. Impact of Object Geometry on Base Policy Performance**

An initial evaluation of the base policy on 100 training objects revealed that while most objects could be reoriented successfully more than 15 consecutive times, a subset consistently failed, often achieving zero successes. Analysis of these failures indicated that objects with pronounced protrusions (e.g., airplane wings) or extreme aspect ratios (e.g., train chassis) frequently became jammed between fingers, preventing further rotation. This observation motivated the need for a specialized approach like the MoE framework to address the learning difficulties posed by such complex geometries.

**B. Ablation Experiments**

1. **Number of Expert Policies:** Comparison of configurations with 1, 4, 6, and 8 expert policies indicated that the 4-expert configuration provided the most balanced performance. It achieved superior S_min and ¯S_5− values compared to both the single-expert case and configurations with a higher number of experts. The authors hypothesize that exceeding an optimal number of experts can lead to routing inefficiencies, diminished per-expert data, imbalanced training, and overfitting, thereby undermining specialization and generalization on challenging geometries.
2. **Gating Network Inputs:** Experiments comparing the full router (using both point-cloud and category embeddings) against variants relying solely on point-cloud or category embeddings demonstrated that the combined input yielded better S_min and ¯S_5− results. This suggests that category embeddings provide general directional cues, while point-cloud data refines expert probability distributions, contributing to improved performance on complex shapes.

**C. Evaluation of the Soft MoE Policy (Within Training Distribution)**

DexReMoE was evaluated on objects from the training distribution, showing substantial performance improvements compared to baselines.
- **Worst-case Performance (S_min):** DexReMoE achieved an S_min of 6.05, which is a notable increase from the baseline values (e.g., SparseMoE: 0.52, MMoE: 3.36, DR: 0.11). This indicates improved robustness for the most challenging objects.
- **Overall Average Performance (¯S):** The method achieved an average of 19.62 consecutive successes, outperforming all nine baselines (e.g., MMoE: 18.97, SparseMoE: 19.02, PrivFeat: 15.13).
- **Ceiling Performance (S_max, ¯S_5+):** DexReMoE either matched or exceeded the top baselines, achieving S_max of 23.56 and ¯S_5+ of 23.43.
- The comparison indicated that MoE-based baselines (MMoE, SparseMoE) performed better than traditional methods (DR, ADR, PrivShape), but DexReMoE further enhanced both the performance floor and overall average.
- The paper also highlights that while baselines like DR and ADR required curriculum learning to converge under strict success criteria, DexReMoE's modular design allowed for robust learning in a single training phase without such staged progression.

**D. Out-of-Distribution Robustness**

DexReMoE's ability to generalize to object shapes not encountered during training was assessed on 50 novel objects.
- **Robustness on Complex Shapes:** On out-of-distribution objects, DexReMoE maintained its performance advantage. The S_min for the hardest objects increased to 4.11 (compared to MMoE's 3.80), and the average for the five most challenging objects (¯S_5−) reached 9.14 (compared to MMoE's 8.67).
- **Overall Generalization (¯S):** An overall average of 19.12 consecutive successes was achieved, outperforming the strongest baseline, MMoE (18.18).
- For simpler out-of-distribution objects, DexReMoE and baselines showed comparable success. However, as object complexity increased, baseline performance degraded significantly, while DexReMoE continued to deliver consistent, higher success rates. This indicates strong zero-shot performance without further training or adaptation.

**E. Clustering Analysis of Gating Network Outputs**

A t-SNE projection of the gating network's weight vectors demonstrated that the network effectively differentiated object geometries. Objects with different geometric complexities, correlated with their base policy success rates, occupied distinct regions in the embedding space. Furthermore, objects with similar shapes, such as pairs of shoes or sculpted human heads, formed tight clusters. This analysis indicates that the gating network learns to capture geometric distinctions and assign experts in a shape-dependent manner.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The DexReMoE framework represents an advancement in robotic in-hand object reorientation, particularly for objects with complex and diverse geometries.

**Significance:**
- **Enhanced Generalization:** The work introduces a method that effectively generalizes across a wide range of object shapes, including those unseen during training, without requiring additional fine-tuning. This addresses a core limitation of previous approaches that often struggled with diverse or complex geometries.
- **Improved Robustness:** By leveraging a Mixture-of-Experts architecture, DexReMoE significantly improves the worst-case performance on challenging objects, making robotic manipulation more reliable across varied scenarios.
- **Efficient Training:** The modular reinforcement learning framework allows for robust policy acquisition in a single training phase, circumventing the need for curriculum learning strategies that some baselines required to converge under strict success criteria.
- **Expressive Object Representation:** The integration of point-cloud features with object category information within a low-dimensional extrinsics embedding provides a compact yet expressive representation of object attributes, which is crucial for generalized control.
- **Demonstration of MoE Potential:** The research underscores the utility of Mixture-of-Experts networks in robotic policy learning, showing how diverse expert models can enhance training efficiency and bolster robustness.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Advancement in Dexterous Manipulation:** The capabilities demonstrated by DexReMoE could contribute to more versatile and adaptable robotic systems, enabling them to perform a broader array of manipulation tasks in unstructured environments, from industrial applications to domestic assistance.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The proposed framework, along with the authors' commitment to releasing their codebase and simulation environment, provides a foundation for further research in dexterous manipulation, particularly concerning generalization and learning from complex object geometries.
- **Applications in Tool Use and Assembly:** The ability to reliably reorient objects is fundamental for complex tasks like tool manipulation and assembly, where precise object alignment is critical. DexReMoE's robust performance could lead to more capable robotic systems in these domains.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The authors acknowledge two primary limitations in their current work:
1. **Manual Object Categorization:** The current reliance on manually labeled object categories for the shape descriptor limits the scalability of the approach. Future work aims to explore the use of multimodal large-language models to automate this labeling process.
2. **Lack of Physical Hardware Validation:** The DexReMoE framework has currently only been validated in simulation. A crucial next step for future research is to conduct real-world trials on physical hardware to confirm its performance and assess the challenges of sim-to-real transfer.
