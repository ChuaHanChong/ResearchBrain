# Learning Adaptive Solvers for Distributed Factor Graph Optimization on Matrix Lie Groups

> [!summary] Summary
> DeepCORD, developed by researchers at the University of Michigan, introduces a learning-augmented framework for distributed factor graph optimization on general matrix Lie groups. It employs a neural network to adaptively tune solver parameters, achieving improved objective values and enhanced robustness compared to hand-tuned baselines across both SE(3) pose graph optimization and SL(4) projective submap alignment tasks.

## Problem

- Large-scale geometric optimization in multi-robot/multi-device systems requires distributed solvers but faces challenges with partitioned data, limited bandwidth, and asynchronous communication.
- Existing distributed solvers rely on manually tuned parameters (e.g., step sizes, damping) that are brittle and do not generalize well across varying graph topologies, noise levels, or communication conditions.
- Current distributed solvers predominantly focus on SE(3) pose graphs, failing to address optimization over broader matrix Lie groups like SL(4) which are increasingly relevant in modern perception systems.

## Method

- **DeepCORD** deeply unfolds the iterations of CORD, a distributed solver for matrix Lie groups, transforming it into a learning-augmented framework.
- An adaptive local feedback policy, implemented as a ==Graph Position-encoding-enhanced Transformer (GPS)==, learns to predict solver parameters (mass, damping, step size) dynamically based on local graph features and communication status.
- The model is trained in a ==self-supervised== manner using ==deep unfolding==, minimizing the cost function along unrolled iterates without requiring ground-truth optimal solutions, and incorporates ==implicit differentiation== for efficient backpropagation.

## Results

- DeepCORD achieved the best or tied-best objective values on **11 out of 13** SE(3) datasets and significantly outperformed CORD and DJ baselines on all SL(4) datasets after **100 iterations**, under both synchronous and asynchronous communication.
- Analysis showed the learned policy adaptively tuned parameters: increasing communication delays led to more conservative dynamics (smaller mass, larger damping) early on, while step size adjusted to mitigate stale information, indicating context-aware parameter scheduling.
- The framework demonstrated enhanced robustness to initialization perturbations and generalized effectively to larger problem scales (**over 10,000 nodes**), consistently maintaining performance where fixed-parameter CORD often diverged.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A learned feedback policy can effectively adapt critical solver parameters (mass, damping, step size) for distributed geometric optimization, yielding more robust and efficient performance than manual tuning.
> - The learning-augmented approach successfully generalizes distributed optimization from rigid body SE(3) to broader matrix Lie groups like SL(4), expanding its applicability to complex geometric problems.
> - The learned policy dynamically adjusts solver dynamics (e.g., more conservative with increased communication delays) based on the optimization phase and communication conditions, demonstrating intelligent adaptation to network constraints.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Modern robotic perception systems frequently necessitate the solution of large-scale geometric optimization problems. These problems often involve fusing numerous noisy measurements to produce a globally consistent spatial estimate. Factor graphs serve as a common abstraction for these problems, enabling optimization across various matrix Lie groups, including rigid motions (SE(3)) for pose graph SLAM, similarity transforms for monocular reconstruction, and 3D homographies (SL(4)) for systems based on geometric foundation models.

While centralized nonlinear solvers are effective when complete graph access is available, many multi-robot, multi-device, and multi-session systems naturally generate factor graphs that are partitioned across different entities. In such distributed environments, optimization must account for factors such as data locality, limited communication bandwidth, and potentially asynchronous communication protocols.

Existing distributed solvers address this deployment context by decomposing the optimization problem across robots or graph partitions. Methods based on distributed optimization, accelerated majorization minimization, asynchronous parallel gradient descent, on-manifold consensus ADMM, and probabilistic message passing (e.g., Gaussian Belief Propagation) have demonstrated the feasibility of achieving optimization through local computation and periodic or asynchronous communication. However, a notable limitation of these solvers is their reliance on manually tuned solver parameters, such as step sizes, penalty coefficients, damping values, and restart rules. This dependency renders deployment sensitive, as optimal parameter settings often do not transfer effectively across different graph topologies, noise levels, or communication conditions. Furthermore, current distributed solvers primarily address rigid body SE(3) pose graphs, even as contemporary perception systems increasingly require optimization over a broader range of matrix Lie groups.

This work integrates into the "learning to optimize" (L2O) framework, which employs neural networks to enhance iterative solvers by learning updates, initializations, or algorithmic parameters. Within L2O, model-based approaches, often utilizing deep unfolding techniques, maintain the underlying solver's update rules while learning iteration-dependent parameters. Prior applications of L2O have targeted quadratic programming, nonlinear programming, distributed ADMM, and specific geometric estimation tasks such as two-view reconstruction, rotation averaging, and pose graph optimization. However, a gap exists in applying L2O to the complex scenario of distributed factor graph optimization on general matrix Lie groups, especially under asynchronous communication. DeepCORD aims to bridge this gap by introducing a learning-augmented solver capable of adapting parameters for broader Lie groups in distributed and asynchronous settings.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an adaptive, learning-augmented framework, named DeepCORD, for distributed factor graph optimization on general matrix Lie groups. This framework aims to enhance the robustness and performance of distributed solvers by dynamically adapting their operational parameters, thereby addressing critical limitations present in current methods.

The motivation for DeepCORD stems from several challenges inherent in modern robotic perception and multi-robot systems:
- **Sensitivity to Manual Parameter Tuning:** Current distributed optimization solvers are highly sensitive to manually selected parameters (e.g., mass, damping, step size). This sensitivity makes them brittle in deployment, as parameters tuned for one scenario may not perform well in others with different graph topologies, noise characteristics, or communication constraints. The goal is to replace this manual tuning with an intelligent, adaptive mechanism.
- **Limited Scope of Existing Solvers:** Many distributed solvers predominantly focus on Pose Graph Optimization (PGO) over the Special Euclidean Group SE(3), which represents rigid body motions. However, contemporary perception systems, particularly those involving 3D reconstruction from uncalibrated images, increasingly require optimization over broader matrix Lie groups, such as the Special Linear Group SL(4) for projective transformations. DeepCORD seeks to extend distributed optimization capabilities to these more general Lie groups.
- **Operational Challenges in Distributed Systems:** Robotic perception backends in multi-robot, multi-device, and multi-session setups naturally involve partitioned data and operate under constraints such as limited bandwidth and potentially asynchronous communication. Traditional solvers struggle to maintain optimal performance under these varied and often unpredictable conditions. The objective is to design a solver that can adapt its dynamics to both synchronous and asynchronous communication regimes.
- **Leveraging Learning-to-Optimize (L2O):** Inspired by the advancements in L2O, the authors propose that a learned feedback policy can predict adaptive solver parameters. This approach aims to maintain the principled geometric updates of the underlying solver while benefiting from the generalization capabilities of neural networks. The motivation is to achieve empirical robustness and accelerated convergence without requiring explicit supervision from optimal solutions.

In summary, DeepCORD is motivated by the need for a distributed factor graph optimizer that is robust, adaptive, and broadly applicable across various matrix Lie groups and communication environments, overcoming the practical limitations of parameter tuning in existing distributed methods.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DeepCORD's methodology is based on deeply unfolding the iterations of CORD (Continuous Riemannian Dynamics), a distributed solver, into a learning-augmented framework. This transformation enables the system to learn adaptive parameters for its optimization process.

**Problem Formulation:**
The work considers distributed factor graph optimization problems with pairwise relative measurements between group-valued variables. The objective is to minimize a sum of local cost terms, each associated with an edge (u,v) in the factor graph: $\min_{\mathbf{X} \in G^{|V|}} \sum_{(u,v) \in E} c_{uv}(X_u Z_{uv}, X_v)$.
In the distributed setting, nodes and edges are partitioned among N robots, with each robot $i$ owning a subset of variables $V_i$ and edges $E_i$.
Two specific instantiations are studied:
- **Pose Graph Optimization (PGO) over SE(3):** Variables $X_u \in SE(3)$ represent robot poses. Edge costs are defined by the squared chordal distance, $c_{uv} = \|X_u Z_{uv} - X_v\|^2_{\Omega_{uv}}$, with a precision matrix $\Omega$.
- **Projective Submap Alignment over SL(4):** Variables $X_u \in SL(4)$ represent 3D homographies. Edge costs are defined by the squared geodesic residual, $c_{uv} = \|\log(Z_{uv}^{-1} X_u^{-1} X_v)\|^2_{\Omega_{uv}}$, using the logarithm map to the Lie algebra $sl(4)$.

**Background: CORD:**
CORD solves distributed optimization over general matrix Lie groups by applying the Euler–Poincaré equation. It formulates a continuous-time system governing the evolution of body velocities $\xi_u = (X_u^{-1} \dot{X}_u) \in \mathfrak{g}$. The core equation is $M \dot{\xi} = -\text{grad}_{\mathbf{X}} C(\mathbf{X}) - D\xi + \text{ad}^*_\xi (M\xi) - \dot{M}\xi$, where $M$ is a momentum term, $-\text{grad}_{\mathbf{X}} C(\mathbf{X})$ is the body-trivialized negative gradient, $-D\xi$ is a damping term, $\text{ad}^*_\xi (M\xi)$ accounts for Lie-group geometry, and $\dot{M}\xi$ handles time-varying mass matrices.
In its distributed implementation, CORD uses block-diagonal mass and damping matrices, $M = \text{blkdiag}(M_1, \ldots, M_N)$ and $D = \text{blkdiag}(D_1, \ldots, D_N)$, where $M_i = mH_i$ and $D_i = dH_i$. $m$ and $d$ are constant scalar parameters, and $H_i$ is the Gauss-Newton Hessian approximation for robot $i$. At each iteration $k$, each robot $i$ computes an acceleration $\dot{\xi}_i^k$ and updates its body velocity $\xi_i^{k+1}$ and state $X_i^{k+1}$ using a semi-implicit Euler step with a fixed step size $\Delta t$.

**DeepCORD: Learning Adaptive Parameters via Local Feedback Policy:**
The central approach of DeepCORD is to replace the manually tuned parameters $(m, d, \Delta t)$ with an adaptive local feedback policy $\pi_\theta$. This policy's weights $\theta$ are shared across all robots and problem instances, promoting generalization.
- **Policy Input:** At each iteration $k$, robot $i$ uses its local augmented graph $G_i^k$ as input to $\pi_\theta$. This augmented graph includes the robot's own subgraph plus a two-hop neighborhood of boundary nodes, incorporating recently received states from neighboring robots.
- **Feature Engineering:**
 - **Node Attributes:** For each node $u \in V_i^k$, a vector combines the current body velocity $\xi_k$, body-trivialized local gradient $g_k$, a random-walk structural encoding (RWSE), and a communication feature $c_k$. The communication feature indicates local nodes, synchronously received nodes, or asynchronously received nodes with a scaled measure of staleness (delay $q \Delta t_j^{k-q}$).
 - **Edge Attributes:** For each edge $(u,v) \in E_i^k$, attributes include the current chordal residual $E_{uv}^k = X_u^k Z_{uv} - X_v^k$, the measurement precision $\Omega$, and a global context vector $f_k$ from the previous iteration.
 - **Graph-level Attributes:** Four scalar statistics summarize the robot's overall optimization state: RMS weighted residual for intra-robot edges, RMS weighted residual for inter-robot edges, RMS local gradient magnitude, and RMS update magnitude over local nodes.
- **Network Architecture (Figure 4):**
 - Raw features undergo symlog transformation and are embedded into a 128-dimensional latent space using small MLPs. RWSE is added to node embeddings.
 - The embedded graph is processed by a stack of four GPS (Graph Position-encoding-enhanced Transformer) layers, which combine local message passing with global multi-head attention.
 - After the GPS layers, node and edge embeddings are mean-pooled and concatenated with processed graph-level features.
 - A parameter-head MLP takes this concatenated feature vector and outputs three unconstrained scalars ($o_m, o_d, o_{\Delta t}$), which are then mapped to valid positive ranges using softplus for $m$ and $d$, and sigmoid for $\Delta t$.
- **Communication:** DeepCORD retains CORD's communication efficiency, requiring a single round of communication per iteration.

**Self-Supervised Learning via Deep Unfolding:**
DeepCORD is trained in a self-supervised manner, minimizing the cost function along the unrolled iterates without requiring ground-truth optimal solutions.
- **Loss Function:** For each training problem instance $\zeta_j$, the loss $L^{(j)}$ averages normalized local costs $\bar{C}_i(X_k)$ over all robots $i$ and $K=50$ unrolled iterations.
 - Two regularization terms are added:
 - **Monotonicity Term:** $B_i^k = \max(0, \bar{C}_i(X_k) - \bar{C}_i(X_{k-1}))$, penalizing cost increases.
 - **Damping Term:** $D_i^k = \max\{0, d_i^k - d_i^{k-1} \exp[-\beta/(t_i^{k-1} + \tau)]\}$, encouraging a decaying damping coefficient.
- **Overall Training Loss:** $L = (\sum_{j=1}^H L^{(j)})/H$, averaged over all training problems.
- **Numerical Implementation:**
 - Hessian inversion required for CORD updates is performed using a sparse preconditioned conjugate gradient (PCG) solver.
 - Gradients are backpropagated through the linear solve via implicit differentiation for numerical efficiency and to avoid prohibitive memory footprints from unrolling all PCG inner iterations.
 - For training stability, a simplified ODE, omitting the co-adjoint and time-dependent mass terms from the full CORD dynamics, is unrolled. This simplified dynamics is shown to preserve the energy dissipation property.

**Training Setup:**
- Separate DeepCORD models are trained for SE(3) and SL(4) optimization.
- Training data comprises 1,000 synthetic multi-robot pose graphs (various topologies) and 1,440 real-trajectory sequences from public datasets. Graph sizes are curated to be around 350-500 poses, with 2 to 8 robots.
- SL(4) training uses the same graph setup as PGO, with relative measurements generated according to the VGGT-SLAM 2.0 model.
- Relative edge measurements and initial node states are perturbed with Gaussian noise to simulate real-world conditions.
- The model uses the Adam optimizer with a learning rate of $5 \times 10^{-5}$ and a batch size of 16. Early stopping is employed, and specific regularization weights ($\lambda_b=1.0, \lambda_d=5.0$) and damping decay parameters ($\beta=0.5, \tau=5.0$) are used.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of DeepCORD focused on its performance on unseen problems in both SE(3) Pose Graph Optimization (PGO) and SL(4) projective submap alignment, under synchronous and asynchronous communication.

#### 4.1. Pose Graph Optimization over SE(3)

- **Initialization:** The primary results utilize chordal initialization, with additional analysis on coarse alignment initialization presented in the Appendix.
- **Synchronous Communication (Table 1):**
 - DeepCORD achieved the best or tied-best objective values on 11 out of 13 datasets after 100 synchronous iterations.
 - It generally yielded lower costs compared to other methods, including ROBO (which uses a similar 2-hop information structure) and AMM-PGO, indicating that the learned policy contributes to accelerated convergence and reduced objective residuals.
 - DeepCORD outperformed CORD, even when CORD’s parameters were hand-tuned for specific datasets (e.g., S3E). This suggests the adaptive module's ability to adjust parameters across graph topologies and initialization conditions.
 - The method demonstrated generalization to larger problem scales, achieving the lowest cost on the Rim dataset (over 10,000 nodes) despite being trained on graphs with approximately 500 nodes.
- **Asynchronous Communication (Table 1):**
 - Under asynchronous conditions (random delays up to 10 steps, 10% packet drop), DeepCORD achieved the lowest cost on the majority of datasets after 100 asynchronous iterations.
 - Improvements were particularly noted on datasets such as Grid, Dormitory1, Library1, and Library2, where DeepCORD secured lower costs than CORD by leveraging its learned adaptive parameters.
- **Performance Profiles (Figure 5):**
 - Over 500 iterations, DeepCORD exhibited the highest Area Under the Curve (AUC) for the first 100 iterations in both synchronous and asynchronous settings, indicating its efficiency in solving problems to a specified tolerance.
 - It maintained stable and competitive convergence across the full 500 iterations, despite training using only 50 unrolled iterations.
- **Analysis of Adaptive Parameters (Figure 6):**
 - An analysis of DeepCORD's predicted parameters (mass $m$, damping $d$, step size $\Delta t$) on the Rim dataset under various communication delays (2 to 10 iterations) revealed adaptive behaviors.
 - Generally, increased communication delays led to more conservative dynamics, characterized by smaller mass and larger damping in the early optimization stages to mitigate overshooting and oscillations.
 - In later stages, the policy progressively increased mass and decreased damping, indicating a shift towards accelerated updates as the solution stabilized.
 - The step size $\Delta t$ also showed adaptive behavior: larger in the early stage to reduce residuals amplified by stale information, conservatively reduced as delay increased, and then increased again in the late stage to accelerate convergence. This suggests DeepCORD learns a coupled parameter schedule adapting to both optimization phase and communication state.
- **Robustness to Initialization Perturbations (Appendix, Figure 9):**
 - DeepCORD maintained near-optimal convergence across varying initial perturbation levels on the Rim dataset, whereas CORD's optimality gap increased rapidly with poorer initialization. This indicates DeepCORD's reduced sensitivity to initialization quality.
- **Coarse Alignment Initialization (Appendix, Table 4):**
 - DeepCORD achieved the best performance on 20 out of 26 sequences with coarse alignment initialization, demonstrating consistent robustness. In contrast, CORD with fixed parameters diverged on several asynchronous datasets, underscoring the advantage of adaptive parameter tuning.
- **Runtime Analysis (Appendix, Table 5):**
 - DeepCORD's average per-iteration runtime was comparable to CORD, and in some cases, faster (e.g., on S3E datasets). The efficiency gain was attributed to the use of a sparse preconditioned conjugate gradient solver, which offset the neural network inference overhead, particularly when CORD's dense Cholesky decomposition became computationally intensive for larger local systems. Distributed Jacobi (DJ) generally had the lowest runtime due to its simpler update rule.

#### 4.2. Projective Submap Alignment over SL(4)

- **Performance (Table 2):**
 - DeepCORD significantly outperformed the CORD and DJ baselines on all three TUM RGB-D sequences (TUM1, TUM2, TUM3) in asynchronous projective submap alignment after 100 iterations, even with substantial initial perturbations. This indicates its effective generalization to Lie groups beyond SE(3).
- **Qualitative and Quantitative Reconstruction (Appendix, Figures 1, 8, Table 3):**
 - DeepCORD successfully fused local dense submaps into globally consistent maps.
 - Reconstruction evaluation (accuracy, completion, Chamfer distance) confirmed that DeepCORD achieved the best results on TUM1 and TUM3 sequences, indicating that the improved optimization objective translated to improved downstream map quality.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

DeepCORD contributes to the field of robotic perception and distributed optimization by addressing several challenges associated with large-scale geometric estimation problems. The significance and potential impact of this work include:

- **Enhanced Robustness and Adaptability:** A primary impact is the creation of a distributed solver that is less reliant on brittle, manual parameter tuning. By learning an adaptive feedback policy, DeepCORD demonstrates improved robustness across varied graph topologies, noise levels, and communication regimes (synchronous and asynchronous). This adaptability is crucial for real-world deployments where environmental and operational conditions are dynamic.
- **Broader Applicability to Geometric Problems:** The extension of distributed optimization capabilities from rigid body SE(3) pose graphs to general matrix Lie groups, as demonstrated with SL(4) projective submap alignment, significantly broadens the scope of problems that can be effectively addressed. This is particularly relevant for emerging perception systems that utilize more complex geometric representations beyond rigid transformations, such as those based on geometric foundation models.
- **Improved Performance in Distributed Settings:** DeepCORD consistently achieves lower objective values compared to conventional hand-tuned distributed baselines across multiple benchmarks and realistic operating scenarios. This indicates a practical improvement in optimization quality, leading to more accurate and consistent global estimates in multi-robot and multi-session applications.
- **Facilitating Practical System Deployment:** By automating parameter adaptation, DeepCORD reduces the expertise and effort required to configure and deploy distributed optimization backends. This streamlines the development of scalable multi-robot, multi-device, and multi-session systems that must operate under constraints of data locality, limited bandwidth, and asynchronous communication.
- **Advancement in Learning-to-Optimize (L2O) for Robotics:** This research showcases a successful application of model-based L2O via deep unfolding to a complex, real-world robotic problem. It illustrates how neural networks can learn to modulate the dynamics of principled geometric optimizers, maintaining their stability properties while enhancing their empirical performance and generalization.
- **Insights into Adaptive Dynamics:** The analysis of DeepCORD's adaptive parameters provides insights into how an intelligent system adjusts its momentum, damping, and step size in response to the optimization phase and communication status. These insights can inform future designs of both learned and hand-tuned optimization algorithms.
- **Path for Future Research:** DeepCORD opens avenues for further research, including formalizing convergence guarantees for learned adaptive dynamics, improving long-horizon convergence, and integrating more global contextual features to enhance optimality assessment. These directions could lead to even more powerful and theoretically sound distributed geometric optimization solutions.

In conclusion, DeepCORD represents a step towards developing more autonomous, robust, and versatile distributed optimization solvers, which are essential for advancing multi-robot systems and complex robotic perception applications.
