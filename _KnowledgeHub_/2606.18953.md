# Object-Centric Residual RL for Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real VLA Enhancement

> [!summary] Summary
> An object-centric residual reinforcement learning framework enhances the robustness of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, achieving zero-shot sim-to-real transfer by training a corrective policy exclusively in simulation. The approach leverages a domain-invariant observation space and robustness training, leading to a 34 percentage point improvement in real-robot task success rates across various manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, while generalizable, often exhibit brittleness in precise physical interactions due to compounding execution errors from imitation learning.
- Applying traditional reinforcement learning (RL) directly to modern VLA architectures (e.g., diffusion models) is difficult due to their non-differentiable iterative processes.
- Existing residual RL approaches struggle with sim-to-real transfer, either losing performance through distillation from privileged simulator states or facing substantial visual domain gaps with image-based policies, while real-world RL is resource-intensive and risky.

## Method

- A residual RL framework is introduced where a lightweight corrective policy is learned in simulation and combined with a frozen, real-world base VLA action.
- A ==domain-invariant observation space==, consisting of 6-DoF object poses, robot proprioception, and the base VLA action, is used for the residual policy to bridge the sim-to-real gap.
- Robustness to real-world perception noise and failures is achieved by injecting random noise into object poses and applying pose dropout during simulation training of the residual policy.

## Results

- The sim-trained residual policy achieved zero-shot transfer to a real robot, improving the average task success rate by 34 percentage points (from **42%** to **76%**) across five manipulation tasks.
- Ablation studies confirmed that object pose dropout and noise injection were critical for robust sim-to-real transfer, and the object-centric observation space outperformed image-based and distillation baselines.
- The framework enabled a VLA self-improvement loop: supervised fine-tuning of the base VLA with residual-corrected real-robot rollouts increased its average success rate from **42%** to **59%**, demonstrating the generation of higher-quality training data.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A domain-invariant observation space composed of object poses, proprioception, and base VLA actions enables zero-shot sim-to-real transfer for residual policies, bypassing visual domain adaptation challenges.
> - Training the residual policy with object pose noise injection and dropout mechanisms significantly improves its robustness to real-world pose estimation inaccuracies and failures, which is crucial for successful deployment.
> - Aligning the action distributions of simulation and real-world VLAs (by training both on identical teleoperation replays) ensures the sim-trained residual policy's corrections are relevant to the real-world VLA's behavior.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represent a significant development in robot manipulation, enabling robots to generalize across a variety of tasks by leveraging large-scale pretraining and real-world demonstrations. These models typically operate via imitation learning, where a policy learns to mimic expert behaviors. While effective for broad generalization, policies trained through imitation learning can exhibit brittleness in precise physical interactions. Small execution errors can accumulate, leading to deviations from the demonstrated trajectory and eventual task failure, particularly in situations not explicitly covered by the training data.

Reinforcement Learning (RL) is a paradigm capable of improving robotic robustness by learning through online interaction and self-correction. However, directly applying standard RL methods to modern VLA architectures, which often rely on diffusion models or flow matching for action generation, presents difficulties. These architectures typically involve iterative denoising processes that are not directly differentiable, complicating the application of traditional policy gradient methods.

Residual Reinforcement Learning (Residual RL) has emerged as an approach to integrate RL with pre-existing policies. In this framework, a lightweight corrective policy is learned on top of a frozen base policy, such as a VLA. This allows the combined system to benefit from the generalization capabilities of the base policy while the residual policy provides precise, learned corrections. However, a primary challenge for residual RL is the transfer of policies trained in simulation to real-world robotic systems (sim-to-real transfer).

Existing approaches to residual RL encounter specific limitations:
1. **Distillation-based residual RL**: These methods often train a residual policy using privileged state information available only in simulation (e.g., exact contact forces, ground truth object poses). For deployment on a real robot, this privileged policy must be distilled into an image-based student policy, which can result in performance loss due to the information compression and domain shift.
2. **Image-based sim residual RL**: Policies directly trained on image observations in simulation aim to avoid distillation. However, the visual domain gap between synthetic simulation images and real-world camera feeds (differences in lighting, textures, occlusions) can be substantial, hindering zero-shot transfer of the sim-trained residual policy to a real robot.
3. **Real-world residual RL**: This approach bypasses sim-to-real transfer challenges by training the residual policy directly on the physical robot. While eliminating domain gaps, real-world RL is resource-intensive, requiring significant time, computational power, and expert oversight for data collection. Furthermore, exploration in the real world can pose safety risks and necessitate complex infrastructure for automatic episode resets and reward detection.

This research addresses the fundamental sim-to-real dilemma prevalent in existing residual RL paradigms. It identifies that the failure of prior sim-trained residual policies to transfer effectively stems from their reliance on observation spaces that are inherently domain-dependent, whether through privileged simulator states or raw visual inputs. The work aims to bridge this gap by proposing a residual RL framework that uses a domain-invariant observation space, thereby enabling zero-shot transfer of a purely simulation-trained residual policy to a real robot. This approach seeks to enhance the robustness of VLAs without incurring the costs or limitations of prior methods, representing a progression towards more practical and scalable robot learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to investigate whether a reinforcement learning (RL) policy, trained exclusively in simulation, can enhance the robustness of real-world Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models through zero-shot transfer. This central objective is motivated by several challenges and observations in the current landscape of robot learning:

1. **Addressing VLA Brittleness**: VLAs, despite their ability to generalize across diverse manipulation tasks, often exhibit brittleness in precise physical interactions. This brittleness is attributed to the compounding of execution errors inherent in imitation-learning-based policies, leading to failures in unseen or slightly perturbed states. The motivation is to imbue VLAs with a corrective capability that can recover from these deviations and maintain task success.

2. **Overcoming Sim-to-Real Challenges in Residual RL**: Existing residual RL approaches, while conceptually promising, face a "sim-to-real dilemma."
 - **Privileged-state methods** require lossy distillation to convert simulation-specific observations into deployable image-based policies, leading to performance degradation.
 - **Image-based methods** suffer from the visual domain gap, where discrepancies between simulated and real-world visual inputs prevent effective zero-shot transfer.
 - **Real-world RL** is resource-intensive, costly, and potentially unsafe, making it impractical for widespread application.
 The key motivation here is to develop a residual RL framework that avoids these pitfalls, achieving zero-shot transfer without distillation, complex visual domain adaptation, or extensive real-world training.

3. **Designing a Domain-Invariant Observation Space**: The authors observe that the failure of previous sim-trained residual policies to transfer is often due to their reliance on observations that are inherently domain-dependent. A core motivation is to construct an observation space for the residual policy that remains consistently recoverable and informative in both simulation and reality. This observation space is designed around 6-DoF object poses, proprioceptive state, and the base VLA action, hypothesizing that these quantities exhibit smaller discrepancies across domains than privileged states or raw images.

4. **Aligning Simulation and Reality for Base VLA Actions**: To ensure that the residual policy, trained in simulation, can effectively correct the actions of a real-world VLA, it is crucial that the base VLA's action distribution in simulation closely mirrors that in reality. A specific objective is to achieve this alignment by replaying the same teleoperation demonstrations in simulation to train a simulation counterpart of the real-world VLA. This ensures the residual policy learns corrections relevant to the real-world VLA's behavior.

5. **Enhancing Robustness to Real-World Noise**: Real-world object pose estimation is inherently noisy and can occasionally fail. A motivated objective is to train the residual policy to be robust to these real-world imperfections. This is achieved through structured noise injection and dropout mechanisms applied to the object pose inputs during simulation training.

6. **Enabling VLA Self-Improvement**: Beyond direct deployment, the research aims to establish a mechanism for continuous VLA improvement. The objective is to demonstrate that successful real-robot rollouts, generated by the residual-corrected policy, can be reused as higher-quality training data to retrain the base VLA. This would enable a self-improvement loop, allowing VLAs to learn from their own enhanced performance without requiring additional human teleoperation.

In summary, the motivation is to develop a practical, scalable, and safe framework for enhancing VLA robustness by leveraging the precision of RL in a sim-to-real transferrable manner, thereby advancing robotic manipulation capabilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed object-centric residual RL framework operates in three sequential stages to enhance Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models: (1) building a simulation counterpart of the VLA, (2) training an object-centric residual policy in simulation, and (3) zero-shot deployment on the real robot. The framework also includes a mechanism for VLA self-improvement.

**Overall Pipeline:**
The core idea involves combining a base VLA's action with a residual policy's correction. The combined action $a_t$ at time $t$ is expressed as $a_t = a_{\text{base},t} \oplus \pi^{\text{sim}}_{\text{res}}(s_t)$, where $a_{\text{base},t}$ is the action from the real VLA, $\pi^{\text{sim}}_{\text{res}}$ is the sim-trained residual policy, and $s_t$ is the object-centric observation. The symbol $\oplus$ denotes component-wise action composition (addition for position and gripper, quaternion multiplication for rotation). The base VLA is frozen during residual training and deployment.

#### 3.1. Stage 1: Paired Sim/Real VLA via Teleoperation Replay

To ensure that the simulation-trained residual policy is relevant to the real-world VLA, the framework establishes aligned base policies across domains.
- Standard VLA fine-tuning involves collecting teleoperation demonstrations on a real robot to train a real-world VLA ($\pi^{\text{real}}_{\text{VLA}}$) using real (RGB, action) pairs.
- This work extends this by replaying the *identical* teleoperation actions within a simulation environment. This process generates a parallel simulation dataset, which is then used to train a simulation VLA ($\pi^{\text{sim}}_{\text{VLA}}$).
- By supervising both VLAs with the same teleoperated actions, their learned action distributions are aligned, even though they perceive different visual domains. This alignment allows the residual policy to be trained alongside $\pi^{\text{sim}}_{\text{VLA}}$ in simulation and then transfer to correct $\pi^{\text{real}}_{\text{VLA}}$ zero-shot during deployment.
- A notable aspect is that the simulation environment does not require visual realism for $\pi^{\text{sim}}_{\text{VLA}}$ training, reducing engineering effort.

#### 3.2. Stage 2: Object-Centric Residual RL

This stage focuses on training the residual policy purely in simulation. The key design choice is the observation space for $\pi^{\text{sim}}_{\text{res}}$.

- **Observation Space**: The residual policy's observation $s_t$ is constructed from three components:
 - $s^{\text{obj}}_t$: The 6-DoF pose (position and orientation) of task-relevant objects. These are manually specified for the current tasks. For multi-object tasks, poses of all relevant objects are concatenated.
 - $s^{\text{prop}}_t$: The robot's proprioceptive state (e.g., end-effector pose, gripper state).
 - $a^{\text{base}}_t$: The current base action sampled from the VLA's action chunk.
 This observation space is selected because its components are both informative for precise correction and accurately recoverable in reality, avoiding privileged simulator states or raw images which suffer from domain gaps.

- **Zero-Shot Transfer Condition**: The feasibility of zero-shot transfer relies on the residual policy being robust to noise ($\eta_t$) between simulation and reality, i.e., $s^{\text{real}}_t = s^{\text{sim}}_t + \eta_t$. The chosen observation space minimizes this noise:
 - Proprioception and base VLA action contribute negligible $\eta_t$ due to their inherent domain-invariance and aligned training, respectively.
 - Object pose, estimated in reality, is the primary source of non-negligible $\eta_t$.

- **Robust Object Pose Training**: To address real-world pose estimation noise and potential failures, two augmentation techniques are applied during simulation training:
 - **Noise Injection**: At each timestep, random noise is added to the 6-DoF object pose (position $p_{\text{obj}}$ and orientation $q_{\text{obj}}$). Position components are perturbed by $\epsilon_p$ sampled from a uniform distribution with a dynamically resampled maximum range (e.g., $U(-\tilde{\sigma}_p, \tilde{\sigma}_p)$ with $\tilde{\sigma}_p \sim U(0, \sigma^{\text{max}}_p)$). Orientation is perturbed by a small random rotation $\epsilon_q$ with a similar magnitude sampling scheme. This exposes the policy to a continuous range of small pose perturbations, mirroring real-world estimator variability.
 - **Pose Dropout**: With a probability $\rho_{\text{drop}}$, the entire object pose vector is set to zero. This forces the residual policy to learn fallback strategies relying solely on proprioception and the base action, ensuring graceful degradation if the pose estimator fails in the real world.

- **Reinforcement Learning Algorithm**: The residual policy is trained using TD3 (Twin-Delayed Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient), an off-policy algorithm known for stable optimization. Exploration is facilitated by adding clipped Gaussian noise to the combined action. The $\pi^{\text{sim}}_{\text{VLA}}$ is queried periodically (e.g., every H steps) to produce an H-length action chunk, from which $a^{\text{base}}_t$ is extracted at each timestep. The residual policy is trained with dense, shaped rewards (details in Appendix A.1) in simulation.

#### 3.3. Stage 3: Zero-Shot Deployment

Once trained, both $\pi^{\text{real}}_{\text{VLA}}$ and $\pi^{\text{sim}}_{\text{res}}$ are frozen for real-world deployment, requiring no further training or adaptation.
- The base action $a^{\text{base}}_t$ is obtained from $\pi^{\text{real}}_{\text{VLA}}$'s action chunk.
- At each timestep, a 6-DoF pose estimator (FoundationPose with SAM2 for instance segmentation) provides $\tilde{x}_{\text{obj}}$ and a confidence score $c_t$.
- If $c_t$ falls below a predefined threshold $\tau_c$, the object pose input to the residual policy is zeroed out, activating the dropout fallback strategy learned during training.
- The final action $a_t$ is the combination of $a^{\text{base}}_t$ and $\pi^{\text{sim}}_{\text{res}}(s_t)$.
- The computational overhead of the residual module is minimal (actor forward pass < 1 ms), and pose estimation runs asynchronously.

#### 3.4. VLA Self-Improvement

Beyond immediate deployment enhancement, the framework enables a self-improvement loop for the base VLA.
- Successful real-robot rollouts, collected by executing the residual-corrected policy, can be aggregated across various tasks.
- This expanded dataset of high-quality trajectories is then used for supervised fine-tuning (SFT) of the original multi-task base VLA.
- This process allows the base VLA to learn improved behaviors directly from successful executions, effectively generating higher-quality training data without requiring additional human teleoperation.

**Experimental Setup:**
The evaluation involved five tabletop manipulation tasks (Cube Lift, Pick-and-Place, Stack Cube, Close Drawer, Stand Cup Up) implemented on a Franka Research 3 (FR3) robot and in a MuJoCo simulation. GR00T-N1.5 was used as the base VLA, fine-tuned on 30 teleoperation demonstrations per task. The residual policy is a lightweight 2-layer MLP.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates the effectiveness of the object-centric residual RL framework across five manipulation tasks in both simulation and real-world deployment.

#### 4.1. Main Results

The sim-trained residual policy consistently improved the performance of the base VLA, both in simulation and on a real robot.
- **Simulation**: The residual policy improved success rates across all five tasks. The average success rate in simulation increased from 7.6/20 (base VLA) to 17.2/20 (base + residual), representing substantial gains, particularly for tasks where the base VLA initially performed poorly (Table 1, Figure 4).
- **Real Robot Zero-Shot Deployment**: On the real Franka Research 3 (FR3) robot, the simulation-trained residual policy transferred zero-shot to all five tasks without any real-world adaptation or fine-tuning. The average success rate on the real robot increased from 8.4/20 (42%) for the base VLA to 15.2/20 (76%) with the residual policy. This represents an absolute improvement of 34 percentage points (Table 1).

#### 4.2. Generalization Across VLA Architectures

The effectiveness of the residual RL framework was also demonstrated with a different base VLA model, $\pi_{0.5}$.
- When applied to $\pi_{0.5}$, the residual RL consistently improved real-robot performance on selected tasks (Close Drawer, Cube Lift, Stand Cup Up). This suggests that the object-centric observation interface is compatible with various VLA backbones (Figure 6a).

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

Ablation experiments were conducted on the real robot to determine the contribution of the proposed design elements (Table 2).
- **Robustness Training**:
 - **Pose dropout**: This mechanism, which forces the policy to learn a fallback strategy when object poses are unavailable, contributed most significantly to real-world transfer.
 - **Noise injection**: Perturbing object poses with random noise during training also contributed to robustness, particularly for tasks requiring tighter tolerances.
 - Combining both pose dropout and noise injection yielded the strongest sim-to-real transfer performance.
- **Observation Space**:
 - The object-centric observation space demonstrated superior performance compared to alternative designs.
 - An image-based baseline suffered from the visual sim-to-real gap, indicating its inadequacy for zero-shot transfer.
 - A distillation-based baseline, which distilled a privileged-state teacher into an image-based student, experienced performance loss during the distillation process.
 These results indicate that avoiding the visual domain gap through the object-centric observation space is a critical factor for effective zero-shot transfer (Table 2b, Figure 6b).

#### 4.4. Analysis of Residual Intervention

Analysis of the residual policy's behavior revealed its corrective nature:
- **Selective Correction**: The residual policy tends to steer the combined action towards the goal when the base VLA's action is misaligned. When the base action is already well-aligned with the goal, the residual contributes less, indicating selective intervention (Figure 5, Figure 7a).
- **Trajectory Efficiency**: The application of the residual policy resulted in more efficient task completion. Across successful real-robot episodes, the residual-corrected policies consistently reduced episode lengths by 9% to 22% compared to the base VLA alone (Figure 7b).

#### 4.5. VLA Self-Improvement

The study investigated the potential for the framework to enable VLA self-improvement:
- Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) of the base VLA using rollouts generated by the residual-corrected policy demonstrated improved performance.
- Retraining the base VLA with these higher-quality trajectories led to an increased average real-robot success rate (from 42% for base VLA to 59% after SFT on residual rollouts) and a reduction in average episode length (Figure 6c,d). This suggests that residual-corrected trajectories provide superior supervision for retraining the base VLA without additional teleoperation.

#### 4.6. Emergent Behaviors

Qualitative analysis revealed that residual RL induced new behaviors not present in the original demonstration data (Appendix A.9, Figure 10). Examples include:
- Pre-rotation of a cube before grasping (Cube Lift, Pick-and-Place).
- Corrective pushing to achieve a full grasp (Stack Cube).
- Sustained downward contact during drawer closing to prevent premature disengagement (Close Drawer).
These behaviors emerged from RL exploration in simulation, indicating the policy's ability to autonomously discover effective corrective strategies.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents an object-centric residual reinforcement learning framework that addresses a notable challenge in robot learning: the zero-shot sim-to-real transfer of corrective policies for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. The work offers several contributions and potential impacts:

1. **Enabling Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer**: The primary significance lies in demonstrating a viable method for transferring a residual policy trained purely in simulation to a real robot without any real-world reinforcement learning, fine-tuning, or distillation. This capability circumvents the high costs, safety concerns, and engineering complexities associated with real-world RL or the performance degradation often seen with distillation-based methods. This contributes to making advanced robotic policies more practical for real-world deployment.

2. **Robustness Enhancement for VLAs**: The framework enhances the robustness and precision of VLAs in physical interactions. By learning a corrective policy, the system can mitigate compounding execution errors inherent in imitation learning, leading to significantly improved task success rates on a physical robot. This is particularly relevant as VLAs are deployed in increasingly diverse and unstructured environments where precise manipulation is critical.

3. **Introduction of a Domain-Invariant Observation Space**: The design of an object-centric observation space, comprising 6-DoF object poses, proprioceptive state, and the base VLA action, is a key methodological contribution. This approach reduces the sim-to-real domain gap by focusing on quantities that are consistently recoverable across domains, rather than relying on visually distinct image data or privileged simulator states. This design principle could influence future work on transferable robot policies.

4. **Practical Robustness Training Mechanisms**: The integration of pose noise injection and pose dropout during simulation training provides practical strategies for building policies robust to real-world perception inaccuracies and failures. These mechanisms equip the residual policy with the ability to handle typical noise from pose estimators and to gracefully degrade performance when pose information is unreliable, which is a common challenge in real-world robotics.

5. **Autonomous VLA Self-Improvement Loop**: The proposed self-improvement mechanism, where successful residual-corrected real-robot rollouts are used to retrain the base VLA, offers a path toward autonomous data generation and continuous policy refinement. This could reduce the reliance on human teleoperation for generating high-quality training data, potentially accelerating the development of more capable and adaptive VLA models. This concept moves towards robots learning and improving from their own experiences in a structured manner.

6. **Compatibility with Diverse VLA Architectures**: The demonstration of compatibility with different VLA backbones suggests that the residual RL framework can be broadly applied to various base VLA models, enhancing their performance without requiring modifications to their core architecture or training pipelines. This modularity could extend the utility of existing and future VLA research.

Despite its contributions, the paper acknowledges certain limitations, which also highlight areas for future research. These include the reliance on real-time 6-DoF pose tracking, which can fail under occlusion or heavy clutter; the manual specification of task-relevant objects; the framework's current inability to bridge the dynamics gap between simulation and reality (e.g., differences in friction or gripper compliance); and the inherent limitation of a residual architecture to correct only mild deviations, not catastrophic failures. Addressing these limitations could further expand the framework's applicability to more complex, open-world manipulation scenarios and higher-precision tasks.

Overall, this work contributes to the development of more robust, autonomous, and practical robotic manipulation systems by synergistically combining the generalization capabilities of VLAs with the precision of RL through an innovative sim-to-real transfer paradigm.
