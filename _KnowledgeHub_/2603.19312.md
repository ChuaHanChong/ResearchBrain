# LeWorldModel: Stable End-to-End Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture from Pixels

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Mila, Université de Montréal, New York University, Samsung SAIL, and Brown University developed LeWorldModel (LeWM), a Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture that achieves stable, end-to-end learning of latent world models from raw pixels using a simplified two-term objective. This approach enables competitive control performance across various tasks while demonstrating up to 48x faster planning speeds compared to existing methods and fostering an emergent understanding of physical dynamics.

## Problem

- Existing Joint Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) for world models are prone to representation collapse, where the model learns uninformative embeddings.
- Solutions to prevent representation collapse in JEPAs often introduce complex multi-term loss functions, heuristic regularization, or reliance on pre-trained encoders, leading to training instability and increased complexity.
- Prior end-to-end JEPA methods suffer from numerous hyperparameters and computational inefficiency, hindering their applicability in real-time or resource-constrained settings.

## Method

- **LeWorldModel** (**LeWM**) employs a simplified ==two-term training objective== consisting of a ==mean-squared error (MSE)== prediction loss and a ==Sketched-Isotropic-Gaussian Regularizer (SIGReg)== to prevent ==representation collapse==.
- The architecture uses a ==Vision Transformer (ViT-Tiny)== encoder to map raw pixels to a compact ==latent space== and a Transformer predictor with ==Adaptive Layer Normalization (AdaLN)== to forecast future latent states, all trained ==end-to-end== without stop-gradients or EMAs.
- For planning, **LeWM** utilizes ==Model Predictive Control (MPC)== with the ==Cross-Entropy Method (CEM)== to optimize action sequences in its learned latent space, aiming to minimize the distance to a goal latent state.

## Results

- LeWM achieved competitive control performance across tasks like Push-T and Reacher, outperforming end-to-end baselines (e.g., **18%** higher success rate on Push-T than PLDM) and remaining competitive with foundation-model baselines.
- The model demonstrated up to **48x** faster planning speeds than foundation-model-based world models, completing a full planning cycle in under one second, significantly improving efficiency for real-time control.
- LeWM's training objective is robust and simple, with only one primary tunable hyperparameter (regularization weight λ) that showed stable performance across a wide range of values.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The two-term objective with SIGReg provides provable anti-collapse guarantees, leading to significantly more stable and monotonic training dynamics compared to prior multi-term approaches.
> - LeWM's latent space inherently captures meaningful physical structure, allowing the recovery of physical quantities and the detection of physically implausible events without explicit supervision.
> - The model exhibits an emergent property of temporal latent path straightening, where consecutive latent velocity vectors become increasingly collinear during training, suggesting a natural acquisition of smooth dynamics.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of autonomous agents capable of learning diverse skills directly from raw sensory inputs, without relying on hand-engineered state representations, is a central objective in artificial intelligence research. World Models (WMs) represent a significant class of methods designed to achieve this by learning to predict the consequences of actions within an environment. Successful WMs enable agents to plan and refine behaviors through internal simulation.

Joint Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) are a specific approach within the WM paradigm. JEPAs aim to learn compact, low-dimensional latent representations of observations and model temporal dynamics by predicting future latent states. This contrasts with generative WMs that explicitly model dynamics in pixel space, or other approaches that require reward signals or privileged state access.

However, a persistent challenge in training JEPA models is the susceptibility to representation collapse, where the model produces trivial, undifferentiated latent representations. Existing strategies to counter this issue frequently involve complex multi-term loss functions with numerous hyperparameters, heuristic regularizations (such as stop-gradient or exponential moving averages), or reliance on external information like pre-trained encoders or auxiliary supervision. These approaches can introduce training instability, limit the expressivity of learned representations, or significantly increase overall training complexity.

The paper categorizes existing latent world model approaches based on their characteristics:
- **End-to-End Methods (e.g., PLDM):** These methods learn the encoder and predictor jointly from pixels without pre-trained representations or heuristics. However, they often entail many hyperparameters and may lack robust guarantees against representation collapse.
- **Foundation-Model-Based Methods (e.g., DINO-WM):** These approaches address collapse by freezing a pre-trained vision encoder (e.g., DINOv2), thereby foregoing end-to-end learning. Their effectiveness is bounded by the knowledge acquired during pre-training.
- **Task-Specific Methods (e.g., Dreamer, TD-MPC):** These methods typically require reward signals or access to privileged state information, making them less suitable for generic, task-agnostic world modeling in a reward-free setting.

LeWorldModel (LeWM) is positioned as an advancement that combines the strengths of these categories while mitigating their limitations. It aims to provide an end-to-end, task-agnostic, pixel-based, and reward/reconstruction-free solution that offers stability and a simplified training objective with robust anti-collapse guarantees, addressing a key fragility in current JEPA implementations.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce LeWorldModel (LeWM), a Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture that achieves stable end-to-end training of latent world models directly from raw pixel observations.

This objective is motivated by several identified limitations and challenges within the existing landscape of JEPA models:
- **Prevalence of Representation Collapse:** A fundamental problem in JEPA training is representation collapse, where the model learns to map diverse inputs to nearly identical latent representations to trivially satisfy the prediction objective. This renders the learned representations unusable for downstream tasks.
- **Fragility of Existing Solutions:** Current methods to prevent collapse are often complex and prone to their own issues:
 - **Complex Loss Functions:** Many approaches rely on multi-objective loss functions with numerous tunable hyperparameters. This increases training complexity, makes hyperparameter optimization difficult, and can lead to unstable training dynamics due to conflicting gradients. The paper notes that existing end-to-end alternatives can require six or more hyperparameters.
 - **Heuristic Regularization:** Techniques such as exponential moving averages (EMA) or stop-gradient updates are commonly employed to stabilize training and avoid collapse. However, the theoretical understanding of these heuristics is often limited, and they may not correspond to the minimization of a well-defined objective, potentially introducing further instability.
 - **Reliance on External Information:** Some methods mitigate collapse by using pre-trained encoders (e.g., foundation models) or auxiliary supervision. While effective, this limits the expressivity of the learned representations to the knowledge encoded in the pre-trained model and foregoes truly end-to-end learning from raw pixels.
- **Need for Efficiency and Scalability:** Developing world models that are efficient to train (e.g., on single GPUs in a few hours) and enable fast planning is crucial for their practical applicability, especially in real-time control scenarios.

Given these motivations, LeWM aims to fulfill the following specific goals:
- To propose an end-to-end JEPA method that trains stably from raw pixels without relying on heuristics like stop-gradient or EMA, or pre-trained representations.
- To utilize a simple, principled two-term objective function (a prediction loss and an anti-collapse regularizer) that is robust across architectures and hyperparameter choices, significantly reducing the number of tunable loss hyperparameters (to effectively one).
- To demonstrate that this simplified approach can achieve competitive control performance across diverse 2D and 3D tasks with a compact model (15M parameters), enabling substantially faster planning compared to foundation-model-based world models.
- To evaluate the physical understanding encoded within LeWM's latent space through probing of physical quantities and a violation-of-expectation test for detecting physically implausible events.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

LeWorldModel (LeWM) is structured as a Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) designed for learning latent world models. Its core methodology focuses on a streamlined training procedure and a principled anti-collapse mechanism.

#### 3.1. Learning the Latent World Model

- **Offline Dataset:** LeWM operates in a fully offline and reward-free setting. It is trained exclusively on unannotated trajectories consisting of raw pixel observations (`o_1:T`) and corresponding actions (`a_1:T`). The goal is to learn generic, task-agnostic representations of environment dynamics, rather than optimizing behavior for specific tasks. Trajectories can be collected from various behavior policies, provided they adequately cover the environment dynamics. A frame-skip of 5 is applied for efficiency, and sub-trajectories of length 4 (corresponding to 4 frames and 4 action blocks) are used with a batch size of 128 and 224x224 pixel resolution.

- **Model Architecture:** LeWM consists of two main components: an encoder and a predictor.
 - **Encoder:** The encoder (`enc_θ`) maps a raw frame observation `o_t` to a compact, low-dimensional latent representation `z_t`. It is implemented as a Vision Transformer (ViT-Tiny, approximately 5 million parameters) with a patch size of 14, 12 layers, 3 attention heads, and hidden dimensions of 192. The latent embedding `z_t` is derived from the [CLS] token embedding of the last layer, followed by a 1-layer MLP with Batch Normalization. This projection step is crucial because the ViT's final Layer Normalization can interfere with the anti-collapse objective.
 - **Predictor:** The predictor (`pred_ϕ`) models environment dynamics in the latent space by autoregressively predicting the next latent state (`ẑ_{t+1}`) given the current latent state `z_t` and action `a_t`. It is a transformer-based model (6 layers, 16 attention heads, 10% dropout, approximately 10 million parameters). Actions are integrated through Adaptive Layer Normalization (AdaLN) at each layer, with AdaLN parameters initialized to zero for training stability. The predictor takes a history of `N` frame representations and uses temporal causal masking. A projector network, similar to the encoder's, follows the predictor.

- **Training Objective:** LeWM's training objective (`L_LeWM`) is a sum of two terms: a prediction loss and a regularization loss. All components of the world model are learned jointly in an end-to-end manner, without stop-gradient, exponential moving averages, or additional stabilization heuristics.
 - **Prediction Loss (`L_pred`):** This is a Mean Squared Error (MSE) between the predicted next embedding (`ẑ_{t+1}`) and the ground-truth next embedding (`z_{t+1}`). It incentivizes the encoder to produce predictable latent representations for the predictor.
 `L_pred = ||ẑ_{t+1} - z_{t+1}||^2_2`
 - **Regularization Loss (`SIGReg(Z)`):** To prevent representation collapse, the Sketched-Isotropic-Gaussian Regularizer (SIGReg) is employed. SIGReg encourages the latent embeddings to conform to an isotropic Gaussian target distribution. This is achieved by:
 1. Projecting the tensor of latent embeddings `Z` onto `M` randomly sampled unit-norm directions (`u^(m)`).
 2. Applying the univariate Epps–Pulley test statistic (`T(·)`) along each of these one-dimensional projections (`h^(m)`).
 The Cramér–Wold theorem provides the theoretical basis, stating that matching all one-dimensional marginals is equivalent to matching the full joint distribution asymptotically.
 The overall SIGReg term is `SIGReg(Z) = (1/M) * Σ_{m=1 to M} T(h^(m))`.
 - **Complete Objective:** `L_LeWM = L_pred + λ SIGReg(Z)`.
 The method introduces two hyperparameters: the number of random projections `M` (typically 1024) and the regularization weight `λ` (typically 0.1). `M` has been empirically shown to have a negligible impact on performance, making `λ` the primary effective hyperparameter for tuning, which can be optimized efficiently via bisection search.

#### 3.2. Latent Planning

At inference time, LeWM performs trajectory optimization directly in its learned latent space, using Model Predictive Control (MPC) with the Cross-Entropy Method (CEM) solver.
- **Initialization:** Given an initial observation `o_1` and a goal observation `o_g`, the encoder generates their respective latent embeddings, `z_1` and `z_g`.
- **Action Optimization:** CEM iteratively optimizes a candidate action sequence `a_1:H` over a planning horizon `H`. In each iteration, CEM samples a pool of action sequences, evaluates their cost using the world model, selects the best-performing "elite" sequences, and updates the sampling distribution parameters based on these elites.
- **Cost Function:** The objective for CEM is to minimize a terminal latent goal-matching cost: `C(ẑ_H) = ||ẑ_H - z_g||^2_2`, where `ẑ_H` is the predicted latent state at the end of the rollout, and `z_g` is the latent embedding of the goal observation.
- **Latent Rollouts:** The predictor is used autoregressively to roll out future latent states (`ẑ_{t+1} = pred_ϕ(ẑ_t, a_t)`), with `ẑ_1 = enc_θ(o_1)`. The world model's parameters remain fixed during planning.
- **Model Predictive Control:** To mitigate the accumulation of prediction errors over long horizons, an MPC strategy is employed. Only the first `K` planned actions are executed in the environment, after which the process is repeated by replanning from the updated observation. This iterative replanning helps adapt to real-world deviations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of LeWorldModel (LeWM) encompasses its performance in control tasks, training stability, and its ability to capture physical understanding in the latent space.

#### 4.1. Efficiency and Control Performance

- **Planning Speed:** LeWM demonstrates significant computational efficiency, achieving planning speeds up to 48 times faster than foundation-model-based world models like DINO-WM. Full planning completes in under one second, bridging the gap towards real-time control applications. This efficiency is partly attributed to LeWM's compact size (15M parameters) and its ability to encode observations with substantially fewer tokens compared to DINO-WM.
- **Task Performance:** LeWM exhibits competitive control performance across a diverse set of 2D and 3D tasks, including PushT, Reacher, OGBench-Cube, and Two-Room environments.
 - On challenging tasks like **PushT**, LeWM (96% success rate) significantly outperforms PLDM (78%) and DINO-WM (74%). Notably, LeWM, operating solely from pixels, even surpasses DINO-WM when the latter has access to additional proprioceptive information (DINO-WM+prop, 92%).
 - For the **Reacher** task, LeWM (86%) also outperforms PLDM (78%) and DINO-WM (79%).
 - On **OGBench-Cube**, DINO-WM (86%) slightly outperforms LeWM (74%), which the authors suggest might be due to the higher visual complexity and 3D nature of the environment, posing a greater challenge for LeWM's encoder training.
 - In the simpler **Two-Room** environment, PLDM (100%) and DINO-WM (97%) show higher success rates than LeWM (87%). This is attributed to the low diversity and intrinsic dimensionality of the dataset, making it challenging for SIGReg to enforce a high-dimensional isotropic Gaussian prior effectively, potentially leading to a less structured latent representation for this specific context.

#### 4.2. Training Stability and Simplicity

- **Hyperparameter Simplicity:** Ablation studies confirm that the internal parameters of SIGReg (number of random projections, integration knots) have a negligible impact on downstream performance. This effectively reduces LeWM's tunable loss hyperparameters from six (in the case of PLDM) to a single one: the regularization weight `λ`. This simplification enables efficient hyperparameter search, such as a bisection search (O(log n)), compared to the polynomial time complexity (O(n^6)) often required for multi-term objectives. The method also shows robustness to the choice of `λ` across a wide range of values.
- **Smooth Convergence:** Training loss curves demonstrate that LeWM's two-term objective exhibits smooth and monotonic convergence. The prediction loss decreases steadily, while the SIGReg regularization term drops sharply in early training before plateauing, indicating a rapid convergence to the isotropic Gaussian target. In contrast, PLDM's seven-term objective displays noisy and non-monotonic behavior across its various components, highlighting LeWM's improved stability.
- **Low Training Variance:** Retraining the model with multiple random seeds on PushT shows consistently high success rates with low variance, indicating that the training procedure is stable and reproducible.
- **Architecture Agnosticism:** LeWM achieves competitive performance with both a ViT and a ResNet-18 encoder backbone, suggesting its design is largely agnostic to the specific choice of vision encoder architecture.
- **Effect of Predictor Dropout:** A moderate dropout rate (0.1) in the predictor significantly improves control performance, indicating that regularization contributes to generalization.
- **Decoder Impact:** Adding a reconstruction loss during training does not improve downstream control performance and can even slightly decrease it, suggesting that the JEPA objective effectively captures task-relevant information without needing to reconstruct pixel-level details.

#### 4.3. Quantifying Physical Understanding in Latent Space

- **Probing Physical Quantities:** Linear and non-linear probes are used to assess the recoverability of physical quantities from LeWM's latent representations.
 - On **PushT**, LeWM consistently outperforms PLDM in predicting quantities like agent location, block location, and block angle, while remaining competitive with DINO-WM.
 - On **TwoRoom**, LeWM matches or outperforms PLDM in probing agent position, and both substantially outperform DINO-WM, suggesting comparable latent space quality despite LeWM's lower planning performance in this specific environment.
 - On **OGBench-Cube**, LeWM performs comparably to or better than PLDM for most properties, particularly positional ones (block position, end-effector position). DINO-WM often maintains an advantage on dynamic and rotational properties (joint velocity, end-effector yaw), potentially due to its extensive foundation-model pre-training. A shared limitation across all methods is the difficulty in recovering fine-grained block orientation.
- **Decoding Latent Space:** Despite never being trained with a reconstruction loss, a decoder (trained *a posteriori*) is able to reconstruct visual scenes from LeWM's low-dimensional latent embeddings. This confirms that the latent space retains sufficient information about the underlying physical state.
- **Latent Space Visualization:** t-SNE visualizations of the PushT latent space suggest that LeWM's representations capture the spatial structure of the environment, preserving neighborhood relationships and relative positions.
- **Temporal Latent Path Straightening:** LeWM's latent trajectories exhibit increasing "temporal straightness" (higher cosine similarity between consecutive latent velocity vectors) as an emergent phenomenon during training, even without explicit temporal regularization. This property is found to be higher in LeWM than in PLDM, despite PLDM incorporating a dedicated temporal smoothness term.
- **Violation-of-Expectation (VoE):** This framework evaluates the model's ability to detect physically implausible events. LeWM consistently assigns higher "surprise" (prediction error) to trajectories where objects are physically teleported compared to unperturbed trajectories or those with only visual perturbations (e.g., color changes). This indicates a stronger sensitivity to violations of physical continuity than to mere visual alterations across TwoRoom, PushT, and OGBench-Cube environments, suggesting that LeWM captures a meaningful understanding of physical regularities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of LeWorldModel (LeWM) represents a notable advancement in the field of latent world modeling, particularly within the Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) paradigm. Its significance and potential impact stem from addressing critical limitations of prior approaches and offering a more robust and efficient framework.

#### 5.1. Core Contributions and Impact

- **Stable and Principled JEPA Training:** LeWM offers a stable, end-to-end method for learning JEPAs from raw pixels, resolving the challenge of representation collapse through a principled, scalable, and simple anti-collapse regularizer (SIGReg). This moves away from complex multi-term losses and heuristic-based solutions, making JEPA training more reliable and accessible.
- **Simplified Training Objective:** By reducing the number of effective tunable hyperparameters from six (in prior end-to-end JEPAs) to effectively one (`λ`), LeWM significantly simplifies the training process. This streamlines hyperparameter tuning, potentially accelerating research and deployment cycles.
- **Efficiency and Scalability:** LeWM's compact size (15M parameters) and ability to train on a single GPU in a few hours, coupled with its 48x faster planning speeds compared to foundation-model-based approaches, make it a more practical solution for real-world applications requiring efficient online planning and control. This efficiency democratizes access to advanced world modeling capabilities.
- **Robustness and Generalization:** The method's demonstrated stability across architectures (ViT, ResNet-18) and robustness to hyperparameter choices suggest a higher degree of generalizability and reliability in diverse operational contexts.
- **Emergent Physical Understanding:** LeWM's ability to implicitly learn meaningful physical structures in its latent space—evidenced by probing physical quantities, successful scene reconstruction from latent embeddings, and robust detection of physically implausible events—is crucial. This indicates that the learned representations are not merely abstract codes but capture aspects of the underlying physics, which is foundational for intuitive reasoning and adaptive behavior in embodied AI.

#### 5.2. Potential Applications and Future Directions

LeWM's capabilities lay groundwork for numerous applications in reinforcement learning, robotics, and intelligent agents:
- **Enhanced Robotic Control:** Faster planning speeds and robust world models could enable more agile and intelligent robotic systems capable of operating in complex, dynamic environments with minimal human intervention.
- **Offline Reinforcement Learning:** The reward-free and offline nature of LeWM makes it a valuable tool for learning from fixed datasets, potentially allowing agents to generate synthetic experiences and evaluate counterfactual actions, improving sample efficiency in offline RL settings.
- **General-Purpose AI Agents:** By fostering a deeper, emergent understanding of physical world dynamics, LeWM contributes to the development of more general-purpose AI agents that can adapt to unforeseen situations and reason about consequences in a more human-like manner.

#### 5.3. Identified Limitations and Future Work

The paper also outlines several limitations that point to future research directions:
- **Limited Planning Horizon:** Current planning in LeWM remains restricted to short horizons. Future work could explore hierarchical world modeling approaches to enable longer-horizon reasoning and planning.
- **Data Diversity Requirements:** LeWM's performance in environments with low data diversity and intrinsic dimensionality (e.g., Two-Room) can be suboptimal. The SIGReg regularizer, by enforcing a high-dimensional Gaussian prior, might struggle in such scenarios. Pre-training LeWM on large, diverse video datasets could provide stronger priors and reduce the need for extensive domain-specific data.
- **Dependence on Action Labels:** The current method relies on action labels in the offline datasets. Future research could investigate inverse dynamics modeling to alleviate this dependency, further enhancing the model's autonomy and applicability in contexts where action labels are scarce or difficult to obtain.

In summary, LeWM represents a significant step towards more stable, efficient, and physically aware end-to-end latent world models, addressing key challenges that have hindered the practical deployment of JEPAs. Its impact is poised to extend across various domains requiring intelligent agents with robust predictive capabilities.
