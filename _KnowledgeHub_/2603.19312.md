# LeWorldModel: Stable End-to-End Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture from Pixels

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Mila, Université de Montréal, New York University, Samsung SAIL, and Brown University developed LeWorldModel (LeWM), a Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture that achieves stable, end-to-end learning of latent world models from raw pixels using a simplified two-term objective. This approach enables competitive control performance across various tasks while demonstrating up to 48x faster planning speeds compared to existing methods and fostering an emergent understanding of physical dynamics.

## Problem

- Existing Joint Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) for world models are prone to representation collapse, where the model learns uninformative embeddings.
- Solutions to prevent representation collapse in JEPAs often introduce complex multi-term loss functions, heuristic regularization, or reliance on pre-trained encoders, leading to training instability and increased complexity.
- Prior end-to-end JEPA methods suffer from numerous hyperparameters and computational inefficiency, hindering their applicability in real-time or resource-constrained settings.

## Method

- **LeWorldModel** (**LeWM**) employs a simplified ==two-term training objective== consisting of a ==mean-squared error (MSE)== prediction loss and a ==Sketched-Isotropic-Gaussian Regularizer (SIGReg)== to prevent ==representation collapse==.
- The architecture uses a ==Vision Transformer (ViT-Tiny)== encoder to map raw pixels to a compact ==latent space== and a Transformer predictor with ==Adaptive Layer Normalization (AdaLN)== to forecast future latent states, all trained ==end-to-end== without stop-gradients or EMAs.
- For planning, **LeWM** utilizes ==Model Predictive Control (MPC)== with the ==Cross-Entropy Method (CEM)== to optimize action sequences in its learned latent space, aiming to minimize the distance to a goal latent state.

## Results

- LeWM achieved competitive control performance across tasks like Push-T and Reacher, outperforming end-to-end baselines (e.g., **18%** higher success rate on Push-T than PLDM) and remaining competitive with foundation-model baselines.
- The model demonstrated up to **48x** faster planning speeds than foundation-model-based world models, completing a full planning cycle in under one second, significantly improving efficiency for real-time control.
- LeWM's training objective is robust and simple, with only one primary tunable hyperparameter (regularization weight λ) that showed stable performance across a wide range of values.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The two-term objective with SIGReg provides provable anti-collapse guarantees, leading to significantly more stable and monotonic training dynamics compared to prior multi-term approaches.
> - LeWM's latent space inherently captures meaningful physical structure, allowing the recovery of physical quantities and the detection of physically implausible events without explicit supervision.
> - The model exhibits an emergent property of temporal latent path straightening, where consecutive latent velocity vectors become increasingly collinear during training, suggesting a natural acquisition of smooth dynamics.
