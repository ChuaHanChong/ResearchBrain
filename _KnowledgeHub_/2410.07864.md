# RDT-1B: a Diffusion Foundation Model for Bimanual Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Tsinghua University developed RDT-1B, the first diffusion-based foundation model for bimanual robotic manipulation, featuring 1.2 billion parameters. This model achieved an average 56% improvement in success rates over state-of-the-art baselines across diverse bimanual tasks, showcasing strong zero-shot generalization to unseen objects and scenes, and few-shot learning capabilities.

## Problem

- Developing generalizable bimanual manipulation policies is challenging due to the inherent complexity of coordinating two arms for diverse tasks.
- Data scarcity for dual-arm robot systems hinders the training of data-hungry foundation models.
- Bimanual tasks often exhibit multi-modal action distributions, which traditional imitation learning struggles to capture, potentially leading to physically infeasible "average" actions.

## Method

- Introduces **RDT-1B**, a 1.2-billion-parameter ==Diffusion Transformer (DiT)== model, adapted with ==QKNorm==, ==RMSNorm==, ==MLP Decoder==, and ==Alternating Condition Injection== for stable large-scale training on robotic data.
- Employs a "==Physically Interpretable Unified Action Space==" to map heterogeneous robot actions into a common 128-dimensional representation, enabling extensive ==cross-robot pre-training==.
- Utilizes a ==pre-training and fine-tuning pipeline==: pre-trains on over 1 million trajectories from 46 diverse multi-robot datasets, then fine-tunes on a self-collected bimanual dataset of 6,000 trajectories.

## Results

- RDT-1B achieved an average **56%** improvement in success rates compared to state-of-the-art baselines across a wide range of challenging bimanual tasks on a real ALOHA dual-arm robot.
- The model demonstrated exceptional zero-shot generalization, successfully performing tasks with unseen objects, scenes, and novel language instructions (e.g., precise pouring volumes) without prior exposure.
- RDT-1B exhibited strong few-shot learning, adapting to complex new skills from as few as **1–5** demonstrations, significantly outperforming baselines which failed.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Diffusion models are effective for capturing and sampling from multi-modal action distributions in complex bimanual tasks, avoiding "average" and infeasible actions.
> - Large-scale cross-robot pre-training on diverse datasets, combined with a unified action space, is crucial for overcoming data scarcity and enabling robust generalization in bimanual manipulation.
> - Architectural adaptations to Diffusion Transformers (e.g., QKNorm, RMSNorm, non-linear MLP decoder, alternating condition injection) are essential for stable scaling and handling the unique, high-frequency, and non-linear properties of robotic data.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Bimanual manipulation is a critical capability for robots to perform complex real-world tasks that often require the coordinated use of two arms, akin to human interaction with the environment. However, the development of generalizable bimanual manipulation policies faces significant hurdles. Historically, approaches have relied on task-specific primitives or were limited to small-scale models and data, leading to narrow generalization capabilities unsuitable for complex, real-world scenarios.

The broader research landscape in AI has seen a paradigm shift with the emergence of "foundation models" in fields like natural language processing (e.g., GPT series, Llama) and computer vision (e.g., CLIP, Segment Anything). These models, trained on vast datasets, exhibit remarkable generalization and few-shot learning abilities. This success has inspired researchers in robotics to pursue similar "generalist" models capable of performing a wide range of tasks and generalizing to unseen situations.

However, extending the foundation model paradigm to *bimanual* manipulation introduces unique and amplified challenges:
1. **Data Scarcity:** Dual-arm robot systems are expensive and complex, making large-scale data collection prohibitive compared to single-arm setups. This fundamentally clashes with the data-hungry nature of foundation models.
2. **Architectural Limitations:** Bimanual tasks inherently involve a high-dimensional action space and lead to *multi-modal action distributions* (multiple valid ways to achieve a goal, chosen by human demonstrators). Traditional imitation learning methods, which might average these modes, often yield infeasible or suboptimal actions. Furthermore, foundation models require architectures that can scalably process heterogeneous multi-modal inputs (vision, language, proprioception).

Prior efforts in robotics foundation models, such as RT-1, RT-2, OpenVLA, and Octo, have primarily focused on unimanual manipulation or have addressed cross-robot generalization by limiting themselves to subsets of robots with similar action spaces or by discarding valuable heterogeneous data. While some, like Octo, have explored diffusion models for continuous control, they haven't scaled to the magnitude or specifically addressed the complexities of bimanual manipulation at the foundation model level.

"RDT-1B" directly confronts these gaps. It positions itself as a *pioneering* effort to build the first diffusion-based foundation model explicitly designed for bimanual manipulation. By leveraging cross-robot pre-training on the largest multi-robot dataset to date and introducing a novel "Physically Interpretable Unified Action Space," RDT-1B aims to overcome data scarcity and heterogeneous action spaces. Architecturally, it adapts Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) to specifically handle the multi-modality and unique properties of robotic data, scaling to an unprecedented 1.2 billion parameters for a diffusion-based robot model. This work represents a significant step towards developing truly general-purpose robotic policies capable of dexterous, adaptable bimanual interaction in diverse and unseen environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a highly generalizable and adaptable bimanual manipulation policy capable of performing diverse real-world tasks. This overarching goal is driven by addressing two fundamental and interconnected challenges in robotics:

1. **Addressing Data Scarcity for Foundation Models:**
 - **Motivation:** Training data for dual-arm robotic systems is inherently scarce due to the high costs associated with hardware, maintenance, and expert human demonstrations. This directly conflicts with the massive data requirements typically associated with training large foundation models.
 - **Objective:** To overcome this limitation by significantly amplifying the available data volume. This involves a **cross-robot pre-training strategy**, where the model first learns from a vast collection of demonstrations across many different robots (mostly single-arm) and then fine-tunes on a smaller, high-quality dataset specific to the target dual-arm robot. This necessitates a mechanism to unify heterogeneous action representations across diverse robots.

2. **Developing a Powerful and Scalable Architecture for Bimanual Manipulation:**
 - **Motivation:** Bimanual tasks often present highly multi-modal action distributions (i.e., multiple distinct ways for two arms to achieve a goal). Traditional deterministic policies struggle to capture this, potentially leading to "average" actions that are physically infeasible. Moreover, a foundation model needs to seamlessly integrate various input modalities (vision, language, proprioception) and scale stably to billions of parameters while handling the unique characteristics of robotic data (nonlinearity, high-frequency changes, unstable numerical ranges).
 - **Objective:** To design the **Robotics Diffusion Transformer (RDT)**, an innovative diffusion-based architecture capable of:
 - **Capturing Multi-modality:** Effectively representing and sampling from complex, multi-modal action distributions.
 - **Scalability and Heterogeneous Input Handling:** Processing diverse sensory inputs (text, images, robot states) and scaling to a large number of parameters for stable training on massive datasets.
 - **Robustness to Robotic Data Properties:** Adapting the architecture to account for the inherent nonlinearity, high-frequency dynamics, and numerical instability common in real-world robotic data.

In essence, the paper aims to bridge the gap between the theoretical promise of foundation models and the practical realities of bimanual robotic control, by developing a novel model and data strategy that addresses both the quantity and quality of data and the expressiveness and stability of the underlying policy architecture.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The RDT-1B methodology centers around a **pre-training and fine-tuning pipeline** combined with architectural innovations and a novel action space representation to tackle the core challenges of data scarcity and multi-modal action distributions in bimanual manipulation.

#### 3.1. RDT Model Architecture

RDT builds upon the **Diffusion Transformer (DiT)** architecture, a scalable backbone for generative models, but introduces crucial modifications tailored for robotic control:

- **Diffusion Modeling for Multi-Modality:** Instead of predicting a deterministic action, RDT models the continuous conditional distribution `p(action | language_instruction, observation)` using a diffusion process. This allows it to capture and sample from multi-modal action distributions, avoiding the issue of "average" actions that can be physically infeasible for bimanual tasks. The action is predicted as an "action chunk" (`a_t:t+T_a`) to encourage temporal consistency and reduce error accumulation.
- **Adaptations for Robotic Data Properties:**
 - **QKNorm & RMSNorm:** To enhance training stability and address the unstable numerical range of robotic physical quantities, the authors integrate Query-Key Normalization (QKNorm) in attention layers and replace LayerNorm with Root Mean Square Normalization (RMSNorm). RMSNorm avoids centering, which can disrupt time series symmetry, contributing to more stable large-scale training.
 - **MLP Decoder:** The final linear decoder of standard DiTs is replaced with a non-linear Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) decoder. This modification significantly improves the model's capacity to approximate the non-linear dynamics inherent in robot actions, which is critical for dexterous manipulation tasks.
 - **Alternating Condition Injection (ACI):** To prevent one modality from overshadowing another (e.g., image tokens dominating language tokens due to their higher dimensionality), RDT strategically injects image and language tokens alternately into successive cross-attention layers of the Transformer backbone, rather than simultaneously. This ensures a balanced integration of multi-modal conditions.
- **Encoding of Heterogeneous Multi-Modal Inputs:**
 - **Low-Dimensional Inputs (Proprioception, Noisy Action Chunk, Control Frequency):** These are embedded using MLPs with Fourier features to capture high-frequency changes, then concatenated as in-context conditions.
 - **Image Inputs (Exterior, Right-Wrist, Left-Wrist Cameras):** A frozen SigLIP (image-text-aligned pre-trained vision encoder) extracts features, which are then projected via an MLP. Multi-dimensional positional embeddings are used to encode spatio-temporal information (viewpoint, time steps). Stochastic independent masking is applied to prevent over-reliance on specific views.
 - **Language Inputs:** A frozen T5-XXL (Transformer-based language model) encodes instructions, followed by an MLP projection. Language attention masks handle variable lengths, and independent masking promotes balanced integration.

#### 3.2. Data Strategy

The data strategy is crucial for addressing data scarcity and heterogeneity:

- **Physically Interpretable Unified Action Space:** This innovative concept is central to enabling multi-robot pre-training. A 128-dimensional unified action space is defined to encompass the main physical quantities relevant to most robots with gripper arms (e.g., joint positions/velocities, end-effector poses/velocities, gripper states, base velocities). Actions from different robots are mapped into this space by filling corresponding elements and padding the rest, crucially preserving the physical meaning of each dimension. This encourages the model to learn transferable physical knowledge across diverse robot embodiments. A 0-1 vector is concatenated to indicate padded dimensions, avoiding ambiguity.
- **Pre-training Data:** RDT is pre-trained on the **largest collection of multi-robot datasets to date**, comprising 46 datasets totaling over 1 million trajectories and 21TB of data (including RT-1, DROID, RH20T, Mobile ALOHA, etc.). Sampling weights for each dataset are adjusted based on their size (square root scaling) and quality to ensure diversity in mini-batches. Data cleaning and consistent preprocessing (unit alignment, 6D rotation for EEF, fixed image input order/format) are applied.
- **Fine-tuning Data:** A **self-collected, comprehensive multi-task bimanual dataset** is created specifically for the target ALOHA dual-arm robot. This dataset comprises over 6,000 trajectories covering 300+ challenging tasks, 100+ objects (rigid/non-rigid), and 15+ diverse scenes, making it one of the largest bimanual datasets. To enhance language diversity and quality, GPT-4-Turbo is used to augment and expand human-annotated instructions.

#### 3.3. Training and Inference

RDT-1B scales to **1.2 billion parameters**. Pre-training took one month on 48 H100 GPUs, followed by three days of fine-tuning on the same hardware. For real-time inference, DPM-Solver++ is used, reducing diffusion steps from 100 to 5, achieving an impressive 6 Hz action chunk inference frequency (381 Hz average action inference frequency) on the robot's onboard GPU. Data augmentation techniques (color jittering, image corruption, Gaussian noise, instruction augmentation) are employed to combat overfitting during fine-tuning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on a real ALOHA dual-arm robot demonstrate RDT-1B's superior capabilities across various dimensions of robotic manipulation.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance

RDT-1B consistently and significantly outperforms state-of-the-art baselines, including Action Chunking with Transformers (ACT), OpenVLA, and Octo. Across a wide spectrum of challenging bimanual tasks, RDT-1B achieves an average improvement of **56% in success rates** compared to baselines. This broad superiority is attributed to its diffusion-based modeling of multi-modal actions, powerful network architecture, and the extensive prior knowledge gained from large-scale pre-training.

#### 4.2. Zero-Shot Generalization (Q1 & Q2)

- **Unseen Objects and Scenes:** RDT-1B exhibits exceptional zero-shot generalization. In tasks like "Wash Cup" (unseen cups) and "Pour Water" (unseen rooms), RDT-1B maintains high success rates, comparable to its performance on seen scenarios. In contrast, most baselines struggle significantly or fail entirely on these unseen elements.
- **Instruction Following for Unseen Modalities:** For tasks like "Pour Water-L-1/3" and "Pour Water-R-2/3," RDT-1B accurately interprets novel instructions (e.g., "one-third," "two-thirds") and executes the task with precise hand and water level control, despite never seeing these specific phrases during training. This highlights a deep understanding derived from its extensive vision-language pre-training.

#### 4.3. Few-Shot Learning (Q3)

- RDT-1B demonstrates remarkable ability to learn new and complex skills from very limited demonstrations. In "Handover" (5-shot) and "Fold Shorts" (1-shot) tasks, RDT-1B achieves substantial success rates, whereas other baselines perform near zero. This rapid adaptation to novel action patterns underscores the effectiveness of its large-scale pre-training in building transferable skills.

#### 4.4. Dexterous Manipulation (Q4)

- In the "Robot Dog" task, which requires fine-grained control to push a joystick straight, RDT-1B accurately controls the robot arm to achieve the desired outcome, while baselines cause the robot dog to deviate. This indicates RDT-1B's superior precision in modeling multi-modal and non-linear actions, essential for dexterous operations. The robust vision-language representation learned during pre-training also aids in recognizing subtle visual cues (e.g., the black joystick on a black remote).

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies (Q5)

The ablation studies conclusively validate the necessity of the key contributions:
- **Diffusion Modeling:** RDT (regress), which uses deterministic regression instead of diffusion, shows a significant drop in success rates (e.g., from 100% to 12.5% for instruction following), confirming diffusion's importance for handling multi-modal action distributions.
- **Large Model Size:** RDT (small), with only 166M parameters compared to 1.2B, exhibits noticeably lower performance, demonstrating that scaling the model size is crucial for superior capabilities.
- **Extensive Data (Pre-training):** RDT (scratch), trained without the large-scale multi-robot pre-training, performs poorly on unseen objects and scenes, emphasizing that the prior knowledge acquired from massive, diverse datasets is critical for robust generalization.

In summary, RDT-1B sets a new benchmark for bimanual manipulation, showcasing unprecedented generalization, few-shot learning, and dexterity by effectively addressing the data and architectural challenges through its innovative design and large-scale training.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The RDT-1B model represents a significant leap forward in the field of robotic manipulation, particularly for complex bimanual tasks. Its contributions hold substantial significance and potential impact:

1. **Pioneering Foundation Model for Bimanual Manipulation:** RDT-1B is positioned as the first diffusion-based foundation model specifically designed for bimanual manipulation, and at 1.2 billion parameters, it is the largest diffusion-based pre-trained model for robotic manipulation to date. This establishes a new benchmark and research direction for general-purpose robotics.

2. **Effective Solution to Core Robotics Challenges:** The work directly tackles two of the most pressing challenges in developing generalizable robot policies:
 - **Data Scarcity:** By successfully leveraging a massive collection of diverse multi-robot datasets and introducing the Physically Interpretable Unified Action Space, RDT-1B demonstrates a viable path to overcome the prohibitive cost of collecting bimanual demonstration data. This cross-robot transfer learning paradigm can drastically reduce the data burden for new robot deployments.
 - **Multi-modal Action Distributions:** The innovative application and adaptation of diffusion models, coupled with specialized Transformer designs (QKNorm, RMSNorm, MLP Decoder, ACI), enable RDT-1B to accurately model and execute multi-modal and non-linear robot actions, which is crucial for dexterous and human-like manipulation.

3. **Unprecedented Generalization Capabilities:** The experimental results on real robots are compelling, showcasing RDT-1B's remarkable:
 - **Zero-Shot Generalization:** The ability to operate effectively in entirely unseen objects, scenes, and to follow nuanced language instructions never encountered during training is a critical step towards truly adaptable robots.
 - **Few-Shot Learning:** Learning complex new skills from as few as 1-5 demonstrations dramatically reduces the effort required to teach robots new tasks, making deployment in dynamic environments much more practical.
 - **Dexterous Manipulation:** The demonstrated capacity for fine-grained control necessary for tasks like guiding a robot dog signifies a level of precision that many previous methods struggled to achieve.

4. **Enabling General-Purpose Robots:** The robust generalization and few-shot learning abilities mean that robots equipped with RDT-1B-like policies could be deployed in a wider variety of real-world scenarios, adapting to variations in objects, environments, and task specifications with minimal retraining. This paves the way for more autonomous and versatile robots in domains such as household chores, industrial assembly, and service robotics.

5. **Catalyst for Future Research:** By open-sourcing the code, model weights, and the comprehensive fine-tuning dataset, the authors significantly contribute to the broader robotics research community. This will accelerate further research into:
 - Developing even larger and more capable foundation models for robotics.
 - Exploring more sophisticated techniques for cross-robot transfer learning.
 - Advancing the understanding and application of diffusion models for continuous control and multi-modal action generation.
 - Building more diverse and high-quality multi-modal robotic datasets.

In conclusion, RDT-1B is a landmark achievement that pushes the boundaries of robotic manipulation. It effectively demonstrates that the foundation model paradigm, when carefully adapted, can unlock unprecedented levels of generalizability and dexterity in bimanual robot control, bringing closer the vision of intelligent, adaptable robots in our daily lives.
