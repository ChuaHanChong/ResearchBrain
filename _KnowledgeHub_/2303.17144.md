# DAMO-StreamNet: Optimizing Streaming Perception in Autonomous Driving

> [!summary] Summary
> DAMO-StreamNet, from DAMO Academy and Carnegie Mellon University, presents a real-time perception framework for autonomous driving, incorporating a dynamic receptive field FPN and asymmetric knowledge distillation. The framework achieves 37.8% sAP on the Argoverse-HD dataset, demonstrating improved object detection across scales and enhanced long-term forecasting capabilities for continuous video streams.

## Problem

- Existing streaming perception methods struggle with adaptively handling rapidly changing object scales, leading to receptive field conflicts.
- Learning long-term motion consistency from video streams for accurate real-time prediction remains challenging, as many methods rely on limited historical data or assume uniform motion.
- Current approaches often build on general object detectors without extensive modifications for real-time video stream processing, limiting optimal performance for streaming perception.

## Method

- Developed Dynamic Receptive Field FPN (DRFPN) with deformable and re-parameterized convolutions to adaptively handle object scale variations and improve feature alignment.
- Integrated a dual-path architecture with a Long-Short-term Fusion Module (LSFM-Lf-Dil) to efficiently capture and fuse long-term temporal motion and short-term spatial semantic features.
- Implemented Asymmetric Knowledge Distillation (AK-Distillation) to transfer future semantic and spatial position knowledge from a teacher network to the student during training, without incurring inference costs.

## Results

- Achieved an sAP of **37.8%** on the Argoverse-HD dataset at normal input resolution, outperforming previous state-of-the-art methods.
- Demonstrated superior performance in detecting large-sized objects with sAP_l values of **64.6%** (normal resolution) and **64.1%** (large resolution), showing absolute improvements of up to **3.6%** over prior methods.
- Maintained real-time inference efficiency, with the L-model processing frames in **26.6** ms, making it practical for autonomous driving applications.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - DRFPN consistently enhances feature aggregation and temporal feature alignment, providing adaptive receptive fields crucial for detecting objects across varying scales.
> - Utilizing longer-term temporal information (up to N=3 frames) improves future prediction accuracy, though an optimal balance is necessary to avoid performance degradation.
> - Logits-based knowledge distillation effectively transfers 'future knowledge' to the current prediction, particularly benefiting models with shorter temporal modeling capabilities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Autonomous driving systems necessitate real-time perception capabilities to accurately understand dynamic traffic environments. This requirement is addressed by "streaming perception," a task that involves simultaneously detecting and tracking objects within a continuous video stream. Unlike traditional image object detection, which processes individual frames independently, or general video object detection methods that often operate in offline settings, streaming perception requires online, real-time processing to facilitate timely decision-making in autonomous vehicles.

The existing research landscape for streaming perception has primarily focused on adapting current state-of-the-art object detectors, such as those based on the YOLO series, and integrating temporal modeling techniques. However, several limitations persist. These include challenges in adaptively handling rapidly changing object scales, which can lead to receptive field conflicts when detecting objects of varying sizes. Furthermore, efficiently and accurately learning long-term motion consistency from video streams remains an ill-posed problem, as many temporal aggregation techniques are designed for offline scenarios or rely on limited historical data, often assuming uniform linear motion. This work positions itself by addressing these specific gaps, aiming to develop a practical real-time perception pipeline that optimizes the base detector and incorporates more sophisticated spatial and temporal reasoning mechanisms suitable for online autonomous driving applications.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an optimized framework, DAMO-StreamNet, for real-time perception in autonomous driving systems. This framework aims to overcome current limitations in existing streaming perception methods by integrating advanced detection techniques with comprehensive spatial and temporal perception mechanisms.

The specific motivations and objectives are detailed as follows:

- **Addressing Rapidly Changing Object Scales:** Autonomous driving environments feature objects of diverse and rapidly changing scales due to vehicle motion. This poses a challenge for conventional detectors with fixed receptive fields, which can struggle to simultaneously detect both large and small objects effectively. The motivation is to design a base detector capable of adaptively handling these scale variations and improving feature alignment across different object sizes.
- **Learning Long-term Motion Consistency:** Accurate prediction of object motion states in real-time requires understanding long-term temporal correlations. Existing methods often rely on limited historical frames (e.g., only the last two), which are insufficient for complex scenarios involving non-uniform motion, non-linear motion, or occlusions. The objective is to efficiently learn and utilize longer-term motion consistency to enhance the accuracy of motion state prediction.
- **Enhancing Base Detector Performance:** Current streaming perception methods often build upon existing object detectors (e.g., YOLOX) without extensive modifications to their core architecture for real-time video stream processing. A motivation is to improve the base detector's feature aggregation and representation learning capabilities specifically for the streaming perception task.
- **Efficient Knowledge Transfer for Future Prediction:** To enable accurate real-time forecasting without incurring additional inference costs, there is a need for an effective mechanism to transfer "future knowledge" to the current frame's prediction. The motivation is to develop a knowledge distillation strategy that facilitates this transfer at the semantic level.
- **Ensuring Real-time Inference:** The entire perception pipeline must operate in real-time, meaning predictions for the current frame must be completed before the arrival of the next frame. An objective is to implement an efficient mechanism for updating support frame features that ensures seamless and timely streaming perception during inference.
- **Proposing a Comprehensive Evaluation Metric:** Existing metrics, such as Streaming Average Precision (sAP), primarily evaluate short-term forecasting. The motivation is to introduce a more comprehensive metric that assesses the long-term forecasting prowess of streaming perception algorithms, which is critical for real-world autonomous driving scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The DAMO-StreamNet framework is designed as a real-time perception pipeline, integrating recent advances from the YOLO series with specialized modules for spatial and temporal perception. The overall framework consists of a student network used for inference and a teacher network employed exclusively during training for knowledge distillation.

**Overall Framework:**
During training, a video frame sequence is fed into the student network, which comprises a backbone, neck, long-short-term fusion module, and head to extract spatiotemporal features. Simultaneously, a teacher network processes a future frame ($I_{t+1}$) to generate its logits. An Asymmetric Knowledge Distillation (AK-Distillation) module then transfers semantic and spatial position knowledge from the teacher's future logits to the student's predicted future logits. In the inference phase, only the student network is utilized.

**Network Architecture:**
The student network is composed of a backbone, a neck, and a head. CSPDarknet-53 is adopted as the backbone, and TALHead is used as the head, consistent with benchmarks like StreamYOLO. The key innovations within the network architecture are:

- **Dynamic Receptive Field FPN (DRFPN):** To address issues of fixed receptive fields and feature alignment, particularly for objects with rapidly changing scales, DAMO-StreamNet introduces DRFPN as the neck structure. DRFPN builds upon PAFPN but incorporates several modifications:
 - **Deformable Convolution Layers:** These layers are integrated to provide the network with learnable receptive fields, allowing for adaptive sampling of features and better alignment of features across varying object scales.
 - **Re-parameterized Convolutional Layers:** These layers are used to enhance feature representation without incurring additional inference costs.
 - **ELAN and Bottom-up Auxiliary Connect (BuAC):** These components are employed to bridge the semantic gap between low-level and high-level features, ensuring effective detection across diverse object scales.

- **Dual-Path Architecture:** To capture long-term temporal motion and calibrate it with short-term spatial semantics, a dual-path structure, inspired by LongShortNet, is integrated. This architecture separates spatial and temporal information:
 - **ShortPath:** Processes the current frame ($I_t$) to extract short-term spatial semantic features.
 - **LongPath:** Processes historical frames ($I_{t-\delta t}, \dots, I_{t-N\delta t}$) to capture long-term temporal motion information.
 - **Long-Short-term Fusion Module (LSFM-Lf-Dil):** Features from both paths are fused to provide a comprehensive spatiotemporal representation. The weights for ShortPath and LongPath backbones and necks are shared. This dual-path approach is designed to handle complex motion scenarios beyond simple uniform linear motion.

**Asymmetric Knowledge Distillation (AK-Distillation):**
This module is designed to transfer "future knowledge" from a teacher network to the student network during training without adding inference costs. Given the asymmetric inputs (teacher sees the future frame $I_{t+1}$, student sees current and historical frames $S = \{I_t, \dots, I_{t-N\delta t}\}$ to predict $I_{t+1}$), logits-based distillation is chosen to align knowledge distribution at the semantic level, simplifying optimization.
- The teacher model is an image detector taking $I_{t+1}$ as input, generating classification, objectness, and regression logits ($F^{cls}_{tea}, F^{obj}_{tea}, F^{reg}_{tea}$).
- The student model is the streaming perception pipeline, generating its own logits ($F^{cls}_{stu}, F^{obj}_{stu}, F^{reg}_{stu}$) for the predicted $I_{t+1}$.
- The distillation loss (AKDM) combines Mean Squared Error (MSE) for classification and objectness logits, and GIoU loss for regression logits. Regression distillation is applied only to positive samples (filtered by OTA assignment) to reduce noise.

**Real-time Forecasting Mechanism:**
During inference, to satisfy real-time forecasting requirements, the support frame features (historical features used by the LongPath) are updated with features from the current frame before processing the next prediction. This mechanism ensures that the pipeline continuously adapts to the most recent visual information.

**K-step Streaming Metric:**
The authors introduce the K-step Streaming Metric ($sAP_K$) as an extension of the conventional sAP metric (which becomes $1-sAP_K$). This new metric evaluates the algorithm's ability to forecast results K steps into the future, thereby assessing long-term forecasting prowess, which is crucial for autonomous driving.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

DAMO-StreamNet was evaluated on the Argoverse-HD dataset, comparing its performance against existing state-of-the-art methods in streaming perception.

**Comparison with State-of-the-art Methods:**
- **Overall Performance:** DAMO-StreamNet demonstrated an sAP of 37.8% at a normal input resolution (600, 960), outperforming previous state-of-the-art (SOTA) methods.
 - DAMO-StreamNet-L achieved 37.8% sAP, compared to LongShortNet-L at 37.1% and StreamYOLO-L at 36.1%.
- **Large Resolution Performance:** At a large input resolution (1200, 1920) and without using extra training data, DAMO-StreamNet-L achieved 43.3% sAP. This surpassed StreamYOLO-L, which achieved 41.6% sAP *with* extra training data, and LongShortNet-L (42.7% sAP without extra data).
- **Performance on Large Objects (sAP_l):** DAMO-StreamNet-L showed significant improvements in detecting large-sized objects, with sAP_l values of 64.6% (normal resolution) and 64.1% (large resolution), representing absolute improvements of 3.6% and 2.4% over StreamYOLO-L and LongShortNet-L, respectively. This suggests that the DRFPN effectively provides an adaptive and sufficient receptive field for large objects.
- **Performance on High Precision (sAP_75):** A slight decline in performance was observed for DAMO-StreamNet-L under the stricter sAP_75 metric (44.6% vs. LongShortNet-L's 45.0% at large resolution). The authors attribute this to potential imprecision in the offset prediction of deformable convolution layers in high-precision scenarios.

**Ablation Studies:**

- **Investigation of DRFPN:** Integrating DRFPN into baseline models (StreamYOLO and LongShortNet) consistently improved their performance.
 - StreamYOLO-S saw a 1.9% increase in sAP (from 28.7% to 30.6%) with DRFPN.
 - LongShortNet-S experienced a 1.7% increase (from 29.8% to 31.5%). This confirms DRFPN's effectiveness in enhancing feature aggregation and temporal feature alignment due to its dynamic receptive field mechanism.

- **Investigation of Temporal Range (N and $\delta t$):**
 - Increasing the number of input frames (N) initially improved performance, with optimal values found at N=2 for S/M models and N=3 for L models.
 - Further increases in N beyond these optimal points led to performance declines, indicating that effectively utilizing long-term temporal information remains a challenge. For instance, StreamNet-M showed a 3.7% improvement from (0,-) to (2,1) frames, but then declined again.

- **Investigation of AK-Distillation:** AK-Distillation consistently yielded performance improvements, particularly for models with shorter temporal modeling (N=1).
 - DAMO-StreamNet (N=1) models showed sAP improvements ranging from 0.2% to 0.9% across different scales.
 - For DAMO-StreamNet with longer-term temporal modeling (N=2/3), the improvements were smaller (e.g., 0.3% for S and L models), and a slight decline was observed for the M-scale model. This suggests that the impact of knowledge distillation diminishes when the performance gap between the teacher and student networks is already narrow.

- **Investigation of K-step Streaming Metric:**
 - As the forecast step K increased (from 1 to 6), the performance (sAP_K) progressively declined across all DAMO-StreamNet scales (S, M, L). This highlights the inherent difficulty of long-term forecasting in streaming perception.
 - However, models utilizing longer time-series information (N=2/3) consistently outperformed those with shorter temporal context (N=1) for all K-step values, indicating the benefit of longer-term temporal reasoning for future prediction. For example, StreamNet-S (N=2/3) showed a 1.5% improvement over StreamNet-S (N=1) at sAP_2.

- **Inference Efficiency Analysis:** Despite the added complexity of DRFPN, DAMO-StreamNet maintained real-time capabilities. The multi-frame feature fusion mechanism adopted from StreamYOLO (buffer mechanism) incurred minimal additional computational costs, ensuring practical inference times (e.g., 26.6 ms for DAMO-StreamNet-L with N=3 on V100 GPU).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The DAMO-StreamNet framework contributes to the field of autonomous driving by advancing real-time perception capabilities. By integrating innovations such as the Dynamic Receptive Field FPN (DRFPN), a dual-path architecture for spatial-temporal fusion, logits-level Asymmetric Knowledge Distillation (AK-Distillation), and an efficient real-time forecasting mechanism, the work addresses critical challenges in streaming perception.

The demonstrated performance improvements, particularly achieving higher sAP scores on the Argoverse-HD dataset without requiring auxiliary training data, establish a new benchmark for real-time object detection in video streams. The enhanced ability to detect objects across varying scales and to leverage long-term motion consistency contributes to more robust environmental understanding for autonomous systems.

The introduction of the K-step Streaming Metric also provides a more comprehensive evaluation tool for long-term forecasting, which is directly relevant to the predictive capabilities required for safe and efficient autonomous navigation. This metric can guide future research towards developing algorithms with improved foresight.

Beyond autonomous driving, the principles and architectural components of DAMO-StreamNet, specifically its approach to handling dynamic object scales, fusing spatiotemporal information, and leveraging knowledge distillation for real-time prediction, can be applied to other autonomous systems. These include drones, robotics, and surveillance systems, where real-time and accurate environmental perception is a prerequisite for operational safety and efficiency. The insights gained from this work regarding the interplay of receptive fields, temporal context length, and knowledge transfer mechanisms can inform the design of future real-time perception systems across various domains.
