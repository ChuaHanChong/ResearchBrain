# Bootstrapped Model Predictive Control

> [!summary] Summary
> Bootstrapped Model Predictive Control (BMPC) enhances model-based reinforcement learning by integrating expert imitation for policy learning and model-based TD-learning for value function estimation. The approach improves data efficiency and asymptotic performance across continuous control tasks, while operating with a smaller network size and reducing the performance gap between the learned policy and the planner.

## Problem

- Existing Model Predictive Control (MPC)-based MBRL algorithms rely on model-free approaches for policy and value function learning, which often struggle with complex continuous control tasks.
- A significant performance gap exists between the robust MPC policy (from online planning) and the learned network policy, indicating suboptimal policy learning.
- Ineffective policy learning leads to inaccurate value function estimation, which consequently degrades the quality of the MPC policy itself and reduces overall data efficiency.

## Method

- The network policy is learned through expert imitation, minimizing the KL divergence between its action distribution and the distribution produced by the ==MPC== expert.
- A state value function is learned using a model-based, on-policy n-step TD-learning approach, constructing TD-targets by rolling out trajectories within the learned world model.
- A 'lazy reanalyze' mechanism efficiently updates expert imitation targets in the replay buffer by re-planning for only a small batch of samples at fixed intervals, minimizing computational overhead.

## Results

- BMPC achieves superior data efficiency and asymptotic performance on 28 DMControl and 14 HumanoidBench locomotion tasks, particularly on challenging high-dimensional environments.
- On 7 challenging DMControl tasks, BMPC reduces the average 'steps-to-solve' by **300%** compared to TD-MPC2 (from 360k to 90k steps) with a smaller network size (3M parameters vs. 5M).
- The performance gap between BMPC's learned network policy and its MPC planner is minimal, enabling the network policy to perform nearly on par with online planning, which allows for fast, low-latency inference.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Effective policy and value learning in MBRL requires leveraging the high-quality signals from the MPC planner, not just improving the policy prior for guidance.
> - Model-based on-policy TD-learning improves data efficiency and stability by mitigating off-policy issues commonly found in Q-iteration methods.
> - Expert imitation can be integrated into computationally intensive MPC frameworks efficiently through selective re-planning (lazy reanalyze), striking a balance between freshness of expert data and computational cost.
