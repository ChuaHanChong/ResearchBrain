# Bootstrapped Model Predictive Control

> [!summary] Summary
> Bootstrapped Model Predictive Control (BMPC) enhances model-based reinforcement learning by integrating expert imitation for policy learning and model-based TD-learning for value function estimation. The approach improves data efficiency and asymptotic performance across continuous control tasks, while operating with a smaller network size and reducing the performance gap between the learned policy and the planner.

## Problem

- Existing Model Predictive Control (MPC)-based MBRL algorithms rely on model-free approaches for policy and value function learning, which often struggle with complex continuous control tasks.
- A significant performance gap exists between the robust MPC policy (from online planning) and the learned network policy, indicating suboptimal policy learning.
- Ineffective policy learning leads to inaccurate value function estimation, which consequently degrades the quality of the MPC policy itself and reduces overall data efficiency.

## Method

- The network policy is learned through expert imitation, minimizing the KL divergence between its action distribution and the distribution produced by the ==MPC== expert.
- A state value function is learned using a model-based, on-policy n-step TD-learning approach, constructing TD-targets by rolling out trajectories within the learned world model.
- A 'lazy reanalyze' mechanism efficiently updates expert imitation targets in the replay buffer by re-planning for only a small batch of samples at fixed intervals, minimizing computational overhead.

## Results

- BMPC achieves superior data efficiency and asymptotic performance on 28 DMControl and 14 HumanoidBench locomotion tasks, particularly on challenging high-dimensional environments.
- On 7 challenging DMControl tasks, BMPC reduces the average 'steps-to-solve' by **300%** compared to TD-MPC2 (from 360k to 90k steps) with a smaller network size (3M parameters vs. 5M).
- The performance gap between BMPC's learned network policy and its MPC planner is minimal, enabling the network policy to perform nearly on par with online planning, which allows for fast, low-latency inference.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Effective policy and value learning in MBRL requires leveraging the high-quality signals from the MPC planner, not just improving the policy prior for guidance.
> - Model-based on-policy TD-learning improves data efficiency and stability by mitigating off-policy issues commonly found in Q-iteration methods.
> - Expert imitation can be integrated into computationally intensive MPC frameworks efficiently through selective re-planning (lazy reanalyze), striking a balance between freshness of expert data and computational cost.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is situated within the field of model-based reinforcement learning (MBRL), specifically focusing on plan-based methods that leverage a learned world model for decision-making. MBRL algorithms, which learn a model of the environment to simulate future states and rewards, have demonstrated advantages over model-free approaches in terms of data efficiency and policy robustness across various domains, including continuous control.

Model Predictive Control (MPC) has emerged as an effective planning strategy within MBRL for continuous control tasks. Algorithms like TD-MPC2 (Hansen et al., 2023) exemplify this by integrating a robust world model and a value function with online planning to derive policies. These methods typically learn a neural network policy and a value function in a model-free manner, using the MPC policy's high-quality samples to improve learning.

Another relevant area is Expert Iteration (EI), which aims to enhance planning efficiency by using a network policy to guide the search, and then updating the network policy by imitating the "expert" planning algorithm. This iterative process can bootstrap the capabilities of both the planner and the policy. While tree-search-based EI has shown strong performance in some domains, its application to continuous control with pure MPC-based MBRL has been less explored, with existing MPC methods often not adopting an iterative policy optimization scheme like EI.

Prior work has also focused on imitating or enhancing MPC controllers, typically where the MPC policy is derived from a pre-designed real model rather than a learned one. These approaches often aim for faster policies or more efficient neural planners, or to learn residual policies on top of MPC.

The current work addresses an observed limitation in existing MPC-based MBRL algorithms, such as TD-MPC2. While these algorithms utilize MPC for planning, the underlying policy and value function learning often remains model-free. This paper identifies that this model-free approach can struggle with complex tasks, leading to a performance gap between the strong MPC policy and the learned network policy. This gap suggests inaccurate value estimation, which can consequently degrade the overall performance of the MPC. Bootstrapped Model Predictive Control (BMPC) aims to close this gap by integrating expert imitation and model-based TD-learning into the MPC framework, thereby enhancing policy and value learning by leveraging the strengths of MPC itself in a bootstrapped manner, rather than relying solely on model-free updates.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind Bootstrapped Model Predictive Control (BMPC) stems from an identified limitation in existing Model Predictive Control (MPC)-based Model-Based Reinforcement Learning (MBRL) algorithms, particularly their reliance on model-free approaches for policy and value function learning.

**Problem Identification:**
The authors observe that current state-of-the-art MPC-based MBRL methods, exemplified by TD-MPC2, learn their network policy and Q-function in a model-free manner. During inference, these methods employ policy-guided MPC for online planning. While MPC generates high-quality interaction data, the model-free policy learning component, according to the authors' experiments, struggles with more challenging continuous control tasks. This struggle manifests as a notable performance gap between the robust MPC policy (which performs online planning) and the network policy (which is learned offline). This performance disparity suggests two key issues:
1. **Poor Policy Learning:** The network policy fails to effectively learn from the high-quality samples provided by the MPC expert, especially in complex environments.
2. **Inaccurate Value Estimation:** The ineffective policy learning leads to imprecise value function estimation. Since the value network informs the terminal value calculation within the MPC planning horizon, inaccurate estimations can degrade the quality of the MPC policy itself. This inefficiency in policy and value learning ultimately reduces the overall data efficiency of the algorithm.

**Key Objectives:**
To address these problems, BMPC aims to:
1. **Improve Policy Learning:** Develop a mechanism for learning the network policy more effectively by leveraging the inherent strengths of MPC planning. This involves moving away from a purely model-free policy update towards a bootstrapped approach.
2. **Enhance Value Estimation:** Achieve more accurate value function estimation by integrating a better-learned policy and a model-based approach for computing TD-targets.
3. **Boost MPC Efficiency:** Utilize the improved network policy to guide the MPC process more effectively, thereby further enhancing the performance and data efficiency of the overall MPC framework.
4. **Maintain Computational Efficiency:** Introduce a mechanism for expert imitation that is computationally viable for MPC algorithms, which typically involve expensive re-planning operations.
5. **Demonstrate Superior Performance:** Achieve superior performance, data efficiency, asymptotic performance, and training stability across diverse and challenging continuous control tasks, while maintaining comparable training time and reduced network complexity.

By achieving these objectives, BMPC seeks to bridge the performance gap between the powerful online planning capabilities of MPC and the often-suboptimal learned network policies in current MBRL frameworks, thereby creating a more robust and efficient continuous control algorithm.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Bootstrapped Model Predictive Control (BMPC) algorithm builds upon the foundation of TD-MPC2, adopting its world model architecture and Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) planning algorithm. The core innovation of BMPC lies in its bootstrapped approach to policy and value learning, moving away from the purely model-free methods used in TD-MPC2.

**Critique of Model-Free Policy Learning in TD-MPC2:**
The authors empirically demonstrate a performance gap between the MPC policy and the learned network policy in TD-MPC2, particularly on complex tasks like Humanoid Run and Dog Run. While the MPC policy maintains high performance through online planning, the network policy, learned via a Soft Actor-Critic (SAC)-style maximum Q-value approach, struggles to improve. This discrepancy is quantified by analyzing the difference in estimated action values between the MPC-selected action sequence and the average of network-policy-generated sequences (∆ˆQ). A persistently large ∆ˆQ indicates that the network policy provides suboptimal guidance to MPC and that the value function, which is supposed to represent this weaker policy, yields inaccurate terminal value estimates during MPC planning. This motivated the development of BMPC’s alternative learning mechanisms.

**Bootstrapped Model Predictive Control (BMPC) Components:**

1. **Policy Learning through Expert Imitation:**
 - **MPC Expert Definition:** BMPC defines an "expert policy" `π(·|z, pθ)` as the policy produced by the MPC algorithm itself, guided by the current network policy `pθ`.
 - **Imitation Objective:** The network policy `pθ` is updated by minimizing the Kullback–Leibler (KL) divergence between the MPC expert's action distribution `π` and its own action distribution `pθ`. Both `π` and `pθ` are parameterized as multivariate Gaussians, allowing for a closed-form KL divergence computation.
 - **Loss Normalization:** To ensure training stability across tasks and action spaces, the KL loss is normalized using moving percentiles (S). An entropy regularization term is also included.
 - **Exploration in Re-planning:** During re-planning for expert imitation, noise is strategically added to the prior policy by adjusting the `log std min` parameter, which enhances exploration and helps prevent premature convergence to local optima.

2. **Model-Based TD-Learning:**
 - **Value Function:** Instead of learning a state-action Q-function, BMPC learns a state value function `Vϕ`. This choice is motivated by not using a max-Q objective for policy improvement, which is typical for Q-learning.
 - **TD-Target Construction:** An n-step TD-target `ˆV` is constructed using the latest world model (dynamics `d` and reward `R` functions), the current network policy `pθ`, and a target value network `Vϕ−`. This is done in an *on-policy* manner by rolling out trajectories using `pθ` within the learned model.
 - **Loss Function:** The value network `Vϕ` is optimized to minimize the cross-entropy loss with respect to the discretized TD-target.
 - **TD Horizon:** The authors found that a short TD horizon `N=1` was most suitable in practice, attributing this to the world model's limited long-horizon prediction capabilities (trained with a short horizon of H=3).
 - **Rationale:** This model-based on-policy TD-learning approach aims to mitigate off-policy issues that can arise in Q-iteration methods when the policy undergoes significant changes during training, thereby improving data efficiency and stability.

3. **Lazy Reanalyze Mechanism:**
 - **Computational Challenge:** Directly computing the policy objective for every sample during every network update by re-planning with MPC is computationally prohibitive.
 - **Solution:** BMPC introduces "lazy reanalyze" to efficiently maintain a dataset of expert imitation targets in the replay buffer.
 - **Process:** Instead of re-planning all samples at every update, a small batch of `b` samples is randomly drawn from the replay buffer at a fixed interval (every `k` network updates). For these samples, the MPC expert `π` re-plans and generates fresh action distributions `πt`. These updated `πt` are then stored back into the replay buffer, overwriting old imitation targets.
 - **Efficiency:** This mechanism significantly reduces the computational cost. For example, with `k=10` and `b=20` (batch size of 256 for network updates), the effective "reanalyze ratio" is approximately 0.8%, which is over 100 times lower than typical reanalyze ratios (99%-100%) in other expert iteration algorithms.
 - **Impact:** Combined with batched MPPI planning on GPU, lazy reanalyze increases training wall-time by only 10-20%. The large replay buffer ensures sufficient data freshness for effective imitation learning despite low re-planning frequency.

**Network Architecture and Parameters:**
BMPC utilizes a network architecture almost identical to TD-MPC2's, but replaces the Q-network with a V-network. Critically, BMPC reduces the number of ensemble value networks from 5 (in TD-MPC2) to 2. This design choice results in a smaller network size for BMPC (3M parameters) compared to TD-MPC2 (5M parameters). Hyperparameters are kept consistent across all tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on 28 DMControl tasks and 14 HumanoidBench locomotion tasks demonstrate the performance characteristics of BMPC relative to baseline algorithms.

#### 4.1. Benchmark Performance

- **Overall Performance:** BMPC consistently achieves either superior or comparable performance against state-of-the-art data-efficient reinforcement learning methods, including SAC, DreamerV3, and TD-MPC2, across a diverse set of continuous control tasks.
- **High-Dimensional Locomotion Tasks:** On challenging high-dimensional locomotion tasks (e.g., Dog and Humanoid tasks in DMControl, and HumanoidBench suite), BMPC exhibits notable improvements in data efficiency, asymptotic performance, and training stability.
 - For the 7 most challenging high-dimensional tasks in DMControl, BMPC achieves an average "steps-to-solve" (90% of asymptotic reward) of 90k steps, which is a 300% improvement over TD-MPC2's 360k steps.
 - BMPC maintains superior performance on the HumanoidBench locomotion suite, which features a complex Unitree robot with a 61-dimensional action space, further affirming its advantage in high-dimensional control.
- **Network Size:** BMPC operates with a smaller network size (3M parameters) compared to TD-MPC2 (5M parameters) and DreamerV3 (12M parameters).

#### 4.2. Leveraging Better Policy Learning

- **Reduced Performance Gap:** A key finding is the minimal performance gap between BMPC's network policy and its MPC policy. In contrast to TD-MPC2, where a substantial gap exists, especially in complex tasks, BMPC’s network policy performs nearly on par with the MPC policy. This indicates that BMPC’s policy learning through expert imitation is more effective.
- **Real-time Inference Potential:** The strong performance of BMPC's network policy suggests it can serve as a viable final inference strategy for real-time control applications that require low latency, as it eliminates the need for computationally intensive online planning.
- **Ablation Studies on Policy & Value Learning:**
 - **Variant 1 & 2 (Improved Policy for Guidance):** Simply using a better-learned network policy (via expert imitation) to guide MPPI planning (without changing value learning) did not lead to performance improvements over the baseline TD-MPC2. This suggests that merely providing a better policy prior is insufficient.
 - **Variant 3 (Improved Policy for Value Learning with Q-Iteration):** Replacing TD-MPC2's policy learning with expert imitation *and* using this improved policy for value learning (while still employing Q-iteration) showed performance gains. This indicates that the crucial factor is utilizing the improved policy to learn a better value function.
 - **BMPC (Improved Policy for Value Learning with Model-Based TD):** Further enhancing Variant 3 by adopting model-based on-policy TD-learning (as in BMPC) led to additional improvements in data efficiency. This highlights the benefit of model-based TD-learning in avoiding off-policy issues and improving learning stability.

#### 4.3. Lazy Reanalyze Ablation

- **Impact of Reanalyze Interval:** The frequency of re-planning, controlled by the lazy reanalyze interval `k`, directly affects BMPC's performance. A `k=∞` (no reanalyze) results in significantly poorer performance, confirming the necessity of fresh expert targets.
- **Efficiency vs. Performance Trade-off:** Increasing the reanalyze frequency (decreasing `k`) improves performance, but this effect becomes less pronounced beyond `k=10`. Setting `k=10` (corresponding to a 0.8% reanalyze ratio) provides a balance between computational efficiency and effective policy learning. Further increases in reanalyze frequency incur substantial computational overhead without commensurate performance gains.

#### 4.4. Training Wall-Time

- **Comparable Training Time:** BMPC and TD-MPC2 exhibit similar training wall-time per 500k environment steps. While lazy reanalyze introduces approximately a 20% increase in training time, this is offset by BMPC's smaller network size.
- **Reduced Time-to-Solve:** Due to its significantly higher data efficiency, BMPC achieves a substantially shorter time-to-solve. For example, on the Dog Walk task, BMPC’s time-to-solve is 2 times shorter than TD-MPC2.
- **Potential for Further Optimization:** The authors note that lazy reanalyze could be parallelized using a separate thread, potentially further reducing overall training time. The capability of BMPC's network policy to perform inference without planning also offers a significant reduction in inference time for deployment.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Bootstrapped Model Predictive Control (BMPC) algorithm presents a significant advancement in Model-Based Reinforcement Learning (MBRL) for continuous control, particularly by addressing a fundamental limitation observed in prior MPC-based methods.

**Primary Significance:**
1. **Enhanced Policy and Value Learning:** BMPC directly tackles the challenge of suboptimal policy and value function learning in complex continuous control tasks, which often plagued model-free components of previous MPC-based MBRL algorithms. By leveraging the MPC expert itself for policy imitation and employing model-based on-policy TD-learning for value function updates, BMPC achieves more accurate value estimation and a more effective network policy.
2. **Superior Data Efficiency and Performance:** The method demonstrates superior data efficiency, asymptotic performance, and training stability across a wide range of continuous control tasks, especially in challenging high-dimensional locomotion scenarios. This efficiency is critical for real-world applications where data collection is expensive or time-consuming.
3. **Reduced Gap Between Planner and Policy:** BMPC effectively bridges the performance gap between the online MPC planner and the learned neural network policy. This means the learned policy can nearly match the performance of the computationally intensive online planning, which has significant implications for deployment.
4. **Low-Latency Inference:** The high performance of the learned network policy implies that BMPC can enable fast, low-latency inference without the need for online planning during deployment. This is crucial for real-time control systems in robotics and other applications where immediate responses are required.
5. **Computational Efficiency for Expert Imitation:** The introduction of the "lazy reanalyze" mechanism provides a computationally efficient approach to integrating expert imitation within an MPC framework. This addresses a practical hurdle by minimizing the overhead associated with frequent re-planning, making the bootstrapped learning paradigm scalable.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Robotics and Autonomous Systems:** The improvements in data efficiency and the ability of a trained network policy to perform robustly without online planning can significantly benefit applications in robotics, where training data is scarce and real-time control is paramount. This could lead to more agile and capable autonomous agents.
- **Challenging Control Problems:** BMPC’s demonstrated strength in high-dimensional locomotion tasks indicates its potential for solving other complex control problems where high-fidelity models and precise control are necessary.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The work lays a foundation for further research in integrating planning and learning. It suggests new avenues for combining expert imitation with other policy optimization schemes and for enhancing world models to support longer-horizon planning and value estimation.
- **Broader RL Community:** The findings contribute to the understanding of effective policy and value learning in MBRL, highlighting the benefits of expert bootstrapping and model-based TD-learning over purely model-free approaches when high-quality planning signals are available.

**Future Directions (as outlined by authors):**
The authors suggest several promising avenues for future work, including:
- Extending the TD-horizon for value learning as world model capabilities improve in long-horizon prediction.
- Integrating advanced expert iteration techniques, such as plan-based value estimation.
- Exploring hybrid policy improvement schemes that combine expert imitation with max-Q gradients.
- Applying BMPC to multi-task and offline reinforcement learning settings, where its data efficiency and robust learning could offer further advantages.
