# EndoWAM: A Grounded World-Action Model for Generalizable Endoscopic Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> EndoWAM, a World Action Model, provides generalizable autonomous navigation for flexible endoscopes by predicting future anatomical dynamics and employing a "future grounding" strategy to maintain target focus. The system achieved an 80.2% average success rate across diverse endoscopic procedures and demonstrated 90.1% zero-shot generalization to unseen conditions, while maintaining a real-time control frequency of 7.5 Hz.

## Problem

- Existing visuomotor control methods, including imitation learning and VLA models, lack explicit predictive capabilities for future environment dynamics, hindering robustness in dynamic surgical scenarios.
- Autonomous endoscopic navigation faces unique challenges like deformable anatomy, transient occlusions, limited training data, and constrained viewpoint diversity.
- Many advanced predictive models (WAMs) suffer from high inference latency, making them unsuitable for real-time, time-sensitive surgical applications.

## Method

- **EndoWAM** develops a ==World Action Model== framework that predicts latent future visual dynamics and jointly models multi-step actions and future task-relevant target regions.
- It introduces a novel ==future grounding== paradigm that reconstructs target regions in future latent states, providing explicit target-aware supervision to shape predictive visual representations.
- A new multi-procedure dataset, ==EndoMotion==, was created with dense target annotations and augmented with rotated trajectories to enhance viewpoint diversity and robustness.

## Results

- EndoWAM achieved an average success rate of **80.2%** across ureteroscopy, esophagoscopy, and ERCP tasks, outperforming the strongest baseline (GR00T-N1.7) by **53.1 percentage points**.
- The model demonstrated strong zero-shot generalization with a **90.1%** average success rate across six unseen cross-environment and cross-target conditions.
- EndoWAM maintained a real-time closed-loop control frequency of **7.5 Hz**, balancing navigation accuracy, generalization, and the computational demands for surgical deployment.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reconstructing future target regions from predictive denoising features is an effective strategy for robustly coupling visual dynamics, target-aware attention, and multi-step action generation.
> - Viewpoint augmentation during training is crucial for enabling robust generalization to unseen orientations encountered in endoscopic navigation.
> - Integrating predictive dynamics and future grounding benefits during training, while performing action generation from a single denoising pass at inference, allows for real-time control without significant latency.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Autonomous endoscopic navigation is a challenging area within medical robotics, aiming to reduce the operational burden on clinicians during interventional procedures. Existing approaches for visuomotor control in robotics generally fall into several categories:

- **Imitation Learning (IL):** These methods learn policies directly from human demonstrations. While effective for specific tasks, they often lack predictive capabilities regarding future environment dynamics and can struggle with variations not seen in the training data.
- **Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Models:** Building on IL, VLAs integrate semantic reasoning from pre-trained vision-language models, enabling more nuanced instruction following. However, like traditional IL, they typically infer actions based on current observations and do not explicitly model how the environment will evolve.
- **World Action Models (WAMs):** These models represent a more recent paradigm, particularly for robotic manipulation and visuomotor control. WAMs incorporate inverse-dynamics objectives or jointly model visual observations and action distributions. This allows them to capture physical dynamics and task-relevant temporal structures beyond the current observation, leading to more predictable and robust behavior. Existing WAMs have primarily focused on robotic manipulation tasks in more structured environments.

The current work by Lin et al. addresses a critical gap by extending the WAM paradigm to the complex and safety-critical domain of flexible endoscopy. Endoscopic environments present unique challenges that limit the direct applicability of existing WAMs and other learning-based methods:

- **Deformable Anatomy:** Tissues constantly move and deform, leading to complex and rapidly changing visual dynamics.
- **Transient Occlusions:** Blood, mucus, or tissue contact can temporarily obscure the field of view, degrading visual observations.
- **Limited Training Data:** Ethical constraints, high costs, and the specialized nature of clinical procedures restrict the collection of large-scale, diverse endoscopic navigation data.
- **Restricted Viewpoint Diversity:** Confined anatomical workspaces naturally limit the range of possible camera viewpoints, providing sparse supervision for learning visual dynamics.
- **High Inference Latency:** Many advanced models, especially WAMs, can be computationally intensive, leading to inference delays that are unacceptable in time-sensitive surgical scenarios where real-time control is paramount.

EndoWAM positions itself as a solution to these challenges by introducing a WAM specifically designed for generalizable robotic endoscopic navigation, incorporating novel "future grounding" and leveraging a new, purpose-built dataset. It aims to bridge the gap between advanced predictive models and the practical requirements of real-time, robust, and generalizable autonomous endoscopic control.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust, generalizable, and real-time autonomous navigation system for flexible endoscopes across diverse anatomical environments. This overarching goal is motivated by the desire to alleviate the operational burden on clinicians and enhance the consistency and safety of interventional endoscopic procedures.

The key objectives and motivations underpinning the development of EndoWAM can be detailed as follows:

1. **Enhance Robustness and Reliability through Predictive Dynamics:**
 - **Motivation:** Existing learning-based policies for endoscopic navigation often rely solely on current visual observations. This limited predictive capability renders them less robust in dynamic, safety-critical surgical scenarios where sudden tissue deformation, transient occlusions, and rapidly changing viewpoints are common. Such policies may struggle to anticipate future states or recover from unexpected visual degradation.
 - **Objective:** To move beyond reactive control by explicitly modeling future environment dynamics. EndoWAM aims to leverage the principles of World Action Models (WAMs) to predict how the anatomical environment will evolve under the robot's actions, thereby enabling more reliable and predictable navigation.

2. **Overcome Data Scarcity and Limited Viewpoint Diversity in Endoscopy:**
 - **Motivation:** Training sophisticated deep learning models, especially WAMs, typically requires extensive and diverse datasets. However, obtaining large-scale, high-quality endoscopic navigation data is challenging due to ethical constraints, high procedural costs, and the inherently confined nature of anatomical workspaces, which limits viewpoint variations.
 - **Objective:** To address data limitations by:
 - Constructing a novel, multi-procedure robotic endoscopy dataset named EndoMotion, comprising long-horizon trajectories across ureteroscopy, esophagoscopy, and ERCP.
 - Employing dense target annotations using an off-the-shelf grounding model (Grounding DINO) and Kalman filtering to provide target-relative action labels and future target regions.
 - Introducing viewpoint augmentation by rotating trajectories and their annotations to artificially increase visual diversity and improve robustness to unseen orientations.

3. **Improve Target-Directed Navigation in Deformable and Constrained Environments:**
 - **Motivation:** Navigating precisely to anatomical targets within a dynamic and deformable lumen is critical. Current methods may lose track of targets due to tissue movement or occlusions, increasing the risk of unsafe tissue interactions or procedural delays.
 - **Objective:** To introduce a novel "future grounding" paradigm. This approach aims to predict task-relevant target regions not just in the current observation, but in *future* latent states. This explicit, target-aware supervision is intended to shape the predictive visual representations, making the action policy more robust to visual degradation and enabling more precise and temporally coherent navigation towards the target.

4. **Achieve Real-Time Control for Surgical Deployment:**
 - **Motivation:** Many existing WAMs, while powerful, can suffer from high inference latency due to complex iterative generation processes. This makes them unsuitable for time-sensitive surgical applications where a rapid control frequency is essential for responsive and safe operation.
 - **Objective:** To design EndoWAM for real-time performance by enabling action generation from intermediate denoising features of a video world model in a *single* denoising pass. This design aims to incorporate predictive dynamics and future grounding benefits without incurring significant computational overhead during inference.

In essence, EndoWAM's motivation stems from the need for a more intelligent, adaptable, and efficient autonomous endoscopic navigation system that can learn from limited data, reason about future anatomical states, maintain focus on critical targets, and operate safely in real-world clinical settings.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

EndoWAM operates on a "predict-latent-dynamics-then-ground-and-act" paradigm, distinguishing it from conventional Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies that directly map current observations to actions.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

Given a current visual observation ($o_t$) and a language instruction ($l$), EndoWAM first predicts a latent representation of future visual dynamics ($z_{t+1}$). This prediction is achieved by a video world model. Intermediate denoising features from this prediction, denoted as $H(z^\tau_{v, t+1})$, jointly condition two subsequent processes: multi-step action generation and future target grounding.

The model aims to learn the conditional latent-action-region distribution: $z_{t+1}, a_t, r_{t+1} \sim p_{var}(\cdot | o_t, l)$, where $a_t$ represents the action at time $t$ and $r_{t+1}$ signifies task-relevant target regions in future observations. This formulation emphasizes the joint modeling of future visual dynamics, multi-step actions, and future target regions.

#### 3.2. Model Architecture

EndoWAM is designed to generate instruction-conditioned three-degrees-of-freedom (3-DoF) controls for a flexible endoscope.

- **World Model:**
 - It is initialized from Cosmos-Predict2.5-2B, a pre-trained video Diffusion Transformer (DiT) which includes a text encoder and a video Variational Autoencoder (VAE).
 - The language instruction is encoded and integrated into the DiT tokens using cross-attention.
 - Endoscopic observations are transformed into latent video tokens by the VAE.
 - The current observation is maintained as a clean conditioning latent, while future latent tokens are perturbed (using a rectified-flow schedule) and denoised collaboratively.
 - LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) is applied to the attention and feed-forward projections to efficiently fine-tune the pre-trained video prior to the specific endoscopic domain.

- **Shared Representation (H):**
 - An intermediate activation from the world model, specifically after a single denoising pass (from block-17 activation), is extracted as the shared representation $H$. This representation is then flattened into a sequence of tokens.

- **Prediction Heads:** Two distinct prediction heads are built upon this shared representation $H$:
 - **Action Decoder:** This component processes learnable action queries through transformer blocks. These blocks integrate bidirectional self-attention with cross-attention to $H$. An MLP then predicts per-axis categorical logits for the 3-DoF endoscope control. Bidirectional attention allows each action query to consider the full temporal context of the action chunk.
 - **Grounding DiT:** This module is responsible for reconstructing the VAE latents of target crops in future frames. It is conditioned on a projected and resampled representation of $H$. This "future grounding" objective encourages $H$ to encode the spatial evolution of task-relevant targets, generating predictive features that are then utilized by the action decoder.

#### 3.3. Training and Inference

- **Dataset (EndoMotion):**
 - A novel multi-procedure dataset was constructed, comprising 515 long-horizon trajectories (0.98 million frames) from ureteroscopy, esophagoscopy, and ERCP procedures.
 - Task-relevant anatomical targets are localized using a fine-tuned Grounding DINO model, generating Oriented Bounding Box (OBB) tracks. Kalman filtering ensures temporal consistency of these tracks.
 - Each OBB track provides two supervision signals: target-center offset (converted into discrete action labels) and future target regions (cropped for future grounding supervision).
 - **Viewpoint Augmentation:** To improve robustness, each trajectory and its OBB annotations are rotated at 45-degree intervals, expanding the dataset eightfold to 4,120 sequences and 7.84 million frames.

- **Training Objective:** EndoWAM is optimized using a combined loss function that jointly trains for predictive visual dynamics, multi-step action generation, and future target grounding:
 - **Video Co-training Loss ($L_{video}$):** A rectified-flow objective that regresses the velocity field over future latent positions, enabling the world model to capture visual dynamics.
 - **Action Loss ($L_{action}$):** Per-axis weighted cross-entropy loss applied to the action decoder, using a validity mask and class weights.
 - **Future Grounding Loss ($L_{ground}$):** A diffusion-based reconstruction loss where the Grounding DiT denoises the target-region latent of future frames. A target mask ensures that only frames with detected targets contribute gradients.
 - **Overall Loss ($L$):** A weighted sum of $L_{action}$, $L_{video}$, and $L_{ground}$, with $\lambda_v$ and $\lambda_r$ as balancing coefficients.

- **Inference:** During deployment, the world model performs a single denoising pass to produce the shared representation $H$. Crucially, the future-frame decoding branch and the Grounding DiT branch are dropped. This design ensures that the benefits of future-grounded representation learning are integrated during training without adding inference latency.

#### 3.4. Controlled Variants for Grounding WAM

To empirically evaluate the efficacy of their future grounding strategy, the authors developed several controlled EndoWAM variants, all sharing the same backbone, tokenization, and training protocol where applicable:
- **EndoWAM-EG (Explicit Input Grounding):** Augments the current observation with a cropped and resized target region from a fine-tuned grounding expert.
- **EndoWAM-CG (CoT Grounding):** Directly regresses future OBB parameters from the shared predictive representation $H$.
- **EndoWAM-FG-Gen (Future Grounding Generative):** Integrates future target regions into the world model's generative target, learning grounding through the world model's generation pathway.
- **EndoWAM (Ours):** Performs future grounding by reconstructing latent target regions of future frames using a dedicated Grounding DiT.

These variants represent different grounding strategies (input-level, regression-based, generative, and reconstruction-based) for comparison.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents detailed experimental results across various setups, demonstrating EndoWAM's performance, generalization capabilities, and efficiency.

#### 4.1. Comparison with State-of-the-Art

- **Performance:** EndoWAM achieved an average success rate of **80.2%** (77 out of 96 trials) across ureteroscopy, esophagoscopy, and ERCP tasks. This significantly outperformed the strongest baseline, GR00T-N1.7, which achieved 27.1%, representing a 53.1 percentage point gain.
- **Procedure-Specific Success:** EndoWAM demonstrated high success rates in specific procedures: 90.6% on ureteroscopy, 56.3% on esophagoscopy, and 93.8% on ERCP.
- **Generalization to Unseen Viewpoints:** Policies were tested on uniformly spaced roll offsets spanning 360 degrees, which were unaligned with the training augmentation angles. EndoWAM's high performance indicates strong generalization to unseen viewpoints.
- **Action Parameterization:** The study also found that a discrete action expert (Qwen3AE) outperformed a diffusion-based action head (Qwen3DiT) when using the same vision-language backbone, supporting the design choice of EndoWAM's discrete action decoder.

#### 4.2. Comparison with EndoWAM Variants

- **Future Grounding Superiority:** EndoWAM, utilizing its reconstruction-based future grounding, consistently outperformed its controlled variants.
 - EndoWAM-EG (explicit input grounding) achieved an average success rate of 61.5%.
 - EndoWAM-FG-Gen (generative grounding) achieved 51.0%.
 - EndoWAM-CG (regression-based grounding) achieved 45.8%.
- **Conclusion:** The results indicate that reconstructing future target regions from predictive denoising features, as implemented in EndoWAM, is a more effective strategy for coupling visual dynamics, target-aware attention, and multi-step action generation compared to other grounding paradigms tested.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

- **Impact of Grounding DiT:** Removing the Grounding DiT significantly reduced the average success rate from 80.2% to **37.5%**. This highlights the critical role of future target supervision in enabling reliable navigation and robust performance. Qualitative analysis showed that without the Grounding DiT, the policy's attention was diffuse, often distracted by specular highlights and mucosal textures, whereas the full model focused precisely on the task-relevant target.
- **Impact of Viewpoint Augmentation:** Removing viewpoint augmentation led to a substantial drop in average success rate to **28.1%**. This confirms that exposing the model to diverse viewpoints during training is essential for its ability to generalize to unseen orientations encountered during deployment.

#### 4.4. Generalization Capability

- **Zero-Shot Generalization:** EndoWAM was evaluated for zero-shot generalization across six unseen conditions (three cross-environment shifts: reflectance, geometry, texture; and three cross-target shifts: shape, size, color).
- **Performance:** EndoWAM achieved an impressive **90.1%** average success rate across these challenging conditions, significantly outperforming GR00T-N1.7 (46.9%) by 43.2 percentage points.
- **Robustness to Variations:** The model maintained high performance, achieving 100.0% success under texture shift and consistently over 80% across all target variations.
- **Conclusion:** These results indicate that the joint modeling of visual dynamics and target-aware grounding enables robust transfer capabilities to novel lumen appearances and target configurations.

#### 4.5. Deployment Efficiency

- **Control Frequency:** EndoWAM achieved a closed-loop control frequency of **7.5 Hz** on a single NVIDIA RTX 5090.
- **Comparison with Baselines:** This frequency is competitive with other VLA policies of comparable parameter scale (e.g., Qwen3AE at 12.1 Hz, GR00T-N1.7 at 8.9 Hz) while retaining an explicit dynamics prior.
- **Efficiency Advantage:** Notably, EndoWAM was 6.8 times faster than Cosmos Policy (1.1 Hz), which also uses a dynamics prior. This efficiency is attributed to EndoWAM's design, which predicts action chunks from intermediate features obtained in a single denoising pass, unlike Cosmos Policy's iterative denoising process.
- **Conclusion:** EndoWAM demonstrates an effective balance between navigation accuracy, generalization, and the real-time control requirements for endoscopic applications.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

EndoWAM represents a development in autonomous robotic endoscopic navigation, marking what the authors state is the first World Action Model (WAM) tailored for this domain. The significance and potential impact of this work stem from several key contributions:

1. **Advancement in Autonomous Endoscopic Navigation:** By introducing future grounding within a WAM framework, EndoWAM addresses fundamental challenges in robotic endoscopy, such as managing tissue deformation, transient occlusions, and rapidly changing viewpoints. This approach moves beyond purely reactive control, enabling the system to predict and reason about future anatomical states and target evolution, which is critical for robust and precise navigation in highly dynamic environments.

2. **Enhanced Robustness and Generalization:** The demonstrated ability of EndoWAM to achieve high success rates across diverse endoscopic procedures (ureteroscopy, esophagoscopy, ERCP) and to generalize effectively to unseen viewpoints, environments, and target characteristics (shape, size, color, texture) suggests a significant leap in the adaptability and reliability of autonomous endoscopes. This enhanced generalization is crucial for real-world clinical deployment, where variability is inherent.

3. **Real-Time Performance for Clinical Applicability:** A major challenge for advanced predictive models is their computational cost and inference latency. EndoWAM's design, which extracts predictive representations from a single denoising pass, enables a competitive control frequency of 7.5 Hz. This real-time capability is essential for responsive and safe operation in time-sensitive surgical scenarios, making the technology more viable for clinical integration.

4. **Novel Grounding Paradigm:** The introduction of "future grounding"—predicting task-relevant regions in *future* observations from intermediate world model features—offers a novel method for injecting target-aware supervision into predictive dynamics modeling. The comparative studies validate this approach as more effective than other grounding strategies, potentially influencing future research in visuomotor control.

5. **Contribution of the EndoMotion Dataset:** The creation of EndoMotion, a multi-procedure robotic endoscopy dataset with dense target annotations and viewpoint augmentation, is a valuable resource. It directly addresses the scarcity of high-quality, temporally aligned endoscopic navigation data and can serve as a benchmark for future research in surgical robotics and autonomous navigation.

6. **Potential for Reduced Clinical Burden and Improved Outcomes:** By enabling more autonomous and precise navigation, EndoWAM has the potential to significantly reduce the operational burden on clinicians, allowing them to focus more on the interventional aspects of a procedure. Improved navigation accuracy, consistency, and reduced risk of complications (e.g., lumen loss, wall impaction) could ultimately lead to safer procedures and better patient outcomes.

In conclusion, EndoWAM establishes a predictive, target-grounded framework that balances navigation accuracy, generalization, and real-time control, positioning it as a foundational step towards generalizable and reliable autonomous endoscopic navigation systems.
