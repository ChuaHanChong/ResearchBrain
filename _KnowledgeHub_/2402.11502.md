# GenAD: Generative End-to-End Autonomous Driving

> [!summary] Summary
> GenAD reframes end-to-end autonomous driving as trajectory generation instead of a serial perception-prediction-planning pipeline. It builds map-aware instance-centric scene tokens, learns a VAE latent space capturing structural trajectory priors, then uses a GRU to autoregressively generate future waypoints for agents and ego jointly, achieving state-of-the-art nuScenes planning accuracy at high inference speed.

## Problem

- Serial perception-prediction-planning pipelines ignore future interactions between the ego vehicle and surrounding traffic participants, causing inaccurate downstream planning decisions.
- Existing motion prediction and planning methods decode trajectories directly from latent features, ignoring the structural prior that most realistic trajectories are smooth and continuous.
- Prior end-to-end methods treat prediction and planning as separate tasks despite sharing the same output space, missing an opportunity for unified modeling.

## Method

- ==Instance-centric scene tokenizer== builds map-aware agent and ego tokens via ==BEV encoding==, ==deformable cross-attention==, and ==self-attention== for high-order ego-agent interaction.
- ==Variational autoencoder== maps ground-truth future trajectories into a structural latent space, modeling trajectory uncertainty as Gaussian distributions to capture realistic motion priors.
- ==GRU-based future trajectory generator== autoregressively decodes waypoints in the latent space, factorizing the joint trajectory distribution into a temporal sequence of conditional steps.

## Results

- GenAD achieves average L2 error of **0.91m** versus **1.30m** for VAD-Tiny and **1.03m** for UniAD on nuScenes planning, at **6.7 FPS** inference speed.
- Ablation shows combining trajectory prior modeling and latent future generation modules together improves L2 error from **1.11m** to **0.91m** and collision rate from **0.70%** to **0.43%**.
- Adding ego-to-agent interaction to VAD-Tiny alone drops average L2 from **1.30m** to **1.10m**, confirming instance-centric representation independently improves planning quality.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Modeling planning as distribution matching between scene representations and ground-truth trajectory priors can outperform direct regression-based decoding approaches.
> - Explicitly enabling ego-agent interaction before prediction, rather than after, better captures how ego actions causally influence surrounding agent behavior.
> - Factorizing trajectory generation into autoregressive latent steps rather than one-shot decoding yields more temporally coherent and realistic future predictions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of autonomous driving is rapidly advancing, with a fundamental goal of enabling vehicles to perceive their environment, predict the behavior of other agents, and plan safe and efficient trajectories. Historically, autonomous driving systems have been designed as a modular pipeline, separating tasks such as perception (3D object detection, map segmentation), prediction (of other agents' movements), and planning (of the ego vehicle's path). However, recent research has increasingly focused on **end-to-end autonomous driving**, aiming to directly map raw sensor inputs to planning outputs. This approach promises greater simplicity, potentially better optimization, and improved efficiency by avoiding cascading errors inherent in multi-stage pipelines.

Within this evolving landscape, "GenAD" positions itself squarely in the **vision-centric end-to-end autonomous driving** paradigm. Vision-centric methods are gaining prominence due to the economic convenience and richness of data provided by RGB cameras, contrasting with more expensive LiDAR-based systems.

Most existing end-to-end autonomous driving models, such as UniAD, ST-P3, and VAD, still adhere to a *serial design* that progressively performs perception, motion prediction, and then planning. While these methods have shown significant progress and even demonstrated that a planning objective can improve intermediate tasks, they suffer from two critical limitations that GenAD seeks to address:

1. **Lack of Comprehensive Interaction Modeling:** The serial design inherently assumes that motion prediction can occur independently of the ego vehicle's future planning. However, in reality, the ego car's actions (e.g., a lane change) can significantly influence the behavior of other traffic participants, which, in turn, affects the optimal planning for the ego car. This forms a complex, high-order interaction loop that conventional sequential pipelines struggle to model.
2. **Neglect of Structural Trajectory Priors:** Realistic vehicle trajectories are not arbitrary; they are highly structured, continuous, and adhere to physical constraints (e.g., most are straight or smoothly curved). Many existing methods directly decode trajectories from latent features, often failing to explicitly incorporate these inherent structural priors, which can lead to less realistic or suboptimal predictions and plans.

GenAD proposes a **new paradigm** that re-casts autonomous driving as a **generative modeling problem**. Instead of a sequential pipeline, GenAD focuses on predicting how the ego car and its surroundings *evolve together* given past scenes. This allows for *simultaneous* motion prediction and planning within a unified framework, explicitly addressing the high-order interactions. Furthermore, by learning a structural latent space for trajectories using a Variational Autoencoder (VAE), GenAD inherently models and leverages the natural priors of real-world movements, contributing to more authentic and safer outputs. This departure from the traditional factorized pipeline represents a significant shift towards a more holistic and integrated understanding of traffic dynamics in autonomous systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of GenAD is to develop an advanced, end-to-end autonomous driving system that can directly produce planning results from raw sensor inputs, moving beyond the limitations of conventional sequential pipelines. The core motivation stems from the observed shortcomings in existing end-to-end methods, particularly their inability to comprehensively model complex traffic evolution and integrate inherent trajectory priors.

The key objectives and motivations can be broken down as follows:

1. **To Overcome Limitations of Serial Pipelines:** The primary motivation is to address the fragmented nature of existing end-to-end autonomous driving systems, which typically decompose the problem into perception, prediction, and planning modules. This serial design often leads to a failure in comprehensively modeling the entire traffic evolution process, specifically neglecting crucial future interactions between the ego vehicle and other traffic participants. GenAD aims to provide a more holistic approach that inherently captures these high-order dependencies.
2. **To Model High-Order Ego-Agent Interactions:** A critical objective is to enable the system to understand and predict how the ego car's planned actions might influence the behavior of surrounding agents, and vice versa. This mutual influence, vital for accurate and safe planning, is largely overlooked in methods where prediction precedes planning. GenAD aims to build an architecture that facilitates these interactions from the outset.
3. **To Incorporate Structural Trajectory Priors:** Realistic vehicle trajectories possess inherent structural properties (e.g., continuity, smoothness, adherence to road geometry). Existing methods often fail to explicitly account for these priors, potentially leading to less natural or suboptimal predicted paths. A key objective is to learn and leverage this structural prior by embedding trajectories into a meaningful latent space.
4. **To Unify Motion Prediction and Planning:** Instead of treating motion prediction for surrounding agents and planning for the ego vehicle as separate, sequentially dependent tasks, GenAD seeks to unify them. The objective is to simultaneously perform both by casting autonomous driving as a *generative modeling problem* of future trajectories for all relevant instances, including the ego car.
5. **To Develop an Instance-Centric Scene Representation:** To support the generative approach, an efficient and comprehensive scene representation is necessary. The objective is to design a tokenizer that transforms raw sensor data into "map-aware instance tokens," which compactly encode critical information about the ego vehicle, other agents, and the surrounding map.
6. **To Validate Performance and Efficiency:** A crucial objective is to demonstrate that the proposed generative framework not only addresses the conceptual limitations but also achieves state-of-the-art performance on established benchmarks (like nuScenes) while maintaining high computational efficiency suitable for real-time autonomous driving applications.

In essence, GenAD aims to shift the paradigm of end-to-end autonomous driving from a chain of dependent modules to a unified generative process that captures the intricate, dynamic interplay between all elements within a traffic scene, yielding more intelligent, realistic, and safer driving behaviors.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

GenAD introduces a novel generative framework for vision-based end-to-end autonomous driving, structured around three main components: an instance-centric scene representation, trajectory prior modeling, and latent future trajectory generation.

#### 3.1. Instance-Centric Scene Representation

The first step involves transforming raw sensor inputs (surrounding images) into a rich, compact representation that captures critical scene information.
- **Image to Bird's Eye View (BEV):** GenAD follows established practices (e.g., BEVFormer) by using an image backbone (ResNet50) and Feature Pyramid Network (FPN) to extract multi-scale image features. These are then transformed into BEV features (B) using deformable cross-attention, effectively lifting 2D image information into a 3D bird's-eye perspective. Past BEV features are aligned to the current coordinate system and concatenated.
- **BEV to Map:** To represent semantic map elements (lane dividers, road boundaries, pedestrian crossings), GenAD employs learnable map tokens (M) that are refined from BEV features using global cross-attention (similar to VAD). Each map token can be decoded into specific points in the BEV space.
- **BEV to Agent:** Similarly, agent tokens (A) are extracted from BEV features using deformable cross-attention to represent the 3D positions of surrounding instances. A 3D object detection head then decodes bounding box and category information from these agent tokens.
- **Instance-Centric Design with High-Order Interactions:** This is a crucial innovation. An ego token (e) is added to the agent tokens to form a set of **instance tokens (I)**. To model the often-ignored high-order interactions between the ego vehicle and other agents, self-attention is performed on these instance tokens. This allows the system to learn how the ego vehicle's potential actions might influence other agents' movements, and vice versa, breaking the serial dependency of prediction before planning. Finally, to ensure instances are aware of the road context, cross-attention is applied between the instance tokens (I) and the learned map tokens (M), resulting in "map-aware instance-centric scene representations."

#### 3.2. Trajectory Prior Modeling

GenAD recognizes that both motion prediction for agents and planning for the ego vehicle involve generating realistic trajectories. To embed the inherent structural properties (e.g., continuity, smoothness) and uncertainty of real-world trajectories, GenAD employs a **Variational Autoencoder (VAE)**.
- A **future trajectory encoder (e_f)** maps ground-truth future trajectories (T) to a diagonal Gaussian distribution (N(μ_f, σ_f)) in a learned **latent space (Z)**. This allows the model to capture the distribution of possible future trajectories rather than just a single deterministic path, reflecting the uncertain nature of real-world motion. The latent space serves as a structured prior for realistic trajectories.

#### 3.3. Latent Future Trajectory Generation

Instead of directly decoding an entire trajectory from the latent space, GenAD adopts a temporal modeling approach to capture the evolution of movements.
- The joint distribution of future waypoints p(T|z_T) is factorized into a sequence of conditional probabilities.
- A **simple MLP-based waypoint decoder (d_w)** decodes a single waypoint (w) from a latent state (z).
- A **Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU)** acts as the "future trajectory generator (g)." It models the temporal evolution by progressively transforming the current latent state (z_t) into the next state (z_{t+1}).
- During inference, a latent vector is sampled from the learned distribution in Z. The GRU then iteratively updates this latent state, and the waypoint decoder decodes a new waypoint at each time step, generating a complete, temporally consistent trajectory. This approach ensures that generated trajectories are not only structurally sound (due to the VAE prior) but also dynamically coherent.

#### 3.4. Generative End-to-End Autonomous Driving (Training & Inference)

The entire GenAD framework is trained in an end-to-end manner with a multi-objective loss function:
- **J_prior:** This loss component trains the VAE (encoder e_f, generator g, and waypoint decoder d_w) using ground-truth trajectories. It includes an L1 trajectory discrepancy, ego-agent collision constraint, ego-boundary overstepping constraint, and ego-lane directional constraint, along with a focal loss for agent classification.
- **J_plan:** This is a Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence loss that matches the distribution in the latent space inferred from the instance tokens (p(z|I) via an **instance encoder e_i**) with the ground-truth trajectory distribution (p(z|T)). This loss effectively enforces that the high-level scene representation can accurately predict the distribution of future motions.
- **Auxiliary Tasks:** To further improve scene understanding, GenAD incorporates auxiliary losses for map segmentation (J_map) and 3D object detection (J_det), using task decoders (d_m, d_o) from map and agent tokens respectively.
- **Total Loss:** J_GenAD = J_prior + λ_plan J_plan + λ_map J_map + λ_det J_det, where λ are balance factors.

For inference, the ground-truth trajectory encoder (e_f) is discarded. The instance encoder (e_i) maps the current instance tokens (I) to a latent distribution in Z. A latent state is sampled from this distribution, which then seeds the GRU-based future trajectory generator (g) and waypoint decoder (d_w) to simultaneously produce future trajectories for both the ego vehicle (planning) and surrounding agents (prediction). This unified generative approach allows GenAD to consider complex interactions and trajectory priors, leading to more robust and realistic driving behaviors.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

GenAD was extensively evaluated on the widely adopted nuScenes dataset, a benchmark for autonomous driving that provides diverse urban driving scenes. The evaluation metrics primarily focused on planning performance (L2 displacement error, collision rate) and also included perception (mAP for 3D object detection, mAP@x for map segmentation) and prediction (End-to-End Prediction Accuracy - EPA) metrics.

#### 4.1. Planning Performance (Table 1)

- **State-of-the-Art L2 Error:** GenAD achieved the best L2 displacement error across all prediction horizons (1s, 2s, 3s) and on average (0.91m), significantly outperforming existing state-of-the-art methods like UniAD (1.03m), VAD-Base (1.22m), and ST-P3 (2.11m). This indicates superior accuracy in generating planned trajectories compared to ground truth.
- **Competitive Collision Rate:** While UniAD reported slightly lower collision rates by leveraging additional supervision (tracking and occupancy), GenAD's collision rate (0.43% average) was highly competitive, especially considering it uses fewer auxiliary supervision signals. This demonstrates GenAD's ability to produce safe plans.
- **High Efficiency:** GenAD maintained high inference efficiency (6.7 FPS) on a single RTX 3090 GPU, comparable to the more lightweight VAD-Tiny (6.9 FPS) and significantly faster than UniAD (1.8 FPS). This efficiency is critical for real-time deployment in autonomous vehicles.

#### 4.2. Perception, Prediction, and Planning Integrated Performance (Table 2)

- GenAD was compared to VAD-Tiny, a model with a similar size, across perception, prediction, and planning tasks.
- **Improved Holistic Performance:** GenAD consistently outperformed VAD-Tiny across all reported metrics, including 3D object detection mAP (0.29 vs 0.27), map segmentation mAP (e.g., 0.56 vs 0.44 at 1.0m), and motion prediction EPA for both cars (0.59 vs 0.56) and pedestrians (0.34 vs 0.29). This holistic improvement suggests that GenAD's integrated generative framework fosters better consistency and performance across traditionally separated tasks. The improved motion prediction is particularly attributed to GenAD's ability to consider ego vehicle influence on other agents.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Effect of Instance-Centric Scene Representation (Table 3):**
 - Adding the proposed ego-to-agent interaction mechanism to VAD-Tiny resulted in significant improvements in both L2 error and collision rate, highlighting its effectiveness.
 - Conversely, removing ego-to-agent interaction from GenAD (by masking self-attention) caused a notable increase in collision rate (0.43% to 1.22%). This strong negative impact confirms that modeling high-order interactions between the ego vehicle and other agents is crucial for learning accurate underlying trajectory distributions and ensuring safety.
- **Effect of Generative Framework (Table 4):**
 - The generative framework consists of Trajectory Prior Modeling (TPM) and Latent Future Trajectory Generation (LFTG).
 - Ablations showed that both TPM and LFTG modules individually contribute to performance improvement.
 - Combining both modules (`✓✓`) yielded the best planning performance (0.91m Avg L2, 0.43% Avg CR), demonstrating that factorizing the joint distribution and leveraging the learned structural latent space unlocks the full potential of the generative approach.

#### 4.4. Visualizations (Figure 4)

- Qualitative results visually demonstrated GenAD's superiority over VAD-Tiny. GenAD generated safer and more accurate trajectories in various complex scenarios, including straight driving, overtaking, and turning, especially in challenging situations with multiple interacting agents.

In summary, GenAD establishes itself as a leading method in vision-centric end-to-end autonomous driving, achieving state-of-the-art planning accuracy and strong overall performance across perception and prediction tasks. Its robust design, particularly the generative framework and explicit modeling of ego-agent interactions, is validated by both quantitative metrics and qualitative observations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in "GenAD: Generative End-to-End Autonomous Driving" carries significant implications for the future development and deployment of autonomous driving systems. Its contributions extend beyond incremental improvements, proposing a fundamentally new paradigm that addresses long-standing challenges in the field.

#### 5.1. Paradigm Shift in End-to-End Autonomous Driving

The most significant impact of GenAD is its successful demonstration of a **generative framework** for end-to-end autonomous driving. By casting the problem as predicting the co-evolution of the ego car and its surroundings, GenAD moves away from the conventional, rigid serial pipelines (perception -> prediction -> planning). This shift enables a more holistic understanding of the driving scene, where interactions and causality are modeled more naturally within a unified framework. This foundational change opens doors for more intelligent and robust driving systems that can handle complex, dynamic environments more effectively.

#### 5.2. Enhanced Safety Through Interaction Modeling

A critical contribution of GenAD is its explicit modeling of **high-order ego-agent interactions**. Traditional systems often predict the behavior of other agents independently before the ego vehicle plans its own path, overlooking the mutual influence. GenAD's self-attention mechanism on instance tokens allows the ego vehicle's future intentions to influence the prediction of other agents, and vice versa. This leads to more realistic and, crucially, safer trajectory planning, as the system can anticipate and react to the ripple effects of its own actions on the surrounding traffic. The significant drop in collision rate observed in ablation studies without this interaction modeling underscores its importance for safety.

#### 5.3. More Realistic and Robust Trajectories via Latent Priors

By employing a Variational Autoencoder (VAE) to learn a structural latent space for trajectories, GenAD incorporates the **inherent priors of real-world movements**. This ensures that the generated trajectories are not arbitrary but adhere to physical constraints and typical driving patterns (e.g., continuity, smooth curves). This ability to generate more authentic trajectories improves the realism of motion prediction and planning, contributing to smoother and more predictable driving behavior, which is essential for passenger comfort and public acceptance of autonomous vehicles.

#### 5.4. Improved Efficiency and Holistic Performance

GenAD achieves state-of-the-art planning performance with high inference efficiency, making it suitable for real-time applications. Furthermore, the unified framework shows a synergistic improvement across perception, prediction, and planning tasks. This indicates that optimizing for the end-to-end objective within a generative model leads to better performance in intermediate tasks as well, suggesting a more coherent and well-integrated system than those with separate, loosely coupled modules.

#### 5.5. Future Research Directions

The success of GenAD, particularly its generative approach, opens up exciting avenues for future research. The paper itself suggests exploring other advanced generative modeling techniques, such as Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) or diffusion models, for end-to-end autonomous driving. These models have shown remarkable capabilities in generating complex data distributions and could potentially further enhance the realism, diversity, and safety of generated driving behaviors. GenAD provides a strong foundation and validation for the effectiveness of generative models in this challenging domain.

In conclusion, GenAD represents a significant step forward in autonomous driving, offering a more integrated, intelligent, and safe approach to motion planning from raw sensor data. Its innovative generative framework and explicit modeling of complex interactions set a new benchmark and inspire future research towards truly autonomous and human-like driving systems.
