# Synthetic Curriculum Reinforces Compositional Text-to-Image Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework named CompGen, which utilizes a synthetic curriculum derived from scene graphs and reinforcement learning, enhances compositional text-to-image generation. This approach significantly improves the ability of T2I models to accurately render complex scenes, achieving an average improvement of 11.72 percentage points on diffusion models and 7.61 points on auto-regressive models across various benchmarks.

## Problem

- Current Text-to-Image (T2I) models struggle with compositional synthesis, frequently exhibiting object omission, incorrect attribute binding, and misinterpreting complex relationships.
- Many existing methods for improving compositional generation rely on synthesized ground-truth images or intermediate structural representations, which can increase data annotation burden or inference costs.
- Large-scale reinforcement learning for compositional T2I generation faces stability challenges due to the heterogeneous nature of required compositional capabilities, encompassing object existence, attribute binding, relations, and counting.

## Method

- **CompGen** introduces a two-stage methodology: generating ==scene graphs== with controlled difficulty to construct a synthetic curriculum, and training T2I models using curriculum-based reinforcement learning.
- It employs a principled scene graph-based difficulty measure, a multiplicative metric accounting for the number of objects, attribute density, and relational connectivity, to systematically quantify compositional complexity.
- The framework utilizes Curriculum-based Group Relative Policy Optimization (C-GRPO) with a multimodal LLM (MLLM) as a fine-grained reward model, integrating curriculum scheduling (e.g., Gaussian) to stabilize and optimize reinforcement learning.

## Results

- CompGen improved Stable-Diffusion-1.5 by an average of 11.72 percentage points across five compositional benchmarks, surpassing larger baseline models like Stable-Diffusion-2.1 and Playground-V2.
- It enhanced SimpleAR performance by 7.61 percentage points, establishing a new state-of-the-art that outperformed even larger models like Emu3 on most individual benchmarks.
- Adaptive MCMC sampling for scene graph generation achieved high success rates (exceeding **91%**) and superior node type diversity across varying difficulty levels, demonstrating robustness and efficiency for curriculum construction.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The proposed multiplicative scene graph-based difficulty measure effectively models the combinatorial nature of compositional complexity, significantly outperforming existing additive metrics.
> - The capability of the multimodal LLM serving as the reward model directly correlates with the final compositional generation performance, with fine-grained rewards proving more effective than coarse-grained alternatives.
> - Curriculum learning, particularly Gaussian scheduling, extends the scaling regime of T2I models and significantly improves compositional generalization and training efficiency compared to random or no curriculum strategies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Text-to-Image (T2I) generation has advanced significantly, enabling the synthesis of diverse visual content from textual descriptions. Despite progress in visual quality, a persistent challenge in T2I models is compositional synthesis. This involves accurately rendering complex scenes that contain multiple objects with varied attributes and intricate spatial and semantic relationships, requiring precise object placement and coherent inter-object interactions. Current T2I models frequently exhibit limitations such as object omission, incorrect attribute binding, and failures in understanding complex relational instructions.

Existing approaches to improve compositional alignment in T2I models generally fall into three categories:
1. **Attention-based methods:** These modify attention mechanisms within the UNet architecture of diffusion models to enforce object presence and spatial separation. Examples include adjusting attention scores in cross-attention and self-attention layers or strengthening associations between relevant objects and attributes through contrastive objectives. However, these methods often operate during inference, potentially facing scalability and computational efficiency constraints.
2. **Planning-based methods:** These utilize intermediate structures, such as object layouts (either manually defined or generated by Large Language Models), to guide image synthesis. Some approaches integrate additional modules like visual question-answering or captioning models for refinement, which can increase inference costs and may not fully resolve attribute binding issues.
3. **Learning-based methods:** This category focuses on improvements during training, including fine-tuning diffusion models with vision-language supervision or employing reinforcement learning (RL) techniques. Caption-guided optimization is another direction within this category. However, many of these methods often require synthesized ground-truth images or intermediate skeletons for supervised fine-tuning.

This work introduces a data-centric approach that contrasts with methods requiring synthesized ground-truth images or intermediate structures. Instead, it focuses on enhancing compositional generalization through synthetic curriculum reinforcement learning. A key distinction is that the proposed method operates solely on textual prompts and applies reinforcement learning without the need for ground-truth image outputs or architectural modifications, thereby avoiding increased inference costs. It aims to address the stability challenges encountered in large-scale RL training for compositional T2I generation, which stems from the heterogeneous nature of compositional capabilities encompassing object existence, attribute binding, relational understanding, and numerical counting.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to address and enhance the compositional generation capabilities of existing Text-to-Image (T2I) models, which exhibit significant weaknesses in accurately rendering complex scenes. The paper proposes a novel framework, CompGen, to achieve this.

The motivations behind this work are multi-faceted:

1. **Addressing Compositional Weakness:** Current T2I models struggle with generating images that accurately reflect complex compositional prompts. This includes difficulties in handling multiple objects, diverse attributes, and intricate spatial or semantic relationships, leading to issues such as objects being omitted, attributes being incorrectly bound to objects, or relationships being misinterpreted. The goal is to improve the precise placement of objects and ensure coherent interactions between them.

2. **Avoiding Ground-Truth Image Dependency:** Many existing methods for improving compositional generation rely on synthesized ground-truth images or intermediate structural representations (like object layouts) for supervised fine-tuning. A key motivation for CompGen is to develop an approach that is data-centric and relies only on textual prompts, leveraging reinforcement learning without the need for ground-truth image outputs. This reduces the burden of data annotation or synthetic image generation.

3. **Overcoming RL Stability Challenges:** Large-scale reinforcement learning (RL) training for compositional T2I generation faces stability challenges. These arise from the diverse and heterogeneous nature of compositional abilities required, which include verifying object existence, correctly binding attributes, understanding relational dynamics, and accurately counting numerical entities. A structured training approach is motivated to stabilize and optimize this complex learning process.

4. **Inspiration from Cognitive Development:** The framework draws inspiration from human cognitive development, which typically progresses by first mastering simpler concepts before advancing to more complex ones. This motivates the development of a curriculum learning approach where T2I models progressively learn compositional skills, starting from individual objects and simple relations, and gradually moving towards complex multi-object compositions.

5. **Establishing a Principled Difficulty Metric:** A core motivation is to establish a systematic and quantifiable framework for defining and measuring the difficulty of compositional samples. This criterion is essential for creating an effective curriculum that guides the model through progressively challenging tasks.

6. **Enhancing Generalizability and Efficiency:** The work aims to develop a method that can be integrated into existing T2I architectures (both diffusion-based and auto-regressive) without requiring architectural modifications, thereby avoiding increased inference costs. This emphasizes a data-driven improvement rather than a model-architecture-driven one.

In summary, the key objective is to significantly improve compositional text-to-image generation by introducing a novel, data-centric, curriculum-based reinforcement learning framework that leverages scene graphs to control training difficulty, without relying on ground-truth images or architectural changes.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The CompGen framework operates as a novel compositional curriculum reinforcement learning system designed to enhance the compositional generation capabilities of Text-to-Image (T2I) models. It employs a two-stage methodology: first, generating scene graphs with controlled difficulty to construct a synthetic curriculum, and second, training T2I models using curriculum-based reinforcement learning.

#### 3.1. Scene Graph as a Difficulty Measurer

The core of CompGen’s curriculum learning is a principled framework for quantifying the difficulty of compositional samples using scene graphs. A scene graph G is defined by its set of objects (O), attributes (A) associated with objects, and relationships (R) between objects. The difficulty of a scene graph G, denoted Diff(G), is formally measured by:

Diff(G) = ||O|| ⋅ max(1, ||A||/||O||) ⋅ max(1, ||R||/||O||)

This multiplicative formulation considers three factors: the total number of objects (||O||), the average attribute density (average attributes per object), and the average relational connectivity (average relations per object). This metric reflects both the structural density and semantic richness, with difficulty positively correlating with these factors.

#### 3.2. Scene Graph Generation via Adaptive Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) Sampling

To create a synthetic curriculum, scene graphs (G) are generated within a target difficulty range [Diff_min, Diff_max]. Given the vast and high-dimensional space of possible scene graphs, a brute-force approach is intractable. CompGen reformulates this as an iterative sampling problem using an adaptive Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) strategy.

1. **Initialization:** The process begins with an initial minimally complex scene graph (G₀), typically comprising a small, randomly selected number of object nodes.
2. **Graph Transformation Operations:** Two types of reversible graph transformation operations are defined:
 - **T_add:** Introduces a new element (e.g., an attribute, a relation node, and its associated edges).
 - **T_delete:** Eliminates an existing element (e.g., a node and its incident edges).
 These operations are designed to maintain detailed balance through symmetric transition probabilities.
3. **Energy Function:** An energy function, Energy(G), quantifies how much a graph's difficulty deviates from the target range. It is zero if Diff(G) is within [Diff_min, Diff_max], and positive otherwise, guiding the sampling toward minimal energy states.
4. **Metropolis-Hastings Algorithm:** This algorithm makes stochastic acceptance decisions for proposed graph transformations. Transitions to lower-energy states are unconditionally accepted. Transitions to higher-energy states are accepted with a probability determined by the Metropolis criterion (Acc(G'|G) = min(1, exp((Energy(G) - Energy(G'))/τ))). This allows for exploration and prevents convergence to local minima.
5. **Adaptive Temperature (Simulated Annealing):** A temperature parameter (τ) controls the exploration-exploitation trade-off. It starts high to facilitate broad exploration and is progressively reduced (annealed) throughout the sampling process, allowing the sampler to initially identify promising regions before refining the search for precise convergence.

#### 3.3. Curriculum-based Group Relative Policy Optimization (C-GRPO)

After a scene graph G is sampled, it is instantiated with concrete semantic values (objects, attributes, relations) drawn from an LLM-generated library. This instantiated graph then guides the subsequent RL training.

1. **Generation of Input Text T:** A constrained LLM (Deepseek-V3) transforms the structured scene graph into a natural language prompt (T). This process ensures that all objects, attributes, and relationships from the scene graph are accurately reflected in the prompt, thereby preserving its compositional difficulty.
2. **Generation of Curriculum (Binary Question-Answer Pairs):** For each sampled scene graph, a set of comprehensive binary visual question-answer (VQA) pairs is programmatically generated. These questions exhaustively cover the structural elements of the scene graph, categorized into:
 - Object verification (Q_object): assesses presence of each object.
 - Count verification (Q_count): verifies correct object cardinality.
 - Attribute validation (Q_attribute): confirms attributes for corresponding objects.
 - Relation confirmation (Q_relation): verifies relationships between object pairs.
3. **Generation of Reward r:** A multimodal LLM (MLLM, LLaVA-v1.6-13B) serves as a reward model. It evaluates image-text alignment by calculating the predicted probability of answering "yes" to each binary question, providing a fine-grained reward score (r^(i)_j ∈). The average of these VQA scores across all questions for an image forms the scalar reward signal for RL training.
4. **Model Training (C-GRPO):** Curriculum Learning is integrated with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) to iteratively improve the T2I model's compositional generation ability.
 - **Curriculum Scheduling:** The framework dynamically adjusts the emphasis on different compositional difficulty levels. Two scheduling strategies are explored: Easy-to-Hard (deterministic progression from simple to complex) and Gaussian scheduling (a smooth, bell-shaped transition in task difficulty). The curriculum-weighted reward (br^(i)_j(t)) incorporates a sampling probability (bp(t, j')) for difficulty level j' at step t.
 - **Policy Optimization:** For each text prompt, C-GRPO generates a group of G images. The curriculum-aware advantages (A_i(t)) are normalized within each group. The T2I model's policy is then optimized by maximizing a C-GRPO objective function, which includes a clipped importance sampling term and a KL regularization term, to update the model parameters.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted demonstrate the effectiveness of the CompGen framework in enhancing compositional Text-to-Image (T2I) generation.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance Improvements

CompGen significantly improves compositional generation capabilities across both diffusion-based and auto-regressive T2I architectures.
- **Diffusion Models:** When applied to Stable-Diffusion-1.5 (0.9B parameters), CompGen achieved an average improvement of 11.72 percentage points across five compositional benchmarks (GenEval, DPG, TIFA, T2I-CompBench, and DSG). This performance surpassed larger baseline models such as Stable-Diffusion-2.1 and Playground-V2 (2.6B parameters).
- **Auto-regressive Models:** For SimpleAR (0.5B parameters), CompGen improved performance by 7.61 percentage points, establishing a new state-of-the-art that outperformed even larger models like Emu3 (14B parameters) across most individual benchmarks.
- **Qualitative Results:** Qualitative comparisons (Figure 4) indicated that CompGen accurately rendered complex details (e.g., object counts, attribute binding, relationships) while maintaining visual quality, addressing common errors observed in strong baselines like SDXL and Lumina-Next.

#### 4.2. Impact of Reward Model

The study investigated the impact of the multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM) used as the reward model on CompGen's performance.
- **Correlation with Capability:** A positive correlation was observed between the capability of the reward model and CompGen's performance. LLaVA-v1.6-13B, identified as the strongest MLLM, yielded an average score of 66.62%, outperforming InstructBLIP (the weakest MLLM tested) by 9.4 percentage points. This suggests that the effectiveness of CompGen scales with advancements in vision-language models.
- **Fine-grained vs. Coarse-grained Rewards:** An ablation study (Appendix G.1) indicated that the fine-grained, multi-aspect reward function used in CompGen (evaluating object existence, attribute binding, relations, and counting) was more effective than a coarse-grained single-question reward function like VQAScore, achieving a 5.2% higher average score using the same LLaVA-v1.6-13B model.

#### 4.3. Impact of Difficulty Measures

The research evaluated different difficulty measures for constructing the curriculum.
- **Multiplicative vs. Additive:** The proposed multiplicative difficulty measure (Diff(G) = ||O|| ⋅ max(1, ||A||/||O||) ⋅ max(1, ||R||/||O||)) outperformed existing additive metrics by an average of 4.56 percentage points. This gain underscores that correctly modeling the combinatorial nature of compositional complexity, which grows exponentially rather than linearly, is crucial for effective curriculum learning in T2I.
- **Data Difficulty Distribution:** Experiments (Appendix G.2) showed that a balanced training curriculum covering a wide range of data difficulties (uniform distribution) was essential. Training exclusively on "skew-easy" or "skew-hard" samples resulted in substantially poorer compositional generalization, indicating that both too simple and too complex early training can hinder overall learning.

#### 4.4. Analysis of Curriculum Scheduling Strategy

The impact of various curriculum scheduling strategies on model performance and scaling behavior was analyzed.
- **Curriculum Benefits:** All curriculum strategies (Random, Easy-to-Hard, Gaussian) significantly improved CompGen performance compared to a baseline without curriculum learning. The baseline model plateaued early at 42% accuracy, while curriculum approaches achieved substantial gains.
- **Strategy Effectiveness:**
 - **Random Sampling:** Provided improvement, peaking at 52.1% accuracy.
 - **Easy-to-Hard Scheduling:** Showed an extended scaling range, maintaining competitive performance over prolonged training.
 - **Gaussian Scheduling:** Achieved the highest accuracy of 54.6% (a 30% relative improvement over baseline) and demonstrated the most efficient scaling trajectory, reaching optimal performance with fewer training steps.
- **Extended Scaling Regime:** Curriculum learning generally extended the scaling regime, allowing models to continue improving beyond the point where the baseline model plateaued.

#### 4.5. Scene Graph Generation Efficiency and Robustness

Additional experiments in the supplementary material provided insights into the adaptive MCMC sampling for scene graph generation.
- **Efficiency:** Adaptive MCMC sampling exhibited high success rates (exceeding 91%) and superior node type diversity across easy, medium, and hard difficulty levels compared to random rejection sampling (which failed on complex tasks) and greedy sampling (which suffered from mode collapse).
- **Robustness to Initialization:** The Adaptive MCMC sampler demonstrated robustness to different initial prior graph states (empty, dense, or minimally complex), with the default minimally complex initialization striking the best balance between success rate and graph diversity.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents CompGen, a framework that offers a method for addressing the persistent challenge of compositional text-to-image (T2I) generation, which has broad implications for the development and application of generative AI models.

### 6. Significance

1. **Addresses a Core Limitation:** CompGen directly tackles a fundamental limitation of current T2I models—their struggle with accurately rendering complex scenes involving multiple objects, diverse attributes, and intricate relationships. By improving compositional synthesis, the framework enhances the fidelity and controllability of T2I outputs.
2. **Novel Data-Centric Approach:** A significant aspect of this work is its data-centric approach which leverages a synthetic curriculum derived from scene graphs and reinforcement learning. Crucially, it achieves this without requiring ground-truth images for supervised fine-tuning or modifications to the T2I model's architecture, thereby avoiding increased inference costs. This contrasts with many existing methods and provides an alternative pathway to improve model performance.
3. **Principled Curriculum Design:** The introduction of a scene graph-based difficulty criterion and an adaptive MCMC sampling algorithm provides a systematic and quantifiable method for constructing a training curriculum. This structured approach, inspired by human cognitive development, enables models to progress from simpler to more complex compositional tasks effectively.
4. **Broad Applicability:** The demonstrated effectiveness across both diffusion-based (Stable-Diffusion-1.5) and auto-regressive (SimpleAR) T2I architectures highlights the generalizability of CompGen. This indicates that the framework can be applied to a variety of existing and future T2I models, enhancing their compositional capabilities regardless of their underlying generative paradigm.
5. **Insights into RL Training:** The analysis of reward model capabilities and curriculum scheduling strategies provides valuable insights into the dynamics of reinforcement learning for complex generative tasks. The finding that performance scales with MLLM capability and that structured curriculum strategies (like Gaussian scheduling) are more effective than random sampling offers guidance for future RL-based training paradigms.

**Potential Impact:**

1. **Enhanced T2I Model Reliability:** By improving the ability of T2I models to follow complex instructions and generate compositionally accurate images, CompGen can lead to more reliable and predictable generative systems. This is critical for professional applications where precise control over image content is necessary.
2. **Improved Content Creation Tools:** Content creators, designers, and artists could benefit from T2I models that better understand and execute complex compositional prompts, enabling the generation of more specific and high-quality visual assets for various media.
3. **Advancements in Synthetic Data Generation:** For applications requiring synthetic datasets with controlled compositional properties (e.g., for training computer vision models), CompGen could facilitate the creation of highly accurate and diverse synthetic images that reflect precise scene structures.
4. **Foundation for Future Research:** The framework lays a foundation for future research in several directions, including exploring more sophisticated metrics for semantic complexity and visual realism, and developing adaptive curriculum strategies that dynamically respond to model performance.
5. **Reduced Annotation Burden:** By demonstrating an RL approach that does not rely on extensive ground-truth image annotations, CompGen opens avenues for developing advanced generative models with reduced manual data labeling effort.

In summary, CompGen represents a methodological advancement in compositional T2I generation, offering a flexible and effective framework that could significantly enhance the capabilities of generative AI models and broaden their applicability in practical scenarios.
