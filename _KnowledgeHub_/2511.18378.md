# Synthetic Curriculum Reinforces Compositional Text-to-Image Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework named CompGen, which utilizes a synthetic curriculum derived from scene graphs and reinforcement learning, enhances compositional text-to-image generation. This approach significantly improves the ability of T2I models to accurately render complex scenes, achieving an average improvement of 11.72 percentage points on diffusion models and 7.61 points on auto-regressive models across various benchmarks.

## Problem

- Current Text-to-Image (T2I) models struggle with compositional synthesis, frequently exhibiting object omission, incorrect attribute binding, and misinterpreting complex relationships.
- Many existing methods for improving compositional generation rely on synthesized ground-truth images or intermediate structural representations, which can increase data annotation burden or inference costs.
- Large-scale reinforcement learning for compositional T2I generation faces stability challenges due to the heterogeneous nature of required compositional capabilities, encompassing object existence, attribute binding, relations, and counting.

## Method

- **CompGen** introduces a two-stage methodology: generating ==scene graphs== with controlled difficulty to construct a synthetic curriculum, and training T2I models using curriculum-based reinforcement learning.
- It employs a principled scene graph-based difficulty measure, a multiplicative metric accounting for the number of objects, attribute density, and relational connectivity, to systematically quantify compositional complexity.
- The framework utilizes Curriculum-based Group Relative Policy Optimization (C-GRPO) with a multimodal LLM (MLLM) as a fine-grained reward model, integrating curriculum scheduling (e.g., Gaussian) to stabilize and optimize reinforcement learning.

## Results

- CompGen improved Stable-Diffusion-1.5 by an average of 11.72 percentage points across five compositional benchmarks, surpassing larger baseline models like Stable-Diffusion-2.1 and Playground-V2.
- It enhanced SimpleAR performance by 7.61 percentage points, establishing a new state-of-the-art that outperformed even larger models like Emu3 on most individual benchmarks.
- Adaptive MCMC sampling for scene graph generation achieved high success rates (exceeding **91%**) and superior node type diversity across varying difficulty levels, demonstrating robustness and efficiency for curriculum construction.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The proposed multiplicative scene graph-based difficulty measure effectively models the combinatorial nature of compositional complexity, significantly outperforming existing additive metrics.
> - The capability of the multimodal LLM serving as the reward model directly correlates with the final compositional generation performance, with fine-grained rewards proving more effective than coarse-grained alternatives.
> - Curriculum learning, particularly Gaussian scheduling, extends the scaling regime of T2I models and significantly improves compositional generalization and training efficiency compared to random or no curriculum strategies.
