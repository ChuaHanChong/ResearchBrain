# Grounding Multimodal LLMs to Embodied Agents that Ask for Help with Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces AUTOASK, a method for training embodied agents to interpret ambiguous human instructions in household environments. It leverages Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) and online Reinforcement Learning (RL) with rewards generated by another LLM to enable agents to ask clarifying questions and resolve ambiguities under partial observability, achieving significant improvements over baseline models in success rate and ambiguity resolution efficiency.

## Problem

- Embodied AI agents often struggle to interpret and act upon ambiguous or under-specified human instructions in partially observable, real-world-like environments.
- Training interactive embodied agents that can both act and ask clarifying questions is challenging due to the high cost of human demonstrations for imitation learning or the impracticality of manually engineering dense reward functions for complex reasoning tasks.
- Existing zero-shot LLM approaches for embodied tasks frequently assume full observability or over-rely on assistance, failing to effectively handle partial observability and strategic ambiguity resolution.

## Method

- The paper introduces the ==ASK-TO-ACT== task, which requires an embodied agent to perform multi-object rearrangement based on ambiguous natural language instructions in a partially observable Habitat 3.0 environment, with the ability to ask clarifying questions.
- The proposed AUTOASK method adapts a LLaVA-OneVision MLLM into a Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policy trained using online Reinforcement Learning (DD-PPO).
- A Llama-3 LLM is utilized as a process reward model (PRM) to generate dense, per-step rewards that incentivize both task completion and strategically useful clarification questions, thereby circumventing the need for extensive human demonstrations or manual reward engineering.

## Results

- AUTOASK significantly outperformed all baselines operating under partial observability, achieving a **45.9%** success rate and **40.6%** Ambiguity-Resolution Efficiency Score (ARS) on UNSEEN SCENES, and **38.7%** SR / **35.2%** ARS on UNSEEN TASKS.
- The method demonstrated an absolute improvement of **24.4**-**30.2%** in success rate compared to the best zero-shot visual MLLM baseline and **6.5**-**12.3%** over a supervised fine-tuned MLLM.
- Ablation studies confirmed that LLM-generated rewards are indispensable for the ASK-TO-ACT task, as policies trained using only sparse success or subgoal rewards resulted in near-random or very low success rates.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LLMs can effectively serve as process reward models, providing dense, high-level common sense and deductive reasoning rewards crucial for training complex interactive embodied agents without human demonstrations.
> - Integrating MLLMs with online RL enables agents to actively explore, ground questions visually in partially observable environments, and strategically resolve ambiguities, leading to robust performance on novel tasks and scenes.
> - Sparse or programmatically defined subgoal rewards alone are insufficient for complex ambiguity resolution tasks, highlighting the critical role of precise, LLM-generated rewards in guiding agents to ask meaningful questions.
