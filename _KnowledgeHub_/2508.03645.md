# DiWA: Diffusion Policy Adaptation with World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> DiWA, developed by researchers from the University of Freiburg and University of Technology Nuremberg, introduces a framework for entirely offline fine-tuning of diffusion policies for robotic skills by leveraging learned world models. This approach enables policy adaptation with zero additional real-world interactions during the fine-tuning phase, yielding substantial performance improvements and zero-shot transfer to physical robots.

## Problem

- Online reinforcement learning (RL) for fine-tuning diffusion policies in robotics is prohibitively sample-inefficient and unsafe, requiring millions of physical interactions.
- Imitation learning (IL) alone is brittle to distribution shifts and cannot adapt to novel scenarios not covered by expert demonstrations.
- Effective reward propagation and credit assignment for standard RL algorithms are complex due to the multi-step denoising process inherent in diffusion policies.

## Method

- DiWA employs a four-phase framework: first, a world model learns environment dynamics from unstructured robot play data; second, diffusion policies are pre-trained on expert demonstrations.
- A latent reward classifier is trained on expert demonstrations to provide task-specific reward signals within the world model's latent space.
- Pre-trained policies are fine-tuned entirely offline using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) within a 'Dream Diffusion MDP,' which embeds the multi-step diffusion process into the world model's latent space, further stabilized with behavior cloning regularization.

## Results

- DiWA achieved orders of magnitude improvement in sample efficiency, fine-tuning policies with zero additional physical environment interactions compared to hundreds of thousands or millions of steps required by online RL baselines on the CALVIN benchmark.
- The framework consistently improved success rates of pre-trained diffusion policies across all eight CALVIN tasks (e.g., `close-drawer` success increased from 59.14% to 91.95%), and four LIBERO-90 tasks.
- Demonstrated the first empirical evidence of zero-shot real-world transfer for diffusion policies fine-tuned solely in an offline world model trained on real-world play data, achieving substantial performance gains on a Franka Emika Panda robot.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Diffusion policies can be effectively fine-tuned entirely offline through imagined rollouts within a learned world model, drastically reducing the need for real-world interactions.
> - The 'Dream Diffusion MDP' formulation allows standard RL algorithms to optimize diffusion policies by framing the denoising process as an extended MDP within the world model's latent space.
> - Behavior cloning regularization is crucial during fine-tuning to prevent policies from overfitting to world model inaccuracies, thereby ensuring robust zero-shot transfer to real robots.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research sits at the intersection of several rapidly advancing fields in artificial intelligence and robotics: **diffusion models**, **imitation learning (IL)**, **reinforcement learning (RL)**, and **world models**.

- **Diffusion Models in Robotics:** Diffusion models have recently gained prominence for their ability to generate high-quality, diverse outputs by denoising random noise. In robotics, they are being leveraged for policy learning, demonstrating strong training stability and the capacity to model complex, multi-modal behaviors (e.g., "Diffusion Policy"). However, when purely trained via imitation learning, diffusion policies inherit the fundamental limitations of IL, such as susceptibility to distribution shifts and failure in novel scenarios not covered by the expert demonstrations.

- **Imitation Learning vs. Reinforcement Learning:** Imitation learning provides an efficient way to initialize policies from expert demonstrations but struggles with generalization. Reinforcement learning, conversely, offers a powerful mechanism for agents to learn through trial and error, adapting to new situations and discovering optimal strategies. The "pretrain-and-finetune" paradigm, common in large language and vision models, is increasingly vital in robotics to refine initially learned skills.

- **Challenges of RL in Robotics:** Applying RL directly to real-world robotics is notoriously difficult due to its inherent sample inefficiency (requiring millions of interactions) and safety concerns associated with extensive physical exploration. This bottleneck prevents widespread adoption of online RL for real-world robot skill adaptation.

- **Emergence of World Models:** To overcome these challenges, **world models** have emerged as a promising alternative. These models learn predictive representations of environment dynamics, allowing agents to simulate interactions and improve policies through "imagined" rollouts rather than costly real-world trials. This significantly boosts sample efficiency and safety. Prior work like DreamerV2 has shown success in model-based RL using latent-space imagination. More recent work explores general-purpose, task-agnostic world models learned from unstructured data (e.g., "play" data), which can then be reused across multiple tasks.

- **RL for Diffusion Policies (Prior Work):** A recent state-of-the-art method, "Diffusion Policy Policy Optimization (DPPO)", demonstrates that diffusion policies can be effectively fine-tuned using on-policy RL algorithms like PPO by framing the denoising process itself as a multi-step Markov Decision Process (MDP). While successful in simulation, DPPO still suffers from the fundamental sample inefficiency of online RL and often relies on access to ground-truth state information, making it impractical for direct real-world deployment with vision-based observations.

**DiWA's Contribution to the Landscape:**
DiWA directly addresses the critical gap left by DPPO and other online RL methods: the inability to *efficiently and safely fine-tune diffusion policies for real-world robotic skills without requiring extensive online interaction*. By integrating a learned world model, DiWA enables the entire fine-tuning process to occur *offline* in a simulated "dream" environment. This work is groundbreaking as it presents the first demonstration of fine-tuning diffusion policies for real-world robotic skills using an offline world model, thereby providing a practical and scalable solution for policy adaptation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation for DiWA stems from the significant practical challenges of fine-tuning powerful diffusion policies for real-world robot tasks.

**Key Challenges Addressed:**
1. **Inefficient Reward Propagation in Diffusion Policies:** The multi-step denoising process inherent in diffusion policies, where each action prediction involves a sequence of denoising steps, complicates effective reward propagation and credit assignment for standard RL algorithms.
2. **High Sample Complexity of Online RL:** Traditional model-free RL methods require millions of physical interactions with the environment to achieve proficiency. This is prohibitively expensive, time-consuming, and often unsafe for real-world robotic systems.
3. **Limitations of Imitation Learning:** While diffusion policies excel at imitation, they are brittle to distribution shifts and cannot self-correct or adapt to unforeseen circumstances if solely trained on demonstrations.
4. **Impracticality of Online Fine-Tuning for Real-World Vision:** Existing online fine-tuning methods like DPPO, while effective in simulation, rely heavily on direct environment interaction and often ground-truth state information, making them ill-suited for real-world vision-only setups where accurate simulators are unavailable and the sim-to-real gap is significant.

**Key Objectives of DiWA:**
1. **Enable Offline Fine-Tuning:** To develop a novel framework that allows diffusion policies for robotic skills to be fine-tuned entirely offline, eliminating the need for real-world or high-fidelity simulated environment interactions during the adaptation phase.
2. **Achieve High Sample Efficiency:** To dramatically reduce the number of physical interactions required for policy improvement, leveraging a learned world model for "imagined" practice.
3. **Ensure Practicality and Safety:** To provide a safer and more practical approach for robot learning by confining the computationally intensive and potentially risky RL fine-tuning to an internal model.
4. **Demonstrate Zero-Shot Real-World Deployment:** To show that policies fine-tuned within a learned world model (trained on real-world play data) can transfer effectively and immediately to physical robots without any further on-robot adaptation.

By addressing these objectives, DiWA aims to bridge the gap between advanced diffusion models and the practical demands of deploying adaptive robotic skills in real-world scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DiWA (Diffusion Policy Adaptation with World Models) is a four-phase framework designed for fully offline fine-tuning of diffusion-based robotic policies. Its core idea is to replace costly real-world interactions with imagined rollouts within a learned world model.

The four phases are:

**1. World Model Learning:**
- **Goal:** Learn a latent dynamics model that can predict future states and observations without relying on external environment interactions.
- **Data:** Trained on a large, task-agnostic dataset of unstructured and unlabeled robot "play" data (D_play). This data is typically collected via teleoperation and captures diverse environment dynamics.
- **Architecture:** Adopts a DreamerV2-style recurrent state-space model (RSSM). This model compresses high-dimensional observations (e.g., RGB images from multiple cameras) into a compact latent space (Z).
- **Components:**
 - **Encoder:** Maps raw observations (x_t) to a stochastic latent variable (z_t) conditioned on a recurrent state (h_t).
 - **Recurrent Model:** Updates a deterministic recurrent state (h_t) based on the previous state and action.
 - **Dynamics Predictor:** Predicts the next stochastic latent (z_t) from the recurrent state, enabling imagination without new observations.
 - **Decoder:** Reconstructs observations (x_t) from the latent state (s_t = (h_t, z_t)).
- **Training:** Optimized by minimizing the negative variational Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO), which balances reconstruction accuracy and KL divergence between posterior and prior distributions. KL balancing is used to stabilize learning and improve imagined rollout consistency. Once trained, the world model (M_ϕ) is frozen.

**2. Pre-training Diffusion Policies:**
- **Goal:** Initialize a base diffusion policy with a foundational understanding of the target skill.
- **Data:** Uses a small set of expert demonstrations (D_exp) specific to the target skill.
- **Process:** Observations from D_exp are first encoded into latent representations (z_t) using the *frozen world model's encoder*. The diffusion policy (π_θ) is then trained via behavior cloning (BC) to iteratively denoise random noise into expert actions, conditioned on these latent states. This ensures the policy's input modality (latent states) is consistent with the world model's output during fine-tuning.

**3. Latent Reward Estimation from Expert Demonstrations:**
- **Goal:** Provide the world model with a task-specific reward signal, as the world model itself is task-agnostic.
- **Data:** Uses the same small set of expert demonstrations (D_exp) used for policy pre-training.
- **Process:** A binary classifier (C_ψ) is trained on latent states (z_t) extracted from D_exp using the world model encoder. Successful states from annotated expert trajectories are labeled as positives. The classifier learns to predict task success.
- **Reward Function:** During imagined rollouts, the reward R_ψ(z_t, a_t) is derived from the predicted probability of success of the next latent state, C_ψ(z_{t+1}). This allows for a dense, task-aligned reward signal entirely within the latent space.

**4. Fine-tuning within the Dream Diffusion MDP:**
- **Goal:** Adapt the pre-trained diffusion policy to maximize cumulative reward using imagined interactions, without any real-world trials.
- **Dream Diffusion MDP (M_DD):** This is a key conceptual contribution. The diffusion denoising process is inherently multi-step. DiWA formally embeds this process into the world model's latent MDP.
 - **State:** The state in M_DD is augmented to include both the world model's latent state (z_t) and the current noisy intermediate action (¯a^k_t) being denoised.
 - **Action:** The action in M_DD is the next denoised intermediate action (¯a^{k-1}_t).
 - **Reward:** A reward is only received at the final denoising step (k=1) of the diffusion process for a given world model timestep (t), based on the learned reward classifier R_ψ(z_t, ¯a^0_t). All intermediate denoising steps have zero reward.
 - **Transitions:** For k > 1, the world model latent state z_t remains constant while the diffusion policy generates the next denoised action. When k=1, the final action (¯a^0_t) is executed in the *world model*, which then transitions to a new latent state (z_{t+1}), and a new diffusion process begins.
- **Fine-tuning Algorithm:** Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) is used to optimize the diffusion policy within the M_DD.
 - **Imagined Rollouts:** The policy generates long-horizon trajectories purely within the learned world model's latent space.
 - **Advantage Estimation:** Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE) is applied at the world model timestep level (k=1) and then propagated to earlier denoising steps using a denoising discount factor.
 - **Regularization:** A crucial behavior cloning (BC) regularization term is added to the PPO objective. This term encourages the fine-tuned policy to stay close to the pre-trained policy (π_θ_pre), mitigating overfitting to potential inaccuracies or artifacts within the frozen world model and ensuring better real-world transfer.
- **Inference:** The fine-tuned policy is deployed directly on the real robot, taking raw observations, encoding them into latents via the *frozen world model encoder*, and then executing the actions predicted by the diffusion policy.

This comprehensive approach allows DiWA to leverage the strengths of diffusion models (expressiveness), RL (adaptation), and world models (sample efficiency and safety) to address the practical challenges of robotic skill fine-tuning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

DiWA demonstrates significant advancements in offline fine-tuning of diffusion policies for robotic skills, both in simulation and real-world scenarios.

#### 4.1. Simulation Results (CALVIN Benchmark)

- **Sample Efficiency:** On the challenging CALVIN benchmark (eight distinct manipulation tasks), DiWA successfully fine-tunes all skills *entirely offline*, requiring **zero additional physical environment interactions** during the fine-tuning phase. In stark contrast, baseline methods like DPPO (Diffusion Policy Policy Optimization), which perform online fine-tuning, require **hundreds of thousands to millions of real environment steps** to achieve comparable performance. For instance, `close-drawer` required 345,600 (DPPO with WM encoder) or 1,545,600 (DPPO with Vision) steps, while `turn-on-LED` needed 494,933 or 2,251,200 steps respectively. This highlights an **orders of magnitude improvement in sample efficiency**.
- **Performance Improvement:** DiWA consistently and substantially improves the success rates of pre-trained diffusion policies across all eight tasks. For example, `open-drawer` success increased from 57.78% to 74.44%, `close-drawer` from 59.14% to 91.95%, and `turn-on-lightbulb` from 60.61% to 91.92%.
- **Impact of World Model Latents:** A comparison with DPPO variants revealed that using the same world-model latent encoder as DiWA (DPPO Vision WM Encoder) significantly boosted DPPO's performance compared to using a standard Vision Transformer (ViT) encoder on raw pixels (DPPO Vision). This indicates that the latent representations learned by DiWA's world model are richer and more effective for policy learning than raw visual features or ground-truth state, even for online methods.
- **Component Ablations:**
 - **Hybrid World Models:** Incorporating privileged scene state (DiWA Hybrid WM) during world model training (in addition to visual inputs) resulted in faster and more stable fine-tuning compared to models using only visual inputs (DiWA Vision WM). This suggests that more accurate latent dynamics improve the quality of imagined rollouts.
 - **Reward Signal Accuracy:** Inferring rewards by decoding latents into scene state (DiWA Hybrid WM + Latent Decoder) generally yielded stronger performance than relying solely on a learned reward classifier (DiWA Hybrid WM + Reward Classifier). This confirms that more precise reward signals within the world model further enhance fine-tuning.
 - **Behavior Cloning Regularization (α_BC):** This was found to be crucial for robust real-world transfer. Without it (α_BC = 0.0), policies overfit to world model inaccuracies, performing well in imagination but failing in reality. Too high a value (e.g., 0.5) prevented effective adaptation. Moderate values provided the best balance between adaptation and stability.
- **Generality:** DiWA's fine-tuning procedure also successfully improved a simpler unimodal Gaussian policy across tasks, demonstrating that the underlying world model MDP and reward estimation mechanism are not limited to diffusion policies.
- **LIBERO-90 Results:** Despite sparser data and challenging conditions, DiWA successfully fine-tuned four kitchen manipulation skills on the LIBERO-90 benchmark entirely offline, showing improvements (e.g., `open-top-drawer` from 40.67% to 77.33%).
- **Comparison to Offline RL:** Standard offline RL methods like CQL were found to be fundamentally ill-suited to DiWA's setting, failing rapidly due to noisy reward signals, sparse coverage of successful behaviors, and value extrapolation errors when applied to task-agnostic play data with estimated rewards.

#### 4.2. Real-World Results

- **Setup:** Experiments were conducted with a Franka Emika Panda robot on three manipulation tasks: `opening the drawer`, `closing the drawer`, and `pushing the cabinet slider to the right`. A world model was trained on **four hours of real-world teleoperated play data** (~450,000 transitions).
- **World Model Fidelity:** The learned world model was capable of generating visually coherent and temporally consistent long-horizon predictions from real-world hold-out trajectories, indicating its ability to capture meaningful dynamics from unstructured play.
- **Zero-Shot Real-World Transfer:** Pre-trained diffusion policies showed limited initial success on these real-world tasks. However, after fine-tuning for ~2 million imagination steps *entirely within the latent space of the learned real-world model*, DiWA significantly improved their performance. This constitutes the **first empirical evidence** that diffusion policies fine-tuned solely in an offline world model trained on real-world play data can achieve zero-shot transfer and substantial performance gains on physical robots. For example, the `closing the drawer` task showed a clear improvement in success rate after fine-tuning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

DiWA represents a significant step forward in the field of robot learning, offering a practical and scalable approach to developing adaptive robotic skills.

**Key Significance:**
1. **Bridging the Gap for Diffusion Policies:** DiWA successfully addresses the critical challenge of fine-tuning powerful diffusion policies in real-world robotic scenarios, which was previously limited by the high cost and risks of online reinforcement learning. It makes the benefits of diffusion models more accessible for practical robotics.
2. **Unprecedented Sample Efficiency and Safety:** By enabling reinforcement learning entirely within a learned "dream" environment, DiWA dramatically reduces the need for physical interaction. This is transformative for robotics, where real-world data collection is expensive, slow, and potentially damaging. The approach makes robot learning significantly safer and more resource-efficient.
3. **Real-World Applicability with Zero-Shot Transfer:** The demonstration of zero-shot transfer from policies fine-tuned in a real-world trained world model to physical robots is a major achievement. This proves that complex skill adaptation can occur without any on-robot fine-tuning, accelerating deployment and reducing development cycles.
4. **Leveraging Unstructured Data:** DiWA effectively utilizes large amounts of readily available unstructured "play" data to learn general-purpose world models. This shifts the data collection paradigm from task-specific expert demonstrations to broader, less constrained interaction data, which is easier and faster to acquire.
5. **Robustness through Behavior Cloning Regularization:** The inclusion of behavior cloning regularization highlights an important design principle for model-based RL in the presence of imperfect world models. It ensures that adaptation to the world model's internal rewards does not lead to policies that exploit model inaccuracies, thereby improving robustness and real-world transfer.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Accelerated Robot Skill Development:** DiWA can significantly speed up the development and deployment of new robotic skills in various applications, from manufacturing and logistics to service robotics.
- **Safer Robot Learning:** By minimizing direct physical interaction during the learning process, DiWA reduces wear and tear on robots, lowers the risk of accidents, and potentially enables the learning of more dangerous or delicate tasks in imagination.
- **Foundation for General-Purpose Robot Policies:** The framework's ability to learn from task-agnostic play data and adapt to specific tasks offline could contribute to the development of more general-purpose robot foundation models that are then rapidly fine-tuned for specific deployments.
- **Human-Inspired Learning:** The approach aligns with the human ability to mentally simulate and plan, suggesting a pathway towards more intelligent and intuitive robot learning systems.

**Limitations and Future Work (as stated by authors):**
The authors acknowledge two main limitations:
1. **Frozen World Model:** The current framework uses a world model that is trained once and then frozen. This means any inherent modeling errors or artifacts persist throughout fine-tuning, potentially leading to policies that overfit to these flaws in imagination. Future work could explore hybrid approaches that combine offline training with limited online interaction to incrementally update and refine the world model.
2. **Imagination-Reality Mismatch:** Improvements observed within the world model do not always perfectly translate to real-world performance due to potential discrepancies between the imagined and physical environments. Consequently, real-world evaluation of intermediate checkpoints remains necessary.

Despite these limitations, DiWA presents a compelling and practical solution for robot skill adaptation, demonstrating a powerful synergy between diffusion policies, reinforcement learning, and world models.
