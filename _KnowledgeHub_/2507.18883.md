# Success in Humanoid Reinforcement Learning under Partial Observation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at The University of Newcastle developed a model-free reinforcement learning method utilizing a novel history encoder, achieving the first stable training of humanoid locomotion policies under partial observability in the Gymnasium Humanoid-v4 environment. The method demonstrated performance comparable to or exceeding full-state observation baselines, even with up to two-thirds of the state information removed.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning (RL) performance significantly degrades in high-dimensional systems like humanoids under partial observability, transitioning from Markov Decision Processes (MDPs) to more complex Partially Observable Markov Decision Processes (POMDPs).
- Prior memory-based methods, such as those employing RNNs or LSTMs, have not consistently achieved stable training or effective policy learning for humanoid locomotion in complex, high-dimensional POMDP benchmarks like Gymnasium Humanoid-v4.
- The inherent partial observability of real-world robotic systems, stemming from incomplete sensor data, measurement noise, or bandwidth limitations, poses a major challenge for deploying RL policies learned in fully observed simulations.

## Method

- Introduces a novel model-free reinforcement learning method that integrates a history encoder into a standard RL algorithm.
- The history encoder processes a fixed-length sequence of past observations in parallel, designed to flexibly combine information across the observation window and infer complete latent states from incomplete observation sequences.
- Evaluated the approach by systematically simulating partial observability through the selective removal of velocity, mass/inertia, and force attributes from the 348-dimensional state space in the ==Gymnasium Humanoid==-v4 environment.

## Results

- The proposed method consistently outperformed baseline recurrent memory (RMF, ODERMF) algorithms in all single-attribute removal scenarios, achieving average maximum rewards up to **6680.8**, which surpassed the full-state TD3 baseline (**4547.2**) in some configurations.
- Even with two observation attributes removed (reducing observable state dimensions by up to two-thirds), the method continued to learn effective policies, notably achieving an average maximum reward of **5707.7** when mass/inertia and force were absent.
- Policies trained with the proposed method demonstrated adaptability to dynamically varying robot body part masses during training and maintained high average performance (ranging from **5492.9** to **6697.8**) when evaluated on static mass variations of ±**50%** for individual parts.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Stable and effective policy learning for humanoid locomotion under partial observability is achievable in high-dimensional, continuous control environments, demonstrating success in the challenging Humanoid-v4 benchmark where previous attempts struggled.
> - A parallel history encoder, capable of aggregating contextual information from a fixed window of past observations, can reconstruct sufficient latent state information for robust decision-making, outperforming traditional recurrent architectures in this complex domain.
> - Different observation attributes have varying criticality; velocity information is most critical, mass/inertia influences convergence speed, and force information appears least essential for successful humanoid locomotion.
