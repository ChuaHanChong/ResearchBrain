# CHILD (Controller for Humanoid Imitation and Live Demonstration): a Whole-Body Humanoid Teleoperation System

> [!summary] Summary
> A team at the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign introduced CHILD, a compact and reconfigurable system for direct, joint-level teleoperation of humanoid robots across their entire body. The system facilitated complex manipulation and full-body tasks on platforms like the Unitree G1, and proved adaptable for controlling other multi-limbed robotic systems.

## Problem

- Teleoperating humanoid robots with high degrees of freedom (DoFs) is challenging due to inherent dynamic instability and the complexity of whole-body movements.
- Existing teleoperation systems often provide limited direct, joint-level control for humanoids, particularly for full-body movements, which restricts the range of achievable tasks.
- Many full-body teleoperation solutions are expensive, cumbersome, or lack intuitive physical feedback, which can hinder operator experience and safety.

## Method

- Developed a compact, reconfigurable, and low-cost hardware system using 3D-printed parts and off-the-shelf servo motors, allowing direct joint mapping for various robot configurations.
- Implemented an onboard ==Raspberry Pi== with a DYNAMIXEL U2D2 and a 9-axis IMU to facilitate real-time, low-latency control, orientation sensing, and untethered operation via Wi-Fi.
- Designed software with asynchronous modules for subscribing to leader joint states, a locomotion controller for legs, a direct joint controller for full-body mapping, and adaptive force feedback for enhanced operator intuition.

## Results

- Demonstrated successful upper-body teleoperation combined with the Unitree G1's autonomous locomotion controller for tasks involving navigation and object manipulation.
- Achieved full-body direct joint-level control on the Unitree G1, enabling complex leg-based manipulations like catching a ball with its feet (with gantry support), and multi-operator crawling simulations in MuJoCo.
- The system's reconfigurable design allowed it to adapt and perform tasks on other multi-limbed systems, including a custom dual-arm kitchen setup and the Orthrus dual-arm mobile manipulator.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Direct joint-level mapping can provide intuitive and fine-grained control for the entire body of high-DoF humanoid robots, extending capabilities beyond traditional high-level locomotion commands.
> - A compact, reconfigurable, and low-cost hardware design, leveraging 3D printing and commercial components, significantly enhances accessibility and adaptability to diverse robot platforms.
> - Integrating adaptive force feedback and IMU-based orientation control substantially improves operator experience, aids in preventing unsafe joint configurations, and supports intuitive mode switching.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Teleoperation is a established method for enabling robots to perform complex tasks and to collect demonstration data for learning-based policies. While effective for single or dual-arm manipulation, teleoperating humanoid robots presents distinct challenges due to their high degrees of freedom (DoFs), inherent dynamic instability, and the complexity of unstructured environments.

Existing approaches to humanoid teleoperation generally fall into several categories:
- **Loco-manipulation:** These systems typically control the upper body through methods like exoskeletons or vision-based systems (VR, RGB-D cameras) while the lower body relies on a separate, high-level locomotion controller. This approach is efficient for tasks where the lower body acts as a mobile base but limits direct control over leg movements. Vision-based methods can also suffer from pose estimation inaccuracies due to occlusions and often provide only unilateral teleoperation, lacking physical feedback to the operator.
- **Full-body teleoperation:** Methods include trained control policies with VR/voice/camera input, full-body exoskeletons, or inertial measurement units (IMUs) placed on the operator. These systems can achieve whole-body retargeting and walking, but are often expensive, require significant operator effort, and may not be suitable for long-duration use. Motion capture systems offer high fidelity but share similar drawbacks regarding cost, portability, and operator fatigue.
- **Joint-level teleoperation:** This approach has gained traction for dual-arm systems, often using scaled models of the follower robots made from servo motors. It simplifies the control pipeline by directly mapping joint states and can support bilateral teleoperation with force feedback, offering a more intuitive and responsive user experience.

The work presented in this paper, CHILD, addresses a gap in the existing landscape by providing a compact, reconfigurable system specifically designed for **whole-body joint-level teleoperation of humanoid robots**. While joint-level teleoperation has been explored for dual-arm systems, its application to full-body humanoid control, particularly with a direct joint mapping approach, is noted as not having been extensively explored. CHILD aims to combine the advantages of direct joint-space control (simplicity, direct feedback on limits/singularities, bilateral operation) with a general-purpose, whole-body architecture for humanoids and multi-limbed systems. The system's reconfigurability also distinguishes it from systems tailored to a single robot configuration.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop and validate a teleoperation system that enables direct, joint-level control over the entire body of humanoid robots. The motivation stems from the limitations of current teleoperation systems when applied to humanoids:

- **Limited Diversity of Tasks:** Existing systems often lack full joint-level control for humanoid robots, particularly for whole-body movements, which restricts the range and complexity of tasks that can be accomplished. Many focus on upper-body manipulation with high-level locomotion commands, thereby underutilizing the full kinematic capabilities of humanoid legs for direct interaction with the environment.
- **Complexity of High DoF Systems:** Teleoperating humanoid robots, characterized by a large number of degrees of freedom and inherent dynamic instability, poses significant control challenges. Direct joint mapping is proposed as a method to simplify control and provide intuitive feedback.
- **Operator Experience and Safety:** The absence of physical feedback in many systems, particularly vision-based ones, can hinder operator experience and make it difficult to anticipate or avoid unsafe joint movements or singularities. The integration of adaptive force feedback is motivated by the need to enhance operator intuition and prevent undesirable robot states.
- **Accessibility and Adaptability:** Many full-body teleoperation systems are expensive, cumbersome (e.g., full exoskeletons), or specialized for a particular robot. The authors aim to create a compact, reconfigurable, and cost-effective system that can adapt to various robot configurations, promoting broader accessibility and utility within the robotics community.

The key contributions of CHILD, as outlined by the authors, are:
- A whole-body teleoperation system that enables direct, joint-level control of full-body humanoids or multi-limbed robotic systems.
- A reconfigurable teleoperation system capable of adapting to and controlling a wide range of robot configurations.
- The open-source release of the hardware design to promote accessibility, reproducibility, and further development.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The CHILD system is designed with a focus on reconfigurability, compactness, and direct joint-level control.

**Hardware Design:**
- **Reconfigurability:** The system features seven mounts for leader limbs, allowing easy attachment and detachment via a simple clip mechanism. These mounts are strategically placed to accommodate various shoulder configurations common in humanoid robots, including arm mounts parallel to the ground and inclined by 45 degrees, as well as two leg mounts and one top mount for neck control. This allows adaptation to different robot types (full humanoids, dual-arm systems, single-arm manipulators). Off-the-shelf servo motors (DYNAMIXEL XL330-M288-T) and 3D-printed parts contribute to low cost and customizability.
- **Compactness:** The entire system, including power and electronics, is designed to fit within a standard baby carrier, enabling a single mobile operator to control all four leaders. An alternative desktop monitor stand adapter allows for stationary deployment and multi-operator use, facilitating complex motions like crawling simulations.
- **Kinematic Mapping:** Leader limbs are designed to maintain the same kinematic configuration as their follower counterparts. Translational transformations between joint coordinate frames are scaled by a factor α (e.g., 0.65 for custom arms, 0.9 for G1 arms) to keep the leader workspace within the operator's reach. Each leader end effector includes a handle with a single-DoF trigger for controlling parallel grippers.
- **Mechanical Details:** The torso width is set to 90% of the humanoid robot's shoulder width, with bottom mounts inset to maintain the shoulder-to-hip ratio. Pogo pin connectors are used for power and communication, eliminating external wiring between the torso and leaders. The hardware is entirely 3D printed using PLA on an FDM printer, resulting in a total system cost under $1k.

**Electronics:**
- **Power:** Two 4S 75C LiPo batteries, stepped down by a 5V 15A buck converter, power the onboard computer and motors, providing over two hours of continuous teleoperation.
- **Control:** A Raspberry Pi 5 serves as the onboard computer, reading leader joint positions and velocities. A DYNAMIXEL U2D2 communicates with the servo motors.
- **Orientation Sensing:** A BNO055 9-axis IMU, located in the torso, measures orientation and communicates via I2C. It utilizes an onboard sensor fusion algorithm for absolute orientation readings.
- **Communication:** All components communicate with the follower robot via Wi-Fi, enabling untethered operation.

**Software:**
- **Control Interface:** A reconfigurable design allows users to define motor-joint mappings through configuration files. The onboard computer publishes leader joint states and IMU data via ROS2 over Wi-Fi. The follower robot's onboard computer subscribes to this data and sends corresponding motor commands.
- **Asynchronous Modules:** Three modules run asynchronously on separate threads, sharing a `Joint States` variable to ensure real-time, low-latency control (average latency ~14ms):
 - **Joint State Subscriber:** Reads joint states and IMU data from CHILD.
 - **Locomotion Controller:** Translates leader leg joint states into velocity messages for a walking controller, managing balance and locomotion for the lower body. This allows for tasks like navigation and pick-and-place.
 - **Direct Joint Controller:** Maps leader joint states directly to follower joint states, primarily for upper body (arms and torso) control. It converts IMU data to Euler angles for direct position commands to torso joints. This mode enables full-body joint-level teleoperation, where humanoid legs directly follow leader leg joint positions.
- **Teleoperation Session Protocol:** The session activates when the operator holds leader grippers for three seconds, followed by a slow synchronization of the robot's pose to the leader's.
- **Mode Switching:** When the lower body is in locomotion mode, a single gripper closure for one second activates joystick-based control. The corresponding arm is deactivated, and leg hip joint angles are interpreted as velocity commands (roll for left/right, pitch for forward/backward, yaw for rotational velocities). Releasing the gripper for one second reactivates the arm.
- **Adaptive Force Feedback:** A virtual spring applies bias torques to each leader joint ($\tau_{bias} = k \cdot (q(t) - q_{base})$) to enhance operator experience and prevent unsafe joint configurations. The force feedback strength varies; for example, a stronger bias is applied to deactivated arms during joystick control to help them return to a previous position.
- **Simulation Integration:** A separate MuJoCo simulation node can replace the physical robot, subscribing to leader joint states. The IMU can control the robot's torso orientation relative to the world frame, enabling motions like crawling demonstrations with a fixed pelvis.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research validates the CHILD system through several demonstrations across multiple robot platforms and control modes. The accompanying video provides visual evidence of these demonstrations.

**A. Humanoid Control (Unitree G1 Humanoid Robot):**

1. **Upper Body Teleoperation (Loco-manipulation):**
 - The system was demonstrated using the direct joint controller for the upper body and the locomotion controller for the legs, utilizing the G1's onboard walking and stability controller.
 - A task involving navigating between furniture, grasping a box from a table, transporting it, and setting it down on a second table was successfully executed. This showcased the system's ability to coordinate upper-body manipulation with mobile locomotion.

2. **Full-Body Teleoperation (Direct Joint-Level Control):**
 - Demonstrations requiring fine control over all joint states were performed. For leg leaders, ankle joints were neglected to simplify control.
 - One task involved catching a ball with the feet, dropping it, and passing it back to a thrower. This highlighted the low latency and fine control afforded by direct joint-level teleoperation of the legs. (Note: The humanoid robot was supported by a gantry and a person during this specific demonstration.)
 - A crawling motion was simulated in MuJoCo. With the robot's pelvis fixed and orientation controlled by the leader IMU (waist joints consequently fixed), two operators simultaneously controlled the four limbs via CHILD mounted on a monitor stand, demonstrating the system's capacity for complex, multi-limbed movements.

**B. Alternate Configurations (Non-Humanoid Robot Systems):**

The reconfigurable design of CHILD allowed for teleoperation of other robotic arm systems:

1. **Custom Dual-Arm Kitchen Setup:**
 - By utilizing the top and one side mount, CHILD was configured to approximate a custom dual-arm kitchen setup.
 - Manipulation tasks in a kitchen environment, including an intermediate step of handing over a plate, were performed, demonstrating the system's adaptability for household tasks.

2. **Orthrus (Dual-Arm Mobile Manipulator on Boston Dynamics Spot):**
 - The system was tested on Orthrus, a dual-arm robot where arms are mounted at a 45-degree inclination. The corresponding inclined mounts on CHILD were used.
 - An intermediate step of a pick-and-place task was shown: grasping a toy fish, opening an air fryer basket, placing the fish inside, and closing the basket. (Note: The Spot mobile base was controlled separately via a handheld controller, not integrated with the CHILD teleoperation framework).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The CHILD teleoperation system presents a contribution to the field of humanoid robotics by introducing a method for whole-body, joint-level control through direct joint state mapping.

- **Enabling Diverse Humanoid Tasks:** By allowing direct joint-level control of all four limbs, CHILD extends the range of tasks that humanoid robots can perform beyond typical loco-manipulation scenarios that often rely on high-level locomotion controllers. This includes fine motor control for leg-based manipulation, as demonstrated by the ball-catching task, and complex full-body poses.
- **Increased Accessibility and Reproducibility:** The system's compact, reconfigurable, and low-cost design, coupled with the open-source release of its hardware, lowers the barrier to entry for researchers and developers. This promotes wider adoption, reproducibility of experiments, and facilitates further development and innovation within the robotics community.
- **Enhanced Operator Experience:** The incorporation of adaptive force feedback provides a more intuitive and responsive teleoperation experience. This feedback mechanism aids operators in avoiding joint limits or singularities and improves usability during mode switching, potentially reducing operator fatigue and increasing task efficiency.
- **Versatile Platform for Research:** The reconfigurable nature allows CHILD to control a variety of multi-limbed robotic systems, not just humanoids. This versatility makes it a suitable tool for research in robot learning, human-robot interaction, and manipulation tasks across different robot platforms. The ability to easily integrate with simulation environments further enhances its utility for development and testing.

While the current system requires external support for free-standing whole-body joint-level control, the authors identify this as an area for future work (integrating stability controllers), indicating potential for even more robust and autonomous operation in the future. The design also lays groundwork for more dexterous end effectors to control advanced grippers. Overall, CHILD provides a flexible and accessible platform that could accelerate the development and application of advanced teleoperated humanoid capabilities.
