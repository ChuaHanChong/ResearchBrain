# Reading or Reasoning? Format Decoupled Reinforcement Learning for Document OCR

> [!summary] Summary
> Meituan researchers developed Format Decoupled Reinforcement Learning (FD-RL) to enhance document OCR, specifically addressing high output uncertainty in formatted content like formulas and tables. The method, which integrates supervised fine-tuning with targeted reinforcement learning, established a new state-of-the-art score of 90.41 on the OmniDocBench benchmark.

## Problem

- Advanced OCR models exhibit significantly higher output entropy and uncertainty when processing formatted text (e.g., formulas, tables) compared to plain text.
- Existing supervised fine-tuning (SFT) approaches primarily achieve token-level memorization rather than format-level validation, especially for complex document structures.
- Prior reinforcement learning (RL) attempts for OCR lacked a principled design to specifically target high-entropy formatted text and relied on empirically constructed datasets.

## Method

- The proposed ==Format Decoupled== Reinforcement Learning (**FD-RL**) framework uses a two-stage SFT-then-RL training paradigm, built upon the Qwen3-VL-4B model and a large-scale, format-rich dataset.
- An entropy-based data filtration strategy selects a subset of high-entropy samples (e.g., top 50%) for RL training, focusing optimization on instances with greater structural complexity and prediction uncertainty.
- Format Decoupled Rewards are designed with content-specific metrics (normalized edit distance for text, BLEU for formulas, TEDS for tables) to optimize for structural validity and format correctness.

## Results

- FD-RL achieved an overall score of **90.41** on OmniDocBench, setting a new state-of-the-art for end-to-end models and surpassing previous leading methods by up to **3.4** points.
- The method established best performance in formula recognition (CDM **88.67**) and table parsing (TEDS **87.35**, TEDS-S **92.10**) on the benchmark.
- Ablation studies confirmed that reinforcement learning, entropy-based data filtration, and format-decoupled rewards each contribute substantially to performance gains, particularly improving formula recognition by +**3.07** CDM and table parsing by +**6.08** TEDS.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Document OCR, particularly for formatted content, fundamentally requires "reasoning over diverse reading pathways" beyond mere perceptual accuracy.
> - High output entropy in formatted text serves as a crucial indicator of underlying reasoning challenges, which can be effectively targeted and optimized by reinforcement learning.
> - Decoupling reward functions by specific content type (plain text, formulas, tables) allows models to learn robust format rules and significantly improve structural coherence and validity.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Optical Character Recognition (OCR) and document parsing has seen continuous evolution, driven by the need to efficiently digitize and extract information from images and scanned documents. Historically, document OCR relied on **traditional pipeline-based methods** that involved sequential stages: first detecting layout information (e.g., text blocks, tables, formulas) and then employing specialized parsers for recognition and ordering. While stable, these methods often suffered from limited flexibility and high fine-tuning costs for diverse document types.

The advent of **Vision-Language Models (VLMs)** marked a significant paradigm shift. End-to-end VLMs, such as Deepseek-OCR, dots.ocr, and Qwen3-VL, aim to directly decode text from visual inputs using unified architectures, simplifying workflows and offering superior generalization. More recently, **pipeline-based VLMs** emerged, integrating the strengths of both approaches by leveraging pre-detected layout information (often from traditional OCR components) to enhance the accuracy of VLM-based methods.

Despite these advancements, the authors identify a critical unmet challenge: even advanced OCR models exhibit significantly higher output entropy when processing **formatted text** (e.g., mathematical formulas, tables) compared to plain text. This high entropy signifies considerable uncertainty and potential for diverse, yet semantically equivalent, reading pathways. Existing methods predominantly focus on supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and meticulous data engineering to enhance perceptual capabilities, often overlooking the inherent "reasoning" aspect required to navigate these high-entropy formatted elements. The paper explicitly argues that current SFT approaches, while powerful, primarily achieve "token-level memorization" rather than "format-level validation," especially for complex structures.

While some prior work has explored **Reinforcement Learning (RL) for OCR** to optimize complex layout analysis and reading order, these attempts have largely relied on empirically constructed RL datasets and lacked a principled design to target high-entropy formatted text. This paper directly addresses this gap by observing the statistical patterns of high entropy in formatted text and proposing a novel RL framework specifically designed to leverage these patterns for targeted optimization. By shifting the focus from purely perceptual tasks to incorporating a "reasoning-after-reading" paradigm guided by format-specific rewards, this work aims to push the boundaries of OCR beyond mere recognition towards a deeper understanding of document structure and content.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this research stems from a critical observation: advanced OCR models, despite their sophistication, exhibit significantly higher output entropy when dealing with formatted text (such as formulas and tables) compared to plain text. This phenomenon, which can be an order of magnitude higher, indicates a struggle with high output uncertainty and the existence of multiple valid interpretations or "reading pathways" for complex structures. For example, a fraction could be represented as `1/2` or `\frac{1}{2}`, both semantically correct but structurally different. Existing OCR approaches, largely built on supervised fine-tuning (SFT) with extensive data engineering, primarily focus on improving perceptual accuracy and token-level memorization. This focus, the authors argue, often overlooks the need for robust reasoning capabilities to handle the inherent ambiguity and structural complexity of formatted content.

Given this insight, the **key objectives** of this paper are:

1. **To demonstrate that "reasoning over diverse reading pathways" is crucial for improving OCR performance, particularly for format-sensitive documents.** This challenges the prevailing notion that OCR is primarily a straightforward perceptual task.
2. **To propose a novel framework, Format Decoupled Reinforcement Learning (FD-RL), that specifically targets and optimizes these high-entropy patterns.** The goal is to move beyond token-level memorization towards format-level validation and structural coherence.
3. **To construct a large-scale, format-rich training corpus** that can effectively support both initial supervised learning and subsequent reinforcement learning for diverse document parsing tasks.
4. **To devise innovative RL strategies**, including entropy-based data filtration and format-decoupled rewards, that enable targeted optimization for different content types (plain text, formulas, tables) and foster learning of underlying format rules rather than rote memorization.
5. **To achieve state-of-the-art performance** on comprehensive document parsing benchmarks, validating the effectiveness of the proposed reasoning-after-reading paradigm.

In essence, the research aims to fundamentally enhance OCR models' ability to "reason" about the structure and context of formatted content, leading to more accurate and robust document parsing, especially in challenging, format-intensive scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed Format Decoupled Reinforcement Learning (FD-RL) method adopts a sophisticated two-stage SFT-then-RL training paradigm, complemented by meticulous data engineering.

#### 3.1. Dataset Engineering

The foundation of FD-RL is a comprehensive and format-rich training corpus constructed through three main strategies:
1. **Open-Source Dataset Quality Enhancement:** Existing open-source datasets (e.g., PDFA, DocStruct4M, DocGenome for office documents; IAM, ORAND-CAR, HME for handwritten text/formulas) are collected. A quality inspection is performed to identify issues like missing content, incorrect reading order, and repeated sentences. To rectify this, the lightweight GOT model is used for re-annotation, followed by similarity-based filtering to retain high-quality samples.
2. **Real-World PDF Data Construction:** To bridge the gap between open-source and diverse real-world scenarios, a large collection of real-world PDF documents is acquired and annotated. This process is categorized into:
 - **Layout-Aware Data Construction:** Focuses on structural understanding at page-level (using MinerU for Markdown labels, LaTeX validation), region-level (using Mathpix for coordinate-aware OCR, color-guided annotations), and multi-page sequences (2-6 pages).
 - **Content-Aware Data Construction:** Targets accurate recognition of text, formulas (converted to LaTeX), and tables (converted to HTML) in reading order, using dots.ocr and post-processing steps (e.g., removing image links, headers/footers).
3. **Synthetic OCR Data Generation:** To address the scarcity of educational and academic data, synthetic samples are generated from online resources (practice problems, academic Q&A). After cleaning and categorization, HTML templates with realistic visual parameters are designed, content is injected (with MathJax for LaTeX), and Playwright is used to render high-resolution images paired with Markdown labels.

This multi-source approach yields a large-scale dataset of 566k samples across nine document categories, ensuring comprehensive coverage of various document types, layouts, and formats.

#### 3.2. Model Training

The training pipeline consists of two distinct stages:

**Stage 1: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)**
- **Base Model:** The pre-trained Vision-Language Model Qwen3-VL-4B is used as the backbone.
- **Fine-tuning Process:** SFT is performed on the entire large-scale, format-rich dataset. Crucially, the visual encoder (ViT) and multi-layer perceptron (MLP) modules are frozen, while only the parameters of the large language model (LLM) are updated. This strategy leverages the VLM's existing visual representations while focusing computational resources on improving sequence decoding and format generation capabilities specific to OCR tasks. Data is progressively sampled from the three sources during training.

**Stage 2: Reinforcement Learning (RL)**
This stage is designed to specifically address the limitations of SFT in handling format-intensive content by optimizing for format correctness and structural validity.
1. **Entropy-based Data Filtration Strategy:**
 - **Initial Selection:** A subset of SFT data with a high proportion of formulas and tables is selected, and language ratios (Chinese/English) are balanced. Pure plain text samples are removed.
 - **Entropy Calculation:** The SFT-trained model performs inference on these candidate samples to obtain log probabilities for each generated token. The average output entropy for each sample is then calculated using the formula: $D_{filtered} = (d \in D_{raw} | -\frac{1}{N_d}\sum_{i=1}^{N_d} \log p_i(d) \geq \tau)$, where $N_d$ is the number of tokens, $p_i(d)$ is the probability of the $i$-th token, and $\tau$ is an entropy threshold.
 - **Filtering:** Only samples exhibiting high average output entropy (e.g., top 50%) are retained for RL training. This focuses the RL process on instances where the SFT model shows greater structural complexity and prediction uncertainty, thereby strengthening reasoning capacity on format-intensive content.
2. **Format Decoupled Reward:**
 - To prevent format errors from being overshadowed by content errors, rewards are calculated separately for different content types. Regular expressions are used to extract plain text, formulas, and tables from both the model's output and the ground truth.
 - **Plain Text Reward:** Normalized edit distance (character-level supervision).
 - **Formula Reward:** BLEU score, applied after converting formulas to equivalent LaTeX expressions (correctness supervision).
 - **Table Reward:** TEDS (Tree-Edit-Distance-based Similarity) for structural consistency.
 - **Overall Reward:** A weighted average of these content-specific rewards (Equation 2), where each reward is activated only if the corresponding ground truth content type is present.
3. **Format-Decoupled RL (GRPO):**
 - The model is fine-tuned using GRPO (Group-normalized Policy Optimization), an RL algorithm that optimizes policy directly with group-normalized rewards from sampled responses without requiring a separate critic. For each input, multiple responses are sampled, and group-normalized advantages are computed (Equation 3) to guide the policy update (Equation 4). This allows the model to learn from diverse outputs and prioritize format-level validity.

The entire RL stage is conducted using the EasyR1 framework, with specific hyperparameters for batch sizes, temperature, and learning rates, over one epoch on the filtered, high-entropy dataset.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The FD-RL method demonstrates significant advancements in document OCR, particularly for format-intensive content, achieving state-of-the-art results on a prominent benchmark and validating its core hypotheses through extensive ablation studies.

#### 4.1. State-of-the-Art Performance on OmniDocBench

FD-RL achieved an impressive **overall score of 90.41 on OmniDocBench**, a comprehensive document parsing benchmark, establishing a new record for end-to-end models. This performance surpasses leading end-to-end specialized VLMs like dots.ocr (88.41) by 2.0 points and Deepseek-OCR (87.01) by 3.4 points.

- **Text Recognition:** FD-RL achieved an Edit Distance of 0.049, ranking second, very closely trailing dots.ocr (0.048).
- **Formula Recognition:** It set the best CDM (Content Distance Metric) score of 88.67, outperforming olmOCR-7B (86.04) and Nanonets-OCR-s (85.90).
- **Table Parsing:** FD-RL achieved the top TEDS (Tree-Edit-Distance-based Similarity) score of 87.35 and TEDS-S (Table Edit Distance Similarity with Structure) score of 92.10.
- **Reading Order Prediction:** It secured the second-best Edit Distance of 0.055.

These results highlight FD-RL's superior capability in handling complex document parsing tasks, especially those involving structured and formatted content.

#### 4.2. Performance Across Document Types

FD-RL demonstrates robust cross-domain generalization, securing **top performance in 4 out of 9 document categories** on OmniDocBench (Slides, Exam Papers, Books, Notes) and ranking second in the remaining 5. This consistent performance across diverse document types underscores the effectiveness of its comprehensive training corpus and format-decoupled optimization.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

The paper presents thorough ablation studies that meticulously validate the contribution of each component of the FD-RL framework:

- **Multi-Source Data:** Progressive integration of open-source datasets (OSD), real-world PDF data (RWPD), synthetic OCR data (SOD), and reinforcement learning data (RLD) consistently improved performance. Starting from a baseline of 46.06 (no data), OSD brought it to 78.25, RWPD to 84.16, SOD to 87.13, and finally RLD pushed it to 90.41. This confirms that each data source plays a complementary and essential role in building robust OCR capabilities.
- **Two-Stage Training Strategy (SFT-then-RL):**
 - RL applied directly to a general VLM (without SFT) yielded minimal improvement (49.37 overall), emphasizing the necessity of foundational OCR capabilities.
 - SFT alone achieved substantial gains (87.13), validating its importance for domain adaptation.
 - The full SFT+RL pipeline achieved the best performance (90.41), with RL providing specific and significant improvements in formula (+3.07 CDM) and table recognition (+6.08 TEDS, +7.19 TEDS-S), demonstrating its role in shifting optimization from token-level accuracy to format-level validity.
- **Entropy-based Data Filtration Strategy:** The study revealed that an **optimal filtration rate of 50%** achieved the best overall performance (90.41). Both no filtration (0% rate, 88.47) and excessive filtration (75% rate, 88.58) led to performance degradation. This validates that targeting high-entropy, complex samples is crucial for RL effectiveness, and an appropriate balance is needed to ensure both quality and diversity.
- **Format Decoupled Reward:** This ablation study demonstrated the necessity of specialized reward components:
 - Unified string match (SM) without format separation resulted in 88.64.
 - Introducing format separation with SM improved performance to 89.61.
 - Adding expression correctness (EC) for formulas further enhanced it to 89.80.
 - The full scheme with structural coherence (SC) rewards for tables achieved the highest 90.41. This strongly confirms that decomposing rewards for different content types is essential for robust, format-level parsing.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Visualizations

Qualitative examples provided in the paper visually compare FD-RL with baselines (Qwen3-VL-4B and FD-RL(SFT)). FD-RL consistently demonstrated superior performance in generating well-formatted HTML code for tables and accurately recognizing complex mathematical formulas and puzzles, while baselines often exhibited instruction-following errors or failed to preserve structural integrity.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in "Reading or Reasoning? Format Decoupled Reinforcement Learning for Document OCR" offers several significant contributions and has considerable potential impact:

1. **Novel Conceptualization of OCR as a Reasoning Task:** The most impactful contribution is the authors' fundamental insight that document OCR, particularly for formatted content, is not merely a perceptual task but inherently involves "reasoning over diverse reading pathways." By identifying high entropy in formatted text as a critical indicator of this underlying reasoning challenge, the paper shifts the paradigm of OCR research from solely focusing on data engineering and token-level accuracy to incorporating explicit mechanisms for format-level validation and structural coherence.

2. **Methodological Innovation in Reinforcement Learning for OCR:** FD-RL introduces a principled and effective way to integrate reinforcement learning into the OCR pipeline.
 - **Entropy-based Data Filtration:** This novel strategy provides a data-driven approach to identify and prioritize challenging, format-intensive samples, ensuring that RL training focuses on the most impactful instances. This is a generalizable technique that could benefit other tasks involving complex, high-uncertainty outputs.
 - **Format Decoupled Rewards:** By tailoring reward functions to specific content types (plain text, formulas, tables), FD-RL moves beyond generic token-level supervision. This fine-grained feedback enables the model to learn robust format rules and structural integrity, which is crucial for real-world document understanding where structural errors can be as detrimental as textual errors.

3. **Establishment of New State-of-the-Art:** By achieving a new record of 90.41 on OmniDocBench for end-to-end models, FD-RL demonstrates superior performance, especially in recognizing and structuring formulas and tables. This significantly advances the capabilities of document OCR systems, making them more reliable for handling complex documents.

4. **Broad Practical Implications:** The improved accuracy in parsing complex documents (e.g., scientific papers, financial reports, legal documents, educational materials) has vast practical implications across various industries:
 - **Enhanced Information Extraction:** More accurate extraction of structured data from diverse document types will boost efficiency in fields like finance, healthcare, and research.
 - **Improved Document Digitization:** Facilitates the creation of high-quality digital archives from scanned documents, preserving not just text but also crucial formatting.
 - **Advanced AI-powered Automation:** Enables more sophisticated automated processes that rely on understanding document structure, such as intelligent process automation (IPA) and document analysis for large language models (LLMs).
 - **Accessibility:** Better recognition of complex structures can improve accessibility tools for visually impaired individuals.

5. **Future Research Directions:** This work opens several avenues for future research, including:
 - Exploring other forms of reasoning (e.g., semantic reasoning, spatial reasoning) within document OCR.
 - Applying entropy-based data selection and decoupled reward mechanisms to other multimodal or sequence generation tasks with complex output structures.
 - Further refining reward functions to incorporate more sophisticated metrics for structural and semantic correctness, possibly with human feedback in the loop.
 - Investigating the scalability of FD-RL to even larger and more diverse document corpora and models.

In conclusion, FD-RL represents a significant step forward in document OCR by addressing a previously overlooked aspect of the problem: the need for reasoning in handling formatted, high-entropy content. Its novel methodology and demonstrated superior performance are poised to influence both academic research and industrial applications in document intelligence.
