# Waymax: An Accelerated, Data-Driven Simulator for Large-Scale Autonomous Driving Research

> [!summary] Summary
> Waymax is a JAX-based, hardware-accelerated multi-agent driving simulator that initializes scenarios from real Waymo Open Motion Dataset logs, runs entirely in-graph on GPU/TPU, and is differentiable. It supplies roadgraph-derived on/off-route paths, closed-loop safety and progress metrics, IDM reactive sim agents, and a Brax-style pure functional reset/step API. Benchmarks show over 5000 Hz per-example stepping and reveal RL overfitting to sim agents.

## Problem

- Photo-realistic driving simulators emphasize rendering but still lack diverse scenario generation and realistic closed-loop behavior for surrounding vehicles, pedestrians, and cyclists.
- Existing log-based multi-agent simulators run stateful on CPU, blocking in-graph compilation and differentiable rollouts needed by large-scale distributed machine learning workflows.
- Prior benchmarks rarely provide routes or goals alongside real expert data, so planning policies cannot be scored consistently in closed loop at dataset scale.

## Method

- **Waymax** is a ==JAX/XLA== functionally pure environment following ==Brax== design: stateless reset and step allow jit compilation, batching, in-graph training, backtracking search, and differentiable simulation on TPU/GPU.
- Scenario construction from Waymo Open Motion Dataset logs plus depth-first roadgraph traversal producing on-route and off-route path sets for goal conditioning and progress scoring.
- Pluggable dynamics (delta and bicycle action spaces, continuous or discrete), closed-loop metric suite (off-road, off-route, collision, kinematic infeasibility, ADE, route progress), and ==IDM== reactive sim agents.

## Results

- On a V100 with batch size 16, step takes 2.86 ms, an equivalent per-example rate above **5000 Hz**; evaluating 44K scenarios on 8 V100s takes under 2 minutes.
- Route conditioning cuts behavior-cloning off-route rate from **2.31%** to **0.96%**, collision from **4.92%** to **4.59%**, and log ADE from 2.41 m to 2.26 m.
- A DQN agent trained against IDM agents collides **6.50%** against IDM but **25.15%** against log playback, over **4x** higher, exposing exploitation of compliant sim agents.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Writing a simulator as pure functions in an accelerator-compiled framework removes host-device transfer bottlenecks and unlocks batching, differentiability, and search for free.
> - Reactive rule-based sim agents flatter evaluation and weaken training signal, since policies learn to rely on other agents yielding rather than avoiding collisions themselves.
> - High-level route information is a strong, cheap conditioning signal: it improves route adherence, safety, and imitation accuracy simultaneously without architectural change.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

The development of safe and reliable autonomous vehicles requires extensive testing in diverse scenarios before deployment on real roads. Simulation has become a crucial tool in this process, allowing researchers to test autonomous driving systems at scale without the costs and risks of physical testing. However, existing simulators often face trade-offs between realism, speed, and compatibility with modern machine learning frameworks.

Waymax addresses these challenges by combining data-driven realism with hardware acceleration to create a high-throughput simulator specifically designed for autonomous driving research. Developed collaboratively by Waymo Research and Google DeepMind, Waymax leverages real-world driving data from the Waymo Open Motion Dataset (WOMD) and utilizes the JAX library to enable efficient execution on GPUs and TPUs.

### 2. Background and Context

Autonomous driving research has seen significant advancements in recent years, with machine learning approaches increasingly being applied to planning and decision-making tasks. This shift has created a need for simulators that can:

1. Generate realistic scenarios that capture the complexity of real-world driving
2. Efficiently scale to produce large amounts of synthetic driving experience
3. Integrate seamlessly with modern machine learning frameworks

Existing simulators like CARLA, SUMMIT, and nuPlan provide valuable capabilities but often emphasize photorealistic rendering over simulation throughput. While visual fidelity is important for perception tasks, planning and decision-making research primarily requires accurate behavioral simulation of traffic participants.

Waymax fills this gap by prioritizing:
- Data-driven realism derived from real-world driving records
- Hardware acceleration for high-throughput simulation
- Seamless integration with machine learning training pipelines
- Flexible API design for research customization

### 3. Core Architecture and Design

Waymax is built using JAX, a numerical computing library that enables automatic differentiation and just-in-time compilation. This choice allows the simulator to run entirely on accelerator hardware like GPUs and TPUs, significantly improving simulation speed compared to CPU-based alternatives.

The simulator architecture consists of several key components:

1. **Simulation State**: Contains all information about the current state of the environment, including agent poses, road network information, and simulation metadata.

2. **Dynamics Model**: Controls how agents move in response to actions, implementing simple bicycle model dynamics for vehicles.

3. **Environment Interface**: Provides standardized APIs for agent interaction, supporting both single-agent and multi-agent training paradigms.

4. **Metrics and Evaluation**: Implements a suite of metrics to evaluate agent performance on safety, comfort, and progress.

One of Waymax's most significant architectural advantages is its support for "in-graph" simulation. This means the entire simulation can run within the same computational graph as model training, eliminating the need for expensive inter-process communication.

The code below demonstrates the simplicity of the Waymax API for running a simulation:

```python
# Initialize simulator with data from WOMD
simulator = waymax.Simulator(scenario_data)

# Reset simulator to initial state
sim_state = simulator.reset()

# Run simulation for 10 steps
for _ in range(10):
    # Get observations for the SDC (self-driving car)
    observations = simulator.observe(sim_state)

    # Compute actions using a policy
    actions = policy(observations)

    # Apply actions and step the simulation
    sim_state = simulator.step(sim_state, actions)
```

### 4. Hardware Acceleration Performance

A key contribution of Waymax is its focus on hardware acceleration to enable high-throughput simulation. The authors conducted benchmarks comparing the performance of Waymax on different hardware platforms (CPU vs. GPU) and with different batch sizes.

The benchmarks demonstrate that:

1. Waymax achieves significant speedups when running on GPUs compared to CPUs
2. Performance scales efficiently with batch size, enabling parallel simulation of many scenarios
3. Memory usage remains manageable even with large batch sizes

For example, with a batch size of 16, Waymax can simulate approximately 130 steps per second on CPU and scales efficiently when increasing the number of concurrent scenarios. On GPU, performance remains more consistent across different numbers of scenarios, demonstrating the benefits of hardware acceleration.

### 5. Data-Driven Simulation

Rather than generating synthetic scenarios, Waymax leverages real-world driving data from the Waymo Open Motion Dataset (WOMD) to initialize simulations. This approach ensures that simulated scenarios capture the complexity and diversity of real-world driving situations.

Each scenario in WOMD contains:
- Road network information (lane geometry, traffic signals, etc.)
- Traffic agent trajectories (vehicles, pedestrians, cyclists)
- Metadata about the scenario (time of day, location, etc.)

Waymax uses this data to:
1. Initialize the positions and velocities of all agents
2. Define the road network and routing information
3. Provide realistic context for planning decisions

The simulator supports two modes for simulating background agents:
1. **Log playback**: Agents follow their recorded trajectories from the dataset
2. **Interactive agents**: Agents react to the controlled vehicle using behavior models

This flexibility allows researchers to study how different agent behaviors affect the performance of autonomous driving systems.

### 6. Agent Implementation and Benchmarking

Waymax includes several baseline planning agents to serve as benchmarks and starting points for research:

1. **Expert Imitation Agent**: Trained using behavior cloning to mimic human driving behavior
2. **Rule-based IDM Agent**: Implements the Intelligent Driver Model for longitudinal control
3. **Reinforcement Learning Agent**: Trained using PPO (Proximal Policy Optimization)

These agents are evaluated on a suite of metrics including:
- **Progress**: Distance traveled along the route
- **Success rate**: Percentage of scenarios completed without collisions or violations
- **Comfort**: Smoothness of acceleration and jerk profiles
- **Safety**: Collision and near-collision events

The simulator also provides tools for rendering and visualization, enabling qualitative analysis of agent behavior. These visualizations help researchers identify failure modes and understand the decision-making process of different algorithms.

### 7. Routing and Behavior Modeling

One key aspect of autonomous driving is following a specified route while interacting safely with other road users. Waymax provides explicit routing information to planning agents and allows researchers to study how different routing representations affect performance.

The behavior of simulated agents significantly impacts the training and evaluation of autonomous driving systems. Waymax supports two approaches to modeling background agent behavior:

1. **Replay-based**: Agents follow pre-recorded trajectories from the dataset
2. **Interactive**: Agents dynamically respond to the controlled vehicle

Experiments showed that training against interactive agents can lead to different behaviors than training against replay agents. For example, reinforcement learning agents tended to overfit to the relatively simpler behavior of rule-based IDM (Intelligent Driver Model) agents, resulting in poor generalization to more complex real-world behavior.

### 8. Key Findings and Results

The authors' experiments with Waymax yielded several important findings:

1. **Hardware acceleration provides significant speedups**: Using GPUs instead of CPUs can increase simulation throughput by an order of magnitude, enabling more efficient training and evaluation of learning-based agents.

2. **Batch processing further increases efficiency**: Simulating multiple scenarios in parallel provides substantial efficiency gains, as shown in the performance benchmarks with different batch sizes.

3. **Route information improves agent performance**: Experiments showed that providing explicit route information to planning agents significantly improves their ability to follow desired paths and increases success rates.

4. **Agent behavior modeling affects learning outcomes**: The behavior of simulated background agents (rule-based vs. replay) significantly impacts the training and evaluation of autonomous driving algorithms. Agents trained against simpler rule-based models often fail to generalize to more complex real-world behaviors.

5. **Memory usage scales efficiently**: Even with large batch sizes, memory consumption remains manageable, allowing for efficient simulation on consumer-grade hardware.

### 9. Impact and Significance

Waymax represents a significant advancement in autonomous driving simulation technology with several important implications:

1. **Democratizing autonomous driving research**: By providing an efficient, open-source simulator that runs on widely available hardware, Waymax lowers the barrier to entry for autonomous driving research.

2. **Enabling large-scale machine learning**: The simulator's hardware acceleration and JAX integration enable training machine learning models on vast amounts of synthetic driving experience.

3. **Bridging the sim-to-real gap**: By using real-world driving data and realistic behavior models, Waymax helps minimize the discrepancy between simulation and real-world performance.

4. **Standardizing evaluation**: The included benchmarks and metrics provide a common framework for evaluating and comparing different autonomous driving approaches.

5. **Accelerating development cycles**: Fast simulation enables rapid iteration on algorithms and ideas, potentially accelerating the development of safe and reliable autonomous vehicles.

### 10. Conclusion

Waymax introduces a new paradigm for autonomous driving simulation that prioritizes data-driven realism, hardware acceleration, and seamless integration with machine learning workflows. By leveraging real-world driving data from the Waymo Open Motion Dataset and the computational efficiency of JAX, it enables researchers to conduct large-scale experiments that would be impractical with traditional simulation approaches.

The simulator's design choices reflect a deep understanding of the needs of autonomous driving researchers, focusing on the aspects of simulation that are most critical for developing and evaluating planning and decision-making algorithms. Its modular architecture and flexible API allow for customization and extension to support diverse research needs.

As autonomous driving technology continues to evolve, simulators like Waymax will play an increasingly important role in testing and validating new approaches before they are deployed on real roads. By making large-scale simulation more accessible and efficient, Waymax contributes to the broader goal of developing safer and more reliable autonomous vehicles.
