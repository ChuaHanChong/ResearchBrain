# PILOT: A Perceptive Integrated Low-level Controller for Loco-manipulation over Unstructured Scenes

> [!summary] Summary
> A perceptive integrated low-level controller (PILOT) enables humanoid robots to perform stable loco-manipulation, such as traversing stairs and platforms while handling objects. This framework successfully transfers from simulation to a physical Unitree G1 robot without fine-tuning, demonstrating robust whole-body coordination.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots traditionally struggle with robust whole-body control for loco-manipulation tasks in complex, unstructured environments designed for humans.
- Most existing loco-manipulation controllers lack 'exteroceptive awareness,' failing to perceive and adapt to terrain variations, which limits their stability and safety in non-planar scenes.
- Developing unified policies for whole-body control is challenging due to high-dimensional state-action spaces, potential gradient interference between locomotion and manipulation objectives, and inefficient exploration during training.

## Method

- PILOT is a unified, single-stage reinforcement learning framework using PPO, trained to generate a 29-dimensional action vector for humanoid joint torques.
- It integrates a cross-modal context encoder that fuses historical proprioceptive observations with real-time egocentric elevation maps via an attention-based mechanism to identify optimal steppable areas.
- A Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) actor policy, featuring a gating network and four expert networks, manages the high-dimensional action space and utilizes residual action parameterization for upper-body control, dynamically switching skills based on task demands.

## Results

- In simulation, PILOT achieved superior command tracking performance with the lowest overall errors for both locomotion and manipulation, outperforming state-of-the-art blind loco-manipulation baselines.
- Ablation studies confirmed that perceptive input, the attention-based encoder, and the MoE architecture are critical for reducing foot stumbling and maintaining robust performance on challenging terrains.
- The trained policy successfully performed zero-shot transfer to a Unitree G1 humanoid robot, demonstrating stable object transport tasks over multi-step staircases and elevated platforms via teleoperation, and autonomously executing a 'Lift-Box' task when integrated with a high-level planner.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Fusing proprioceptive and exteroceptive perception through a prediction-based and attention-based cross-modal encoder is crucial for enabling robust and safe humanoid navigation on complex terrains.
> - The Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architecture effectively mitigates gradient interference and promotes specialization among experts, leading to superior whole-body coordination and an expansive operational workspace for diverse motor skills.
> - A carefully designed adaptive command curriculum, progressively introducing complex locomotion and manipulation tasks, facilitates the training of a generalizable low-level controller capable of zero-shot transfer to real-world humanoid robots.
