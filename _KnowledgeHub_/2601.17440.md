# PILOT: A Perceptive Integrated Low-level Controller for Loco-manipulation over Unstructured Scenes

> [!summary] Summary
> A perceptive integrated low-level controller (PILOT) enables humanoid robots to perform stable loco-manipulation, such as traversing stairs and platforms while handling objects. This framework successfully transfers from simulation to a physical Unitree G1 robot without fine-tuning, demonstrating robust whole-body coordination.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots traditionally struggle with robust whole-body control for loco-manipulation tasks in complex, unstructured environments designed for humans.
- Most existing loco-manipulation controllers lack 'exteroceptive awareness,' failing to perceive and adapt to terrain variations, which limits their stability and safety in non-planar scenes.
- Developing unified policies for whole-body control is challenging due to high-dimensional state-action spaces, potential gradient interference between locomotion and manipulation objectives, and inefficient exploration during training.

## Method

- PILOT is a unified, single-stage reinforcement learning framework using PPO, trained to generate a 29-dimensional action vector for humanoid joint torques.
- It integrates a cross-modal context encoder that fuses historical proprioceptive observations with real-time egocentric elevation maps via an attention-based mechanism to identify optimal steppable areas.
- A Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) actor policy, featuring a gating network and four expert networks, manages the high-dimensional action space and utilizes residual action parameterization for upper-body control, dynamically switching skills based on task demands.

## Results

- In simulation, PILOT achieved superior command tracking performance with the lowest overall errors for both locomotion and manipulation, outperforming state-of-the-art blind loco-manipulation baselines.
- Ablation studies confirmed that perceptive input, the attention-based encoder, and the MoE architecture are critical for reducing foot stumbling and maintaining robust performance on challenging terrains.
- The trained policy successfully performed zero-shot transfer to a Unitree G1 humanoid robot, demonstrating stable object transport tasks over multi-step staircases and elevated platforms via teleoperation, and autonomously executing a 'Lift-Box' task when integrated with a high-level planner.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Fusing proprioceptive and exteroceptive perception through a prediction-based and attention-based cross-modal encoder is crucial for enabling robust and safe humanoid navigation on complex terrains.
> - The Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architecture effectively mitigates gradient interference and promotes specialization among experts, leading to superior whole-body coordination and an expansive operational workspace for diverse motor skills.
> - A carefully designed adaptive command curriculum, progressively introducing complex locomotion and manipulation tasks, facilitates the training of a generalizable low-level controller capable of zero-shot transfer to real-world humanoid robots.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of humanoid robots capable of operating effectively in human-centric, unstructured environments is a grand challenge in robotics. This paper, PILOT, directly contributes to this objective by addressing critical limitations in current humanoid whole-body control. The broader research landscape can be broadly categorized into several areas:

**From Model-Based to Learning-Based Control:** Traditionally, robot control, especially for complex systems like humanoids, relied heavily on model-based methods (e.g., inverse kinematics, whole-body optimization, model predictive control). While effective in controlled environments, these methods often struggle with unmodeled disturbances, environmental variability, and the inherent complexity of high-dimensional, dynamically coupled systems. The field has seen a significant paradigm shift towards data-driven, end-to-end learning methods, particularly reinforcement learning (RL), which offers greater robustness and adaptability to unknown conditions. PILOT is firmly situated within this modern, learning-based paradigm.

**Evolution of Legged Locomotion:** Early RL approaches for legged robots focused on "blind" locomotion, using only proprioceptive feedback to traverse relatively flat or mildly irregular terrains. While impressive, these policies lacked explicit awareness of the surrounding environment, severely limiting their applicability to complex terrains with stairs, gaps, or significant height variations. Recent advancements have integrated exteroceptive perception, such as LiDAR-based elevation maps, into the control loop. Works like PIM and He et al. have demonstrated improved traversability for humanoids and quadrupedal robots by incorporating terrain awareness. PILOT builds upon these perceptive locomotion advancements, but crucially extends them beyond mere locomotion to full loco-manipulation.

**Humanoid Loco-Manipulation Controllers:** The simultaneous control of a humanoid robot's locomotion and manipulation capabilities – known as loco-manipulation – presents a formidable challenge due to the high-dimensional state-action space and dynamic coupling between the floating base and end-effectors. Existing approaches typically fall into two categories:
- **Decoupled Methods:** These simplify the problem by separating lower-body locomotion control from upper-body manipulation (e.g., HOMIE, FALCON). While easier to train and potentially more stable for individual tasks, they often sacrifice natural whole-body synergies, limit the operational workspace, and can lead to suboptimal or rigid behaviors due to lack of coordination.
- **Unified Methods:** These aim to control all degrees of freedom with a single policy to maximize coordination and exploit natural synergies (e.g., ULC, some motion-capture mimicry works). However, training monolithic unified policies is challenging due to exploration inefficiency in high-dimensional spaces, gradient interference between conflicting objectives (e.g., balance vs. end-effector precision), and potential distribution bias if relying on kinematically feasible motion-capture data.

**Addressing Gaps in the Landscape:** PILOT directly addresses two critical gaps in the existing landscape:
1. **Lack of Exteroceptive Awareness in Loco-Manipulation:** Most existing loco-manipulation controllers, whether decoupled or unified, operate "blindly," assuming planar or simple terrains. This severely limits their real-world utility in unstructured environments where terrain variations are common and critical for safe and effective task execution. PILOT integrates sophisticated terrain perception to enable traversal-aware loco-manipulation.
2. **Robust, Coordinated Whole-Body Control for Large Workspaces:** While unified controllers attempt this, they often struggle with the inherent complexities. PILOT's design explicitly tackles high-dimensional control and conflicting objectives through its Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architecture and residual action parameterization, offering improved coordination and an expansive workspace without relying on potentially biased motion-capture data.

In summary, PILOT represents a significant step forward by unifying perceptive locomotion with large-workspace whole-body control into a single, robust low-level controller. It leverages recent successes in RL for legged locomotion and intelligent control architectures to overcome the limitations of both blind and decoupled loco-manipulation systems, pushing humanoids closer to practical deployment in complex, dynamic, real-world settings.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of the PILOT framework is to develop a robust, perceptive, and unified low-level controller that enables humanoid robots to perform seamless loco-manipulation tasks within complex, unstructured, and human-centered environments. This ambitious goal is driven by several key motivations and aims to address specific challenges prevalent in the field of humanoid robotics:

1. **Enabling Humanoids in Unstructured Environments:** Humanoid robots are envisioned for diverse interactions and daily service tasks in environments designed for humans. However, such environments are rarely perfectly flat or structured. A major motivation is to equip humanoids with controllers that can traverse non-planar scenes (e.g., stairs, rugged terrain, elevated platforms) while concurrently executing dexterous manipulation tasks, a capability largely absent in existing low-level whole-body controllers.

2. **Integrating Perception for Enhanced Stability and Safety:** Most traditional whole-body controllers lack "exteroceptive awareness" – they cannot perceive and adapt to their immediate surroundings. This leads to instability and vulnerability when encountering unmodeled terrain variations. A primary objective of PILOT is to tightly couple real-time perception of terrain geometry (e.g., via LiDAR-based elevation maps) with locomotion and manipulation planning to ensure precise foot placement, minimize collisions, and maintain balance, thereby increasing robustness and safety.

3. **Achieving Coordinated Whole-Body Control over Large Workspaces:** Human tasks often require coordinated movements involving legs, torso, and arms across a wide range of motion. Separating control for different body parts (decoupled control) sacrifices natural synergies and limits the robot's expressive capabilities and workspace. The motivation is to develop a single, unified policy that can concurrently control all degrees of freedom, enabling fluid whole-body behaviors and maximizing reachable workspace. This objective is complicated by the inherent challenges of high-dimensional state-action spaces and often conflicting objectives (e.g., maintaining balance versus reaching for an object).

4. **Mitigating Training Challenges in Unified Policies:** Training a monolithic unified policy for loco-manipulation is technically difficult due to issues like gradient interference between different task objectives and inefficient exploration in vast action spaces. Additionally, reliance on motion-capture data for bootstrapping learning can introduce kinematic bias and lead to fragile behavior in out-of-distribution scenarios. PILOT aims to overcome these training obstacles through novel architectural designs.

5. **Establishing a Foundational Low-Level Controller:** Ultimately, the goal is to create a low-level controller that is so robust and versatile that it can serve as a foundational layer for higher-level planners. This would abstract away the complexities of whole-body dynamics and allow researchers to focus on developing high-level cognitive and task-planning capabilities, bringing autonomous humanoid operation closer to reality.

In essence, PILOT is motivated by the desire to bridge the gap between impressive but often lab-constrained humanoid robot demonstrations and their practical deployment in unpredictable, real-world environments by providing a perceptive, unified, and robust low-level control solution.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PILOT employs a unified single-stage reinforcement learning (RL) framework, specifically utilizing the Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) algorithm, to achieve perceptive loco-manipulation. The methodology is structured to address the challenges of high-dimensional control, terrain awareness, and coordinated whole-body movements.

**Problem Formulation:** The problem is framed as a goal-conditioned Markov Decision Process (MDP). The agent receives a state ($s_t$) and a goal command ($c_t$) at each time step, and outputs an action ($a_t$). This action, a 29-dimensional vector, is then converted into desired joint torques via a PD controller.

**State Space Design:** The state space ($S$) is comprehensive, integrating both internal and external information:
- **Proprioceptive Observation ($o_n$):** Includes joint positions ($q_t \in \mathbb{R}^{29}$), joint velocities ($\dot{q}_t \in \mathbb{R}^{29}$), base angular velocity ($\omega_{base_t} \in \mathbb{R}^3$), projected gravity ($g_t \in \mathbb{R}^3$), current commands ($c_t$), and previous actions ($a_{t-1}$). To account for partial observability and model dynamics, a history of these proprioceptive observations ($H=5$) is incorporated.
- **Perceptive Observation ($o_p$):** An egocentric elevation map of the robot's surroundings, represented as an $11 \times 11$ grid with 0.1m resolution. Each point indicates the vertical distance between the terrain and the robot's base. This is derived from a LiDAR-based sensor in the real world.

**Goal Space Design:** The robot is trained to track a procedurally generated command vector ($c_t$) that defines desired base linear velocities ($v_x, v_y$), base angular velocity ($\omega_{yaw}$), base height ($h_{base}$), torso orientation ($rpy$), and upper-body target joint configurations ($q_{upper}^*$). Training occurs across diverse and challenging environments (stairs, slopes, platforms, rough terrains) to ensure generalizability.

**Policy Learning Architecture (Fig. 1 Overview):** PILOT's core architecture features two main innovations:

1. **Cross-modal Context Encoder:** This component effectively extracts latent features from both proprioceptive and exteroceptive states.
 - **Prediction-based Proprioceptive Encoder:** Inspired by prior work, this encoder processes the history of proprioceptive states ($o_{n_{t-H:t}}$) and the current perceptive observation ($o_{p_t}$). It implicitly models system dynamics by predicting the base linear velocity ($\hat{v}_t$) (supervised by MSE loss) and a latent representation of the future state ($\hat{z}_{t+1}$) (optimized via contrastive learning against a target future encoder).
 - **Attention-based Exteroceptive Encoder:** To overcome limitations of blindly feeding raw elevation maps into MLPs, this encoder explicitly identifies optimal steppable areas and minimizes foot-terrain collisions. It operates on two scales:
 - A global semantic feature ($\phi(o_{p_t})$) is extracted via an MLP for coarse context.
 - Fine-grained local geometry is captured using a PointNet-inspired architecture, which aggregates neighborhood features from the elevation map.
 - A **proprioception-guided cross-attention mechanism** (Multi-Head Attention, MHA) uses the current proprioceptive state ($o_{n_t}$) as the Query and local terrain features as Keys and Values. This dynamically attends to critical terrain patches (footholds, obstacles) based on the robot's current pose. The final perceptual latent ($z_{p_t}$) fuses this attention-weighted fine-grained encoding with the global context.

2. **Unified Whole-Body Motion Generation (MoE-based Actor):** To manage the high-dimensional action space and enable diverse motor skills without gradient interference, a Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) actor policy is introduced.
 - The policy network consists of a gating network and N=4 expert networks, each with distinct parameters.
 - All experts and the gating network receive the combined input: perceptual latent ($z_{p_t}$), proprioceptive encoding ($z_{o_t}$, which includes history-encoded latent, estimated base velocity, and raw proprioceptive state), and a binary indicator ($I_t$).
 - The indicator $I_t$ modulates the control strategy: $I_t=0$ prioritizes natural locomotion with nominal arm configurations, while $I_t=1$ activates a tracking mode for sampled upper-body commands.
 - The gating network dynamically computes a probability distribution across experts. The final action ($a_t$) is a weighted sum of the experts' outputs, encouraging specialization.
 - **Residual Action Parameterization:** For upper-body control, the policy outputs a residual delta ($a_{upper_t}$) that is added to the user-specified target joint configurations ($q_{upper}^*$) to generate the final reference positions for the PD controller. This simplifies learning by focusing the policy on corrective adjustments rather than full kinematics.

**Reward Design:** The reward function ($r_t$) is a weighted sum of task rewards (encouraging precise tracking of velocity, height, orientation, and arm positions) and regularization rewards (penalizing undesirable behaviors like high action rates, energy cost, joint deviation, feet stumbling, slippage, and promoting air time for feet, and "no fly" for stability).

**Adaptive Command Curriculum and Training Strategy:** A progressive curriculum ensures robust skill acquisition:
- **Command Sampling:** Target commands for base height, torso orientation, and upper-body manipulation are initially constrained and then dynamically expanded using curriculum factors (e.g., uniform sampling for base height, exponential distribution for arm joint configurations). This avoids sudden exposure to complex tasks.
- **Structured Training Stages:**
 1. Initial training for robust locomotion.
 2. Once locomotion performance reaches a threshold, randomized base height goals are introduced.
 3. Sequentially, torso orientation targets (roll, pitch, yaw) are added.
 4. Finally, upper-body manipulation tasks are integrated using the exponential sampling strategy.

**Real-World Deployment:** The trained policy is directly deployed onto a Unitree G1 humanoid robot via zero-shot transfer. A LiDAR-based robot-centric elevation mapping system (using Fast-LIO for state estimation and probabilistic terrain mapping for height map generation) provides real-time environmental perception at 10 Hz. The policy infers actions at 50 Hz, which are sent to motor PD controllers at 500 Hz. Both VR teleoperation and a hierarchical autonomous control framework (with a separate high-level planner) are demonstrated.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy and robustness of PILOT were validated through extensive experiments in both simulation and on the physical Unitree G1 humanoid robot, addressing specific research questions regarding its performance, architectural contributions, and real-world applicability.

**Simulation Experiments (Q1: Comparison with Baselines):**
PILOT was benchmarked against state-of-the-art blind loco-manipulation controllers (HOMIE, FALCON, AMO) on simple terrains (flat ground and minor irregularities) for fairness, as existing baselines lack perceptive capabilities for complex terrains.
- **Superior Whole-Body Coordination:** PILOT, even without vision, achieved the lowest overall command tracking errors across both lower-body locomotion and upper-body manipulation, demonstrating superior whole-body coordination.
- **Locomotion Agility:** PILOT exhibited robust locomotion with minimal linear velocity ($E_v = 0.145$) and angular velocity ($E_\omega = 0.099$) tracking errors, significantly outperforming decoupled baselines which showed higher errors ($E_v > 0.270$). This highlights the advantage of PILOT's unified control architecture.
- **Precise Torso and Base Height Control:** PILOT achieved high precision in base height tracking ($E_h = 0.010$) and superior torso orientation tracking ($E_r, E_p, E_y$) compared to AMO, which also supports full torso actuation. The MoE-based policy was attributed to this precision.
- **Robust Upper-Body Manipulation:** PILOT maintained robust upper-body manipulation ($E_{arm} = 0.206$), matching the best baseline while providing a significantly larger effective workspace, especially compared to motion-capture-dependent methods.
- **Conclusion:** PILOT functions as a versatile low-level controller offering superior command tracking performance, expansive operational workspace (base heights from 0.3m to 0.76m, full torso control range), and robust whole-body coordination.

**Simulation Experiments (Q2: Ablation Studies on Policy Learning):**
To evaluate the individual contributions of PILOT's key components, three ablated variants (PILOT w/o vision, w/o attention-based encoder, and w/o MoE) were tested on diverse challenging terrains (staircases, elevated platforms, slopes, rough surfaces). A foot stumble metric ($E_{stumble}$) was introduced to quantify traversal safety.
- **Necessity of Visual Perception:** The "PILOT w/o vision" variant showed the most severe performance degradation across all metrics and a high stumble rate ($E_{stumble} = 0.087$), confirming that proprioception alone is insufficient for robust loco-manipulation on complex terrains.
- **Importance of Attention-Based Perception Encoder:** Replacing the attention-based encoder with a simple MLP ("PILOT w/o attention-based encoder") led to a dramatic increase in stumble rate (from 0.006 to 0.066), validating that the attention mechanism is critical for identifying optimal steppable areas and safe terrain traversal by extracting state-dependent geometric features.
- **Effectiveness of Mixture-of-Experts (MoE):** Replacing the MoE policy with a standard monolithic network ("PILOT w/o MoE") significantly impaired velocity tracking and torso orientation precision ($E_{arm}$ increased to 0.246), and stumble rate increased to 0.017. This confirms that the MoE architecture is essential for effectively coordinating diverse motor skills, mitigating gradient interference, and enabling adaptive skill switching.
- **MoE Specialization Visualization:** Analysis of expert activation weights across different motion primitives (walk, platform, stair, stand, squat, bend) revealed clear specialization. For example, Expert 2 showed increased activation for dynamic traversal tasks requiring perception (stairs, platforms), while Experts 1 and 4 were dominant in proprioception-intensive tasks like squatting and deep bending. This empirically validates the learned functional decoupling and adaptive skill switching capability of the MoE.

**Real-World Results (Q3: Zero-shot Transfer on Unitree G1):**
The simulation-trained policy was successfully deployed onto the physical Unitree G1 humanoid robot via zero-shot transfer, using a LiDAR-based elevation mapping system for real-time perception.
- **Teleoperation Results:**
 - **Object Transport via Stair Traversal (Fig. 3a):** PILOT successfully performed a challenging long-horizon task involving deep squatting, retrieving a weighted bag (≈1.0 kg), traversing a multi-step staircase, placing the payload, and descending the stairs. It achieved a 100% success rate across five trials, demonstrating stable, stumble-free gait and steady payload handling despite coupled locomotion-induced instability and manipulation-induced disturbances.
 - **Object Transport via High Platform Traversal (Fig. 3b):** The robot successfully retrieved a tissue box, traversed an elevated platform (0.3m high, 0.5m wide), and placed the object. PILOT exhibited exceptional whole-body coordination and stable object grasp during dynamic locomotion over demanding terrain.
- **Autonomous Task via Hierarchical Integration (Fig. 4):**
 - Using PILOT as a low-level tracking controller, a high-level policy (trained in simulation and zero-shot transferred) autonomously executed a "Lift-Box" task. The robot successfully navigated to a target, performed a deep squat, bimanually grasped the box, and recovered to a stable standing posture.
 - **Conclusion:** These real-world demonstrations empirically validate PILOT's robustness, versatility, and stability as a foundational low-level controller, capable of abstracting whole-body dynamics for seamless integration with higher-level planners for autonomous loco-manipulation tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The PILOT framework represents a significant advancement in the field of humanoid robotics, with substantial potential impact on the capabilities and deployment of these complex machines.

**Enabling Humanoid Robots in Unstructured Real-World Environments:** The most immediate and profound impact of PILOT is its ability to push humanoid robots beyond the confines of laboratory settings or structured environments. By seamlessly integrating perceptive locomotion with dexterous whole-body manipulation over complex and unstructured terrains like stairs and elevated platforms, PILOT allows humanoids to operate in scenarios much closer to everyday human living and working spaces. This is a critical step towards realizing the promise of humanoids for service, logistics, search and rescue, and other human-centered applications.

**Robust and Generalizable Low-Level Control:** PILOT demonstrates superior command tracking precision, terrain traversability, and overall system robustness compared to prior low-level controllers. Its successful zero-shot transfer from simulation to the physical Unitree G1 robot is a testament to its generalizability and robustness, effectively bridging the notorious "sim-to-real" gap that often hinders robotics research. This robust low-level controller can serve as a dependable foundation for future, more complex behaviors.

**Methodological Innovation in RL for Robotics:** The architectural innovations within PILOT are highly significant:
- **Cross-modal Context Encoder:** The combination of a prediction-based proprioceptive encoder with an attention-based multi-scale exteroceptive encoder is a powerful approach to fusing internal robot state with external environmental awareness. The proprioception-guided cross-attention mechanism for identifying optimal steppable areas is particularly innovative for improving terrain interaction stability.
- **Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) Policy Architecture:** Employing an MoE to coordinate diverse motor skills into a unified policy is a clever solution to the challenges of high-dimensional action spaces, gradient interference, and exploration inefficiency in complex RL tasks. The demonstrated specialization of experts in different motion patterns opens new avenues for learning and adapting heterogeneous skills.

**Foundation for Higher-Level Autonomy:** By providing a stable and reliable low-level controller that effectively abstracts away the intricacies of whole-body dynamics, PILOT significantly simplifies the task of developing higher-level cognitive and task-planning policies. The successful demonstration of autonomous execution of a "Lift-Box" task via hierarchical integration proves its utility as a foundational component for future fully autonomous humanoid systems. This will allow researchers to focus on perception, reasoning, and high-level decision-making without being bogged down by the complexities of dynamic whole-body control.

**Advancing Multi-Task Robotics:** The ability of PILOT to handle simultaneous locomotion and manipulation, with robust coordination across a large operational workspace, pushes the boundaries of multi-task robotics. It provides a blueprint for how robots can learn to interact with their environment in a holistic and integrated manner, rather than treating movement and manipulation as separate, decoupled problems.

**Future Research Directions:** The authors explicitly mention future work on developing a high-level visuomotor policy that uses PILOT as a low-level controller to autonomously execute long-horizon loco-manipulation tasks. This indicates a clear path towards developing truly intelligent humanoids that can perceive, understand, and interact with complex environments without relying on teleoperation or external tracking systems, marking a crucial step towards generalized humanoid intelligence.

In essence, PILOT not only solves critical technical challenges in humanoid control but also lays a robust foundation for the next generation of humanoid robots capable of truly adaptive, intelligent, and autonomous operation in the real world.
