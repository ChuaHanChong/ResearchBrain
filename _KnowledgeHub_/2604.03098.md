# Co-Evolution of Policy and Internal Reward for Language Agents

> [!summary] Summary
> The Self-Guide framework enables language agents to generate internal verbal self-guidance signals, which concurrently guide real-time decision-making during inference and provide dense, step-level internal rewards for policy optimization. This method fosters a co-evolving loop where an improving policy enhances guidance quality, leading to better internal rewards that further strengthen the policy, yielding consistent performance gains across diverse interactive benchmarks.

## Problem

- Language agents face sparse and delayed environmental rewards in long-horizon interactive tasks, making it difficult to effectively assign credit or blame to intermediate actions.
- This sparsity impairs both efficient decision-making during inference and effective policy optimization during training.
- Existing solutions often provide only post-hoc credit assignment, introduce computational overhead with external reward models, or decouple policy and reward signal improvements.

## Method

- The **Self-Guide** mechanism allows the agent to generate a ==verbal self-guidance signal== at each step, which then conditions its subsequent action, providing real-time inference guidance.
- These verbal self-guidance signals are converted into ==scalar internal rewards==, creating dense, step-level ==credit assignment== that augments the sparse environmental feedback for policy optimization.
- A ==co-evolving training loop==, using ==Group-Relative Policy Optimization== (GRPO) and a ==stage-wise trapezoid trust schedule==, jointly optimizes the agent's policy and the quality of its self-generated guidance and internal rewards.

## Results

- **Self-Guide** consistently achieved performance improvements over GRPO baselines across ALFWorld, ScienceWorld, and WebShop, demonstrating an approximate **8%** average improvement over GRPO with the Qwen3-4B model.
- Inference-time self-guidance significantly reduced looping errors in ALFWorld (from **90.0%** to **38.5%**) and also mitigated other error types in ScienceWorld, showing immediate benefits even without RL training.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the stage-wise trust schedule is vital for learning, as immediate full internal rewards or early activation degraded performance, and removing late annealing slightly reduced overall gains.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Language agents can effectively leverage their generative capabilities to create internal, verbal self-guidance signals that directly improve decision-making during task execution.
> - Converting these self-guidance signals into dense internal rewards enables a self-reinforcing co-evolutionary process where policy improvement and guidance quality mutually enhance each other.
> - A stage-wise trust schedule is crucial for stably integrating potentially imperfect early self-generated rewards into the reinforcement learning objective, preventing destabilization and ensuring alignment with the ultimate environmental goal.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The training of large language model (LLM) agents that interact with environments over long horizons is challenged by the sparse and delayed nature of environmental rewards. This issue makes it difficult to ascertain which intermediate actions contribute positively to task completion and which are detrimental, impacting both decision-making during inference and policy optimization during training.

Existing research has attempted to mitigate this sparse-reward bottleneck through various approaches. One category involves post-hoc credit assignment methods, which retrospectively redistribute credit or refine rollout optimization after entire trajectories have been collected. Examples include work on hierarchical relative advantages (Feng et al., 2025b), step-level advantages over trajectory graphs (Li et al., 2026), and leveraging LLMs as critics for hindsight reasoning to refine Q-values (Tan et al., 2026). Another line of research introduces explicit process-level supervision, such as process reward models (Xi et al., 2025; Choudhury, 2025) or methods that infer dense process rewards from rollouts and outcome labels (Cui et al., 2026; Liu et al., 2026b).

However, many of these approaches primarily utilize intermediate signals for training *after* rollouts, rather than for *guiding* decisions during inference. When these signals are generated by separate evaluators or reward models, they can introduce computational overhead and may diverge from the policy's evolving online rollout distribution. Moreover, existing methods often treat reward improvement and policy improvement as distinct processes.

This work addresses these limitations by proposing a unified framework, Self-Guide, which integrates internal self-generated signals for both real-time inference guidance and denser training supervision within a single training loop. Unlike methods relying on external reward models or purely post-hoc analysis, Self-Guide fosters a co-evolutionary loop where the agent's policy and its self-generated internal reward mature together online. This places the work within the broader field of agentic reinforcement learning, particularly contributing to the sub-area of credit assignment and reward shaping for long-horizon tasks, while also touching upon co-evolutionary training paradigms where agent and environment or agent and reward models evolve (e.g., Wang et al., 2026b; Guo et al., 2025). The approach contrasts with traditional reward shaping by dynamically evolving the internal reward signal rather than using a fixed, manually designed one, and differs from offline distillation of guidance signals by emphasizing online, closed-loop learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observation that human cognition often involves internal self-guidance and learning from both outcomes and self-generated judgments. In contrast, language agents operating in complex, long-horizon interactive environments (e.g., web navigation, scientific experimentation) typically receive only sparse, delayed feedback, usually at the conclusion of a task. This sparsity presents a fundamental challenge for two main aspects of agent intelligence:
1. **Inference-time decision making:** Without intermediate feedback, agents lack an explicit mechanism to assess the immediate utility of their ongoing trajectory, making it difficult to steer subsequent actions effectively.
2. **Training-time policy improvement:** Sparse terminal rewards provide insufficient signal for attributing credit or blame to individual intermediate actions, hindering efficient policy optimization.

Existing methods attempt to alleviate the sparse reward problem but often introduce new limitations. Post-hoc credit assignment techniques operate after trajectories are completed, thus providing no real-time guidance. External process reward models offer denser signals but incur additional computational overhead and can suffer from distribution shifts if they are not continuously aligned with the agent's evolving behavior. Furthermore, many approaches maintain a separation between the process of improving the agent's policy and improving the quality of its internal guidance or reward signals.

Against this background, the key objectives of this work are:
- To propose and develop "Self-Guide," a novel mechanism for language agents to generate their own internal, verbal self-guidance signals.
- To enable these self-generated signals to serve a dual purpose: providing short-term guidance for the next action during inference, and being converted into dense, step-level internal rewards for policy optimization during training.
- To establish a "co-evolving loop" where improvements in the agent's policy lead to more accurate self-guidance, which in turn provides better internal rewards, further strengthening the policy.
- To develop a stable training methodology, specifically a stage-wise trust schedule, to manage the inherent imperfection of early self-generated signals and integrate them effectively into reinforcement learning without destabilizing optimization.
- To empirically demonstrate that this unified, self-reinforcing approach can lead to performance improvements in language agents across diverse interactive benchmarks, both at inference time and through training, when compared to baselines relying solely on sparse environment rewards or fixed guidance mechanisms.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Self-Guide, addresses sparse rewards in long-horizon tasks by integrating self-generated internal signals for both inference-time guidance and training-time reward.

#### 3.1. Problem Setup

An episode is modeled as a sequence of observations ($o_t$), self-guidance signals ($z_t$), and actions ($a_t$). The history $h_{t-1}$ comprises all interaction context up to step $t-1$. The environment provides a sparse task-level reward $R_{env}(\tau)$ (typically 1 for success, 0 otherwise) only at the end of a trajectory $\tau$, offering no intermediate credit assignment. The objective is to augment this setting with a self-generated signal that functions both during acting and learning.

#### 3.2. Inference-Time Self-Guidance

At each step $t$, before taking an action, the agent generates a short verbal self-guidance signal $z_t \sim \pi_{\theta}(\cdot | h_{t-1}, o_t)$. This signal is a natural-language assessment of the trajectory's progress (e.g., "making progress," "off-track," "approaching subgoal"). Subsequently, the agent selects an action $a_t \sim \pi_{\theta}(\cdot | h_{t-1}, o_t, z_t)$, conditioning its decision on both the interaction history and its own self-generated guidance. Because $z_t$ and $a_t$ are produced by the same policy model $\pi_{\theta}$, the model is continuously trained to generate guidance that is relevant and actionable for its own decision-making, thereby improving guidance quality over time.

#### 3.3. Self-Guidance as Internal Reward

The verbal self-guidance signal $z_t$ is converted into a scalar internal reward $r_{sg_t} = g(z_t)$. The function $g(\cdot)$ maps verbal assessments to discrete polarity values (e.g., +0.1 for "positive," 0 for "neutral," -0.1 for "negative"). This provides dense, step-level credit assignment, complementing the sparse environmental reward.

A unified trajectory-level training signal is constructed by linearly combining the environmental reward and the aggregated self-guidance reward: $R(\tau; u) = R_{env}(\tau) + \lambda(u) \sum_{t=1}^{T} r_{sg_t}$. Here, $u$ denotes the current training stage, and $\lambda(u) \in$ modulates the contribution of the internal reward. This dual-purpose design initiates a self-reinforcing loop: an improved policy generates more coherent trajectories, leading to more reliable self-guidance signals, which in turn provide more accurate internal rewards that further strengthen the policy.

#### 3.4. Joint Optimization with Self-Guide

The policy and Self-Guide are jointly optimized using a single Group-Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) objective. For each environment instance, a group of rollouts is sampled, and each rollout receives the composite return $R_i = R(\tau_i; u)$. Group-relative advantages are computed as $\hat{A}_i = (R_i - \mu_R) / (\sigma_R + \epsilon)$, where $\mu_R$ and $\sigma_R$ are the mean and standard deviation of returns within the group. The GRPO objective aims to maximize the policy's performance based on these advantages, with probability ratio clipping. Since $\pi_{\theta}$ generates both $z_t$ and $a_t$, this objective simultaneously trains the model to produce better self-guidance and to act more effectively based on that guidance. The method's compatibility with other advantage-based RL algorithms, such as DAPO, is also noted.

#### 3.5. Stage-Wise Trust Schedule

To address the bootstrap problem where unreliable early self-generated rewards could destabilize learning, a trapezoid trust schedule $\lambda(u)$ is introduced to control the internal reward's contribution across training stages:
- **Phase I (Guidance-only warm-up, $\lambda=0$):** The model learns to generate and act on guidance purely through environmental feedback, without optimizing against its immature self-assessments. This phase allows guidance quality to stabilize.
- **Phase II (Reward activation, $\lambda: 0 \to 1$):** $\lambda(u)$ is smoothly ramped up linearly from 0 to 1. This gradual introduction of internal reward prevents optimization shock and allows the policy to adapt to the denser signal.
- **Phase III (Full internal reward, $\lambda=1$):** The co-evolving loop operates at full strength, with self-guidance providing dense, step-level credit alongside sparse environmental outcomes.
- **Phase IV (Late annealing, $\lambda: 1 \to 0$):** $\lambda$ is gradually reduced from 1 to 0. Since self-guidance derived rewards are not potential-based, annealing ensures that the final policy converges aligned with the true environment objective, preventing bias. The specific timing of these phases is a hyperparameter, but the overall structure (warm-up, activation, annealing) is considered essential.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research conducted experiments across three benchmarks: ALFWorld (structured household navigation), ScienceWorld (multi-step scientific reasoning), and WebShop (noisy web interaction), utilizing Qwen3-1.7B, Qwen3-4B, and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct as backbone models. The evaluation encompassed both prompting and reinforcement learning (RL) settings.

#### 4.1. Inference-Time Self-Guidance Performance

In the prompting setting, ReAct with Self-Guidance (ReAct w/ SG) outperformed ReAct and other baselines like Reflexion on ALFWorld. This indicated that self-guidance signals, even without RL training, provided immediate benefits in environments where task progress is structured and guidance can be reliably formulated. However, on WebShop, ReAct w/ SG showed only marginal gains, suggesting that the quality and utility of frozen self-guidance depend on the task's complexity and the model's inherent familiarity with the environment dynamics. This outcome motivated the joint learning of guidance with the policy.

#### 4.2. RL Training Performance

In the RL setting, the full method, GRPO with Self-Guidance and Guidance Reward (GRPO w/ SG & GR), consistently achieved performance improvements over the GRPO baseline across all three benchmarks and backbone models.
- **Immediate Benefit of Guidance:** GRPO with Self-Guidance (GRPO w/ SG), which only incorporates inference-time guidance without the internal reward, already yielded meaningful gains over vanilla GRPO. This demonstrated that decision-time steering provides a direct benefit to agent performance.
- **Complementary Role of Internal Reward:** The addition of the self-guidance reward via the staged schedule (GRPO w/ SG & GR) further improved performance beyond GRPO w/ SG. For instance, with Qwen3-4B, the method achieved an approximate 8% average improvement over GRPO. This indicated that the internal guidance reward provides a complementary and beneficial signal for policy optimization, beyond what decision-time guidance alone contributes.

#### 4.3. Self-Guidance Reward Learning Dynamics

Analysis of training curves (Figure 4) showed that GRPO w/ SG began outperforming vanilla GRPO from early training steps. Crucially, after approximately step 40, when the stage-wise schedule activated the self-guidance reward, GRPO w/ SG & GR started to diverge from GRPO w/ SG, maintaining a growing advantage. This divergence supported the hypothesis of a self-reinforcing loop: as self-guidance quality matured through acting, its promotion to a reward source accelerated policy improvement.

#### 4.4. Ablation on Stage-Wise Guidance Reward Scheduling

Ablation studies on the trapezoid trust schedule (Figure 5) validated its design principles:
- Introducing the guidance reward immediately ("Immediate Full Reward") performed worse than not using it at all, confirming that early, immature self-guidance signals are detrimental as optimization targets.
- Entering the reward phase earlier than the default schedule ("Early Entry") generally resulted in lower performance than the default, indicating that "trust follows competence" is an important principle.
- Removing the late annealing phase ("No Annealing") slightly degraded performance compared to the full schedule, suggesting that sustained full-strength internal reward can eventually over-constrain the policy and bias it away from the true environment objective.

#### 4.5. Ablation on Co-Evolution

Experiments comparing online co-evolution with offline distillation of self-guidance (Figure 6) demonstrated that a self-guidance signal distilled from a larger teacher model offline did not reliably transfer to downstream RL. Using this distilled guidance either as dense reward or for rollout guidance led to unstable or unsustained gains. This was attributed to a distribution mismatch between fixed offline trajectories and the evolving rollout distribution of the online policy, supporting the necessity of online co-evolution. Further analysis (Appendix D.2) showed that fixing the policy and varying only the self-guidance model checkpoint resulted in consistent performance gains as training progressed, indicating that the co-evolution process strengthens the self-guidance model itself, not just the policy's ability to act given the guidance.

#### 4.6. Error Pattern Analysis

Error analysis (Appendix B) revealed how self-guidance altered failure modes. On ALFWorld, self-guidance substantially reduced looping errors (from 90.0% to 38.5%). On ScienceWorld, it produced broader reductions across looping, redundant exploration, and wrong-object focus errors. On WebShop, self-guidance sharply reduced premature purchases but increased query looping, suggesting a shift in error patterns rather than complete error elimination, potentially due to the base model's difficulty in formulating correct product evaluation strategies.

#### 4.7. Compatibility with Other RL Algorithms

The method's compatibility was verified by applying it on top of DAPO, another RL algorithm (Appendix C). Similar performance gains were observed, indicating that the benefits of learned self-guidance and guidance reward are complementary to the choice of the underlying RL optimizer.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces a framework that addresses a fundamental challenge in training LLM agents: the problem of sparse and delayed rewards in long-horizon interactive tasks. The proposed Self-Guide mechanism offers a novel approach by creating a self-generated, internal reward system that avoids reliance on external reward models or extensive human annotation.

The primary significance of this work lies in its demonstration that language agents can learn to generate and refine their own internal guidance signals, which simultaneously serve two critical functions:
1. **Inference-time steering:** Providing immediate, step-level feedback to guide subsequent actions, potentially leading to more coherent and efficient task execution.
2. **Training-time supervision:** Generating dense, step-level internal rewards that augment sparse environmental feedback, enabling more efficient and stable policy optimization.

The establishment of a "co-evolving loop" where policy improvement leads to better guidance, and better guidance in turn strengthens the policy, represents a shift from decoupled reward engineering or external reward modeling. This approach fosters a more autonomous and self-improving learning paradigm for agents. The stage-wise trust schedule is a significant methodological contribution, demonstrating how imperfect self-generated signals can be safely integrated into an RL objective without destabilizing training, ensuring that the agent benefits from internal signals while remaining aligned with the ultimate task objective.

The empirical results, showing consistent performance gains across diverse environments (ALFWorld, ScienceWorld, WebShop) and different model scales, indicate the robustness and general applicability of the Self-Guide framework. The ablations further clarify the importance of both the dual-role design and the staged introduction of internal rewards, as well as the necessity of online co-evolution over offline distillation.

The potential impact of this work is substantial. By enabling agents to generate and learn from their own internal assessments, it paves the way for:
- **More autonomous agents:** Reducing the reliance on meticulously crafted external reward functions or human feedback, thus potentially accelerating the development of agents in complex, open-ended environments.
- **Improved long-horizon task performance:** Providing denser and more relevant feedback throughout extended decision sequences can lead to agents that are more adept at planning and executing multi-step tasks.
- **Enhanced interpretability:** The verbal nature of the self-guidance signals could offer insights into the agent's internal reasoning and decision-making process.
- **Broader applicability:** The modular nature of Self-Guide, demonstrated by its compatibility with different RL optimizers, suggests it could be integrated into various existing agentic RL frameworks.

In summary, this research contributes to the understanding of how language agents can leverage their generative capabilities to overcome fundamental reinforcement learning challenges, potentially enabling the development of more intelligent and self-sufficient AI agents.
