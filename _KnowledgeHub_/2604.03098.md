# Co-Evolution of Policy and Internal Reward for Language Agents

> [!summary] Summary
> The Self-Guide framework enables language agents to generate internal verbal self-guidance signals, which concurrently guide real-time decision-making during inference and provide dense, step-level internal rewards for policy optimization. This method fosters a co-evolving loop where an improving policy enhances guidance quality, leading to better internal rewards that further strengthen the policy, yielding consistent performance gains across diverse interactive benchmarks.

## Problem

- Language agents face sparse and delayed environmental rewards in long-horizon interactive tasks, making it difficult to effectively assign credit or blame to intermediate actions.
- This sparsity impairs both efficient decision-making during inference and effective policy optimization during training.
- Existing solutions often provide only post-hoc credit assignment, introduce computational overhead with external reward models, or decouple policy and reward signal improvements.

## Method

- The **Self-Guide** mechanism allows the agent to generate a ==verbal self-guidance signal== at each step, which then conditions its subsequent action, providing real-time inference guidance.
- These verbal self-guidance signals are converted into ==scalar internal rewards==, creating dense, step-level ==credit assignment== that augments the sparse environmental feedback for policy optimization.
- A ==co-evolving training loop==, using ==Group-Relative Policy Optimization== (GRPO) and a ==stage-wise trapezoid trust schedule==, jointly optimizes the agent's policy and the quality of its self-generated guidance and internal rewards.

## Results

- **Self-Guide** consistently achieved performance improvements over GRPO baselines across ALFWorld, ScienceWorld, and WebShop, demonstrating an approximate **8%** average improvement over GRPO with the Qwen3-4B model.
- Inference-time self-guidance significantly reduced looping errors in ALFWorld (from **90.0%** to **38.5%**) and also mitigated other error types in ScienceWorld, showing immediate benefits even without RL training.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the stage-wise trust schedule is vital for learning, as immediate full internal rewards or early activation degraded performance, and removing late annealing slightly reduced overall gains.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Language agents can effectively leverage their generative capabilities to create internal, verbal self-guidance signals that directly improve decision-making during task execution.
> - Converting these self-guidance signals into dense internal rewards enables a self-reinforcing co-evolutionary process where policy improvement and guidance quality mutually enhance each other.
> - A stage-wise trust schedule is crucial for stably integrating potentially imperfect early self-generated rewards into the reinforcement learning objective, preventing destabilization and ensuring alignment with the ultimate environmental goal.
