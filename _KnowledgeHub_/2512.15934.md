# In-Context Semi-Supervised Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Duke University introduced In-Context Semi-Supervised Learning (IC-SSL), a Transformer framework designed to leverage both labeled and unlabeled data within the input context. The framework mechanistically integrates representation learning from unlabeled data via Laplacian Eigenmaps and classification via functional gradient descent, demonstrating improved performance, enhanced sample efficiency in low-label regimes, and robust out-of-distribution generalization.

## Problem

- Supervised In-Context Learning (ICL) typically requires abundant labeled data, which is often scarce and costly to obtain in real-world scenarios.
- Existing ICL research primarily focused on supervised settings, overlooking the valuable structural information inherent in plentiful unlabeled data.
- A lack of mechanistic understanding regarding how Transformers can implicitly leverage contextual information to perform semi-supervised learning tasks.

## Method

- Developed a two-stage Transformer architecture, `TF_rep` for ==representation learning== and `TF_sup` for in-context supervised learning, trained end-to-end to implement IC-SSL.
- `TF_rep` learns to construct a discrete ==Laplacian matrix== from unlabeled input covariates using RBF attention, and then computes its bottom-k eigenvectors (==Laplacian Eigenmaps==) via linear attention, providing context-dependent feature representations.
- `TF_sup` performs classification on these learned features by implementing ==functional gradient descent== in its forward pass, leveraging scarce labeled examples to infer a latent function for prediction.

## Results

- The `Orig+E2E-ICL` model consistently outperforms strong baselines across synthetic, product, and image manifolds by **5-7 percentage points**, achieving ~**90%** accuracy with **25%** labeled data and remaining strong with as few as **3-15%** labeled data.
- Exhibits robust out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization, including exceptional zero-shot OOD transfer to novel image manifolds (generated by Stable Diffusion) with **~77%** accuracy (**15%** labels), suggesting learning of fundamental geometric computations.
- Achieves higher accuracy and separation scores on ImageNet100 compared to a standard Transformer baseline, which displayed a sharp 'phase transition' in its performance and representational structure at higher training episodes.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Transformers can mechanistically implement well-understood algorithms, such as Laplacian Eigenmaps for manifold learning and functional gradient descent for classification, entirely within their forward pass.
> - Embedding algorithmic inductive biases into Transformer architecture significantly improves sample efficiency in low-label regimes and enables robust generalization by leveraging underlying data geometry.
> - The structured IC-SSL model provides a 'transparent account' of how Transformers can acquire geometric awareness, serving as a reference to understand implicit mechanisms and 'phase transitions' in standard Transformer baselines.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The Transformer architecture has demonstrated significant capabilities in various fields, notably in natural language processing, but also extending to computer vision, network analysis, and biology. A particular area of recent interest is In-Context Learning (ICL), where Transformers learn latent functions from contextual data pairs during their forward pass, without requiring parameter fine-tuning. Initial research on Transformer-based ICL primarily focused on supervised settings, examining the model's ability to infer linear or non-linear (e.g., RKHS-based) functions from explicitly labeled input-output pairs. This has progressed from real-valued outcomes to categorical observations relevant for classification tasks.

However, a notable gap in the existing ICL literature concerns the exploitation of unlabeled data within the provided context. In practical applications, labeled data are often scarce, while abundant unlabeled samples are available. Traditional supervised ICL approaches typically disregard the structural information present in unlabeled contextual demonstrations, or infer their labels independently without leveraging the full unlabeled set.

This paper addresses this gap by introducing In-Context Semi-Supervised Learning (IC-SSL). It bridges the established field of semi-supervised learning (SSL), which aims to leverage unlabeled data to improve model performance when labels are limited, with the emergent field of Transformer-based ICL. Previous work at this intersection has largely focused on scaling prompt sizes, data selection strategies, or pseudo-labeling techniques. Some theoretical investigations have explored how depth or looping constructs in linear-attention models might implicitly implement algorithms like Expectation-Maximization for unlabeled data.

In contrast, this research distinguishes itself by focusing on the *fundamental mechanism* through which Transformers can directly extract geometry-aligned structure from unlabeled tokens in-context. This approach draws connections to spectral geometry and graph-based embeddings, where eigenvectors of graph Laplacians are traditionally computed offline as positional embeddings (PEs). While prior work showed Transformers can compute certain PEs during inference, this paper aims to fully integrate this capability into a comprehensive semi-supervised ICL framework, offering a more transparent and end-to-end approach to leveraging unlabeled context for representation learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce and rigorously study **In-Context Semi-Supervised Learning (IC-SSL)**. This involves demonstrating that Transformers can effectively leverage both a small set of labeled examples and a larger collection of unlabeled points within the contextual demonstrations to learn a robust, context-dependent representation. This learned representation is then used to achieve accurate predictions, particularly improving performance in low-label regimes.

The motivations driving this work are multi-faceted:

- **Addressing a Practical Gap:** In real-world scenarios, labeled data are often sparse, while unlabeled data are abundant. Existing Transformer-based ICL typically focuses on fully supervised settings, neglecting the potential information contained within unlabeled contextual demonstrations. The paper aims to develop an ICL framework that can harness this unlabeled context, reflecting practical data availability.
- **Understanding Transformer Capacity:** The observation that Transformers often perform well even when labels are sparse suggests that these models inherently capture crucial structure from unlabeled contextual information. A key motivation is to provide foundational insights into *how* Transformers exploit this unlabeled context for representation learning within the ICL framework.
- **Mechanistic Transparency:** The authors aim to construct a Transformer architecture that explicitly performs distinct semi-supervised learning algorithms in its forward pass. By designing a two-stage model, where the first stage is motivated by learning an Eigenmap of the Laplacian and the second by implementing functional gradient descent, the research seeks to offer a more transparent and mechanistic account of how attention mechanisms and Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs) realize complex semi-supervised ICL computations. This contrasts with end-to-end models whose internal workings can be more opaque.
- **Inductive Bias for Efficiency and Robustness:** The proposed modular, two-stage construction is intended to impose a concrete inductive bias. This bias is designed to make IC-SSL notably more sample-efficient in data-scarce environments and to support robust generalization to diverse and unseen data geometries and modalities.
- **Reference Model for Analysis:** The constructed two-stage Transformer is designed to serve as a reference model. This reference allows for comparative analysis with unconstrained, end-to-end Transformers, helping to interpret what representations are learned by standard models and to understand phenomena such as phase transitions observed in their learning curves.

In summary, the work aims to extend the theoretical and practical understanding of Transformer-based ICL to a semi-supervised setting, providing a structured approach and mechanistic insights into how these models can effectively learn from limited supervision by leveraging the broader contextual data landscape.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research centers on the development and evaluation of a novel two-stage Transformer architecture designed for In-Context Semi-Supervised Learning (IC-SSL).

**Problem Setup:**
The problem is defined as receiving a context `C` comprising `m` labeled data points `{(x^(i), y^(i))}` and `n-m` unlabeled data points `{x^(i)}`. The objective is to predict the labels `y^(m+1)..., y^(n)` for the unlabeled points. A key concept introduced is `ϕ(X)`, a context-dependent feature representation for `x^(i)` that leverages all observed covariates `X = (x^(1)..., x^(n))`. The relationship between `x^(i)` and `y^(i)` is modeled via a softmax function applied to `f(ϕ(i))`, where `f` is a latent function acting on the learned features.

**In-context Semi-Supervised Loss:**
The model is trained end-to-end by minimizing an in-context semi-supervised cross-entropy loss, `L(θ; C)`. This loss is calculated over the `n-m` unlabeled samples (for which true labels are known during training) and is optimized with respect to the union of all Transformer parameters `θ = θ_rep ∪ θ_sup ∪ {w_c}`. Crucially, the features `ϕ(i)` and function `f(ϕ(i))` are manifested directly during the Transformer's forward pass, not optimized independently.

**Two-Stage Transformer Construction:**
The core of the methodology is a two-stage Transformer, conceptually decomposed into `TF_rep` (for representation learning) and `TF_sup` (for in-context supervised learning), which are then combined and trained end-to-end.

- **Stage 1: Transformer for In-context Representation Learning (TF_rep):**
 This stage focuses on learning a context-dependent feature representation `ϕ(x^(i))` from all `n` input covariates. Its design is motivated by classical manifold learning techniques, specifically Laplacian Eigenmaps. `TF_rep` is further divided into two sub-modules:
 - **Transformer for Computing Discrete Laplacian (TF_L):** This module takes the raw input coordinates `x^(1)..., x^(n)` and constructs an approximation of the Laplace-Beltrami operator. It uses an RBF-activated attention mechanism (`Attn_rbf`) to compute an affinity matrix `A` based on local Euclidean distances. From `A`, it derives the right-normalized discrete Laplacian `L̂ = I - AD^-1`. The authors demonstrate that a specific, simply parameterized 1-layer Transformer can explicitly compute `L̂`. In practice, a multi-layer, multi-head Transformer is used for `TF_L` to allow for learning combinations of Laplacians with different bandwidths and to leverage higher expressivity during end-to-end training.
 - **Transformer for Computing Eigenmap (TF_ϕ):** Given `L̂` as input, this module computes a Laplacian Eigenmap, which corresponds to the bottom-k eigenvectors of `L̂`. The architecture is a modification of prior work for eigenvector computation, employing linear attention (`Attn_linear`). The authors demonstrate that a Transformer with specific diagonal parameter matrices can implement a block-power-method-like algorithm to efficiently compute these eigenvectors. The output of this stage, `ϕ(i)`, for each `x^(i)` provides the geometry-aligned feature representation.

- **Stage 2: Transformer for In-context Supervised Learning (TF_sup):**
 This stage takes the learned features `ϕ(i)` from `TF_rep` and the `m` observed labels to predict labels for the unlabeled samples. Its design is based on implementing functional gradient descent (GD) in a Reproducing Kernel Hilbert Space (RKHS) for categorical observations.
 - The Transformer's forward pass is constructed to mimic the iterative GD update rule: `f^(i)_ℓ+1 = f^(i)_ℓ + Δf^(i)_ℓ`.
 - `Δf^(i)_ℓ` is computed as a weighted sum over the `m` labeled samples, where weights are determined by a kernel `κ(ϕ(i), ϕ(j))` and the difference between the true label embedding `w_y_j` and the expected label embedding `E(w|f^(j)_ℓ)`.
 - `E(w|f^(j)_ℓ)` is approximated by an MLP, whose parameters are learned end-to-end alongside other Transformer parameters.
 - The architecture of `TF_sup` includes a multi-head self-attention layer for implementing `Δf` and clearing previous expectations, followed by an MLP layer for computing new expectations. The MLP parameters are tied across Transformer layers.

**End-to-End Training:**
Both `TF_rep` and `TF_sup` are instantiated within a single, unified Transformer and trained end-to-end. The modular construction provides an inductive bias and initialization, but joint optimization allows the model to deviate from and potentially improve upon the strict two-stage algorithms.

**Experimental Design:**
The models were evaluated on diverse benchmarks:
1. **Synthetic Manifolds:** Five classic 3D Riemannian manifolds (Sphere, Cylinder, Cone, Archimedean Spiral/Swiss-Roll, Flat Torus). Data points were sampled, subjected to random rigid motions, and assigned binary labels based on geodesic distance from a random center point.
2. **Product Manifolds:** Cartesian products of the elementary manifolds, creating higher-dimensional and geometrically complex spaces.
3. **Image Manifolds:** Generated using Stable Diffusion v1.5 through spherical linear interpolation in latent space, decoded into images, then downsampled and converted to grayscale for experiments. Labels were assigned based on position in the interpolation sequence.
4. **ImageNet100:** A 100-class subset of ILSVRC-2012, using VGG-29 features as inputs to simulate a real-world, high-dimensional dataset. Episodes were constructed by sampling two random classes.

**Evaluation Metrics and Baselines:**
- **Metrics:** Prediction accuracy for unlabeled samples was the primary metric. For ImageNet100, "separation score" (intra-class similarity minus inter-class similarity) and mutual kNN (mNN) alignment were also used to assess representation quality and geometric alignment.
- **Baselines:** A comprehensive set of baselines was employed:
 - **Eig+ICL:** ICL head applied to ground-truth Laplacian eigenvectors (computed offline).
 - **Eig+LR:** `ℓ2`-regularized logistic regression on ground-truth Laplacian eigenvectors.
 - **Orig+RBF-LR:** Kernel logistic regression with an RBF kernel on original raw coordinates.
 - **Orig+ICL:** ICL head applied directly to original raw coordinates.
 - **Transformer Baseline:** A scale-matched, 2-layer encoder Transformer (MetaICL formulation) trained end-to-end on the same episodes, using tokenized labels.

The experiments varied the percentage of labeled data (e.g., 3%, 21%, 39% of 100 total samples) and evaluated in-distribution (ID) and out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings regarding the capabilities of the proposed IC-SSL Transformer and its performance across various benchmarks.

**Performance on Synthetic Manifolds:**
- **Consistent Superiority:** The "Orig+E2E-ICL" model consistently outperformed all baselines across all tested elementary manifolds (Sphere, Cylinder, Cone, Torus, Swiss-Roll) and their higher-dimensional Cartesian products. For example, on the cylinder manifold, the model surpassed the strongest competitor (Eig+ICL) by approximately 5–7 percentage points, reaching around 90% accuracy with ≥ 25% labeled data.
- **Low-Label Regime Advantage:** The performance advantage of the end-to-end model was particularly pronounced in low-label regimes (e.g., 3% to 15% labeled data), indicating the effectiveness of its contextual representation learning when supervision is scarce.
- **Importance of Geometric Structure:** Direct classification on raw coordinates (Orig+RBF-LR) performed substantially worse than any manifold-aware alternative, highlighting the benefit of exploiting geometric structure.
- **Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Generalization:** When trained on a diverse set of source manifolds and tested on an unseen target manifold, Orig+E2E-ICL generally matched or exceeded its in-distribution (ID) performance for simpler geometries (e.g., cylinder, sphere, torus). This indicates that the model learns geometry-aware computations that transfer effectively. For more complex manifolds like Swiss Roll and Cone, OOD performance was typically lower than ID.
- **Product Manifolds:** On complex 5-factor product manifolds, the Orig+E2E-ICL Transformer maintained an 8–10% margin over the strongest baseline (Eig+ICL), suggesting its ability to extract useful Laplacian-based features and propagate labels effectively even in higher-dimensional spaces with intricate decision boundaries.

**Performance on Image Manifolds:**
- **Zero-Shot OOD Transfer:** The model demonstrated zero-shot OOD transfer capabilities. Despite being trained exclusively on simple synthetic manifolds and having no prior exposure to image data, Orig+E2E-ICL achieved approximately 77% accuracy with only 15% labeled examples on diffusion-generated image manifolds.
- **Significant Baseline Outperformance:** This performance consistently surpassed baseline methods by margins of 10–20 percentage points across nearly the entire labeled data regime. Even with extremely sparse labeling (3/100 labeled images), the model sustained performance above 62% accuracy.
- **Data Efficiency:** The network showed high data efficiency, with three labels guiding it to a correctness level that baselines could not match even with ten times more labels. This implies the implementation of an efficient, modality-agnostic algorithm for constructing geometry-aware representations.

**ImageNet100 Experiments (Real-World Features):**
- **Higher Accuracy and Separation:** On ImageNet100 features, Orig+E2E-ICL achieved higher accuracy and separation scores (intra-class minus inter-class similarity) compared to a scale-matched Transformer baseline, especially when training set sizes were small.
- **Phase Transition in Baseline:** The Transformer baseline exhibited a sharp phase transition in performance around 1200 training sets, where accuracy and separation scores abruptly increased. This finding linked the performance transition to a change in the representational structure learned by the baseline.
- **Representational Alignment:** Analysis using mutual kNN (mNN) alignment showed that the proposed model started highly aligned to ground-truth Laplacian Eigenmap (EIG) features due to its spectral inductive bias. The baseline Transformer progressively aligned with both the EIG features and the proposed model's representations as training data increased. At larger scales, both models appeared to approximate similar underlying geometries. The proposed model reached comparable performance with substantially fewer samples.

**Ablation Studies (Appendices):**
- **ICL Transformer Depth:** A 2-layer ICL architecture consistently outperformed a 1-layer architecture, particularly in low-label regimes, supporting the theoretical benefits of increased depth for learning sophisticated manifold representations.
- **Kernel Choices:** While the RBF kernel generally yielded better results, the dominance of the end-to-end approach remained consistent across both linear and RBF kernels on various manifolds.
- **Laplacian and PE Modules:** Ablations showed that replacing the RBF activation in the Laplacian-forming module with a linear mapping or using a non-iterative 1-layer variant of the Laplacian/PE modules significantly reduced performance, confirming the importance of iterative refinement and non-linear kernels for effective manifold alignment.

In summary, the core findings indicate that the proposed two-stage Transformer construction successfully integrates semi-supervised representation learning into the ICL framework, leading to marked performance improvements in data-scarce scenarios, robust generalization capabilities, and provides a reference model for understanding the learning dynamics of standard Transformers.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces In-Context Semi-Supervised Learning (IC-SSL), marking an expansion of the in-context learning paradigm to settings where unlabeled data are prevalent. The work's significance and potential impact can be delineated as follows:

- **Advancement of In-Context Learning (ICL):** By demonstrating that Transformers can effectively leverage unlabeled contextual demonstrations, the paper extends the applicability and understanding of ICL beyond purely supervised scenarios. This is particularly relevant given the practical challenges of acquiring large quantities of labeled data.
- **Mechanistic Understanding of Transformers:** The explicit two-stage Transformer construction, designed to mimic classical semi-supervised manifold learning algorithms (Laplacian Eigenmaps and functional gradient descent), offers a more transparent and mechanistic explanation for how complex computations are realized within Transformer architectures. This contributes to the broader effort to demystify the internal workings of large language models and other Transformer-based systems, providing a framework for analyzing how attention and MLPs contribute to specific computational steps.
- **Enhanced Data Efficiency:** The demonstrated superior performance in low-label regimes and the high data efficiency (e.g., achieving strong results on image manifolds with very few labels) suggest that IC-SSL can significantly reduce the dependency on extensive labeled datasets. This has practical implications for applications in domains where labeling is costly, time-consuming, or requires expert knowledge.
- **Robust Generalization:** The model's robust out-of-distribution (OOD) transfer capabilities across diverse synthetic manifolds and its zero-shot transfer to high-dimensional image manifolds indicate that it learns fundamental, geometry-aware computations rather than merely overfitting to specific datasets or coordinate systems. This suggests the potential for developing more broadly applicable and generalizable foundation models.
- **Inductive Bias for Performance:** The structured two-stage design acts as an effective inductive bias, enabling the Transformer to learn better representations and perform more efficiently. This finding suggests that incorporating algorithmic insights into architectural design can be a fruitful approach for developing more capable and interpretable models.
- **Reference for Future Research:** The proposed model serves as a valuable reference point for analyzing and interpreting the behavior of unconstrained, end-to-end Transformers. The observation of representational alignment and phase transitions in the baseline Transformer, interpreted through the lens of the proposed model, opens new avenues for understanding how standard Transformers acquire and refine their internal representations.
- **Potential for Real-World Applications:** The ability to learn robust representations and make accurate predictions with minimal labels could be impactful in various fields, including medical imaging, scientific discovery, and resource-constrained environments, where data might be abundant but labels are scarce.

Overall, this research represents a step towards building more data-efficient, robust, and interpretable Transformer models by explicitly integrating semi-supervised learning principles into the in-context learning paradigm.
