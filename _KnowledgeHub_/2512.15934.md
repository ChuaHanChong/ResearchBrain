# In-Context Semi-Supervised Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Duke University introduced In-Context Semi-Supervised Learning (IC-SSL), a Transformer framework designed to leverage both labeled and unlabeled data within the input context. The framework mechanistically integrates representation learning from unlabeled data via Laplacian Eigenmaps and classification via functional gradient descent, demonstrating improved performance, enhanced sample efficiency in low-label regimes, and robust out-of-distribution generalization.

## Problem

- Supervised In-Context Learning (ICL) typically requires abundant labeled data, which is often scarce and costly to obtain in real-world scenarios.
- Existing ICL research primarily focused on supervised settings, overlooking the valuable structural information inherent in plentiful unlabeled data.
- A lack of mechanistic understanding regarding how Transformers can implicitly leverage contextual information to perform semi-supervised learning tasks.

## Method

- Developed a two-stage Transformer architecture, `TF_rep` for ==representation learning== and `TF_sup` for in-context supervised learning, trained end-to-end to implement IC-SSL.
- `TF_rep` learns to construct a discrete ==Laplacian matrix== from unlabeled input covariates using RBF attention, and then computes its bottom-k eigenvectors (==Laplacian Eigenmaps==) via linear attention, providing context-dependent feature representations.
- `TF_sup` performs classification on these learned features by implementing ==functional gradient descent== in its forward pass, leveraging scarce labeled examples to infer a latent function for prediction.

## Results

- The `Orig+E2E-ICL` model consistently outperforms strong baselines across synthetic, product, and image manifolds, showing improvements of **5-7 percentage points** and achieving around **90%** accuracy on some tasks with **25%** labeled data.
- Demonstrates marked sample efficiency, achieving high performance with as few as **3-15%** labeled data, outperforming baselines that require significantly more labels.
- Exhibits robust out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization, including exceptional zero-shot OOD transfer to novel image manifolds (generated by Stable Diffusion) with **~77%** accuracy (**15%** labels), suggesting learning of fundamental geometric computations.
- Achieves higher accuracy and separation scores on ImageNet100 compared to a standard Transformer baseline, which displayed a sharp 'phase transition' in its performance and representational structure at higher training episodes.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Transformers can mechanistically implement well-understood algorithms, such as Laplacian Eigenmaps for manifold learning and functional gradient descent for classification, entirely within their forward pass.
> - Embedding algorithmic inductive biases into Transformer architecture significantly improves sample efficiency in low-label regimes and enables robust generalization by leveraging underlying data geometry.
> - The structured IC-SSL model provides a 'transparent account' of how Transformers can acquire geometric awareness, serving as a reference to understand implicit mechanisms and 'phase transitions' in standard Transformer baselines.
