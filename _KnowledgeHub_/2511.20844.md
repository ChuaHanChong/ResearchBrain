# Pre-train to Gain: Robust Learning Without Clean Labels

> [!summary] Summary
> This work explores how self-supervised pre-training (SSL) can enhance deep learning model robustness to noisy labels without requiring clean data subsets. Researchers demonstrate that SSL pre-training consistently improves classification accuracy on clean test sets and label error detection when models are trained on corrupted datasets.

## Problem

- Deep neural networks are prone to memorizing incorrect labels when trained on noisy datasets, leading to poor generalization.
- Many existing Learning with Noisy Labels (LNL) methods rely on restrictive assumptions, such as access to clean label subsets or prior knowledge of noise distribution, limiting their real-world applicability.
- Obtaining large, high-quality, meticulously labeled datasets for deep learning is often expensive, time-consuming, or impossible in practical applications.

## Method

- Implement a ==two-stage training pipeline==: first, ==self-supervised pre-training== (using SimCLR or Barlow Twins) on the entire noisy dataset without labels, then supervised fine-tuning on the same noisy dataset.
- Evaluate the approach on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100 with synthetic uniform noise, and CIFAR-10N, CIFAR-100N with ==real-world human annotation noise==.
- Compare SSL pre-training against training from scratch and against models initialized with ImageNet pre-trained weights.

## Results

- SSL pre-training consistently increased classification accuracy on clean test sets by **5-30%** on CIFAR-10/100 (synthetic noise) and **5-8%** on CIFAR-10N/100N (real-world noise), particularly under high noise rates.
- Models pre-trained with SSL improved label error detection (F1 Score and Balanced Accuracy) by **4-7** percentage points, showing enhanced ability to distinguish correct from incorrect labels.
- SSL pre-trained models demonstrated greater resistance to overfitting during prolonged supervised training, maintaining higher accuracy and slower loss increase compared to models trained from scratch.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Self-supervised pre-training enables models to learn robust, noise-invariant feature representations by completely bypassing the label space during the initial training phase.
> - The benefits of SSL pre-training emerge rapidly (e.g., within 10 epochs) and tend to plateau around 50 epochs, indicating computational efficiency.
> - Domain-aligned self-supervised pre-training offers a substantial advantage over generic external pre-training (like ImageNet) under high label noise conditions.
