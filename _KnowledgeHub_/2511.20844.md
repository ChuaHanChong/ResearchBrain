# Pre-train to Gain: Robust Learning Without Clean Labels

> [!summary] Summary
> This work explores how self-supervised pre-training (SSL) can enhance deep learning model robustness to noisy labels without requiring clean data subsets. Researchers demonstrate that SSL pre-training consistently improves classification accuracy on clean test sets and label error detection when models are trained on corrupted datasets.

## Problem

- Deep neural networks are prone to memorizing incorrect labels when trained on noisy datasets, leading to poor generalization.
- Many existing Learning with Noisy Labels (LNL) methods rely on restrictive assumptions, such as access to clean label subsets or prior knowledge of noise distribution, limiting their real-world applicability.
- Obtaining large, high-quality, meticulously labeled datasets for deep learning is often expensive, time-consuming, or impossible in practical applications.

## Method

- Implement a ==two-stage training pipeline==: first, ==self-supervised pre-training== (using SimCLR or Barlow Twins) on the entire noisy dataset without labels, then supervised fine-tuning on the same noisy dataset.
- Evaluate the approach on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100 with synthetic uniform noise, and CIFAR-10N, CIFAR-100N with ==real-world human annotation noise==.
- Compare SSL pre-training against training from scratch and against models initialized with ImageNet pre-trained weights.

## Results

- SSL pre-training consistently increased classification accuracy on clean test sets by **5-30%** on CIFAR-10/100 (synthetic noise) and **5-8%** on CIFAR-10N/100N (real-world noise), particularly under high noise rates.
- Models pre-trained with SSL improved label error detection (F1 Score and Balanced Accuracy) by **4-7** percentage points, showing enhanced ability to distinguish correct from incorrect labels.
- SSL pre-trained models demonstrated greater resistance to overfitting during prolonged supervised training, maintaining higher accuracy and slower loss increase compared to models trained from scratch.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Self-supervised pre-training enables models to learn robust, noise-invariant feature representations by completely bypassing the label space during the initial training phase.
> - The benefits of SSL pre-training emerge rapidly (e.g., within 10 epochs) and tend to plateau around 50 epochs, indicating computational efficiency.
> - Domain-aligned self-supervised pre-training offers a substantial advantage over generic external pre-training (like ImageNet) under high label noise conditions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of deep learning has made extraordinary progress across numerous domains, from image recognition to natural language processing. However, a foundational prerequisite for this success is the availability of large, high-quality, and meticulously labeled datasets. In practical, real-world applications, obtaining such clean datasets is often prohibitively expensive, time-consuming, or even impossible, leading to the pervasive issue of **noisy labels**. Training deep neural networks directly on datasets with corrupted labels is known to lead to several critical problems, most notably the networks' tendency to "memorize" incorrect labels, resulting in poor generalization capabilities and degraded accuracy on unseen, clean data.

Addressing the challenge of learning with noisy labels (LNL) is a highly active research area. Existing strategies can be broadly categorized into several approaches:
- **Robust Loss Functions:** Modifying the loss function to be less sensitive to mislabeled examples (e.g., Generalized Cross Entropy Loss, Active Negative Loss Functions).
- **Sample Re-weighting/Selection:** Identifying and down-weighting or removing potentially noisy samples during training, often based on model confidence or disagreement among multiple models (e.g., MentorNet, Co-sampling).
- **Semi-supervised and Weakly Supervised Learning:** Leveraging a small subset of clean labels or additional unlabeled data to guide the learning process (e.g., DivideMix, Early-Learning Regularization).

A significant limitation of many state-of-the-art LNL methods is their reliance on critical assumptions, such as access to a small, pristine subset of clean labels or prior knowledge about the exact distribution of noise. These assumptions frequently do not hold in real-world large-scale or low-resource settings, thus limiting the applicability of these advanced techniques.

Into this landscape, **Self-Supervised Learning (SSL)** has emerged as a powerful paradigm. SSL methods enable models to learn rich, semantically meaningful feature representations from unlabeled data by designing "pretext tasks" that generate supervisory signals from the data itself, often through data augmentations. Prominent examples include contrastive learning methods like SimCLR and MoCo, and redundancy reduction methods like Barlow Twins and BYOL. The key advantage of SSL in the context of noisy labels is its inherent ability to learn directly from the raw data, completely bypassing the label space during the pre-training phase, thereby remaining unaffected by label corruption.

This paper specifically aims to bridge the gap between the power of SSL and the practical challenge of noisy labels *without* introducing the complexities or restrictive assumptions of many existing LNL approaches. While related work like Contrast to Divide (C2D) has explored integrating SSL pre-training with existing LNL algorithms (e.g., DivideMix, ELR+), this work takes a simpler, more direct approach. It investigates the *isolated and standalone effect* of self-supervised pre-training as a direct strategy to enhance noise robustness, without requiring a clean subset of data or coupling with additional LNL algorithms. By comparing SSL pre-training on the target dataset against a baseline and ImageNet pre-training, the authors position their work as a highly practical and broadly applicable solution to a fundamental problem in deep learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to thoroughly investigate whether **self-supervised pre-training (SSL pre-training)** can significantly enhance a deep learning model's robustness to noisy labels when training on corrupted datasets, specifically *without requiring any clean subset of data*.

This objective is driven by several key motivations:
1. **Addressing Deep Network Vulnerability:** Deep neural networks are notoriously prone to memorizing incorrect labels, especially in the absence of explicit noise-handling mechanisms. This memorization leads to severe degradation in generalization performance and accuracy on clean test data. The authors seek a method to counteract this inherent vulnerability.
2. **Overcoming Limitations of Existing LNL Methods:** Many current approaches for learning with noisy labels necessitate access to a small set of clean labels or knowledge about the noise distribution. Such assumptions are often unrealistic in practical deployments, limiting the widespread applicability of these methods. The paper aims to propose a solution that bypasses these restrictive requirements.
3. **Leveraging Label-Agnostic Representation Learning:** Self-supervised learning offers a powerful paradigm for learning robust feature representations directly from the raw data, entirely independently of labels. By utilizing SSL for pre-training, the model can learn semantically meaningful and noise-invariant features before ever being exposed to the corrupted labels during supervised fine-tuning. This label-agnostic nature of SSL makes it a natural candidate for tackling label noise.
4. **Simplicity and Scalability:** The authors are motivated to explore a solution that is simple to implement, scalable to various datasets, and does not require complex modifications to the downstream supervised training pipeline or the introduction of specialized loss functions. A two-stage "pre-train then fine-tune" approach using SSL is inherently straightforward.
5. **Comparative Analysis with ImageNet Pre-training:** ImageNet pre-trained weights are a common initialization strategy for many computer vision tasks. The research aims to understand if SSL pre-training on the target (potentially noisy) dataset can offer superior robustness, particularly under high noise conditions, compared to generic pre-training on a large, clean external dataset like ImageNet. This comparison assesses the real-world relevance and advantage of domain-aligned, noise-agnostic pre-training.
6. **Improved Label Error Detection:** Beyond just classification accuracy, the authors are motivated to see if SSL pre-training also improves the model's intrinsic ability to detect label errors, which is crucial for potential data cleaning or quality assessment efforts.

In essence, the research seeks to demonstrate that SSL pre-training can serve as a simple, effective, and broadly applicable strategy to gain substantial robustness against label noise, making deep learning models more reliable in real-world data environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors adopt a clear, comparative methodology to evaluate the effectiveness of self-supervised pre-training in the context of noisy labels. Their approach is primarily centered around a two-stage training pipeline and a rigorous evaluation against established baselines and alternative pre-training strategies.

**Core Training Pipeline:**
1. **Baseline Supervised Method:** A standard ResNet-18 model is trained from scratch on the noisy dataset for 10 epochs. This serves as the control group, representing typical supervised training without any specific noise-handling mechanism or pre-training.
2. **Self-Supervised + Supervised Fine-tuning:** An identical ResNet-18 model first undergoes a self-supervised pre-training phase for a specified number of epochs (10, 25, 50, or 100 epochs). This pre-trained feature extractor is then fine-tuned via standard supervised training on the *same noisy dataset* for 10 epochs. The key here is that the SSL pre-training explicitly avoids using any label information, ensuring noise-agnostic representation learning.

**Self-Supervised Learning (SSL) Methods:**
To ensure that findings are not specific to a single SSL objective, two fundamentally different and widely used SSL methods are employed:
- **SimCLR:** A contrastive learning method that learns representations by maximizing agreement between different augmented views of the same image instance and minimizing agreement with negative samples.
- **Barlow Twins:** A redundancy reduction method that aims to make the cross-correlation matrix between the outputs of two identical networks (fed with distorted versions of an image) as close to the identity matrix as possible, thereby reducing redundancy between learned features.
Both methods are trained on the *entire noisy training dataset*, without utilizing any label information.

**Datasets and Noise Generation:**
The experiments are conducted on widely recognized image classification benchmarks:
- **CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100:** These datasets are used with synthetically injected uniform label noise. For a given corruption rate η (fraction of incorrect labels), a percentage η of training labels are randomly flipped. This allows for controlled study across varying noise levels (η = 0.0, 0.2, 0.4, 0.6, 0.8). The authors assume the inherent label error rates in these clean datasets are negligible compared to the induced noise.
- **CIFAR-10N and CIFAR-100N:** To evaluate performance under more realistic, non-uniform noise conditions, these datasets with real-world human annotation noise are utilized. CIFAR-100N has η ≈ 0.4, while CIFAR-10N has two versions with η ≈ 0.1 and η ≈ 0.4.

**Model Architecture and Training Parameters:**
- A **ResNet-18** backbone is consistently used across all experiments for feature extraction.
- The **Adam optimizer** is employed for both SSL pre-training and supervised fine-tuning.
- The supervised fine-tuning duration is fixed at 10 epochs, a duration determined through preliminary experiments to be the point where overfitting begins on the original uncorrupted datasets, ensuring a fair comparison across models.
- All results are averaged over 5 random seeds to ensure statistical robustness.

**Evaluation Metrics:**
The performance is assessed using a comprehensive set of metrics:
- **Classification Accuracy:** Measured on a *clean test set* to evaluate the model's generalization capability despite training on noisy labels.
- **Label Error Detection:** To quantify how well the trained models can identify mislabeled instances, **Confident Learning** is used. This framework estimates the probability of label error based on the model's predicted probabilities. The model is evaluated on a *corrupted test set* (where labels are artificially flipped for a controlled environment), and the following metrics are reported:
 - **F1 Score:** A harmonic mean of precision and recall, particularly useful for imbalanced classification tasks (identifying errors, which are typically a minority).
 - **Balanced Accuracy (BA):** The average of recall obtained on each class, providing a more balanced view of performance when class distributions are skewed. These metrics directly gauge the model's ability to distinguish between correctly and incorrectly labeled data points.

**Additional Experiments:**
- **Pre-train Duration Variation:** Investigating how different SSL pre-training durations (10, 25, 50, 100 epochs) impact downstream robustness.
- **ImageNet Pre-trained Weights Comparison:** A critical comparison against models initialized with ImageNet pre-trained weights to assess the advantage of domain-aligned SSL pre-training versus generic transfer learning.
- **Increased Supervised Training:** Exploring whether the benefits of SSL pre-training persist when the supervised fine-tuning stage is extended significantly (e.g., from 10 to 100 epochs), to understand its impact on overfitting dynamics.

This rigorous methodological framework allows the authors to systematically isolate and quantify the benefits of self-supervised pre-training as a robust strategy against label noise.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents a consistent and compelling set of findings demonstrating the significant benefits of self-supervised pre-training for learning with noisy labels.

1. **Consistent Improvement in Classification Accuracy and Robustness:**
 - Across all tested noise rates (η = 0.0 to 0.8) and datasets (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100), SSL pre-training (using either SimCLR or Barlow Twins) consistently led to higher classification accuracy on clean test sets compared to the baseline (training from scratch).
 - The performance gap between SSL pre-trained models and the baseline widened significantly as the label noise rate increased, highlighting improved robustness. For instance, on CIFAR-10, SimCLR boosted accuracy by 5–30%, and on CIFAR-100, Barlow Twins improved it by 6–20%.
 - For real-world noise (CIFAR-10N, CIFAR-100N), SimCLR pre-training yielded 5-8% improvements in classification accuracy, demonstrating the method's effectiveness beyond synthetic noise.

2. **Enhanced Label Error Detection:**
 - SSL pre-training substantially improved the model's ability to detect label errors, as measured by F1 Score and Balanced Accuracy (BA) using Confident Learning.
 - On average, SSL pre-trained methods improved F1 scores and Balanced Accuracy by 4–7 percentage points across all corruption levels and datasets. This indicates that the learned representations provide a better basis for distinguishing correct from incorrect labels.

3. **Impact of Pre-training Duration:**
 - The benefits of SSL pre-training emerged rapidly. Noticeable improvements were observed with as few as 10 pre-training epochs.
 - Gains tended to plateau after approximately 50 epochs of pre-training, suggesting that extensive pre-training might not be necessary for substantial benefits, making the approach computationally efficient. This trend was consistent across both SimCLR and Barlow Twins, and for both classification accuracy and label error detection metrics.

4. **Superiority over ImageNet Pre-training under High Noise:**
 - At low corruption rates (e.g., η = 0.0, 0.2), ImageNet pre-trained models performed comparably to, or slightly better than, SSL pre-trained models. This is expected, as ImageNet provides strong general features.
 - However, as label noise increased significantly (e.g., η = 0.6, 0.8), the effectiveness of ImageNet pre-training diminished rapidly, offering little to no advantage over training from scratch. In stark contrast, SSL pre-training continued to provide substantial performance improvements, particularly at η = 0.8 where it dramatically outperformed ImageNet pre-training. This is a critical finding, demonstrating the advantage of *domain-aligned* self-supervised pre-training when dealing with noisy target datasets.

5. **Resistance to Overfitting During Prolonged Supervised Training:**
 - When supervised fine-tuning was extended from 10 to 100 epochs, baseline models trained from scratch overfit rapidly to the corrupted labels, leading to a sharp decline in test accuracy and a pronounced rise in training loss.
 - SSL pre-trained models, however, exhibited remarkable stability: they achieved higher accuracy, overfit more slowly, and showed a much weaker increase in loss during the overfitting phase. This indicates that the noise-resilient representations learned during SSL pre-training effectively shield the model from memorizing corrupted labels, maintaining robustness even during prolonged exposure to noise.

In summary, the results unequivocally demonstrate that a simple, two-stage approach of self-supervised pre-training followed by standard supervised fine-tuning on the *same noisy dataset* is a highly effective, robust, and practical strategy to mitigate the detrimental effects of label noise.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The findings of "Pre-train to Gain: Robust Learning Without Clean Labels" carry significant implications for the development and deployment of deep learning models, particularly in real-world scenarios where perfectly clean data is a luxury rather than a given.

1. **A Simple Yet Powerful Noise-Robust Strategy:** The most significant contribution is the demonstration that self-supervised pre-training, as a standalone and straightforward technique, can substantially improve robustness to label errors. Unlike many existing learning with noisy labels (LNL) methods that require complex algorithmic modifications, robust loss functions, access to clean subsets, or knowledge of noise distributions, this approach is remarkably simple. It integrates seamlessly into existing deep learning pipelines by merely adding an initial, label-agnostic pre-training phase. This simplicity dramatically lowers the barrier to entry for practitioners seeking to mitigate label noise.

2. **Broad Applicability and Scalability:** Since SSL methods can learn representations from any unlabeled dataset, this approach is broadly applicable across various domains and data types where deep learning is used. Its scalability is inherent in the design of popular SSL algorithms, making it suitable for both small and large datasets. The fact that its benefits emerge early during pre-training also makes it computationally practical and cost-effective.

3. **Challenging the Dominance of External Pre-training:** The finding that domain-aligned SSL pre-training significantly outperforms ImageNet pre-trained models under high label noise is particularly impactful. This suggests that for applications where the target dataset is substantially noisy, investing in SSL pre-training *on that specific dataset* might be a more effective strategy than relying on generic, externally derived pre-trained weights, even from massive datasets like ImageNet. This could lead to a paradigm shift in how model initialization is approached in noisy data environments.

4. **Enhanced Model Reliability and Trustworthiness:** By preventing deep networks from memorizing incorrect labels, SSL pre-training leads to models that generalize better and maintain higher accuracy on clean, unseen data. This directly translates to more reliable and trustworthy AI systems, which is crucial for applications in sensitive domains like healthcare, autonomous driving, or financial analysis, where even small errors can have severe consequences.

5. **Improved Data Quality Assessment:** The enhanced ability of SSL pre-trained models to detect label errors has practical implications for data curation. These models could be used as a front-end tool to identify and flag potentially noisy samples, enabling targeted human review or automated correction. This iterative process could lead to the continuous improvement of dataset quality, further boosting model performance.

6. **Foundation for Future Research:** This work opens several avenues for future research, as highlighted by the authors. Exploring the integration of this simple pre-training scheme with more advanced SSL techniques designed for modern architectures like Vision Transformers (ViTs) could yield even greater robustness. Scaling the approach to much larger and more complex real-world datasets, and investigating its interplay with different types of label noise (e.g., class-dependent noise), are natural next steps.

In conclusion, "Pre-train to Gain" presents a pragmatic, effective, and broadly applicable solution to a fundamental challenge in deep learning. By leveraging the power of self-supervised learning, it empowers practitioners to build more robust and reliable AI systems, even when faced with imperfect real-world data, thereby accelerating the deployment and impact of deep learning across industries.
