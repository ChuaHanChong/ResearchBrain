# EDAR: Learning Environment-Dependent Action Representations for Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Fudan University and TeleAI developed EDAR, a method for robotic manipulation that learns latent action representations by explicitly grounding them in both executable control structures and environment-conditioned visual consequences. This approach improved average success rates on the LIBERO benchmark by 4.5 percentage points for the SmolVLA backbone and achieved significantly higher normalized scores in real-world long-horizon tasks.

## Problem

- Raw control trajectories in robotic manipulation are high-dimensional, noisy, and redundant, complicating policy learning.
- Existing action representations primarily focus on the intrinsic structure of action streams, often neglecting the environment-dependent semantics of actions.
- Without explicit constraints, latent action spaces can collapse to only encode trajectory information, failing to capture how actions change the world.

## Method

- **EDAR** jointly encodes ==action chunks==, current visual context, and learnable ==register tokens== using a ==Transformer encoder==, allowing action and scene information to interact.
- A ==dual-target decoder== is employed to simultaneously reconstruct the original continuous control trajectory and predict future visual feature tokens (action consequences).
- The decoder uses shared ==self-attention== with token-specialized feed-forward branches and an ==asymmetric attention mask== to effectively manage heterogeneous prediction targets.

## Results

- EDAR improved average success rates on the LIBERO benchmark, achieving **91.8%** for SmolVLA-EDAR (a **4.5 percentage point** increase) and **97.1%** for π0-EDAR (a **2.9 percentage point** increase), with larger gains in long-horizon tasks.
- The method enhanced performance on long-horizon, language-conditioned tasks like CALVIN ABCD→\rightarrow→D, increasing the average completed sequence length from **3.34** to **3.80** for SmolVLA-EDAR.
- In real-world long-horizon manipulation tasks, SmolVLA-EDAR achieved an average normalized score of **69.0%**, significantly outperforming SmolVLA (**45.0%**) and SmolVLA-VAE (**60.5%**), demonstrating improved robustness to compounding errors.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The semantic meaning of a robot action is inherently dependent on the environmental context in which it is executed and its observable visual effects.
> - Explicitly supervising latent action representations with both action reconstruction and future visual consequence prediction prevents latent space collapse and generates an executable, effect-aware action interface.
> - A specialized decoder design that couples action and visual consequence prediction, rather than fully decoupling them, is crucial for learning robust and effective environment-dependent action representations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is situated within the field of robotic manipulation, specifically focusing on the development of effective action representations for policy learning. Robotic manipulation often involves complex, noisy, and redundant raw control trajectories obtained from demonstrations or teleoperation. Learning a compact and informative representation of these actions is a critical challenge.

Existing methods for action representation learning primarily focus on the intrinsic structure of the action stream itself. These "trajectory-structure-based" approaches aim to compress, regularize, or tokenize raw control sequences, preserving properties like geometry, temporal variation, or smoothness. Examples include methods using compact trajectory codes, action tokens, frequency-based representations, or vector-quantized codes. While these methods are effective in reducing redundancy and simplifying modeling, the paper argues that they largely neglect the environmental context in which actions are performed. This means the semantic role of an action in changing the world remains implicit.

The proposed EDAR (Environment-Dependent Action Representation) addresses this limitation by explicitly grounding action representations in both the executable control structure and the expected visual consequences of actions within their specific environmental context. This perspective contrasts with prior work by emphasizing that the semantics of a robot action are inherently dependent on the scene in which it is executed. For example, the same gripper-closing motion can lead to different outcomes (e.g., grasping an object, closing a drawer) depending on the surrounding environment.

The research also relates to Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models and World Models. VLA models like RT-style systems use large sequence models and multimodal backbones for robotic control, where action representation is a central interface. EDAR aims to improve this interface. World models for robot learning often use future-aware supervision, such as future observation or video prediction, to model robot-world dynamics. EDAR leverages future visual consequences but uses them specifically to organize the latent action space, rather than building a general pixel-level world model or for rollout-based planning. This work contributes to the landscape by proposing an action representation that integrates environmental context and effect prediction more directly into the action latent space itself, rather than treating visual context merely as an external conditioning signal.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an effective action representation for robotic manipulation that inherently captures the environment-dependent semantics of actions. The authors identify several key motivations and challenges:

1. **Addressing Noisy and Redundant Raw Action Trajectories:** Raw control trajectories collected from demonstrations are often high-dimensional, noisy, and contain redundant information. This makes them difficult to directly model as an interface for policy learning, particularly for long-horizon manipulation tasks that require temporally coherent and physically feasible behaviors. The goal is to create a compact and learnable action interface.
2. **Recognizing Environment-Dependent Action Semantics:** A central motivation is the argument that the meaning or "semantics" of an action in manipulation is not solely determined by the motor commands themselves. The same sequence of motor commands can lead to vastly different outcomes depending on the current state of the environment. For instance, a "gripper-closing" and "rightward-motion" segment could grasp an object, close a drawer, or be inconsequential in free space. Existing action representations often treat actions as trajectory-centric, implicitly handling environmental context, which limits their ability to capture these context-dependent semantics.
3. **Preventing Latent Space Collapse:** Simply feeding visual context into an action encoder under an action-reconstruction objective is insufficient. Without further constraints, the model may find a shortcut by encoding only trajectory information, leaving the latent space organized by command-level similarity rather than interaction semantics. This can lead to different environment-dependent effects being mapped to similar latent actions.
4. **Grounding Action in Execution and Effect:** To overcome these limitations, the authors motivate the need for an action representation that is simultaneously executable (can reconstruct the original control trajectory) and environment-dependent (reflects the action's role under the current scene context). This requires the representation to be constrained by what the action *does* to the environment, not just how it is executed.
5. **Improving Downstream Policy Learning:** The ultimate objective is to provide a more effective policy interface that facilitates downstream policy learning. A more semantically meaningful action representation is hypothesized to lead to improved performance, particularly in complex and long-horizon manipulation tasks where policy robustness across compounding scene transitions is crucial.

In summary, the key objective is to explicitly embed environment-conditioned interaction semantics into the latent action space, moving beyond purely action-stream-centric representations, to create a more robust and effective interface for robotic control policies.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

EDAR (Environment-Dependent Action Representation) is designed to learn latent action tokens that encapsulate both executable control structure and environment-conditioned visual consequences. The methodology involves a two-stage training pipeline: first, learning the EDAR representation, and then integrating it into a policy for downstream task execution.

#### 3.1. Environment-Dependent Representation Learning

The core of EDAR lies in how it forms latent action representations and the objectives used to train them.

- **Visual Token Extraction:** Multi-view observations at the current time step ($I_t$) and after action execution ($I_{t+H}$) are processed by a pretrained image encoder ($E_{im}$) to extract visual feature tokens ($X_t$ and $X_{t+H}$). Using visual features instead of raw pixels aims to emphasize task-relevant semantic scene changes. The DINOv3-Base encoder is used as a frozen feature extractor.
- **Latent Action Token Formation:** A Transformer encoder ($f_\theta$) is used to jointly encode the action chunk ($A_t$), current visual context ($X_t$), and a set of learnable register tokens ($R$). These register tokens provide a fixed-size interface for variable-length action chunks. Crucially, shared self-attention within this encoder allows action content and scene context to interact, ensuring that the latent action tokens ($Z_t$) are formed with respect to the current environment, rather than solely from the action chunk's structure.
- **Dual-Target Decoding:** A decoder ($g_\phi$) takes the latent action tokens ($Z_t$), current visual tokens ($X_t$), and a learnable query embedding ($V$) as input. It is designed to predict two distinct targets simultaneously:
 - **Action Reconstruction ($\hat{A}_t$):** Reconstructs the original continuous control trajectory. This ensures the latent representation preserves the executability and fidelity of the motor commands. The loss function ($L_{act}$) is an L2 loss between the predicted and ground-truth action chunks.
 - **Visual Consequence Prediction ($\hat{X}_{t+H}$):** Predicts the future visual feature tokens, representing the scene state after the action is executed. This forces the latent tokens to capture the environment-conditioned effect of the action. The loss function ($L_{eff}$) is based on cosine similarity between predicted and ground-truth future visual features, with a stop-gradient applied to the ground truth target to prevent the visual encoder from being updated.
- **Combined Objective:** The total representation learning objective ($L_{EDAR}$) is a weighted sum of the action reconstruction loss and the visual consequence loss: $L_{EDAR} = L_{act} + \lambda L_{eff}$. This combination aims to prevent two potential degeneracies: without $L_{act}$, $Z_t$ might only capture scene change without being a reliable action interface; without $L_{eff}$, $Z_t$ might collapse back to an action-only trajectory code.

#### 3.2. Dual-Target Decoding with Specialized Branches

To manage the heterogeneous nature of the two prediction targets (continuous actions and visual features) while maintaining their coupling, EDAR employs a decoder with shared attention and token-specialized feed-forward branches:

- **Shared Hidden Space:** Latent action tokens ($Z_t$) and current visual tokens ($X_t$) are projected into a common hidden space.
- **Shared Self-Attention:** At each decoder layer, all tokens interact through a shared self-attention module, allowing action and visual information to influence each other.
- **Specialized Feed-Forward Networks:** The feed-forward update within each layer is specialized based on the token type: FFN$_{act}$ for latent-action tokens and FFN$_{vis}$ for visual tokens.
- **Separate Output Heads:** After the final decoder layer, separate output heads ($o_{act}$ and $o_{vis}$) predict the respective targets ($\hat{A}_t$ and $\hat{X}_{t+H}$).
- **Asymmetric Attention Mask:** The decoder uses an asymmetric attention mask. Visual queries can attend to the full token set for future prediction. Action queries, however, follow a causal mask, attending only to latent action tokens and preceding action context, preventing them from exploiting future action or visual prediction tokens as a shortcut.

#### 3.3. Downstream Policy Evaluation

After the EDAR module is trained and its parameters are frozen, it serves as an action interface for downstream policies. Instead of predicting raw action chunks, the policy predicts latent action tokens ($Z_t$), which are then mapped to executable robot controls by the learned EDAR action decoder.

- **Flow-Matching Policy:** The paper instantiates the downstream policy using a flow-matching approach. Given visual observations and a language instruction (combined into condition $c$), the policy learns a velocity field in the latent action space to denoise Gaussian noise into target latent action tokens.
- **Inference:** During inference, the policy generates latent action tokens, which are subsequently decoded by the EDAR decoder into continuous robot controls for execution. This setup evaluates whether the environment-dependent latent space offers a more effective and easily modelable interface for policy learning.

#### 3.4. Training Details

The EDAR module itself is implemented as an 8-layer Transformer encoder and an 8-layer Transformer decoder, with RMSNorm. The image encoder for visual features is DINOv3-Base. The representation learning uses specific learning rates, batch sizes, and chunk sizes for simulation and real-world experiments. For downstream policy training, EDAR replaces the action head of existing VLA backbones (e.g., SmolVLA, $\pi_0$, FLOWER-VLA), keeping other training parameters consistent with the baselines for controlled comparison.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were designed to evaluate EDAR's performance across diverse simulation and real-world robotic manipulation benchmarks, focusing on its environment-dependent advantage, robustness, and stability in long-horizon tasks.

#### 4.1. Simulation Experiments

- **LIBERO Benchmark:**
 - EDAR consistently improved performance compared to trajectory-centric action representations and environment-independent latent action spaces.
 - Using SmolVLA as a backbone, SmolVLA-EDAR achieved an average success rate of 91.8%, an increase of 4.5 points over standard SmolVLA (87.3%). Significant gains were observed in LIBERO-Long tasks (+9.6 points), demonstrating EDAR's benefit for extended behaviors.
 - It also surpassed SmolVLA-FAST and SmolVLA-VQ-VLA, indicating its superiority over alternative tokenization methods under the same backbone.
 - Compared to SmolVLA-VAE (an environment-independent latent interface), SmolVLA-EDAR showed further improvements, confirming the value of explicit environment conditioning and visual consequence supervision.
 - When integrated into the stronger $\pi_0$ backbone, $\pi_0$-EDAR improved the average success rate from 94.2% to 97.1%, with a notable +9.0 points gain on LIBERO-Long, suggesting its generalizability across different VLA architectures.
- **CALVIN ABCD→D Benchmark:**
 - EDAR enhanced performance on long-horizon, language-conditioned manipulation tasks involving sequential execution and compounding scene transitions.
 - SmolVLA-EDAR increased the average completed sequence length from 3.34 to 3.80. The improvements were more pronounced for later stages of the instruction chain, with gains of +1.9 to +14.2 points for 1/5 to 5/5 success rates, respectively. This indicated improved coherence over multiple environment transitions.
 - Similar trends were observed with the FLOWER-VLA backbone, where FLOWER-VLA-EDAR increased the average completed sequence length from 4.58 to 4.75.
- **Meta-World Benchmark:**
 - EDAR proved effective on a multi-task benchmark with varying difficulties.
 - SmolVLA-EDAR improved the average success rate from 69.3% to 80.5% (an absolute gain of +11.2 points) compared to the reproduced SmolVLA baseline.
 - The gains were more substantial on Medium (+4.6), Hard (+23.3), and Very Hard (+16.0) tasks, suggesting EDAR's particular advantage in more complex scenarios.

#### 4.2. Long-Horizon Real-World Manipulation

- EDAR was evaluated on four real-world long-horizon tasks: Stack-5, Drawer, Rope, and Flower, requiring sequential completion and sensitivity to intermediate scene states.
- SmolVLA-EDAR achieved the highest average normalized score of 69.0%, significantly outperforming SmolVLA (45.0%) and SmolVLA-VAE (60.5%).
- Specific task improvements included +24.0 points for Stack-5, +20.0 for Drawer, +12.0 for Rope, and +40.0 for Flower compared to SmolVLA.
- The results suggested that EDAR's grounding of action latents in current visual context and future visual consequences improved robustness against compounding errors in real-world sequential manipulation.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Environment-conditioned action encoding:** Removing current environment context from the encoder reduced the average success rate on LIBERO from 91.8% to 90.9% (a drop of 0.9 points), affirming that action encoding benefits from scene-dependent information.
- **Consequence prediction supervision:** Eliminating the future visual prediction objective decreased performance from 91.8% to 90.1% (a drop of 1.7 points). This indicated that explicitly modeling action-induced scene changes through future visual prediction is crucial for shaping an effect-aware latent space.
- **Dual-target decoder design:**
 - The specialized branches design achieved 91.8%.
 - A single shared decoder reduced performance to 90.2% (a drop of 1.6 points), suggesting conflicts in optimizing for heterogeneous targets.
 - Using two fully separate decoders further reduced performance to 88.7% (a drop of 3.1 points), indicating that decoupling the action and effect predictions weakens their beneficial interaction.
- **Visual feature encoder for future prediction:** DINOv3 features (DINOv3-B: 91.8%, DINOv3-L: 92.0%) generally yielded better performance than JEPA-based targets (I-JEPA: 89.2%, V-JEPA 2.1: 89.7%) for visual consequence prediction. This suggested that spatially stable and locally interpretable features are more effective targets for dense feature prediction, which helps in organizing action latents based on object-centric scene changes.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of EDAR offers several significant contributions and potential impacts on the field of robotic manipulation:

1. **Shift in Action Representation Paradigm:** EDAR proposes a fundamental shift from action-stream-centric action representations to environment-dependent ones. By explicitly grounding action tokens in both executable control structure and expected visual consequences, it acknowledges that the semantic meaning of an action is inherently context-dependent. This re-conceptualization of action semantics for embodied agents could influence future designs of robotic control systems.
2. **Improved Policy Learning and Robustness:** The consistent performance gains observed across diverse simulated and real-world benchmarks, particularly in challenging long-horizon tasks, indicate that EDAR provides a more effective and robust action interface for downstream policy learning. Its ability to reduce compounding errors and better handle dynamic scene changes in sequential manipulation tasks is a notable advantage.
3. **Enhanced Understanding of Robot-Environment Interaction:** By forcing the latent action space to capture interaction-relevant scene changes, EDAR encourages the learning of action representations that are more aligned with the robot's impact on its environment. This can lead to policies that are not merely mimicking trajectories but are "understanding" and generating actions based on their anticipated effects.
4. **Generality and Plug-in Compatibility:** EDAR is demonstrated to be a "plug-in" component that can improve various Vision-Language-Action (VLA) backbones (e.g., SmolVLA, $\pi_0$, FLOWER-VLA). This suggests its potential for broad applicability across different existing and future VLA architectures without requiring extensive redesigns of the entire policy pipeline.
5. **Addressing Data Efficiency Challenges:** While not explicitly a core contribution, the improved performance with EDAR, especially in real-world settings with limited demonstrations, suggests that learning more semantically rich action representations could contribute to more data-efficient learning for complex robotic tasks.
6. **Guidance for World Model Design:** The ablation study on visual feature encoders offers practical guidance for designing auxiliary prediction tasks in robot learning. It highlights the importance of choosing a target feature space that provides stable and locally interpretable supervision for action-induced scene changes, rather than relying solely on general-purpose image-video representations.

Overall, EDAR's approach to learning environment-dependent action representations represents a step towards more capable and generalist robotic manipulation systems, fostering a deeper integration between perception, action, and environmental context.
