# AnchorWorld: Embodied Egocentric World Simulation with View-based Evolution Customization

> [!summary] Summary
> AnchorWorld, a novel egocentric world simulation framework, integrates hybrid-view human action control with evolvable anchor-view customization to provide enhanced interaction integrity and flexible environmental definition. The framework demonstrates superior scene consistency, camera accuracy, and text alignment across diverse simulation scenarios, enabling users to drive exploration and interaction within customizable, self-evolving worlds.

## Problem

- Existing egocentric world models struggle to achieve robust embodied action control due to sparse and incomplete full-body motion supervision from first-person videos.
- Current methods for defining simulated worlds typically rely on implicit cues, limiting precise, localized control over specific 3D content or the temporal evolution of local scene states, especially for unobserved regions.
- Prior egocentric world models address action control and world-state customization as separate problems, lacking a unified framework for versatile controllability in practical simulation scenarios.

## Method

- **AnchorWorld** implements a ==hybrid-view== action control mechanism by pre-training on Third-Person View (TPV) videos for robust full-body context, then adapting to First-Person View (FPV) data with projection-based action conditioning and spatial pose attention.
- The framework enables evolvable anchor-view customization through localized anchor views (image, 6-DoF pose, evolution prompt) as in-context priors, leveraging 3D Rotary Positional Embeddings and view pose injection for spatial grounding.
- A progressive multi-stage training strategy (TPV action, FPV action, static anchor-view, dynamic anchor-view) incrementally builds capabilities, ensuring stability and performance for each component.

## Results

- AnchorWorld achieved superior scene consistency (higher GIM-based Mat. Pix., CLIP-V, PSNR, SSIM) and camera accuracy (lower ATE, RTE, RRE) compared to baselines across egocentric static, Unreal Engine, and dynamic scenarios.
- The framework demonstrated effective egocentric human motion control, maintained scene coherence under significant viewpoint changes, and enabled dynamic scene evolution that accurately adhered to localized textual prompts.
- AnchorWorld exhibited generalization to out-of-distribution scenes and specialized capabilities such as inferring and generating out-of-sight dynamic scene elements based on anchor views and flexibly controlling local evolution via prompts.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating auxiliary Third-Person View (TPV) data during training significantly improves the model's ability to learn robust 3D human motion grounding and spatial awareness for egocentric simulation, even when the full body is not visible in FPV.
> - Explicitly defining and conditioning the world with pose-aware anchor views, augmented by localized text prompts, provides a powerful and precise mechanism for controlling both static scene content and dynamic local evolution.
> - A multi-stage training approach allows complex generative models to build capabilities sequentially, effectively tackling the intricate challenges of integrating embodied action control with dynamic, customizable world states.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of interactive world models aims to simulate dynamic visual environments that respond to user input. This area is particularly relevant for first-person applications such as virtual reality (VR) and embodied artificial intelligence (AI), where the simulator must not only predict visually plausible continuations but also accurately reflect user actions and movements.

Previous research in interactive world models has explored various control signals. Early approaches often relied on simplified inputs like keyboard commands, camera trajectories, or global text prompts for navigation and environment manipulation. More recent egocentric methods have advanced towards more natural forms of control, incorporating hand actions or full-body human motion. However, these methods face challenges. Learning full-body action control from egocentric videos is difficult because many body parts are often outside the camera's field of view, resulting in sparse and indirectly aligned motion supervision.

Another limitation in existing interactive world models pertains to how the "world" itself is defined and constrained. Many methods implicitly determine the environment based on an initial frame, a global prompt, or historical context. This implicit definition can lead to weakly constrained newly observed regions, making it challenging to specify content at particular 3D locations or guide the temporal evolution of local scene states.

AnchorWorld addresses these specific limitations by introducing a framework designed to provide both robust embodied action control and explicit, localized world-state customization. It seeks to bridge the gap in versatile controllability required for practical egocentric simulation scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to advance egocentric simulation by developing a framework, named AnchorWorld, that offers enhanced interaction integrity and a flexible mechanism for world customization. This overarching goal is driven by two main motivations:

1. **Robust Embodied Action Control in Egocentric Simulation**: Current egocentric world models that attempt to integrate human actions face a significant challenge: the majority of the human body, which drives navigation and interaction, is often out of the first-person camera's view. This makes direct supervision of full-body motion sparse and incomplete, hindering the model's ability to learn a robust spatial grounding between human actions and their visual consequences in the environment. The authors motivate the need for a mechanism that allows the model to observe the agent's full-body positioning relative to the environment, thereby facilitating more accurate and spatially aware human-world interactions. The objective is to enable simulation that is truly responsive to complex human body motion.

2. **Flexible and Localized World-State Customization**: Existing methods for defining the simulated world typically rely on implicit cues, such as an initial frame or a global text prompt. This approach limits the user's ability to precisely control what should appear at specific 3D locations or how local parts of the scene should change over time. When an agent moves into a previously unobserved area, the environment's properties in that region are often weakly constrained. The motivation here is to develop a mechanism that allows for explicit, localized control over the world's appearance and its dynamic evolution, even for regions initially outside the current view. This aims to provide users with greater creative and interactive agency in defining their simulated environments.

In summary, the authors aim to formulate "world-customizable embodied egocentric simulation" as a task and propose AnchorWorld as a unified framework to achieve human-motion-driven exploration and interaction within customizable, self-evolving worlds.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

AnchorWorld is implemented as a video generation model conditioned on both embodied human actions and customizable world specifications. It builds upon Wan2.2 TI2V 5B, a flow-matching-based DiT (Diffusion Transformer) model, generating 77-frame videos at 480p resolution.

#### 3.1. Input Signals

The framework takes two main types of control signals:
- **Embodied Human Motion**: Represented as a sequence of body actions derived from the SMPL-X parametric body model, $M \in \mathbb{R}^{f \times k \times 6}$, where $f$ is the number of frames, $k$ is the number of major joints (22, excluding hands), and each joint state includes 3D position and 3D axis-angle rotation.
- **Customizable World Specification**: Defined by an initial egocentric view $I_0$ and a set of localized anchor views $S = \{(I_i, c_i, t_i)\}_{i=1}^n$. Each anchor view comprises an RGB image $I_i$ (local visual appearance), a 6-DoF viewpoint pose $c_i = [R_i | p_i] \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 4}$ (3D spatial grounding), and an evolution prompt $t_i$ (textual description of local scene changes).

#### 3.2. Hybrid-View Human Action Control

To address the issue of incomplete full-body motion supervision in first-person videos, AnchorWorld incorporates Third-Person View (TPV) videos as auxiliary training data. In TPVs, the full human body and its interactions with the scene are explicitly visible, providing rich context.

- **Projection-Based Action Conditioning**: The action condition combines the full-body motion sequence with the camera trajectory. This formulation allows the model to learn how to project 3D human motion into 2D visual observations from arbitrary viewpoints.
- **Training Strategy**: The model is initially pre-trained on large-scale TPV videos, where camera parameters correspond to external observation viewpoints. Subsequently, it is adapted to egocentric simulation by aligning camera parameters with the human head perspective in First-Person View (FPV) data. This hybrid-view approach aims to facilitate learning a robust spatial grounding between human motion and scene responses.
- **Spatial Pose Attention**: Motion sequences ($M$) are encoded into latent embeddings ($z_m$). Similarly, camera pose sequences ($C$) are encoded into embeddings ($z_c$). These motion and camera pose tokens are concatenated with the VAE-encoded video latent tokens ($z_v^{(t)}$) along the spatial dimension. A spatial self-attention block processes this unified sequence, and auxiliary pose tokens are truncated after attention, retaining only the updated video features. This explicit concatenation ensures frame-wise correspondence between motion and video tokens.

#### 3.3. Evolvable Anchor-View Customization

This component enables localized world customization and dynamic evolution.

- **In-Context Anchor-View Priors**: Anchor-view images ($I_i$) are encoded into latent tokens ($z_s$). These are then concatenated with the video latent tokens ($z_v^{(t)}$) along the frame dimension. This in-context conditioning strategy allows anchor views to guide world synthesis without requiring modifications to the base model's architecture. 3D Rotary Positional Embeddings (RoPE) are used to assign distinct frame-axis positions to anchor views, differentiating them spatially.
- **View Pose Injection**: The 6-DoF camera poses for both generated video frames and anchor views are encoded into embeddings ($z_{pose}$). These embeddings are spatially broadcast and added to the visual tokens. This mechanism couples visual information with spatial poses, enabling the model to distinguish anchor views based on their 3D locations and associate the generated egocentric trajectory with the appropriate local constraints.
- **Text-Driven Anchor-View Evolution**: Each anchor view is associated with a localized evolution prompt ($t_i$) describing temporal scene changes. These prompts are injected via cross-attention. To maintain the locality of dynamic instructions, an attention mask restricts text prompt interaction only to the generated video tokens and their corresponding anchor-view tokens. This prevents interference between different anchor views' evolution instructions.

#### 3.4. Progressive Multi-Stage Training Strategy

AnchorWorld employs a four-stage training strategy:
- **Stage I: TPV Action Training**: Model learns action-conditioned generation from large-scale third-person videos, focusing on full-body motion supervision from external viewpoints. (Data: 200K internal videos + 101K MultiCamVideo)
- **Stage II: FPV Action Training**: Model adapts to first-person videos, aligning camera parameters with the human head perspective. (Data: 100K Ego-Exo4D + LEMMA samples)
- **Stage III: Static Anchor-View Customization**: Model learns pose-aware anchor-view conditioning for consistent egocentric roaming in static scenes. (Data: 25K filtered Ego-Exo4D + LEMMA samples)
- **Stage IV: Dynamic Anchor-View Evolution**: Dynamic data with evolution descriptions are introduced to model text-driven local state changes. (Data: 25K static + 10K dynamic filtered samples)

This progressive strategy allows each component to build upon a stable, pre-established base. Data for egocentric stages are sourced from Ego-Exo4D and LEMMA, utilizing GVHMR for 3D human motion and anchor-view pose estimation in a shared coordinate system.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

AnchorWorld was evaluated across various scenarios, including egocentric, synthetic Unreal Engine (UE), and real-world captured scenes, and compared against adapted state-of-the-art baselines.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Results

- **Overall Performance**: AnchorWorld consistently achieved superior results in scene consistency, camera accuracy, and text alignment across all test scenarios (Egocentric Static, UE Static CineScene, Egocentric Dynamic), while maintaining comparable visual quality according to VBench scores.
- **Action Accuracy**: Baselines like PlayerOne, which rely on incomplete supervision from FPVs, exhibited weaker motion control. AnchorWorld, leveraging hybrid-view training and projection-based control, demonstrated improved camera accuracy, measured by Absolute Translation Error (ATE), Relative Translation Error (RTE), and Relative Rotation Error (RRE).
- **Scene Consistency**: AnchorWorld achieved higher scores in GIM-based Mat. Pix., CLIP-V, PSNR, SSIM, and lower LPIPS, indicating better scene consistency compared to baselines such as PlayerOne (lacking scene input) and CaM-UE (trained on limited data). This indicates its ability to maintain spatial coherence despite viewpoint changes.
- **Dynamic Evolution**: In dynamic scene scenarios, AnchorWorld showed a pronounced advantage in text alignment, measured by VideoAlign-TA, due to its explicit support for state evolution control via evolution prompts. Baselines adapted for dynamic scenes performed less effectively.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Results

- **Egocentric Control and Scene Consistency**: Visual comparisons indicated that AnchorWorld generated videos with more accurate egocentric human motion control, better scene consistency under significant viewpoint changes, and dynamic scene evolution that adheres to evolution prompts. For example, CaM-Ego primarily controlled viewpoint changes without body motion, and PlayerOne-Scene exhibited limitations in motion accuracy.
- **Generalization**: The method demonstrated generalization to out-of-distribution UE scenes and real-world captured scenarios, even when there was limited or no overlap between the initial ego-view and the provided anchor views.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies validated the effectiveness of key design choices:
- **Hybrid-View Action Control**: Removing Stage I (TPV training) or the projection-based control design led to degraded action accuracy and weakened scene consistency in later stages, highlighting their importance for robust spatial perception.
- **Anchor-View Customization**: The absence of anchor-view pose injection or 3D RoPE resulted in poorer scene consistency, confirming their roles in providing pose-aware view conditioning and distinguishing multiple anchor views.
- **Multi-Stage Training**: The progressive multi-stage training strategy was validated to be more effective than joint training of corresponding stages.

#### 4.4. Specialized Capabilities

- **Out-of-Sight Scene Evolution**: AnchorWorld demonstrated the ability to infer and generate dynamic scene elements that were initially outside the egocentric view but present in an anchor view. The model adjusted the state of these elements based on the timing of the viewpoint transition induced by the agent's action (e.g., revealing a person standing up at different frames depending on when the camera turned).
- **Spatial Pose Awareness**: Experiments involving flipped human and anchor-view poses showed that the model correctly understood spatial pose relationships and retrieved appropriate appearance details when poses overlapped, indicating a reliance on 3D pose cues rather than solely 2D visual overlap.
- **Evolution Prompt Control**: By modifying the evolution prompt for the same anchor-view image, AnchorWorld could generate different dynamic evolutions, demonstrating flexible control over local scene changes.
- **Scene Coherence Challenges**: Experiments involving an anchor-view image with a mismatched style or a mirrored world space due to flipped poses and anchor views revealed that the model struggled to generate coherent results. This suggests that the model's internal representation requires a continuous and spatially consistent world geometry.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

AnchorWorld introduces a framework that addresses key challenges in egocentric world simulation, offering advancements in both embodied interaction and scene customization.

- **Novel Task Formulation**: The work formally defines "world-customizable embodied egocentric simulation," providing a new perspective on integrating complex human actions with dynamic, controllable environments.
- **Unified and Flexible Framework**: AnchorWorld provides a unified framework that synergistically combines full-body human motion as a natural embodied action control modality with pose-associated anchor views for explicit world customization. This integration allows for more realistic and interactive experiences in virtual environments.
- **Enhanced Controllability and Consistency**: By leveraging hybrid-view training (TPV and FPV) and a projection-based action control mechanism, the framework achieves accurate egocentric human action control and strong spatial awareness. The anchor-view customization, supported by in-context priors, view pose injection, and text-driven evolution prompts, enables geometrically consistent and dynamically evolving worlds.
- **Improved Generalization**: The demonstrated ability to generalize to out-of-distribution scenes, including those with limited or no overlap between the initial ego-view and anchor views, suggests robustness for real-world applications.
- **Impact on Embodied AI and VR**: AnchorWorld's capabilities could significantly contribute to the development of more sophisticated embodied AI agents that can navigate and interact with dynamically changing environments, and to more immersive and flexible virtual reality experiences where users can intuitively customize and influence their surroundings.

**Limitations Acknowledged by the Authors**:
Despite its contributions, the authors identify several limitations:
- **Long-Term Exploration**: The current work primarily focuses on short video clips. Extending the framework to support long-horizon world exploration and real-time autoregressive interaction with persistent environmental state updates remains a challenge.
- **Open-World Generalization**: The training data is currently focused on a constrained set of scenarios. Expanding to open-world datasets is necessary for broader applicability.
- **Diverse Dynamic Scenarios**: The current implementation primarily focuses on human-related activities with globally consistent evolution descriptions. Future work aims to support more diverse dynamic scenarios and anchor-specific dynamic controls, incorporating natural world evolution.
- **Inconsistent Scene Details**: The base model's VAE compression ratio may lead to a loss of fine-grained texture details in complex scenes, resulting in inconsistent visual elements.
- **Blurry Results**: Fast motion in training data (egocentric videos) can introduce motion blur, which may propagate to generated frames, affecting visual quality, especially for details like hands.

These limitations indicate avenues for future research to further enhance the capabilities of embodied egocentric world simulation.
