# EDGE-GRPO: Entropy-Driven GRPO with Guided Error Correction for Advantage Diversity

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Beihang University developed EDGE-GRPO, an algorithm that combines Guided Error Correction for response diversity and Entropy-Driven Advantage for signal refinement to overcome the "advantage collapse problem" in Group Relative Policy Optimization for LLMs. This approach yields over 20% performance gains on mathematical reasoning benchmarks while requiring only 1K training samples for competitive results.

## Problem

- The "advantage collapse problem" in Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) for LLMs, where sparse, rule-based reward functions lead to uniform rewards and zero advantages within sampled response groups.
- This collapse halts effective policy gradient updates, severely limiting sample efficiency and hindering performance on challenging reasoning problems.
- Existing methods for enhancing response diversity or augmenting training signals are often inconclusive, insufficient for limited capacity models, or miscalibrated with respect to response correctness.

## Method

- ==Guided Error Correction (GEC)== enhances response diversity by strategically introducing external guidance (reflection prompts, direct answer injection, or reference solution replacement) for incorrect responses within a group.
- ==Entropy-Driven Advantage (EDA)== refines advantage signals by scaling GRPO advantages with policy entropy, providing finer-grained differentiation among responses.
- The EDGE-GRPO algorithm integrates GEC and EDA to simultaneously enhance response diversity and ==advantage diversity==, fundamentally alleviating the ==advantage collapse problem==.

## Results

- EDGE-GRPO consistently achieved over **20%** performance improvement compared to baseline Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B and Qwen2.5-Math-7B models on mathematical reasoning benchmarks, with the 7B model reaching **53.21%** average accuracy on DeepScaleR-Hard-1K.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both GEC and EDA contribute to performance gains, and their combination effectively mitigates the advantage collapse problem by maintaining consistently higher intra-group advantage variance.
- The method demonstrated exceptional data efficiency, achieving competitive results using only **1K** training samples for one epoch, significantly less than other state-of-the-art open-source models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Relying solely on model reflection for self-correction is often ineffective for LLMs, especially those with limited capacity, and spontaneous reflection can even correlate with lower accuracy.
> - Policy entropy can be miscalibrated, with models showing overconfidence in incorrect answers and underconfidence in correct ones, making simple entropy-based adjustments insufficient.
> - A dual intervention strategy, addressing both response generation diversity and the granularity of advantage signals, is necessary to effectively overcome the advantage collapse problem in GRPO.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research operates within the rapidly evolving field of Large Language Models (LLMs) and their application to complex reasoning tasks, such as mathematics and coding. A key area of recent progress involves enhancing LLM reasoning through reinforcement learning (RL). Specifically, the paper focuses on the **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)** algorithm (Shao et al. 2024), which has gained attention for its ability to reduce resource consumption during training while improving reasoning performance, primarily by foregoing a value function and calculating relative advantages within sampled response groups.

However, the authors identify a critical challenge within GRPO: the "advantage collapse problem." This occurs when the reliance on sparse, rule-based reward functions leads to identical rewards for all responses within a group (e.g., all correct or all incorrect). Consequently, the calculated advantages become zero, effectively halting effective policy gradient updates and severely limiting sample efficiency, especially for challenging problems.

Existing research has attempted to address this problem from two main angles:
1. **Enhancing response diversity:** This often involves techniques like enforcing model reflection to encourage varied outputs, particularly for incorrect answers (e.g., Wang et al. 2025a; Wan et al. 2025). The current paper critically analyzes the limitations of this approach, finding that reflection's effectiveness is often inconclusive or even detrimental, especially for models with limited capacity.
2. **Augmenting the training signal with internal feedback:** This involves incorporating measures like semantic entropy or policy entropy into advantage calculations to differentiate between responses (e.g., Chen et al. 2025; Cheng et al. 2025). The paper argues that these methods often lack a fine-grained understanding of the relationship between response correctness and policy entropy, either blindly pursuing low entropy for accuracy or high entropy for exploration.

EDGE-GRPO positions itself as an advancement that addresses the fundamental limitations observed in these prior approaches. By first conducting a detailed analysis of reflection's actual efficacy and the fine-grained miscalibration of policy entropy, the authors propose a dual-pronged solution that directly tackles both response diversity and signal diversity with a more nuanced, "entropy-driven" approach. This work contributes to making RL-based LLM training more robust, efficient, and effective, particularly for complex reasoning tasks where existing methods struggle with sparse rewards and diverse response generation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **effectively mitigate the "advantage collapse problem" encountered in the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) algorithm** when applied to enhancing LLM reasoning. The authors are motivated by the observation that sparse, rule-based reward functions in GRPO often lead to all responses within a group receiving identical rewards, resulting in zero advantages and thus ineffective policy gradient updates. This phenomenon severely limits the efficiency of sample utilization and hinders performance improvement, especially on challenging problems.

The specific motivations and objectives stem from a critical analysis of existing approaches:
- **Addressing the limitations of model reflection:** The paper aims to quantitatively demonstrate that relying solely on model reflection for enhancing response diversity is often ineffective. Initial experiments reveal that spontaneous reflection often correlates with lower accuracy, and even forced reflection yields minimal improvements, particularly for models with limited capacity. This motivates the need for a more robust method to ensure response diversity, especially for difficult problems.
- **Refining the use of policy entropy:** The authors aim to challenge the simplistic assumptions made by prior works regarding policy entropy. They identify a "misconception" where incorrect responses can exhibit surprisingly low entropy (overconfidence), and correct responses can have high entropy (underconfidence). This fine-grained miscalibration undermines crude entropy-based advantage augmentation. The objective is to develop a method that aligns policy entropy with response correctness in a more nuanced way to provide a better guiding signal.
- **Developing a "simple and effective" algorithm:** Ultimately, the goal is to propose a novel algorithm, EDGE-GRPO, that integrates solutions at both the response level (to ensure diversity) and the signal level (to refine advantages) to fundamentally alleviate advantage collapse, leading to significant and consistent performance gains in LLM reasoning with high sample efficiency.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The EDGE-GRPO (Entropy-Driven GRPO with Guided Error Correction) algorithm is designed to address the advantage collapse problem by intervening at two crucial levels: enhancing response diversity and augmenting signal diversity.

#### 3.1. Initial Investigations

Before proposing their solution, the authors conducted thorough investigations into the limitations of existing methods:
- **Response-level: Limitations of Reflection:** They quantitatively analyzed model reflection by identifying reflection-related keywords in responses. They found that spontaneous reflections often exhibit low accuracy (Figure 2, upper part), suggesting ineffectiveness. While distillation from large-scale models or fine-tuning on high-quality chain-of-thought data (like S1K) can improve reflection accuracy, forcing models to reflect on incorrect answers still yielded very limited accuracy improvements (typically below 5% for most models, Figure 2, lower right). This led to the conclusion that relying solely on self-correction is insufficient for models with limited capacity and that external guidance is needed.
- **Signal-level: Policy Entropy Analysis:** The policy entropy of each generated chain-of-thought response was calculated as an uncertainty measure (Equation 3, 4). While overall, correct responses tended to have lower average entropy (higher confidence) than incorrect ones (Table 3), a fine-grained analysis revealed significant miscalibration. Approximately half of incorrect responses showed lower-than-average entropy (overconfidence), and nearly one-third of correct responses exhibited higher-than-average entropy (underconfidence) (Figure 3, right; Figure 6). This indicated that a simple high/low entropy strategy was inadequate, and a more nuanced, fine-grained approach was required to align policy entropy with response correctness.

#### 3.2. EDGE-GRPO Framework

Based on these insights, EDGE-GRPO integrates two core components:

##### 3.2.1. Guided Error Correction (GEC)

GEC is a response-level intervention designed to enhance response diversity and ensure that each group of generated answers contains both positive and negative examples, even when the model struggles with a question. This directly combats the issue of all responses within a group being uniformly incorrect, which leads to zero advantages. For every incorrect response generated by the policy model, GEC performs one of three operations with predefined probabilities:
- **Prompt and Regenerate (P=0.5):** A simple reflection prompt (e.g., "Wait! Something is wrong here.") is provided, and the model is prompted to regenerate its answer. This allows for a chance of self-correction while maintaining model autonomy for a majority of cases.
- **Direct Answer Injection (P=0.25):** The reflection prompt is combined with the correct answer directly provided to the model. This offers explicit guidance, moving beyond pure self-correction.
- **Reference Solution Replacement (P=0.25):** The incorrect response is entirely replaced with an external reference solution. This is the strongest form of intervention, guaranteeing a correct example in the group.

By strategically introducing these external solutions, GEC ensures a diverse set of responses, maintaining non-zero advantages and providing effective training signals.

##### 3.2.2. Entropy-Driven Advantage (EDA)

EDA is a signal-level mechanism that utilizes policy entropy to augment advantage diversity, enabling finer-grained differentiation among responses that might otherwise receive uniform rewards. This addresses the miscalibration observed in the initial analysis.
For each response `O_i` and its calculated policy entropy `P_i`, EDA performs the following:
1. **Entropy Scaling:** The policy entropy `P_i` is scaled relative to the average entropy within its group: `P_i_hat = P_i / mean({P_1..., P_G})` (Equation 6).
2. **Advantage Adjustment:** The initial GRPO advantage `A_i` (derived from the difference between the response's reward `r_i` and the group mean reward) is then adjusted using the scaled entropy: `A_i_hat = A_i / P_i_hat` (Equation 7).

The effect of this division by scaled entropy is critical:
- **Confident and Correct (low `P_i_hat`):** If a response is correct (positive `A_i`) and the model is confident (low `P_i_hat`), its advantage `A_i_hat` is amplified, rewarding this desirable behavior more strongly.
- **Incorrect but Overconfident (low `P_i_hat`):** If a response is incorrect (negative `A_i`) but the model is overly confident (low `P_i_hat`), its penalty (negative advantage) is amplified, discouraging stubborn errors.
- **Correct but Uncertain (high `P_i_hat`):** If a response is correct but the model is uncertain (high `P_i_hat`), its positive advantage is attenuated, acknowledging correctness but subtly encouraging more confident future outputs.
- **Incorrect but Uncertain (high `P_i_hat`):** If a response is incorrect and the model is uncertain (high `P_i_hat`), its negative advantage is attenuated, tolerating exploration and avoiding excessive punishment for uncertain incorrect attempts.

This fine-grained adjustment significantly increases the diversity of advantage signals, allowing the model to distinguish more effectively between responses and further alleviating advantage collapse. The combination of GEC and EDA creates a robust training framework that addresses the core issues in GRPO from both data and signal perspectives.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate the effectiveness and superiority of the EDGE-GRPO algorithm across multiple challenging mathematical reasoning benchmarks (AIME24, AMC, MATH500, Minerva, OlympiadBench).

- **Overall Performance and Significant Gains:** EDGE-GRPO consistently achieved substantial performance improvements over baseline models. For instance, it showed over 20% improvement compared to the base Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B and Qwen2.5-Math-7B models. On the DeepScaleR-Random-1K dataset, EDGE-GRPO-1.5B achieved an average accuracy of 48.08%, and EDGE-GRPO-7B reached 49.30% (Table 1). When trained on the more challenging DeepScaleR-Hard-1K dataset, the 7B model's performance further amplified, reaching 53.21% average accuracy (Table 2), validating the method's effectiveness on difficult data.

- **Ablation Studies and Advantage Variance:**
 - **Limited Impact of Forced Reflection:** Ablation studies showed that merely forcing reflection ("+Force-R") in the vanilla GRPO algorithm offered only limited performance improvements (Tables 1 and 2), supporting the initial analysis that self-reflection alone is often insufficient.
 - **Contribution of Entropy-Driven Advantage (EDA):** Adding EDA ("+Force-R & EDA") to the GRPO framework led to noticeable performance gains. By providing finer-grained rewards and penalties based on entropy, EDA increased the diversity of advantage signals, aiding in exploration and learning.
 - **Full Synergy with Guided Error Correction (GEC):** The full EDGE-GRPO algorithm, incorporating both GEC and EDA, yielded the most significant performance boosts. GEC's role in enhancing response diversity ensured that non-zero advantages could be generated for a wider range of samples, which then allowed EDA to apply its nuanced entropy-driven adjustments effectively.
 - **Mitigation of Advantage Collapse:** Crucially, the visualization of advantage variance during training (Figure 5) demonstrated that EDGE-GRPO maintained a consistently higher level of intra-group advantage variance compared to vanilla GRPO and its forced reflection variant. This directly confirms that the algorithm successfully mitigates the "advantage collapse problem."

- **Efficiency and Comparison to Other Models:** A particularly striking finding is EDGE-GRPO's exceptional data efficiency. The method achieved competitive and even excellent performance using only **1K training samples** for one epoch (Figure 1, Tables 1, 2, 6). This is remarkable, as many other state-of-the-art open-source models (e.g., Oat-Zero, SimpleRL-Zoo, Eurus-2, OpenReasoner-Zero) require orders of magnitude more training data (e.g., 5.7K to 800K samples) to achieve similar or slightly higher results (Table 6). This highlights that EDGE-GRPO can achieve strong mathematical reasoning capabilities with significantly reduced data and computational requirements. Performance comparison during training steps (Figure 7) further illustrated that EDGE-GRPO consistently outperformed baselines throughout the learning process.

In summary, the main findings confirm that EDGE-GRPO successfully addresses the advantage collapse problem by simultaneously enhancing response diversity through Guided Error Correction and refining advantage signals through Entropy-Driven Advantage. This dual approach leads to substantial and consistent performance improvements across various reasoning tasks, all while demonstrating impressive sample efficiency.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The EDGE-GRPO algorithm presents a significant advancement in the field of Large Language Model (LLM) reasoning, particularly for models trained with reinforcement learning. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Enhanced LLM Reasoning Capabilities:** By effectively mitigating the "advantage collapse problem," EDGE-GRPO makes the GRPO algorithm a more robust and reliable method for training LLMs on complex reasoning tasks. The consistent and substantial performance gains (over 20% improvement) across multiple mathematical benchmarks demonstrate a tangible step forward in pushing the boundaries of LLM's problem-solving abilities.
- **Improved Sample Efficiency:** Perhaps one of the most impactful aspects of EDGE-GRPO is its ability to achieve competitive performance with significantly less training data (only 1K samples). This dramatically reduces the computational cost and data curation effort associated with training high-performing reasoning models. For resource-constrained environments or applications where high-quality labeled data is scarce, this efficiency is invaluable. It suggests that highly effective reasoning models can be developed and fine-tuned more rapidly and economically.
- **Foundational Insights for RL-based LLM Training:** The detailed analysis of the limitations of model reflection and the fine-grained miscalibration of policy entropy provides crucial insights for future research. It challenges common assumptions and guides the development of more sophisticated RL training strategies for LLMs. The findings suggest that simplistic approaches to self-correction or entropy regularization are insufficient, paving the way for more nuanced mechanisms like GEC and EDA.
- **Towards More Robust and Reliable AI:** The ability to provide more effective training signals, especially for challenging problems where models initially struggle, contributes to creating more robust and less "stubborn" AI systems. By penalizing overconfidence in incorrect answers and appropriately rewarding confidence in correct ones, EDGE-GRPO helps LLMs become better calibrated, leading to more trustworthy reasoning outputs.
- **Broader Applicability:** While demonstrated on mathematical reasoning, the underlying principles of enhancing response diversity and refining advantage signals using fine-grained entropy could be applicable to other domains where LLMs are employed for step-by-step reasoning, such as code generation, scientific problem-solving, or complex decision-making.
- **Open-Source Contribution:** The availability of the algorithm at a GitHub repository (https://github.com/ZhangXJ199/EDGE-GRPO) is a valuable contribution to the research community. It encourages reproducibility, further development, and broader adoption of the proposed techniques, accelerating progress in the field.

In conclusion, EDGE-GRPO offers a practical and theoretically grounded solution to a critical challenge in RL-based LLM training. Its focus on efficiency, effectiveness, and nuanced understanding of model behavior positions it as an important contribution that could influence the development of the next generation of reasoning-capable LLMs.
