# WorldRFT: Latent World Model Planning with Reinforcement Fine-Tuning for Autonomous Driving

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the Institute of Automation, Chinese Academy of Sciences, and Li Auto developed WorldRFT, a framework that reorients latent world models for autonomous driving towards planning, enhancing 3D spatial awareness and safety through geometric priors and reinforcement learning. The approach achieves state-of-the-art performance, reducing the collision rate by 83% on nuScenes and nearly matching LiDAR-based methods in closed-loop simulation using only vision input.

## Problem

- Traditional end-to-end autonomous driving systems rely on extensive and costly 3D perception annotations, which are resource-intensive.
- Existing latent world models, while annotation-free, generate generic representations with limited 3D spatial awareness and inefficient planning interaction mechanisms, struggling with fine-grained local structures.
- Current imitation learning-based world models lack explicit safety objectives, making them passive in collision avoidance and potentially unsafe in novel or challenging scenarios.

## Method

- A ==Spatial-aware World Encoder (SWE)== integrates a frozen ==Visual Geometry Grounded Transformer (VGGT)== to inject multi-view consistent 3D geometric priors into latent representations from raw 2D images, without explicit 3D annotations.
- A ==Hierarchical Planning Refinement (HPR)== module decomposes planning into parallel subtasks (target region, spatial path, temporal trajectory) and iteratively refines them using local-aware deformable convolution, conditioned by target uncertainty.
- **WorldRFT** uses Reinforcement Learning Fine-tuning (RFT) with a ==collision-aware sparse reward function== and ==Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)==, enabling active collision avoidance rather than passive imitation.

## Results

- Achieved state-of-the-art performance on the nuScenes open-loop benchmark, reducing L2 error by **21%** and the collision rate by **83%** (from **0.30%** to **0.05%**) compared to the LAW baseline.
- Demonstrated state-of-the-art vision-only performance on the NavSim closed-loop benchmark with a PDMS of **87.8**, nearly matching LiDAR-based approaches and achieving the highest Drivable Area Compliance (**96.8%**).
- Ablation studies confirmed that VGGT spatial encoding, hierarchical planning refinement, local-aware iterative refinement, and safety-oriented reinforcement fine-tuning each contributed significantly to improved accuracy and collision avoidance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Fusing geometric foundation models (like VGGT) into latent world encoders effectively enhances 3D spatial awareness for planning without requiring costly 3D annotations.
> - Decomposing complex end-to-end planning into hierarchical, iteratively refined subtasks significantly improves both trajectory accuracy and the model's ability to extract relevant local features.
> - Actively optimizing for safety using reinforcement learning with collision-aware rewards enables latent world models to move beyond behavior cloning, leading to substantial reductions in collision rates.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper positions WorldRFT within the rapidly evolving landscape of end-to-end autonomous driving, particularly focusing on the "latent world model" paradigm.

Traditionally, end-to-end autonomous driving systems relied on multi-task architectures that integrate explicit perception modules (e.g., object detection, mapping, occupancy prediction) with planning. These "perception-based imitation learning (P-IL)" approaches, while successful, demand extensive and expensive 3D perception annotations.

A newer paradigm has emerged, utilizing **latent world models (WMs)**. These models learn unified scene representations from raw sensor inputs (like surround-view images) through temporal self-supervised learning, aiming for an "perception annotation-free" approach. This promises to reduce development complexity and costs. Early latent WMs like LAW and World4Drive have shown promise in understanding future scene states and supporting basic path planning.

However, the authors identify critical limitations in existing latent WMs:
1. **Lack of 3D spatial awareness:** Reconstruction-oriented learning often yields generic representations that lack the precise 3D understanding crucial for trajectory planning.
2. **Inefficient planning interaction mechanisms:** Current models struggle to extract fine-grained local structures essential for detailed planning, often using single global queries that dilute attention.
3. **Limited safety awareness:** Most imitation learning-based WM approaches focus on minimizing deviations from expert trajectories without explicit mechanisms for active collision avoidance or understanding underlying safety principles in novel scenarios.

WorldRFT directly addresses these three challenges. It aims to *align latent representation learning more closely with planning requirements* and *enhance safety-critical performance through reinforcement learning*. By doing so, it attempts to bridge the gap between the efficiency of self-supervised latent world models and the robustness and safety demands of real-world autonomous driving. The introduction of geometric priors and reinforcement learning fine-tuning represents a significant step towards developing truly planning-oriented, safe, and scalable end-to-end autonomous driving systems that are less reliant on costly manual annotations. This work pushes the boundary of world model applicability beyond mere scene reconstruction towards reliable, safety-aware decision-making.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of WorldRFT is to develop a planning-oriented latent world model framework for end-to-end autonomous driving that overcomes the limitations of existing reconstruction-oriented latent world models. This overarching goal is broken down into specific aims, motivated by the identified challenges:

1. **Improve 3D spatial awareness in latent representations:** Existing world models generate generic representations with limited 3D spatial understanding, which is critical for accurate trajectory planning. WorldRFT aims to infuse robust 3D geometric information into these representations without requiring explicit 3D annotations.
2. **Develop more efficient and refined planning interaction mechanisms:** Current models employ coarse global planning queries that fail to capture the local structures vital for fine-grained planning. The objective is to design a mechanism that enables better comprehension and extraction of planning-relevant features from the latent space.
3. **Enhance safety-critical planning capabilities beyond imitation learning:** Perception-based and imitation learning approaches often lack explicit safety objectives, making them passive in collision avoidance. WorldRFT seeks to transition from passive behavior cloning to active collision avoidance by integrating safety-aware optimization.
4. **Achieve state-of-the-art performance in both open-loop and closed-loop benchmarks:** The ultimate objective is to demonstrate that the proposed innovations lead to superior performance in trajectory accuracy and, crucially, safety metrics across standard autonomous driving benchmarks.

In essence, the motivation stems from the recognition that while latent world models offer a promising annotation-free paradigm, their current formulation is suboptimal for the nuances of autonomous driving planning, especially concerning safety and precise spatial interaction. WorldRFT is designed to realign the learning objectives of world models with the practical demands of autonomous driving.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

WorldRFT is structured around three synergistic modules: a Spatial-aware World Encoder (SWE), a Hierarchical Planning Refinement (HPR) module, and a Reinforcement Learning Fine-tuning (RFT) phase.

#### 3.1. Spatial-aware World Encoder (SWE)

This module is responsible for constructing 3D spatially-aware latent world representations from raw surround-view RGB images.
- **Basic Visual Feature Encoding:** It first extracts features from multi-view images using an image backbone. Similar to previous work (World4Drive), it incorporates a vision-language foundation model, **Grounded-SAM**, for semantic supervised learning using a semantic loss (L_sem) to enrich representations with semantic understanding.
- **3D Spatial Encoding with VGGT:** To overcome the weak 3D spatial understanding of current WMs, SWE integrates **VGGT (Visual Geometry Grounded Transformer)**. VGGT, a geometric foundation model, provides multi-view consistent geometric priors from 2D images. A *frozen* VGGT acts as a 3D spatial encoder, extracting 3D tokens. These 3D tokens are then fused with the 2D visual features (from the image backbone) using a lightweight cross-attention layer. This process creates a unified, spatially-aware latent world representation (W_t^latent) without requiring explicit 3D inputs or annotations.

#### 3.2. Hierarchical Planning Refinement (HPR)

This module addresses inefficient planning interaction by decomposing complex end-to-end planning into three parallel, hierarchically distinct subtasks and refining them iteratively.
- **Unified Query Interaction Framework:** Dedicated queries (Q_target, Q_path, Q_traj) are initialized for each subtask. These queries first aggregate planning-relevant features from W_t^latent via cross-attention. Subsequently, they undergo self-attention to facilitate inter-task communication and mutual awareness of planning intentions.
- **Target Region Localization:** Instead of predicting a deterministic target point, WorldRFT models targets as probabilistic regions using **Laplace distributions**. This captures the inherent uncertainty of valid target points. The predicted parameters (mean μ and scale b) define the region, with 'b' reflecting scene complexity and guiding feature fusion in subsequent refinement. Trained with negative log-likelihood loss (L_Laplace-NLL).
- **Spatial Path Planning:** This subtask generates a time-independent spatial path (T_path) composed of N points sampled at fixed spatial intervals (e.g., 2 meters). This focuses the model solely on spatial consistency, decoded via an MLP from Q_path.
- **Temporal Trajectory Prediction:** This module predicts future temporal trajectories (T_traj) for T timesteps (e.g., 0.5-second intervals). It decodes both spatial and temporal dynamics from Q_traj using an MLP.
- **Local-aware Iterative Refinement:** This key mechanism iteratively refines the initial planning outputs over K iterations. For each iteration:
 1. Current planning information (target region, path, trajectory) is encoded into a unified state representation.
 2. Trajectory points are projected onto the latent space feature map, and **deformable convolution** adaptively samples local features around these projected positions from W_t^latent.
 3. Local features are fused with global planning intentions (Q_traj) and conditioned by the uncertainty scale parameter 'b' from target region modeling.
 4. Prediction heads generate incremental trajectory offsets, which are then used to update the trajectory via residual connections. This ensures fine-grained local adjustments while maintaining global consistency.
- **Training Loss:** The HPR module is pretrained with a total loss (L_Total) combining L_sem (semantic), L_rec (reconstruction loss for future world prediction, enabling self-supervised learning), L_target (for Laplace distribution of target region), and L_traj (L1 loss for spatial path and temporal trajectory against expert demonstrations).

#### 3.3. Reinforcement Learning Fine-tuning (RFT)

This phase enhances safety-critical planning by optimizing explicit safety objectives.
- **Collision-aware Reward Design:** The RL framework uses a sparse reward function. A penalty of -1 is given for any collision between the ego vehicle and surrounding agents along the trajectory, and 0 otherwise. This directly incentivizes collision avoidance.
- **Gaussianized-Trajectory Modeling:** To enable RL, which typically works with probabilistic policies, the predicted trajectories are recast as Gaussian distributions. The pre-trained trajectory serves as the mean (μ_θ), and an auxiliary variance network estimates the covariance (Σ_θ).
- **Policy Optimization with GRPO:** WorldRFT employs **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)**, a method that estimates advantage functions based on relative rewards within sampled trajectory groups. The policy is optimized using a clipped objective function (similar to PPO) with a KL divergence penalty to keep the updated policy close to the reference (pre-trained) policy. This ensures stable learning and prevents aggressive deviations from learned safe behaviors.
- **RFT Training Loss:** The RL loss (L_RL) combines the GRPO objective function (negative) with a KL divergence term to regularize policy updates towards the reference model and a reference loss term for aligning mean trajectories. A maximum entropy loss is also used to prevent premature convergence.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

WorldRFT demonstrates state-of-the-art (SOTA) performance on both open-loop (nuScenes) and closed-loop (NavSim) autonomous driving benchmarks, with significant improvements in safety metrics.

#### 4.1. nuScenes Benchmark (Open-Loop Evaluation)

- **SOTA Performance:** WorldRFT achieves SOTA performance among perception annotation-free approaches.
- **L2 Error Reduction:** It reduces the average displacement error (L2) by 21% (from 0.61m to 0.48m) compared to the strong baseline LAW (a perception-free self-supervised learning method).
- **Collision Rate Reduction:** Most notably, it achieves an impressive **83% reduction in collision rate** (from 0.30% to 0.05%) compared to LAW. This is the lowest collision rate among *all* evaluated methods, including perception-based approaches, highlighting the superior safety performance of WorldRFT's planning-oriented design.
- With ego status (vehicle's current speed, acceleration), WorldRFT further reduces the collision rate to 0.04% and L2 error to 0.33m, demonstrating its robust performance.

#### 4.2. NavSim Benchmark (Closed-Loop Evaluation)

- **SOTA Performance for Vision-Only:** WorldRFT achieves SOTA performance for vision-only solutions with a PDMS (Performance, Drivability, Metrics Score) of 87.8.
- **Approaching LiDAR-based SOTA:** It significantly improves upon the baseline LAW (84.6 PDMS) and nearly matches the performance of DiffusionDrive (88.1 PDMS), which utilizes LiDAR inputs.
- **Safety-Critical Metrics Improvement:** WorldRFT shows notable improvements across all safety-critical metrics: No At-fault Collision (NC) from 96.4 to 97.8, Time-to-Collision (TTC) from 88.7 to 94.0, and Drivable Area Compliance (DAC) from 95.4 to 96.8. The DAC score of 96.8 is the highest among all methods, including LiDAR-based approaches, underscoring the effectiveness of incorporating VGGT geometric priors for 3D spatial understanding without explicit depth supervision.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies (Key Insights)

- **VGGT Spatial Encoding:** Incorporating VGGT significantly improves both L2 error (7.7% reduction) and collision rate (37.5% reduction), validating its role in enhancing 3D spatial awareness.
- **Hierarchical Planning Tasks:** Decomposing planning into target region, spatial path, and temporal trajectory subtasks with refinement leads to substantial improvements (11.9% L2 reduction, 50% collision rate reduction), affirming the hypothesis that hierarchical decomposition aids feature extraction.
- **Local-aware Iterative Refinement:** This module reduces L2 error by 4.0% and collision rate by 16.7%, confirming its role in fine-grained trajectory adjustments for accuracy and safety.
- **Safety-Oriented Reinforcement Fine-tuning (RFT):** RFT dramatically reduces the collision rate by 66.7% (0.15% to 0.05%) with only a slight increase in L2 error. This highlights RFT's ability to prioritize safety by enabling active collision avoidance over passive behavior imitation. A comparison with Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) further reinforced that RFT's active learning of collision avoidance is superior for safety.
- **Probabilistic Target Region:** Modeling targets as probabilistic Laplace distributions significantly reduced both L2 error (9.4%) and collision rate (50%) compared to deterministic target prediction, effectively handling prediction uncertainty.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Results

Visualizations confirm that RFT-tuned trajectories proactively adjust to maintain safe distances from obstacles, contrasting with pre-trained trajectories that dangerously approach them. Attention maps show WorldRFT focusing more precisely on critical agents and planning-relevant regions, improving perception-planning integration.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

WorldRFT introduces a significant paradigm shift in latent world model research for autonomous driving by fundamentally reorienting these models from reconstruction-focused learning to planning-oriented learning. Its contributions have several key implications and potential impacts:

1. **Bridging the Gap between World Models and Real-world Safety:** The most significant contribution is demonstrating how to effectively bridge the gap between the theoretical promise of perception annotation-free latent world models and the stringent safety requirements of real-world autonomous driving. By directly tackling the misalignment between representation learning and planning, WorldRFT produces models that are not only efficient but also remarkably safer.

2. **Enhanced Safety through Active Collision Avoidance:** The introduction of safety-aware Reinforcement Learning Fine-tuning (RFT) with collision-aware rewards and GRPO represents a critical step beyond traditional imitation learning. This enables the autonomous vehicle to actively learn collision avoidance strategies rather than merely mimicking expert behavior, which is crucial for handling novel and safety-critical scenarios that may not be present in demonstration data. The dramatic reduction in collision rates (up to 83% on nuScenes) is a powerful validation of this approach.

3. **Improved Spatial Understanding without 3D Annotations:** The Spatial-aware World Encoder (SWE) leveraging VGGT demonstrates an effective method to infuse robust 3D geometric priors into latent representations using only 2D images. This innovation significantly enhances the model's spatial awareness, which is vital for precise trajectory planning, without incurring the high costs and complexity associated with 3D perception annotations. This could democratize advanced autonomous driving research by lowering data labeling barriers.

4. **Refined and Interpretable Planning:** The Hierarchical Planning Refinement (HPR) module, with its decomposition of planning into target region localization, spatial path, and temporal trajectory prediction, leads to more robust and interpretable planning. The probabilistic target region modeling effectively captures uncertainty, allowing for more cautious and adaptive decision-making, which is a key characteristic of human-like driving.

5. **Competitiveness with LiDAR-based Systems using Camera-Only Input:** Achieving performance comparable to, and in some metrics surpassing, methods that utilize LiDAR inputs, especially in closed-loop simulations like NavSim, with only camera-only input, is a remarkable achievement. This highlights the potential for developing highly capable and cost-effective autonomous driving systems that rely primarily on vision sensors.

6. **Potential for Real-World Deployment:** The combination of perception annotation-free learning, enhanced safety, and strong performance on both open-loop and closed-loop benchmarks makes WorldRFT a highly promising candidate for real-world autonomous driving deployment. By reducing reliance on extensive manual annotations and prioritizing safety, this work pushes the boundary towards scalable and safe end-to-end autonomous driving systems.

In summary, WorldRFT marks a significant advancement by synthesizing geometric priors, hierarchical planning, and reinforcement learning to create a latent world model that is intrinsically planning-oriented and safety-aware. This approach offers a compelling blueprint for the next generation of end-to-end autonomous driving systems.
