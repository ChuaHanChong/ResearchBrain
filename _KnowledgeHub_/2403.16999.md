# Visual CoT: Advancing Multi-Modal Language Models with a Comprehensive Dataset and Benchmark for Chain-of-Thought Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> This work introduces a Visual Chain-of-Thought (CoT) framework to enhance the interpretability and reasoning capabilities of Multi-Modal Large Language Models (MLLMs) by enabling them to dynamically focus on specific visual regions. It includes a large-scale dataset with bounding box annotations and textual reasoning steps, demonstrating improved performance in tasks requiring fine-grained visual attention and competitive results on general MLLM benchmarks.

## Problem

- Current Multi-Modal Large Language Models (MLLMs) often lack interpretability, operating as "black boxes" without revealing their reasoning steps.
- MLLMs struggle with complex visual inputs, particularly high-resolution images or small, crucial regions, due to fixed-grain processing.
- The application of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting for visual understanding in MLLMs remains largely underexplored, with existing approaches focusing mainly on text-level reasoning.

## Method

- A comprehensive ==Visual CoT dataset== was created with **438,000** question-answer pairs, each annotated with a bounding box highlighting the key image region, and **98,000** with detailed textual reasoning steps, spanning diverse domains (Text/Doc, Charts, Fine-Grained, General VQA, Relation Reasoning).
- A ==multi-turn processing pipeline called VisCoT== was developed, which first prompts the MLLM to predict a bounding box for the most informative region, then extracts ==localized image features== from that region, and finally integrates both global and local visual information for precise answer generation.
- The VisCoT framework leverages pre-trained vision encoders (ViT-L/14) and LLMs (Vicuna-7B/13B) and is fine-tuned in a ==two-stage process== using the Visual CoT dataset alongside other multimodal datasets.

## Results

- VisCoT consistently outperforms existing MLLMs like LLaVA-1.5 and SPHINX-13B on the Visual CoT benchmark, achieving up to an **8x** performance increase in doc/text-related tasks (e.g., SROIE dataset).
- Ablation studies confirm that using model-predicted bounding boxes significantly improves performance compared to baselines, and careful visual sampler design (expanded/centered cropping) further enhances accuracy.
- VisCoT demonstrates improved token efficiency, achieving higher accuracy at lower resolutions (e.g., **224x224** using double visual tokens) than standard pipelines at higher resolutions (e.g., **448x448**), and shows competitive performance on general MLLM and visual grounding benchmarks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit intermediate visual grounding through bounding box prediction enables MLLMs to dynamically focus on relevant image regions, mimicking human visual processing.
> - A multi-turn processing pipeline integrating both original and localized image features allows for more precise and contextually aware reasoning.
> - The benefits of Visual CoT are particularly pronounced for tasks requiring fine-grained attention to small objects or text, an area where standard MLLMs often struggle.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Artificial Intelligence has witnessed remarkable progress with the advent of Large Language Models (LLMs) such as GPT-4 and Gemini. A natural extension of this success has been the integration of visual understanding capabilities, leading to the development of Multi-Modal Large Language Models (MLLMs) like LLaVA, SPHINX, and Qwen-VL. These models typically operate by aligning visual tokens from images with linguistic modalities, enabling them to perform various tasks such as image captioning, visual question answering (VQA), and optical character recognition (OCR).

However, the authors identify several critical limitations within the current MLLM paradigm:
1. **Lack of Interpretability:** Most MLLMs function as "black boxes," providing answers without revealing their internal reasoning, which hinders trust and debugging.
2. **Struggle with Complex Visual Inputs:** MLLMs often face challenges when dealing with high-resolution images or identifying small, crucial regions that contain key information. They tend to process visual contexts in a fixed-grain manner, unlike humans who dynamically focus on relevant details.
3. **Limited Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Exploration:** While CoT prompting has significantly enhanced the reasoning abilities of LLMs by breaking down complex problems into intermediate steps, its application and benefits for visual understanding in MLLMs remain largely underexplored. Existing multimodal CoT approaches primarily focus on text-level reasoning.

This paper directly addresses these gaps by proposing a novel approach that imbues MLLMs with a "Visual Chain-of-Thought" capability. By focusing on local region identification and interpretable reasoning steps, this work aims to bridge the current limitations and propel MLLMs towards more human-like visual comprehension. It contributes to the growing trend of making AI models more transparent and capable of handling fine-grained visual details, complementing research in visual grounding, referential expressions, and explainable AI.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to enhance the interpretability and reasoning capabilities of Multi-Modal Large Language Models (MLLMs), particularly when dealing with complex or high-resolution visual inputs. The authors are driven by the observation that current MLLMs, despite their impressive performance in VQA tasks, lack the ability to show their "thought process" and struggle to dynamically focus on specific, critical regions within an image, a cognitive behavior characteristic of human visual understanding.

The key specific objectives and motivations are:

1. **Develop a Large-Scale Visual CoT Dataset:** The primary motivation is the severe lack of intermediate visual chain-of-thought supervision in existing VQA datasets. To address this, the authors aim to create a comprehensive dataset that not only contains question-answer pairs but also annotates them with bounding boxes highlighting key visual regions essential for answering the questions. A subset of this data is further annotated with detailed textual reasoning steps, aiming to explicitly guide MLLMs through a logical, step-by-step visual reasoning process. This dataset is crucial for training models to exhibit interpretable visual thoughts.
2. **Propose a Multi-Turn Processing Pipeline for Dynamic Visual Focusing:** Motivated by the fixed-grain processing of current MLLMs and the human ability to dynamically zoom into relevant areas, the authors seek to design a novel MLLM architecture. This pipeline should enable MLLMs to identify a specific region in an image, process its detailed information, and then integrate this localized context with the overall image understanding to generate more accurate and comprehensive answers. The goal is to mimic human-like efficient reasoning behaviors.
3. **Introduce a Benchmark for Evaluating Visual CoT Capabilities:** To systematically assess the efficacy of models incorporating visual CoT, a dedicated benchmark is proposed. This benchmark will evaluate MLLMs in scenarios specifically requiring the identification of local regions and the reasoning based on those regions, moving beyond simple VQA to more nuanced visual comprehension tasks.
4. **Enhance MLLM Interpretability and Reduce Hallucinations:** By forcing models to explicitly identify and reason about specific visual regions, the authors aim to increase the interpretability of MLLM outputs and potentially mitigate issues like inaccurate information and hallucinations, which are common in black-box generative models.

In summary, the motivation stems from a recognized gap in MLLM capabilities – the absence of interpretable, dynamic, and localized visual reasoning – which prevents them from truly mimicking human-like understanding of complex visual information. This work seeks to fill this gap by providing both the necessary data and a foundational architectural approach.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors' methodology centers on two main pillars: the creation of a novel, large-scale visual chain-of-thought (CoT) dataset and the development of a multi-turn processing pipeline called VisCoT to leverage this dataset.

#### 3.1. Visual CoT Dataset Creation

**Motivation:** The core motivation for the dataset is to provide explicit intermediate visual supervision for MLLMs, allowing them to learn to identify critical image regions and reason step-by-step.
**Size and Scope:** The Visual CoT dataset comprises 438,000 question-answer pairs. Crucially, each pair is annotated with an intermediate bounding box that highlights the key image region necessary for answering the question. Additionally, about 98,000 of these pairs are further annotated with detailed textual reasoning steps.
**Domain Diversity:** To ensure comprehensive training and evaluation, the dataset spans five distinct domains, integrating a diverse selection of data sources:
- **Text/Doc:** Addresses OCR and contextual understanding using datasets like TextVQA, TextCaps, DocVQA, DUDE, and SROIE.
- **Fine-Grained Understanding:** Focuses on discerning subtle visual differences, using Birds-200-2011.
- **Charts:** Cultivates the ability to interpret graphical data from InfographicsVQA.
- **General VQA:** Exposes models to a wide array of visual queries using Flickr30k and Visual7W.
- **Relation Reasoning:** Develops spatial and contextual awareness using Visual Spatial Reasoning (VSR), GQA, and Open Images.

**Data Generation Process:** The construction combines human and AI annotation:
- **Linguistic Annotations:** For datasets lacking QA pairs (e.g., TextCaps, Flickr30k), GPT-4 is employed to generate questions and answers that require focusing on small or specific objects/text. For GQA, GPT-4 is used to generate detailed reasoning steps from structured semantic representations.
- **Visual Annotations (Bounding Boxes):**
 - For Text/Doc domains, PaddleOCR is used to detect text regions, and bounding boxes are specified as regions containing words/sentences relevant to the answer.
 - For Fine-Grained, General VQA, and Relation Reasoning domains, existing bounding box annotations from source datasets (e.g., object bboxes in Flickr30k, bird part annotations in Birds-200-2011, object relations in GQA) are reused or aligned to the questions.
- **Quality Control:** A filtering pipeline is applied to improve content quality and ensure bounding boxes are directly relevant to the questions.
**Dataset Analysis:** Statistical analysis shows that the annotated key regions are often small (average 247.8 sq pixels, ~13.2% of image area), emphasizing the need for focused processing.

#### 3.2. VisCoT Framework

**Architecture:** VisCoT is built upon standard MLLM components, specifically:
- **Vision Encoder:** Pre-trained ViT-L/14 from CLIP.
- **Large Language Model (LLM):** Vicuna-7B/13B, chosen for its strong instruction-following capabilities.
- **Projector:** A simple linear layer to map image features into the LLM's word embedding space, similar to LLaVA.

**VisCoT Pipeline (Multi-Turn Processing):**
1. **Initial Visual Token Extraction:** The vision encoder extracts visual features from the original image (X_0), projected into visual tokens (H_0).
2. **Visual CoT Prompting:** A specific prompt ("Please provide the bounding box coordinate of the region that can help you answer the question better.") is appended to the question, instructing the MLLM to predict the most informative region.
3. **Bounding Box Prediction:** The MLLM, trained on the visual CoT dataset, outputs the bounding box coordinates of the key region.
4. **Localized Image Extraction (Visual Sampler):** Based on the predicted (or ground truth during training) bounding box, a "visual sampler" crops a localized image (X_1). This sampler accounts for vision encoder requirements (e.g., square receptive fields) and edge cases by adjusting the crop to include more context and stay within image boundaries.
5. **Localized Visual Token Extraction:** The same vision encoder and projector extract visual tokens (H_1) from the localized image.
6. **Integrated Reasoning and Answer Generation:** The MLLM then integrates visual tokens from both the original image (H_0) and the localized image (H_1) along with the textual prompt to generate a more precise and comprehensive answer.
**Adaptability:** For data without visual CoT annotations, the bounding box prediction and localized image processing steps are skipped, and the MLLM directly answers based on the original image, making the pipeline adaptable.

**Model Training:**
- **Two-Stage Process:** Consistent with LLaVA-1.5, VisCoT follows a two-stage training regimen.
 - **Stage 1 (Pre-training):** The vision encoder and LLM weights are frozen. The model is trained on image-text caption data to align visual and linguistic modalities.
 - **Stage 2 (Fine-tuning):** All weights become trainable. The model is fine-tuned on a composite dataset including LLaVA-1.5's second-stage data, a subset of Shikra's data (with positional annotations like RefCOCO, Visual Genome), and the newly proposed Visual CoT dataset. This extensive training, particularly with positional data, enhances VisCoT's ability to accurately identify and understand locations.
- **Computational Resources:** Training uses 32x A100 GPUs, with FSDP (Full Shard Data Parallel) and ZeRO3-style for memory conservation.

#### 3.3. Visual CoT Benchmark and Evaluation

A dedicated Visual CoT benchmark is introduced, focusing on scenarios where MLLMs need to concentrate on specific image regions. Evaluation is primarily done using ChatGPT to assign a numerical score (0-1) based on prediction accuracy, a common practice in MLLM evaluation. This setup allows for quantitative assessment of the proposed framework's effectiveness in achieving its objectives.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate the significant effectiveness of the Visual CoT framework, particularly its ability to enhance interpretability and performance in tasks requiring focused visual attention.

#### 4.1. Performance on the Visual CoT Benchmark

- **Significant Improvements:** VisCoT consistently outperforms existing MLLMs like LLaVA-1.5 (7B/13B) and SPHINX-13B on the proposed Visual CoT benchmark across all five domains (Doc/Text, Chart, General VQA, Relation Reasoning, Fine-grained Understanding).
- **Doc/Text and High-Resolution Images:** The improvements are particularly notable in doc/text-related tasks and scenarios involving high-resolution image processing. For instance, on the SROIE dataset (extracting info from receipts), VisCoT achieves an 8x performance increase compared to a standard pipeline without CoT. This highlights its efficacy in detailed, text-heavy visual analysis.
- **Zero-Shot Capabilities:** VisCoT also demonstrates strong zero-shot visual CoT capabilities on unseen test splits (SROIE, DUDE, Visual7W), indicating its generalization power.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies

- **Impact of BBox Selection Strategies (Table 4):**
 - **Ground Truth BBoxes (Upper Bound):** Using ground truth bounding boxes yields the highest performance, significantly surpassing the baseline. This confirms the immense potential if the model could perfectly identify the relevant region.
 - **Model Prediction vs. Baselines:** VisCoT with its predicted bounding boxes substantially outperforms the "w/o CoT" (standard, direct inference), "Random," and "Center" bbox strategies. This proves the accuracy and value of VisCoT's learned bounding box prediction capability.
 - **Random/Center Bboxes:** Random box selection shows limited impact, akin to no CoT. Selecting the "Center" box shows some improvement, implying that central regions often contain more relevant information, but it's far inferior to model-predicted or ground truth boxes.
- **Visual Sampler Design (Table 5):**
 - **Expanded Cropping:** Enlarging the cropped region when smaller than the vision encoder's input size improves performance, indicating that more context is beneficial.
 - **Centered Cropping:** Adjusting the cropped region towards the center if it extends beyond image boundaries also contributes to better performance.
 - **Combined Effect:** The combination of expanded and centered cropping leads to the best results, suggesting that careful design of the visual sampler mitigates detection inaccuracies and optimizes context capture.
- **Token Efficiency (Figure 4):**
 - VisCoT demonstrates improved token efficiency. For example, VisCoT at 224x224 resolution (using double the visual tokens, effectively two 224x224 inputs) can achieve higher accuracy than a standard pipeline at 448x448 resolution (using a single high-resolution input). This implies that selectively processing relevant visual information is more efficient than processing the entire high-resolution image in a fixed-grain manner.

#### 4.3. Performance on General MLLM Benchmarks

- **Competitive General Performance:** Even without employing the visual CoT process (i.e., in a standard direct answering mode), VisCoT achieves competitive results on established MLLM benchmarks such as MME, POPE, MMbench, ScienceQA, TextVQA, and GQA (Table 8). This indicates that the visual CoT data and training approach not only enhance CoT-specific scenarios but also boost the model's overall visual understanding capabilities.
- **Visual Grounding (Table 9):** VisCoT also excels in Referring Expression Comprehension (REC) tasks on RefCOCO datasets, outperforming even specialist models like G-DINO-L and UNINEXT. This further validates that the dataset, with its intermediate bounding box annotations, significantly improves the model's precision in locating and understanding referred objects.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Analysis (Figure 5)

Qualitative examples clearly illustrate how VisCoT's ability to correctly identify and focus on critical image regions leads to more accurate answers compared to both the "w/o CoT" baseline and situations where the model incorrectly predicts the bounding box. The visualization of model-generated bounding boxes (red) versus ground truth (blue) provides concrete evidence of its interpretable reasoning steps.

#### 4.5. Performance Analysis by BBox Size (Figure 6)

The analysis reveals that VisCoT achieves greater performance improvement when the queried object (and thus the corresponding bounding box) is relatively smaller. This reinforces the paper's claim that the method is particularly effective in scenarios where fine-grained attention to small details is crucial, a known weakness of many MLLMs.

In summary, the results strongly validate the effectiveness of the Visual CoT approach, showcasing its ability to foster interpretable, precise, and efficient visual reasoning in MLLMs across a diverse range of tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Visual CoT" paper makes several significant contributions to the field of multi-modal AI, with far-reaching potential impacts on the development and application of MLLMs.

#### 5.1. Significance and Contributions

1. **Pioneering Visual Chain-of-Thought:** This work introduces a novel paradigm of "visual chain-of-thought" reasoning for MLLMs. By integrating explicit intermediate visual grounding (via bounding boxes) and detailed reasoning steps, it moves beyond purely textual CoT, providing a more human-like, interpretable approach to visual understanding.
2. **Comprehensive and High-Quality Dataset:** The creation and release of the large-scale Visual CoT dataset (438k pairs, 98k with detailed reasoning steps) is a major contribution. This dataset, covering diverse domains and annotated with critical region bounding boxes, addresses a significant gap in existing multimodal datasets. It provides the necessary supervision for training MLLMs to dynamically focus on relevant visual information, thereby fostering more accurate and interpretable responses. Its availability to the research community will undoubtedly accelerate further research in this area.
3. **Novel Multi-Turn Processing Pipeline (VisCoT):** The proposed VisCoT framework effectively leverages the new dataset by allowing MLLMs to dynamically pinpoint and zoom into key visual regions. This architecture, while built on standard components, demonstrates how a carefully designed multi-turn interaction with visual input can significantly enhance performance, especially in scenarios requiring fine-grained visual attention (e.g., text in documents, small objects).
4. **Advancing MLLM Interpretability:** By explicitly forcing the model to output a bounding box as an intermediate "thought," VisCoT makes the reasoning process of MLLMs more transparent. This is a crucial step towards building more trustworthy and explainable AI systems, allowing users to understand *why* a model arrived at a particular answer.
5. **Improved Performance and Efficiency:** The extensive experiments demonstrate that VisCoT achieves superior performance on its proposed benchmark and competitive results on general MLLM and visual grounding benchmarks. The observed token efficiency, where lower resolution VisCoT can outperform higher-resolution standard models, highlights the computational advantages of intelligent visual focusing.
6. **Setting a New Benchmark:** The introduction of the Visual CoT benchmark provides a standardized evaluation framework for assessing MLLMs' ability to focus on specific local regions and engage in interpretable visual reasoning, which is critical for guiding future research efforts.

#### 5.2. Limitations

The paper acknowledges several limitations:
- **Complexity and Ambiguity:** In highly complex images or with extremely nuanced questions, VisCoT may still struggle to accurately identify the most relevant region, potentially leading to incorrect responses. This points to the inherent challenges of perfect visual grounding.
- **Dependency on GPT-4:** The data generation pipeline relies on the GPT-4 API for linguistic annotations. This introduces potential issues regarding:
 - **Accuracy and Misinformation:** Generated content may sometimes be inaccurate, requiring post-processing filtering.
 - **Bias and Fairness:** As GPT-4's training data is proprietary, inherent biases within the model could be propagated into the Visual CoT dataset, potentially reinforcing social or cultural inequalities. The authors acknowledge this and state adherence to OpenAI's terms for research use only.

#### 5.3. Potential Societal Impact

The potential societal impacts of Visual CoT are largely aligned with those of other advanced MLLMs and LLMs:
- **Positive Impacts:**
 - **Enhanced Accessibility:** Improved document understanding (DocVQA, SROIE) could lead to better automated processing of invoices, forms, and reports, benefiting businesses and individuals.
 - **Improved Human-AI Collaboration:** More interpretable MLLMs could facilitate better interaction and trust, especially in domains like medical imaging or scientific research where understanding the AI's "thought process" is crucial.
 - **Advancements in Robotics and Autonomous Systems:** Better fine-grained visual understanding and spatial reasoning could benefit applications requiring precise object identification and interaction.
- **Negative Impacts (as noted by authors):**
 - **Privacy Concerns:** Enhanced image analysis capabilities could increase the risk of privacy invasion if not carefully regulated and ethically deployed.
 - **Bias Perpetuation:** Biases present in the training data, particularly from large foundation models like GPT-4, could be amplified, leading to unfair or discriminatory outcomes.
 - **Misinformation and Hallucinations:** While the work aims to mitigate hallucinations, the inherent generative nature of MLLMs means they can still produce inaccurate or fabricated information, necessitating robust fact-checking mechanisms.
 - **Job Displacement:** Automation powered by highly capable MLLMs could impact jobs requiring visual data analysis or information extraction.
 - **Ethical Concerns:** Issues of accountability, consent, and responsible AI governance become increasingly important as these models become more capable.

#### 5.4. Future Directions

This work opens several promising avenues for future research:
- **Improving Bounding Box Prediction Accuracy:** Further research could focus on refining the visual CoT bbox prediction mechanism, potentially by incorporating more advanced detection models or stronger self-correction loops.
- **Dynamic Resolution Adaptation:** Exploring more sophisticated ways for the visual sampler to dynamically adjust input resolution based on the complexity of the identified region.
- **Broader Application of Visual CoT:** Applying the Visual CoT concept to other multimodal tasks beyond VQA, such as visual navigation, image editing, or video understanding.
- **Quantifying Interpretability:** Developing more objective and quantitative metrics for evaluating the interpretability of visual CoT, moving beyond human scoring.
- **Mitigating Bias:** Dedicated efforts to detect and mitigate biases in the dataset and model's reasoning processes.
- **Integration with Embodied AI:** Combining visual CoT with embodied agents to enable more intelligent and interactive decision-making in real-world environments.

In conclusion, "Visual CoT" represents a significant step forward in making MLLMs more intelligent, interpretable, and adaptable to complex visual environments. By providing a novel dataset, a robust framework, and a dedicated benchmark, the authors have laid a strong foundation for future research in visual reasoning for large language models.
