# Robust Model-Based In-Hand Manipulation with Integrated Real-Time Motion-Contact Planning and Tracking

> [!summary] Summary
> A hierarchical framework integrates real-time contact-implicit motion planning with tactile-feedback tracking control for robust in-hand manipulation, enabling complex dexterous tasks like rotating objects and opening doors while maintaining high precision and adapting to external disturbances without requiring extensive training data.

## Problem

- Existing model-based methods struggle to balance real-time performance with accuracy/robustness in in-hand manipulation
- Contact-implicit methods, while fast, suffer from modeling errors that limit real-world performance
- Current approaches lack effective integration of planning and feedback control

## Method

- Two-level hierarchical framework combining contact-implicit ==MPC== planner with tactile-feedback tracking controller
- Smoothed contact-implicit dynamics model enables efficient planning while maintaining accuracy
- Integration of tactile feedback compensates for modeling errors during execution

## Results

- Successfully demonstrated on 5 challenging real-world manipulation tasks including rotating objects and opening doors
- Maintained manipulation precision despite external disturbances and varying object geometries
- Outperformed baseline methods in simulation studies for contact force tracking and motion accuracy
- Achieved real-time planning performance without requiring extensive training data

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Warm-starting the DDP solver with previous solutions enables real-time replanning capabilities
> - Explicit tracking of both planned motions and contact forces improves robustness
> - Hybrid force-motion control effectively handles multi-contact scenarios

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

- **Context:** The paper addresses a critical challenge in robotics: achieving human-like dexterity in robotic manipulation, particularly in-hand manipulation. This is a highly active area of research, driven by the potential to improve robotic capabilities in manufacturing, healthcare, and service industries.
- **Existing Approaches:** The paper highlights two dominant approaches to in-hand manipulation:
 - **Learning-Based Methods (Reinforcement Learning and Imitation Learning):** These methods have shown promising results but require extensive training data and often struggle to generalize to new tasks or objects. They also face challenges in sim-to-real transfer.
 - **Model-Based Methods:** These methods offer an efficient alternative by relying on analytic models for online inference. Model-based approaches are further categorized as:
 - **Contact-Explicit:** Model contact interactions as a hybrid problem, considering discrete contact sequences and continuous control inputs. Such methods can be time-consuming for online replanning.
 - **Contact-Implicit:** Directly plan through contacts without explicit contact sequences, by adopting simplified and approximate models. Such methods can be susceptible to modeling errors.
- **Gap Addressed:** The paper identifies the limitations of existing model-based methods, particularly the trade-off between real-time performance and accuracy/robustness. Contact-implicit methods, while fast, often suffer from modeling errors (e.g., the "force-at-a-distance" effect).
- **Positioning:** This research aims to bridge this gap by developing a framework that combines the advantages of both approaches: real-time planning using a contact-implicit model with a low-level controller that explicitly tracks planned contacts to compensate for modeling errors.
- **Related Works:** The paper thoroughly reviews relevant literature, including recent work in reinforcement learning, imitation learning, contact-explicit methods (Cruciani et al., Cheng et al.), and contact-implicit methods (Posa et al., Pang et al.). The paper also cites research in contact force control and multi-fingered hands.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

- **Primary Objective:** To develop a robust and efficient model-based framework for dexterous in-hand manipulation that overcomes the limitations of existing approaches.
- **Specific Goals:**
 - Enable real-time (re-)planning of contacts and motions to react to external disturbances.
 - Explicitly track planned contacts to compensate for internal modeling errors.
 - Achieve high accuracy and robustness in manipulation tasks without relying on extensive training data.
 - Demonstrate the effectiveness of the framework in real-world environments.
- **Motivation:** The motivation stems from the desire to create robotic systems capable of performing complex manipulation tasks with dexterity and adaptability comparable to humans, without the data and computational burden of pure learning-based approaches. Model-based methods offer promise for flexible task adaptation but require improved robustness and real-time performance.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

- **Overall Approach:** The authors propose a hierarchical framework with two key components:
 - **High-Level Real-Time Motion-Contact Planner:** This module uses a contact-implicit Model Predictive Control (CIMPC) scheme to generate reference finger motions and contact forces in real time. A differential dynamic programming (DDP) algorithm is used with a contact-implicit dynamics model.
 - **Low-Level Tactile-Feedback Tracking Controller:** This module tracks the reference motions and contact forces concurrently using a Model Predictive Control (MPC)-based hybrid force-motion control (HFMC). Tactile feedback is integrated to compensate for modeling errors and enhance robustness.
- **Specific Techniques:**
 - **Contact-Implicit Model Predictive Control (CIMPC):** A key element is the use of a "smoothed" contact-implicit dynamics model, which approximates the complex contact forces and allows for efficient planning.
 - **Differential Dynamic Programming (DDP):** An iterative algorithm is employed to solve the optimal control problem, generating trajectories that satisfy the system dynamics and minimize a cost function.
 - **Numerical Differentiation:** The contact Hessian was computed with numerical differentiation to approximate the contact dynamics.
 - **Model Predictive Control (MPC):** Used at both levels, MPC allows for continuous re-planning based on new sensor data and disturbance estimation.
 - **Hybrid Force-Motion Control (HFMC):** Enables the robot to simultaneously control both the position and force at the contact points, improving robustness against modeling errors.
 - **Multi-Contact Force-Motion Model:** Incorporates coupling effects between multiple contacts to improve force control accuracy.
 - **Warm Starting:** The DDP solver is warm-started with the previous solution to accelerate convergence.
 - **Trajectory Shifting:** Mitigates discontinuities in joint commands when switching to a new solution from DDP.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

- **Simulation Results:**
 - The proposed method achieves high precision and success rates with smooth finger motions in simulation experiments.
 - It outperforms an "open-loop" baseline (without contact tracking) and predictive sampling methods in the "Rotate Sphere" task.
 - It demonstrates robustness against sensor noise and modeling errors.
 - The method effectively tracks both finger motions and contact forces.
- **Real-World Experiments:**
 - The framework is successfully demonstrated on five challenging in-hand manipulation tasks: "Open Door," "Rotate Card," "Slide Board," "Open Box," and "Rotate Sphere."
 - The method demonstrates robustness against human disturbances in the "Open Door" and "Rotate Card" tasks.
 - The experiments show that the method can generalize to different task setups and object geometries without retraining.
 - The proposed method successfully tracked desired contact forces.
 - The low-level tactile feedback enhances manipulation performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

- **Significance:** This research makes significant contributions to the field of robotic manipulation by:
 - Developing a robust and efficient model-based framework that combines real-time planning with tactile feedback.
 - Overcoming the limitations of existing contact-implicit methods by explicitly addressing modeling errors.
 - Demonstrating the feasibility of complex in-hand manipulation tasks in real-world environments without requiring extensive training data.
- **Potential Impact:**
 - **Advanced Robotics:** This research has the potential to advance the state-of-the-art in robotic manipulation, enabling robots to perform more complex and dexterous tasks.
 - **Industrial Automation:** The framework could be used to improve robotic capabilities in manufacturing, enabling robots to handle a wider range of objects and tasks with greater efficiency and precision.
 - **Healthcare and Assistive Robotics:** The research could lead to the development of robots capable of assisting humans with daily tasks, such as preparing food or providing medical care.
 - **Future Research:** The paper highlights several avenues for future research, including improving the control frequency, integrating arm-hand coordination, and developing more advanced grasping techniques.

In conclusion, this paper presents a significant advancement in model-based in-hand manipulation, offering a robust and efficient framework that combines real-time planning with tactile feedback. The results demonstrate the potential of this approach to enable robots to perform complex manipulation tasks in real-world environments.
