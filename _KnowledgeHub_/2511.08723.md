# ParaS2S: Benchmarking and Aligning Spoken Language Models for Paralinguistic-aware Speech-to-Speech Interaction

> [!summary] Summary
> ParaS2S offers a framework for developing and evaluating speech-to-speech models that appropriately respond to paralinguistic cues like emotion and tone. Researchers from National Taiwan University and ByteDance Seed demonstrated that their reinforcement learning alignment method improved paralinguistic awareness by over 10% relative to supervised fine-tuning, while also achieving better performance with five times less labeled data.

## Problem

- Existing speech-to-speech (S2S) benchmarks primarily evaluate linguistic content, overlooking the assessment of paralinguistic cues (emotion, tone, speaker attributes) in generated speech.
- Current S2S models frequently exhibit "tone-deafness," failing to appropriately modulate their response speaking styles based on user's paralinguistic cues.
- The development of paralinguistic-aware S2S models is hindered by the high cost and difficulty of collecting large-scale, expressively annotated spoken dialogue datasets.

## Method

- Introduction of ==ParaS2SBench==, a benchmark designed for end-to-end evaluation of S2S models, assessing both content and speaking style appropriateness directly from speech waveforms using challenging, contrastive queries.
- Development of a multi-stage automatic judge pipeline that employs "PolyTone training" to mitigate "style hallucination" and leverages natural-language tone descriptions for evaluating output speech.
- ParaS2SAlign, a reinforcement learning (RL) framework using GRPO, trains S2S models to adapt response styles to input paralinguistic cues, guided by a distilled reward model derived from the automatic judge.

## Results

- The multi-stage automatic judge achieved a Pearson correlation of **0.776** with human scores, significantly outperforming end-to-end ALLMs and demonstrating reliability in preserving model rankings.
- ParaS2SAlign's reinforcement learning (GRPO) framework improved the appropriateness of response content and speaking style by over **10%** relative to a supervised fine-tuning baseline.
- The RL approach was more data-efficient, enabling a model trained with 20 hours of warm-up data (plus RL) to surpass a pure SFT model trained with 100 hours of data, while preserving general dialogue capabilities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - End-to-end Audio Large Language Models (ALLMs) tend to "hallucinate" paralinguistic cues from spoken content, indicating that a multi-stage judge grounded in vocal cues is more reliable for S2S evaluation.
> - Natural-language tone descriptions are more effective than discrete emotion tags for capturing the nuanced pragmatic and interactional aspects of dialogue tone in automatic evaluation.
> - Reinforcement learning can enable paralinguistic-aware dialogue capabilities to emerge with minimal supervised data, providing a data-efficient solution to overcome the scarcity of expressive dialogue datasets.
