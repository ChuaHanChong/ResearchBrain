# Attention Residuals

> [!summary] Summary
> The Kimi Team introduced Attention Residuals (AttnRes) for deep neural networks, which replaces standard residual connections' fixed additive accumulation with learned, content-dependent softmax attention over preceding layer outputs. The scalable Block AttnRes variant mitigated hidden-state growth and dilution, achieving lower validation loss and outperforming baselines by up to 7.5 points on multi-step reasoning tasks like GPQA-Diamond.

## Problem

- Standard residual connections in deep networks, especially with PreNorm, lead to uncontrolled growth in hidden-state magnitudes, progressively diluting early-layer information and limiting effective model depth.
- The uniform, fixed-weight aggregation of preceding layer outputs prevents selective, content-aware retrieval of information, hindering deeper layers from effectively accessing relevant earlier representations.
- Implementing full cross-layer attention directly incurs substantial memory and communication overhead, making it impractical for large-scale model training and inference.

## Method

- **AttnRes** (Attention Residuals) replaces the additive residual update with a ==softmax attention-weighted sum== over all preceding layer outputs, using layer-specific learnable pseudo-queries to enable content-dependent information aggregation.
- **Block AttnRes** (Block Attention Residuals) partitions layers into blocks, performing standard residual summations within blocks and applying ==cross-layer attention== only over these block-level representations, reducing overhead from O(Ld) to O(Nd).
- Infrastructure optimizations, including cross-stage caching for pipeline parallelism during training and a two-phase computation strategy with memory-efficient prefilling for inference, ensure practical scalability with minimal overhead.

## Results

- Block AttnRes consistently achieved lower validation loss across all model sizes, matching the baseline's performance with **1.25x** less compute.
- The model maintained bounded and periodically patterned output magnitudes across depth, mitigating hidden-state growth, and resulted in a more uniform distribution of gradient magnitudes compared to the baseline.
- Block AttnRes demonstrated improved performance on downstream benchmarks, including **+7.5 points** on GPQA-Diamond, **+3.6 points** on Minerva Math, and **+3.1 points** on HumanEval, across multi-step reasoning and code generation tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A formal duality exists between depth-wise accumulation in residual networks and sequential recurrence in RNNs, suggesting that content-dependent attention can similarly enhance information flow across network depth.
> - Replacing fixed residual aggregation with learnable softmax attention effectively mitigates the 'PreNorm dilution' problem, leading to bounded hidden-state magnitudes and more uniform gradient distributions across layers.
> - Optimal architectural scaling shifts towards deeper, narrower networks when using AttnRes, indicating its ability to better leverage increased model depth under fixed compute and parameter budgets.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a challenge in the design of deep neural networks, particularly Large Language Models (LLMs), concerning the accumulation of information across layers via residual connections. Standard residual connections, often combined with PreNorm normalization, have become a fundamental component in modern LLMs. However, this additive mechanism results in a uniform aggregation of all preceding layer outputs, leading to several limitations. Notably, hidden-state magnitudes can grow unboundedly with depth, progressively diluting the relative contribution of each layer and potentially burying early-layer information. This phenomenon, referred to as "PreNorm dilution," can render deeper layers less effective, with previous research indicating that a notable fraction of layers can be pruned with minimal performance loss.

Existing attempts to address this issue include scaled residual paths (e.g., DeepNet) and multi-stream recurrences (e.g., Hyper-Connections, mHC), which primarily modify the additive update rule but generally retain a reliance on the immediately preceding hidden state. Other methods introduce cross-layer access but often face scalability challenges or use static, input-independent weighting. The paper draws a parallel between this depth-wise information flow problem and the historical challenges faced by Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) in sequence modeling before the advent of attention mechanisms. By framing residual connections as a form of depth-wise linear attention, the authors position their work as a generalization that introduces depth-wise softmax attention, analogous to how Transformers improved upon RNNs for sequential data. This places the proposed method within a broader context of architectural advancements aimed at enhancing information flow and stability in deep neural networks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an improved mechanism for depth-wise information aggregation in deep neural networks, specifically LLMs, that overcomes the limitations of standard residual connections. The key motivations are as follows:

1. **Addressing PreNorm Dilution and Uncontrolled Hidden-State Growth:** Standard residual connections, especially with PreNorm, aggregate all layer outputs with fixed unit weights. This uniform accumulation leads to a monotonic increase in hidden-state magnitudes with network depth (O(L)), causing a progressive dilution of each layer's contribution. Consequently, early-layer information becomes difficult to retrieve or selectively emphasize, potentially limiting the effective depth of the model and training stability. The authors aim to mitigate this uncontrolled growth and ensure more uniform influence of layers across depth.

2. **Enabling Selective, Content-Aware Information Retrieval:** Unlike sequence mixing or expert routing, which employ learnable, input-dependent weighting, depth-wise aggregation typically relies on fixed unit weights. This lack of selectivity means different layer types (e.g., attention vs. MLP) receive the same aggregated state, despite potentially benefiting from distinct weightings. The objective is to introduce a mechanism that allows each layer to selectively aggregate information from *all* preceding layers based on content-dependent, learned weights, thereby improving information flow and enabling deeper layers to access relevant earlier representations more effectively.

3. **Drawing on Sequence-Depth Duality:** The paper identifies a formal duality between the depth-wise accumulation in residual networks and the sequential recurrence in RNNs. Just as attention mechanisms revolutionized sequence modeling by replacing recurrence with direct, data-dependent access to all previous positions, the authors are motivated to apply a similar principle to the depth dimension. This involves moving from a single compressed state (like RNNs) to a mechanism that can selectively access individual prior layer outputs.

4. **Ensuring Practical Scalability:** While the concept of full cross-layer attention offers potential benefits, a direct implementation can incur substantial memory and communication overhead, especially in large-scale model training regimes that utilize activation recomputation and pipeline parallelism. A crucial objective is to develop a practical and efficient variant that can serve as a "drop-in replacement" for standard residual connections with minimal training and inference overhead, thereby enabling its deployment in state-of-the-art LLMs.

In summary, the motivation stems from a recognized limitation in current LLM architectures, informed by a conceptual duality with sequence modeling, and driven by the goal of achieving improved model performance through more intelligent information aggregation across depth, while simultaneously ensuring system-level efficiency and practicality.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology introduces **Attention Residuals (AttnRes)**, a mechanism designed to replace the fixed, uniform accumulation of standard residual connections with a learnable, input-dependent aggregation across network depth.

#### 3.1. Full Attention Residuals (Full AttnRes)

The core idea of AttnRes is to replace the additive residual update `h_l = h_l-1 + f_l-1(h_l-1)` with a softmax attention-weighted sum over all preceding layer outputs. Specifically, the input to layer `l`, `h_l`, is computed as:
`h_l = sum_{i=0}^{l-1} (alpha_i->l * v_i)`
where `v_0 = h_1` (the token embedding) and `v_i = f_i(h_i)` for `i >= 1` (the output of layer `i`). The `alpha_i->l` are softmax attention weights calculated using a kernel function `phi(q, k)`. The chosen kernel is `phi(q, k) = exp(q^T RMSNorm(k))`, which involves a layer-specific learnable pseudo-query `w_l` for `q_l` and the RMS-normalized previous layer outputs `v_i` for `k_i`. This design allows each layer to selectively retrieve and combine information from any preceding layer based on content. The pseudo-query `w_l` is a `d`-dimensional vector, learned during training, and decoupled from the layer's forward computation. This decoupling facilitates certain optimizations. To ensure training stability, all pseudo-query vectors `w_l` are initialized to zero, making initial attention weights uniform.

#### 3.2. Block Attention Residuals (Block AttnRes)

Full AttnRes, while conceptually potent, introduces `O(Ld)` memory and communication overhead at scale due to activation recomputation and pipeline parallelism. To address this, Block AttnRes is proposed. This variant partitions the `L` layers into `N` blocks.
- **Intra-Block Accumulation:** Within each block, layer outputs are accumulated using standard residual summations, effectively compressing them. For instance, `b_n` represents the sum of outputs within block `n`. A `partial_block` state (`b_i^n`) tracks the sum of outputs up to layer `i` within block `n`.
- **Inter-Block Attention:** Cross-layer attention is then applied only over these `N` block-level representations (and the initial token embedding `b_0`), rather than all `L` individual layer outputs. The input to a layer within block `n` attends over `b_0, b_1..., b_{n-1}` and potentially the `partial_block` state `b_{i-1}^n`.
This approach reduces memory and communication overhead from `O(Ld)` to `O(Nd)`. The number of blocks `N` acts as an interpolation parameter, where `N=L` recovers Full AttnRes and `N=1` approximates standard residual connections (with isolated embedding access). The empirical findings suggest `N ≈ 8` recovers most benefits.

#### 3.3. Infrastructure Optimizations for Scale

To make Block AttnRes practical for large-scale training and inference, several system-level optimizations are implemented:
- **Training: Cross-Stage Caching for Pipeline Parallelism:** In pipeline parallelism, block representations must be communicated between stages. A naive approach would re-transmit the entire accumulated history at each stage transition, leading to `O(C^2 N_p d)` communication (`C` chunks, `N_p` blocks per physical stage). The proposed **cross-stage caching** mechanism stores previously received blocks locally within each physical stage. Subsequent virtual stages only transmit incremental blocks, reducing communication overhead from `O(C)` to `O(P)` (where `P` is the number of physical stages) per transition and enabling overlap with computation.
- **Inference: Two-Phase Computation Strategy:** During inference, direct computation of AttnRes at every layer would involve `O(L * N)` memory accesses. The two-phase strategy amortizes this cost:
 - **Phase 1 (Parallel Inter-Block Attention):** For a given block of `S` layers, all `S` pseudo-queries are known. These queries are batched into a single matrix multiplication against the cached `N` block representations. This allows all inter-block attention computations for the entire block to be performed in parallel, amortizing memory access.
 - **Phase 2 (Sequential Intra-Block Attention + Online Softmax Merge):** This phase handles the sequential dependencies within the current block. Intra-block attention is computed sequentially using the evolving partial sum. The outputs from Phase 1 and Phase 2 are then merged using online softmax, preserving exact equivalence. This approach reduces per-layer memory access to `O((N/S + 5)d)` for Block AttnRes.
- **Inference: Memory-Efficient Prefilling:** For long contexts, caching `N * T * d` block representations can be memory-intensive. This is mitigated by sharding these representations along the sequence dimension across tensor-parallel devices. Phase 1 executes on local shards, and Phase 2's online-softmax merge integrates with the tensor-parallel all-reduce communication path. This significantly reduces per-device memory footprint.

#### 3.4. Integration and Evaluation

AttnRes is integrated into the Kimi Linear architecture, which is a Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) Transformer using Kimi Delta Attention (KDA) and Multi-Head Latent Attention (MLA). The model depth, hidden dimensions, expert routing, and MLP structure remain unchanged, with AttnRes affecting only the residual connections. The approach is validated through scaling law experiments, ablation studies, and pre-training on a 48B-parameter model over 1.4T tokens, followed by evaluation on various downstream benchmarks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several findings regarding the performance and characteristics of Attention Residuals:

#### 4.1. Scaling Laws

Experiments conducted across five model sizes demonstrated that both Full AttnRes and Block AttnRes consistently achieved lower validation loss compared to the PreNorm baseline across the entire range of compute budgets. At a specific compute budget (5.6 PFLOP/s-days), Block AttnRes achieved a loss of 1.692, which was comparable to the baseline requiring 1.25 times more compute to reach a similar loss (1.714). The performance gap between Full AttnRes and Block AttnRes diminished with increasing model scale, becoming marginal (0.001 loss difference) at the largest size. Comparisons with mHC(-lite) indicated that Full AttnRes outperformed it, while Block AttnRes matched its performance with significantly lower memory I/O per layer (5.5d vs. 34d for mHC with m=4 streams).

#### 4.2. Training Dynamics

Analysis of the training dynamics for the largest models (48B total / 3B activated parameters, 1T tokens) revealed key differences:
- **Validation Loss:** AttnRes consistently achieved lower validation loss throughout the training process, with the performance gap widening during the learning rate decay phase.
- **Output Magnitude:** The baseline model exhibited an increase in hidden-state magnitudes with depth, consistent with the PreNorm dilution problem. Block AttnRes mitigated this, maintaining bounded and periodically patterned output magnitudes across depth, as the selective aggregation at block boundaries reset the accumulation.
- **Gradient Magnitude:** The baseline displayed disproportionately large gradient magnitudes in the earliest layers. Block AttnRes, benefiting from its learnable softmax weights, resulted in a more uniform distribution of gradient magnitudes across layers.

#### 4.3. Downstream Performance

On a suite of downstream benchmarks, the Block AttnRes model (48B parameters, 1.4T tokens pre-trained) demonstrated performance that either matched or exceeded the baseline across all evaluated tasks. Notable improvements were observed in:
- **Multi-step Reasoning:** GPQA-Diamond (+7.5 points) and Minerva Math (+3.6 points).
- **Code Generation:** HumanEval (+3.1 points).
- **Knowledge-Oriented Benchmarks:** MMLU (+1.1 points) and TriviaQA (+1.9 points).
These improvements support the hypothesis that enhanced depth-wise information flow benefits tasks requiring compositional reasoning.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study

A detailed ablation study on a 16-layer model validated key design choices:
- **Comparison with Prior Methods:** AttnRes (Full: 1.737, Block: 1.746) outperformed DenseFormer (1.767) and mHC (1.747), underscoring the benefit of content-dependent softmax attention.
- **Cross-Layer Access Granularity:** Sliding-window aggregation, which only considers recent layers, yielded less improvement (1.764) compared to Full or Block AttnRes, suggesting the importance of selective access to distant layers.
- **Block Size Impact:** Increasing the block size (S) in Block AttnRes gradually degraded performance, but settings like S=2, 4, or 8 maintained most of the gains (around 1.746 loss), indicating a favorable trade-off for practical implementation.
- **Component Analysis:**
 - Using an input-dependent query further reduced loss (1.731) but increased complexity.
 - Replacing softmax with sigmoid for attention weights degraded performance (1.741 vs. 1.737), implying the benefit of softmax's competitive normalization.
 - Removing RMSNorm on keys worsened performance for both Full (1.743) and Block AttnRes (1.750), highlighting its role in preventing large-magnitude outputs from dominating attention.
 - Multihead attention for depth-wise aggregation (H=16) was found to be detrimental (1.752 vs. 1.746), suggesting that the optimal depth-wise mixture is largely uniform across channels.

#### 4.5. Optimal Architecture Analysis

A controlled study investigating the optimal architecture under fixed compute and parameter budgets showed that AttnRes consistently achieved lower loss across all configurations compared to the baseline. AttnRes shifted the preferred architecture towards deeper, narrower networks (optimal `d_model/L_b ≈ 45` for AttnRes vs. `≈ 60` for baseline), indicating a greater ability to leverage additional depth.

#### 4.6. Learned AttnRes Patterns

Visualization of the learned attention weights `alpha_i->l` revealed several patterns:
- **Preserved Locality:** Layers primarily attended to their immediate predecessors.
- **Learned Skip Connections:** Selective off-diagonal attention concentrations indicated the formation of learned skip connections to more distant layers.
- **Layer Specialization:** The token embedding (`h_1`) retained notable weight, particularly in pre-attention layers. Pre-MLP inputs showed stronger reliance on recent representations, while pre-attention inputs maintained broader receptive fields.
- **Block AttnRes Fidelity:** The block-wise compression maintained the essential structure of these learned attention patterns.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Attention Residuals (AttnRes) and its scalable variant, Block AttnRes, presents a development in the architectural design of deep neural networks, particularly Large Language Models.

One significant impact is the **mitigation of the PreNorm dilution problem**. By replacing fixed additive accumulation with learned, input-dependent softmax attention over preceding layer outputs, AttnRes addresses the issue of uncontrolled hidden-state growth and diluted layer contributions observed in standard PreNorm residual connections. This leads to more uniform output magnitudes and gradient distributions across network depth, which can enhance the stability and effective depth of large models.

The consistent **improvements in validation loss and downstream task performance** across various benchmarks, especially in multi-step reasoning and code generation, suggest that AttnRes facilitates more effective information flow and utilization across layers. This indicates a potential for models to better integrate and selectively retrieve relevant information from earlier processing stages, contributing to more robust and capable LLMs.

From an architectural perspective, AttnRes **reshapes optimal architectural scaling**. The finding that AttnRes allows for more effective exploitation of deeper, narrower networks under fixed compute budgets provides new considerations for model design, potentially enabling the exploration of different depth-width trade-offs in future architectures.

Crucially, **Block AttnRes addresses the practical scalability challenges** associated with full cross-layer attention. The combination of block-wise aggregation with infrastructure optimizations like cross-stage caching for training and a two-phase computation strategy for inference enables its deployment in large-scale settings with minimal overhead. This positions AttnRes as a practical "drop-in replacement" for standard residual connections, facilitating its adoption in current and future LLM development.

The work also contributes to a **unified theoretical understanding of residual connections**. By framing existing residual variants as forms of depth-wise linear attention and introducing depth-wise softmax attention, the paper establishes a broader framework. This sequence-depth duality analogy can inspire further research into novel residual connection designs, drawing on the extensive literature in sequence modeling.

In summary, AttnRes offers a mechanism to enhance information processing within deep neural networks, with demonstrated performance benefits and practical scalability. Its adoption could lead to more stable, efficient, and performant LLMs, influencing architectural choices and fostering further innovation in neural network design.
