# TIPS: Text-Image Pretraining with Spatial awareness

> [!summary] Summary
> Google DeepMind's TIPS model creates general-purpose image representations by unifying image-text and self-supervised learning, producing features competitive with DINOv2 for dense understanding while maintaining strong performance on global and multimodal vision-language tasks. This is achieved through a dual embedding mechanism for both synthetic and noisy captions, combined with self-distillation and masked image modeling.

## Problem

- Current weakly-supervised image-text models (e.g., CLIP) excel at global semantic understanding but lack fine-grained spatial awareness for dense pixel-level tasks.
- Self-supervised image-only models (e.g., DINOv2) produce strong spatially coherent features but are limited to vision-only applications as they cannot directly interpret text.
- Noisy web-scraped image captions, typically used for weak supervision, often lack the detailed spatial and background information needed for learning rich, spatially-aware representations.

## Method

- Leverages an off-the-shelf multimodal generative model (PaliGemma) to create high-quality, ==spatially rich synthetic captions== to augment or replace noisy web captions.
- Introduces a ==dual embedding mechanism== in the Vision Transformer (ViT) image encoder, training one global embedding with noisy web captions and a second with synthetic captions via InfoNCE losses.
- Integrates ==self-distillation== and ==Masked Image Modeling (MIM)==, by reconstructing masked patch tokens, into the image-text pre-training objective.

## Results

- TIPS-g/14 achieves competitive performance with DINOv2-g/14 on various dense prediction tasks (e.g., **49.9** mIoU on ADE20k segmentation, **0.353** RMSE on NYUv2 depth).
- The model maintains strong or competitive performance across multimodal retrieval (e.g., **93.0%** R@1 for I→T retrieval on Flickr) and zero-shot image classification benchmarks.
- TIPS features improve performance in downstream applications such as single-image to 3D reconstruction, leading to higher novel view synthesis quality (**21.75** PSNR) compared to DINO-B/16 features (**21.13** PSNR).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Enriching noisy web captions with high-quality, synthetically generated descriptions significantly improves a model's spatial awareness without compromising global understanding.
> - A dual embedding mechanism for image-text models effectively leverages both object-centric, discriminative information from noisy captions and detailed spatial information from synthetic captions.
> - The combination of self-distillation and Masked Image Modeling (MIM) within an image-text pre-training framework synergistically boosts the spatial coherence and robustness of learned visual features.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of computer vision has undergone a significant transformation in the past two decades, evolving from hand-crafted features like SIFT and HOG to the data-driven representations learned by deep neural networks. In the deep learning era, representation learning has diversified into several paradigms:
- **Supervised Learning:** Training on large, meticulously labeled datasets (e.g., ImageNet), as seen with early CNNs (Krizhevsky et al., 2012) and Vision Transformers (ViT) (Dosovitskiy et al., 2021). While powerful, this relies on expensive and often domain-specific human annotations.
- **Weakly-Supervised Learning:** Leveraging abundant, noisy text-image pairs from the web (e.g., alt-text). Pioneered by models like CLIP (Radford et al., 2021) and ALIGN (Jia et al., 2021), these models excel at learning robust, semantically meaningful global image representations, enabling zero-shot capabilities and multimodal retrieval. However, these models typically lack fine-grained spatial awareness.
- **Self-Supervised Learning (SSL):** Learning from images alone by designing pretext tasks that generate supervisory signals from the data itself (e.g., predicting masked patches, contrastive learning between augmented views). Recent SSL models like DINO (Caron et al., 2021) and DINOv2 (Oquab et al., 2024) have proven exceptionally effective for dense prediction tasks such as semantic segmentation, depth estimation, and surface normal estimation, as they implicitly learn spatially coherent features.

The core challenge addressed by TIPS lies in the **gap between weakly-supervised image-text models and self-supervised image-only models**. While image-text models offer semantic grounding and multimodal capabilities, their applicability to dense pixel-level understanding tasks has been limited. Conversely, self-supervised models, while excellent for dense tasks, cannot directly interpret text and are thus confined to vision-only applications. Researchers and practitioners often find themselves choosing between these two paradigms based on the downstream task: CLIP-style models for global, semantic tasks, and DINOv2-style models for dense, spatial tasks.

TIPS aims to **bridge this divide**, proposing a unified pre-training framework that combines the strengths of both. It seeks to create a *single, general-purpose image-text encoder* whose learned representations are powerful enough to be used *off-the-shelf* for both global (e.g., image classification, image-text retrieval) and dense (e.g., segmentation, depth estimation) vision tasks, without requiring task-specific fine-tuning. This work is a significant step towards developing true foundation models for vision that can flexibly serve a wide array of applications, leveraging the rich semantic information from text while maintaining critical spatial awareness.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a **general-purpose image-text model** that can produce highly capable image representations, useful "off-the-shelf" for a wide variety of computer vision tasks. More specifically, the authors aim for a model that excels in:
1. **Dense Understanding Tasks:** Such as semantic segmentation, monocular depth estimation, and surface normal estimation, which require fine-grained spatial awareness at the pixel or patch level.
2. **Global Understanding Tasks:** Including image classification and image-text retrieval, which involve reasoning about the image as a whole.
3. **Multimodal Vision-Language Tasks:** Leveraging both image and text inputs for tasks like text-to-image and image-to-text retrieval.

The motivation stems directly from the limitations of existing approaches:
- **Current image-text models (e.g., CLIP) lack spatial awareness:** While proficient at aligning global image semantics with text for tasks like zero-shot classification and retrieval, they often struggle when applied directly to dense prediction tasks, failing to capture the intricate spatial relationships and detailed object structures crucial for these applications. This deficiency necessitates additional fine-tuning or separate pre-training methods.
- **Self-supervised models (e.g., DINOv2) are vision-only:** While they generate excellent features for dense vision tasks by enforcing spatial consistency and feature coherence, they inherently lack explicit textual supervision. This limits their applicability to multimodal or vision-language tasks.
- **Noisy Web Captions:** The very source of weak supervision for image-text models—noisy web-scraped captions—often lacks the descriptive richness needed for spatially-aware representations, focusing only on salient objects rather than their spatial arrangements or background details.

By addressing these limitations, TIPS seeks to provide a unified solution, eliminating the need for separate pre-training paradigms and fostering a new generation of visual foundation models that are simultaneously semantically grounded by language and spatially adept.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

TIPS (Text-Image Pretraining with Spatial awareness) builds upon the foundational CLIP framework but introduces two core insights and associated technical innovations to achieve its objectives:

#### 3.1. Enhancing Weak Supervision with Synthetic Image Captions

The first insight focuses on improving the quality and richness of textual supervision.
- **Problem with Noisy Web Captions:** Traditional image-text models rely on web-scraped captions, which are often noisy, describe only salient objects, and lack detailed spatial relationships or background information (e.g., "2007 Cadillac Escalade for sale dealership $30k"). This limits their utility for learning spatially-aware representations.
- **Leveraging Synthetic Captions:** TIPS proposes to replace or augment these noisy captions with high-quality, **synthetically generated textual descriptions**. The authors use an off-the-shelf, publicly available multimodal generative model, PaliGemma (Beyer et al., 2024), to generate these synthetic captions. These captions tend to be more comprehensive, describing multiple objects, their spatial arrangements (e.g., "A black SUV parked in front of a building"), and finer details like color and background. This provides a much richer signal for learning dense, spatially-aware representations.
- **Dual Image-Text Embedding:** While synthetic captions are great for spatial details, they might miss fine-grained object semantics often present in noisy web captions (e.g., "2007 Cadillac Escalade"). To leverage the best of both worlds, TIPS introduces a **dual embedding mechanism** in the Vision Transformer (ViT) image encoder. Instead of a single global `[CLS]` token, the model uses an *additional* `[CLS]` token (`ˆe_g`).
 - The original `[CLS]` token (`e_g`) is trained with the noisy web caption (`T`) via a standard InfoNCE (CLIP) loss (`L_CLIP`), encouraging object-centric and discriminative representations.
 - The new `[CLS]` token (`ˆe_g`) is trained with the synthetic caption (`ˆT`) via a separate InfoNCE loss (`ˆL_CLIP`), focusing on learning spatially-aware image embeddings.
 This dual approach allows both types of information to propagate back into the dense feature maps, leading to improved patch embeddings. At inference, the choice of global embedding can be adapted to the downstream task.

#### 3.2. Integrating Self-Distillation and Masking to Boost Image Features

The second insight is to incorporate techniques from the self-supervised learning paradigm, known for producing spatially coherent features, directly into the image-text pre-training framework.
- **Teacher-Student Architecture:** TIPS uses a teacher Vision Transformer (`f_t`) alongside the main (student) ViT (`f_s = f`). The teacher's weights are updated via Exponential Moving Average (EMA) of the student's weights, a common practice in self-supervised learning (e.g., MoCo, DINO). The teacher processes the full image.
- **Self-Distillation Loss (`L_distill`):** To encourage consistency and holistic understanding, TIPS generates multiple local crops (`M` local crops) from the input image. The student encoder processes these crops to obtain local `[CLS]` embeddings. A self-distillation loss is then applied to enforce that these local crop embeddings match the predictions of the teacher's global `[CLS]` token. This ensures that representations are consistent across different views and scales, crucial for dense tasks. Importantly, TIPS simplifies this by using only one global crop, unlike some prior self-supervised methods that use two global crops, significantly increasing throughput.
- **Masked Image Modeling (MIM) Loss (`L_mask`):** To enforce spatial coherence and understanding of local contexts, a masked version of the input image is fed to the student encoder. Masked patches are replaced by special mask tokens. The encoded mask tokens are then projected and compared against the corresponding unmasked patch tokens from the teacher's output. This forces the model to recover masked visual information from surrounding visible patches, thereby learning robust and spatially aware dense features. Unlike some MIM variants, TIPS uses simple random masking.
- **Total Loss:** The final training objective is a combination of these components: `L_total = 0.5 * (L_CLIP + ˆL_CLIP) + αL_distill + βL_mask`. This unique combination is designed to leverage the complementary strengths of contrastive image-text learning with self-distillation and MIM.

#### 3.3. Scaling TIPS

To create a high-capability general-purpose model, TIPS is scaled in terms of both architecture and training data:
- **Model Architecture:** A Vision Transformer (ViT-g) with a patch size of 14 is used for the image encoder, similar to DINOv2-g, totaling 1.1 billion parameters. The text encoder is also a transformer, scaled to 12 layers with matching embedding dimensions.
- **Training Data:** TIPS utilizes a carefully curated subset of the large and noisy WebLI dataset (Chen et al., 2023). Starting from 10 billion image-text pairs, successive filtering rounds are applied (based on image-text similarity, English captions, and similarity to curated datasets) to obtain a high-quality dataset of 116 million image-text pairs. Additionally, 1 million images from Mapillary SLS are added, resulting in a total of 117 million training images, with synthetic captions generated for Mapillary data lacking alt-text. Near-duplicate images from evaluation datasets are meticulously removed.

This comprehensive approach, combining enriched textual supervision with self-supervised spatial learning signals and scaling to large models and datasets, is what sets TIPS apart.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate TIPS's strong performance across a diverse suite of 8 tasks involving 16 datasets, spanning dense, global, and multimodal vision applications. All evaluations are conducted with frozen image-text representations, highlighting TIPS's effectiveness as an off-the-shelf feature extractor.

#### 4.1. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies using a ViT-B backbone reveal the contribution of each proposed component:
- **Synthetic Captions vs. Noisy Captions:** Simply replacing noisy web captions with PaliGemma-generated synthetic captions significantly boosts dense understanding (e.g., +10.1 mIoU in segmentation, -0.076 RMSE in depth). However, it degrades performance on global tasks (e.g., -6.9 points in ImageNet KNN), as synthetic captions, while spatially rich, may lack fine-grained object-centric details.
- **Dual Embedding (Combining Captions):** The proposed dual embedding approach effectively resolves the degradation on global tasks while maintaining gains on dense tasks. It outperforms other caption combination strategies (e.g., sampling or multi-text approaches). This validates the utility of separate `[CLS]` tokens for different types of textual supervision.
- **Self-Distillation (SD):** Adding self-distillation to baseline CLIP (using noisy captions) improves performance across all tasks, including dense ones, confirming findings from prior work like SILC (Naeem et al., 2024).
- **Masked Image Modeling (MIM):** Critically, the addition of MIM *on top of self-distillation* yields substantial further improvements for dense tasks (e.g., +5.6 mIoU in segmentation, -0.078 RMSE in depth), while maintaining high scores on global and retrieval tasks. This demonstrates the strong synergy between self-distillation and MIM in fostering spatial awareness within an image-text framework.
- **Combined Effect:** The full TIPS model, incorporating both dual embedding with synthetic captions and the combined self-distillation + MIM losses, achieves very substantial improvements across all tasks compared to the CLIP baseline: +14.6 mIoU in segmentation, -0.142 RMSE in depth, and notable gains in image-text retrieval.

#### 4.2. Comparisons with State-of-the-Art Models

TIPS-g/14 models (1.1B parameters) are benchmarked against leading self-supervised, weakly-supervised, and supervised models:

- **Image-Only Tasks (Dense & Global):**
 - TIPS achieves **competitive performance with DINOv2-g/14** (the current gold standard for dense self-supervised features) across various dense tasks. For example, TIPS-g/14 HR reaches 49.9 mIoU on ADE20k segmentation (vs. DINOv2's 49.0) and 0.353 RMSE on NYUv2 depth (vs. DINOv2's 0.344). In several instances, TIPS either surpasses or is negligibly behind DINOv2.
 - Crucially, TIPS **significantly outperforms other image-text models** (e.g., CLIP-L, SigLIP-SO, EVA-CLIP) on dense prediction tasks, demonstrating that its features are indeed "dense-ready." For instance, TIPS achieves 49.9 mIoU on ADE20k, while CLIP-L gets 39.0 and SigLIP-SO gets 35.8.
 - For fine-grained retrieval, TIPS also demonstrates strong performance, outperforming DINOv2 by a large margin (71.4% vs. 62.2% Recall@1 on UnED).
 - On ImageNet classification, TIPS maintains competitive performance (86.2% linear probe) with other large models.

- **Image-Text Tasks (Multimodal Retrieval & Zero-shot Classification):**
 - TIPS **outperforms or is highly competitive** with existing weakly-supervised image-text models across multimodal retrieval benchmarks (I→T and T→I on COCO, Flickr30K, DOCCI). For example, TIPS-g/14 HR achieves 93.0% R@1 for I→T retrieval on Flickr, surpassing CLIP-L (85.2%) and EVA-CLIP (91.6%).
 - In Zero-shot ImageNet classification, TIPS maintains strong performance (79.9% top-1 accuracy), competitive with other state-of-the-art image-text models.

#### 4.3. Application: Single-Image to 3D

TIPS's spatial awareness is further validated in a novel application:
- When used as the image encoder for LRM (Large Reconstruction Model, Hong et al., 2024), a state-of-the-art single-image to 3D reconstruction model, TIPS-B/14 features lead to improved novel view synthesis (21.75 PSNR) compared to DINO-B/16 (21.13 PSNR). This highlights that TIPS's learned features capture structural and texture information critical for 3D understanding, even outperforming a model specifically designed for robust visual features without supervision.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Results

Qualitative examples demonstrate that TIPS can produce high-quality dense feature maps, which translate directly into accurate predictions for depth, surface normals, and semantic segmentation when probed with simple decoders. The principal components of the learned spatial features visually separate different objects and regions effectively.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The TIPS paper presents a significant advancement in the pursuit of general-purpose visual foundation models, marking a crucial step towards unifying previously disparate pre-training paradigms.

**Key Significance:**
- **Bridging the Gap:** TIPS is the first model to effectively bridge the performance gap between weakly-supervised image-text models (excelling in global, semantic tasks) and self-supervised models (excelling in dense, spatial tasks). It demonstrates that a single pre-training framework can simultaneously deliver state-of-the-art performance in both areas with frozen features. This is a major breakthrough, as previous models often required a trade-off or specialized pre-training for one domain.
- **Truly General-Purpose Representations:** By integrating diverse supervisory signals (noisy web text, synthetic rich text, self-supervised spatial consistency and masking), TIPS learns highly versatile image representations. These representations are "off-the-shelf" ready, meaning they can be directly applied to a wide range of downstream tasks without extensive fine-tuning, significantly reducing computational costs and development time for new applications.
- **Enhanced Data Utilization:** The innovative use of synthetically generated captions to augment noisy web data is a powerful technique. This approach effectively enriches the supervisory signal for spatial understanding, proving that the quality of weak labels can be significantly improved through advanced generative AI, paving the way for more efficient and robust pre-training.
- **Synergistic Learning Objectives:** The paper rigorously demonstrates the positive synergy between contrastive image-text learning, self-distillation, and masked image modeling. This combination proves more effective than individual components or sequential training, offering a new blueprint for future multimodal pre-training architectures.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Accelerated AI Development:** The availability of a single, highly capable visual backbone capable of both high-level semantic reasoning and low-level spatial understanding will simplify the development of new computer vision applications. Researchers and engineers will no longer need to choose specialized backbones or combine features from different pre-trained models.
- **Advancements in Multimodal AI:** TIPS can enable new frontiers in multimodal AI applications requiring deep understanding of both language and detailed visual scene content. This includes advanced robotics (e.g., precise object manipulation based on textual commands), augmented/virtual reality (e.g., context-aware content generation), detailed scene understanding for autonomous systems, and more sophisticated human-computer interaction.
- **Foundation for Downstream Tasks:** The superior performance in applications like single-image to 3D reconstruction suggests that TIPS features are robust and contain rich 3D-aware information, crucial for emerging fields in neural rendering and 3D vision.
- **Future Research Directions:** The findings will undoubtedly inspire further research into integrated multimodal pre-training. It encourages exploration of more sophisticated ways to combine diverse supervisory signals, leverage generative models for data augmentation, and design unified architectures that can understand the world from multiple modalities and at multiple granularities.
- **Accessibility:** The release of code and models by Google DeepMind ensures that the broader research community can build upon this work, fostering collaboration and accelerating progress in developing the next generation of visual foundation models.

In conclusion, TIPS represents a landmark achievement in visual representation learning, offering a potent, versatile solution that pushes the boundaries of what unified vision-language models can achieve, especially for tasks requiring fine-grained spatial awareness.
