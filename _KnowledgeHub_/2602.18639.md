# Learning Invariant Visual Representations for Planning with Joint-Embedding Predictive World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Columbia University introduce DINO-Bisim, a method that learns invariant visual representations for planning by integrating a bisimulation encoder into Joint Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs). This approach enhances world model robustness against task-irrelevant visual changes like background alterations and distractors, maintaining high planning success rates in challenging environments while reducing latent space dimensionality by a factor of 10.

## Problem

- Joint Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) for world models are sensitive to "slow features" (e.g., background textures, lighting, camera viewpoints) that are visually consistent but irrelevant for task planning.
- This sensitivity leads to a degradation of test-time robustness when visual conditions shift, causing planning failures even for identical core dynamics.
- Existing large visual foundation models, while semantically rich, still extract control-irrelevant information, which can entangle task-relevant states with nuisance features in learned latent dynamics.

## Method

- A ==bisimulation encoder== is trained on top of frozen, pretrained visual feature extractors (e.g., DINOv2) to map high-dimensional visual embeddings into a lower-dimensional, control-relevant latent space.
- A reward-free bisimulation loss enforces that states with similar future transition dynamics are mapped to nearby latent states, actively limiting the contribution of slow features.
- PCA-based Variance Covariance Regularization (PCA-VICReg) is employed to selectively allow collapse along principal component directions correlated with dominant nuisance features, while maintaining variance in other directions.

## Results

- DINO-Bisim maintained high success rates (**0.76**-**0.86**) across various background changes and moving distractors in the PointMaze task, significantly outperforming DINO-WM (**0.48**-**0.72**) under similar shifts.
- The method achieved enhanced robustness while operating in a latent space approximately 10 times smaller than that of the original DINO-WM.
- The bisimulation-based invariance mechanism generalized across different pretrained visual encoders (DINOv2, SimDINOv2, iBOT), consistently improving their robustness to visual distribution shifts.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reward-free bisimulation, relying solely on transition dynamics, can effectively induce control-relevant invariant representations without requiring explicit reward signals.
> - The learned invariant latent space can be significantly more compact, demonstrating that increased robustness does not necessitate higher dimensional representations.
> - The approach is agnostic to the choice of pretrained visual encoder, effectively improving robustness across various state-of-the-art self-supervised visual backbones.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated within the field of model-based reinforcement learning and control, specifically focusing on the development of visual world models for planning and decision-making.

**World Models and Latent Space Planning:** The paper aligns with a line of research that posits the human brain’s ability to predict future outcomes and guide decisions through spatial and temporal world models. In machine learning, this translates to learning world models from high-dimensional visual observations, enabling efficient prediction, planning, and control by reasoning over learned latent dynamics rather than raw sensory inputs. This approach has become fundamental in modern robotics, model-based reinforcement learning, and control, offering benefits such as data efficiency, generalization across tasks, and avoidance of pixel-level reconstruction.

**Joint Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs):** A significant development in this area is Joint Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs). These models learn feature representations by predicting future latent states rather than reconstructing raw observations. The DINO world model (DINO-WM) is a notable JEPA that learns latent predictive dynamics by encoding visual observations using a pretrained DINOv2 feature extractor, enabling zero-shot planning on new tasks without explicit reward signals.

**Limitations of Current JEPAs: Sensitivity to Slow Features:** A central concern addressed by this work is a limitation of current JEPAs, including DINO-WM: their sensitivity to "slow features." These are visual variations that change gradually over time but are irrelevant for the task and planning, such as background textures, lighting, camera viewpoints, and distractors. This sensitivity can lead to degenerate solutions where the predictive objective is minimized by encoding these temporally consistent but task-irrelevant features, resulting in a degradation of test-time robustness when visual conditions change. The paper empirically notes this issue for DINO-WM. The authors argue that even larger visual foundation models (e.g., DINOv2, SimDINOv2, iBOT) cannot fully resolve this, as they still extract control-irrelevant information.

**Bisimulation Metrics for Invariant Representations:** To address the "slow features" problem, the paper draws upon bisimulation metrics. Bisimulation provides a principled framework for defining state equivalences in Markov Decision Processes (MDPs) based on immediate rewards and future transition dynamics. It has been explored for state abstraction, transfer learning, and learning invariant representations. By leveraging bisimulation, the authors aim to learn representations that discard nuisance features, thereby making the world model robust to irrelevant visual changes.

**Model-Based Control and Planning:** The work integrates its invariant representation learning with standard model-based control techniques. Specifically, it uses learned latent dynamics for planning through Model Predictive Control (MPC) and the Cross-Entropy Method (CEM) for action selection, consistent with prevalent practices in the field.

In summary, this research advances the robustness of visual world models by integrating bisimulation-based invariant representation learning with JEPA architectures, aiming to overcome the challenge of "slow features" that limit generalization in existing models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop latent world models that maintain the predictive strengths of Joint Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) while being robust to "slow features" that are irrelevant to the task and planning.

The motivation for this work stems from observed limitations in current state-of-the-art world models:

- **Sensitivity of JEPAs to Task-Irrelevant Features:** JEPAs, which learn representations by predicting future latent embeddings, are known to inadvertently focus on "slow features." These include visual elements like background changes, lighting variations, camera viewpoints, or distractors that change slowly over time but do not influence the underlying dynamics relevant for decision-making.
- **Degradation in Test-Time Robustness:** This focus on slow features causes world models trained under fixed visual conditions to fail when evaluated under visual distribution shifts at test time. For instance, a model might perform poorly if the background changes, even if the core task dynamics remain identical. The DINO World Model (DINO-WM), which uses pretrained DINOv2 features, is explicitly identified as suffering from this degradation in test-time robustness, as illustrated by its performance drops under background changes and visual distractors in navigation tasks.
- **Insufficient Role of Foundation Models:** The authors argue that simply employing larger or more advanced pretrained visual foundation models (such as DINOv2, SimDINOv2, or iBOT) does not fully resolve this issue. While these models extract semantically rich features, they also capture control-irrelevant information. Consequently, the latent dynamics learned on top of these features can still entangle task-relevant states with nuisance visual features, leading to brittle planning under visual shifts.
- **Need for Control-Relevant Invariance:** The ideal world model should encode only the aspects of the state that are essential for predicting future states, while remaining invariant to task-irrelevant visual factors. The absence of such invariance in existing JEPA-based models is a core problem that this research aims to solve.

To address these motivations, the key objectives are:

- **Proposing a Bisimulation Encoder:** Introduce a bisimulation encoder that operates on top of pretrained visual feature extractors (e.g., DINOv2, SimDINOv2, iBOT). This encoder is designed to enforce control-relevant state equivalence, mapping states with similar transition dynamics to nearby latent states while actively limiting the contribution of slow features.
- **Integrating Bisimulation into Latent Predictive World Models:** Develop an architecture where the bisimulation objective augments the predictive objective of a JEPA. The bisimulation encoder is trained jointly with the latent transition model.
- **Achieving Reward-Free Invariance:** Demonstrate that learning invariant world-model representations through bisimulation does not fundamentally require explicit reward prediction, thus simplifying the training process and maintaining modularity.
- **Operating in a Reduced Latent Space:** Aim to achieve robustness while simultaneously reducing the dimensionality of the latent space significantly compared to existing models like DINO-WM.
- **Ensuring Agnosticism to Pretrained Visual Encoders:** Validate that the proposed method is effective and maintains robustness when paired with a variety of pretrained visual encoder backbones.

By achieving these objectives, the research seeks to enable more robust and generalizable planning performance for vision-based agents in dynamic and visually diverse environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology integrates a bisimulation encoder into a Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) to learn invariant visual representations for planning. The approach focuses on filtering out control-irrelevant "slow features" from pretrained visual embeddings.

#### 3.1. Overall Architecture and Training Objectives

The model (termed DINO-Bisim when using DINOv2 features) processes visual observations through a pipeline comprising a frozen pretrained visual encoder, a trainable bisimulation encoder, and a latent transition model.

#### 3.2. Pretrained Visual Encoders

- **Role:** These encoders provide an initial, rich visual representation of observations. They are **frozen** during training, meaning their parameters are not updated.
- **Models Used:** DINOv2 (ViT-S/14), SimDINOv2 (ViT-B/16), and iBOT (ViT-S/16).
- **Output Format:** These are patch-based encoders, producing embeddings `z_t ∈ R^{N_p × d_z}`, where `N_p` is the number of patches (e.g., 196) and `d_z` is the dimensionality per patch (e.g., 384 for DINOv2).
- **Limitation:** These encoders are trained generically and are not inherently tailored to capture control-relevant structure or invariance to task-irrelevant visual shifts.

#### 3.3. Bisimulation Encoder (`h_η`)

- **Purpose:** To map the high-dimensional, potentially nuisance-laden `z_t` from the pretrained encoder into a lower-dimensional, control-relevant latent representation `w_t ∈ R^{N_p × d_w}`, where `d_w ≪ d_z` (e.g., 32 dimensions per patch).
- **Architecture:** Implemented as a per-patch residual MLP projection head (Linear-ResMLP-Linear). This design processes each patch embedding independently using shared weights, significantly reducing the number of trainable parameters compared to a flattened input. It also includes learned spatial positional embeddings and LayerNorm.
- **Bisimulation Loss (`L_bisim`):** This loss enforces that distances in the `w`-space reflect on-policy bisimulation distances. For a pair of states `(o_t, a_t, o_{t+1})` and `(o'_t, a'_t, o'_{t+1})` from the replay buffer, the loss minimizes `(∥w_t - w'_t∥^2 - ∆_bisim(η, ϕ))^2`. Here, `∆_bisim(η, ϕ) = γ ∥T_ϕ(w_t, a_t) - T_ϕ(w'_t, a'_t)∥^2` quantifies the similarity of predicted next states under the learned dynamics `T_ϕ`.
- **Reward-Free Design:** Crucially, the bisimulation objective in this work omits the reward similarity term typically found in bisimulation metrics. The authors claim that the transition term alone provides a sufficient notion of equivalence for learning invariant representations, aligning with the reward-free nature of JEPAs.
- **Bisimulation Memory Buffer:** A replay buffer stores past latent states for pairwise comparisons, providing a diverse set of states to compute the bisimulation loss and stabilize representation learning.

#### 3.4. PCA-based Variance Covariance Regularization (PCA-VICReg)

- **Problem Addressed:** Standard VICReg, which enforces uniform variance across latent dimensions, can conflict with the goal of collapsing nuisance features if these features dominate a few principal components (e.g., background variations in PC1).
- **Solution:** PCA-VICReg adapts VICReg to target specific directions.
 1. Compute the empirical covariance matrix and its eigendecomposition on a batch of centered `w` embeddings.
 2. Transform the embeddings into their Principal Component (PC) coordinates.
 3. **Variance Term (`L_pca-var`):** A minimum variance floor (`σ_min`) is enforced *only on the tail PCs* (e.g., `i ≥ i_0`, excluding the dominant PCs). This allows the bisimulation encoder to collapse directions associated with slow features (e.g., PC1) without violating the variance constraint.
 4. **Covariance Term (`L_pca-cov`):** Cross-covariances in PC coordinates are regularized to encourage decorrelation and information spread across dimensions.
- **Training Strategy:** The bisimulation encoder is initially trained with standard VICReg for a few epochs for stabilization. Then, a one-time PCA is performed to identify dominant principal components. Subsequently, the standard VICReg is replaced with PCA-VICReg for the remainder of training.

#### 3.5. Transition Model (`T_ϕ`)

- **Architecture:** A decoder-only Vision Transformer, adapted for patch-based latent states. It takes a history of past bisimulation latent states and actions as input.
- **Input:** `{(w_{j,t-H:t-1}, a_{j,t-H:t-1})}` across multiple trajectories, where `H` is the context length.
- **Output:** Predicts the next bisimulation latent state `ˆw_t`.
- **Causal Attention:** Employs a causal attention mask to ensure predictions at time `t` depend only on past states and actions.
- **Dynamics Loss (`L_dyn`):** Standard Mean Squared Error (MSE) between the predicted `ˆw_{t+1}` and the target `h_η(f_θ(o_{t+1}))`.

#### 3.6. Overall Training Objective

The full objective is `L_jepa-bisim (η, ϕ) = L_dyn (η, ϕ) + λ_bisim L_bisim (η, ϕ) + L_pca-vc (η)`.

#### 3.7. Planning

- **Method:** Model Predictive Control (MPC) with the Cross-Entropy Method (CEM).
- **Process:** Given an initial observation `o_0` and a goal observation `o_g`, their latent representations `w_0` and `w_g` are computed. CEM samples candidate action sequences, uses the learned transition model `T_ϕ` to rollout predicted states `ˆw_t`, and selects actions that minimize a terminal cost: `c(w_T, {a_t}_t) = ∥ˆw_T({a_t}_t) - w_g∥^2`. The first action of the optimized sequence is executed, and the process repeats with a receding horizon.

#### 3.8. Theoretical Justification (Generalization Bound)

The paper provides a theoretical generalization bound for reward-free planning. It states that if the bisimulation encoder approximately preserves the reward-free bisimulation metric, then the difference in the T-step cost-to-go between two states is bounded by `O(d_π(z, z') T)`, where `d_π` is the bisimulation distance. This implies that if two states differ only by nuisance visual variations, their planning behavior in the bisimulation latent space remains similar, supporting the method's robustness.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation was conducted on the MuJoCo PointMaze navigation task, a benchmark for goal-conditioned planning from visual observations, under various test-time visual distribution shifts. Performance was measured by the success rate (SR) over 50 independently sampled initial and goal pairs.

#### 4.1. Robustness to Background Changes and Moving Distractors (Table 1)

The study compared the proposed DINO-Bisim model against the original DINO-WM and DINO-WM with Domain Randomization (DR) under six scenarios: No Change (NC), Slight background Change (SC), Color gradient background (C), Large Color background Change (LC), Large Color Gradient background change (LCG), and Moving Distractors (D).

- **DINO-WM Performance:** The DINO-WM showed a clear degradation in planning performance when the visual background differed from training conditions. While achieving an 0.80 SR under NC, its performance substantially dropped to 0.72 (SC), 0.60 (C), 0.56 (LC), and 0.48 (LCG) under increasing background changes. Performance also decreased with moving distractors (0.78 SR), indicating sensitivity to task-irrelevant motion.
- **DINO-Bisim Robustness:** In contrast, DINO-Bisim consistently maintained high success rates across all background changes and the moving distractor scenario. It achieved 0.78 (NC), 0.80 (SC), 0.76 (C), 0.86 (LC), 0.78 (LCG), and 0.82 (D). These results indicate that the bisimulation encoder effectively filtered out background-dependent features from the latent dynamics, leading to reliable planning despite significant visual alterations.
- **Domain Randomization (DR):** DINO-WM with DR performed well when test-time backgrounds resembled the randomized training distribution (0.82 SR for NC, SC, C). However, its performance degraded under aggressive background changes not fully covered by the training distribution (0.68 for LC, 0.64 for LCG), suggesting that DR alone has limitations in generalizing to entirely novel visual shifts.

#### 4.2. Latent Space Dimensionality

The DINO-Bisim model operates in a latent space that is approximately **10 times smaller** than that of DINO-WM (196 patches each mapped to 32-dimensional latents in DINO-Bisim vs. 384-dimensional latents in DINO-WM). This demonstrates that increased robustness and generalization were achieved with a more compact representation.

#### 4.3. Performance with Different Pretrained Visual Encoders (Table 2)

The study evaluated DINO-Bisim with different pretrained visual encoder backbones: DINOv2, SimDINOv2, and iBOT, and also included a baseline ("No Encoder") where the visual features were not derived from a strong pretrained model.

- **Necessity of Informative Features:** The "No Encoder" baseline showed significantly degraded performance (e.g., 0.26 SR under LC), indicating that the bisimulation-based invariance mechanism relies on sufficiently informative underlying visual representations provided by powerful pretrained encoders.
- **Generalizability Across Encoders:**
 - **SimDINOv2:** Although the overall success rates were lower (e.g., 0.40 for NC), the model maintained relative robustness across background changes (e.g., 0.38 for SC, 0.42 for LC). The authors suggest the lower absolute performance might be attributed to the higher dimensionality of SimDINOv2 features (768-dim patches) when trained with the same number of samples, potentially leading to overfitting.
 - **iBOT:** The model achieved strong and consistent planning performance (around 0.72 SR across all scenarios) and robustness comparable to the DINOv2-based model.
- These findings indicate that the proposed bisimulation approach effectively generalizes and improves robustness across various state-of-the-art self-supervised visual encoders.

#### 4.4. Role of Reward Term in Bisimulation (Appendix A.6, Figure 11)

Empirical analysis revealed that when an explicit reward predictor was included in a reward-aware bisimulation objective, its loss rapidly converged to near zero early in training. This suggests that for the PointMaze task, reward prediction provided minimal additional learning signal beyond what was already captured by the transition dynamics, supporting the design choice to omit the reward term for simplicity and modularity.

#### 4.5. Efficacy of PCA-based VICReg (Appendix A.2, Figure 7 & 8)

The analysis of principal components demonstrated that DINOv2, SimDINOv2, and iBOT embeddings allocated a large fraction of variance to their first principal component, which correlated with task-irrelevant background and layout information. PCA-based VICReg, by selectively allowing collapse along these dominant, slow-feature directions while maintaining variance in others, was shown to improve the clustering of identical states across different backgrounds in the bisimulation latent space, confirming its role in suppressing task-irrelevant variations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of model-based reinforcement learning and robotics by addressing a critical challenge in visual world models: their sensitivity to task-irrelevant visual variations.

**Enhanced Robustness and Generalization:** The primary significance of this work lies in its demonstration of substantially improved robustness and generalization capabilities for Joint Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) under visual distribution shifts. By effectively "filtering out" slow features like background changes and distractors, the proposed bisimulation encoder allows world models to maintain reliable planning performance in diverse visual environments, where traditional JEPAs (like DINO-WM) often degrade. This advancement is crucial for deploying agents in real-world settings that are characterized by visual variability.

**Efficient and Compact Representations:** The ability to achieve this enhanced robustness while simultaneously reducing the latent space dimensionality by a factor of 10 compared to DINO-WM is a notable outcome. More compact latent representations can lead to more memory-efficient models, faster dynamics learning, and potentially more efficient planning, which are valuable considerations for resource-constrained applications or complex environments.

**Modular and Reward-Free Design:** The modular nature of the approach, building upon frozen, off-the-shelf pretrained visual encoders, offers flexibility. Moreover, the empirical finding that reward prediction is not fundamental for learning invariant representations in this context simplifies the training process. This aligns with the principles of reward-free JEPAs, facilitating zero-shot planning without requiring task-specific reward supervision, thus broadening the applicability of these world models to new tasks.

**Generalizability Across Foundation Models:** The validation of the method across different state-of-the-art self-supervised visual encoders (DINOv2, SimDINOv2, iBOT) underscores its generalizability. This suggests that the bisimulation-based invariance mechanism is not tied to a specific pretrained feature extractor but can enhance the robustness of various vision backbone models.

**Theoretical Grounding:** The provision of a theoretical generalization bound offers insights into the mechanisms through which reward-free bisimulation metrics can improve planning performance by bounding the cost-to-go difference based on state similarity. While the horizon-dependent nature of the bound represents a limitation compared to reward-aware counterparts, it provides a foundational understanding for future theoretical extensions.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Robotics and Autonomous Systems:** The development of more robust visual world models could enable robotic agents to perform tasks more reliably in dynamic and visually changing environments, such as factories with varying lighting or outdoor settings with natural background changes.
- **Data Efficiency and Transfer Learning:** By learning truly invariant representations, the approach could facilitate more effective transfer of learned policies or world models across visually different but dynamically similar environments, reducing the need for extensive data collection for each new visual condition.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The identified limitations (e.g., dependency on informative pretrained features, horizon-dependent theoretical bounds) and proposed future directions (e.g., integration with gradient-based planning, adaptive policies, complex manipulation tasks) lay groundwork for further research in robust representation learning for control.

The authors state that there are "many potential societal impacts of this work, none of which we feel must be specifically highlighted here," indicating a general positive outlook on the advancements in robust machine learning for decision-making systems.
