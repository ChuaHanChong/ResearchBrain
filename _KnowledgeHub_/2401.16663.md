# VR-GS: A Physical Dynamics-Aware Interactive Gaussian Splatting System in Virtual Reality

> [!summary] Summary
> VR-GS is an interactive system enabling real-time, physics-aware manipulation of 3D Gaussian Splatting content within Virtual Reality, utilizing a novel two-level deformation embedding to ensure realistic dynamics without artifacts. The system achieved significantly faster simulation speeds than prior methods and garnered positive user feedback for immersion and realism, with an 83.5 System Usability Scale score.

## Problem

- Traditional 3D content creation tools are complex, time-intensive, and require specialized expertise, limiting accessibility for non-expert users.
- Existing implicit 3D representations like NeRFs are computationally inefficient for real-time interactive VR applications, especially when handling dynamic deformations and collisions.
- Directly deforming 3D Gaussian Splatting kernels using piece-wise linear deformation fields from a simulated mesh can introduce undesirable visual 'spiky artifacts' during large deformations.

## Method

- A unified system called VR-GS was developed, integrating 3D ==Gaussian Splatting== for high-fidelity rendering with an eXtended Position-based Dynamics (**XPBD**) solver for real-time physics simulation within a VR environment.
- A novel two-level deformation embedding strategy was introduced, where each Gaussian is first enveloped by a local tetrahedron whose vertices are then embedded into a global simulation mesh, smoothing deformation gradients and preventing 'spiky artifacts'.
- An offline asset preparation pipeline was implemented for object segmentation, scene inpainting to fill occluded regions, and generation of tetrahedral simulation meshes from ==3D Gaussian== reconstructions.

## Results

- The proposed two-level embedding successfully eliminated 'spiky artifacts' observed with naive embedding methods during significant stretching or twisting of Gaussian splats.
- VR-GS demonstrated significantly faster simulation times, with frame times of 0.017-0.022 seconds, outperforming PhysGaussian (0.112-0.39s) and PAC-NeRF (0.625-0.813s).
- A user study resulted in an 'excellent' System Usability Scale (SUS) score of 83.5, with participants reporting significantly enhanced immersion and realism from physics-based interactions (average Likert score of 6.1 vs. 4.8 for transform-based, p=0.0227).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The two-level deformation embedding effectively mitigates 'spiky artifacts' by providing a smoother, averaged deformation gradient to individual Gaussians, enhancing the visual realism of deforming objects.
> - Leveraging a coarser tetrahedral mesh to drive XPBD physics on 3D Gaussian Splatting content offers a crucial balance between maintaining high frame rates required for VR and achieving accurate, physically plausible dynamics.
> - Integrating essential auxiliary components like object segmentation, scene inpainting, and dynamic shadow casting is critical for creating a comprehensive, immersive, and visually consistent interactive VR experience from real-world captured scenes.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of "VR-GS" addresses a challenge in the creation, editing, and interaction with 3D virtual content within the fields of Computer Graphics (CG) and Computer Vision (CV). Traditional methods for 3D content creation, which rely on explicit 3D modeling tools and game engines, are often time-intensive, complex, and require specialized expertise, limiting accessibility for non-expert users.

The research landscape has seen significant advancements in implicit 3D representations, notably Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs). NeRFs excel in synthesizing high-fidelity 3D content from multi-view images and are known for their photorealistic rendering quality. However, NeRFs possess limitations concerning computational efficiency, particularly for interactive applications demanding high frame rates, such as Virtual Reality (VR). Additionally, handling deformations in NeRFs is computationally expensive due as it typically involves bending query rays through an inverse deformation map. Collision detection and resolution are also challenging due to their implicit nature.

More recently, 3D Gaussian Splatting (GS) has emerged as an explicit and efficient alternative to NeRF. GS represents scenes using an array of 3D anisotropic Gaussian kernels, offering faster optimization and rendering while maintaining high visual quality. Its explicit geometric representation facilitates direct manipulation and editing. This characteristic makes GS more amenable to post-processing tasks, including animation and physics-based simulations, compared to NeRF. Existing works have demonstrated the application of 3D GS for 4D dynamics, physics-based simulations for novel dynamics, and the creation of animatable avatars.

This paper positions VR-GS within this context by leveraging the rendering efficiency and explicit nature of 3D GS to enable real-time, physics-aware interactive manipulation of 3D content within a VR environment. It aims to overcome the real-time interaction limitations of NeRF-based systems and the challenges of integrating physics into explicit GS representations while maintaining high visual fidelity. The system contributes to the field by proposing specific technical solutions for deformation embedding and real-time simulation that address artifacts observed in prior GS deformation methods, and by integrating these capabilities into a VR-compatible framework.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop a human-centered 3D content interaction system, named VR-GS, that offers a seamless and intuitive user experience within a Virtual Reality (VR) setting. This overarching goal is motivated by several identified challenges and limitations in current 3D content creation and interaction paradigms.

**Detailed Motivation and Objectives:**

1. **Addressing Accessibility and Expertise Barriers:** Traditional 3D content creation tools and game engines are characterized by their engineering intensity and the requirement for extensive expertise. This creates a barrier for non-expert users who wish to create or manipulate high-quality 3D content. VR-GS aims to democratize 3D content interaction by providing an intuitive system that reduces the need for specialized skills.

2. **Enhancing Visual Quality and Ease of Creation:** The project seeks to move beyond traditional 3D models in graphics pipelines, which can be time-intensive and complex to create, by adopting state-of-the-art radiance field techniques. While Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs) offer high visual quality, their inherent inefficiencies in volume rendering and challenges in handling deformations (e.g., bending query rays) make them unsuitable for interactive, real-time applications requiring high frame rates. This motivates the adoption of 3D Gaussian Splatting (GS), which provides efficient rendering and an explicit geometric representation more conducive to direct manipulation and deformation.

3. **Achieving Immersive and Realistic Generative Dynamics:** A core motivation is to provide users with an immersive and realistic experience where dynamic motions, 3D virtual shapes, and illuminations closely mirror the real world. Unlike methods that merely reconstruct motion from time-dependent datasets or use generative AI to drive models, VR-GS specifically focuses on physics-based 3D deformations and dynamics. The objective is to ensure that interactions lead to physically plausible responses, moving beyond purely geometric manipulations.

4. **Ensuring Real-Time Interaction in VR:** For an interactive system, especially in VR, low latency is critical to prevent disorientation or motion sickness. The system aims to offer instantaneous output and feedback, including not only basic transformations (rotation, scaling, translation) but also real-time physics-based interactions. This necessitates the implementation of highly efficient algorithms and a reduced representation model to stay within the frame budget. Specifically, directly simulating physics on individual Gaussian kernels (as in some prior works) is deemed impractical for real-time VR. Therefore, an objective is to utilize a more efficient, coarser mesh representation derived from Gaussian kernels to drive their motion.

5. **Developing a Unified Framework for Rendering and Simulation:** A key objective is to integrate rendering and simulation into a single, cohesive pipeline that adheres to the principle of "what you see is what you simulate." This involves seamlessly combining Gaussian Splatting for high-fidelity rendering with a physical simulator for real-time dynamics. A specific challenge arises from the distinct geometrical representations used by simulation (mesh) and rendering (Gaussians). The goal is to address potential issues like "spiky artifacts" that can occur when directly mapping mesh deformations to Gaussian kernels, leading to the development of a novel embedding strategy.

6. **Integrating Essential Auxiliary Components for VR Experience:** To create a comprehensive and intuitive VR experience derived from real-world captures, the system aims to incorporate additional functionalities. These include:
 - **Object Segmentation:** To allow interaction with individual objects within a larger scene.
 - **Scene Inpainting:** To address visual voids or "black holes" that appear when objects are removed from their original positions in a scene.
 - **Dynamic Shadow Casting:** To provide real-time visual cues for spatial relationships and enhance the overall realism of the environment when objects move or deform, as original GS may bake shadows as static textures.

In summary, the VR-GS project is motivated by the desire to make high-fidelity, interactive, and physically realistic 3D content manipulation accessible and engaging within VR, overcoming the limitations of previous methods in terms of efficiency, visual quality, and intuitive physical interaction.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VR-GS is designed as a unified system that integrates 3D Gaussian Splatting (GS) for rendering with eXtended Position-based Dynamics (XPBD) for real-time physics simulation within a Virtual Reality (VR) environment. The methodology involves an offline asset preparation pipeline followed by a real-time unified framework for simulation and rendering.

**System Design Overview (Figure 2):**
The pipeline begins with real scene capture (multi-view images) and goes through COLMAP calibration, segmentation, and inpainting processes. This results in object-level 3D scene reconstruction using GS kernels. These kernels are then used to reconstruct embedded geometry (VDB representation, marching cubes, TetGen tetrahedralization) which forms a cage mesh for simulation. The core of the real-time system involves a novel two-level Gaussian embedding, XPBD-based dynamics, collision handling, and a Gaussian Rasterizer with shadow rays.

#### 3.1. Gaussian Splatting (GS)

The system utilizes 3D Gaussian Splatting [Kerbl et al. 2023] as the explicit 3D representation. Each 3D Gaussian kernel is defined by its mean ($\mu$), opacity ($\sigma$), covariance matrix ($\Sigma$), and spherical harmonic coefficients (C) for view-dependent color. Rendering involves projecting 3D Gaussians to 2D screen space, ordering them by z-depth, and $\alpha$-blending to compute pixel colors. The explicit nature and efficient rasterization of 3D Gaussians are leveraged for post-processing and VR integration.

#### 3.2. VR-GS Assets Preparation (Offline)

This stage prepares high-fidelity 3D GS reconstructions and corresponding simulatable tetrahedral meshes for each interactable object.

- **4.2.1 Segmentation:** To allow interaction with individual objects, segmented GS reconstructions are generated. This involves:
 - Applying a 2D segmentation model [Cheng et al. 2023] to multi-view RGB images to generate 2D masks.
 - Assigning unique colors to each segmented part, consistent across views.
 - Integrating three additional learnable RGB attributes into 3D Gaussian kernels.
 - During GS training, a segmentation loss ($L_{seg} = L_1(M_{2d}, I')$) is used, where $M_{2d}$ are colored 2D segmentation results, and $I'$ is the rendering of 3D Gaussians using the extended RGB attributes. This loss, combined with standard $L_1$ and $L_{SSIM}$ losses, enables 3D Gaussians to learn object belonging.

- **4.2.2 Inpainting:** When objects are extracted from a scene, "black holes" or unreconstructed regions can appear where the object previously occluded the background. To address this:
 - A 2D inpainting tool (LaMa [Suvorov et al. 2022]) is used to generate inpainted multi-view images.
 - Gaussian kernels outside the holes are frozen.
 - An inpainting loss ($L_{inpaint} = L_1(I_{inpainted}, I)$) guides the optimization of a Gaussian kernel patch, leveraging the 2D inpainted images for current 3D GS rendering.

- **4.2.3 Mesh Generation:** For physics simulation, a tetrahedral mesh is generated for each segmented group of 3D GS kernels. These meshes are not rendered but serve as simulation media.
 - Internal filling [Xie et al. 2024] is applied to fill void internal regions not reconstructed by GS.
 - Gaussian centers are treated as a point cloud, converted to a voxelized VDB representation [Museth 2013].
 - A watertight surface mesh is extracted using marching cubes [Lorensen and Cline 1987].
 - This surface mesh is then tetrahedralized into a finite element mesh using TetGen [Si 2015]. Moderate mesh resolution is chosen to balance performance and detail.

#### 3.3. Unified Framework for Simulation and Rendering

This framework operates in real-time within the VR environment, integrating physics with rendering.

- **4.3.1 Physical Parameters:** Physical parameters (Young's modulus $E$, Poisson ratio $\nu$, and density $\rho$) are manually set for interactable objects. An iterative process of tuning these parameters (starting from $E=1000$ Pa, $\nu=0.3$, $\rho=1000$ kg) is performed to achieve visually plausible dynamics, observing free-fall collisions and relative deformations.

- **4.3.2 Embedding (Two-Level Deformation Embedding - Novelty):** This component addresses the challenge of deforming Gaussian kernels based on the piece-wise linear deformation field of the simulated tetrahedral mesh, which can cause "spiky artifacts."
 - **Problem:** A direct embedding of Gaussian centers into a tetrahedron, where the deformation gradient is piece-wise constant, can lead to undesirable spiky artifacts, especially for Gaussians that are not entirely enveloped or under large deformations.
 - **Solution: Two-level Embedding:**
 1. **Local Embedding:** Each individual Gaussian kernel is independently enveloped by an "as-tight-as-possible" tetrahedron. These local tetrahedra have no connectivity among themselves.
 2. **Global Embedding:** The four vertices of each local tetrahedron are then embedded into the global simulation mesh (which is driven by XPBD).
 - **Mechanism:** As the global mesh deforms, the vertices of the local tetrahedra are moved. The deformation of a Gaussian kernel is then determined by the average deformation of its local tetrahedron's vertices. This process effectively smooths the deformation gradient field experienced by individual Gaussians, significantly reducing spiky artifacts and improving deformation realism. The mean ($\mu$) and covariance matrix ($\Sigma$) of the deformed Gaussian are updated based on the deformation gradient ($F$) derived from the local tetrahedron's current and rest-shape configurations.

- **4.3.3 Shadow Map:** To enhance realism and assist spatial perception in VR, dynamic shadow casting is integrated.
 - The original global illumination learned by spherical harmonics on each Gaussian is static. When objects move or deform, their shadows would not align.
 - A shadow map approach [Wanger et al. 1992] is used, which is compatible with the GS rasterization pipeline.
 - A depth map is estimated from the light source by applying the $\alpha$-blending equation (1) for GS.
 - Visibility for each Gaussian is then tested against this depth map to render dynamic shadows, providing real-time visual feedback on object distances and interactions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation section of the paper assesses the core components of the VR-GS system, benchmarks its performance against existing methods, and demonstrates its capabilities through various VR applications and a user study.

#### 4.1. Evaluation of Essential Components

- **Two-Level Embedding:** An ablation study demonstrates the effectiveness of the proposed two-level embedding. Under conditions of significant stretching or twisting, a "naive embedding" (directly embedding Gaussians within the closest tetrahedron in the global mesh) results in severe "spiky artifacts" due to the piece-wise constant deformation gradient. The two-level embedding strategy, by first enclosing each Gaussian in a local tetrahedron and then embedding that local tetrahedron's vertices into the global mesh, allows Gaussians to experience a smoother, averaged deformation. This significantly reduces the spiky artifacts, enhancing the realism of deformed GS kernels.
- **Inpainting:** The inpainting mechanism effectively addresses the issue of "black holes" or unreconstructed surfaces that appear when objects are removed from a scene, as GS primarily reconstructs visible surfaces. By integrating 2D inpainting (LAMA) to guide 3D GS scene fine-tuning, the system can fill these voids, leading to a more complete and visually realistic scene representation, crucial for interactive object manipulation.
- **Shadow Map:** The integration of dynamic shadow maps improves realism. While traditional GS bakes shadows as static textures, VR-GS generates time-dependent shadows that align with moving and deforming objects. This provides important visual cues for spatial relationships between objects, enhancing the immersive experience in VR. Users can adjust light source parameters for customized lighting.

#### 4.2. Performance Benchmarking

- **Mesh Resolution vs. Performance:** Experiments were conducted to analyze the trade-off between the quality of the cage mesh (number of vertices/tetrahedra) and system performance (frame rate, FPS).
 - Coarse meshes (e.g., 2K vertices, 7K tetrahedra) yield high frame rates (135 FPS) but may fail to capture fine dynamic details.
 - High-resolution meshes (e.g., 55K vertices, 242K tetrahedra) increase computational cost, significantly reducing FPS (23 FPS), and require more XPBD iterations for convergence to avoid overly soft or unrealistic dynamics.
 - The authors found an optimal balance using mid-resolution meshes (10K-30K vertices) to achieve both high frame rates and accurate physical dynamics.
- **Comparison with State-of-the-Art Methods:** VR-GS was benchmarked against PAC-NeRF [Li et al. 2023a] and PhysGaussian [Xie et al. 2024] in terms of visual quality and simulation speed.
 - **Visual Quality:** VR-GS synthesizes visual results comparable to PhysGaussian (known for photorealistic dynamics) and significantly outperforms PAC-NeRF in clarity and realism.
 - **Simulation Speed:** VR-GS demonstrates significantly faster simulation times. For various examples, VR-GS achieved frame times of 0.017-0.022 seconds, compared to PhysGaussian (0.112-0.39 seconds) and PAC-NeRF (0.625-0.813 seconds). This efficiency, attributed to the XPBD-based framework and mesh-based simulation, makes VR-GS suitable for real-time physics-aware interactions.

#### 4.3. VR Demos and Qualitative Results

A range of VR demonstrations showcased VR-GS's capabilities:
- **Object Manipulation:** Deformable objects like a fox, bear, and horse (reconstructed from various datasets) could be manipulated in real-time with physically plausible responses.
- **Scene Integration and Games:** New virtual objects (rings, bricks modeled in Blender) were seamlessly integrated into a reconstructed room scene, enabling interactive games like ring toss and table brick game with collision boundaries and elastic/rigid body simulations.
- **Object Collection:** A living room scene with plush toys (some reconstructed, one generated by text-to-3D) allowed users to manipulate and collect them, demonstrating realistic placement and deformation.
- **Human Animation:** A reconstructed human character was animated using Mixamo's auto-rig, with the resulting motion sequences serving as boundary conditions for GS human animation in the immersive system.

#### 4.4. Modeling Details (Time and Labor)

A breakdown of the "Toy Collection" demo showed that the total preparation time for creating an interactive VR environment was approximately 2 hours and 30 minutes. This includes scene staging, video capture, COLMAP processing, image processing (segmentation, inpainting), GS reconstruction, mesh generation, and physical parameter estimation. Manual tasks were limited to scene setup, video recording, object class specification, and parameter tuning, with most other processes being automated.

#### 4.5. User Study

A user study with 10 participants (2 professionals, 8 novices) involved two tasks:
- **Task 1 (Goal-directed):** Participants played VR games (ring toss, toy collection) in two settings: physics-based interaction (S1) and transform-based interaction (S2). A paired t-test revealed that physics-based interaction significantly enhanced user immersion and realism compared to transform-based interaction (average Likert score of 6.1 vs. 4.8 on a 7-point scale, p = 0.0227).
- **Task 2 (Open-ended):** Participants freely edited scenes and generated dynamics.
- **Results and Discussion:**
 - Users provided overall positive feedback, particularly highlighting the realistic dynamics and physics-driven placement of virtual content (e.g., toys conforming to a sofa).
 - Professional users praised the high-fidelity generative dynamics and illumination.
 - The system received high ratings for ease of use (4.6/5) and overall satisfaction (4.8/5).
 - The System Usability Scale (SUS) score was 83.5, which is classified as "excellent."

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The VR-GS system presents a significant contribution to the field of interactive 3D content creation and manipulation, particularly within Virtual Reality (VR) environments. Its significance and potential impact can be understood through several aspects:

- **Bridging the Gap in 3D Content Interaction:** The system directly addresses the long-standing challenge of enabling intuitive, real-time, and physically plausible interactions with high-fidelity 3D content. By leveraging the explicit and efficient representation of 3D Gaussian Splatting (GS), VR-GS bridges the quality gap between machine-generated content and manually created 3D assets, while simultaneously overcoming the real-time interaction limitations of prior radiance field methods (like NeRFs).

- **Enhanced User Experience and Accessibility:** VR-GS offers a human-centered design approach, providing a seamless and intuitive user experience. The integration of physics-based dynamics for deformation and interaction significantly enhances realism and immersion in VR, as validated by the user study. This intuitiveness can make 3D content manipulation more accessible to non-expert users, reducing the need for specialized engineering knowledge or extensive training traditionally associated with 3D modeling and animation. The positive user study results, particularly the high System Usability Scale (SUS) score, underscore its potential for broad adoption.

- **Novel Technical Contributions:**
 - **Two-Level Deformation Embedding:** The introduction of a novel two-level embedding approach is a key technical innovation. This method effectively resolves "spiky artifacts" that can arise during large deformations when directly applying piece-wise linear deformation fields from a mesh to Gaussian kernels. By providing a smoother deformation gradient, it ensures more realistic and visually appealing dynamic responses of the Gaussians, which is crucial for maintaining visual fidelity during interaction.
 - **Efficient Physics-Aware Integration:** The system successfully integrates a robust physical simulator (eXtended Position-based Dynamics, XPBD) with GS for real-time physics-aware interactions. This is achieved by utilizing a reduced tetrahedral mesh representation to drive the Gaussian deformations, balancing detailed dynamics with computational efficiency necessary for VR applications.
 - **Comprehensive Interactive System:** Beyond core physics and rendering, the inclusion of object segmentation, scene inpainting for occluded regions, and dynamic shadow casting contributes to a complete and immersive virtual experience. These components address common practical issues encountered when working with real-world captured data in interactive environments.

- **Broadening Applications of Radiance Fields:** VR-GS extends the utility of modern radiance field techniques (specifically GS) beyond novel view synthesis and static scene representation into dynamic, interactive applications. This opens up new possibilities for:
 - **Interactive Games and Entertainment:** Enabling physically realistic gameplay with deformable virtual objects in captured environments.
 - **Virtual Prototyping and Design:** Allowing designers to manipulate and test virtual objects with real-world physics.
 - **Education and Training:** Creating highly immersive and interactive learning scenarios.
 - **Augmented/Virtual Reality (AR/VR) Content Creation:** Streamlining the process of creating dynamic virtual content from real-world captures for immersive platforms.

- **Foundation for Future Research:** The paper also outlines clear directions for future work, indicating its potential to inspire further advancements. These include addressing computational demands for larger scenes, automating the estimation of physical parameters (currently manual), incorporating a wider range of material types (e.g., fluids, cloth), and leveraging large multimodal models for assessing dynamics fidelity. These areas highlight the potential for VR-GS to serve as a foundational system for more sophisticated and intelligent interactive virtual environments.

In conclusion, VR-GS represents a notable step forward in enabling intuitive, real-time, and physically consistent interaction with high-fidelity 3D content in VR, offering both practical utility and a strong basis for future research in interactive neural graphics.
