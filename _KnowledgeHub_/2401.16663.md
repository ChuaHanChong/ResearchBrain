# VR-GS: A Physical Dynamics-Aware Interactive Gaussian Splatting System in Virtual Reality

> [!summary] Summary
> VR-GS is an interactive system enabling real-time, physics-aware manipulation of 3D Gaussian Splatting content within Virtual Reality, utilizing a novel two-level deformation embedding to ensure realistic dynamics without artifacts. The system achieved significantly faster simulation speeds than prior methods and garnered positive user feedback for immersion and realism, with an 83.5 System Usability Scale score.

## Problem

- Traditional 3D content creation tools are complex, time-intensive, and require specialized expertise, limiting accessibility for non-expert users.
- Existing implicit 3D representations like NeRFs are computationally inefficient for real-time interactive VR applications, especially when handling dynamic deformations and collisions.
- Directly deforming 3D Gaussian Splatting kernels using piece-wise linear deformation fields from a simulated mesh can introduce undesirable visual 'spiky artifacts' during large deformations.

## Method

- A unified system called VR-GS was developed, integrating 3D ==Gaussian Splatting== for high-fidelity rendering with an eXtended Position-based Dynamics (**XPBD**) solver for real-time physics simulation within a VR environment.
- A novel two-level deformation embedding strategy was introduced, where each Gaussian is first enveloped by a local tetrahedron whose vertices are then embedded into a global simulation mesh, smoothing deformation gradients and preventing 'spiky artifacts'.
- An offline asset preparation pipeline was implemented for object segmentation, scene inpainting to fill occluded regions, and generation of tetrahedral simulation meshes from ==3D Gaussian== reconstructions.

## Results

- The proposed two-level embedding successfully eliminated 'spiky artifacts' observed with naive embedding methods during significant stretching or twisting of Gaussian splats.
- VR-GS demonstrated significantly faster simulation times, with frame times of 0.017-0.022 seconds, outperforming PhysGaussian (0.112-0.39s) and PAC-NeRF (0.625-0.813s).
- A user study resulted in an 'excellent' System Usability Scale (SUS) score of 83.5, with participants reporting significantly enhanced immersion and realism from physics-based interactions (average Likert score of 6.1 vs. 4.8 for transform-based, p=0.0227).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The two-level deformation embedding effectively mitigates 'spiky artifacts' by providing a smoother, averaged deformation gradient to individual Gaussians, enhancing the visual realism of deforming objects.
> - Leveraging a coarser tetrahedral mesh to drive XPBD physics on 3D Gaussian Splatting content offers a crucial balance between maintaining high frame rates required for VR and achieving accurate, physically plausible dynamics.
> - Integrating essential auxiliary components like object segmentation, scene inpainting, and dynamic shadow casting is critical for creating a comprehensive, immersive, and visually consistent interactive VR experience from real-world captured scenes.
