# LAGEA: Language Guided Embodied Agents for Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> The LAGEA framework allows embodied robotic agents to learn from failures by converting VLM-generated natural language reflections into temporally grounded, adaptive reward signals for reinforcement learning. This approach achieved an average success rate improvement of 5.3% to 9% on Meta-World MT10 benchmarks and 17% on Robotic Fetch tasks, alongside faster policy convergence.

## Problem

- Difficulty in converting high-level VLM outputs into consistent, granular, and stable reward signals for reinforcement learning in robotic manipulation.
- Lack of a systematic method for embodied agents to diagnose errors, understand why actions failed, and receive temporally grounded feedback in complex, visually rich environments.
- Challenges in temporal credit assignment, where current VLM-derived rewards provide overall outcomes but not precise step-wise feedback on critical moments of failure.

## Method

- Utilizes a VLM to generate structured, schema-constrained natural language feedback for failed trajectories, encoding it into feedback vectors.
- Identifies "key frames" within trajectories using saliency scores derived from image embeddings and their temporal derivatives to localize critical moments of success or failure.
- Aligns language feedback with visual states in a shared embedding space using calibrated contrastive losses and converts this into dense, failure-focused, and adaptively shaped reward signals for ==reinforcement learning==.

## Results

- Achieved an average success rate of **80%** on Meta-World MT10 (fixed goal) and **70.4%** (random goal), representing improvements of **5.3%** and **9%** over baselines, respectively.
- Outperformed state-of-the-art baselines on Robotic Fetch tasks with an average success rate of **51.67%**, an absolute improvement of **17%**.
- Demonstrated significantly faster policy convergence across multiple Meta-World tasks, reaching higher success rates in fewer environment steps compared to existing methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Structured VLM feedback, constrained by a schema, is essential for reducing hallucination and ensuring consistent, actionable guidance in robotic learning.
> - Localizing VLM feedback to "key frames" within a trajectory provides crucial temporal grounding, enabling more precise credit assignment for failures.
> - Dynamically shaping rewards, where VLM feedback provides dense signals during early failures and its influence recedes with increasing competence, improves learning efficiency and stability.
