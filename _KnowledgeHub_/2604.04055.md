# DINO-VO: Learning Where to Focus for Enhanced State Estimation

> [!summary] Summary
> DINO-VO is an end-to-end monocular visual odometry system designed by Fudan University researchers, which learns to prioritize informative features for state estimation. It achieves state-of-the-art accuracy and generalization across diverse datasets by employing an adaptive patch selector and leveraging pre-trained multi-task feature extraction with geometric priors.

## Problem

- Existing VO/SLAM systems often use inefficient heuristic or random strategies for feature selection, leading to computational waste on less informative patches.
- Many learning-based SLAM systems lack direct coupling between learned feature extraction and the downstream pose estimation task, hindering optimal performance.
- Monocular learning-based VO frequently struggles with scale ambiguity and the computational cost of learning robust geometric priors from scratch.

## Method

- Introduces **DINO-VO**, an end-to-end monocular VO system integrating a ==Multi-task Feature Extractor==, an ==Adaptive Patch Selector==, and a ==Sparse Bundle Adjustment Layer==.
- Employs a ==Multi-task Feature Extractor== leveraging a pre-trained ==DINOv2== backbone and ==Depth Anything v2== for concurrent prediction of matching features, context features, inverse depth maps, and prior weight maps.
- Implements a differentiable ==Adaptive Patch Selector== that identifies and selects the most geometrically informative patches for bundle adjustment, trained via ==distillation from posterior weights==.

## Results

- Achieved an average ATE RMSE of **0.18m** on the TartanAir test set, demonstrating a **14%** relative accuracy improvement over DPVO.
- Attained state-of-the-art performance on the KITTI odometry dataset with an average ATE RMSE of **46.32m** (without loop closure) and **22.38m** (with loop closure), and secured the lowest average ATE RMSEs on both TUM-RGBD (**0.081m**) and EuRoC MAV (**0.113m**), consistently outperforming previous learning-based methods.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both the adaptive patch selection and the inverse depth prior are critical for DINO-VO's superior accuracy and stability, particularly in challenging scenarios.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Intelligently selecting high-contribution features via an adaptive, learned mechanism significantly improves efficiency and accuracy in bundle adjustment compared to random sampling.
> - Integrating robust pre-trained models for vision and depth provides strong appearance and geometric priors, enhancing generalization across diverse environments and mitigating monocular scale ambiguity.
> - An end-to-end learning architecture that directly optimizes feature extraction for the state estimation task via differentiable bundle adjustment leads to a more cohesive and performant system.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Visual Odometry (VO) and Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) are fundamental technologies in robotics and autonomous systems, enabling agents to estimate their pose and understand their environment. Monocular visual systems, which rely on a single camera, are particularly relevant due to their low cost and lightweight nature.

Historically, VO/SLAM systems have been dominated by traditional, hand-crafted methods such as MonoSLAM, PTAM, and the ORB-SLAM series. These systems leverage meticulously designed modules for feature extraction (e.g., Shi-Tomasi corners, FAST, ORB features) and backend optimization (e.g., Extended Kalman Filters, non-linear optimization, bundle adjustment). While robust in certain scenarios, they often exhibit limitations in cross-dataset generalization and can be sensitive to hyperparameter tuning due to their reliance on heuristic feature importance assessment.

More recently, deep learning has influenced the VO/SLAM field. Initial integration involved replacing specific modules with learned components, such as context feature extraction, depth prediction, or pose optimization. However, many of these hybrid systems retained hand-crafted elements, preventing true end-to-end learning.

The emergence of fully end-to-end learning-based VO/SLAM systems represents a significant shift. Examples include DeepVO, UnDeepVO, DeepTAM, and more recent NeRF- or 3DGS-based SLAM systems. A notable advancement in this direction is the DROID-SLAM and DPVO series, which replace most traditional components with learning-based methods, achieving improved cross-dataset generalization and reduced reliance on hyperparameters. DPVO, in particular, utilizes sparse optical flow and a dense bundle adjustment layer.

This work, DINO-VO, directly positions itself as an advancement upon the DPVO framework. It addresses a specific inefficiency identified in DPVO: the use of a random patch selection strategy for bundle adjustment. The authors note that this random selection often leads to a high proportion of "useless" patches (those with low contribution to pose optimization), particularly in feature-poor regions like the sky. DINO-VO aims to improve upon end-to-end monocular VO systems by integrating a mechanism to learn *where to focus* feature extraction for enhanced state estimation, thereby bridging a gap between feature learning and the downstream state estimation task.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an end-to-end monocular visual odometry system, DINO-VO, that offers enhanced accuracy, robustness, and scene generalization compared to existing methods, particularly in diverse and challenging environments. This overarching goal is motivated by several identified limitations in the current VO/SLAM landscape:

1. **Inefficiency of Heuristic and Random Feature Selection:** Traditional VO/SLAM systems often rely on heuristic strategies for feature extraction, which can limit their robustness and generalization across varying environments. Even advanced end-to-end learning systems like DPVO employ a random patch selection strategy. The authors highlight that this random approach often includes patches that do not significantly contribute to bundle adjustment, leading to wasted computational effort and suboptimal accuracy. The motivation is to replace this with a learned, adaptive strategy that identifies and prioritizes informative patches.

2. **Lack of Direct Coupling between Feature Learning and State Estimation:** In many learning-based SLAM systems, learned feature extraction modules are designed to enhance feature matching, but they are not directly optimized for downstream tasks like state estimation. This often necessitates additional tuning of optimization modules. DINO-VO seeks to bridge this gap by creating an end-to-end pipeline where feature extraction is inherently linked to and optimized for the bundle adjustment process.

3. **Absence of Geometric Priors in Monocular Learning-based VO:** Previous deep learning-based monocular SLAM systems often prioritize learning appearance features, with limited explicit incorporation of scene geometry, especially reliable depth information. While some systems integrate geometry prediction, doing so from scratch can be computationally intensive and suffer from domain shift issues when trained on limited datasets. The motivation is to leverage pre-trained models to efficiently integrate robust geometric priors, specifically inverse depth, to mitigate monocular scale ambiguity and improve mapping accuracy without requiring extensive geometry-specific training from scratch.

4. **Improving Generalization Across Diverse Environments:** Traditional systems struggle with generalization across different datasets (e.g., synthetic to real-world, indoor to outdoor). While end-to-end learning methods have shown improvements, there is still room for further enhancement. DINO-VO aims to achieve strong generalization by using a robust, pre-trained vision backbone and a learning architecture that can adaptively select features relevant to various scene types.

In summary, DINO-VO aims to overcome these limitations by introducing a novel framework that: (1) incorporates a differentiable adaptive patch selector to focus on high-contribution features; (2) integrates a multi-task feature extraction module, leveraging pre-trained large-scale models to provide both appearance and geometric (inverse depth) information; and (3) enables end-to-end learning where feature extraction is directly informed by and contributes to state estimation via bundle adjustment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DINO-VO is an end-to-end monocular visual odometry system comprising three main components: a Multi-task Feature Extractor, an Adaptive Patch Selector, and a Sparse Bundle Adjustment Layer. The system takes sequential monocular images and camera intrinsics as input and outputs camera poses and a sparse map.

#### 3.1. System Pipeline

The pipeline operates as follows:
1. **Feature Extraction:** For each input image, the Multi-task Feature Extractor produces four types of features: matching features ($M_j$), context features ($F^c_j$), an inverse depth map ($D_j$), and a prior weight map ($W^p_j$). All these outputs are at a quarter resolution of the input image.
2. **Adaptive Patch Selection:** Based on the prior weight map ($W^p_j$) and inverse depth map ($D_j$), the Adaptive Patch Selector identifies and extracts N candidate patches. Each patch is represented by its pixel coordinates (x, y) and inverse depth (d).
3. **Patch Graph Construction:** A bipartite patch graph (G) is constructed by connecting each selected patch to its projections onto neighboring frames within a predefined distance (r). The projection of a patch $P^l_j$ from frame j to frame i is denoted $P^l_{ji}$.
4. **Correlation and Optical Flow Correction:** Matching features ($M_j$) are used to compute a correlation volume ($C$) between each patch and its projected location in other frames. This correlation, along with context features ($F^c$), is fed into a hidden state updater. The updater estimates a posterior weight ($w^l_{ji}$) for each patch projection and a 2D optical flow correction ($\Delta^l_{ji}$).
5. **Sparse Bundle Adjustment:** Finally, the Sparse Bundle Adjustment Layer uses these corrected patch projections and their weights to optimize the camera poses in the factor graph.

#### 3.2. Multi-task Feature Extractor

This module is designed to extract all necessary features from a single RGB image using a single backbone, aiming to avoid the computational overhead of multiple backbones and address the challenge of learning geometry from scratch in monocular settings.

- **Backbone:** The system leverages the ViT-S version of DINOv2, a robust pre-trained vision model, as its feature extraction backbone. This model is chosen for its strong generalization capabilities across diverse datasets, having been trained on millions of unlabeled images, and for ensuring real-time performance.
- **Architecture:** The feature extractor employs a multi-task architecture based on Depth Anything v2. During training, the Depth Anything v2 model is frozen. All four extractor heads (disparity, match, context, and prior weight heads) are simplified, consisting of four Fusion layers and sharing the same Reassemble Layers from Depth Anything v2, rather than using full DPT heads. This design choice minimizes training and inference time and reduces VRAM consumption.
- **Outputs:** The module concurrently predicts:
 - **Matching features ($M_j$):** Used for computing correlations between patches.
 - **Context features ($F^c_j$):** Provide contextual information for the hidden state updater.
 - **Inverse depth map ($D_j$):** Provides geometric priors, crucial for monocular settings.
 - **Prior weight map ($W^p_j$):** Used by the Adaptive Patch Selector to identify important regions.

#### 3.3. Adaptive Patch Selector

This component aims to improve efficiency and accuracy by selecting only the most informative patches for bundle adjustment, addressing the inefficiencies of random patch selection in prior works like DPVO.

- **Inference Phase:**
 1. **Infinite Depth Removal:** The predicted prior weight map ($W^p_j$) is pre-processed by masking out regions where the corresponding pixel's depth in $D_j$ is infinite (or zero inverse depth), as these areas are less useful for pose estimation.
 2. **Peak Selection:** Inspired by SuperPoint, the post-processed prior weight map ($W^{p'}_j$) is divided into n-by-n pixel regions. The position with the maximum prior weight in each region is identified.
 3. **Top-N Selection:** These peak positions are then sorted by their weights, and the top N positions are selected as the final patches for bundle adjustment.
- **Training by Distillation:**
 1. **Grid-Random Strategy:** During training, a grid-random strategy is used to select patches, dividing feature maps into m-by-m regions and randomly extracting p-by-p patches for mapping.
 2. **Ground Truth Generation:** The ground truth for the prior weight head ($w^p_l$) is derived from the maximum posterior weight ($w_l$) of a patch projection to its original frame and neighboring frames (j-1, j, j+1), using ground truth poses.
 3. **Refined Ground Truth:** To handle the prevalence of near-zero weights from random selection, zero weights are discarded, and only one-tenth of non-zero weights below 0.1 are retained as negative samples, ensuring a balanced training signal.
 4. **Loss Function:** An L1 loss ($L_{pw}$) is used to train the prior weight head, distilling knowledge from the hidden state updater's posterior weights. Training of the prior weight head begins after the rest of the model has stabilized (e.g., after 5000 iterations).

#### 3.4. Sparse Bundle Adjustment Layer

This layer optimizes camera poses using the selected patches and their associated information.

- **Objective Function:** The core of the sparse bundle adjustment is an objective function that minimizes the squared difference between the corrected patch projection ($\hat{P}^l_{ji} + \Delta^l_{ji}$) and the reprojected patch ($\omega(T_i, T_j, P^l_j)$), weighted by the posterior weight ($w^l_{ji}$) for each edge in the factor graph.
- **Inverse Depth Initialization:** Unlike DPVO, which uses a constant inverse depth, DINO-VO leverages the inverse depth priors from the disparity head. Initially, all inverse depths are set to 0.5 for stability. After initialization, the inverse depth of each patch ($d^l_j$) is rescaled based on the median inverse depth of optimized patches from previous frames, ensuring consistent scale.

#### 3.5. Objective Function

The overall objective function for end-to-end training combines pose supervision loss ($L_{pose}$), flow supervision loss ($L_{flow}$), and the prior weight loss ($L_{pw}$) with weighted coefficients ($\lambda_{pose}$, $\lambda_{flow}$, $\lambda_{pw}$).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The DINO-VO system underwent extensive evaluation across multiple datasets, including synthetic (TartanAir), indoor (TUM-RGBD, EuRoC), and outdoor (KITTI) environments. The results demonstrate that DINO-VO consistently achieves high accuracy and exhibits strong generalization, often surpassing existing state-of-the-art traditional and learning-based SLAM methods.

#### 4.1. Performance on TartanAir Dataset

- **Validation Split:** DINO-VO achieved an AUC of 0.85 in them error window, outperforming DPVO (0.8) and DROID-SLAM (0.71).
- **Test Split:** On the TartanAir test set, DINO-VO exhibited the lowest average Absolute Trajectory Error (ATE) RMSE (0.18m), improving accuracy relatively by 14% compared to DPVO (0.21m). The system showed particular strength in outdoor synthetic scenarios (e.g., ME001, ME002, ME003), which include forest and town environments.
- **Qualitative Reconstruction:** The sparse 3D maps generated by DINO-VO were observed to be more informative than those from DPVO, especially in texture-rich areas like floor patterns, which DPVO's random patch selection often failed to capture effectively.

#### 4.2. Performance on KITTI Odometry Dataset

- DINO-VO demonstrated state-of-the-art performance, achieving an average ATE RMSE of 46.32m without loop closure, and significantly improving to 22.38m with loop closure (Ours*). This represents an improvement of 7.29m compared to DPVO (53.61m) in the odometry setting.
- The system performed effectively in large-scale outdoor areas (Sequence 00) and challenging degenerated scenarios (Sequence 01).
- **Qualitative Patch Selection and Trajectory:** Visual comparisons indicated that DINO-VO's adaptive patch selector focused on meaningful objects (e.g., pipelines, buildings) rather than feature-poor regions like the sky, unlike DPVO. Trajectory comparisons on KITTI Sequence 00 showed DINO-VO achieving a more accurate trajectory in segments with high vehicle speeds and noisy features, attributing this to its selection of higher-weight features.

#### 4.3. Performance on TUM-RGBD Dataset

- DINO-VO achieved the lowest average ATE RMSE of 0.081m across all nine tested sequences, outperforming DROID-VO (0.098m) and DPVO (0.114m).
- The system provided reliable and accurate results in challenging indoor conditions, including scenarios with blur and shake, indicating robustness and adaptability.

#### 4.4. Performance on EuRoC MAV Dataset

- DINO-VO achieved the lowest average ATE RMSE of 0.113m across all sequences, outperforming DPVO (0.127m) and DROID-VO (0.186m).
- The system exhibited consistent accuracy and reliability compared to other methods that showed more variability across sequences.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies

- **Patch Selection:**
 - Statistical analysis on EuRoC MH03 showed that DINO-VO selected a higher percentage of high-weight patches for bundle adjustment compared to the random selection strategy.
 - Replacing DINO-VO's adaptive patch selection with random selection (Ours(random) in Table 4) resulted in an increased average ATE RMSE (0.175m vs 0.113m for Ours). This modified system struggled significantly with sequences involving rapid rotations (e.g., V203), where random selection led to system instability and a substantial increase in ATE.
- **Depth Prior:**
 - Removing the inverse depth prior from the Multi-task Feature Extractor and initializing patches with a constant inverse depth (Ours(w/o depth) in Table 4) led to a degraded average ATE RMSE (0.184m vs 0.113m for Ours).
 - Performance particularly deteriorated in challenging sequences with pure rotations and minimal translation (e.g., V103, V203), highlighting the importance of stable inverse depth priors for system stability in such conditions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The DINO-VO system presents advancements in end-to-end monocular visual odometry with several significant implications:

1. **Enhanced Accuracy and Robustness through Adaptive Feature Selection:** By introducing a differentiable adaptive patch selector, DINO-VO addresses a key inefficiency in previous end-to-end systems that relied on random feature sampling. This learned strategy allows the system to intelligently focus on geometrically informative regions, leading to more stable and accurate state estimation, particularly in environments with sparse features or challenging dynamics (e.g., rapid rotations, texture-poor areas). This approach could reduce computational overhead by processing more "useful" features, improving real-time performance.

2. **Improved Generalization via Multi-task Feature Extraction and Depth Priors:** The integration of a multi-task feature extractor leveraging a robust, pre-trained vision backbone (DINOv2) and pre-trained depth estimation (Depth Anything v2) contributes significantly to cross-dataset generalization. By providing reliable inverse depth priors, the system effectively mitigates scale ambiguities inherent in monocular VO and enhances mapping accuracy, without requiring extensive self-supervised geometry learning from scratch. This makes DINO-VO more adaptable to diverse real-world environments, from synthetic to indoor to complex outdoor scenes.

3. **Bridging Feature Learning and State Estimation:** DINO-VO's end-to-end architecture directly links feature learning with the downstream task of pose optimization. The distillation training for the prior weight head ensures that feature extraction is optimized to select patches that are most beneficial for bundle adjustment. This deeper integration reduces reliance on heuristic methods and manual tuning, contributing to a more cohesive and self-optimizing VO system.

4. **Advancement in Learning-based VO/SLAM:** DINO-VO demonstrates that careful design of learning-based modules, specifically the patch selection and feature extraction, can lead to substantial improvements over prior end-to-end learning methods like DPVO. Its state-of-the-art performance across multiple benchmarks suggests a promising direction for developing more reliable and scalable SLAM systems.

The potential impact of DINO-VO extends to various applications requiring robust and accurate visual odometry, including autonomous vehicles, robotics, augmented reality, and drone navigation. Its ability to maintain real-time performance while achieving high accuracy and generalization, even in challenging conditions, suggests that DINO-VO could facilitate the deployment of advanced monocular perception systems in a broader range of real-world scenarios.
