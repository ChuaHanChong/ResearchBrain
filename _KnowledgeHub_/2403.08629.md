# Scaling Up Dynamic Human-Scene Interaction Modeling

> [!summary] Summary
> TRUMANS is a 15-hour, 100-scene motion-capture dataset of whole-body human-scene interaction with part-level dynamic object tracking, scaled by digital-twin scene replication and IK-based augmentation. Paired with it, an autoregressive diffusion model conditioned on a local scene-occupancy perceiver and frame-wise action labels generates arbitrary-length interactions in real time, nearly indistinguishable from motion capture.

## Problem

- Early interaction datasets from RGBD video yield noisy poses and limited scale, while optical MoCap sets stay small, static-scene, or single-object, missing cluttered real interaction.
- Synthetic datasets are cheap and adaptable but fail to capture realistic dynamic 3D contact and per-part object pose tracking inside their simulated environments.
- Prior generators condition on a single action description for a fixed-length clip, so they cannot take new instructions mid-sequence or produce arbitrary-length scene-aware motion.

## Method

- Scene-aware capture pipeline: synthetic furnished scenes physically replicated with affordance placeholders, VICON-tracked markered objects, vertex-to-vertex ==SMPL-X== fitting giving per-vertex contact labels and photorealistic multi-view and ego renders.
- MoCap augmentation pipeline: contact target-joint relocation, temporal trajectory smoothing, then a clipped ==CCD inverse-kinematics== solver recomputes motion so interactions stay valid when objects are resized or swapped.
- ==Autoregressive motion diffusion==: a ==DDPM== transformer inpaints each episode from the previous frames and subgoal masks, conditioned on a ==ViT== local occupancy perceiver and a progress-indicator action encoder.

## Results

- Static setting: contact **0.992** and max penetration **11.74** beat cVAE, SceneDiff and GMD baselines; dropping augmentation raises mean penetration from **1.820** to **2.010**.
- Dynamic setting: FID falls to **0.313** versus **0.512** for GOAL and **0.711** for IMoS; removing the action progress indicator collapses FID to **2.104** and diversity to **1.318**.
- Only about **25.8%** of participants spotted the real MoCap clip against **20%** chance; adding the dataset to 3DPW training cut MPVE from **101.3** to **78.5**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Physically replicating synthetic scenes inside an optical MoCap stage buys capture fidelity and renderable photorealistic ground truth at once, loosening the usual quality-versus-scale trade-off.
> - Coarse locally queried voxel occupancy suffices for collision-aware generation and is roughly 300 times cheaper than exact mesh queries, so discretization costs little quality.
> - Long-horizon conditioning needs explicit phase information: a scalar per-frame progress signal on action labels prevents the collapse seen when actions span multiple generated segments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The intricate relationship between humans and their environments, known as Human-Scene Interaction (HSI), is a cornerstone of various fields, including computer graphics, robotics, virtual reality, and human behavior analysis. This paper directly addresses two long-standing and critical challenges within this landscape: the scarcity of high-quality, comprehensive datasets for HSI and the limitations of existing methods for synthesizing realistic and controllable human motions within complex 3D scenes.

Historically, HSI research has been hampered by data-related issues. Early datasets like PiGraphs and PROX initiated exploration but struggled with scalability and data quality, particularly in capturing dynamic interactions. While high-fidelity motion capture (MoCap) systems like VICON provide excellent human motion data, they often operate in controlled, static environments, lacking the diversity and immersive HSI required for real-world scenarios. Conversely, scalable datasets derived from RGBD videos offer broader utility but frequently suffer from lower quality in human pose and object tracking. The recent advent of synthetic datasets (e.g., BEDLAM, CIRCLE) promised cost efficiency and adaptability but often fell short in encapsulating the full spectrum of realistic HSIs, especially concerning dynamic 3D contacts and object tracking within simulated environments.

In terms of HSI generation, progress has been made with models like conditional Variational Auto-Encoders (cVAE) and diffusion models for synthesizing human body poses and temporal motion sequences. Techniques for generating arbitrary-length motions, such as autoregressive methods and anchor frame generation, have also emerged. Furthermore, researchers have explored enhancing controllability through semantic guidance, including action labels and language descriptions. However, existing methods often struggle with generating physically plausible and diverse long-term interactions that are deeply cognizant of complex 3D scene geometry and dynamic objects, especially with zero-shot generalizability to unseen environments.

This work, through the introduction of the TRUMANS dataset and its accompanying motion synthesis framework, positions itself as a significant advancement. It directly confronts the limitations of prior work by offering a dataset that uniquely balances quality and scalability, capturing extensive, high-fidelity human and dynamic object interactions across a large number of diverse indoor scenes using optical MoCap in physically replicated virtual environments. Furthermore, its diffusion-based autoregressive model combines the strengths of advanced generative models with enhanced scene and action conditioning, enabling the synthesis of highly controllable, physically plausible, and arbitrarily long HSI sequences. By demonstrating robust zero-shot generalizability and applicability to other image-based tasks, this research pushes the boundaries of HSI modeling towards more realistic, versatile, and context-aware virtual humans.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this research stems from the recognized challenges in dynamic Human-Scene Interaction (HSI) modeling: the pervasive scarcity of high-quality, diverse, and scalable datasets, and the resultant difficulty in developing advanced motion synthesis methods capable of generating realistic and controllable human behaviors within complex 3D environments. Existing datasets either lack the fidelity of motion capture, the diversity of scene configurations, or the intricate details of dynamic human-object interactions (HOI), particularly concerning realistic contact. Consequently, state-of-the-art motion synthesis models often struggle with producing long-term, physically plausible, and context-aware interactions that can generalize to novel scenes.

Against this backdrop, the authors set out to achieve several key objectives:

1. **Develop TRUMANS (Tracking Human Actions in Scenes):** The foremost objective was to create the most comprehensive motion-captured HSI dataset to date. This dataset aimed to overcome the limitations of existing resources by providing:
 - **Extensive Scale and Diversity:** Encompassing over 15 hours of human interactions across 100 indoor scenes.
 - **High Fidelity:** Capturing whole-body human motions and part-level object dynamics with a strong emphasis on the realism of contact.
 - **Realistic Context:** Replicating physical environments into exact virtual models and applying extensive augmentations to appearance and motion for both humans and objects while maintaining interaction fidelity.
 - **Versatile Annotations:** Including photorealistic RGBD renderings in multi-view and ego-view, segmentation masks, and per-vertex 3D contact annotations suitable for various perceptual tasks.

2. **Devise a Novel HSI Motion Synthesis Method:** Utilizing the richness of the TRUMANS dataset, the second core objective was to propose a computational model capable of generating HSI sequences that are:
 - **Arbitrary Length:** Efficiently generating motions of any desired duration.
 - **Scene-Aware:** Incorporating 3D scene context to ensure physically plausible interactions and collision avoidance.
 - **Action-Controllable:** Allowing for nuanced control through frame-wise action labels, empowering the model to accept instructions anytime.
 - **Highly Realistic:** Producing motions that closely mimic original motion-captured sequences, exhibiting naturalness in interactions and collision avoidance.
 - **Zero-Shot Generalizable:** Demonstrating robust performance on unseen 3D scene datasets.

3. **Benchmark and Validate:** A crucial objective was to comprehensively evaluate both the TRUMANS dataset and the proposed motion synthesis method. This involved:
 - **Dataset Impact:** Demonstrating that TRUMANS significantly improves the performance of existing state-of-the-art HSI approaches.
 - **Model Superiority:** Quantitatively and qualitatively showcasing the proposed method's superiority over existing motion synthesis techniques in terms of quality, realism, and generalizability.
 - **Broader Utility:** Benchmarking TRUMANS for other related tasks, such as human pose and contact estimation, to establish its versatility as a research asset.

By achieving these objectives, the authors aim to significantly advance the field of HSI modeling, providing both a critical data resource and a powerful tool for generating highly realistic and controllable human behaviors in diverse virtual environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research encompasses two major pillars: the creation of the TRUMANS dataset and the development of a diffusion-based autoregressive model for HSI motion synthesis.

#### 3.1. TRUMANS Dataset Creation

The TRUMANS dataset is designed to be the most comprehensive motion-captured HSI dataset, balancing high-quality capture with large-scale diversity.

- **Scale and Diversity:** It comprises over 15 hours of human interactions across 100 distinct indoor scenes (e.g., dining rooms, living rooms, bedrooms, kitchens). These scenes incorporate 20 different common object types, each with a minimum of 5 instances, ranging from static items (sofas, beds) to dynamic objects (bottles) and articulated items (laptops, cabinets). Seven participants (4 male, 3 female) enacted various actions.
- **Scene-aware Motion Capture:**
 - **Virtual-Real Alignment:** 3D scenes and objects from datasets like 3D-FRONT and BlenderKit are replicated within a physical environment equipped with a VICON optical MoCap system. Real-world placeholders are meticulously created to correspond to the affordances of synthetic objects, ensuring natural human interactions.
 - **Precise Tracking:** All movable objects are tagged with VICON markers for precise pose tracking. Actors perform randomly selected actions from a pre-defined pool.
 - **Post-capture Processing:** Human poses are converted into the SMPL-X format via a vertex-to-vertex optimization technique, ensuring accurate 3D ground truths. Per-vertex contact annotations are calculated by measuring vertex-to-vertex distances between human and object meshes. Blender is used to render photorealistic RGBD videos from multiple views and ego-centric perspectives, alongside segmentation masks. Over 200 digital human models from Character Creator 4 are incorporated for appearance diversity.
- **MoCap Data Augmentation:** This pipeline is crucial for adapting captured human motions to variations in 3D scene objects while maintaining physical plausibility, especially in complex scenarios.
 - **Calculate Target Joint:** Identifies contact points between human joints and object meshes. When an object is transformed or replaced, the target joint's position is adjusted to maintain the original contact relationship.
 - **Refine Trajectory:** To smooth abrupt changes from target joint calculations or Inverse Kinematic (IK) computations, temporal smoothing with a linearly decreasing norm is applied to joint offsets within a defined proximity window. This ensures seamless motion transitions, particularly across multiple object interactions.
 - **Recompute Motion with IK:** Human motion is recomputed using the smoothed trajectories via an enhanced CCD-based IK solver. This solver incorporates clipping and regularizations on bone movements, increasing rotational limits for bones further from the root to enhance natural motion fluidity and reduce jitteriness.

#### 3.2. Autoregressive Motion Diffusion Model

The model generates HSI sequences that are physically plausible and highly controllable, capable of arbitrary lengths in real time.

- **Problem Formulation:** The goal is to synthesize a human motion sequence (SMPL-X parameters) and corresponding dynamic object pose sequences, conditioned on a 3D scene (voxel grid), a goal location (2D for navigation, 3D for joint-specific control), and frame-wise action labels (multi-hot vectors).
- **Autoregressive Diffusion Strategy:** Long motion sequences are generated progressively in "episodes" (e.g., L_epi frames). Each new episode extends from the final `k` frames of the previous one, with noise zeroed out on these transition frames using a mask (M_trans). The model then "inpaints" the remaining unmasked frames of the episode.
- **Conditional Diffusion Model:** A standard Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Model (DDPM) is employed. Gaussian noise is added to the unmasked components of the initial human joint data. A neural network (ϵ_θ) is trained to predict this noise, allowing a reversed diffusion process to gradually denoise and generate the motion.
- **Transformer Architecture:** The diffusion model utilizes a Transformer, where the first token encodes information about the diffusion step, scene, and action. Subsequent tokens represent noisy joint locations for each frame. After sampling, joint locations are translated into SMPL-X parameters via a lightweight MLP and further refined through an optimization process.
- **Object Interaction Optimization:** After human motion generation, the trajectory of interacting objects is optimized to ensure natural Human-Object Interactions (HOIs) by minimizing the variance in distance between the object and the interacting hand.
- **Local Scene Perceiver:** This component conditions the motion generation on the local 3D scene context. It analyzes a local occupancy grid centered around the current episode's subgoal (G_i), extending vertically from 0 to 1.8m and oriented with the agent's pelvis yaw. This voxel grid is then encoded using a Vision Transformer (ViT), providing a scene embedding to the diffusion model. This discretization balances real-time efficiency with capturing sufficient scene detail for collision avoidance.
- **Frame-wise Action Embedding:** To provide fine-grained control, the model incorporates frame-wise action labels. A novel "progress indicator" (A_ind) is integrated into these labels, which is a real number (0-1) appended to the action label, representing the action's advancement over time. This allows the model to understand the temporal evolution of actions, even if they span multiple episodes. A Transformer encoder processes these progress-augmented action labels to generate the final action embedding.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents compelling findings, validating both the quality of the TRUMANS dataset and the efficacy of the proposed motion synthesis method through extensive quantitative and qualitative evaluations, including human studies and applications to additional image-based tasks.

#### 4.1. TRUMANS Dataset Efficacy

The paper asserts that TRUMANS markedly improves the performance of existing state-of-the-art approaches. This is evident through its application in enhancing motion synthesis baselines and its utility in image-based tasks. The dataset's diversity, high-quality MoCap, and detailed contact annotations establish it as a valuable asset for a broad range of HSI research.

#### 4.2. Motion Synthesis Performance

- **Qualitative Results:** Visualizations (Figure 4) demonstrate the method's proficiency in complex scenarios. It can initiate context-aware motions in diverse environments, dynamically interact with objects, adeptly avoid collisions, and robustly synthesize long-term motions, particularly in HOI scenarios involving dynamic object manipulation (e.g., reading a book, opening a cabinet, picking up a phone). The supplementary video further highlights these strengths and the real-time control capabilities.
- **Static Setting (Locomotion and Scene-level Interaction):** In evaluations concerning locomotion and interaction with static objects, the proposed method, trained on TRUMANS, consistently surpasses established baselines such as cVAE, SceneDiff, and GMD across most metrics. Notably, it achieves a Contact score of 0.992 and significantly lower Penetration metrics (mean 1.820, max 11.74) compared to baselines. An ablation study revealed that disabling data augmentation led to increased penetration, underscoring the critical role of the augmentation pipeline in producing physically plausible motions. Compared to models trained on PROX, the improvements are substantial, highlighting TRUMANS as a superior resource for HSI research.
- **Dynamic Setting (Object-level Interaction):** For interactions with dynamic objects, our approach demonstrates superior performance in 3D HOI generation. It achieves a lower FID score (0.313) and a higher Diversity score (2.693) compared to IMoS and GOAL, indicating better quality and variety in generated motions. Crucially, its scene penetration (11.74) is significantly lower than that of GRAB-trained methods, which often show high penetration rates (e.g., 34.10 for GOAL, 37.48 for IMoS), indicating TRUMANS's capability to capture more detailed and physically accurate interactions. An ablation study revealed that the absence of the progress indicator (A_ind) resulted in method failure (FID 2.104, Diversity 1.318), affirming its importance for long-term action progression.
- **Zero-Shot Generalizability:** The method exhibits remarkable zero-shot generalizability on a diverse range of unseen 3D scene datasets, including PROX, Replica, ScanNet, and ScanNet++. It produces motions that closely mimic original motion-captured sequences without prior training on these specific environments.
- **Human Studies:** A novel MoCap-differentiating human study provided compelling evidence of the generated motions' realism. Only about a quarter of participants (25.8% for static, 22.6% for dynamic settings) could correctly distinguish the synthesized motions from real MoCap data. This low Success Rate of Discrimination (SucRate-Dis) is close to random guessing (1/5 or 20%), suggesting that the synthesized motions are nearly indistinguishable from high-quality MoCap data. Comparative evaluations against other recent methods (SAMP, DIMOS, LAMA, and Wang et al.) showed our model's superiority, outperforming the second-best model by over 30% in participant preference.
- **Real-time Control:** The model can sample an episode of motion (1.6 seconds at 10 FPS) in 0.7 seconds on an A800 GPU. An incremental sampling strategy (initially 2 frames, then exponentially increasing) helps minimize delays for new control signals, balancing real-time control with smooth motion continuity.

#### 4.3. Additional Image-based Tasks

TRUMANS, with its photorealistic renderings and per-vertex 3D contact annotations, proved valuable for enhancing image-based tasks.
- **3D Human Mesh Estimation:** Integrating TRUMANS data with the 3DPW dataset for training the state-of-the-art method by Ma et al. (and other methods like I2L and SGRE) significantly improved performance, reducing metrics like MPVE, MPJPE, and PA-MPJPE. For instance, combining 3DPW with TRUMANS (1:1 ratio) reduced MPVE from 101.3 to 78.5 for Ma et al..
- **3D Contact Estimation:** When combined with RICH and DAMON datasets for training BSTRO and DECO respectively, TRUMANS enhanced contact estimation performance, particularly reducing geodesic error (e.g., from 10.27 to 9.459 with RICH, and 25.06 to 18.87 with DAMON at 1:1 ratio). These results underscore the benefit of combining synthetic data from TRUMANS with real-world data for image-based perception tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "Scaling Up Dynamic Human-Scene Interaction Modeling" carries significant implications for the fields of artificial intelligence, computer graphics, and robotics, marking a substantial step forward in creating believable and interactive virtual agents.

**Significance:**

1. **Addressing Data Scarcity and Quality:** The introduction of **TRUMANS** is a monumental contribution. By providing the most comprehensive motion-captured Human-Scene Interaction (HSI) dataset to date, it directly tackles the long-standing challenges of data scarcity, low quality, and lack of diversity that have hindered progress in HSI modeling. Its emphasis on dynamic and articulated object interactions, coupled with precise contact annotations and photorealistic renderings, sets a new benchmark for dataset quality and utility. This dataset will serve as a foundational resource, accelerating future research by providing a robust environment for training and evaluating models.
2. **Advancing Motion Synthesis Capabilities:** The proposed **diffusion-based autoregressive motion synthesis method** represents a significant leap in generative AI for human motion. Its ability to produce arbitrary-length HSI sequences in real-time, conditioned on detailed 3D scene context and fine-grained action labels, offers an unprecedented level of control and realism. The demonstrated zero-shot generalizability to unseen environments is particularly impactful, indicating a robust understanding of interaction dynamics rather than mere memorization.
3. **Benchmarking and Versatility:** Beyond motion synthesis, the paper showcases TRUMANS's versatility by demonstrating its utility in improving performance for image-based tasks like 3D human mesh estimation and 3D contact estimation. This highlights the dataset's broad applicability and its potential to foster advancements across various computer vision domains.
4. **Closing the Reality Gap:** The human studies, revealing that synthesized motions are nearly indistinguishable from real MoCap data, underscore the extraordinary realism achieved by the proposed method. This achievement brings AI-generated human behavior much closer to human-level authenticity.

**Potential Impact:**

1. **Computer Graphics and Animation:** The technology can revolutionize character animation in video games, virtual reality (VR), augmented reality (AR), and film production. It enables animators to generate highly realistic, scene-aware, and controllable character movements with unprecedented efficiency, greatly reducing manual labor and enhancing immersive experiences.
2. **Robotics:** For humanoid robots and intelligent agents, the ability to synthesize physically plausible human-object interactions in diverse environments is invaluable for learning and imitation. It can facilitate the development of robots that interact more naturally and safely with human environments and tools.
3. **Virtual and Augmented Reality:** Creating believable virtual inhabitants and interactive scenarios is central to VR/AR. This work provides the tools to populate virtual worlds with agents that exhibit natural, context-aware behaviors, making virtual experiences more immersive and engaging.
4. **Human Behavior Analysis and Simulation:** Researchers can leverage this technology to simulate complex human behaviors in various environments for psychological studies, ergonomic design, or accessibility assessments, leading to better understanding and design of human-centric systems.
5. **AI Research:** The dataset and methodology provide a fertile ground for exploring more complex aspects of HSI, such as predictive modeling of human intent, multi-agent interactions, and integration with advanced reasoning capabilities.

**Limitations:**

Despite its significant advancements, the paper acknowledges a key limitation: the method's current inability to generate human-object interaction behaviors beyond those present in its training set. For actions unfamiliar to the model, such as "climbing off a table," it may produce unrealistic interactions or object penetrations. This highlights a common challenge in data-driven generative models, suggesting that while the dataset is extensive, the infinite variety of human interaction still requires either broader data coverage or more sophisticated high-level reasoning capabilities to truly generalize to novel, out-of-distribution actions. Future work could explore incorporating symbolic knowledge, physics-based priors, or more abstract action representations to overcome this.

In conclusion, this research not only delivers a critical dataset for the HSI community but also introduces a powerful generative model that sets a new standard for realism and control in human motion synthesis. Its contributions pave the way for more intelligent virtual agents and a deeper understanding of human interaction in digital and robotic contexts.
