# Grasp as You Dream: Imitating Functional Grasping from Generated Human Demonstrations

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from KTH Royal Institute of Technology and Southern University of Science and Technology developed GraspDreamer, a framework enabling robots to perform functional grasping by imitating generated human demonstrations. The system achieved over 70% real-world success rates and surpassed specialized baselines in simulation across various robot hands for both grasping and short-horizon manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Existing data-driven robotic functional grasping methods struggle to generalize to the vast diversity of objects and tasks in open-world environments.
- Developing generalist robots for functional grasping is hindered by the labor-intensive and costly requirement for large-scale, robot-specific data collection.
- Translating generalized knowledge from foundation models into precise, executable robot actions for functional grasping often requires substantial task-specific data or fine-tuning.

## Method

- **GraspDreamer** leverages ==visual generative models (VGMs)== to synthesize human functional grasping demonstrations based on scene observations and language instructions.
- It extracts and refines human hand trajectories from generated videos, optimizing for ==metric scale and pose consistency== to address VGM inaccuracies.
- A ==taxonomy-aware functional retargeting== process transfers optimized human motion to target robot hands, incorporating ==VLM-predicted grasp types== and contact refinement for physical plausibility.

## Results

- GraspDreamer demonstrated superior performance in simulation, achieving **78.6%** and **79.5%** success on object and task generalization respectively for parallel-jaw grippers, and consistently outperforming specialized baselines for dexterous hands (e.g., **80.2%** for Shadow hand on kitchenware).
- In real-world experiments, the system achieved overall success rates above **70%** (**76/100** for parallel-jaw, **73/100** for Allegro Hand) for functional grasping on diverse household objects.
- The approach successfully extended to short-horizon manipulation, achieving **70-80%** success on tasks like 'Take Flower from Vase' and 'Pull Tissue', and its generated data enabled Diffusion Policies to achieve **73.3-86.7%** success rates for visuomotor policy learning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Visual generative models, pre-trained on internet-scale human interaction data, implicitly encode generalized interaction priors that can be effectively leveraged for robot functional grasping.
> - Effective functional grasping requires understanding both 'where' and 'how' to grasp, emphasizing task-oriented semantic intent over mere functional region localization.
> - A multi-stage framework combining human demonstration generation, hand motion estimation, and taxonomy-aware retargeting can bridge the gap between human interaction priors and executable robot actions across diverse robot embodiments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation faces challenges in enabling generalist robots to perform functional grasping in diverse, open-world environments. Functional grasping refers to the act of grasping an object in a manner that facilitates its intended use, rather than merely lifting it. This requires understanding both the object's geometry and the task's semantic intent.

Existing approaches to functional grasping can be broadly categorized. One line of research involves data-driven methods trained on curated functional grasping datasets. While these methods have demonstrated performance on closed-set benchmarks, their ability to generalize to novel objects and tasks in open-world settings is often limited due to the inherent variability in object geometry, appearance, and task requirements.

More recently, researchers have explored incorporating foundation models into their pipelines to enhance generalization. Some methods fine-tune these large pre-trained models for specific functional grasping and manipulation tasks, while others leverage pre-trained models to provide grasp-related knowledge. However, both directions frequently necessitate substantial data collection efforts to bridge the gap between the generic priors learned by foundation models and the specific, executable actions required for robot manipulation. This data collection can be labor-intensive and challenging to scale for broad applications.

This paper introduces GraspDreamer as an alternative approach that seeks to overcome the data collection bottleneck. It situates itself within the trend of leveraging generative models for robotics. Specifically, GraspDreamer distinguishes itself by utilizing visual generative models (VGMs) pre-trained on internet-scale human interaction data as a source of generalized interaction priors. Unlike prior work that relies on extensive robot-specific data collection or simulation-first synthesis, GraspDreamer aims to enable zero-shot functional grasping by imitating human demonstrations synthesized by VGMs. This approach is intended to provide a unified framework adaptable across different robot hands, contrasting with concurrent studies that may focus on specific robot embodiments or lack explicit functional and contact constraints during retargeting.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a method that enables generalist robots to perform functional grasping in everyday, open-world environments without requiring labor-intensive, large-scale robot-specific data collection.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges in the current landscape of robotic functional grasping:
1. **Limited Generalization:** Existing data-driven methods, despite performing well on specific datasets, struggle to generalize to the vast diversity of objects and tasks encountered in open-world environments. The variability in object geometry, appearance, and task intent often exceeds the scope of collected training data.
2. **Prohibitive Data Collection:** To achieve broader generalization, many current approaches, particularly those incorporating foundation models, necessitate large-scale data collection. This process is time-consuming, expensive, and logistically complex, creating a significant barrier to developing truly generalist robots.
3. **Gap Between Priors and Actions:** While foundation models offer generalized knowledge, translating this knowledge into precise, executable robot actions for functional grasping often requires substantial task-specific data or fine-tuning, diminishing the "zero-shot" potential.
4. **Capturing Functional Intent:** Functional grasping requires understanding not just how to physically grasp an object, but *where* and *how* to grasp it to facilitate its intended use. This functional intent is implicitly encoded in human behavior but is difficult to capture and transfer directly to robots.

The core hypothesis motivating GraspDreamer is that visual generative models (VGMs), pre-trained on internet-scale human interaction data, implicitly encode generalized priors about how humans interact with the physical world. By leveraging these models, it may be possible to synthesize human demonstrations that reflect task-oriented motion and semantic understanding. The key idea is to combine these generalized human interaction priors with embodiment-specific action optimization to generate physically executable functional grasps with minimal effort and without requiring new robot data collection. This aims to provide an efficient and scalable solution for functional grasping and manipulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

GraspDreamer is a VGM-based framework designed for zero-shot functional grasping. Given an RGB-D scene observation, a language instruction specifying the task intent, and a URDF description of the robot end-effector, the system generates a grasp plan. The methodology is structured into three main stages: Human Demonstration Generation, Human Hand Motion Estimation, and Human-to-Robot (H2R) Functional Retargeting.

#### 3.1. A. Human Demonstration Generation

This stage focuses on creating visual human demonstrations that guide the subsequent robot actions.
- **Task-Relevant Information Extraction:** To mitigate ambiguity in natural language instructions, a Vision-Language Model (VLM) is employed. It processes the scene observation and language instruction to extract a task label (T). Based on T, the VLM identifies the most relevant object (O) and its functional part (pT). This structured extraction (task-object-part sequence) is intended to regularize the subsequent visual generation.
- **Visual Demonstration Generation:** With the extracted task-relevant information, GraspDreamer constructs a prompt using a fixed template (e.g., "A human hand grasps the P of O to T") combined with the original language instruction. This prompt is fed to a visual generative model (VGM) to synthesize an RGB-D human demonstration video (V_H). This demonstration serves as a universal intermediate representation adaptable to various robot embodiments. To address potential visual hallucinations by the VGM, a closed-loop verification process is implemented, where keyframes from the generated demonstration are periodically sent to the VLM for consistency checks against the task intent.
- **Depth Calibration:** Although the Video-Depth-Anything (VDA) model is used to predict temporally consistent metric depth for each frame in V_H, monocular depth estimation inherently suffers from scale ambiguity. To address this, the system leverages the ground-truth depth information of the target object from the initial scene observation (o). By reconstructing the target object point cloud using both true and predicted depths, a rigid alignment is estimated via a weighted Umeyama algorithm. This transform is then applied to all frames in V_H to calibrate the depth maps, providing more precise metric depth for grasping.

#### 3.2. B. Human Hand Motion Estimation

This stage extracts and refines the human hand's trajectory from the generated demonstrations.
- **Hand Trajectory Extraction:** For each frame of the generated human demonstration (V_H), the HaMeR model is used to estimate the human hand's state. This includes the 6-Degrees-of-Freedom (DoF) wrist pose (orientation and translation) in the camera frame and the articulated MANO hand pose parameters. From these parameters, 3D hand joint locations are derived. Object meshes are optionally reconstructed using off-the-shelf models, and RANSAC and ICP are applied to estimate the scale and transformation of the reconstructed object.
- **Hand Trajectory Optimization:** A challenge arises because VGMs lack precise metric-scale awareness, potentially leading to discrepancies in hand-object size ratios. Directly fitting a fixed MANO template can result in scale mismatch and depth-axis drift. To mitigate this, the method optimizes a per-frame scale factor (σ_hand) for global correction and a 6D rigid transform (T_hand) for local refinement. This optimization involves two main terms:
 - **ICP-Based Geometric Alignment:** Minimizes an Iterative Closest Point (ICP) objective between the transformed MANO point cloud and the observed hand point cloud extracted from the frame.
 - **Depth-Rendering Consistency:** Enforces consistency in image space by rasterizing the transformed MANO mesh to generate a rendered depth map, which is then compared to the observed hand depth map. The local refinement transform (T_hand) is regularized to remain close to an identity transform.

#### 3.3. C. H2R Functional Retargeting

This final stage transfers the optimized human hand motion to the target robot hand, ensuring functional and physical plausibility.
- **Hand Affordance Reasoning:** To enable functional retargeting, GraspDreamer first infers task-specific hand affordances. This includes determining which robot digits should establish task-relevant contacts and how inter-finger coordination should realize the grasp function. Unlike methods relying on fixed manual mappings, a VLM is used for in-context learning. It is prompted with few-shot examples comprising grasp images, object/task/part labels, grasp taxonomy labels (based on 12 basic types from), robot hand URDFs, and human-defined optimal mappings. At inference, the VLM predicts both the grasp taxonomy and the finger mapping for the target robot hand.
- **Taxonomy-Aware Kinematic Retargeting:** The predicted hand affordances are incorporated into the retargeting process. For each frame, the robot joint configuration (q_t) is optimized by minimizing a taxonomy-aware vector matching loss. This loss compares vectors computed via forward kinematics of the robot hand to corresponding reference vectors derived from the human hand joints. Crucially, taxonomy-dependent weights are applied to different vector pairs, emphasizing digit roles and coupling patterns relevant to the inferred grasp type (e.g., higher weights for global enclosure in a wrap grasp, or for thumb-index-middle relations in a lateral pinch). A temporal smoothness loss is added to ensure continuity between consecutive robot configurations. This constrained optimization is solved using Sequential Least Squares Programming (SLSQP).
- **Hand-Object Contact Refinement:** For frames where contact is essential, the robot hand configuration is further refined. This involves an alternating optimization scheme between the articulated joint configuration and a rigid correction of the wrist pose. A hand-object contact loss is minimized, which seeks to align the 3D positions of the robot's visible fingertips (within the task-relevant region) with the corresponding human fingertip points extracted from the demonstration. Regularization terms are included to maintain temporal smoothness and prevent over-correction. For parallel-jaw grasping, a contact refinement approach based on prior work is applied to improve performance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The effectiveness of GraspDreamer was evaluated through extensive experiments in both simulation and real-world environments, along with ablation studies.

#### 4.1. A. Simulation Experiments

**TaskGrasp Evaluation (Parallel-Jaw Gripper):**
- GraspDreamer demonstrated superior performance on the TaskGrasp dataset, which evaluates task and object generalization. It achieved the highest Success rates (78.6% for object generalization, 79.5% for task generalization) and Intent scores (3.6 and 3.8, respectively).
- These results surpassed both training-free baselines (LAN-Grasp) and training-based methods (GraspGPT, FoundationGrasp, GraspMolmo) without requiring additional robot data collection.
- The findings indicated that GraspDreamer's benefits from generalized interaction priors encoded in VGMs extended beyond mere functional region localization (as in LAN-Grasp) to inform both *where* and *how* to grasp. The evaluation highlighted that high Part Identification Accuracy (PIA) does not necessarily correlate with intent-aligned grasps, emphasizing the importance of functional understanding.

**DexGraspNet Evaluation (Dexterous Hands - Allegro and Shadow):**
- For dexterous functional grasping, GraspDreamer consistently outperformed specialized baselines like DexGYS and DexDiffuser across kitchenware and mechanical tool categories.
- With the Shadow hand, GraspDreamer achieved Success rates of 80.2% (kitchenware) and 83.1% (mechanical tools), along with Intent scores of 3.4 and 3.6, respectively. These were substantially higher than DexGYS (Success: 56.2% and 56.3%; Intent: 2.3 and 2.6).
- Similarly, with the Allegro hand, GraspDreamer recorded Success rates of 78.5% (kitchenware) and 82.6% (mechanical tools), and Intent scores of 3.3 and 3.5, outperforming DexDiffuser (Success: 49.3% and 60.4%; Intent: 2.2 and 2.7).
- The results indicated that while DexGYS optimized finger contacts, it did not adequately capture functional intent, and DexDiffuser, despite generating stable grasps, lacked an explicit mechanism for grounding task functionality.

#### 4.2. B. Real-Robot Experiments

**Functional Grasping:**
- Real-world evaluations were conducted using Franka Panda arms equipped with a Robotiq 2F-85 parallel-jaw gripper and a 16-DoF Allegro Hand. GraspDreamer achieved overall success rates above 70% for both setups (76/100 for parallel-jaw, 73/100 for dexterous grasping) on a diverse set of household objects.
- Performance was observed to be lower for tasks requiring highly precise contact and force control, such as grasping a headphone headband, suggesting increased sensitivity to minor pose errors and contact dynamics in such scenarios.

**Extension to Manipulation:**
- The approach was extended to three short-horizon manipulation tasks. GraspDreamer achieved manipulation success rates of 70% for "Take Flower from Vase," 50% for "Open Pot," and 80% for "Pull Tissue."
- The "Open Pot" task proved more challenging due to stringent contact constraints and higher sensitivity to pose and force errors. The gap between grasping and manipulation success highlighted that VGMs could be more prone to generating visually or physically infeasible post-grasp motions over longer horizons.

**Extension to Policy Learning:**
- GraspDreamer's ability to generate training data was demonstrated by using 50 generated demonstrations for "Pull Tissue" and "Pick up Bottle" to train Diffusion Policies.
- The resulting policies achieved success rates of 73.3% and 86.7% respectively, indicating that the automatically generated trajectories could serve as effective supervision for downstream visuomotor policy learning without human teleoperation.

#### 4.3. C. Ablation Studies

**Ablation on System Components:**
- The study on DexGraspNet revealed the contribution of each GraspDreamer component. Removing hand trajectory optimization ("w/o hand opt") yielded the lowest performance, indicating the necessity of refining the raw VGM-generated hand poses.
- Removing hand-object contact refinement ("w/o contact") significantly reduced contact accuracy and grasp feasibility due to the morphological gap between human and robot hands.
- Removing taxonomy-aware retargeting ("w/o taxonomy") also degraded performance, underscoring the importance of functionally guided retargeting. These trends were consistent across both dexterous hands.

**Ablation on Generative Models:**
- A comparison of different VGMs showed that video generation models (Veo 3.1 and Kling Video 2.1) produced more temporally coherent interactions and physically plausible motions, leading to better downstream grasp performance compared to an image generation model (Gemini 2.5 Image). Veo 3.1 slightly outperformed Kling Video 2.1 in this context.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

GraspDreamer presents a contribution to the field of robotic manipulation by introducing an alternative methodology for achieving functional grasping. Its primary significance lies in demonstrating that generalizable functional grasping can be accomplished without the extensive, labor-intensive robot data collection typically required by previous methods.

The key implications and potential impact are:
- **Reduced Data Dependency:** By leveraging visual generative models (VGMs) pre-trained on internet-scale human data, GraspDreamer mitigates the reliance on large-scale, robot-specific datasets for functional grasping. This addresses a significant bottleneck in developing generalist robots.
- **Enhanced Generalization:** The approach's ability to transfer generalized interaction priors implicitly encoded in VGMs allows it to demonstrate superior data efficiency and generalization performance on public benchmarks, suggesting a pathway towards more robust functional grasping in diverse, open-world environments.
- **Adaptability Across Embodiments:** The unified framework's capacity to adapt across different robot hands (parallel-jaw grippers and dexterous hands) indicates its potential for broader applicability to various robotic platforms without requiring redesign for each specific end-effector.
- **Facilitating Downstream Tasks:** The demonstration that GraspDreamer can be extended to short-horizon manipulation tasks and, critically, can generate sufficient data to train visuomotor policies, indicates its potential beyond static grasping. This suggests a scalable method for providing supervision for more complex robotic behaviors, potentially reducing the need for human teleoperation in policy learning.
- **Advancing Human-Robot Interaction Understanding:** The methodology's focus on interpreting human demonstrations and functionally retargeting them to robots contributes to a deeper understanding of how human interaction priors can be effectively translated into robot capabilities.
- **Future Directions for Data Generation:** The work points towards future research in developing scalable and efficient frameworks for large-scale data generation specifically for functional grasping and manipulation, potentially accelerating progress in robotic skill acquisition.

In summary, GraspDreamer offers a pathway to equipping robots with functional grasping capabilities in complex, real-world settings by shifting the paradigm from extensive robot-specific data collection to leveraging and interpreting generalized human interaction knowledge derived from visual generative models.
