# RLGF: Reinforcement Learning with Geometric Feedback for Autonomous Driving Video Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of Macau and Li Auto Inc. developed RLGF, a system that employs reinforcement learning with geometric feedback to enhance the structural accuracy of synthetic autonomous driving videos. This approach substantially reduced geometric errors and boosted 3D object detection performance on generated data by an absolute 12.7% mAP.

## Problem

- State-of-the-art diffusion models generate visually realistic autonomous driving videos but contain subtle geometric distortions.
- These geometric inaccuracies severely limit the utility of synthetic data for critical downstream 3D perception tasks, leading to a performance gap (e.g., 3D object detection mAP of 25.7 on synthetic vs. 35.5 on real data).
- Traditional pixel-wise loss functions and general preference-based reinforcement learning methods fail to enforce the precise, multi-scale geometric consistency required for autonomous driving.

## Method

- **RLGF** (Reinforcement Learning with Geometric Feedback) refines pre-trained ==video diffusion== models by integrating perception-model-driven geometric constraints as reward signals.
- It introduces Latent-Space Windowing Optimization, efficiently applying rewards within intermediate denoising steps on noisy latent features to guide geometry formation progressively.
- A Hierarchical Geometric Reward (HGR) system provides multi-level feedback (vanishing points, lane topology, depth, 3D occupancy) using specialized latent-space perception networks and a Micro-Decode Module.

## Results

- RLGF reduced geometric errors in synthetic videos, including a **21%** decrease in Vanishing Point Error and a **57%** reduction in Depth Error on the DiVE baseline.
- The framework significantly improved 3D object detection performance on synthetic data, boosting mAP from 25.75 to 31.42 for the DiVE baseline, an absolute increase of **12.7%**.
- Geometric improvements were achieved while maintaining or slightly improving visual realism, with comparable Fréchet Video Distance (FVD) scores.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Geometric structures in generated videos emerge progressively during diffusion, allowing targeted feedback at intermediate latent states to efficiently correct specific inaccuracies.
> - Integrating explicit, quantifiable geometric and scene-level rewards derived from dedicated autonomous driving perception models, rather than subjective preferences, is crucial for achieving task-aware synthetic data.
> - Current generative models prioritize 2D appearance, overlooking critical 3D scene structure, which necessitates dedicated geometric evaluation metrics like GeoScores.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The advancement of autonomous driving (AD) systems is heavily reliant on vast quantities of high-quality data to train and validate complex perception and planning modules. Collecting real-world driving data is inherently expensive, time-consuming, and limited by the diversity of scenarios encountered, leading to a critical demand for synthetic data. In recent years, generative models, particularly diffusion models, have revolutionized the field of image and video synthesis, achieving unprecedented levels of visual realism.

This paper positions itself within this vibrant landscape by first acknowledging the significant progress made by state-of-the-art diffusion-based video generation methods (e.g., Panacea, Drive-WM, MagicDrive, DiVE). These models have excelled in metrics of visual fidelity, such as Fréchet Video Distance (FVD), producing outputs that are often indistinguishable from real videos to the human eye. However, the authors identify a critical, yet underexplored, limitation: despite their visual realism, these synthetic videos frequently suffer from subtle but impactful geometric distortions. This flaw is not immediately apparent through traditional visual metrics but severely compromises their utility for downstream AD tasks, especially those requiring precise 3D understanding like object detection, depth estimation, and motion forecasting.

The research explicitly highlights this gap by demonstrating that while 2D object detection performance on synthetic data can be comparable to real data (mAP: 43.8 vs. 44.7), 3D object detection shows a significant performance drop (mAP: 25.7 vs. 35.5). This divergence underscores that current generative models prioritize 2D appearance over accurate 3D scene structure. The paper then situates its contribution as a direct response to this limitation.

Furthermore, the work draws parallels and distinctions with the emerging trend of applying Reinforcement Learning (RL) to fine-tune generative models, a concept popularized by Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) in large language models. While RLHF and its variants (like DPO) have been successfully adapted to image and video generation for aligning with human preferences (e.g., improving general aesthetic quality), these approaches typically rely on holistic preference scores that lack the precise, quantifiable, and multi-scale geometric feedback essential for autonomous driving. The proposed RLGF framework differentiates itself by introducing *explicit, quantifiable geometric and scene-level rewards* derived from dedicated AD perception models, rather than subjective human preferences or general perceptual quality metrics. This tailored approach allows RLGF to provide targeted, locally-aware feedback to correct specific geometric inaccuracies, making the synthetic data genuinely suitable for rigorous AD tasks.

In essence, RLGF carves out a new niche by addressing the "geometric realism gap" in synthetic AD video generation, moving beyond purely visual fidelity to enforce structural and physical plausibility, which is paramount for safety-critical applications like autonomous driving. It represents a significant step towards creating truly reliable and task-aware synthetic data.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind the RLGF framework stems from a critical observation: current state-of-the-art video generation models for autonomous driving, despite their impressive visual realism, fail to accurately capture the intricate geometric relationships of real-world scenes. This deficiency, though often subtle, profoundly limits the applicability of synthetic data for crucial downstream tasks in AD perception and planning.

The authors thoroughly investigate and quantify this problem. They conduct targeted experiments revealing a substantial performance degradation in 3D object detection when using synthetic data (e.g., a 3D object detection mAP of 25.7 on synthetic videos compared to 35.5 on real data). In stark contrast, 2D object detection on the same synthetic data shows comparable performance to real data (mAP: 43.8 vs. 44.7). This key finding strongly indicates that while existing diffusion models preserve 2D appearance, they fundamentally struggle with accurate 3D scene structure and geometric consistency.

To systematically quantify these distortions, the paper introduces a novel metric suite called **GeoScores**. GeoScores evaluate geometric fidelity by applying pre-trained AD perception models to both synthetic and real videos, using the real-video outputs as ground truth references. This analysis reveals pervasive geometric inaccuracies in current synthetic data, including:
1. **Vanishing point shifts:** Indicating incorrect global perspective.
2. **Lane topology inconsistencies:** Reflecting misaligned road markings and implausible lane structures.
3. **Depth errors:** Particularly on road surfaces, signifying incorrect spatial placement.
For a typical baseline, these errors are significant (e.g., average VP shift of 0.086 normalized units, Lane F1-Score of 0.792, average depth error of 1.822).

These quantified geometric deficiencies, coupled with the inherent limitations of conventional pixel-wise loss functions (which treat pixels independently and oversimplify conditional inputs, failing to enforce higher-order geometric relationships), serve as the direct motivation for the RLGF framework.

Therefore, the **key objectives** of this work are:
- **To systematically quantify and highlight the geometric distortion problem** in autonomous driving video generation, establishing GeoScores as a dedicated evaluation metric.
- **To develop a novel framework, Reinforcement Learning with Geometric Feedback (RLGF),** that addresses these geometric inconsistencies.
- **To inject perception-model-driven geometric spatial constraints directly into the video generation process**, moving beyond pixel-wise optimization.
- **To ensure that generated synthetic videos are not only visually realistic but also geometrically sound and reliable** for improving the performance of downstream 3D perception and planning tasks in autonomous driving.
- **To offer a plug-and-play solution** that can be easily integrated with existing video diffusion models, making it widely applicable for AD development.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The RLGF framework introduces a novel approach to enhance the geometric and spatio-temporal consistency of videos generated by conditional diffusion models. It achieves this by refining a pre-trained video diffusion model through reinforcement learning, leveraging specific, model-interpretable geometric constraints as reward signals.

The core of RLGF is built upon two technical innovations:

#### 3.1. Latent-Space Windowing Optimization

Conventional RL-based fine-tuning of diffusion models often involves unrolling the entire multi-step denoising process to obtain a final output, which is then rewarded. This approach is computationally expensive due to large memory requirements and long sampling chains. RLGF addresses this by proposing **Latent-Space Windowing Optimization**.

This strategy is motivated by the observation that geometric structures emerge progressively during the diffusion denoising process: coarse global geometry forms in earlier steps, while finer details are refined in later steps. Instead of only rewarding the final generated video, RLGF provides feedback based on noisy latent features ($z_k$) at intermediate denoising steps $k$.
- During the T-step sampling process, rewards are applied within a randomly sampled sliding window of *w* steps (e.g., from a random starting step $t'$ down to $k = t' - w$).
- The perception models (detailed below) are designed to operate directly on these noisy latent features $z_k$ and the corresponding timestep $k$.
This approach offers two significant advantages:
1. **Efficiency:** It substantially reduces the computational graph for backpropagation, making the fine-tuning process more feasible.
2. **Effectiveness:** It provides targeted corrective signals at different stages of generation, allowing for precise guidance in forming both global structure and local geometric details.
The optimization maximizes the expected reward obtained by evaluating the noisy latent $z_k$ against a VAE-encoded latent $z_v$ derived from a real video, updating only the LoRA parameters ($\theta_{LoRA}$) of the diffusion model, while keeping the pre-trained perception models frozen.

#### 3.2. Hierarchical Geometric Reward (HGR)

The HGR system provides comprehensive, multi-level feedback on geometric integrity and scene coherence. It evaluates consistency across point, line, plane, perceptual feature, and voxel-level representations. To enable efficient operation directly on latent features without costly full VAE decoding, RLGF employs a lightweight **Micro-Decode Module ($F_{micro}$)**. This module, using shallow layers from the VAE decoder, processes noisy latent features $z_k$ and timestep $k$ to produce enhanced per-frame features suitable for downstream perception tasks.

HGR utilizes two specialized, pre-trained latent-space perception networks:

1. **Latent Geometry Perception Model ($P_{geo}$):** This multi-task model assesses static 2.5D geometry from micro-decoded features. It comprises a DINOv2 backbone with task-specific heads for:
 - **Vanishing Point (VP) Detection:** Reformulated as heatmap regression to enforce accurate global perspective. The reward function $r_{vp}$ is based on the negative L2 norm of the predicted VP versus the ground truth.
 - **Lane Parsing:** Detects road markings through a topology-aware segmentation head, ensuring realistic road structures. The reward $r_{lane}$ uses the F1-Score of predicted lanes against reference lanes.
 - **Depth Estimation:** Fine-tuned to promote depth coherence for correct surface and object geometry. The reward $r_{depth}$ is based on the negative RMSE between predicted and reference depth maps, specifically on road and vehicle regions.
 The combined geometric reward $R_{geo}$ is a weighted sum of $r_{vp}$, $r_{lane}$, and $r_{depth}$.

2. **Latent Occupancy Prediction Model ($P_{occ}$):** This model infers 3D scene layout and object dynamics from sequences of micro-decoded features. It predicts intermediate backbone features and a 3D occupancy grid for each frame. The occupancy reward $R_{occ}$ consists of two parts:
 - **Feature Alignment ($r_{align}$):** Encourages similarity in high-level scene interpretation by aligning distributions of intermediate occupancy features between generated and real latents using KL divergence.
 - **3D Occupancy IoU ($r_{iou}$):** Maximizes the Intersection over Union (IoU) of the predicted 3D occupancy grids with the real ones, ensuring accurate volumetric object layout and dynamics.

By integrating these multi-level reward signals, the HGR system effectively guides the video diffusion model to produce physically principled and geometrically sound autonomous driving scenarios. The overall RLGF framework is designed as a "plug-and-play" solution, using LoRA to fine-tune existing well-trained diffusion models like MagicDrive-V2 and DiVE, thereby correcting geometric flaws without retraining the entire generative model.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of RLGF on the nuScenes dataset demonstrates its effectiveness in significantly improving the geometric fidelity and downstream utility of synthetic autonomous driving videos.

#### 4.1. GeoScores Validation and Perception Model Performance

- The paper first establishes the geometric distortion problem by using its proposed GeoScores. It quantifies significant errors in baseline diffusion models (e.g., DiVE baseline: Vanishing Point Error of 0.086, Lane F1-Score of 0.792, and Depth Error of 1.822).
- The pre-trained latent-space perception models ($P_{geo}$ and $P_{occ}$) demonstrate strong capabilities in extracting meaningful geometric and scene information directly from latent features, validating their suitability as reward providers. $P_{geo}$ achieves a VP normalized distance error of 0.024 and a lane parsing F1-score of 0.865. $P_{occ}$ achieves an mIoU of 29.96 for 3D occupancy prediction, closely competing with pixel-based methods.

#### 4.2. Improved Geometric Fidelity (GeoScores) and Visual Quality

- RLGF substantially reduces geometric errors across both tested baselines (MagicDrive-V2 and DiVE). For DiVE+RLGF:
 - Vanishing Point Error decreases from 0.086 to 0.068 (a 21% reduction).
 - Lane F1-Score improves from 0.792 to 0.879 (a significant increase in lane topology validity).
 - Depth Error (RMSE) dramatically decreases from 1.822 to 0.772 (a 57% reduction).
- Crucially, these geometric improvements are achieved while maintaining or even slightly improving the visual realism, as indicated by comparable or lower Fréchet Video Distance (FVD) scores (e.g., DiVE FVD from 68.4 to 67.6). This confirms that RLGF addresses geometric flaws without degrading the visual quality of the generated videos.

#### 4.3. Enhanced Downstream Task Performance (3D Object Detection)

- The most significant finding is the dramatic improvement in 3D object detection performance on synthetic data refined by RLGF.
 - For the DiVE baseline, RLGF boosts 3D object detection mAP from 25.75 to 31.42 and NDS from 33.61 to 36.07. This represents an absolute increase of 12.7% in mAP, substantially narrowing the performance gap between synthetic and real data.
 - Similar improvements are observed for MagicDrive-V2, where mAP increases from 18.95 to 23.21, and NDS from 21.10 to 27.80.
- Qualitative results (visualizations of 3D bounding box alignment) further corroborate these findings, showing that RLGF-enhanced videos exhibit much better alignment with the underlying scene geometry.
- Further evaluations using an additional 3D object detector, StreamPETR, confirm the generalizability of RLGF's benefits across diverse downstream architectures, yielding a substantial +4.75% mAP gain.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- Ablation studies confirm the synergistic contributions of the Hierarchical Geometric Reward (HGR) components. Incrementally adding reward signals (vanishing point, lane, depth, and occupancy rewards) progressively improves 3D object detection performance. The full HGR system, combining all five components, achieves the highest mAP (31.42) and NDS (36.07), underscoring the benefits of comprehensive multi-level feedback.
- Hyperparameter ablations confirm the optimal settings for latent-space windowing (window size, starting step) and reward weights, demonstrating the robustness of the chosen configuration.

In summary, RLGF successfully addresses the geometric fidelity issue in synthetic AD video generation, leading to a quantifiable reduction in geometric errors and, more importantly, a significant boost in the utility of synthetic data for critical 3D perception tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The RLGF framework represents a significant advancement in the field of autonomous driving data generation and has several profound implications and potential impacts:

#### 5.1. Enabling Reliable Synthetic Data for Autonomous Driving

The most critical impact of RLGF is its ability to generate synthetic autonomous driving videos that are not only visually realistic but also geometrically sound and reliable. By addressing the subtle yet impactful geometric distortions prevalent in previous state-of-the-art generative models, RLGF makes synthetic data genuinely useful for training, evaluating, and validating safety-critical AD systems. This directly tackles a major bottleneck in AD development, which is the prohibitive cost and inherent limitations of collecting sufficient diverse real-world driving data.

#### 5.2. Accelerating Autonomous Driving Development

By providing a means to produce high-fidelity, geometrically accurate synthetic data, RLGF can significantly accelerate the development lifecycle of autonomous driving technologies.
- **Enhanced Training:** AD perception models trained on RLGF-enhanced synthetic data will learn more robust and accurate 3D scene representations, leading to better performance in real-world scenarios.
- **Improved Simulation and Validation:** The generated videos can be used in advanced simulation platforms for rigorous system validation, testing AD systems under a vast array of geometrically complex and challenging conditions that are difficult or dangerous to replicate in the real world. This contributes to the development of safer and more robust autonomous vehicles.
- **Reduced Development Costs:** Less reliance on expensive real-world data collection translates to reduced operational costs for AD companies.

#### 5.3. A New Paradigm for Task-Aware Generative Models

RLGF establishes a new paradigm for generative models, moving beyond purely perceptual metrics (like FVD) towards explicit task-aware feedback. By integrating dedicated AD perception models into the reinforcement learning loop, the generative model is guided to produce outputs optimized for specific downstream tasks (e.g., 3D object detection). This shift from general realism to "utility realism" is crucial for specialized applications like AD, robotics, and medical imaging, where precise physical or geometric properties are paramount.

#### 5.4. Plug-and-Play Adaptability

The design of RLGF as a plug-and-play solution, leveraging LoRA to fine-tune existing diffusion models, significantly enhances its practical applicability. It allows AD developers and researchers to easily integrate geometric feedback into their preferred video generation baselines without requiring extensive re-engineering or retraining of entire large-scale models. This modularity democratizes access to geometrically sound synthetic data generation.

#### 5.5. Quantitative Metric for Geometric Fidelity

The introduction of **GeoScores** provides a standardized and quantitative method to evaluate geometric fidelity in synthetic AD videos. This metric suite fills a critical gap, enabling researchers to objectively assess and compare generative models not just on visual quality but also on their structural accuracy, fostering targeted improvements in future research.

#### 5.6. Broader Impact on AI and Safety

Beyond autonomous driving, the principles of incorporating explicit, multi-level geometric or physical constraints via reinforcement learning could be extended to other domains where generative models need to adhere to specific structural or physical laws. This research contributes to the broader quest for more controllable, reliable, and trustworthy AI systems, particularly in safety-critical applications.

#### 5.7. Limitations and Future Directions

The authors also acknowledge limitations, which are important for contextualizing the impact. The framework's performance is dependent on the accuracy and robustness of the pre-trained perception models, and while efficient, RL-based fine-tuning remains computationally intensive. Future work could explore more sample-efficient RL algorithms, adaptive reward functions, and generalization across more diverse environmental conditions and diffusion architectures. Expanding GeoScores to include LiDAR consistency or multi-modal agreement could also provide a more holistic evaluation. Despite these, RLGF marks a substantial step forward in creating reliable synthetic data foundations for autonomous systems.
