# Learning Modal-Mixed Chain-of-Thought Reasoning with Latent Embeddings

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at UCSD developed a modal-mixed Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning framework for Vision-Language Models (VLMs) that interweaves textual tokens with compact visual "sketches" in the form of latent embeddings. This method improves performance across 11 diverse multimodal reasoning and perception tasks and achieves higher efficiency than external tool-based approaches, completing 32 latent steps in 3.10 seconds compared to 8.36 seconds for a single tool-use cycle.

## Problem

- Traditional Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning in LLMs and VLMs is limited to text, often failing on vision-intensive tasks where crucial intermediate steps are inherently visual.
- Existing multimodal CoT approaches frequently rely on external visual tools (e.g., zooming, drawing), which introduces high latency and limited flexibility.
- There is a challenge in enabling VLMs to natively integrate and reason with dynamic visual information in a step-by-step manner, analogous to human mental imagery.

## Method

- Introduced a ==modal-mixed CoT method== that allows VLMs to fluidly interleave textual tokens with compact visual "sketches" (==latent embeddings==) throughout their reasoning process.
- Integrated a ==conditional diffusion decoder== into the VLM architecture to synthesize these latent visual embeddings, conditioned on high-level semantic intent from the VLM's language backbone.
- Implemented a ==two-stage training strategy==: supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on modal-interleaved CoT traces, followed by reinforcement learning (RL) to enable the model to self-adapt its modality switching.

## Results

- The modal-mixed CoT method consistently outperforms state-of-the-art baselines on **11** diverse vision-intensive reasoning and perception tasks, demonstrating improved accuracy on benchmarks like VCog-Bench, LogicVista, and MME-Unify.
- The approach shows significant efficiency gains, completing **32** latent steps in **3.10 seconds**, which is substantially faster than the **8.36 seconds** required for a single tool-use cycle in external tool-based methods.
- Ablation studies confirm the critical role of latent visual embeddings and the diffusion decoder, with a **15%** average performance improvement over text-only training and better results than simpler latent generation techniques.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Disentangling the VLM's high-level reasoning from fine-grained visual generation via a lightweight diffusion decoder reduces optimization pressure and helps preserve the VLM's original linguistic knowledge.
> - Reusing the VLM's own visual encoder to generate ground truth latent embeddings for intermediate images ensures semantic alignment and continuity within the model's representations.
> - Autoregressive generation of latent visual tokens directly within the VLM's output sequence provides a more efficient and flexible mechanism for "mental visualization" compared to external tool-based methods.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research on "modal-mixed Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning" is situated at the intersection of several rapidly evolving fields: large language models, vision-language models, and multimodal reasoning.

**Chain-of-Thought Reasoning:** CoT, introduced by Wei et al. (2022), revolutionized how LLMs and subsequently VLMs approach complex tasks by enabling them to articulate intermediate reasoning steps. This explicit step-by-step deduction significantly improves performance and interpretability, moving models beyond simple input-output mapping to more structured problem-solving. Recent work has extended CoT with techniques like tree-structured reasoning and reinforcement learning (RL) for longer, more complex chains.

**Limitations of Traditional CoT in Multimodal Settings:** Despite its success, the primary limitation of conventional CoT, as highlighted by the authors, is its text-only nature. While effective for linguistic reasoning, it "often fails on vision-intensive problems where key intermediate states are inherently visual." Tasks requiring mental rotation, fine-grained spatial relations, or complex spatio-temporal dependencies are cumbersome or impossible to fully describe in words. Existing multimodal CoT approaches, such as those that equip VLMs with external visual tools (e.g., zooming, drawing lines), suffer from reliance on external processes, leading to high latency and limited flexibility (e.g., inability to handle irregular masks). Unified generative models that can produce either text or vision exist (Wang et al., 2025b) but often struggle to match the performance of state-of-the-art VLMs for reasoning tasks.

**Inspiration from Human Cognition and Latent Reasoning:** The authors draw inspiration from human cognitive processes, specifically the concept of "mental imagery" or a "visuospatial sketchpad" that complements verbal working memory. This human ability to mentally manipulate visual information motivates the development of a computational analogue. This aligns with a growing body of research in "latent reasoning," which explores how models can perform reasoning in hidden states or continuous embedding spaces rather than solely through explicit language tokens. Prior works in this area include introducing special latent tokens, knowledge distillation, architectural modifications, and "continuous CoT" (Hao et al., 2024b; Zhu et al., 2025) which replaces language-based CoT with continuous embeddings for efficiency.

**Bridging the Modality Gap:** This paper directly addresses the "modality gap" by proposing a novel method to natively interleave text with compact visual "sketches" in the form of latent embeddings within the VLM's reasoning process. This approach is a significant step beyond text-only CoT and tool-based multimodal CoT. It particularly builds upon and improves prior work like "Mirage" (Yang et al., 2025b), which also introduced latent visual tokens but relied on a simpler cosine similarity loss for training. The present work distinguishes itself by integrating a more sophisticated diffusion-based latent decoder, aiming for better visual generation quality and a clearer disentanglement of model responsibilities, thereby reducing the "optimization pressure of the VLM." By doing so, it aims to create a more integrated and efficient multimodal reasoning system that can truly reason "beyond what text can describe."

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objectives and motivations of this research are clearly articulated in the paper's introduction and abstract:

1. **Extend Chain-of-Thought beyond Language:** The primary motivation is to overcome the limitations of text-only CoT when applied to multimodal, especially vision-intensive, reasoning problems. The authors recognize that many crucial intermediate reasoning steps in such tasks are inherently visual and cannot be adequately captured by textual descriptions alone.
2. **Equip VLMs with Modal-Mixed Reasoning:** The central goal is to enable Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to perform "modal-mixed CoT," meaning they can fluidly interleave textual tokens with compact visual "sketches" (represented as latent embeddings) during their reasoning process. This aims to mimic human cognitive processes involving mental imagery.
3. **Bridge the Modality Gap without Degrading VLM Capabilities:** A critical objective is to introduce this new visual generation pathway without "eroding the VLM’s original linguistic knowledge and capabilities." This involves ensuring semantic alignment between the generated visual latent space and the VLM's internal representations, and designing the architecture to prevent catastrophic forgetting.
4. **Improve Grounding and Efficiency for Complex Vision-Intensive Tasks:** By allowing VLMs to "offload visual details to condensed latent embeddings while reserving language for high-level logic control," the authors aim to improve the grounding of reasoning in visual context and enhance the efficiency of solving complex vision-intensive and long-horizon spatio-temporal problems.
5. **Disentangle Roles for Better Optimization:** The motivation behind using a diffusion-based latent decoder is to "cleanly disentangle roles" between the VLM and the visual generation component. The VLM focuses on high-level intent, while the diffusion head handles fine-grained perceptual details, simplifying the VLM's learning objective and easing optimization pressure.

In essence, the research seeks to create more capable, robust, and human-like multimodal reasoners that can dynamically switch between and integrate textual and visual forms of thought.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, **modal-mixed Chain-of-Thought (CoT)**, enhances Vision-Language Models (VLMs) by allowing them to interleave textual tokens with compact visual "sketches" (latent embeddings) during reasoning. The approach involves architectural modifications and a two-stage training strategy.

#### 3.1. Model Architecture

The core architecture builds upon a standard VLM (comprising a vision encoder, a connector, and an LLM backbone) and integrates a novel component:

- **VLM as Visual Latent Encoder:** To ensure semantic alignment and prevent erosion of the VLM's existing knowledge, the model reuses the VLM's own visual encoder and connection layer to generate ground truth latent embeddings for intermediate images. Specifically, for an intermediate image `I`, the VLM's `vision encoder (ϕ_v)` produces features `ϕ_v(I)`, which are then projected by the `connector (g)` into dense visual token embeddings `V`. These `V` are further compressed into a fixed-length latent sketch `z` (e.g., via average pooling) to reduce context length while retaining high-level semantics. This `z` serves as the target for the diffusion decoder.
- **Diffusion Model based Decoder:** A conditional diffusion decoder is attached to the VLM to synthesize these latent visual embeddings. This design "cleanly splits roles":
 - The **LLM** (VLM's language backbone) provides high-level semantic intent.
 - A lightweight **stacked-MLP diffusion network** reconstructs fine-grained visual details, conditioned on the LLM's intent.
 - The LLM's last-layer hidden state (`h_k`) serves as the conditioning vector (`c_k = Wh_k`) for the diffusion process.
 - The decoder performs denoising steps, transforming Gaussian noise into a latent embedding (`e_k`). Crucially, these latents are generated autoregressively: `e_k` is fed back into the VLM to obtain `c_k+1` for generating `e_k+1`. This unifies the generation paradigm for text and latents.
- **Modal-Mixed CoT Reasoning with Special Tokens:** Two special tokens, `<START>` and `<END>`, are introduced with randomly initialized embeddings. When `<START>` is generated by the VLM's language head, it triggers the diffusion decoder to produce a fixed number (`K`) of latent embeddings autoregressively. After generating these latents, an `<END>` token is appended, and the VLM resumes textual reasoning. This scheme allows the model to decide *when* to "sketch" to facilitate subsequent textual reasoning.

#### 3.2. Learning to Reason with Latent Embeddings

The training strategy comprises two stages:

1. **Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT) with VLM as Visual Latent Encoder:**
 - The VLM is fine-tuned on curated datasets containing **annotated, modal-interleaved CoT traces**, where text rationales alternate with helpful intermediate images.
 - For each intermediate image in a trace, the VLM's own vision encoder and connector are used to convert it into a compressed latent sketch `z`.
 - A **joint autoregressive objective** is applied:
 - For **text positions**, a standard cross-entropy loss for next-token prediction (`-Σ log P_θ(y_t | x, y_<t, Z_≤t)`) is used.
 - For **latent blocks** (`Z = [z_1..., z_M]`), a diffusion loss is applied. It samples noise (`ϵ`) and a timestep (`t`), creates a noisy latent (`z_k^(t)`), and regresses the diffusion decoder's velocity prediction (`D_ϕ(z_k^(t), t, c_k)`) to `ϵ`. The `L2` norm of the difference is minimized.
 - A hyperparameter `λ` balances these two objectives.
 - During training, the visual encoder is frozen, and different learning rates are used for the LLM and diffusion head for better convergence. The Zebra-CoT dataset (Li et al., 2025a) is used for SFT.

2. **Reinforcement Learning (RL) for Self-adaptation:**
 - After SFT, an RL phase (specifically, GRPO - Shao et al., 2024) is applied to enable the model to learn *when* to switch modalities and *how* to compose textual and latent steps effectively for complex problems.
 - For each query, the model generates a group of interleaved outputs.
 - A **task accuracy reward** (`r_i`) is assigned (1 for correct, 0 for incorrect answers).
 - The objective maximizes an expected reward, using the ratio of current and old policy probabilities (`ρ_i`) and an advantage estimate (`A_i`).
 - This stage helps the VLM discover modal-interleavings where latent visual reasoning genuinely supports textual reasoning and vice-versa, leading to a "self-adapted, tightly integrated modal-mixed CoT policy."
 - For stability, the RL loss is computed only on text tokens, without backpropagating from latent visual embeddings. VisuLogic (Xu et al., 2025) is used as training data for RL.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate that the proposed modal-mixed CoT method consistently improves performance across a diverse set of multimodal reasoning and perception tasks.

#### 4.1. Performance on Vision-Intensive Reasoning Tasks (Table 1)

- **Consistent Outperformance:** The "Ours (SFT)" and "Ours (RL)" models generally outperform or remain highly competitive with state-of-the-art baselines, including standard VLMs (Qwen2.5-VL, LLaVA-OneVision, InternVL2.5) and reasoning-enhanced models (R1-onevision-RL, MM-EurekaQwen).
- **Addressing VLM Limitations:** The results highlight that text-only CoT models (e.g., R1-onevision-RL, MM-Eureka-Qwen) are limited by a static encoding of the input image, hindering their ability to reinterpret visual information or simulate transformations. Thyme, relying on tool-based pixel edits, is also shown to be less flexible.
- **Benefits of Latent Visual States:** Our model's ability to generate intermediate latent visual states interleaved with text effectively leverages spatial and perceptual information, enabling it to "imagine" outcomes not present in the input. The disentangled design (lightweight diffusion decoder) liberates the reasoning process from pixel-level distractions, leading to deeper and more effective reasoning.
- **SFT vs. RL for Reasoning:** SFT achieves the highest average score (26.7) across VCog-Bench, LogicVista, and MM-IQ. RL shows slightly lower average performance (25.7) but improves some specific subtasks. The authors hypothesize that RL's degradation in LogicVista (inductive and spatial) might be due to long CoT patterns not being well-captured in RL training data.

#### 4.2. Performance on Vision-Intensive Perception Tasks (Table 2)

- **Strong Performance without External Tools:** Our model demonstrates strong performance on V* benchmark (Attribute, Spatial) and MME-Unify (Spot Diff., Aux. Lines). Notably, for V*, baseline VLMs often rely on external "zoom-in" tools, which our latent reasoning intrinsically replaces, offering better robustness.
- **SFT vs. RL for Perception:** Here, RL slightly outperforms SFT (55.1 vs 55.2 average), suggesting that the preference optimization in RL is more effective for fine-grained perceptual tasks that require better coordination between visual embedding and text generation. Janus-Pro-7B, a unified model with generative capabilities, also performs well on "Spot Difference," indicating the value of visual generation for such tasks.

#### 4.3. Further Analysis

- **Ablation Study (Table 3):**
 - **Effectiveness of Latent Embeddings:** "Text-only Training" performs significantly worse (40.6 average vs. 46.7 for Ours (SFT)), confirming the critical role of latent visual embeddings in modal-mixed CoT.
 - **Importance of Diffusion Decoder:** Replacing the diffusion decoder with a simpler MLP head using "Similarity Loss" also leads to lower performance (44.7 average) compared to the full model, demonstrating the diffusion decoder's importance in generating higher-quality, more informative latent visual tokens by explicitly modeling their distribution.
- **Catastrophic Forgetting Study (Table 4):**
 - The study confirms that the proposed method "well keeps the original knowledge and capability of the VLM." A VLM fine-tuned with our method, when stripped of latent reasoning modules and used for language-only CoT, maintains comparable performance to the original backbone model (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL). This validates the design choice of using the VLM's own encoder and a disentangled diffusion decoder.
- **Efficiency Study (Table 5):**
 - The latent reasoning mechanism is significantly more efficient than external tool-based approaches. A single tool-use cycle in Thyme (generating Python code, executing image edits, re-encoding) takes 8.36s. In contrast, 32 latent steps (with 50 denoising steps) in our model takes only 3.10s, with approximately 0.10s per step. This efficiency gain stems from operating directly within the embedding space, bypassing external GPU forward passes for code generation and image re-encoding.
- **Hyper-parameter Tuning (Table 6):**
 - The balancing hyperparameter `λ` (weight for latent diffusion supervision) is crucial. `λ=1.0` was found to be optimal, achieving the best balance between text and latent supervision. Too small (`λ=0.1`) under-emphasizes visual details, while too large (`λ=10`) over-emphasizes them, leading to performance degradation.
- **Ablation Study on Latent Token Budget (Table 7):**
 - The number of latent tokens impacts performance, especially for complex spatial reasoning. For the V* Spatial subset, performance peaks at 64 tokens, demonstrating that complex spatial relationships benefit from a larger latent representation. Attribute tasks are less sensitive.
- **Generalization on Different Base Models (Table 8):**
 - The method successfully generalizes to Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct, a different and newer model architecture, showing consistent performance improvements over the baseline. This indicates the robustness and scalability of the approach.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Analysis (Figures 2, 3, 4, 5)

- **Attention Maps:** Visualization of visual token attention maps confirms that the model successfully focuses on semantic regions relevant to the question during reasoning.
- **Case Studies:** Qualitative examples illustrate how our model's generated visual tokens explicitly "visualize" intermediate steps, aiding fine-grained attribute recognition, abstract logic reasoning (tracking symbol transformations), and spatial fitting. Baselines often suffer from hallucination or erroneous textual descriptions in such cases.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant advancement in the field of multimodal AI, particularly for vision-language models and their reasoning capabilities.

1. **Novel Multimodal Reasoning Paradigm:** The introduction of "modal-mixed chain-of-thought" reasoning, which seamlessly interleaves textual and compact visual "sketches" as latent embeddings, offers a powerful new paradigm. This approach directly addresses the limitations of text-only CoT in vision-intensive tasks, opening doors for more effective problem-solving in areas previously challenging for VLMs.
2. **Enhanced Reasoning Capabilities:** By allowing the model to "mentally visualize" intermediate steps, the method enables deeper, more grounded, and more accurate reasoning on complex multimodal problems, as demonstrated by consistent improvements across 11 diverse tasks. This moves VLMs closer to human-like cognitive processes that utilize mental imagery.
3. **Architectural Elegance and Efficiency:** The clean disentanglement of roles—where the VLM provides high-level intent and a lightweight diffusion decoder handles fine-grained perceptual details—is an elegant architectural design. This approach not only improves visual generation quality but also reduces the VLM's optimization pressure and prevents catastrophic forgetting of its original capabilities. The efficiency study further highlights its practical advantage over external tool-based methods, offering lower latency.
4. **Preservation of Original VLM Knowledge:** A crucial contribution is the method's ability to augment VLMs with new capabilities without degrading their existing linguistic knowledge. This makes the approach practical for integration with pre-trained, powerful VLMs.
5. **Broad Applicability:** The demonstrated effectiveness across a wide range of tasks, from abstract visual reasoning (VCog-Bench, LogicVista) to fine-grained perception (V*, MME-Unify), suggests broad applicability. The generalization to different base models further strengthens its potential for widespread adoption.

The potential impact of this work is substantial. It could lead to more robust and intelligent AI systems capable of handling complex real-world scenarios that demand both textual and visual understanding and reasoning. Applications could span advanced robotics, autonomous systems, complex scientific data analysis, and highly interactive AI assistants. The public release of the code will facilitate further research and development in this promising direction. Looking ahead, the authors' stated intention to extend this method to other modalities like audio and 3D suggests a future path towards a truly unified and versatile multimodal reasoning framework.
