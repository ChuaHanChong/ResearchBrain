# Strategyproof Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from ETH Zurich and the University of Oxford introduce a formal framework for Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) that accounts for strategic human labelers who might misreport preferences. They propose the Pessimistic Median of MLEs (Pessimistic MoMLEs) algorithm, which achieves approximate strategyproofness and converges to optimal social welfare, while also identifying a fundamental trade-off between incentive alignment and policy alignment in multi-labeler settings.

## Problem

- Existing RLHF methods assume truthful human feedback, making them vulnerable to strategic misreporting when labelers aim to influence the learned policy.
- Strategic behavior in pluralistic alignment settings can introduce self-serving biases, degrading the fairness, robustness, and overall effectiveness of AI systems.
- There is a lack of formal analysis and robust algorithms to ensure strategyproofness in RLHF without relying on external payment mechanisms.

## Method

- The work formalizes offline RLHF with multiple ==strategic human labelers== in episodic Markov Decision Processes, assuming linearly realizable reward functions and using the Bradley-Terry model for preferences.
- The Pessimistic Median of MLEs (Pessimistic MoMLEs) algorithm is proposed, which computes Maximum Likelihood Estimates (MLEs) and confidence sets for individual labeler reward parameters.
- It then optimizes for a 'pessimistic median return' within a constructed 'median confidence set' to find a policy robust to strategic manipulation, applicable to contextual bandits and general MDPs.

## Results

- Existing methods showed substantial degradation in social welfare suboptimality under strategic labeling in synthetic contextual bandit experiments, with the difference increasing with sample size.
- Pessimistic MoMLEs was proven to be approximately strategyproof, with the incentive to misreport bounded by ~O(κ_i * √(d/n)), diminishing as the number of samples (n) increases.
- Under truthful reporting, Pessimistic MoMLEs' suboptimality is bounded by ~O(√(d log(k/δ)/k) + max_{i∈[k]} κ*_i * k√(d + log(k/δ)/n)), demonstrating convergence to the optimal policy as both n and k increase.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Existing pluralistic RLHF methods (e.g., Pessimistic Social Welfare) are not strategyproof, allowing a single strategic labeler to cause arbitrary degradation in social welfare.
> - There is an inherent trade-off in strategyproof RLHF: any such algorithm incurs a worst-case constant suboptimality of at least (k-1)/k and an approximation ratio no better than 1/k, where k is the number of labelers.
> - Approximate strategyproofness and convergence to optimal social welfare can be achieved by optimizing a pessimistic median return, balancing robustness against strategic behavior with policy alignment.
