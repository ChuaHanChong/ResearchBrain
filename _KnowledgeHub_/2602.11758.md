# HAIC: Humanoid Agile Object Interaction Control via Dynamics-Aware World Model

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Tsinghua University, HKUST(Guangzhou), ETH Zurich, and Xiaomi Robotics Lab developed HAIC, a control framework enabling humanoid robots to interact agilely with underactuated objects despite visual occlusion by inferring high-order object dynamics solely from proprioceptive feedback. The system demonstrated 100% glide success on skateboarding and robust performance in multi-terrain navigation on a Unitree G1 robot, outperforming proprioception-only baselines.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots struggle with whole-body interaction tasks involving underactuated objects that possess independent dynamics and non-holonomic constraints.
- During interactions, large objects frequently cause visual occlusion, hindering direct observation of the terrain and the object itself by onboard sensors.
- Complex dynamics coupling between the robot and underactuated objects generates disturbances that challenge robot balance and stability, often requiring impractical external state estimation.

## Method

- The **HAIC** framework formulates humanoid-object interaction as a partially observable Markov decision process, leveraging proprioceptive history to predict object dynamics.
- A ==Dynamics-Aware World Model (DWM)== features an object adapter that infers high-order object states (position, orientation, linear/angular velocities, and accelerations) from proprioceptive and reference motion history.
- An explicit geometric projection mechanism transforms a nominal object point cloud using predicted dynamic states, creating a spatially grounded representation of dynamic occupancy for the robot's policy.

## Results

- HAIC achieved **100%** glide success for skateboarding and **60%** complete success, along with **100%** success for cart manipulation, significantly outperforming a proprioception-only baseline on a physical Unitree G1 robot.
- The framework enabled robust multi-terrain navigation while carrying visually occluding objects (**100%** success across flat, slope, stair, and mixed terrains) and successful sequential multi-object tasks.
- HAIC demonstrated generalization capabilities by successfully interacting with objects of varying sizes and weights, adapting to terrain orientation perturbations and external disturbances beyond its training distribution.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Inferring high-order object dynamics (velocities and accelerations) from proprioceptive feedback alone is crucial for proactive inertia compensation, enabling robust interaction with underactuated objects.
> - Explicitly projecting abstract dynamic state predictions onto a static geometric prior provides a spatially grounded understanding of object occupancy and contact affordances, compensating for visual occlusion.
> - A two-stage asymmetric training strategy, coupled with continuous online world model adaptation to the student policy's exploration, effectively bridges the sim-to-real gap and ensures robust state estimation despite distribution shifts.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of humanoid robotics and whole-body control has seen advancements, particularly in leveraging deep reinforcement learning (RL) to enable robots to track complex human motion capture datasets. These data-driven approaches have facilitated the acquisition of diverse kinematic behaviors and general motion tracking capabilities, with ongoing research exploring generalized whole-body control through large-scale motion datasets and unified latent representations.

Within this context, Human-Object Interaction (HOI) is a significant and long-standing challenge. Existing research has focused on enabling robots to perform versatile whole-body interactions, often using large HOI datasets for data-driven learning or extracting skills directly from human videos. However, a notable limitation in many prevailing methodologies is their predominant focus on the manipulation of *fully actuated objects*. In these scenarios, the object's state is rigidly coupled to the robot's end-effector, and its dynamics are largely constrained by the robot's kinematics. While methods such as PhysHSI, HDMI, VisualMimic, and DemoHLM have utilized external sensors (LiDAR, markers, depth, RGB) for object state estimation, they are typically restricted to static environments or fully actuated objects.

Concurrently, world models have emerged as a paradigm in RL, acting as internal simulators to predict future environmental outcomes. These models have been applied to robotic manipulation for anticipating tactile feedback and object dynamics and for addressing high-dimensional humanoid dynamics and complex contact sequences. Examples include DWL, RWM, WMR, and Any2Track. However, these world models primarily focus on modeling proprioceptive dynamics for the robot's whole-body control, often overlooking the *coupled dynamics* inherent in interactions with external objects, particularly underactuated ones.

This work addresses a specific gap: the control of humanoid robots interacting with *underactuated objects*. Such objects possess independent dynamics and non-holonomic constraints, posing challenges not adequately met by approaches designed for fully actuated objects or by existing world models primarily focused on internal robot dynamics. HAIC aims to bridge this gap by offering a unified framework for robust interaction with a spectrum of object dynamics without requiring external state estimation, specifically tackling the challenges of underactuated objects, visual occlusion, and complex dynamics coupling.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observation that humanoid robots, despite their potential, face significant challenges in performing complex whole-body interaction tasks in unstructured environments, particularly when dealing with objects whose dynamics are not fully controlled by the robot.

The challenges highlighted include:
- **Prevalence of Underactuated Objects**: The real world contains numerous underactuated objects (e.g., skateboards, carts, luggage) that possess independent dynamics and non-holonomic constraints. Interacting with these objects is more complex than with fully actuated objects, where the object's state is directly dictated by the robot's end-effector.
- **Frequent Visual Occlusion**: During interaction, large objects often obstruct the robot's onboard visual sensors, impeding direct observation of both the terrain and the object itself. This partial observability necessitates an internal understanding of the environment.
- **Complex Dynamics Coupling**: Underactuated objects generate independent acceleration and inertial forces that couple with the robot's own dynamics. This coupling can introduce significant disturbances to the robot's balance and stability. Traditional control methods often rely on high-precision external state estimation to manage these disturbances, which is frequently impractical for real-world deployment. Moreover, existing reinforcement learning policies, without explicit modeling of higher-order dynamics, may exhibit response lag, leading to instability during dynamic interactions.

Inspired by human adaptability in similar scenarios (e.g., balancing on a skateboard or carrying an object that obstructs vision), where proprioception and an internal mental model are used to infer object states through geometric reasoning and dynamic sensing, the objectives of this work are as follows:
- To propose HAIC, a unified learning framework designed to enable robust and agile object interaction, especially in scenarios involving visual blind spots and underactuated objects.
- To develop a novel dynamics predictor within a dynamics-aware world model (DWM) that infers high-order object states, specifically linear and angular velocities and accelerations, solely from proprioceptive history. This aims to capture the independent motion trends of underactuated objects for proactive inertia compensation.
- To implement an explicit geometric projection mechanism that maps these predicted dynamic states onto a static geometric prior (e.g., a point cloud of the object). This creates a spatially grounded representation of dynamic occupancy, allowing the robot's policy to perceive collision boundaries and contact affordances even without direct visual feedback.
- To bridge the simulation-to-reality gap by employing an asymmetric fine-tuning strategy, where the world model continuously adapts online to the student policy's exploration, thereby ensuring robust state estimation despite distribution shifts between simulation and real-world deployment.
- To empirically evaluate and demonstrate the framework's effectiveness on a physical humanoid robot (Unitree G1) across a range of complex tasks, including agile interactions with underactuated objects (skateboarding, cart manipulation), sequential multi-object tasks, and multi-terrain navigation while carrying objects.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed HAIC framework formulates the humanoid-object interaction task as a partially observable Markov decision process (POMDP), where the robot's policy must rely on partial observations for decision-making.

**A. Problem Formulation**
During training, a privileged teacher has access to the full state, including the robot's proprioception ($s^{prop}_t$), reference motion ($s^{ref}_t$), object point cloud ($\mathcal{P}$), and privileged state ($s^{priv}_t$). In contrast, during deployment, the student policy operates with limited observations: onboard proprioception ($s^{prop}_t$), reference motion ($s^{ref}_t$), and a nominal geometric template ($\mathcal{P}$). The objective is to train a policy $\pi(a_t|s^{prop}_t, s^{ref}_t, a_{t-1}, \mathcal{P})$ in simulation for real-world deployment.

**B. Dynamics-Aware World Model (DWM)**
The core of HAIC is a dynamics-aware world model that internalizes object dynamics relative to the robot. This model comprises three main components:
1. **Object Adapter**: This module functions as a real-time dynamics predictor. It takes the history of previously predicted object states ($\hat{s}^{obj}_{t-1}$), current robot observations ($H_t = \{s^{prop}_{t-n}..., s^{prop}_t\}$), and future reference motion ($F_t = \{s^{ref}_t..., s^{ref}_{t+m}\}$) as input. Its output is the predicted object's relative spatial and temporal state ($\hat{s}^{obj}_t$), which includes position, orientation, linear/angular velocities, and linear/angular accelerations.
2. **Explicit Geometric Projection**: To connect abstract state estimation with physical interaction, this step transforms a canonical point cloud ($\mathcal{P}$) of the object using the predicted object state ($\hat{s}^{obj}_t$). This results in a dynamic object point cloud ($\mathcal{P}^{dyn}_t$) represented in the robot's root coordinate frame, providing a spatially grounded representation of the object's dynamic occupancy.
3. **Privilege Adapter**: This module fuses the dynamic object point cloud ($\mathcal{P}^{dyn}_t$), current proprioception ($s^{prop}_t$), and reference motion ($s^{ref}_t$) to generate a predicted privileged feature ($\hat{z}^{priv}_t$). This feature serves as input to the student actor, mimicking the information flow of a privileged actor.

**C. Two-Stage Training Framework**
The framework employs a two-stage training pipeline leveraging a teacher-student paradigm:
1. **Stage 1: Feature Alignment & Warm-up**: In this initial phase, a privileged Teacher actor ($\pi_T$) is trained using reinforcement learning (PPO). Concurrently, the world model and a Student actor ($\pi_S$) are trained to align with the teacher's behavior and internal representations. The objective function ($L_{Stage1}$) combines the PPO objective for the teacher, the unified world model loss ($L_{WM}$), and a KL-divergence term ($\lambda_{distill} D_{KL}(\pi_S || \pi_T)$) to distill actions from the teacher to the student. The $L_{WM}$ is a weighted sum of dynamics prediction error and latent feature alignment errors.
2. **Stage 2: Asymmetric Fine-tuning**: The student actor ($\pi_S$) is fine-tuned using an asymmetric actor-critic setup, where the critic retains access to privileged states to reduce variance. The world model continues to be optimized during this stage by minimizing $L_{WM}$ alongside the student's PPO objective ($L_{Stage2} = L_{PPO}(\pi_S, V_T) + L_{WM}$). This continuous adaptation of the world model to the student's evolving trajectory distribution is designed to prevent performance degradation due to inaccurate state estimation. To further stabilize training, an Exponential Moving Average (EMA) of the world model weights is used for the rollout policy.

**D. Multiple Objects Contact Reward**
For tasks involving multiple objects, a contact reward ($r_{contact}$) is introduced. This reward unifies geometric alignment and force regulation, guiding the robot through pre-contact (guidance) and post-contact (execution) phases. It minimizes the distance between end-effectors and semantic targets before contact and ensures appropriate force application while maintaining geometric constraints upon contact. The reward is calculated as an average across active objects and end-effectors, with terms for position matching and force thresholds.

**E. Implementation Details**
- **Data Collection**: High-fidelity interaction behaviors were captured using an optical motion capture system tracking both human motion and interactive objects. The raw kinematic data was retargeted to the Unitree G1 robot and refined through a physics-based simulation pipeline (Isaac Sim) to generate physically valid states with contact information.
- **Training and Sim-to-Real Transfer**: Policies were trained using PPO in Isaac Sim. Extensive domain randomization was applied to bridge the reality gap. For real-world deployment, the policy runs on an external PC, sending joint position targets to the robot's onboard control board via Ethernet, with a PD controller operating at 500 Hz (policy at 50 Hz).
- **Action Space**: The policy outputs 23-DoF target joint positions, which are then translated into joint torques by a low-level proportional-derivative (PD) controller.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The HAIC framework was evaluated on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot in real-world scenarios, utilizing only onboard computation, proprioceptive feedback, and the dynamics-aware world model (DWM), without external motion capture systems. Comparisons were made against HDMI*, a proprioception-only variant of the HDMI framework.

**A. Real-world Deployment and Comparison**
HAIC's DWM functioned as a core estimator across three distinct scenarios:

1. **Underactuated Object Interaction**:
 - **Skateboarding**: HAIC demonstrated robust balance control, achieving a 100% glide success rate and a 60% complete success rate (including dismount). In contrast, the baseline (HDMI*) struggled significantly, achieving only a 20% glide success rate and failing to complete the task (0%). HAIC also exhibited lower robot state tracking errors (e.g., Mean Per Body Position Error (Empbpe) of 81.5 mm for HAIC vs. 132.1 mm for HDMI*).
 - **Cart Manipulation (Pushing/Pulling)**: HAIC achieved a 100% success rate for both "Pull Cart" and "Push Cart" tasks. The baseline failed in the "Push Cart" task (0% success) and achieved only 40% for "Pull Cart," indicating its inability to account for the cart’s external dynamics and non-holonomic constraints. HAIC's proactive compensation for object dynamics was attributed to its ability to predict object velocity and acceleration.

2. **Sequential Interaction**: For sequential tasks such as "Pull Cart w/ Box" and "Push Cart w/ Box," HAIC enabled the robot to smoothly transition between interacting with a box and then manipulating a loaded cart. HAIC achieved a 100% success rate for "Push Cart w/ Box" and 40% for "Pull Cart w/ Box." The baseline was unable to complete these composite tasks, as it failed to reliably perform the prerequisite single cart manipulation.

3. **Multi-terrain Interaction**: HAIC enabled the robot to carry a box across various terrains (flat ground, slopes, stairs, and mixed slope-stair terrains) while the box occluded the robot's visual sensors. HAIC maintained a 100% success rate across all terrains, including the complex "w/ Slope + Stair" scenario where the baseline failed completely (0% success). HAIC also exhibited lower orientation errors, suggesting that the geometric prior effectively compensated for the lack of visual feedback on uneven terrain.

**B. Ablation Study on HAIC**
An ablation study was conducted with five model variants: "Proprio" (proprioception-only), "Vec-Pose" (predicts position/orientation), "Vec-Dyn" (predicts comprehensive dynamics as flattened vector), "Geo-Pose" (predicts pose and projects onto geometric feature), and "HAIC" (full method: comprehensive dynamics prediction with geometric projection).

- **Training Efficiency**: On the "Push Cart w/ Box" task, HAIC demonstrated superior sample efficiency and achieved the highest asymptotic return compared to all other variants.
- **Skateboarding**: "Proprio" struggled to maintain balance (40% glide, 0% complete). "Vec-Pose" achieved 100% glide but failed at dismount (0% complete), indicating that position perception alone was insufficient for managing impulsive forces. Dynamics-aware methods ("Vec-Dyn," "Geo-Pose," "HAIC") achieved high completion rates, with HAIC demonstrating the lowest tracking errors. This highlighted the importance of predicting object acceleration for handling transient disturbances.
- **Cart Manipulation**: "Proprio" failed. "Vec-Pose" and "Vec-Dyn" completed tasks but suffered from trajectory drift. Methods employing explicit geometric projection ("Geo-Pose" and "HAIC") achieved tighter global tracking. HAIC delivered the best overall performance by combining geometric alignment with dynamics prediction.
- **Other Complex Scenarios**: HAIC consistently showed superior stability in sequential tasks, mitigating error accumulation and enabling precise object steering. For multi-terrain tasks, HAIC maintained a high success rate, predicting dynamic disturbances caused by uneven ground, where "Proprio" and "Vec-Pose" experienced stability issues.

**C. Robustness and Generalization**
HAIC demonstrated generalization capabilities beyond its training distribution:
- **Object Size and Weight**: The policy successfully transported larger (0.25m side length) and heavier (3kg) boxes, adjusting its center of gravity, movement speed, and arm posture to avoid self-collisions, indicating an learned understanding of mass, inertia, and motion dynamics.
- **Terrain Orientation Perturbations**: The policy adapted to randomly rotated slopes and stairs by adjusting foot landing angles and body orientation, suggesting it learned general equilibrium principles rather than memorizing specific terrain geometries.
- **External Perturbations**: The robot quickly estimated changes in momentum from instantaneous lateral thrusts using the world model, swiftly restoring balance through stepping and arm swinging.
- **Extended Experiments**: HAIC achieved a 100% success rate on various everyday furniture interaction tasks, including pushing tables, suitcases, and chairs with extra yaw freedom.

**D. Analysis and Explanation of World Model**
- **Comparison with RMA**: HAIC exhibited superior or competitive success rates and significantly lower tracking errors compared to Rapid Motor Adaptation (RMA) in cart manipulation tasks. This suggested that explicit dynamics prediction is more robust for complex underactuated interactions than implicit adaptation.
- **DWM Visualization**: While DWM predictions for box states closely matched simulated results, for more challenging tasks like cart manipulation and skateboarding, predicted states showed some drift from simulated trajectories, tending towards the reference motion. This drift could lead to observation mismatch and failure if the error threshold was exceeded.
- **Hyperparameter Robustness**: HAIC maintained a high success rate with minor kinematic degradation even when contact reward thresholds (tolerance and force) were imprecise. The binary contact masks were computed algorithmically, avoiding manual annotation noise.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work presents a notable contribution to humanoid robotics and human-object interaction by introducing HAIC, a unified framework designed to overcome limitations in existing methodologies.

The primary significance lies in its ability to enable robust whole-body interaction with *underactuated objects* under conditions of visual occlusion. This addresses a critical gap in the field, as previous work largely focused on fully actuated objects or relied on external sensing. The novel Dynamics-Aware World Model (DWM) is a key innovation, as it effectively infers high-order object states (velocity and acceleration) from proprioceptive data and explicitly projects these dynamics onto a geometric prior. This mechanism allows the robot to build an internal, spatially grounded representation of dynamic occupancy, facilitating proactive inertia compensation and contact affordance perception even when objects are visually obscured.

The framework's two-stage asymmetric training strategy, coupled with continuous world model adaptation, contributes to its robustness against distribution shifts, a common challenge in sim-to-real transfer. Empirical validation on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot in real-world scenarios demonstrates high success rates across diverse and challenging tasks, including agile skateboarding, cart manipulation, sequential multi-object interactions, and multi-terrain navigation. The observed generalization capabilities across varying object sizes, weights, and terrain orientations suggest that HAIC learns fundamental principles of physical interaction rather than task-specific memorization.

The potential impact of HAIC extends to applications requiring humanoid robots to operate autonomously and interact dynamically with a wide range of objects in unstructured, sensor-limited environments. By reducing reliance on external state estimation and enhancing adaptability to dynamic, underactuated objects, this research moves humanoid control closer to versatile, human-like capabilities, potentially fostering advancements in domestic assistance, logistics, and other real-world deployments.
