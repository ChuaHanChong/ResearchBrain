# Kimi k1.5: Scaling Reinforcement Learning with LLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> The Kimi Team developed Kimi k1.5, a multimodal large language model that demonstrates Reinforcement Learning (RL) can achieve state-of-the-art reasoning performance in LLMs by leveraging long context processing up to 128k tokens. This model attains competitive or superior results on challenging math, code, and vision benchmarks compared to leading proprietary models and effectively distills its long-context capabilities into efficient short-context models.

## Problem

- Traditional large language model (LLM) pretraining relies on finite human-generated datasets, which limits continuous improvement and scaling beyond current data availability.
- Prior published work on applying Reinforcement Learning (RL) to LLMs had not yielded competitive results for complex reasoning tasks, leaving RL's potential for LLM training unproven.
- The high computational cost and latency associated with powerful long Chain-of-Thought (CoT) models restrict their practical deployment and accessibility for real-world applications.

## Method

- **Kimi k1.5** employs a ==multi-stage training pipeline== comprising extensive multimodal pretraining, ==supervised fine-tuning (SFT)==, a dedicated ==long-CoT SFT warm-up==, and a final ==Reinforcement Learning== phase.
- A simplistic RL framework is utilized based on ==online policy mirror descent== with a binary reward on the final answer, notably excluding a ==value network==, and incorporates a ==length penalty== to encourage efficient responses.
- ==Long2short== distillation methods, including ==model merging==, ==shortest rejection sampling==, ==Direct Preference Optimization (DPO)==, and a second RL phase, are developed to transfer superior long-CoT reasoning to more token-efficient short-CoT models.

## Results

- Kimi k1.5's long-CoT model achieves state-of-the-art performance on various benchmarks, matching or slightly surpassing OpenAI's o1 on AIME 2024 (**77.5** vs **74.4**) and MATH 500 (**96.2** vs **94.8**), and reaching the **94th percentile** on Codeforces.
- "Long2short" methods successfully distill long-CoT model capabilities into more token-efficient short-CoT models, yielding significantly higher scores (e.g., **60.8** on AIME 2024, compared to GPT-4o's **9.3** and Claude 3.5 Sonnet's **16.0**).
- Empirical studies validate that increased context length directly correlates with improved problem-solving capabilities, supporting the hypothesis that long context is a key dimension for scaling RL with LLMs.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reinforcement Learning (RL) can unlock a new axis for LLM scaling by enabling models to generate and learn from their own 'training data' through environmental exploration, moving beyond static human-curated datasets.
> - Long context scaling (up to 128k tokens) is crucial for RL-trained LLMs, allowing models to implicitly perform complex reasoning like planning, reflection, and correction through extended Chain-of-Thought generation.
> - A simplistic RL framework, without complex value functions or explicit process reward models, can achieve state-of-the-art reasoning when combined with long-context scaling and improved policy optimization.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research directly addresses several critical challenges and trends in the field of large language models (LLMs) and artificial intelligence (AI) at large:

- **Scaling Laws and Data Limitations:** The paper immediately frames its contribution within the context of established scaling laws for LLMs (Kaplan et al. 2020; Hoffmann et al. 2022). It highlights a fundamental limitation of traditional language model pretraining (next token prediction): its reliance on a finite amount of available high-quality human-generated training data (Villalobos et al. 2024; Muennighoff et al. 2023). This work proposes Reinforcement Learning (RL) as a "new axis for continued improvement," allowing models to generate and learn from their own "training data" by exploring environments with rewards, thereby overcoming static data constraints. This is a significant conceptual shift, moving beyond passive consumption of human data towards active, self-supervised data generation.

- **Bridging the Gap in RL for LLMs:** The paper explicitly acknowledges that "prior published work has not produced competitive results" when applying RL to LLMs. Kimi k1.5 aims to demonstrate that RL *can* indeed yield state-of-the-art performance for complex reasoning tasks, effectively pushing the frontier of practical RL application in the LLM domain. This positions the work as a pivotal step in validating RL as a viable and effective training paradigm for advanced LLMs.

- **Long-Context Models and Reasoning:** A prominent trend in LLM development is the expansion of context windows to enable more sophisticated reasoning and problem-solving. Kimi k1.5 pushes this to 128k tokens, a capacity competitive with leading models like OpenAI's o1 and recent Claude iterations. The paper argues that long context inherently allows models to perform "planning, reflection, and correction" implicitly, analogous to increasing search steps in traditional planning algorithms (Yao et al. 2024; Y. Wu et al. 2024; Snell et al. 2024). This offers an intriguing alternative to explicit search algorithms like Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS), suggesting that sufficient context length can simplify the architectural requirements for complex reasoning.

- **Multimodality:** Kimi k1.5 is a multimodal LLM, trained jointly on text and vision data. This aligns with the broader industry trend toward unified AI models capable of processing and reasoning across different modalities, moving closer to the vision of Artificial General Intelligence (AGI) (Team et al. 2024). The paper details the diverse sources of multimodal data curated for pretraining, from real-world images to synthetic visual reasoning data and text-rendered data.

- **Efficiency and Practical Deployment:** The report delves into significant infrastructure innovations (partial rollouts, hybrid deployment, code sandbox) crucial for large-scale RL training. This addresses the practical challenges of computational cost and latency associated with powerful LLMs, particularly long-CoT models. The "long2short" methods, designed to distill the capabilities of high-performing long-CoT models into more token-efficient short-CoT models, are a direct response to real-world deployment constraints, making advanced reasoning capabilities more accessible and usable.

In essence, Kimi k1.5 represents a substantial effort to integrate and advance several cutting-edge techniques—RL for data scaling, long-context processing for implicit reasoning, multimodality for comprehensive understanding, and infrastructure optimization for efficiency—to create a new generation of highly capable and practical LLMs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the development of Kimi k1.5 are multi-faceted, aiming to push the boundaries of LLM capabilities and training paradigms:

- **Unlock New Scaling Axis with RL:** The fundamental motivation is to overcome the data limitations of traditional LLM pretraining, which relies on fixed, pre-existing datasets. By integrating Reinforcement Learning, the Kimi Team aims to enable LLMs to "scale their training data by learning to explore with rewards," thus providing a continuous pathway for improvement that is not constrained by static data availability. This is articulated as unlocking a "new axis for the continued improvement of artificial intelligence."

- **Achieve Competitive Performance with RL-Trained LLMs:** Acknowledging that "prior published work has not produced competitive results" for RL-trained LLMs, a core objective was to demonstrate that a well-engineered RL framework could indeed lead to state-of-the-art reasoning performance across various benchmarks and modalities. The goal was to match or even surpass leading proprietary models like OpenAI's o1.

- **Leverage Long Context for Enhanced Reasoning:** The team sought to prove that "long context scaling" (up to 128k tokens) is a "key ingredient" for the continued improvement of RL-trained LLMs. The motivation here is that an extended context window allows the model to implicitly perform complex reasoning processes like "planning, reflection, and correction" by simply generating longer chains of thought (CoTs), effectively increasing the "number of search steps" without needing explicit search algorithms.

- **Develop a Simplistic Yet Effective RL Framework:** A key design objective was to establish a "simplistic, effective RL framework" that could achieve high performance without relying on more complex techniques commonly associated with planning and value estimation in classical RL, such as Monte Carlo tree search, value functions, and explicit process reward models. This simplification aims for greater efficiency and robustness.

- **Integrate Multimodal Reasoning:** To create a more general and capable AI, the Kimi Team aimed for a model jointly trained on text and vision data, enabling it to "reason over the two modalities" concurrently. This reflects a motivation to build AI systems that can interact with and understand the world in a more holistic way.

- **Enhance Short-Context Model Performance through Long-Context Distillation:** Recognizing the computational cost of long-CoT models, a practical objective was to develop "long2short" methods. This involved using insights and techniques from the powerful long-CoT model to significantly improve the reasoning performance of more token-efficient short-CoT models, addressing real-world deployment constraints.

In summary, the Kimi Team's motivation for Kimi k1.5 was to demonstrate a viable, high-performing pathway for scaling LLMs through reinforcement learning, specifically by leveraging long contexts and streamlined RL techniques, while also pushing multimodal capabilities and developing practical solutions for token efficiency.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Kimi k1.5 development involved a sophisticated multi-stage training pipeline and innovative infrastructure, centered around the application of Reinforcement Learning (RL) with a focus on long Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning.

#### 3.1. Multi-Stage Training Pipeline

The training of Kimi k1.5 proceeds through four distinct stages:
1. **Pretraining:** The base model is initially trained on a vast, diverse, and high-quality multimodal corpus. This stage is divided into:
 - **Vision-language pretraining:** Establishing a strong language foundation, then gradually integrating interleaved vision-language data. The visual tower is first trained in isolation, then the language model parameters are unfrozen, increasing vision-text data proportion.
 - **Cooldown:** Continuous training with high-quality language and vision-language datasets, incorporating synthetic data (especially for math, knowledge, and code) to boost performance.
 - **Long-context activation:** Upsampling long-context cooldown data and progressively increasing the maximum sequence length (from 4k to 32k to 128k/131k tokens) with a mix of full and partial attention data to enable extended sequence processing.
2. **Vanilla Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** The pretrained model undergoes SFT on a corpus of approximately 1 million text examples (general QA, coding, math/science, creative writing, long-context tasks) and 1 million text-vision examples. Data curation involves human annotation and ranking, and rejection sampling for reasoning tasks.
3. **Long-CoT Supervised Fine-Tuning (Warm-up):** A crucial step prior to RL, this involves prompt engineering to create a small, high-quality "warm-up" dataset of accurately verified long-CoT reasoning paths. This primes the model to exhibit key cognitive processes like planning, evaluation, reflection, and exploration, which are essential for effective RL.
4. **Reinforcement Learning (RL):** This is the core innovation, aiming to scale LLM training by learning from rewards.

#### 3.2. Reinforcement Learning Approach

- **RL Prompt Set Curation:** To ensure effective and robust RL training, the Kimi Team meticulously curates the prompt set. Key properties include:
 - **Diverse Coverage:** Spanning STEM, coding, and general reasoning (text-only and image-text).
 - **Balanced Difficulty:** An adaptive, model-based approach where an SFT model generates answers multiple times, and the pass rate serves as a proxy for difficulty, allowing for filtering of trivial cases.
 - **Accurate Evaluability:** Exclusion of questions prone to "reward hacking" (e.g., multiple-choice, true/false, or easily guessable answers without CoT), using a simple method of guessing without CoT to identify and remove such prompts.
- **Problem Setting for Long CoT:** The goal is to train a policy model `πθ` to generate a Chain-of-Thought `z` followed by a final answer `y`, maximizing a binary reward `r(x,y,y*)` (correct/incorrect). The paper views long CoT as an implicit "planning algorithm," where the number of tokens (thoughts) serves as an analogy for computational budget, enabling the model to perform "trial and error" and solution refinement directly via auto-regressive predictions.
- **Policy Optimization:** A variant of **online policy mirror descent** is employed. The algorithm optimizes a relative entropy regularized policy optimization problem. Crucially, the system **excludes a value network**, which is a common component in classical RL for credit assignment. The hypothesis is that a value function might penalize exploratory steps that, while seemingly "wrong" locally, contribute to a successful final solution in a long CoT. By focusing the reward on the final answer, the model is encouraged to explore diverse reasoning paths and learn from trial and error.
- **Length Penalty:** To mitigate "overthinking" and excessively long responses, a length reward is introduced. It promotes shorter correct responses and penalizes longer incorrect ones, improving token efficiency. This penalty is gradually warmed up during training.
- **Sampling Strategies:** To enhance training efficiency, two strategies are used:
 - **Curriculum Sampling:** Starting with easier tasks and gradually progressing to more challenging ones based on pre-defined difficulty labels or model-assessed difficulty.
 - **Prioritized Sampling:** Focusing on problems where the model underperforms (lower success rates) by sampling them with higher probability.
- **Reward Modeling:**
 - **Coding:** Automatic generation of test cases for coding problems using the `CYaRon` library and Kimi k1.5 itself, followed by rigorous filtering based on ground truth submissions.
 - **Mathematics:** Utilizes a **Chain-of-Thought Reward Model (CoT-RM)**, which achieved significantly higher accuracy (98.5%) compared to a Classic RM (84.4%). The CoT-RM generates a step-by-step reasoning process before giving a final correctness judgment, providing more robust feedback.
- **Multimodal Data:** Vision RL data is sourced from real-world science and reasoning tasks, synthetic visual reasoning data (spatial relationships, geometric patterns), and text-rendered data (text converted to images) to ensure robust visual reasoning.

#### 3.3. Long2Short Methods

To transfer the superior reasoning abilities of long-CoT models to more token-efficient short-CoT models for practical deployment:
- **Model Merging:** Averaging weights of a long-CoT and a short-CoT model.
- **Shortest Rejection Sampling:** Fine-tuning on the shortest correct response sampled multiple times.
- **Direct Preference Optimization (DPO):** Creating positive-negative pairs where the shortest correct solution is positive, and longer (incorrect or excessive) responses are negative.
- **Long2short RL:** A second RL phase that applies the length penalty more aggressively and reduces maximum rollout length to explicitly penalize long, even if correct, responses.

#### 3.4. Infrastructure and System Design

The Kimi Team built a sophisticated RL training system designed for large-scale operations:
- **Iterative Synchronous RL Framework:** Comprises a rollout phase (workers generate trajectories, stored in replay buffer) and a training phase (trainer workers update weights). A central master coordinates.
- **Partial Rollouts for Long CoT RL:** A key innovation. It caps the length of each rollout trajectory. If a trajectory exceeds the limit, the unfinished portion is saved and continued in subsequent iterations, significantly reducing computational overhead and allowing for much longer CoTs without resource monopolization. It also includes repeat detection to terminate redundant generation early.
- **Hybrid Deployment of Training and Inference:** A novel approach using Kubernetes Sidecar containers to collocate Megatron (training) and vLLM (inference) on the same GPUs within a single pod. This minimizes GPU idle time, allows independent iteration of training/inference frameworks, and supports dynamic scaling. A "Checkpoint Engine" manages the lifecycle and weight transfer between Megatron and vLLM.
- **Code Sandbox:** A secure, optimized environment for executing user-submitted code, crucial for reliable reward feedback in coding tasks. It uses `crun` for faster container startup, pre-created `cgroups` for concurrency, and `tmpfs` for disk usage optimization.

This comprehensive methodology, integrating advanced RL techniques with robust infrastructure and practical deployment considerations, forms the backbone of Kimi k1.5's performance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The Kimi k1.5 technical report presents compelling results that validate its core hypotheses and methodologies:

- **State-of-the-Art Reasoning Performance (Long-CoT):** Kimi k1.5's long-CoT model achieves state-of-the-art results across a diverse set of challenging reasoning benchmarks and modalities.
 - **Math:** 77.5 on AIME 2024 and 96.2 on MATH 500, directly matching or slightly surpassing OpenAI's o1 (74.4 on AIME, 94.8 on MATH 500).
 - **Code:** 94th percentile on Codeforces, matching OpenAI's o1, and 62.5 on LiveCodeBench v5.
 - **Vision:** 74.9 on MathVista (Pass@1), outperforming OpenAI o1 (71.0) and QVQ-72B Preview (71.4). It achieves 70.0 on MMMU, slightly lower than OpenAI o1 (77.3) but competitive.
 This demonstrates that RL can indeed be scaled to achieve highly competitive reasoning capabilities in LLMs.

- **State-of-the-Art Short-CoT Reasoning Performance:** The "long2short" methods successfully transfer advanced reasoning capabilities to more token-efficient short-CoT models, yielding remarkably high results.
 - **Math:** 60.8 on AIME 2024 (compared to GPT-4o's 9.3 and Claude 3.5 Sonnet's 16.0 – an improvement of up to +550%) and 94.6 on MATH 500 (vs. GPT-4o 74.6, Claude 3.5 Sonnet 78.3).
 - **Code:** 47.3 on LiveCodeBench v4 (vs. GPT-4o 33.4, Claude 3.5 Sonnet 36.3).
 - **General/Vision:** Achieves competitive or superior scores on MMLU (87.4), IF-Eval (87.2), CLUEWSC (91.7), C-Eval (88.3), MathVista (70.1), and MMMU (68.0) compared to leading models like GPT-4o and Claude 3.5 Sonnet.
 This highlights the practical utility of distilling long-CoT models for efficiency without sacrificing too much performance.

- **Long Context Scaling is Crucial:** Empirical studies show a strong correlation between the model's output context length (mean token length) and its problem-solving capabilities. Performance continues to improve as context length increases, suggesting that longer CoTs enable more elaborate and successful solutions, especially for challenging problems. This validates the "context length as a key dimension for continued scaling of RL with LLMs."

- **Effectiveness of Simplistic RL Framework:** The achieved strong performance without relying on complex techniques such as Monte Carlo tree search, explicit value functions, or process reward models confirms the paper's hypothesis that long context scaling combined with improved policy optimization can establish a surprisingly simplistic yet powerful RL framework for LLMs.

- **Impact of Policy Optimization and Negative Gradients:** An ablation study comparing their method to ReST (Reinforced Self-Training) showed that the Kimi Team's approach, which incorporates negative gradients to penalize incorrect responses, exhibits "superior sample complexity." This indicates that actively learning from failures and applying corrective gradients significantly enhances the model's efficiency in generating long CoTs, which is crucial for complex reasoning tasks.

- **Benefits of Sampling Strategies:** Curriculum sampling is shown to significantly enhance performance by progressively challenging the model with harder problems after an initial warm-up, demonstrating its value in improving training efficiency and overall competency.

- **Model Size vs. Context Length Scaling Insights:** While larger models generally show better token efficiency (achieving a given accuracy with fewer tokens), smaller models can reach comparable performance by utilizing longer CoTs optimized through RL. This suggests a trade-off: for peak performance, scaling both model size and context is ideal, but for compute-constrained scenarios, scaling context length in smaller models is a viable strategy.

These findings collectively demonstrate Kimi k1.5's success in leveraging RL and long-context processing to achieve competitive, and in many cases, superior reasoning performance, alongside practical solutions for model efficiency.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in the Kimi k1.5 technical report carries significant implications for the future of large language model development and artificial intelligence research:

- **Validation of RL as a Scalable LLM Training Paradigm:** The most profound impact is the robust demonstration that Reinforcement Learning can be effectively scaled to train LLMs, achieving state-of-the-art results that match or surpass leading proprietary models. This addresses a critical gap where prior published work on RL for LLMs had not yielded competitive outcomes. It opens a vital new avenue for continued AI improvement, allowing models to learn from self-generated experience and rewards, moving beyond the inherent limitations of static human-curated datasets. This could lead to a paradigm shift in how frontier models are developed.

- **Simplified Reasoning Architectures:** The finding that strong performance in complex reasoning can be achieved by simply scaling context length, without relying on computationally intensive and architecturally complex techniques like Monte Carlo Tree Search or explicit value functions, is highly significant. This "simplistic framework" suggests that LLMs, given sufficient context, can implicitly develop "planning, reflection, and correction" capabilities through iterative self-correction and exploration guided by reward. This could simplify future research efforts in reasoning, focusing more on data quality, context scaling, and robust policy optimization rather than complex search algorithms.

- **Enhanced Practical Deployability:** The development of "long2short" methods is a major contribution to the practical deployment of highly capable LLMs. While long-CoT models are powerful, their high token consumption limits real-world application due to latency and cost. By effectively distilling the reasoning prowess of long-CoT models into more efficient short-CoT variants, the Kimi Team makes advanced reasoning capabilities more accessible for a wider range of applications, addressing a critical pain point in the industry.

- **Advancements in Multimodal AI:** The successful joint training and reasoning across text and vision modalities contribute to the ongoing quest for more general and human-like AI systems. As AI increasingly interacts with the real world, the ability to seamlessly integrate and reason about diverse input types becomes paramount. Kimi k1.5 pushes the boundaries in this crucial area.

- **Pioneering Large-Scale RL Infrastructure:** The detailed exposition of the RL training system, including innovations like partial rollouts and hybrid deployment (Megatron/vLLM on shared GPUs), provides valuable insights into the engineering challenges and solutions for training frontier models. These infrastructure advancements are crucial enablers for the scale and efficiency required for future AI development and could serve as a blueprint for other research groups.

- **New Benchmarking and Research Targets:** The competitive results established by Kimi k1.5 on various benchmarks, particularly in math and coding reasoning, set new targets for the AI community. This will likely spur further research and competition, leading to accelerated progress in these domains.

- **Future Research Directions:** The report explicitly points to future challenges, such as improving credit assignments and reducing overthinking without harming exploration, and exploring iterative long2short methods. These open questions signify continued opportunities for innovation spurred by this work.

In conclusion, Kimi k1.5 represents a substantial stride in advancing LLM capabilities through scalable reinforcement learning. It not only achieves remarkable performance but also provides a blueprint for effective RL training, innovative infrastructure, and practical deployment strategies, solidifying its position as a highly influential work in the evolving landscape of artificial intelligence.
