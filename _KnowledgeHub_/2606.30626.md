# DOPD: Dual On-policy Distillation

> [!summary] Summary
> Dual On-policy Distillation (DOPD) introduces an adaptive, dual distillation strategy to transfer knowledge from large teacher models to smaller student models, mitigating the "privilege illusion" observed when privileged information is used. The framework improved distillation efficiency, leading to an average 12.3-point gain for LLMs and 10.1-point gain for VLMs on challenging benchmarks.

## Problem

- Traditional on-policy distillation (OPD) faces a "privilege illusion" where teacher performance gains from privileged information (e.g., hints, annotations) do not consistently translate into transferable capabilities for student models.
- Existing OPD methods apply uniform token-level supervision, failing to account for the non-uniform quality of signals and that only a subset of tokens carries pivotal, capability-bearing information, especially when privileged inputs are present.
- Indiscriminately distilling privileged information can cause students to mimic outcomes based on unlearnable information asymmetry, leading to unstable training and performance bottlenecks.

## Method

- **DOPD** develops an ==advantage-aware dual distillation== paradigm that dynamically routes ==token-level supervision== based on its transferability.
- It introduces a ==privilege advantage gap== metric, quantifying the prediction discrepancy between privileged teacher and student policies, to differentiate genuine capability gaps from unlearnable information asymmetry.
- Tokens are categorized into four distinct regimes, each receiving a tailored distillation strategy (e.g., ==light teacher==, ==weak self-regularizing==, ==strong full-vocabulary teacher== using ==JS divergence==, or ==light privileged-student distillation== via ==Top-K reverse KL divergence==), based on the privilege advantage gap and predicted probabilities.

## Results

- DOPD achieved a **12.3-point** average performance gain for LLM distillation (Qwen3-8B to Qwen3-1.7B), recovering **89.8%** of the teacher-student gap and outperforming baselines by **4.4 to 5.3 points**.
- For VLM distillation (Qwen3-VL-8B to Qwen3-VL-2B), DOPD showed a **10.1-point** absolute gain, recovering **69.2%** of the teacher-student gap and surpassing VLM-oriented baselines by **2.1 to 6.0 points**.
- The method demonstrated superior robustness and scalability across various teacher-student size ratios, improved out-of-distribution generalization by **3.1-4.3 points** on OOD tasks, and enabled better continual learning with stable training.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The "privilege illusion" arises when privileged information, while beneficial for a teacher, creates an unlearnable information asymmetry rather than transferable capability for a student, leading to unstable distillation.
> - Not all token-level supervision is equally valuable; identifying the "privilege advantage gap" allows for pinpointing high-value, capability-bearing tokens crucial for effective knowledge transfer.
> - An adaptive, dual distillation approach, leveraging both teacher-based and self-based supervision and dynamically routing distillation objectives per token, is critical for stable learning and efficient capability transfer under privileged contexts.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Distillation is a widely adopted paradigm for transferring capabilities from a larger, high-performing "teacher" model to a smaller, more efficient "student" model. Traditional distillation methods often rely on off-policy trajectories, which can lead to a distribution shift where the student encounters states misaligned with its own behavior. On-policy distillation (OPD) emerged to address this by having the student generate its own trajectories, which are then supervised by the teacher at a dense, token-level. This approach has shown improved distillation efficiency and performance by mitigating distribution shift and providing rich supervision signals.

However, the effectiveness of OPD is inherently limited by the quality of its supervision source. An intuitive direction for improvement has been to incorporate privileged information (e.g., reasoning hints for Large Language Models or structured visual annotations for Vision-Language Models) during training. While privileged information can theoretically enhance a teacher's performance, the authors identify a potential failure mode termed "privilege illusion." This phenomenon suggests that the apparent performance gains from privileged information may not always translate into transferable capabilities for the student, but rather stem from an unlearnable information asymmetry. Furthermore, existing OPD methods often apply uniform supervision across all tokens, failing to account for the non-uniformity of signal quality, where only a subset of tokens might carry pivotal, capability-bearing information.

DOPD positions itself within the OPD landscape by directly tackling the "privilege illusion" problem and the non-uniformity of token-level supervision, especially when privileged information is present. It departs from monolithic supervision sources and uniform distillation by introducing an adaptive, dual distillation strategy. This work aims to advance OPD by enabling more effective and stable knowledge transfer under privileged contexts, ensuring that students acquire genuine capabilities rather than fitting non-transferable information shortcuts.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are to:
1. **Identify and characterize the "privilege illusion" failure mode in on-policy distillation.** The authors observe that while privileged information (e.g., verified reasoning hints, structured visual annotations) can improve a teacher model's prediction distribution, these gains do not consistently translate into transferable supervisory signals for a student model. This leads to a performance gap that conflates two distinct components: a transferable intrinsic teacher-student capability gap and an information asymmetry gap arising from unlearnable privileged inputs. Indiscriminately distilling such signals can cause the student to mimic privileged outcomes rather than acquire genuine abilities.
2. **Address the issue of non-uniform token-level supervision in OPD, particularly when privileged information is involved.** The authors contend that in realistic trajectories, only a small fraction of tokens contains critical, capacity-centric information (e.g., decisive branches, grounded evidence), while many others offer low-value supervision that might be privilege-dependent. Most existing OPD methods treat all tokens equally, which, in the presence of privileged information, can bias the student towards learning easier-to-fit, privilege-related shortcuts, thereby amplifying the privilege illusion.
3. **Develop an adaptive distillation paradigm that dynamically routes token-level supervision to mitigate privilege illusion and enhance capability transfer.** The core motivation is to move beyond uniform, monolithic supervision. The authors propose to leverage the complementary properties of teacher-based and self-based supervision, along with a mechanism to assess the "privilege advantage gap" and relative prediction probabilities between teacher and student, to assign appropriate supervision strength, objective, and source for each token. This aims to transfer credible capabilities while providing auxiliary signals for stability and exploration, thus reducing the negative effects of privilege illusion.

In summary, the motivation stems from the limitations of current OPD methods when privileged information is introduced. The authors seek to overcome the challenge of disentangling transferable knowledge from non-transferable privileged cues, thereby enabling students to genuinely internalize core competencies and achieve higher, more stable performance.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Dual On-policy Distillation (DOPD), introduces an advantage-aware dual distillation paradigm. It builds upon the general framework of On-Policy Distillation (OPD) but critically modifies the supervision mechanism to address the identified "privilege illusion" and non-uniformity of token-level supervision.

**Background of On-Policy Distillation (OPD):**
Standard OPD involves a student policy generating a sequence of tokens ($y \sim \Pi_S(\cdot | x)$) for a given input $x$. A stronger teacher policy then provides token-level supervisory signals for optimization. The objective is to minimize a loss function $L_n(y_n; t_{<n})$ for each token $y_n$ within the trajectory, where $t_{<n}$ represents the preceding context. This typically uses divergence-based objectives like Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence (forward or reverse) or Jensen-Shannon (JS) divergence, which aim to align the student's probability distribution with the teacher's. OPD can also vary in granularity, from sampled-token to Top-K token or full-vocabulary distillation.

**Addressing Privilege Illusion and Non-Uniformity:**

1. **Privilege Illusion:** The authors formalize privilege illusion as the phenomenon where performance advantages from privileged information (e.g., step-wise hints for LLMs, bounding boxes for VLMs) stem from information asymmetry rather than transferable capability. Straightforwardly distilling such signals can lead to students overfitting privileged outcomes, resulting in unstable distillation and entropy collapse, as empirically demonstrated.

2. **Privilege Advantage Gap:** To disentangle capability gaps from information gaps, DOPD introduces the "privilege advantage gap" ($A$). This metric quantifies the prediction discrepancy between a *privileged teacher policy* ($\Pi_T$) and a *privileged student policy* ($\Pi_S$) when both have access to identical privileged inputs ($p$). It is calculated as the absolute difference of their log-probabilities for a given token $y_n$:
 $A = |\log \Pi_T(y_n | x, p, y_{<n}) - \log \Pi_S(y_n | x, p, y_{<n})|$
 A large advantage gap implies a credible capability discrepancy under controlled privileged conditions, indicating high-value, transferrable knowledge. Empirical ablation studies confirm that tokens with high advantage gaps are critical for distillation, while low-advantage tokens contribute negligibly.

**Advantage-aware Dual Distillation (DOPD):**
DOPD dynamically routes token-level supervision based on the calculated privilege advantage gap ($A_n$) and the predicted probabilities of the privileged teacher ($q_T$) and privileged student ($q_S$) for each token. The approach categorizes tokens into four distinct regimes, each with a tailored distillation strategy:

1. **Low Advantage Gap ($A_n < \bar{A}$) with High Predicted Probabilities ($q_S + q_T \geq \bar{q}_S + \bar{q}_T$) (Regime $I_{LH}$):**
 - **Condition:** Both privileged policies are confident and consistent in their predictions, suggesting the bottleneck is primarily the absence of privileged information rather than a capability gap.
 - **Strategy:** Apply a "light teacher distillation" objective using Top-K reverse KL divergence (Equation 6). This conservatively transfers useful privileged knowledge without over-transferring potential shortcuts.

2. **Low Advantage Gap ($A_n < \bar{A}$) with Low Predicted Probabilities ($q_S + q_T < \bar{q}_S + \bar{q}_T$) (Regime $I_{LL}$):**
 - **Condition:** Both privileged policies assign low probability, indicating the token might be outside their reliable competence regions.
 - **Strategy:** Utilize a "weak self-regularizing distillation" using Top-K reverse KL divergence from the privileged student itself, with a smaller coefficient and stop-gradient (Equation 7). This stabilizes training and prevents forcing the student to imitate uncertain teacher predictions. The privileged student acts as a consistency anchor.

3. **High Advantage Gap ($A_n \geq \bar{A}$) with High Teacher Probability ($q_T \geq q_S$) (Regime $I_{HT}$):**
 - **Condition:** The privileged teacher shows a clear and confident advantage over the privileged student, implying critical transferable capability.
 - **Strategy:** Employ "strong full-vocabulary teacher distillation" using JS divergence (Equation 8). Full-vocabulary alignment provides dense signals, enabling the student to acquire dominant decisions and informative secondary preferences.

4. **High Advantage Gap ($A_n \geq \bar{A}$) with High Student Probability ($q_S > q_T$) (Regime $I_{HS}$):**
 - **Condition:** The privileged student is relatively more confident than the privileged teacher, suggesting that strongly constraining the student to the teacher might suppress valid exploratory behavior.
 - **Strategy:** Apply a "light privileged-student distillation" objective using Top-K reverse KL divergence with stop-gradient (Equation 9). This encourages consistency with the student's own privileged counterpart while avoiding over-regularization.

The total objective (Equation 10) combines these four token-wise losses using indicator masks, exhaustively partitioning the token space. The overall optimization objective (Equation 11) is the expected value of this combined loss over input data and student-sampled trajectories.

This adaptive routing mechanism enables DOPD to selectively transfer knowledge, stabilize training, and encourage exploration, effectively disentangling capability transfer from privilege-information imitation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on both Large Language Model (LLM) and Vision-Language Model (VLM) settings demonstrate the effectiveness of DOPD across various aspects:

1. **Distillation Performance:**
 - **LLM-based OPD (Qwen3-8B -> Qwen3-1.7B):** DOPD achieved a 12.3-point average performance gain, recovering 89.8% of the original teacher-student gap. It surpassed Vanilla OPD by 7.5 points and outperformed the strongest baselines (ExOPD, Uni-OPD, EOPD) by 4.4, 4.8, and 5.3 points, respectively. Notably, DOPD even exceeded the teacher policy's performance on four challenging benchmarks (MATH500, AIME25, ZebraLogic, LCBv5), particularly in reasoning and coding tasks.
 - **VLM-based OPD (Qwen3-VL-8B -> Qwen3-VL-2B):** DOPD demonstrated a 10.1-point absolute gain, recovering 69.2% of the teacher-student gap. It outperformed Vanilla OPD by 6.0 points and other VLM-oriented baselines (Uni-OPD, Vision-OPD, VA-OPD) by 4.2, 2.8, and 2.1 points, respectively. Improvements were more significant in visual understanding tasks.

2. **Robustness and Scalability:**
 - DOPD consistently outperformed Vanilla OPD across five different teacher-student model pairs, yielding average gains of 11.1 to 14.1 points, which is two to three times greater than Vanilla OPD.
 - The method maintained robustness even with increasing teacher-student size ratios (e.g., Qwen3-8B to Qwen3-0.6B). While Vanilla OPD's performance gain decreased with larger capacity mismatches, DOPD showed gradually increasing gains and effectively mitigated the reduction in gap recovery ratio. This suggests DOPD provides a more scalable and reliable distillation mechanism for compact students learning from substantially larger teachers.

3. **Continual Learning Evaluation:**
 - DOPD exhibited superior sustained learning performance in a three-stage continual learning setup (general, reasoning, coding tasks). It enabled consistent capability accumulation with minimal performance degradation on previously learned domains, indicating effective incremental learning.

4. **Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Evaluation:**
 - DOPD demonstrated stronger cross-domain generalization, outperforming the second-best counterparts (ExOPD, Uni-OPD, EOPD) by 3.1 and 4.3 points on OOD reasoning and coding tasks, respectively.

5. **Training Stability:**
 - DOPD showed consistent and stable performance throughout training, achieving higher distillation efficiency and reaching the performance of competing methods much earlier.
 - The method maintained a healthy entropy trajectory, reflecting stable learning with calibrated exploration, in contrast to a self-distillation baseline that experienced entropy collapse and performance degradation.

6. **Privileged Information Analysis:**
 - The *form* of privileged information significantly impacted performance. Directly providing final answers led to severe information gaps and performance degradation, sometimes even worse than no privileged input.
 - For LLMs, step-level high-level hints *without* detailed execution steps yielded the largest gains.
 - For VLMs, bounding boxes paired with corresponding object labels were the most suitable modality. This confirmed that the efficacy of privileged information lies in providing capability-oriented guidance rather than just correctness of final answers.

7. **Token and Divergence Analysis:**
 - Token-level visualization confirmed that different token categories (based on advantage gap and probabilities) correspond to distinct functional roles (stable knowledge, low-value connectives, inherent ability gaps, self-consistent exploration).
 - Quantitative token ablation showed that tokens with high teacher probability and low student probability (representing inherent ability gaps) were crucial, and the adaptive distillation mechanism was essential for effectively leveraging all token types, leading to an 8-point improvement over uniform distillation.
 - Comparison of divergence objectives and strategies indicated that full-vocabulary alignment generally improved performance at the cost of computational overhead, and JS divergence provided relatively superior performance in the studied settings.

8. **Sensitivity and Ablation Studies:**
 - Sensitivity analysis on distillation intensity coefficients ($\beta_w, \beta_l$) identified optimal values for balancing critical and auxiliary token contributions.
 - Ablation studies confirmed the indispensable role of privileged input, the necessity of both teacher and student policy as distillation sources, and the effectiveness of advantage-aware and adaptive divergence designs.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The DOPD framework contributes to the field of large model distillation by addressing a critical, previously uncharacterized failure mode: privilege illusion. By systematically identifying and mitigating the impact of information asymmetry from privileged inputs, DOPD allows for more effective transfer of genuine capabilities rather than mere imitation of non-transferable shortcuts. This has several significant implications and potential impacts:

1. **Enhanced Distillation Effectiveness:** The consistent and substantial performance gains demonstrated across diverse LLM and VLM benchmarks, often recovering a large portion of the teacher-student gap and even surpassing teacher performance in specific tasks, indicate a breakthrough in distillation efficiency and quality. This could enable the deployment of smaller, more efficient student models with capabilities approaching or sometimes exceeding their larger, more resource-intensive teachers.

2. **Improved Robustness and Scalability:** DOPD's ability to maintain high performance gains across varying teacher-student size ratios, particularly in scenarios with large capacity mismatches, is crucial for scaling down very large foundation models. This suggests that DOPD can reliably transfer knowledge even when the student has significantly fewer parameters, making it a viable solution for resource-constrained environments or edge devices.

3. **Advanced Continual Learning and Generalization:** The superior performance in continual learning tasks, with reduced catastrophic forgetting, and improved out-of-distribution generalization capabilities, suggests that DOPD fosters a deeper, more robust understanding in student models. This could lead to more versatile and adaptable models capable of acquiring new skills incrementally and performing well on unseen tasks.

4. **Refined Understanding of Knowledge Transfer:** The detailed token analysis, privilege information analysis, and the formalization of the "privilege advantage gap" provide a more nuanced understanding of how knowledge is transferred and where challenges arise. This framework offers a valuable analytical lens for future research into distillation mechanisms.

5. **Practical Applicability:** By enabling smaller models to achieve higher performance with greater stability, DOPD has the potential to accelerate the adoption of large language and vision-language models in real-world applications where computational efficiency, low latency, and reduced memory footprint are critical. This could include personalized AI assistants, on-device language processing, and efficient multimodal understanding systems.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
The authors acknowledge a few limitations: the reliance on the availability and quality of privileged information, which incurs additional annotation/generation costs; increased computational overhead due to an extra forward pass of the student model; and the heuristic nature of the current token routing strategy.

Future research directions include developing more cost-effective mechanisms for acquiring privileged information, exploring alternative strategies for detecting advantage gaps, and designing more principled or learnable distillation routing mechanisms. More broadly, the dynamic dual distillation paradigm proposed by DOPD offers a new avenue for selective capacity transfer, inviting further work on more interpretable, efficient, and trustworthy distillation approaches beyond LLMs and VLMs.
