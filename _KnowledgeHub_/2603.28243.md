# Cost-Matching Model Predictive Control for Efficient Reinforcement Learning in Humanoid Locomotion

> [!summary] Summary
> A Cost-Matching Model Predictive Control (CM-MPC) framework enables efficient training for humanoid locomotion by learning MPC parameters through a cost-matching procedure, where a model-predicted cost-to-go aligns with measured long-horizon returns, thereby eliminating the need for repeated MPC solves during gradient-based optimization. This method improved disturbance rejection, reducing settling time by 31.25% and post-push peak error by 4.80%, while delivering smoother control actions.

## Problem

- Humanoid locomotion presents challenges due to high degrees of freedom, intermittent contacts, and strict safety requirements, compounded by the need for robustness against disturbances and modeling inaccuracies.
- Traditional Model Predictive Control (MPC) for humanoids often uses simplified models, leading to performance degradation from unmodeled dynamics and requiring extensive expert tuning of cost weights.
- Existing MPC-RL frameworks typically incur high computational costs during training because they necessitate repeatedly solving complex MPC optimization problems within the learning loop for gradient-based updates.

## Method

- CM-==MPC== treats a parameterized MPC scheme as a structured Q-function approximator, parameterizing the predictive model, stage costs, and terminal costs.
- It calculates an "MPC-induced action-value" by performing a forward rollout of the parameterized model along recorded action segments, eliminating the need to solve an optimization problem during the learning phase.
- MPC parameters are learned by minimizing the discrepancy between this efficiently computed "MPC-induced action-value" and a "measured long-horizon return" derived from actual closed-loop trajectories.

## Results

- The value mismatch between the MPC-induced cost-to-go and measured long-horizon returns rapidly decreased and converged during training, confirming improved fidelity of the MPC's internal value approximation.
- The learned CM-MPC controller demonstrated enhanced robustness to external disturbances, achieving a **31.25%** improvement in settling time and a **4.80%** reduction in post-push peak error compared to a baseline MPC.
- Adaptive reshaping of MPC parameters resulted in smoother control actions, with a **5.26%** decrease in high-percentile control effort and **1.10%** decrease in max jitter, while balancing overall stability with momentum tracking.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Matching an efficiently calculated, model-predicted cost-to-go with observed long-horizon returns from executed trajectories enables gradient-based learning of MPC parameters without repeatedly solving the optimization problem, significantly reducing training computational cost.
> - The learning procedure implicitly tunes the MPC's stage and terminal cost functions, effectively mitigating the short-horizon bias of finite-horizon MPC by adapting terminal cost weights to approximate long-term tail-costs.
> - Parameterized model dynamics and cost weights allow the MPC to adaptively compensate for systematic discrepancies between its internal planning model and high-fidelity execution dynamics, enhancing robustness.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Humanoid locomotion presents challenges due to its high degrees of freedom, intermittent contacts, and stringent safety requirements, compounded by the need for robustness against disturbances and modeling inaccuracies. Two prominent paradigms address these challenges: Model Predictive Control (MPC) and Reinforcement Learning (RL).

MPC offers a systematic framework for encoding task objectives and enforcing constraints through online optimization. To achieve real-time operation in humanoid robots, MPC often relies on simplified or reduced-order models. While computationally efficient, these simplifications can lead to performance degradation due to unmodeled dynamics, contact realization, or actuator abstractions, necessitating extensive expert tuning of cost weights and terminal penalties. More complex MPC formulations can increase fidelity but typically incur higher computational costs, often leading to hierarchical control architectures where MPC provides high-level plans to a fast tracking controller. This architectural separation can introduce mismatches between planned and executed actions, making performance sensitive to cost design, especially with short prediction horizons.

Reinforcement Learning (RL) provides a data-driven approach to learning robust locomotion behaviors. However, training RL policies for humanoids is often sample-inefficient and highly sensitive to reward function design. Successful deployments frequently involve large-scale simulation, domain randomization, or system identification to bridge the sim-to-real gap. A significant limitation of purely learned policies is their inability to naturally enforce hard safety and contact constraints, which are critical for robot operation.

The limitations of both pure MPC and pure RL have motivated the development of hybrid MPC-RL frameworks. These approaches aim to combine the strengths of MPC—such as explicit constraint handling and interpretability—with RL's capacity for data-driven adaptation to improve performance under model mismatch and uncertainty. Prior work by some of the authors (e.g.,) established an MPC-RL framework that parameterizes MPC components (predictive model, cost, constraints) and adapts them using RL signals. A computational bottleneck in this prior framework, particularly for complex and time-critical systems like humanoids, is the necessity to repeatedly solve the MPC optimization problem within the learning loop for gradient-based updates.

This paper addresses this specific computational limitation within the MPC-RL landscape. It introduces a "cost-matching" learning procedure designed to maintain the benefits of MPC-RL while significantly reducing training computational burden by eliminating the need for repeated MPC solves during the learning phase. The work thus contributes to the development of more efficient and scalable hybrid control-learning methods for complex robotic systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to propose and validate a computationally efficient learning procedure, termed Cost-Matching MPC (CM-MPC), for optimal humanoid locomotion. This procedure integrates into an MPC-based Reinforcement Learning (RL) framework, aiming to enhance control performance and robustness without the high computational costs associated with repeatedly solving optimal control problems during training.

The motivation for this work arises from several challenges inherent in humanoid locomotion control:

- **Complexity of Humanoid Locomotion:** Humanoid robots possess high degrees of freedom, engage in intermittent contacts with the environment, and operate under strict safety constraints. Achieving stable, agile, and robust locomotion in real-world scenarios is a complex control problem.
- **Limitations of Traditional MPC:** While MPC provides a principled way to handle constraints and objectives, its application in humanoids often necessitates simplified models to meet real-time computational demands. These simplifications can introduce model-plant mismatch, leading to suboptimal performance or reliance on extensive, expert-driven tuning of cost functions and terminal penalties. Furthermore, the typical hierarchical architecture, separating an MPC planner from a low-level tracking controller, can exacerbate planning-execution discrepancies, especially with short prediction horizons.
- **Limitations of Pure Reinforcement Learning:** Pure RL approaches, while capable of learning complex behaviors, are often data-intensive, requiring vast amounts of interaction data for training. They are also sensitive to reward function design and curriculum choices. Crucially, RL policies generally do not provide inherent guarantees for satisfying hard safety and contact constraints, which are fundamental in robotics. Mitigating sim-to-real gaps also adds complexity.
- **Computational Burden of Existing MPC-RL Frameworks:** The authors' prior MPC-RL work offered a method to parameterize and adapt MPC components using RL signals, thereby combining the strengths of both paradigms. However, standard gradient-based MPC-RL methods typically require differentiating through the MPC solver at each learning step. For complex systems like humanoids, where the MPC itself is computationally demanding and operates near real-time limits, this "solve-in-the-loop" training makes the learning process prohibitively expensive.

To address these motivations, the paper proposes CM-MPC. The core idea is to learn the MPC parameters by matching a computationally efficient "MPC-induced cost-to-go" (derived from forward rollouts with the parameterized model) to a "measured long-horizon return" obtained from actual closed-loop trajectories. This approach aims to preserve the benefits of MPC-RL—namely, data-driven adaptation, constraint handling, and interpretability—while significantly reducing the computational cost of training, making it feasible for complex humanoid control applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology introduces Cost-Matching Model Predictive Control (CM-MPC), an MPC-based Reinforcement Learning (RL) framework designed for efficient training in humanoid locomotion.

**A. Framework Overview:**
The system operates within a hierarchical control architecture where an upper-level nonlinear MPC planner generates reference commands for a lower-level, fixed PD-type tracking controller. This work focuses on learning-based parameterization of the MPC layer.

**B. Centroidal Dynamics Model:**
A complete centroidal dynamics model, based on the OCS2 framework, is used for the humanoid robot. The state `x` concatenates linear and angular centroidal momenta (`h_lin`, `h_ang`) and generalized coordinates (`q`, including base position/orientation and joint angles). The control input `u` comprises contact wrenches for each foot and joint velocities. The system dynamics `ẋ = f_dyn(x, u)` describe the evolution of linear and angular momentum, and the configuration `q`, ensuring kinematic consistency via the Centroidal Momentum Matrix (CMM).

**C. Constraints:**
The feasible state-input set is defined by both inequality and equality constraints:
- **Inequality:** Joint limits, foot collision (via Signed Distance Fields), friction cone for active feet, and Center of Pressure (CoP) constraints within foot dimensions.
- **Equality:** Zero foot velocity for feet in stance, zero contact wrench for feet in swing, and tracking a reference normal velocity for swing feet.

**D. Optimal Control Objective:**
Humanoid locomotion is formulated as a constrained Optimal Control Problem (OCP) over a finite prediction horizon `N`. The MPC seeks to minimize a cost function `J_MPC`, which includes a terminal cost `T(x_N)` and a sum of stage costs `L(x_i, u_i)`. The stage cost `L` is composed of several terms:
- `L_trac`: State and control effort tracking.
- `L_base`: Task-space kinematic penalty for upper-body motion.
- `L_com`: Horizontal CoM position regularization towards the midpoint of foot contacts.
- `L_swin`: Swing-foot pose and twist deviation penalties.
- `L_torq`: Regularization of contact wrenches through induced generalized torques.
The terminal cost `T(x_N)` is a quadratic penalty on the terminal state deviation from a reference.

**E. Parameterized MPC as a Q Function:**
The core innovation lies in treating the parameterized MPC scheme as a structured Q-function approximator.
1. **Parameterized MPC:** The predictive model `f_θ`, stage cost `L_θ`, and terminal cost `T_θ` are parameterized by a vector `θ`. The MPC problem (minimizing `J_MPC` subject to dynamics and constraints) defines the optimal control.
2. **MPC-Induced Action-Value Function:** To avoid solving the OCP during training, an "MPC-induced action-value" `Q_MPC_θ(s, a_0:N-1)` is defined. This value is computed by performing a *forward rollout* of the parameterized predictive model (`x_i+1 = f_θ(x_i, a_i)`) starting from state `s` and using a *recorded action segment* `a_0:N-1`. The accumulated cost includes the parameterized stage costs `L_θ(x_i, a_i)` and `T_θ(x_N)`, augmented by differentiable penalty terms for *state-related constraint violations* (`ϕ(r_i)`). This allows `Q_MPC_θ` to be evaluated and differentiated with respect to `θ` without solving an optimization problem, scaling linearly with horizon length `N`.

**F. Action-Value Evaluation via Data Rollouts:**
- **Data Collection:** A dataset `D = {(s_k, a_k, ℓ_k)}` of trajectories is collected from executing the current parameterized MPC policy in a high-fidelity simulator.
- **Measured Return:** For each state `s_k` in a trajectory, a "measured discounted return-to-go" `Q_meas(s_k, a_k)` is computed by summing the discounted observed stage costs `ℓ_k` along the *remaining recorded trajectory*. This `Q_meas` serves as the learning target, representing the true long-term cost incurred by the real system under the executed actions.
- `Q_MPC_θ` provides a finite-horizon, model-based approximation evaluated along the same recorded action segment `a_k:k+N-1`. The discrepancy between `Q_MPC_θ` and `Q_meas` is minimized.

**G. Cost-Matching Objective and Gradient:**
The learning objective is to minimize the discrepancy between `Q_MPC_θ` and `Q_meas`:
`min_θ L(θ):= E_D[(Q_MPC_θ - Q_meas)^2]`.
The gradient `∇_θ L(θ)` is computed analytically by differentiating `Q_MPC_θ` through the forward rollout and cost/penalty terms. Parameters `θ` are updated using a first-order optimization method.

**H. Convergence and Constraints:**
A theoretical guarantee (Theorem 1) establishes convergence of the cost-matching update to a first-order stationary point under standard smoothness and bounded-variance assumptions. During training, state constraints are handled via soft, differentiable penalties in `Q_MPC_θ`. However, upon deployment, the learned parameters `θ*` define a standard MPC controller where all input and state constraints are enforced as hard constraints through online constrained optimization, ensuring feasibility and safety.

**I. Instantiation for Humanoid Locomotion:**
- **Parameterized Model:** The centroidal momentum propagation dynamics (1a, 1b) are parameterized with learnable diagonal gains `θ_hl`, `θ_ha` to absorb systematic discrepancies (e.g., imperfect contact wrench realization, unmodeled compliance).
- **Parameterized Costs:** Quadratic weights for state tracking, control effort, and terminal costs (e.g., `Q_θ`, `R_θ`, `Q_f_θ`) are parameterized through learnable diagonal scalings.
- **Training Loop (Algorithm 1):** An on-policy training loop collects trajectories, computes `Q_meas` and `Q_MPC_θ` for mini-batches, and updates `θ` iteratively.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates the CM-MPC framework through simulations using a commercial humanoid platform (Unitree G1) and compares it against a manually tuned baseline MPC controller.

**A. Cost-Matching Diagnostics:**
- **Value Mismatch Reduction:** Figure 2 demonstrates that the validation Mean Squared Error (MSE) of the value mismatch (between `Q_MPC_θ` and `Q_meas`) rapidly decreases in early training iterations and converges as learning progresses. This indicates that the learned parameters significantly improve the fidelity of the MPC's internal cost-to-go approximation.
- **Parameter Evolution (Figure 2 inset):** The analysis of parameter evolution shows adaptive reshaping of the MPC objective. Specifically, the terminal cost weights (`Q_f`) increase substantially, suggesting that the learning process tunes these terms to act as an implicit approximation of the long-horizon tail-cost, mitigating the inherent myopia of finite-horizon MPC. Concurrently, torque-regularization parameters increase (leading to smoother control actions), while swing-foot tracking weights slightly decrease (allowing for greater flexibility in kinematic tracking to prioritize whole-body balance).
- **Value Density Plots (Figure 3):** Density plots comparing `Q_MPC_θ` and `Q_meas` before and after training show that the distribution of residuals tightens significantly after convergence. This further confirms improved consistency between the MPC surrogate value and the measured long-term return.

**B. Robustness Evaluation under Disturbances:**
- **Disturbance Benchmark:** The controllers were subjected to a scripted disturbance benchmark comprising lateral force pulses and a yaw torque pulse applied to the humanoid (Figure 4). These disturbances excite both linear and angular momentum channels.
- **Representative Rollout (Figure 5):** A representative closed-loop response under disturbance indicates that CM-MPC exhibits superior recovery behavior compared to the baseline.
 - **Faster Settling:** CM-MPC settles earlier to the pre-disturbance error band.
 - **Smoother Actions:** It produces smoother corrective actions, evidenced by reduced control effort (∥u∥) and lower control jitter (∥u̇∥) during the recovery phase.
 - **Momentum Trade-off:** While momentum tracking errors (∥h_lin - h_lin,ref∥ and ∥h_ang - h_ang,ref∥) do not consistently decrease, this is interpreted as a deliberate trade-off. The learned parameters prioritize stabilizing whole-body motion and rejecting disturbances through smoother actuation, rather than enforcing strict, potentially aggressive, momentum reference tracking.

**C. Comprehensive Statistical Analysis (Table I):**
Statistical results over six disturbance trials confirm the observed improvements:
- **Overall Tracking:** CM-MPC shows minor improvements in RMS and Peak Error (1.29% and 1.41% reduction, respectively), indicating better nominal tracking.
- **Control Effort & Smoothness:** A notable reduction in high-percentile control effort (Max Effort ∥u∥_99%: 5.26% decrease) and Max Jitter (∥u̇∥_99%: 1.10% decrease) confirms that the learned controller produces smoother, less impulsive control actions.
- **Momentum Analysis:** Linear and angular momentum tracking errors remain largely comparable, with a minor increase in linear momentum error (0.99%). This aligns with the qualitative observation of prioritizing overall stability and smooth actuation over exact momentum tracking during transients.
- **Post-Push Recovery:** This is where CM-MPC demonstrates the most significant advantages:
 - **Reduced Post-Push RMS:** A decrease in the root-mean-square error post-disturbance.
 - **Reduced Post-Push Peak Error:** A 4.80% reduction indicates smaller overshoot following disturbances.
 - **Reduced Integral Absolute Error (IAE):** A 6.82% reduction in accumulated deviation after disturbances.
 - **Faster Settling Time:** A substantial 31.25% improvement in settling time, signifying a quicker return to stable operation after disturbances.

In summary, the simulation results indicate that the cost-matching procedure effectively minimizes the discrepancy between the MPC's internal value estimate and the true long-term returns. The learned parameters lead to an MPC controller with improved disturbance rejection, characterized by faster and smoother recovery, while adaptively compensating for modeling discrepancies in the centroidal dynamics.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The proposed Cost-Matching Model Predictive Control (CM-MPC) framework contributes to the field of robotic locomotion and control-learning by addressing several critical challenges.

**A. Enhanced Training Efficiency:**
A primary significance is the substantial reduction in computational burden during training. By calculating the MPC-induced action-value through a forward rollout along recorded trajectories, rather than repeatedly solving the full MPC optimization problem, CM-MPC enables efficient gradient-based learning. This makes it feasible to train complex MPC parameters for time-critical systems like humanoids, where solving the MPC itself is already computationally intensive. The linear scaling of training cost with the prediction horizon `N` is a notable advantage.

**B. Improved Control Performance and Robustness:**
The results demonstrate that the learned CM-MPC controller achieves improved locomotion performance. Specifically, it exhibits enhanced robustness to external disturbances, characterized by faster recovery times, smaller overshoots, and reduced accumulated deviations after impacts, compared to manually tuned baselines. This suggests that the data-driven adaptation allows the MPC to better cope with model mismatch and unmodeled effects inherent in real-world scenarios.

**C. Synergistic Integration of MPC and RL:**
CM-MPC effectively combines the strengths of Model Predictive Control and Reinforcement Learning. It leverages MPC's capacity for explicit constraint handling, safety guarantees, and interpretability, while integrating RL's ability for data-driven policy improvement under uncertainty. This hybrid approach enables the system to learn optimal behaviors that respect physical limitations and safety requirements.

**D. Mitigation of Short-Horizon Bias:**
By matching the MPC's internal cost-to-go to long-horizon measured returns, the learning process implicitly tunes the MPC's stage and terminal cost functions. The observed increase in terminal cost weights acts as a learned tail-cost approximation, which helps to mitigate the inherent "short-horizon bias" of finite-horizon MPC formulations, aligning the controller's immediate decisions with better long-term performance.

**E. Adaptive Model and Objective Reshaping:**
The parameterization of both the predictive model (e.g., centroidal momentum gains) and the cost function weights allows the MPC to adapt to systematic discrepancies between its internal planning model and the high-fidelity execution dynamics. This data-driven compensation for modeling inaccuracies and abstraction effects from lower-level controllers leads to more effective control policies. The implicit tuning for disturbance rejection, by learning from trajectories collected under disturbances, further enhances the controller's adaptability.

**F. Generality and Expandability:**
The proposed cost-matching framework is presented as a general approach applicable to any system formulated as a constrained Optimal Control Problem where real-world (or high-fidelity simulation) data can be collected. This suggests potential for application across various robotic and control domains beyond humanoid locomotion.

**G. Open-Source Contribution:**
The provision of an open-source implementation facilitates reproducibility, further research, and broader adoption of the framework within the robotics and control communities.

In conclusion, CM-MPC represents a significant step towards enabling efficient and robust learning for complex robotic control problems. By decoupling the MPC solution from the learning loop, it makes sophisticated, data-driven adaptive control more tractable, with demonstrated benefits in humanoid locomotion performance and disturbance rejection.
