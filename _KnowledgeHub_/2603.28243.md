# Cost-Matching Model Predictive Control for Efficient Reinforcement Learning in Humanoid Locomotion

> [!summary] Summary
> A Cost-Matching Model Predictive Control (CM-MPC) framework enables efficient training for humanoid locomotion by learning MPC parameters through a cost-matching procedure, where a model-predicted cost-to-go aligns with measured long-horizon returns, thereby eliminating the need for repeated MPC solves during gradient-based optimization. This method improved disturbance rejection, reducing settling time by 31.25% and post-push peak error by 4.80%, while delivering smoother control actions.

## Problem

- Humanoid locomotion presents challenges due to high degrees of freedom, intermittent contacts, and strict safety requirements, compounded by the need for robustness against disturbances and modeling inaccuracies.
- Traditional Model Predictive Control (MPC) for humanoids often uses simplified models, leading to performance degradation from unmodeled dynamics and requiring extensive expert tuning of cost weights.
- Existing MPC-RL frameworks typically incur high computational costs during training because they necessitate repeatedly solving complex MPC optimization problems within the learning loop for gradient-based updates.

## Method

- CM-==MPC== treats a parameterized MPC scheme as a structured Q-function approximator, parameterizing the predictive model, stage costs, and terminal costs.
- It calculates an "MPC-induced action-value" by performing a forward rollout of the parameterized model along recorded action segments, eliminating the need to solve an optimization problem during the learning phase.
- MPC parameters are learned by minimizing the discrepancy between this efficiently computed "MPC-induced action-value" and a "measured long-horizon return" derived from actual closed-loop trajectories.

## Results

- The value mismatch between the MPC-induced cost-to-go and measured long-horizon returns rapidly decreased and converged during training, confirming improved fidelity of the MPC's internal value approximation.
- The learned CM-MPC controller demonstrated enhanced robustness to external disturbances, achieving a **31.25%** improvement in settling time and a **4.80%** reduction in post-push peak error compared to a baseline MPC.
- Adaptive reshaping of MPC parameters resulted in smoother control actions, with a **5.26%** decrease in high-percentile control effort and **1.10%** decrease in max jitter, while balancing overall stability with momentum tracking.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Matching an efficiently calculated, model-predicted cost-to-go with observed long-horizon returns from executed trajectories enables gradient-based learning of MPC parameters without repeatedly solving the optimization problem, significantly reducing training computational cost.
> - The learning procedure implicitly tunes the MPC's stage and terminal cost functions, effectively mitigating the short-horizon bias of finite-horizon MPC by adapting terminal cost weights to approximate long-term tail-costs.
> - Parameterized model dynamics and cost weights allow the MPC to adaptively compensate for systematic discrepancies between its internal planning model and high-fidelity execution dynamics, enhancing robustness.
