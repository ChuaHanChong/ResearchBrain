# Do What You Say: Steering Vision-Language-Action Models via Runtime Reasoning-Action Alignment Verification

> [!summary] Summary
> A new runtime policy steering framework, SEAL, enhances the reliability and generalizability of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models by verifying the semantic alignment between their high-level textual plans and low-level action outcomes. This method achieved up to a 15% increase in success rate on novel compositional tasks and improved robustness against various out-of-distribution shifts.

## Problem

- Reasoning Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models exhibit an "embodied Chain-of-Thought faithfulness gap," where generated textual plans do not reliably translate into actions achieving the intended physical outcomes.
- VLA models often struggle with generalization and robustness in out-of-distribution scenarios, including novel objects, backgrounds, or complex instructions.
- Prior runtime steering methods for robotics typically verify actions in isolation, lacking explicit semantic coherence checks between a model's high-level reasoning and its low-level actions.

## Method

- Develop SEAL (Steering for Embodied reasoning-action ALignment), a training-free, runtime policy steering framework for reasoning VLA models.
- Implement a three-stage process: Hypothesize by generating K candidate action sequences, Predict their outcomes using a dynamics model, and Verify semantic alignment using an off-the-shelf pre-trained VLM like GPT-4o.
- Select and execute the action sequence that best aligns with the VLA's self-generated textual plan, effectively using action diversity as a robustness mechanism.

## Results

- SEAL achieved 94-97% success rates on in-distribution tasks, consistently outperforming other baselines.
- On novel behavior composition, SEAL improved success rates by up to 15% (achieving 53%) compared to the next-best reasoning policy and significantly surpassed non-reasoning baselines.
- The method demonstrated superior robustness across four out-of-distribution shifts, maintaining 45% success in challenging visual viewpoint changes, a 17% improvement over the next-best method.
- The runtime steering framework operates with an average inference time of 347ms per step (K=10), which is efficient and comparable to or faster than prior steering methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A critical challenge in reasoning VLAs is the "embodied CoT faithfulness gap," where a model's textual reasoning fails to reliably translate into consistent low-level actions, particularly in novel situations.
> - Actively verifying the semantic alignment between a VLA's textual plan and the outcomes of its generated actions at runtime can enhance robustness and generalization.
> - The inherent diversity in VLA action generation, often a source of error, can be repurposed as a strength by sampling multiple action sequences and selecting the most faithful one through verification.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of generalizable robotic control has been a longstanding goal in artificial intelligence and robotics. Traditional robotic systems often struggle with adaptability to novel situations, complex instructions, and long-horizon tasks. Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represent a significant step towards addressing these challenges.

#### 1.1. Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Models

VLA models are emerging as powerful tools for robotic control. Inspired by the success of large language models (LLMs) and large vision models (LVAs), these models integrate pre-trained vision-language backbones with action-generation heads, fine-tuned on extensive robot demonstration data. Projects like Open X-Embodiment and models such as π0 have demonstrated impressive capabilities in interpreting natural language instructions and generating low-level actions for various manipulation tasks. However, despite their advancements, current VLA models are not yet "true generalists." Their performance tends to degrade in novel scenes, with novel objects, over extended task horizons, and when confronted with complex or abstract language instructions, leading to brittle behavior.

#### 1.2. Reasoning VLAs and Embodied Chain-of-Thought

To enhance robustness and leverage the sophisticated reasoning capabilities inherent in large language and vision models, a growing body of research has introduced "reasoning VLAs." This approach draws inspiration from the "Chain-of-Thought" (CoT) prompting technique in LLMs, where models generate intermediate step-by-step textual reasoning before producing a final response. In an embodied context, reasoning VLAs generate intermediate textual plans (e.g., "pick up the soup," "place it in the basket") that decompose a complex task into smaller, more manageable subgoals. These textual plans are often grounded in visual observations and then conditioned to generate subsequent low-level actions. This "embodied CoT" aims to improve the complexity and reliability of robotic task execution by providing a structured approach to problem-solving.

#### 1.3. The "Embodied CoT Faithfulness Gap"

This paper identifies a critical, yet often overlooked, failure mode within reasoning VLAs: the "embodied CoT faithfulness gap." Even when a reasoning VLA generates a semantically correct and logically sound textual plan, the actual low-level actions it produces may fail to realize the intended outcomes of that plan. This misalignment—where the *intended* outcome of the textual reasoning differs from the *actual* outcome of the generated actions—is particularly acute in out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios, involving novel objects, backgrounds, or rephrased instructions. This phenomenon mirrors "CoT faithfulness" issues in LLMs, where a model's stated reasoning might not genuinely reflect its internal computation process. The core problem, therefore, is ensuring that the robot *does what it says* (i.e., its actions faithfully execute its self-generated plan).

#### 1.4. Related Work and Differentiators

The proposed work differentiates itself from existing approaches in several key ways:

- **Runtime Optimization/Steering:** Prior work in runtime steering for robotics has explored methods like sampling multiple action chunks and verifying them against learned critics (e.g., Q-functions in) or fine-tuned VLMs. However, these methods typically verify actions in isolation or against a task-specific metric, without explicitly addressing the semantic coherence between a model's *high-level reasoning* and its *low-level actions*. This paper's novel contribution is to enforce this reasoning-action alignment.
- **OOD Robustness & Generalization:** While many studies focus on robustness to visual or semantic shifts, this work also emphasizes "compositional generalization"—the ability to recombine learned skills for novel tasks. The paper uses an extended LIBERO benchmark to systematically evaluate these OOD scenarios, moving beyond simple visual/semantic shifts to more complex behavioral compositions.
- **Training-free Nature:** Unlike methods requiring extensive re-training or fine-tuning of critics, this approach is training-free post-VLA training, leveraging powerful pre-trained VLMs as a verifier. This makes it more adaptable and less computationally intensive to deploy in new scenarios.

By formalizing the embodied CoT faithfulness gap and proposing a runtime verification mechanism, this research aims to unlock the full potential of reasoning VLAs, moving them closer to truly robust and generalizable robotic agents that can reliably follow complex instructions even in unforeseen circumstances.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **address and minimize the "embodied Chain-of-Thought (CoT) faithfulness gap" at runtime** in reasoning Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. This gap refers to the critical misalignment where a VLA's self-generated textual plan—its high-level reasoning—does not reliably translate into actions that achieve the intended physical outcomes, particularly in out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios.

The motivation for this work stems from several observations and challenges:

1. **Bridging High-Level Reasoning and Low-Level Control:** Reasoning VLAs are designed to enhance robotic instruction-following by incorporating step-by-step textual planning, akin to Chain-of-Thought in LLMs. However, despite generating correct plans, the generated actions can still be imprecise or fail to achieve the plan's outcomes, especially when encountering novel environments, objects, or instructions.
2. **Addressing Failures in OOD Scenarios:** Existing VLA models often struggle with generalization. The misalignment between reasoning and action becomes particularly pronounced in OOD contexts, where the policy might lack sufficient training data to robustly execute novel plans or react to unfamiliar visual cues.
3. **Leveraging Action Diversity:** The inherent stochasticity and diversity in action generation by VLA models, while sometimes leading to errors, can also be a source of strength if properly leveraged. The goal is to transform this diversity from a source of failure into an opportunity for improved robustness by selecting the most appropriate action sequence.
4. **Enabling Novel Behavior Composition:** For robots to be truly general-purpose, they must be able to recombine learned skills to solve entirely new tasks not explicitly demonstrated during training. Ensuring faithful execution of intermediate plans is crucial for successfully composing these novel behaviors.
5. **Achieving Training-Free Robustness:** The paper seeks a solution that does not require costly re-training or extensive fine-tuning for new tasks or OOD conditions. This emphasizes a practical, runtime-based approach that can adapt on-the-fly.

In essence, the paper aims to develop a method that guarantees a robot *does what it says* by actively verifying, at runtime, that the outcomes of its generated actions semantically align with its self-generated textual reasoning. This approach is designed to boost robustness against semantic and visual OOD perturbations and enable novel behavior composition without the need for additional fine-tuning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the proposed methodology, named **SEAL (Steering for Embodied reasoning-action ALignment)**, is a training-free, runtime policy steering framework. It ensures that a reasoning VLA's generated actions faithfully induce the outcomes described by its own intermediate textual plans. This is achieved through a three-stage process: Hypothesize, Predict, and Verify.

#### 3.1. Training a Reasoning VLA Model

Before the runtime steering can be applied, a base reasoning VLA model capable of interleaving textual planning and action generation needs to be established.

#### 3.1.1. Reasoning Data Annotation Pipeline

Most robotic demonstration datasets only provide a single high-level language instruction per episode, lacking the fine-grained intermediate textual plans necessary for training reasoning VLAs. To overcome this, the authors developed an automated annotation pipeline.
- **Input:** Demonstration videos (agentview images compiled into 10 fps video) and their high-level task instructions (ℓg).
- **Tool:** Gemini-2.5-Pro, a powerful multimodal LLM with video understanding capabilities.
- **Process:** Gemini is prompted to jointly generate a sequence of one-sentence textual sub-plans (ℓr) and their corresponding end-timesteps (t'j) for each sub-task. The output is structured as JSON, which includes a list of all plans, completed plans, and the next plan to execute.
- **Verification:** Manual verification of all generated annotations ensures quality.
This process yields a reasoning-annotated dataset (e.g., Libero-10-R, Libero-100-Basket-R, Libero-100-R) suitable for supervised fine-tuning.

#### 3.1.2. Reasoning VLA Training

The base reasoning VLA used in the experiments is based on the π0 architecture, fine-tuned following the recipe from.
- **Architecture:** The model features an adaptive switching capability between text and action generation, controlled by special `<think>` and `<act>` tokens. When `<think>` is generated, subsequent tokens are decoded as textual reasoning; when `<act>` is generated, subsequent tokens are passed to an action head to produce continuous action sequences (e.g., relative end-effector poses) via a diffusion process. This allows for variable-length action sequences corresponding to textual plans.
- **Training Objective:** The model is fine-tuned end-to-end on the reasoning-annotated dataset using a combined loss function (Eq. 2), which includes a cross-entropy loss for text generation and a flow-matching loss for action generation. This trains the model to generate both intermediate textual plans and plan-conditioned action sequences.

#### 3.2. Runtime Policy Steering via Semantic Verification (SEAL)

Once the reasoning VLA is trained, SEAL intervenes at runtime to ensure embodied CoT faithfulness. After a new textual plan (ˆℓr) is generated by the base VLA, the following three stages unfold:

#### 3.2.1. Hypothesize

- **Process:** The policy generates a batch of `K` candidate action sequences (A_t) in parallel from the trained VLA (πr-VLAθ). Each sequence is generated autoregressively, one action at a time, conditioned on the current observation (o_t), the newly generated textual plan (ˆℓr), and the global instruction (ℓg).
- **Termination:** Action sequence generation for a candidate stops when the model outputs a `<think>` token, resulting in variable-length action sequences.

#### 3.2.2. Predict

- **Process:** Simultaneously with the Hypothesize stage, a dynamics model (ˆP) is used to predict the outcomes of each candidate action sequence. In a simulation environment, this involves executing each of the `K` candidate action sequences in separate, parallel environment instances to generate corresponding observation sequences (ˆo(k)t).
- **Coupling:** This Hypothesize-Predict loop is tightly coupled and interleaved because the autoregressive policy requires predicted observations to generate subsequent actions within a sequence. This continues until a `<think>` token is generated for each candidate.

#### 3.2.3. Verify

- **Process:** An off-the-shelf pre-trained Vision Language Model (VLM), specifically GPT-4o, acts as an open-world verifier (Rψ) to score the alignment between the predicted outcome of each candidate action sequence and the textual plan (ˆℓr).
- **Input to VLM:** For each candidate, the VLM is provided with the initial image of the episode (I1), the predicted final image from the candidate sequence (ˆI(k)t+Hk), and the textual plan (ˆℓr).
- **Output:** The VLM is prompted (using a structured prompt, Fig. 8 in Appendix) to return a binary score (Rψ ∈ {0, 1}) indicating whether the predicted outcome successfully fulfills the plan.
- **Selection:** The action sequence that receives the highest alignment score from the VLM (ideally, a "Success") is selected for execution on the robot.

#### 3.2.4. Runtime Considerations

- **Low Latency:** The framework is optimized for low latency through parallel and asynchronous execution. Candidate action sequences are generated in parallel, and verification starts asynchronously as soon as a sequence completes.
- **Early-Exit Strategy:** The system employs an early-exit strategy: as soon as the VLM successfully verifies any of the `K` candidates, that sequence is executed, without waiting for all `K` candidates to be evaluated. This significantly reduces decision-making time.
- **Synchronization:** After executing a verified sequence, all parallel simulation environments are synchronized to match the final state of the successfully executed sequence, ensuring a consistent starting point for the next planning step.

This three-stage, runtime-verified approach allows SEAL to dynamically select the most faithful action sequence, transforming the base VLA's natural action diversity into a robustness mechanism.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted in simulation using an extended LIBERO benchmark systematically evaluate SEAL's effectiveness across various dimensions. The findings consistently demonstrate the benefits of runtime reasoning-action alignment verification.

#### 4.1. In-Distribution (ID) Performance

On in-distribution tasks from LIBERO-10, SEAL achieved the highest success rates (94-97%) compared to all baselines (vanilla π0, π0-V-GPS, and π0-reason). Both reasoning-enabled models (π0-reason and SEAL) generally outperformed the non-reasoning π0 policy. While π0-V-GPS, which uses a learned Q-function as a critic, also showed good performance in ID tasks due to reliable Q-value guidance (Figure 3, left), SEAL's plan-level verification (evaluating entire variable-length action sequences against textual plans) proved superior to chunk-based steering by filtering out imprecise executions or those leading to incorrect subgoals. This highlights SEAL's ability to maintain semantic coherence over a longer horizon.

#### 4.2. Novel Behavior Composition

For novel behavior composition tasks, which require recombining learned skills in unseen ways, SEAL's advantage became more pronounced.
- As the VLA training dataset grew in size and diversity (from LIBERO-10 to LIBERO-100-R), reasoning-enabled models (π0-reason and SEAL) showed substantial performance gains.
- In contrast, non-reasoning baselines (π0 and π0-V-GPS) stagnated at low success rates (around 15%). This is attributed to the weak language understanding and generalization capabilities of π0-V-GPS's MUSE encoder and its learned Q-function, which gave misleading value signals in novel scenarios (Figure 3, right).
- π0-reason showed stronger generalization due to its VLM backbone generating correct plans for novel long-horizon problems. However, its performance was limited by increased action diversity leading to misalignment.
- SEAL specifically resolved this tension by verifying and selecting actions that faithfully executed the generated plans, leveraging action diversity as a strength. On the largest LIBERO-100-Compose dataset, SEAL achieved a 53% success rate, significantly outperforming π0-reason's 38% and the baselines' 16%.

#### 4.3. Robustness to OOD Shifts

SEAL demonstrated superior robustness across four challenging OOD shifts (Figure 4):
- **Semantic OOD (Lang-Rephrase & Lang-Object-Property):** Reasoning-based models (π0-reason and SEAL) proved most robust. While π0-reason was robust to paraphrasing, SEAL further improved performance by filtering action errors stemming from spurious word-object correlations, consistently maintaining over 91% success.
- **Visual OOD (Visual-Scene & Visual-Viewpoint):**
 - Replacing distractor objects (Visual-Scene) had minimal impact on most methods, except for a 4% drop for π0-V-GPS, suggesting its Q-function overfit to visual context. SEAL achieved 98% success.
 - Changing viewpoint and background (Visual-Viewpoint) was the most challenging, causing significant performance drops for all methods. However, SEAL maintained a 45% success rate, outperforming the next-best baseline (π0-reason) by over 17% (25% for π0-reason). This highlights SEAL's ability to filter out noisy actions even under severe visual shifts (Figure 5).

Overall, SEAL was consistently the most robust method across all OOD stress tests, demonstrating that verifying reasoning-action alignment is crucial for generalization. The results also indicate that current VLA models are more vulnerable to major visual shifts than to semantic changes.

#### 4.4. Runtime Scaling and Efficiency

The study on runtime scaling (Figure 6) revealed that increasing the number of candidate action sequences (K) directly boosted performance.
- **Success Rate & Episode Length:** As K increased from 1 to 10, SEAL showed higher success rates (e.g., a 15% gain on compositional tasks) and shorter average episode lengths, meaning tasks were completed more efficiently.
- **Inference Time:** Inference time scaled linearly with K. At K=10, SEAL's average inference time per step was 347ms, which is lower than comparable prior work (e.g., ~520ms in).
- **Bottleneck:** The VLM verifier query time was identified as the main bottleneck, with batch action sampling scaling more efficiently.
The diminishing returns in performance gain as K increased indicated that performance is ultimately bounded by the VLM verifier's occasional inaccuracy and, more fundamentally, the quality of the base model's proposed actions. If the base policy fails to generate any viable sequences, verification cannot succeed.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research makes a significant contribution to the field of robotic learning and generalizable AI by identifying and effectively addressing the "embodied Chain-of-Thought faithfulness gap." By introducing a novel, training-free, runtime steering method called SEAL, the authors enhance the reliability and generalization capabilities of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models.

#### 5.1. Key Contributions and Advantages

- **Enhanced Reliability and Generalization:** SEAL consistently outperforms strong baselines across in-distribution tasks, challenging out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios (semantic and visual shifts), and novel behavior composition tasks. Performance gains of up to 15% are demonstrated, particularly in complex and unseen situations. This is crucial for deploying robots in dynamic, real-world environments.
- **Runtime Reasoning-Action Alignment Verification:** The core innovation lies in actively verifying the semantic alignment between a VLA's high-level textual plan and the outcomes of its low-level actions *at runtime*. This ensures that the robot truly "does what it says," making its behavior more trustworthy and predictable.
- **Leveraging Action Diversity as a Strength:** Instead of treating the stochasticity and diversity of action generation as a source of error, SEAL transforms it into an asset. By sampling multiple action sequences and selecting the one that best aligns with the textual plan, the method effectively leverages the policy's diverse capabilities.
- **Training-Free Policy Steering:** SEAL operates without requiring additional fine-tuning or re-training of the base VLA policy or a specialized critic. It leverages the inherent strengths of pre-trained, off-the-shelf Vision-Language Models (VLMs) like GPT-4o as a verifier, making it highly adaptable and cost-effective for new tasks and environments.
- **Scalability and Efficiency:** The framework is designed for low latency, employing parallel execution, asynchronous verification, and an early-exit strategy. While VLM query time is a bottleneck, the overall inference time scales favorably compared to prior methods.
- **New Benchmarks for Generalization:** The contribution of a reasoning-annotated LIBERO-100 dataset and an extended LIBERO benchmark with tailored OOD variations is invaluable for the community. These resources will facilitate further research into generalization capabilities and OOD robustness of reasoning VLAs.

The potential impact of this work is substantial. By making VLA models more robust and capable of generalization, SEAL pushes the boundaries of what embodied AI systems can achieve. This can lead to robots that are more reliable in manufacturing, logistics, domestic assistance, and other complex real-world applications where precise instruction following and adaptability to unforeseen circumstances are paramount. It lays a groundwork for more trustworthy and autonomous robotic agents that can operate effectively even when faced with significant novelty.

#### 5.2. Limitations and Future Directions

The authors acknowledge several limitations that also highlight promising avenues for future research:

- **Dependence on Base VLA Quality:** SEAL's performance is inherently bounded by the quality and diversity of the candidate action sequences generated by the underlying reasoning VLA. If the base model fails to propose any viable actions, verification cannot salvage the situation. Future work could explore methods to improve the *diversity* and *quality* of action proposals.
- **Verifier Reliability:** While GPT-4o performs well, VLMs can make incorrect judgments, especially for fine-grained gripper-object interactions in wrist-view images or scenes with severe occlusion, which might be out-of-distribution for the VLM's training. Research into more robust and physically grounded VLM verifiers, or specialized critics for low-level interactions, could address this.
- **Latency and Compute Overhead:** Despite optimizations, the method introduces additional inference-time latency and computational cost due to parallel sampling and VLM evaluation. Optimizing inference infrastructure, exploring quantization techniques, or developing more efficient verifiers (e.g., faster, smaller models) are crucial for real-time deployment in latency-sensitive applications.
- **Domain Gap:** While tested in simulation, bridging the sim-to-real gap for such methods, particularly for learned world models and VLM verification, remains a challenge for real-world robotic deployment.

In conclusion, "Do What You Say" presents an important step towards robust and generalizable robotic control. By rigorously enforcing the alignment between a robot's reasoning and its actions, this work provides a powerful framework for building more intelligent, reliable, and adaptable embodied AI agents.
