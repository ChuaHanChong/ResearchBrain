# Responsive Noise-Relaying Diffusion Policy: Responsive and Efficient Visuomotor Control

> [!summary] Summary
> The Responsive Noise-Relaying Diffusion Policy (RNR-DP) framework improves visuomotor control by enabling single-action rollouts while maintaining motion consistency and responsiveness. It achieves an average of 18.0% higher success rates on response-sensitive tasks and operates approximately 12.5 times faster than standard Diffusion Policy.

## Problem

- Diffusion Policy (DP) often requires a large action horizon (TaT_aTa) to prevent 'mode bouncing' in multi-modal environments, leading to inconsistent actions.
- A large action horizon compromises responsiveness, as later actions are not conditioned on the most recent observations.
- DP struggles with tasks demanding highly responsive control, such as contact-rich or dynamic object manipulation.

## Method

- Introduces a noise-relaying buffer that holds a sequence of actions, each perturbed with a progressively increasing level of noise.
- Employs a sequential denoising mechanism that executes the most denoised action and reuses denoising steps for subsequent actions, ensuring responsiveness and computational efficiency.
- Utilizes a UNet-based architecture with noise-aware conditioning, integrating separate time embeddings for each action's specific noise level.

## Results

- Achieved an average of **18.0%** higher success rate on response-sensitive tasks (e.g., PushT, RollBall, Adroit Relocate) compared to Diffusion Policy.
- Demonstrated approximately **12.5** times faster inference speed than standard Diffusion Policy, while maintaining comparable or superior performance on regular tasks.
- Outperformed other acceleration methods (DDIM, Consistency Policy) by **6.9%** to **32.0%** in average success rate on regular tasks with comparable computational budgets.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Training with a mixture of linear and random noise schedules is crucial for the denoiser to effectively handle both independent denoising and smooth sequential action generation.
> - A 'laddering initialization' process for the noise-relaying buffer is essential at the start of an episode to transition from random noise to a monotonically increasing noise schedule.
> - The noise-relaying buffer allows for action consistency without needing a large action horizon, by ensuring a smooth progression of actions within the same behavioral mode.
