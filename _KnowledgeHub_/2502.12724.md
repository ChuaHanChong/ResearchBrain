# Responsive Noise-Relaying Diffusion Policy: Responsive and Efficient Visuomotor Control

> [!summary] Summary
> The Responsive Noise-Relaying Diffusion Policy (RNR-DP) framework improves visuomotor control by enabling single-action rollouts while maintaining motion consistency and responsiveness. It achieves an average of 18.0% higher success rates on response-sensitive tasks and operates approximately 12.5 times faster than standard Diffusion Policy.

## Problem

- Diffusion Policy (DP) often requires a large action horizon (TaT_aTa) to prevent 'mode bouncing' in multi-modal environments, leading to inconsistent actions.
- A large action horizon compromises responsiveness, as later actions are not conditioned on the most recent observations.
- DP struggles with tasks demanding highly responsive control, such as contact-rich or dynamic object manipulation.

## Method

- Introduces a noise-relaying buffer that holds a sequence of actions, each perturbed with a progressively increasing level of noise.
- Employs a sequential denoising mechanism that executes the most denoised action and reuses denoising steps for subsequent actions, ensuring responsiveness and computational efficiency.
- Utilizes a UNet-based architecture with noise-aware conditioning, integrating separate time embeddings for each action's specific noise level.

## Results

- Achieved an average of **18.0%** higher success rate on response-sensitive tasks (e.g., PushT, RollBall, Adroit Relocate) compared to Diffusion Policy.
- Demonstrated approximately **12.5** times faster inference speed than standard Diffusion Policy, while maintaining comparable or superior performance on regular tasks.
- Outperformed other acceleration methods (DDIM, Consistency Policy) by **6.9%** to **32.0%** in average success rate on regular tasks with comparable computational budgets.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Training with a mixture of linear and random noise schedules is crucial for the denoiser to effectively handle both independent denoising and smooth sequential action generation.
> - A 'laddering initialization' process for the noise-relaying buffer is essential at the start of an episode to transition from random noise to a monotonically increasing noise schedule.
> - The noise-relaying buffer allows for action consistency without needing a large action horizon, by ensuring a smooth progression of actions within the same behavioral mode.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is situated within the field of imitation learning for robotic control, an area that seeks to train robots by observing expert demonstrations. Imitation learning is a powerful approach for teaching robots a wide range of complex tasks, including grasping, locomotion, and dexterous and mobile manipulation. Recent advancements in computer vision and natural language processing have led to increasingly sophisticated imitation learning frameworks.

A significant development in this area is Diffusion Policy (Chi et al., 2023), which models robot action sequences using conditional denoising diffusion probabilistic models. Diffusion Policy has established new performance benchmarks compared to traditional imitation learning techniques and has gained adoption as a foundational framework in both research and practical applications.

However, Diffusion Policy faces a recognized limitation: its performance is often contingent on using a relatively large action horizon ($T_a$), meaning multiple actions are executed per inference step. This design choice is necessary to mitigate "mode bouncing," where independently sampled actions in multi-modal environments might switch between different behavioral modes, leading to inconsistent motion. While a large action horizon helps maintain consistency, it simultaneously introduces a drawback: most actions within the predicted sequence are not conditioned on the most recent observations from the environment. This characteristic compromises the policy's responsiveness and adaptability, particularly in tasks that demand real-time reaction to environmental changes or dynamic objects.

In parallel, research into diffusion models has also focused on improving their computational efficiency. Techniques like Denoising Diffusion Implicit Models (DDIM) (Song et al., 2020) and Consistency Models (Song et al., 2023) aim to accelerate the iterative sampling processes that can be computationally intensive. These acceleration methods are increasingly explored in the robotics community to speed up diffusion policies. Furthermore, the concept of using motion buffers within diffusion models for motion synthesis (e.g., TEDi (Zhang et al., 2024)) has emerged, providing a precedent for managing sequences of progressively noised states or actions.

The Responsive Noise-Relaying Diffusion Policy (RNR-DP) positions itself as an advancement over Diffusion Policy by directly addressing its responsiveness limitation while also offering computational efficiency benefits. It integrates ideas from diffusion model acceleration and motion buffer concepts to enable consistent, yet highly responsive, visuomotor control, particularly for tasks that have proven challenging for the original Diffusion Policy.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for developing the Responsive Noise-Relaying Diffusion Policy (RNR-DP) stems from identifying and addressing a fundamental limitation within the established Diffusion Policy framework.

**Identified Limitations of Diffusion Policy:**

1. **Dependence on Large Action Horizon ($T_a$):** Diffusion Policy's optimal performance is often observed when it predicts and executes a sequence of multiple actions (e.g., $T_a = 8$) rather than a single action per inference step ($T_a = 1$). A significant degradation in performance is noted when $T_a = 1$.
2. **Mitigation of Mode Bouncing:** The necessity for a large $T_a$ is attributed to the nature of modeling multi-modal data. In scenarios where a robot might have multiple valid ways to perform a sub-task, each inference step might independently sample actions aligned with a specific mode. Executing only one action from such a potentially "mode-switching" prediction can lead to "mode bouncing," causing jerky, inconsistent, and often failing behaviors. A larger action horizon helps ensure that a sequence of actions adheres to the same mode, maintaining consistency. Empirical validation showed that this performance degradation at $T_a=1$ is pronounced in multi-modal settings but not in single-modal settings.
3. **Compromised Responsiveness:** While a large action horizon ensures consistency, it inherently limits responsiveness. When a policy commits to executing a sequence of actions predicted from a single observation, the later actions in that sequence are not conditioned on the most recent environmental feedback. This delay in incorporating updated observations makes the policy less adaptable to sudden environmental changes, unforeseen disturbances, or dynamic object interactions.
4. **Struggles with Response-Sensitive Tasks:** Consequently, Diffusion Policy has been observed to struggle with tasks that intrinsically demand highly responsive control. Examples include contact-rich object manipulation where fine-grained, real-time adjustments are necessary (e.g., handling a slipping ball or pushing a dynamic T-shaped block).

**Key Objectives of RNR-DP:**

Based on these identified limitations, the key objectives of RNR-DP are:

1. **Achieve Responsive Control:** The primary objective is to enable the robot to generate actions that are always conditioned on the latest available observations. This means reducing the lag between environmental state changes and the policy's reactive behavior.
2. **Maintain Motion Consistency without Large Action Horizons:** To overcome the mode bouncing issue that necessitates large $T_a$ in Diffusion Policy, RNR-DP aims to provide a mechanism that ensures action consistency even when generating and executing a single, immediate action per inference step.
3. **Improve Computational Efficiency (Acceleration):** As a secondary but important objective, RNR-DP seeks to accelerate the action generation process compared to traditional iterative denoising, making the policy more efficient for real-time deployment. This is achieved by reusing denoising steps from previous computations.
4. **Handle Dynamic and Contact-Rich Tasks:** The ultimate goal is to develop a policy capable of robustly performing tasks that require responsive and adaptive control, such as manipulating dynamic objects or engaging in complex contact interactions, which are challenging for existing Diffusion Policy implementations.

In essence, RNR-DP aims to resolve the dilemma between action consistency (requiring a long action horizon in DP) and responsiveness (requiring actions based on the latest observation).

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Responsive Noise-Relaying Diffusion Policy (RNR-DP) introduces a novel architectural and procedural design to address the responsiveness and efficiency limitations of Diffusion Policy. Its core components include a noise-relaying buffer and a sequential denoising mechanism, supported by specific design choices for training and inference.

#### 3.1. Noise-Relaying Buffer and Sequential Denoising Mechanism

At the heart of RNR-DP is the **noise-relaying buffer** ($\tilde{Q}_t$). This buffer is a queue that holds a sequence of actions, each perturbed with a progressively increasing level of noise. Specifically, for a buffer capacity $f$, the buffer contains $f$ noisy actions, with noise levels linearly increasing from 1 (least noisy) to $f$ (most noisy).

The **sequential denoising mechanism** operates within a continuous control loop:
1. **Denoising Step:** At each time step $t$, the current buffer $\tilde{Q}_t$ (containing noisy actions $a^{(1)}_t, \dots, a^{(f-1)}_{t+f-2}, a^{(f)}_{t+f-1}$) is passed through the trained denoising network, conditioned on the most recent observation $O_t$. The network processes all $f$ noise levels simultaneously.
2. **Immediate Action Execution:** The denoising process transforms $\tilde{Q}_t$ into $Q_t = \{a^{(0)}_t, a^{(1)}_{t+1}, \dots, a^{(f-1)}_{t+f-1}\}$. The action at the head of this new sequence, $a^{(0)}_t$, is now noise-free (fully denoised). This clean action is immediately dequeued and executed in the environment. This ensures that the robot's action is based on the current observation $O_t$, maximizing responsiveness.
3. **Buffer Update (Shifting and Enqueuing):** After $a^{(0)}_t$ is executed, the remaining $f-1$ actions in $Q_t$ are left-shifted one position within the buffer. A new, *fully noisy* action (sampled from a standard Gaussian distribution $N(0, I)$) is then appended to the tail of the buffer.
4. **Reuse of Denoising Steps:** This design enables the reuse of $f-1$ denoising steps from the previous output. In essence, each single denoising step of the network generates one immediate, clean action ($a^{(0)}_t$) while simultaneously preparing the next $f-1$ actions in a progressively denoised state for future steps. This mechanism maintains motion consistency by ensuring a smooth progression of actions within the same mode and provides a computational efficiency benefit by avoiding full denoising from scratch at each step.

#### 3.2. Key Design Choices

To support the noise-relaying buffer and sequential denoising, RNR-DP incorporates several key design elements:

1. **Mixture Noise Scheduling (Training):**
 - Unlike Diffusion Policy, which applies a single noise level to an entire action sequence during training, RNR-DP trains its denoiser to handle actions with independent noise levels.
 - During training, each action $a_j$ in a target sequence $A_t$ can be perturbed by one of $f$ predefined noise levels, drawn from a fixed variance schedule ($\beta_1, \dots, \beta_f$).
 - A "mixture schedule" is employed: with a probability $p_{linear}$ (e.g., 0.4), actions are perturbed by linearly increasing variances (a linear schedule). With probability $1 - p_{linear}$, actions are perturbed by random variances chosen independently from $\beta_1$ to $\beta_f$ (a random schedule).
 - **Motivation:** The random schedule trains the model to denoise actions independently (similar to $T_a=1$ scenarios), while the linear schedule enforces the smooth transitions needed for the noise-relaying buffer during inference. This combined approach fosters robustness and adaptability.

2. **Laddering Initialization (Inference):**
 - At the very beginning of an episode, the noise-relaying buffer is initialized with $f$ fully noisy actions sampled from $N(0, I)$.
 - To prepare the buffer for the sequential denoising process and align it with the linear noise schedule used during inference, RNR-DP performs an "initialization" phase. This involves iteratively denoising the buffer $f$ times using the *random schedule* and conditioning on the initial observation $O_0$.
 - **Motivation:** This "laddering" process transitions the buffer from a state of uniform random noise to one where noise levels are monotonically increasing, as required by the noise-relaying buffer for smooth and responsive control from the outset.

3. **Noise-Aware Conditioning:**
 - In Diffusion Policy, a single diffusion step is encoded into one time embedding. RNR-DP, due to its mixed scheduling and noise-relaying buffer, deals with multiple noise levels simultaneously.
 - For each action's noise level $k_j$ (from $1$ to $f$), an MLP encodes a separate time embedding. These $f$ embeddings are then appended to the observation features derived from the encoder $E_{obs}$.
 - **Motivation:** This allows the denoising network to be aware of the specific noise level associated with each action in the buffer, enabling it to accurately decode actions at various noise levels using the most up-to-date observations, leading to more dynamic and consistent behaviors.

#### 3.3. Policy Architecture

RNR-DP utilizes a UNet-based architecture, similar to the original Diffusion Policy. This architecture typically comprises downsampling and upsampling modules with residual blocks, 1D temporal convolutions, group normalizations, and Mish activation layers. The noise-aware conditioning data is integrated into each residual block via FiLM transformations (Perez et al., 2018). This UNet design choice prioritizes stability and ease of tuning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of Responsive Noise-Relaying Diffusion Policy (RNR-DP) focused on two primary questions: its ability to deliver responsive control and its efficiency as an acceleration method. Experiments were conducted on 9 tasks from ManiSkill2, ManiSkill3, and Adroit benchmarks, categorized into "response-sensitive" and "regular" groups. Baselines included various Diffusion Policy (DP) configurations (DDPM, EDM, DDIM) and Consistency Policy (CP), with an additional comparison to Streaming Diffusion Policy (SDP) provided in the appendix.

#### 4.1. Results on Responsive Control (Response-sensitive Group)

Five challenging tasks involving contact-rich, dynamic object manipulation were used to assess responsiveness: PushT, RollBall, PushChair, Adroit Relocate, and Adroit Door.
- **Performance Improvement:** RNR-DP consistently demonstrated improved performance compared to Diffusion Policy across all tested scenarios, encompassing both state-based and visual-based observations.
 - In state-based experiments, RNR-DP achieved a 15.1% improvement in average success rate over Diffusion Policy.
 - In visual-based experiments, it showed a 24.9% improvement.
 - Overall, RNR-DP surpassed Diffusion Policy by an average of 18.0% in success rate on these tasks.
- **Specific Task Success:** Notably, for the Adroit Relocate task, RNR-DP yielded a 38.6% performance increase over Diffusion Policy.
- **Analysis:** These performance gains are attributed to the design of the noise-relaying buffer and sequential denoising mechanism. This setup ensures action consistency, effectively eliminating frequent mode bouncing and enabling robust single-action rollouts ($T_a=1$). By rolling out single actions, RNR-DP ensures that all executed actions are conditioned on the latest observations, leading to more responsive control in dynamic environments.

#### 4.2. Results on Efficient Control (Regular Group)

Four simpler tasks, identified as not requiring highly responsive control (StackCube, TurnFaucet, Adroit Pen, Adroit Hammer), were used to evaluate RNR-DP's efficiency as an acceleration method.

- **Neural Function Evaluations per Action (NFEs/a):** A metric was introduced for fair efficiency comparison: NFEs/a = NFEs / $T_a$. RNR-DP inherently operates with $T_a=1$, meaning NFEs/a equals the number of denoising steps (which is 1 for RNR-DP in steady-state operation).
- **Comparison with Acceleration Methods:**
 - RNR-DP consistently achieved the highest overall performance among all DDIM and Consistency Policy (CP) variants tested.
 - When compared fairly against acceleration methods with comparable efficiency (NFEs/a = 1), such as 8-step DDIM and 8-step-chaining CP:
 - In state-based experiments, RNR-DP outperformed 8-step DDIM by 5.7% and 8-step-chaining CP by 66.2% in average success rate.
 - In vision-based experiments, RNR-DP surpassed 8-step DDIM by 8.5% and 8-step-chaining CP by 3.4%.
 - Overall, RNR-DP showed an average improvement of 6.9% over 8-step DDIM and 32.0% over 8-step-chaining Consistency Policy.
- **Robustness and Speed:** RNR-DP maintained an average success rate comparable to the full Diffusion Policy across all state and visual experiments, while operating approximately 12.5 times faster (DP typically uses 100 steps and $T_a=8$, leading to 12.5 NFEs/a, while RNR-DP uses 1 step and $T_a=1$, leading to 1 NFEs/a). Furthermore, RNR-DP demonstrated consistent and robust performance across all tasks, in contrast to DDIM and CP, which struggled in certain scenarios (e.g., 8-step DDIM achieved 0% on Adroit Hammer).
- **Analysis:** This efficiency is attributed to the noise-relaying buffer and sequential denoising, which allow the reuse of denoising steps and require only one denoising step to generate a single action. This design ensures that actions undergo sufficient denoising to maintain high quality, unlike DDIM and CP which achieve speedup by reducing denoising steps per action, potentially compromising performance.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies were conducted to examine the contribution of RNR-DP's key components:
- **Noise Scheduling Scheme:** Using a mixture of linear and random noise schedules during training significantly outperformed reliance on either a linear-only or random-only schedule. This highlights the importance of training the model with diverse noise perturbation patterns to leverage the full potential of the noise-relaying buffer.
- **Noise-Relaying Buffer Initialization:** The "laddering initialization" scheme, which iteratively denoises the initial noisy buffer to align with the linear noise schedule, was critical for performance. It achieved notable gains compared to directly using a fully noisy buffer ("Pure noise").
- **Noise-Relaying Buffer Capacity:** RNR-DP demonstrated robust performance over a wide range of buffer capacities (e.g., 56 to 92 for Adroit Relocate), with peak performance achieved at a capacity of 84. Performance declined when the capacity was either too small or too large, but still consistently outperformed Diffusion Policy within this range.
- **Model Prediction Type:** Models trained to predict the added noise component (epsilon) consistently outperformed those trained to directly predict the clean action sequence, aligning with established practices in diffusion models.

#### 4.4. Comparison with Streaming Diffusion Policy (SDP)

A comparison with Streaming Diffusion Policy (SDP) in the appendix revealed:
- **Responsiveness:** On response-sensitive tasks, SDP performed similarly to Diffusion Policy, indicating that it did not primarily address the responsiveness issue. RNR-DP, in contrast, achieved significantly more responsive control.
- **Efficiency:** On regular tasks, both SDP and RNR-DP preserved the performance of Diffusion Policy. However, RNR-DP demonstrated 6.25 times faster inference than SDP (1 NFEs/a vs 6.25 NFEs/a).
- **Methodology:** RNR-DP's sequential denoising with single-action rollouts and noise-aware conditioning with multiple time embeddings contributed to its advantages over SDP's chunk-wise noise and merged time embeddings.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Responsive Noise-Relaying Diffusion Policy (RNR-DP) presents advancements in the field of imitation learning for robotic control, particularly in addressing limitations of the Diffusion Policy framework.

#### 5.1. Significance

- **Addressing a Fundamental Limitation:** RNR-DP directly tackles the trade-off between action consistency and responsiveness inherent in Diffusion Policy. By enabling stable single-action rollouts ($T_a=1$) while preserving multi-modal capabilities, it allows actions to be conditioned on the most recent observations without suffering from mode bouncing. This is a crucial step towards robust control in dynamic environments.
- **Enhanced Performance in Response-Sensitive Tasks:** The consistent and substantial performance improvements on tasks involving dynamic object manipulation and contact-rich interactions demonstrate RNR-DP's capacity to handle scenarios where existing Diffusion Policy implementations struggle. This expansion of capabilities is significant for deploying robots in more complex and interactive real-world settings.
- **Superior Computational Efficiency:** Beyond responsiveness, RNR-DP also functions as an efficient acceleration method. It achieves performance comparable to the full Diffusion Policy while being significantly faster (e.g., 12.5 times faster) and outperforming other acceleration techniques like DDIM and Consistency Policy across various tasks. This efficiency is critical for real-time control applications where latency is a concern.
- **Robustness Across Task Types:** The consistent performance across both response-sensitive and regular tasks, and its resilience compared to other acceleration methods that sometimes fail completely on specific tasks, highlights RNR-DP's robustness and generalizability.
- **Novel Architectural Contributions:** The introduction of the noise-relaying buffer, sequential denoising mechanism, mixture noise scheduling, laddering initialization, and noise-aware conditioning are specific methodological contributions that could influence future designs of diffusion-based policies.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

- **Real-World Robotics Deployment:** The enhanced responsiveness and computational efficiency of RNR-DP are critical for the deployment of learned policies on physical robots. Real-world environments are inherently dynamic and unpredictable, requiring immediate adaptation to changes, disturbances, and object interactions. RNR-DP's ability to maintain a high control rate without sacrificing performance makes it a more viable candidate for practical robotic systems, especially those with stringent latency requirements.
- **Advancement in Complex Manipulation:** RNR-DP could facilitate more sophisticated robot manipulation tasks involving dexterous hands, mobile manipulators, and articulated objects, where fine-grained, adaptive control is paramount. This could open doors to automation in areas like delicate assembly, dynamic object handling, and human-robot interaction.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The method's ability to leverage the latest observations while maintaining action quality could serve as a foundation for integrating other advanced control concepts, such as reactive planning or model predictive control, with imitation learning. Its novel architectural elements may inspire further research into efficient and responsive policy learning using generative models.
- **Reduced Training-to-Deployment Gap:** By offering a policy that is both responsive and efficient, RNR-DP may help bridge the gap between policies that perform well in simulated environments and those that can robustly transfer to and operate effectively in real-world conditions.

While the current work identifies the lack of real-robot evaluations as a limitation, the theoretical and empirical advantages observed in simulation provide a strong basis for its potential impact on physical systems. Future research involving complex real-world deployment will be crucial to fully realize and validate this potential.
