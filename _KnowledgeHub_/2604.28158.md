# Intern-Atlas: A Methodological Evolution Graph as Research Infrastructure for AI Scientists

> [!summary] Summary
> Intern-Atlas constructs a methodological evolution graph from 1 million AI papers, explicitly mapping how research methods evolve, driven by identified bottlenecks and proposed mechanisms. This infrastructure provides AI agents with a structured view of scientific knowledge, improving lineage reconstruction, idea evaluation (with a 0.81 Spearman correlation to human experts), and idea generation (outperforming baselines by 1.02 points on overall quality scores).

## Problem

- Existing scientific knowledge organization is document-centric, making implicit methodological evolution difficult for AI agents to reconstruct and reason about.
- AI research agents struggle with unstructured textual data, leading to underrepresentation of low-frequency knowledge, issues with graph traversal, and difficulties in identifying genuine research gaps.
- Current methods for evaluating and generating research ideas often lack deterministic, structured grounding, leading to biases or inconsistent assessments.

## Method

- **Intern-Atlas** introduces a ==methodological evolution graph== by processing over 1 million AI papers, performing ==entity resolution== for methods and linking them with seven types of ==semantic edges==.
- For causal edges, a structured ==evidence record== is extracted, detailing bottlenecks, mechanisms, and trade-offs, all grounded in verbatim quotes and categorized via a taxonomy.
- Operators are developed for the graph, including ==Self-Guided Temporal Monte Carlo Tree Search (SGT-MCTS)== for lineage reconstruction, ==graph-grounded metrics== for idea evaluation, and ==strategy-driven generation== for new research ideas with evidence certificates.

## Results

- The constructed graph achieved a Node Match Ratio of **91.0%** and an Edge Reachable Ratio of **89.7%** on a method-evolution benchmark, demonstrating high fidelity in capturing methods and their relationships.
- SGT-MCTS for lineage reconstruction significantly outperformed baselines, achieving **84.8%** Node Recall and **79.0%** Edge Recall, a substantial improvement over Beam@10 (**44.9%** NR, **23.2%** ER).
- The graph-grounded idea evaluator showed a **Spearman correlation of 0.81** with human expert ratings (vs. **0.58** for LLM-only) and generated ideas received higher quality scores across dimensions, surpassing the best baseline by **1.02 points** in overall score.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly structuring methodological evolution, including typed causal relationships and verbatim evidence of bottlenecks and mechanisms, is critical for empowering AI research agents.
> - A graph-aware search algorithm like SGT-MCTS can more faithfully reconstruct complex method lineages compared to traditional graph search methods.
> - Deterministic, graph-derived metrics for evaluating research ideas align more closely with human expert judgment and actual publication quality than purely LLM-based assessments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The current scientific research infrastructure, including platforms like Google Scholar, Semantic Scholar, and OpenAlex, primarily operates on a document-centric paradigm. These systems index papers, abstracts, and citation counts, linking papers through general citations. While effective for human researchers who can infer complex relationships from unstructured text, this approach provides only an implicit representation of how methodologies evolve. It lacks explicit structural information detailing how methods emerge, adapt, or build upon one another, or the specific bottlenecks that drive these transitions.

This limitation is becoming increasingly pronounced with the emergence of AI-driven research agents. These agents, designed to automate aspects of the scientific pipeline from hypothesis generation to experimentation, face challenges in reliably reconstructing methodological evolution from unstructured text. Their parametric memory often underrepresents specific methodological knowledge, and their autoregressive inference struggles with explicit graph traversal, limiting their ability to identify genuine research gaps or generate high-quality ideas that depend on a structural understanding of the methodological landscape.

The paper positions this situation as a recurring pattern in scientific infrastructure development, drawing parallels to the Protein Data Bank (PDB) and ImageNet. In both cases, structured knowledge infrastructure became essential when new computational consumers (like AlphaFold for protein structures or convolutional neural networks for visual data) required explicit, machine-consumable data layers to function effectively. Intern-Atlas aims to address a similar inflection point in AI research, providing the missing structured data layer for methodological evolution to support AI research agents. It departs from the document-centric view to a method-centric approach, converting flat citation networks into a directed, queryable topology of methodological evolution.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to introduce **Intern-Atlas**, a methodological evolution graph designed to serve as research infrastructure for AI scientists. This graph aims to provide a structured, machine-consumable representation of how AI research methods develop and relate to one another.

The specific objectives of Intern-Atlas are:
1. **Automatic Identification of Method-Level Entities:** To identify and resolve distinct methodological entities and their aliases within scientific literature.
2. **Inference of Lineage Relationships:** To infer specific, typed relationships (e.g., extends, improves, replaces) between methodologies.
3. **Capture of Bottlenecks and Transitions:** To capture the specific problems or "bottlenecks" that drive transitions between successive methodological innovations, grounding these relationships in verbatim evidence.
4. **Creation of a Queryable Causal Network:** To construct a persistent, queryable causal topology of methodological development that can be directly consumed by downstream automated systems.
5. **Operationalization for AI Agents:** To develop operators over this graph for practical applications such as lineage reconstruction, idea evaluation, and automated idea generation.

The motivation behind these objectives stems from several limitations of the current research infrastructure and the evolving needs of scientific discovery:
- **Limitations of Document-Centric Infrastructure:** Existing platforms are fundamentally paper-centric, providing only general citation links. They do not explicitly represent the "how" and "why" of method evolution, leaving the reconstruction of these complex relationships to human researchers.
- **Needs of AI-Driven Research Agents:** The emerging class of AI-driven research agents cannot reliably reconstruct method evolution topologies from unstructured text. Their internal representations often lack the structural understanding necessary for discerning genuine research gaps, evaluating idea quality, or generating novel, well-grounded ideas. This deficit directly impacts their ability to contribute meaningfully to scientific discovery.
- **The "Structured Data Layer" Gap:** Similar to how the PDB enabled AlphaFold or ImageNet facilitated modern computer vision, there is a recognized need for a structured data layer that explicitly organizes scientific methodology. Such a layer would transform latent structural information (currently embedded in narrative text) into an explicit, machine-consumable format, serving as a foundational prerequisite for advanced automated scientific discovery.
- **Addressing Idea Generation Challenges:** A critical limitation for AI agents is idea generation, which requires a deep structural understanding of the methodological landscape, including how methods evolved, the constraints they addressed, and which directions remain unexplored. Intern-Atlas aims to provide this context directly.

By providing an explicit, typed, and evidence-grounded graph of methodological evolution, Intern-Atlas seeks to provide the necessary infrastructure for AI agents to systematically traverse and reason over the scientific landscape, thereby fostering automated scientific discovery.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Intern-Atlas is constructed and operated through a two-stage process: **Methodological Graph Construction** and **Operators over the Graph**.

#### 3.1. Methodological Graph Construction

The construction process transforms raw AI papers into a method-centric heterogeneous graph, $G$, comprising nodes for papers, methods, and stub references. This is built from a corpus of 1,030,314 AI papers published between 1965 and 2025, sourced from major AI conferences, journals, and arXiv preprints.

1. **Step 1: Entity Resolution**
 - **Node Sets:** The graph includes three types of nodes:
 - $V_P$: Paper nodes, representing parsed full texts from the corpus.
 - $V_M$: Method entity nodes, representing canonical methods (e.g., Transformer, BERT). An alias registry $A$: string $\rightarrow V_M$ maps various surface forms of a method name to its canonical node, handling variations, acronyms, and versioning. This registry is seeded from hand-curated lists and expanded by an LLM scanning the corpus.
 - $V_S$: Stub nodes, placeholders for cited works outside the corpus, ensuring historical lineages remain traceable.
 - **Publication Year Mapping:** Each paper and stub node is associated with a publication year ($\tau$).

2. **Step 2: Edge Typing**
 - For every resolved reference (citation) from a method $u \in V_M$ to a target $v \in V_M \cup V_S$, an LLM classifier analyzes the text near the reference in the citing paper.
 - It assigns one of seven semantic labels (types) to the edge $e = (u,v)$: `extends`, `improves`, `replaces`, `adapts`, `uses_component`, `compares`, or `background`.
 - The first four types (`extends`, `improves`, `replaces`, `adapts`) constitute the `strong-causal` subset, $E_{sc}$, critical for lineage reconstruction.
 - Edges classified as `background` are filtered out from further causal processing but retained for retrieval context.

3. **Step 3: Evidence Extraction**
 - For every causal edge (i.e., not `background`), an LLM extractor populates a four-field evidence record, $\rho(e) = \langle b_e, m_e, t_e, c_e \rangle$:
 - $b_e$: The specific "bottleneck" (problem) addressed by the citing method. This is a text span quoted verbatim from the citing paper and tagged with one of 14 categories from a predefined taxonomy (e.g., computational cost, memory efficiency, accuracy).
 - $m_e$: The "mechanism" (solution) introduced by the citing method, also a verbatim quote.
 - $t_e$: The "trade-off" associated with the new mechanism, a verbatim quote.
 - $c_e$: An LLM-reported confidence score for the extraction.
 - A deterministic post-checker validates extracted evidence by ensuring verbatim quotes match the source text, publication years are ordered correctly, and no conflicting reverse edges exist.
 - **Derived Structures:**
 - **Bottleneck Dimension Taxonomy (D):** 14 categories (e.g., `computational complexity`, `accuracy`, `scalability`) used to classify bottlenecks.
 - **Method-level DAG ($G_M$):** Derived from the paper-level causal edges by a deterministic projection, where types like `extends` and `improves` become `variant_of`, and `uses_component` is reversed to become `component_of`.

#### 3.2. Operators over the Methodological Graph

Three distinct operators leverage the constructed graph, all beginning with a common retrieval routine to localize relevant context $C_x$ around a given input $x$.

1. **Lineage Reconstruction with SGT-MCTS:**
 - **Purpose:** To trace the evolutionary history (ancestors and descendants) of a target method.
 - **Algorithm:** Self-Guided Temporal Monte Carlo Tree Search (SGT-MCTS) is employed to navigate the `strong-causal` subgraph ($V_M, E_{sc}$).
 - **SGT-UCT:** A modified Upper Confidence Bound 1 (UCT) selection rule guides the search, balancing exploitation of known good paths with exploration of new branches. It incorporates a graph-aware prior $\alpha_G(u,v)$, which combines the LLM-reported edge confidence ($c_e$) with a temporal coherence function ($TC(\Delta\tau)$) that favors temporally plausible advancements (peaked at 1-3 year gaps).
 - **Chain Ranking:** Retrieved evolution chains are ranked by normalized length, mean edge confidence, and mean MCTS visit count.
 - **Branch Discovery:** The algorithm re-runs from branch points to identify parallel evolutionary trajectories, providing a more comprehensive view of method development.

2. **Graph-Grounded Idea Evaluation:**
 - **Purpose:** To assess the quality of a research idea based on the structural properties of its constituent methods within the graph.
 - **Mechanism:** It resolves methods named in an idea description into nodes $M_d \subseteq V_M$. Evaluation is performed through deterministic graph queries, avoiding free-text LLM reasoning to mitigate bias.
 - **Per-Dimension Scoring:** Five dimensions (Novelty, Feasibility, Significance, Validity, Clarity) are scored independently using graph statistics computed from $M_d$ and its context $C_d$. For example, novelty might be based on topological disconnection, while feasibility considers method maturity (paper count with a non-monotonic curve to prevent over-popularization).
 - **Cross-Dimensional Aggregation:** A final score combines a linear weighted sum with a non-linear correction, $\Omega_{cross}$, which applies penalties for certain dimension combinations (e.g., high novelty but low feasibility). An optional LLM reviewer can provide a one-sided veto to lower scores, but cannot increase them.

3. **Strategy-Driven Idea Generation:**
 - **Purpose:** To propose novel research ideas by identifying under-explored structural regions in the graph.
 - **Structural Gap Patterns:** The generator extracts four types of structural patterns from the query context and lineage chains: open axes (unresolved bottlenecks), recent improvement directions, sacrifice axes (trade-offs), and disconnected pairs (potential cross-pollination opportunities).
 - **Strategy-Conditioned Proposal:** Each pattern is linked to a specific generation strategy (e.g., Bottleneck Resolution, Trend Extrapolation, Cross-Pollination, Paradigm Challenge). An LLM then fills in the technical specifics, but its output is constrained.
 - **Evidence Certificate:** Crucially, every generated proposal must include an "evidence certificate," consisting of a specific causal edge from the graph, a verbatim bottleneck quote from that edge, and a justification for why this bottleneck remains unresolved. This ensures proposals are grounded in existing literature and prevents the LLM from fabricating motivations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of Intern-Atlas encompassed its ability to reliably represent methodological evolution and its utility for downstream research tasks, including idea evaluation and generation.

#### 4.1. Evaluation of Graph Construction and Lineage Reconstruction

- **Graph Quality:** Intern-Atlas demonstrated high quality in capturing method-evolution structure against an expert-curated benchmark of 30 high-impact survey papers.
 - **Node Match Ratio (NMR):** 91.0% of survey methods were matched to nodes in Intern-Atlas, indicating broad coverage.
 - **Edge Reachable Ratio (ERR):** 89.7% of reference evolution relations from surveys were recoverable as directed paths in Intern-Atlas.
 - **Path Semantic Correctness (PSC):** 92.0% of the recovered paths correctly captured the intended evolution semantics, confirming the accuracy of the extracted relationships.
- **SGT-MCTS Performance:** The Self-Guided Temporal Monte Carlo Tree Search (SGT-MCTS) algorithm significantly outperformed baseline graph search methods for lineage reconstruction.
 - Compared to Beam@10 (the strongest baseline), SGT-MCTS achieved improvements of 39.9 points in Node Recall (NR), 55.8 points in Edge Recall (ER), and 39.9 points in Chain Alignment Score (CAS). These metrics indicate a superior ability to recover reference methods, transitions, and the correct ordering within evolution chains.

#### 4.2. Evaluation of Graph-Grounded Idea Evaluator

- **Validation Across Publication Strata:** The idea evaluator’s scores exhibited a monotonic decrease across publication strata, aligning with expected academic quality hierarchies.
 - Top-tier conference papers received the highest mean overall score (8.48), followed by core conference papers (7.83), workshop papers (6.85), and rejected submissions (5.84).
 - This trend was consistent across all five evaluation dimensions (Novelty, Feasibility, Significance, Validity, Clarity), with the largest gaps observed in Significance and Validity, suggesting the graph is particularly effective in assessing the importance and evidence base of ideas.
- **Human Alignment:** Intern-Atlas scores showed higher correlation with expert human ratings compared to a pure LLM-as-Judge baseline.
 - The overall Spearman correlation with expert judgments was 0.81 for Intern-Atlas, versus 0.58 for the pure LLM baseline.
 - The difference was most pronounced in Novelty (0.84 vs. 0.52) and Significance (0.82 vs. 0.55), indicating that graph-derived structural context provides a more reliable basis for judging these aspects than an LLM’s parametric knowledge alone.

#### 4.3. Evaluation of Graph-Grounded Idea Generator

- **Idea Quality Scores:** Ideas generated using Intern-Atlas achieved the highest quality scores across multiple dimensions.
 - Intern-Atlas yielded the best Overall score (7.20), outperforming baselines such as No-KB (5.78), OpenAlex (6.03), Semantic Scholar (6.18), and BM25 RAG (6.15).
 - It ranked first in Novelty (6.37), Significance (6.30), and Validity (6.26), with particular gains in Significance. This suggests that access to method-evolution chains helps identify recurring limitations and propose solutions for more central research problems.
- **Human Expert Win-Rate:** Blind pairwise human evaluation confirmed the superiority of Intern-Atlas-generated ideas.
 - Intern-Atlas proposals were preferred in 88.0% of comparisons against No-KB, 82.0% against OpenAlex, and 81.0% against BM25 RAG, indicating a strong human preference for ideas generated with method-evolution context.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Intern-Atlas represents a methodological evolution in research infrastructure, with significant implications for the emerging field of automated scientific discovery.

**Foundational Data Layer:** The work positions methodological evolution graphs as a foundational data layer essential for advanced AI research agents. By converting implicit, document-level relationships into explicit, typed, and verifiable causal networks, Intern-Atlas provides a machine-consumable substrate for AI systems to operate on scientific knowledge. This parallels the impact of the Protein Data Bank for structural biology or ImageNet for computer vision, where structured data became a prerequisite for significant computational advancements.

**Enabling Automated Scientific Discovery:** Intern-Atlas addresses a critical gap in current AI research infrastructure by providing the structural understanding necessary for AI agents to move beyond basic information retrieval. By explicitly representing method lineage, bottlenecks, and transitions, it empowers AI systems to:
- **Understand Evolution:** Accurately reconstruct the historical development of methods.
- **Evaluate Ideas:** Assess the novelty, feasibility, and significance of research proposals based on their structural position within the evolving landscape.
- **Generate Novel Ideas:** Propose well-grounded, high-quality research ideas by identifying structural gaps and unresolved bottlenecks in the existing literature, overcoming limitations of free-text LLM ideation.

**Improved Quality and Reliability:** The empirical results demonstrate that incorporating method-evolution context leads to more reliable and higher-quality outcomes in idea evaluation and generation compared to document-centric or purely LLM-based approaches. This suggests that structured knowledge graphs offer a robust framework for improving the efficacy of AI tools in research.

**Open Infrastructure for Future Research:** The authors' commitment to releasing the graph and pipeline as open infrastructure encourages further development and integration by the broader AI research community. This can foster an ecosystem where AI agents can collectively reason over the causal lineage of human knowledge, rather than being limited to isolated papers.

**Addressing Limitations of Current LLMs:** By externalizing complex structural knowledge into a queryable graph, Intern-Atlas provides a mechanism to mitigate inherent limitations of large language models, such as their difficulty in explicit graph traversal, their tendency to underrepresent long-tail knowledge, and their potential for fabricating ungrounded ideas. The verbatim evidence grounding also enhances transparency and auditability.

In conclusion, Intern-Atlas offers a systematic approach to organize and operationalize scientific methodology, providing a critical infrastructure component for the advancement of automated scientific discovery.
