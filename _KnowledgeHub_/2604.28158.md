# Intern-Atlas: A Methodological Evolution Graph as Research Infrastructure for AI Scientists

> [!summary] Summary
> Intern-Atlas constructs a methodological evolution graph from 1 million AI papers, explicitly mapping how research methods evolve, driven by identified bottlenecks and proposed mechanisms. This infrastructure provides AI agents with a structured view of scientific knowledge, improving lineage reconstruction, idea evaluation (with a 0.81 Spearman correlation to human experts), and idea generation (outperforming baselines by 1.02 points on overall quality scores).

## Problem

- Existing scientific knowledge organization is document-centric, making implicit methodological evolution difficult for AI agents to reconstruct and reason about.
- AI research agents struggle with unstructured textual data, leading to underrepresentation of low-frequency knowledge, issues with graph traversal, and difficulties in identifying genuine research gaps.
- Current methods for evaluating and generating research ideas often lack deterministic, structured grounding, leading to biases or inconsistent assessments.

## Method

- **Intern-Atlas** introduces a ==methodological evolution graph== by processing over 1 million AI papers, performing ==entity resolution== for methods and linking them with seven types of ==semantic edges==.
- For causal edges, a structured ==evidence record== is extracted, detailing bottlenecks, mechanisms, and trade-offs, all grounded in verbatim quotes and categorized via a taxonomy.
- Operators are developed for the graph, including ==Self-Guided Temporal Monte Carlo Tree Search (SGT-MCTS)== for lineage reconstruction, ==graph-grounded metrics== for idea evaluation, and ==strategy-driven generation== for new research ideas with evidence certificates.

## Results

- The constructed graph achieved a Node Match Ratio of **91.0%** and an Edge Reachable Ratio of **89.7%** on a method-evolution benchmark, demonstrating high fidelity in capturing methods and their relationships.
- SGT-MCTS for lineage reconstruction significantly outperformed baselines, achieving **84.8%** Node Recall and **79.0%** Edge Recall, a substantial improvement over Beam@10 (**44.9%** NR, **23.2%** ER).
- The graph-grounded idea evaluator showed a **Spearman correlation of 0.81** with human expert ratings (vs. **0.58** for LLM-only) and generated ideas received higher quality scores across dimensions, surpassing the best baseline by **1.02 points** in overall score.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly structuring methodological evolution, including typed causal relationships and verbatim evidence of bottlenecks and mechanisms, is critical for empowering AI research agents.
> - A graph-aware search algorithm like SGT-MCTS can more faithfully reconstruct complex method lineages compared to traditional graph search methods.
> - Deterministic, graph-derived metrics for evaluating research ideas align more closely with human expert judgment and actual publication quality than purely LLM-based assessments.
