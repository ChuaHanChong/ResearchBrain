# AeroPlace-Flow: Language-Grounded Object Placement for Aerial Manipulators via Visual Foresight and Object Flow

> [!summary] Summary
> AeroPlace-Flow is a training-free framework that enables aerial manipulators to perform object placement tasks specified by natural language. It integrates language-conditioned image editing for visual foresight with 3D geometric reasoning and object flow inference, achieving a 75% end-to-end placement success rate on hardware.

## Problem

- Object placement by aerial manipulators is an underexplored area, often requiring cumbersome manual 3D pose specification.
- Current aerial manipulation systems lack intuitive, language-grounded interfaces for task specification, limiting autonomy in dynamic environments.
- Bridging the gap between high-level semantic instructions and metrically consistent, collision-free 3D actions for aerial robots presents a significant challenge.

## Method

- The framework utilizes off-the-shelf ==image editing models== to generate a ==visual foresight goal image== from natural language instructions and real-time scene observations.
- It translates this semantic visual foresight into precise, collision-free ==3D object flow== by reconstructing the scene, estimating contact footprints, and ==optimizing trajectories==.
- The inferred 3D object flow guides the aerial manipulator's end-effector to track the desired path and release the object at the final placement configuration.

## Results

- Google Nano Banana Pro achieved the highest visual foresight generation success with **88 out of 100** tasks, reliably creating semantic placement hypotheses.
- The object flow inference module successfully extracted collision-free 3D trajectories in **80%** of cases where visual foresight was successful, with Centroid Pose Errors ranging from **1.8 cm** to **3.1 cm**.
- AeroPlace-Flow demonstrated a **75%** end-to-end placement success rate in real-world hardware experiments on a custom aerial manipulator, validating its practical feasibility across diverse scenarios.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Language-conditioned image editing models can effectively serve as a semantic intermediate representation, providing visual foresight for complex robotic manipulation tasks.
> - Explicit 3D geometric reasoning, combining monocular depth estimation and contact footprint identification, robustly grounds semantic visual goals into executable metric actions for aerial robots.
> - Trajectory optimization based on object flow allows for collision-free and smooth movement, enabling precise object placement by aerial manipulators despite perception noise.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Aerial manipulators (AMs) represent an advancement in robotics, extending the capabilities of robots to navigate and interact within complex, elevated, and hard-to-access environments. Prior research has demonstrated their potential in diverse applications such as inspection, infrastructure maintenance, warehouse logistics, and disaster response. Within this field, significant attention has been dedicated to the challenges of grasping objects and controlling the aerial platform's movement and stability.

However, the precise placement of objects by aerial manipulators has remained a comparatively less explored area. Existing systems often rely on predefined target coordinates or poses, which users must specify manually. This approach is widely recognized as cumbersome and unintuitive, particularly in dynamic real-world scenarios where precise metric information may not be readily available or easily communicable.

Concurrently, a growing body of research in robotic manipulation has focused on integrating natural language as a more intuitive interface for task specification. Advances in large generative models, including vision-language models, have enabled robots to interpret high-level human instructions. Within this domain, concepts like visual foresight and generative subgoal imagination—where models synthesize future visual states or predict task-relevant motions—have shown promise for guiding manipulation tasks. Approaches utilizing object flow, which extracts continuous motion trajectories from visual dynamics, have also been demonstrated in ground-based manipulation for tasks such as drawer opening or cloth folding.

This work addresses the gap between these two research trajectories. It seeks to transfer the advancements in language-grounded control and visual foresight, primarily developed for ground-based manipulators, to the unique challenges of aerial manipulation. Specifically, AeroPlace-Flow aims to enable aerial manipulators to perform object placement tasks specified through natural language, without requiring precise, manually defined 3D target poses. It does so by integrating visual foresight from image editing models with explicit 3D geometric reasoning and object flow inference, thereby moving beyond the limitations of pre-programmed trajectories and enabling more autonomous and user-friendly aerial manipulation systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations for developing AeroPlace-Flow are rooted in the limitations of current aerial manipulation systems and the desire to enhance their autonomy and usability:

- **Address the Underexplored Area of Object Placement in Aerial Manipulation:** While aerial manipulation has seen progress in grasping and control, object placement, which is critical for task completion, has received less attention. Many existing pipelines simplify placement to a two-stage process: manual 3D pose specification followed by coarse object release. This work aims to develop a more sophisticated and autonomous approach to aerial object placement.
- **Overcome the Cumbersome Nature of Manual Pose Specification:** Current aerial manipulation systems often require users to provide exact metric coordinates or 3D poses for placement targets. This method is unintuitive and time-consuming in real-world settings, making complex tasks difficult to define and execute. The researchers are motivated to replace this manual input with a more natural and scalable interface.
- **Enable Language-Grounded Task Specification:** Natural language offers an intuitive and flexible medium for humans to communicate goals. The objective is to allow users to specify aerial placement tasks using high-level language instructions (e.g., "place the object on the rack") instead of precise numerical coordinates, thereby making aerial robots more accessible and adaptable to human commands.
- **Integrate Visual Foresight for Semantic Placement Hypotheses:** Inspired by human cognitive abilities and recent advancements in generative models, a key objective is to leverage off-the-shelf image editing models to generate "visual foresight." This involves synthesizing a goal image that visually depicts the object correctly placed according to the language instruction, serving as a semantic hypothesis for the desired placement.
- **Ground Visual Foresight into Metric 3D Space for Executable Actions:** The visual hypotheses generated by image editing models are inherently 2D and semantic. A core objective is to translate these semantic cues into metrically consistent 3D information. This involves reconstructing the 3D scene from the generated image and inferring a precise 3D object flow, ensuring physical consistency and collision avoidance for actual execution.
- **Infer Collision-Free Object Flow for Robust Trajectory Generation:** Beyond determining *where* an object should be placed, the system must also determine *how* it should move to that target. An objective is to infer a collision-aware object flow that guides the grasped object from its initial configuration to the desired placement configuration, avoiding obstacles in the environment. This flow should be executable by standard aerial manipulator trajectory tracking methods.
- **Develop a Training-Free Framework:** The research aims to develop a framework that does not require task-specific training. By leveraging pre-trained generative models and explicit geometric reasoning, the system intends to generalize to new objects and scenes without extensive data collection or model fine-tuning for each new scenario.
- **Demonstrate Real-World Feasibility:** A crucial motivation is to validate the proposed framework through extensive simulation and, critically, through real-world experiments on an aerial manipulator platform, demonstrating its reliability and transferability to hardware execution in diverse aerial scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

AeroPlace-Flow is a training-free framework designed for language-grounded aerial object placement. It unifies visual foresight with explicit 3D geometric reasoning and object flow inference. The methodology can be broken down into three main steps: Generating Visual Foresight, Inferring Object Flow, and Placement Execution.

**A. Problem Formulation and Overview**
Given a natural language instruction (L), RGB-D observations of the object (I_obj, D_obj) and the placement scene (I_scene, D_scene), camera intrinsics (K), and extrinsics (T_cam_obj, T_cam_scene), the system's objective is to infer a semantically correct and metrically consistent object flow (P_1:T) that transports the object from its gripped configuration to a desired placement configuration. This effectively reduces the placement task to a trajectory tracking problem for the aerial manipulator.

**B. Generating Visual Foresight**
1. **Input:** Natural language instruction (L), object RGB image (I_obj), and scene RGB image (I_scene).
2. **Model:** An off-the-shelf image editing model (π) with multi-image context is utilized (e.g., Google Nano Banana Pro).
3. **Prompt Construction:** A text prompt (P) is constructed from the language instruction (L) and incorporates four constraints:
 - The object must be placed according to L.
 - The generated image (I_gen) should maintain the same camera field of view as I_scene.
 - The global scene layout of I_scene must remain unchanged, except for the added object.
 - The object must be placed with an orientation consistent with its appearance in I_obj, assuming stable grasping.
4. **Output:** A task-complete goal image (I_gen) depicting the object placed in the scene according to the instruction. This image serves as a semantic placement hypothesis.

**C. Inferring Object Flow**
This module translates the semantic goal image into a precise, collision-free 3D motion trajectory.

1. **Metric 3D Scene Reconstruction:**
 - **Monocular Depth Estimation:** A monocular depth estimation model (e.g., DepthAnythingV3) is applied to the generated image (I_gen) to estimate a depth map (D_est).
 - **Depth Alignment:** D_est is aligned to the observed scene metric depth (D_scene) using a global scale and shift (s*, b*) to produce a metrically consistent depth map (D_gen).
 - **Object Segmentation:** Image segmentation models (e.g., SAM3) are used to generate object masks for I_obj and I_gen.
 - **3D Point Cloud Projection:** Using camera intrinsics (K) and extrinsics (T_cam_obj, T_cam_scene), the masked depths (D_obj, D_gen, D_scene) are projected into 3D space and transformed into the world frame. RANSAC-based filtering removes outliers. This yields three point clouds:
 - P_obj: Metric geometry of the object from its initial observation.
 - P_obj-gen: Geometry of the placed object from the generated image (may have inconsistencies in scale/geometry).
 - P_world: Metric geometry of the environment from the scene observation.

2. **Contact Footprint Estimation:**
 - **Support Region Identification:** Since P_obj-gen may not precisely match the real object's geometry, its semantic placement is leveraged. The lowest surface points of P_obj-gen are extracted via height-based thresholding as contact candidates.
 - **Footprint Definition:** For each candidate, distance and tolerance criteria are applied to identify corresponding support surfaces in P_world. This process defines a dense contact footprint in the world frame, representing the intended support region for the object.

3. **Object Flow Computation:**
 - **Current Gripper Pose Integration:** P_obj (the original object geometry) is transformed to the current gripper's position (P_obj-grip) using feedback from the aerial manipulator's pose and arm joint angles.
 - **Target Pose Generation:** A copy of P_obj-grip is aligned and virtually placed onto the center of the estimated contact footprint, effectively replacing P_obj-gen with the accurate P_obj geometry at the semantically correct location.
 - **Initial Linear Flow:** Point-wise correspondences between the gripped (P_obj-grip) and the desired placed object (on the contact footprint) are known. A straight-line interpolation between these two configurations creates an initial linear object flow (P_1:T).
 - **Trajectory Optimization:** The initial linear flow is refined using trajectory optimization (similar to TrajOpt) to ensure collision avoidance and motion smoothness. This sequential convex optimization iteratively adjusts the object trajectory to maintain clearance from P_world while ensuring smooth transitions between consecutive configurations.
 - **Output:** A collision-free 3D object flow (P_1:T) representing the optimized sequence of intermediate object configurations.

**D. Placement Execution**
1. **Trajectory Conversion:** The collision-aware object flow (P_1:T) specifies the desired time-indexed trajectory of all object points in the world frame. Since the object is rigidly attached to the gripper, this implicitly defines the end-effector motion.
2. **Controller:** The aerial manipulator is operated in Cartesian end-effector space. For each step in P_1:T, a target end-effector configuration is computed.
3. **Execution:** A standard control module tracks the requested end-effector motion through coordinated movement of the drone base and arm joints, while maintaining stable flight. The object is then released at the final inferred placement configuration.

**E. Implementation Details and Hardware**
- **Image Generation:** Google Nano Banana Pro, Qwen-Image-Edit, FLUX.1 Kontext, GPT-Image, Omni-Gen2 via APIs or local hosting.
- **Depth Estimation:** DepthAnythingV3.
- **Segmentation:** SAM3.
- **Aerial Manipulator Hardware:** A custom Tarot 650 quadcopter with a CUAV X7+ flight controller (PX4 firmware), ZED depth camera, NVIDIA Jetson Nano, and a 3DOF 3D-printed manipulator with a gripper. ROS2 Humble is used for control. Experiments were conducted in an indoor lab with a motion capture system for ground truth.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of AeroPlace-Flow focused on its ability to generate visual foresight, infer object flow, and execute placements on hardware, using a custom benchmark of 100 language-conditioned tasks across various scenarios.

**A. Evaluation: Generating Visual Foresight**
Five image editing models were tested on the benchmark, and metrics included Image Gen Success (IGS), Incorrect Placement (IP), Hallucination (HA), and Average Runtime.

- **Model Performance:** Google Nano Banana Pro achieved the highest IGS with 88 out of 100 successful generations, exhibiting 9 incorrect placements and 3 hallucinations. This was followed by Qwen-Image-Edit (84 IGS, 11 IP, 5 HA), FLUX.1 Kontext (78 IGS, 14 IP, 8 HA), GPT-Image (73 IGS, 15 IP, 12 HA), and Omni-Gen2 (63 IGS, 20 IP, 17 HA).
- **Scenario Consistency:** Performance for Nano Banana Pro remained relatively consistent across Tabletop (23 IGS), Shelf (22 IGS), Stacking (21 IGS), and Relative (22 IGS) tasks, with stacking showing a slightly higher rate of incorrect placements.
- **Runtime:** Nano Banana Pro had an average runtime of 12.5 seconds, comparable to other models like Qwen-Image-Edit (11.5s) and GPT-Image (11.0s), while Omni-Gen2 was slower (21.0s).
- **Reliability:** The results indicate that modern multi-image editing models can reliably generate semantically consistent placement hypotheses, with model choice significantly impacting reliability, particularly concerning hallucination errors.

**B. Evaluation: Object Flow Inference**
This stage was evaluated on the 88 successful visual foresight generations from the Nano Banana Pro model, using metrics such as Flow Extraction Success (FES) and Centroid Pose Error (CPE).

- **Overall Success:** The object flow inference module achieved an FES of 80% (80 out of 88 successful visual foresight generations).
- **Scenario Performance:**
 - Tabletop (22/23 FES, 1.8 cm CPE)
 - Shelf (20/22 FES, 2.3 cm CPE)
 - Stacking (18/21 FES, 3.1 cm CPE)
 - Relative (20/22 FES, 2.5 cm CPE)
- **Error Analysis:** Most failures during object flow inference stemmed from inaccuracies in monocular depth estimation, particularly under challenging lighting, uniform textures, or similar object/scene colors. A smaller proportion of failures was due to incorrect contact footprint identification, often caused by noise in 3D reconstruction. Despite these issues, the module demonstrated robustness to moderate perception noise.

**C. Evaluation: End-to-End Aerial Execution**
Twenty tasks suitable for the lab environment and hardware constraints were sampled. Metrics included Image Gen Success (IGS), Flow Extraction Success (FES), and Placement Success (PS). A trial was successful if visual foresight, object flow inference, and physical placement (within 5cm tolerance, collision-free, stably supported) were all successful.

- **Overall Hardware Success:** Out of 20 trials, visual foresight succeeded in 18 cases, object flow inference succeeded in 17 of those 18 cases, and the aerial manipulator successfully completed 15 placements. This corresponds to an end-to-end hardware success rate of 75%.
- **Scenario Performance:**
 - Tabletop: 5 IGS, 5 FES, 4 PS.
 - Relative: 5 IGS, 4 FES, 4 PS.
 - Stacking: 4 IGS, 4 FES, 4 PS.
 - Shelf: 4 IGS, 4 FES, 3 PS.
- **Execution Robustness:** Tabletop and relative positioning tasks generally showed more reliable execution. Shelf placements experienced occasional failures, primarily attributed to tighter support regions and increased sensitivity to small pose errors. These results demonstrate that the inferred object flows provide sufficient accuracy for execution on a real aerial manipulator.

**D. Summary of Findings**
The experiments demonstrated that language-conditioned visual foresight can serve as an effective intermediate representation for aerial object placement. The object flow inference module was capable of converting these visual foresight outputs into reliable metric placement targets in 80% of successful visual foresight generations. The inferred flows transferred effectively to real-world aerial manipulator execution, achieving a 75% placement success rate in hardware trials. The combination of visual editing, geometric reconstruction, and trajectory execution enabled consistent language-conditioned aerial object placement across diverse scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

AeroPlace-Flow addresses a notable gap in aerial manipulation by enabling language-conditioned object placement, a capability that has been less explored compared to grasping and control. The work contributes a training-free framework that translates high-level linguistic intent into precise spatial actions for aerial manipulators, without requiring predefined metric poses.

The significance of this work lies in several key areas:

- **Enhanced Human-Robot Interaction:** By allowing users to specify placement tasks using natural language, AeroPlace-Flow significantly improves the intuitiveness and accessibility of aerial manipulation systems. This removes the burden of manually specifying precise 3D coordinates, making aerial robots more user-friendly for complex tasks in real-world, dynamic environments. This approach aligns with the broader trend towards more natural interfaces in robotics.
- **Leveraging Generative Models for Robotic Planning:** The work demonstrates the effective integration of off-the-shelf language-conditioned image editing models as a "visual foresight" mechanism. This showcases how powerful generative priors, often used for content creation, can be repurposed as a semantic interface for robotic planning, inferring desired future states without task-specific training. This could reduce the data and training requirements typically associated with learning-based manipulation.
- **Robust 3D Geometric Reasoning from Semantic Cues:** AeroPlace-Flow proposes a method to bridge the gap between abstract semantic goals (from language and generated images) and precise metric actions in 3D space. By aligning generated visual hypotheses with real-world depth, estimating contact footprints, and optimizing for collision-free object flow, the framework effectively grounds high-level instructions into executable robot trajectories. This explicit geometric reasoning complements the implicit reasoning of generative models.
- **Development of a Comprehensive Benchmark:** The creation of a 100-task benchmark for language-conditioned object placement provides a valuable resource for future research in aerial manipulation. It covers diverse scenarios (tabletop, relative positioning, stacking, shelf placement) and allows for systematic evaluation of both visual foresight generation and object flow inference modules.
- **Demonstrated Real-World Feasibility:** The successful hardware demonstrations on a custom aerial manipulator platform validate the practicality and transferability of the inferred object flows to real-world execution. Achieving a 75% success rate in physical placements under practical constraints suggests the robustness of the system and its potential for deployment in various applications.

The potential impact of AeroPlace-Flow extends to scenarios where aerial manipulators can perform tasks requiring object relocation or assembly in environments inaccessible to ground robots. This includes infrastructure maintenance (e.g., placing sensors, performing repairs), logistics (e.g., precise package placement in warehouses), and disaster response (e.g., distributing aid). The framework's training-free nature suggests a higher degree of adaptability to new objects and environments, potentially accelerating the development and deployment of autonomous aerial manipulation solutions. Future research directions, such as improving geometric robustness in tight-contact scenarios and incorporating uncertainty-aware perception, could further enhance the system's capabilities.
