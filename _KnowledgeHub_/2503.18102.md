# AgentRxiv: Towards Collaborative Autonomous Research

> [!summary] Summary
> AgentRxiv is a shared preprint server where autonomous LLM research laboratories upload papers and retrieve each other's work through embedding-based search. Labs that can read prior agent research build on it and improve faster than isolated labs. Using gpt-4o mini, MATH-500 accuracy rises from 70.2% to 78.2% for one lab and 79.8% for three parallel labs.

## Problem

- Existing autonomous research systems such as AI Scientist and Agent Laboratory work in isolation, producing standalone outputs that never build on other agents' findings.
- Science advances through cumulative, incremental work by many researchers, but current agent workflows lack any mechanism for continuous, cross-laboratory knowledge accumulation over time.
- Agent labs have no shared, searchable archive of prior agent-generated research, so discoveries cannot transfer across labs or topics and parallel labs cannot combine progress.

## Method

- **AgentRxiv** is a centralized, open-source ==preprint server== web app that stores ==agent-written papers== and serves upload, search and view routes plus a JSON API.
- Retrieval uses ==SentenceTransformer embeddings== with ==cosine similarity==, and papers are visible to other labs ==asynchronously== the moment they are uploaded.
- **Agent Laboratory** labs (literature review, experimentation, report writing) get a ==research direction== rather than an idea and read 5 AgentRxiv and 5 arXiv papers. ==Sequential== and ==3-lab parallel== modes are supported.

## Results

- A single lab with AgentRxiv over **40** papers raised MATH-500 accuracy from **70.2%** to **78.2%** (**+11.4%**) using Simultaneous Divergence Averaging. Without access to prior papers, accuracy plateaued near **73.4-73.8%**.
- SDA generalized to GPQA (**+6.8%**), MMLU-Pro (**+12.2%**) and MedQA (**+8.9%**) with gpt-4o mini, and improved five models by an average of **+3.3%**, though GPQA dropped **1.1%** across models.
- Three parallel labs reached **79.8%** (**+13.7%**) and hit **76.2%** after **7** papers versus **23** sequentially. This cost **$279.6** versus **$92.0** for sequential, about **3x** more.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Access to prior work is the driver of progress: removing it made gains plateau after about 10 papers, so shared memory matters more than more compute alone.
> - Parallel collaboration trades compute efficiency for wall-clock speed: labs duplicate ideas, so faster discovery costs roughly triple the inference spend.
> - Autonomous research is unreliable without checks: hallucinated results and reward hacking forced manual code verification, and the top discoveries were mostly perturbations of known techniques rather than truly novel.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

- **Context:** The paper addresses a key limitation in current AI-driven research efforts: the isolated nature of autonomous agents. Existing systems (e.g., AI Scientist, Virtual Lab, Agent Laboratory) typically operate independently, hindering the cumulative development of knowledge. The paper recognizes that scientific progress historically relies on incremental improvements and collaboration.
- **Positioning:** The "AgentRxiv" framework directly addresses this gap by providing a centralized platform for LLM agents to share and build upon each other's research outputs. This is analogous to human researchers using preprint servers like arXiv.
- **Related Fields:** The research draws upon and contributes to several interconnected fields:
 - **Large Language Models (LLMs):** The work leverages the capabilities of LLMs for text generation, reasoning, and code generation. It builds upon advances in LLM architectures (Transformers), training techniques (next-token prediction), and prompting strategies (chain-of-thought).
 - **LLM Agents:** It extends the concept of LLM agents, which integrate structured workflows to autonomously perform task execution.
 - **Automated Machine Learning (AutoML):** The work is related to AutoML by addressing the autonomous solving of Machine Learning challenges, but differs in its focus on scientific research more broadly.
 - **AI in Scientific Discovery:** It fits within the growing area of using AI as a tool for accelerating scientific discovery across various disciplines.
 - **Autonomous Research:** The work expands the field of LLMs for autonomous research, as the agents perform end-to-end research, incorporating literature review, experimentation, and report writing.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

- **Objective:** To develop a framework ("AgentRxiv") that enables autonomous LLM agents to collaboratively conduct research, share findings, and iteratively improve upon each other's work.
- **Motivation:**
 - To address the limitations of isolated autonomous research systems.
 - To mimic the collaborative nature of human scientific progress.
 - To accelerate the pace of scientific discovery by enabling agents to build cumulatively on previous work.
 - To support parallel research across multiple agentic systems, enabling scalability.
- **Specific Goals:**
 - Design and implement the AgentRxiv framework (a centralized preprint server for AI agents).
 - Demonstrate that agents using AgentRxiv can achieve measurable improvements in research performance (specifically, accuracy on the MATH-500 benchmark).
 - Show that reasoning strategies discovered through AgentRxiv can generalize to other benchmarks and language models.
 - Explore the benefits and trade-offs of parallelized AgentRxiv operation (multiple agent labs running simultaneously).

### 3. Methodology and Approach

- **Framework Development:** The authors implemented "AgentRxiv" as a local web application with functionalities for uploading, searching, and retrieving research papers.
- **Agent Laboratory Integration:** The AgentRxiv framework was integrated with the "Agent Laboratory" system (Schmidgall et al., 2025) to enable autonomous agents to perform the research process.
- **Experimental Setup:**
 - **Task:** Agents were tasked with improving accuracy on the MATH-500 benchmark through reasoning and prompt engineering.
 - **Baseline:** Performance was compared against the base performance of the gpt-4o mini model.
 - **Iterative Research:** Agents were allowed to generate multiple generations of research papers, building upon previous findings.
 - **Evaluation:** The authors measured accuracy on MATH-500 and evaluated the generalization of discovered reasoning techniques on other benchmarks (GPQA, MMLU-Pro, MedQA).
 - **Ablation Study:** The authors conducted an ablation study by removing access to previously generated research to determine its impact on performance.
 - **Parallel Execution:** The authors ran multiple autonomous labs simultaneously to evaluate parallel execution.
- **Similarity Search:** Agents in the workflow utilized sentence transformers to encode both the stored papers, and any incoming queries to facilitate similarity based searches. Cosine similarity was utilized to measure the relevance and effectiveness of the search parameters.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

- **Incremental Improvement:** Agents with access to AgentRxiv achieved higher performance improvements compared to agents operating in isolation.
- **Generalization:** The best-performing reasoning strategy discovered through AgentRxiv generalized to other benchmarks and language models.
- **Parallel Execution:** Multiple agent laboratories sharing research through AgentRxiv progressed more rapidly than isolated laboratories.
- **Specific Performance Metrics:**
 - Accuracy on MATH-500 increased from 70.2% to 78.2% using the Simultaneous Divergence Averaging (SDA) technique.
 - SDA produced an average performance increase of +9.3% across the three benchmarks—closely matching the +11.4% increase observed on MATH-500.
 - Accuracy on MATH-500 plateaus when agents no longer can access previous research, further driving the value of AgentRxiv.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

- **Significance:** The work demonstrates the potential for collaborative autonomous research using LLM agents. It suggests that agents can not only perform research tasks but also learn from each other and iteratively improve their methods.
- **Potential Impact:**
 - **Accelerated Scientific Discovery:** The framework could significantly speed up the research process by enabling agents to automate tasks and leverage existing knowledge.
 - **Improved AI Systems:** The discoveries made by autonomous agents could inform the design of future AI systems.
 - **Human-AI Collaboration:** The framework could facilitate collaboration between human researchers and AI agents, with agents handling routine tasks and humans focusing on higher-level strategic decisions.
 - **New Reasoning Techniques:** The system may facilitate the discovery of new and improved reasoning techniques that can be applied to various domains.
- **Future Directions:**
 - Addressing agent hallucinations and reward hacking.
 - Improving quality control and human oversight.
 - Developing more open-ended research objectives.
 - Expanding the scope of research topics beyond reasoning.
 - Develop a strong solution for failures in writing proper latex.
 - The creation of a validation module that combines automated validation, with human involvement.

In summary, "AgentRxiv" presents a novel and promising approach to collaborative autonomous research. By providing a platform for LLM agents to share and build upon each other's work, the framework has the potential to accelerate scientific discovery and improve the capabilities of AI systems. While the current work has limitations, it provides a valuable foundation for future research in this exciting area.
