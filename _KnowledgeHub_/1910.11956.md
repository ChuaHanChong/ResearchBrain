# Relay Policy Learning: Solving Long-Horizon Tasks via Imitation and Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Relay Policy Learning (RPL) combines imitation and reinforcement learning within a hierarchical framework to solve multi-stage, long-horizon robotic manipulation tasks. It leverages unstructured 'play' demonstrations to initialize policies, then refines them with reinforcement learning, achieving higher success rates on complex kitchen tasks compared to non-hierarchical or from-scratch methods.

## Problem

- Applying reinforcement learning to multi-stage, long-horizon robotic tasks is challenging due to issues like sparse rewards and difficult exploration in large state-action spaces.
- Traditional imitation learning often requires carefully segmented or labeled demonstrations, which are expensive to collect and can suffer from compounding errors when executed in novel states.
- Hierarchical reinforcement learning (HRL) methods have struggled with practical hurdles such as defining meaningful subgoals, effective exploration, and segmenting skills in complex scenarios.

## Method

- A bi-level hierarchical policy architecture is used, where a high-level policy outputs subgoals every H steps, and a low-level policy executes primitive actions towards the subgoal at every time step.
- Relay Imitation Learning (RIL) pre-trains these policies using 'relay data relabeling' on unstructured demonstrations to automatically generate goal-conditioned supervision for both high-level subgoals and low-level actions.
- Relay Reinforcement Fine-tuning (RRF) refines RIL-initialized policies through goal-conditioned reinforcement learning, incorporating off-policy data relabeling and regularization to maintain consistency with demonstrations.

## Results

- Relay Imitation Learning (RIL) achieved an average step completion score of 2.4 out of 4 and a 21.7% full success rate from unstructured demonstrations, outperforming flat behavior cloning baselines.
- Reinforcement learning fine-tuning (RRF) substantially improved policy performance, yielding significantly higher success rates on 17 different compound goals compared to RIL-only, from-scratch HRL, and flat policy fine-tuning baselines.
- The method successfully distilled individually fine-tuned policies for multiple tasks into a single multi-task goal-conditioned policy with only a slight performance drop, demonstrating generalization across complex sequences.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Unstructured 'play' demonstrations can be effectively leveraged for hierarchical policy pre-training through novel data relabeling schemes, avoiding the need for meticulously segmented data.
> - Initializing hierarchical policies with imitation learning from demonstrations significantly improves exploration and sample efficiency for subsequent reinforcement learning in complex, long-horizon tasks.
> - A fixed-horizon low-level policy combined with goal-conditioned learning simplifies hierarchical policy design and makes it more robust to fine-tuning compared to methods requiring variable-length segmentation.
