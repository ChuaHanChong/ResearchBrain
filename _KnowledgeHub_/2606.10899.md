# MV-Actor: Aligning Multi-View Semantics and Spatial Awareness for Bimanual Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> MV-Actor introduces a multi-view perception framework that creates a unified semantic-spatial representation for bimanual robot manipulation. The framework improved bimanual manipulation success rates to 87.8% on the PerAct2 simulation benchmark and 63.1% on a real robot across diverse tasks by enhancing semantic sharing, spatial awareness, and repairing degraded depth sensor data.

## Problem

- Effectively utilizing multi-view observations from multiple cameras (e.g., wrist-mounted and external) in bimanual robot manipulation remains a challenge.
- Existing multi-view perception methods either process views independently, limiting semantic sharing, or rely on depth sensors for 3D lifting, which is susceptible to noise and inaccuracies in consumer-grade hardware.
- Spatial priors derived from feed-forward reconstruction models, which can provide implicit spatial geometry from RGB images alone, have not been fully integrated into robot manipulation policies to overcome depth sensor limitations.

## Method

- **MV-Actor** develops a ==multi-view perception== framework that builds a unified semantic-spatial representation by integrating multi-view RGB-D observations, language instructions, and robot proprioception.
- It introduces a Multi-view Semantic Interaction (MSI) module to propagate semantic evidence across physically corresponding regions in different views, using calibrated reprojection and spatial consistency filtering.
- A Semantic-Spatial Token Interaction (SSTI) module enhances 2D semantic tokens with local 3D structural information by integrating Pi3X spatial tokens, while a Head-Aware Routing Gate adaptively fuses semantic and spatial residuals for position and rotation prediction.

## Results

- Achieved an average success rate of **87.8%** on the PerAct2 bimanual simulation benchmark, outperforming existing RGB-only (ACT, π0-keypose) and RGB-D (3DFA, **85.1%**) baselines.
- Demonstrated robust performance on a real bimanual robot platform (ARK-Lift) with consumer-grade depth cameras, achieving the highest average staged score of **63.1%** across three manipulation tasks, surpassing 3DFA (**58.1%**) and other baselines.
- Ablation studies confirmed that Multi-view Semantic Interaction primarily improved rotation prediction, Semantic-Spatial Token Interaction enhanced position prediction, and the Guided Metric Depth Repair module significantly improved metric point cloud quality by combining RGB texture guidance and feed-forward depth priors.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly sharing semantic information across physically matched regions from multiple camera views is crucial for robust perception in bimanual manipulation, particularly in scenarios with occlusions.
> - Integrating implicit spatial geometry priors from feed-forward reconstruction models into visual semantic features provides more reliable spatial awareness than methods solely dependent on potentially noisy depth sensors.
> - Repairing degraded depth sensor data using a fusion of raw sensor depth, RGB images, and a feed-forward depth prior significantly improves the quality of metric 3D anchors, enhancing overall robustness in real-world environments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic manipulation is a critical area with applications in industrial automation and other domains. While single-arm systems typically rely on a single camera, limiting their visual perception, bimanual systems commonly incorporate multiple cameras (e.g., wrist-mounted and external views). Effectively utilizing these multi-view observations is a significant challenge in developing robust bimanual manipulation policies.

Existing multi-view perception methods for robotic manipulation can be broadly categorized into two paradigms:
1. **Independent encoding or shallow fusion**: Methods like ACT, π0, and RDT-1B process each view independently or concatenate per-view tokens, fusing features at a policy level. While allowing policies to receive multi-camera input, these approaches do not explicitly associate observations across different viewpoints, potentially limiting the sharing of semantic perception.
2. **3D lifting with sensor depth**: Approaches such as PerAct, Act3D, and 3D Diffuser Actor leverage depth sensor information to lift visual features into a shared 3D space (e.g., voxels or point clouds). This enables explicit multi-view alignment. However, the reliability of these methods is heavily dependent on the quality of depth sensors, which can be prone to noise, holes, and inaccuracies, especially with consumer-grade hardware, particularly on reflective or transparent surfaces.

Recent advancements in feed-forward reconstruction models, such as DUSt3R, MeMix, and Pi3, have demonstrated the capability to produce dense pointmaps and spatial tokens from multi-view RGB images alone, providing implicit spatial geometry priors. Furthermore, studies in Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have indicated that such spatial features can enhance spatial awareness in language-conditioned tasks. However, these spatial priors have not been extensively integrated into robot manipulation policies to fully address the limitations of sensor-depth quality.

MV-Actor positions itself within this landscape by addressing the limitations of existing multi-view perception strategies. It aims to overcome the issues of insufficient semantic sharing in independent encoding methods and the fragility of depth-based alignment by constructing a unified semantic-spatial representation. This involves explicitly sharing semantic perception across views and grounding visual semantics with reliable spatial awareness derived from feed-forward reconstruction models, while also mitigating depth sensor degradation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a multi-view perception framework, named MV-Actor, that builds a unified semantic-spatial representation for bimanual robotic manipulation. The motivation stems from observed limitations in current multi-view policies:

1. **Limited Sharing of Semantic Perception**: Many existing multi-view policies either encode each camera view independently or fuse view features superficially. This approach often prevents the system from effectively sharing semantic information across observations of the same physical region from different viewpoints, leading to an incomplete or inconsistent understanding of the scene. For instance, if a target object is partially occluded in one view, other views might capture the missing information, but without explicit interaction, this complementary semantic evidence may not be fully utilized.

2. **Unreliable Spatial Awareness**: While 3D-lifting methods attempt to create a shared spatial understanding by projecting features into 3D space using depth sensors, their effectiveness is contingent on the accuracy and completeness of the depth data. Consumer-grade depth sensors frequently produce noisy measurements, holes, or inaccuracies on challenging surfaces (e.g., transparent, reflective), which degrades the 3D representations and consequently the robot's spatial awareness. This fragility makes precise bimanual coordination challenging, as accurate knowledge of object positions and orientations in 3D space is critical.

To address these challenges, MV-Actor pursues the following specific objectives:
- To enable robust **sharing of semantic perception** across physically matched regions observed from multiple cameras, allowing visual tokens to draw semantic evidence from different viewpoints.
- To integrate **reliable spatial awareness** into visual semantic features by interacting them with implicit spatial geometry priors derived from feed-forward reconstruction models, which are less susceptible to direct depth sensor noise.
- To enhance the robustness of the system by introducing a **depth repair mechanism** that refines degraded sensor depth, providing more reliable metric anchors for all subsequent token interactions and action predictions, especially under consumer-grade depth noise.
- To adaptively combine these semantic and spatial refinements for downstream tasks, acknowledging that different action components (e.g., position vs. rotation) may benefit from varying mixtures of semantic and spatial evidence.

By achieving these objectives, the research aims to improve the overall success rate and precision of bimanual manipulation tasks, particularly in complex scenarios involving occlusions, viewpoint changes, and challenging object properties in real-world environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MV-Actor is a multi-view perception framework designed to construct a unified semantic-spatial representation for bimanual manipulation, leveraging multi-view RGB-D observations, language instructions, and robot proprioception.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The policy receives multi-view RGB-D observations $\{I^{(v)}_t, D^{(v)}_t\}_{v \in V}$, a language instruction $\ell$, and proprioceptive state $q_t$. For each view, frozen CLIP features are extracted, forming a $32 \times 32$ semantic token grid. These tokens are back-projected into the robot frame using metric depth, forming a point cloud as the shared spatial carrier. Simultaneously, Pi3X provides spatial tokens encoding multi-view geometric structure, and the language instruction is encoded by a frozen CLIP text encoder. Based on these scene tokens, MV-Actor predicts a bimanual action chunk $A_t = \{a_\tau\}^{t+H-1}_{\tau=t}$, where each action $a_\tau$ includes end-effector position, rotation, and gripper state for both left and right arms.

#### 3.2. Multi-view and Semantic-Spatial Token Interaction

To address inconsistent semantic responses and lack of spatial awareness in 2D semantic tokens, MV-Actor introduces two parallel interaction pathways:

#### 3.2.1. Multi-view Semantic Interaction (MSI)

This module propagates semantic evidence across physically corresponding regions in different views. Instead of direct 3D k-nearest neighbors (KNN) aggregation, which can be prone to physically disparate surfaces, MSI uses calibrated reprojection and strict spatial consistency filtering.
- **Locating multi-view correspondences**: For a semantic token in view $v$ with a 3D anchor $p^{(v)}_i$, its projection onto a target view $u$ is calculated.
- **Spatially consistent candidate filtering**: Candidate tokens in the target view $u$ are considered valid only if they satisfy both a grid-plane alignment constraint (Euclidean distance on the token grid) and a depth consistency constraint (absolute difference in camera-frame depth). This ensures semantic propagation only occurs between regions referring to the same physical location.
- **Local attention aggregation**: Valid candidates form a correspondence set. The current semantic token is updated with a masked local attention residual, where softmax is normalized only over valid candidates. This process is applied twice.

#### 3.2.2. Semantic-Spatial Token Interaction (SSTI)

This module enhances 2D semantic tokens with local 3D structural information.
- Pi3X spatial tokens from each view are processed, layer-normalized, and resized to match the $32 \times 32$ semantic-token grid.
- Each semantic token attends to its aligned spatial token through a per-head sigmoid-gated residual, thereby acquiring spatial awareness.

#### 3.2.3. Parallel Residual Fusion

MSI and SSTI operate in parallel on shared base scene tokens, producing complementary semantic ($\Delta s_{sem}$) and spatial ($\Delta s_{spa}$) residuals. These residuals are kept separate, recognizing that position and rotation prediction heads may require different mixtures of semantic and spatial evidence.

#### 3.3. Head-Aware Routing Gate

A lightweight Head-Aware Routing Gate adaptively controls the fusion of semantic and spatial residuals for position and rotation prediction streams.
- It takes base scene tokens, instruction tokens, proprioception, and the two residuals as input.
- A context vector is formed by mean pooling these inputs and projecting proprioception.
- A two-layer MLP predicts four sigmoid gates ($\gamma_{pos}^{sem}, \gamma_{pos}^{spa}, \gamma_{rot}^{sem}, \gamma_{rot}^{spa}$), which are then used to weight and combine the semantic and spatial residuals before adding them to the base scene tokens for the respective position and rotation heads. The gripper head shares the rotation-routed representation.

#### 3.4. Guided Metric Depth Repair

To address the degradation of consumer-grade depth sensors, MV-Actor repairs depth information before back-projection, which is crucial for the 3D Rotary Position Encoding (RoPE) in the policy backbone.
- The repair module fuses three complementary inputs using a three-branch U-Net encoder-decoder: (i) raw sensor depth, (ii) RGB image (for sharp boundary cues), and (iii) a Pi3X feed-forward depth prior (for filling missing regions with cross-view consistent geometry).
- The decoder produces a non-negative repaired depth map $d^{(v)}_{out}$, which retains the sensor's metric scale, recovers missing geometry, and preserves object boundaries. This repaired depth is then used to construct reliable robot-frame 3D anchors for all subsequent token interactions.

#### 3.5. Policy Backbone and Training Objective

MV-Actor employs a 3D denoising policy backbone. This backbone is conditioned on the enhanced scene tokens, instruction tokens, proprioception, and noisy action trajectories.
- The action decoder predicts bimanual end-effector pose updates (position, rotation) and gripper states from these noisy action inputs.
- Positions are represented as workspace-normalized 3D coordinates, rotations as a continuous 6D representation, and gripper states as binary labels.
- The policy is trained using a total objective comprising element-wise mean L1 losses for position and rotation denoising targets, and binary cross-entropy for the gripper state.

### 4. Main Findings and Results: Simulation Experiments

Evaluations were conducted on the PerAct2 bimanual benchmark, comprising 13 manipulation tasks requiring coordination, contact reasoning, and spatial precision, using three calibrated RGB-D views.
- **Overall Performance**: MV-Actor achieved an average success rate of 87.8%, outperforming RGB-only baselines (ACT, π0-keypose) and the RGB-D method, 3DFA (85.1%).
- **Tasks with Shared Spatial Overlap**: For tasks such as lift\_ball and push\_buttons, where multiple views provide substantial spatial overlap, MV-Actor's Multi-view Semantic Interaction contributed to near-perfect success rates by aggregating multi-angle semantic cues.
- **Tasks Demanding High Spatial Precision**: On tasks like pick\_plate (+19.3% over 3DFA) and straighten\_rope (+14.3%), Semantic-Spatial Token Interaction's injection of geometric awareness into semantic tokens led to improved end-effector positioning accuracy.
- **Long-Horizon Tasks**: MV-Actor exhibited slightly lower performance than 3DFA on some long-horizon tasks (e.g., sweep\_dust, lift\_tray, handover\_easy), which was attributed to reduced multi-view overlap and degraded feed-forward reconstruction quality during extended manipulation.

#### 4.2. Real-Robot Experiments

MV-Actor was further evaluated on the ARK-Lift bimanual robot platform, equipped with consumer-grade Intel RealSense D405 cameras, to assess real-world deployment robustness under depth degradation and arm occlusion.
- **Overall Performance**: MV-Actor achieved the highest average staged score of 63.1% across three bimanual tasks (Lift Plate, Block Handover, Stick Insertion), improving over 3DFA (58.1%), π0 (52.5%), and PerAct2 (16.6%).
- **Block Handover**: MV-Actor achieved 80.0%, outperforming 3DFA (75.0%) and π0 (55.0%). This performance was attributed to the effectiveness of multi-view semantic consistency and spatial awareness in scenarios with frequent object occlusion and movement between arms.
- **Lift Plate**: MV-Actor improved over 3DFA and π0 by 6.7 percentage points. The depth repair module's ability to combine RGB boundary cues with a Pi3X feed-forward depth prior was critical for handling noisy depth from the metallic, reflective plate, leading to a more continuous and reliable metric spatial carrier.
- **Stick Insertion**: Performance was similar to 3DFA and π0, possibly due to the task's requirement for precise grasping of a thin object and long manipulation horizon with limited multi-view overlap.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies: Cross-View and Semantic-Spatial Interaction

Ablation studies on offline action prediction metrics demonstrated the individual contributions of MV-Actor's interaction modules:
- **Multi-view Semantic Interaction (MSI)**: Primarily improved rotation prediction, with `rot_l1` decreasing to 0.045 at 200k steps, suggesting enhanced multi-view local semantic consistency.
- **Semantic-Spatial Token Interaction (SSTI)**: Primarily improved position prediction, increasing `pos_acc@0.01` from 81.0% (baseline) to 82.6% at 200k steps, indicating geometric awareness for semantic features.
- **Head-Aware Routing Gate**: The full MV-Actor design, incorporating both interactions and the routing gate, achieved the best overall performance (e.g., `pos_l2` of 0.009, `pos_acc@0.01` of 86.3% at 200k steps). This module adaptively routes semantic and spatial residuals to different prediction heads, allowing the position head to prioritize local spatial information and the rotation head to leverage multi-view semantic consistency.

#### 4.3.2. Metric Point-Cloud Construction Ablation (Depth Repair)

Evaluation of the Guided Metric Depth Repair module showed:
- **Joint Guidance**: Combining RGB texture guidance and a Pi3X feed-forward depth prior (RGB+Pi3X) yielded the lowest `all` and `obs` (observed pixels) MAE, indicating optimal overall metric point cloud quality.
- **Large Hole Recovery**: While RGB+Pi3X performed well, RGB+MoGe2 (another feed-forward depth model) achieved slightly lower `miss` (missing pixels) error, indicating its effectiveness in large-hole recovery.
- **Complementary Inputs**: Both joint-guidance settings significantly outperformed single-guidance counterparts. RGB provided local boundary cues, while feed-forward depth offered a global geometric prior, jointly improving hole filling, boundary recovery, and global structure consistency. The module also generalized across different feed-forward depth sources (Pi3X and MoGe2).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The MV-Actor framework contributes to the field of robotic manipulation by introducing a multi-view perception strategy that explicitly addresses critical limitations in existing bimanual policies. Its primary significance lies in constructing a unified semantic-spatial representation that enhances both shared semantic perception and reliable spatial awareness across multiple camera views.

The **Multi-view Semantic Interaction** module enables robust semantic evidence sharing across physically corresponding regions, which is crucial for handling occlusions and complex scenes where a single view may be insufficient. The **Semantic-Spatial Token Interaction** module integrates implicit spatial geometry priors from feed-forward reconstruction models into visual features, providing a more reliable spatial understanding independent of direct, potentially noisy, depth sensor measurements. Furthermore, the **Guided Metric Depth Repair** module enhances the robustness of the system by refining consumer-grade depth sensor data, providing stable metric anchors that are essential for accurate 3D reasoning and action execution in real-world environments. The **Head-Aware Routing Gate** refines this integration by adaptively combining semantic and spatial information based on the specific requirements of position and rotation prediction tasks.

The experimental results, both in simulation on the PerAct2 benchmark and on a real-world bimanual robot platform (ARK-Lift), demonstrate MV-Actor's capacity to improve manipulation performance over current RGB-only and explicit 3D baselines. This suggests that the proposed approach offers a more effective way to leverage the rich information from multi-camera observations, translating into higher success rates and greater precision in complex bimanual tasks, particularly those involving challenging object properties or dynamic interactions.

The potential impact of this work includes:
- **Enhanced Robot Autonomy**: By providing robots with a more coherent and reliable perception of their environment, MV-Actor could contribute to more autonomous and adaptable bimanual manipulation systems.
- **Broader Real-world Application**: The robustness to consumer-grade depth noise expands the applicability of bimanual robots to a wider range of industrial and service scenarios where high-end depth sensors may not be feasible or where environments present challenging visual conditions (e.g., reflective surfaces).
- **Advancement in Multi-modal Perception**: The framework's ability to fuse semantic and spatial information from multiple views, augmented by language and proprioception, represents a step towards more comprehensive multi-modal perception systems for robotics.

Future work outlined by the authors includes investigating adaptive view selection and temporal consistency to further enhance the robustness of multi-view semantic-spatial representations under extended manipulation horizons, indicating a pathway for continued development in this area.
