# DyWA: Dynamics-adaptive World Action Model for Generalizable Non-prehensile Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework from Peking University and Galbot, DyWA, enables generalizable non-prehensile robotic manipulation using only a single-view camera and without explicit pose tracking. It achieves an 82.2% success rate on seen objects and 75.0% on unseen objects in challenging simulation scenarios, while adapting to varying physical properties like mass and friction.

## Problem

- Existing learning-based methods for non-prehensile manipulation heavily rely on multi-view cameras and precise pose tracking, limiting their practical deployment.
- Current policies primarily focus on object geometry, struggling to generalize across varying physical properties such as mass, friction, or non-uniform mass distributions.
- Teacher-student policy distillation under partial observability (single-view camera) and dynamic variations leads to suboptimal student performance due to insufficient cues and an inability to adapt to diverse physics.

## Method

- Develops **DyWA**, a Dynamics-adaptive World Action Model, using a ==teacher-student distillation== framework that learns from privileged information but operates on limited, real-world feasible observations (single-view partial point cloud).
- Introduces a 'world action model' where the student policy jointly predicts both robot actions and the corresponding future object states, providing additional supervisory signals.
- Incorporates a ==Dynamics Adaptation Module== that infers underlying physical properties from a sequence of past observation-action pairs and uses ==FiLM conditioning== to adapt the world action model's behavior.

## Results

- Achieved at least a **31.5%** improvement in success rate over state-of-the-art baselines in simulation, reaching an **82.2%** success rate on seen objects and **75.0%** on unseen objects in the most challenging single-view, unknown-state scenarios.
- Demonstrated robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer, achieving an average **68%** success rate on 10 unseen real-world objects (including slippery and non-uniform mass items) without external pose tracking, significantly outperforming CORN (**36%** success).
- Maintained consistent high success rates and stable execution times across varying real-world surface friction levels, showcasing its adaptive capability to diverse contact dynamics, unlike models without dynamics adaptation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Jointly learning to predict future states alongside actions within the 'world action model' significantly enhances the student policy's convergence and action learning, demonstrating a synergistic benefit.
> - The Dynamics Adaptation Module, which infers and conditions on physical properties, complements the world modeling approach, leading to substantial performance gains in generalizing across diverse dynamics.
> - FiLM (Feature-wise Linear Modulation) provides an effective and lightweight mechanism for dynamically integrating inferred dynamics embeddings, enabling the policy to adapt its behavior without direct weight modification.
