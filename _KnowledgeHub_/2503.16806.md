# DyWA: Dynamics-adaptive World Action Model for Generalizable Non-prehensile Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework from Peking University and Galbot, DyWA, enables generalizable non-prehensile robotic manipulation using only a single-view camera and without explicit pose tracking. It achieves an 82.2% success rate on seen objects and 75.0% on unseen objects in challenging simulation scenarios, while adapting to varying physical properties like mass and friction.

## Problem

- Existing learning-based methods for non-prehensile manipulation heavily rely on multi-view cameras and precise pose tracking, limiting their practical deployment.
- Current policies primarily focus on object geometry, struggling to generalize across varying physical properties such as mass, friction, or non-uniform mass distributions.
- Teacher-student policy distillation under partial observability (single-view camera) and dynamic variations leads to suboptimal student performance due to insufficient cues and an inability to adapt to diverse physics.

## Method

- Develops **DyWA**, a Dynamics-adaptive World Action Model, using a ==teacher-student distillation== framework that learns from privileged information but operates on limited, real-world feasible observations (single-view partial point cloud).
- Introduces a 'world action model' where the student policy jointly predicts both robot actions and the corresponding future object states, providing additional supervisory signals.
- Incorporates a ==Dynamics Adaptation Module== that infers underlying physical properties from a sequence of past observation-action pairs and uses ==FiLM conditioning== to adapt the world action model's behavior.

## Results

- Achieved at least a **31.5%** improvement in success rate over state-of-the-art baselines in simulation, reaching an **82.2%** success rate on seen objects and **75.0%** on unseen objects in the most challenging single-view, unknown-state scenarios.
- Demonstrated robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer, achieving an average **68%** success rate on 10 unseen real-world objects (including slippery and non-uniform mass items) without external pose tracking, significantly outperforming CORN (**36%** success).
- Maintained consistent high success rates and stable execution times across varying real-world surface friction levels, showcasing its adaptive capability to diverse contact dynamics, unlike models without dynamics adaptation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Jointly learning to predict future states alongside actions within the 'world action model' significantly enhances the student policy's convergence and action learning, demonstrating a synergistic benefit.
> - The Dynamics Adaptation Module, which infers and conditions on physical properties, complements the world modeling approach, leading to substantial performance gains in generalizing across diverse dynamics.
> - FiLM (Feature-wise Linear Modulation) provides an effective and lightweight mechanism for dynamically integrating inferred dynamics embeddings, enabling the policy to adapt its behavior without direct weight modification.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation is continuously evolving, with a growing emphasis on enabling robots to interact with the physical world in increasingly complex and adaptable ways. Non-prehensile manipulation, which involves interacting with objects without directly grasping them (e.g., pushing, sliding, toppling), is a crucial subset of this field. It extends robot capabilities beyond simple pick-and-place tasks, allowing them to handle objects that are geometrically challenging, too large, or in cluttered environments where grasping is not feasible.

Historically, non-prehensile manipulation has been tackled by planning-based approaches. These methods typically rely on precise analytical models of object properties such as mass, friction coefficients, and often require full CAD models. While effective in controlled environments, their dependence on prior knowledge severely limits their practical applicability in unstructured, real-world scenarios where such information is often unavailable or uncertain.

More recently, learning-based methods have emerged as a promising alternative. These approaches aim to learn manipulation policies directly from data, often in simulation, and then transfer them to the real world, ideally in a zero-shot manner. Prominent examples include HACMan and CORN, which leverage vision-based techniques, often using point cloud observations, to enable 6D object rearrangement. These methods have demonstrated improved generalization across diverse, unseen object geometries compared to their planning-based predecessors.

However, existing learning-based approaches still face significant limitations that DyWA aims to address. A critical challenge is their heavy reliance on multi-view cameras for accurate object geometry reconstruction and on precise pose tracking modules for state estimation. In many real-world settings, multi-view setups are impractical, and tracking modules are prone to errors or simply unavailable. Furthermore, most existing learning-based models predominantly focus on object geometry, often overlooking the crucial role of underlying physical dynamics. This oversight leads to poor generalization when physical conditions, such as object mass, slipperiness, or surface friction, vary significantly.

DyWA positions itself as a significant advancement in this landscape by tackling these two core limitations simultaneously. It proposes a novel framework that enables generalizable non-prehensile manipulation using only a single-view camera and without relying on external pose tracking modules, while also demonstrating robust adaptation to varying physical properties. By addressing these practical constraints, DyWA moves the field closer to deploying truly robust and adaptable non-prehensile manipulation systems in real-world, unstructured environments. It represents a paradigm shift towards more sensor-efficient and dynamics-aware learning for complex robotic tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of the DyWA research is to develop a highly generalizable and robust non-prehensile manipulation policy for robots that can operate effectively in realistic, unstructured environments with minimal sensory input. The authors explicitly state their aim to overcome two major limitations of existing learning-based approaches in this domain: their dependence on multi-view cameras and precise pose tracking, and their failure to generalize across diverse physical conditions.

The motivation behind these objectives stems directly from the practical shortcomings of the current state-of-the-art:
1. **Addressing Sensory Over-reliance:** Current methods often necessitate complex multi-view camera setups and highly accurate, but often imperfect or unavailable, pose tracking modules for object state estimation. This requirement makes deployment in real-world settings costly, cumbersome, and susceptible to errors. DyWA seeks to free policies from this burden, enabling robust manipulation using only a single-view camera and without explicit pose tracking.
2. **Enhancing Physical Dynamics Generalization:** Most learning-based policies tend to focus primarily on object geometry, leading to policies that struggle when physical properties like mass, friction coefficients, or non-uniform mass distributions vary. The authors argue that a truly generalizable policy must not only accommodate diverse object geometries but also dynamically adapt to changes in physical properties. This is crucial for handling the vast variability encountered in real-world objects and environments.
3. **Improving Learning Efficiency in Challenging Settings:** The authors specifically investigate the performance of a popular teacher-student policy distillation framework under the challenging conditions of partial observability (single-view) and dynamic variations. They identify three key issues that hinder the student policy's performance:
 - **Severe Partial Observability:** A single-view camera omits critical geometric cues, harming action learning.
 - **Markovian Model Limitations:** A standard Markovian student model inherently learns only an "averaged" behavior, failing to adapt optimally to diverse physical variations.
 - **Insufficient Supervisory Signals:** Conventional distillation methods, which primarily supervise latent features and final actions, do not provide enough information to effectively capture the complex underlying dynamics essential for contact-rich actions.

By addressing these motivations, DyWA aims to achieve a new level of generalization and robustness, making non-prehensile manipulation more practical and deployable for a wider range of real-world robotic applications. The core motivation is to move beyond idealized lab settings towards more realistic and challenging scenarios, thereby broadening the applicability of robotic manipulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DyWA proposes a novel policy learning framework built upon a teacher-student distillation paradigm, specifically designed to address partial observability and dynamic variations in non-prehensile manipulation.

#### 3.1. Pipeline Overview

The training process starts with a **Teacher Policy**, which is a state-based Reinforcement Learning (RL) policy. This teacher is trained in simulation with privileged information, including the full object point cloud, the true task state, and physical parameters. This privileged access allows the teacher to learn optimal behaviors across diverse dynamic conditions. The reward function for the teacher policy follows the design established by CORN, incorporating task success, goal-reaching, contact-inducing rewards, and an energy penalty.

The core of the proposed method is the **Student Policy**, termed the **Dynamics-adaptive World Action Model (DyWA)**. Unlike the teacher, DyWA operates solely on limited, real-world feasible observations (single-view partial point cloud, robot joint states, and end-effector pose).

#### 3.2. World Action Model

DyWA is defined as a policy model that jointly predicts actions and forecasts corresponding future states. This "world model" characteristic introduces additional supervision beyond just action imitation.

- **Observation and Goal Encoding:**
 - **Partial Point Cloud:** A simplified PointNet++ architecture processes the single-view partial point cloud observation.
 - **Robot Proprioception:** Shallow MLPs encode joint positions and velocities, and the end-effector pose.
 - **Goal Description:** Instead of relying on an unknown task state, a visual goal representation is constructed by transforming the initial point cloud to the target goal pose, which is then encoded by the same point cloud encoder.
- **State-based World Modeling:** The model takes encoded observations and goal descriptions to predict both the current action (A_t) and the next task state (S_t+1). This joint prediction enables synergistic learning. The task state S_t+1 represents the relative transformation (translation and rotation) of the object, with rotations handled by a 9D representation.
 - **World Model Loss (L_world):** Measures the L2 error for predicted translation and L1 error for predicted rotation between the model's output and the ground-truth transformation obtained from the simulation after action execution.
 - **Imitation Loss (L_imitation):** Standard L2 loss between the student's predicted action and the teacher's action.

#### 3.3. Dynamics Adaptation

To enable adaptation to varying physical properties, DyWA incorporates a Dynamics Adaptation Module:

- **Adaptation Embedding (z_t):** This module processes a sequence of `L` past observation-action pairs (concatenated observation embeddings and previous action embeddings) using a 1D CNN. This sequence provides historical context to infer dynamic properties that are not immediately evident from a single, partial observation.
- **Adaptation Loss (L_adapt):** The learned adaptation embedding is supervised by a privileged signal from the teacher – the concatenation of the full point cloud embedding and physics embedding. This forces the student's adaptation module to learn meaningful representations of underlying geometry and physics from limited historical data.
- **Dynamics Conditioning (FiLM):** The decoded dynamics embedding from the adaptation module conditions the world action model using Feature-wise Linear Modulation (FiLM). FiLM dynamically modulates the intermediate feature representations of the world action model by applying learned scaling (γ) and shifting (β) transformations. This allows the model to adjust its behavior adaptively based on the inferred dynamics, without directly modifying the policy network weights. FiLM blocks are densely integrated into the early layers of the world action model.

#### 3.4. Action Space with Variable Impedance

The robot's actions are defined in an impedance-controlled space, allowing for adaptive force interactions. The policy predicts:
- **Subgoal Residual of the End-Effector (ΔT_ee):** A 6DoF relative pose.
- **Joint-Space Impedance Parameters:** Positional gains (P ∈ R^7) and damping factors (ρ ∈ R^7).
These parameters are then used by a joint-space impedance controller (Polymetis API) to generate control commands.

#### 3.5. Training Protocol

The overall learning objective is a weighted sum of the imitation loss, world model loss, and adaptation loss. The teacher policy is trained for 200K iterations using PPO. The student policy is then trained for 500K iterations using DAgger, an imitation learning algorithm that incrementally collects data from the student's actions and queries the teacher for expert labels. To enhance robustness and sim-to-real transferability, significant domain randomization is applied during student training, varying object mass, scale, friction, and restitution properties. Small perturbations are also injected into torque commands, point clouds, and goal poses.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy and generalization capabilities of DyWA were rigorously evaluated through extensive experiments in both simulation and the real world, comparing it against state-of-the-art baselines.

#### 4.1. Simulation Benchmark for Tabletop Non-prehensile Rearrangement

To provide a fair and comprehensive comparison, the authors developed a new benchmark in the IsaacGym simulation environment, using 323 training objects and 10 unseen test objects (each with five scales, totaling 50 evaluation objects). The benchmark includes variations in perception (single-view vs. multi-view) and state knowledge (known vs. unknown object poses).

- **Overall Performance:** DyWA consistently outperformed all baselines (HACMan, CORN, and an enhanced CORN with a PointNet++ encoder) across all evaluation tracks.
- **Significant Improvement:** DyWA demonstrated a substantial performance gain, achieving at least a 31.5% improvement in success rate over the baselines.
- **Challenging Scenarios:** The performance gap was most pronounced in the most realistic and challenging scenarios: unknown state with single-view observations. In this critical track, DyWA achieved an 82.2% success rate on seen objects and 75.0% on unseen objects, significantly higher than CORN (50.7% seen, 49.4% unseen) and HACMan (1.5% seen, 2.9% unseen). This highlights the crucial role of DyWA's dynamics modeling in conditions with limited sensory input.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies were conducted on the most challenging simulation track (unknown state with single-view observation) to dissect the contributions of DyWA's key components.

- **Synergy between Next State Prediction and Action Learning:** Visualization of loss curves demonstrated that the simultaneous learning of next states (World Model) significantly improved the convergence of the action loss during distillation, confirming the synergistic benefits of jointly predicting actions and their outcomes. The teacher policy, however, already performs near its upper bound, meaning the world model's main impact is on student learning.
- **Complementarity of Dynamics Adaptation and World Modeling:**
 - Using only the World Model or Dynamics Adaptation (RMA) alone yielded only marginal improvements over the naive DAgger baseline (1.7% and 5.7% respectively).
 - However, combining both modules led to a substantial performance jump from 59.9% (DAgger) to 73.3%, indicating a strong, non-trivial complementarity. Dynamics adaptation provides context for reasoning about dynamic effects, while the world model offers a structured learning target.
- **Effectiveness of FiLM Conditioning:** FiLM proved to be a highly effective mechanism for integrating dynamics embeddings. Incorporating FiLM into a dynamics adaptation-only model (RMA) boosted performance from 65.6% to 70.0%. When all three modules (world modeling, dynamics adaptation, and FiLM) were combined, the success rate reached 82.2%, with FiLM contributing an additional 8.9% improvement. This confirmed FiLM as a lightweight and structured method for adaptive conditioning.

#### 4.3. Real-World Experiments

Real-world experiments validated DyWA's zero-shot sim-to-real transferability on a Franka robot arm with a RealSense D435 camera.

- **Generalization across Diverse Objects:** DyWA achieved an impressive average success rate of 68% across 10 unseen real-world objects, including challenging slippery and non-uniform mass objects (e.g., half-filled water bottles). This significantly outperformed CORN, which only managed a 36% success rate even with an external tracking module for pose estimation. DyWA's ability to operate without external pose tracking while achieving higher success rates is a key real-world advantage.
- **Robustness to Surface Friction Variations:** Experiments on four different tablecloths with progressively varying friction levels demonstrated DyWA's robustness. While a model without dynamics adaptation showed significant performance degradation and erratic execution times across different friction levels, DyWA maintained consistent high success rates and stable execution times, highlighting its adaptive capability to diverse contact dynamics.

#### 4.4. Applications

The paper showcased practical applications, integrating DyWA with Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and grasping models.
- **Language-Guided Manipulation:** DyWA can execute goal-conditioned policies based on natural language instructions by leveraging VLMs like SoFar to generate target object transformations (e.g., "Put the grip of the electric drill into a person’s hand").
- **Complementing Prehensile Manipulation:** DyWA effectively handles objects difficult for traditional grasping (e.g., flipping a tiny switch without gripper-table collisions) and serves as a pre-grasping step to reorient thin cards or broad cracker boxes into grasp-friendly configurations, thereby improving overall grasp success rates.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in "DyWA: Dynamics-adaptive World Action Model for Generalizable Non-prehensile Manipulation" holds significant implications for the field of robotics, particularly in enhancing the capabilities and applicability of robotic manipulation in real-world scenarios.

**1. Advancing Generalizable and Robust Manipulation:** DyWA makes a crucial step forward in addressing the grand challenge of generalizable robotic manipulation. By demonstrating robust performance across diverse object geometries and varying physical properties (mass, friction, non-uniform mass distribution), the work pushes beyond the limitations of policies confined to specific object types or environments. This enhanced generalization is critical for deploying robots in dynamic, unstructured settings like homes, warehouses, or service industries.

**2. Reducing Sensory and Computational Burden:** A major impact of DyWA is its ability to achieve high-performance manipulation using only a single-view camera and without relying on precise pose tracking modules. This significantly reduces the hardware complexity, cost, and computational overhead associated with traditional multi-camera setups and sophisticated state estimation pipelines. This simplification makes advanced non-prehensile manipulation more accessible and practical for a wider range of real-world robotic systems.

**3. A Novel Synergistic Learning Paradigm:** The proposed "Dynamics-Adaptive World Action Model" (DyWA) framework itself is a significant methodological contribution. The synergistic learning process, which jointly predicts actions and future states while dynamically adapting to physics from historical trajectories (via FiLM conditioning), provides a robust recipe for tackling complex, contact-rich manipulation tasks. This unified modeling approach, which integrates geometry, state, physics, and robot actions, offers a powerful template for future research in robot learning.

**4. Enabling Real-World Deployment:** The impressive zero-shot sim-to-real transfer performance and the successful real-world experiments on a variety of challenging objects (including slippery and non-uniform mass items) underscore DyWA's potential for immediate real-world deployment. Its demonstrated robustness to varied surface friction further solidifies its applicability outside of meticulously controlled laboratory conditions.

**5. Establishment of a Standardized Benchmark:** The creation of a comprehensive simulation benchmark for generalizable non-prehensile manipulation is an invaluable contribution to the research community. This benchmark provides a standardized platform for fair comparison and evaluation of future methods, accelerating progress in the field by enabling researchers to quickly test and validate novel approaches.

**6. Expanding Robotic Application Domains:** The showcased applications, such as integration with Vision-Language Models for natural language instruction-driven tasks and enhancing pre-grasping for difficult-to-handle objects, highlight DyWA's potential to broaden the scope of robotic functionalities. It can enable more intuitive human-robot interaction and allow robots to perform tasks that were previously challenging for traditional prehensile manipulators.

In conclusion, DyWA represents a substantial leap in making non-prehensile robotic manipulation more practical, robust, and generalizable. By addressing critical real-world constraints related to sensing and physical variability, it paves the way for a new generation of adaptable robotic systems capable of operating effectively in complex, unstructured environments. While the authors acknowledge limitations with symmetric, transparent, and specular objects due to reliance on point clouds, their proposed direction of incorporating additional appearance information points to a clear path for future advancements building upon this impactful work.
