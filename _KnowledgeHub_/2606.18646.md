# A Scalable Embodied Intelligence Platform for Seamless Real-to-Sim-to-Real Transfer of Household Mobile Manipulation Tasks

> [!summary] Summary
> BestMan is a real-to-sim-to-real platform for household mobile manipulation. It couples an Automated Scene Generation module that rebuilds articulated, part-level simulation scenes from single RGB images, a modular Sense-Plan-Act skill architecture with the THMM benchmark, and a hardware-agnostic unified middleware. Reconstruction beats URDFormer and digital-cousin baselines, and deployment time onto real robots drops three to five times versus ROS.

## Problem

- Simulation frameworks build scenes manually or procedurally, so they cannot automatically reconstruct real environments and their disturbances, limiting the data available for robust training.
- Real-world platforms are tightly coupled to ROS and specific hardware, forcing heavy reconfiguration before algorithms transfer to heterogeneous mobile manipulators or connect back to simulation.
- Long-horizon household tasks span multi-stage perception, planning and control, yet prior platforms offer no standardized skill decomposition or evaluation protocol for them.

## Method

- **BestMan**'s ==Automated Scene Generation== pairs GPT-4 labelling, ==Grounded-SAM== masks and ==Depth-Anything-v2== depth with ==CLIP== and ==DINOv2== asset retrieval, falling back to ==URDFormer== and ==AutoURDF== when no matching articulated asset exists.
- The ==THMM benchmark== decomposes language instructions into Approach, Manipulation and Placement sub-tasks over six skills, executed by an eleven-module ==Sense-Plan-Act architecture== with hierarchical configuration and containerized dependency isolation.
- ==Hybrid planning== fuses an LLM instruction parser with a ==PDDL symbolic planner== under a consistency-threshold replanning operator; the ==HUM middleware== bijectively maps simulated and real control APIs across heterogeneous bases, arms and grippers.

## Results

- ASG reaches **90%** joint accuracy and **3.11** pose error on simulation inputs, beating URDFormer (**88%**, **7.65**) and ACDC (**78%**, **5.21**); real-image human composite scores stay above **85%**.
- Modular skill execution beats non-modular baselines at every setting; in the hardest cluttered case it succeeds **42.3%** versus **32.8%** for reinforcement learning and **27.5%** for behavior cloning.
- HUM cuts sim-to-real transfer time from **65** to **14** minutes and **78** to **18** minutes on two robots, with lower latency and comparable success (**49.0%** versus **47.8%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Modular decomposition pays off more as difficulty rises: its advantage over end-to-end learned policies widens with longer horizons and heavier scene clutter.
> - Strategies that dominate in simulation need not dominate in reality; the fully reinforcement-learned skill stack led in sim (66.2%) yet trailed fine-tuned hybrids on real hardware.
> - Abstracting hardware behind a unified middleware buys large deployment-time savings at essentially no cost in task success, making cross-platform reproducibility cheap.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Embodied Intelligence (EI) is a paradigm for autonomous systems that perceive, reason, and act in the physical world. Mobile manipulators, which combine mobility and dexterous manipulation, are central to EI, particularly for performing complex interactions in unstructured household environments, such as following natural language instructions for object retrieval and placement. However, the direct deployment and testing of these systems on physical robots present challenges, including high hardware costs, lengthy experimentation cycles, safety concerns, and limited generalization across diverse scenes. These factors underscore the necessity of simulation for developing and scaling mobile manipulation capabilities.

The current landscape of EI platforms for mobile manipulation is characterized by rapid development but also by several limitations. General-purpose simulators, such as Gazebo, PyBullet, MuJoCo, and SAPIEN, have advanced algorithmic development in simulated environments. However, they frequently rely on manual or procedural scene generation, lacking automated pipelines for reconstructing simulation environments that accurately reflect real-world disturbances to enhance robustness, which limits effective real-to-sim transfer. Conversely, existing real-world platforms, often built on the Robot Operating System (ROS), are typically tightly coupled with specific hardware. This coupling hinders seamless integration with simulations for large-scale testing and necessitates significant reconfigurations for deployment on heterogeneous robotic hardware. While some platforms, like robosuite and Orbit, aim to integrate both simulation and real-world deployments, they are often tailored to specific tasks, thereby lacking the generality and scalability required for complex mobile manipulation in diverse household settings.

This context reveals a critical need for a scalable and seamless real-to-sim-to-real platform capable of bridging the entire cycle of scene reconstruction, strategy learning, and real-world deployment. The paper identifies three specific challenges:
1. **Costly and Inaccurate Real-to-Sim Scene Reconstruction:** Manual construction of high-fidelity simulation environments from 3D scanned assets is labor-intensive and lacks scalability, impacting the quality and quantity of training data.
2. **Complexity of Systematic Strategy Evaluation:** Long-horizon household tasks involve multi-stage objectives, cluttered environments, and fine-grained object interactions. These tasks often lack standardized decomposition and evaluation protocols, making systematic algorithm development challenging.
3. **Incompatible Real-World Deployments:** Mismatches between simulation frameworks and real-world hardware interfaces, coupled with the diversity of robot platforms and control APIs, impede reliable and seamless sim-to-real transfer and limit the reusability and scalability of learned policies.

This work introduces BestMan as an extensible platform designed to address these identified challenges by integrating scene understanding, skill-based task formalization, and sim-to-real transfer capabilities into a unified system for end-to-end deployment of task-specific algorithms. It aims to extend previous simulation-centric work by establishing a comprehensive real-to-sim-to-real pipeline with formalized benchmarks and real-world deployment capabilities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop BestMan, a scalable and seamless mobile manipulation platform that integrates real-to-sim scene reconstructions, algorithm developments, and deployments across heterogeneous platforms. This overarching goal is motivated by the limitations of current embodied intelligence platforms and the growing demand for robust and generalizable mobile manipulation in unstructured household environments.

Specifically, the key objectives and motivations are:

1. **To overcome limitations of manual simulation setup and achieve accurate, scalable real-to-sim scene reconstruction:**
 - **Motivation:** Manually constructing simulation environments from real-world observations is labor-intensive, time-consuming, and not scalable. This limits the realism and diversity of simulated scenes, which are crucial for training robust embodied AI agents. Existing methods often fail to accurately capture the semantic and geometric details, particularly the articulated structures of objects in real environments, necessary for physically plausible interactions.
 - **Objective:** Develop an **Automated Scene Generation (ASG) module** that reconstructs articulated and semantically aligned simulation scenes directly from real-world images. This module is intended to enable data-efficient training and evaluation by providing high-fidelity simulated environments that reflect real-world conditions.

2. **To support systematic algorithm development and evaluation for complex, long-horizon household tasks:**
 - **Motivation:** Household mobile manipulation tasks are typically long-horizon and driven by natural language instructions, posing significant challenges for perception, planning, and control due to their multi-stage nature, cluttered environments, and fine-grained interactions. The lack of standardized task decomposition and evaluation protocols hinders systematic algorithmic development and comparison across different approaches.
 - **Objective:** Establish the **Transferable Household Mobile Manipulation (THMM) benchmark** and propose a **simulation-guided task formalization and skill learning architecture**. This involves decomposing long-horizon tasks into reusable perception and planning skills based on a modular Sense–Plan–Act (SPA) architecture, thereby enabling standardized task formalization and large-scale evaluation of hybrid skill strategies.

3. **To enhance real-world scalability and ensure reliable, compatible sim-to-real deployments across diverse hardware:**
 - **Motivation:** A significant challenge in embodied AI is the difficulty of transferring learned policies from simulation to diverse real-world robotic platforms. This is often due to mismatches between simulation frameworks and real-world hardware interfaces, as well as the variety of robot control APIs. The tight coupling of existing real-world platforms with specific hardware limits their reusability, accessibility, and ability to test algorithms on heterogeneous robots.
 - **Objective:** Design a **Hardware-agnostic Unified Middleware (HUM)** that bridges simulations and real-world systems. This middleware aims to ensure seamless and compatible sim-to-real transfer across heterogeneous mobile manipulators for real deployments, thereby promoting wider hardware compatibility and reproducibility of research findings.

By addressing these challenges, BestMan aims to provide a unified, extensible platform that accelerates research in mobile manipulation, facilitating robust algorithm development and effective deployment in real-world household environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

BestMan is designed as a scalable and seamless real-to-sim-to-real platform for household mobile manipulation tasks. Its architecture integrates three primary modules: Automated Scene Generation (ASG) for real-to-sim transfer, a simulation-guided module for task formalization and skill learning, and Hardware-agnostic Unified Middleware (HUM) for sim-to-real deployments. The platform utilizes PyBullet for physics simulation and Blender for rendering, in conjunction with a unified real-world robotic system supporting heterogeneous hardware.

#### 3.1. ASG: Automated Real-to-Sim Scene Generation

The ASG module addresses the challenge of creating geometrically and semantically realistic simulated scenes from real-world observations. This process is structured into two main steps:

- **Real-World Perception and Extraction:** This step extracts object-level and robot-specific information from raw camera data.
 - **Environmental Scene Processing:** A single-view RGB image ($X_1$) is used. GPT-4 is prompted to retrieve object labels ($L_i$), which are then passed to Grounded-SAM-v2 to generate object masks ($M$). Depth-Anything-v2 extracts a depth map ($D$) from $X_1$, from which a scene point cloud ($P$) is generated. Individual object masks ($m_i$) are used to create point subsets ($P_i$).
 - **Robot Information Extraction:** Multi-view RGBD images ($X_2$) of the robot are used for point cloud fusion to extract a complete robot point cloud. This is an optional step, as manufacturer-provided models can also be used.
- **Simulated Scene and Asset Generation:** Based on the extracted information, digital assets are generated and integrated into a simulated scene.
 - **Object Modeling:** For each extracted object point cloud ($P_i$), hierarchical retrieval is performed from a 3D simulation asset dataset. CLIP scores are used to select the top-k most similar categories based on object label ($l_i$), followed by DINOv2 feature embedding distance to identify the closest digital asset. If no suitable asset is found within a predefined threshold, URDFormer is employed to model the object's articulated structure.
 - **Robot Modeling:** Similarly, if a suitable robot model is unavailable, AutoURDF is used to construct its articulated structure from the point clouds.
 - **Scene Integration:** All generated assets are then integrated into a simulation scene, aligned with the extracted spatial information to ensure physical consistency.
- **Asset Dataset:** The platform's asset library includes high-fidelity rigid objects (YCB, IPA-3D1K, Google Scanned Objects) and articulated models (PartNet-Mobility), providing diverse 3D geometries and part-level kinematic information. It also comprises modular URDF models for mobile manipulators, including various mobile bases (e.g., Segbot, Ranger Mini), robotic arms (e.g., Realman RML, Franka Panda, UR5), and end effectors (e.g., vacuum grippers, parallel-jaw grippers), designed for rapid assembly and configuration.

#### 3.2. Simulation-Guided Task Formalization and Skill Learning

This module focuses on systematic algorithm development for long-horizon tasks in the generated simulation environments.

- **THMM Benchmark and Task Formalization:** The Transferable Household Mobile Manipulation (THMM) benchmark formalizes language-conditioned instructions (e.g., "Move object from start receptacle to goal receptacle") into sequential sub-tasks following a Sense–Plan–Act paradigm. Tasks are categorized by difficulty (Easy, Medium, Hard) based on object and receptacle locations (surface, open container, closed container), requiring fundamental skills like Find, Navigation, Grasp Estimation, Open, Pick, and Place.
- **Modular Skill Architecture:** BestMan features an architecture with eleven top-level modules (e.g., Perception, Task Planning, Navigation, Manipulation, Asset, Visualization, Controller, Digital Twin, Sensor, Robotics API, Config). Each module contains submodules supporting different algorithms. The platform integrates Blender for improved rendering and includes supplementary modules for control (PID, MPC), collision detection, and SLAM.
- **Multimodal Perception:** A multimodal perception layer supports task-oriented sensory processing and semantic understanding. It maps observations (S) and semantic reasoning methods (M) to structured environmental representations (O). The perception module facilitates a robust grasping pipeline, integrating state-of-the-art backbones like LangSAM/Grounded-SAM for object segmentation, AnyGrasp/Contact-GraspNet for grasp pose estimation, and Locate for affordance detection.
- **Hybrid Planning:** To ensure robust planning in cluttered environments, a hybrid approach integrates task and motion planning.
 - **Task Planning:** Combines symbolic reasoning with Large Language Models (LLMs) to generate sub-tasks from ambiguous instructions. This is formalized as $P = \Gamma_{symb}(S) \circ \Phi_{LLM}(I)$, where $\Gamma_{symb}$ is a symbolic planner, $S$ is the PDDL-defined state space, $\Phi_{LLM}$ is an LLM-based semantic parser, and $I$ is natural language instruction.
 - **Motion Planning:** Divided into navigation and manipulation. Navigation uses global (A*, RRT, PRM) and local (DWA, MPC) path planning. Manipulation combines sampling-based methods from OMPL (RRT, RRT*, BIT*) with reinforcement learning and imitation learning.
- **Hierarchical Management and Modular Integration:** A hierarchical architecture manages configuration complexity and dependency conflicts. Each functional module has a dedicated configuration submodule for parameter tuning, coordinated by a global configuration module. Dependency conflicts are resolved using a hybrid strategy of containerization and virtual environments, ensuring isolated runtimes and reliable data exchange.

#### 3.3. HUM: Hardware-agnostic Unified Middleware

The HUM module aims to facilitate seamless sim-to-real deployments and ensure compatibility with heterogeneous hardware.

- **Unified API for Mobile Manipulators:** HUM provides an abstracted, aligned Python API architecture, establishing a bijective mapping ($C_{sim} \leftrightarrow C_{real}$) between simulated and real-world domains. This enables consistent high-level commands for controlling mobile bases, robotic arms, end effectors, and sensors, abstracting away underlying hardware-specific drivers and protocols.
- **Cross-Platform Compatibility and Deployments:** HUM decouples the platform from specific simulators and hardware, enabling "real-to-real" transfer of algorithms across diverse robotic hardware setups. This improves real-world reproducibility and allows for exploring algorithm robustness across various hardware configurations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments aimed to evaluate the ASG module's accuracy, the effectiveness of the modular design, the efficiency of HUM, and the overall performance of BestMan in completing real-world mobile manipulation tasks.

#### 4.1. Accuracy Analysis of ASG (Q1)

- **Scene Reconstruction Performance:** The ASG module demonstrated high accuracy in reconstructing simulated scenes from both simulated and real-world image inputs.
 - For simulation inputs (Table 2), BestMan achieved 100% categorization accuracy, up to 94% modeling accuracy, and 90% joint accuracy. Geometric alignment was precise, with low L2 Distance (e.g., 4.85 ± 2.60 cm for Kitchen) and Orientation Difference (e.g., 0.05 ± 0.04 rad for Kitchen), alongside high Bbox IoU and Center IoU.
 - For real-world inputs, human evaluation (C. Score) confirmed high semantic fidelity, with 100% category accuracy, over 85% modeling accuracy, and a composite score exceeding 89%.
- **Comparison with Baselines (Articulated Scene Generation):** ASG outperformed baselines such as URDFormer and ACDC (Table 3). For simulation inputs, ASG achieved 90% joint accuracy and a pose error of 3.11 ± 1.54, significantly better than URDFormer (88% joint, 7.65 ± 3.18 pose error) and ACDC (78% joint, 5.21 ± 2.16 pose error). Similar trends were observed for real-world inputs. This superiority is attributed to the hierarchical structural parsing and refined modeling of articulated parameters. Challenges related to multi-layer occlusions and texture homogeneity were noted.

#### 4.2. Effectiveness of Modular Design (Q2)

- The modular design of BestMan was evaluated against non-modular baselines, Reinforcement Learning (RL) and Behavior Cloning (BC), across various task difficulties (Easy, Medium, Hard) and environment complexities (Simple, Complex) in ASG-generated simulated scenes (Figure 9).
- BestMan consistently outperformed both RL and BC across all settings. The performance gap increased with task difficulty and environmental complexity. In the hardest setting (HC), BestMan achieved a 42.3% success rate, which was higher than RL (32.8%) and BC (27.5%). These results support the assertion that the modular design is more effective for complex mobile manipulation tasks and environments.

#### 4.3. Efficiency Comparison of HUM (Q3)

- The Hardware-agnostic Unified Middleware (HUM) was compared with ROS for sim-to-real transfer efficiency on two heterogeneous robotic hardware setups (RH1 and RH2) (Table 4).
- **Transfer Time (TT):** HUM significantly reduced the sim-to-real deployment time, achieving a 3x to 5x improvement over ROS (14 min vs. 65 min on RH1; 18 min vs. 78 min on RH2).
- **Response Time (RT):** HUM consistently exhibited lower response latency, with reductions of 1.3 ms on RH1 (4.5 ms vs. 5.8 ms) and 0.9 ms on RH2 (5.3 ms vs. 6.2 ms), attributed to its lightweight architecture.
- **Task Success Rate (SR):** HUM maintained comparable or slightly better task success rates compared to ROS (45.2% vs. 47.5% on RH1; 49.0% vs. 47.8% on RH2).
- These findings indicate that HUM enhances the efficiency of sim-to-real deployments while maintaining competitive task performance and offering improved compatibility with heterogeneous hardware.

#### 4.4. Overall Verification of BestMan on Real-World Tasks (Q4)

- BestMan was evaluated on the THMM benchmark in both simulation and real-world settings, using various skill strategies (Heuristic, Fine-tuned, Reinforcement Learning) across three sub-tasks: Approach, Manipulation, and Placement (Table 5).
- Hybrid strategies that integrate prior knowledge through fine-tuning with adaptive policy learning via reinforcement (e.g., F-R-F-R) consistently outperformed purely heuristic methods (H-H-H-H) and demonstrated greater reliability than RL-only strategies. For example, in simulation, the F-R-F-R strategy achieved an Overall Success Rate (OSR) of 60.7%, compared to 38.5% for H-H-H-H. In real-world settings, F-R-F-R achieved an OSR of 52.2%, significantly higher than H-H-H-H (23.4%).
- The complete real-to-sim-to-real cycle (Figure 10) demonstrated BestMan's capability to map real-world environments to high-fidelity simulations using ASG, enable rapid composition and optimization of hybrid strategies, and seamlessly deploy learned strategies back to physical robots, executing language-guided tasks effectively.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The BestMan platform addresses critical gaps in embodied intelligence research by providing a scalable and seamless real-to-sim-to-real transfer pipeline for household mobile manipulation tasks. Its comprehensive design and demonstrated capabilities contribute significantly to the field in several ways:

1. **Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap:** By offering high-quality articulated scene reconstruction (ASG), BestMan mitigates the challenges of manually creating realistic simulation environments. This allows for more representative data generation for training, potentially reducing the sim-to-real reality gap and improving the transferability of learned policies to physical robots. The detailed reconstruction of articulated objects and realistic physical parameters enables more accurate simulation of complex interactions.
2. **Facilitating Scalable Algorithm Development:** The modular skill architecture and the Transferable Household Mobile Manipulation (THMM) benchmark provide a standardized framework for decomposing long-horizon tasks and systematically evaluating different algorithmic strategies. This modularity allows researchers to integrate new perception, planning, and control algorithms efficiently, promoting rapid iteration and comparative analysis. The support for hybrid planning strategies further allows for leveraging the strengths of both symbolic reasoning (LLMs) and learning-based approaches, which is crucial for robustness in cluttered and unstructured environments.
3. **Enhancing Real-World Deployability and Reproducibility:** The Hardware-agnostic Unified Middleware (HUM) significantly reduces the time and effort required for sim-to-real deployment across heterogeneous robotic platforms. By abstracting hardware interfaces and offering a unified API, HUM increases the accessibility and reproducibility of mobile manipulation research. This means that algorithms developed and tested in simulation can be deployed on a wider range of physical robots with minimal modifications, facilitating validation and benchmarking across diverse hardware setups.
4. **Advancing Generalizable Embodied AI:** The platform's emphasis on flexibility and scalability, through its automated scene generation, modular design, and hardware-agnostic middleware, lays a foundation for developing more generalizable embodied AI agents. Such agents are capable of performing a variety of complex tasks in diverse, unstructured real-world environments, which is a key objective in the field.
5. **Diverse Application Areas:** BestMan's architecture and capabilities open avenues for research and application in various domains:
 - **Visual Navigation:** Integrating sensor, perception, and control modules for real-time navigation in complex scenarios.
 - **Long-horizon Tasks:** Decomposing and executing multi-stage tasks like cleaning or item sorting with consistent task planning.
 - **Rearrangement Tasks:** Supporting object rearrangement in local environments with its extensive asset library for testing generalization.
 - **Sim2Real Transfer:** Providing an efficient pipeline for algorithm deployment verification.
 - **Multi-agent Collaboration:** Serving as a framework for developing and optimizing decentralized multi-heterogeneous robot collaboration systems based on large language models.

In conclusion, BestMan represents a comprehensive and unified platform that addresses key challenges in embodied intelligence for mobile manipulation. Its contributions in automated scene generation, modular skill learning, and hardware-agnostic deployment are significant, offering a robust foundation for future research and accelerating the development of capable robotic systems for household environments. The open-source nature of the code further enhances its potential impact on the broader scientific community.
