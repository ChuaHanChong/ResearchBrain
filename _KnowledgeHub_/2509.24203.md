# Group-Relative REINFORCE Is Secretly an Off-Policy Algorithm: Demystifying Some Myths About GRPO and Its Friends

> [!summary] Summary
> This research provides a first-principles derivation of group-relative REINFORCE, revealing its inherent off-policy nature for Large Language Model (LLM) reinforcement learning. The work shows that clipping is more critical than importance sampling for stability in off-policy settings and offers a theoretical justification for data-weighting methods, improving performance on various LLM-RL benchmarks.

## Problem

- Many prominent LLM-RL algorithms (e.g., PPO, GRPO) are on-policy or near-on-policy, limiting their effectiveness with diverse, 'truly' off-policy data.
- Real-world LLM deployment faces practical constraints like asynchronous training, delayed rewards, and costly environments, requiring robust off-policy methods.
- Existing LLM-RL infrastructure, optimized for REINFORCE-style on-policy algorithms, struggles to support specialized off-policy approaches without significant overhauls.

## Method

- The paper provides a ==first-principles derivation== of group-relative REINFORCE as a single gradient step of a KL-regularized surrogate loss, demonstrating its native off-policy interpretation.
- Two general principles are proposed for augmenting REINFORCE in off-policy scenarios: regularizing the policy update (e.g., clipping) and actively shaping the data distribution (e.g., data weighting).
- Existing algorithms like OPMD and AsymRE are reinterpreted as regularized REINFORCE loss variants, and a generalized pairwise-weighted REINFORCE gradient is derived to justify heuristic data-weighting strategies.

## Results

- Empirical comparisons show that 'REINFORCE-with-Clipping' (REC) variants without importance sampling perform nearly identically to GRPO, highlighting the non-essential role of importance sampling.
- Enlarging the clipping range in REC-ONESIDE-NOIS accelerates training across various off-policy settings on Math reasoning (GSM8k, MATH, Guru-Math) and Tool-use (ToolACE) benchmarks.
- Data-weighting methods (RED-DROP, RED-WEIGHT) demonstrate strong empirical performance, with RED-WEIGHT achieving higher rewards than GRPO on Guru-Math and MATH for larger models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Clipping, as a regularization mechanism, is more crucial than importance sampling for stabilizing REINFORCE-style algorithms in off-policy LLM-RL.
> - Wider clipping ranges (e.g., 0.6 to 2.0) can significantly accelerate training in GRPO-like algorithms without compromising stability, challenging conventional narrow clipping ranges.
> - Heuristic data-weighting methods, such as dropping negative samples (RED-DROP) or up-weighting high-reward samples (RED-WEIGHT), can be theoretically justified and effectively shape policy updates in off-policy settings.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of reinforcement learning (RL) for large language models (LLMs) has seen substantial advancements, progressing from initial alignment via reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) to more complex applications such as reasoning-oriented RL and agentic RL, which involve tool use, long-horizon planning, and multi-step task execution.

A significant challenge in these advanced LLM-RL applications is the practical implementation of training paradigms. Real-world deployment often faces constraints such as mismatched speeds between rollout generation and model training, data collection from diverse or older policies, irregular or delayed reward feedback, and costly or unstable environments. These factors necessitate the use of off-policy RL methods, which can leverage experience replay, synthesize high-quality experiences, or incorporate expert demonstrations to improve sample efficiency and performance.

However, many prominent algorithms in LLM-RL, including Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) and Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), are fundamentally on-policy or near-on-policy algorithms. These methods, largely modern variants of REINFORCE, rely on generating unbiased estimates of the policy gradient, which ideally requires data sampled from the current policy. While they can incorporate importance sampling to tolerate limited degrees of off-policyness, their effectiveness diminishes when the behavior policy deviates significantly from the target policy. Existing RL infrastructure is typically optimized for REINFORCE-style on-policy algorithms, which limits their support for specialized off-policy RL approaches.

This research addresses this gap by re-evaluating the theoretical foundations of group-relative REINFORCE, seeking to establish a native off-policy interpretation that could facilitate the development of principled and infrastructure-friendly off-policy RL algorithms for LLMs. It aims to provide a unified framework to understand and augment current LLM-RL methods in off-policy settings.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to provide a first-principles derivation for group-relative REINFORCE that demonstrates its inherent off-policy nature, without making assumptions about the distribution from which training data is sampled. This derivation aims to offer a novel perspective for understanding the algorithm's operation and to lay a theoretical groundwork for off-policy algorithm design in LLM-RL.

The motivation behind this work stems from several critical needs and observations:
1. **Bridging the On-Policy/Off-Policy Gap in LLM-RL:** Current dominant LLM-RL algorithms (e.g., PPO, GRPO) are primarily on-policy. While they use importance sampling to handle minor policy shifts, they struggle with "truly" off-policy data, which is increasingly common and desirable in real-world LLM applications. The goal is to develop methods that can robustly handle data collected from diverse or older policies without requiring frequent, costly on-policy interactions.
2. **Addressing Practical Constraints:** Real-world LLM deployment scenarios often involve practical constraints such as asynchronous rollout generation and model training, delayed reward feedback, and the need to reuse past experiences. Off-policy learning is crucial for addressing these challenges, but a solid theoretical basis for adapting REINFORCE-style algorithms to these settings has been lacking.
3. **Infrastructure Compatibility:** Existing LLM-RL infrastructure is often optimized for REINFORCE-style algorithms. Introducing fundamentally different off-policy algorithms might require significant infrastructure overhaul. This work seeks to develop "infrastructure-friendly" off-policy algorithms by re-interpreting and augmenting existing REINFORCE variants.
4. **Demystifying Existing Algorithms:** The paper aims to provide a unified theoretical lens through which various recent RL algorithms can be understood. Specifically, it seeks to:
 - Clarify the relative importance of importance sampling versus clipping in algorithms like GRPO.
 - Reinterpret methods such as Kimi's Online Policy Mirror Descent (OPMD) and Meta's Asymmetric REINFORCE (AsymRE) as forms of regularized REINFORCE loss, offering an alternative to their original rationales.
 - Provide theoretical justification for heuristic data-weighting strategies (e.g., dropping low-reward samples or up-weighting high-reward ones), which lack formal policy gradient theory support but are observed to be effective.

By achieving these objectives, the research intends to foster the development of principled, stable, and efficient off-policy RL algorithms for LLMs, moving beyond ad-hoc solutions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on a novel theoretical derivation for group-relative REINFORCE and its subsequent application to analyze and augment existing LLM-RL algorithms. This theoretical work is complemented by empirical validation across various LLM-RL benchmarks.

**Theoretical Derivation of Off-Policy Group-Relative REINFORCE:**
The core of the methodology involves a three-step derivation for group-relative REINFORCE in a one-step RL setting, without assuming on-policy data:
1. **Surrogate Objective and Consistency Condition:** The derivation begins by defining a KL-regularized surrogate objective function. This objective encourages policy improvement while maintaining proximity to a reference policy (π_θ_t), controlled by a regularization coefficient τ. It is shown that the optimal policy for this objective satisfies a specific pairwise consistency condition (Eq. 5), relating the rewards and log-probabilities of any two responses for a given prompt.
2. **Surrogate Loss with Finite Samples:** To make the theoretical framework practical, a mean-squared surrogate loss function (Eq. 6) is constructed using a finite set of K responses. This loss is designed to enforce the previously established pairwise consistency condition among the sampled responses.
3. **One Gradient Step of the Surrogate Loss:** The crucial step involves calculating the gradient of this finite-sample surrogate loss with respect to the policy parameters, evaluated at the current policy θ_t. The result of this gradient calculation is shown to be equivalent to the group-relative REINFORCE update rule (Eq. 7). This derivation is significant because it arrives at the REINFORCE update without any explicit assumption about the sampling distribution of the responses, thus providing an off-policy interpretation.

The paper notes that this derivation can be generalized to multi-step RL settings by considering full trajectories and trajectory-level rewards and KL divergence, as detailed in Appendix A.

**Identifying Pitfalls and Augmentation Principles:**
Based on the off-policy interpretation, the paper analyzes the limitations of vanilla REINFORCE, particularly when data is drawn from a sub-optimal distribution. It highlights that a single gradient step of the surrogate loss with finite, potentially unrepresentative samples may not align well with the true optimal direction. This analysis leads to the proposal of two general principles for augmenting REINFORCE in off-policy scenarios:
1. **Regularize the Policy Update Step:** Methods to stabilize learning and bound the optimization trajectory, such as clipping or adding explicit regularization terms to the loss function.
2. **Actively Shape the Data Distribution:** Strategies to steer the policy update direction by differentially weighting or filtering training samples.

**Rethinking Existing RL Algorithms:**
The proposed framework is then used to re-evaluate several contemporary LLM-RL algorithms:
- **GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization):** Its loss function, incorporating token-wise importance sampling and clipping (Eq. 8), is analyzed. Ablation studies with "REINFORCE-with-Clipping" (REC) variants (REC-ONESIDE-IS, REC-ONESIDE-NOIS, REC-TWOSIDE, REC-RING) are designed to isolate the effects of importance sampling and different clipping mechanisms.
- **Kimi’s Online Policy Mirror Descent (OPMD) and Meta’s Asymmetric REINFORCE (AsymRE):** The paper reinterprets their loss functions as standard REINFORCE loss augmented with a regularization term, providing a unified view that contrasts with their original derivations.
- **Data-Weighting Methods:** The theoretical framework is extended to justify heuristic data-weighting strategies. A generalized pairwise-weighted REINFORCE gradient (Eq. 9) is derived. This forms the basis for two proposed "REINFORCE-with-data-weighting" (RED) methods: RED-DROP (discarding negative samples) and RED-WEIGHT (up-weighting high-reward samples, Eq. 10).

**Empirical Validation:**
Extensive empirical studies are conducted to validate the theoretical insights and evaluate the efficacy of the reinterpreted and augmented algorithms.
- **Framework:** All experiments utilize the Trinity-RFT framework.
- **Off-policyness Control:** The degree of off-policyness is controlled by two parameters: `sync_interval` (frequency of model synchronization) and `sync_offset` (lag between rollout generation and training). This allows for simulating various off-policy scenarios, including delayed environmental feedback and offline data.
- **Benchmarks:** Experiments are performed on a range of tasks:
 - Math reasoning: GSM8k, MATH, Guru-Math.
 - Tool-use: ToolACE.
- **LLMs:** Diverse LLMs are used, including Qwen2.5-1.5B/7B-Instruct, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct, and Qwen3-30B-A3B (a mixture-of-experts model).
- **Metrics:** Performance is evaluated using metrics such as evaluation accuracy, training reward, KL divergence from the initial policy, clipping fraction, entropy, and response length.

This comprehensive methodology, combining theoretical re-derivation, conceptual analysis, and broad empirical validation, forms the basis for the paper's findings.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several key findings derived from its theoretical analysis and validated through extensive empirical studies:

**Finding F1: Demystifying Myths about GRPO and the Role of Clipping:**
- **Importance Sampling vs. Clipping:** Empirical comparisons between GRPO and the REC-ONESIDE-IS/NOIS variants (Figures 2, 3, 8) indicate that the importance sampling component in GRPO is non-essential for its effectiveness in off-policy settings. REC-ONESIDE-NOIS, which omits importance sampling but retains clipping, exhibits nearly identical performance to GRPO and REC-ONESIDE-IS. This suggests that clipping, as a form of regularization, plays a more critical role than importance sampling in stabilizing learning under off-policy data.
- **Critical Role of Clipping:** In contrast to the stable performance of clipping-based methods, vanilla REINFORCE without clipping consistently collapses, highlighting that clipping is crucial for maintaining stability, especially in off-policy contexts.
- **Wider Clipping Range:** The study reveals that enlarging the clipping range (e.g., setting `ϵ_low` = 0.6, `ϵ_high` = 2.0 instead of the conventional 0.2 for both) in REC-ONESIDE-NOIS can accelerate training significantly without compromising stability across various off-policy settings (Figures 2, 3). This challenges the conventional wisdom of using narrow clipping ranges, often justified by unbiased policy gradient estimation.
- **Clipping Geometry:** Different clipping mechanisms (one-side, two-side, ring) demonstrate varying trade-offs between learning speed and stability (Figure 8). REC-TWOSIDE shows initial fast improvement but tends to collapse later, while REC-RING (which combines aspects of both) provides a better balance.
- **Learning Rate vs. Clipping Range:** Experiments confirm that the performance gains from a wider clipping range are not simply an artifact of a larger effective learning rate (Figure 9).
- **GSPO Implication:** Preliminary results (Figure 12) extend this insight to Group Sequence Policy Optimization (GSPO), suggesting that sequence-level importance sampling might also be non-essential, with sequence-level clipping being the primary stabilizing mechanism.

**Finding F2: Reinterpreting Kimi’s OPMD and Meta’s AsymRE as Regularized REINFORCE:**
- The theoretical analysis reinterprets both OPMD and AsymRE as variants of the REINFORCE loss with an added regularization term.
 - OPMD's final loss formulation is shown to be equivalent to the standard REINFORCE loss plus a mean-squared regularization term on the log probabilities, which explicitly regularizes the policy's deviation from an older version.
 - AsymRE, by tuning down the baseline in advantage calculation, is re-expressed as the REINFORCE loss plus a term that, when combined with an auxiliary term and taken to the K→∞ limit, approximates a KL regularization term (D_KL(π_old || π_θ)).
- Empirical studies (Figure 10) validate the efficacy of OPMD and AsymRE across various off-policy settings, including those with significant `sync_offset`, supporting their robustness as regularized REINFORCE algorithms.

**Finding F3: Justifying Data-Weighting Methods:**
- The generalized pairwise-weighted REINFORCE gradient (Eq. 9) provides a theoretical justification for heuristic data-weighting strategies.
- **RED-DROP:** The strategy of dropping "excess negative samples" is shown to be a special case of this weighted framework (Eq. 10), where only a filtered subset of responses is used for training, and advantages are recomputed within this subset.
- **RED-WEIGHT:** Up-weighting high-reward responses (using a temperature-controlled exponential weighting) is also interpreted within this framework. This method effectively transforms the objective to prioritize learning from more valuable experiences and includes a regularization component that implicitly encourages imitation of high-reward trajectories.
- **Empirical Efficacy:** Both RED-DROP and RED-WEIGHT demonstrate strong empirical performance (Figures 4, 5, 11) in both on-policy and various off-policy settings. Notably, RED-WEIGHT achieves higher rewards than GRPO on Guru-Math (Figure 5) and MATH (Figure 11) for larger models, often with lower KL divergence and more stable entropy/response length, indicating efficient learning.

These findings collectively contribute to a deeper understanding of REINFORCE-style algorithms in off-policy LLM-RL, challenging established assumptions and providing practical insights for algorithm design.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research holds several significant implications for the field of reinforcement learning for large language models, particularly in off-policy settings.

**Theoretical Significance:**
The most prominent contribution is the novel, first-principles derivation of group-relative REINFORCE, which inherently reveals its off-policy nature. This re-interpretation moves beyond the conventional view of REINFORCE as a strictly on-policy algorithm requiring unbiased gradient estimates. By demonstrating that REINFORCE effectively takes a single gradient step of a KL-regularized surrogate loss without assuming on-policy data, the paper provides a new theoretical lens for understanding a broad class of LLM-RL algorithms. This unification demystifies the roles of components like importance sampling and clipping, clarifying their mechanistic contributions to learning stability rather than solely their role in bias reduction.

**Algorithmic Design and Improvement:**
The work offers two general principles for augmenting REINFORCE in off-policy scenarios: regularizing policy updates and actively shaping the data distribution. These principles provide a structured framework for designing more robust and efficient off-policy algorithms.
- **Challenging Conventional Wisdom:** The finding that larger clipping ranges can accelerate learning without sacrificing stability in GRPO-like algorithms challenges the prevailing practice of using narrow clipping boundaries (e.g., 0.2), which is often rooted in on-policy policy gradient theory. This opens avenues for immediate practical improvements to existing PPO/GRPO implementations.
- **Reinterpretation of Algorithms:** By reinterpreting algorithms like OPMD and AsymRE as forms of regularized REINFORCE loss, the paper provides a common theoretical ground, which can facilitate cross-pollination of ideas and lead to hybrid or improved designs.
- **Justification of Heuristics:** The theoretical justification for data-weighting strategies like RED-DROP and RED-WEIGHT transforms empirically observed heuristics into principled components, encouraging their more widespread and informed adoption.

**Practical Impact and Infrastructure-Friendliness:**
The insights derived from this research are actionable and align with practical constraints in large-scale LLM-RL deployments.
- **Enhanced Off-Policy Robustness:** By clarifying how REINFORCE-style algorithms can operate effectively with off-policy data, the work supports the development of LLM agents that can be continually updated with data from various sources and older policies, which is crucial in the "era of experience" where agents interact continuously with dynamic environments.
- **Efficiency Gains:** The ability to leverage off-policy data, coupled with insights into effective regularization and data weighting, can lead to higher sample efficiency and faster training convergence, reducing the computational cost and time associated with LLM fine-tuning.
- **Compatibility with Existing Systems:** The focus on re-interpreting and augmenting REINFORCE-style algorithms makes the proposed improvements and insights largely compatible with existing LLM-RL infrastructure (e.g., Trinity-RFT), which is often optimized for these algorithms. This reduces the need for extensive architectural changes, accelerating adoption.

**Future Research Directions:**
The identified limitations, such as the absence of formal guarantees for policy improvement or convergence under arbitrary off-policy data, open clear paths for future theoretical work. Expanding the scope to include step-level rewards, single rollouts per query, and more advanced off-policy settings (e.g., experience synthesis, expert data integration) promises to further solidify the theoretical foundations and practical utility of off-policy LLM-RL.

In summary, this research provides a fundamental re-evaluation of group-relative REINFORCE, offering a robust off-policy interpretation that not only unifies existing algorithms but also provides principled guidelines for designing the next generation of efficient and stable off-policy RL methods for large language models.
