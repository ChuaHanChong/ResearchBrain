# Language Models that Think, Chat Better

> [!summary] Summary
> Princeton University researchers developed Reinforcement Learning with Model-rewarded Thinking (RLMT), an approach that enables large language models to employ explicit reasoning for diverse, open-ended conversational tasks. An 8B parameter model trained with RLMT achieved a 58.7 score on AlpacaEval2 and 50.4 on WildBench, outperforming larger 70B models and, in some cases, even frontier models like GPT-4o on chat and creative writing benchmarks.

## Problem

- Existing Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) models, despite strong performance in specific domains like math and code, demonstrate limited generalization of their reasoning abilities to open-ended, subjective tasks.
- Large Language Models (LLMs) struggle to engage in robust, human-like "system 2 thinking" for diverse, general-purpose chat and creative problem-solving.
- Current LLM alignment methods like RLHF primarily optimize final outputs, often neglecting to explicitly encourage or optimize the internal reasoning processes that lead to high-quality responses.

## Method

- RLMT trains LLMs to generate an explicit ==Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning trace== prior to the final response, extending structured thinking to general conversational tasks.
- This approach leverages a ==preference-based reward model== to evaluate the final response, enabling supervision for thinking in subjective, open-ended domains where rule-based verification is impractical.
- The framework was evaluated with various online RL algorithms, with ==Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)== demonstrating superior performance, and tested on models both with and without initial Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT).

## Results

- RLMT models consistently outperformed RLHF baselines by **1.5–4** points across benchmarks, achieving average gains of **3–8** points in chat and creative writing tasks.
- The top 8B RLMT model (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct with GRPO) scored **58.7** on AlpacaEval2 and **50.4** on WildBench, surpassing 70B instruction-tuned models by **9–22** points and outperforming GPT-4o on specific general chat and creative writing benchmarks.
- Applying RLMT directly to base models without prior Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) yielded substantial improvements, with Llama-3.1-8B-Base-RLMT outperforming its SFT-trained counterpart by **5.5** points on chat benchmarks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reinforcement Learning with Model-rewarded Thinking (RLMT) effectively bridges the generalization gap of prior reasoning-focused methods by applying explicit Chain-of-Thought training to subjective, open-ended domains.
> - The research indicates that superior performance in general chat and creative tasks is not solely dependent on model scale, as smaller models with RLMT can outperform significantly larger and frontier LLMs.
> - RLMT demonstrates the potential to simplify the LLM post-training pipeline, achieving strong results by directly training base models without extensive Supervised Fine-Tuning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses a critical challenge in the development of Large Language Models (LLMs): enhancing their ability to perform complex, open-ended reasoning, moving beyond simple instruction following or fact retrieval towards more human-like "system 2 thinking" (Kahneman, 2011). The current LLM training paradigm typically involves three stages: pre-training, supervised fine-tuning (SFT), and reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF). This work primarily intersects with the final, alignment stage of this pipeline, proposing a novel approach that blends elements from two existing RL-based methods:

1. **Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF):** This is the dominant method for aligning LLMs with human preferences, typically using a reward model trained on human judgments. However, RLHF often treats the LLM's output as a monolithic entity, without explicitly encouraging or optimizing internal reasoning processes.
2. **Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR):** A more recent paradigm, RLVR has shown significant success in domains where answers can be objectively verified, such as mathematics and code generation. In RLVR, models are encouraged to produce a "Chain-of-Thought" (CoT) reasoning trace before the final answer, and these traces are optimized using rule-based rewards (e.g., correctness of the final answer). Notable work in this area includes DeepSeek-R1 (DeepSeek-AI, 2025).

The authors identify a crucial gap: while RLVR excels in verifiable domains, the reasoning skills acquired often do not generalize to open-ended, general-purpose tasks like drafting essays or making meal plans, which are routine for humans. Figure 3 in the paper vividly illustrates this limitation, showing that math-focused RLVR models lag significantly behind standard instruction-tuned models on a general chat benchmark like WildBench. This limited generalization has also been observed in complementary studies (Huan et al., 2025; Zhou et al., 2025).

"Language Models that Think, Chat Better" directly addresses this generalization gap by introducing **Reinforcement Learning with Model-rewarded Thinking (RLMT)**. RLMT bridges the strengths of RLVR (explicit CoT reasoning, online RL optimization) with the broad applicability of RLHF (preference-based reward models for general domains). Instead of relying on rule-based verifiers, RLMT uses a reward model trained on human preferences, allowing it to apply the benefits of structured thinking to a wider array of subjective, open-ended tasks. This innovative combination positions RLMT as a significant advancement in LLM alignment, offering a pathway to instill more robust and transferable reasoning capabilities into models for general conversational AI. Furthermore, the paper's exploration of applying RLMT directly to base models without an initial SFT stage challenges the conventional three-stage training pipeline, suggesting a potentially more streamlined and efficient approach to developing highly capable LLMs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to significantly enhance the general-purpose chat capabilities of Large Language Models by explicitly integrating and optimizing their internal reasoning processes. The authors are motivated by the observation that while Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning has proven highly effective in verifiable domains like mathematics and coding through Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR), these benefits do not readily transfer to the diverse, open-ended tasks that characterize everyday human communication and problem-solving.

The key motivations driving this work include:

- **Addressing the Generalization Gap of RLVR:** Existing RLVR models, despite their strong performance in specific, verifiable domains, exhibit limited generalization to broader reasoning problems and general chat benchmarks. The authors illustrate this with quantitative data (Figure 3), showing math-focused RLVR models underperforming standard instruction-tuned models on general chat tasks. This highlights a need for a mechanism to extend the benefits of structured reasoning beyond narrowly defined, objective tasks.
- **Emulating Human "System 2 Thinking":** Humans routinely engage in conscious, sequential reasoning (often termed "system 2 thinking") for a wide array of everyday tasks, from planning a meal to drafting an email. The authors aspire to instill a similar capability in LLMs, enabling them to "think through the consequences of one’s actions" before generating a response, thereby improving the quality and coherence of their outputs in open-ended contexts.
- **Developing a General-Purpose Thinking Paradigm:** The goal is to move beyond domain-specific rule-based rewards by leveraging preference-based reward models (akin to RLHF) to provide supervision for thinking in general domains. This would allow for the systematic training of LLMs to generate detailed reasoning traces even for tasks where a "ground truth" answer is subjective or non-existent.
- **Improving LLM Alignment and Performance:** By encouraging explicit reasoning, the authors hypothesize that models can achieve better alignment with user intent and produce higher-quality, more thoughtful responses, leading to superior performance on a variety of benchmarks, particularly in conversational AI and creative tasks.
- **Rethinking the Post-Training Pipeline:** The research also aims to explore the feasibility and efficacy of applying this reasoning-focused reinforcement learning directly to base models, potentially simplifying the complex multi-staged post-training pipelines currently in use (e.g., for instruct models that rely on millions of SFT examples). This objective seeks to find more efficient ways to imbue LLMs with advanced capabilities.

In essence, the paper aims to democratize the benefits of explicit reasoning training, extending it from specialized, verifiable niches to the broad landscape of general-purpose language interactions, ultimately leading to more intelligent and aligned chat models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of this research lies in introducing **Reinforcement Learning with Model-rewarded Thinking (RLMT)**, a novel training paradigm that combines elements from RLHF and RLVR to enable LLMs to "think" for general-purpose chat.

#### 3.1. Core RLMT Design

RLMT trains LLMs to generate a detailed Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning trace `z` *before* producing the final response `y` for a given prompt `x`. Unlike RLVR, which uses rule-based verifiers for reward, RLMT employs a **preference-based reward model `r`**, akin to RLHF, to score the final response `y` (Equation 3: `max_θ E_{x~X} E_{(y,z) ~π_θ(·|x)} r(y, x)`). This allows the benefits of structured thinking to be applied to subjective, open-ended tasks where rule-based verification is impractical.

#### 3.2. Key Design Choices for RLMT

- **Training Algorithms:** The authors experimented with three widely adopted online RL or on-policy preference learning algorithms:
 - **Direct Preference Optimization (DPO):** An offline preference optimization method that implicitly constrains the policy to stay close to a reference policy by optimizing a logistic preference objective. For RLMT, a variant that builds preference pairs sampled from the policy model is used.
 - **Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO):** An on-policy algorithm that samples responses, computes rewards, estimates advantages, and updates the policy with a clipped surrogate objective and a KL penalty to a reference policy.
 - **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO):** An on-policy algorithm where for each prompt, a group of candidates is generated, rewards are obtained, and group-centered advantages (mean-centered rewards) are computed, eliminating the need for a learned critic. GRPO emerged as the best-performing algorithm in their experiments.
- **Reward Model:** The **Skywork-v1-Llama-3.1-8B-v0.2** reward model was adopted as `r`, noted for its strong performance on reward benchmarks. Ablation studies confirmed the critical importance of a robust reward model for RLMT's success.
- **Prompt Mixture:** A carefully curated, diverse set of **7.5k real-world user requests from the WildChat-IF subset** of the Tulu 3 SFT mixture was used. This subset prioritizes conversational prompts and avoids an overrepresentation of math or jailbreak prompts, which are common in other mixtures like UltraChat or unfiltered Tulu 3, ensuring better capture of general chat usage.

#### 3.3. Training Settings

The effectiveness of RLMT was evaluated in two distinct settings:

1. **Warm-Start SFT Training:**
 - **SFT Stage:** Models were first fine-tuned using Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) to teach them the desired thinking format (`<think>...</think> <response>...</response>`).
 - **Data Source:** 6k prompts (disjoint from RLMT prompts) were sampled from the Tulu 3 SFT mixture. Responses and simulated thinking traces were generated by **Gemini 2.5 Flash (0417 Preview)**, a powerful teacher model. GPT-4.1-mini was also used for ablation studies to confirm robustness.
 - **Backbones:** This setting was applied to both base and instruct versions of **Llama-3.1-8B** and **Qwen-2.5-7B**.
2. **"Zero" Training with Base Models (No SFT):**
 - RLMT was applied directly to **un-tuned base models** (Llama-3.1-8B Base and Qwen-2.5-7B Base) without any prior SFT.
 - The desired output structure was elicited by prepending a **fixed instruction prefix** (e.g., "A conversation between User and Assistant..."). The subsequent RL training procedure was identical to the warm-start setting.

#### 3.4. Evaluation

Models were rigorously evaluated on a comprehensive suite of 7 benchmarks, designed to assess broad applicability:

- **Chat:** AlpacaEval 2 (AE2), WildBench (WB), ArenaHardV2 (AH2).
- **Creative Writing:** CreativeWritingV3 (CWv3).
- **Instruction Following:** IFBench (IF_Ben).
- **General Knowledge:** PopQA, MMLU-Redux (MMLU_R).
- **Judge Models:** GPT-4o or GPT-4.1 were used for evaluation, with specific length control and debiasing as recommended for AlpacaEval2.
- **Baselines:** For a fair comparison, every RLMT model was paired with an **RLHF baseline** trained under identical conditions but without the explicit thinking component. Comparisons were also made against larger open-source models (Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct), and frontier closed models (GPT-4o, Claude-3.7-Sonnet), as well as other math-trained RLVR models and concurrent work using alternative reward signals.

#### 3.5. Analysis

The authors conducted qualitative and quantitative analyses to understand *how* RLMT changes model behavior, including:

- **Trait Extraction:** Using GPT-4.1-mini to compare reasoning styles between pre-RL and post-RL models on WildBench, identifying amplified or suppressed planning traits.
- **CoT Length Analysis:** Quantifying the increase in CoT and response length as training progresses.
- **Ablation Studies:** Systematically varying the prompt mixture, warm-start data source, and reward model to determine their impact on performance.

This comprehensive methodological approach allowed the researchers to thoroughly validate RLMT, analyze its effectiveness, and gain insights into its mechanisms.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several significant findings demonstrating the effectiveness and broad applicability of Reinforcement Learning with Model-rewarded Thinking (RLMT):

- **Consistent Outperformance of RLHF Baselines:** Across all tested model families (Llama-3.1-8B and Qwen-2.5-7B, both base and instruct versions) and optimization algorithms (DPO, PPO, GRPO), RLMT consistently and substantially outperformed standard RLHF pipelines. Thinking models (RLMT) achieved average gains of 1.5–4 points across all benchmarks compared to their non-thinking counterparts.

- **Significant Gains in Chat and Creative Writing:** The most pronounced improvements were observed in chat benchmarks (WildBench, AlpacaEval2, ArenaHardV2), with average gains of 3–8 points. Creative Writing (CWv3) also saw notable improvements. This directly addresses the paper's core motivation to extend thinking capabilities beyond verifiable domains.

- **Competitive with Frontier Models:**
 - The best RLMT model, **Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct trained with GRPO (RLMT)**, scored 58.7 on AlpacaEval2 and 50.4 on WildBench.
 - Remarkably, this 8B model **comfortably surpassed models 10 times larger** (e.g., Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct and Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct) by margins of 9–22 points on chat and creative writing.
 - It also **outperformed GPT-4o** on WildBench and creative writing, and **rivaled Claude-3.7-Sonnet** (a frontier thinking model) on AlpacaEval2 and WildBench, although it lagged on ArenaHardV2, which has a higher proportion of math and coding tasks.

- **Effectiveness in "Zero Training" (No SFT):** RLMT demonstrated substantial gains even when applied directly to base models without a preceding SFT stage. Llama-3.1-8B base trained with RLMT-Zero outperformed Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct on chat benchmarks by 5.5 points, despite the instruct model undergoing a far more complex multi-staged pipeline with millions of examples. Similarly, Qwen-RLMT-Zero outperformed Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct. This finding challenges conventional wisdom regarding the necessity of extensive SFT.

- **Optimal Algorithm Choice:** While RLMT benefits were observed across DPO, PPO, and GRPO, **GRPO consistently yielded the best performance**, outperforming PPO by 1–3 points and DPO by about 5 points on average. This highlights the importance of the specific online RL algorithm used for optimizing reasoning.

- **Performance Shifts Across Model Families:** An intriguing observation was that Llama-3.1 models, which initially underperformed Qwen-2.5 models before RL, surpassed them after RLMT. This suggests that RLMT is capable of reinforcing and unlocking latent capabilities in models that might not have been fully optimized during pre-training or SFT.

- **Ablation Studies Insights:**
 - **Prompt Mixture is Critical:** The choice of prompt distribution significantly impacts performance. The WildChat-IF subset, focusing on diverse conversational prompts, outperformed simpler prompts (UltraFeedback) or mixtures with high proportions of non-chat content (unfiltered Tulu-3).
 - **Robustness to Warm-Start Data Source:** Using GPT-4.1-mini instead of Gemini 2.5 Flash for warm-start SFT data generated similar positive trends, indicating that the benefits of RLMT are not an artifact of a specific teacher model.
 - **Reward Model Strength Matters:** Stronger reward models (Skywork-V2) led to better overall performance, maintaining non-chat benchmark scores while boosting chat capabilities. Weaker reward models (ArmoRM) resulted in performance drops, especially on non-chat tasks. However, RLMT still outperformed standard RLHF on chat benchmarks even with weaker reward models.

- **Qualitative Changes in Reasoning:**
 - RLMT-trained models exhibited a shift from linear, checklist-style planning to more sophisticated and iterative reasoning strategies. These included constraint enumeration, grouping ideas into common themes, and iterative refinement, closely mirroring traits of effective human planning and writing.
 - Training with RLMT also led to **increased Chain-of-Thought length and longer final responses** as training progressed (Figure 5), indicating that models learned to engage in more extensive internal deliberation.

- **Outperformance of Math-Trained Thinking Models:** RLMT models consistently and handily outperformed "thinking" models trained primarily on verifiable math data (e.g., DeepSeek-R1-Distill, SimpleRL-Zoo) by 10–25 points on general chat and creative writing benchmarks, confirming the limited generalization of domain-specific RLVR and validating RLMT's broad applicability.

These findings collectively underscore RLMT's efficacy in endowing LLMs with robust, general-purpose reasoning capabilities, leading to superior chat performance and challenging current paradigms in LLM post-training.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Reinforcement Learning with Model-rewarded Thinking (RLMT) represents a significant advancement in the field of Large Language Models, with several profound implications and potential impacts:

- **Revolutionizing LLM Post-Training:** RLMT offers a fresh perspective on the standard three-stage LLM training pipeline. The remarkable ability of RLMT to achieve high performance even when applied directly to base models, bypassing the extensive Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) stage, could lead to significantly simpler and more efficient post-training processes. This could reduce the computational and data-collection burden associated with current alignment methods, making advanced LLM development more accessible.

- **Unlocking General-Purpose Reasoning:** Previously, the benefits of explicit Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning via reinforcement learning were largely confined to verifiable, formal domains like mathematics and coding. RLMT successfully extends this paradigm to subjective, open-ended, and everyday tasks, where humans routinely reason. This breakthrough enables LLMs to develop more sophisticated, human-like "system 2 thinking" capabilities for a broader range of applications, beyond just solving factual or logical problems.

- **Democratizing High-Performance LLMs:** The fact that an 8-billion-parameter model trained with RLMT can rival or even surpass much larger, state-of-the-art closed-source models (like GPT-4o) and open-source models (like Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct) on general chat and creative writing benchmarks is transformative. This demonstrates that impressive performance in complex tasks doesn't solely depend on model scale, but also on intelligent training methodologies. This could accelerate the development of powerful, yet resource-efficient, LLMs for a wider community.

- **Enhanced User Experience and Trust:** Models that explicitly "think" before responding are likely to generate more coherent, well-structured, and thoughtful answers. This improved quality of interaction can lead to a more satisfying user experience, fostering greater trust and reliability in AI-powered conversational agents. The qualitative analysis showing richer planning behaviors (constraint enumeration, theme grouping, iterative refinement) points towards more robust and adaptable responses.

- **Deepening the Understanding of LLM Cognition:** The analysis of how RLMT changes model behavior offers valuable insights into the mechanisms underlying LLM reasoning. Observing shifts from linear planning to iterative, theme-based strategies indicates that these training processes can instill complex cognitive-like functions. This opens new avenues for research into understanding, interpreting, and further sculpting the internal "thought" processes of LLMs.

- **Guiding Future Research:** The paper clearly outlines several promising directions for future work, including deeper analyses of how learned traits are amplified, optimization of CoT formats and hyperparameters, and exploring the generalizability of RLMT to other challenging domains. Its findings call upon the research community to "understand and employ thinking more broadly" in LLM development. The open-sourcing of code and models will facilitate rapid iteration and exploration of these directions.

In summary, RLMT presents a compelling case for integrating explicit, model-rewarded thinking into general LLM training. Its ability to elevate the performance of smaller models, challenge established training pipelines, and broaden the scope of LLM reasoning represents a significant stride towards more intelligent, versatile, and aligned artificial general intelligence.
