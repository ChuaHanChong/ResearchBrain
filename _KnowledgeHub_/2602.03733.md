# RegionReasoner: Region-Grounded Multi-Round Visual Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of Amsterdam, Anhui University, and Westlake University developed REGIONREASONER, a reinforcement learning framework that enables Vision-Language Models to perform multi-round visual reasoning. It enforces explicit spatial grounding and global-local semantic consistency, achieving state-of-the-art performance on a new benchmark, RegionDial-Bench, with detection improvements of up to 17.7 AP in later dialogue turns.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language Models (VLMs) primarily rely on single-step or text-only reasoning, struggling with iterative refinement across multiple visual contexts.
- Current VLMs lack explicit spatial grounding within their reasoning, leading to 'coordinate hallucinations' and unverifiable references, and suffer from 'semantic drift' in multi-round interactions, where global scene understanding becomes inconsistent with localized region details.
- A lack of standardized benchmarks specifically designed for multi-round, visually-grounded reasoning tasks hinders progress in this area.

## Method

- Developed an autoregressive VLM policy that generates structured text actions each turn (global context, local focus, explicit thinking, final answer), optimized via ==GRPO== with novel ==Reference Citation== and ==Global-Local Consistency Rewards==, designed for multi-round interaction.
- Introduced a =='Reference-Grounded Thinking' mechanism== mandating citation of prior-turn reference boxes to prevent hallucination, alongside =='Global-Local Semantic Consistency'== enforcing keyword alignment between scene descriptions, region captions, and reasoning trace to prevent semantic drift.
- Created ==RegionDial-Bench==, a new multi-round benchmark derived from RefCOCO+ and RefCOCOg, supporting both referring detection and segmentation tasks.

## Results

- RegionReasoner-7B achieved state-of-the-art performance on RegionDial-Bench, with **80.7** AP for detection on RefCOCO+ and **78.2** AP on RefCOCOg, outperforming baselines by **4.6-7.6** AP.
- For referring segmentation, the model attained **69.6** gIoU on RefCOCO+ and **66.5** gIoU on RefCOCOg, showing improvements of **5.3-9.8** gIoU over prior methods.
- The framework demonstrated enhanced robustness to error accumulation, with performance gains up to **+17.7** AP over baselines at deeper turns (Round 7), effectively mitigating degradation in longer dialogues.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly requiring VLM reasoning to cite specific reference bounding boxes significantly improves verifiability and prevents coordinate hallucinations in multi-round dialogues.
> - Enforcing semantic consistency between global scene descriptions, localized region captions, and the reasoning trace is crucial for preventing semantic drift and maintaining coherence over dialogue turns; tailored reinforcement learning signals - particularly those rewarding reference citation and global-local consistency - effectively train VLMs to handle complex, iterative visual reasoning tasks.
> - A structured output format that includes explicit 'thinking' steps enhances the interpretability of the VLM's reasoning process, making it more transparent and auditable.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Recent advancements in large Vision-Language Models (VLMs), exemplified by systems like OpenAI GPT-4o, Gemini-2.5, and DeepSeek, have demonstrated considerable progress in multimodal reasoning across benchmarks such as MathVista and MMMU. These systems typically operate by processing multimodal inputs, extracting textual cues, and then performing chain-of-thought reasoning predominantly within the text domain.

Within the vision community, two relevant lines of research have pushed the field forward:
- **VisionReasoner (Liu et al., 2025b)** introduced a structured perception-reasoning paradigm using explicit output tags and reward shaping, yielding robust single-turn grounding and interpretable trajectories. However, when extended to multi-round interactions, it faces limitations in explicitly citing regions from previous turns, leading to brittle reference propagation and ambiguous credit assignment. Its reward shaping primarily targets final outputs, providing limited guidance for stabilizing the reasoning trace itself, which can result in semantic drift.
- **SegLLM (Wang et al., 2025b)** demonstrated the utility of multi-round interaction for challenging referring segmentation tasks, organizing dialogue-style supervision and evaluation. Nevertheless, SegLLM does not explicitly model a verifiable thinking process, lacks mechanisms to enforce global-local semantic coherence, and does not provide learning signals to shape intermediate reasoning steps. Its supervision is mask-centric, making it less extensible to detection tasks.

This work, RegionReasoner, aims to address these identified limitations by extending structured outputs and reward shaping to a multi-round setting. It integrates explicit spatial grounding and global-local consistency within iterative visual reasoning, thereby providing a framework that models and reinforces the reasoning process across multiple conversational turns. It positions itself as an advancement that combines the structured output benefits of VisionReasoner with the multi-round interaction paradigm of SegLLM, while explicitly tackling their respective limitations in reference propagation, reasoning trace stability, and semantic coherence.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observation that while large vision-language models have advanced multimodal reasoning, most existing systems are limited to single-step or text-only reasoning. This restricts their capacity to iteratively refine understanding and maintain coherence across multiple visual contexts in a dialogue-style interaction.

The key objectives and motivations are detailed as follows:

1. **Address the lack of explicit region citation in multi-round reasoning:** Existing frameworks, when naively stacked for multi-round interactions, do not require the reasoning process to explicitly refer to regions grounded in previous turns. This makes reference propagation brittle, complicates credit assignment for spatial understanding, and hinders the detection of hallucinated coordinates. RegionReasoner seeks to enforce explicit citation, making evidence use verifiable and stabilizing reference propagation.

2. **Mitigate semantic drift and stabilize the reasoning trace:** Current reward shaping in related models primarily targets the final outputs (e.g., bounding boxes or points) and the validity of tags. This provides insufficient signal to guide and stabilize the reasoning trace itself as dialogue context accumulates. Consequently, semantic drift can occur between global scene descriptions and local evidence in deeper rounds of interaction. The research aims to introduce a mechanism, specifically a global-local consistency reward, to provide finer-grained signals for intermediate reasoning steps, thereby preventing semantic divergence.

3. **Develop a comprehensive benchmark for multi-round visual reasoning:** A lack of standardized benchmarks for systematically evaluating models under iterative reasoning scenarios, particularly those spanning both detection and segmentation tasks, was identified. A key objective is to introduce "RegionDial-Bench" to facilitate quantitative assessment of reasoning accuracy, grounding fidelity, and global-local alignment in multi-round visual dialogue.

4. **Propose a reinforcement learning framework for grounded and coherent reasoning:** The paper's core objective is to introduce RegionReasoner, an RL-optimized framework designed to:
 - **Enforce grounded reasoning**: By requiring each reasoning trace within the model's structured output (specifically, the `<think>` block) to explicitly cite corresponding reference bounding boxes. This makes the reasoning verifiable.
 - **Maintain semantic coherence**: Through a "global-local consistency reward" that extracts key objects and nouns from both global scene captions (`<scene>`) and region-level captions (`<focus>`), aligning them with the reasoning trace (`<think>`). This ensures consistency across reasoning steps and prevents semantic drift.
 - **Optimize with structured rewards**: By combining grounding fidelity (via reference citation) and global-local semantic alignment, along with standard task correctness metrics, to comprehensively shape the model's behavior.

In summary, the research is motivated by the need for VLMs to perform more robust, verifiable, and semantically coherent reasoning in multi-round visual dialogues, moving beyond single-turn or text-only paradigms by explicitly addressing spatial grounding and context consistency.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RegionReasoner is presented as a reinforcement learning (RL) framework designed for multi-round visual reasoning, building upon and extending the structured output approach of VisionReasoner (Liu et al., 2025b).

#### 3.1. Pipeline Formulation

- **Inputs and State**: At each turn *t*, the agent receives an image *I*, a textual query *q<sub>t</sub>*, an optional set of reference boxes *B<sub>ref,t</sub>* (propagated from previous turns or externally provided), and a memory *M<sub>t-1</sub>* storing structured outputs from previous turns. *B<sub>ref,t</sub>* and *M<sub>t-1</sub>* are serialized into the prompt.
- **Policy and Action Space**: RegionReasoner functions as an auto-regressive VLM policy, *π<sub>θ</sub>*, generating a structured text action *y<sub>t</sub> = (s<sub>t</sub>, f<sub>t</sub>, h<sub>t</sub>, a<sub>t</sub>)*. This action consists of four tagged blocks: `<scene>`, `<focus>`, `<think>`, and `<answer>`. Constrained decoding is applied to ensure tag schema and JSON validity for `<answer>`, while allowing natural language for other blocks.
- **Turn Update and Termination**: After decoding, the task outputs (e.g., 2D boxes or points) are parsed from *a<sub>t</sub>*, and the memory is updated (*M<sub>t</sub> = M<sub>t-1</sub> ∪ {(s<sub>t</sub>, f<sub>t</sub>, h<sub>t</sub>, a<sub>t</sub>)}*). An episode consists of *T* turns, with per-turn rewards *R(t)* aggregated across turns.

#### 3.2. RegionReasoner Model

- **Unified Perception-Reasoning Backbone**: The model extends VisionReasoner's framework for structured and verifiable trajectories in a multi-round setting. It is initialized from a large VLM backbone (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) and performs chain-of-thought reasoning in text, grounded to image regions via serialized bounding-box references.
- **Reference-Grounded Thinking**: To enhance verifiability and reduce hallucination, reasoning within the `<think>` block is required to cite evidence. When a query specifies references, *B<sub>ref,t</sub>* is encoded canonically in the prompt, instructing the model to use verbatim coordinate mentions within `<think>`. The query *q<sub>t</sub>* also includes these coordinates to align attention. This design enables automatic grounding checks and precise credit assignment. In multi-round interactions, this explicit citation promotes stable interface and temporal coherence across turns, mitigating region drift.
- **Global-Local Semantic Consistency**: To prevent divergence between global and local contexts, RegionReasoner jointly generates a global scene caption (`s<sub>t</sub>` in `<scene>`) and a localized caption tied to a reference box (`f<sub>t</sub>` in `<focus>`) before generating the reasoning trace (`h<sub>t</sub>` in `<think>`). A deterministic pipeline extracts keyword sets (lowercasing, stop-word removal, lemmatization, noun/object filter) from *s<sub>t</sub>*, *f<sub>t</sub>*, and *h<sub>t</sub>*. Asymmetric overlaps between *s<sub>t</sub>*/ *h<sub>t</sub>* and *f<sub>t</sub>*/ *h<sub>t</sub>* are computed for reward calculation. A lightweight lexicon of spatial/comparison/localization terms is also integrated to encourage explicit relational language.
- **Task Output without Extra Heads**: Detection and segmentation outputs are generated directly as JSON within the `<answer>` block, eliminating the need for task-specific heads. This design maintains a unified learning signal, attributing structural validity and geometric precision to `<answer>`, while grounding fidelity and global-local agreement are attributed to `<think>` in conjunction with `<scene>` and `<focus>`.

#### 3.3. Reward Functions

RegionReasoner is optimized using reinforcement learning with structured rewards, building on base rewards from VisionReasoner (Thinking Format, Answer Format, Non-Repeat, Bboxes IoU, Bboxes L1, Points L1). Two new multi-round objectives are introduced:
- **Reference Citation Reward (R<sub>ref</sub>(t))**: Rewards correct citation of required reference boxes *B<sub>ref,t</sub>* within *h<sub>t</sub>* and penalizes hallucinated coordinates.
- **Global-Local Consistency Reward (R<sub>cons</sub>(t))**: Aligns keyword sets between *s<sub>t</sub>* and *h<sub>t</sub>*, and *f<sub>t</sub>* and *h<sub>t</sub>*. It also includes a logic prior term *ℓ(h<sub>t</sub>)* for spatial/comparison/localization terms.
The total per-turn reward is *R(t) = R<sub>base</sub>(t) + α R<sub>ref</sub>(t) + β R<sub>cons</sub>(t)*, where each component is normalized to.

#### 3.4. Training

The policy *π<sub>θ</sub>* is optimized using GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) over multi-turn rollouts. The clipped policy objective from GRPO is used, with advantages estimated using Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE). A learned value head *V<sub>ϕ</sub>(s)* predicts state values. An entropy bonus encourages exploration, and an optional KL penalty to a frozen reference policy can be added for stability. A sliding memory *M<sub>t-1</sub>* manages context budget, and a turn-depth curriculum gradually increases the maximum number of turns during early training. Constrained decoding ensures reward well-definition.

#### 3.5. RegionDial-Benchmark Construction

- **Purpose**: To enable quantitative assessment of reasoning accuracy, grounding fidelity, and global-local alignment under iterative interaction for both detection and segmentation.
- **Data Source**: Constructed from public referring-expression datasets RefCOCO+ and RefCOCOg, which are built on the MSCOCO image backbone.
- **Procedure**: Image-wise related expressions are consolidated into dialogues. Later turns are rewritten to include explicit references to previously localized boxes (ground-truth references for training, model-predicted for testing). Eight spatial relation templates are used (adjacency, directional, containment, overlap/contact).
- **Splits**: RefCOCO+ Multi-turn (715 images, 2355 turns) and RefCOCOg Multi-turn (1,580 images, 4405 turns). Dialogue lengths range from 1 to 7 rounds.
- **Evaluation Metrics**: Per-turn AP@IoU=0.5 for detection, and per-turn generalized IoU (gIoU) for segmentation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted on the newly introduced RegionDial-Bench, a multi-round benchmark spanning detection and segmentation, derived from RefCOCO+ and RefCOCOg. RegionReasoner-7B was initialized from Qwen2.5-VL-7B.

#### 4.1. Referring Detection under Multi-round Interaction (Table 1)

- RegionReasoner-7B consistently achieved the highest average AP on both RefCOCO+ Multi-turn (80.7 AP) and RefCOCOg Multi-turn (78.2 AP).
- Compared to VisionReasoner-7B, RegionReasoner-7B demonstrated improvements of 5.9 points on RefCOCO+ and 4.6 points on RefCOCOg.
- Against Seg-Zero-7B, the gains were 7.6 points on RefCOCO+ and 7.1 points on RefCOCOg.
- The performance gains were more pronounced at later turns, indicating enhanced robustness to error accumulation. For instance, on RefCOCO+, margins over VisionReasoner-7B at R5/R6/R7 were +5.6/+11.8/+17.7 AP respectively.

#### 4.2. Referring Segmentation under Multi-round Interaction (Table 2)

- RegionReasoner-7B achieved the highest average gIoU on both RefCOCO+ Multi-turn (69.6 gIoU) and RefCOCOg Multi-turn (66.5 gIoU).
- Compared to VisionReasoner-7B, average gains were 5.3 points on RefCOCO+ and 6.6 points on RefCOCOg.
- RegionReasoner-7B also improved over SegLLM-7B by approximately 8.9 points on RefCOCO+ and 9.8 points on RefCOCOg.
- Similar to detection, the performance gap widened at deeper turns, suggesting that explicit reference citation and global-local consistency effectively mitigate error accumulation and preserve spatial fidelity as dialogue context grows.

#### 4.3. Ablation Analysis (Tables 3 and 4)

- **Effect of Reference Citation (Ref-cite)**: This component consistently boosted multi-round performance for both detection and segmentation. The largest gains were observed at later turns, implying that explicit citation of previously grounded boxes helps curb drift and avoids spurious localizations, improving cross-turn dependence.
- **Effect of Global-Local Consistency (Consist.)**: Aligning keywords between global scene descriptions and localized region captions improved the reasoning trace. This signal helped to semantically anchor the reasoning, focusing the trajectory on relevant entities and stabilizing segmentation quality, particularly in cluttered scenes or where spatial cues in the query were weak (e.g., RefCOCO+).
- **Effect of Logic Prior**: The inclusion of a lightweight lexicon for spatial/comparison/localization terms yielded small but consistent gains, predominantly at deeper turns. This encouraged explicit articulation of relations, assisting the model in resolving ambiguities between visually similar candidates.
- **Combined Signals**: The full RegionReasoner configuration, incorporating reference citation, global-local consistency, and the logic prior, delivered the strongest multi-round performance and exhibited a slower degradation with increasing turn depth, indicating better robustness against error accumulation.

#### 4.4. Depth Robustness and Single vs. Multi-round Difficulty

- All methods showed a degradation in performance with increasing turn depth, highlighting the intrinsic difficulty of multi-round reasoning where errors can compound. RegionReasoner exhibited a slower degradation rate, suggesting its mechanisms effectively limit error accumulation.
- Single-round results (Round 1) were consistently higher than their multi-round counterparts, confirming that multi-turn error propagation and robust reference management are significant challenges.

#### 4.5. Generalization to External Benchmark (V*) (Table 9)

- When evaluated on the V\* benchmark, RegionReasoner-7B achieved the strongest overall performance among models based on the Qwen2.5-VL backbone that do not employ a specialized visual-search module.
- It demonstrated substantial gains on the "Spatial" dimension (+7.9 over Qwen2.5-VL and VisionReasoner), indicating that the reference-grounded reasoning and global-local consistency rewards contribute to transferable improvements in spatial localization and visual search capabilities.

#### 4.6. Standard Single-Round REC and RES Results (Table 10)

- RegionReasoner-7B maintained strong performance in conventional single-round referring expression comprehension (detection AP) and referring expression segmentation (gIoU) on RefCOCO+ and RefCOCOg, confirming its foundational grounding capability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work addresses a core challenge in vision-language understanding by moving beyond single-shot or text-only reasoning towards interactive, multi-round visual dialogue. The proposed RegionReasoner framework and RegionDial-Bench benchmark contribute to the field in several ways:

1. **Advancement in Multi-Round Visual Reasoning**: RegionReasoner provides a method for large vision-language models to iteratively refine their understanding across multiple visual contexts. By enforcing explicit spatial grounding and global-local semantic consistency, it offers a more robust and coherent approach to multi-turn interactions compared to prior systems. The demonstrated superior performance, particularly at deeper turns, indicates a reduced susceptibility to error accumulation, which is critical for practical conversational AI systems.

2. **Enhanced Interpretability and Verifiability**: The framework's requirement for reference-grounded thinking, where reasoning traces explicitly cite bounding boxes, promotes interpretability. This allows for verification of how the model utilizes visual evidence in its decision-making, moving towards more transparent AI systems. This feature could be valuable in applications requiring explainability or auditability of reasoning processes.

3. **Introduction of a Standardized Benchmark**: RegionDial-Bench fills a gap by providing a systematically constructed multi-turn benchmark for both referring detection and segmentation. Derived from existing high-quality datasets, it offers a reliable resource for evaluating iterative reasoning, grounding fidelity, and semantic alignment without introducing synthetic or LLM-generated artifacts. This benchmark is poised to foster further research and standardized comparisons in the emerging field of multi-round visual reasoning.

4. **Novel Application of Reinforcement Learning**: The use of structured rewards, including the reference citation reward and global-local consistency reward, demonstrates how reinforcement learning can be effectively employed to shape fine-grained behaviors in VLMs. This allows for optimization of not just the final output but also the intermediate reasoning steps, leading to more stable and semantically aligned thought processes.

5. **Generalizability of Spatial Reasoning**: The results on the external V\* benchmark suggest that the improvements in spatial grounding and reasoning achieved by RegionReasoner are not limited to its training domain but generalize to new visual search tasks. This indicates the broader applicability and robustness of the architectural and training innovations.

6. **Foundation for Future Research**: RegionReasoner establishes a strong baseline for multi-round visual reasoning. The identified limitations, such as reliance on lightweight keyword extraction and hand-crafted logic priors, point to promising avenues for future research, including exploring richer relation graphs, mask-level grounding, longer dialogues, video integration, and learnable entailment-based consistency mechanisms. This work lays foundational groundwork for developing more sophisticated and context-aware interactive visual AI.
