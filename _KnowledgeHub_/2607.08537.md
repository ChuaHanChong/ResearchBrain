# Whareformer: Learning to Track What is Where in Long Egocentric Videos

> [!summary] Summary
> Whareformer introduces a learning-based framework for long-term 3D object tracking in egocentric videos, effectively addressing the "Out of Sight, Not out of Mind" (OSNOM) task by jointly reasoning about object appearance and 3D location. The model achieved an absolute improvement of +19.2% in mean Percentage of Correct Locations (mPCL) and +14.0% in IDF1 over previous state-of-the-art methods on the EPIC-KITCHENS-100 dataset.

## Problem

- Existing 2D egocentric tracking methods struggle with maintaining object permanence over long periods due to rapid viewpoint shifts, large apparent motion, and prolonged occlusions, lacking critical 3D information.
- Prior 3D tracking solutions for the "Out of Sight, Not out of Mind" (OSNOM) task rely on heuristic association strategies and fixed thresholds, limiting adaptability and failing to explicitly learn when to create new object tracks.
- The challenge involves robustly recalling 3D object locations and preserving unique identities across extended non-observation periods and appearance changes in dynamic egocentric videos.

## Method

- **Whareformer** is an online, transformer-based model that jointly reasons over object appearance (using ==DINOv2 features== and ==DenStream clustering==) and 3D location (via a temporal buffer of recent coordinates).
- It computes learnable track assignment likelihoods by embedding appearance and location distances, which are then processed by a ==transformer encoder== for contextual decision-making across all potential assignments.
- A dedicated **"New Track" (NT) token** is integrated, allowing the model to explicitly learn and decide when to initiate a new object track, rather than inferring it from exceeding heuristic cost thresholds.

## Results

- Whareformer improved mPCL by **+19.2%** and IDF1 by **+14.0%** on the EPIC-KITCHENS-100 test split, surpassing the prior state-of-the-art LMK, with consistent gains across short and long-term tracking.
- The model demonstrated strong cross-dataset generalization, achieving absolute improvements of **+27.1%** mPCL and **+18.7%** IDF1 on IT3DEgo, and **+7.1%** mPCL and **+23.6%** IDF1 on HD-EPIC without retraining.
- Ablation studies confirmed that appearance, location, and the explicit "New Track" token are crucial for performance, with a single-layer transformer encoder and DenStream providing optimal performance and efficiency for the respective components.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Learning-based association, specifically through a transformer architecture jointly considering appearance and 3D location, substantially outperforms heuristic methods for long-term egocentric object tracking.
> - The explicit, learnable "New Track" token is fundamental for robust track creation and adaptability in dynamic environments, enabling more accurate and deliberate handling of novel objects.
> - The combination of online appearance clustering (DenStream) and a temporal location buffer provides an efficient and robust memory mechanism for tracks, effectively managing evolving object states and noisy observations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses the challenge of long-term object tracking in egocentric videos, a domain characterized by dynamic camera motion, frequent occlusions, and the necessity of maintaining object permanence over extended periods. This task, formalized as "Out of Sight, Not out of Mind" (OSNOM) by Plizzari et al., requires an agent to recall the 3D locations of objects and maintain their identities even when they are out of the field of view or their appearance changes due to interactions. Replicating this human-like spatial intelligence, or the ability to maintain a mental map of "what is where," is identified as a step towards more capable autonomous agents operating in complex human environments.

Traditional 2D egocentric tracking methods often struggle with rapid viewpoint shifts, large apparent object motion, and prolonged occlusions, leading to limitations in recovering objects unseen for minutes. While specialized designs for 2D trackers exist, they are inherently constrained by the lack of 3D information, which is critical for object permanence across diverse viewpoints and extended absence.

In response, 3D tracking in egocentric videos leverages spatial information to ground objects in the physical world. Prior methods in this category, such as IT3DEgo, Ego3DT, and LMK, have explored lifting 2D objects to 3D, reconstructing scene geometry, and employing hierarchical or heuristic-based association strategies. LMK, in particular, formalized the OSNOM task and represents a previous state-of-the-art by reconstructing a static scene and associating observations using hand-crafted appearance-location costs and the Hungarian algorithm.

The current paper distinguishes itself by proposing a *learning-based* solution, Whareformer, to the OSNOM task. Previous approaches relied on fixed, manually defined thresholds and heuristic association rules, which can limit adaptability and make explicit reasoning about new track creation challenging. Whareformer departs from these by employing trainable modules that jointly reason over appearance and 3D cues, aiming to learn these associations directly from data. It also relates to general 3D tracking methods that use transformer-based models, but addresses the unique challenges of egocentric videos, which involve longer non-observation periods and significant object appearance changes. Whareformer represents an advancement by integrating learned, dynamic decision-making into the OSNOM framework, particularly through an explicit mechanism for new track creation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and efficient learning-based method for long-term 3D object tracking in egocentric videos, specifically addressing the Out of Sight, Not out of Mind (OSNOM) task. This encompasses two core requirements:
1. **Recall 3D Object Locations Over Time:** The system must accurately estimate and recall the 3D positions of objects throughout a video, even when they are temporarily outside the camera's field of view due to egomotion, object movement, or occlusion.
2. **Maintain Object Identity:** The system must preserve the unique identity of objects despite changes in their appearance caused by interactions, manipulations, or varying viewpoints and illumination.

The motivation for this work stems from several factors:
- **Replicating Human Spatial Cognition:** Humans possess an inherent ability to maintain a mental map of their surroundings, tracking multiple objects and understanding their locations even when not directly observed. Replicating this "what is where" understanding in autonomous agents is considered a fundamental step towards human-like spatial intelligence, enabling more complex interactions with the environment.
- **Limitations of Prior Work:** Existing solutions for the OSNOM task primarily rely on heuristic association strategies. These methods typically involve fixed, manually defined thresholds for matching observations to existing tracks. Such an approach can restrict the model's adaptability to diverse and complex scenarios, potentially forcing ambiguous associations or failing to generalize effectively. Additionally, the creation of new tracks in these systems is often an implicit failure case—triggered only when all match costs exceed a pre-defined threshold—rather than a deliberate, learned decision.
- **Need for Joint Reasoning:** Objects in egocentric videos undergo dynamic changes in both their visual appearance (e.g., due to interactions, occlusion, viewpoint shifts) and their 3D location. A robust tracking system needs to jointly reason over these two cues in an integrated manner, rather than treating them as separate or heuristically combined factors.
- **Generalization to Unseen Scenarios:** Heuristic-based methods may struggle to generalize to unseen objects, environments, or activities without re-tuning. A learning-based approach, trained on sufficient data, is hypothesized to learn underlying patterns that enable better generalization.
- **Robustness to Egocentric Challenges:** Egocentric videos present specific challenges such as rapid camera motion, frequent occlusions, and the need for object permanence over extended timescales (up to half an hour). The proposed method aims to explicitly address these by learning to handle complex scenarios like objects moving to previously occupied locations or reappearing from different viewpoints.

By introducing Whareformer, the authors aim to overcome these limitations through trainable modules that learn to associate observations with tracks, including an explicit mechanism to decide when to initiate a new track, thereby providing a more adaptable and generalized solution to the OSNOM task.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Whareformer is a transformer-based model designed for online, long-term 3D object tracking in egocentric videos. It jointly reasons over object appearance ("what") and 3D location ("where") to assign current observations to existing tracks or to initiate new ones.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The problem is defined as assigning a set of `N` object observations `O = {o_1, ..., o_N}` (each `o_t^n = (a_t^n, l_t^n)` consisting of an appearance descriptor `a_t^n` and a 3D location `l_t^n`) to `K` unique object tracks `T = {T_1, ..., T_K}`. Each track `T_k` maintains an appearance representation `A(T_k)` and a location representation `L(T_k)`, and the goal is to provide accurate 3D position estimates `l_t^m` for all objects, even when not visible.

#### 3.2. Representing Observations

- **Visual Appearance:** High-dimensional features are extracted using a pre-trained DINOv2 model. To focus on the object, the corresponding segmentation mask is used. Features are then L2-normalized, projected to a D=256 dimensional space via Principal Component Analysis (PCA) trained on the training set, and L2-normalized again, resulting in `a_n`.
- **3D Location:** Location estimates `l_n` are obtained by following the pipeline from, which aligns monocular depth with 3D scene reconstructions and lifts the object's center into the world coordinate frame.

#### 3.3. Representing Object Tracks

- **Track Appearance `A(T_k, t)`:** The DenStream algorithm is employed for online clustering of appearance descriptors. It maintains both persistent micro-clusters (for stable, recurring appearances) and transient micro-clusters (for newly emerging views or short-term changes). This approach efficiently approximates an infinite buffer, balancing context with computational efficiency and memory usage.
- **Track Location `L(T_k, t)`:** A temporal buffer stores the last `W` 3D locations assigned to the track, with `W` set to cover approximately one second of video. This provides robustness to noisy estimates and recent object movements.

#### 3.4. Learnable Track Assignment Likelihoods

For each observation `o_n` and existing track `T_k`, two distance measures are computed:
- **Appearance Distance `c_A_n,k`:** The minimum Euclidean distance between `a_n` and all appearance descriptors in `A(T_k, t)`.
- **Location Distance `c_L_n,k`:** The minimum Euclidean distance between `l_n` and all location descriptors in `L(T_k, t)`.
These two distances are concatenated (`[c_A_n,k, c_L_n,k]`) and then embedded into a `d`-dimensional vector `e_n,k` using a learned linear projection `g`. This `e_n,k` vector serves as a learned likelihood representation, jointly encoding appearance and spatial information.

#### 3.5. Learnable Assignment Module (Whareformer Architecture)

The core of Whareformer is a transformer-based module responsible for making assignment decisions:
- **New Track Token (NT):** An explicit `d`-dimensional `NT` token is prepended to the input sequence. This token allows the model to explicitly *learn* when to create a new track, treating it as a first-class decision rather than an implicit outcome of exceeding a heuristic cost threshold.
- **Input Sequence `S_n`:** For an observation `o_n`, the input sequence `S_n` is formed by concatenating the `NT` token with the embedded likelihood vectors for all `T` existing tracks: `S_n = [NT, e_n,1, e_n,2, ..., e_n,T]`. The model is designed to be permutation-invariant, with no positional encodings for the track tokens.
- **Transformer Encoder:** The input sequence `S_n` is passed through a transformer encoder. This allows all tokens (representing potential assignments) to attend to each other, enabling the model to contextually evaluate the suitability of assigning an observation to a specific track relative to all other tracks and the option of creating a new one. The output is a contextually-refined representation `Z_n`.
- **Track Assignment:** A linear classification head `h` is applied to each of the `T+1` output tokens from the transformer, producing logits. A softmax function converts these logits into a probability distribution `y_n` over all potential assignments (existing tracks or new track). The final assignment decision is the `argmax` of this distribution.

#### 3.6. Updating the Memory of Tracks

After an assignment decision is made:
- **Existing Tracks:** The observation's location `l_n` is appended to the track's location buffer, with the oldest entry discarded if the buffer exceeds size `W`. The appearance descriptor `a_n` is fed into the DenStream algorithm to update the track's appearance clusters.
- **New Tracks:** If a new track is initiated, its representation is initialized with `a_n` (forming the first appearance cluster) and `l_n` (starting a location buffer of size 1).

#### 3.7. Training and Inference

- **Training:** Whareformer is trained using teacher forcing with ground-truth object track IDs. Training samples consist of an observation at time `t` and a memory constructed from ground-truth tracks up to `t-1`. A cross-entropy loss is used. The paper also explores DAgger-style correction to mitigate the distribution shift between training (ground-truth tracks) and inference (predicted tracks).
- **Inference:** During online inference, for each new observation, the track assignment likelihoods are computed, the transformer input sequence is constructed, and the model predicts assignments. Conflicts (multiple observations assigned to the same track) are resolved iteratively by accepting the highest-confident assignment first.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

Whareformer demonstrated performance improvements on the OSNOM task across multiple egocentric video datasets. The model was trained on a subset of EPIC-KITCHENS-100 videos and evaluated on unseen videos from EPIC-KITCHENS-100, as well as two other distinct datasets: IT3DEgo and HD-EPIC.

#### 4.1. Performance on EPIC-KITCHENS Dataset (Test Split)

- **Overall Improvement:** Whareformer achieved an mPCL (mean Percentage of Correct Locations) of 72.2% and an IDF1 score of 84.0%. This represents an absolute improvement of +19.2% in mPCL and +14.0% in IDF1 over LMK, the prior state-of-the-art method for the OSNOM task. It also surpassed LMK-Inf (an LMK variant with infinite memory) by a notable margin (72.2% vs 60.9% mPCL, and 84.0% vs 76.6% IDF1).
- **Long-term Tracking:** The improvements were consistent across various timescales, with Whareformer maintaining higher PCL scores for both short-term (e.g., 81.8% at 1 min vs. LMK's 68.5%) and long-term tracking (e.g., 65.4% at 12 min vs. LMK's 39.9%).
- **Qualitative Results:** Qualitative examples illustrated Whareformer's ability to maintain full, consistent trajectories for objects like a mug or chopping board, whereas LMK often fragmented tracks or incorrectly associated objects.

#### 4.2. Cross-dataset Generalization

Whareformer demonstrated the ability to generalize to unseen objects and environments without retraining.
- **IT3DEgo Dataset:** On this dataset, featuring non-kitchen activities and a different recording device, Whareformer achieved 58.1% mPCL and 87.9% IDF1. This represented a substantial absolute improvement of +27.1% in mPCL and +18.7% in IDF1 over LMK, which experienced a significant performance drop compared to its tuned environment.
- **HD-EPIC Dataset:** On the challenging HD-EPIC dataset (sparse annotations, digital twinning), Whareformer obtained 41.6% mPCL and 51.6% IDF1. This resulted in a +7.1% mPCL and a more pronounced +23.6% IDF1 improvement over LMK. It also outperformed IT3DEgo, despite IT3DEgo having an advantage from an enrolment procedure.
- **Qualitative Generalization:** Further qualitative examples demonstrated Whareformer's robustness in scenarios like pick-and-place motions, motion blur, disambiguating spatially close objects, and tracking objects through cluttered environments or long room transits across HD-EPIC and IT3DEgo.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Core Components:** All three core components—appearance information, location information, and the explicit New Track (NT) token—were found to be crucial. Removing appearance data caused a significant performance collapse (34.0% mPCL, 50.5% IDF1). Excluding location also degraded performance (62.6% mPCL, 79.5% IDF1). Disabling the NT token and relying on a heuristic threshold notably reduced performance (53.0% mPCL, 69.7% IDF1), confirming its role in robust track creation.
- **Appearance Representation (DenStream):** DenStream demonstrated the highest performance (72.2% mPCL, 84.0% IDF1) while maintaining a practical balance of memory usage (220.7 MB) and runtime (322 FPS) compared to simpler or infinite-memory alternatives.
- **Location Representation (1-second window):** A one-second temporal window for location history provided the best performance and scalability.
- **Assignment Encoder Design:** A single-layer Transformer encoder yielded optimal performance (72.2% mPCL, 84.0% IDF1), outperforming models without an encoder, linear/MLP encoders, and deeper transformer variants, suggesting the benefit of contextual attention across assignments.
- **Depth Alignment:** The model showed robustness to the presence or absence of depth alignment for 3D location acquisition, with only a modest performance drop when alignment was removed.
- **DAgger Correction:** Training with DAgger-style correction improved cross-dataset generalization on IT3DEgo and HD-EPIC, indicating its utility in mitigating inference distribution shift.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces Whareformer, a learning-based framework that advances the state of the art in long-term 3D object tracking for egocentric videos, particularly for the OSNOM task.

The significance of this work can be understood through several key aspects:
- **Advancement in OSNOM Task Performance:** Whareformer provides a substantial empirical improvement over prior heuristic-based state-of-the-art methods on multiple egocentric benchmarks. This indicates progress in the core capabilities of recalling 3D object locations and maintaining object identities over extended periods, even when objects are out of sight.
- **Transition to Learning-Based Association:** By replacing hand-crafted heuristics and fixed thresholds with trainable modules, Whareformer demonstrates that learning-based association can be more effective and adaptable. This paradigm shift offers the potential for more robust performance in dynamic and unstructured environments common in egocentric vision.
- **Explicit New Track Reasoning:** The introduction of a dedicated "New Track" token allows the model to explicitly learn when to initialize a new object track. This contrasts with previous implicit or threshold-based approaches and is identified as a key contributor to the model's performance and adaptability, particularly in crowded scenes or when novel objects appear.
- **Robust Generalization Capabilities:** The model's ability to generalize effectively across diverse egocentric datasets (e.g., from kitchen scenes to offices/garages, or different recording devices) from a relatively small training set suggests that its learned representations and reasoning mechanisms capture fundamental aspects of object permanence that are not specific to particular environments or object types. This generalization capacity is crucial for real-world applications.
- **Integrated "What and Where" Reasoning:** The joint reasoning over evolving object appearance ("what") and updated 3D location ("where") within a transformer architecture enables the model to resolve ambiguities that either cue alone might struggle with, such as visually similar objects or objects moving into previously occupied locations.
- **Potential for Human-like Spatial Intelligence:** By improving the ability of autonomous agents to maintain a coherent understanding of their spatial environment and the objects within it, Whareformer contributes towards the development of more intelligent systems capable of human-like interaction and task execution in complex, dynamic settings.

The potential impact of this research extends to various applications requiring robust object permanence and spatial understanding. This includes advanced egocentric AI assistants, robotics operating in human-centric environments, augmented and virtual reality systems that need to anchor virtual content to persistent real-world objects, and assistive technologies for individuals with spatial cognition challenges. The demonstrated generalization across diverse egocentric video characteristics suggests that Whareformer or similar learning-based approaches could be foundational for future egocentric AI systems.
