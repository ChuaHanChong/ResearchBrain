# Policy Regularized Distributionally Robust Markov Decision Processes with Linear Function Approximation

> [!summary] Summary
> This research from Cornell University and Duke University introduces Distributionally Robust Regularized Policy Optimization (DR-RPO), the first provably efficient online policy optimization algorithm for robust Markov Decision Processes with linear function approximation. The method achieves sublinear regret while enabling robust learning in environments with uncertain dynamics, supporting naturally stochastic policies and scalability to high-dimensional action spaces.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning (RL) agents often degrade in performance when deployed in real-world environments due to discrepancies between training and deployment dynamics (sim-to-real gap).
- Existing robust RL algorithms, predominantly value-iteration based, struggle with scalability to large or continuous action spaces and typically yield only deterministic policies, limiting exploration and continuous control applications.
- There was a lack of provably efficient online policy optimization algorithms for robust RL settings, despite policy optimization's empirical success in standard RL.

## Method

- The authors developed new theoretical frameworks: ==policy-regularized d-rectangular Distributionally Robust MDPs (DRMDPs)== and ==Robust Regularized MDPs (RRMDPs)==, which incorporate a ==KL-divergence== term to regularize learned policies towards a reference policy.
- They designed **DR-RPO**, a ==model-free online algorithm== that uses a ==softmax-based policy update==, estimates robust Q-functions via ridge linear regression, and applies ==Upper Confidence Bound (UCB)== bonuses for optimistic exploration.
- The approach leverages ==linear function approximation==, demonstrating that robust Q-functions remain linear in feature mappings even with policy regularization, crucial for tractability.

## Results

- **DR-RPO** achieves an average suboptimality upper bound of **Õ(d²H²/√K)** (up to logarithmic factors), matching the sample efficiency of existing value-based robust RL methods like DR-LSVI-UCB.
- Empirical evaluations on a simulated off-dynamics linear MDP demonstrated DR-RPO's **superior robustness** to distribution shifts compared to non-robust baselines, performing on par with or slightly exceeding value-based robust methods.
- On the American Put Option problem, DR-RPO exhibited robust performance comparable to DR-LSVI-UCB under high perturbation levels, showcasing its effectiveness in continuous state spaces with discrete actions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dynamic programming principles and robust Bellman equations are validated for the new policy-regularized DRMDP and RRMDP frameworks, providing a theoretical foundation for iterative policy optimization.
> - Optimal policies under policy regularization are inherently stochastic and possess a tractable softmax-like closed-form solution, balancing Q-values with adherence to a reference policy.
> - The robust Q-functions maintain a linear structure in the feature space, enabling efficient approximation and scalability for complex state-action spaces.
