# Policy Regularized Distributionally Robust Markov Decision Processes with Linear Function Approximation

> [!summary] Summary
> This research from Cornell University and Duke University introduces Distributionally Robust Regularized Policy Optimization (DR-RPO), the first provably efficient online policy optimization algorithm for robust Markov Decision Processes with linear function approximation. The method achieves sublinear regret while enabling robust learning in environments with uncertain dynamics, supporting naturally stochastic policies and scalability to high-dimensional action spaces.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning (RL) agents often degrade in performance when deployed in real-world environments due to discrepancies between training and deployment dynamics (sim-to-real gap).
- Existing robust RL algorithms, predominantly value-iteration based, struggle with scalability to large or continuous action spaces and typically yield only deterministic policies, limiting exploration and continuous control applications.
- There was a lack of provably efficient online policy optimization algorithms for robust RL settings, despite policy optimization's empirical success in standard RL.

## Method

- The authors developed new theoretical frameworks: ==policy-regularized d-rectangular Distributionally Robust MDPs (DRMDPs)== and ==Robust Regularized MDPs (RRMDPs)==, which incorporate a ==KL-divergence== term to regularize learned policies towards a reference policy.
- They designed **DR-RPO**, a ==model-free online algorithm== that uses a ==softmax-based policy update==, estimates robust Q-functions via ridge linear regression, and applies ==Upper Confidence Bound (UCB)== bonuses for optimistic exploration.
- The approach leverages ==linear function approximation==, demonstrating that robust Q-functions remain linear in feature mappings even with policy regularization, crucial for tractability.

## Results

- **DR-RPO** achieves an average suboptimality upper bound of **Õ(d²H²/√K)** (up to logarithmic factors), matching the sample efficiency of existing value-based robust RL methods like DR-LSVI-UCB.
- Empirical evaluations on a simulated off-dynamics linear MDP demonstrated DR-RPO's **superior robustness** to distribution shifts compared to non-robust baselines, performing on par with or slightly exceeding value-based robust methods.
- On the American Put Option problem, DR-RPO exhibited robust performance comparable to DR-LSVI-UCB under high perturbation levels, showcasing its effectiveness in continuous state spaces with discrete actions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dynamic programming principles and robust Bellman equations are validated for the new policy-regularized DRMDP and RRMDP frameworks, providing a theoretical foundation for iterative policy optimization.
> - Optimal policies under policy regularization are inherently stochastic and possess a tractable softmax-like closed-form solution, balancing Q-values with adherence to a reference policy.
> - The robust Q-functions maintain a linear structure in the feature space, enabling efficient approximation and scalability for complex state-action spaces.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning (RL) faces a significant challenge when deployed in real-world systems: the "sim-to-real gap," where discrepancies between a training environment (source domain) and the actual deployment environment (target domain) can lead to severe performance degradation. This problem, often framed as decision-making under distribution shift, is critical for practical applications of RL.

The current work addresses this challenge through the lens of Robust Markov Decision Processes (RMDPs), a framework designed to optimize policy performance against adversarial or uncertain transition dynamics. Within RMDPs, two primary formulations exist:
- **Distributionally Robust Markov Decision Processes (DRMDPs):** These optimize policies against the worst-case transition dynamics within a defined "uncertainty set" around a nominal transition kernel.
- **Robust Regularized Markov Decision Processes (RRMDPs):** These replace the hard uncertainty set with a regularization term that penalizes deviations from the nominal transition, offering a "softer" approach to robustness.

Recent advancements in online robust RL have introduced provably efficient algorithms for DRMDPs, particularly with linear function approximation and d-rectangular uncertainty sets. These methods typically rely on optimistic estimations of the optimal robust Q-function and greedy policy updates (e.g., DR-LSVI-UCB). While effective for certain scenarios, these value-iteration based approaches often become computationally intractable in settings with high-dimensional or continuous action spaces.

This paper identifies a critical gap: policy optimization, a dominant paradigm in standard RL known for its empirical success and suitability for large or continuous action spaces (e.g., policy gradient, actor-critic, trust-region methods), remains theoretically and empirically underexplored in robust RL. Policy optimization methods also naturally support stochastic policies and policy regularization against a reference policy, which are beneficial for exploration, continuous control, adversarial resistance, and safety-critical applications (e.g., warm-starting from a known safe policy).

This research bridges this gap by developing the first provably efficient online policy optimization algorithm for RMDPs with linear function approximation. It extends the theoretical understanding of robust RL to policy-based methods, demonstrating that they can achieve comparable sample efficiency and regret bounds to their value-based counterparts while offering distinct practical advantages.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this work stems from the limitations of existing robust RL algorithms, particularly their inability to scale effectively to complex environments and their lack of support for stochastic policies and policy regularization. The authors explicitly state the open question: **"Is it possible to design provably efficient policy optimization algorithms for online RMDPs with linear function approximation?"**

The key objectives are to:
- **Introduce new theoretical frameworks:** Define "policy-regularized d-rectangular DRMDPs and RRMDPs," which impose constraints on both transition dynamics (for robustness) and policies (for exploration, stochasticity, and adherence to reference behavior).
- **Develop a novel online algorithm:** Design Distributionally Robust Regularized Policy Optimization (DR-RPO), a model-free online policy optimization method.
- **Provide theoretical guarantees:** Prove that DR-RPO can learn robust policies with sublinear regret and polynomial suboptimality bounds, matching the performance of value-based approaches.
- **Validate empirically:** Demonstrate the effectiveness and robustness of DR-RPO across various simulated environments.

The motivation is multifaceted:
- **Bridging the sim-to-real gap:** Robust policies are essential for RL deployment in real-world systems where environmental dynamics are uncertain.
- **Addressing scalability issues:** Value-based greedy policies struggle with large or continuous action spaces, a common feature in many practical applications. Policy optimization offers a natural solution.
- **Incorporating practical benefits of policy optimization:** Policy regularization allows for stochastic optimal policies, which are often desirable for exploration or continuous control, and enables warm-starting or fine-tuning from safe or prior policies. This connects RL to probabilistic inference and can improve stability.
- **Advancing theoretical understanding:** Extending provably efficient algorithms to policy-based robust RL expands the theoretical landscape of the field.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of this work is the development of the Distributionally Robust Regularized Policy Optimization (DR-RPO) algorithm, built upon novel policy-regularized RMDP frameworks.

#### 3.1. Policy-Regularized RMDP Frameworks

The authors introduce two new frameworks: policy-regularized d-rectangular DRMDP and RRMDP. These frameworks modify the standard robust RL objective by adding a Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence term, $D_{KL}[\pi(·|s)∥π_{ref}(·|s)]$, to the reward function. This regularization term encourages the learned policy to stay close to a predefined "reference policy" ($\pi_{ref}$), with $\eta$ controlling the strength of this adherence. This modification leads to "soft" Bellman optimality equations, which balance maximizing rewards with maintaining proximity to the reference policy. The paper formally proves that dynamic programming principles (Bellman equations) still hold under these doubly constrained settings, and that the optimal policies are stochastic and take a softmax-like closed form: $\pi^*(a|s) \propto \pi_{ref}(a|s) \exp(\eta \tilde{Q}^*(s,a))$.

#### 3.2. Linear Function Approximation and Robust Q-function Linearity

To scale to large state-action spaces, the paper adopts the d-rectangular linear MDP formulation, where transition kernels and reward functions are linear in a known feature mapping $\phi(s,a) \in \mathbb{R}^d$. A key methodological step is demonstrating that the robust Q-functions ($\tilde{Q}^{\pi,\rho}$ for DRMDP, $\tilde{Q}^{\pi,\sigma}$ for RRMDP) remain linear in $\phi(s,a)$ even with policy regularization. This linearity is crucial for tractable optimization.
- For **DRMDPs**, this relies on a dual formulation of the infimum over the uncertainty set, simplified by a "fail-state assumption" (a state where reward is zero and transitions are self-loops). This assumption ensures that a minimum value in the value function can be set to zero, simplifying the dual problem. The robust Q-function then takes the form $\tilde{Q}(s,a) = \langle\phi(s,a), \theta_h + \nu\rangle$.
- For **RRMDPs**, a similar dual formulation from prior work (Tang et al., 2025) and the fail-state assumption confirm the linearity of the robust Q-function.

#### 3.3. DR-RPO Algorithm Design

DR-RPO is a model-free online algorithm that operates iteratively across K episodes:
1. **Policy Update (Softmax):** At each step $h$ of episode $k$, the current policy $\pi_h^k$ is updated using a softmax rule: $\pi_h^k(a|s) \propto \pi_{ref,h}(a|s) \exp(\eta \tilde{Q}_{h}^{k-1}(s,a))$. This balances exploitation of current Q-value estimates with adherence to the reference policy.
2. **Interaction and Data Collection:** The agent executes the updated policy $\pi_h^k$ in the nominal environment, collecting a trajectory $(s_h^k, a_h^k, r_h^k, s_{h+1}^k)$.
3. **Q-function Estimation (Ridge Regression):** In a backward pass (from $H$ to $1$), the algorithm estimates the components of the robust Q-function (specifically, $\nu$ in $\tilde{Q}(s,a) = \langle\phi(s,a), \theta_h + \nu\rangle$). This is done by solving a ridge linear regression problem using accumulated historical data.
 - For DRMDP, it involves estimating factor distributions $z_h^k(\alpha)$ and then finding $\nu_{h,i}^k = \max_{\alpha} \{z_{h,i}^k(\alpha) - \rho\alpha\}$.
 - For RRMDP, it directly estimates $\nu_{h,i}^k = E_{s\sim\mu_{0,h,i}} [\tilde{V}_{h+1}^k(s)]_\sigma$.
4. **Optimistic Exploration (UCB Bonus):** To encourage exploration of less-visited state-action pairs and maintain optimism, an Upper Confidence Bound (UCB) bonus $\Gamma_h^k(s,a)$ is added to the estimated Q-function. This bonus is constructed based on the uncertainty in the linear regression estimates.
5. **Value Function Update:** The robust value function $\tilde{V}_h^k(s)$ is then computed from the estimated $\tilde{Q}_h^k(s,a)$ using the policy-regularized Bellman equation.

This iterative process ensures that the algorithm continually refines its policy while exploring the state-action space efficiently.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents both theoretical guarantees and empirical validations for the DR-RPO algorithm.

#### 4.1. Theoretical Findings

- **Dynamic Programming Principles:** The work confirms that dynamic programming principles, specifically robust Bellman equations, hold for the newly introduced policy-regularized d-rectangular DRMDP and RRMDP frameworks. This provides a solid theoretical foundation for the iterative algorithm design.
- **Stochastic Optimal Policies:** Under policy regularization, the optimal policies are shown to be inherently stochastic and possess a tractable closed-form solution (softmax policy), directly balancing Q-values with the reference policy. This contrasts with many prior robust RL methods that yield deterministic policies.
- **Linearity of Robust Q-functions:** The paper demonstrates that the robust Q-functions maintain a linear structure in the feature mapping $\phi(s,a)$, even with policy regularization, enabling efficient approximation using linear methods.
- **Average Suboptimality Bound:** DR-RPO achieves an average suboptimality upper bound of $\tilde{O}(d^2 H^2 / \sqrt{K})$ (up to logarithmic terms) for both DRMDP and RRMDP settings, assuming a uniform exploration condition. This result demonstrates polynomial sample complexity and is explicitly stated to match the performance of existing value-based greedy iteration methods in robust RL, specifically the DR-LSVI-UCB algorithm. The additional logarithmic factor of $\text{log vol}(A)$ in the bound is attributed to the softmax policy update rule.

#### 4.2. Empirical Results

DR-RPO's performance was evaluated against several baselines (non-robust LSVI-UCB, robust DR-LSVI-UCB, non-robust policy optimization methods OPPO and Optimistic NPG, and a uniform reference policy) on two distinct environments:

- **Simulated Off-dynamics Linear MDP:**
 - DR-RPO consistently demonstrated superior robustness to distribution shifts compared to non-robust baselines (LSVI-UCB, OPPO, Optimistic NPG), especially at high perturbation levels.
 - Its robustness was on par with or slightly exceeded that of the value-based robust baseline, DR-LSVI-UCB.
 - DR-RPO significantly outperformed the uniform reference policy, indicating effective policy optimization.
 - Similar strong performance was observed for both DRMDP and RRMDP settings under varying uncertainty levels ($\rho$) and regularization parameters ($\sigma$).
 - Ablation studies confirmed that DR-RPO's robustness is consistent across different factor distributions, reference policies, and policy optimization step sizes ($\eta$), with more robust reference policies and higher $\eta$ generally leading to better performance.

- **American Put Option Problem:**
 - In this continuous state space problem with two discrete actions, DR-RPO again exhibited more robust performance than the uniform reference policy and LSVI-UCB.
 - It achieved robustness comparable to DR-LSVI-UCB, particularly under high perturbation levels in the price-up probability.
 - These results further corroborate DR-RPO's ability to learn policies that generalize well under distributional shifts in both DRMDP and RRMDP formulations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work makes a significant contribution to the field of robust reinforcement learning by developing the first provably efficient policy optimization algorithm for online RMDPs with linear function approximation. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Bridging a Critical Gap:** The paper successfully bridges the gap between the established empirical success and practical advantages of policy optimization in standard RL and the theoretical demands of provable efficiency in robust RL. This opens up new avenues for designing robust agents in complex, real-world scenarios.
- **Enhanced Applicability:** By demonstrating that policy optimization can achieve comparable theoretical guarantees (sublinear regret) to value-based methods while addressing their limitations, DR-RPO expands the applicability of robust RL. It is particularly well-suited for problems with high-dimensional or continuous action spaces, where greedy value iteration is intractable.
- **Flexible Policy Design:** The introduction of policy regularization allows for the learning of naturally stochastic optimal policies. This is valuable for exploration, continuous control tasks, and scenarios where a degree of inherent randomness is desired. Furthermore, regularization against a reference policy provides a mechanism for incorporating prior knowledge, warm-starting policy learning, or ensuring adherence to safety-relevant baselines, which is crucial for applications in healthcare or autonomous driving.
- **Theoretical Advancements:** The paper's rigorous theoretical analysis, confirming the linearity of robust Q-functions and the validity of dynamic programming principles under policy regularization, extends the foundational understanding of robust RL. The tight regret bounds further solidify the theoretical standing of policy optimization in this domain.
- **Practical Implications:** The empirical results across diverse environments (simulated linear MDP and American put option) demonstrate DR-RPO's practical effectiveness in mitigating performance degradation due to distribution shifts. This provides a strong impetus for researchers and practitioners to consider policy optimization approaches for building robust and reliable RL systems for real-world deployment.

In conclusion, this research not only provides a novel and provably efficient algorithm but also significantly broadens the methodological toolkit available for tackling robust decision-making under uncertainty, particularly in settings that demand the flexibility and scalability offered by policy optimization.
