# PhyCritic: Multimodal Critic Models for Physical AI

> [!summary] Summary
> PhyCritic introduces a multimodal critic model trained with a two-stage Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) pipeline, leveraging self-referential finetuning to enhance its ability to evaluate and reason about physical AI tasks. The model achieves 68.0% accuracy on the new PhyCritic-Bench for physical judgment, outperforming existing open-source models by over 12 percentage points, and also improves the base model's physical reasoning capabilities.

## Problem

- Existing multimodal critic models lack sufficient "physics awareness" to accurately evaluate responses in physical AI tasks, often failing to distinguish physically plausible from impossible reasoning.
- Current Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) struggle with nuanced physical correctness, causal validity, and adherence to environmental constraints in complex, interactive scenarios.
- A general framework for multimodal critics optimized for physical AI was missing, leading to unreliable evaluations for embodied AI systems.

## Method

- A two-stage Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) pipeline is proposed, starting with a 'Physical Skill Warmup' to build foundational perception and reasoning, followed by 'Self-Referential Critic Finetuning'.
- During finetuning, the critic model is explicitly instructed to first generate its own internal reasoning and prediction for a given problem before evaluating candidate responses, grounding its judgment on this self-prediction.
- A new benchmark, **PhyCritic**-Bench, was introduced for rigorous evaluation, alongside a high-quality critic training dataset derived from diverse robotics and embodied datasets.

## Results

- PhyCritic-7B achieved state-of-the-art open-source performance on the PhyCritic-Bench with **68.0%** accuracy, surpassing baselines like Eagle-2.5-8B by 12.0 percentage points.
- The model demonstrated improved physical reasoning as a policy, achieving **63.9%** accuracy on CosmosReason1-Bench and strong performance on CV-Bench (**79.7%** average, **83.9%** 3D score) and EgoPlanBench2 (**42.3%**).
- PhyCritic also generalized effectively to general-domain judging, outperforming its Qwen2.5-VL base model by +4.1 and +1.9 points on VL-RewardBench and Multimodal-RewardBench, respectively, while being highly data-efficient (4,058 training samples).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A self-referential learning framework, where a critic first solves the problem itself before judging, significantly enhances the accuracy and physical consistency of multimodal evaluations.
> - The two-stage RLVR pipeline effectively separates foundational skill acquisition from critic judgment training, leading to complementary improvements in physical reasoning and evaluation capabilities.
> - Training a model to be a physics-aware critic also improves its performance as a policy for solving physical tasks, demonstrating a synergistic relationship between evaluation and problem-solving skills.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of PhyCritic is situated at the intersection of several rapidly evolving fields within AI: Physical AI, Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), Multimodal Critic/Reward Models, and Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR).

**Physical AI** has emerged as a critical frontier for AI systems, moving beyond passive perception to tasks requiring visual understanding, physical commonsense, spatial reasoning, and action-centric decision-making in complex environments. This paradigm is crucial for applications such as 3D perception, robot-centric interaction understanding, and safety-critical domains like autonomous driving. Unlike traditional AI tasks, physical AI demands an interpretation of multi-view observations, understanding object affordances, reasoning about causal dynamics, and assessing hypothetical actions in real or simulated physical settings. Current MLLMs, while powerful, often struggle with the nuanced physical correctness and causal validity required for these tasks.

**Multimodal Critic Models** (also known as reward or judge models) are systems designed to evaluate the outputs of other AI models, providing preferences, scores, and justifications. These critics are essential for open-ended evaluation and for aligning model-generated responses with human preferences. Existing critic models, however, are predominantly trained and evaluated on general visual domains like image captioning or visual question answering. They often lack the "physics awareness" necessary to distinguish between visually plausible but physically impossible reasoning, or to correctly assess adherence to temporal, spatial, and dynamic constraints inherent in physical AI tasks. Early efforts, such as DriveCritic for autonomous driving, have highlighted the need for domain-specific judgment capabilities, but a general framework for physical AI remained largely underexplored.

**Reinforcement Finetuning (RLVR)** has significantly advanced the capabilities of MLLMs. By leveraging verifiable ground-truth supervision, RLVR-style methods have improved reasoning in areas like multimodal mathematics, science, perception, and video understanding. More recently, RLVR has been extended to multimodal critic finetuning, enhancing judgment accuracy in image-grounded tasks. PhyCritic extends this trend by systematically applying RLVR to develop physical critics that evaluate responses involving perception, action, and planning in physical contexts, simultaneously enhancing the model's ability for physical reasoning.

PhyCritic fills a critical gap by proposing a novel class of multimodal critics specifically optimized for physical AI. It addresses the limitations of existing critics by incorporating physics-aware reasoning and self-referential grounding, aiming to produce judgments that reflect physical truth rather than just linguistic coherence. This work represents a significant step towards more reliable and robust evaluation of embodied and interactive AI systems, which is crucial for their deployment in real-world scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind PhyCritic stems from the recognized inadequacy of existing multimodal critic models for evaluating responses in physical AI tasks. While MLLMs have made rapid progress, their ability to assess the physical correctness, causal validity, and adherence to environmental constraints in complex interactive scenarios remains limited.

The **key objectives** of this research are:

1. **Develop a physics-aware multimodal critic:** The primary goal is to create a critic model, named PhyCritic, that can reliably evaluate multimodal responses involving physical perception, causal reasoning, and action/plan assessment. This critic must be capable of discerning physically plausible from impossible reasoning.
2. **Ensure grounded, stable, and physically correct evaluations:** The model should produce judgments that are not only accurate but also deeply rooted in an understanding of physical laws and real-world interactions. This implies moving beyond superficial linguistic or visual cues.
3. **Introduce a novel self-referential critic learning framework:** The authors propose that a strong physical critic should emulate human expert judges by first "solving the problem itself" before evaluating others' responses. This intuition leads to the design of a self-referential training mechanism.
4. **Construct a dedicated benchmark for physical AI critics:** To rigorously evaluate the performance of physical critics, the paper aims to introduce PhyCritic-Bench, a new benchmark explicitly targeting multimodal critic models in physical AI domains.

The overarching **motivation** is to bridge the gap between general-purpose multimodal critics and the specialized requirements of physical AI. Reliable evaluation is foundational for the development and alignment of robust physical AI systems. Without critics capable of understanding and assessing physical truth, it becomes challenging to measure, diagnose, and improve the reasoning and planning capabilities of MLLMs in embodied environments. By enabling critics to ground their decisions in their own physical understanding, the authors aim to foster more consistent and interpretable verdicts that drive progress in this critical domain.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PhyCritic introduces a novel, two-stage Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) pipeline designed to optimize multimodal critic models for physical AI. The approach emphasizes developing intrinsic physical perception and reasoning capabilities before focusing on critique.

#### 3.1. Two-Stage Training Pipeline

The training pipeline starts from a base Vision-Language Model (VLM) and proceeds in two distinct stages:

- **Stage 1: Physical Skill Warmup with RLVR:** This initial stage aims to endow the base VLM with foundational physical perception and reasoning abilities. The model is fine-tuned using vanilla Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) on a small set of physical-related question-answer pairs (𝑄, 𝐴_𝑄). The reward function for this stage is a simple accuracy reward: `r = I( ˆ𝐴_pred(𝑄) = 𝐴_𝑄)`, where `ˆ𝐴_pred` is the model's prediction and `𝐴_𝑄` is the ground-truth answer. This stage acts as a warm-up, establishing a solid understanding of physical contexts before more complex critic tasks are introduced.

- **Stage 2: Self-Referential Critic Finetuning:** Building upon the warmed-up model, this stage focuses on developing the critic's judgment capacity. The model is trained on complete tuples `(𝑄, 𝐿_𝐴, 𝐿_𝐵, 𝐴_𝑄, 𝑃)`, where `𝐿_𝐴` and `𝐿_𝐵` are candidate responses, `𝐴_𝑄` is the ground-truth answer to `𝑄`, and `𝑃` is a binary preference label indicating the superior response. The core idea is that the critic first generates its own internal reasoning and prediction (`ˆ𝐴_pred`) for the given question, and then explicitly references this self-prediction when evaluating the candidate responses.

#### 3.2. Self-Referential Critic Finetuning Details

During Stage 2, the model (`𝜋_𝜃`) performs two concurrent tasks:

1. **Self-Prediction:** The model generates its own internal prediction (`ˆ𝐴_pred`) and associated reasoning (`<pred_think>`) in response to the multimodal prompt `𝑄`.
2. **Preference Judgment:** Acting as a critic, the model then produces its preference prediction (`ˆ𝑃_pred`) for the pair of candidate responses (`𝐿_𝐴`, `𝐿_𝐵`), explicitly grounding its evaluation on its previously generated `ˆ𝐴_pred`. This process is guided by a specific critic prompt.

**Reward Design:** The total reward (`𝑟_total`) for Stage 2 is a weighted sum of three components:

- **Accuracy Reward (`𝑟_acc`):**
 - **Self-Prediction Reward (`𝑟_sp`):** `𝑟_sp = I(ˆ𝐴_pred = 𝐴_𝑄)`. This component encourages the VLM to be a reliable problem solver by rewarding correct self-predictions against the ground-truth answer.
 - **Critic Reward (`𝑟_crit`):** `𝑟_crit = I(ˆ𝑃_crit(𝑄, 𝐿_𝐴, 𝐿_𝐵) = 𝑃)`. This component evaluates the model's judgment capability by comparing its predicted preference with the golden preference.
 - `𝑟_acc` is a weighted sum: `𝛼_sp * 𝑟_sp + 𝛼_crit * 𝑟_crit`.
- **Format Reward (`𝑟_form`):** This reward ensures the critic's output adheres to the predefined self-referential structure (e.g., presence of `<pred_think>`, `<pred>`, `<think>`, and `\boxed{}` tags). It assigns 1.0 for full adherence, 0.5 for partial, and 0 otherwise.
- The total reward is `𝑟_total = 𝑟_acc + 𝑟_form * 𝛼_form`.

**Optimization Algorithm:** Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) is employed for fine-tuning. GRPO is a policy-gradient method that computes advantages by comparing multiple sampled trajectories within a group, eliminating the need for a learned value network as in PPO.

**Critic Prompt:** A rigorous critic prompt template is designed (Table 1 in the paper). It explicitly instructs the model on evaluation criteria (truthfulness, visual groundedness, logical validity, efficiency, and clarity of reasoning) and mandates the self-referential process: first generate its own reasoning and prediction (`<pred_think>`, `<pred>`), then use this as a reference for comparison (`<think>`), and finally provide a decision (`\boxed{}`).

#### 3.3. Critic Training Dataset

To facilitate training, a high-quality dataset is constructed from diverse robotics and embodied datasets: RoboVQA, BridgeData V2, HoloAssist, and AgiBot World. Questions are adapted from Cosmos-Reason1, focusing on perception, reasoning, and causality. Candidate responses are collected from seven diverse MLLMs (proprietary and open-source). Ground-truth preference labels are generated by GPT-4o, which verifies each response against the true answer, creating pairs of one preferred and one rejected response. The final dataset comprises 3,258 samples.

#### 3.4. PhyCritic-Bench

A new benchmark, PhyCritic-Bench, is introduced to evaluate multimodal critic models in physical AI domains. It consists of 225 evaluation samples covering robotics tasks (from RoboVQA, BridgeData V2, HoloAssist, AgiBot, RoboFail) and autonomous driving (from LingoQA). Preference labels are constructed using a pipeline similar to JudgeBench, where GPT-4o verifies responses and identifies correct/incorrect pairs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

PhyCritic demonstrates significant performance improvements across various benchmarks, validating its two-stage RLVR pipeline and self-referential finetuning.

#### 4.1. SOTA Open-Source 7B/8B Critic on Physical Judgment

On the newly introduced **PhyCritic-Bench**, PhyCritic-7B achieves an overall accuracy of 68.0%, outperforming all open-source 7B/8B baselines. This includes a +12.0 point gain over Eagle-2.5-8B (56.0%), +16.4 over Qwen2.5-VL-7B (51.6%), +13.3 over RoboBrain2.0-7B (54.7%), and +16.9 over Cosmos-R1-7B (51.1%). PhyCritic-7B shows strong performance across various sub-suites, achieving best or tied-best results on AgiBot (78.8%), HoloAssist (65.5%), and RoboVQA (86.7%), and ranking second on Bridge-v2 (65.6%). Its strong generalization to unseen subdomains like RoboFail (57.4%) and LingoQA (60.0%) is also notable, indicating robust judgment capabilities for embodied tasks.

#### 4.2. From Physical Critic to General-Domain Judging

Despite being exclusively tuned in physical contexts, PhyCritic generalizes effectively to general-domain judging scenarios. On **VL-RewardBench** and **Multimodal-RewardBench**, it outperforms its Qwen2.5-VL base model by +4.1 and +1.9 points overall, respectively, showing consistent gains across general, hallucination-related, and reasoning-related judgments. This suggests that the physical critic training process, which focuses on physical perception, planning, and action evaluation, fosters transferable capabilities for broader multimodal judging.

#### 4.3. Improved Physical Reasoning as a Policy

PhyCritic not only excels as a critic but also enhances the model's performance as a policy for handling physical tasks. On **CosmosReason1-Bench**, PhyCritic-7B achieves the best open-source accuracy (63.9%), surpassing even Cosmos-R1-7B (63.0%), which was trained on millions of in-domain distilled reasoning traces and RL finetuning. This indicates that the critic training process further strengthens the model's problem-solving capabilities.
Furthermore, on **CV-Bench**, PhyCritic-7B secures the second-best average (79.7%) and the best 3D score (83.9%), demonstrating improved spatial grounding and cross-view consistency. On **EgoPlanBench2**, it ranks second overall (42.3%), with top-two performance across all domains, highlighting robust egocentric planning capabilities.

#### 4.4. Data Efficiency

The two-stage RL pipeline of PhyCritic requires only 80 + 300 RL steps, utilizing a total of 4,058 training samples. This demonstrates remarkable data efficiency compared to approaches that rely on millions of supervised traces, showcasing the power of verifiable physical QA and self-referential critic signals.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies

- **Two-stage RL is necessary and complementary:** Ablations reveal that the full two-stage pipeline significantly outperforms individual stages or different RL strategies. Stage 1 (physical RL warmup) primarily improves physical reasoning (+7.5 on CosmosReason1-Bench) with limited critic gains. Stage 2 (self-referential critic finetuning) substantially strengthens critic capability (+14.4 on PhyCritic-Bench) and further enhances reasoning (+2.1 on CosmosReason1-Bench). This confirms that Stage 1 builds foundational skills, while Stage 2 consolidates judgment consistency and generalization.
- **Self-referential critic finetuning drives the gains:** Removing the explicit self-referential process (`no self-refer.`) reduces PhyCritic-Bench performance from 68.0% to 64.4% (-3.6%). Removing the self-prediction reward `R_sp` (`no r_sp`) results in a drop to 65.8% (-2.2%). These findings underscore the importance of instructing the model to ground its evaluations in its own reasoning.

#### 4.6. Analysis

- **Better self-predictions imply better judgments:** Chi-square tests demonstrate a strong positive association between the correctness of PhyCritic’s self-prediction and its subsequent judgment quality. This association is further strengthened by self-referential critic finetuning, supporting the "solve before judging" principle.
- **Robustness of reward weight hyperparameters and critic prompt design:** Additional analyses in the appendix show that PhyCritic's performance is relatively robust to variations in the self-prediction reward weight (𝛼_sp). Moreover, detailed evaluation criteria in the critic prompts are crucial for shaping and regularizing PhyCritic’s reasoning, indicating that building reliable physical AI critics requires explicit and structured guidance.
- **PhyCritic for best-of-N sampling and downstream policy training:** PhyCritic effectively serves as a test-time scaling judge for best-of-N sampling, consistently improving the base policy model's performance. It also acts as an effective reward signal for guiding downstream policy training via DPO, achieving substantial gains over baselines.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

PhyCritic represents a significant advancement in the evaluation and development of physical AI systems. Its contributions have several important implications:

1. **Enabling Reliable Evaluation of Physical AI:** By introducing a multimodal critic model specifically designed for physical AI, PhyCritic provides a crucial tool for accurately assessing the perception, causal reasoning, and planning capabilities of MLLMs in embodied environments. This will allow researchers and developers to rigorously benchmark and compare models, identify weaknesses, and track progress in a domain where traditional evaluation metrics often fall short.
2. **Advancing Physics-Aware AI:** The explicit incorporation of "physics awareness" through its two-stage RLVR pipeline and self-referential finetuning marks a step towards AI systems that understand and adhere to physical laws, rather than merely mimicking superficial correlations. This is vital for deploying AI in safety-critical applications like autonomous driving and robotics, where physical correctness is paramount.
3. **Improving AI Reasoning and Policy Learning:** The finding that PhyCritic not only judges well but also improves the underlying model's physical reasoning capabilities (as a policy model) is profound. It suggests a synergistic relationship where the act of learning to critique enhances the model's internal problem-solving skills, leading to more robust and generalized reasoning. This opens new avenues for self-improvement in AI systems.
4. **Data Efficiency in Training:** PhyCritic's ability to achieve strong performance with a relatively small dataset (4,058 samples) compared to approaches requiring millions of supervised traces highlights its data efficiency. This makes advanced critic training more accessible and resource-friendly.
5. **New Benchmark for Physical AI Critics:** The introduction of PhyCritic-Bench provides a standardized, challenging benchmark tailored to the unique requirements of physical AI evaluation. This will facilitate focused research and stimulate innovation in this specialized area.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The primary limitation acknowledged by the authors is the requirement for ground-truth answers for the multimodal prompts during the self-referential critic finetuning. While this is often verifiable in physical-world feedback, it restricts applicability in fully open-ended scenarios. Future work could explore methods for self-verification or meta-judging strategies to replace explicit accuracy rewards, potentially leading to fully autonomous critic self-improvement. Additionally, extending PhyCritic towards multi-round critic self-refinement could leverage iterative feedback to further enhance self-generation.

In conclusion, PhyCritic paves the way for a new generation of physics-aware multimodal AI evaluation systems, fostering more reliable, interpretable, and ultimately more capable AI in physically grounded domains.
