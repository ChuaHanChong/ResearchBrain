# Vlaser: Vision-Language-Action Model with Synergistic Embodied Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed VLASER, an open-source Vision-Language-Action model, and a Vlaser-6M dataset to enhance embodied reasoning. The study demonstrated VLASER's state-of-the-art performance on embodied reasoning benchmarks and revealed that in-domain multimodal data is critical for successful transfer to robot control, significantly outperforming models trained only on out-of-domain data.

## Problem

- A gap existed in directly connecting upstream VLM-based reasoning with downstream VLA policy learning for robot control.
- There was an incomplete understanding of which specific multi-modal data streams or capabilities are most effective for improving downstream VLA models.
- The direct application of general VLMs to embodied agents was constrained by the domain differences between internet data and task-specific robot interaction data.

## Method

- Developed VLASER, a Vision-Language-Action model, which extends an InternVL3 VLM backbone with an action expert module for low-level robot control using a flow-matching-based approach.
- Constructed **Vlaser**-6M, a 6-million sample dataset comprising curated and newly generated data for embodied grounding, general/spatial reasoning, planning, and specific in-domain data for downstream VLAs.
- Employed a two-stage training recipe: vision-language pre-training on embodied reasoning data, followed by vision-language-action fine-tuning on robot-specific datasets to optimize the action expert and VLM.

## Results

- VLASER achieved state-of-the-art performance across 12 embodied reasoning benchmarks, increasing the average score for the 2B model from 15.2 to 45.3 and for the 8B model from 22.3 to 51.3 compared to its InternVL3 base.
- On downstream closed-loop robot tasks, VLASER fine-tuned with all in-domain data (VLASER-All 2B) significantly outperformed baselines, achieving average success rates of **65.1%** on WidowX tasks and **67.5%** on RoboTwin tasks.
- Models fine-tuned only on out-of-domain embodied reasoning data showed no clear improvement over the base InternVL3 on downstream VLA tasks, in contrast to the substantial gains from in-domain data.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Performance on general embodied reasoning benchmarks does not strongly correlate with success rates in closed-loop robot control for specific robot embodiments.
> - In-domain multimodal data (general QA, grounding, spatial intelligence from robot environments) significantly improves VLA policy learning by mitigating visual observation domain shift.
> - A diverse combination of in-domain data types further enhances transfer learning for VLA policy development.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Embodied Artificial Intelligence (AI) seeks to equip agents with the ability to perceive, comprehend, and interact within physical environments. This necessitates robust capabilities in perception, language understanding, embodied reasoning, and effective control, collectively defining the paradigm of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. The development of foundational models that integrate strong reasoning and control is considered an important step towards achieving general-purpose embodied AI.

Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have emerged as candidates for enhancing embodied agents by providing perception generalization and reasoning abilities. This has led to the development of embodied VLMs focusing on grounding, planning, and spatial reasoning. Concurrently, a significant research direction involves extending VLMs into VLAs for direct robot control. While some approaches have demonstrated the utility of co-training VLAs with web data for generalization in robot manipulation, there remains an incomplete understanding of which specific multi-modal data streams or capabilities are most effective for improving downstream VLA models.

This research addresses a specific gap: the direct connection between upstream VLM-based reasoning and downstream VLA policy learning. Previous work has often focused on enhancing either VLM reasoning or VLA control in isolation, or on integrating advanced VLMs into VLA models without a systematic investigation into the transfer effectiveness of VLM pre-training for VLA fine-tuning. The paper aims to construct a VLM specifically enhanced for embodied reasoning and then investigate its impact on VLA models, particularly exploring the critical data streams required for improving VLA performance. It also touches upon the reliance of traditional navigation and manipulation on planning-based control, underscoring the foundational importance of grounding, planning, and spatial understanding for embodied agents.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to bridge the identified gap between upstream VLM-based reasoning and downstream VLA policy learning. The researchers aim to achieve this through several specific objectives:

1. **Develop a Foundational Vision-Language-Action Model**: Introduce Vlaser, a Vision-Language-Action Model, designed to integrate high-level reasoning capabilities with low-level control for embodied agents. This model is intended to serve as a foundational VLM for the embodied AI domain.
2. **Construct a High-Quality, Million-Scale Embodied Dataset**: Create a comprehensive dataset, Vlaser-6M, by curating, reorganizing, and annotating public datasets, as well as generating new data. This dataset is designed to encompass a broad spectrum of embodied reasoning tasks, including general embodied Question Answering (QA), visual grounding, spatial intelligence, and task planning, alongside in-domain simulation data. This aims to provide a robust data foundation for enhancing embodied reasoning.
3. **Achieve State-of-the-Art Embodied Reasoning Performance**: Leverage the Vlaser-6M dataset to train Vlaser and achieve high performance across various embodied reasoning benchmarks, demonstrating its generalization capabilities in both open-loop inference and closed-loop control scenarios.
4. **Systematically Investigate VLM-to-VLA Adaptation**: Conduct an investigation into how different VLM initializations, particularly those enhanced with various types of embodied reasoning data, affect the efficiency and success rates of supervised VLA fine-tuning for robot control.
5. **Provide Insights into Domain Shift Mitigation**: Based on the systematic analysis, offer novel insights into reducing the domain gap between large-scale internet pre-training data and embodied-specific policy learning data. This is motivated by the observation that despite advancements in VLMs, their direct application to embodied agents remains constrained, partly due to the differences between general internet data and task-specific robot interaction data.
6. **Answer Critical Questions on Data Effectiveness**: Determine which multi-modal data streams and abilities are most critical for improving downstream VLA models, specifically focusing on the contributions of different types of embodied reasoning data (grounding, planning, spatial understanding) to end-to-end robot control performance.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Vlaser integrates embodied reasoning with end-to-end robot control. The methodology comprises a two-component model structure, a comprehensive data engine, and a two-stage training recipe.

#### 3.1. Model Structure

The Vlaser architecture consists of two main components:
- **VLM Backbone**: Vlaser is built upon InternVL3 (Zhu et al., 2025), leveraging its capabilities in multimodal and linguistic tasks. The study focuses on 2B and 8B parameter sizes for computational efficiency in robotics. It uses InternViT (Chen et al., 2024) as the vision encoder, coupled with Qwen2.5-1.5B and Qwen2.5-7B LLMs (Qwen et al., 2025). The emphasis is on embodied common-sense reasoning and end-to-end robot control, rather than general multimodal tasks.
- **Action Expert**: This component extends the VLM backbone with capabilities for low-level robot control. It utilizes a flow-matching-based approach (Lipman et al., 2023a) for action prediction, integrated into a LLaVA-like vision-language structure. The action expert shares self-attention mechanisms with the language model. Robot state is encoded as a state token, and noised actions are encoded as action tokens. Non-causal attention is employed for the VLA stream. During inference, actions are generated by denoising from random noise based on image observations, language instructions, and current robot state. The action horizon (H) is set to 4, and 10 integration steps are used for inference efficiency.

#### 3.2. Vlaser Data Engine

The Vlaser-6M data engine is foundational for the model's embodied reasoning. It is composed of curated, reorganized, and annotated public datasets, along with newly generated data, totaling 6 million data samples.
- **Embodied Grounding Data (1.8M)**: This includes 1.5 million high-quality question-answer pairs for 2D grounding (bounding boxes and center points, normalized to). Sources include RoboPoint, ShareRobot, Pixmo-Points, Paco-LaVIS, and RefSpatial. An additional 300k point and bounding box annotations are generated from segmentation masks in the SA-1B dataset. This generation involves converting masks to bounding boxes/points, filtering by IoU (0.9 threshold), initial captioning with BLIP-2, and subsequent refinement with Qwen2.5-VL-7B.
- **General and Spatial Reasoning Data (1.7M)**: The dataset integrates 1.2 million RoboVQA pairs (from RoboVQA, Robo2VLM, RoboPoint, RefSpatial, OWMM-Agent) for general robotic visual question answering. An additional 500k samples are dedicated to spatial intelligence, sourced from SPAR, SpaceR-151k, and VILASR. This is augmented with 100k manually annotated spatial understanding samples generated from 3D scene datasets (ScanNet, ScanNet++, CA-1M, ARKitScenes), using spatio-temporal scene graphs to generate questions about layout, object relationships, and geometric properties.
- **Planning Data (0.4M)**: This component includes 400k training data samples designed to enhance planning capabilities, encompassing language-based planning (Alpaca-15k-Instruction, MuEP) and multimodal tasks. Data with detailed reasoning processes from WAP are incorporated. Planning tasks in the Habitat simulator are initialized using LLaRP annotations, and an LLM agent (gpt-4o) rolls out tasks, recording successful instruction-action-feedback trajectories. Egocentric video datasets (EgoPlan-IT, EgoCOT) are also integrated.
- **In-Domain Data for Downstream VLAs (2M)**: To facilitate end-to-end policy learning, 2 million multimodal QA pairs are generated specifically to align with embodied reasoning contexts and interactive environments. This data is sourced from simulation platforms: SimplerEnv (Google Robot, WidowX) and RoboTwin (dual-arm Aloha-AgileX). The QA pairs cover embodied grounding, spatial intelligence, and general VQA for robot states. Generation employs Qwen2.5VL-7B. A post-processing filtering pipeline using Qwen2.5VL-32B as an LLM-as-a-judge scores samples (1 to 3), with approximately 10% of low-scoring (score 1) samples filtered out.

#### 3.3. Training Recipe

Vlaser employs a two-stage training recipe:
- **Vision-Language Pretraining**: The InternVL3 backbone is fine-tuned (Supervised Fine-Tuning - SFT) on embodied-related datasets, including those for grounding, planning, and spatial intelligence. This phase uses an auto-regressive language modeling loss (L_lm). All parameters, including those of the LLM, vision-language projector, and visual encoder, are kept trainable. Training configurations include an LLM sequence length of 16384, a patch size of 448, a peak learning rate of 2e-5, and a global batch size of 128 for 1 epoch.
- **Vision-Language-Action Finetuning**: For robot policy learning, an additional action expert module is incorporated and optimized on robot-specific datasets. The action expert is conceptually similar to a mixture-of-experts (MoE) architecture. The VLA optimization loss (L_vla) targets matching a denoising vector field. This phase involves sampling action chunks from robot episodes and flow-matching timesteps. All parameters of both the VLM and the action expert are optimized. Training configurations for VLA fine-tuning include an LLM sequence length of 384, an image size of 448, a global batch size of 1024 for 10 epochs, and VLM/Action Expert peak learning rates of 5e-5.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents findings across two main areas: embodied reasoning capabilities and performance on downstream closed-loop robot tasks, supported by ablation studies.

#### 4.1. Performance on Embodied Reasoning Capability

- **Benchmarks**: Vlaser was evaluated on 12 benchmarks covering embodied QA (ERQA, Ego-Plan2), planning (Where2place, Pointarena), embodied grounding (Paco-Lavis, Pixmo-Points), spatial intelligence (VSIBench, RefSpatial-Bench, MMSIBench), and closed-loop simulation (VLABench, EmbodiedBench on ALFRED and Habitat).
- **Comparison**: Vlaser was compared against state-of-the-art closed-source models (e.g., GPT-4o, Claude-3.7-Sonnet, Gemini-2.5-Pro) and numerous open-source MLLMs across small (2B-3B) and medium (7B-8B) scales, including InternVL3, Qwen2.5VL, Embodied-R1, and RoboBrain2.0.
- **Key Results (Table 1)**: Vlaser demonstrates substantial improvements over its base InternVL3 models, achieving state-of-the-art performance across the embodied reasoning benchmarks. For instance, the average score for the 2B model increased from 15.2 to 45.3, and for the 8B model, from 22.3 to 51.3. This performance enhancement is attributed to the quality and effectiveness of the Vlaser-6M dataset.
- **Scaling Behavior**: Vlaser-2B exhibited superior performance on simpler point grounding tasks requiring direct, short answers. In contrast, Vlaser-8B demonstrated stronger results on more complex tasks such as multi-step planning and closed-loop simulation, which may benefit from chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning.
- **Overall Competence**: Vlaser achieved the highest overall average score among comparable models, outperforming embodied-specific VLMs like RoboBrain2.0 and Embodied-R1 by over 10%. This indicates a balanced and robust set of capabilities across multiple dimensions of embodied intelligence.

#### 4.2. Performance on Downstream Closed-Loop Robot Tasks

- **Evaluation Platforms**: Experiments were conducted on SimplerEnv (WidowX Robot Tasks and Google Robot Tasks) and RoboTwin (Aloha-AgileX Robot Tasks), which are designed to reliably reflect real-robot outcomes.
- **Ablation Studies on Data Types**: A key aspect was the systematic comparison of fine-tuning different Vlaser-2B variants: InternVL3-2B (base), Vlaser-OOD (fine-tuned only on out-of-domain embodied reasoning data), and Vlaser-QA, Vlaser-Spatial, Vlaser-Grounding (fine-tuned on respective in-domain data types), and Vlaser-All (fine-tuned on all in-domain data).
- **WidowX Robot Tasks (Table 2)**: Vlaser-All (2B) achieved an average success rate of 65.1%, outperforming strong baselines like π_0 (54.9%) and SpatialVLA (42.7%).
- **Google Robot Tasks (Table 3)**: Vlaser-All (2B) showed competitive performance, achieving 76.2% average success rate for Visual Matching and 59.0% for Variant Aggregation tasks.
- **RoboTwin Tasks (Table 4)**: Vlaser-All (2B) secured an average success rate of 67.5%, significantly surpassing InternVL3-2B (55.8%) and RDT-1B (36.8%).
- **Key Finding on Domain Shift**:
 - Vlaser-OOD, despite being trained on general embodied reasoning benchmarks, showed "no clear improvement" or remained comparable to the InternVL3-2B baseline on downstream VLA tasks across all three robot benchmarks. This indicates an absence of a strong positive correlation between performance on common embodied reasoning benchmarks and closed-loop control success rates for specific robot embodiments.
 - In contrast, all models fine-tuned with in-domain data (Vlaser-QA, Vlaser-Spatial, Vlaser-Grounding, and Vlaser-All) exhibited "significant performance gains." This improvement is primarily attributed to the "mitigation of visual observation domain shift."
 - Combining all three types of in-domain data (Vlaser-All) resulted in further performance enhancements, suggesting that diverse in-domain multimodal data (general QA, grounding, spatial intelligence) effectively facilitates transfer learning for VLA policy learning.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies on Hyperparameters (Table 5)

- Ablations on predicted action length, execute action length, and flow-matching sampling steps confirmed the robustness of the primary finding. The limited benefit of Vlaser-OOD and the significant gains from in-domain data (Vlaser-QA) remained consistent across different hyperparameter settings.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of embodied AI through several avenues:

- **Open-Source Foundational Model and Dataset**: The introduction of Vlaser, an open-source vision-language model, along with its modular data generation pipeline, training/evaluation code, and the comprehensive Vlaser-6M dataset, provides resources for reproducibility and future research in embodied reasoning and robot control.
- **Systematic Analysis of Data Effectiveness**: The study provides a systematic investigation into the types of vision-language pre-training data that most effectively contribute to downstream VLA policy learning. This offers practical insights for developing task-aware data streams that can bridge the performance gap between internet-scale pre-training and embodied-specific fine-tuning.
- **State-of-the-Art Performance**: Vlaser establishes new state-of-the-art results across a range of embodied reasoning benchmarks and achieves competitive or superior performance on simulation-based robot evaluation tasks, demonstrating its robustness and generalization capabilities in both open-loop and closed-loop scenarios.

The potential impact of this work primarily stems from its findings regarding the **domain gap** between generic embodied reasoning benchmarks and real-world robot control. The observation that out-of-domain embodied reasoning data does not directly translate to significant improvements in downstream VLA performance, while in-domain data does, highlights a critical challenge. This suggests that current embodied perception and reasoning benchmarks, often relying on internet-sourced visual data, may not adequately capture the specifics of a robot's egocentric viewpoint and interaction context.

This revelation emphasizes the urgent need for:
- **Developing Alignment Techniques**: Future research should focus on creating techniques to bridge the representational domain gap between the diverse viewpoints of internet datasets and the specific perspectives of robot embodiments.
- **Re-evaluating Data Strategies**: The findings advocate for a data collection strategy that prioritizes, or at least significantly integrates, in-domain, robot-specific multimodal data tailored to the target embodiment and task. This could lead to more efficient and effective transfer learning for VLA policy development.
- **Advancing VLA Model Construction**: Vlaser, with its balanced capabilities across reasoning and control, serves as a strong foundational model for future embodied AI systems. The insights gained from this study can guide the construction of more effective embodied VLMs by informing which multimodal abilities and data types are most impactful for closed-loop task completion.

While the study's limitation of not including real-robot experiments is acknowledged, the use of SimplerEnv, a benchmark with reported strong real-to-sim correlations, suggests the findings have relevant implications for practical robotics.
