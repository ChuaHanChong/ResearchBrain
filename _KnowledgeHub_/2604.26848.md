# STARRY: Spatial-Temporal Action-Centric World Modeling for Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed STARRY, an action-centric world modeling policy for robotic manipulation that jointly denoises future spatial-temporal latents and action sequences with Geometry-Aware Selective Attention Modulation. The system achieved 93.82% average success in simulation and 70.8% in real-world bimanual tasks, outperforming previous approaches by improving spatial-temporal coordination and object alignment.

## Problem

- Existing reactive Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies often struggle with tasks needing anticipation of future robot-object geometric evolution and contact states.
- Many predictive world models prioritize visually plausible futures but lack explicit encoding of action-relevant spatial constraints, limiting their direct utility for robotic control.
- Policies typically rely on globally shared spatial representations, making it difficult to prioritize decision-critical regions over background context for fine-grained interactions.

## Method

- **STARRY** proposes an ==action-centric world model== that jointly denoises future ==spatial-temporal latents== and ==action sequences== over a unified horizon.
- Integrates multi-view RGB-D, 3D end-effector trajectories, and language instructions into a unified ==spatial-temporal representation== via a diffusion-based model.
- ==Geometry-Aware Selective Attention Modulation (GASAM)== generates geometry-aware weights from predicted depth and end-effector positions to bias action attention toward geometrically relevant visual tokens.

## Results

- **STARRY** achieved **93.82%** Clean-setting success on RoboTwin 2.0, surpassing LingBot-VA (**92.93%**) and Motus (**88.66%**).
- Real-world bimanual: **70.8%** average across six tasks, a **31.7 pp** improvement over π0.5 in multi-step execution.
- Ablations confirm full spatial-temporal foresight and GASAM each contribute; GASAM provides substantial gains for spatially sensitive tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Effective manipulation world models must be inherently action-centric and geometry-grounded, identifying not only how a scene evolves but also where future interactions are critical for action generation.
> - Jointly denoising future spatial-temporal latents and action sequences over the same horizon fosters action-centric foresight by explicitly aligning prediction with action generation.
> - Modulating attention based on predicted end-effector geometry provides a direct mechanism to bridge 2D visual tokens and 3D physical control, enhancing precision in spatially demanding tasks.
