# STARRY: Spatial-Temporal Action-Centric World Modeling for Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed STARRY, an action-centric world modeling policy for robotic manipulation that jointly denoises future spatial-temporal latents and action sequences with Geometry-Aware Selective Attention Modulation. The system achieved 93.82% average success in simulation and 70.8% in real-world bimanual tasks, outperforming previous approaches by improving spatial-temporal coordination and object alignment.

## Problem

- Existing reactive Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies often struggle with tasks needing anticipation of future robot-object geometric evolution and contact states.
- Many predictive world models prioritize visually plausible futures but lack explicit encoding of action-relevant spatial constraints, limiting their direct utility for robotic control.
- Policies typically rely on globally shared spatial representations, making it difficult to prioritize decision-critical regions over background context for fine-grained interactions.

## Method

- **STARRY** proposes an ==action-centric world model== that jointly denoises future ==spatial-temporal latents== and ==action sequences== over a unified horizon.
- Integrates multi-view RGB-D, 3D end-effector trajectories, and language instructions into a unified ==spatial-temporal representation== via a diffusion-based model.
- ==Geometry-Aware Selective Attention Modulation (GASAM)== generates geometry-aware weights from predicted depth and end-effector positions to bias action attention toward geometrically relevant visual tokens.

## Results

- **STARRY** achieved **93.82%** Clean-setting success on RoboTwin 2.0, surpassing LingBot-VA (**92.93%**) and Motus (**88.66%**).
- Real-world bimanual: **70.8%** average across six tasks, a **31.7 pp** improvement over π0.5 in multi-step execution.
- Ablations confirm full spatial-temporal foresight and GASAM each contribute; GASAM provides substantial gains for spatially sensitive tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Effective manipulation world models must be inherently action-centric and geometry-grounded, identifying not only how a scene evolves but also where future interactions are critical for action generation.
> - Jointly denoising future spatial-temporal latents and action sequences over the same horizon fosters action-centric foresight by explicitly aligning prediction with action generation.
> - Modulating attention based on predicted end-effector geometry provides a direct mechanism to bridge 2D visual tokens and 3D physical control, enhancing precision in spatially demanding tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic manipulation research has increasingly focused on Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which aim to integrate perception, natural language understanding, and action generation for general-purpose embodied agents. Models such as RT-2 and π0.5 leverage large-scale vision-language pretraining to acquire semantic understanding and instruction-following capabilities. However, a common limitation in many existing VLA policies is their reactive nature; they primarily rely on current or recent observations without explicitly modeling future robot-object interaction states. This approach can restrict their performance in tasks demanding precise spatial-temporal coordination, such as handling a mug or performing a handover, where anticipating object geometry, contact regions, and end-effector trajectories is critical.

To address these limitations, a growing body of work has incorporated world models into policy learning, enabling the prediction of future observations or latent video states. These world-model-enhanced policies aim to provide temporal foresight for action generation. Nevertheless, existing predictive models often face a challenge: their future representations may be optimized for perceptual fidelity or temporal consistency rather than for direct action relevance. This can lead to a discrepancy between the quality of the prediction and its utility for control, as visually plausible futures may still fail to highlight action-critical spatial constraints (e.g., object handles, contact surfaces, obstacles). Furthermore, many existing methods fuse spatial information through globally shared representations, making it difficult to selectively focus on decision-critical regions within the visual input.

STARRY positions itself within this landscape by seeking to bridge the gap between predictive world modeling and effective action generation. It addresses the identified limitations by proposing an approach that explicitly aims for *action-centric* and *geometry-grounded* world models. This involves not only predicting scene evolution but also identifying and emphasizing future interaction regions that are crucial for generating precise actions, thereby converting predictive capabilities into more effective control.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robotic manipulation policy that can effectively reason about future spatial-temporal interactions and geometric constraints, thereby overcoming limitations observed in existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. The motivation stems from two identified shortcomings in current predictive manipulation policies:

1. **Mismatch between Prediction and Control:** Existing world models often generate future representations optimized for perceptual fidelity or temporal consistency rather than direct action relevance. This can create a disconnect between the predictive capacity of the world model and its utility for generating effective actions. For instance, visually accurate future predictions may still lack the specific spatial details (e.g., contact points, grasping regions, clearance areas) required for precise manipulation, leading to failures in tasks demanding fine-grained spatial-temporal coordination.
2. **Lack of Targeted Spatial Reasoning:** Many policies fuse spatial information globally, making it challenging to differentiate and prioritize decision-critical regions from background context. In contact-rich or spatially constrained tasks, local spatial-temporal relations are paramount, and a global representation may dilute the attention on crucial interaction areas around the end-effector or target objects.

Based on these observations, the authors propose that effective world models for manipulation should be **action-centric** (predicting future interactions critical for action generation) and **geometry-grounded** (incorporating explicit geometric guidance).

The key objectives and contributions of STARRY are detailed as follows:

- **To propose STARRY, an action-centric spatial-temporal world model:** This involves designing a world-model-enhanced action-generation policy that jointly models future spatial-temporal dynamics and action sequences. The goal is to align future prediction directly with action generation by denoising future spatial-temporal latent variables and actions within a unified generative process, thereby enabling more effective language-conditioned robotic manipulation.
- **To introduce Geometry-Aware Selective Attention Modulation (GASAM):** This mechanism aims to integrate geometric guidance into the action generation process. GASAM converts predicted depth and end-effector geometry into token-aligned weights. These weights are then selectively injected into the action attention branch, allowing the policy to emphasize decision-critical visual regions relevant to the predicted future geometry, while maintaining the overall video modeling and semantic understanding.
- **To validate the effectiveness of STARRY through empirical evaluation:** This involves conducting experiments in both simulated and real-world robotic environments. The validation aims to demonstrate that the proposed spatial-temporal modeling and geometry-aware modulation enhance execution reliability and success rates in spatially and temporally demanding manipulation tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

STARRY (Spatial-Temporal Action-centric Representation and Reasoning PolicY) is a world-model-enhanced action-generation policy designed for language-conditioned embodied manipulation. The system observes multi-view RGB-D images, camera parameters, current robot pose, and a language instruction, with the objective of generating a future action sequence. STARRY achieves this through a multi-module architecture that jointly models future spatial-temporal latent variables and geometry-aware modulation weights.

#### 3.1. Architecture Overview

STARRY comprises four main modules:
- **Understanding Expert:** Responsible for semantic grounding from visual-language inputs.
- **Spatial-Temporal (ST) World Model:** Predicts future spatial-temporal latent variables.
- **Geometry Expert:** Predicts future geometric states, such as depth and end-effector positions.
- **Action Expert:** Generates action sequences, modulated by geometry-aware weights.

The core design principle is the joint denoising of future spatial-temporal latents and action sequences over the same horizon, coupled with the selective application of geometry-aware weights to the action attention branch.

#### 3.2. ST World Model

The ST World Model aims to learn a unified future spatial-temporal latent variable, z, which integrates appearance, motion, and geometry. This contrasts with models that primarily predict future observations or latent video states optimized for perceptual consistency.

- **Input Construction:** Given observations within a temporal window, the system constructs a unified spatial-temporal representation. This involves processing multi-view RGB images, depth observations, and 3D end-effector trajectories. End-effector trajectories are projected into each camera view using camera intrinsics and extrinsics. These components (RGB frames, depth, and projected trajectories) are then composed into a structured representation (x_t), forming a sequence of past spatial-temporal states (X_t0:t).
- **Future Latent Prediction:** The sequence X_t0:t is encoded into video tokens. These video tokens are combined with historical actions to predict future latent variables (z_t+1:t+H) using a diffusion-based model (f_STθ). This latent variable is designed to capture structured spatial-temporal information, including scene evolution, end-effector motion, and geometric constraints, serving as conditioning for action generation.

#### 3.3. Geometry-Aware Selective Attention Modulation (GASAM)

GASAM addresses the challenge of aligning 2D visual tokens with 3D physical control, which is often implicitly inferred by standard attention mechanisms. It provides explicit metric spatial guidance to the action attention branch.

- **Geometry Prediction:** To provide stable geometric guidance, a separate **Geometry Expert** (g_ϕ) is introduced. Conditioned on current observations, video tokens, historical actions, and diffusion timesteps, this expert predicts future depth sequences (D_hat_t+1:t+H) and end-effector positions (p_hat_t+1:t+H). This prediction supplies a stable and explicit estimate of future geometry, complementing the implicit latent modeling of the ST World Model.
- **Geometry-Aware Weight Construction:** Using the predicted future geometry, GASAM recovers 3D points from the predicted depth map. For each visual location (token), it computes its 3D distance to the predicted end-effector position. These distances are then transformed into geometry-aware weights (w_t+1:t+H) using a monotonically decreasing mapping and aligned to the video-token grid. These weights highlight spatial regions that are geometrically salient for action execution.
- **Modulation of Action Attention:** The generated geometry-aware weights (w) are used to selectively modulate the action-to-video attention within the Action Expert. Specifically, a logarithmic term of the weights is added to the attention scores (query-key dot product) before the softmax operation. This biases the attention mechanism from action queries toward geometrically relevant visual tokens. Crucially, this modulation is applied *only* to the action attention branch, preserving the original spatial-temporal modeling and visual-language understanding within other parts of the network.

#### 3.4. Training and Data

STARRY's training involves a multi-stage approach and a hierarchical dataset organization.

- **Initialization:** The ST World Model is initialized from Wan, and the Understanding Expert from Qwen-VL. The Action Expert utilizes a Transformer architecture identical to the ST World Model but with action-specific parameters. The Geometry Expert is implemented as a diffusion-aligned Transformer.
- **Diffusion Objective:** The policy jointly models spatial-temporal observations and actions using a diffusion objective that predicts velocity fields. Separate loss weights and diffusion timesteps are used for observations (L_obs) and actions (L_action) to account for modality differences.
- **Geometry Supervision:** The Geometry Expert is supervised using losses for depth prediction (L_depth), end-effector pose prediction (L_pose), and consistency between predicted geometry-aware weights and target weights (L_weight). Target weights are derived from ground-truth spatial distances between the end-effector and scene points. The Geometry Expert is initially trained independently before joint fine-tuning.
- **Training Pipeline:**
 1. **Spatial-temporal pretraining:** The ST World Model and Understanding Expert are trained on large-scale video and multi-modal data (e.g., Ego4D, Ego-Dex) to acquire general spatial-temporal dynamics and semantic representations.
 2. **Action and geometry learning:** The Geometry Expert and Action Expert are introduced and jointly trained under the diffusion objective, enabling the mapping from spatial-temporal representations to actions with geometric constraints.
 3. **Joint finetuning:** An end-to-end finetuning stage is performed with GASAM enabled, optimizing all modules concurrently for enhanced spatial-temporal consistency and action generation.
- **Dataset:** A hierarchical dataset (L1-L6) is utilized, progressively introducing different types of supervision: from large-scale web/egocentric videos (semantic and temporal), to synthetic/interaction data (geometry), and finally to multi-robot and target-robot datasets (action learning and task-specific adaptation).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The effectiveness of STARRY was evaluated through both simulated and real-world experiments, along with ablation studies.

#### 4.1. Evaluation in Simulation Environment

STARRY was benchmarked on RoboTwin 2.0, a dataset comprising 50 bimanual manipulation tasks under both Clean and Randomized settings. The model was compared against several baselines, including π0.5, X-VLA, Motus, and LingBot-VA.

- **Overall Performance:** STARRY achieved average success rates of 93.82% under the Clean setting and 93.30% under the Randomized setting. These figures surpassed the next best baseline, LingBot-VA (92.93% Clean / 91.55% Randomized), and Motus (88.66% Clean / 87.02% Randomized).
- **Performance on Challenging Tasks:** The improvements were particularly notable in tasks requiring precise temporal coordination or local geometric reasoning. For instance, in "Handover Mic," STARRY achieved 100% / 99% success, significantly outperforming Motus (78% / 63%). On tasks like "Hanging Mug," "Turn Switch," and "Press Stapler," which rely on fine-grained alignment and contact location, STARRY obtained 69% / 72%, 85% / 89%, and 100% / 100% success rates, respectively. The improvement on "Hanging Mug" was over 30 percentage points higher than the previous best result, indicating that GASAM contributes to focusing action generation on geometry-relevant regions.

#### 4.2. Real-World Experiments

To assess practical deployment performance, STARRY was tested on the ARX R5 bimanual robot platform on three representative two-stage bimanual manipulation tasks: "Hand Over Vegetables," "Tidy Up Room," and "Wash Baby Bottle." Performance was compared against π0.5.

- **Average Success:** STARRY achieved an average success rate of 70.8% across the six real-world evaluations (three tasks, two stages each), compared to 42.5% for π0.5.
- **Multi-Step Task Performance:** The performance gain for STARRY was more pronounced in the more challenging Stage 2 settings of each task, with an average improvement of 31.7 percentage points. This suggests that STARRY's advantage extends beyond stabilizing short-horizon actions, providing stronger spatial-temporal modeling for multi-step task execution.
- **Qualitative Observations:** In "Hand Over Vegetables," STARRY demonstrated more reliable bimanual handover sequences compared to π0.5, which frequently encountered grasping or transfer errors. For "Tidy Up Room," STARRY maintained better alignment of the object with the container opening during placement. In "Wash Baby Bottle," STARRY successfully managed stable grasping, execution of the shaking motion, and final placement despite the irregular bottle geometry and sensitivity to local contact.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

An ablation study was conducted to quantify the individual contributions of predictive representation types and Geometry-Aware Selective Attention Modulation (GASAM).

- **Impact of Predictive Representation:**
 - Transitioning from "action-only denoising" to "appearance-only prediction" resulted in substantial performance gains (e.g., from 64.96% to 85.80% in Randomized settings), confirming the utility of future appearance prediction for temporal context.
 - Further incorporating "full spatial-temporal modeling" (ST) beyond appearance-only prediction yielded additional improvements (e.g., from 85.80% to 88.82% in Randomized settings). This suggests that explicitly modeling trajectory evolution and spatial geometry provides critical constraints for manipulation tasks beyond mere appearance changes.
- **Impact of GASAM:**
 - Enabling GASAM consistently improved performance across all representation variants. The most significant gain was observed for "action-only denoising" (+10.92% on Randomized, +9.08% on Clean), indicating that geometry-aware weights provide an effective spatial prior even without extensive future prediction.
 - When combined with full ST modeling, GASAM further enhanced performance, increasing success rates from 88.82% to 93.30% on Randomized and from 90.40% to 93.82% on Clean. This demonstrates that GASAM complements spatial-temporal prediction by offering precise geometric grounding.

#### 4.4. Visualization of Future Spatial-Temporal Latents

Visualizations of decoded future predictions showed that the learned spatial-temporal latents captured coherent scene evolution, end-effector motion trends, and structured depth patterns in both simulated and real-world environments. This suggests that the ST World Model learns a representation that encodes manipulation-relevant spatial-temporal information beyond just visually plausible RGB frames.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of STARRY contributes to the field of robotic manipulation by introducing a method that enhances the integration of predictive world modeling with action generation. By emphasizing an "action-centric" and "geometry-grounded" approach, this work addresses a notable gap in existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies, where predictive representations may not be optimally aligned with the requirements for precise control.

The **significance** of STARRY lies in several aspects:

- **Improved Execution Reliability:** The experimental results in both simulated (RoboTwin 2.0) and real-world environments demonstrate substantial improvements in success rates, particularly in complex bimanual tasks requiring high spatial-temporal coordination and geometric precision. This indicates that STARRY's approach can lead to more robust and reliable robotic manipulation.
- **Methodological Innovation:** The introduction of jointly denoised future spatial-temporal latents and actions, coupled with the Geometry-Aware Selective Attention Modulation (GASAM), provides a novel mechanism for bridging the gap between high-level perception/prediction and low-level physical control. GASAM's ability to selectively modulate attention based on predicted 3D geometry is a targeted solution to the implicit 2D-to-3D reasoning challenge in many VLA models.
- **Structured Representation Learning:** The ST World Model's capacity to learn a unified spatial-temporal latent variable that integrates appearance, motion, and geometry offers a more comprehensive internal representation of future interaction states, moving beyond purely appearance-based predictions.

The **potential impact** of STARRY's approach is broad:

- **Advancements in Embodied AI:** By providing robots with a more effective way to anticipate and reason about future interactions and geometric constraints, STARRY could accelerate the development of more capable and autonomous embodied agents.
- **Applications in Robotics:** Enhanced reliability and precision in manipulation tasks could directly benefit various application domains, including:
 - **Assistive Robotics:** Enabling robots to perform delicate tasks with greater accuracy and safety in human environments.
 - **Manufacturing and Assembly:** Improving the efficiency and success rates of automated assembly lines and complex manipulation operations.
 - **Household Automation:** Allowing robots to perform a wider range of chores and interactions with household objects that require fine motor skills.
- **Future Research Directions:** STARRY's success highlights the importance of explicit geometric reasoning and action-centric prediction in world models, potentially inspiring further research into multi-modal fusion strategies and attention mechanisms that more effectively leverage 3D spatial information.

However, the authors acknowledge limitations, including the scope of real-world validation to a limited set of bimanual tasks and robot platforms. The effectiveness of GASAM also relies on accurate predicted depth and end-effector geometry, which might be challenging under highly ambiguous observations. Furthermore, STARRY's reliance on large video and vision-language backbones results in increased training costs. The authors conclude with an impact statement emphasizing that STARRY is a research-stage system and should be deployed with appropriate physical safety constraints, monitoring, and human oversight to mitigate potential risks associated with unsafe robot behaviors in unstructured environments.
