# PhysFlow: Unleashing the Potential of Multi-modal Foundation Models and Video Diffusion for 4D Dynamic Physical Scene Simulation

> [!summary] Summary
> PhysFlow integrates multi-modal foundation models and video diffusion to automate material property inference and optimize physical parameters using optical flow guidance. It enables realistic 4D dynamic physical scene simulations for diverse materials and input types, achieving human evaluation scores of 3.44 for physical-realism and 3.53 for photo-realism, outperforming prior methods.

## Problem

- Existing dynamic scene simulation methods are limited to basic material types and require manual initialization or prior knowledge of material properties.
- Current optimization losses (e.g., render loss, SDS loss) for physics-based simulation are often unstable for large motions or computationally demanding.
- Many approaches lack the flexibility for comprehensive 4D reconstruction and dynamic simulation from diverse input types like single images or general dynamic videos.

## Method

- Infers 3D Gaussian splats for scene representation, supporting reconstruction from multi-view images, dynamic videos, or single images.
- Automates material property identification and initialization by querying a ==multi-modal== foundation model (GPT-4) with an input image.
- Optimizes material parameters using ==video diffusion==-generated content and ==optical flow== guidance, employing a differentiable ==Material Point Method== (==MPM==).

## Results

- PhysFlow achieved the highest average human evaluation scores for physical-realism (**3.44**) and photo-realism (**3.53**) on real-world datasets, outperforming baseline methods (all below **3.0**).
- The method demonstrated lower End-Point Error (EPE) and Relative Error (RE) for material parameters on synthetic datasets, and recorded the lowest Energy-Constrained Motion Score (ECMS) (**3.08**) in real-world scenarios.
- It qualitatively produced deformations that closely matched ground truth and preserved sharper textures across various material types, including elastic, plastic, granular, and fluids.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Optical flow-guided optimization provides a more stable and memory-efficient alternative to render loss or SDS loss for refining material parameters, especially for large object motions.
> - Leveraging multi-modal foundation models for material property initialization significantly enhances the accuracy and physical realism of simulations compared to manual assignments.
> - The integration of 3D Gaussian splats, differentiable MPM, and video diffusion guidance creates a versatile framework for complex 4D dynamic scene reconstruction from varied inputs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper addresses challenges within the field of dynamic scene simulation in computer vision and graphics, which is critical for applications such as robotics manipulation and video generation. Existing methods for video generation can produce visually plausible results, but they often lack physical realism, leading to inaccuracies in object motion and deformation. Enhancing simulation realism requires automatically capturing diverse material properties and accurately modeling physical interactions under varying forces.

Current approaches to learning material parameters from dynamic videos, such as PAC-NeRF and GIC, integrate neural radiance fields (NeRF) or 3D Gaussian Splatting with differentiable Material Point Methods (MPM). However, these methods often face limitations. PAC-NeRF, while enabling simulation of various material types, is constrained to simpler objects and textures, resulting in lower rendering fidelity. GIC, which uses 3D Gaussian Splatting, requires multi-view images of deforming objects with known camera poses and prior knowledge of material types, thus limiting its applicability to arbitrary dynamic scene videos. PhysGaussian improves visual quality by coupling 3D Gaussian Splatting with MPM but necessitates manual initialization of material properties, which restricts its adaptability to complex dynamic scenarios.

More recent methods leverage video diffusion for motion guidance to estimate material properties. However, these are typically restricted to elastic materials and minor object movements. Furthermore, they commonly use render loss or Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) loss for parameter estimation. Render loss is noted for instability and noise in generated videos, making it less suitable for large motions, while SDS loss requires substantial computational memory.

PhysGen introduces physics reasoning using large pre-trained visual foundation models, reducing the need for manual parameter initialization. However, it is restricted to single input images, simulates objects at a fixed depth, and does not perform 4D reconstruction or dynamic simulations, thereby limiting its flexibility in complex scene simulation.

This paper positions PhysFlow as an advancement that integrates multi-modal foundation models and video diffusion to overcome these limitations. It seeks to provide a more comprehensive and flexible solution for 4D dynamic physical scene simulation by enabling automatic material property inference, handling diverse material types, and optimizing parameters efficiently for large and complex motions, all while supporting varied input types and 4D reconstruction.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to achieve realistic simulation of 4D dynamic scenes by accurately capturing diverse material properties and modeling complex object interactions grounded in physical principles. The authors motivate this work by identifying several limitations in existing simulation methods:

1. **Constraint to Basic Material Types and Limited Predictable Parameters**: Many current methods are confined to simulating basic material types with a restricted set of predictable parameters. This prevents them from accurately representing the complexity and variety of real-world materials and their behaviors. The paper aims to expand the range of accurately simulatable material types and their corresponding physical parameters.
2. **Dependence on Specific Input Requirements and Prior Knowledge**: Existing techniques often require multi-view images with known camera poses or prior knowledge of the object's material type. Such requirements limit their applicability to arbitrary real-world dynamic scene videos where this information may not be readily available. A key motivation is to develop a method that can operate with greater input flexibility and infer necessary properties automatically.
3. **Manual Material Property Initialization**: Some high-fidelity simulation methods, like PhysGaussian, require manual initialization of material properties. This manual step hinders their adaptability and scalability for diverse, complex dynamic scenarios. The authors aim for an automated approach to infer and initialize material properties.
4. **Inefficiencies and Limitations of Existing Optimization Losses**: Current video diffusion-based methods for material property estimation often employ render loss or Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) loss. Render loss is reported to be unstable and noisy, suitable only for small motions, while SDS loss demands high computational memory, particularly when applied to complex video diffusion models. The paper is motivated to find a more memory-efficient and robust guidance mechanism capable of handling large and complex object motions.
5. **Lack of Comprehensive 4D Reconstruction and Dynamic Simulation**: Methods like PhysGen, while using foundation models for physics reasoning, are restricted to single input images, fixed depth simulations, and lack the capability for full 4D reconstruction and dynamic simulations. The objective is to provide an integrated framework that allows for 4D dynamic scene reconstruction from diverse input types.

To address these motivations, the specific objectives of PhysFlow are:
- To leverage multi-modal foundation models to automatically identify material types and initialize material parameters from image queries.
- To infer 3D Gaussian splats for detailed scene representation, adaptable to multi-view images, dynamic scene videos, or single images.
- To refine these material parameters using video diffusion, integrated with a differentiable Material Point Method (MPM), guided by optical flow rather than render loss or SDS loss.
- To enable accurate prediction and realistic simulation of dynamic interactions in real-world scenarios, thereby enhancing both accuracy and flexibility in physics-based simulations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PhysFlow proposes a pipeline for 4D dynamic physical scene simulation that integrates 3D scene reconstruction, material property initialization, and physics-based parameter optimization.

**Overall Pipeline:**
The process begins with input image(s) (multi-view frames, video frames, or a single frame). First, 3D Gaussian splats are inferred for scene representation. Simultaneously, a multi-modal foundation model identifies material types and initializes parameters. These properties are assigned to the 3D Gaussian splats. Object movements are then simulated using a differentiable Material Point Method (MPM) under an applied force. A video diffusion model generates a video sequence depicting the object's motion. Optical flow derived from this video guides the optimization of material parameters. Finally, optimized parameters facilitate 4D reconstruction from various viewpoints.

**3D Scene Reconstruction:**
The method reparameterizes the 3D scene using unstructured 3D Gaussian kernels, which include centers, opacities, covariance matrices, and spherical harmonic coefficients. This representation is selected for its differentiability and memory efficiency. The specific method for generating these Gaussian splats varies based on the input type:
- **Multi-view images with known camera poses:** PGSR is utilized to construct a static 3D representation.
- **Dynamic scene video:** Grid4D is employed to generate a time-dependent 3D representation, capturing temporal evolution.
- **Single image:** Splatt3R infers the scene structure using learned priors.

**Continuum Mechanics:**
Continuum mechanics describes material motion and deformation through a time-dependent continuous deformation map. The deformation gradient (F) encodes local transformations. The framework simulates seven material types: elastic, plasticine, metal, foam, sand, Newtonian fluids, and non-Newtonian fluids. Each material type is characterized by distinct physical properties (e.g., Young’s modulus E, Poisson’s ratio ν, yield stress τ_Y, plastic viscosity η, friction angle θ_fric, fluid viscosity μ, bulk modulus κ). The simulation adheres to conservation of mass and momentum, with momentum conservation expressed via the Cauchy stress tensor and external forces. The deformation gradient F is decomposed into elastic (F_E) and plastic (F_P) components to model permanent deformations.

**Material Point Method (MPM):**
MPM combines Lagrangian particles with an Eulerian grid. Material is discretized into particles, each tracking properties like position, velocity, and deformation gradient. Particles ensure mass conservation, while the grid facilitates momentum conservation. B-spline kernel functions handle particle-grid interactions. Momentum conservation is discretized, updating grid velocities, which are then transferred back to particles to adjust their positions. The elastic deformation gradient (F_E) is updated, with adjustments for plasticity. The Moving Least Squares Material Point Method (MLS-MPM) is specifically used for its smoother interpolation, enhancing accuracy and stability during complex deformations.

**Material Property Initialization:**
To establish a physically grounded starting point for simulations, the method utilizes a large pre-trained visual foundation model, GPT-4. Given an input image and specific textual queries, GPT-4 identifies the closest material type (M), infers its density (ρ), and provides initial values for the corresponding material parameters (M_p0). This step automates the typically manual process of setting initial physical properties.

**Material Parameter Optimization with Optical Flow Guidance:**
Following initialization, material parameters are optimized to reflect realistic object interactions.
1. **Video Generation:** Given an input image and prompts, a video input V = {I_0..., I_t} is generated using various video diffusion models. The choice of model depends on the input and desired control: Stable Video Diffusion (SVD) for image-only input, CogVideoX for image and text prompts, MotionCtrl for trajectory/camera control, and MOFA-Video or Kling 1.5 Model for complex brush-stroke guided motions.
2. **Optical Flow Computation:** Optical flow U(I_t, I_t+1) between consecutive frames of the generated video is computed using RAFT to track motion.
3. **Loss Function and Optimization:** Instead of render loss or SDS loss, which have limitations (instability for large motions, high memory usage), optical flow is used to guide optimization. A flow-based loss, L_flow, is minimized:
 L_flow = Σ_t ||U(I_t, I_t+1) - Û(Î_t, Î_t+1)||²
 where Û is the optical flow derived from the simulated frames. This loss efficiently captures motion and enables optimization of material parameters (M_p = {E, ν, τ_Y, η}).
4. **Iterative Refinement:** The material parameters are iteratively optimized using the MLS-MPM framework.
5. **4D Reconstruction:** After optimizing M_p, the updated properties are integrated into the differentiable MPM framework to control the motion and deformation of the 3D Gaussian splats. The final step involves rendering the simulation from new viewpoints for 4D reconstruction.

For real-world datasets, foreground points where forces are applied are segmented using SAM2Point. All optimization tasks are performed on a single NVIDIA A800 GPU.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates PhysFlow on both synthetic and real-world datasets, demonstrating its performance in material parameter optimization and 4D dynamic scene simulation.

**Evaluation on Synthetic Dataset:**
- **Dataset:** The synthetic dataset from PAC-NeRF, comprising 9 objects made of different materials (elastic, plasticine, sand, Newtonian/non-Newtonian fluids). The 3D Gaussian splats from the first frame of GIC's dynamic reconstruction are used as simulation objects.
- **Baselines:** PAC-NeRF and GIC.
- **Metrics:** Relative Error (RE = |M_p - M̂_p|/M_p) for material parameters and End-Point Error (EPE) for optical flow deviations.
- **Results:** PhysFlow consistently achieves lower RE and EPE compared to PAC-NeRF in most cases and remains competitive with GIC. Specifically, for Newtonian fluids (Droplet, Letter), PhysFlow yields the lowest RE and EPE. For non-Newtonian fluids (Toothpaste), it delivers competitive results with low shear and bulk modulus errors. For granular material (Trophy, representing sand), the method achieves the lowest error in friction angle estimation. Qualitatively, PhysFlow produces deformations that closely match the ground truth and preserves sharper textures across various material types (Figure 2, Figure 9).

**Evaluation on Real-world Dataset:**
- **Dataset:** A comprehensive set of scenes including publicly available data (Alocasia, Carnation, Hat, Telephone from PhysDreamer; Fox from Instant-NGP; Plane from NeRFStudio; Kitchen from Mip-NeRF 360) and two self-collected scenes (Jam, Sandcastle) to cover all seven material types.
- **Baselines:** PhysGaussian, PhysDreamer, and Physics3D.
- **Metrics:** A human evaluation by 32 participants rating 36 videos on a 5-point Likert scale for "physical-realism" and "photo-realism." The Energy-Constrained Motion Score (ECMS) is also used to assess motion quality.
- **Results:** PhysFlow achieves the highest average scores for both physical-realism (3.44) and photo-realism (3.53) among all methods, significantly outperforming baselines (all below 3.0) as shown in Table 2 and Figure 6. PhysFlow also records the lowest ECMS (3.08) (Table 3), indicating more physically plausible motion. The qualitative results (Figure 5, Figure 11) illustrate that PhysFlow produces realistic and stable simulations, capturing material deformation and dynamic interactions with higher fidelity, even under larger input forces designed to induce substantial deformations.

**Ablation Study:**
- **Effectiveness of Optical Flow Guidance:** A comparison on the synthetic dataset (Table 4, Table 10, Figure 8) shows that using optical flow loss (L_flow) leads to lower RE and EPE across most material parameters (e.g., viscosity η, bulk modulus κ, yield stress τ_Y) compared to render loss (L_render) and SDS loss (L_sds). This indicates more accurate material parameter optimization and realistic motion. Additionally, L_flow performed better under large force applications and motions in real-world scenes (Figure 3, Table 5).
- **Effect of Physics Reasoning with Foundation Model (GPT-4 Initialization):** Simulations on the real-world dataset (Figure 4, Table 5, Figure 7) compare configurations with and without GPT-4 initialization and optimization. Manually defined initial properties, even with optimization, resulted in less realistic outcomes (e.g., overly rigid parameters). Simulations initialized with GPT-4 inferred values, especially when followed by optimization, achieved significantly more accurate material dynamics and visually convincing simulations, resulting in a lower ECMS. The study highlights that accurate material classification by the foundation model directly influences the object's physical response, demonstrating its importance over incorrect or generic manual assignments.

**Additional Evaluation Using Single-view Input:**
Using Splatt3R for 3D Gaussian splat reconstruction from single images and an inpainting technique for background recovery, PhysFlow demonstrated its capability to capture realistic deformations and motion trajectories guided by videos from PhysGen and Kling (Figure 10). The ECMS results on PhysGen scenes (Table 8) also show competitive performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of PhysFlow represents an advancement in the field of 4D dynamic physical scene simulation by integrating multi-modal foundation models and video diffusion with a differentiable physics engine. This approach addresses several identified limitations in existing methodologies, leading to enhanced accuracy and flexibility in simulations.

One significant impact is the **automation of material property inference and initialization**. By leveraging multi-modal foundation models like GPT-4, PhysFlow removes the requirement for manual input of material types and parameters, a common bottleneck in previous systems. This automation makes the simulation process more accessible, adaptable to diverse real-world scenarios, and reduces human effort and potential errors.

The introduction of **optical flow guidance for material parameter optimization** offers a robust and memory-efficient alternative to traditional render loss or Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) loss. This is particularly impactful for simulating large and complex object motions, where other methods often struggle with instability or high computational demands. The ability to accurately capture and optimize for such complex dynamics enhances the physical realism of the simulated scenes.

Furthermore, PhysFlow's capability for **4D reconstruction from various input types** (multi-view images, dynamic scene videos, or even single images) broadens the scope of its applicability. This flexibility allows for detailed dynamic simulations across a wider range of real-world data sources, providing a more versatile tool for researchers and practitioners.

The combined advancements contribute to **higher fidelity and physically plausible simulations** across a diverse range of material behaviors, including elastic, plastic, granular, and fluid dynamics. This has potential implications for several downstream applications:
- **Robotics**: More realistic simulation of object interactions can lead to improved training environments for robotic manipulation tasks, enabling robots to learn more robust and physically accurate behaviors.
- **Video Generation and Special Effects**: The ability to generate physically realistic dynamic content can enhance the quality and believability of synthesized videos, potentially impacting areas like movie production, virtual reality, and augmented reality experiences.
- **Engineering and Design**: Simulating material deformations with greater accuracy could aid in prototyping and testing designs in virtual environments, reducing the need for physical prototypes.

Overall, PhysFlow advances the state of the art in dynamic scene simulation by providing an integrated, automated, and robust framework that can more accurately model complex physical interactions in various real-world contexts.
