# Can We Detect Failures Without Failure Data? Uncertainty-Aware Runtime Failure Detection for Imitation Learning Policies

> [!summary] Summary
> The Toyota Research Institute developed FAIL-Detect, a framework for runtime failure detection in imitation learning robot policies that operates without requiring explicit failure data. It uses uncertainty quantification with Conformal Prediction and a novel `logpZO` score, achieving average balanced accuracies up to 78% in simulation and 72% on robot hardware with practical detection times.

## Problem

- Existing failure detection methods for robot policies, especially those learned via imitation, largely require extensive and costly datasets of explicit failure examples for training.
- Complex, long-horizon imitation learning tasks often lead to diverse, unpredictable, and 'out-of-distribution' failure modes that are difficult to anticipate or enumerate beforehand.
- Ensuring trustworthy and safe deployment of autonomous robots in safety-critical environments necessitates robust, real-time failure detection without introducing prohibitive computational latency.

## Method

- **FAIL-Detect** introduces a ==two-stage framework==: first, learning or computing a scalar score indicative of successful trajectory characteristics using only in-distribution data, and second, designing a sequential threshold.
- Proposes ==logpZO==, a novel ==flow-based density estimator==, which computes the likelihood in the latent noise space (==log p(Z_O_t')==) to identify Out-of-Distribution events indicating potential failures.
- Leverages ==functional Conformal Prediction== to construct time-varying thresholds with statistical guarantees on the false positive rate, calibrating solely on successful rollouts to adapt to changing task dynamics.

## Results

- **FAIL-Detect** achieved average best balanced accuracies of approximately **78%** in simulation and **72%** on robot hardware tasks for failure detection without using failure data.
- The novel ==logpZO== score emerged as the top performer, securing highest balanced accuracy in **10 out of 16** simulation scenarios and **8 out of 12** hardware scenarios, while being significantly faster than baselines like STAC.
- Detected failure alerts from FAIL-Detect show a strong correlation with observable physical indications of failure, providing diagnostic value for pinpointing critical moments of failure.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Learned scalar scores, particularly the novel flow-based `logpZO` method, consistently outperform post-hoc analytical metrics for discriminating between successful and failing robot executions.
> - Uncertainty quantification using time-varying Conformal Prediction thresholds is crucial for reliable failure detection, providing statistical guarantees on the false positive rate and adapting to task dynamics.
> - Framing failure detection as an Out-of-Distribution (OOD) problem, relying solely on successful demonstration data, effectively circumvents the significant practical challenge of acquiring explicit failure data.
