# From Blind Spots to Gains: Diagnostic-Driven Iterative Training for Large Multimodal Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Peking University and Shandong University developed Diagnostic-driven Progressive Evolution (DPE), an iterative framework that diagnoses and corrects specific weaknesses in Large Multimodal Models (LMMs). DPE enables stable, continual performance gains, achieving state-of-the-art results in visual math (e.g., MathVista 76.2) and hallucination mitigation (e.g., HallusionBench 74.13) with significantly less data than traditional methods.

## Problem

- Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) struggle with identifying and correcting 'capability blind spots' due to reliance on static training data and fixed recipes, leading to inefficient learning on long-tail weaknesses.
- There is a scarcity of high-quality, human-annotated multimodal reasoning data, which hinders the scalable and continuous improvement of LMMs.
- Existing self-evolving LMM frameworks often lack interpretable diagnostics, leading to 'blind' evolution and potential distribution drift, and are limited by static image sets that restrict visual diversity and cause performance plateaus.

## Method

- Introducing the ==Diagnostic-driven Progressive Evolution (DPE)== framework, an iterative 'diagnose-and-correct' loop that explicitly controls the category composition and question emphasis of training data based on identified model weaknesses.
- Implementing an ==Adaptive Diagnosis Mechanism== that provides interpretable failure attribution and capability decomposition across **12** dimensions, generating a structured report to guide subsequent data generation.
- Developing a ==Multiple Agents Questioner System== equipped with tool-use capabilities (e.g., web search, image editing) to dynamically source, filter, edit, and compose diverse, high-quality, and weakness-focused visual content and questions.

## Results

- DPE consistently achieved stable and continual performance improvements, setting new state-of-the-art records in visual math on MathVista (**76.2**) and MathVision (**53.88**), and leading HallusionBench with **74.13**, surpassing larger models like GPT-4o (**56.1** overall average) with an 8B parameter backbone (**64.39** overall average).
- The framework demonstrated exceptional data efficiency, achieving superior performance across key benchmarks (e.g., MMMU **56.44** vs **54.8**, MathVista **69.5** vs **68.8**) using only **3,000** iteratively generated samples, roughly **1/15th** of a comparative static dataset.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the diagnostic module is indispensable for maintaining stable evolution and preventing performance stagnation or regression, while image retrieval and editing tools are critical for expanding visual diversity and avoiding early performance plateaus on tasks like OCR and chart understanding.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly diagnosing a model's specific weaknesses and then generating targeted training data is crucial for stable and continuous improvement, mirroring efficient human learning processes.
> - Dynamically expanding visual context through image retrieval and editing tools, coupled with weakness-focused data generation, is essential for addressing long-tail reasoning challenges and preventing overfitting to narrow visual distributions.
> - Focusing on generating high-quality, moderately difficult samples for identified blind spots can achieve superior LMM performance with substantially less data compared to using large, untargeted static datasets.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) have demonstrated advancements in complex reasoning and decision-making, particularly with the maturation of reinforcement learning (RL) methods. Models such as GPT-5.2, Claude Sonnet 4.5, and Qwen3-VL exhibit capabilities in these areas. However, the availability of large-scale annotated data for multimodal reasoning remains a constraint for training LMMs.

To address this data scarcity, prior research has explored self-evolving training frameworks. These frameworks typically involve an iterative cycle of self-questioning and self-answering to refine model capabilities continuously. These existing self-evolving methodologies face two primary limitations. First, they often lack interpretable diagnostics, relying on heuristic signals like perplexity rather than explicit attribution of failure modes. This can lead to the evolutionary process prioritizing superficial complexity over genuine capability gaps, potentially resulting in unstable data quality or noise. Second, these methods are frequently constrained by the scarcity of visual diversity, as they often rely on static image sets. While textual queries may evolve, an unchanging visual context can limit the coverage of long-tail scenarios, leading to performance plateaus or regressions on rare or complex concepts.

This research introduces Diagnostic-driven Progressive Evolution (DPE) to address these identified limitations. DPE positions itself as a paradigm that moves beyond static data and fixed training recipes by incorporating a dynamic, targeted approach to identify and address capability blind spots. It aims to integrate a principled diagnostic mechanism and a tool-use data evolution system to achieve more stable and comprehensive LMM improvement, particularly for long-tail multimodal reasoning tasks that are not adequately covered by current self-evolving methods.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The motivation for developing DPE stems from observed limitations in current LMM training paradigms and insights from educational psychology regarding effective learning.

**Motivation:**
1. **Limitations of Current LMM Training:** Despite progress in LMMs, their training processes often rely on static datasets and fixed recipes. This approach makes it challenging to identify specific weaknesses or "blind spots" in a model's capabilities and hinders the provision of dynamic, targeted reinforcement. Existing self-evolving frameworks, while addressing data scarcity, are primarily limited by:
 - **Lack of Interpretable Diagnostics:** They often use heuristic signals (e.g., perplexity) rather than explicit failure attribution. This prevents a principled decomposition of model capabilities and can lead to the model pursuing superficial complexity rather than addressing actual gaps. The result can be unstable data quality and noise.
 - **Scarcity of Visual Diversity:** Reliance on static image sets restricts the semantic scope of training. As textual queries evolve, the immutable visual context limits the coverage of long-tail scenarios, causing performance to stagnate or decline on rare or complex visual concepts.
2. **Inspiration from Educational Psychology:** The authors draw inspiration from findings in educational psychology, which emphasize that diagnosis of errors and feedback-based correction are more effective for learning than repetitive practice. This principle suggests that a targeted "diagnose-and-correct" mechanism could improve the efficiency and efficacy of LMM training. This translates to the idea that instead of indiscriminate data expansion, prioritizing diagnosis of capability gaps can steer targeted data generation and optimization, thereby addressing the multimodal long-tail bottleneck.

#### 2.1. Key Objectives

1. **Develop a Diagnostic-driven Progressive Evolution (DPE) Framework:** To create an iterative, spiral training loop where explicit diagnosis of model weaknesses directly steers the generation of new training data and guides reinforcement. Each iteration should re-diagnose the updated model to drive subsequent targeted improvements.
2. **Incorporate Adaptive Diagnosis:** To design a diagnostic agent that analyzes model failure patterns, identifies specific weaknesses and blind spots, and uses these insights to dynamically optimize the training data mixture. This aims to create a closed loop of diagnosis, data generation, and reinforcement for targeted capability enhancement.
3. **Implement Tool-Use Data Evolution:** To move beyond static datasets and template-based text rewriting by employing a multi-agent system. This system, equipped with tools such as web search and image editing, should collaboratively source and annotate diverse visual content from external pools, enabling the construction of high-quality, weakness-focused training samples with deliberately controlled distributions.
4. **Demonstrate Scalable and Continual Gains:** To show that DPE can achieve stable, continual improvements across a range of multimodal reasoning benchmarks, particularly on challenging long-tail tasks, under open task distributions and with minimal initial data.
5. **Provide Systematic Analysis:** To quantitatively evaluate the impact of the diagnostic mechanism on training stability and the effectiveness of the unlabeled multimodal annotation mechanism in alleviating static data bottlenecks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DPE is presented as a closed-loop training framework designed to iteratively improve Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) by addressing data scarcity and long-tail coverage gaps. The framework operates through an iterative cycle of diagnosis, targeted data generation, and reinforcement-based model updating.

#### 3.1. Overall Framework

The core of DPE is an iterative process defined by three operators:
- $A_{diag}$: The diagnostic operator, which assesses the current model policy ($\pi_{\theta}^{(k)}$) to produce a structured diagnostic report ($R^{(k)}$).
- $A_{gen}$: The generation operator, which uses the diagnostic report ($R^{(k)}$) to construct a new training dataset ($T^{(k)}$).
- $A_{RL}$: The reinforcement learning update operator, which updates the model parameters from $\theta^{(k)}$ to $\theta^{(k+1)}$ using the generated training set ($T^{(k)}$) and verifiable rewards.

This sequential process ensures that training resources are aligned with identified capability blind spots, aiming to reduce instability and diminishing returns on long-tail skills.

#### 3.2. Diagnostic Mechanism

This mechanism provides an interpretable and actionable assessment of the model's current policy. It moves beyond heuristic proxies by performing explicit failure attribution and capability decomposition.

- **Capability Space:** Multimodal logical reasoning is mapped into a capability space of 12 dimensions (e.g., geometry images, medical images, statistical charts, text-intensive images, mathematical formulas, natural scenes).
- **Diagnostic Sampling and Step-aware Scoring:** At each iteration, 200 instances are sampled from a diagnostic pool. The model's responses ($\hat{y}_n$) are scored by diagnostic agents using a function $v(\cdot)$ that evaluates both reasoning steps and final results, converting them into a scalar correctness signal ($z_n$).
- **Failure Attribution and Diagnostic Summary:** The system analyzes errors within each category ($E_c$) and summarizes recurring patterns ($F_c$), such as specific OCR errors or mathematical reasoning flaws. These attributions are used to formulate executable prompts for data generation.
- **Category Proportions:** Unnormalized weights ($\tilde{\alpha}_c$) are assigned to categories based on their accuracy ($Acc_c$), which are then normalized to create a category proportion vector ($\alpha^{(k)}$) for the next generation round. This vector guides the distribution of newly generated data.
- **Structured Diagnostic Report ($R^{(k)}$):** The final report includes the category proportions ($\alpha^{(k)}$), within-category weaknesses ($\{F_c^{(k)}\}_{c=1}^C$), and actionable generation instructions ($\{H_c^{(k)}\}_{c=1}^C$) (e.g., for localization, reasoning chain length, or output format).

#### 3.3. Multiple Agents Questioner System

This system translates the diagnostic report into a collection of training samples with controlled distribution, quality, and verifiable answers. It operates with four specialized agents:

- **Category Quota Constraint:** Given a target generation budget ($M$) and the diagnostic proportions ($\alpha^{(k)}$), the system enforces hard quotas ($m_c = \lfloor M \cdot \alpha_c^{(k)} \rfloor$) for each category in the generated training set ($T^{(k)}$).
- **Planner Agent:** Translates diagnostic outputs into instructions for individual samples. For each sample $j$, it specifies the target category ($c_j$), image requirements ($req_{I_j}$), question requirements ($req_{Q_j}$), and a direction constraint ($dir_j$) based on identified weaknesses. It ensures category quotas are met.
- **Image Selector Agent:** Given image requirements, this agent obtains visual inputs from an external image pool ($P_{ext}$) and can perform editing or composition. Its capabilities include:
 - **Search:** Large-scale retrieval using keywords, category tags, and structural hints.
 - **Filter:** Quality screening for resolution, size, consistency, and readability.
 - **Edit/Compose:** Constructs targeted scenarios through cropping, overlaying text, stitching images, or fusing regions, particularly for long-tail concepts and boundary cases. This tool-use expands visual diversity beyond static datasets.
- **Question Generator Agent:** Based on the selected image ($I_j$) and planning instructions, this agent produces a question ($q_j$) and a verifiable reference answer ($a_j$), following the specified question requirements and weakness-focused directions.
- **Validation Agent:** This agent explicitly gates data quality to ensure accepted samples meet minimum standards. It checks for category consistency ($g_{cat}$), solvability and information completeness ($g_{sol}$), answer verifiability ($g_{ver}$), and format compliance ($g_{fmt}$). Only samples satisfying all checks are added to the training set, reducing noise and preventing distribution drift.

#### 3.4. LMM Training

The target LMM is optimized using Group-normalized Reinforcement Learning with Policy Optimization (GRPO).

- **GRPO Objective:** For each prompt, trajectories are generated by the old policy, and each trajectory is assigned a scalar reward. GRPO optimizes a clipped surrogate objective that includes a PPO-style clipping threshold ($\epsilon$) and KL regularization ($\beta$) against a reference policy ($\pi_{init}$).
- **Group-normalized Advantages:** GRPO utilizes trajectory-level group-normalized advantages ($\hat{A}_i$), calculated based on the reward of a trajectory relative to the mean and standard deviation of rewards within a group of trajectories.
- **Maximum-Entropy View of Learnability:** This perspective suggests that the update magnitude in GRPO is governed by within-group reward variance. This analysis implies that DPE retains moderately difficult samples to enhance learning efficiency, as the gradient for improvement is highest when the model's pass rate is around 0.5.
- **Iterative Training:** In each iteration, DPE generates and validates a dataset ($T^{(k)}$), applies difficulty-aware filtering to obtain $T_{train}^{(k)}$, and then uses GRPO to update the model. This iterative process aims to strengthen weak capabilities and continuously expand visual coverage.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluated DPE on two base models, Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct and Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct, across 11 benchmarks spanning STEM, Visual Math, OCR, Multi-image, and Hallucination tasks.

#### 4.1. Comparison with Self-evolving Methods (VisPlay)

DPE consistently demonstrated improvements compared to VisPlay, a prior self-evolving method:
- **Comprehensive Capability Enhancement:** DPE yielded consistent gains across STEM, OCR, and hallucination mitigation benchmarks. For instance, on Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct, DPE improved CharXiv by 4.11 points and surpassed VisPlay on HallusionBench (69.19% vs. 68.35%).
- **Robust Training Dynamics:** DPE maintained a stable, upward performance trend across iterations (e.g., MMMU increasing from 54.44% to 56.44%). In contrast, VisPlay exhibited performance fluctuations and regressions on benchmarks such as MMMU and BLINK.
- **Transferability:** DPE demonstrated effectiveness when applied to the stronger Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct model, providing substantial improvements on MMMU (+3.67 points) and MMStar (+10.86 points).

#### 4.2. Comparison with State-of-the-Art Models

DPE, utilizing an 8B backbone, achieved competitive performance compared to significantly larger or proprietary models:
- **Overall Performance:** DPE achieved an average score of 64.39, which surpassed the 72B-parameter Qwen2.5-VL (61.9) and GPT-4o (56.1).
- **Complex Reasoning Tasks:** DPE established new performance levels on visual math benchmarks, achieving 76.2 on MathVista and 53.88 on MathVision. This represented an increase of +1.4 and +15.7 points, respectively, over Qwen2.5-VL-72B.
- **Hallucination Mitigation:** DPE led on HallusionBench with a score of 74.13, demonstrating improved grounding compared to GPT-4o (67.5). These results suggest that the quality of data derived from DPE's closed-loop evolution contributes more significantly to solving complex multimodal problems than model parameter scale alone.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

Ablation experiments investigated the contribution of DPE's core components:
- **Impact of Static Data:** DPE, using approximately 3,000 iteratively generated samples, achieved superior performance compared to training with a static 47,000-sample Vision-SR1-47K dataset. For example, DPE improved MMMU (from 54.8 to 56.44), HallusionBench (from 67.6 to 69.0), MathVista (from 68.8 to 69.5), and RealWorldQA (from 69.9 to 70.5). This indicates that data distribution and targeted generation are more critical than sheer data volume for overcoming performance saturation and addressing long-tail capabilities.
- **Impact of the Diagnostic Module:** Removing the diagnostic module resulted in reduced and less stable iterative gains, often leading to performance plateaus or regressions. On CharXiv, DPE achieved continuous improvements (36.8 -> 40.91), while the ablated version remained near the baseline (36.8 -> 36.7). A similar trend was observed on MathVision, highlighting the diagnostic module's role in maintaining a consistent and effective evolutionary direction.
- **Diagnosis-Guided Data Distribution:** Analysis of category distribution revealed that the diagnostic module adaptively increased the sampling ratios for underperforming categories based on identified failure patterns. This targeted redistribution correlated with observed performance improvements on relevant benchmarks like CharXiv and MathVision.
- **Impact of Image Retrieval and Editing (Image Tools):** Ablating the image retrieval and editing module led to earlier performance plateaus and limited gains in later iterations, particularly affecting OCR and chart-related tasks. For instance, on CharXiv, full DPE reached 40.91, whereas without image tools, it only reached 38.1. This suggests that relying on static or minimally varied images can lead to overfitting narrow visual distributions, thus limiting improvements on tasks requiring diverse visual contexts.

#### 4.4. Diversity Analysis

Diversity of generated data was assessed using mean pairwise cosine distance of Qwen3-VL-Embedding embeddings:
- **Text Diversity:** DPE exhibited higher and more stable text diversity across iterations compared to VisPlay, indicating broader question coverage and reduced distribution collapse.
- **Visual Diversity:** DPE also showed improved visual diversity, attributable to its image retrieval and editing mechanisms, which expanded visual sources and variations beyond static image sets, thus improving long-tail visual coverage.

#### 4.5. Quality Analysis of Generated Questions

An evaluation by three independent LLM judges (Claude Sonnet 4, OpenAI o3, Gemini 2.5 Pro) rated generated questions on Clarity, Solvability, and Correctness (5-point Likert scale):
- DPE consistently produced higher-quality questions than VisPlay across all iterations, maintaining stable scores (e.g., overall quality score (QS) of 4.96, 4.74, 4.80).
- VisPlay's question quality, particularly solvability and correctness, declined in later iterations, suggesting late-stage degradation (QS dropped to 3.32 at Iteration 3).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work introduces a methodological advancement in the training of Large Multimodal Models (LMMs). The Diagnostic-driven Progressive Evolution (DPE) framework proposes a structured, iterative approach that aims to enhance LMM capabilities more effectively than prior self-evolution methods.

#### 5.1. Significance

- **Improved Data Efficiency:** DPE demonstrates that significant LMM capability improvements can be achieved with a relatively small amount of training data (e.g., 3,000 generated samples outperforming 47,000 static samples). This suggests that data quality, relevance, and targeted generation are more critical than sheer volume.
- **Enhanced Training Stability and Direction:** The explicit diagnostic mechanism allows for the identification of specific model blind spots and weaknesses. This targeted approach guides data generation, leading to more stable training dynamics and mitigating the performance plateaus or regressions often observed in frameworks that lack interpretable diagnostics.
- **Addressing Long-Tail Capabilities:** By adaptively sourcing and generating diverse multimodal data through a multi-agent system with image tools, DPE addresses the scarcity of visual diversity in training sets. This is particularly significant for improving performance on long-tail reasoning abilities and complex, less common scenarios that static datasets struggle to cover.
- **Higher-Quality Synthetic Data:** The multi-agent questioner system, combined with robust validation, produces training samples of consistently higher quality, characterized by better clarity, solvability, and correctness compared to alternative self-evolving methods.
- **Competitive Performance:** DPE, even with a smaller backbone model (8B), achieved competitive or superior performance against much larger or proprietary state-of-the-art LMMs on several challenging benchmarks, indicating the efficacy of its diagnostic and generation principles.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Advancement in Machine Learning Methodology:** DPE offers a generalizable paradigm for continual LMM training under open task distributions, potentially influencing future LMM development by emphasizing diagnosis-driven learning loops.
- **Broader Deployment of LMMs:** By enabling more data-efficient capability enhancement and reducing training instability, DPE could contribute to the development of more robust LMMs suitable for a wider range of real-world applications where precise reasoning and adaptation to diverse scenarios are crucial.
- **Improved Transparency:** The framework provides a degree of interpretability by explicitly attributing failures to specific weaknesses, which can contribute to a more transparent understanding of LMM evolution processes.

The authors acknowledge potential societal implications related to the broader deployment of enhanced LMMs. They state that risks associated with synthetic data generation, such as the reinforcement of biases or propagation of inaccurate annotations, are mitigated through explicit validation mechanisms and controlled data distributions within the DPE framework. The use of publicly available or synthetically generated inputs in experiments avoids direct privacy concerns. The ethical and societal considerations are considered consistent with those generally encountered in large-scale multimodal learning research.
