# From Blind Spots to Gains: Diagnostic-Driven Iterative Training for Large Multimodal Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Peking University and Shandong University developed Diagnostic-driven Progressive Evolution (DPE), an iterative framework that diagnoses and corrects specific weaknesses in Large Multimodal Models (LMMs). DPE enables stable, continual performance gains, achieving state-of-the-art results in visual math (e.g., MathVista 76.2) and hallucination mitigation (e.g., HallusionBench 74.13) with significantly less data than traditional methods.

## Problem

- Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) struggle with identifying and correcting 'capability blind spots' due to reliance on static training data and fixed recipes, leading to inefficient learning on long-tail weaknesses.
- There is a scarcity of high-quality, human-annotated multimodal reasoning data, which hinders the scalable and continuous improvement of LMMs.
- Existing self-evolving LMM frameworks often lack interpretable diagnostics, leading to 'blind' evolution and potential distribution drift, and are limited by static image sets that restrict visual diversity and cause performance plateaus.

## Method

- Introducing the ==Diagnostic-driven Progressive Evolution (DPE)== framework, an iterative 'diagnose-and-correct' loop that explicitly controls the category composition and question emphasis of training data based on identified model weaknesses.
- Implementing an ==Adaptive Diagnosis Mechanism== that provides interpretable failure attribution and capability decomposition across **12** dimensions, generating a structured report to guide subsequent data generation.
- Developing a ==Multiple Agents Questioner System== equipped with tool-use capabilities (e.g., web search, image editing) to dynamically source, filter, edit, and compose diverse, high-quality, and weakness-focused visual content and questions.

## Results

- DPE consistently achieved stable and continual performance improvements, setting new state-of-the-art records in visual math on MathVista (**76.2**) and MathVision (**53.88**), and leading HallusionBench with **74.13**, surpassing larger models like GPT-4o (**56.1** overall average) with an 8B parameter backbone (**64.39** overall average).
- The framework demonstrated exceptional data efficiency, achieving superior performance across key benchmarks (e.g., MMMU **56.44** vs **54.8**, MathVista **69.5** vs **68.8**) using only **3,000** iteratively generated samples, roughly **1/15th** of a comparative static dataset.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the diagnostic module is indispensable for maintaining stable evolution and preventing performance stagnation or regression, while image retrieval and editing tools are critical for expanding visual diversity and avoiding early performance plateaus on tasks like OCR and chart understanding.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly diagnosing a model's specific weaknesses and then generating targeted training data is crucial for stable and continuous improvement, mirroring efficient human learning processes.
> - Dynamically expanding visual context through image retrieval and editing tools, coupled with weakness-focused data generation, is essential for addressing long-tail reasoning challenges and preventing overfitting to narrow visual distributions.
> - Focusing on generating high-quality, moderately difficult samples for identified blind spots can achieve superior LMM performance with substantially less data compared to using large, untargeted static datasets.
