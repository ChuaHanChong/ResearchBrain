# Agentic ESOpt: Fine-Tuning Long-Horizon LLM Agents with Minimal GPU Requirements

> [!summary] Summary
> Agentic ESOpt fine-tunes long-horizon LLM agents with evolution strategies instead of RL: it samples full-parameter Gaussian perturbations, scores each perturbed agent by scalar environment reward, and applies a z-score reward-weighted forward-only update, with a cosine-decayed perturbation radius. Needing only inference-level GPU memory, it beats GRPO by 12.5 points on 15-turn Sudoku and lifts a 27B WebArena agent by 6.69 points.

## Problem

- Agentic RL stores activations, optimizer states and backpropagates through trajectories, making full-parameter fine-tuning of larger LLM agents impractical on modest GPU budgets.
- Policy-gradient estimators sum action-level score terms across turns, so variance grows with horizon and sparse terminal rewards make turn-level credit assignment unreliable.
- Prompt-space and test-time search methods freeze model weights, so they only recombine behaviors the current policy already reaches, and prior ES work targets only single-turn reasoning.

## Method

- **Agentic ESOpt** estimates a ==Gaussian-smoothed pseudo-gradient== from G perturbed rollouts, ==z-score normalizes rewards== within the population, and updates weights in place via ==stored noise seeds== only.
- A ==cosine decay schedule on perturbation radius sigma== trades early exploration and ==flatness regularization== for later exploitation, keeping nonzero terminal sigma at train time and decaying to zero at test time.
- ==Black-box trajectory feedback== is shared with prompt-space optimizers, giving an ==alternating outer loop== for prompt-parameter co-evolution with ==Trace2Skill== skill distillation and ==EoH== heuristic-design mutation operators.

## Results

- On Sudoku the ordering reverses with horizon: PPO leads at H*=5 (**90.63%**), GRPO at H*=10, and Agentic ESOpt strongest at H*=15 with **53.13%** versus GRPO's **40.63%**.
- It trains with **8.41GB**, **85.7%** below GRPO's **58.88GB**, at comparable FLOPs and lower wall-clock; on Math and DocVQA it gains **13.7 points** over the 4B base and **8.3** over GRPO.
- Full-parameter 27B WebArena-Lite tuning raises No Skill from **29.47%** to **36.16%** and Trace2Skill from **33.94%** to **36.36%**; heuristic design improves **28 of 36** matched comparisons.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Trajectory-level parameter attribution wins where per-turn credit assignment fails, so gradient-free search becomes preferable, not merely cheaper, as horizon length and reward sparsity grow.
> - Annealing the perturbation radius matters: fixed sigma underperforms, and decaying it fully to zero overfits, so exploration and smoothing regularization must be traded gradually.
> - Black-box scalar feedback composes cheaply with prompt, skill and test-time search loops, but the approach trades backpropagation for many extra environment rollouts.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of advanced Large Language Models (LLMs) such as Qwen3, DeepSeek-R1, and Gemini 2.5 has demonstrated their capabilities as general-purpose agents, enabling them to perform complex tasks like website navigation, repository editing, and multi-step workflow coordination. However, these models can exhibit performance limitations on less common tool APIs or specialized scientific and algorithmic tasks. Consequently, the efficient fine-tuning of LLM agents to acquire task-specific expertise remains an active area of research.

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has shown effectiveness in fine-tuning LLMs for single-turn interactions. However, its application to long-horizon agentic reasoning, characterized by increasingly branching interactions and sparse rewards, exposes several limitations. Traditional Agentic RL methods require substantial GPU memory due to the need to store activations and optimizer states for backpropagation through extensive trajectories, rendering full-parameter fine-tuning impractical for larger LLMs. Additionally, the challenge of credit assignment becomes significantly more complex as trajectories lengthen and rewards become sparse, making it difficult to attribute successful or unsuccessful outcomes to specific decisions within a long sequence of actions.

Evolution Strategies (ES) have been explored for single-turn LLM reasoning, demonstrating higher GPU memory efficiency, though sometimes at a slight performance cost compared to RL. This paper argues that the structural advantages of ES are particularly salient in the context of fine-tuning long-horizon agents, where ES may offer a preferable alternative to RL rather than merely a computationally cheaper one.

The broader landscape also includes approaches that improve LLM agents by optimizing external, non-parametric components while keeping the LLM parameters fixed. These include methods like Reflexion for verbal feedback, Voyager for skill accumulation, and various memory systems for long-horizon interactions. More recent techniques such as SkillOpt and Trace2Skill explicitly optimize skill documents. Similarly, test-time compute methods enhance task performance through additional search, sampling, reflection, or evolution at inference time, without altering the model parameters. These non-parametric approaches are lightweight and flexible but are constrained by the inherent behaviors of the frozen underlying LLM policy.

This work positions Agentic ESOpt as a complementary approach. By enabling parameter adaptation, it adds a new dimension to existing self-improvement and test-time compute pipelines. Its black-box update mechanism allows it to reuse trajectory-level feedback generated for skill, memory, or test-time search, facilitating co-evolution of external contextual information and LLM parameters. This integration aims to overcome the limitations of parameter-frozen methods while addressing the computational and credit assignment challenges of RL in long-horizon agentic settings.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an efficient and scalable framework for fine-tuning Large Language Model (LLM) agents, particularly for tasks involving long-horizon reasoning and sparse reward signals. This objective is motivated by several key challenges inherent in current LLM agent fine-tuning paradigms, especially when utilizing Reinforcement Learning (RL) for complex, multi-step tasks.

The detailed motivations are as follows:

1. **Addressing Computational and Memory Limitations of Agentic RL for Large LLMs**:
 - **Problem**: Current Agentic RL methods, such as PPO and GRPO, necessitate backpropagation through interaction trajectories. This process requires storing substantial computational graphs, including activations and optimizer states, leading to very high GPU memory consumption.
 - **Impact**: For larger LLMs (e.g., those with tens of billions of parameters), performing full-parameter fine-tuning using RL becomes computationally prohibitive and often infeasible on commonly available hardware configurations. This memory barrier limits the scalability of fine-tuning efforts for advanced LLM agents.
 - **Motivation for ES**: The paper seeks to leverage Evolution Strategies (ES) to enable full-parameter optimization with only minimal, inference-level GPU memory. This would significantly reduce the hardware requirements, making it possible to fine-tune larger LLM agents.

2. **Mitigating the Difficulties of Credit Assignment in Long-Horizon Tasks**:
 - **Problem**: Long-horizon agentic reasoning tasks involve extended, often branching interaction trajectories, where rewards are typically sparse and only provided at the end of an episode (terminal rewards). In such scenarios, RL algorithms struggle with the "credit assignment problem"—effectively determining which specific actions or decisions within a long sequence contributed to the final outcome.
 - **Impact**: The variance of policy gradient estimators in RL tends to increase with the horizon length, making learning inefficient and unreliable. This poor long-horizon scalability limits the effectiveness of RL in complex, multi-turn agentic environments.
 - **Motivation for ES**: The research aims to explore ES as a method that performs trajectory-level parameter attribution directly, without needing to decompose rewards across individual turns. This approach is theorized to offer better scalability as the horizon length grows, by attributing outcomes to coherent policy variations rather than individual action scores.

3. **Enhancing Flexibility and Composability with Other Agentic Improvement Techniques**:
 - **Problem**: Many existing agent self-improvement methods (e.g., prompt-space optimization, skill distillation, test-time compute) operate by modifying external components (like prompts or skill libraries) while keeping the LLM's parameters fixed. While lightweight, these methods are limited to eliciting behaviors already encoded within the frozen policy.
 - **Impact**: To achieve deeper behavioral changes and adaptation to specialized tasks, modifying the underlying LLM parameters is often necessary. However, integrating parameter fine-tuning with external prompt-space or test-time optimization loops is challenging with gradient-based RL due to its complexity and computational demands.
 - **Motivation for ES**: The paper proposes ES due to its lightweight, black-box feedback interface. This characteristic is expected to make ES fine-tuning highly composable with prompt-space evolution and test-time compute, enabling "prompt-parameter co-evolution" where both external context and internal parameters adapt simultaneously.

In summary, the core motivation is to develop Agentic ESOpt as a robust, scalable, and flexible fine-tuning framework that specifically addresses the memory, credit assignment, and composability limitations of existing methods when training LLM agents for long-horizon tasks, thereby enabling more powerful and adaptable LLM-based agents.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core methodology presented in this paper is **Agentic ESOpt**, a full-parameter Evolution Strategy (ES) framework designed for fine-tuning Large Language Model (LLM) agents. This approach distinguishes itself from traditional Reinforcement Learning (RL) by eschewing backpropagation, thereby offering advantages in model scalability, flexibility, and long-horizon performance.

The methodology can be broken down into several key components:

1. **Evolution Strategy Core Mechanism**:
 - **Parameter Perturbation**: At each optimization step (generation), Agentic ESOpt samples a population of *G* full-parameter perturbations (ϵ) from a standard Gaussian distribution (N(0, I)). These perturbations are then added to the current LLM parameters (θ) to create *G* slightly varied versions of the agent: (θ + σϵ_i), where σ is a perturbation scale.
 - **Black-Box Evaluation**: Each of these *G* perturbed agents is rolled out in the environment to generate an interaction trajectory (τ_i). A scalar reward (R_i) is then collected for each trajectory. This reward is typically a terminal task score, signifying the success or failure of the entire sequence of actions. Crucially, this evaluation process is treated as a black box; no gradients are computed through the environment or the agent's internal operations.
 - **Reward Normalization**: To stabilize learning and reduce variance, the collected rewards (R_i) from the population are normalized using a z-score: R̂_i = (R_i - μ_R) / (s_R + ε), where μ_R and s_R are the mean and standard deviation of rewards within the current population, and ε is a small constant for numerical stability.
 - **Parameter Update**: The current LLM parameters (θ) are updated based on a reward-weighted sum of the perturbations: θ_{t+1} = θ_t + (α/G) * Σ(R̂_i * ϵ_i). Here, α is the learning rate, acting as the effective update scale.

2. **GPU Memory Efficiency**:
 - Agentic ESOpt achieves minimal GPU memory requirements, equivalent to those for inference. This is accomplished by storing only the noise seed for each perturbation and performing in-place addition and subtraction of the perturbations. This design avoids the need to store activations, intermediate computation graphs, or optimizer states typically required for backpropagation in RL, making it feasible to fine-tune significantly larger LLMs.

3. **Prompt-Space Composition and Prompt-Parameter Co-Evolution**:
 - The black-box nature of ES updates provides high flexibility. It allows parameter adaptation to be performed "on the fly" alongside prompt-space optimization methods, such as skill-space evolution (e.g., Trace2Skill) or test-time compute.
 - This enables a framework for *prompt-parameter co-evolution*, where both the external agent context (c_t, e.g., prompt, skills) and the LLM's internal parameters (θ_t) can be updated in an alternating or joint manner. This means that parameter improvements can influence the effectiveness of prompts, and improved prompts can generate better trajectories that inform parameter updates.

4. **Cosine Decay of the Perturbation Radius (σ)**:
 - **Addressing Smoothing Bias**: The use of a non-zero perturbation radius (σ) in ES introduces a "smoothing bias," as the optimized objective (J_σ) is a Gaussian-smoothed version of the true objective (J). This smoothing favors flatter parameter neighborhoods, acting as a form of regularization.
 - **Exploration-Adaptation Trade-off**: To manage this bias and optimize the exploration-adaptation trade-off, Agentic ESOpt introduces a cosine decay schedule for σ: σ_t = σ_T + (σ_0 - σ_T) * (1 + cos(πt/T)) / 2.
 - **Schedule Rationale**: This schedule gradually decreases σ over *T* update steps. A larger σ in early stages facilitates broader exploration and stronger regularization, while a smaller σ in later stages enables finer exploitation and reduces the objective bias as the optimization converges.
 - **Contextual Application**: For train-time fine-tuning, a non-zero terminal σ_T is retained to maintain some level of exploration and regularization. For test-time compute, where the goal is typically to find the best immediate outcome, σ_T is decayed to zero to minimize objective bias and achieve precise adaptation.

5. **Theoretical Basis for Long-Horizon Scalability**:
 - The paper theoretically argues that ES exhibits better long-horizon scalability compared to Agentic RL. A policy gradient (PG) estimator's variance grows approximately linearly with the realized horizon (H) because it sums action-level score terms over all turns (Var[bg_PG] ∝ H).
 - In contrast, the ES estimator (bg_ES) samples a single parameter perturbation for the entire trajectory, and its parameter-score factor does not sum over H. This allows ES to attribute the terminal return directly to a coherent policy variation, avoiding the compounding credit assignment difficulty that plagues RL as the horizon lengthens.

This comprehensive methodology positions Agentic ESOpt as a robust and efficient solution for fine-tuning LLM agents in challenging long-horizon, sparse-reward environments, while also enabling synergistic interactions with other agent improvement paradigms.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates Agentic ESOpt across various long-horizon agentic reasoning tasks, demonstrating its advantages in model scalability, long-horizon performance, and flexibility in composition with other techniques.

#### 4.1. Agentic Sudoku: Controlled Experiments on Long-Horizon Scalability

- **Context**: Controlled environment with strictly defined minimum successful horizons (H\* = 5, 10, 15), providing only sparse terminal rewards. Used Qwen3.5-4B.
- **GPU Memory & Compute Efficiency**: Agentic ESOpt required only 8.41GB of GPU memory, equivalent to inference memory for Qwen3.5-4B, significantly less than Agentic GRPO (58.88GB) and Agentic PPO (89.40GB). Despite evaluating a larger population (G=32 vs. 8 rollouts for GRPO), Agentic ESOpt maintained comparable model FLOPs and achieved faster wall-clock times (e.g., 9.4 h vs. 19.0 h for H\*=15) due to avoiding backpropagation and leveraging parallel inference.
- **Performance vs. Horizon Length**:
 - H\* = 5: Agentic PPO (90.63%) led, followed closely by Agentic ESOpt (89.58%) and GRPO (85.42%).
 - H\* = 10: GRPO (67.71%) performed best, with Agentic ESOpt (62.50%) next, and PPO (56.25%).
 - H\* = 15: Agentic ESOpt (53.13%) became the strongest, outperforming GRPO (40.63%) by 12.50 percentage points, while PPO's performance dropped to 0.00% due to ineffective credit assignment with sparse terminal rewards.
- **Conclusion**: This horizon-dependent ordering reversal indicates that Agentic ESOpt's relative advantage increases with the minimum successful horizon, aligning with the theoretical prediction that ES is more robust to long-horizon credit assignment challenges. The cosine decay schedule for σ improved final performance significantly (e.g., 53.13% vs. 42.71% for Vanilla ES at H\*=15).

#### 4.2. Agentic ReAct-Style Tool Usage on Math and DocVQA

- **Context**: Fine-tuning Qwen3.5-4B for ReAct-style tool use in Math (DAPO, AIME 2026) and DocVQA, both long-horizon scenarios (H > 10).
- **Performance (No Skill Context)**: Agentic ESOpt consistently outperformed Agentic GRPO.
 - Improved Qwen3.5-4B base model by an average of 13.7 percentage points across DAPO, AIME 2026 (Mean@4), and DocVQA (Mean@4).
 - Improved Agentic GRPO by an average of 8.3 percentage points across these metrics.
- **Composition with Trace2Skill**: When combined with Trace2Skill, Agentic ESOpt + Trace2Skill achieved the strongest Qwen3.5-4B Mean@4 results across all three tasks, demonstrating its flexibility to compose with external skill optimization.
- **Pass@K Performance**: Agentic ESOpt variants improved every reported Pass@K metric over their matched GRPO baselines, suggesting a broader coverage of successful trajectories after parameter optimization, rather than merely boosting average performance.

#### 4.3. Agentic Reasoning for WebArena: Experiments on Model Scalability

- **Context**: Fine-tuning Qwen3.5-27B on WebArena-Lite, a browser benchmark with 165 tasks. This experiment focused on full-parameter adaptation of a large LLM, a scenario where Agentic RL is often impractical due to memory constraints.
- **Model Scalability**: Agentic ESOpt's inference-level memory footprint enabled full-parameter adaptation of Qwen3.5-27B on four NVIDIA H100 80GB GPUs, a feat not feasible with Agentic RL.
- **Performance**:
 - **No Skill Context**: Agentic ESOpt improved the Qwen3.5-27B No Skill baseline from 29.47% to 36.16%, a gain of 6.69 percentage points.
 - **Trace2Skill Context**: Combining Agentic ESOpt with Trace2Skill further improved the Trace2Skill baseline from 33.94% to 36.36%, a 2.42 percentage point gain.
- **Conclusion**: Agentic ESOpt successfully enables full-parameter adaptation of large LLMs, making fine-tuning of 27B-scale agents practical, and demonstrates effective composition with prompt-space skill optimization in this demanding environment.

#### 4.4. Agentic ESOpt on Test-Time Compute: Automatic Heuristic Design (AHD)

- **Context**: Integrating Agentic ESOpt into existing test-time compute frameworks for Automatic Heuristic Design (AHD) using LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct. Tasks included constructive (TSP, KP, ASP) and ACO-style (TSP, CVRP, BPP) combinatorial optimization.
- **Performance**:
 - Agentic ESOpt + EoH improved all six constructive test sets at both T=1000 and T=2000 evaluation budgets.
 - Agentic ESOpt + Sample improved nine out of twelve constructive comparisons.
 - Overall, across constructive and ACO-style settings, Agentic ESOpt enhanced performance in 28 out of 36 matched method-budget comparisons.
- **Conclusion**: Agentic ESOpt effectively extends test-time compute by enabling coupled heuristic-parameter optimization. Its lightweight, black-box parameter adaptation systematically improved existing search methods without modifying their outer search scaffold or increasing the overall evaluation budget.

#### 4.5. Population Scaling with Model Size (Preliminary Study)

- **Context**: Investigated population sensitivity of Vanilla ES on 15-turn Sudoku for Qwen3.5-4B and Qwen3.5-9B models.
- **Results**: Increasing population size (G) from 8 to 16 for the 4B model resulted in a substantial +677.0% increase in final test success. In contrast, for the 9B model, the same increase in G yielded no relative improvement (0.0% change) in final test success, although best test success improved by 24.1%.
- **Conclusion**: This preliminary evidence suggests that stronger LLM backbones may be less sensitive to smaller population sizes, implying that useful ES updates might be obtainable with fewer sampled directions for more capable models.

In summary, the findings consistently support the paper's central argument: Agentic ESOpt offers a robust, scalable, and flexible alternative to Agentic RL for fine-tuning long-horizon LLM agents, particularly advantageous in scenarios demanding memory efficiency and effective credit assignment across extended interaction sequences.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research on Agentic ESOpt introduces a significant contribution to the field of Large Language Model (LLM) fine-tuning for agentic tasks, particularly those characterized by long interaction horizons and sparse feedback. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Paradigm Shift in LLM Agent Optimization**: The work reframes Evolution Strategies (ES) not merely as a cheaper substitute for Reinforcement Learning (RL), but as a fundamentally *better-matched optimization mechanism* for long-horizon, sparse-feedback LLM agents. This shift challenges the prevailing reliance on gradient-based RL for complex agentic scenarios and proposes a more suitable alternative for specific problem structures.

2. **Enhanced Model Scalability and Accessibility**: By requiring only inference-level GPU memory, Agentic ESOpt drastically reduces the hardware barrier for full-parameter fine-tuning of large LLMs. This is a critical advantage for models exceeding tens of billions of parameters, for which traditional RL methods are often computationally infeasible on conventional GPU setups. This increased accessibility can democratize advanced LLM fine-tuning, enabling researchers and practitioners with more modest resources to adapt larger models to their specific agentic tasks. The demonstrated ability to fine-tune a Qwen3.5-27B WebArena agent on four H100 GPUs exemplifies this impact.

3. **Improved Long-Horizon Performance and Credit Assignment**: The study provides empirical evidence and theoretical arguments supporting ES's superior scalability in long-horizon tasks compared to RL. By attributing trajectory-level outcomes to coherent policy perturbations rather than summing action-level scores, Agentic ESOpt effectively navigates the challenging credit assignment problem inherent in sparse-reward, multi-step interactions. This translates to higher success rates in tasks like long-horizon Sudoku and ReAct-style tool use, where RL methods struggle as the interaction length increases.

4. **Flexible and Composable Agent Self-Improvement**: Agentic ESOpt's black-box feedback interface makes it inherently flexible and easily composable with other agentic improvement paradigms. Its seamless integration with prompt-space optimization, skill distillation (e.g., Trace2Skill), and test-time compute (e.g., Automatic Heuristic Design) allows for "prompt-parameter co-evolution." This means that LLMs can adapt their internal parameters simultaneously with evolving external prompts or skills, leading to more comprehensive and effective agent self-improvement capabilities. The successful application in enhancing 28 out of 36 AHD comparisons underscores this flexibility.

5. **Efficiency in Diverse Settings**: Beyond memory advantages, Agentic ESOpt demonstrates competitive, and in some cases superior, performance in terms of model FLOPs and wall-clock time compared to Agentic GRPO. This efficiency is achieved by trading the computational cost of backpropagation for a larger number of parallelizable forward passes, optimizing the overall resource utilization for agent fine-tuning.

6. **Potential for Future LLM Agent Development**: As LLM-based agentic systems continue to evolve towards longer, more complex interactions, larger model sizes, and intricate compositions of prompt-space components (memory, tools, skills), Agentic ESOpt offers a robust foundation. It could become a central paradigm for online self-improvement, allowing agents to continuously adapt and refine their behaviors in dynamic environments, including industrial applications involving proprietary tools and complex workflows. The preliminary findings on population scaling with model size further suggest potential for even greater efficiency in fine-tuning frontier LLMs with fewer sampled directions.

In essence, Agentic ESOpt offers a timely solution to critical challenges in LLM agent fine-tuning, paving the way for more capable, adaptable, and resource-efficient intelligent agents in increasingly complex real-world scenarios.
