# Implicit Kinodynamic Motion Retargeting for Human-to-humanoid Imitation Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> The Implicit Kinodynamic Motion Retargeting (IKMR) framework translates human motion into robot-executable trajectories for humanoid imitation learning, leveraging a two-stage neural network approach. This method achieves a retargeting speed of 5000 frames per second and enables a Unitree G1 humanoid robot to accurately track diverse, dynamically feasible motions.

## Problem

- Existing human-to-humanoid motion retargeting methods are computationally expensive and lack scalability for large-scale human motion datasets.
- Many learning-based retargeting approaches, particularly neural network-based ones, often neglect dynamic constraints, leading to physically impossible or unstable robot motions.
- Obtaining diverse and large-scale motion data directly from humanoid robots is challenging, creating a need for robust methods to leverage abundant human motion data efficiently.

## Method

- IKMR employs a two-stage learning framework consisting of kinematics-aware pretraining and dynamics-aware fine-tuning.
- Kinematics-aware pretraining utilizes a dual encoder-decoder architecture with skeleton-based graph convolutional networks to learn a unified latent motion representation for both human and humanoid skeletons.
- Dynamics-aware fine-tuning refines the retargeted motions by training an imitation tracker in a simulator to generate physically feasible data, then fine-tuning only the humanoid decoder to ensure dynamic plausibility.

## Results

- IKMR achieved a retargeting speed of 5000 frames per second on an Intel Core i7-14700HX with an RTX 4090, which is approximately 100 times faster than traditional numerical optimization methods.
- Dynamics-aware fine-tuning produced smoother motions with lower mean acceleration (29.15 rad/s²) and jerk (435.37 rad/s³) compared to pre-trained motions, effectively mitigating unrealistic physical phenomena.
- The framework demonstrated superior robustness to noise in source human motion, maintaining lower absolute keypoints trajectory error and mean key joints acceleration, and successfully enabled a full-size Unitree G1 humanoid robot to track diverse human-like movements in real-world deployment.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Learning a unified topological latent space via graph convolutional networks allows for efficient and generalized kinematic motion transfer between anatomically different human and humanoid skeletons.
> - Separating kinematic mapping from dynamic refinement into distinct learning stages enables both efficient latent space learning and precise integration of physical constraints.
> - Fine-tuning the decoder with physically feasible motions generated by a simulator-trained imitation tracker effectively imbues the retargeted motions with dynamic properties, crucial for stable real-robot deployment.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a fundamental challenge in humanoid robotics: the scarcity of high-quality robot motion data for imitation learning. Unlike fields such as natural language processing and computer vision, which benefit from vast datasets, physical robotics faces significant limitations in acquiring diverse and agile motor repertoires due to high operational costs, risk of hardware damage, and the inherent difficulty of data collection in the physical world. This scarcity hinders the development of robust, general-purpose humanoid controllers and the training of advanced humanoid foundation models.

A prevalent approach to mitigate this data bottleneck involves leveraging abundant human motion data. Human movements can be acquired from various sources, including optical motion capture (mocap) systems, in-the-wild videos, and generative models. This human motion data, however, often exists in a different kinematic structure (human skeleton) and frequently contains noise, jitter, and flickering. Directly applying this data to robots requires a motion retargeting process, which translates human movements to the robot's distinct kinematic structure, joint limits, and physical constraints.

Existing motion retargeting methods broadly fall into several categories:
- **Traditional numerical optimization**: These methods (e.g., PHC, Mink, GMR) treat retargeting as a frame-by-frame optimization problem to achieve geometric alignment. While effective for individual frames, they are computationally intensive, limiting their scalability for large datasets. They are also prone to amplifying noise from source data, leading to physically infeasible or unsafe robot motions.
- **Dynamics-based methods**: These incorporate physical constraints to improve realism but often exacerbate computational costs.
- **Neural implicit retargeting**: Recent deep learning approaches have aimed to improve efficiency by learning motion mappings. However, many primarily focus on virtual avatars and may not adequately enforce dynamic constraints, leading to motions that are physically infeasible for real hardware.

The current research positions itself at the intersection of these challenges. It aims to develop a scalable and robust motion transformation pipeline that can efficiently convert large-scale, potentially noisy human motion datasets into physically viable, robot-executable trajectories. This contributes to bridging the "data-to-control" gap, enabling the continuous assimilation of diverse human experiences into robot motor repertoires, which is critical for bootstrapping and generalizing humanoid imitation learning and supporting the development of next-generation humanoid foundation models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a method that can efficiently and robustly transform heterogeneous, unconstrained human motion datasets into high-fidelity, robot-executable training data at scale. This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Addressing Data Scarcity in Humanoid Robotics**: The core motivation stems from the severe data scarcity bottleneck in humanoid robotics. Collecting diverse, high-quality, and physically viable motion data directly from humanoid robots is exceptionally difficult, costly, and time-consuming. This limits the training of robust imitation learning policies and hinders the development of generalist robot behaviors. In contrast, human motion data is abundant and growing, offering a vast potential resource if it can be effectively utilized.

2. **Overcoming Computational Scalability Issues of Current Retargeting Methods**: Traditional motion retargeting methods typically rely on frame-by-frame numerical optimization. This approach, while capable of geometric alignment, is computationally expensive and slow, making it impractical for processing the massive volumes of human motion data required for large-scale dataset synthesis. A key motivation is to shift from this per-frame optimization paradigm to a more computationally tractable, vectorized, and batch-scalable approach.

3. **Ensuring Data Quality and Hardware Safety**: Human motion data, whether from videos, motion capture systems, or generative models, often contains imperfections such as spatial noise, jitter, and frame-level flickering. Existing retargeting methods tend to amplify these artifacts, producing robot trajectories that are physically unrealistic, dynamically infeasible, or potentially unsafe for physical hardware. A critical motivation is to develop a mechanism that can inherently filter out these disturbances and generate smooth, physically viable motions that respect robot kinematics and dynamics, thereby ensuring hardware safety.

4. **Leveraging Homomorphic Kinematic Chains**: The researchers identify a core insight that despite significant differences in physical geometry (bone lengths, degrees of freedom, joint limits), human and humanoid robot skeletons share a homeomorphic and topologically equivalent kinematic chain. This suggests that their temporal dynamics can be projected into a unified set of topological prototypes, decoupling motion content from specific physical embodiments. This insight motivates the development of a shared latent representation space to enable efficient cross-structural motion transformation.

By addressing these challenges, the research aims to establish a continuous data assimilation loop, where readily available human motion data can be seamlessly converted into robot training assets, fostering the development of agile and versatile motor repertoires for humanoid robots without incurring prohibitive costs or risks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Implicit Kinodynamic Motion Retargeting (IKMR), is a neural-based data transformation pipeline designed to convert human motions into physically feasible robot trajectories at scale. The framework comprises two main phases: Kinematics-Aware Pretraining and Physics-informed Finetuning.

**A. Problem Formulation**
The problem is formalized as learning a functional mapping $f: (Q_A, S_A, S_B) \rightarrow Q_B$, where $M_A = (S_A, Q_A)$ represents source human motion (skeleton $S_A$, joint rotations $Q_A$) and $M_B = (S_B, Q_B)$ represents target humanoid robot motion (skeleton $S_B$, joint rotations $Q_B$). The goal is to avoid frame-by-frame numerical optimization and achieve a fully vectorized transformation.

**B. Topology-Aware Motion Representation**
IKMR's approach hinges on the observation that human and humanoid skeletons are homeomorphic, sharing a topologically equivalent kinematic chain. Rather than explicit spatial fitting, the method enforces consistency within a shared latent feature space. Motions from heterogeneous structures are projected into a unified set of topological prototypes, effectively decoupling motion content from physical embodiment.
- **Feature Extraction**: A skeleton-based graph convolutional dual autoencoder is employed. For each joint $i$, its hidden feature representation $\hat{M}_i$ is computed by aggregating information from its topological neighbors $N_i$. This involves concatenating skeleton offsets ($S_j$) with rotational sequences ($Q_j$) for each neighbor $j$, followed by a convolutional kernel.
- **Pooling**: An average pooling operation condenses the structural features across the neighborhood, ensuring that graph convolution traverses the entire kinematic tree.

**C. Kinematics-Aware Pretraining**
This phase utilizes a symmetric dual autoencoder architecture to map cross-structural configurations into an aligned, topology-aware latent representation space ($Z$). Each encoder-decoder pair has a static structural branch and a dynamic motion branch.
- **Encoders**: $E_A^\theta$ and $E_B^\theta$ map source human motion $(Q_A, S_A)$ to latent $z_A$ and target robot motion $(Q_B, S_B)$ to latent $z_B$, respectively.
- **Decoders**: $D_A^\phi$ and $D_B^\phi$ reconstruct motions from the latent space. The cross-domain transformation is performed as $\hat{Q}_B = D_B^\phi(E_A^\theta(Q_A, S_A), S_B)$.
- **Optimization Objectives**:
 - **Topology Alignment Loss ($L_{align}$)**: Regularizes the latent space by coercing $z_A$ and $z_B$ from paired motions to be identical, i.e., minimizing the L2 distance between $E_A^\theta(Q_A, S_A)$ and $E_B^\theta(Q_B, S_B)$.
 - **Reconstruction Loss ($L_{recon}$)**: Ensures that the encoders and decoders can accurately reconstruct original motions within their respective domains, minimizing the L2 distance between reconstructed and original joint rotations for both human and robot.
 - **Cycle Consistency Loss ($L_{cycle}$)**: Maintains semantic stability by ensuring that a motion mapped across domains and then re-encoded returns to its original latent representation.
- **Total Pretraining Objective**: $L_{pretrain} = L_{recon} + \lambda_{align} L_{align} + \lambda_{cycle} L_{cycle}$.

**D. Physics-Informed Finetuning**
This phase aims to imbue IKMR with a robust motion prior to ensure physical viability and filter noise, as kinematics-aware pretraining alone does not guarantee hardware-safe trajectories.
- **Simulator-in-the-Loop Reinforcement Learning**: The finetuning is framed as a reinforcement learning problem. A tracking policy $\pi_\theta$ interacts with a physics simulator to maximize an expected reward $J(\theta)$.
 - **Reward Function**: Primarily encourages precise joint position tracking ($r_t = \exp(-\frac{\lVert q_t - \hat{q}_t \rVert_2^2}{\sigma_{jpos}})$), where $q_t$ is the reference target position and $\hat{q}_t$ is the simulated state. Additional penalties are incorporated for joint limits, collisions, and excessive torques to promote stability and physical realism.
 - **Data Curation**: By training policies to track diverse pretrained reference motions (e.g., walking, running), the simulator acts as a physical filter, generating a dataset of highly purified, dynamically verified trajectories.
- **Target Decoder Finetuning**: After policy convergence and data curation, the source encoder $E_A^\theta$ is frozen. Only the target decoder $D_B^\phi$ is finetuned using the dynamically verified trajectories.
 - **End-Effector Loss ($L_{ee}$)**: To ensure precise spatial interactions crucial for real-world tasks, a Forward Kinematics (FK) end-effector loss is added. This loss enforces accuracy at critical contact points, such as hands and feet, by minimizing the L2 distance between the FK-derived end-effector positions from the finetuned output and the purified reference.
- **Total Finetuning Objective**: $L_{finetune} = L_{recon} + \lambda_{ee} L_{ee}$.
This phase shifts the computational burden of ensuring dynamic feasibility from online optimization to offline network inference, teaching the decoder a hardware-safe motion prior.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of IKMR involved experiments in both simulation and real-world deployment on the Unitree G1 humanoid robot, assessing its performance in topological feature alignment, trajectory smoothness, computational efficiency, robustness to noise, and practical utility.

**A. Topology Feature Analysis**
To quantify the effectiveness of the unified topological representation, 24 paired human-to-humanoid motion clips were selected. The Pearson correlation coefficient was used to measure the similarity between the encoded latent vectors of human and humanoid motions.
- **Result**: The maximum correlation coefficient between paired motions reached 0.8638, indicating substantial topological feature alignment across disparate physical embodiments. The mean correlation coefficient for *unpaired* motions was approximately 0.0195, approaching zero, which suggests a uniformly distributed and well-disentangled latent space. This implies that the representation successfully preserves unique motion patterns while ensuring high similarity for corresponding cross-structural motions and low similarity for non-corresponding ones.

**B. Physics-Informed Finetuning (Trajectory Smoothness)**
The physics-informed finetuning phase was designed to embed a hardware-safe motion prior and mitigate physically unrealistic artifacts. Joint acceleration and jerk were used as metrics to compare trajectories before and after finetuning.
- **Result**: After finetuning, the transformed trajectories exhibited significantly lower mean joint acceleration (29.15 rad/s² versus 30.20 rad/s² during pretraining) and mean joint jerk (435.37 rad/s³ versus 456.60 rad/s³).
- **Conclusion**: The finetuning process effectively reduces high-frequency angular changes, yielding smoother and more coordinated movements that are safer for physical hardware, while preserving the semantic characteristics of the source motion.

**C. Comparison and Robustness Analysis**
IKMR was compared against three numerical whole-body control methods: PHC, Mink, and GMR, focusing on computational efficiency and robustness to noisy input data.
- **Computational Efficiency**: IKMR achieved a data transformation throughput of 5,000 frames per second (FPS) on an Intel Core i7-14700HX CPU and NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU. This is approximately 78 times faster than GMR (64 FPS) and significantly outperforms PHC (21 FPS) and Mink (49 FPS). Furthermore, IKMR supports fully vectorized tensor operations, enabling efficient batch processing for large-scale dataset synthesis, a capability lacking in the compared numerical optimization methods.
- **Robustness to Noise**: Gaussian noise with increasing standard deviations was artificially injected into the root translations of source human motions. The outputs were evaluated using Absolute Keypoint Trajectory Error (AKTE) and Mean Key Joint Acceleration (MKJA).
 - **Result**: As noise levels increased, numerical methods (PHC, Mink) showed substantial trajectory drifts and a significant increase in MKJA, indicating sudden and aggressive joint accelerations. In contrast, IKMR maintained significantly lower and more stable AKTE and MKJA values across all noise levels (Figure 9).
 - **Conclusion**: IKMR functions as an intrinsic data curation mechanism, effectively filtering out spatial perturbations and local tremors from noisy inputs. This robustness enhances motor safety and makes the pipeline suitable for processing imperfect real-world data.

**D. Real-World Hardware Validation**
The ultimate utility of IKMR was validated by deploying whole-body imitation controllers, trained on IKMR-curated robotic data, on the physical Unitree G1 robot.
- **Evaluated Skills**: The robot successfully reproduced diverse motor skills, including upper-body manipulations (arm swinging), dynamic lower-body movements (leg kicks), forward locomotion with a natural gait, and agile backward running with a leaning-back posture.
- **Result**: The downstream policies accurately captured and reproduced nuanced human-like stylistic details present in the transformed trajectories, such as hip rotation during walking and torso stabilization during backward running.
- **Conclusion**: The real-world deployments confirmed that IKMR serves as a robust, end-to-end data pipeline, effectively converting unconstrained human motion collections into high-quality, executable training assets for physical humanoid robot learning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presents Implicit Kinodynamic Motion Retargeting (IKMR) as a significant advancement in bridging the gap between human motion data and robotic control, with several potential impacts on the field of humanoid robotics and beyond:

1. **Alleviating Data Scarcity in Robotics**: By offering a scalable and efficient method to transform abundant human motion data into robot-executable trajectories, IKMR directly addresses the critical data scarcity bottleneck in humanoid robot learning. This reduces the prohibitive costs and risks associated with collecting extensive robot-specific motion data, democratizing access to diverse motor repertoires.

2. **Enabling Large-Scale Dataset Generation**: The high throughput of 5,000 frames per second and batch scalability of IKMR fundamentally changes the paradigm of data preparation for robotics. It enables the creation of massive, high-fidelity robotic motion datasets at an unprecedented rate. Such large-scale datasets are crucial for training the next generation of generalist humanoid foundation models, similar to the role of vast text and image datasets in language and vision AI.

3. **Ensuring Hardware Safety and Physical Feasibility**: The physics-informed finetuning phase is a key contribution, as it instills a hardware-safe motion prior into the generated trajectories. This mechanism implicitly filters out noise, jitter, and flickering from imperfect human source data, producing smooth, dynamically feasible motions. This directly translates to safer and more robust deployment on physical robots, minimizing the risk of mechanical degradation or unsafe behaviors.

4. **Robustness to Imperfect "In-the-Wild" Data**: IKMR's ability to act as an intrinsic data curation engine, purifying noisy inputs from sources like in-the-wild videos, significantly broadens the usable spectrum of human motion data. This makes it practical to leverage highly diverse, unconstrained human movements without extensive manual cleaning or pre-processing, which is often a barrier for traditional methods.

5. **Bridging the Simulation-to-Reality Gap**: The successful real-world deployment on the Unitree G1 robot validates that the IKMR-transformed data is not merely theoretically sound but practically effective for training controllers that operate on physical hardware. This demonstrates a reliable pathway for transferring skills learned from human demonstrations to actual robot platforms.

6. **Catalyst for Continuous Learning and Generalization**: IKMR facilitates a "continuous assimilation loop," where newly encountered unconstrained human motions can be safely regularized and integrated into the robot's existing skill repertoire. This prevents the accumulation of disjointed "long-tail" corner cases and promotes structural bootstrapping of robust generalization, allowing robots to continuously expand their capabilities.

In the long term, scalable data pipelines like IKMR are poised to serve as an indispensable bridge between multimodal generative models (which can synthesize diverse human-like motions) and physical robot learning systems. This integration has the potential to unlock a vast amount of synthetic and real-world data, fundamentally accelerating the development of more intelligent, versatile, and human-like humanoid robots.
