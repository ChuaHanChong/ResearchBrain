# Implicit Kinodynamic Motion Retargeting for Human-to-humanoid Imitation Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> The Implicit Kinodynamic Motion Retargeting (IKMR) framework translates human motion into robot-executable trajectories for humanoid imitation learning, leveraging a two-stage neural network approach. This method achieves a retargeting speed of 5000 frames per second and enables a Unitree G1 humanoid robot to accurately track diverse, dynamically feasible motions.

## Problem

- Existing human-to-humanoid motion retargeting methods are computationally expensive and lack scalability for large-scale human motion datasets.
- Many learning-based retargeting approaches, particularly neural network-based ones, often neglect dynamic constraints, leading to physically impossible or unstable robot motions.
- Obtaining diverse and large-scale motion data directly from humanoid robots is challenging, creating a need for robust methods to leverage abundant human motion data efficiently.

## Method

- IKMR employs a two-stage learning framework consisting of kinematics-aware pretraining and dynamics-aware fine-tuning.
- Kinematics-aware pretraining utilizes a dual encoder-decoder architecture with skeleton-based graph convolutional networks to learn a unified latent motion representation for both human and humanoid skeletons.
- Dynamics-aware fine-tuning refines the retargeted motions by training an imitation tracker in a simulator to generate physically feasible data, then fine-tuning only the humanoid decoder to ensure dynamic plausibility.

## Results

- IKMR achieved a retargeting speed of 5000 frames per second on an Intel Core i7-14700HX with an RTX 4090, which is approximately 100 times faster than traditional numerical optimization methods.
- Dynamics-aware fine-tuning produced smoother motions with lower mean acceleration (29.15 rad/s²) and jerk (435.37 rad/s³) compared to pre-trained motions, effectively mitigating unrealistic physical phenomena.
- The framework demonstrated superior robustness to noise in source human motion, maintaining lower absolute keypoints trajectory error and mean key joints acceleration, and successfully enabled a full-size Unitree G1 humanoid robot to track diverse human-like movements in real-world deployment.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Learning a unified topological latent space via graph convolutional networks allows for efficient and generalized kinematic motion transfer between anatomically different human and humanoid skeletons.
> - Separating kinematic mapping from dynamic refinement into distinct learning stages enables both efficient latent space learning and precise integration of physical constraints.
> - Fine-tuning the decoder with physically feasible motions generated by a simulator-trained imitation tracker effectively imbues the retargeted motions with dynamic properties, crucial for stable real-robot deployment.
