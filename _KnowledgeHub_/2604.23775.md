# Vision-Language-Action Safety: Threats, Challenges, Evaluations, and Mechanisms

> [!summary] Summary
> Li et al. (2026) provide the first comprehensive review of safety issues in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, consolidating fragmented research across various communities. The work establishes taxonomies for threats and defenses, analyzes evaluation methodologies, and outlines critical open problems for the safe development and deployment of embodied AI.

## Problem

- Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models introduce new, amplified safety challenges due to their embodied nature, leading to irreversible physical consequences, multimodal attack surfaces, and real-time operational constraints.
- Research on VLA safety is currently fragmented across disparate academic communities, resulting in a lack of unified understanding of the threat landscape, defense mechanisms, and evaluation protocols.
- Minor perception errors can cascade across long-horizon action sequences into catastrophic failures, while training on diverse, often unvetted demonstration data exposes VLA models to poisoning and backdoor vulnerabilities absent in text-only LLMs.

## Method

- Established a unified ==taxonomy for VLA safety==, classifying threats and defense mechanisms based on attack and defense timing.
- Systematically reviewed both ==training-time== and ==inference-time attacks== (data poisoning, jailbreaks) alongside corresponding defense mechanisms (policy optimization, runtime monitoring).
- Analyzed current VLA safety benchmarks and evaluation metrics, identifying critical methodology gaps across **six major deployment domains**.

## Results

- Established a unified taxonomy categorizing VLA threats and defense mechanisms by timing (training/inference), mapping specific attacks and defenses.
- Analysis revealed diverse attacks: training-time backdoors (**GoBA**, **SilentDrift**), inference-time semantic jailbreaks (**RoboPAIR**), and physical interventions.
- Survey identified a **fragmented VLA safety evaluation landscape**, highlighting deficiencies in benchmarks and metrics for real-world, long-horizon assessment.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Embodied VLA systems face unique, amplified safety challenges compared to text-only models, driven by real-world physical interactions and multimodal attack surfaces.
> - Both training-time (e.g., backdoors, data poisoning) and inference-time attacks (e.g., semantic jailbreaks, physical interventions) pose diverse and complex threats to VLA models.
> - Addressing VLA safety requires a multi-layered defense strategy, balancing proactive training-time alignment with reactive inference-time guardrails, while current evaluation methodologies remain fragmented.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represent an evolving paradigm in embodied artificial intelligence, unifying visual perception, natural language understanding, and physical action generation within a single neural framework. Historically, robotic systems relied on modular perception-planning-control stacks. However, VLA policies, by integrating large pretrained vision-language models, have demonstrated enhanced generalization capabilities, enabling robots to execute diverse manipulation tasks from natural language instructions, adapt to novel environments, and perform rudimentary commonsense reasoning about physical interactions. This shift has been propelled by innovations such as language-conditioned robot policies (e.g., SayCan), LLM reasoning for robot program generation (e.g., Code as Policies, VoxPoser), and direct adaptation of vision-language pretraining for robotic control (e.g., RT-2, OpenVLA, Octo, π
0, SpatialVLA).

The increasing capability and deployment of VLA models in real-world domains, including autonomous driving, healthcare, industrial manufacturing, and service robotics, introduce a new class of safety challenges. These challenges differ from those faced by text-only Large Language Models (LLMs) due to the embodied nature of VLA systems, which entails irreversible physical consequences, a multimodal attack surface, real-time latency constraints for defense mechanisms, error propagation over long-horizon trajectories, and vulnerabilities within the data supply chain.

Despite the growing recognition of these issues, the existing VLA safety literature remains fragmented across various research communities, including robotic learning, adversarial machine learning, AI alignment, and autonomous systems safety. Previous surveys have addressed adjacent topics such as LLM safety or embodied AI capabilities but have not provided a unified and comprehensive treatment of the VLA safety landscape. This survey aims to consolidate and structure the knowledge in this emergent field, bridging the gaps between these disparate research areas.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The motivation for this survey stems from the unique and amplified safety challenges posed by Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models compared to their predecessors.

**Motivation:**
1. **Physical Consequences:** Unlike text-only LLMs, VLA models operate in the physical world, where unsafe actions can lead to irreversible physical damage, injury, or loss of life. A miscalculation by an autonomous vehicle or a misplaced surgical tool cannot be mitigated by a content filter after the fact, necessitating proactive and robust safety measures.
2. **Multimodal Attack Surface:** VLA systems integrate visual, linguistic, and proprioceptive state inputs, offering adversaries multiple channels for attack. This expanded attack surface includes adversarial patches on visual inputs, textual jailbreaks in language instructions, and poisoning of proprioceptive state data, requiring defenses that span multiple modalities.
3. **Real-Time Latency Constraints:** Embodied VLA systems often require millisecond-scale decision-making. Safety interventions, if computationally intensive, can introduce latency that renders correct decisions ineffective in time-critical scenarios, posing fundamental trade-offs between safety and operational efficiency.
4. **Error Propagation over Long-Horizon Trajectories:** A minor perception error or an adversarial perturbation in a VLA system can cascade and accumulate over extended action sequences, leading to catastrophic failures in multi-step tasks. This makes robustness under distribution shift a safety-critical rather than merely a performance concern.
5. **Data Supply Chain Vulnerabilities:** VLA models are typically fine-tuned on diverse demonstration datasets, often collected from various, potentially unvetted sources. This exposes the data supply chain to training-time vulnerabilities, such as data poisoning and backdoor attacks, which have no direct analog in text-only models and can embed malicious behaviors before deployment.
6. **Fragmented Research Landscape:** The research on VLA safety is currently distributed across multiple academic communities (e.g., robotic learning, adversarial machine learning, AI alignment), leading to limited cross-pollination and a lack of a unified understanding of the threat landscape, defense mechanisms, and evaluation protocols.

#### 2.1. Key Objectives

To address these motivations, the survey outlines four key contributions:
1. **Unified Threat and Defense Taxonomy:** To establish a structured framework for understanding VLA safety, organizing adversarial threats and protective mechanisms along two parallel timing axes: attack timing (training-time vs. inference-time) and defense timing (training-time vs. inference-time). This taxonomy aims to link specific threats to the stages at which they can be mitigated and identify existing research gaps.
2. **Comprehensive Review of Attacks and Defenses:** To systematically survey both training-time attacks (e.g., data poisoning, backdoors) and inference-time attacks (e.g., adversarial perturbations, jailbreaks, freezing attacks), along with their corresponding defense mechanisms and practical implications for deployment.
3. **Safety Benchmark and Metric Analysis:** To provide the first structured analysis of existing VLA safety benchmarks and evaluation metrics, identifying critical gaps in current evaluation methodologies and proposing desiderata for future benchmark design.
4. **Real-World Deployment Perspective:** To survey safety challenges across six major real-world deployment domains (e.g., autonomous driving, household robotics), synthesize cross-domain safety patterns, and identify pressing open problems such as certified robustness for embodied trajectories, physically realizable defenses, safety-aware training, unified runtime safety architectures, and standardized evaluation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

This paper functions as a comprehensive survey, systematically organizing and analyzing the emergent field of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) safety. The methodology primarily involves a structured review and synthesis of existing literature rather than presenting new empirical research.

The core organizational principle of the survey is a taxonomy based on two parallel timing axes:
1. **Attack Timing:** Distinguishing between **training-time attacks** (vulnerabilities exploited during model training, such as data poisoning or backdoor injection) and **inference-time attacks** (vulnerabilities exploited during real-time model deployment, such as adversarial perturbations or semantic jailbreaks).
2. **Defense Timing:** Categorizing defense mechanisms based on when they are applied, either during **training-time** (e.g., data alignment, policy optimization) or **inference-time** (e.g., runtime monitoring, physical fail-safes). This framework aims to pair each identified threat with the appropriate stage for mitigation.

The survey examines the literature through four primary lenses:
1. **Attacks:** This lens details various adversarial threats.
 - **Training-Time Attacks (Section 3):** Covers input-centric backdoors (e.g., physical triggers, cross-modal triggers) and temporal and state-space backdoors (e.g., exploiting action chunking mechanisms, proprioceptive state-space poisoning).
 - **Inference-Time Attacks (Section 5.1):** Explores semantic jailbreaks that manipulate language instructions, visual perturbations (digital and physical), and physical interventions that interact with the robot's environment or sensors.
2. **Defenses:** This lens reviews mechanisms designed to counteract these threats.
 - **Training-Time Defenses (Section 4):** Discusses strategies like data, perception, and reward-centric alignment (e.g., pedagogical supervision, self-evolving training, multimodal perception augmentation), policy-centric safety optimization (e.g., constrained safety alignment, post-training safety unlearning, online safety filtering), and human-in-the-loop policy refinement.
 - **Inference-Time Defenses (Section 5.2):** Addresses decision-layer guardrails (e.g., CBF-based filtering, LLM-translated logic), runtime monitoring (e.g., heuristic interventions, VLM-based supervision), and physical fail-safes (e.g., kinematic projections, compliant control).
3. **Evaluation (Section 6):** This lens critically assesses how VLA safety is currently measured.
 - **Safety Benchmarks (Section 6.1):** Categorizes and analyzes existing benchmarks into groups such as adversarial robustness benchmarks (e.g., VLA-Risk, VLATest), task-level safety benchmarks (e.g., SafeAgentBench, AgentSafe), comprehensive capability-and-safety benchmarks (e.g., VLA-Arena, LIBERO-PRO), jailbreak and alignment benchmarks (e.g., BadRobot, RoboPAIR), and runtime monitoring and semantic alignment benchmarks (e.g., ASIMOV, SAFE-SMART).
 - **Metrics (Section 6.2):** Discusses the various metrics used to quantify safety performance, including task-level safety metrics (e.g., Safety Violation Rate, Rejection Rate), behavioral safety metrics (e.g., Collision Rate, Safety Score), robustness metrics (e.g., Attack Success Rate, Performance Drop Rate), and composite/deployment-oriented metrics (e.g., Net Value, Pareto frontiers).
4. **Real-World Deployment Scenarios (Section 7):** This lens examines the practical safety implications across diverse application domains. It covers autonomous driving, household and domestic robotics, industrial manufacturing, healthcare and assistive robotics, service and public-space robotics, and outdoor and field deployment, synthesizing cross-domain safety challenges such as the sim-to-reality gap, runtime verification, and the safety–capability trade-off.

Finally, the survey identifies and discusses key open problems and future research directions (Section 8), emphasizing the need for certified robustness, physically realizable defenses, safety-aware training paradigms, unified runtime architectures, standardized evaluation, lifecycle safety, and considerations related to regulation, ethics, and societal impact. The authors also maintain an associated GitHub repository for ongoing updates and community contributions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The survey provides a synthesis of the current state of VLA safety research, highlighting the complexity and multi-faceted nature of the challenges.

#### 4.1. Threat Landscape

The survey identifies a diverse and expanding range of threats against VLA models, categorized by their timing:
- **Training-Time Attacks:**
 - **Input-Centric Backdoors:** These involve injecting poisoned samples during training to establish a hidden mapping between a trigger and a malicious action. Examples include `BadVLA` (objective-decoupled optimization for stealthy manipulation) and `DropVLA` (targeted hijacking using composite visual/linguistic triggers). `GoBA` further demonstrates that common 3D physical objects can serve as robust backdoor triggers, highlighting vulnerabilities to real-world environmental manipulation.
 - **Temporal and State-Space Backdoors:** These exploit the dynamic architectural features of VLA models. `SilentDrift` shows how action chunking mechanisms (which create open-loop visual periods) can be exploited to inject dynamically smooth, C2 continuous adversarial drifts that evade traditional detectors. `Clean-Action` explores how small, benign perturbations can accumulate over long-horizon tasks to induce catastrophic failures without explicit label corruption. `State Backdoor` demonstrates poisoning in the proprioceptive state space, where initial joint configurations can act as stealthy triggers.
- **Inference-Time Attacks:**
 - **Semantic Jailbreaks:** These attacks leverage the natural language interface. `RoboPAIR` and `BadRobot` demonstrate that natural language prompts can bypass safety alignment, leading to "Output-Action Mismatch" where a model linguistically refuses but physically executes an unsafe action. Gradient-guided discrete search methods (e.g., `Adv-Robo`) further optimize prompts to hijack global action spaces.
 - **Visual Perturbations:** Attacks like `VLA-Fool` exploit the fragility of cross-modal alignment, inducing representation drift that propagates errors into continuous control, potentially causing "action-freezing" (e.g., `FreezeVLA`). Physical visual injections, such as deceptive signs, can exploit trust boundary confusion in the real world (e.g., `Visual Injection`).
 - **Physical Interventions:** These target the physical-digital boundary. `AARONS` demonstrates manipulating environmental landmarks to cause navigation errors. `Phantom Menace` illustrates direct sensor signal injection (e.g., optical patterns). As mentioned, `SilentDrift` can also be seen as an inference-time physical intervention once the backdoor is planted, exploiting action chunking to cause subtle cumulative drifts during execution.

#### 4.2. Defense Mechanisms

Defenses are also categorized by timing, addressing the vulnerabilities at different stages:
- **Training-Time Defenses:** Focus on preventing unsafe behaviors from being learned.
 - **Data, Perception, and Reward-Centric Alignment:** Strategies like `EvoVLA` (stage-aligned rewards, pose-based exploration) and `Pedagogical Alignment` (structured supervision) guide learning towards semantically meaningful progress signals. `Safe-Night VLA` augments perception with thermal and depth inputs to reduce ambiguity in safety-critical scenes.
 - **Policy-Centric Safety Optimization:** Methods like `SafeVLA` formulate safety as a constrained Markov Decision Process (CMDP), integrating safety constraints directly into the learning objective. `SORL` uses a safety critic for multi-objective optimization. `VLA-Forget` explores post-training unlearning to selectively remove undesirable behaviors.
 - **Human-in-the-Loop Policy Refinement:** `APO` converts human interventions into preference alignment signals, while `Hi-ORS` uses reward-aware rejection sampling for online refinement, ensuring that harmful experiences are filtered before reinforcement.
- **Inference-Time Defenses:** Aim to mitigate threats during real-time operation, often balancing safety-latency and safety-performance trade-offs.
 - **Decision-Layer Guardrails:** These intercept unsafe actions before execution. `AEGIS` uses Control Barrier Functions (CBFs) for high-speed collision avoidance, offering geometric rigidity but risking "over-refusal." `RoboGuard` and modular MPC frameworks use LLMs to translate natural language safety rules into formal logic (e.g., Signal Temporal Logic, STL), providing semantic comprehension at the cost of latency. Lightweight semantic constraints like those in `HazardArena` offer a compromise.
 - **Runtime Monitoring:** `REFLECT` and `FailSafe` use external VLMs to inspect execution rollouts, diagnose root causes, and trigger re-planning or halts, but are limited by computational burden. Heuristic methods like `BYOVLA` offer faster, perception-based interventions.
 - **Physical Fail-safes:** Operating at ultra-high frequencies, these enforce strict hardware boundaries and dynamic force constraints. `ATACOM` projects collision-prone commands onto safe boundaries. `CompliantVLA` translates intent into variable impedance control for safe contact-rich manipulation.

#### 4.3. Evaluation and Benchmarks

The survey reveals a rapidly evolving but fragmented evaluation landscape.
- **Multi-Level Benchmarks:** Range from basic capability (e.g., `VLA-Arena`) and physical robustness (e.g., `VLA-Risk`, `VLATest`) to normative alignment (e.g., `ASIMOV` for constitutional rules), semantic awareness (e.g., `HazardArena`), linguistic robustness (e.g., `ICR-Drive`), and human-centric awareness. Benchmarks like `LIBERO-PRO` highlight the importance of robust evaluation beyond memorization.
- **Metrics:** Include task-level safety metrics (Safety Violation Rate, Rejection Rate, Task Success Rate), behavioral safety metrics (Collision Rate, Safety Score, Success Weighted by Path Length), robustness metrics (Attack Success Rate, Performance Drop Rate, Certified Robustness Radius), and composite/deployment-oriented metrics (Net Value, Safety-Performance Pareto frontier, Temporal Persistence).
- **Uncertainty Calibration:** Acknowledges the shift from reactive failure counting to proactive self-awareness. Frameworks like `SAFE` use latent features for failure detection, while Expected Calibration Error (ECE) quantifies a model's predictive confidence, essential for triggering appropriate defense mechanisms.

#### 4.4. Real-World Deployment Scenarios

The survey details how safety challenges manifest differently across six critical domains:
- **Autonomous Driving:** Hallucinations, misinterpretations of traffic semantics, latency-safety trade-offs, and compliance with explicit traffic rules are paramount.
- **Household Robotics:** Generalization beyond training data, safe interaction with vulnerable users, and hazard-aware long-horizon planning in unstructured environments are key.
- **Industrial Manufacturing:** Strict safety standards (e.g., ISO 10218), high physical consequences of errors, human-robot collaboration, and demand for reliability/reproducibility are critical.
- **Healthcare and Assistive Robotics:** Near-zero tolerance for errors, domain-specific constraints (e.g., sterility, precise interaction), and stringent regulatory requirements define the safety landscape.
- **Service and Public-Space Robotics:** Socially compliant behavior, robustness to public uncertainty, resilience against intentional interference, and nuanced safety evaluation (beyond simple collisions) are crucial.
- **Outdoor and Field Deployment:** Strong environmental variation (weather, terrain), sensor degradation, and the potential for delayed/distributed consequences of errors pose unique challenges.

#### 4.5. Cross-Domain Safety Challenges

Recurring themes include:
- **Simulation-to-Reality Gap:** Most evaluations are in simulation, but physical deployment introduces noise, latency, and environmental variability not fully captured by benchmarks.
- **Scalable Runtime Verification:** Exhaustive pre-deployment testing is infeasible, necessitating continuous monitoring and adaptive fallback mechanisms.
- **Safety–Capability Pareto Frontier:** A fundamental tension exists between achieving high task performance and ensuring robust safety, requiring domain-dependent operating points.
- **Regulation, Accountability, and Safety Drift:** VLA models' stochastic and opaque nature challenges traditional certification, especially with post-deployment updates that can introduce safety regressions.
- **Fleet-Level and Multi-Agent Safety:** Failures can cascade across coordinated agents, requiring fleet-wide monitoring and coordination-aware safety policies.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This survey holds significant importance for the advancement of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models and their responsible deployment. Its primary significance lies in being the first comprehensive and unified synthesis of the rapidly evolving VLA safety literature, which has historically been fragmented across disparate research communities.

#### 5.1. Significance

1. **Unified Framework:** By proposing a structured taxonomy based on attack and defense timing, the survey provides a principled framework for understanding the VLA safety landscape. This organization helps researchers situate existing work, identify interconnections, and clarify the appropriate stage for mitigating various threats.
2. **Identifies Critical Gaps:** The detailed review of attacks, defenses, and evaluation methodologies highlights specific vulnerabilities (e.g., temporal action chunking exploits, state-space poisoning), limitations in current defense mechanisms (e.g., lack of formal guarantees, safety-latency trade-offs), and deficiencies in evaluation protocols (e.g., simulation dominance, heterogeneous metrics). This systematic identification of gaps is crucial for directing future research efforts.
3. **Cross-Disciplinary Bridge:** The survey explicitly connects concepts from robotic learning, adversarial machine learning, AI alignment, and control theory. This cross-pollination of ideas is essential for developing holistic safety solutions that address the multimodal, embodied nature of VLA systems.
4. **Highlights Embodiment-Specific Challenges:** The paper meticulously distinguishes VLA safety challenges from those of text-only LLMs, emphasizing the unique complexities arising from physical consequences, real-time constraints, and long-horizon error propagation. This helps to tailor research specifically to embodied AI.

**Potential Impact:**
1. **Guidance for Future Research:** By outlining urgent open problems, such as certified robustness for embodied trajectories, physically realizable defenses, safety-aware training paradigms, unified runtime architectures, and standardized evaluation, the survey provides a roadmap for future research. It encourages a shift from reactive problem-solving to proactive safety-by-design.
2. **Accelerating Development of Safe VLA Systems:** By consolidating knowledge and clarifying the state of the art, the survey can accelerate the development of more robust, reliable, and ethically deployable VLA systems. It promotes building safety as a "first-class design objective" alongside capability and efficiency.
3. **Fostering Collaboration:** The identified interconnectedness of challenges across different research areas calls for increased collaboration among diverse scientific communities, which this survey is designed to facilitate.
4. **Informing Policy and Regulation:** The analysis of real-world deployment scenarios and cross-domain challenges, including the sim-to-reality gap, regulatory misalignment, and lifecycle safety, can inform policymakers and regulatory bodies in developing appropriate guidelines and frameworks for high-risk AI systems.
5. **Resource for Practitioners:** The comprehensive nature of the survey, along with the accompanying GitHub repository, serves as a valuable living resource for researchers, practitioners, and policymakers entering the VLA safety domain, providing a foundation for understanding and addressing the complexities of VLA safety in practice.

In conclusion, this survey serves as a foundational document for the VLA safety community, aiming to transform a fragmented field into a cumulative scientific discipline equipped with shared threat models, reproducible benchmarks, and theoretical frameworks, ultimately working towards the safe and responsible realization of general-purpose embodied intelligence.
