# Attend Before Attention: Efficient and Scalable Video Understanding via Autoregressive Gazing

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from UC Berkeley, NVIDIA, MIT, and Clarifai developed AutoGaze, a lightweight module that enables Multi-modal Large Language Models to efficiently process long-form, high-resolution videos by adaptively selecting multi-scale informative visual patches before attention. This approach achieved up to 19x Vision Transformer and 10x MLLM latency reduction, scaling MLLMs to 1K frames at 4K resolution and improving performance by 10.1% on the new HLVid benchmark compared to baselines.

## Problem

- Multi-modal Large Language Models (MLLMs) face significant computational and memory challenges when processing long-form, high-resolution videos.
- Existing MLLM architectures process every pixel uniformly across frames, leading to high computational costs due to spatiotemporal redundancy in video data.
- Current token reduction techniques primarily prune tokens *after* the Vision Transformer (ViT), leaving the ViT as a bottleneck unable to scale efficiently for high-resolution, long-duration content.

## Method

- Introduces **AutoGaze**, a compact (3M parameters) module that employs ==autoregressive gazing== to identify and select a minimal set of multi-scale visual patches *before* ViT processing.
- AutoGaze utilizes an autoregressive transformer decoder with a multi-scale patch vocabulary and an automatic gazing length decision head to select patches that reconstruct the original video within a user-defined error.
- The model is trained with a two-stage pipeline: Next-Token Prediction (NTP) pre-training on ground-truth gazing sequences, followed by Reinforcement Learning (RL) post-training to refine gazing quality.

## Results

- Achieved significant visual token reduction (**4
x** to **100
x**) and substantial latency reductions (up to **19
x** for ViT, **10
x** for MLLM), enabling processing of **1K** frames at **4K** resolution without Out-Of-Memory errors.
- Enabled an MLLM (NVILA-**8B**-Video) to achieve a **10.1%** performance gain on the new HLVid benchmark (from **42.5%** to **52.6%**), outperforming previous state-of-the-art MLLMs like GPT-4o (**49.3%**).
- Outperformed heuristic gazing methods and existing MLLM token reduction techniques by achieving higher reconstruction quality with fewer patches and providing more comprehensive efficiency improvements.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - AutoGaze effectively mimics human visual perception by prioritizing dynamic (high optical flow) and detailed regions, adaptively using coarser scales for broad areas and finer scales for intricate content.
> - The system demonstrates robust generalization to out-of-distribution video content, maintaining consistent and effective gazing patterns across varied styles and semantics.
> - Pre-ViT token reduction is critical for comprehensive efficiency gains, as it alleviates the primary computational bottleneck in video MLLMs beyond what post-ViT pruning can achieve.
