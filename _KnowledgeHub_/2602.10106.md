# EgoHumanoid: Unlocking In-the-Wild Loco-Manipulation with Robot-Free Egocentric Demonstration

> [!summary] Summary
> The EgoHumanoid framework equips humanoid robots with loco-manipulation capabilities in diverse real-world environments, by integrating robot-free egocentric human demonstrations. A dedicated alignment pipeline effectively bridges human-robot embodiment differences, significantly enhancing generalization over robot-only training.

## Problem

- Humanoid loco-manipulation is bottlenecked by the scarcity of diverse, large-scale demonstration data.
- Existing data collection methods rely on expensive robot teleoperation, confining learning to laboratory settings and hindering real-world generalization.
- A significant embodiment gap exists between humans and humanoid robots, complicating the transfer of skills for whole-body loco-manipulation.

## Method

- Developed a portable, ==VR-based data collection system== adaptable for capturing both egocentric human demonstrations and robot teleoperation.
- Implemented a ==human-to-humanoid alignment pipeline==, including ==depth-based view alignment== and unified ==6-DoF delta end-effector poses== with discrete locomotion commands for action alignment.
- Co-trained a single **EgoHumanoid** ==Vision-Language-Action (VLA)== policy on the combined, aligned human and robot datasets, omitting proprioceptive states for compatibility.

## Results

- Co-training improved in-domain task success rates by **19** percentage points (**78%** vs. **59%**) and generalization to novel environments by **51** percentage points (**82%** vs. **31%**) compared to robot-only policies.
- Human-only models achieved **100%** success on navigation-dominated subtasks, demonstrating high transferability of locomotion skills, while co-training was essential for precision manipulation.
- Policy performance consistently scaled with increased human demonstration data, and optimal robot-to-human sampling ratios varied based on task precision requirements.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Egocentric human demonstrations offer a scalable, efficient, and diverse data source (approximately 2x faster than robot teleoperation) for complex robot skill learning.
> - Principled view and action alignment pipelines are critical for successfully bridging the human-robot embodiment gap, enabling effective cross-embodiment transfer.
> - Co-training with human data provides robust priors for robot navigation and coarse manipulation, which can then be refined for precision tasks when combined with robot-specific demonstrations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Humanoid robots are envisioned for diverse human-centric environments, requiring complex loco-manipulation capabilities—the coordinated execution of whole-body locomotion and dexterous manipulation. Developing these capabilities is significantly constrained by the scarcity of diverse, large-scale demonstration data. Current methods predominantly rely on robot teleoperation, which is expensive, complex, and limits data collection to controlled laboratory settings due to hardware and safety considerations. This restriction hinders the generalization of policies to real-world, varied environments.

Recent advancements in wearable sensing have enabled the collection of egocentric human demonstrations using portable devices, offering a scalable source of behaviorally rich and environmentally diverse data. This paradigm has shown promise in robot-arm manipulation and navigation, often through pre-training visual representations or motion priors, or via co-training on aligned observation and action spaces. However, applying egocentric human demonstrations to the more challenging domain of *humanoid loco-manipulation* remains largely unexplored. The fundamental "embodiment gap" between humans and humanoids—encompassing differences in physical morphology, limb proportions, joint limits, egocentric visual observations (e.g., perceiving human hands versus metallic manipulators from distinct viewpoints), and motion dynamics (e.g., walking patterns, balance strategies)—presents substantial hurdles for direct transfer. These discrepancies are particularly pronounced in loco-manipulation, where whole-body movement amplifies viewpoint changes and motion variations.

This research addresses these challenges by introducing a framework for human-to-humanoid co-training for whole-body loco-manipulation. It builds upon previous efforts in egocentric data utilization and cross-embodiment data alignment, distinguishing itself by tackling the unique complexities of humanoid robots. Existing co-training approaches primarily focus on fixed-base manipulation or decouple locomotion and manipulation for mobile robots. This work, in contrast, investigates the tightly-coupled whole-body coordination required for humanoid loco-manipulation, aiming to leverage the environmental diversity inherent in human demonstrations to enhance robot generalization. It extends vision-language-action (VLA) policies, which have been developed for manipulation by incorporating lower-body robot action commands, to a paradigm where human demonstrations serve as a primary source of scalable, diverse behavioral data.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable humanoid robots to perform loco-manipulation tasks across diverse, real-world environments by leveraging abundant egocentric human demonstrations. This overarching goal is driven by several specific motivations and sub-objectives:

1. **Address Data Scarcity and Lack of Diversity:** Current robot learning for humanoid loco-manipulation is bottlenecked by the limited scale and diversity of available robot teleoperation data, which is typically confined to laboratory settings. Human demonstrations offer a scalable and environmentally rich alternative. The motivation is to unlock this data source to expand the behavioral repertoire and generalization capabilities of humanoids.
2. **Bridge the Human-Humanoid Embodiment Gap:** A significant challenge is the inherent discrepancy between human and humanoid morphology, kinematics, and visual viewpoints. The research aims to develop a systematic alignment pipeline to effectively bridge this gap, ensuring that human demonstrations can be converted into robot-compatible training signals. This involves addressing visual domain shifts (e.g., camera height, perspective) and action representation differences (e.g., limb proportions, joint limits).
3. **Enable Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Co-Training:** The objective is to establish a framework that can co-train a single VLA policy using both large-scale, robot-free human demonstrations and a smaller set of embodiment-accurate robot data. This approach seeks to combine the diversity and scalability of human data with the precision and embodiment-consistency of robot data.
4. **Validate Generalization to Unseen Environments:** A key motivation is to demonstrate that incorporating human data can significantly improve the generalization abilities of humanoid policies, allowing them to operate effectively in "in-the-wild" scenes that were not part of the robot's training environment. This directly tests the hypothesis that human demonstrations expose policies to variations that robot-only data rarely covers.
5. **Analyze Transfer Mechanisms and Data Scaling:** The study aims to provide insights into which specific behaviors (e.g., locomotion vs. manipulation) transfer most effectively from human demonstrations. Furthermore, it seeks to investigate how policy performance scales with increasing amounts of human demonstration data and how data sampling strategies influence co-training outcomes.
6. **Develop Practical Data Collection and Alignment Protocols:** To facilitate the transfer, the research endeavors to design a portable, unified hardware setup for both human and robot data collection and establish practical protocols for effective human data capture and subsequent alignment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The EGOHUMANOID framework is designed to co-train a vision-language-action (VLA) policy for humanoid loco-manipulation by integrating egocentric human demonstrations and teleoperated robot data. The methodology encompasses problem formalization, a unified data collection system, a multi-stage embodiment alignment pipeline, and a co-training procedure.

**A. Problem Setup:**
The goal is to train a VLA model on a combined dataset (D = D_robot ∪ D_human) to perform loco-manipulation tasks in novel real-world environments. D_robot comprises teleoperated robot demonstrations in controlled laboratory settings, while D_human consists of egocentric human demonstrations collected across diverse real-world scenarios. The policy's performance is evaluated both in-domain (laboratory settings) and in generalization settings (scenes covered by human data but absent from robot data). A Unitree G1 humanoid robot (1.3m tall, 29 DoF) is used, focusing on tasks requiring whole-body coordination (locomotion and manipulation).

**B. Data Collection System:**
A unified, portable VR-based system is developed to mitigate embodiment and visual gaps at the data collection level and support human-robot co-training.
- **Human Data Collection:** A PICO VR headset is worn by a human subject, along with five PICO Motion Trackers. A head-mounted ZED X Mini camera captures synchronized egocentric RGB images. The PICO SDK records full-body human motion, including 24 body keypoints and 26 hand keypoints per hand. This setup enables scalable, robot-free data capture in varied indoor and outdoor scenarios.
- **Robot Data Collection:** Robot demonstrations are acquired via VR-based teleoperation. An operator uses a PICO VR headset and handheld controllers to issue discrete navigation commands (e.g., forward/backward, turn) and wrist pose commands (derived from controller-to-headset relative pose). These commands are converted to joint-level actions via inverse kinematics and executed on the Unitree G1 humanoid. A low-level locomotion policy ensures stable whole-body execution. Grasping actions for the Dex3 dexterous hand are controlled by a controller trigger. A head-mounted ZED X Mini camera records synchronized egocentric RGB images. Human data collection is reported to be approximately 2x faster than robot teleoperation.

**C. Human-to-Humanoid Alignment:**
This is a critical component for transforming human demonstrations into robot-compatible training data, addressing visual and action representation discrepancies.
- **View Alignment:** A three-stage process transforms human egocentric images to approximate robot camera viewpoints, compensating for height differences.
 1. **Depth Estimation:** MoGe infers an affine-invariant per-pixel 3D point map and a scale-invariant depth map from human egocentric images.
 2. **Point Cloud Transformation & Reprojection:** The recovered 3D points are transformed into the target robot camera frame (simulating a 0.25m downward shift with ±0.05m noise) and projected onto the target image plane.
 3. **Inpainting:** Latent diffusion-based inpainting fills missing regions resulting from invalid 3D predictions or view-dependent disocclusions, generating complete RGB images that mimic robot egocentric inputs.
- **Action Alignment:** A unified action space is designed to accommodate both embodiments while respecting kinematic differences. All human-derived signals are downsampled from 100 Hz to 20 Hz to match robot control frequency.
 - **Upper Body:** Actions are parameterized as 6-DoF delta end-effector poses (changes in position and orientation). Human wrist poses are expressed in a pelvis-centric frame, smoothed using a Savitzky-Golay filter, and filtered in the SO(3) tangent space for rotations. This avoids global frame dependence and joint-level retargeting complexities.
 - **Lower Body:** Human pelvis trajectories are converted into the same discrete commands used for robot teleoperation (forward/backward, left/right, turn left/right, stand/squat). This involves Savitzky-Golay smoothing, instantaneous heading estimation, projection to local frame velocities, quantization into discrete bins, and thresholding pelvis height changes for stand/squat primitives.
 - **Gripper:** Represented as a binary variable (0 for open, 1 for closed). For robot data, this is directly recorded. For human demonstrations, 26 hand joint keypoints are tracked. After low-pass filtering and smoothing, finger-level curvature (`κf`) is computed, averaged across fingers (`¯κ`), and thresholded to infer the binary grasping state.

**D. Loco-Manipulation Policy Co-Training:**
A single policy is co-trained on the aligned human and robot demonstrations.
- **Policy Architecture:** The state-of-the-art VLA model π_0.5 is finetuned.
- **Inputs/Outputs:** Inputs are egocentric RGB observations (224x224) and language instructions. Outputs are 18-dimensional actions: 12-DoF delta end-effector pose (6-DoF per arm), 3-dimensional discrete locomotion commands, 2-dimensional binary gripper commands, and 1-dimensional delta height command.
- **Proprioceptive States:** Proprioceptive states are intentionally omitted due to incompatible distributions stemming from morphological differences, which would confound co-training.
- **Multi-source Data Sampling:** Due to potential data imbalance (more human data than robot data), balanced sampling ratios are investigated within mini-batches to ensure sufficient exposure to robot demonstrations for manipulation-intensive behaviors, which are particularly susceptible to the embodiment gap. Training is performed on 8 NVIDIA A100 GPUs with mixed-precision, AdamW optimizer, and a cosine decay learning rate schedule.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research conducted real-world experiments on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot across four loco-manipulation tasks: Pillow Placement, Trash Disposal, Toy Transfer, and Cart Stowing. These tasks require coordinated locomotion and manipulation, with navigation accuracy directly impacting manipulation success.

**A. Generalization to Diverse Environments (Q1):**
Co-training with robot-free egocentric human demonstrations significantly improved policy performance, especially in environments unseen during robot-only training.
- **In-Domain Evaluation (Laboratory):** Co-training achieved an average score of 78% compared to 59% for robot-only baselines.
- **Generalization Evaluation (Human-Centric, Unseen by Robot Data):** The performance gap widened considerably, with co-training reaching an average score of 82% against 31% for robot-only policies, representing a 51% gain.
- The generalization performance exceeded in-domain results, indicating that human data effectively bridges the domain gap to novel environments, enabling humanoids to operate in diverse real-world settings without requiring in-the-wild robot data collection.

**B. Analysis of Subskill Transfer (Q2):**
An analysis of task sub-steps revealed differential transfer effectiveness across locomotion and manipulation skills.
- **Navigation:** Human data alone proved highly effective for navigation-dominated subtasks, with a "Human-only" model achieving 100% success on initial locomotion stages. The "Robot-only" model often yielded near-zero success in these areas, confirming the transferability of navigation knowledge from human demonstrations.
- **Manipulation:** Manipulation skills also transferred from human data, but effectiveness was inversely related to the precision required. Human-only models outperformed Robot-only on coarse manipulation subtasks. However, for precision-critical manipulation (e.g., Cart Stowing s2), Human-only achieved only 5% success compared to Robot-only's 15%. Co-training, by combining both data sources, achieved 60% on this subtask, indicating that human data provides beneficial priors that become effective when supplemented with robot demonstrations.
- **Failure Analysis:** A failure mode analysis indicated that Human-only policies exhibited three times more manipulation failures than locomotion failures, while Robot-only failures were more balanced across both phases.

**C. Data Scaling Behavior (Q3):**
Policy performance generally scaled positively with increasing amounts of human demonstration data.
- As human demonstrations increased from 0 (robot-only) to 300 episodes (3x robot data), performance consistently improved in generalized scenarios across all four tasks.
- The optimal sampling ratio between robot and human data during co-training varied with task characteristics:
 - Tasks requiring coarse grasping (e.g., Pillow Placement) benefited from higher human data ratios (e.g., Robot:Human 1:2).
 - Tasks involving fine manipulation (e.g., Toy Transfer, Cart Stowing) favored higher robot data ratios (e.g., Robot:Human 2:1), aligning with the observation that precision-critical manipulation is more sensitive to the embodiment gap.

**D. View Alignment Ablation:**
View alignment was found to be a critical component of the pipeline.
- Ablation studies demonstrated that view alignment consistently improved performance across all tasks. The largest gains were observed in tasks involving objects at varying heights (e.g., Toy Transfer, Cart Stowing), where viewpoint shifts are more pronounced. View transformation, by reprojecting human observations to approximate the robot's lower camera height and introducing pose perturbations, exposes the policy to a wider range of relevant viewpoints during training.

**E. Scene Diversity in Human Data:**
Scene diversity within human demonstrations positively impacted zero-shot generalization.
- For a fixed quantity of robot and human data, increasing the number of distinct human scenes (from 1 to 3) monotonically improved generalization performance in a novel, unseen scene. For instance, in the Trash Disposal task, the average score rose from 57.5% (robot-only) to 82.5% with three diverse human scenes.

**F. Data Collection Efficiency:**
Human demonstrations offered a practical advantage in data collection efficiency.
- Human demonstrations were approximately 2x faster to collect than robot teleoperation, reducing the average per-episode collection time from 62.1 seconds for robots to 39.7 seconds for humans.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The EGOHUMANOID framework represents a significant step towards enabling humanoid robots to operate in complex, unstructured environments by effectively leveraging human expertise. Its significance and potential impact are multifold:

1. **Pioneering Human-to-Humanoid Transfer:** This work provides the first endorsement of human-to-humanoid transfer for whole-body loco-manipulation tasks. It establishes the feasibility of using egocentric human data to enhance humanoid policies through co-training, bridging a critical gap in robot learning.
2. **Scalable Data Sourcing:** By demonstrating that robot-free egocentric human data can effectively complement limited robot teleoperation data, the research offers a scalable pathway to overcome the data scarcity bottleneck in humanoid robot learning. This reduces the reliance on expensive and laborious robot-specific data collection, particularly in diverse, real-world settings.
3. **Enhanced Generalization:** The substantial improvements in generalization performance to "in-the-wild" environments, unseen by robot-only training, indicate that human demonstrations introduce crucial environmental diversity. This capability allows humanoids to operate effectively outside controlled laboratory conditions, moving closer to practical deployment in human-centric spaces.
4. **Principled Embodiment Alignment:** The proposed systematic embodiment alignment pipeline, comprising view transformation and unified action space design, offers practical strategies to address the inherent morphological and perceptual disparities between humans and robots. This principled approach can inform future research on cross-embodiment learning.
5. **Democratization of Humanoid Robotics:** By reducing the need for extensive, specialized robot hardware for data collection, this paradigm has the potential to democratize humanoid robot development. It could enable broader participation from researchers and institutions without access to advanced teleoperation setups, fostering innovation.
6. **Foundation for Future Research:** The study lays groundwork for exploring more advanced applications of egocentric human data, such as pre-training internet-scale egocentric videos for generalist humanoid control. It suggests the potential for "humanoid-embodiment-agnostic learning," where foundation models learn from human behaviors and adapt to various robot platforms with minimal task-specific training.

While acknowledging limitations such as orientation ambiguity in delta end-effector representations, increased data requirements for loco-manipulation, and discrete locomotion commands, this research offers a substantial contribution to the field of embodied AI and humanoid robotics.
