# Openfly: A comprehensive platform for aerial vision-language navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> OpenFly introduces a comprehensive platform for aerial vision-language navigation, providing a large-scale dataset of 100,000 trajectories generated through an automated toolchain and a keyframe-aware VLN model. The platform and model achieved up to a 14.0% increase in success rate on seen scenarios and demonstrated superior real-world performance compared to existing methods.

## Problem

- Existing aerial Vision-Language Navigation (VLN) datasets suffer from limited data diversity and small scale, hindering the development of generalizable models.
- High data collection costs are associated with manual UAV piloting and labor-intensive language instruction annotation.
- There is a need for efficient VLN models to process sequential aerial visual information while managing redundancy.

## Method

- OpenFly integrates diverse rendering engines, including ==Unreal Engine==, GTA V, Google Earth, and 3D Gaussian Splatting, to generate varied and realistic aerial environments.
- An automated data generation toolchain leverages Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and Large Language Models (LLMs) to create 100,000 trajectories with corresponding instructions.
- The OpenFly-Agent, a keyframe-aware VLN model, employs keyframe selection and visual token merging strategies to efficiently process long visual sequences and reduce redundancy.

## Results

- OpenFly released the largest aerial VLN dataset to date, comprising 100,000 trajectories across 18 diverse scenes with varied lengths and flight heights.
- OpenFly-Agent achieved a **34.3%** success rate (SR) and **24.9%** success weighted by path length (SPL) on test-seen scenarios, representing a **14.0%** SR and **7.1%** SPL improvement over the next best method, NaVila.
- Real-world experiments demonstrated OpenFly-Agent's superior performance (**26.09%** SR) and better sim-to-real transfer compared to existing baselines, validating the platform's practical applicability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating diverse rendering technologies, including 3D Gaussian Splatting for real-to-sim reconstruction, is crucial for creating aerial VLN datasets with enhanced realism and environmental diversity.
> - Automating trajectory and instruction generation using VLMs and LLMs significantly scales dataset creation, overcoming the limitations of manual annotation.
> - Intelligent keyframe selection and visual token merging effectively handle redundant visual information in sequential aerial observations, improving navigation efficiency and performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Vision-Language Navigation (VLN) is a core component of embodied AI, focusing on guiding agents to a target location using natural language instructions and visual observations. Previous research in VLN has predominantly concentrated on indoor or ground-based environments, leading to the establishment of various benchmark datasets such as R2R, RxR, TouchDown, REVERIE, CVDN, VLN-CE, and LANI. These efforts have significantly advanced the development of VLN methodologies for ground-level agents.

However, the application of VLN to Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) in aerial spaces, despite its potential for applications in aerial photography, rescue operations, and cargo transport, has remained largely underexplored. Recent works like AerialVLN and OpenUAV have initiated efforts in this direction by leveraging UAV simulators to generate initial datasets for aerial VLN. These initial endeavors have highlighted several limitations, including restricted data diversity due to reliance on specific digital assets (e.g., AirSim, Unreal Engine), high data collection costs associated with manual pilot operation and instruction annotation, and relatively small dataset scales (around 10K trajectories) compared to other embodied manipulation datasets.

OpenFly positions itself within this evolving landscape by directly addressing these identified challenges in aerial VLN. The work aims to overcome the limitations of existing aerial VLN datasets and methodologies by providing a comprehensive platform that integrates diverse rendering technologies, an automated data generation toolchain, and a substantially larger-scale dataset. Furthermore, it introduces a novel VLN model designed to process aerial visual-linguistic information efficiently. By offering an open-source platform comprising tools, datasets, and code, OpenFly seeks to catalyze further research and establish a more robust benchmark for the development of advanced aerial VLN systems capable of operating in complex and varied real-world environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of OpenFly is to address significant challenges hindering the advancement of aerial Vision-Language Navigation (VLN), thereby establishing a more robust foundation for human-UAV interaction. The motivation for this work stems from several critical limitations observed in the existing aerial VLN research landscape:

1. **Limited Data Diversity**: Current aerial VLN approaches predominantly rely on specific UAV simulators, such as AirSim and Unreal Engine. This dependency constrains the types of digital assets and environments available, resulting in datasets with limited diversity. Such limitations hinder the development of generalizable VLN models that can perform effectively across a wide range of real-world aerial scenarios, which often feature varied architectural styles, natural landscapes, and urban complexities.
 - **Objective**: To significantly enhance the diversity of scenario resources for aerial VLN. This is achieved by integrating multiple rendering engines and advanced simulation techniques, including Unreal Engine, Grand Theft Auto V (GTA V), Google Earth, and particularly, 3D Gaussian Splatting (3D GS). The incorporation of 3D GS is aimed at supporting real-to-simulation rendering, thereby increasing the realism and representativeness of the simulated environments.

2. **High Data Collection Cost**: The generation of aerial VLN datasets traditionally involves labor-intensive and time-consuming processes. This includes manual piloting of UAVs within simulators to construct flight trajectories, followed by human annotators manually crafting corresponding language instructions. This manual annotation burden significantly impedes the scalability of dataset creation.
 - **Objective**: To reduce the human effort and cost associated with data collection by developing a highly automated toolchain for aerial VLN data generation. This toolchain automates several critical steps: point cloud acquisition, scene semantic segmentation for landmark identification, collision-free flight trajectory creation, and the generation of linguistic instructions using advanced Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and Large Language Models (LLMs).

3. **Small Data Scale**: Existing aerial VLN datasets are comparatively small, typically comprising around 10,000 trajectories. This scale is considerably smaller than datasets used in other embodied manipulation tasks, which can exceed one million episodes. The limited data volume restricts the capacity of models to learn robust visual-linguistic groundings and complex navigational policies, thereby impeding the development of high-performance and generalizable aerial VLN agents.
 - **Objective**: To construct a large-scale aerial VLN dataset that significantly surpasses the size of existing benchmarks. The goal is to generate 100,000 trajectories across a diverse set of 18 high-quality scenes, encompassing varied altitudes, lengths, and environmental characteristics. This larger scale is intended to provide ample data for training more capable and robust VLN models.

4. **Need for an Efficient Navigation Model**: Given the sequential nature of VLN tasks and the potential for redundant visual information across adjacent frames in aerial footage, there is a need for a model that can process visual history efficiently while focusing on critical observations.
 - **Objective**: To propose OpenFly-Agent, a keyframe-aware VLN model. This model is designed to emphasize critical observations containing instruction-related landmarks and to reduce computational overhead through an adaptive frame-level sampling mechanism and visual token pruning strategies. This aims to improve navigation success rates and efficiency.

In summary, OpenFly is motivated by the need to overcome fundamental data-related barriers in aerial VLN, thereby accelerating research and facilitating the practical deployment of language-guided UAVs in complex real-world environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

OpenFly is presented as a comprehensive platform for aerial Vision-Language Navigation, encompassing diverse rendering engines, an automated data generation toolchain, and a keyframe-aware VLN model.

#### 3.1. Rendering Engines and Data Resources

The platform integrates four distinct rendering technologies to ensure diverse and realistic environments:
- **Unreal Engine (UE5 & UE4)**: Provides eight urban scenes covering over 100 km², featuring varied assets. UnrealCV (for UE5) and AirSim (for UE4) plugins are utilized for UAV control and image acquisition.
- **Grand Theft Auto V (GTA V)**: Contributes a realistic cityscape modeled after Los Angeles, with dynamic elements. A custom interface built with Script Hook V enables data collection.
- **Google Earth**: Offers four urban regions (Berkeley, Osaka, Washington D.C., St. Louis) spanning 53.60 km². Google Earth Studio is employed for route planning and video generation.
- **3D Gaussian Splatting (3D GS)**: Reconstructs real-world environments from UAV data, covering five university campuses (over 7 km²). DJI M30T drones are used for data capture, and a developed HTTP RESTful API based on SIBR viewers renders images from arbitrary poses. This enables "real-to-sim" reconstruction.

To ensure interoperability, unified interfaces are designed for agent movement (with a CoorTrans module for coordinate system unification), Lidar data acquisition (integrating various methods), and image acquisition (via HTTP RESTful and TCP/IP protocols).

#### 3.2. Automated Data Generation Toolchain

This toolchain automates the construction of trajectories and corresponding instructions, minimizing manual effort:
- **3D Point Cloud Acquisition**:
 - *Rasterized Sampling Reconstruction* is used for UE and GTA V scenes, involving customized sampling and stitching of local point clouds.
 - *Image-Based Sparse Reconstruction* is applied to 3D GS scenes, utilizing COLMAP to generate sparse point clouds from input images.
- **Scene Semantic Segmentation**: Identifies meaningful landmarks for navigation.
 - *3D Scene Understanding*: Employs top-down views and the Octree-Graph method to extract semantic 3D instances.
 - *Point Cloud Projection and Contour Extraction*: Projects voxelized point clouds, segments contours, determines instance height, and uses GPT-4o for semantic captioning.
 - *Manual Annotation*: An interface is provided for manual annotation when higher precision or specific segmentation is required.
- **Automatic Trajectory Generation**: Two methods are implemented:
 - *Path search based on customized action space (for UE, GTA V, 3D GS)*: Constructs a global voxel map (M_global) and a bird's eye view (BEV) occupancy map (M_bev). Randomly selects a flight altitude, a landmark as the target, and a collision-free starting and endpoint. An A* pathfinding algorithm generates trajectories using a predefined discrete action space (Forward at 3m, 6m, 9m; Turn Left/Right; Move Up/Down; Stop). Complex trajectories are generated by iteratively selecting the endpoint as a new starting point. UAV-egocentric images are captured along these trajectories.
 - *Path search based on grid (for Google Earth)*: Rasterizes a pre-selected area and collects images from each grid point in all orientations. Trajectories are generated between grid points, and corresponding images are selected from the pre-collected set.
 - The DAgger algorithm is integrated for data augmentation.
- **Automatic Instruction Generation**: Leverages Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and Large Language Models (LLMs) to overcome manual annotation costs.
 - Trajectories are split into sub-trajectories based on action transitions, and key actions with the last three images of each sub-trajectory are extracted.
 - A VLM (e.g., GPT-4o) generates sub-instructions for these key actions and landmarks.
 - An LLM then integrates these sub-instructions into a coherent, complete instruction.
 - **Data Quality Control**: Includes filtering damaged/low-quality data and refining instructions. The NLTK library and sentence-transformer models are used to identify and merge similar landmark descriptions, replacing them with referential pronouns to enhance fluency and reduce redundancy. A manual inspection of 3,000 samples indicated a 91% qualification rate.

#### 3.3. OpenFly-Agent: Keyframe-aware VLN Model

OpenFly-Agent is built upon the OpenVLA baseline, adapting it to handle sequences of images as observation history. Its architecture includes a Vision Encoder, Llama2-7b, an Action Head, and a Grounding Head.
- **Keyframe Selection**: To manage visual redundancy in videos, a strategy is proposed:
 - A heuristic method identifies sudden changes in UAV motion, extracting the corresponding frame and two adjacent frames as candidate keyframes.
 - A landmark grounding module (three cross-attention layers) predicts bounding boxes of instruction-indicated landmarks.
 - Candidate frames with bounding box areas exceeding a threshold (θ) are selected as final keyframes. Ground truth bounding boxes are used during training, and predicted ones during testing.
- **Visual Token Pruning (VTM)**: Further reduces redundancy within keyframes.
 - After visual encoding, similar visual tokens in adjacent keyframes are merged by averaging, with unmerged tokens discarded. The frame with the largest bounding box in a keyframe set serves as a reference.
 - A memory bank (capacity K=2) retains the latest keyframes using a First-In-First-Out (FIFO) policy.
 - Grid pooling compresses visual tokens within each keyframe, except for the current frame, which remains uncompressed to capture immediate observations.
- **Action Decoder**: Discretizes predicted tokens into 6 specific UAV action types: {Forward, Turn Left, Turn Right, Move Up, Move Down, Stop}.
- **Training Details**: The model is trained with a batch size of 64 and a learning rate of 2e-5. The grounding module uses a GIoU loss function, and the keyframe selection threshold θ is set to 0.25 times the input image size.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings regarding the OpenFly platform, its generated dataset, and the performance of the OpenFly-Agent model.

#### 4.1. Dataset Scale and Diversity

- **Scale**: OpenFly has generated a large-scale aerial VLN dataset comprising 100,000 trajectories, making it the largest aerial VLN benchmark to date.
- **Environment Diversity**: The dataset spans 18 distinct scenes, utilizing data from diverse rendering engines: 7 scenes from Unreal Engine (UE), 4 from 3D Gaussian Splatting (3D GS), 4 from Google Earth, and 1 from GTA V. This integration, particularly the real-to-sim reconstruction via 3D GS, significantly enhances environmental diversity and realism.
- **Trajectory Characteristics**: Trajectories exhibit variations in length (0 to 300 meters) and flight height (0 to 210 meters), reflecting a range of aerial navigation challenges. The average trajectory length is 99.1 meters, and instructions average 59 words.
- **Action and Language Analysis**: The dataset incorporates discrete actions (Forward at 3m, 6m, 9m; Turn Left/Right; Move Up/Down; Stop). A vocabulary size of 15.6K words is observed, with common nouns like 'building', 'windows', and 'skyscraper' and verbs such as 'proceed' and 'turn' frequently used, indicating typical navigational cues.
- **Data Quality**: Manual inspection of 3,000 randomly selected samples indicated a 91% qualification rate for instructions, with problematic data often involving vague descriptions but still considered acceptable by examiners.

#### 4.2. Quantitative Performance of OpenFly-Agent

The OpenFly-Agent model was evaluated against several baseline VLN methods on the generated dataset using standard metrics: navigation error (NE), success rate (SR), oracle success rate (OSR), and success weighted by path length (SPL).
- **Performance on Test Seen Scenarios**: OpenFly-Agent achieved a success rate (SR) of 34.3% and an SPL of 24.9%, with an NE of 93m. This represents a substantial improvement of 14.0% in SR and 7.1% in SPL compared to the next best method, NaVila (SR 20.3%, SPL 17.8%).
- **Performance on Test Unseen Scenarios**: On unseen environments, OpenFly-Agent maintained its leading performance with an SR of 22.6% and an SPL of 19.1%, and an NE of 154m. This improvement translates to an SR increase of 7.9% and an SPL increase of 9.5% over NaVila (SR 14.7%, SPL 9.6%). While all methods experienced performance degradation on unseen data, OpenFly-Agent demonstrated improved generalization capabilities.

#### 4.3. Real-World Experiments

- **Deployment**: Real-world experiments were conducted in 23 diverse outdoor scenes using a Q250 drone equipped with an NVIDIA Jetson Xavier NX. The drone utilized the "Super" trajectory planning framework and Model Predictive Control (MPC) for local planning and trajectory tracking.
- **Results**: OpenFly-Agent achieved a 26.09% SR and 34.78% OSR in real-world settings, outperforming Navid and NaVila.
- **Sim-to-Real Transfer**: Training OpenFly-Agent exclusively on the OpenFly dataset yielded superior real-world performance compared to training on the AerialVLN dataset, indicating the effectiveness of OpenFly's data generation methodology in bridging the sim-to-real gap.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study

An ablation study on the test-seen split examined the contributions of keyframe selection (KS) and visual token merging (VTM) to OpenFly-Agent's performance:
- **Baseline (OpenVLA)**: Achieved a low SR of 2.3%.
- **History (uniform frame sampling)**: Improved SR to 6.9%.
- **History + VTM**: Significantly increased SR to 16.6%, demonstrating the benefit of visual token merging.
- **Keyframe Selection (KS) only**: Showed a moderate improvement to 9.2% SR.
- **OpenFly-Agent (KS + VTM)**: Achieved the highest SR of 34.3%, confirming that the combination of keyframe selection and visual token merging is crucial for performance. This indicates that intelligently selecting key observations and compressing redundant visual information effectively addresses challenges posed by long visual sequences and prevents dilution of cross-modal signals.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The OpenFly platform represents a notable contribution to the field of aerial Vision-Language Navigation (VLN), with several significant implications and potential impacts:

1. **Addressing Core Bottlenecks in Aerial VLN**: OpenFly directly tackles the fundamental challenges of data scarcity, limited diversity, and high collection costs that have previously hindered the progress of aerial VLN. By providing a comprehensive solution for large-scale, automated data generation, it removes significant practical barriers to research and development in this domain.

2. **Enabling Large-Scale Model Training**: The creation of a 100,000-trajectory dataset, significantly larger than previous aerial VLN benchmarks, provides an unprecedented resource for training advanced vision-language models. This scale is crucial for developing robust, generalizable, and high-performing aerial navigation agents, akin to the advancements seen in other data-intensive embodied AI tasks.

3. **Enhancing Realism and Generalizability**: The integration of diverse rendering engines, including Unreal Engine, GTA V, Google Earth, and particularly 3D Gaussian Splatting for real-to-sim reconstruction, substantially broadens the environmental complexity and realism of the generated data. This diversity is essential for training models that can generalize effectively from simulated environments to unpredictable real-world aerial scenarios, thereby improving their practical applicability.

4. **Establishing a New Benchmark**: The OpenFly dataset, alongside the performance achieved by OpenFly-Agent, establishes a new, rigorous benchmark for evaluating future aerial VLN methods. This provides a standardized platform for comparison, fostering innovation and accelerating the development of more sophisticated navigation algorithms.

5. **Advancing VLM Capabilities for Video Data**: The proposed OpenFly-Agent, with its keyframe selection and visual token merging strategies, offers a methodological contribution to the broader field of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) processing sequential visual data. These techniques for efficiently handling long contextual observations and reducing redundancy can be beneficial for various video-based VLM applications beyond aerial navigation.

6. **Paving the Way for Practical UAV Applications**: The demonstrated success of OpenFly-Agent in real-world experiments, including superior performance over existing methods and effective sim-to-real transfer, indicates a tangible step towards practical deployment. This progress has the potential to enable more intuitive and versatile human-UAV interaction for applications such as autonomous aerial photography, search and rescue operations, inspection, and cargo delivery, where natural language guidance could enhance operational flexibility and safety.

7. **Fostering Open Science and Collaboration**: The commitment to open-sourcing the toolchain, dataset, and code is a significant impact factor. This will facilitate community engagement, allow researchers worldwide to generate custom data for specific needs, and accelerate collective advancements in embodied AI and robotics. It promotes a collaborative environment for addressing the remaining challenges, such as further improving generalization to entirely unseen environments.
