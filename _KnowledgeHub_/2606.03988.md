# Imaginative Perception Tokens Enhance Spatial Reasoning in Multimodal Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Imaginative Perception Tokens (IPTs) allow multimodal language models to reason about unobserved spatial structures by simulating alternative configurations, leading to improved performance on challenging tasks. This method achieves 67.3% accuracy on Multiview Counting and enhances Perspective Taking and Path Tracing, even without explicit imagination generation during inference.

## Problem

- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) frequently struggle with spatial reasoning, particularly when critical information is not directly observable.
- Existing intermediate visual representations primarily focus on refining or visualizing already visible structure, failing to predict missing or implied spatial information.
- Many spatial tasks require a form of 'imaginative perception,' such as simulating unseen viewpoints or integrating partial observations, a capability currently lacking in VLMs.

## Method

- Introducing **Imaginative Perception Tokens** (IPTs) as intermediate visual representations that externalize what a VLM would perceive under an alternative spatial configuration (e.g., novel viewpoint, top-down map).
- Formulating three novel spatial reasoning tasks—Perspective Taking, Path Tracing, and Multiview Counting—that intrinsically demand imaginative perception for their resolution.
- Training a unified multimodal language model (BAGEL) to generate these IPTs using a ==flow-matching objective==, complemented by a language modeling loss for the final answer.

## Results

- IPT training consistently outperformed baselines, achieving **67.3%** accuracy on Multiview Counting and strong results on Perspective Taking (**87.7%** on Habitat with Mixed Training) and Path Tracing (**66.7%** synthetic, **58.6%** real).
- Increasing the resolution of generated IPTs from 64x64 to 1024x1024 substantially improved downstream accuracy, for example, on PET (from **87.4%** to **96.8%**) and MVC (from **53.5%** to **63.1%**).
- Training with the proposed datasets led to improved performance on external spatial benchmarks, with Mixed Training improving SAT (perspective-taking) accuracy from **34.9%** to **63.6%** and MessyTable (multiview counting) from **29.0%** to **37.0%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - IPT training significantly improves VLM performance on spatial reasoning tasks that require inferring unobserved structures, demonstrating the effectiveness of visual intermediates.
> - Supervision with IPTs fosters stronger internal spatial representations within models, as performance benefits persist even when explicit image generation is not performed during inference.
> - Textual Chain-of-Thought (CoT) approaches are less effective for inherently spatial tasks compared to IPTs, highlighting a modality mismatch where complex spatial relationships are difficult to serialize accurately into language.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) has achieved proficiency across numerous tasks, yet spatial reasoning remains a persistent challenge. Existing literature highlights that current VLMs frequently struggle with problems requiring an understanding of three-dimensional environments, transformations under viewpoint changes, or the integration of partial observations into a coherent scene representation. Benchmarks such as SpatialSense, VSR, What'sUp, 3DSRBench, ViewSpatial-Bench, VSI-Bench, MMSI-Bench, MindCube, and Counting Stacked Objects have systematically identified and documented these limitations, revealing a "perspective gap" where models succeed in camera-centered views but fail when asked to adopt human-centered viewpoints or maintain consistent spatial state across multiple observations.

Prior research has attempted to address reasoning limitations by incorporating intermediate representations. Approaches like Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting facilitate multi-step reasoning through language. Other methods introduce visual intermediates, such as "visual thoughts" (e.g., Visual Sketchpad, MVoT, ThinkMorph) or structured perceptual tokens representing depth maps or bounding boxes (e.g., Mirage, Mull-Tokens). While these techniques demonstrate the utility of intermediate steps, they primarily focus on refining or visualizing structure *already visible* in the input.

This work distinguishes itself by targeting spatial reasoning problems that fundamentally require modeling *unobserved* spatial structure. It draws an analogy to human imagination, where individuals mentally simulate unseen viewpoints or integrate partial observations to form a unified spatial understanding. The paper argues that current intermediate representations are insufficient for such tasks because they do not predict *missing* spatial information.

Architecturally, the research leverages unified multimodal models (e.g., Chameleon, Show-o2, Janus) that are designed to handle interleaved text and image understanding and generation within a single framework. The chosen backbone, BAGEL, exemplifies this class of models, possessing the generative capabilities necessary to produce visual intermediates. This approach positions the work as an advancement in equipping VLMs with a mechanism for "imaginative perception" to infer and externalize implied spatial structures, thereby complementing existing efforts that primarily operate on directly observed visual information.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observation that while Vision-Language Models (VLMs) excel in many areas, they exhibit significant deficiencies in spatial reasoning, particularly when the crucial information is not directly observable in the input. Many spatial challenges necessitate a form of "imaginative perception"—the ability to mentally simulate an unseen viewpoint, trace a trajectory through an occluded area, or synthesize multiple partial views into a cohesive spatial map. Humans routinely employ such imaginative processes to navigate and comprehend their physical environment.

The paper identifies a gap in existing VLM approaches to spatial reasoning. Previous methods that incorporate intermediate visual representations (e.g., visual thoughts, depth maps, bounding box tokens) primarily focus on refining or structuring information that is already present and discernible within the input. These intermediates generally do not aim to predict or construct spatial structures that are *not directly observable* but are *implied* by the available evidence. For instance, they might enhance the understanding of visible objects but do not inherently simulate how a scene would appear from a completely novel, unobserved perspective.

To address this limitation, the key objectives of this work are:

1. **Introduce Imaginative Perception Tokens (IPT):** To conceptualize and implement intermediate perceptual representations that externalize what a VLM would perceive under an *alternative spatial configuration*. These tokens are designed to represent unobserved spatial structure (e.g., a rotated view, an integrated top-down map) while remaining consistent with the observed input.
2. **Formulate Novel Spatial Reasoning Tasks:** To develop and formalize three distinct tasks that intrinsically demand imaginative perception for their resolution:
 - **Perspective Taking (PET):** Requires predicting how a scene would appear from a new viewpoint, given a single first-person observation and a proposed movement/rotation.
 - **Path Tracing (PT):** Involves inferring what an agent would observe along a navigation path, based on a top-down map and limited egocentric views.
 - **Multiview Counting (MVC):** Necessitates integrating multiple partial observations (first-person frames) into a unified spatial representation to accurately count objects, resolving occlusions and cross-view duplicates.
3. **Construct Comprehensive Datasets and Benchmarks:** For each of the proposed tasks, to create large-scale datasets (approximately 20,000 examples per task) spanning both simulated (e.g., AI2-THOR, Habitat) and real-world (e.g., Visual Spatial Tuning, Matterport3D, MessyTable, ScanNet++) environments. These datasets are explicitly designed with ground-truth intermediate imaginations alongside final answers, and are coupled with human-filtered benchmarks for robust evaluation.
4. **Empirically Evaluate IPT Effectiveness:** To demonstrate, using a unified VLM (BAGEL) as a backbone, that supervision with IPTs can improve spatial reasoning performance across various settings. A specific objective is to compare IPT training against answer-only supervision and textual Chain-of-Thought (CoT) approaches, including scenarios where no explicit image generation occurs during inference, to assess the development of stronger *internal* spatial representations.
5. **Analyze Modality Mismatch:** To investigate the relative effectiveness of visual (IPT) versus textual (CoT) intermediate representations for spatial tasks, particularly highlighting potential detriments of forcing inherently spatial computations through language.

In essence, the motivation is to bridge the gap between current VLM capabilities and the demands of complex spatial reasoning by endowing models with a form of computationally supported "imagination," enabling them to reason about implied, rather than just observed, spatial structures.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of this research is the introduction and application of Imaginative Perception Tokens (IPT) within Multimodal Language Models (MLLMs) to facilitate spatial reasoning over unobserved structures.

#### 3.1. Problem Formalization and Architecture

The problem is framed as a two-stage generative process. Given an input context `C` (observed images `I_obs` and a spatial language query `Q`), the model first generates imaginative perception tokens `Î_imag`, representing the implied spatial structure (e.g., a novel viewpoint). This is modeled as `P(Î_imag | I_obs, Q)`. Second, conditioned on `I_obs`, `Q`, and the generated `Î_imag`, the model produces the final answer `A`, modeled as `P(A | I_obs, Q, Î_imag)`.

The implementation utilizes **BAGEL**, a unified decoder-only transformer, as the architectural backbone. BAGEL employs a Mixture-of-Transformer-Experts (MoT) design, with dedicated experts for multimodal understanding and generation, both interacting through shared self-attention. Images are processed via a SigLIP2 ViT encoder for understanding tokens (U) and a FLUX VAE for generation tokens (G), enabling seamless text-image interleaving. The generative capacity, typically for open-ended image synthesis, is repurposed in this work for the precise generation of view imaginations that represent unobserved 3D scene structure.

#### 3.2. Spatial Imagination Tasks and Datasets

Three novel spatial reasoning tasks are introduced, each requiring the construction of a missing spatial representation:

1. **Perspective Taking (PET):**
 - **Task:** Answer a spatial question about a scene from a new viewpoint (e.g., "will the object be closer or further?") given a single first-person view and marked target positions. This requires simulating spatial transformation.
 - **Sub-categories:** Six balanced sub-categories covering distance change (closer/further) and relative position (left→left, left→right, right→left, right→right).
 - **IPT Target:** A ground-truth novel-viewpoint rendering of the scene from the target position.
 - **Data:** A training set of 55,529 examples combining synthetic data from AI2-THOR and Habitat with real-world examples from the Visual Spatial Tuning dataset. Evaluation is performed on human-verified AI2-THOR (238 examples) and Habitat (300 examples) benchmarks.
2. **Path Tracing (PT):**
 - **Task:** Identify an object visible on a queried side at a midpoint (M1) along a marked path (1 → 2), given a top-down map and egocentric forward views at waypoints 1 and 2. This requires imagining first-person visibility at an unobserved midpoint.
 - **Input Settings:** Evaluated under three settings: Path (map only), PathArr (map + query direction arrow), and EgoDir (map + egocentric endpoint views).
 - **IPT Target:** A ground-truth first-person sideview image from M1.
 - **Data:** A synthetic training set of 11,204 examples from AI2-THOR and ProcTHOR. Evaluation is on human-verified AI2-THOR (329 examples) and a real-world benchmark from Matterport3D (332 examples).
3. **Multiview Counting (MVC):**
 - **Task:** Count queried objects by integrating several first-person frames of the same environment, necessitating the construction of a unified spatial representation to resolve occlusions and duplicates.
 - **IPT Target:** A ground-truth top-down bird’s-eye view (BEV) map aggregating all input views.
 - **Data:** A training set of 19,499 examples combining synthetic data from AI2-THOR with real-world data from MessyTable and ScanNet++. Evaluation is on a human-verified benchmark of 260 samples.

#### 3.3. Training and Inference

- **Training Objective:** The model is optimized using a multi-task loss `L_total = λ_fm * L_fm + λ_lm * L_lm`.
 - **Flow-Matching Loss (L_fm):** For IPT generation, BAGEL learns to predict the velocity field `v_t` required to transform Gaussian noise into the target VAE latent `G_gt` (Rectified Flow method).
 - **Language Modeling Loss (L_lm):** Minimizes the negative log-likelihood of the final VQA answer tokens `A`, conditioned on the observed context and the ground-truth imaginative tokens.
- **Inference:**
 - **Text-only mode:** The model directly produces a textual answer without generating any visual intermediate, serving as a baseline.
 - **Imagination mode:** The model first performs iterative denoising to generate the imaginative latent `Î_imag`. This decoded image `Î_imag` is then re-encoded and appended to the context as both ViT understanding tokens and VAE generation tokens. The model then attends to its self-generated imagination to predict the final answer.
- **Model Variants:** Experiments are conducted with several fine-tuned BAGEL configurations:
 - `Bagel (base)`: Pretrained model, no task-specific fine-tuning.
 - `Bagel (label-only)`: Fine-tuned with answer supervision only.
 - `+ Text CoT`: Trained to generate a textual chain-of-thought (generated by GPT-5.1 using ground-truth metadata) before answering.
 - `+ IPT`: Trained to generate an intermediate image (IPT) before answering.
 - `+ Mixed Training`: Trained on a mixture of IPT examples (image-generation targets) and label-only examples (answer supervision only).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted on the three spatial reasoning tasks: Perspective Taking (PET), Path Tracing (PT), and Multiview Counting (MVC), primarily using the AI2-THOR subset for training, with evaluation on cross-environment and real-world benchmarks.

#### 4.1. Spatial Reasoning Difficulty for Baselines

- Current VLMs and unified models generally exhibited difficulty with the proposed spatial reasoning tasks. Among zero-shot baselines, GPT-5 was the strongest but still lagged behind the best fine-tuned variants. Smaller open VLM models (e.g., InternVL3.5-8B, Qwen2.5-VL-7B) performed near chance on PET and struggled on PT, suggesting the tasks require more than superficial cues. Unified models like Chameleon 7B performed notably worse on some tasks, indicating a potential trade-off between generation capability and understanding robustness in current designs.

#### 4.2. Impact of Answer-Only Supervision

- Fine-tuning BAGEL with label-only answer supervision yielded substantial improvements over the base pretrained model. For instance, on AI2-THOR PET, accuracy rose from 40.3% to 97.5%. Similar gains were observed on PT (29.9% to 65.7%) and MVC (35.4% to 63.9%). These improvements also transferred to new environments, with label-only reaching 82.0% on Habitat PET, indicating the learnability and generalization of spatial reasoning from simulation.

#### 4.3. Effectiveness of Imagination Supervision (IPT)

- **Outperformance:** IPT training consistently improved performance on tasks where language is a suboptimal interface. On MVC, IPT achieved the best accuracy (67.3%), surpassing label-only (63.9%) and Text CoT (62.3%). For different-environment PET (Habitat), IPT achieved 87.0%, further improved to 87.7% with Mixed Training. On PT, Mixed Training achieved the best results on both synthetic (66.7%) and real (58.6%) benchmarks.
- **Internal Representation:** A significant finding was that IPT benefits persisted even when the model was evaluated in an answer-only mode (without explicit image generation at inference). This suggests that training with imagination targets helps models develop stronger *internal* spatial representations, which then contribute to improved spatial reasoning capabilities.
- **Text CoT Limitations:** Text CoT generally underperformed both label-only and IPT training. For example, on PET, Text CoT achieved 83.1% compared to IPT's 96.8%. This gap is attributed to a modality mismatch; complex spatial relationships (like viewpoint changes, occlusions, cross-view correspondences) are difficult to serialize accurately into natural language, and textual traces can introduce noise.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Latent Resolution:** Increasing the resolution of the generated imaginative perception tokens (from Latent-4 at 64x64 to Latent-64 at 1024x1024) significantly improved downstream accuracy. For AI2-THOR PET, accuracy increased from 87.4% to 96.8%, and for MVC, from 53.5% to 63.1%. This indicates that imagination quality directly impacts reasoning. However, very high resolutions (Latent-64) showed a slight drop in Habitat PET, potentially due to overfitting to AI2-THOR appearance statistics.
- **Thought Modality and Inference Mode:**
 - IPT training built stronger spatial representations than Text CoT, evidenced by better performance of IPT with answer-only inference (e.g., PT 61.1% vs. Text CoT 55.8%).
 - Explicit imagination generation at inference time was not always necessary. For IPT models, answer-only inference often outperformed generating the image explicitly (e.g., PT 61.1% answer-only vs. 50.4% with generation). This suggests that imperfect model-generated imaginations can sometimes mislead, but the training signal itself is valuable.
 - Providing ground-truth imaginations at inference time revealed significant headroom, particularly for PT (accuracy jumped from 50.4% to 86.7%), indicating that imagination quality remains a bottleneck for some tasks.

#### 4.5. Transfer to External Benchmarks

- Training with the proposed datasets improved performance on external benchmarks testing similar spatial capabilities. Mixed Training on PET tasks improved SAT (perspective-taking) accuracy from 34.9% (base) to 63.6%. On MessyTable (multiview counting), Mixed Training reached 37.0% from 29.0% (base).
- Fine-tuning on AI2-THOR MVC data using only answer supervision also improved performance on other spatial tasks like ScanNet (multiview counting), MindCube (abstract geometric reasoning), and All-Angles-Bench (cross-view matching), suggesting that the learned spatial representations are broadly useful, even if the specific IPT target format does not directly transfer.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces Imaginative Perception Tokens (IPTs) as a novel and principled approach to supervise spatial reasoning in multimodal language models, specifically addressing problems that require inferring or constructing *unobserved* spatial structures. This is a significant contribution given that existing VLM approaches primarily focus on visible information.

The key significance and potential impact include:

1. **Enhanced Spatial Reasoning Capabilities:** The work demonstrates that training VLMs with IPTs substantially improves their ability to perform complex spatial reasoning tasks, such as Perspective Taking, Path Tracing, and Multiview Counting. This directly addresses a known limitation of current VLMs, pushing the boundaries of what these models can infer about 3D environments.
2. **Development of Stronger Internal Representations:** A crucial finding is that the benefits of IPT supervision can manifest even when models do not explicitly generate intermediate images during inference. This suggests that IPT training helps models develop more robust and generalized *internal* spatial representations, enabling them to reason more effectively without necessarily externalizing every step. This has implications for efficiency and real-world deployment where explicit image generation might be computationally expensive.
3. **Addressing Modality Mismatch:** The research highlights the limitations of textual Chain-of-Thought (CoT) for inherently spatial tasks, where serializing geometric transformations and occlusions into language can be awkward and error-prone. IPTs offer a more natural and aligned modality for representing such spatial information, demonstrating that visual intermediates can be more effective than textual ones for certain types of reasoning.
4. **Creation of Novel Datasets:** The introduction of three new, large-scale datasets (PET, PT, MVC) with ground-truth intermediate imaginations is a valuable resource for the research community. These datasets provide a standardized framework for training and evaluating models on imaginative perception, fostering further research in this area.
5. **Interpretability and Alignment:** IPTs offer a more interpretable intermediate step that is directly aligned with the underlying geometry of the task. Externalizing the model's perception under alternative configurations provides insights into its reasoning process, which can be crucial for debugging and improving AI systems.
6. **Broader Applications:** The ability of models to "imagine" and reason about unobserved spatial configurations has potential implications for various applications in AI. This could include advancements in robotics (e.g., navigation in unknown or partially observed environments), augmented and virtual reality (e.g., predicting scene changes or object placements), complex scene understanding, and even human-computer interaction where systems need to anticipate spatial changes.

Overall, this work moves beyond simply recognizing objects and attributes in images towards endowing VLMs with a foundational capacity for mental simulation, a capability that is central to human spatial intelligence.
