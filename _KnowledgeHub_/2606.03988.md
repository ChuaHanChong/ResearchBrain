# Imaginative Perception Tokens Enhance Spatial Reasoning in Multimodal Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Imaginative Perception Tokens (IPTs) allow multimodal language models to reason about unobserved spatial structures by simulating alternative configurations, leading to improved performance on challenging tasks. This method achieves 67.3% accuracy on Multiview Counting and enhances Perspective Taking and Path Tracing, even without explicit imagination generation during inference.

## Problem

- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) frequently struggle with spatial reasoning, particularly when critical information is not directly observable.
- Existing intermediate visual representations primarily focus on refining or visualizing already visible structure, failing to predict missing or implied spatial information.
- Many spatial tasks require a form of 'imaginative perception,' such as simulating unseen viewpoints or integrating partial observations, a capability currently lacking in VLMs.

## Method

- Introducing **Imaginative Perception Tokens** (IPTs) as intermediate visual representations that externalize what a VLM would perceive under an alternative spatial configuration (e.g., novel viewpoint, top-down map).
- Formulating three novel spatial reasoning tasks—Perspective Taking, Path Tracing, and Multiview Counting—that intrinsically demand imaginative perception for their resolution.
- Training a unified multimodal language model (BAGEL) to generate these IPTs using a ==flow-matching objective==, complemented by a language modeling loss for the final answer.

## Results

- IPT training consistently outperformed baselines, achieving **67.3%** accuracy on Multiview Counting and strong results on Perspective Taking (**87.7%** on Habitat with Mixed Training) and Path Tracing (**66.7%** synthetic, **58.6%** real).
- Increasing the resolution of generated IPTs from 64x64 to 1024x1024 substantially improved downstream accuracy, for example, on PET (from **87.4%** to **96.8%**) and MVC (from **53.5%** to **63.1%**).
- Training with the proposed datasets led to improved performance on external spatial benchmarks, with Mixed Training improving SAT (perspective-taking) accuracy from **34.9%** to **63.6%** and MessyTable (multiview counting) from **29.0%** to **37.0%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - IPT training significantly improves VLM performance on spatial reasoning tasks that require inferring unobserved structures, demonstrating the effectiveness of visual intermediates.
> - Supervision with IPTs fosters stronger internal spatial representations within models, as performance benefits persist even when explicit image generation is not performed during inference.
> - Textual Chain-of-Thought (CoT) approaches are less effective for inherently spatial tasks compared to IPTs, highlighting a modality mismatch where complex spatial relationships are difficult to serialize accurately into language.
